Sins commited and then paid. But in full?

10 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5.10.03 May 10, 2003, the streets of Dallas. Not so silent, the streets of Dallas bustle with an ever growing cities nightlife. Engines and voices, radios and horns, mixed with the nonstop sounds of foot steps echo through the night. With dozens of cars and even more people attempting to make their way down or across the street, a traffic jam has slowed the normally rushed Dallas roads to match the speed of a tired snail. A cherry red Corvette is in the center of the traffic jam, its paint glistening with the reflections of the city lights and the headlights of other vehicles. “Damn, I hate this city. I’ve got to get back to Boston soon or I’m going to go nuts!” The thought is heard above all else, the other sounds muffled in comparison. The lights from the Corvette blare through the night, bending and bouncing off of the metallic surfaces of the other cars around. We zoom in closer, the black convertible top already lowered so that we can see inside of the vehicle. Black leather seats are reminders of the money spent on the automobile, the stereo system and other electronic enhancements inside the car remind one of the same. A GPS system on the dash as well as an in-dash 6 CD changer are also reminders. The driver, a road weary Tyler Pierce, sits with his elbow atop the door and his head leaning partially out the window. A break in the traffic would be a God send at this moment. The sounds of angry motorists are heard from all around, people screaming and slamming on their horns. “Won’t these fleas shut the hell up” Fucking plebeians!” Road rage had always been one of Tyler’s imperfections and it always would be. Tyler, however, would never see it this way. You see, to Tyler, road rage was simply a common ailment that haunts all of the world’s motorists. The boon was simply that Tyler never let it get the best of him, until tonight. Tonight, his 2002 cherry red Chevrolet Corvette would be the victim of road rage and the straw that broke the camels back. The nearby motorists continued to scream as Tyler sat there, staring at the red brake lights ahead of him, brake lights that never seemed to move. Lying in the passenger seat was a contract from Global Wrestling. It had just been signed by some Global Wrestling crack lawyer. Their meeting had been rather simple. Infact it was so simple, Pierce found himself on the other end of an experience he had taken place in many times before and he wasn’t too pleased with it. Our scene fades to black and then back in. May 10, 2003, earlier that evening in the Global Arena Office of Salaok. Tyler Pierce is seated in what looks to be a rather uncomfortable brown chair. He’s dressed the same as later; a pair of blue jeans, white tennis shoes, and a white t-shirt. It doesn’t seem that Tyler found this situation warranting anything more or anything less. A grunt can be heard from Tyler as he shifts in the chair and crosses his legs. Patience doesn’t seem to be much of a virtue for Tyler. Finally he stands and turns the corner, making his way towards a secretary seated behind a desk in the adjacent room. She glances up at Tyler and smiles. “Mr. Salaok will be with you shortly, Mr. Pierce.” Without speaking, she knew his intentions in returning to her presence. He nods and turns again to make his way back into the office. He takes a seat and glances about the office, his eyes cutting from one distinctive object to the next. “Strange guy?” the words are muttered more than spoken, nearly inaudible. Tyler leans back, resting his head against the wall. The clock reads 12:15 am. The clock reads 2:30 pm. Tyler pushes himself up, now visibly angered. He walks back towards the secretaries desk and, before he’s able to speak, the secretary again answers his query. “Mr. Salaok has been delayed. Please continue to wait in his office, he should be here shortly.” This time, Tyler comments however. “You realize I’ve been here for three hours now?” The words go unheard as the secretary’s eyes divert to the magazine on her desk. A mutter escapes the lips of Tyler as he jerks around, walking back into the office. He takes a seat. The clock reads 5:00 pm. A short, balding, man walks into the office and leans over the desk to pick up a pen. Tyler sits up and speaks quietly. “Mr. Salaok?” The man just shakes his head and puts his black leather suitcase on the desk. He quietly opens the suitcase and pulls out a medium sized stack of papers and quickly begins filling through them. He removes four of them, lining them up on the desk and then places a pen atop the two in the center. His obsessive compulsion shows through with his attention to detail. The papers are perfectly lined up and the pen is perfectly parallel to the top of the pages. It’s also perfectly in the center of the two pages, dividing the pen perfectly. Tyler stands and approaches the desk as the short man walks behind it quietly. With a shrug, he lifts the pen and begins signing one and then another. Upon finishing the fourth, he tosses the pen onto the desk. The short man quickly grabs it and realigns it with the other papers. “Mr. Pierce. You are hereby inducted onto the roster of Global Wrestling. Your first match will be on the sixteenth of the fifth month, in the year two thousand and three against fellow roster patron Chaz Bryant. You will be expected to show up for your match. If you fail to do so, your contract becomes null and void. If you do appear on the sixteenth, your contract will officially be verified by President Salaok. Thank you for your time, Mr. Pierce.” The words are spewed off as if this man were reading the disclaimer on a radio broadcast. Tyler stands there for a split second, soaking the information in before glancing down at the four papers on the desk. He speaks slowly, almost mocking the lawyer. “So, this is it” I thought I had a meeting with Mr. Salaok. And this is it? I sit here for five and a half fucking hours when this was all I was here to do!” This is fucked up.” The man, once again in his no nonsense tone and quick paced words speaks to Tyler. “I apologize for the inconvenience sir. You will not be meeting Mr. Salaok today. Thank you for your time.” The sudden realization that this man sounds much like an auctioneer strikes Tyler. “Ever thought of selling expensive art?” The words are mocking and sarcastic. “I used to sir.” The words make the ones before it null. Tyler shakes his head and grabs his jacket, quickly making his way out towards the door. “Well, isn’t this a bunch of shit?” May 10, 2003, the streets of Dallas. Tyler is where first we saw him, seated in his car waiting for a break in traffic. Tyler Pierce stares at the car in front of him with thoughts of the torturous wait in Salaok’s office echoing through his mind thus building his frustration even more as he’s now been waiting for a total of six hours with no sign of an end in sight. A horn sounds from behind, startling Tyler. He jumps slightly, growing more agitated with every minute. “No” just stay calm, Ty, stay calm.” His sense of determination to keep his cool is strong in this situation; the question is, is it strong enough? A break in the traffic comes when the light turns green a few yards ahead. Cars begin to flow more easily and Tyler’s Corvette begins its move forward. Suddenly, the car ahead of Tyler’s stops and slams on its brakes, causing Tyler to slam on his. Some imbecile made a run across the street, dodging cars and this was the result. “Fuck!” is a response quickly shouted out by Tyler. It would be tripled moments later. Suddenly, a large Dodge Ram 4X4 slams into the back of Tyler’s Vette, sending that slamming into the car ahead of him. Tyler’s forehead slams into the steering wheel and the airbag deploys, knocking him back against the seat. The monotonous sound of a horn blasts into the night air, increasing the tension ten fold. The sound of a door opening is head behind us and then Tyler’s door swings open. He slips out on the asphalt, a small cut on his forehead from the broken windshield. “What the hell are you doing?” is muttered from Tyler but no one can hear it. The man from the car in front of Tyler gets out and begins to speak but is cut off by the man who started this catastrophe. “Sir!” Are you ok!” Sorry, I hit the wrong pedal!” The wrong pedal? The wrong fucking pedal? Tyler slowly pushes himself up, the tension in his body and mind snapping suddenly. He tilts his head and cracks his neck, the pop sounding loudly in the area. He turns, glancing towards the source of the voice. A large man with a beer gut is standing there. He’s dressed in a pair of jeans, cowboy boots, a wife beater and a cowboy hat. A stupid expression sets off the clich?. He’s a pure, unadulterated, uninhibited, unquestionable redneck. And Tyler stands before him, a time bomb with a fuse lit hours before. “You’re telling me that you hit the wrong fucking pedal” You hit the wrong damned pedal and you destroyed my Corvette!” WHAT KIND OF FUCKING IDIOT ARE YOU!”” The voice trails off through the night, silencing everything aside from the motors and the horn inside of the Corvette. The man takes a few steps towards Tyler, trying to calm the situation by apologizing in his Texan dialect. Tyler merely twitches, a sign of anger in him, and slams his fist hard into the man’s beer belly. “I HIT THE WRONG FUCKING THING!” The comment mocks the statements made before. The Dallas local buckles with the punch and Tyler follows up, grabbing the wife beater by the waist near his back and jerks it up and over his head, tying his arms up with it. With this, Tyler twists the wife beater around the arms of the man, tightening the hold and replaces the hat to the man’s head. The reckless driver shakes off the hit and the quick movements of the much smaller man but only in time for Tyler to spin and back hand him, knocking him flat on the road with a bloody nose. Tyler begins screaming at the man, one hand still keeping the shirt tied about the man’s arms to keep him from moving. A stiff kick to the gut causes him to jerk. “Who the fuck is going to pay for my car!” You think your insurance will cover all of this? You fucking life isn’t even worth what that car was worth! You son of a fucking bitch! I should just take it out of your ass right here and now!” The thought hadn’t really struck Tyler until he realized he said it. Once it did, the consequences didn’t process with it. Tyler grabs the man by the shoulders and pulls him up and quickly shoves him against the blue Dodge truck. He jumps and slams the heel of his shoe into his face, knocking him against the truck and then to the hard and unforgiving ground. He tries to push himself up but not before a heel can be brought down against the back of his head. Motorists all around the area have left their vehicles, surrounding the two men as Tyler systematically beats the hell out of him. He quickly opens the cab door of the truck and pulls the man up, placing his head against the door mount and begins slamming the heavy metal door against the side of his head. He coughs, blood dripping from his mouth upon the last and falls backwards, sitting on his knees directly next to the truck. With a quick movement, Tyler slams the door open and into the face of the cowboy, knocking him backwards and completely unconscious. Two motorists quickly grab Tyler and pull him back before he can send another kick to the man, restraining him. Within moments, the police have arrived on the scene. We fade to black as they approach Tyler Pierce. May 11, 2003, a Dallas Police Precinct. Tyler Pierce is seated on a bench inside of a small square jail cell. Iron bars surround him on four sides, a concrete barrier below and above him. He’s surrounded by dozens of other similar cells, all located in the basement of a Police Department in Dallas, Texas. He looks as if he’s not slept in days, his hair is a mess and his clothes are dirty. Blood has dried on his right hand where a diamond studded ring had once been placed, now confiscated by the police officers who booked him. He stares at the door to the cell, his expression not rigid nor is it angered. He has an expression of remorse and guilt. Voices are heard from a distance, somewhat echoing in the empty halls beneath the department. Only a few other persons are being held here in this containment center and those are asleep. After all, it is only 7:00 am. “How much is his bail, officer?” “His bail has not been set, Mr?”” “My name is not important. When will it be set?” “The bail hearing is in an hour, sir. I can ca” sir? Sir? Where are you going? sir?” Tyler continues to stare straight ahead. Moments later, a police officer approaches the jail cell that holds Tyler Pierce. He glances up at him and stands as the officer unlocks the jail cell. “Gotta get you ready for your hearin”, son. “sides, daddy sent over a lawyer for ya.” The remark explained a lot and very little. Of course, in a short time everything would come out. Tyler had already planned how he was going to talk to his lawyer, he wanted responsibility and he wanted to pay for everything. He snapped and he’s sorry. The two men left the jail cell as a man in a suit with gray hair waited in the opposite room. This scene soon fades to black. May 11, 2003, a Dallas Police Precinct. The time is now nine o’clock and Tyler has just been returned to his jail cell. The gray haired gentlemen from before stands outside the cell. “Tyler, I called your Father. He said that Kyle would be here with the bail money in less than an hour.” Tyler merely nods to the man and then speaks, quietly. The guilt and remorse very evident. “And the money for Mr. Larson’s bills” and everything else? Will that also be sent?” The lawyer nods and wishes him luck before turning and making his way towards the exit. Tyler sighs and lays back on the bench, closing his eyes. “Maybe a little sleep will help...” Moments pass as voices in the background are heard. A quick greeting and then farewell followed by a discussion within which the same officers voice is heard and then a moment of silence. A pair of foot steps now and then only one, the officer stops and glances behind to see no one. We quickly return to the jail cell where Tyler is laying. The sound of keys rattling is heard and Tyler sits up, glancing at the officer. “You’re free ta go, son. Let’s get your stuff and get ya outta here.” The two men walk towards the lobby and the officer hands him a small box of things. Tyler places the box on the officer’s desk and begins digging through it, replacing a ring on his finger and a necklace. He then grabs a pair of sunglasses and slips them into his coat pocket. He then pulls a set of keys from the box and stares at them for a moment. “Officer, these aren’t mine.” “Yes, they are, son. The man who paid your bail told me to slip em into your things.” He sighs and shakes his head. He mutters to himself and shoves the Chevy keys into his pocket before shaking the officers hand and walking towards the exit. May 10, 2003, outside of the Police Department. Once outside, Tyler begins his walk through the police parking lot, his eyes wandering over the vehicles there. He lifts the keys from inside his pocket and sighs, shaking his head. “Damn it, Dad, why?” A limo pulls up a few feet away and the front door open. Out steps a well kept gentlemen in a chauffeur’s uniform. He quickly walks to the back and opens another door. Tyler stops and glances at the limo, his eyes watching as a dark haired man in his twenties steps out, dressed in a suit and carrying a briefcase. He glances at Tyler and approaches him slowly. “Tyler, what the hell are you doing” Daddy is fuming!” Tyler glances at the briefcase and then up at his brother, a look of curiosity on his face. “I really don’t have time to argue with you, Kyle. Just tell me which car Dad sent.” The man we now know as Kyle laughed, almost offended by Tyler’s words. He grabs him by the shoulder. “Car” The Car DAD sent? You’ve got to be kidding. Dad definitely didn’t send you a car, Kyle. Hell, he barely wanted to send me with the bail money!” With this, Kyle lifts the briefcase into the air, motioning. Tyler glances at it for a moment and then up at him, curious. “You mean you didn’t get me out, yet?” “Yet” Hell, I was going to ask how you got out, Ty.” Tyler raises a brow and then glances at the keys curiously. Kyle continues to speak but his words are blurred and inaudible, covered and overlaid by Tyler’s thoughts. “Then who did” And who the hell gave me a car?” He presses the button on the keyless entry and suddenly we notice a car parked nearby spring to life, the engine starting and the driver’s side door popping open. A red Honda Civic with red ground FX, shaved door handles, red viper lights, 16 inch chrome rims, and red gloss mirror tint sits idling just ten feet from the Pierce brothers. Both stare at it completely shocked. “Well, I’ll be damned.” Tyler’s words are spoken without thought. “Where did that come from, Ty?” Tyler walks towards it, his brother about two feet behind him. He smiles suddenly, nodding to himself. “So where did it come from, Ty?” His brother’s inquiry floated again through the air but Tyler showed no sign of answering. He simply walked towards the car and pushed the door open, slipping into the full black leather interior. Pierce had been inked into the leather in red on each seat. Kyle walks towards the car, trying to peek over the door and inside but as he approaches, he begins to ask the question again and Tyler simply shuts the door, throws it into reverse and does a 180 degree turn out of the parking place. Suddenly the passenger side window rolls down and Tyler, placing a pair of sunglasses on, looks up at his brother. “Just tell Daddy” thanks, but I’ve got it covered.” With this, Kyle begins to say something but the window rolls back up, obscuring sound and view between the car’s interior and the outside world. The sunroof moves back and the sounds of Eminem’s 8 Mile soundtrack can be heard from inside. The car’s engine fires and quickly burns out, the car flies through the parking lot with the third Pierce brother standing amongst a cloud of dust. The scene fades to black.
