The lonely life.

15 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 15th, 2004. Rain fell from the ceiling of the sky in the beautiful shape of a heavenly crystalline teardrop. The gentle pitter-patter of rain was soothing to my ears as it soaked into the soft green grass beneath my black Chuck Taylor's. The wind accompanied the rain, howling ominously through the canopy of darkness that nightfall had cast upon us. My ragged black locks were plastered to my face by natures sweet shower, hair fell across my face and into my bluish gray eyes, brushing these scattered locks from my face I walked away from the gray Jeep Wrangler that I had been leaning against. Walking forward my destination was in plain sight, only it was hidden. I knew where it was, only…I didn't know where it was. It was somewhere in there… My thoughts trailed off as I glanced into the cemetery that I had entered, the tombstones circled me as if they were miniscule signposts of death each tombstone screaming out the life that had once been lived upon this earth. This dead forest of tombstones, glowing with the gentle luminescent rain and the brilliant full moon seemed to surround me, reminding me of my existence. The fact that I was the only living, breathing, walking, talking creature on these premises was an awaking and alarming realization. The revelation of this was breathtaking and proved to unsettle me, glancing about for some strange reason I peered over my shoulder into the darkness. Perhaps it was the cemetery, the jutting tombstones, the coffins beneath my feet, the rotting flesh that laid to rest only six feet below, the peculiar sterling silver hue of the moon on this particular night, or even all of the above. Yet as goose bumps ran down my flesh I could sense that it was something deeper than that…something darker than that. Or was I just being paranoid? Was I being Crazy? Was I being Illogical? 'No.' I whispered to myself in reassurance, my tone loaded with false confidence. 'Nothing is wrong…' I told myself that nothing was wrong, but somewhere down in the pit of my stomach butterflies were fluttering their wings about, causing a stir amidst my intestines and not allowing me to believe my own courageous lie. Something wasn't right; I could feel it…somehow, somewhere. Ignoring this gut instinct I walked past a row of beautifully decorated tombstones, granite and marble, decorated with all of the fancy designs you could ask for, names and loving words were beautifully scrawled upon these markers of death. Eyeing each one carefully I read the surname aloud. 'Sparks…Ramorez…Camero…Robson…James…Shigeru…' My voice continued on, speaking in a hushed tone almost as if I feared resurrecting the fallen souls in this graveyard I held my tongue in silent submission. These elaborately decorated headstones shined beneath the light of the moon, and as I read this morbid roll call I finally reached the name I was looking for. 'Jeremy.' I sighed, in a deflated tone as I arrived at the tombstone that I had been searching for. Looking down at this tombstone it wasn't like the rest, it was smaller, not quite so beautifully decorated. No flowers rested at this graveside, only a name and a few loving words. The name… 'Jeremy Edward.' The words… 'An extraordinary child who cast light in the life of many, light that will shine forever, keeping him alive in our hearts for eternity.' Looking down at this tombstone I couldn't help but sigh softly, my heart aching just a bit. This wasn't any random tombstone, it wasn't any random grave, and it wasn't any random person. This was my flesh…my blood…this was the brother that I never knew. Jeremy Edward, the child who had died before I had been brought into this world. His life had been taken in an unfortunate hunting accident, an accident that left a family torn to shreds. An accident that left behind a mourning father, a man plagued with the remorse of being the unintentional reaper of death. The accident had left behind a grieving mother, one that spilt tears upon her pillow for many nights, crying herself to sleep over the son she had lost. It left behind grandparents that were stunned, shocked, dismayed and stricken with sorrow at the loss of such a beautiful child. So full of life…so handsome…athletic…intelligent…and loving. He was everything that you could ever ask for in a son. Mature and wise beyond his nine years everyone who had met him was torn to pieces by the tragedy that had fallen upon the family. Yet, here I stood…born a year after his death, the product of sorrow, the son of loneliness, the child of tragedy. Every single thing I did was because of him; I had been conceived for the mere purpose of being his replacement. My entire life hinged upon his death, and the realization of this cast me into a lonely pit of despair. This dead child…was the reason I was alive today. Living, breathing, walking, talking…all because of his inability to do so. Closing my eyes I fell to my knees, ignoring the fact that I was immersing a pair of two hundred dollar Dolce & Gabana jeans in the wet grass and dirt that was quickly turning into a thick mud. Raising my trembling right hand my index finger ran across the words, almost as if I had to feel them to believe it was true. First the name… 'Jeremy Edward.' Then the inscription… 'An extraordinary child who cast light in the life of many, light that will shine forever, keeping him alive in our hearts for eternity.' My wet finger traveled over the slick tombstone once again, retracing the words on the cold stone. My fallen brother…my reason for life… Leaning forward I knelt my head against the tombstone, whispering softly, under my breath the lonely words of sorrow spilled forth. 'I don't know who you are…' I gasped, my heart wracked with emotional pain and suffering. '…But I do know that I only have my life because you lost yours… My face slid down the tombstone and I collapsed across the grave, my sleeveless white Skillet T-shirt absorbing the rain from the moist blades of grass. My hands remained on the base of the tombstone and my face rested against the soft grass, rain splattering up and running across my features. 'If you only knew what an existence of loneliness it was to know that every second of every day you were simply brought to life to function as a replacement… I closed my eyes, fighting away emotions strong grip. 'You can't even begin to imagine what it's like to wake up every day and draw breath, knowing that if it wasn't for the death of another then you wouldn't have the opportunity to live.' The emotion became too much, biting my bottom lip I squeezed my eyelids down harder, refusing to allow my tears to get the better of me. My entire body trembled with emotion, my tongue was reduced to a stammer, and my face felt numb, numb with tears welling up in my eyes…tears that I fought away desperately. 'You are the reason I live…so then I must ask God, am I the reason you had to die?' I mused this question, to the rotten flesh beneath me, flesh that I couldn't see, nor even fathom it's existence…much like I couldn't fathom my very own purpose in this theater of the bizarre, we all affectionately refer to as 'life'. Pushing myself up with my hands I stared into the heavens, letting the rain wash my face, prayerfully wishing that it would cleanse me of all of these lonely thoughts, that it would cleanse me of my undying guilt. Somewhere in the rain, I felt something-warm run down my cheek, only this time it wasn't a rain drop, but rather a tear drop. These were the tears of a man who had grown up alone and in essence…a man who was still alone, and would forever be alone. It was a lonely life… The tear fell upon the grave… Raising my eyes through the blur of tears I could no longer make out the name correctly, reading it through orbs flowing with emotion the words could hardly be recognized. The name… 'Joshua Caleb.' My breath caught and I fell back, scrambling away I crawled until I ran into something, a human…in the graveyard. Raising my tear filled eyes up I saw a man dressed in a black robe, it was Xavier Crysto. As I knelt before him on my hands and knees he looked down, his normally haunting ice blue eyes filled with compassion. Kneeling down he placed one of his bony hands upon my shoulder and his voice was barely audible as he spoke. 'My son, life leads us in many different directions, but the final destination is the same for all lonely souls. We must not fear the inevitable for you cannot run from the reaper, you cannot hide from him, you cannot outwit him…instead you can only willingly submit yourself after leading a full life. Child, your destiny is there…with your brother, the day is coming.' Looking back…another tear fell upon the grave…upon my brother…
