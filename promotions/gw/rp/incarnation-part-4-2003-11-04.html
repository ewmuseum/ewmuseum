Incarnation: Part 4

4 November 2003

Date: 11-04-03 The Incarnation: Part 4 There he stood, lost in the echoes of his own mind again. All these thoughts he wished were gone kept flooding his head. The boy he met, sick of his life, and wanting to get away from the parents he once had. It sounded so much like Jesse’s life. So much like when he was a young child just urging for a way to get out into the world. Something bothered him however, not like a jinx or a curse, but as if he was going through the same thing over and over again, a cycle, and then it finally hit him. Jesse was beginning to realize it… Story continues… The train station was empty, not a person in sight. The ticket booth closed the gates locked, and the tracks untouched for days. Sleeping on the bench was uncomfortable, yet it was better than on the ground. What ever had happened to the train station sure did leave many in wonder. The area was taped up by yellow police tape, saying “Criminal Investigation – DO NOT ENTER!” every few feet on the yellow tape. It seemed as if Jesse was back in the world of murder that he once had committed himself, but to him he was still unsure he did anything wrong. Getting up after a night’s rest, Jesse began to journey on into the streets. Hopping the gate and walking along it until life was seen, he spent many hours traveling. Finally he came across a sign, there on the side of the high way was a billboard, and on top it wrote “Small Town Life for a Small Town Man.” Jesse wondered where he could be. Riding the train he had been on was like being on a car trip to Nebraska, you saw the same thing over and over and finally after an hour or two, you lost time all together. Then he saw a smaller print in the left-hand corner showing that he was in Alabama. The trees, the tracks, and the high way were all that were ahead of him now. For a while it seemed desperate that Jesse would run across a man or woman that could point him in the right direction, however hope found it’s way, like always. Jesse had reached the small town of Cherryville, Alabama. The town had one bus station and one bank, no restaurants, but one gas station, and maybe a trailer park. Jesse sat at the bus station for a tad longer, thinking of what happened, of all that had gone through his head. Some say he did, and then there were men like Kevin who said he didn’t. What was true? What was a lie? Jesse had no clue. There at that exact moment, Jesse began to have yet another flashback. The lights of a car blinded him as he saw himself gagged and tied up. His hair bloody and his face the same, he was a wreck, and it was clear he was being hurt by someone. Jesse then could make out a body. Coming from the car was a woman, the same woman he’d saw in the woods in a previous flashback. Though this time she had a gun, and was pointing it at Jesse’s head. Jesse walked forward and toward her not to do it, and she had stopped. But it was not really him who walked forward, another man had. Someone facing to his back had, and he couldn’t make out a picture. As if in a third person view Jesse saw himself, and then two others, the mystery man and the woman named Rebecca, and then himself who were gagged. Did Jesse not kill the woman? Or did he just see himself being mixed up in some type of kidnapping? Unsure, he got up; for the bus had stopped and it’s doors opening snapped him back to reality. Entering the bus Jesse dropped a dollar and twenty-five cents in the machine, and grabbed his ticket. He walked to the back and sat down near the window. Only a few elderly women sat on the bus and all were sleeping. Hours later he arrived at a hotel, where he signed in and got a room for the night. The Dream begins… The lifeless souls of his loved ones lay there upon the carpet, blood covered them, and blood surrounded their carcasses. Not a person in Jesse’s life was worth watching, not even the ones he had murdered some 20 years before. Of coarse they weren’t his real parent’s he thought they were foster parents, but they had loved him the same, and from what he had done, he could not realize how much suffering could go through him all at once. In a dead sleep his eyes began to water, just as he did in the dream. He could feel there dead lifeless bodies as if they were real, and with each touch, a memory had entered his head. The memories of his foster parents first time meeting him, or when he had gone out for the football team and his foster dad had helped him stay motivated. But they all ended with him remembering the slicing he had done to them. The scene in his dreams then goes dark and the flashback of his wife’s funeral enters his mind. The funeral so calm, so normal to others, yet to Jesse the first in his life. Walking step by step he came closer to the coffin, as it had been displayed to show the upper most part of Julie’s body. Placing the red rose on her chest it was a faint memory of disbelief. However, the memory changed, for as soon as he dropped the rose on her chest, it became black, and the dead woman he once called honey rose from the coffin and started to choke him. Her body pale, her eyes ghost like with near total white peering a hole through Jesse. He looked at her face in awe, for he remembered it so well, yet now it was the face of the devil. The surroundings went a dark and cloudy color, making the belief that he was in hell much more. Pushing away, he fell on his back and crawled away from the demon woman, as she looked at him and spoke. “YOU WILL KILL! You will kill for me! And if you shall turn your back on me… I WILL KILL YOU!” With that he awoke in a sweat. His body shaking, his neck sore, and his mouth dry. He rose from the bed and walked to the bathroom to check his neck. Flicking the lights on Jesse paced his way to the mirror, and wiped the sweat from his face off on a towel. Peering into the mirror, an outline of red hands had appeared upon his neck. Not the normal man like hands he may get during a match, but scratched hand types, as if the person were wearing fake fingernails. Jesse through the towel over his shoulder and walked out of the bathroom, flicking the light switch off as he left. Going to his bed again, he sat down near the edge and dialed up a number on the phone. “Hello, this is an old patient, I need help…” (To Be Continued…)
