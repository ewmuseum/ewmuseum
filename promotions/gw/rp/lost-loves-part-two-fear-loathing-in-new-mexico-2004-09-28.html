Lost Loves: Part Two: Fear & Loathing In New Mexico

28 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/28/2004 Life. Death. It’s odd, when your life is fading? there really is that clich? of your life passing before your eyes? and I found? in this moment, sheer happiness and peace? life wasn’t that bad after all. A series of snapshots, of inconsequential events? that meant the world to me retrospect. The curve of her neck, her delicate wrist? the smell of her perfume. Thirty years, and she was the only highlight on the reel. How sad is that? All my accomplishments meant nothing, overshadowed by her. A woman. The bane of my existence. I wanted to go back, and correct that, remove her from the picture, but it wasn’t that easy. I’d given her something, and then taken it away again, she’d never known. Something I’d never wanted. Something, I wanted more than anything. You see, it all boiled down to this? I loved her. I died loving her, and now I was stuck in that mindset? broken. I could go on, but she haunted my days, and plagued me during my sleepless nights. Her laughter rang in my ears, a phantom sound? and I could still taste the sweetness of her lips. I hated myself for what I did to her? ---- He sat in the dark, cradling the pistol in his hand, absently spinning the cylinder. This wasn’t new. The depression was the same. The numbness was the same. The pain, ever a constant. The anger, however, was new. Utterly self-directed. He sat there, staring down at the cold steel, thinking about all that had come and gone. What did he have? In this screwed up world, what did he have to keep him sane? Friends? Family? No. Nothing. The CID was dead, all save Gabriel, who had vanished like a thief in the night, without a trace. He grunted to himself, almost a chuckle, and shook his head. Gabriel was nothing. He didn’t matter. Nothing but a lackey. He turned the gun over and over, watching the light play off the metal. This was the gun. The one that had ended his life. Perhaps he should have used it on her, instead of letting her go. Loose ends, what if she went to the police, and exposed him. What if she managed to find the other straggling agents that he hadn’t been able to find? What if she set the very hounds of hell loose on him? “So be it. I would gladly go into the great hereafter, if she wished it so. I would go to the ends of the earth for her. Why doesn’t she understand that?” He turned the gun, looking now into the dark barrel, trying to see into the chasm, wanting to play Russian roulette. Instead, he returned it to his lap, adjusting his position on the stone floor, letting the cold seep into his marrow. Night had fallen in the desert, bringing the coyotes, who howled mournfully beyond the walls. He remained still, his expression unreadable in the moonlight dappled gloom, eyes flashing dangerously as he continued to turn the gun over and over in his hands. He was balanced on the precipice, treading precariously on the edge of Ocham’s razor. On the brink of madness, he could feel the gaping chasm of his good intentions yawning at his heels, waiting for the one misstep that would spill him into the grave. A soft moan shattered his introspective silence. He rose without a sound, and moved to her side, looking down on her where she lay atop the makeshift bed, which was really nothing more than a pile of old, musty blankets. He crouched beside her, and waited for her eyes to open. With a sigh, he pulled the sunglasses down from atop his head, settling them over his eyes. The moon was bright tonight, the light piercing. When they did, she sat up, blinking in the darkness, at first gasping. That sound became a sob, and then a scream when she saw him sitting beside her. She scuttled backwards, her eyes wide with terror, the scar on her cheek making her lips twist into a frightening parody of a grimace. “Please, don’t kill me?” She whispered between cracked lips, tasting the bitter copper of blood in her mouth. Jackson laid the gun down atop the blankets, and reached inside his jacket, withdrawing a small silver flask. He held it out to her, saying nothing, his expression camouflaged by the sunglasses, and the wild hair that covered his face. She took it with a shaking hand, and uncapped it, sniffing the contents. Whiskey, by the smell of it. She shook her head, and reached out to hand it back. It could be poison, or worse. She didn’t know who he was; he didn’t seem familiar, like the other men who had roughed her up. She squinted in the gloom, waiting for her eyes to adjust to the shifting moonlight as clouds passed overhead. His voice startled her, even though it was soft. “Just drink it. You will need something for the pain.” Pain? She felt nothing but an odd numbness, and pins and needles in her arms. She hadn’t yet noticed that her shoulder had been dislocated in the struggle, or perhaps in her fall. One arm hung loosely at her side, her shoulder slumped. “Pain?” “Yes. Pain.” He reached out with one hand, and she scrambled back, colliding with the wall. He shook his head once, his voice filled with an emotion she couldn’t identify at first. “Please, don’t run from me, Tina. I just want to help you.” “Who are you?” She stammered as she put her hands on the floor and tried to move away again, this time creeping along the wall. She didn’t make it far before the pain tore along her nerve endings as her arm touched the wall, jolting her shoulder. She looked down, and saw with horror what he had been alluding to. She whimpered, and the sound cut through him like a hot knife through butter. “Please,” he said again, “just let me help you. I’m not with them.” She nodded, not trusting herself to do anything else. She didn’t believe him, but something in his voice made her want to. He seemed sincere, and sad. Yes, that’s what she’d heard in his rusty voice, sadness. He stood slowly, moving out of the shadows, crossing to her side. He knelt, and gripped her useless arm in one hand, pressing the other against where her neck met her shoulder joint. Before she could recoil at the touch of his icy hands on her bare skin, she heard, rather than felt, a wet popping sound. Fire burned up her arm, making spots dance before her eyes. He released her, and picked up the flask, holding it to her lips. She drank greedily of the liquor, gasping as the fire burned down her throat, igniting her insides. But this was a good fire. Tears glimmered in her eyes, as she looked up at him. He could read the questions in her gaze, and wanted to answer them, but knew he shouldn’t. If he told her who he was, and moreover, what he was, she would have to die. Enough innocent blood already covered his hands. In answer to her unspoken query, he retreated to the shadows, where she could only see the glint of moonlight off the sunglasses. “I am a friend, Tina” nothing more.” “Why are you here?” His sigh carried to her ears, as soft as it was. He decided to tell her the truth. “I was looking for you, when I saw them abducting you. I had some questions. About” the girl.” A frown marred Tina’s features, making her look even more severe in the unflattering moonlight. “Why does everyone want to know about her” First that Christian freak, and now you. I don’t understand, why is everyone looking for her?” “I don’t know how to answer that, my dear. I don’t know those men who harmed you” they are not my associates. Their reasons for finding her are their own, I’m afraid.” Tina chuckled, the sound bordering on hysteria. “He said she was his sister. He said she?” The laughter turned to hiccupping sobs, and she wrapped her arms around her middle, crying softly. For a moment he sat there, still as a statue, and then he returned to her side, taking her in his arms. She relaxed in his embrace, crying against the soft leather jacket he wore, smelling the leather? smelling tobacco, and something almost earthy on him, like the scent of a freshly plowed field. Or perhaps a freshly dug grave. The scent was pleasant, and sickening at the same time. “Shhhh,” he whispered against her hair, “it’s all right, tell me what he said Tina, he can’t hurt you now. You’re safe here?” “He, he said she was a liar, that she had aliases all over the place. He said she was a killer” a heartless assassin? he said she would kill me?” He stiffened, and shoved her away, holding her at arm’s length. He looked at her face, and then nodded. “Perhaps he was right. She does have a nasty habit of killing people who get close to her?” Tina sniffled, and rubbed a hand across her nose, rather indelicately. “Is that why you’re after her” Revenge? Did she kill someone you love?” He threw his head back and laughed, his amusement lost on Tina. “No,” he replied, his tone filled with bitterness, “well perhaps in part, but it has more to do with love. You see, Tina, she’s a disease with no cure, a cancer that plagues my body” I?” She nodded knowingly, staring straight into the dark lenses of his glasses, as though she could divine the secrets of his soul. “Oh, I see. You love her.” “No. No. I don’t. I?” She cut his protest off with a finger to his lips. “Doc Bailey. That’s where they’re going. He lives in Dallas” let me give you the address. You have to stop them before they hurt her again!” She reached in her pocket and withdrew a man’s wallet, pulling out a sweat-soaked card which she pressed into his hand. He nodded once, and then stood slowly, inclining his head to indicate the gun he’d left on the blanket. “Take it, it was hers. Perhaps she would have wanted you to have it. Either way, you may need protection.” He crossed to the door, his footsteps echoing in the silence. As he pulled it open, he said one last thing, a thing that made Tina wish she had died at the hands of Zenith. “Oh, by the way, they didn’t hurt her. I did?” And with that, he was gone, a shadow amid the darkness. ---- Sometimes it feels better to tell the truth? and sometimes it just hurt more. Accountability. I felt her hatred, and I heard her cry out as I left. I knew she wanted nothing more than to kill me. Perhaps I deserved that. I wanted the same. To die. That would be preferable, an end to this purgatory that I now called life. But, I would not go without her. My angel. My devil. My? Love.
