Memory Remains #7

7 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7703 Bend me shape me misdirect me It's all the same to me Look at all this useless talk You look at me but you don't see Understand I'm a sinner The black Mitsubishi Eclipse pulled in front of the mansion like house that resided on the Zero Estate. John Zero and Deacon Holmes exited the car simultaneously, with Zero lifting the seat forward for the young lady in the back seat to exit. She came out slowly, taking Zero’s outstretched hand cautiously, not knowing what to expect. “What’s your name, m’dear?” Zero inquired in a low voice. “Violet,” came the meek reply. “Violet, my name is John, and this is my associate Deacon. This is my estate. We will go inside and I will have my driver take you to wherever you wish to go. How does that sound?” “What do you want in return?” Violet asked, suspicion on her voice. “Nothing. Absolutely nothing. I am not an animal, like the men we left in the alley. Just take my thanks that I was able to help.” The woman said nothing as the three of them climbed the stairs to the front entrance. Upon entering the mansion, Zero made arrangements for his chauffer to take Violet back to her home. As she prepared to leave, she motioned for Zero to come to her. “Yes?” “My hero,” Violet whispered, kissing him lightly on the cheek, and whispering something in his ear. At the sight, Holmes cocked his head to one side, half laughing to himself. After Violet was out the door, Deacon walked over to where John Zero stood, looking out the window as Violet stepped into the stretch limo. “What was that all about?” “What was what all about?” “The kiss and all that whispering bullshit.” “Oh. She gave me her phone number and address. I think she wants me to come over later.” “You got digits off of her?” “Yeah.” “Just like that?” “Just like that.” “Man, and you didn’t do jack shit.” “Well, I did save her life.” “I could’ve done that.” “Yeah, but you decided to sit that one out, remember?” “Man, fuck you.” “I think that’s what she has in mind, so I believe I will turn down your offer. Sorry.” “Man?” Deacon shakes his head and walks away. “You got anything to eat in this joint. “Of course.” John walks over to a small intercom system, pushing a button, then speaking after a static reply. “Could you bring two lunches up to the dining room” Thank you.” Zero motions to Deacon, “This way.” Zero leads Holmes through the house, stopping in a large dining room with a huge twenty foot table. “Drink?” Zero asks Holmes. “Sure, whatever.” Zero walked over to the small wet bar, pulling out a bottle of scotch, pouring out equal portions into two glasses, presenting one of them to Deacon when he was done. “So, tell me about the tournament I am now in.” “Simple standard elimination tournament, the grand prize being the International Title. There are four starting brackets, and you will be facing off against Max Roswell.” “The match against Roswell I knew of. It being a tournament, I did not know of.” “Well, shit happens, John. Like I said, you think you may be low man on the totem pole, but Il Capo, he sees something else. What he sees in you, I’m not sure, but sometimes, I catch that look in your eye, the same look you had when I ran down that alley. I’ve heard Sean speak of it, but I passed it off as bullshit.” “What does Sean say?” “That you’re one scary motherfucker.” Zero laughs. “You think that’s funny?” Deacon asks. “Yes, I do. Tell me about Roswell.” “Ain’t that much to tell except he thinks he’s a superhero of some sort, the proverbial good guy of sorts.” “Great, I get to face Superman. So, what supervillian does that make me” Lex Luther. Yeah, I like that.” “Luther” Didn’t he go bald?” Zero nodded. “Yeah, but he had panache.” Deacon started to argue but stopped when the doors to the dining room opened and a small man wheeled a cart in. He set two plates on the table, each with a thick steak and a loaded baked potato. He looked at Zero, who nodded, and then the man left. “Bon appetite,” Zero said sitting down, as Deacon joined him. Don't corner me Don't lecture me Raise your hands you're a sinner Is this everything you wanted find another dream You never hear a word I say So pray I'm a sinner Look at all these people in front of me I'm a sinner I got in the car and drove to Bryce’s driveway. Partially down the driveway, I started honking the horn to get him to come out. Pulling to a stop a safe distance from the house, I carefully pulled the glass jar from it’s casing. By the time I was out of the car, Bryce was out of the house. “Who are you?” he asked, an edge of anger on his voice. He started coming down the stairs and stopped halfway down. “Don’t you remember?” I said quietly, my voice a couple of notches above a whisper. Oh yeah, I can hit him from here and not worry about the blast. “I’m the one that left you handcuffed in your own blood and piss.” “You don’t look so tough without your knife, pal. I owe you a lesson.” “Well, before you teach me that lesson, lemme ask you this. Remember what I told you about Lily?” Bryce’s look of thought reminded me of how a deer looks when caught in some car’s headlights. “Nope, I don’t,” came his reply. “Figured. I told you that if you hurt Lily, you get hurt. Well, you hurt her pretty good a while back. And now it’s time to pay your dues.” “What” Little bitty you gonna put me in the hospital?' He was mocking my size. I’m scrawny and he’s built. Hell, I would make fun of me if I had his size. “Nope.” “Then what?” “I’m gonna kill you.” “With what army?” “With this,” I said, holding up the jar. Again with the deer caught in the headlights look. “Think fast.” I threw the bomb at him and started backing up, just in case. Now there is a reason that Bryce was a sports god, especially during football season. Homeboy could catch. And that’s exactly what happened. He caught the jar. Apparently, I threw it a little too well and it went right to his chest and his natural football instincts kicked in and the bastard caught it. “What the hell is this gonna do?” he asked. My mind raced. What now? I had failed. I gotta get outta here. No wait. I can’t let Lily down. I can’t let him torment another woman. How? How can I win? How? And then it came to me. “Explode,” I said, answering his question, while jerking my gun from its holster at my back. It came free easily. I pulled it up, quickly sighted, and squeezed the trigger. The small, pointed bullet zipped through the air and slammed against the jar. A bright flash of light blinded me and the roar deafened me. I was knocked back by the wind of the explosion. I got up slowly. Most of the front stairs leading to the house was gone. And so was Bryce. I looked around. I saw nothing but blackened earth and the occasional clump of bloody flesh that I assumed was some part of Bryce’s anatomy. My job was done. I needed to go. I went to the car. There were a couple of flecks of blood and a gruesome hunk of Bryce on the windshield. I tossed the hunk off the windshield and got into the car. I turned on the wipers, mixing blood and windshield washer fluid and sped home. Once in the door of my apartment, I ran into the bathroom and vomited. I started to shake. I looked into the mirror and stared at my reflection. I looked into my eyes. A killer looked back at me. The zero had faced the jock and won. I was one step closer to what I was to become. Jumping into the shower, I let the hot water run over me, scalding my skin red. Using soap and a washcloth, I got most of the dirt and grime off of me. Maybe there was some of Bryce in there too. It slid off of my body in a brown tinge of water with the occasional glint of red. Throwing on some old clothes and running outside, I double-checked the inside of my car for any incriminating stains. Aside from a little dirt on the floorboard, it was clean as a whistle. I went back inside and collapsed on the couch. I was exhausted both mentally and physically. It was at that moment that the phone rang. If I were any more high strung, I would’ve jumped up to the ceiling. “Hello?” I said into the receiver. “Wanna come over?” Lily. Suddenly, I wasn’t so tired. “Sure. I’ll be there in a sec.”
