Reaching the mountain

1 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/1/04 It was a night I would never forget. It was the end of June 2003. The place was Whiplash. Four long years in the company, I sweated and ached and bled for the chance at the ultimate glory in this industry. Two years after I started in the company, I finally won a shot at the World title. I was ready to make that shot count. But it never happened. I fought a long, hard fight to win that chance and it was taken away from me like it was nothing. I'd seen a whole bunch of superstars pass me over in order to win the title: Hans Kruger, Jean-Claude Dafoe, Ben J. Pailthorpe, Rob Virtuoso, Mr. UK, Gambit, Maverick Reno, Silent Wind, Chris Camero. A lot of people underestimated me. They told me that I would never have what it takes to be World champion, that I would never beat the best, that I would never be the best. They said I wasn't talented enough. They said I wasn't smart enough. I would prove them all wrong. Sage Sabbot was the World champion. He won it when he beat me at Dead Zone. That was an extremely personal loss for three reasons. One, because it was in my hometown. Two, because my family was watching. And three, because Sabbot did what not a lot of others had done: he made me scream out in agony and tap out. I still remember the snap my shoulder made when he dislocated it. I still smell the burning gas when Salaok had the cell we were fighting in set on fire. But it wasn't over. It didn't matter what he did or what Salaok did. It wouldn't be over. I wouldn't let it be. I'd been waiting a whole month for a re-match. At the end of June, I earned that rematch with Sabbot. Given the severity of the last match, there was only one place where we could surpass it: the Pit. By the time it was over, Sabbot was out and I had won. It was one of the happiest moments of my life. Four long years of shed blood, bruised skin, broken bones and near-death experiences had finally paid off. I was finally on top of the mountain where I belonged. I was living a dream. I achieved what no one believed I could. At times, even I didn't believe I could achieve it, but I did. I finally had a taste of World title gold. And then it was gone. Ever since then, I've been trying to reach that goal, to rise back to the top of the mountain, only to be met with defeat. Then my mind comes back from the past and it's 2004 again. I'm in my living room, studying footage from Wrestlecade. Now, I've been given yet another opportunity. I'm now headed into battle with the new champion. Another underdog, another person that they said would never reach the top. I'm facing Josh. Then I hear the phone ring. “Hello?” “Jamal?” It’s my mother. “Hey, Ma. What’s up?” “I just called to see how you were doing. We don’t see each other as much as I’d like. What are you doing Sunday?” “Sunday, I think I have to get back down to Dallas. I got a big match Monday night.” “How big is it?” “It’s the main event. I got a shot at the World title.” “I was hoping you and your sister could come over for dinner.” “Well, I can probably catch a flight the next day, or a red-eye flight after dinner. Hopefully a non-stop flight. I’ll be there, though.” “Good. Because it’s been too long since we’ve spent time as a family. You’ve got your thing. Your sister has her thing.” Ma was right. Both me and Monica had been real busy. Me, with Global Wrestling. Her, becoming a lawyer. We both owed it to Ma to at least make the effort. “Well, I’m gonna make every effort to make it to dinner Sunday night.” “That’s all I ask. OK, I’ll let you get back to your business.” “OK. Bye, Ma.” Monday night, I intend to teach Josh a lesson. I intend to teach him that reaching the top of the mountain was the easy part. It's staying on top of the mountain that's the hard part. Because there are lots of cats looking to take his place. I'm not the only one and maybe I won't be the last. Because even if it isn't the last night of Josh's title reign, it will be the longest night of his life.
