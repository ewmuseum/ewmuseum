A New Kind of Therapy

29 June 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 29, 2004 [Wednesday morning. Business hours. Just outside a light brown-bricked three-story doctor’s office, Mackenzie’s black Dodge Durango pulls efficiently between the yellow lined parking spaces of the crammed parking lot. The driver’s side door flings open and Mac hops from the truck, shutting the door behind her. She’s fully decked out in a camouflage muscle tee, covered by a black fishnet top, a black skirt, just above knee length, black fishnet stocking, and black high-heeled chunky toed boots. With light, almost dance worthy steps; Mac makes her way across the parking lot and into the building, all the while humming and half singing “Milkshake” by Kelis. Once inside the building she makes her way past the front desk, and back towards a set of elevators where she grabs an “up” elevator. Inside the empty elevator, Mac breaks out a milkshake worthy dance before the elevator makes it to the third floor.] “Mackenzie,” the sweet receptionist’s voice came from behind a counter and desk area. “I didn’t see you on the patient’s list today.” “Oh. Nope, not today. Actually, it’s a social call,” Mac smiles sweetly at the elderly receptionist, leaning her arms on the high counter. ?Is she in?? “Sorry, Dr Bower’s at lunch dear. If you’d like, you can have a seat outside her office and wait. She’ll be back in about ten minutes,” she makes a motion of her hand towards the hallway. “Thanks Gale,” Mac gives her another smile and pushes off from the desk and heads down the hall, leaving Gale back at her paperwork. [Bouncing cheerfully down the hallway, Mac seats herself in the airline looking seats just outside Dr. Bower’s office door. Kenz grabs one of the magazines from the small table beside the seats. After flipping through a few pages boredom sets in. Hearing Gale’s aged voice instruct another patient down to this very spot, Mac suddenly gets an evil idea. Throwing the magazine back down, she gets up off her seat and slides into Dr. Bower’s office just as an older looking man is turning the corner. Inside the office she scuttles through masses of Dr. Bower’s papers on her desk, until finally she finds her next patient’s file. Elliot DeWall. Hurriedly, she pushes Dr. Bower’s glasses onto her face, and quickly opens the office door.] ?Elliot?? Mac smiles, as the man nods. “Come on in. Dr. Bower is ill today; I’ll be taking her clients today. I’m Dr. Geller.” [Entering the office, Mac takes a seat in Dr. Bower’s black leather armchair as Elliot makes himself comfortable on one side of the couch. Elliot looks to be in his late thirties, early forties, his hair starting to gray around the receding hairline. Pulling the yellow tablet of pages from Dr. Bower’s desk, Mac sets it on her lap. She then takes a pen and looks up at Elliot.] ?Why don’t we start easy? How about the past couple weeks, what’s been going?? “Well,” he puts his hands in his lap and looks down to the floor. “The divorce papers were finalized last Thursday. Friday was my son’s eighth birthday, and my wife’I mean ex-wife. My ex-wife brought her new boyfriend to the party.” “Umm. Hmm,” Mac jots down a few notes on the yellow paper. ?And how does that make you feel?? “Angry. She may be over us already, but I’m not. She had no right to bring him.” ?I see. How do you feel about that?? Mac looks up from her yellow tablet, over her glasses. ?Angry?? Elliot is somewhat unsure of a response. “Go with that. Feel the anger. Let it take you over,” Mac’s hand are moving in crazy motions in front of her, like they have a will of their own. ?Now. How do you FEEL?? “Um,” Elliot is frightened now. His responses grow weaker. ?Still angry?? ?I see. So this anger is a reoccurrence with you. Have you seen a therapist about these anger management issues?? ?Doctor Bower is my therapist for these issues!? “Oh. Right. Sorry,” Mac scribbles down something else on her lap paper.\n “S’okay,” he replies very meekly. “See, now see. Elliot. You need to stop suppressing this anger. Yes,” Mac sits up in her chair. ?Let it run free! Come on. How does the anger make you feel!”? [Elliot’s jaw drops. He has no response; he just rubs his fingers together with his hands folded on his lap. Getting nothing, Mac sits back again. In the next second she turns the page in her yellow tablet and doodles down something, then holds it up to Elliot.] ?What do you see?? ?What?? He squints a little at the drawing. ?This office didn’t come fully supplied. I can’t find my inkblots. So what do you see?? ?A..horse?? ?A horse?? Mac turns the tablet back to her and examines. ?Hmm. What kind of horse?? “A plastic one. Like one of those plastic horses children have.” [Kenz makes a “thinking” face for a minute. She then turns back a page in her tablet and scribbles more down.] ?What does it mean?? Elliot’s voice comes across the room very small. ?What does what mean?? “The..the horse.” “Oh! Well, I don’t know. I’m not THAT kind of a therapist. I just always wanted to do that,” she shrugs, ignoring the look of shock on his face. ?Do you golf?? ?On occasion. Why?? ?What kind of golf clubs do you own?? Mac puts her pen to her lip. [Elliot is unsure again. He takes a moment before replying.] “Titleist.” “Hmm,” Mac taps the pen on her lip. ?Why?” He’s voice shows a hint of irritation as Mac is obviously spacing off. ?Why what?? She blinks at the question. ?WHY THE GOLFING QUESTION!”? “Oh! Well, it’s just that studies have shown sixty-five percent of men who golf on a regular basis have trouble maintaining a rod in the bedroom,” she says it so matter of fact-ly, that his jaw drops again. ?Could that be a reason for your divorce?? [Total silence. After a few minutes of looking at each other, Mac shrugs.] “Look. Since you seem very defensive today, maybe we should schedule for another day. You know, I was looking through your file and you’re on a whole hell of a lot of meds. I’m going to prescribe you a cat. Yes.” [She grabs a smaller tablet and writes down something before ripping off a page to give to him.] “Now, there is the address of a shelter. Tell them Mac sent you, and they’ll set you up with a nice older cat. I’ll set you up with an appointment for Dr. Bower same time next week. Goodbye now.” [Elliot just sits there for a moment with Mac looking at him and then glancing at the door. Finally he closes his jaw, and heads for the door.] “Oh. And Mr. DeWall? Watch out for those ice cube attacks,” she winks at him and turns her chair away from the door. [Even more shocked and confused than before, Elliot just leaves. Closing the door behind him. It had only been roughly twenty minutes; Dr. Bower must be running late. Mac quickly puts the desk back in order, and jumps onto the couch, just as the door opens and Dr. Bower comes in.] “Mackenzie,” she smiles, in much the same way as Gale had, the receptionist. ?I’m running a bit late today. We didn’t have an appointment today, did we?? “Oh. No, no. Social call. A man was here. Elliot. He said he had to cancel, and he’d come back next week. Same time,” Mac jumps up from the couch as Dr. Bower takes a seat in her chair. “I see. Thank you for relaying the message,” Dr. Bower sets her purse down below her desk, and comes back to Mac. ?What can I do for you today?? “I finished the book you lent me and I just wanted to return it,” Mac digs the book from her shoulder bag and hands it over to Dr. Bower. ?Well thank you. I’ll see you Friday for our appointment?? [Mad nods. She then heads for the door, and slips out, making her way back to her Durango.]
