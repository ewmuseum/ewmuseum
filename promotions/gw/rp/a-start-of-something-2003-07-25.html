A start of something

25 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A start of something Ronnie Long Global Wrestling Promo #464 Date: 7-25-03 Location: back alleys of Denver, Colorado Jack Cassidy sat, his back to the brick wall, turning his knife over and over in his hands. The metal, once shiny, had faded over the last 14 years, to a dull grey the color of lead. Only the very edge of the blade still showed the original color. And this was polished to a shining luster and kept razor sharp. Over the last 14 years, since Jack had lost his job, the knife had been both family and salvation to him. Fired from his job working at the local Dairy Queen for coming into work high on cocaine, dumped by his girlfriend by violent explosions, he retreated to the alleyways. He lead a mean existence. Scraping through garbage cans to obtain enough scraps for dinner, never really enough to satisfy his hunger. Indeed, he had been hungry so long that he sometimes forgot about the gnawing pain inside his stomach, having grown accustomed to it. But sometimes, when the pain became too great, he, and his knife, sought out prey. Living, breathing, human prey. ---- Ronnie Long’s scuffed combat boot crunched down on the broken glass that littered the floor of the alley way. His trench coat flapped in the breeze like a flag. In annoyance, he grabbed it by the edges and forced it around his body, taking time to button the color. Adjusting his shades and shaking his hair back down over his face, he continued walking. From Jack’s perspective, he didn’t see the tormented eyes of the infamous GW wrestler Ronnie Long. He saw a black trench coat, long brown hair flowing in the wind. And a pair of ragged black jeans with deep pockets. A smile crept across Jack’s face. He’d do better than garbage for dinner, this day. ---- Ronnie’s senses were normally far more acute than the normal person. It came from his teenage years, when he had to live his life expecting attacks from behind any second. Reform school isn’t much different from prison, in that everyone you see is your enemy. And even with a less paranoid view of the world, Long hasn’t lost the ability to notice everything. But he’s not thinking about that right now. He’s thinking about Deanna. The last time Deanna got upset with him about his wrestling, was a year ago. This was when he was wrestling with broken ribs and refusing to take time off to heal. And that’s understandable, he tells himself – nothing worse than to see the ones you love suffering, even if the suffering is self imposed. He remembers back when he was a child, so young he can barely remember, the way he felt as he watched his father bully his mother, pushing her around the kitchen and hissing at her. He never yelled and rarely struck her, but the intent was there. Now she’s upset again and he can’t figure out what it is. ---- To Jack’s credit, he was silent. He did his job perfectly. He followed the enshrouded figure of Ronnie down the street, blending in with the pedestrians so well that even one such as Ronnie noticed nothing strange. The knife was clenched in Jack’s fist, but hidden under his leather jacket so well that even a passing police officer didn’t blink as Jack snuck past him. They proceeded down the street. Ronnie, a black cloud, gliding through the packed sidewalks, parting the crowd as if by magic, and Jack, a dirty shadow, skulking after him. ---- Lost in contemplation, Ronnie continues to walk, oblivious, his mind still on Deanna. Briefly, he thinks it might have been his comments regarding Tony Stecchino a week ago, after Stecch attacked him backstage. But no, those were in passing. There’s a difference between deciding to make sure someone doesn’t start with you, and between starting with them. Long, while a protagonist of sorts, doesn’t actively seek out enemies. It’s not his style. What could be bothering her? He wonders if she could be sick or something. With the flu, or… Pregnant. OH SHIT. ---- Jack saw Ronnie stop suddenly. He didn’t know why, nor did he particularly care. THIS was the moment he had been waiting for… the right moment to strike. ---- Ronnie heard the crunch of a boot on asphalt, but before he had time to think of it, he felt himself grabbed from behind, around the neck. He felt his feet briefly leave the ground as his assailant pulled him towards the wall, and then the cold tickle as the back of the knife came to rest, blunt edge against his throat. “Give me your money and you live” Ronnie stood perfectly still, giving no evidence that he had heard the order. His mind was racing – how could he have been so stupid, as to let someone sneak up on him from behind, in the alleys, where muggings were so commonplace, they happened on the streets and didn’t stop traffic? Because of Deanna… “I said, give me your money.” Slowly, Ronnie raised both of his arms in the air, a gesture to show that he had no intentions of fighting. “I don’t have any on me. I don’t carry money in my pocket. Find somebody else”. This wasn’t actually true. Long had $50 in his wallet, as well as a drivers license and a few credit cards. But his senses were starting to come back. He could feel that the unseen man behind him was nervous, thorough the almost imperceptible twitches of the knife against his throat. Ronnie knew that the knife could be turned over in a second, and used to slit his throat and end his life… but something about the bloodshed… left him feeling somewhat wild. Reckless, even. ---- Jack’s breath left his chest in a soft hiss between his teeth. He may have been a mugger, but he had no desire to kill this man. He has not yet killed anybody, and is unsure what to do. He relaxes his grip on the knife, for just a split second… a second that costs him dearly. ---- Feeling the relaxation, Ronnie springs into action with the speed only one who fights as a career can manage. His right hand shoots down from its raised position and grips the knife around the blade. Mindless of the pain, he quickly bends forward, tossing Jack over his shoulder and to the ground in front of him. A stomp on the wrist and Jack releases the knife. He rolls to his feet, cursing. Ronnie shifts the knife to his uninjured left hand, while clenching his right in a fist. Blood seeps out of his hand, running between his fingers and down his wrist. He stands impassively, staring down the would-be mugger who is half sitting, half lying down on the black top road in front of him. “A body can sustain 100, one inch cuts before it dies from loss of blood… someone said this to me once. But who? Does it matter? Is it even true?” ---- Jack leaps to his feet and starts to run down the road, but an arm wrapped in black canvas shoots in front of him. A hand… a hand with blood still running down it… wraps around his neck. If Jack had had anything to eat that day, he would likely have shit his pants. ---- Long, his mind briefly off Deanna, stares at the man in front of him. Jack Cassidy has the frame of a man which, under ideal circumstances, might be powerful, but instead is wasted with malnutrition, shoulders slumped and eyes sunken and empty. A pathetic wretch. It’s not exactly orthodox in a street fight, but since his hand is already wrapped around the man’s neck, Long lifts him up, and slams him down on the alley floor with a chokeslam. Wrapping a bit of his trench coat around his hand to curb the flow of blood down it, Long places one booted foot on Jack’s neck. “Why would you do such a thing?” Jack made a feeble gagging noise. Long lifted his foot and placed it on the man’s upper arm, right where it joined the shoulder, effectively pinning him to the ground. “Because… I’m hungry…” Long continues to glare. The small feeling of pity that he once had for this man has faded away into nothing. “Because you are hungry…” Ronnie lets the sound of his voice slide into a growl as he continues to stare downwards. Jack writhes as best he can with half his body pinned to the asphalt. “It’s people like you who are what is wrong with the world. People who cannot take responsibility for their actions, and people who turn to others to rectify their problems. People who think that others are simply tools to be used so as to promote one’s own best interests. People who are scum.” Long, picking up the knife again, kneels down. “I’m going to let you go. You’re going to turn yourself into the police for what you’ve done. I’m going to go down to the jail in three day’s time, and ask them. And if you’re not there…” Ronnie twirls the knife in his right hand, a skill he picked up after his release from reform school. “I’ll come, and I’ll hunt you down myself.” Pocketing the knife, Long walks away from Jack, leaving him sprawled on the alley, recognition dawning in his eyes. ---- Jack had recognized the man, getting a close up of his face. Ronnie Long, of GW. Jack watched wrestling every evening it came on, with his illegal black box. He knew who Ronnie Long was… and knew that this man, just might be his ticket to a shitload of money, more than he could make in 20 years as a mugger and panhandler. He headed off to the police station. But with no intentions of turning himself in.
