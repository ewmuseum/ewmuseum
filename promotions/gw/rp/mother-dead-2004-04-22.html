Mother Dead

22 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 22 The scene opens up to the Connecticut hospital. The camera switches to the inside of a hospital room. There is a hospital bed in the centre of the room, with a chair beside the bed. There is a little table by the bed also. There is one big window in the corner of the room where the sun from outside is lighting up the room. Suddenly, my mother started to wake. I was sitting on the chair beside the bed praying that my mother would be all right, but i didn’t really think it would happen. “Hey mom.” I said. “Where am I?” she said. My mother was sweating from the head and it started dripping onto his hospital clothes. She had all these things hooked up onto her body. There was a water bag hung on a rope at the back of her bed. That’s where if she needed water she could just suck it up. She also had a oxygen bag hooked up to her. She couldn’t breath properly so she had that hooked up so it would help her breath. I was so depressed. That Roz Blayze was making my life hell. I haven’t slept in 4 days and I can hardly blink. When I get my hands on him at Wrestlecade I will make sure that he will pay for what he did to my mother. “You in the hospital.” I said softly. “How did I get here?” she asked again. “Someone kidnapped you and locked you into the tiger cage at the zoo. Me and a police officer came to rescue you because we didn’t want you to lose you life, in other words, die.” I said. “It was that Roz guy wasn’t it” I remember everything he said to me.” she said. “Mother, can you please tell me what he said to you?” I asked. “He said that your life will be a living hell and that he’s going to do anything he can to kill you or me. He said something about coming to a hospital and killing someone but he had already punched me out and I couldn’t hear him because I was unconscious.” she said. “Uh-Oh. We are in a hospital.” I said. “Oh my god! I’m going to die.” she said. “No your not. Don’t talk like that. If anyone dies, then I die.” I said. “No. I’ve been living longer than you. I want you to live your life fully. I gave birth to you for one reason, so you could grow up and onto an old man so you could die too. I will die, I’m already 64 years of age, I’ve lived my life fully and now its your turn.” she said in a very low voice. “Mom, are you dying?” I asked. I started to cry lightly as she touched my arm. I was balling my head out of my pants. I wiped the tears from mine and her face. I sat down onto the chair since I was standing and started to hug my mother. She was going to pass on just like my dad did the day I was born. I was going to have no one to talk to anymore, I’d be alone. Well, no actually, I would be with my mother and fathers angels. “Goodbye son, see you when you are here.” she said in a really low voice. She started to fade away slowly. I would be without a mother or father at the age of 20. Not that bad since I can take care of myself. My mother’s eyes started to shut and finally she died. I felt her pulse but I couldn’t hear nothing. Her chest didn’t move up and down, she was dead. I needed to go get a doctor to tell them. “Goodbye mom. You’ll alway be in my heart.” I said. I got up off the chair and walked out of the room. I came back a few minutes later with a nurse. On her name tag it said Katie. She walked over to the hospital bed and sat down on the chair. “I’m really sorry about your mother.” Katie said. “Don’t worry. I’ll be ok.” I said. “Tough man I see. I like tough men.” she said again. “Thank you.” I said. The nurse walked out of the room and then back in with a stretcher. There were 2 paramedics holding the side of it. They lifted my mother up and onto the stretcher. They took out a white sheet and put it over my mothers body. They rolled the stretcher out of them room. “Well, see you later.” Katie said. She walked out of the room with the paramedics as I stood there looking at nothing but air. My mother was gone and I would have to live with it for the rest of my life. Well, like everyone I guess I will have to get over it. “Goodbye mother, see you when I get up there.” I said to the empty room. I looked around and found some stuff that my mother had left in the hospital. It was a bracelet I had given her when I was 12 years old. There was also a ring, that would probably be her wedding ring. There was also a picture of my father and my mother. And then there was a picture of me and my mother holding hands at the circus. I took the stuff and out it into my pocket. I walked into the door way and out of the room as the scene started to fade.
