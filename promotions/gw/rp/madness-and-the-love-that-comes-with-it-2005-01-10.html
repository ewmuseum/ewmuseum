Madness And The Love That Comes With It

10 January 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/10/2005 Aris slouches on the couch, he wears a pair of black sweats, and a bottle of water is in his left hand. He stares impassively out into the austere Seattle sky. The wind pounds the heavy rain into his windows, the gusts scream through the cracks between the glass and sill. Aris stands scratching his unclothed torso. Red streaks show the direction of the scratches. Aris looks around the room. Darkness and silence down the hallway to his left, still unsettled calm in the kitchen. Aris sits back down, placing his feet on the glass coffee table in front of him. His hand reaches between two cushions, after a bit of digging between the light green cushions, brings out a black remote. His right index finger pushes down on the red power button at the top, the television clicks on. A man is going crazy over The Magic Bullet in front of really bad actors. Aris changes the channel. Paul Silvi for The Northwest Sports Report talks about how the Seahawks should easily conquer the Redskins setting themselves up for the NFC Championship game. Aris scoffs and changes the channel. “It’s the Seahawks, fucking band wagon jumpers.” Aris says through his teeth as he turns off the television. “Daytime T.V. sucks” The understatement of the day comes out of his mouth and into the silent air. He takes a deep breath in and then sighs, closing his eyes while snuggling into the couch. Soon he is asleep and he begins to dream. Aris stands in an empty arena, on the floor in front an eight sided ring. A crow caws from the rafters overhead, disturbed that an intruder has entered his lair. The air is cold; it bites at the end of his nose with each chilling breath. A golden light illuminates the arena, light miasma clouds waft without direction through the arena. The temperature in the arena drops. The ropes snap at the front of the eight sided ring, stairs unhurriedly materialize in front of them; they are red, velvet, smooth to the touch. “Paradise City” by Guns N Roses echoes through the arena. Each easy note pounding through Aris? brain. The music changes suddenly into “The Wedding March,” Aris slowly walks toward the front of the ramp that leads down from the Global stage. Along the top of stage above the tron and entrance reads, “WELCOME TO GLOBAL ARIS” A woman in a white wedding dress emerges from the curtains at the side of the entrance. She stands tall, a giantress, her skin unseen by Aris, everything covered by the dress and a long hang vale. A shiver runs down Aris spine as she begins to slowly walk down the ramp. Aris unknowingly takes two steps diffident, tripping and falling backward over the first stair. His head bounces off the thin mat lying beneath him. Aris rubs the back of his head with his hand, he looks down over his body, he is now in a tuxedo with an English style tie. The bride stands on the steps now, a cold gaze is brought upon him. Aris freezes, his last breath caught between his lungs and his mouth. The bride takes her final steps; stopping in the middle of the ring. A cold, chilling voice, yet soft and loving comes from under her vale. “I DO. I DO. I DO.” Aris slowly makes his way to his feet. He stares at the bride as she begins to shrink, once standing over eight feet tall, now just above five feet, repeating the phrase over and over. Aris licks his lips, his tongue dry and course. A strong wind rolls over the building causing it to shake, almost leaning over like a tree being pushed over by another tree. Pieces off the roof break off in shards, flying away as if they were dry dead leaves floating in a gentle autumn breeze. Aris covers himself, afraid that a rogue piece of the roof will come down and crush him. “I DO. I DO. I DO. I DO. I DO.” The bride repeats over and over. Her dressed unmoving in the beyond hurricane winds. Thunder roars in the distance, a bright flash of lightning, not white, but red, then to purple, then gone. The sun hangs over head, inviting, Aris, confused, as rain begins to fall into the arena, quickly gaining in depth. Another crash of the multicolored lightning strikes the far wall of the arena. Sparks, splinters of wood and masonry fall down into the arena, silently crushing the seats below it under the wail of the wind over the top of the arena. Water quickly careens down the ramp, forming a makeshift waterfall. The lights along the ramp snap and spark as they cut away into oblivion. The bride continues her soft, now almost romantic chat. A flash of lightning strikes the bride, she explodes into fire. Aris is blasted backward, he splashes into the water, disappearing beneath it’s rippled surface. The bride flames on, her body explodes into a thousand black doves. They flap their wings fighting the wind, trying to gain hold and pull themselves to safety, but they are soon over taken and pulled into the draft, floating away as if smoke from a giant chimney in a winter storm. Aris opens his eyes; a burning sensation rips through ocular cavities and into his head. He opens his mouth to scream, but his mouth is filled with the acidic water. Aris scrambles for the shore, anything to pull him out of this. Something brushes his leg, a feeling of a million needles rips into his calf, he can feel he is bleeding, he continues to thrash for anything to grab on to, but nothing. Something slams into Aris? stomach, his hands and legs and thrust forward as he is pushed deeper and deeper into the unknown, two giant sets of teeth tear at him, pulling him apart, his mind now blank and clear, his eyes open. He watches his legs and most of his internal organs float into a blue void. A shadow closes in above him, the shadow of a massive shark, it disappears. Aris closes his eyes, calm washing over the remainder of his body. Aris feels the eyes of the shark bearing down on him. It’s over, he’ll join his father. “Remember our father isn’t dead” He lives in Vancouver.” “Right.” The words are perfectly audible through the vast ocean. The shark clamps down on Aris head. Everything goes black. The shark shakes and tears the remnants of Aris Cagze Saint. The shark and the rest of Aris float away into an incoming darkness. Aris snaps his eyes open; he lies on the floor of the empty arena. His focus fuzzy, a woman in white, the bride slowly walks up the steps back into the center of the ring. Aris rises to his feet, he looks into the ring. The bride is nowhere to be seen. The ring is completely empty. A tumbleweed rolls down the ramp away, up the stairs, through the ring, which has now become a reddish, yellow rock bed. The seats in the arena are now rocks and cacti. The isles are now miles of perfect, untouched desert sand seemingly stretching forever into the roof, which now seems to form the sky, the lights each acting as a blazing star that is hotter than a sun. Aris rolls up his sweats to around his knees. The suns beat down on him, each one seems to be reaching into his soul stealing his energy, he walks up the rocky steps to the rocky table that lies in the center of this new world around him. “I DO. I DO. I DO.” The light wind whispers in his ear, blowing his hair back like a gentle hand, caressing his naked chest. The wind makes him feel loved. “The bride became the wind.” Aris says lightly, not sure even to himself if he said it out loud. “Romantic.” A dark figure lingers higher up hiding in the rocks, watching Aris. Aris circles feeling the eyes upon him, seeming to see out of the strangers eyes, looking down at him. The stranger takes a drink of ice cold water from his canteen. Thirst overwhelms Aris as he falls to his knees on the stone slab. The skin peals back, Aris doesn’t notice the pain or the trickles of blood, he can no longer see out of the stranger’s eyes. He’s gone. There is a clap of thunder. Aris stares to the sky seeing the fresh water coming down at him. “RAIN!” he shouts to no one, but the sky. The rain falls steadily from the sky, but evaporates under the intense heat from the many suns forming a mist just above Aris? outreached tongue, the warm mist making him thirstier by the second; soon he falls over, taking in a mouth full of sand. Out of the corner of his eyes he sees the vultures begin to circle over head, ready for their new meal. Aris sighs blowing the sand out of his mouth. He closes his eyes. He soon feels shaking and a slap across the face. “Wake up!” The voice is familiar, but not soothing there is rage in the voice. Aris snaps his eyes open. He stares into the angered face of Andrea, his fianc?. He groggily looks around the apartment, he’s feels wet and yet hot at the same time. “You ruined my new couch.” Aris looks over at Andrea. “What are you talking about?” “The couch is torn, wet and has sand all over it, what did you do?” Aris stands up to inspect the couch himself. As Andrea said, the couch is completely ruined. The whole thing is soaked, so is the floor underneath it, also littered with sand, each cushion is torn, with spots of blood on it. “What happened here Aris, you owe me an explanation.” “I owe you a new couch.” Aris begins to walk away into the dark hallway toward the bedroom. “Where are you going?” Andrea’s voice chases after him. “Get you money for your couch and I have to pack, I leave in a few hours.” “Your plane left an hour ago.” Aris stops dead in the hallway. He shakes his head, then continues to walk down the hall. “I thought you were broke.” “I am,” Aris replies, “but I have secrets.” He says under his breath. He enters the bedroom and flips on the light. Lying on the bed is a bill. A bill from a plastic surgeon in Japan. Andrea walks into the room, she leans against the doorway, looking at Aris as he examines the bill. “When did you get plastic surgery?” Andrea asks in a calm soothing voice. “You’re features are perfect.” Aris turns and stares at her blankly. “Hurry and pack, there is another flight soon and your parents are waiting for you downstairs.” Aris? blank stare now becomes a look of confusion. “My parent’s?” Aris replies, turning and looking back over the bill. “Oh right” I forgot they were coming.”
