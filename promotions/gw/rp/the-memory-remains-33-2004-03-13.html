The Memory Remains: #33

13 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 31304 This body holding me Reminds me of my own mortality. Embrace this moment. Remember. We are eternal. All this pain is an illusion “And Vincent?” Deac asked, slinging the satchel over his shoulder, adjusting it so that he could still reach his twin Desert Eagles in their specialized holster nested near the small of his back. A haze filled the world. A dark slight shade of red. Deep inside of me, I could feel it start to grow. It started off like a warm, fuzzy feeling. In fact, it was warm. And it was fuzzy. My old friends. Hate. Anger. Vengeance. Long ago, they comforted me. Kept me safe at night. At times, they fueled fits of controlled rage, allowing me to explode at targets with the accuracy of a sniper in the jungle. They sang to me now. Comforting me. Controlling me. I almost didn’t feel my mouth move as I spoke. In fact, I was surprised when the words hit my ears and realized it was my voice speaking them. “That motherfucker is mine.” With that, I turned and left Deac standing there. As I moved away, I pulled one of the four glocks out of its holster, double-checked that it was chambered, and checked the safety. This time, I would not lose her. At the corner of the house, I peered around the edge towards the front gate. About four men stood at the gate, one man up to his wrists in electronics, trying to disarm the bomb that Deac had left for them. The other three had automatic weapons out, scanning the area, itching trigger fingers caressing the cold metal, wanting a reason to let lead fly. From my pocket, I produced Deacon’s favorite toy. A small black box with a single red button in the middle, a plastic piece covering the button to prevent any premature pressing. I flicked the clear plastic piece up and pressed down hard on the red button. I would’ve paid a large sum of money just to see the look on the bomb man’s face when he realized that all of his hard work had been for nothing. Granted is would’ve only lasted for about a quarter of a second before his body was atomized beyond recognition, but I still would’ve liked to of seen it. The bomb did what it was designed to do. Destroy. Or rather create. Creating destruction. Destruction is really a beautiful site to witness, especially when it is done properly. Like with a bomb. The tendrils of flame and fire, licking their way out, consuming, wanting, lusting. Destroying. So much like humans, but not. So alive, but not. In a matter of seconds, the bomb detonated, bodies thrown, screams shouted, and reinforcements were sent to investigate. Leaving the inside of the house practically empty by now. I leaned back from the corner, and pressed my back against the wall. Time to go in. About eight feet back from where I stood, a wall shimmered against the fading sunlight. Moving to it, I reached up the three feet and applied a steady amount of force to the wood surrounding the glass. It didn’t budge. Figured as much. All this security walking around and they still didn’t feel safe enough to leave the windows unlocked. What is this world coming to? Not figuring on a lot of time to work with, and the lack of personnel in the house, I swung my satchel back and slammed it into the pane of glass. The black bag bounced off with a resounding thud. What the fuck. I stared at the sack. Picking it up again, I hurled it against the glass with all of my might. It bounced again. That’s just fucking wrong. I raised the glock in my hand and squeezed off two shots, one high, the other low. This time, between gunshots, the sound of shattering glass filled the air. About fucking time. I jumped up slightly, reaching into the window, and pulled myself in quickly, trying to avoid the broken glass. I crunched loudly as I landed into a kneeling position. The hallway I was in branched off into two directions, to the left and to the right. Hanging on the wall directly in front of me was a painting of two women, both pointing off to the right. Hey, when in Rome, right? I padded my way to the right and turned the corner. Coming down the hall, heading in my direction, were two of Vincent’s men, automatic weapons slung low over their shoulders. They saw me in my full assault gear and assumed that I was not an invited guest. Rather than to question me, they thought I best to try to turn me into a bloody pulp. I leapt back around the corner just as the bullets began to pummel the opposite wall. Pulling out a second glock, I faced the wall and laid down on my stomach, my guns extended out in front of me. Rolling out as fast as I could, I pulled back on the trigger rapidly. The two men’s bodies jerked as the mercury filled hollow points ate away at their flesh, filling their bodies with poison. They dropped heavily to the ground. Okay, so lets not go that way. Turning around, I headed back in the opposite direction, pausing at the picture to look at it once again. Two ladies pointing to the right. I looked closer. They were laughing. At what? A jackass being ridden by a jester. I popped a cap into the jester’s head. Not this time. A quick run and I came to a set of doors. Slowly opening one an inch of so, I peered out into the room. Milling around in this large main room, I could see several of Vincent’s men milling about. In the back of the room, a set of stairs led to a higher open level with several doors visible. A female scream pierced the silence. I slowly shut the door. I needed to get upstairs. Past all of the men with the automatic weapons. Upstairs. Where Vincent was doing god knows what to Vic. Upstairs. Through hell. From my satchel, I removed two grenades. Hell, might as well bring them with me. I pulled the pins out of the two round balls of doom, jerked the door open and threw them into the room, slamming the door back shut. I could hear some of the men saying “What the fuck”!” right before the grenades worked their magic. I could feel the blast through the door. I swung the door back opened and slow walked into the room, both arms raised, glocks poised and ready. The men were sprawled out on the ground, suits ruined, guns dropped, hands grasping bleeding body parts or ruined ears from the noise of the explosion. My world was red. My guns spewed out death to the survivors. There was no ducking, there was no hiding, there was no bullet-tag. This was not a shoot-out. This was target practice. The female scream sounded again. I shot the last man and bolted up the stairs. On the landing, there were six doors. Starting at the closest one, I started kicking in doors, my gun raised. Door one. Empty. Door two. Empty. Door three. Empty. The scream sounded again just as I was about to kick in door four. It came from behind door number five. I ejected both magazines and refilled the glocks with fresh ones. I took a deep breath and kicked it in. The sight shocked me. Another scream filled the air. This time is was me, as I pulled back on the triggers of both guns.
