The Midnight Rider

3 September 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 3rd, 2003 The Dark Horse Saloon was well known for three things: GW stars, buffalo wings, and its mechanical bull. A customized Rockin' B 'Contestant', the machine was such a terror that the Dark Horse's patrons had nicknamed the bull 'Blackie', for the bruises it left on those foolish enough to try and mount it. In the four months the Dark Horse had been in operation, few had been able to stay on Blackie at its highest setting for more than a handful of seconds -- but a picture of one Ely Braxton hung on the wall, the plaque on the frame proclaiming his record ride of two and three-quarter minutes to all who entered the saloon. So of course Ely was more than a little interested when he strolled into the Dark Horse and found a stranger riding Blackie, and a ring of people surrounding the bull, hooting and hollering. 'Hey, Jack,' said Ely, strolling up to the bar, 'who's on Blackie?' 'Some prettyboy I ain't ever seen 'round here before,' replied the bartender, filling a mug with beer and sliding it to Ely. Ely drank deep from the mug, and sighed his approval. 'So,' Ely asked, 'how long he been on there?' 'By my count,' the bartender glanced at his watch, 'about a minute forty-five.' Ely perked up. 'Ain't nobody stayed on that long since the last time Brick got plastered.' 'Yep.' His curiosity piqued, Ely strolled towards Blackie, beer in hand. By the time he'd pushed through the crowd around the mechanical bull, the clock above it read two minutes and fifteen seconds -- the longest anyone had ever stayed on the bull, other than him. Atop the bucking machine was a strapping fellow in jeans and a white T-shirt, desperately holding a cowboy hat on with one hand. Ely chuckled as Blackie bucked hard, and the stranger's hat went flying, releasing a mane of blonde hair. Still, as the seconds ticked by, the stranger clung tenaciously to the machine, riding out every jerk and jostle. And through it all, Ely noticed, the guy maintained a self-assured grin. *** Akodo Rokku wondered how he'd ever let himself get talked into this. He'd gotten all gussied up like a real cowboy -- or at least like what someone who had spent his entire life in Japan and had only seen cowboys on TV and such thought one looked like -- and come down to the Dark Horse Saloon looking for some fun. Just a few minutes ago, he had been flirting and sharing drinks happily with a stacked blonde -- then he made a careless boast, and found himself mounted on this crazed death machine, hanging on for dear life. Still, he thought, gotta make it look easy. After all -- he was the Steel Lion. And the Steel Lion could do anything. For the first few seconds, it wasn't so bad -- and then 'Blackie' kicked it into high gear. Rokku managed to hang on only through sheer tenacity as he tried to shift his weight at each buck and maintain his center of balance. After a minute or two, he felt like he was getting the hang of it -- and the machine bucked hard twice, nearly flinging him off. He managed to maintain his grip on the bull, if not his hat, and at that point decided simply to stop thinking about it and ride it out on instinct and luck. He closed his eyes and slowed his breathing, letting his body react of its own accord -- and trying to shut out the mounting cheers of his audience. The metal beast bucked harder and spun faster as the seconds passed, doing its best to dislodge the man atop it, and Rokku struggled to maintain a trance state. He lost track of time until, with a mighty heave, Blackie sent Rokku flying. Immediately, Rokku snapped back to consciousness, tucking into a tight roll. He hit the safety mat around the mechanical bull, tumbled across it, and skidded to a stop on his butt, just off the edge of the mat. 'Hey,' said a gruff voice. Rokku looked up and saw a hulking square-jawed man with a beer in one hand and a cigar in the other. Chomping down on the cigar, the man offered Rokku his hand -- who took it, and found himself quickly pulled up to his feet. The instant Rokku was standing, the patrons of the saloon broke into a rousing applause. Rokku glanced at the clock. It read 3:14 -- twenty-nine seconds past the previous champion's record. Rokku smiled broadly, and bowed low for the crowd. 'Not bad there, feller.' Said the large, square-jawed man. 'You must be one tough S.O.B. to beat that record!' 'Well,' replied Rokku, smirking, 'I try.' The big man laughed, and offered Rokku his hand again. Rokku clasped it. 'Name's Ely Braxton,' said the big man, 'I *set* that old record.' 'Braxton?' said Rokku, a quirking an eyebrow, 'Well, this is interesting. I'm Akodo Rokku.' Braxton laughed heartily. 'You're the new Japanese guy Christina brought in a few weeks ago, huh? I thought you'd like more, y'know? Japanese.' Rokku shrugged. 'Well, my dad was American, and?' Braxton cut Rokku off. 'Hey now, there's time for stories later. First things first -- I'm buying you a drink. Hell -- a round for the whole place, in honor of our new champion!' The patrons cheered for that even louder than they'd cheered when Rokku broke the record. The two men made their way back to the bar, and Braxton ordered beers for everyone. 'So,' said Rokku, as he sipped on his beverage, 'you're a friend of McCoy-san's, neh?' 'Yeah,' Braxton replied, 'I've known Bubba and Chris for a few years now -- started out as a hand on Bubba's ranch. Lemme tell ya, back then I never thought I'd end up trying to help Chris hold this company together while Bubba was lyin' in the hospital?' *** From a dark corner of the smoky saloon, a shadowed figure watched as Rokku and Ely swapped stories like old friends. They'd been doing this for almost an hour now. It was really getting dull. Then a vivacious blonde woman walked up, leaned on Rokku's shoulder, and whispered something in his ear. The Steel Lion's eyes widened, and he quickly said goodbye to Ely -- then he let the blonde lead him out the front door. The figure wondered for a moment what he should do -- he still wasn't sure if this Akodo Rokku was warrior or fool. The figure rose from his seat, his lips curling into a feral grin. Warrior or fool, it didn't matter -- to Leinad Soma, ALL men were the same thing: Prey.
