Steadfast when will has gone? .:: ::: ::. Just West of Chicago, IL Undisclosed Evening, 2005 Alexis Key had to pause in her running for a few moments. Her legs were burning all the way up from the tendons of the ankles upwards to the backs of her knees. The bottoms of her feet had long ago ceased to simply ache, but the pain had now turned into more of a stabbing throb, each time she put the ball of her foot to the pavement. She honestly felt like the tread of her sneakers was about to be worn through. She sighed, bent over at the waist panting. At the moment, everything was a blur- the past few weeks had been hell. After leaving Cleveland to get away from The Den somehow, she’d been running, ceaselessly. Perhaps the worst part was that she’d been running literally, not figuratively. She’d been able to hitch a ride just outside of Cleveland as far as Dearborn, Michigan, but after that, she’d been on her own, on foot. And she was starting to wonder exactly how on earth she was ever going to get across the country back to California, and the manor house that she and the other members of The Den’s coterie had burned down in search of Shadow and her companion at the time, Gideon de Medici. She was close to the point of giving up. The only thing that kept her moving was the thought that if she didn’t get out there, she’d never know the truth. That, and if she stopped now, The Den would catch up with her, and she’d go the way of her father- obscurity. She would die, and no one would even know what happened to her, just like Edred Key had. In fact, just like Althea Simpson Key had- save that Alexis knew something else had happened to her mother. Her father had acted too depressed for her just to have died. And then, they killed him- The Den killed him. She didn’t know why. She wasn’t even sure that she wanted to find out, but what she did want to know was? well, whatever The Den had tried to keep from her. The questions still ran through her mind. She had only been thirteen when her mother had disappeared. Nobody had any information on what had happened until the day that her father got the letter. She could remember almost like it was yesterday- the ashen color of her father’s face that continually paled as each word in the letter fell past his eyes. He never told her what the letter said. He burned it in the fireplace after he read it, and he turned to her, with a hazy sort of look in his grey-blue eyes. “Your mum’s dead, Alexis,” he had said, in his oddly out-of-place British accent. And that was it. No explanation, nothing. Alexis knew that her mother had been a member of Daddy’s “work” as he called it, but though she had suspected at the time that The Den had something to do with her mother’s disappearance, he never confirmed it. For all Alexis knew, Althea might have simply been sick; perhaps her parents chose to conceal that from her. They might have done something like that, as abnormally as they tended to live their lives. Seven years had passed. She had started college at eighteen, to he father’s great indifference. In fact, as she got older, that indifference only increased. She’d stopped trying to earn his accolades openly by the time she graduated high school. There was a sinking feeling, however, that she had still been trying to do it, even though she knew nothing would come of it. Her father was nothing more than a dead shell when he died. She never even knew the circumstances of his death, only that she had been hard at work at school, studying for midterms. The knock came at her door at around 1:30 in the morning, and she had wondered who it could have been. When she had opened the door, she had seen an unknown-to-her courier, bearing an envelope of pressed white paper, of a very fine grain. He had stood around while she opened it, which she had considered very rude at the time. But when her eyes had skimmed over the paper, she had known what her father felt seven years before, reading a similar notice. Or what he must have felt, at least, because Alexis had felt nothing at all. Her father had just been a dead husk anyway, what was the point of mourning his dying? Or, his being killed, rather- how was it they had dressed it up? “Killed in the line of his duty.” That was how they’d prettied it up for the eyes of his daughter. It should have said something along the lines of how his soul had been dead since Althea had passed to another realm, but things like these only told the surface truth, didn’t they? The courier had stayed because he had instructions to bring her back to The Den’s quarters for briefing on what she was to do with herself now. Though she found that somewhat presumptuous, she had gone along. Once she had gotten there, she had been informed, in front of a Council group, that her father’s last wish had been that she finish her schooling, and then undertake training with The Den. When she reached the highest order of operatives, she was to undertake the work that Edred had been working on when he died. And that was how she had become novice order in The Den. They’d promised her everything- paid tuition to finish college, a monthly stipend for expenses and an apartment, and training as a novice. She had excelled- perhaps she had excelled just to prove to herself that she didn’t miss Althea and Edred, and that she was still just as driven to succeed without their presences in her live. But lately, she’d started to believe that was nothing but a lie. She couldn’t recall when exactly it had been- some time in late 2004. Her father had been dead for nearly two years, and she thought she had proven herself- she thought she was surviving well on her own, that she was going to be just fine. Everyone else might have believed that little veneer, but Alexis, unfortunately, did not. The week that they had gone to Napa Valley had seemingly proved it. She had been devoted to the cause until then- until she realized that the man heading The Den, Geffroi LaBatte, was fallible. That was the first moment that she had ever seen fear in LaBatte’s stony blue eyes, and she was not amused by the chance to see such. He was just a man, wasn’t he? He wasn’t the stoic little god he made himself out to be. He was nothing. He was weak. Just like Edred had been for the ensuing seven years after Althea died. It was from that point that Alexis had begun to examine her life, piece by piece, bursting the balloon, so to speak. And things had fallen into shambles. For some reason, she found it impossible to avoid piecing together exactly what was going on with The Den, no matter how well she knew that it was going to shatter everything she believed in. Geffroi LaBatte was a phony. A phony, a lie, nothing that he said he was. He had some connection to the Kindred that he’d pledged The Den to hunt, though he wouldn’t outright say so, of course- it was just something that she sensed off of him. The Den was serving a higher purpose, certainly- but it wasn’t God’s. It was one of those blood-sucking leeches that they all paid homage to, without ever knowing it. Evidently, Alexis had been getting too close to the truth for comfort. How anyone had figured that out was beyond her, but she wasn’t entirely sure she wanted to know how The Den had found her out. All she knew was that once she got the hearing notice, she was a dead woman. She had envisioned the moment in her head many times- going before the Council so they could figure out how much she knew. And she didn’t like her chances of standing up to their questioning without them discovering what she’d figured out. That was a one-way ticket straight to a six-foot wooden box. And if she’d managed somehow to evade their notice, they just would have brainwashed her back into the novice order and watched her more closely than ever. That was why she had run, of course. She couldn’t stand it anymore- she had to find a way out. Someone had been near her apartment, taking potshots at her, assumedly because of her connection to The Den, in some form or another- and it had happened at least three times. Perhaps it was irrational, but as Alexis had pried her sneakers over her heels that night, she had known that she would never be able to come back. She was leaving behind school, her papers, her belongings, and her life. The road showed on Alexis, its indelible mark left in the tired sagging black circles under her eyes. This woman of barely twenty-two looked years beyond her age, and considering the great exhaustion that plagued her every night after a lot of traveling, and whatever food she could scrounge, she had to admit that she wasn’t doing so well. She had thought about using her bank account to buy at least an extra set of clothes, a knapsack, and a hot meal back in Michigan, but she’d quickly changed her mind on that. All it would take was one ATM transaction, and they would have been able to track her. Granted, she would have probably been long gone by the time anyone from The Den got to Dearborn, but they would know she was headed west, and they’d be able to figure out where she was going from there. Maybe she’d put too much faith in them. But she couldn’t take that chance right now. And so Alexis Key, for better or for worse, dug in her pocket for a hair-tie, pulled back her mussed dirty-blonde hair, and shouldered her load. She had to get to Napa Valley in one piece. It was the only way that she could start from the beginning, and figure out the truth. What she’d do when she found it, she didn’t know yet. Her feet stinging and her shoes echoing as they slapped against the pavement, she turned her uncovered head into the wind and rain and jogged on. .:: ::: ::. Undisclosed Location April 2005 Her boots tapped across the wooden floor, back and forth, back and forth. But no answers were forthcoming, no matter how many times their heels drummed a steady and rhythmic cadence. From underneath the divan, the hulking figure of Damek was all shadow in the dimness. She could still see his ochre eyes glowing in the darkness. They flickered back and forth as she paced, his uneasiness coming from the fact that his mistress was very ill at ease at the moment. Jirina simply paced along at her side, back and forth, licking her mistress? hands whenever they hung at her sides. Shadow couldn’t stand the thought of being alone anymore. She had rifled through every single belonging of hers that she could find until she found her list of contacts, and from there, had finally remembered that it was a Bratovich kennel in Seattle that was keeping her dogs until further notice. She had called at once; despite the fact that it had been the middle of the night, Ekaterina Bratovich had been more than willing to provide her with answers about how her beloved hellhounds were doing, and more than that, had been willing to guarantee next-day transport from Seattle to her current location. So at least she hadn’t been alone- but despite the fact that she had not been alone, she was not any more reassured than she had been a week ago. A victory in Global had fallen quite easily under her fingertips- this normally would have pleased her, but since it had happened, she’d been all distraction. The tiny flash of blonde hair had done enough to startle her, but what was worse than that was the childish laughter that had plagued her attempts at sleep all day long. And each time she had opened her eyes, there was only a flash of greenish-blue light, and nothing more. Things had started moving, too. She’d looked for her brush where she had set it on the dresser that night when she had woken up after fitfully tossing until the sun went down, but it had been gone. Clearly, she remembered setting it there. But upon searching for it, she had found it not anywhere nearby, but across the room, under the bed. Similar incidents had occurred all week long. There was no doubt- someone was fucking with her. And if Shadow could have gotten her hands on whoever it was, there was no doubt that they would have gotten the worst possible end of the bargain, because there were few people who dared to taunt a Fiend and presume to live after doing it. The latest incident was more annoying than it was disturbing. Zenith had kindly forwarded her mail to her, seeing as she couldn’t exactly go and retrieve it during the day. She got the usual suspects, cream-colored envelopes with official seals on the front. One from Global, detailing her official booking this week; one from PTC, letting her know about the scheduled date for the Extreme Royale, which she would no doubt be interested in watching; correspondence from Cyrus, the Kiasyd she had met back in Zdice, telling her that she could come back to visit any time she liked- provided she called first, of course (the Kiasyd were a rather odd lot); a letter from her lawyer detailing the successful negotiations over her getting out of her WMW contract four months sooner than she should have, in incredibly tedious detail; and lastly a bill from the Bratovich kennels in Seattle for $3000 for the care of Damek and Jirina for an eight-month period- this, of course, was accompanied by an incredibly ingratiating letter from Ekaterina, noting how happy they would be to look after Damek and Jirina again, any time she pleased. “Only if the price is as good,” she thought to herself. Or at least, she thought that was all of her mail, until she found the plain white paper envelope slipped within everything else, completely unmarked. At first, she’d thought it a mistake, something of Zenith’s that got mixed in with hers. She made mental preparations to have it sent back to him, but of course, her natural curiosity started to get the best of her. Within a few moments from noticing it, she had pried open the envelope with her talon-sharp fingernails. She peered inside it expectantly, looking for a letter, but she saw nothing, save two small objects. As she opened the envelope wide enough to pull them out, the scent of lilacs heavily assailing her nose as she did so- given how acute her senses were, it was almost enough to make her stomach heave up? well, pretty much nothing, since she probably had no stomach left to speak of. That didn’t matter. She shoved the perception back in her mind as she dumped the contents of the envelope onto the counter. Her eyes widened perceptibly, every muscle tensing around the realization of what was inside. A small knot of blonde hair, tied with a seafoam green ribbon trimmed in a creamy ivory lay upon the counter. The thick knot of hair curled up at the end, as though it had been rumpled in a drawer for quite some time. She snaked her fingers over it, but then jerked back, as though her skin had been scorched. She couldn’t touch it, because she’d just realized how close the color was to? nevermind that. Her eyes turned slowly from the lock of hair to the other object. A ring. The golden circle was still perfectly intact, and on top of it was a citrus-colored fire opal. An exotic stone from back when she was alive. And that got her thinking how much this ring looked like? no, it couldn’t be. She picked it up, ignoring the seething discomfort that shot through her as she did so, glancing at the inside of the little circle, just underneath the stone setting for something that should have been there. Her eyes widened again as she actually saw it- exactly what she was looking for. Initials carved in tiny flowery script: P.M.F.B.A. “Pour ma fille bien-aim’e,” she whispered. Aric had once created a ring just like this for Angela. It was intended to be a gift on her eighteenth birthday, a part of her dowry that would go with her when she married. It was not to be exchanged for any monetary value, though even in the Dark Ages, it would have been worth a substantial sum. This was simply an expression of a father’s love for his daughter- but how could someone have come to possess it? More importantly, why would they have sent it to her? She cast it on the counter in disgust. Somebody was bullshitting her- and somebody was very clever. “Maybe not.” God how she hated that voice in her head. But maybe she should at least listen? She bent at the waist, her eyes frantically examining every detail of the objects laying on the counter. The little strand of hair? about the right color. Tied up with that strand of ribbon that had matched Angela’s favorite “fancy dress.” Lia remembered how she had used that ribbon to tie back Angela’s thick blonde curls on the hotter days in southern France, when they had visited the summer manor. The ring? the ring practically confirmed its own authenticity. The scent of lilac assailed her again, like a fresh slap in the face. She remembered now, the field of lilacs behind the manor house that had bloomed in the springtime. Angela had delighted in picking them, when she was old enough to walk on her own. She’d come back with armload after armload of them, every day that they were still in bloom. Lia remembered teasing her about picking up the whole field once. She had died just as the lilacs had come into bloom- Lia remembered weeping openly as she had laid the little bouquet of lilacs into her little girl’s arms for the last time. As poignant as the memory was, anger surged up through her- anger nearing the point of hatred. Someone was pulling her strings as though she were a puppet. But she wouldn’t dance, not this time. She was tired of playing games for someone else’s amusement. Who could it have been, though? She could only come up with one of two ideas. First, Virgil. He was the only Trickster that she knew, and after all, he had created rather elaborate illusions to foster her remembering this or thinking that before. At the thought, ire surged through her, hot and acidic. She hadn’t thought about Virgil in weeks? “That’s not true,” said the voice in her head. God how she hated that voice when it was right. So, she had been thinking about him- more than just that, she had been missing him. But if this was his doing? She knew he’d said that he didn’t want to see her anymore, but she swore to all that was holy that she would hunt him down and fleshcraft what remained of him into a giant pile of firewood, to be immediately used. This wasn’t funny. How could he do this? “It can’t be him, Lia” he doesn’t know any of this stuff even exists.” That thought gripped her, icy cold, in the midst of her raging. Couldn’t his kind conjure up memories, and turn them corporeal? She shook her head. No, no, that wasn’t the province of Tricksters. They were more apt to create from scratch. But if it wasn’t him, then who was it? The thought assailed her relentlessly, until one name came shooting down the river of her thoughts, crashing into the front of her brain. Corvus. Her eyes narrowed, and her fists clenched at her sides. That was the only thing that made sense. Of course that ass would have kept track of all of this shit, because he was the one who couldn’t ever let go of what had happened over a thousand years ago now. The sheer ridiculousness of the situation made her even angrier. Who did he think he was, deliberately sending these things to her? Who did he think he was, attempting to pluck at her heart strings? Little did he know that she was far too old to really have any heartstrings left, at least as regarded pithy little Kine, whether they had been her daughter or not. She remembered clearly, just a scant few weeks ago (but what were weeks when you had centuries under your belt?)? she had given Corvus a fairly serious lecture on the dangers of hanging on to Angela longer than need be. And 1200 years was certainly long enough, wasn’t it? She snarled. It was obvious what this was about. The past had always been something that Corvus had hung tenaciously to, as though it was the only thing that gave him meaning, and he’d always made her feel awful when she hadn’t acted exactly the same. But he didn’t hold domain over her heart anymore, and the fact that he would try to play the same games sent a shock of indignance and wrath shooting through her, all at once, all the same. Her blood was boiling red-hot right now. He would pay for this. He wasn’t going to get his way this time. She’d gone to too many lengths to make sure she got her revenge, and he wasn’t going to ruin her plans until she was done with them. Ignoring the burn in her fingertips, she snatched up the envelope, stuffing the hair back inside it. With the press of a nearby remote, she started the fire in the little gas fireplace across from the sofa. In a flurry of motion, she dashed over to the fireplace, kicked open the screen with the heel of her boot, and fought of the Red Fear long enough to toss the envelope in. Slowly, the flames crawled up the outside of the paper, immolating it with a sickening smell as the hair caught on fire as well. The rage still darting through her veins uncontrollably, she took the ring and stormed out onto the back balcony of the house. When she reached its edge, she let the ring fly from her hand, vehemently flinging it as far as she could into the deep woods that surrounded this place. After a few moments, the glowing orange in her eyes finally began to calm- whenever she was angry, the powers of the fire koldun inside her seethed dangerously close to the surface. She walked slowly back into the house, her boots clunking across the wood. As she strode back over to the kitchen counter, satisfied, something caught her eye. Lying on top of the smooth black-and-white mottled marble surface was a plain white paper envelope. She stormed over to the precise place where it lay and shredded it open in seconds, smelling the heady fragrance of violets. Inside the envelope were a lock of blonde hair, and a golden signet ring with a fire-opal in the center. Rage roared through her. Hardly in control of herself, she stormed over to the fireplace- the fire was out. She opened the glass screen a little too roughly- it cracked. There was nothing inside the fireplace, save the gas logs. Not even ashes. Her eyes glowed bright ochre once again. “ZMRDE ZKURVENEJ!” The envelope went skittering to the floor again, along with all the other papers and envelopes. The heel of a black boot trampled a pile of them underfoot, leaving a marked black smudge on the backs as she stalked towards the front door of the house in a rage, determined to find Corvus and rend him limb from limb, by force, if necessary. She made it as far as the front door before she realized that she had no clue where he was. Frustration flowed through her, and she looked down at the floor. And when she looked down, she saw something hanging around her neck- the rosary that had appeared in her hotel room last week. The only problem with that was that she hadn’t put it on. With a scream of wrath, she tore it off, rending the pathetic little black metal chain in half. She flung it across the room so hard that the tip of it spiked into the large mirror and stuck there, as a crack splintered its way down the glass. Once it was off her, the near-frenzy quickly subsided. And that was when she noticed the burning sensation on her skin. R’tschreck rising again, she opened her palm, and stared down at it. The perfect imprint of a jeweled rosary cross was burned into it, blood leaking messily down the wound and spilling onto the perfect white Persian rug beneath her feet. .:: ::: ::. Undisclosed Location April 2005 Lia Radilu Vlaszy had everything that she should have wanted. She had her means of sustenance, and she had her privacy. By all rights, that seemed to be all that a being like Lia needed. Yet right now, the pair of eyes that watched her could see that she was full of sorrow and ire. Woefully, She watched. Was this the lot of all the undead? Was it typical for all of them to spend their nights alternately raging at their lot in unlife and conversely seducing others to join their ranks? But She had to wonder, was it really so bad? What was so terrible about never knowing the ravages of disease? She could remember a time when She had been sick, and it had been agony, agony tearing at every part of Her flesh. What was so terrible about never aging? She could remember the days of Her youth, so long ago? just an ancient memory now, hardly anything to get excited about. She had been na’ve, mentally undeveloped, incapable of dealing with anything, save with the help of her guardians. There was nothing so wonderful about youth that needed to be recovered, but Lia never had to worry about that. She simply stopped aging at twenty-five, eternally beautiful. What was terrible about never knowing the grave? A shudder ran through Her. The grave too She knew. How could Lia be so upset about never knowing the cloying, cold and quiet darkness that wrapped around the body with the shroud? How many had She prepared for similar fate, knowing all the time the terror of a soul trapped within the earthly body until it could clamber its way out? And all around, the deathly terror of the coffin, the desperation to claw fingernails against the satin-draped lid- but muscles no longer functioned, and screams no longer reverberated from dead vocal chords. But then it came to Her. Lia lost her daughter to plague? her son when her family had forced her to come home to Zdice? her entire family when the Vlaszy line had been slaughtered during the Anarch Revolt? She had lost her husband and regained him many times over only to lose him again. She had lost name after name after name, all to the rise and fall, ebb and flow of time. When was she ever recompensed for her misfortune? She would be recompensed by another new face that would only slide away into emptiness before the wounds were fully healed, falling away from Lia’s heart and clawing them deep, red, and bleeding afresh. Bitterness weighed down on the being at the window. Her vision of the scene blurred as She pressed her face against the glass. She was getting weaker- She probably couldn’t stay here much longer. But She didn’t want to leave! She was trying so hard to learn what She could about Lia, to learn what it was that made Lia so distant, and so cold, so non-receptive to anyone trying to reach into her world and touch her. But the answers were nil, and She could feel Herself starting to fade. She reached out, incorporeal hand slipping through the window glass to touch Lia, trying desperately to grip at the materia of Lia’s world. .:: ::: ::.
