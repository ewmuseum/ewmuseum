After Evolution’s Main Event Last Week Global Arena Hallway Approximately 12:13am Local Time ?” certainly seems like the women around here get their fair shake? and that’s already an improvement from WMW,” Virgil finished, trailing casually along behind Shadow, with his typical half-smirk on his face. Shadow half-turned to look at him as she walked up to the door of the Hunting Grounds locker room. She dug her key out of the deep pocket of the vinyl cargo pants that she had wrestled in twice that evening. “Yes well, we’ll see. A lot of it rests on how the Television Title is treated as long as I’m holding it” if it’s treated like dirt beneath my feet, the ugly title that nobody wants, all the while management is saying “oh, don’t worry dear, we’ll let you have a shot at one of the grown-up titles soon,”” Then I’ll start worrying.” She finished her sentence with a slight smirk, unlocking the door of the locker room with an almost relieved look on her face. It had been a long night, after all, and increased stamina or no, Shadow was quite tired at the moment, and was looking forward to finding Dawn and getting back to the estate for a much-needed evening of rest’and quite possibly a day off tomorrow as well. She frowned slightly as she flicked the light switch into the “ON” position. The locker room was quiet, and a cursory check with her preternatural senses revealed it to be empty as well. She sighed slightly. Leave it to Dawn to be irritated after managing to win the Cruiserweight Title. What it was this time, Shadow didn’t know, but obviously, Dawn had gone on ahead to the airport, evidently preferring to wait there instead of among her fellow Global stars. Virgil accompanied her silently for the moment, leaning in the doorway until Shadow started towards the row of lockers that lined the wall, preparing to clear out her things. He sauntered casually into the room, shaking out of his face the one errant strand of long chestnut hair that dared to misbehave. Pressing one hand against the sofa back, he leaned, elegant like a panther, his eyes casually scanning the room and all its details. Finally, two observant orbs of blue came to rest on the sofa beneath his hand’an that was when he realized that sofa leather wasn’t the only leather he was feeling. A long coat was stretched out across the sofa back, as though it had been casually tossed there, and then forgotten in someone’s hurry to leave. Of course, Virgil could tell easily enough whose it was’she hardly ever left home without it. He arched an eyebrow. “Hm” It would seem that your mortal roommate is forgetting more than just her manners.” “Hm?” Shadow returned absently. After a few more clangs as she dug around in the metal locker, she turned to look at him, her eyes betraying the tiniest spark of confusion. He looked down, pointing a sharp-nailed finger at her coat. “She’s left her coat. Not that she’ll need it in this weather, but considering it always seems glued to her skin, I wonder how she’ll survive in Toronto without it’unless we take it to her of course.” Shadow’s brow furrowed instantly. It wasn’t like Dawn to be forgetful? and the only way she would have been forgetful was if she was in a hurry to leave, or? if something was wrong. Immediately, thoughts of the worst gripped her, a cold clammy hand of fear snaking through her chest and squeezing her unbeating heart in a vise grip. She took two faltering steps towards the couch before forcing herself to get a grip. The first look of blind fear transformed into one of cool bewilderment, as she pushed herself towards the sofa. “That’s not like her?” Virgil shrugged it off. “I suppose winning the Cruiserweight Title was a bit much, and slipped under her radar of dourness with unexpected joy. I’d imagine she didn’t know how to handle it.” “Virgil!” Shadow snapped, narrowing her eyes to a glare. He might have been right, but that didn’t mean that she wouldn’t chastise him for saying it. “What?” Virgil said, throwing his hands up in a “who, me”!” sort of gesture, a tiny smirk curling at the corners of his lips. Shadow couldn’t help but smile, even if it was a slight one. She was about to return fire when the locker room door opened, displaying for a moment in its rectangular snapshot Vivica Valentine, followed moments later by the unofficial Hunting Grounds “Commanding Officer” Christian Zenith. The two were deep in conversation, which stopped for a moment as they realized that they weren’t alone’but Vivica half-smiled at her tag team partner. “Oh hey Shadow,” said the blonde casually, offering no greeting to Virgil. Zenith nodded to her, and perhaps to him too’had it not been for this being Hunting Grounds turf, Virgil might have been quite offended by the lack of acknowledgement of his presence. Then again, at the very least, he could say for them that they looked after their own. Zenith started over to his locker, and more rummaging sounds ensued shortly. Vivica, on the other hand, folded her arms over her chest, waiting for him rather impatiently. She shrugged, looking between Shadow and Virgil a bit confusedly, before deciding to address her correspondence to Shadow. “So” I thought for sure you and Dawn would have been out of here by now. I know you won and all, but the two of you never seemed the type to stay around for the after-party.” Shadow shrugged. “Well, I came in here ready to leave, true enough. But Dawn’s not in here” just her coat.” Vivica’s face contorted for a brief moment in confusion, the look shooting through her eyes like passing headlights in a window. “Dawn’s not here?” “No,” Shadow responded, shaking her head, “I guess she went on to the airport without us” Virgil and I are both staying up there last week. After that little accident she had, Virgil was the one who dragged her bike back to the house, so she’s not quite as ill-disposed towards him as she was.” Vivica shook her head. “No, she can’t be at the airport already, Shady” Her car is still here.” Time seemed to freeze momentarily as Vivica made that pronouncement. Suddenly, the before-only-seething fear that something was wrong gripped Shadow full on. Gently, she reached out and picked up Dawn’s coat, cradling it over her forearm. “Something’s wrong,” she said softly, almost instinctively. “Oh come on, Shadow,” Vivica said, trying to compensate for the sudden shift of mood, “I’m sure Dawn is just around somewhere” you know, maybe she ducked out for coffee with somebody, or she got caught talking to someone in the office or the lobby.” “No.” The pronouncement was as adamant as steel, and Shadow shook her head vehemently. “Something isn’t right. I don’t sense her anywhere in the arena, and she’d never leave her coat just lying around.” Vivica opened her mouth to respond, but then suddenly closed it. There was no way to argue with something like that, because it was just arguing with a feeling, and that was pointless. Virgil likewise only watched the scene. Shadow, suddenly, sprang into action, walking over to the bench and hastily zipping her bag. She shouldered it roughly, draping the Television Title around its strap, much like she always did. But this time, the coat hanging over her arm seemed to imbue her with a frantic sense of urgency. She stood in the middle of the room, all eyes on her’she didn’t seem to notice how alarmingly quick and desperate her movements seemed, nor how erratic her sudden shift in mood to panic. For a moment, she looked disconcerted, sensing their confusion. But there was no time for that, something inside her said. “I’m going to look for her. If I don’t find her tonight, I’ll be in touch,” she said in a clipped, cold tone, her Eastern European accent coming out more clearly than ever. Without mincing any words, Shadow started towards the door of the locker room with purpose in her stride. A flash of black leather from Dawn’s coat was the last image to flash through the picture frame doorway before Shadow was as good as gone. Virgil watched after her for a moment, before feeling two pair of eyes digging into him. He didn’t really know or care why they were staring, but most likely, the stares were inviting him politely to leave. He didn’t wait to find out. Finally, it was just Vivica and Zenith’but they weren’t alone. They were accompanied instead by the hanging air of “wrongness” in the atmosphere. Nobody knew for sure what was going on at the moment. Maybe Dawn was just somewhere else in the arena? but the cards certainly didn’t feel like they were laid in that orientation. Now, all that was left was crossing fingers’crossing fingers to hope that everything was okay, and they were all being silly and superstitious. .:: ::: ::. Christ at Cavalry Church 25 Miles South of Las Vegas? Outskirts 6:30pm Dusk was beginning to fall on Vegas. Kevin Woodham still sat in the anteroom, waiting to go into the office of the Christ at Cavalry Church, shifting his weight back and forth on the metal bench, which was still uncomfortable, no matter how thick the innocuous khaki and lavender-flowered cushion looked. As innocent as the church looked, something a bit darker lurked in its recesses. It was impractical for the Society of Leopold to lurk in a city like Las Vegas. They needed their solitude, and more than anything else, they needed a place to whip the troops up into a frenzy’and what place better than this? The Christ at Cavalry Church was located in a remote area, populated mostly by tumbleweeds, sidewinders, and the occasional tourist with a broken down car a few miles up the road. Out here, there was no worry of Sin City’s gambling and hookers luring in the Society’s finest hunters. Out here, no one would hear what was said, and no one would care. “Mr. Woodham” The Archbishop will see you now.” A disembodied voice interrupted Kevin’s stream of thoughts. He suddenly snapped his head up, senses alight and eyes casting about everywhere for an explanation? before he realized that what he was so alarmed about was only a speaker, buried in a plant, meaning the announcement must have come from the Archbishop’s clerk herself, and not from the dowdy old nun who was playing secretary out in the anteroom. He stood up, ruffling his fingers gently through his mousy brown hair. He was slightly dizzy’and considering how long he’d been sitting there, the blood had probably just rushed from his head in a torrent. However, he steadied himself; falling on one’s face in front of the Archbishop of Las Vegas would not do well for a first impression. He walked slowly towards the imposing brown hardwood door that would take him into the second anteroom of the office, barely refraining from rolling his eyes at all the pomp and circumstance. Kevin was not a career Catholic, you might say. Litany had gone in one ear and out the other as a young child, and his adolescent years had involved a lot of skipping Mass and smoking pot in the vacant lot behind the church. Nevertheless, Kevin Woodham, dubious Catholic and even more dubious vampire hunter, walked towards his destiny in the Archbishop’s office. Immediately as he walked in, he was accosted by not one but two pairs of eyes. Instantly, he saluted. “General LaSalle!” Kevin exclaimed in surprise, giving the proper military reference to the very martial head of the garrison of the Society of Leopold that was holed up in Las Vegas right now. Normally, the Society was not particularly martial, but increased supernatural activity in the past few years had called for a massive stepping up of mobility, which meant stepping up the number of Society units that were more military, not so much clergy. “At ease, Woodham,” LaSalle snapped. Woodham relaxed, looking back and forth between the two men. “I uh” I thought I was just coming to see the Archbishop, I didn’t know uh? I didn’t know I would? you would?” “Shut up.” Woodham’s eyes widened, and his posture stiffened as he fell completely silent in the presence of a superior officer. “I know you thought you’d just be coming to see Archbishop Monzella, but I know you” and that’s why I came. I didn’t want you wasting his time with a bunch of alarmist little namy pamby fairy tales. I’m here to keep you straight.” “I?” “Have a seat,” intoned Archbishop Monzella, finally unlacing his hands from each other, a smirk twisting across his face. Kevin Woodham plopped down in the chair in front of his desk, his eyes wide with anticipation. Again he opened his mouth to speak, but nothing came out. Archbishop Monzella chuckled, evilly, Kevin thought. “Now Mr. Woodham” there is no need for all of this open-mouthed awe. We want to hear your story, the General simply expressed his interest at being present when you told it.” Kevin nodded numbly, gulping a mouthful of saliva down his throat that he very nearly choked on. “Go ahead whenever you’re ready,” coaxed Archbishop Monzella, the sudden kind shift of his voice sounding to Kevin Woodham as though it were oily with mistrust. But, he was here for one reason, and one reason only, and that was to tell the Archbishop of what he’d seen. His eyes closed as he remembered? ---- Kevin had been looking out the upper window of the church and into the alley. There wasn’t much for him to do in this place anyway’he had the distinct feeling that they’d just put him here to keep him from getting into anything important, and that irked him. Sure, maybe he wasn’t the Society’s best hunter, but they’d hired him to hunt, hadn’t they? “Come on Kev,” he’d said to himself, “You haven’t done anything meaningful since you started working with them. You botched the surveillance job, you got lost the night they brought down that pair of vampires in SoHo, and after that, they put you in a desk job. Yeah, you’re a really great addition.” It was thoughts like that which had propelled Kevin Woodham to the window, and as he looked down, he saw something that was rather disconcerting to him. “Father Henry gasped in shock as a figure suddenly stood in front of him. An abnormally pale woman gazed back at him, half in shade, half in the palsied light of the moth-covered light that hung above the alley on the church side. The stained-glass windows gazed, glazed over behind him like dead eyes. A smile melted over the face of this ebon-haired beauty, and for a few moments, there was a stitch in time that seemed to pause it completely’as though he could see the past reflected in her eyes, the days of witch-burnings and Inquisitions. With a gasp, Father Henry took a step back. He couldn’t quite put a finger on what was wrong with this woman, but it was very disconcerting indeed. ?”” ?”” She trailed off into a grin, and stepped fully into the light, her fangs still hidden; the grin on her face was no less disturbing. Father Henry took another step back, spluttering like a child at its first swimming lesson. Kevin’s eyes widened as he pressed his nose closer to the glass, breathing little carbon-dioxide steam circles. This creature was? was not human, certainly. But what was it? God, how he wished he could hear what they were saying. He tried pressing his ear to the glass, vainly hoping that for some reason, this place didn’t have very thick windows. ?”” The woman’s head shook, almost chidingly. ?”” Father Henry took another step back’and finally, he met brick, brick that had long since cooled from the Las Vegas sunshine. He shivered, but it had not much to do with the cold of the building, and everything to do with the cold radiating off of the woman in front of him. ?”” ?”” ?”” The woman stepped even closer to Father Henry, her face suddenly blanching with rage, her eyes narrowing to slits. ?”” ?”” Father Henry did not have the words. Shadow took a step closer to him, her eyes narrowed. ?”” He turned his head’he found himself nearly powerless to resist. Shadow leaned in close, whispering in his ear with the breath of the tomb. ?”” With that, she was close enough to go in for the kill’her fangs unsheathed in a matter of seconds, and she plunged them into Father Henry’s neck. In surprise, he flung his hands out to the side, papers and books flying everywhere askew. All of this was no distraction to Shadow, who could think only to clap her hand over his mouth to quiet his howls while she took her fill. Kevin felt himself gag, his entire body shaking from the strain of not screaming at that very moment. When she’d drunk enough to slake her thirst at least until tomorrow night, she pulled away, forcefully, her eyes wide, gasping for breath, blood running down one side of her lip. She wiped off the side of her mouth and leaned closer to the trembling Father Henry, running a tongue over his wound to close it up. She turned her head towards him, forcing him to make eye contact. ?”” Father Henry nodded. ?”” Her forceful gaze seemed to yank the man out of his skull for a moment, before Shadow let go of his chin and pushed him in the direction of his scattered papers, disappearing into the relative darkness as quickly as she came. Kevin’s mouth gaped open like a fish gasping for breath after it had been caught. A vampire! A real, live? vampire! He let the heavy curtain fall from the window and staggered backwards, gasping for breath. “My God, I” I have to tell somebody.” In the middle of the night, that was a taller order than he could have imagined’Society members were either sleeping for an early morning, or out hunting. But Kevin Woodham knew, without fumbling for his book of protocol, that somebody had to know that he’d finally found himself a real, live vampire in Las Vegas. ---- The Archbishop Monzella leaned back in his chair, arching a skeptical eyebrow. “So” You think that you saw Father Henry of the bishopric undergo a vampire attack.” Woodham shook his head. “No, I don’t just think I saw that. I know I did. I know.” LaSalle rolled his eyes, but Woodham was far too worked up to notice. He felt instinctively that they weren’t going to listen to him? and that threw him into a panic. “You’re” you’re not going to do anything about it, are you?! Listen, you can’t just brush it off, I? I saw it!” Woodham’s eyes were wide with desperation. Archbishop Monzella merely rolled his eyes. This was all rubbish he had heard before. “That’s quite enough. We will do what we feel is necessary.” “You don’t believe me!” Kevin shouted. The Archbishop sighed, and tapped the intercom button on his desk. “Sister, please bring an escort for Mr. Woodham’s exit.” Almost as though he had materialized out of nowhere, a rather beefy looking man in a black business suit appeared in the office, yanking him bodily out of the chair and towards the door. Kevin Woodham didn’t bother to struggle? there was no point in fighting the Catholic Church, after all. As soon as he was gone, General LaSalle dropped into the chair that he had been occupying, massaging his temple irritatedly. “I apologize” he’s the biggest waste of time in our ranks, and if I could get rid of him without having to encounter that notorious Church bureaucracy, I would. Plus, at this point, he knows too much of us to be terminated.” Monzella waved his hand. “Nonsense. Perhaps what the boy says has some merit to it. After all, we came to Las Vegas for a reason, and I seriously doubt that Rome will tell us to leave without any kills. It merits looking into.” A very unamused eyebrow arched across LaSalle’s face. “And if he’s full of shit, like usual?” Monzella smirked, that smirk sliming its way across his face just like it had before. “Then we make quick work of him. The young man is plainly on his last legs anyway. I’m sure your people can arrange an accident” or worse, make him live in eternal fear of damnation by discharging him dishonorably from the Society. Tell him you did him a favor and saved his ass by keeping him from getting excommunicated? but that he’ll have to be careful from now on. You’ll never have to worry about him again. But in the meantime? if the boy has a point, and we miss it, it won’t be him who’s excommunicated.” LaSalle nodded. The good Archbishop did have a point, after all. Virgil rolled his eyes slightly, the sound beginning to gnaw a hole into his nerves. He sat up slightly, tired of slouching on the divan at the foot of the bed. “Lia” you’re going to wear a hole in the carpet if you keep up this pacing.” She paused, her head snapping suddenly to the side, after being shocked out of her reverie so brutally. “I’m sorry” I just can’t sit still right now, I don’t know where else to look.” Virgil shrugged slightly, using that gesture that always seemed so unconcerned, but had really just become idle habit by now. “Perhaps now is not the time for looking then. She will come home some time.” ?Some time?! That’s not an answer, Virgil. Nobody knows where she is, okay? Nobody! I’ve looked everywhere that I possibly can, save going out and trolling the deserts myself, and I can’t do that, not with my powers so undeveloped still in daylight. Soma’s not contacted me for weeks, which means I don’t have use of Daria as a guide in the daytime, and? I just don’t know where to look! I feel like I’ve exhausted all my options, and?” She stopped at Virgil’s held up hand. “Pax, Lia,” he said, rather too calmly for her liking, “There isn’t any point in getting up in arms about this right now. The best we can tell, there was no struggle, she left on her own, she just left in a hurry. No, we don’t know where she is, but if she has any sense in her head, she’ll be back in time for her match.” Shadow shook her head vehemently, and it was at that moment that Virgil stood up rather hastily and wrapped his arms around her. “Stop, cara mia. Stop.” Shadow struggled for only a moment in his grasp before relaxing. Her muscles were taut as washboards, belying the very great mental stress she’d been putting herself under since the very moment she’d realized that Dawn was gone, and wasn’t coming back that night. “Lia?” Virgil said, speaking gently to her’no matter how frustrated with her stubbornness he was, she needed soothing words right now, “Please listen to me. You’re running yourself crazy thinking about this so much. Calm down” don’t worry about Dawn, she’s an adult, and she won’t do anything too stupid, no matter how reckless she’s been lately. Hopefully she learned last week from that wreck that she is not invincible. You need some rest to keep from going completely insane. I don’t want to have to force you to take it.” There was a hint of a joke in his voice, but all of a sudden, Lia had realized exactly how exhausted she was. She was more leaning against Virgil now than she was standing on her own power. He smiled slightly’the exhausted warrioress was practically sleeping against his chest. “Get some rest, please?” Her arms slid around his back. “Hm” okay.” He smiled softly, scooping her up in his arms and laying her across the long expanse of bed in the guest room before she could think twice about his proposal. He pulled the covers up, tucking them gently underneath her chin’never remembering a time when the vixenish woman who’d always enthralled him had looked so childlike and innocent. His fingers softly stroked her forehead as his eyes scanned her face’a face that he’d spent quite a bit of time memorizing every feature of. “I’ll be right back,” he said, stepping away from the bed. “Where are you going?” He paused in the doorway. “Nowhere far, I promise” just to the kitchen, for some vitae for you, my dear. Consider it like a child’s cup of warm milk before bedtime.” Lia rolled her eyes slightly, but her head dropped back onto the pillow. .:: ::: ::. In the kitchen, a glass hung suspended over a roaring fire, with Virgil guarding it very carefully, lest it shatter. A bit of an odd practice, but he was only using his powers of chimerstry to heat the blood he was about to take her. Though the fire might have been fake, it at least looked, and behaved, like a real fire, which meant that convection, even boiling, was possible. “And all this to avoid modern cooking apparatus,” he muttered to himself, “Someday, Virgil, you’re going to have to just learn to use a stove. It wastes much less energy than this.” A tendril of steam wafted upwards, and that was his cue’the point at which to stop the heating before the blood boiled away much of its essence. He pulled the glass from the fire, feeling none of its heat; after all, he was the conjurer of the illusion, and knowing that it was an illusion did much to dispel its effectiveness. He set the glass on the countertop and took a moment to snap his fingers’instantly, the flame dispersed, quelled without even the hint of rising smoke, or the smell of charred fuel. A thought slipped into his mind, and he briefly considered it: Shadow would worry no matter how hard she tried to slip. What harm would it be to insult a little “home remedy” into her vitae to make sure she slept as she needed to? That was all the questioning that it took for him to slide an unmarked cobalt bottle out of his coat pocket, and tip a few drops of it into her glass. He smiled, almost sadly. “I hate to seem like I don’t trust you” but for better or worse, you’d kill yourself trying to take care of her? and I’m not going to let you do that.” He slipped back up the stairs, a sense of purpose in his movements. .:: ::: ::. Virgil stepped back into the room with a smile at his “patient.” And instantly, he was assailed with a machine-gun rapid hail of words. “Virgil, listen” I was thinking? I can’t go to sleep. I thought of a couple of places that I haven’t checked yet, and I just know that today’s going to be the day.” Virgil shook his head. “No, no” See, I knew if I left you here, you’d come up with some reason. You’re going to sleep, and that’s it. I’ll wake you up if I hear anything new.” Shadow looked like she was going to argue, but suddenly a warm cup of blood was thrust into her hands. And sleep did sound awfully appealing right now? A few minutes after finishing the last drop in the glass, and she was sleeping like a baby, not even moving. Virgil smiled hesitantly for a moment, but then it faded off, dropping into oblivion as he thought again about what he’d done. Was it really so altruistic of him to force Shadow to sleep through medicating her when the prime reason she wasn’t sleeping was because of Dawn? Make her sleep, and you wipe Dawn off her mind, simple as that. And wiping Dawn off of her mind would have made him very happy, wouldn’t it? He sank into absorbed contemplation of his sleeping paramour. Moral considerations would have to wait until later, because thinking of himself being just as selfish about her as everyone else was made her sick. What would happen to him if, after all this effort, he ended up pushing her away? .:: ::: ::.
