Memories Gone #4

28 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 052804 I held her hand in my hand. Why? I squeezed it gentle, trying to coax her out of an unbreakable sleep. Wake up. Emotions flowed through my skull. Anger. Hate. Sadness. Joy. Inside, my soul bled. In a situation like this, what do you do? When god rears his ugly head at you one moment, only to smile upon you the next. What do you do? I pulled my hand away from hers and exited the room. ***** The heavy bag swung lifelessly in front of me. I tapped it quickly with a series of left and right jabs, moving up and down, following the swing of the sand-filled bag. I ducked an imaginary blow and came back up at the bag with a fierce knee to the midsection, coming down with a hard elbow to the back of the head that wasn’t there. I swung at the bag like there was no tomorrow. I swung at the bag like it was a Greensino. Side kick. Side kick. Reverse spin kick. Roundhouse kick into a sweep. I was going through the motions, occupying myself. Occupying my mind. Trying not to think of the woman that I loved, laying in her medical bed, locked away in a semi-forever sleep that I could not break myself. Trying not to think of the fetus growing in her womb, unsure if she would be there to help me raise our child. Trying not to think of the things that I would like to do to the person that put her in that condition. Trying not to think about slowly peeling away his flesh with a dull fillet knife, watching his muscle tissue cry blood onto the floor. Trying not to think about cutting all of his fingers and toes off with a bolt cutter. Trying not to think about raping him with a shotgun and squeezing the trigger. But instead, trying to decide how to go about getting the small sawed-off double-barrel shotgun ringside. So I can paint Terrelle’s brain onto the front row seats. ***** I walked back into Vic’s room, the shotgun in one hand, a cleaning kit in the other. Slowly, I worked on the gun, using a rolling cart as my workbench pulled close to her bed. As I worked over each piece, I told her what it did, how it did it, and how good it needed to be cleaned. All she did was lay there. Finally, I folded away each piece of the kit and place it back in its box. I place two shells into the shotgun, snapping it closed. I gave her hand one final squeeze before leaving. “For you, Vic,” I murmured in her ear. “For you.”
