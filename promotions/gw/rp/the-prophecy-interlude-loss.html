The Prophecy, Interlude- Loss

undated

Date: various .:: ::: ::. The Zdice of Shadow's mind circa 790 CE Big green eyes stared up at Jalena Vlaszy. They were completely guileless eyes, as only children's eyes could truly be- completely unjaded with the world, and still perpetually amazed with everything they saw. 'Grandmama?' 'Yes Lia?' The old woman's voice sounded tired. She had spent much of the day working in the kitchen, as she often did. Cooking for the Vlaszy clan was no small task, even if you were just supervising the servants to make sure that no corners were cut. Her young granddaughter squirmed into her lap. 'Grandmama, tell me about our family.' The old woman smiled. 'But you don't have the patience for such a long story, little one. It would take days to tell the whole story, and I think that you'd get tired of listening before it was over.' 'Tell me where we came from again.' The child leaned against her grandmother, picking at the hem of her long, ornate dress. The Vlaszy were not poor, by any means- they could afford to dress their children in finery; they were cavaliers, and their service to the strange Tzimisce lords of the Carpathian wilds made them no less noble. The Vlaszy had a debt to repay to them; though no one much remembered what it was by this point, it would have been even less noble for them to deny a boon. 'We came from the Magyar lands, little one. That is where our name, Vlaszy, comes from. We are an old, ancient family, and as long as our family history goes back, we have been very powerful, and very strong. But we are never to use our power to rule unjustly- it is a promise that our family made.' Lia's little cloth shoes thudded against her grandmother's leg. She blinked for a few moments. 'What about the man at the big house?' Her grandmother smiled slightly, knowing that the child was referencing Ionescu- the vampire that their family line was blood bound to. How did you explain to a child that her family served demonic beings, kept alive by the strange eldritch magics of their diseased homeland? How did you tell her that, while at the same time emphasizing that their line was pure, noble, true? It was impossible to make her understand at such a young age. She sighed softly. 'Ah, Lia, you will understand more about him when you are older, and learn your purpose. For now, just know that you must be very grateful to him. He has been very kind to us.' The young child nodded, smiling, and Jalena sighed again- this time in relief. The time would come when Lia's questions would not be so simply answered. Until that time, she could only hope that she could help cultivate the proper mind in the child- to understand that though the beings they served were loathsome, the longevity of the Vlaszy line depended upon that service. It was a life or death matter. .:: ::: ::. circa 798 The exotic, elegant face of Lia Radilu Vlaszy was flushed pink with excitement. It was not a happy excitement, though- anxiety tightened the muscles all across her slim frame as the last of her trunks thudded onto the back of the carriage. Today, they were sending her somewhere. She did not know where, but all questions of that nature had been met with no answer- no one even acknowledged that she had asked. It was a simple equation: Lia had done something that necessitated her leaving the manor house, for the safety of everyone, and for the continuance of the family line, untouched by what could be a ruinous scandal, were it made public. Lia did not remember the specifics of her crime. All she could remember was the walk- starting down the snow-covered path with Jaroslav, the man she was supposed to marry in three weeks. She had been uneasy- she had let her guard down, spoken about something she never should have spoken about. 'Jaroslav,' she said sharply, 'Why did you do what you did to Laszla?' 'What do you mean? I did nothing to her,' Jaroslav snapped, suddenly turning. He didn't like the tone of this woman. A wife was never to speak to her husband in this manner. 'I saw what you did to her, last week in the garden,' Lia said, standing simply with her hands hanging at her side, pausing a few steps away from him on the path. The next sensations she could remember were her face pressing against the snow, the weight of Jaroslav's knee against her back, and the sound of the ripping brocade of her dress. He'd thrown her over on her back, tossing away the knife he'd used to slit it open, and the nearly demonic raging of lust and anger in his eyes had terrified her- to the point where she bit down on the hand that was covering her mouth, and scrambled for the knife that was lying only inches out of her grasp in the snow. After that, she remembered nothing. It had only been a week since she'd come home, her hands covered in blood, and her cloak pulled tightly around her back to prevent the shame of anyone knowing what had happened- that she had allowed someone to shame the family. She'd desperately told the priests it had been a wolf attack- that only her forethought at jumping on Jaroslav's horse and riding away had saved her. She was nearly certain that they did not believe her- and was even more certain that Grandmother Jalena did not believe her wild tale either. A column of faces parted, making a clear path to the carriage as the old family matriarch stepped out of the manor house, pressing lightly on her cane as she walked. No one in the family had more respect than Jalena, and as head of the clan, it was her duty to send Lia off to her marriage. Lia could nearly see the family leaning closer, trying to hear what was just out of earshot as Jalena leaned towards the door, her voice low and quiet. 'I am sorry that you must leave, Lia. You were to be confirmed with Master Ionescu soon- he found you quite promising, and was looking forward to it.' Tears brimmed in Lia's eyes. 'I've failed the family, Grandmama... I'm sorry.' Jalena looked up into the eyes of the young woman, who she remembered being such a sweet little girl just a scant eight years ago. She shook her head. 'You've failed no one, child. We're sending you away for your protection- you've too much potential to allow this to get in the way of it. Master Ionescu agrees with this decision... in fact, it was his idea.' Lia swallowed, hard. She'd only set eyes upon the lord that her family served one time, and he was forever impressed in her memory- tall and slim, with the impression of great power rippling underneath his fine clothes, which were all black velvet and gold brocade. He spoke with the smooth voice of an angel, and was possessed of the smoothest skin she had ever seen, fair as milk. In short, she was absolutely terrified of him. If this had all been his idea... she wasn't sure she wanted to go through with it. 'Grandmama, I... I don't want to leave!' The old woman forced her expression into a smile, as she patted her girl's arm. 'It's only natural to want to stay with your family. One day, you'll come back to us, but only when it's safe for you.' 'What is my husband like?' Jalena laughed softly. 'His family is one of our oldest allies. I do not know the young man personally.' 'How old is he?' 'Nineteen, to your fifteen. I believe that the two of you will be suited quite well... he is not his father's oldest son, but from what I am told of him, he is dashingly handsome, well-mannered... and most importantly, he will care for you. He will not let anyone harm you- he is a gentleman, above all.' Nerves shot through Lia. That still left far too much to the imagination, too much that was only guesswork, and on a trip this long, she could very well drive herself insane with guesswork. There was only one more thing she could think to ask- she could hear the horses braying at the head of the carriage expectantly, which meant it would only be a matter of moments before her journey away from Zdice began. 'What is his name?' 'Nevermind his name. You will learn that when he introduces himself to you- you will remember it soon enough, since you'll be married to him the week after your arrival. For now, only remember what you've been taught. You know how to make us proud, my girl, so concern yourself only with honoring the Vlaszy legacy.' Lia opened her mouth to say something, but the carriage suddenly lurched, as the top door of its compartment was yanked forcefully closed and locked into place with its heavy chain and lock. The carriage's window shade shut tightly over all that Lia knew. From here, Zdice, her home, she would journey many paths across the Continent, ending up in the hotter forests near Gaul, if her memory served her well. Her map reading skills were nearly negligible; what need did a noblewoman of her stature have for navigational skills? She went where she was told to go, did what needed to be done there, and asked no questions about it. That was her lot. .:: ::: ::. Aboard Rune's plane Somewhere over the Atlantic Ocean Lia's sleep was not calm. She tossed and turned, her black hair tangling in knots around her. Tears leaked down the sides of her face, tiny droplets of blood that stung even in sleep. Guilt consumed her. Lia had not been present when the Anarch Revolt started- when the younger children of her kind had rebelled against the old benefactors that held them forever in servitude. The Vlaszy line was gone. There was nothing that Lia could have done about it, because they died doing their duty- they died defending the lords who they had pledged loyalty to for the entirety of their mortal lives- down through their family line until there were no more. Lia Radilu Vlaszy would never get over not dying with them. No matter how many times she reassured herself that her death would have just been more meaningless slaughter- no matter how many times she reminded herself that she was already Tzimisce back then, and had been spirited off to Vienna to save her talents... those faces would go away. Mother, father, her twin brother Laszlo, sister Anezka, Grandmama Jalena, cousins, aunts and uncles whose names had fallen into oblivion long ago... they all haunted her in dreams. The history books might have said that the Vlaszy line died to a man during the Anarch Revolt... but the history books had forgotten about one lone woman who had slipped through the fingers of doom. Likely, the history books would never know- had anyone in the Sabbat besides Vykos known her secret, she would have already had a bounty on her head. In fact, Lia was fairly certain that it was only because Vykos knew her secret that she was still alive. She opened her eyes quietly. At the moment, she probably should have been checking up on Cyrus, since Virgil was probably overburdened with the makeshift triage in the back of the plane. Right now, too much else weighed on her. She would have made the worst nurse in the world in this state, and could have possibly injured Cyrus even further were she not focused on the details. How many times had she found herself in this position? How many times had she found herself with absolutely nothing to her name, starting over in territory that was completely unfamiliar to her? The memories of such times had faded into near nothingness, and they wouldn't have been useful anyway. Lia didn't remember who her family had sent her to marry. She thought it had been Corvus, or Martel as she remembered him wanting to be called... but that had all been delusion. Just like Amir before him, her relationship with Corvus had been something constructed out of her mind to devil her. Who's to say this relationship with Virgil wouldn't end up the same? There were times when Lia wondered if she was real herself. After all the parts of her memory that had mysteriously disappeared, and everything that she had once had to her name that had somehow disappeared into oblivion... it was no wonder that at times, she was a little uncertain about what really mattered. The only thing she knew to do was cling to whatever she could find to cling to- Global Wrestling, Dawn McFarlane, Virgil Ambrosi, Emoticon, Christian Zenith, Vivica Valentine... these were all markers, proofs that her existence was not just another lie that she would one day lose. The more she had to prove that, the better, otherwise, there would have been no point in even getting up in the morning. .:: ::: ::. New Orleans 7:50pm They hadn't been at the house long- the first thing had been pulling Cyrus into the manor under cover of darkness, and setting him up in the sitting room. There, she would do the majority of her medical reconstruction, as the lights were better there, and there was more space to work. All of this meant that her chances to heal the rather grievous wounds that Lilith had inflicted upon Cyrus. She hadn't yet had a whole lot of time to think about this situation. The odds of it working out well were rather slim, considering how much was weighing on her shoulders at the moment. She had to be everything to everyone right now, and any chance of getting respite while on the job had been driven into oblivion by finding Cyrus hurt. Every waking moment right now was spent caring for his wounds, making sure Virgil wasn't harassing Alexis, and making sure Virgil was still happy. he felt like everyone's mother- and given what Soma evidently expected of her, perhaps this served as a trial run. Perhaps the only problem was the fact that in taking care of everyone else, Shadow found herself slipping away. There was no way to hang on to it, because there was nothing for her to anchor herself on. As she shouldered her bag and started up to the bedroom she'd be sharing with Virgil, if there was the possibility of much sleep this week, the absence of weight on her bag's strap reminded her once again of that feeling of trying as hard as you could, but just not being good enough. It would be silly of her to miss a title. Wouldn't it? But deny it or not, she did miss the familiar sheen of the TV Title, gleaming as it hung around her bag. It wasn't so much that she needed the Title to feel important to Global- more that she needed it to feel important to herself. She tried to tell herself not to worry- she tried to tell herself that she'd lost to a worthy opponent, and that's what mattered. Better to lose to someone who could honestly best you on a good night than someone who caught you in a vulnerable moment and barely scraped up a win... Right? Shadow still remembered the look in Dawn's eyes. Dawn hadn't wanted to fight her, and that had bothered Shadow- it was Dawn's chance to shine, and she didn't want to take it just based on who was across the ring from her. Shadow had taken a chance- she'd leaned across the ring and voiced her displeasure to Dawn. 'Why aren't you trying your hardest, Dawn? I consider that a grave insult.' She remembered whispering that into the slightly taller woman's ears... and then Dawn had come at her like a tiger. In a sense, it had pleased Shadow immensely to get that kind of reaction. She never wanted a win that she didn't have to fight for. At the same time, she wondered exactly how much it meant to Dawn to win. Everything had been on the line for Shadow- the title she'd worked hard to earn a shot for, and then keep... the chance at winning something that would finally make people realize how important she could be to Global if they'd only give her the chance... the chance to be somebody important, somebody everybody looked up to, with either admiration or jealousy- the chance she'd never had in WMW. Perhaps it was wrong of her to be a little bitter, but she felt like she deserved the right. In one fell swoop, the one woman Shadow would have considered herself even remotely close to had taken all that from her. Shadow didn't know quite how she was supposed to feel about Dawn right now- all she knew was that it was probably best that she didn't see Dawn right now, if only to keep from having a fight with her because emotions were running high. So this week was another chance. Another chance at glory that she'd find a way to blow- that's how it happened. The way of her existence was to lose what had once defined who she was, and spend an incomprehensibly long period of time struggling to the top of her grave again- struggling to define who she was, struggling to justify her existence. She supposed she'd done it well enough throughout her existence. Well enough, at least, to fool a lot of people into believing that she was something special. There were times that she wondered about it- what if she wasn't who Virgil thought she was? What if she wasn't ready for the responsibility that Soma wanted her to take? What if she really wasn't as great a friend as Emoticon seemed to think? What if she was useless, not worth Vivica and Zenith's time? After all... Dawn had two titles now, with a chance at a third- what did Hunting Grounds need Shadow for? She didn't even bring them name recognition. To lay down and die was incomprehensible, however. That was the one principle that she'd never gone back on, the one family value she'd never broken. The Vlaszy did not lay down and die. Master of the Mat was not a loss. It was a redefinition. Luckily for Shadow, though she might have felt that she came up short in many other respects... she always had the will to cut her losses and move on with life. This week would be no different. Win or lose- the title was not the battle. The battle was continued existence. .:: ::: ::. 6:39am Lia's fingers pressed together in a bridge just in front of her face. Her jade eyes seemed to be focused on the point where her fingers met, in a pointed steeple, but in reality, there was very little that she could see at the moment. Sunlight had seeped over the horizon very early on this morning, it seemed. Or perhaps it was just that ever since Shadow had been to Prague, things had been unfolding at a nonstop, breakneck pace, almost too fast to keep up with accurately. Her kind might have had many powers, but alas, the ability to slow time to a more favorable crawl so that no moments or memories were lost was not among them. In fact, you might have said it was one of many curses of vampiric kind, memories of so many ages slipping into nothing but cliched vagaries after a certain amount of time. It was perhaps a relief to her that Cyrus, one of her few Kindred benefactors, had actually stabilized. In Zdice, he had been a sad sight, hung from the wall of his haven like some blue-skinned fairy-tale mockery of Christ. His particular bloodline was especially weak to wounds from iron- hence why the iron stakes driven through his skin had produced such grievous effects. Instinct alone had driven her to find a spot for Rune to land his plane so that Cyrus could be brought to the United States, or more specifically, Virgil's isolated Louisiana haven. Kindred medical skill wasn't something that you read about in the legend. Vampires were monsters, beasts with unnatural urges that were impossible to control... but they were never healers, brilliant geniuses who were masters and mistresses of their arts. Lia liked to think she was an exception among them, but would Emoticon have seen her actions as such? Would Dawn have viewed her terrible flesh-sculpting surgery on Cyrus as mercy or abomination? These were things that weighed heavily upon her. For one of her kind, she was indeed rather deeply concerned with ethics, which might have made her a rare case. She didn't stop to concern herself with that- she only was what she was: a creature who had been miraculously caught in stasis while the world around it changed, a traveler helplessly, hopelessly lost, with no hope of ever finding her original destination, and no one to ask for help. So it seemed at least. Her eyes burned in the early morning sunlight. She was still quite unused to being able to see it without meeting sudden, certain death, or succumbing to instinctual fear so strong that she was just as likely to throw herself into the sun as she was out of it. And now Virgil had the ability too. Now, it was something they shared, something every vampire dreamed of: seeing the sun, even if just one last time. She smiled. It was a terribly bitter smile, if the truth were told. Caught in her reverie, she hadn't quite noticed the light human step coming around the side of the wide veranda of Virgil's antebellum-style home. His taste for nearly gauche ostentation was rather clear as Alexis Key ducked to avoid the gnarling branch of a rose bush, creeping its way from the whitewashed trellis to hang obtrusively over the walkway from the front door to the east wing of the house. 'Shadow?' Shadow turned her head, her eyes catching the sun's rays for a moment. She was nearly blinded; the only thing in front of her eyes was a white spot, and some rather sketchy background imagery. Despite her relative disorientation, she recognized the soft, feminine, alive scent of Alexis. She waited until the young woman was nearly upon her to gesture towards the wicker chair beside her. 'Sit, if you like.' Almost uncertainly, Alexis dropped into the chair. Her eyes roamed uncertainly over Shadow, who cut a most strange figure at the moment. She wasn't exactly looking at Alexis, but neither was she looking completely past her. The expression on her face was rather blank, as though she had been invested in some sort of deep emotional reverie before being interrupted, but had attempted to wipe away every possible trace that would have given her away. Uncomfortable silence reigned for a few moments. Alexis cleared her throat. 'You know, it's still a big change seeing you up this time of day.' Shadow nodded, switching her oddly blank stare away from Alexis, and back out to the rays of pink sinking into blue on the horizon. 'It is more of an adjustment than I prefer to admit.' She nodded. 'Yeah, Virgil kind of scared the hell out of me with it a few days ago, when he...' 'Right, Alexis, are you going to tell her you were checking out her boyfriend's package?' Alexis blushed a rather deep red, thankful that Shadow wasn't looking at her at the moment. Then again, after a few more moments of silence, it was all too evident to Alexis that even had she been looking, she probably wouldn't have noticed. She tilted her head to the side, watching the vampiress intently. Shadow didn't move under the young woman's gaze, and only responded several long moments later. 'It is something we both have to get used to.' 'Really? I'd think after so long, it would be incredibly exciting to finally see the sun again.' Under other circumstances, Alexis might have felt like a real idiot having this conversation- but it was plain that something was bothering Shadow, and Alexis couldn't help but be empathetic. After all, she could be dead right now. Shadow sighed. 'Exciting is not exactly the term that I would choose.' 'No?' The young woman tilted her head. Shadow didn't reiterate; it was rare for her to have patience with someone who kept repeating themselves for this long, but Alexis struck a chord of sympathy within her. Perhaps the girl was lonely, and Shadow could certainly relate to that. She glanced at Alexis out of the corner of her eye, then turned to her full on, the sunlight drawing a half-halo around the girl's head. 'Loss is something my kind get used to. We're given a gift that many would do anything for, but with that one gift, we must give up everything else that we had to our names.' 'What do you mean?' A terrible veil of sadness washed across Shadow's fine features. 'I had a family once... a life that was planned out for me. It might not have been much by your modern standards, but I would have been quite content to marry, have children, care for the manor house, and die at what today is considered the beginnings of middle age. All hope at that future was erased when I became what I am. I may be able to put a name to what that is, but that doesn't mean that I actually have any clue of why I still exist, even after everything that once made me who I was has disappeared.' Alexis chewed her lower lip thoughtfully. 'You don't adapt?' Shadow slowly shook her head. 'Why adapt? You will only have to adapt again when the human paradigm shifts. It is an endless cycle of change that is impossible to keep up with, even with all the time that stretches out before me. All I remember is that once, I had a future, a life ahead of me that would have been both difficult and fulfilling. I would have served my purpose, and died at my appointed time... but for some reason, the cycle passed me over. Some of my kind might consider themselves some sort of elect, chosen to separate the chaff from the grain in the chain of existence... I hold no such delusions. It is difficult to consider yourself anything but damned when you realize that you can no longer express yourself in terms that anyone can understand, that you have become obsolete... that everything that once mattered to you is gone, and will never return.' Alexis' eyes took on the sadness that framed Shadow's features. She opened her mouth to say something, but nothing came out, not even a tiny rush of air. Her eyes felt the unfamiliar sting of tears for a split second before she forced them back. 'So what do you do? Why keep going?' Shadow glanced at her for a moment before smiling wryly and standing up from the chair to go stand at the railing of the veranda. She casually looked back over our shoulder. 'The one constant thing for my kind is losing everything and everyone that comes to be familiar to you. There are only two options from which to choose: adapt and survive, in the process losing every single feature that was once identifiable as a personal trait... or walk into the sun, end it all by surrendering to the most potent symbol of everything that you once had.' She arched an eyebrow. 'I'm afraid I don't have that option anymore.' The phrase hung in the air heavily, and for the longest time, Alexis couldn't find the words to respond. The silent contemplation of the moment still passed between them when the door from the sitting room opened with a quiet squeak of hinges. Virgil's heavy, yet graceful tread pushed down on the groaning wood of the porch as he approached Lia's chair, giving Alexis as wide a berth as he could, as per usual. He leaned over the back of her chair, brushing aside her heavy, thick hair to kiss her cheek before his smooth baritone resounded in her ear. 'You might want to come inside... I am afraid your patient has taken a turn from the worse, and will need your immediate care to survive.' Lia turned her face to the side, her lips softly finding his neck before she stood up, brushing minute motes of dust off of her long velvet skirt. She blinked up at him for a moment; his eyes were bleared from the sunlight as well. It probably would have been time for the two of them to sleep for a few hours had she not been called to her duty. She bowed her head to Alexis. 'I apologize. Duty calls with our guest. I hope you understand.' And with only that, she left the veranda before Alexis could mount a response. Alexis wasn't quite sure what to say because every day, it was becoming more and more difficult to determine whether or not she had totally lost her mind. In between first hunting vampires she wasn't supposed to believe in to traveling the world with vampires she wasn't supposed to believe in, she'd been bad off enough. Add to that, two of these supposedly vile creatures showing very real love for one another, very real concern for the human companions around then, and one of them going so far as to play doctor and nurse with all the trappings to one of her own kind, and Alexis was completely thrown on what to think. She realized, though, that it didn't matter what she thought. While she thought, Shadow was inside the house, acting. For the first time, all this talk about loss started to make sense. Whether or not Shadow's feelings for those around her were real wasn't in question. They were real, otherwise Shadow wouldn't have tried to hard to hold on to them. When time threatened with its memory-erasing serum, you clung to whatever you could, no matter what that happened to be. If Shadow could just hold on to the feelings she had right now for another day, another night, then it would be an affirmation that there was a reason behind all of this existence, other than misery. .:: ::: ::.
