First Time Coming

28 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/28/04 The scene is a small room in an apartment downtown somewhere. A open window let’s the noise of a city leak in. The buzzing of traffic, the chatter of people, the crying of babies, the horns of cars, the sirens from police cars, all pour through the window into the small bedroom. Inside the room, the walls are covered with posters, posters of idols that the average man looks up to. Bruce Lee, Notorious B.I.G, 2Pac and the like. Inside the room sits Duzza. Dressed in a pair of baggy jeans, timberland boots, a light blue XXXL t-shirt and a light blue fitted cap turned backwards. Duzza sits in the room, listening to the sounds of the city, just thinking. The main question that runs through his head is “why?” Why is Duzza coming back to professional wrestling after leaving it over a year ago. Duzza had been on the top, a tag champion in a big fed, but then it all just fell apart. Some blame fake friends, some black bad choices, Duzza blames no one but himself. After spending so long to get to the top of the pro wrestling world, Duzza just threw it all away and never looked back. He was burnt out, sick of all the bullshit that goes along with this wrestling shit. There’s a whole side to it that fans could never imagine. The back stabbing, the politics, the head office and the bookers. Pure talent no longer get’s you where you need to be. Now it takes a lot of ass kissing too. And well, Duzza just never really liked sucking up. Ever since he saw his father beaten to death by neo-nazi’s back in “84, Duzza has had a chip on his shoulder. A “Fuck the World,” mentality and he didn’t care who liked it. Every day of his teenage years he woke up in the ghetto, longing for a way out, a way to make his dreams come true, a dream where him, his siblings and his mother were out of the Harlem ghetto that they were trapped in. Duzza never was fond of the “hood.” The dirty, dangerous place that he called home. Where at night it wasn’t even safe to walk outside. A place were the police were scared to come. A place that Middle America seemed to not even know it exist. But it was through wrestling that Duzza found a way out of the ghetto, a way in the suburbs and a way to make a lot of money. And he sure made a lot of money, but like so many young men who come into a lot of cash after having none, he didn’t know how to handle it, and after living like a superstar for 3 great years, Duzza finds himself back in the ghetto, broke and angry. And it’s because of his current financial situation that Duzza is returning to wrestling. Love of the sport has nothing to do with it, Duzza stopped loving wrestling after his tag team partner Mark Makonie turned on him. But hey, what did he expect from a neo-nazi. The other day, Duzza received a phone call from Global Wrestling, offering him a place and while he hadn’t wrestled in a long time, it’s just like riding a bike, it’s not something you forget. But hearing that Makonie was GW was what clinched the deal. Duzza has his targets set on the nazi and he will get his revenge, trust. “Damn” it’s been a long time boy? a long time? Duzza says out loud, to himself. “Gonna get back to wrestling” gon? get that fool Makonie? shit, the whole of GW best watch they selves, “cos I ain’t afraid of no cracka or nothin”.” Duzza gets up out of his chair and moves to the window. He looks out onto his block, notices a police car patrol by, some thugs standing on a corner, some kids skipping school and can’t help but be disgusted. But it’s still all good. ??I’ma be outta this place soon? Trust?
