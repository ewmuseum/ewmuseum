Lights Out

16 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/16/05 There weren’t many places where Train found peace, but for him this was one of them. His key slide into the lock of the glass, double doors. The lights of the city stood out of the darkness of the night with a pale glow, the dark, mirrored tint of the doors muting them just a bit. The Chicago night was colder than usual, the nip of winter beginning to bite uncharacteristically early this year, however Train found it refreshing. Having moved himself almost exclusively to Las Vegas now that it was the new center of operations for GW his body had managed to acclamate to the heat as much as it was possible to do in the middle of the desert. That’s why it surprised him how good the chill in the air felt as he stepped out of the Lincoln Navigator he left parked in the garage of the building where still maintained his quarter million dollar condo that he’d just recently paid off. No doubt he’d always call Chi-town home, a permanent connection to it established by leaving parts of his body and his soul laying in its streets. However, despite those things, it was really “home’more in name than in practice nowadays and even when he did spend most of his time here the infamous cold of the city was hardly his favorite feature. Yet, as he stepped onto the curb and his feet carried him slowly up the 8 foot wide walk way made up of 2x2 squares of the granite, past the recently planted trees lining it, that were just starting to lose their orange, red and yellow saturated leaves to the onset of the changing season on his way to the brand new, matching granite face of the building... rather than thinking how he disliked the cold, he only took a deep breathe and enjoyed how at home it made him feel. Funny what you miss when you’ve been disconnected for so long. Train readjusted the bag hanging over his shoulder, securing it before pulling the right side door open. It slid open silently, not a creak to disturb the night air and Train could only think how far this place had come. Just a few months ago this was still “The Bottoms”. If he wanted to be PC and respectful of the old building where he’d been taught how to defeat his opponents in the ring he’d tell people it was “rustic”. If he wanted to be real he’d identify it for the dump that it had become. Everything in the old building that stood on this very spot creaked, including the door. That was assuming you could muster the strength to pull the sticking bastard open. Most times however, he chose to be respectful of it. What choice did he have? He knew that the condition the gym had been degraded to was his fault. The place was never the height of technology or luxury when Isaiah owned it. Frankly he never meant it to be. Isaiah Walker had been born and bred in Chicago himself, but his folks were from down “Bama way. When he was a kid Isaiah thought he had it rough, his parents excepting no excuses about anything, but as a man he knew how lucky he’d been. There was too much they’d fought their way through to make a better life for him to just let him get away with being mediocre. There was no doubt that the poor neighborhood they’d transplanted themselves into on the southside of Chicago was no where near a paradise, but was nothing compared to where he could have come up. Down there the hardest, roughest, most run down part of the city or po” dunk town was called “The Bottoms” because it represented just that. It was a low as you could ever fall and that was exactly the place Isaiah would have been had they stayed. Unfortunately he’d never really gotten what they’d tried to tell him until he had to head that way to pursue his in-ring dreams. The hell with shock, it was a culture concussion. Down south was about the only place a black wrestler could get booked and make any where near decent money so going back to his roots was impossible to avoid. That’s when he understood... coming up in the game was a joke. Most restaurants, hotels, stores and any other place you could think of where a man should be afforded the dignity of choosing for himself wouldn’t even let Isaiah through the front door. Hell, if he was dying of thirst the only place he could take the sip of water that would save his life had better have been at a water fountain with text painted above it in the color that the rednecks that wrote it said matched his skin tone. The promoters weren’t really any better. The white ones didn’t give a damn about you beyond the performance they could get out of you. They’d let you on the card, but, no matter how good you were, you might as well forget getting any title shots. The black ones were inclined to believe that every other black man they used in the ring was an ignorant bumpkin from the country that couldn’t count well enough to know they were being ripped off. He was a hell of a talent and would have been a great deal to any company he stuck with. Too bad he’d all every ounce of half-ass bred or beaten out of him. Isaiah could have done a lot better for himself, but instead he lived on a shoe string budget, finding a spot to bed down in whatever rat and roach infested dump he could get cheap for about a year until he saved up enough for his plane ticket to Japan, where athletes were more appreciated, no matter what their melanin content. It wasn’t a whole lot easier for a few months, but when “The Black Bear” finally earned his respect and paid his dues their was no stopping him. Now to go from totally written off as a work horse to be run to death or a chump to be used and discarded to a champion from country to country and state to state? Anyone trying to come up in in neighborhood to pursue any life had no excuses. Isaiah had learned the hard way what it meant to come up’The Bottoms? and, when he finally made his way back to Chicago with a hell of a win/loss record to his credit and multiple championships to his name he planted the gym he’d use to pull anyone in the community who was willing to fight to succeed dead in the heart of every menace that threatened to consume anyone who walked those streets. The Bottom’s gym stood in the heart of the bottom of Chicago. It was nothing pretty. Nor was it meant to be.A ring, a heavybag, some weights and medicine ball and a few rubber mats that didn’t offer much in the way of padding... when you came into “The Bottoms” that’s all there was and that was all you were going to get. It was harsh, but so was life. If you could survive what he was going to put you through inside you could survive just about anything outside. Moreover, there was no grit or grime that he’d allow to ever tear the place down. Not while he was alive anyway. The reading of Isaiah’s will had been the only time in life Train had worn a suit save for the funeral of his brother Leon. He was just as uncomfortable that day as he’d been on the other occasion. He wasn’t for wearing suits even to this day. When people asked him why he always told them the materials that so uncomfortably incased his entire frame, no matter who well tailored it might be, were claustrophobic. Sometimes, when he wasn’t paying attention he couldn’t fight his unconscious instinct to pull and claw at it. It was hard to feel anything but hot and bound inside one. Of course it had nothing to do with the material. You pay three thousand dollars for suits because of how comfortable they are. But the finest materials can’t fight the effects dread. The sweats he broke into didn’t have anything to do with the cheap suit he was wearing. That sick feeling in the pit of his stomach that almost forced him to leave its contents on the the lawyer’s office floor wasn’t the result of his tie being too tight. It was what he heard. Isaiah didn’t have much left in the way of family. He’d been an only child and his parents were long gone. He had some wild nights to talk about to those he thought were old enough to hear such things, but he had no kids besides the ones who looked to him for guidance. That meant he could only leave “The Bottom’s” to the person that felt the most like his real child.... Mr. Anthony James as the will had denoted him. Train paused for a moment in the middle of the open doorway and peered into the door of the darkness inside the building. He knew full well that a few hours before his arrival this place had been buzzing with activity. He could smell the aroma and popcorn and candy that likely been made to occupy the attention of the kids on the after school/community center side. He could also smell the musk of sweat that had obviously been worked up on the “The Bottoms” gym side of the building. A few hours ago, with kids talking and playing and music pumping and athletes grunting it was probably hard to hear yourself think. But not anymore The door slide softly closed behind him as he took a step into what dark their was. He reached behind him, shoving his back to the side a bit before twisting the lock back closed. His heavy footsteps echoed sharply through the open corridor, bouncing off the toupe, stucco walls and the vaulted white ceiling. The pale lights pouring from the two large doorways at the end of the hall gave just enough light to find his way through. They also illuminates a thinly slitted, roll down gate attached to a long metal strip on the tile floor, the metal cage cutting off access to the rest of the building. Another light, small, red and flashing softly lights up the face of the white key pad on the right wall just a few feet from the gate. Train picks up the pace a bit, unable to remember whether the dude that installed it said he has one minute or 30 seconds to input the code before the silent alarm was tripped. Trotting over to the keypad he quickly puts his hand up to it and let’s his fingers do the walking. It’s about a second after he punches in the code that the pad goes from red to green. The electric motor in the ceiling roars to life, pulling the gate slowly up into another slit above. He’d spared no expense in this project, especially on the building’s security. There were four of these gates... one in the front hallway, the other just in front of the door to the community center side that was weighted down with computers to educate the kids and electronics to keep them from rioting of pure boredom. The third was in front of the entrance to the gym that boasted some of the best equipment money could buy. The kids out in the suburbs chasing after the dreams on the field, which ever one it was, had that benefit so it was high time the ones on the south side had the same. The last stood in front Train’s destination... the trophy room It held all the honors and pictures of Isaiah’s accomplishments over the years. All but the one Train thought he deserved the most. But that was ok. He’d rectify that. What’s more he’d make sure that no little punk who wanted a quick buck to snatch an easy score out of here, no matter what room they wanted it from. Yeah, the place was all about helping folks fight out of the hood... but it was still the hood. He knew it way too damn well to let it be any other way. The corporate sponsors were a bit upset with him right now. Dell had donated some bucks and about twenty grand worth of pc’s... about every sports drink and workout equipment company imaginable had jumped at the chance to be a part of the project that the former world champion was cooking up to benefit his community. At least when the project was the “Anthony James Community Center” project. They were just a bit t’d off when it became the “Isaiah Walker Community Center” halfway through. Train supposed he could see it from their point of view. He was a business man and so were they. They paid to be part of one thing and got another... another that Train had been planning on all along despite what he told them when they through their hissy fit. They even threatened to pull their equipment and support... perhaps even sue. That is until some new friends Train made at the NAACP, Urban League, Nation of Islam, a slew of local churches and his congressman from Illinois, who just happened to be a part of the Congressional Black Caucus let them know just how disappointed they would be if the companies pulled their support and just how interested the press would be in the whole ugly affair. Oddly enough, after all those pledges of support for the project, none of them seemed so anxious for a confrontation over the dedication of a building in the name of a deserving pillar of the community. They withdrew their harsh comments stating it was fraud. That despite the fact Train knew damn well it was. It had been a risk, but it was one he had to take. He was doing well, but he didn’t have the money on his own to resurrect the place his mentor had bled and sweated to build... especially after he let it fall to pieces. He knew full well that it was on him to keep the place standing all the years he allowed it to be eaten by time and neglect. The problem was he could barely stand to look at the place. He knew he was a poor replacement for the man who established it. Isaiah had pushed Train and put him through the fire in order to mold him. He had put his life on the line for Train. And lost it. Shit, Train couldn’t even keep the promise he made to Isaiah as he died in his arms. He promised he wouldn’t go back to the streets. He promised he would choose revenge over climbing his way out of the bottom. When he promised it he meant it. The second Isaiah died he said fuck it. Isaiah? Go to the next world by himself? Damn all that. Train’s fingers glided over the second keypad he’d come to heading straight down the hall and soon the second gate rose into the air. As loud rattle of metal sliding against well oiled metal filled the entire building Train just stood there... watching it go up as the final barricade on a long mission. A few moments later the gate locked into place above his head with a loud clank. He was sure how long it had been for an indeterminate chunk of time all he could do is stand and stare. Stare at the oval-like stucco wall that jutted out at him, the tall, dark maple doors at the center of the bend standing above him like the gates of a castle, waiting to be conquered. As he stood there the significance of what he was about to do dawned on him. As he stood there a shiver shot its way up his spine. The silent, inky blackness consumed everything in the room until Train pushed the doors open, allowing the pale blue light outside to kiss the edges of every object in the room with a hint of illumination. Train, the bag over his shoulder adding to his circumference, cut a stark silhouette in the doorway as he paused again to take in the end of his mission and a deep breathe. He reached out and a flick of his wrists full lit up the oval shaped room, including the 15 foot high, dome ceiling. Train cut a slow, circular path around the center of the room... around the bronze statue that jutted up towards the top. Isaiah stared down from also bronze pedestal, surveying anything coming into the room... his prodigal son included. Another deep breathe and Train begins what he came for. Experience from a few times in a steel cage gave him the practical knowledge he needed scale the pedestal in just a few seconds. Gripping a jutting part of Isaiah’s singlet Train holds his balance as he reaches back and unzips the bag. It takes finagling, but finally the large leather strap, expanding wider as eyes make it’s way to the center in order to make room to attached the large gold plate sitting there. and narrowing just enough to leave room for the two gold plates that makes its way down each side. The bag slides off his shoulder and floats to the floor as Train reaches around the waist of the statue and pulls the belt around. A few loud snaps and it’s there... attached for all to see... for as long as people can walk in to see it. When his previously defacto tag team partner Brick pointed out that, among all of Isaiah’s belts, there wasn’t a world championship among them, all in response to Train’s statement that he didn’t feel any of his belts and highlights were worthy of hanging on the wall just yet. Brick thought the trophy room about accomplishment, but really it was about life... rather lives... and saving them... the way his had been. Now he stood up on the pedestal and world champion again. It was an accomplishment made possible by the sacrafice someone else had made. That meant the one who sacarificed was champion too. And now the world would see it. Train hopped down and looked at the giant replica he’d had made. Payback... finally... Even with all he’d accomplished over the years somehow his life, good as it may have been, never felt like he had a light in it. Finally... The light came on. That’s when he heard it... click. click... click... snap... Train’s head snapped around, leaning his body over to peer down the hallway, his body far enough to the side of the statue to be hidden from the corridor by the curved wall. Son of a bitch. Train knew what that sound was... the sound of a lock opening against it’s will. Slipping farther to the side of the door he makes his way to the wall and poises his hand on the light switch. After being happy that his life had progressed so far from where it started just a moment ago, he suddenly ached to go back to the old days. The ones where he’d be strapped when some cocksucker was sneaking up behind him in the dark. Whoever the visitor was he was fairly adept. Train could barely hear him as he approached, despite the emptiness of a building so acoustically inclined to echo side. Now doubt the son of a bitch was hugging the wall as he crept up the hallway. Doing so would keep him out as much of the light pouring from the trophy room directly down the hallway as well as allow him to support some of his weight with his hands, keeping it off the his feet, making his approach more silent. A million questions flooded Train’s mind even as he stood poised, sharp as a tack... just waiting for the motherfucker that wasn’t nearly as cat like as he probably deemed himself. “I’ve been out of the game so long... what good does it do somebody to take me out now?” “Is this just a call from a beat down or is he supposed to disappear?” “Who ordered it?” But most importantly his real curiosity swirled around the question that stuck most in his mind... “Do I know this bastard?” Finally all thought beyond what he had to do next faded away. He was close now... maybe a few feet outside the door, now hugging the same side of the wall that Train was leaning against. Train heard the small squeak of rubber that meant the mystery man lifted his foot for the next step. A flip of his wrist and everything in sight was flooded in darkness. Train could hear the rustle of the bastard’s clothing, a leather jacket at least by the sound of it, stop in an instant. “Yeah... confused now ain’t you muthafucka?” Train asked himself, wishing he could telepathically broadcast it for pure intimidation’s sake. Everything was silent, not a hint of anything as if the darkness was swallowing anything audible. Train was glad the confusion of his visitor allowed him time to recover, never really considering that the lights going out would blind him until his pupils had a chance to dilate. Luckily his opponent seemed to be having the same issue. However the extra time afforded the person just outside the door to utilize an advantage Train didn’t have... He could hear the gun come out of the holster. Another squeak... whoever it was coming forward now. Train breathed a silent sigh of relief as he realized that his eyes had adjusted well enough to make out a shadow cast in the light outside in the hallway. He was coming around the corner.. Train had to play it just right... hoping his perception was dead on or he might wind up dead on the floor. The gun was gripped tightly in the right hand, though it apparently the left wrapped around the outside of the grip. Whoever it was did know how to use a piece... another disadvantage, but then again... knowledge is power. Train slowly crouches down, knowing that he’d come around just a few inches away. He stepped into the room, the gun pointed towards the center. Train’s hands clamped down around the intruders wrist as he drove forward out of his crouch. The little son of a bitch wasn’t a big guy, making it easy to knock him off balance. The man tried to bring the gun down, but didn’t have the power to keep it from going above his head. He didn’t have the wherewithal to stop the knee from slamming into his gut either. Train felt the air as it was viciously pushed out of the man’s lungs, the audible cry of pain, taking up whatever had been left behind. The shot loosened the hands too, allowing the gun to slip right out in to Train’s grasp. The guy obviously didn’t have the constitution to take a shot from a world champion because it took nothing for Train to sling him across the room, the echo of his body slamming into the base of the statue and the second cry of pain that came with it rumbled through the entire building. Time for some answers. Train shuffled back over to the wall and flipped on the lights as he secured the Glock 9mm. in his palm pointing at the downed man. He wasn’t sure what he’d see when the lights came on, but he was prepared for anything... almost. “Son of a bitch.” Train said without being able to hold back the reaction. The man who’d decided to enter uninvited sat up, sliding his back across the base of the statue to support himself, sucks in a deep breathe, trying to refill his empty lungs as best he can. He wore a pair of grungy looking blue jeans and a white with a slightly stained white t-shirt tucked into them. His black biker jacket made a nifty tough guy ensemble matched with his black biker boots. It’s not often you’d catch a white man on this side of Chicago at this time of night, but, with his greasy slicked back hair and rough stubble he looked as grimey as anything on this side of town. What’s more... the face had changed a little, but Train couldn’t help but recognize the greasy little bastard. “Jackie Boy” Fitzpatrick looked the same as he always had. Low rent. No class. Sleazy. Of course it was all those things that had made him such a good tool when he was working the Organized Crime unit of the Chicago PD. He was the type of guy who thought the world owed him something and had no shame about coming with his hand out. He never liked him, but you don’t have to like someone to use them to their full potential. “Now is that any way to greet an old friend?” the weasely little man asked in his raspy voice, too far away to reveal the hint of bourbon on his breathe. “It is when when he’s trying to sneak up behind you with a gun.” Train stated plainly with the hint of a growl. “Besides all that... you and me ain’t never been friends.” “That hurts man,” Jackie uttered with a chuckle as he slowly used the base of the statue to pull his way back to his feet. He may have been bested, but he was smart enough to be on his guard, never allowing his eyes to leave Train. “After all that I’ve done for you?” “You did what you got paid for.” “You mean keeping your ass out of jail?” “Whatever you wanna call it. Now... before I decide to put you out of my misery... what the hell do you want?” “Same thing I always wanted. My little slice of the pie.” “You better take your ass to a bakery then.” “C’mon now big man,” Jackie said, sorting himself out, righting his clothing that had been unceremoniously pulled from it’s place when he was slung across the room. “You got plenty to spare man. And look at me. A little charity my brother.” He chuckled a bit again as he stood his aching body up straight. “I mean don’t tell me you don’t have any in you. I mean if you don’t what’s this community center shit all about, huh” The gangster turned philanthropist. Hahaha... Man that’s a switch.” “You want something from me?” Train asked, trying to keep his finger’s grip on the trigger loose despite every instinct to the contrary. “How bout I let you live and you consider yourself rich with life?” “Oh, so you’re gonna catch a new body now” Is that it? You’re on top of the world and you wanna wind up in the middle of a murder investigation?” “I doubt anybody would miss you.” “Sadly enough, probably not. I mean ever since I got forced to resign from CPD I’m kind of a pariah. Amazing how friends abandon you cuz a few little corruption charges.” “So putting your head on the wall won’t matter much.” “Sure it would Train. Why catch my body when there’s so many others you could catch.” Damn. “You’re treading on dangerous ground dogg.” Train said, piercing him with a cold gaze. “From champion of the world to inmate in D-block. that’s a long damn way to drop.” Train didn’t have anything to say to that. Fact of the matter was the little son of a bitch was in the know. Not neccessarily of anything specific...just enough general info to be a problem. He should do it. He should shoot him, call Biggy to send over some of the boys over to clean up the mess. He still had a favor or two to call in. All it would take is a squeeze and a phone call. Train slowly lowered the pistol. “What the fuck do you want?” Train asked with a sneer. “I think ten grand a month should be good. Champ.” “And I know you won’t come back, sniffing around for more how?” “You know me better than. I’m greedy...not stupid. Damn sure not stupid enough to take so much that it’s easier to kill me.” Train sliced him open and gutted him with his stare. Jackie knew Train was still capable of pulling the trigger. He knew he still had enough to pull to make him disappear. He also knew that Train could afford it...and that Train was smart enough to know he would never have come here without a way to carry this off even if he did disappear. “The old po box still active?” Train asked. “Got a new one.” Jackie responded with a smirk. “Box 220 at that little postage shop of 65th. I’ll let the owner know you’ll be stopping buy. The account number will be in the box. You’re on time... I’m quiet as a church mouse.” Train cuts into him again before tucking the pistol down the front of his jeans. “You’ll have it at the beginning of the month.” “Hahaha... Don’t be so sad. You know you made the right decision.” Jackie said as he strolled up to Train, smart enough to stop just outside his reach. “Now... can’t I get my property back?” Train slowly glanced down at the pistol in his belt. “You just struck it rich.” Train said raising his eyes to slice the man open one more time. “Buy a new one.” Jackie couldn’t help but smile big and hung for a few more seconds looking into Train’s eyes. Finally he stepped around and walked down the hall, every step echoing in Train’s ears. Train didn’t bother to watch him go, knowing he’d gotten what he wanted. Kinda like Train had thought the same thing. For a moment the light was on. Train reached over and flipped the switch. The light went out.
