The Memory Remains #24

9 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 010904 “I’ma kill that muthafucker!” The words stir me from my peaceful slumber, where I lay next to Victoria, her nude body pressed against mine. “I swear, I’m gonna kill that sum bitch!” Deacon’s voice echoes through the halls once more. Shaking my head slightly, I cover Victoria up with a blanket and slide out of bed. Walking to the bedroom door, I wrap a robe around my own nude body just as Deacon starts pounding on the door. Victoria turns and looks at me with her hazel, almond shaped eyes, and I hold up one hand to her, mouthing the words “Go back to sleep,” to her. Opening the door, I step out into the hall quickly, so as not to allow Deac the pleasure of seeing Victoria in her current state. He’s my boy and all, but she’s my lady, and chivalry is not dead, as some may believe. “What’s all the noise about, D” We’re trying to sleep. Late night last night and all.” “We, huh” Late night, huh? You have yourself a booty call last night, John? Which ho you got in there this time?” I shake my head. Same ol? D. Some things never change. “Victoria. It’s been Victoria for a while now.” “Victoria?” Deacon looks at me puzzled for a bit. “AH! The Asian chick that kicked your ass.” Yeah, things definitely do not change. “Nearly, D. She nearly kicked my ass. It was a tie, remember?” “Whatever, John. You just wanna admit that you lost to a woman. I can understand, not wanna to hurt your pride and all. Denial just ain’t a river in Egypt anymore, is it?” I stare blankly at D. “Back to the point. What’s up?” Deacon looks at my, his half-loopy cherish cat grin fading away into a mask of seriousness. “I’ma gonna kick your ass!” “I could tell from the yelling. Why?” “What the fuck did you do with all of my shit?” “Your shit?” “Yeah, the shit I left here while I was gone?” “Oh. That shit. I moved it.” “Man, you do not go messing with a black man’s shit. You could get yo” self hurt. I seen it once. This one dude, he touched my second cousin’s brother’s shit once and BAM! Boy didn’t know what hit him. They all just left him layin? in the backyard. It was sad.” I grabbed Deacon by his shoulder and guided him back down the steps. “You have to understand something D. I try to keep this whole estate nice and neat. A place for everything and everything in its place, so to speak. But your room,” I stop Deacon and myself in front of the room that had been formerly his, the door shut, “was an eyesore.” Reaching out, I swung open the door revealing a neat and tidy guest room. The posters had been carefully removed, graffiti painted over, and furniture repaired. Hell, the cleaning crew had even managed to get the funk smell outta the air. I should probably tip them for that. Looking over at Deacon, I see a tear almost start to well up in his eye. “All is not lost D.” Taking his shoulder, I take him through the house towards the backyard. “Your absence from the house has allowed me to complete many things. The first, cleaning up your room. The second,” I paused dramatically before swinging open the double French doors, “was adding a new section onto the house.” Deacon stares out over the length of the backyard, across the swimming pool at the new addition to the house. He looks at me questioningly and I gesture for him to check it out. He walks over almost carefully, unsure of what to expect. He makes it to the door, and throws it open, leaping out of the way, like he thought something was gonna reach out and grab him. I couldn’t help but laugh at the sight. As he rose out of the bushes, Deac peaked his head inside, just in time to see the hundreds of people start to stream out like water through a burst dam. “Surprise!” the crowd yelled out in an echo, not quite together. Deacon looked over at me with a question on his face. “It’s yours. Do with it what you want. This, my friend, is the Holmes Wing,” I told Deac as two nicely figured women came up to D, wearing bikinis that contained less cotton than a Tylenol bottle, shoving drinks in his hand. “Tonight, we party!” I yell out to the crowd, which roared it’s party roar in approval. I pulled Deacon to the side. “And tomorrow, we have a job.”
