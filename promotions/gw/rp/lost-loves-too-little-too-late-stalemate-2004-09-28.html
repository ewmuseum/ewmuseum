Lost Loves: Too Little, Too Late... Stalemate

28 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/28/2004 Have you ever felt like you were going in circles, making zero headway? Yeah, that’s about where I was. I took a taxi to Bailey’s rundown shithole pad, watching their dark, sleek sports car pull away just as I pulled up. So, they’d been here. I was really beginning to dislike this Zenith asshole? her brother notwithstanding. Likely another dead end. ---- Martin Bailey awoke to clouds of steam, his head spinning. He didn’t recall having that much to drink? and why was he in the bathtub? His mind felt muddy, fluffy and muddled, his thoughts thick and muzzy. Where had the girl gone? He felt sick to his stomach, but at the same time mildly euphoric. He let his eyes drift closed, enjoying the feeling of relaxation. Darkness surrounded him, and he felt like he was floating. Buoyed on some warm, pleasant current. Far away, he could hear voices? a man and a woman, bickering. They didn’t matter, whoever they were. For a fleeting, brief shining moment, he felt at peace. It was like? old times. He could smell coconuts? tanning lotion? he must be on some tropical island, basking in the sun? and still, he could feel that water, all around him, warm and comfortable. The raft behind him felt hard, and cold? rather unpleasant, so when the voices got louder he surrendered himself to their care. His friends? they would inflate his raft? they would give him another drink, with a cute little umbrella in it. The blonde bombshell from the bar would get in the water with him? maybe he would get to touch her wet, naked body. Like a stone, he could feel himself sinking into the dark water, but he wasn’t afraid. He drifted lower and lower, towards the Land of Nod? not the same Nod Cain had fled to after killing Abel? no, this was the Nod to which dreaming children journeyed? like Peter Pan, seeking eternal youth? and adventure? waking in the morning to find themselves safe in the golden embrace of the sun. Still descending through the darkness south of Nod, he thought he heard the words, a roar of pure animal anger, like a lion. Perhaps there be tigers in these here parts. That brought a smile of his face. Tigers. And lions, and bears. He let his arms float on the surface of the water, letting the weight bear them up. His arms dangled at his sides, drifting in the warm water. Through his euphoric musings, he became aware of a tingling in his hands. Pins and needles, numbness. They were cold, like blocks of ice, although the water was warm. He moved his hands? And opened his eyes, his mouth wide in a silent scream. Pain. He looked down, and saw the water that swirled around him, almost to the brim of the tub. It was crimson, his wrists gaping wounds. He pulled his arms out of the water, and blood ran like rivers down his arms, painting them red from finger to elbow. Blood flowed freely from the twin gashes, almost matching the water that poured from the faucet for speed. His hands weren’t just freezing now, they were dead cold and fishbelly gray. The color of death. He would have fought it, but it was then that the alcohol in his remaining blood, and the pints that were filling the tub conspired against him. He slumped against the back, and simply stopped breathing, giving up a life that hadn’t much been worth living anyhow. ---- Jackson paused on the threshold, noticing that the door was slightly ajar. He cocked his head, listening for sounds from within. Nothing but running water. At first, that didn’t strike him as odd. He saw the open filing cabinet, and the open folder lying across the layers of filth and dust that covered the desk. He felt drawn to it, and the glossy photos that were spread out like a grisly collage. It was her, Jade. He recognized his handiwork, but it still made him feel ill. He tore his gaze away, feeling a sense of vertigo that made him grip the edges of the desk to keep from falling over. He could feel the pain now, her pain? he could taste the bitter copper of her blood. “No,” the sound was pure anguish as he picked up the pictures, tearing them up, letting them flutter to the floor, amid the other trash. He picked up the hastily scrawled medical chart, his eyes scanning the words written there, fearing the worst. He had taken her to the hospital; this was a Xerox of the emergency room chart. Internal hemorrhaging? emergency hysterectomy? over one hundred sutures. “I?” Tears came to his eyes, blurring the words as he let the paper fall, not noticing the sticky note affixed to the corner, not seeing her address printed in Bailey’s chicken scratch. “She almost died. I” almost killed her.” Killed her? killed her? the words seemed to echo in the stillness, mocking him. He threw his head back and roared with anger, bringing both of his fists down on the desk, cracking the soft wood. The desk caved in on itself, spilling trash and papers all over the floor. He walked across the room, mindless to the mess, following the faint glow he saw seeping under the bathroom door. The sound of water was louder now, a great splashing. He kicked the door in and splashed into the bathroom, stopping beside the overflowing tub. Crimson waves washed over the sides, soaking his pants, staining them with the dark essence of Bailey’s spent life. “Goddamn it.” He ground his teeth in anger, reaching out with a leather gloved hand to turn off the faucet, halting the bloody cascade. Bailey was slumped in the tub, naked, his skin already shriveled from the water. Jackson removed one glove, grabbing Bailey by the hair with the other hand and pulling his face out of the water, automatically checking for a pulse. Dead, but not by much. Perhaps if he hadn’t lingered with the pictures, the man would have lived. Jackson let his gaze move to the arms that still trailed in the water, at the flaps of the wounds that were bloated and white. No, he would have died regardless. He let Bailey fall back with a splash, not noticing when the displaced, bloody water doused his pants and boots. “Dead ends. Nothing but dead ends, Zenith, you fucker, you’re going to pay. You don’t know who you’re messing with this time.” He withdrew the tiny sensor from his pocket, checking the display, smiling in satisfaction as the tiny blip moved across the screen. Good, they hadn’t found his tracer yet. They likely wouldn’t, unless they knew to look for it. He seriously doubted any of the band of motley thugs were that smart. It was plastic, made from plant material, not a trace of metal, just radioactive chemicals? and good for up to 1,000 miles, give or take. A conventional scan wouldn’t reveal it, someone would have to look under the rear license plate. “Cat and mouse, Zenith” we’ll find out who the real hunter is.” ---- Have you ever felt like you’re walking into a trap? Like it’s Friday the thirteenth and you just walked under a ladder, while a black cat was crossing your path? I’m not normally superstitious, but as I sat back in the cab and gave directions, I felt like that. The closer we got to that inert blip, the more I felt like I was walking into? my own doom. It sounded trite, but I didn’t know how else to classify the feeling. I watched while the cabby kept his eyes on me, trying not to let on that he was terrified. Yes, I had that power. To intimidate. I wasn’t a small man, and I would imagine now, I didn’t look my best, covered in bloody water, smelling of a slaughterhouse, and the stink of doubt. I doubted my sanity, I doubted my actions. Somehow I knew, as we passed the Global Arena, that it would all come full circle. I had already applied and been accepted. My quest would begin with questioning the vampire who called their octagon home. That was another matter, for another time. I’m getting ahead of myself again, you asked for my story, and I shall deliver, one piece at a time. July Alley. Our destination it would seem. I stepped out of the cab, dropping a handful of twenties through the divider before slamming the door. Rain was falling lightly, softly, muting the dazzling colors of the punk rock crowd that waited in line. The marquee declared that some band called The Vines was playing, a sold out show. From the cacophony pouring out the doors, I wondered why it was sold out? ---- The man stepped up to the head of the line, dwarfing the massive bouncer by a good four or five inches. The bouncer looked at him, and then looked away, finding something unsettling about the man’s eyes. He was standing in the rain, but didn’t seem to be getting wet; his dark hair was slicked back in a ponytail. He smiled slowly, clearing his throat, drawing the bouncer’s attention back to him. “Excuse me,” Jackson growled, placing his hands on his hips, his muscles straining against the snug tank top he wore. A few girls in the line giggled, obviously staring at him. He ignored them, focusing on the bouncer who tried to ignore him. “The fuck do you want, buddy?” Jackson waved a hand in the bouncer’s face, and said in a low voice. “You want to let me in?” “What” Who the fuck do you think you are, Obi Wan-fucking-Kenobi? Nobody gets in here without a ticket, and nobody gets a ticket without some Franklins, if you get my drift.” Jackson simply smiled, that odd light still in his eyes. “Is that so?” He lunged forward suddenly, grabbing the bouncer off his feet and slamming him against the brick wall hard enough to leave a dent. It was then that the fire alarm began ringing, and chaos erupted. The doors exploded outwards almost in Jackson’s face, people nearly trampling each other in their haste to get out of the club. “Jade,” he breathed, trying to fight his way though the crowd to get inside, but to no avail. There had to be a back entrance, or something. A fire escape, even. He shoved people aside, striding quickly around the building. He rounded the corner, and promptly collided with a man. Out of instinct, he drew his gun, and found, to his surprise that the man was Zenith. Zenith was also pointing a gun at his forehead. Time spun away, the sounds of the traffic and the night bleeding into silence, leaving just the two of them. Inhale. Exhale. Eyes locked, hatred filling both pairs. Sweat dripped down his bicep, mingling with the rain, drawing attention to the intricate Chinese dragon that covered his arm, curving around to his shoulder. The Walther was steady, so was Zenith’s .357 Magnum. Both capable of blowing a sizeable hole in the other man’s head. Check and mate. Stalemate. He debated speaking, but decided what Zenith didn’t know, wouldn’t hurt him. Footsteps intruded on the stalemate, and Jackson’s eyes darted away from Zenith for a split second, taking in the newcomers. Zenith’s henchmen, and woman. He tensed, and then lowered the gun at the same time as Zenith, never taking his eyes off those of his enemy. Zenith walked away, surrounded by his goons, never once looking back. His companion did, the man Tina knew as Demitri, and Jackson simply met his gaze, his expression unreadable. He watched them go, and watched them pause at the rear of the vehicle while the woman removed his tracer. “Fuck,” he muttered, turning to retreat into the shadows. He could smell her perfume and it made him feel dizzy and disoriented. “Jade” Kaitlynn? Kitty? Kylie? Who are you and why must you torment me so?!” He slammed his fist into the wall, utterly silent save the sweat that poured down his face, mingling with the rain as the skies opened up in a torrent of water. Perfect to mask his tears of shame and frustration. He didn't like losing. He didn't like being bested. He didn't like Zenith. Strike that, he loathed Zenith with every phantom beat of his cold, black heart. His hands squeezed into fists at his sides, the knuckles cracking loudly, like gunshots in the sudden silence. He faded into the darkness, almost as though he’d never been there at all... leaving nothing behind but the soft shushing hiss of the rain. ---- Love. Hate. The whole damn thing, round and round. A never-ending circle, Orobos, the snake devouring its tail. I wanted her dead, I wanted her alive. I wanted to maim her? I wanted to save her. Who was I? What had I become? An angel? A demon? No. I was just a man. A fool in love. Humanity was a curse that I was finally free of? alas, it seemed these emotions were not a part of mortality. If I could cut out my heart to stop this pain, I would, but I knew it wasn’t that dead organ that still carried her mark. She was etched into my mind? she was entangled with my soul. I hated her. I loved her. I? Needed her.
