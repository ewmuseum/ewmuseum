Untitled roleplay

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Here I am, chest heaving as I gasp for air, sweat flooding from every pore on my body before falling to the canvas like droplets man made rain. I stand here exhausted in the wrestling ring recently built in my backyard. I’ve been here practicing moves for hours now in preparation for my title defense at Whiplash... as though CW’s absurdly strenuous workout earlier in the day wasn’t enough. As I continue my breather I look across my backyard and reflect on how much it’s changed within the short amount of time I’ve spent with The Upper Echelon. What was once a large, bland, and empty field now holds a blacktop basketball court with graffiti all over it, a full size wrestling ring, and an Olympic size swimming pool complete with hot tub and waterfall, Sean’s suggestion. The grass is now evenly cut and a bright shade of green. Feeling somewhat rested now, I continue on. I reset the chair I’ve been practicing with in the center of the ring and walk back to jog back into the corner. Staring at the chair, then at the turnbuckle, and once more at the chair I mutter to mutter to myself “Come on Deacon, you can do this.” I’m starting to lose my nerve as I’ve already missed this move twice now. I take a deep breath and I’m off. In my mind I imagine Roswell whipping me across the ring as I race full speed toward the chair. Once within range I plant my right foot down firmly and push off with every ounce of strength in my calf, landing left foot first on the turnbuckle. As my right foot comes down I stumble within what little space I have and am not teetering dangerously close to the edge flailing my arms in the air, a failed attempt to either regain my balance or take flight. Eventually I fall to the ground below just as I had two times prior to now. I always feel like I’m falling in slow motion, it seems like a million things go through my head before hitting the ground. This time it was that I’ve gotta take the trash out tonight and whether or not I should buy a dog. I hit the ground, my body making a dull thud against the concrete that surrounds the ring. Usually there are pad surrounding the ring to cushion my visit but I’ve removed them, at Whiplash there will be burning tables surrounding the ring. After rolling over and shaking off the pain I remain on my back rather than get up. Partially out of disgust, mainly out of fatigue. “Come on man, get it together.” I mumbles as I look up into the sky. The sun is quickly being overtaken by clouds in a rapidly darkening sky. A cool breeze whips through the blades of grass. A storm is coming. Feeling I’ve got to nail this move at least once before going inside, I kick up and climb back into the ring. Upon rising to my feet I notice the front door of my house slowly opening as I stare in through the sliding glass door. Must be CW... He enters the house clad in loud Italian clothes and smoking as usual, he also has a sizeable stack of papers under his arm. He tosses the papers onto my card table and continues on puffing his cigar as he walks through my house. While watching him look for me I contemplate the number of times I’ve told him not to smoke his cheap cigars in my house because it makes my furniture smell like tainted filth. The thought soon fades as I decide to hurry up and give this move another shot before he drags his old ass out here and calls himself giving me pointers. I’m also trying to beat this storm and get in before it start raining. Too Late The first few drops of rain strike my nose as a slight drizzle begins falling from the sky, it’s somewhat refreshing. I close my eyes and take a deep breath. When I reopen them nothing has changes, save the fact that it’s now pouring down rain. “Fuck it.” I say to myself as I run toward the chair. I get there, plant my right foot and jump to the turnbuckle. In my head I imagine Roswell right there behind me to push me off the turnbuckle and send me crashing through a burning chair since he’s nearly as fast as I am. Without even getting my footing, as soon as my right foot hits I spring back into the air performing a backflip and landing on the seat of the chair. A fist pump both represents my current level of satisfaction with myself and concludes my workout. When I turn around CW is standing just barely beyond my glass door hiding from the rain under the roof. He has a fresh white folded towel draped over his shoulder and is clapping in a slow dull tone as if to say he’s not impressed. I hop out of the ring and walk over to him catch the towel along the way, we both then enter my house. I’m barely in the door good and he’s already starting in on me. “What was that?” He questions in a nagging tone. “What was what?” I ask, obviously playing dumb. “All of that” he says throwing his hands up in flipping motions. “Looked like a bunch of showboatin” and hot doggin to me, why couldn’t you just land on the ground?” He inquires, his tone growing increasingly annoying. “Because that would’ve been boring mom...” I fire back. “And how many times have I told you not to smoke your seventy five cent dollar store cigars in here!” Stinkin? up my furniture man!” I exclaim while snatching and extinguishing CW’s cigar. “And why are you here anyway?” CW replies by silently pointing to the sizeable stack of bills and junkmail sitting on my card table. “Oh.” I reply after taking a look. “So what kind of trouble are you getting into tonight?” He says while lighting up another cigar. After shooting him a dirty look I reply. “Shit, did you see Evolution!” I’m gonna go out and celebrate.” CW’s face turns to stone as he knods his head slowly back and forth in disapproval, probably thinking of what happened last time I celebrated. “Best behavior D” he says trying to sound fatherly. “Whiplash is closer than you think” “Yeah yeah” I reply in a lowering tone. “Exemplary behavior, I’m serious.” he says while cocking an eyebrow at me. “Right.” I say fully inflecting my voice as though I’m sounding off. CW continues “In fact I want you to behave as thought I’m standing right next to you for the entire rest of the day” as he makes his way to the door. While letting him out I retort with “Okay, but don’t you think people will wonder why I’m constantly rolling my eyes and sighing?” CW gives me his half assed attempt at an intimidating look as he makes his way out the door. My eyes roll as I slam the door shut behind him. After watching CW’s triple white Cadillac El Dorado back smoothly out of my driveway and begin to glide down the street I decide that it’s time to execute the three s?, shit, shower, and shave. Post grooming I emerge from my room and give myself a final once over in the mirrored hallway of my house. A quick adjustment of my collar and I’m good to go. Once in the garage the press of a single button on my keychain starts the engine and opens the door of my black special edition Porsche Carerra GT. I smirk as I think about how blowing up the octagon filled with the Nevermore was as easy as starting my car... much more satisfying though. Another tap on my keychain causes the middle door of my five car garage to slowly begin so swing open as I hop in the driver seat. While waiting for daylight to shine inside the dimly lit car stable I begin checking radio stations. “No Leaf Clover” is playing on 101.1. I’ve heard this song on numerous occasions but today it reminds me of someone, Max Roswell. “It comes to be that the soothing light at the end of your tunnel, is just a freight train coming your way” I can’t help but smile as I picture Max Roswell being smashed by a train. He probably sees our match at Whiplash as his chance to advance and GW giving him the go ahead when in reality all they’ve done is throw him to the wolves. The door finally opens and as I ease the car down the long driveway of my estate a car flies by. A gunmetal gray nineteen sixty nine Chevy Nova with white flame graphics. Two teenagers in the front seat, one in the back. All boys, probably my age give or take a year, the difference is I’m an eighteen year old man, these were boys. Gangly, grinning kids in T-shirts and blue jeans. The sun had just reemerged from the clouds and is shining brightly again in it’s final moments although the roads are still a little damp. I didn’t know the kids, but I know the car, I see it around here all the time flying up and down the street. I live in what some may call “the middle of nowhere” or “BFE” which means there’s plenty of uninhabited road and not much in the way of cops, the perfect place to push a car to it’s limits. The guy in the passenger seat nodded in my direction when they drove by, but I wasn’t sure if he was nodding at me, or toward my car. They may not know me, but they obviously know my car. Most people in the area do, as I’m the only person with one. The Nova rolled to a stop, and made a left turn onto the highway going towards the downtown area. The car paused, then the driver floored it. The rear tires squealed, testosterone flowed. It was an invitation. I obviously know more about that car than they do: it’s fast, but not a match. All three kids in the car turn around looking puzzled. I smile and give them a one finger salute as I slam the car down into second gear and catch up with them effortlessly. All three of the car’s passengers? jaws drop as I proceed to cut them off. The faces then turn from amazed to angered when I decide to slow down dramatically. They switch lanes and take off again, knowing I’ve won, I let them go. The car pulls out of view as I slow back down to seventy, still over the speed limit. After about two miles I see smoke in the distance. When I get closer I see that same Nova flipped over sitting right on the centerline of the two-lane road, it’s engine one hundred feet away in a ditch causing the grass around it to smolder. A silver Chrysler Sebring sits about fifty feet away on the other side of the road. There is only one bad curve between my house and downtown, and these cars seem to have found it, and each other, head on. Just behind the Nova, two of the boys lay on the concrete, one next to the other, the third was to the left of the car. In the Sebring a woman sit badly hurt but alive in the passenger seat along with a man who obviously has a compound fracture. The bone of his right arm poking out of his skin. “Dumbasses” I whisper to myself shaking my head as I cruise by. I feel their eyes watching me, hoping I’ll stop and help as I roll through the site of the accident at a leisurely pace surveying the scene. I have no intention of helping. Compassion is not a character trait or something that you’re born with, like a sunny disposition or an opposable thumb. Besides, I’ve got somewhere to be...
