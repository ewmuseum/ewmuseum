Brick

16 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-16-03 It’s a sunny day in the cemetery. People stand at gravestones, talking to the loved ones that rest in the graves. Brick walks past a few until he finally comes to a grave at the foot of a hill. The epitaph on the headstone reads: Joseph Randall 1944-1999 Husband, father, friend It’s been four years since Brick’s father passed away from a heart attack. That was the day that Brick became the man of the family. “Hey, Dad,” says Brick. “Been a while since I’ve been here. Not because I didn’t want to come. I’ve just been busy. Mom’s doing okay. There was that lump they found on her breast, but it turned out to be benign. Monica’s doing good. She’s married now to a decent guy. You’d like him. I’ll admit, he and I had a misunderstanding lately, but we straightened everything out. You’d have liked Kyle. He’s a pretty decent brother. He’s definitely better for her than Cedric was. Aaron’s married, too. Yeah, I didn’t believe it either. His wife’s having a baby in about three months. They asked me to be the godfather. So, everyone’s more or less doing fine.” Brick’s mood then changes. “Things aren’t that great with me, though. The singing group I’m managing had their debut album go platinum, and they’re working on their second album, but that’s about it. I don’t know if you’ve been watching from the afterlife, but I’m in some deep mess with this son of a bitch named Salaok. Yeah, I thought it was a stupid name, too. Anyway, he owns half of Global Wrestling now, and he’s got this stipulation where if I lose a match, I’m gone from the company until he decides to bring me back. And considering how much he hates me, that’ll never happen. He almost had me at one point, too, but I’ve been able to beat the odds. He’s been mad at me ever since I stuck my nose in his and his brother’s business with the Welch brothers, which I probably wouldn’t have done if his punk ass brother hadn’t tried to kill me on a number of occasions. Anyway, since then, he and his boys have done nothing but try to make my life miserable. Couple of Novembers ago, they screwed me out of a World title shot. First one I ever got, too. Then, last summer, they kidnapped Aaron and tortured him. At one point, they almost paralyzed him. I was ready to kill those black hearted bastards. I almost did kill one of them. Little faggot named Derek Draven. I know I shouldn’t be cussing so much, but that’s the way I feel. Last October, they threatened to show up at Monica’s wedding. About a week ago, during a match, Salaok whispered in my ear that he was there. I don’t know if he actually was, or if he was just messing with me, but I don’t want to take that chance. I feel like I haven’t been living up to the promise that I made to you the day you died, and that’s what’s really been getting to me.” Brick’s mind begins to wander. It goes back to 1990. Brick is sixteen years old. He walks into the den where his father is sitting. “Dad, you wanted to see me?” “I did. Come, sit down, Jamal. I want to talk to you.” Brick sits down. “If this is about what happened at school?” “This isn’t about school. But it is important. Now, son, I’m not always gonna be around. One day, I’m gonna pass on, and then you’ll have to be the man of the family.” “Dad, why are we talking about this, now” You’re nowhere close to dying.” “JFK once said the best time to prepare for rain is when it’s sunny. Jamal, one of the greatest gifts God can give a human being is a family. And when you receive something, you cherish it. You protect it. Now, I want you to promise me something. That when I pass on, you’ll protect your mother and your sister, no matter what.” “Look, Dad?” “Promise me!” “OK, I promise, Dad. Brick’s mind then wanders back to the day his father died. He remembers seeing his father lying there in his hospital bed, while doctors worked on him, trying to revive him. He remembers his mother and his sister in tears as they waited. He remembers the doctor coming out of the emergency room. Brick, Coretta and Monica rushed right up to him. “Well?” The doctor looked down and said, “I’m sorry.” Brick closed his eyes and clenched his fists. Coretta and Monica instantly broke down. Brick pulled them close to him, although deep inside, he wanted to cry, too. Tears trickled down from his eyes, but he figured he had to be strong, because now he was the man of the family. His mind then flashes to the funeral. It was raining the day they put Joseph Randall into the ground. The soloist sang “His Eye is on the Sparrow”. As everyone began to leave the gravesite, Brick stayed behind. “Jamal, honey, we’re getting ready to go.” “I’ll be there in a minute, Mom.” Brick then turned back to the grave. “Well, Dad, like you said, I’m the man now. I just want you to know that I intend to keep my promise. I’m gonna protect Mom and Monica, whatever it takes. That’s a promise.” Brick flashes back to the present. “Real good job I’ve done protecting my family. Now I’ve got this son of a botch gunning for me that’s twisted enough to hurt them just to get at me. But I’m gonna make up for it, Dad. I meant it when I said that I’d protect Mom and Monica, and I will. Whatever it takes.”
