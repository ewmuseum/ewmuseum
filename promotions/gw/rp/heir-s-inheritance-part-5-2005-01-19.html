Heir's Inheritance Part 5

19 January 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/19/2005 Arena de Los Deportivos, Mexico City January 18, 2005, 2130 hours Maybe the show wasn't as large a production as that American global federation that was doing a World Tour in the area... but the CMLL was doing very well on its own, and its audience, while perhaps not as huge as the American production's world-wide audience, was quite devoted and loyal. La Herencia de Herederos, one of CMLL's most popular factions on the Mexican wrestling scene, was satisfied with another successful house show in Mexico's largest wrestling arena. Juan Ramirez bussed his wife on the cheek as they entered their locker room. 'Good show,' he said. Teresa smiled. As CMLL's only native in the still-new Women's wrestling scene, she was seen as something of a novelty act, but there was no denying her talent and skill in the ring. Her husband wrestled not only in CMLL, but in a couple of smaller indy Lucha Libre circuits, where Teresa moonlighted as his valet. 'The Crimson Comet,' as he called himself, had held several indy Light-Heavyweight championships in his tenure. The two of them were happiest in the preciously rare moments when they could wrestle inter-gender tag-team matches, of course; the two spouses made a perfect duo in the ring together. In their ten years on the circuits, they'd been able to wrestle thirty-nine inter-gender tag-team matches, and were undefeated. Juan read Teresa's face in a heartbeat and tried to put an arm around her shoulder. 'Teresa, I know that--' 'Yes, I know you know,' sighed Teresa, nudging Juan's arm off her shoulders, 'but it doesn't help matters. I miss my sister, Juan. I wish she were with us.' A tear ran down her tanned cheek, and Juan could do little other than hang his head and then hug her. 'I'm sorry things turned out this way, Teresa,' he said softly. 'I'll always be here for you.' Pulling away, Teresa got a look in her eyes that Juan knew all too well. 'Well, then bring her here,' she said pointedly. 'Convince her that her future is here. If she won't listen to me, maybe she'll listen to you.' The conversation was interrupted as they went into the inner part of the locker room where their mentor in the business, Jos? Sanchez, was doing his usual duty as babysitter for their daughter. Sanchez was Melina's godfather, and as close a friend as anyone in the family could ask for. Having been college-educated in the States, he had acquired the joking nickname of Jos? 'can you see' Sanchez after the national anthem of America. Some of his colleagues applied the nickname to poke fun at his undefeated record in blindfold matches, and it stuck. Jos? himself found the nickname appropriate, and so everyone always laughed with him rather than at him. They looked up from their checkers game and Melina ran to her mother's arms. 'Felicitaciones, Mamacita,' the girl said happily. *Congratulations, Mommy. Teresa smiled at her young daughter. 'Gracias, mi chica,' she said in response; Melina still spoke no English. Although a little shy of 10 years old, Melina's situation as the daughter of two professional wrestlers made her education a touch-and-go proposition. The Ramirezes had to hire tutors for her due to the constant travel, even though Mexico's wrestling scene was concentrated mainly in a few cities and the country wasn't as large as, say, the United States. Melina's current tutor spoke only Spanish, and so Melina did too. Juan kneeled down and gave Melina a hug. '?Tienes hambre?' he asked. *Are you hungry? Melina gave a small smile and nodded. 'S?, Pap?,' she said. Jos? leaned over and whispered something in Melina's ear. She listened, and giggled happily. 'Tex-Mex on me tonight,' he said genially. 'I'm meeting with a contact from the States, whom I've known for a little while now. I'd like to introduce him to you.' Juan and Teresa looked at each other, and then back at Jos? and nodded. 'Shower and dress,' said Teresa to Juan. 'I'll do the same after you're done.' 40 minutes later, Teresa was zipping up her street boots as Juan finished packing both their travel-gym bags. 'I'll drive,' said Juan. 'Let's go.' Maria's Cantina January 18, 2005, 2245 hours Stepping into Maria's Cantina was not a comfortable sensation for Juan and Teresa. Nonetheless, the Ramirez family had never really stopped coming here-- in fact, Teresa met her husband there. The cantina was under new ownership, which bore no ill-will over the brawl that led to Mateo's fall from grace, imprisonment and eventual suicide. No heads turned as Juan and Teresa entered with their daughter and with Jos?. The hostess smiled graciously and offered to escort them to an empty table. 'I'm Jos? Sanchez; I'm meeting someone here,' said Jos? to the hostess. 'Oh, yes,' said the hostess. 'He arrived ten minutes ago. He's expecting you. This way.' The hostess led the four of them to a table where one man was waiting. Clearly American, he was fairly short and non-descript; with straight brown hair, a mustache, and unimposing blue eyes. He seemed to be physically fit but was far from a bodybuilder and didn't have half the muscularity required for professional wrestling, even though Juan and Teresa knew he was involved in the sport somehow. 'I'm Juan Ramirez,' said Juan in a friendly introductory tone. 'My wife Teresa and our daughter Melina. And of course you know Jos?, who said he wanted to introduce us to you.' Melina fidgeted uncomfortably. She was a little shy around strangers, especially after the obligatory lecture that it wasn't a good idea to speak to them. 'Mamacita, ¿quién es este hombre?' she asked in a somewhat plaintive voice. *Mommy, who is this man? Taking initiative in trying to calm the child's worries about him, the man extended a hand with a friendly smile. 'Hello there,' he said. 'Don't be afraid. Jos? is a friend of mine.' Melina stared back but didn't respond. 'She doesn't speak English,' explained Teresa, smiling politely. She patted her daughter's shoulder reassuringly, showing how Jos? went up and gave the man a friendly handshake. 'Ah... excuse me, I wasn't aware,' said the man. 'Well, as you know, Jos? and I have been friends for a while. I come down to Mexico every so often scouting out local talent, and offering the truly promising ones a chance to go global. The local Lucha Libre federations and leagues have some very skillful and talented wrestlers, but the promoters have little interest in promoting their talent outside Mexico's borders. That's where talent scouts like I come in. While most of Lucha Libre's finest prefer to stay here in Mexico, some are interested in showcasing their talents abroad and around the world. I give them that chance.' Teresa accepted a glass of red wine from the waitress and raised her eyebrows at the man. 'So, let me guess,' she said. 'You believe my husband and I are interested in such an offer?' She turned to Jos?. 'You talked to him about us?' 'He already knows that you and Juan like being able to tag-team together,' said Jos?. 'A talent scout's job is to know things like these about the popular members of local federations and leagues. They don't just approach any Tomas, Ricardo or Harold backstage at school auditoriums or sports arenas. They do their homework and only speak to people who they know might have some interest. And it's no secret in the CMLL that you and Juan like to tag-team together... something that you generally don't get to do here in the Lucha Libre leagues, or rarely even in the CMLL.' Juan nodded in somewhat reluctant agreement. 'So,' he said to the talent scout, 'What's your name, and who do you represent?' The man handed Juan a card. 'My name's Stuart LaPortia,' he said. 'I represent the federation 'Global Wrestling' in the United States.' (TBC)
