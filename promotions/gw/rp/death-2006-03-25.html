Death

25 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/25/06 Shaun Styles looked on at his battered and bruised friend as he slowly made his way towards him. Every step he took resonated in the hollow, metal facility filled with an array of memories best left forgotten. He locked eyes with Jade Taylor for only a half of a second before the dreary look on his best friend’s face set back in and his heavy lids dropped. Styles leaned in to reach the back of his head and untied the blood-stained bandana covering his mouth. Taylor immediately spat out the crimson-red liquid from his mouth making a lasting mark on Shaun’s ripped white shirt. Don Laproni’s voice came from the distance as he stood in the exit of the abandoned warehouse. “You’re friend don’t look too good’Why don’t you get him up to a hospital, tell “em I sent you. Maybe they’ll cut your broke ass a break an” get him fixed up real nice for a bargain.” Don Laproni spit on the steel floor before signaling to Tony and Young, his two bodyguards. The three men turned their backs on Shaun Styles and Jade Taylor, slowly walking away from the scene of the crime. “Now you know we mean business, kid” Laproni’s voice faded out as his silhouette disappeared into the light snow flurry plaguing the Brooklyn night. Shaun Styles turned back to his friend and untied him from the chair. Gently, he laid him down on the ground right next to his contract to GLOBAL. Taylor looked almost peaceful as his young appeal was only accentuated by the various bruises and open wounds on his face. The snow began to accumulate and blanketed a white background around the two men. Those broken windows weren’t nearly enough protection from the upcoming storm. Jade Taylor let out a raspy cough turning over to his side allowing specks of blood to escape and land on the GLOBAL contract. Styles quickly threw the stack of papers, tossing it against a nearby wall. “You’ll be alright, don’t worry, man.” Shaun tried to reassure his long-time friend. He had a feeling he was lying. “What the hell did I get you into?” Styles murmured as he began to look through Jade Taylor’s red dress shirt. It was torn and covered in blood but almost unnoticeable due to the hue of the shirt. There it was’It hit Shaun Styles like a bag of bricks. Two perfect circular holes in the breast of his shirt. A look of fear and worry crept up on Style’s face as ripped open the buttoned shirt and saw two bullet wounds located in his best friend’s chest. Jade Taylor barely lifted his eyes open and let out a slight groan. “I’m sorry, Shaun’They told me’They said’if I tell “em’where you were’they’d let me live’I’m sorry?” Taylor’s voice broke as he let out his final words and let his eyes fall shut. A tear dripped down Styles? cheek and landed on the fallen corpse. He covered his face with his battered hands, covered in the blood of his best friends and fell back against a wall. He was responsible for this. Shaun Styles couldn’t help but blame himself for what happened to Taylor. It was his debt that his friend died for’And it wasn’t even close to being over. Shaun Styles collected himself and brushed his lengthy black hair back, out of his face. He gained his composure and got to his feet without ever looking at the completely immobile and silent body of his best friend. Walking over to the stack of papers, he noticed that the storm was picking up outside. He picked up the contract, folded it, and placed it in the back pocket of his old, blue denim jeans. There was nothing left to be done with the body. It seemed like a cold and uncaring ending to their friendship, leaving Taylor’s body in this warehouse. But Styles realized that there was nowhere to take it. Drag a dead body up and down the streets of Brooklyn? That didn’t seem like a wise choice. No’He was to be left there as a memorial and Styles’he had to move forward. Shaun stumbled out of the warehouse into a cold and windy back alley of New York City. It was shaping up to be quite a storm.
