She's The Blade: The Other Woman

28 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 28, 2005 Lisa Jameson’s Diary April 24, 2005 Things have finally started to come together in the last week. Mike finally took the hint and proposed to me. It was a beautiful moment, especially since he asked me as we sunbathed on this little island he owns off the coast. That day also marked the first time that I ever made love outdoors, very exciting but sometimes annoying’especially in the sand. When we came back to Beverly Hills I immediately had an announcement published in all the local papers and then invited all of our close friends to a party to celebrate our impending marriage. Before the party Mike tried to get me to sign a pre-nuptial agreement but I was able to convince him that we didn’t need one. I also convinced him to get that icky wheelchair boy out of the house, disabled people make me nervous. We haven’t really set a date yet but we’ve agreed to have the ceremony sometime in the beginning of May. We’ll have the ceremony at “The Chapel of the Bells” which is a few blocks away from Mike’s house. Since I’ve been married so many times I don’t need a big ceremony, I’ll just be happy to have Mike and his money all to myself’especially since there isn’t a pre-nuptial now. Meanwhile, at the offices of The Beverly Hills Star? It was evening and the small office that housed The Beverly Hills Star was almost empty. The only people that remained in the three room home of the gossip rag were the paper’s top reporter, Alice Martinez, and her publisher, Bob Murray. Alice had her dark hair pulled back in a pony tail and wore a blue t-shirt and dark blue jeans. Bob was heavy set man in his late fifties; he wore a black suit and green tie. As Alice sat at her desk, Bob looked down at her with a stern look on his rugged face. “You shouldn’t get involved,” he said with a stern voice that matched the look on his face. “Biddle was the one dumb enough to propose to her; he deserves whatever the hell it is that’s coming to him.” Alice sighed, she had spent the past hour trying her best to explain her idea to the Bob but he refused to listen. “Deep down he’s a really nice guy,” she said, “He doesn’t deserve to lose everything because of some gold digger. Besides, if his ex-girlfriend shows up to break up the wedding it’ll sell more copies than another boring wedding story.” Bob took a moment to consider the last words she had said. He nodded his head and smiled and he reached into his pocket for a cigar. “Go ahead and do it then,” he said before he lit the cigar. As Bob left the room Alice looked down at the clipping of the wedding announcement that she was about to send to Biddle’s former valet/love interest, Hillary Watson. Later that Same Week in Boston? The upstairs bedroom of the Beacon Hill home was dark; it had been that way since the day that he left. The only sign of life within the room was the constant sound of sobbing that came from the bed in the corner. At one time she was radiant and full of life, but now she was pale and sickly. Her blonde hair was unkempt and she had worn the same pair of flannel pajamas for months. Her pale hands covered her beautiful nymph-like features as she sobbed uncontrollably. It had been six months since he had left her alone at the altar and she had not recovered from the rejection. Even her beloved Red Sox finally winning the World Series had not comforted her. If only she could find a way to get him back. While he had wronged her many times she could not help but love him, in a way the relationship had made her a glutton for punishment. As she silently pondered what she had done to deserve the pain of unrequited love there was a knock at the door. “Dear,” came the soft sound of her Father’s thick New England accent, “you decent?” “Go away,” she managed to say in between sobs. The door slowly squeaked open and light invaded the room for the first time in months, she closed her red and puffy eyes in pain as the light reached her pupils. “This came for you in the post,” her Father said as he placed an envelope beside her. He then turned and left the room, the short, stocky bald man had learned months ago that his daughter would ignore his pleas for her to forget about that man and move on. However, he did leave the door open. He figured that it would do her good to actually get out of bed and close it herself. After her Father had left she looked down at the envelope and saw that the return address was Beverly Hills. Her heart leapt with joy as she tore open the envelope; perhaps he had seen the error of his ways and wanted her back! She was disappointed when she found a newspaper clipping that was the announcement of both his engagement and coming wedding to another woman. Her only reaction was to rip the clipping up in a small fit of rage. How dare he mock her in such a way! She lifted the remains of the envelope to throw them away when a small slip of paper fell out. The paper read: Hillary, Mike is making a big mistake by marring Lisa Jameson! She doesn’t really love him, she only wants his money. You’re the only one that can stop this! She read the words and finally recognized the name; Lisa was that woman she had read about when she still lived in Beverly Hills. She couldn’t allow her Butter Muffin to fall victim to such a heinous woman. There was only one thing she could do, and that was stopping this unholy matrimony. Perhaps, she could even re-ignite the flame of the romance she had once shared with him. For the first time in months she left her bedroom, she once again had a purpose in life and she would not allow anything to stand in her way. That Weekend in Beverly Hills? The dining area of the small Italian restaurant would be completely dark if it wasn’t for the candles that were provided for every table. The soft candlelight provided a very romantic setting to the room, which was the very reason why Mike Biddle had brought his future wife, Lisa Jameson, to dine in the fine establishment. They had already completed their meals and the bill had already arrived but they remained seated at their table since the atmosphere was far too enjoyable to give up just yet. They were both well dressed, Mike wore his best tailor made suit. It was an all black suit with a matching vest that he wore beneath the suit coat. Throughout the evening he had tugged at his black tie, he had silently cursed himself for tying the damned thing too tight. Lisa was especially beautiful on this night. Her make-up was flawlessly applied to her angelic face and her long brown locks were perfectly styled. She wore a black gown that showed off her cleavage and almost left nothing to the imagination. Throughout the evening they had discussed their wedding plans. But now that the evening was about to come to a close Lisa decided that now was the perfect time to ask Mike about something that had bothered her for weeks. “Baby,” she said, “you know that guy in the suit where you work?” “You’ll have to be more specific darling,” Mike replied. “You know,” she said, “the guy that would die without his suit. I think his name is Adam?” she trailed off. “What about him?” Mike asked, careful to make sure that no one else listened in on their conversation. “You’re not really in on some plot against that man’s family are you?” She asked with a concerned tone, while she disliked the handicapped she didn’t like the idea of her man being involved in some evil plot. Mike began to laugh after she had asked the question. “Lisa,” he replied, still laughing, “How many times do I have to tell you that wrestling is fake” Nothing that I do on television is real; it’s all part of a script.” “But it seems so real!” She exclaimed. Mike smirked. “Only because I’m such a great actor,” he answered, she finally laughed along with him. “I’m sorry honey,” Lisa said, “sometimes it’s easy to forget that wrestling isn’t real.” “It’s quite alright,” he said. Deep down inside himself he felt relieved, he was glad that she had bought the lie. The conversation then turned back to the wedding plans. He pretended to pay attention to her as she went on and on about whom they should or shouldn’t invite. Strangely, as the conversation went on he began to feel as if he was being watched. He looked about the candlelit room and immediately became suspicious of the woman that sat alone at the table in the far northeast corner of the room. “Lisa,” he interrupted her speech, “I think it’s time we left. Don’t look but appears that stupid gossip rag is following us around again.” Lisa rolled her eyes in annoyance, she was sick of being followed around by that paper. They quietly rose from their table and left. The woman that sat at the table at the northeast corner of the room watched them as they left. “I’m not letting you out of my sight Butter Muffin,” the woman quietly said to herself. She rose from her table and left the dining room. She wore a small red dress and matching stiletto heels, the same garb she used to wear when she was his valet in 4WW. Hillary watched from the front of the restaurant as Mike sped away with the other woman in his silver Aston Martin V12 Vanquish. She knew where they were headed and didn’t bother to pursue them. She’d have her chance to end this whole thing in dramatic fashion soon enough. -To Be Continued-
