Organizing

10 June 2005

Date: Week of 6-10 Nestled behind the wall of low hanging branches, the house stood as a testament to the craftsmanship that created the home. Pushing aside the moss covered branches of the cypress trees; she was struck by the beauty of the scene before her. The location was perfect, situated on one of the many swamp rivers, but the dock needed reconstruction to allow boat traffic once again. The home, well, the home had stood the test of time. Originally, a family of slave owners named Foret in the 1700’s owned the plantation property. The information on the original family was lost, but the plantation history suggested such knowledge had been lost purposefully. The first home was said to be even more brilliant than the one she was looking at, and it had been rendered unto the earth with purifying flame. Dark, evil deeds were supposed to have been performed within the pristine walls. The slaves erected a church, complete with small gathering room and cemetery. When the free men found out what had happened, they came in force to evict the slaves, divide them amongst themselves and claim the property. The church was set upon with torches, but the mark of divinity prevented the wood from burning. Frightened, the plantation was abandoned. Then at the beginning of the 1800’s a new owner came forth to purchase the property. Divine or not, the church had rotted away due to poor construction and no maintenance. The new owner saw the graves, and out of respect for the inhabitants they were moved to a new location ten miles down the road. Ridiculed by the aristocracy, the new owner ignored the jibes preferring to honor the dead and buried. He began erecting the new plantation home atop of the church and cemetery. Such ground was sacred, holy and blessed. The work would take many years to complete the grand vision, although a quick home was established for living until the main work could be completed. Following the construction, the surrounding property was used for producing sugar. The new slave master was kind among those who imprisoned their fellow human beings. The construction outlived the master, and his daughter and her husband took over. They concentrated their efforts, and construction on the mansion was completed in 1840. At the same time, they began to build the sugar production and continued to increase their land holdings, which ultimately grew to 300,000 acres. An Irishman, Jacob O’Connor, bought the plantation in 1857. A businessman and a character, O’Connor increased production of sugar to become one of the country’s leading sugar kings. As an Irishman, he was able to save his home from advancing horde of Union soldiers by declaring himself a citizen of the Crown and not part of the hostilities. Saved, following the war he ensured a railroad line went by his home for easier access to the markets he sold his sugar to. The following history of these times is muddied, as O’Connor and his family became secretive. The divine mark seems to continue as the Foret Plantation was spared the great flood of 1927. The fields were not so lucky, and the family fell into financial ruin. The property lay unused for nearly fifty years until someone else bought and restored the mansion. They too disappeared, leaving it alone for another two decades. So here it stood, once white walls stained with the decay of time, rain and mud. Two stories with a wrap around porch on each level, the home was massive. The columns in the front reflected the preferred architecture of an age when great men were trying to accomplish great things, even if the owners here were nothing more than slave owners imitating greatness. The time had painted the home with a powerful face of strength, showing she could survive anything thrown at her over the years. The Foret Plantation was off the beaten path, down the country highway and even off a dirt road. No access by anything less than a four-wheel drive vehicle at the time, but a drive could be built to allow access. The price of the home was small, owing to the remote location and poor condition of the home. The last owner had wired the home for electricity, and even fixed some of the most serious problems. A work crew could make serious progress in just a month, or a small team could fix the home over the course of a year. She knew Xavier was able to work with his hands, and assumed much the same for Alexandria. Bella she wasn’t so sure of, but the woman was tough enough to learn. Pitt too, once he recovered from the injuries, would be able to do great things with hammer and saw. Until the home was more fit for living, there would need to be a living space. Due to the remote location, temporary homes would be tough to come by, but trucks could bring in a pair of trailer homes easily enough. The list of locations had not yielded many options. Too many of the locations were close to urban areas where they would be easily recognized. They needed a remote area, somewhere they could’if not hide, conceal themselves in the open. No visitors would come out here. There was no traffic by car, boat or foot. Satisfied, Donna decided that this would be the only location suitable to their purposes. She opened her cell phone to make a call, but there was no signal. “This is definitely remote enough.” Donna walked down the path, pushing through the cypress limbs to return to the rented Jeep. She would head back and make her calls. . . . . . . . Jake Hix had returned to the ring for a cover and a distraction, but now the career of his own choosing was becoming a hassle. The management team had decided that he needed to be stripped of his Television Title because of his assault on Chuck Reno. The little shit had returned, and no doubt he had been sent by Sid Hix to distract and harass Jake as he worked as a spy. No matter, he would regain his title soon enough. For now, he had to prepare for everything that would come. The more he embraced what lay ahead the more he noticed changes. His body was changing, morphing into something. He had never expected the change, and it was not external. Muscles weren’t larger than before, but his strength was increasing dramatically. He added two more plates to each side for his bench press and squat today. Unfortunately, he had no one he could talk to about the changes. The path he had been set upon was lonely, and involving anyone else would simply jeopardize them as well. His own conscious couldn’t handle anyone else being in jeopardy. If everything went as planned, the last of the weights pressing on him would be removed soon. Are you ready for that? Can you handle being alone like that? Jake ignored his own question. He wasn’t ready to face the answer, and he didn’t want to disappoint himself. Jake was moving through his form, making sure he did everything as his sensei had taught him during his training. Perhaps soon enough he would be ready to turn all of his training against his brother. Finished for the day, Jake left the workout room and headed to the whirlpool. The Dallas Loft offered him everything he needed when he visited. He needed to relax, and he had loaded some information to his laptop and connected it to the screen in there for his review. Stepping into the warm water, he used a small towel to dry his hands before he picked up the remote and turned on the plasma screen. The information was in Word, and he used a wireless keyboard to scroll through everything he had been given by his Sioux friends. Apparently, after being resurrected Sid required time to convalesce in the Dakotas. During this time, the dark disciples watched over him until his strength returned to him. Finally capable of movement of a regular man, he had made contacts with some group in Nevada. They were? Are you kidding me? . . . . . . . The dirt road framed by the ancient trees led to the poorly maintained asphalt road. Everything in this part of the world seemed dried and dirty. The rains washed it all with muddy water that dried to caked on dust in the harsh sun between swells and falls of the rivers. Donna drove down, making sure to stay inside the posted speed limits. She wouldn’t be stopped, never risking any exposure of the safety of her friends. After an hour, she came to a gas station. Donna pulled in, and stopped right next to the pump. She began filling up her tank, then walked away to use her cell phone. Donna dialed the number and waited for an answer. “Hello?” She heard water of some kind in the background, “Jake, this is Donna. I found a place. The home requires some work, but it will be something they can do to keep their minds occupied.” “Perfect. How long to close?” “The home has been on the market for years. I can close this week easily. I just need to arrange for some living quarters because the home will need some work first.” Jake understood, “Do whatever you have to do, and then let me know when to make the call. We’ll need tight security for whatever we do.” “Well, we can just fly them into?” “No.” “What?” “I can’t know anything about it Donna.” Jake replied. “Why?” Jake sighed, “Once this is done, they will be separated from me in case anything happens. They will be safe, and part of their safety is that no one can know where they are except for them and you. No one will ever find out, at least not until he’s gone.” “Jake, isn’t that being a little paranoid?” Donna regretted the question the moment it had been asked. The sadness and seriousness in Jake’s voice was heartbreaking, “There’s bodies on ice I’ll be putting into coffins one day that say it isn’t paranoid. Just work everything through the separate corporations, and no trail back to anyone or anything related to my family. Take care of everything, and take care of yourself Donna. Once this is done, we won’t speak to each other anymore either.” “I understand’Jake?” “Yeah.” “You don’t have to do this alone.” There was a long pause. “I’ve always been alone. This isn’t any different.” . . . . . . . Arranging the follow-up meeting was much easier. There was still lots of trepidation, but he had checked his messages almost daily now. So Robert agreed to meet at a dive bar in Dallas where no one would pay them any attention. Robert Mugawbe, formerly known as Dr. Robert Mugawbe, was troubled by the message. Jake had questions of a dangerous nature, and he didn’t know why. The thing he described in the voicemail was well known to him, and he had plenty of research material on the subject. However, the reason for inquiry would need to be known. Robert selected the bar. His childhood allowed him to know many of the destitute locations throughout the city, and he didn’t mind returning to them, especially now. Dallas had plenty of hopelessness and danger if a man were looking for it, and the bar was no exception. Originally, the bar had been a small grocery store on the corner of the neighborhood. Hard times and poor workers had seen the store close. A liquor store was burned out, and finally the bar opened. Even at the pristine opening, the floors had been covered with reused carpet from two torn out locations so one side was brown and the other orange. The furniture was all folding tables and folding chairs except for four bar stools that hadn’t been broken or stolen. Robert was waiting at a folding table in the corner, his back not appreciating the warped steel frame. A man of Jake’s size was noticed as he walked into the den. He ignored sideways looks, and proceeded down the darkened walkway to the back where he knew Robert would situate himself. In total, eleven lost souls populated the interior of the wretched hellhole. He took a seat with Robert at the square table. He had no smile on his face. A waitress came over, “What can I get you to drink?” “Beer, bottled. You choose the brand.” Jake said mechanically. The waitress walked off. Robert nursed his own beer, sensing they weren’t going to speak until they were assured of no further interruptions from the waitress. The beer came back, and Jake gave the woman a ten to cover the five dollar beer and the tip. “I need some information.” “Yes, your message spoke of what you needed. Before I give you anything, tell me why you need to know about those creatures.” Robert’s discomfort was obvious. “When he left the Dakotas, they provided him sanctuary. He stayed with them, and possibly left clues there among them that I can use. I need to find them, the clues and of course they won’t want to simply give me the information.” Jake laughed a little. “This isn’t funny.” Robert took another pull off his beer. “Do you know what they are?” “I’ve seen the movies.” “The movies!” he scoffed. “The truth is much more complex and ugly. Look, they have been around since almost the beginning. The history of these beats is dangerous because they value their hidden nature. What I know is because of the sacrifice of many who were able to gather the information. I got it all from Veston from the same network that helped us.” “Tell me.” “Okay, listen up.” Robert leaned forward, “In the bible, there was Adam and Eve. When God banished them for their sins, they had children. Well, Genesis 4 tells us this. Cain talked with Abel his brother: and it came to pass, when they were in the field, that Cain rose up against Abel his brother, and slew him. And the LORD said unto Cain, Where is Abel thy brother” And he said, I know not: Am I my brother's keeper? And He said, What hast thou done? The voice of thy brother's blood crieth unto me from the ground.” “And now art thou cursed from the earth, which hath opened her mouth to receive thy brother's blood from thy hand; When thou tillest the ground, it shall not henceforth yield unto thee her strength; a fugitive and a vagabond shalt thou be in the earth. And Cain said unto the LORD, My punishment is greater than I can bear. Behold, thou hast driven me out this day from the face of the earth; and from thy face shall I be hid; and I shall be a fugitive and a vagabond in the earth; and it shall come to pass, that every one that findeth me shall slay me.” “And the LORD said unto him, Therefore whosoever slayeth Cain, vengeance shall be taken on him sevenfold. And the LORD set a mark upon Cain, lest any finding him should kill him. And Cain went out from the presence of the LORD, and dwelt in the land of Nod, on the east of Eden.” Jake sighed, “I don’t need a bible lesson.” “That’s the beginning. The beast was born of Cain, and his legacy still walks the Earth.” “So Cain was?” Robert nodded, “The first vampire. They say that some have walked the Earth since that day for none would slay Cain. The movies are fantasy. There are men now who battle against them, but we fail because they are something we cannot understand. There are only a few amongst us who are able to fight against them.” “This sounds like a television show I caught a few times with Sarah Michelle Gellar.” Jake smiled. “This isn’t funny.” “It sounds ridiculous.” Robert sighed, “Okay, some men can face them without fear. This isn’t a large group, but also not eternally small. Some stories go so far as to say they control everything, and we are just puppets to their strings. Other legends say they withdrew and live in isolation. There’s all sorts of stories, but we know precious little.” “Crosses, water and that crap doesn’t work. Forget about bad breath through garlic too, because that doesn’t work. You pierce their heart, and they don’t die. If you pierce the heart, they just go down for a little while before they recover. You have to beat them down enough to chop off their head. Some say chop head and limbs to be sure. Sunlight does the trick too, that isn’t a myth. They hate daytime, not just the light, so if you were hunting then hunt during the day. At night they are back at full strength. Then, there’s fire. They don’t like fire either. So if you are thinking about going after them, then this is what you have to expect.” Jake leaned forward onto his hands, “So this isn’t crap.” “No, the older the beast the more powerful they are. Even a newborn beast is stronger than any man.” “Not me.” Robert shook his head, “Their strength is not measured like we measure ours.” “Robert, neither is mine anymore. I added two hundred pounds to my bench press this morning, and I swear I could have done even more if I wanted to. There’s something inside of me changing. I can face them, I can face anything. I feel’this sounds corny. I feel as though my blood flows with grace.” Robert nodded, “It does. The touch that allows you to fight against Sid also allows you to fight against all the evil. You will be able to face them, but we don’t have any idea how well you might fare in battle against them.” “I have a feeling it will be quite good.” “Pride Jake.” Jake shrugged, “Sometimes you have to have it.” Robert was looking past Jake to the door. “We may have a problem.” “What?” “That man you had issues with on television, Reno.” “What about him?” Jake was angry just thinking about him. “He just came in.” Robert pushed across a CD in a jewel case. “There’s information on there you’ll need. I’ll slip out the back. He hasn’t seen you yet, and I can’t let him see me.” “I understand.” Jake pocketed the CD. “I’ll email you after I review everything.” Robert stood, and staying near the wall made his way toward the back. Chuck caught sight of the movement, but dismissed it as the small man was obviously not Jake Hix. He walked forward through the dark bar, and finally saw him leaning over a table. “Jake!” Chuck called out loud. The Big Dawg stood up, and turned to face him. Jake’s scowl told of his displeasure, “Mr. Reno. I’m leaving.” “Not on my account I hope.” Jake ignored the comment as he headed out the front door. Chuck was right behind him, like a dog catching a scent. “Look Jake, you can’t avoid me forever.” Chuck said as they crossed the street toward Jake’s truck. “Why don’t you just sit down and talk about these things with me” Wouldn’t it be easier in the end?” Jake approached his truck. He opened the doors remotely, and as he reached for the door Reno decided to push him a little. “I guess I’ll just have to sit down with Alexandria for an interview.” Jake turned, and almost raised his hand. He wasn’t supposed to get into any further altercations with Reno, but the man was pushing him, baiting him. Jake looked up and down the street, noticing the white van with a camera peering out from an open back door. Reno was trying to trap him once again. Jake refused to fall for the bait. “Reno, you aren’t going to speak to my family because I’m the only member in the public eye. Well, Pitt too, but he’s a businessman now and you aren’t a business reporter. Everyone else ahs moved onto a private life, and any intrusion by you will be met by our lawyers. If you want to sit down with me for an interview, then you need to schedule it with me. I’m not doing this ambush media anymore.” Chuck was frustrated. He was sure he could have gotten Jake with the comment on his sister, “Really, well then let’s schedule an interview.” “I said you could call me. I won’t dignify these tactics by allowing you to schedule an interview now. You won’t be rewarded for this kind of behavior Chuck.” Laughing, Chuck tried to downplay the situation. “Jake, you know how I work. I’m just trying to get my story.” Jake leaned in close, “No, you’re just a puppet and don’t even know it Chuck. So schedule an interview, we’ll set very specific parameters around it and then you can have a talk with me. Oh, do me a favor, and tell my brother I said hello when you talk to him.” Jake opened the door to his truck, started the engine and took off. Chuck returned to the white van where Tony was waiting for him. Chuck was still smiling as he approached. “What was that he said about his brother?” Tony asked. Chuck shook his head at his cameraman, “Come on, where do you think I’ve been getting all of my inside dirt” There’s a family feud, and I get to reap all of the benefits from the infighting. Xavier is going to give me everything I want, and I’ll just keep using it against Jake.” “I was always taught that getting involved in family fights is a bad idea. No matter what happens, you end up losing in the end.” “Yeah, well I was always taught where there’s smoke there’s fire. And I plan on getting my ride back on a permanent basis somewhere. He was here to meet someone, and either we just missed it or ruined the meeting. We’ll get our scoop soon enough.” Tony put away the equipment. He didn’t like this approach, but he was making good money from someone who was making sure Reno was well compensated for his work. He didn’t have many choices thanks t the conviction on his record, and he had kids to take care of. He bit his tongue about what they were doing and finished packing. . . . . . . . Money could accomplish almost anything, especially when the money being discussed was serious. What was once a huge plantation over five hundred acres had been reduced to a small thirty acre plot that hadn’t been sold since hitting the market. The house was in serious need of repair, but it was a home with plenty of history. The opening bid was thought to be a starting point, but little did Donna realize that the owner desperately needed money. They accepted the opening bid, on condition the home and land was being purchased as is so they would make no repairs. The paperwork would be completed in days, but a waiver signed and sealed allowed them to begin making arrangements to move equipment to the property. In finding information, Donna found a pair of Country Coach Motor Homes that she could get at a good price. They would not be permanent investments, and could be turned back around to resale after the house was finished. They might have some trouble on a couple of the roads, but sending a big Ford 350 with them would ensure any problems could be resolved. The charges she was racking up would be impressive, but the money involved was more than cared for via the accounts set-up for the corporation. They would pay full taxes, avoiding nothing to ensure they attracted no undue attentions of the IRS. Donna was on the phone more and more, looking for people she could trust to drive the Motor Homes to the site, finding a contractor to rebuild the dock and an electrician to run power to the Motor Homes. After spending an entire day on the phone, everything was in place. By Saturday they could move to the house, and shipments would be made including building a steel storage building with a small office. The contractor was incented to finish that by Thursday, and the incentive was nice enough to guarantee they wouldn’t miss the target date. Donna’s phone, fax and emails were all burning up. She had a feeling this would be the last support Jake would be asking for. Something in his voice was very final. He didn’t want to put her at risk, but he always forgot that she had volunteered for this duty. Her friends were in danger too after all. Things were moving so quickly she didn’t know if she should be letting all of this happen. Ignoring the doubts, she continued finishing the emails. Everything was lining up as best it could, but she was beginning to feel the strain on her relationship with Arizona. Maybe the sooner this wrapped up the better for her home relationship. The phone rang, and she picked it up. “Hello?” “Donna Martinelli please.” Donna rubber her eyes with her free hand, “This is Donna.” “Donna, this is Al Spencer from Al’s Motor Home and Trailers. My bank just called confirming the transfer of funds into the account. I wanted to let you know. Now, my sales manager was telling me that you were trying to get these delivered to an address in Louisiana, is that right?” Donna nodded to no one as she answered, “Yes, we need them at a property the corporation just purchased.” “Well, considering the price you paid and everything else, I wanted to volunteer to get both vehicles there for you.” Al offered. Donna perked up, “That would be great, but the location is fairly remote. I was trying to arrange a truck to follow them in case anything got stuck on any of the dirt roads. This is in the Louisiana bayou.” “That’s not a problem here ma’am.” Al was laughing. “You might be amazed at some of the places we’ve delivered these to in the past. Heck, once we had to drive all the way up through Canada into Alaska to make the delivery. Of course, the wife and I just made that our vacation trip we were so anxious to see that much of the country and of Canada. That was a crazy trip!” Donna took it all in, and now she could save some money and some trouble. “Yes, I can send you the address via fax. There’s some rather lengthy directions because it’s all back roads and such. I’m sending it from my computer now.” “Great, we’ll head off tomorrow morning, and that should put us there in three days.” “Perfect.” “Okay, we’ll plan on heading out tomorrow. I have your contact information, and if anything happens we’ll contact you from the road.” “Okay, have a good night.” Donna was relaxing a bit. “You too, bye.” Donna hung up the phone. Another item came off her checklist. Everything was getting ready for the arrival of the rest of the family from exile. They would have a home once again. Donna would too, because her life with Arizona would be back to normal for another time. . . . . . . . Far from being a dive bar, the location for this meeting was amazing. The restaurant was nice, with plenty of background noise to mask their conversation. He had never actually been, but there were plenty of them along his travel routes. The outside was simple, with the architecture and stucco walls matching the rest of the buildings in the up scale area. When Chuck Reno walked in, he was put at ease by the gentle interior. Soft, subdued contemporary lighting and hand painted fabrics framed the entire lush restaurant along with the granite entryway. The main floor of the restaurant featured limestone floors and decorative columns under the round shaped lighting fixtures. The meeting had been set for the bar because foot traffic there was heavy with all sorts of nook and crannies to hide. Chuck looked around, and saw his appointment leaning against one of the walls with drink in hand. The dark suit fit him well. Chuck Reno took a breath for courage, and headed over to the bar. The bartender came over, “What can I get for you?” “Vodka rocks.” Quickly, he passed the drink to Chuck who returned a ten-dollar bill. He took the walk over to where he was standing. “I saw him tonight.” “Really?” Sid asked, although as far as Chuck knew he was simply Xavier Black. The fool had no sense of the immense power standing before him, but Chuck was like the rest of the herd of humanity. They desperately needed to be culled. Chuck took a sip of the vodka. He leaned in, “He was meeting someone at this terrible bar. I never saw who it was, but the entire thing seemed strange. I mean, you know your own brother obviously, and there’s no way he would be in this type of bar.” “I see.” He took a sip of his drink. “What happened?” “I tried to bait him again, but he didn’t fall for it. He restrained himself, but with a guy like that it’s just a matter of time before he goes off the deep end. I threatened him with some information about the family, and he knew I was working with you.” “Did he?” He smiled at the reporter. “Yeah, he said “tell my brother I said hi” and stuff. He’s agreed to do a sit down interview with me. I can rake him over the coals when I do it.” Sid thought about the idea for a moment, “No.” “No?” Chuck didn’t understand the sudden change of heart. “Look, I don’t know what went down between the two of you, but I’m going to have that arrogant prick between my crosshairs.” “Yes, and if you squeeze the trigger you’ll only give him a flesh wound, and he’ll never let you get him between his crosshairs again. I want the interview to be strictly about the league. No cheap shots, and he’ll wonder why you backed off. He may even think you decided to back off because I am no longer helping you. Once that happens, later on you can deliver a much more devastating blow.” “That’s smart.” Chuck was all smiles again. “You Hix boys always have the angles. That’s like, keep your friends close and your enemies closer.” Sid had to keep himself from laughing at the flesh bag before him, “Something like that. Just take it easy, keep tabs on him and eventually you and I will speak to each other once again soon. I’ll contact you as I always do to arrange the next meeting.” “Okay, so do you want me to start being actually friendly to Jake?” Sid thought about it, “Just turn on that good old Reno charm and let it take you where it will.” Chuck nodded enthusiastically; horribly unaware of the slight he had just been given. “I’ve got other meetings to attend to. Until we meet again.” “Sure.” Sid Hix walked out of the bar. Unnoticed was the departure of a man and a woman, dressed in black suits who followed him out. Sid was aware his acolytes followed him, so spoken for his own protection. Really, they were meant to ensure no worldly authorities got in his way. Reno didn’t even take note as he began flirting with a woman young enough to be his daughter. That too would go nowhere. . . . . . . . Jake Hix was leaning back in the plush chair, reading the paper and sipping a cup of strong coffee. Caf? Brazil was busy on this morning, but even with the nice morning not too many people had decided to enjoy the early morning breeze on the patio. The sound of someone sitting down across from him was easy to hear, but he didn’t lower the paper. Instead, he spoke right through it, “I hear the winters are harsh in San Antonio.” “Only if one hundred degrees is considered harsh.” Code words seemed so ridiculous, but they were necessary in the age of digital communication when someone could tap into anything. Meeting these men simply meant sending a message on a public bulletin board and getting a reply with a time and place. The pass codes were to be known already, and anyone who didn’t know them would be ignored. Jake lowered the paper, and looked the new arrival over. He was simply the messenger, just a young man who was along enough in the game to be neither worried nor arrogant. He was simply cool in his casual appearance and manner. “I am here to discuss your needs.” “Did you want coffee?” “Cappuccino.” Jake motioned his waitress over, “Darling, could you get my friend here a cappuccino?” “Sure thing.” The young waitress with all the piercings disappeared to get the morning cup of caffeine for his guest. “They know me here.” Jake explained, “Now, as you were saying.” “I am here to get your requirements. As always the price will be sent to your email address in the header using the alphabetic code.” He explained. “Of course, after she brings your drink to ensure we aren’t interrupted.” Jake returned to his paper, speaking through it again, “Lovely day, isn’t it?” “Yeah, sure.” The man was put off a little by the man’s cavalier attitude. “Gonna be a hot ass summer though.” “Quite. We’ve already broken records after all, so the summer will be the dog days once again. Last few years the rain has saved us, but Tlaloc doesn’t like us enough this year to be so kind.” “Who?” “An old God of the Aztecs. No matter really?” The cappuccino came, and they were alone. “What do you need?” He asked before sipping the hot beverage. He blew on it, trying to cool it off as he continued sipping. Jake put the paper aside. He spoke more quietly to ensure no one would overhear, “I need some strange items this time. I need two automatics, untraceable and clean. I need a machete, something special though. Tell them just what I needed last time from a hand weapon like that. He’ll know what I mean.” “What else?” “Three extra magazines each. Ammunition, a set of seven inch throwing knives and a something with fire will also be needed.” “Fire?” That one caught him off guard. “Yes.” He smiled. “Something safe, hand used and powerful. Tell him I want to burn someone hard, and maybe even some new kind of Molotov cocktail’something I can carry on my person without too much fear. He’s smart, he’ll come up with something for me like he always does.” “Fine, when do you need this by?” “This coming weekend. The machete is the only difficult time, well, and maybe the fire. Tell him I understand the timing, and will pay for it.” The young man nodded, “Understood. I’ll finish my cappuccino then head out to begin the order.” Jake picked his paper back up, and began reading once again.
