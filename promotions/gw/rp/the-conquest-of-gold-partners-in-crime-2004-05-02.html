The Conquest of Gold: Partners In Crime

2 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5-02-04 Insight: You could never tell what the world was like inside here… This rotten filthy cell was the undermine project of the government to keep me locked away until my hopes and dreams would fade away, leading me to an early grave. Yet those people didn’t know me like this one person did. He was a younger kid, maybe twenty or so, and he had a mind that would rage against any machine. Whether it was a monster like Ice or a bullet like Deacon Holmes, this kid had guts, and he knew me like a brother. Inside this cell I wait for him to arrive and as I do I hear the scratching of the nails from a fellow inmate on the other side of the wall. He’s gone insane from what I can tell letting his mind ease from reality and his deepest well hidden notions were coming alive in front of his eyes. He could see the answers to everything he had asked god… Sure it was peaceful but when you looked at the man you wondered what could drive a man to such lengths? Well I was sitting in it… Yes, prison… more preferably, the cell you slept in for 365 days a year. You’re eyes would just wake up one morning telling you that life was better and you’d begin to see the holy lands, but then it all disappeared when they came for you. That’s why I chose to think of wrestling, I chose to believe in things, and why I never lingered around when I was in the cell. Boredom was a main reason of insanity, for without the occupying of oneself, the mind will become tired of it’s surroundings and begin to make up it’s own world. Here they came. The troops had come to kill off another one of their experiments gone retarded. I wasn’t surprised, everything in this place lost something before it walked out the front gate into the world we called Freedom. That brings me to another question, what is freedom? What is freedom? We call freedom the ability to live in this world, but what is free? The streets are patrolled with police who are crooked, your neighbor is either the good ole’ old man next store or a rapist, and you can’t go outside without knowing if a bullet will go through your head because of some nut job on the loose. What really is freedom? To me, the freedom I wish to have is to be worried free. I live in a world where the respect I get as a wrestler depends on how insane I can get in the wrestling ring. I just wish that the things I have to worry about are those that aren’t on top of my mind twenty-four-seven, like my girlfriend’s death. People don’t understand how a man like me deals with these. The thing is I don’t… I’ve bottled it all up inside of me, and I’ll probably break come time to wrestle someone I hate. It’s wrong, I know that, but isn’t it wrong to call someone a bad name when their skin color is different, and isn’t it wrong when a baby is killed by abortion, what about when a seven year old child kills her father by cutting his head off when he’s asleep with a chainsaw? Aren’t these things wrong? But when you think of today’s world, my measure of self-wrong is way less important than that of those people who commit those acts… Just think about that. Story Begins: The gold that was around my waist began to feel better and better every time I wore it around my waist. Every week when I went out to that ring and strapped the title around my waist, it felt like heaven… And that’s what kept me alive in this hell hole of a place. I’d been sitting here all day, the news that the guy next to me dying was the only piece of gratification I had to entertainment. I sat their on my cotton bed somewhat hoping that Monday would arrive quicker. Sure it meant going to Global Wrestling where the man who killed my girlfriend was, but in the same aspect of that it was a place I called… home. Staring at the floor I began to notice the pattern this life had on me. I’d go to Evolution on Monday and wrestle, then on Tuesday I’d be licking my wounds right here in this cell. I had a privileged position in this cell, I’d been given the right to do something others WISHED they could do. Sure I valued it, but this was of course life, I’d been through worse. Suddenly my cell door opened up and I looked up. The officer stared at me with a mild look of undeniable hatred, before throwing my stuff on the floor and telling me I was free. I stared at him with a blank expression of gratitude, then smiling I rubbed it in. This man had despised me for what I’d done, hell anyone who was in here he despised. But the bottom line was, now he had to live up the facts, he was letting a criminal go. I grabbed my clothes, smirking as I stood up and held them by my side. I looked at him and he gestured to follow him, for his mouth seemed to be quite dry at the time and his forehead was sweating bullets. Walking down the steel steps, one level at a time, I thought this was a dream. The truth was beginning to set in, I’d been let free, but the question still remained, who paid my bail? It was a $5,000 bail and nobody from my work, family, or any of my friends could do it, as the stipulation was put on me due to the happenings when I was charged for the assault. I walked with power in my legs, for I knew I’d be leaving this hellhole, but before I did I stopped at the cell where Richard Lorenzo stood near the bars. He had a smile on his face, one that showed he was happy for me, but still regretted the fact that he’d be still be here tomorrow and I wouldn’t. ’Looks like you’re getting out eh?’ I smirked as he said the words. Richard was a great guy, never gave me any problems back in Louisiana, and watched my back for me. ’Yeah I guess so… How long before you get out?’ Richard turned his back to me as he went to his playboy calendar. He flipped through the pages and stared at the month of December for a moment glancing up and down the rows of weeks. ’December 15, 2005.’ I knew it was a long time, definitely too long to make any promises, but in my heart I knew something I didn’t want to believe. I looked at Richard, taking a mental picture of his face. He was much older looking for his age, being forty he looked like he was in his mid-fifties. I stared at him wondering whether I should say anything, but my tongue was tied down when those words I wanted to speak tried to form. ’I’ll be waiting for you then, take care.’ I stared at Rich for one last moment. The guard was impatient but he couldn’t do anything about it because he knew I was much larger than him and without my cuffs on I could retaliate whether or not he thought I would. ’Later Jess… Be good now, I don’t want to see you in here again.’ I turned and nodded an ‘okay’ type gesture in response to his words before following the guard down the steel steps to the first floor. Looking up I saw how far I’d seen what exactly was home before I was told I was free. It seemed so distant now, the top seemed like it was miles away, but on the way down it felt like it was a mere ten feet I walked. The guard pointed to a door, I went in and changed into my normal clothes. I had a pair of black jeans, a black T-shirt with my logo on it, and a pair of tinted orange sunglasses that had silver sidings. I wasn’t a rich guy so when I wore good clothes it wasn’t common, and I guess this showed that, for the jeans had a tear in the knee. Looking down at the cement floor I spit out the crap I had in my mouth that inmates called, ‘prison breathe’ for no matter who you met their breathe smelled like crap. I took the toothbrush and spread the toothpaste on the tip, it was the most refreshing taste my mouth had ever had. I breathed a deep sigh as I spit the toothpaste in the sink and brushed my hair back as I gargled. How odd that the littlest things had such an impact on me I thought. I’d never have thought toothpaste taste so good, but when I spit the toothpaste out it was like I just had sex with a real nice woman, it was a refreshing tint of mint mixed in with a whole lot of chocolate. Gazing into the mirror I let the water run down into my cupped hands before I splashed it on my face. The water wasn’t great, but it felt good on a dirty face as this one. Wiping my face clean from all the disgust this prison had placed on it, I walked out the door to where the guard stood. Following him I made it obvious I was content, I wanted this jerk of a guard to realize if he was nicer he’d not get so much trouble from the inmates when he checked the cells. The buzzard rang three times and the doors opened, I was free. I walked out between the doors and down a narrow strip of passage that was covered in gravel. At the end was one last gate with a fence for a door. The guards had already begun to open the fence door as I walked nearer. Those steps to me felt like freedom. I know I explained what freedom was earlier, but boy did those steps feel like freedom. Each one I took was a joy, for I knew at the end of the path I walked I would be away from this and have my rights of self-control again. Final step… ah it was freedom! The air brushed up the side of me, fresh goddamn air… I hadn’t felt it in so long. Being at a wrestling event such as Evolution, I didn’t ever get a chance to go outside… But now, ah the air, it’s a woman’s touch to my face. There was the car, in front of me, in the seat, my tag team partner for the night, Max Roswell. Oh what mischief we had planned, and yet nobody would know because they underestimated two men, one who some supposed to be a teacher, and the other who was the rookie in their eyes… Maybe it was common sense, maybe not, but there would be hell to pay once the team got in the ring. In the back of my mind, I knew we didn’t have a chance, but I was sure that we’d show we weren’t second-rate wrestlers on the Global Wrestling roster. (Tune into Evolution to find out the Conclusion)
