Forever and ever, Amen?

8 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/8/2005 You may think that I'm talking foolish, You've heard that I'm wild and I'm free... You may wonder how, I can promise you now, This love that I feel for you always will be. GW Media Commissary, Las Vegas, NV June 7, 2005; 1426 hours Noi Tsunami was nothing if not persistent, thought Teresa to herself as she and Juan waited for her to arrive at the GW Media Commissary. They were in the recording/broadcasting room for GW's radio station this time, to give an interview over the radio waves for fans to hear live. There would be no fan call-ins this time; a bit different for GW radio segments, but the regular show host had to give an extended commentary on GW's performance in the recent PTC inter-fed CW2 Tournament. Juan sipped a bottle of apple juice nonchalantly as he glanced out of the radio booth's window into the office part of the station. El Serpenti and Melina sat at an untenanted desk, playing a game of gin rummy. They would be able to hear the interview, and Serpenti could translate the majority of it into Spanish for Melina. Her lessons were coming along nicely, but learning a second language was not going to be done in a short time. It had taken her family quite a few months before their English was good enough to carry on a street conversation; longer before they could follow along on a news broadcast that wasn't subtitled. Noi arrived at the radio booth a few minutes later, on time. She smiled politely in greeting as she took her notepad out of her purse and put her headset on. The reporter knew how to work the radio controls, so she was going to be running the booth herself. She flipped a few switches as the console lit up with a sequence of lights and computer readings. Juan and Teresa donned their headsets as well as the countdown light flashed. Three... two... one... mark. 'Good afternoon, and welcome to the GW afternoon radio segment!' said Noi into her microphone in greeting. 'I'm Noi Tsunami here at the GW Media Commissary in Las Vegas, Nevada. Once again, I'm privileged to bring you Juan and Teresa Ramirez, La Herencia de Herederos, for another interview. Thank you for coming, Juan and Teresa... the fans are really getting into your story, and how you came to start the mission you're carrying on here in GW after having spent time in Mexico City's CMLL and the indy Lucha Libre leagues.' Well, you're not just time that I'm killing; I'm no longer one of those guys. As sure as I live, this love that I give Is gonna be yours until the day that I die! Juan nodded politely. 'Thank you for having us,' he said. 'It's good to know that the fans are getting behind us and our back-story... supporting our pursuit of the legacy built by my father-in-law.' 'What's made your story so interesting, Juan,' said Noi, 'is how devoted you've been to this cause, that's tied much more closely to Teresa than it is to you. Please don't take offense, but it's seen as something of a novelty here, and in this day and age where spouses are changed like socks and the family unit is far more subject to rapid change than ever before.' Juan and Teresa glanced at each other for several long seconds; the looks in their eyes, and how they held their mouths, showing a mixture of emotions. Noi shifted a little uneasily. Damn that Serpenti, she thought to herself; It was his idea that Teresa started wearing a mask too. Don't they know that a picture-- or a facial expression-- is worth a thousand words? Maybe they did know; maybe not... if they did, they didn't seem to care. Juan finally turned back to Noi and spoke curtly. 'New isn't always better,' said Juan, 'and change isn't always RIGHT. The way family USED to be here in America... there was nothing wrong with it. Society tried to fix what wasn't broken... and made a mess out of it. Pledging myself to my wife's cause is what a husband does... or SHOULD do. All those husbands out there who think of that as a novelty-- I hope you enjoy sleeping on the living room couch at night.' Teresa snickered briefly before she regained control of herself. Her brown eyes twinkled with amusement through the up-slanted eyeholes in her royal blue mask. The slanted eye cut-outs were meant to give her a somewhat more exotic look. Exotic or not, it made digging for information more of a task for Noi. 'Tell all of us about your wedding,' said Noi suddenly. 'Was it a big affair?' Silence. Juan and Teresa glanced at each other again; clearly debating among each other whether to grant the request. Oh baby, I'm gonna love you forever... forever and ever, amen! As long as old men sit and talk about the weather, As long as old women sit and talk about old men! Noi noted this, but stayed firm behind her question. Even if the Ramirez spouses didn't answer, she couldn't wasn't going to hold back from anything that might be 'a bit too personal.' Fans wanted to know these little things... and it was the wrestlers who were willing to open up about it that got the most audience approval. 'No,' said Teresa. 'Maybe thirty people were at the wedding, excluding Juan and myself, of course. There was the Priest who married us... my grandmother... my husband's parents and his brother... some of his co-workers... what few friends my sister and I had. My sister was the maid of honor, of course.' Noi turned to Juan. 'And your best man?' she asked. 'My employer at the restaurant,' said Juan. 'No uncles or aunts of yours?' Noi asked Teresa. Teresa shook her head. 'My sister and I were not on bad terms with our mother's brother or sister,' she said, 'but they were not invited to the wedding. I knew that they would take pictures and show them to Mama... and my husband and I didn't want that. Papa had no siblings. Our grandmother was invited because she agreed not to speak to Mama about it.' 'I'm sure it must have been one of the happiest days of your lives,' said Noi. 'Tell us a little about it?' Juan looked at Teresa and shrugged, and Teresa nodded, even if a bit reticently. If you wonder how long I'll be faithful, I'll be happy to tell you again. I'm gonna love you, forever and ever, Forever and ever, amen. Iglesia de la Santa Cruz, Mexico City 1000 hours Esteban Campos, owner and manager of the restaurant where Teresa's fianc? worked, walked proudly down the aisle of the church side by side with Teresa's sister. Esteban's dark brown suit, while not an extravagant affair, was freshly pressed with crisp creases on the trousers. A small white flower was tucked into his lapel beside his burgundy tie. Teresa's sister wore an elegant, light pink gown with a fluffed, short-sleeve bodice, ruffled down the front coming to a pink bow cinched around her waist. The chiffon swing-skirt had two tiers, barely clearing the floor. A wide-brimmed white bridesmaid's hat rested on her head, adorned with dark pink carnations that matched her bouquet. She wore basic white stockings and black pumps designed for podiatric comfort. Despite the wedding being a small affair, with only a couple of dozen attendees, she would likely dance nearly all night with a number of them, at the reception to be held at the restaurant where the groom worked. The best man and the maid of honor split just before the altar and walked to opposite sides of it. They turned and smiled down the aisle as the groom made his entrance. Teresa's husband-to-be was the epitome of masculine comeliness. His hair was cut short, and his face smoothly shaven. His tuxedo was inky black, with a crisp white shirt. Fastened to the front of the shirt through the buttons, under the jacket, was a white strip of ruffled material edged in dark purple. Black tasseled shoes and black knee-length socks shod his legs and feet. A fresh white boutonni’re was tucked into the buttonhole on his lapel. He walked with a brisk, confident stride to his spot before the altar; his heart near-bursting with pride and joy. They say that time takes its toll on the body, Makes a young girl's brown hair turn gray. But honey, I don't care-- I ain't in love with your hair, And if it all fell out, well, I'd love you anyway. Everyone stood as the bride made her entrance. Teresa's long hair was piled loosely atop her head, fastened in place with a few strategically-arranged bobby pins that also kept her headdress in place. White roses adorned the headdress; matching her bridal bouquet. Teresa's wedding gown had a silk bodice adorned with long lace sleeves gathered at the wrists. The shoulders were puffed to enhance the appearance, and a criss-cross lace tie along the front carefully hid cleavage while still looking beautiful. The wide white skirt was fluffed full with a chiffon petticoat; the three tiers falling in soft waves down to the hem that brushed the floor as she walked. Teresa's stockings were spun from white silk; held up by a white lace garter belt that hugged her slim waist. Padded insoles hidden in her black pumps provided comfort and support for her feet. Once the reception started, it wasn't likely she'd be doing any sitting, except for the wedding meal. Teresa was being given away by her fianc?'s father. The genial gray-haired man walked proudly beside his soon-to-be daughter-in-law, arm in arm with the bride. He walked Teresa up to the altar, where she joined her husband-to-be. Bride and groom kneeled in front of the altar as the groom's father took his place beside the other groomsmen. The Priest gave the standard preamble in a resonant tenor voice. He spoke of the blessing that is the marriage bond, and its sanctity before God. Teresa smiled joyously as she lightly squeezed her fianc?'s hand. When the Priest asked if any could show just cause why bride and groom should not be joined, Teresa gave a barely audible snicker of mischief. 'Come out, Mama, wherever you are hiding! That's your cue!' Everyone laughed good-naturedly, though a few roving eyes looked around the church to see if Lupe would in fact step out into the open. When she did not, the Priest had the bride and groom recite the traditional vows. The wedding rings were donned as the vows were spoken, and then the Priest pronounced them man and wife. The bridesmaids, groomsmen, and twenty other guests all gave a standing ovation as Teresa and her new husband shared their first marital kiss, and then Teresa was picked up and carried out of the Church as everyone threw rice. They say time can play tricks on a memory, Make people forget things they knew... Well it's easy to see, it's happenin' to me! I've already forgotten every woman but you! 'It sounds like it was a beautiful ceremony,' said Noi, smiling at Juan and Teresa. 'Small, but beautiful... and still very much like a fairy-tale wedding.' Teresa smiled wistfully. 'In many ways, yes I'd have to say it was,' she said. 'The wedding dinner was wonderful... having been prepared by my husband and his co-workers at the restaurant, including Esteban. We all danced for hours, to a small salsa band that played in the neighborhood.' 'Who caught the wedding bouquet?' asked Noi with a mischievous grin. Juan laughed. 'Teresa cheated,' he said. 'I did not!' protested Teresa. 'Yes, you did,' said Juan. 'You threw the bouquet right at your sister.' 'I threw it straight ahead, she just happened to be there!' 'Uh-huh, a likely story,' grinned Juan, and both he and Teresa laughed as Teresa batted a hand playfully at the side of Juan's head. Noi giggled lightly. 'One more question,' she said, looking at Juan. 'How many years have you been married? When's your anniversary?' Oh darlin', I'm gonna love you forever, forever and ever, amen! As long as old men sit and talk about the weather, As long as old women sit and talk about old men! Noi glanced quickly down at her notepad to position her pencil, and then glanced back up at Juan. Her brow furrowed. Juan hesitated in his answer, and it came with a slight bit of a stammer. 'Tw-Twelve years,' he said. 'We were married on M-March 14, 1993,' he said. 'On-one year to the day after the engagement.' Teresa slowly did a double-take, gaping wide at Juan. Teresa's mask didn't cover her lower face and mouth, and there was no mistaking the way her jaw dropped. Juan's lips pressed tight as a tinge of warm blood crept into his face. His jaw line showed the blush even through his suntanned skin and ever-present 5 o'clock shadow, making the uncovered part of his face nearly match his red mask. 'That sounds delightfully romantic,' said Noi suddenly. 'Thank you for the beautiful story, Juan and Teresa... So, you're facing The Enforcers this Friday at Evolution, a rematch from the three-way last show. Feel ready to take them on again?' 'We're ready,' said Teresa curtly, glancing slightly at Juan as he nodded in an affirmative. But it was all they said. Noi nodded and gave a polite smile of acknowledgement. 'Okay, folks, you've been listening to the GW afternoon radio segment. I'm Noi Tsunami, with Juan and Teresa Ramirez, aka La Herencia de Herederos, at the GW Media Commissary in Las Vegas, Nevada. Be sure to tune in to Evolution on Friday, June 10th, where La Herencia de Herederos takes on The Enforcers in tag-team action! Can Herencia repeat from last week? We'll find out on Evolution!' If you wonder how long I'll be faithful, Just listen to how this song ends... I'm gonna love you, forever and ever, Forever and ever, amen! Juan and Teresa nodded briefly; almost curtly Noi as they doffed their headsets. Not a word was spoken as the husband-and-wife Luchadores exited the booth and closed the door behind them. Noi happened to glance out the glass observation window of the radio booth in time to see Juan reach a tentative hand out to rest it on Teresa's shoulder... and she watched open-mouthed as Teresa quickly pushed Juan's hand away, shaking her head in an angry negative as she went to give Melina a hug. Noi quietly wrote something down on a piece of paper and stepped out of the booth, discreetly handing the paper to Juan. 'Jennifer Leather,' she whispered to him. 'You might want to invest in a nice comfortable couch...' I'm gonna love you forever and ever, forever and ever, forever and ever! Forever and ever, amen. Forever and ever, Amen, Randy Travis, ? 1987
