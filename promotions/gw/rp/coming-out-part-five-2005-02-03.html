Coming Out (Part Five)

3 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02.03.05 'When are you moving out?' came the grim icy tone of William Wright, the six and a half foot, red-headed step-father of the seventeen year-old Ken Partridge, Jr. 'What?' Ken questioned in disbelief. 'I said,' came William, again, 'when in the hell are you moving your queer ass out of my house?' 'You are NOT kicking him out for this,' vocalized Connie, Ken's mother. 'Damned if I'm not,' he retorted. 'I'm not living in the same house with some faggot. He can take his shit-packed ass out from under my roof and go be gay with one of his fruity little boyfriends.' 'I'm not gay, you ignorant son of a bitch,' Ken shot out. 'I'm bisexual. There's a difference.' 'If you're not straight,' yelled the towering red-head, 'you're gay.' ---- 'Wow,' expressed Kelli Alberdeane. 'So immediatly after you told him about the investigation and about your sexuality he threated to kick you out?' 'Not threatened,' said the current KPJ to his publicist, 'did. You see, William wasn't brought up like my mother and myself. He was... is a redneck, in all senses of the word. My mom was different... it was easier telling her. When she was going to high school in Alabama, she was getting death threats and rocks thrown through her windows at home because she had befriended the first and only black girl to go to school there. She was past discrimination due to race, religion, creed, or sexuality.' 'So,' said Alberdeane, quickly swallowing some of her decaf skinny sugar free almond latte, 'that's good to hear. Your own mother had dealt with similar issues before your's came up.' 'You could say that,' he responded. 'You could also say that my aunt Poula and her best-friend, Terry, are gay. That helped some also.' 'Well, what happened next?' ---- 'Mom,' Ken yelled at his mother, locked in her bedroom. 'Please, come out. I'm leaving. I'm not going to let it come down to this. I may be seventeen, but I know how to be an adult. I talked to Nanny Faye and yesterday and she said that there was no problem with me moving into the basement apartment at their house now instead of the summer. I'm going to head on over there tonight, and I'll come back tomorrow while William's at work to get my stuff.' 'This isn't right,' sobbed his mother, coming out of the bedroom alone to find that her husband was still gone. He left, saying he'd be back later that night when 'the faggot' was gone. 'I won't let it happen.' 'Mom,' he began as he hugged her, tears streaming from his own eyes as well, 'this could have been so much worse. I mean, we didn't even get into a physical fight this time. He'll come around. Hell, if he doesn't, you'll still be my mom. Besides, I'll be heading to Kentucky in September, anyways.' 'This just isn't fair to put on any seventeen year-old, Kenny,' she cried. 'I know how strong you are. I know that you've been through far more than people three times your age. But, now you're having to deal with this AND that damn investigation and that's not fair.' Ken looked over and hugged his mother again. This woman had loved him when many wouldn't. She had loved him through drug abuse and through rehabilitation. He had come through detox alive only because of this unbelievably strong woman that he called mother. 'God puts nothing on us that we can't handle,' he said. 'He puts us through the hard times to prepare us for the worse times and make the better ones seem even sweeter. I'll come out of this okay... and you will, too. You'll see.' ---- 'That bond between you and your mother... it seems so strong,' said Kelli. 'It is,' retorted Ken. 'She's the strongest person I know. I know of few who could have survived what she has. Sever back and heart problems coupled in with me as a child and the fact that my dad skipped on us when I was six... I'd say that she had to be pretty damn strong.' They were both interrupted by Ken's cell phone. He looked down only to see an unfamiliar number. 'Kelli,' he said looking up, 'please excuse me. This may be urgent.' She nodded in compliance as Ken slid his phone open and stood, heading outside simutaineously. 'This is Ken,' he greeted. 'Did you get my letter?' beckoned the voice on the other end. 'Who is this?' he asked curiously. 'Ken,' replied the voice with a sense of dire in it's tone, 'don't you know... it's Antonique.' Ken quickly slid the phone shut as a look of shock embarked across his face. He quickly sat back at his table with Ms. Alberdeane, but his phone immediatly began ringing again. 'Ms. Alberdeane,' Ken rushed, 'I really must apologize, but I have to go.' He picked up his bottle of water before she had the opportunity to reply and made a bee-line for the door, pulling his phone back to his ear in the process. Just as he was exiting, he shouted without turning around, 'I'll call you tomorrow so we can finish.' Kelli watched as the door shut behind him and took another sip of her latte and hit the stop button on her voice recorder. 'Damn,' she whispered, 'why are all the good one's taken?'
