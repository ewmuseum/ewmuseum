The Temple of the Bushasei

9 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 9, 2004 **The trouble with our times is that the future is not what it used to be. The great dream was once a clear cut piece of fantasy. Settlement, financial success, and a loving surrounding was once the dream of every man. However, in these turbulant times of fire, hate and greed, all one has to look forward to is a constant stream of repetition. A constant stream of repetition followed by death. A slow agonizing plunge into the dark beyond, where the other side holds even less promise than life.** **What do we have to look forward to in these times? What awaits us at the end of the track? Is our civilization just going through times of dispair with only sinful, murderous icons as the only heroes we have to look up to? Will these times cease and give way to idealogies of peace and equality, or are we all doomed to an eternity of inner-rage and self loathing? I choose the latter.** - THE TEMPLE OF THE BUDHASEI: Hiroshima, Japan - Master Horishu: So much anger within. Where does it come from? Why does it exist Takahashi? Why do you allow this evil force to reside within you? Takahashi Jetsui: Evil? How can what I have in my heart be evil? How can something that motivates me so greatly be so evil? If it weren't for this 'evil' that you speak of, there would be no Takahashi Jetsui to speak of. Master Horishu: Why must you take this inner evil as an attribute, Takahashi? You and I both know that peace within is the true path every man should take. Takahashi: Peaceful men are the sloths of this world, you old fool. What do you get from peace? Nothing. What do you get from metitation? Nothing. Where does inner peace get you? Nowhere. In this world, in these times the 'good guy' is the man that takes whatever comes to him. The righteous man is the man who takes the fall and the defiant man is the one who exceeds the limitations to achieve glory. If a man takes the path of the honest, all he will find is failure. If a man takes the hard fought route, all he will get is bumps. I choose the path that the majority of our world has already chosen, kill them all, let the others sort them out. Master Horishu: The easy way out. Takahashi: Call it what you want. Only a fool would attempt to take the hard way out. Master Horishu: Fools are men who let their vision be blinded with rage. A fool lets his past blind the way to his future. There is more to your anger than this hatred for the world you speak of with every vowel that comes from your lips. Come, meditate, we will work on what is plaguing you my son. .: Takahashi hesitates for a moment, and begrudingly pulls up a seat next to Master Horishu. The two men sit cros--legged in front of an assortment or Japanese statues, candles, inscents and portraits. In their spot of meditation, they are also surrounded by numerous plants to mohogomize the environment. Takahashi drifts into past memories... :. - HIROSHIMA: MARCH 1981 - THE JETSUI TEMPLE - We see a shot of a Japanese family gathered around a table eating some sushi. There is an older woman, an older man, and a female child. The family is eating in silence until a loud crash is heard and several men come crashing through the temple walls. Six men rush the family that is eating around the table and begin to get hostile. Off to the left we a quick flash of a blade, followed be a splatter of blood. A blood curdling scream is then let out by the older woman, who is then thrown to the floor and is surrounded by four of the masked men, when they then begin swinging their weapons. A stream of blood begins to poor from the center of masked men, and surrounding their feet as the older man from the family watches the execution. The older man is then let go of, and we see the men in the masks begin yelling at the man, who is on his knees in a pool of blood. After some words are screamed at the man, the men in the masks group around him and repeat the swinging of the swords we had seen just a few moments prior. As the men are swinging, the view begins to get hazy... - BACK AT THE BUDHASEI TEMPLE - Takahashi: Argh! .: Takahashi quickly stands up from his sitting position and proceeds to grab the first object he can find, a flower pot with a green furn in it, and throws it into the shrine that Takahashi and Master Horishu had been meditating in front of moments before... :. CRASH!! .: The flower pot hits the various Japanese statues on the shelf and smashes much of the shrine. Takahashi then turns to Master Horishu:. Takahashi: This is nonsense! What am I going to gain from this? Huh? You're a fool, death is the only way out of this torturous mind-set. If I am to be relieved of these memories from the past, I must die, and if I must die, others will join me. I'm sick of this place, I'm sick of this life, and I'm sick of you Horishu. .: Takahashi grabs Master Horishu by his Kimono and brings him to his feet. Takahashi then throws the elderly master into a rack of swords on the other side of the room:. CRASH! Takahashi: I'll spare you this time old man, but if you try anymore of your spiritual jibberish on me, you aren't going to live to say another prayer for us 'unfortunate souls.' I'm going back to America. I'm going back to a place where my actions will be praised, as opposed to repressed. I don't know what made me come back here, but I'm leaving once again. I am going back to resume what I had already started. .: Takahashi and the old man meet eye to eye, before Takahashi proceeds to the door of the temple, and breaking the sliding door off the hinges in the process:. **Men fear death, as children fear to go in the dark; and as the fear of the dark is increased with tall tales, so is men's fear of death**
