Lip Service

9 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/9/2005 Part 1- Unexpected Visit Las Vegas, Nevada, Virgil's penthouse The last rays of the setting sun slid helplessly over the horizon outside of Las Vegas, somewhere in the desert from the view of Virgil's penthouse, and his eyes slowly opened. His sleep had been fitful as ever, a consequence, he supposed, of having just barely missed Lia last week. During the week, it was less than likely that she would even be within the city, and judging by how much less he was affected during those times with visions and nightmares, this was at least slightly correct. It frustrated him immensely that he was having to wait so long to actually see her... it should have been easy enough to find her backstage at Evolution, but he had not been so fortunate. He had been exceedingly frustrated and irritable, more so than usual, this week, and it was keeping him from getting any kind of work accomplished, at least, as far as his 'business' was concerned. Unlike most of his mortal competition, he had no need to train to keep his body in shape. Simple practice of his tae kwon do movements and conjuring a simple illusion to perfect his submission technique was all he really needed to remain in condition to wrestle. This was fortunate, as unlife had become much more complicated. Leinad Soma was a puzzle to him. He seemed so much like one of the Followers of Set, the useless snakes who wasted all of their time corrupting nothing of importance, but there was something underneath the facade, a horrible power that caused even Virgil a slight twinge of nervousness. There was something about him that seemed slightly more monstrous than Sasha Vykos, more sinister than Hisha Ruhadze, an underlying power that seemed to be very un-Kindred like. At the same time it set Virgil on the defensive, he had to admit, it was... curious. He had left Soma on the premise of giving him a decision at a later time, but his mind was now made up, and he would be joining them. Soma had used the most powerful bargaining chip he could have mustered; that being, of course, Lia, the reason he was here in the first place. Unfortunately, he realized Soma was completely conscious of the power he currently held over Virgil. This could be negated to a more manageable level upon actually meeting her, of course. But Virgil was not quite so negative, perhaps Rex Deus would be helpful in some way. After all, Soma was far too powerful to bother with weaklings, be they human, Kindred, or whatever he was, and allies were always useful. More importantly, though, his long search would be at an end. He climbed out of bed, remembering that one of his ghoul associates would be dropping in on him, more than likely telling him something of his dealings with the Cathayans in Tibet... something he had been unfortunately neglecting as of late. Yet another thing to make Virgil mildly uncomfortable. The last thing he wanted was a brood of Mandarins breathing down his neck about a late arms shipment. Even for a vampire of Virgil's age and power, there were things to fear, and things to respect. He respected Soma, but he feared the Cathayans. Virgil slid gracefully into an antique armchair that he kept in his rather spacious apartment, draping a leg over the side, and waiting for the footsteps he heard in the hallway to reach his door. But then, his muscles tensed, as he smelled something distinctly not right. There was a scent of blood in the air... vampire blood, but not his. Blood burned inside him, forcing raw power into his muscles, as a knock came at his door. Virgil waited a moment before answering. 'Come in...' The door opened with a soft creak, and in stepped the very business partner who had inspired such fear in him; a diminutive Tibetan that he knew only as Wang. Virgil stood, and bowed slightly, attempting some kind of gracious gesture through the fight-or-flight instinct that was taking hold of him. 'I am surprised to see you here, Wang. Was your travel satisfactory?' Wang nodded almost imperceptibly, never one to reveal much of what was going on in his mind. He stepped closer to Virgil, peering up at him through dark sunglasses. 'We had an arrangement, Ambrosi. My people do not have the eternity that you or I have.' Virgil took a step to the side, preferring to avoid Wang's gaze. Few could tell when Virgil was lying, but he rarely took the chance of someone finding out, especially a Cathayan with legions of others to back him up. 'Your people will have what they need shortly. Another shipment is being processed at my warehouse in Seattle, but we have run into an unexpected delay.' Wang stood motionless, still staring directly at Virgil. 'Yes, you have had delays... putting some ridiculous sport above your sworn agreement to our terms of delivery. My superiors are not pleased, and when they are not pleased, we are no longer efficient. This, you must understand, poses a significant danger to our campaign, as well as to you.' This would be a rather tricky negotiation, but Virgil was uncertain, for once, what exactly he would do if confronted by a council of Cathayan ancients... aside from die. This, of course, was not something he wanted. 'You have not been removed as a priority. Exporting weapons from this country is not at all simple, especially the sensitive materials you asked me to acquire. Radioactive ordinance is nearly impossible to deliver, and your superiors should keep that in mind.' Wang began to follow Virgil, the impeccable black suit he was wearing seeming to move with him, as though part of his body. He was allowing himself to get slightly agitated, and his practiced English accent was fading into clipped Chinese syllables. 'Understand, Ambrosi, that convincing them to believe this is nigh impossible. They will continue to stew in anger until they receive their shipment, or until you are destroyed. I would not desire you destroyed, for until recently, you have been a very dependable contact. I understand you will of course have your motives for removing yourself from Seattle, to this desert, but I would recommend that you compensate by increasing productions... I do not threaten you, merely warn.' Virgil's eyes narrowed. Dealing with these things was irritating sometimes... they never comprehended anything but business, the bottom line. He still had a soul, and would not sacrifice it for them, regardless of the power or money involved. 'Your warning is accepted, Wang, but I do not take kindly to being threatened by your elders. I am the only one of my kind who is not living in abject terror of yours, and I realize that you need me for your campaign against China. You require my munitions to use against your low-country brethren, yes?' Wang clenched his hands firmly behind his back. 'Yes. As you are well aware, conventional weapons are of no use against any of us... thus, we will use the same method that destroyed Zapathasura.' Virgil had to keep himself from flying into a rage at the mention of that event. Zapathasura, the progenitor of his clan, had awoken suddenly from a tomb in India, sending every one of his kind on the planet into a delusional frenzy, turning them on each other, and anyone else who happened to be portrayed as enemy through the fitful visions... it had all ended when the Cathayans 'destroyed' Zapathasura, using what was believed to have been tactical nuclear weapons, although the event was quite unconfirmed. He refused to trust Wang regarding it, as well. Cathayans never told their secrets, only lies in their place. 'The next shipment will arrive to you on time. My shipping personnel have arrived safely back from Shanghai, and my foreman is overseeing in my place. You have nothing to be concerned about.' Wang began to step toward the door, but turned back to look at Virgil, pulling his sunglasses off to reveal his black, empty-looking eyes. 'Very well... I was only visiting to ensure that you had not forgotten about us. Perhaps when I return to the elders with this good news, they will be slightly more charitable toward you.' Virgil said nothing, and Wang slipped back out the door as he had come. Pleasantries were of little use to them, and Virgil was more pleased to let him leave without hinderance. Hiding the fear he held of the Cathayans was difficult for him, but he managed to hide it, at least around single encounters. Wang was a long-time associate, and Virgil held little apprehension of suddenly finding a dagger in his back from Wang's hand, but even he was unnerving to deal with. No use thinking about it anymore, though. Operations were back on schedule, and David would be handling things well, whenever he returned. However, now Virgil was set with a slightly more difficult task... replacing the ghoul Wang had more than likely killed before visiting in his place. He forced a sigh, an idle habit of annoyance. This contract was becoming more of a burden every night... especially with thoughts of Lia always present in his mind. Part 2: Cat and Mouse Napa Valley, California, The remains of Gideon de Medici's mansion The bushy trees shot past David Kowalski's truck in a blur, as night fell on Napa Valley. When Virgil had sent him down here to find and capture whoever had been creeping about his mansion, he hadn't told him of all the risks that it would entail. Lupines roamed the hills, and he wouldn't have found out at all, if he hadn't been present for Kenji and the boat crew's return. Kenji had been the one to tell him, and suddenly, he had found himself wishing that someone else had answered the phone in his office that night... but then, Virgil would have killed him for dereliction. It seemed that most ways David turned in this world of the night, it meant his certain demise. He sighed, blinking somewhat sleepily at the dirt road that wound through the valley, eventually coming to an end at Virgil's old mansion, something he'd never gotten to see before it had been burned to the ground. There were times when he wondered why he was doing this, what had brought him to be a servant to a several-hundred year old vampire. Perhaps it had been the power that he'd lusted after all of his life. Growing up under parents who worked corporate jobs until the days they died, he had been imbued with birth with a desire to sit on a pedestal, being worshipped by thousands of underlings and inferiors... but now he was one of them. Of course, he could also conjure minor images that would occasionally fool people, but other than that, he had nothing. He'd never been a family man, and women seemed to have a natural instinct to avoid him. Maybe that was why Virgil had come across him in the first place, he thought. He wouldn't have had anything at all otherwise, even if what he had now was just a foreman's position in an arms smuggling operation. Oh, and eternal life based on Virgil's whim was reasonably handy too... And in order to maximize that life, it was best for him not to question Virgil's judgment. That led to Virgil getting irritated with him, and probably shortly afterward being ripped apart by Muzhi, Virgil's rather irritable tiger that guarded the warehouse. This shouldn't be too difficult of an assignment anyway, he thought to himself, as the trees started to thin out. Just a slayer, probably some ass who's full of himself to the point that he thinks everything in the world bows before him... more than likely not a threat at all, in reality. Of course, David didn't completely believe that. A slayer would, regardless, be armed, and he wasn't built as solidly as his employer. A bullet to the head would still kill him. He killed the engine and let the truck roll to a stop along the side of the road, as he could see the flat expanse of land beginning about a hundred feet in front of him. He reached over, grabbing a silvered pistol, and looking it over, before popping a bullet from the clip into the chamber, and slipping it into his shoulder holster. That was if everything went south, though. The syringe in his coat pocket was what he was supposed to be using. He climbed out of the truck, and pressed the door shut quietly. Several minutes later... David climbed on top of the stalwart foundation, having made it across the front lawn without incident. He worried rather strongly that his target had moved away from the mansion, but he wasn't being pecked at by an insistent ghoul-crow yet, which probably meant that Mobius was still keeping watch over the slayer... or was dead. Best not to think about that. The burned wood crunched under his steps, as the clouds began to drift apart in the sky, revealing a crescent moon and a patchwork of stars. There was nobody here at all... not in this wreckage. The only sound he heard was the wind rushing through the valley, still echoing miles away. He wanted to find whoever this was before that wind found its way to the house, otherwise it would interrupt his keen senses. The old house was dead silent, save a rat or two in the subterranean tunnels that he could hear scratching around. Of course those weren't the hunters. Nothing creaked, nothing made any sort of noise whatsoever, so he continued toward the back of the house, toward the vineyards. He dropped carefully down into the dark tunnels, and pulled out a flashlight, casting an orange glow on perhaps the only part of the entire mansion that hadn't been lavishly decorated. As big as the place was, David was beginning to understand why even an immortal would be irritated at losing the house. He tried to remember what it was that Virgil had told him... the tunnel straight north? Only one way to find out... he began padding silently through the tunnel, toward the vineyard, ever aware that he might possibly be walking directly into a trap. However, as the stars again became visible through the end of the tunnel, his guard dropped... and he almost tripped over Alexis. Her eyes snapped open at David's sudden stop, and she climbed up to her feet, fighting through the dizziness. He grabbed her by the shoulders, but she immediately used her martial arts training to throw him off, tossing him to the side. He stumbled for a moment, before rolling forward, and pushing up to his feet. She came at him with surprising speed for someone who had just woken up, and cracked him in the jaw with a well-placed fist. Thankfully for him, the natural strength that came with vampire blood in the system kept him from going down, and he ducked behind her, wrapping a strong arm around her neck. She clawed at his face, as he reached into his coat pocket, and fumbled with the syringe, before plunging it into her arm, releasing the contents, a mixture of valium and a few other opiates. He dropped the syringe, and held one of her arms down with his. Alexis' eyes rolled back, and she collapsed into David's arms. David's face was stinging from the cuts opened by Alexis' fingernails, as he carried her over his shoulder back to his truck. This would have to be her, most of the bums and winos around California wouldn't have fought like that, even if they had somehow managed to find their way to the ritzy part of Napa Valley. That was good news for him, because he'd keep going another few nights in Virgil's favor... but it probably wouldn't be so great for the woman on his shoulder.
