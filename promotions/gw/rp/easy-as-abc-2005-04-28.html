Easy as ABC?

28 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/28/2005 You went to school to learn, girl Things you never, never knew before... Like I before E except after C... Sit yourself down, take a seat All you gotta do is repeat after me. A,B,C! Easy as... 123! Or simple as... Do re mi A,B,C, 123, baby, you and me girl! A,B,C! Easy as... 123 Or simple as... Do re mi A,B,C, 123, baby, you and me! Ramirez Home; Cochella, CA April 25, 2005; 0904 hours 'And don't EVER come back here again!!!' The angry CRASH of a drinking glass hurled forcefully against the doorframe, where it shattered into hundreds of pieces, accentuated Teresa's near-hysterical screech as the private tutor that had been Melina's equivalent of a schoolteacher for the past seven years, ran out of the Ramirez family's apartment for dear life. A few centimeters closer and the glass would have clocked her viciously upside the back of her skull. Wordlessly, Juan donned his wrestling boots-- the thick soles would provide protection against glass shards being accidentally stepped on-- and took the vacuum cleaner out of the broom closet so he could vacuum up all the broken glass. Scanning the immediate area of the doorframe, he could see that the drinking glass had shattered so completely that none of the shards were bigger than a thumbnail. Not that it made him feel any sympathy for the private tutor that had just been so unceremoniously fired... he'd have gladly grabbed her by the hair and German-suplexed her right into the glass, if it wouldn't have landed him a stint in state prison for attacking a private civilian that wasn't part of the fed. Teresa had already lost her father that way; she wasn't going to lose her husband as well. Teresa's face was still beet-red from her outburst when the phone rang a minute later. 'Hello!' she snapped into it; bald anger still robbing her of pretty much any semblance of emotional control. 'Hm, maybe I should say goodbye,' answered the voice of their agent, Stuart La Portia, as he read the mood in the air. 'I guess it's not a good time.' 'Wait...' Teresa begged him urgently. Stuart waited, and suddenly heard the sound of Teresa bursting into tears. 'Please... Stuart... where are you?' 'About 20 minutes from your house,' answered Stuart. 'I was on my way over to discuss plans for WrestleCade and how to push your team for the month of May.... but it doesn't look like--' '--a good time to discuss wrestling, no,' Teresa admitted. 'But don't turn around... please.' 'All right,' Stuart said after a brief pause. 'I'll be right there.' Twenty minutes later... Melina let Stuart into the house, and the agent found Juan inside, holding his wife and trying to comfort her... although Stuart could tell it was just as much to try and comfort himself as well. Stuart smiled briefly at Melina and went into the living room. Hearing his footsteps, Teresa and Juan turned and let go of each other. 'Please... sit,' Teresa said laconically. They all sat, and Melina took a seat beside her mother, snuggling into her side. Teresa looked clearly grateful for her presence as she put an arm around her daughter's shoulders. 'I don't mean to involve you in our personal affairs like this,' said Teresa, 'but you said you were right in the area anyway, and we needed to talk to someone right now... Serpenti and Jos? are both on personal business at the time.' 'I always try to be as much a friend as an agent to anyone I'm working with,' Stuart said unhesitatingly. 'What's wrong, Teresa?' Teresa hung her head and tears trickled from her eyes. The pain was still raw in her body. Her right arm tightened around Melina and pulled her closer. Stuart understood the gesture. 'Is she...?' Juan answered for Teresa, though his voice was choked with emotion as well. 'She's not ill... but God help us, we almost wish she was. A cold, or the flu, or even the chicken pox, is something that you catch, suffer with a bit, and then it leaves you... but finding out just how badly we failed her... the complete incompetence of the one with whom we entrusted her... that doesn't leave you. You remember it the rest of your life.' Stuart blinked and looked closely at Melina. The child's face was lightly flushed and she looked very contrite as she clung to Teresa. Stuart looked up again. 'I'm not sure I follow,' he said. 'It was the damned TUTOR we've employed to see to Melina's schooling,' growled Teresa, her anger bubbling to the surface. 'We even helped arrange for her to come up to the States to continue working with Melina, when we joined GW. We've always had to arrange for Melina to be privately tutored because of the schedule Juan and I both have to work, and the amount of traveling we do. Especially now that we are traveling all about the world, it's impossible to keep Melina in a public school.' Stuart understood what Teresa was getting at, even if he didn't yet know the specifics. 'How behind is she, academically?' he asked. 'How much further behind can she BE,' said Juan bitterly, 'if the tutor never instructed her on how to READ?' The Crimson Comet shuddered slightly as he looked at Melina. 'That's why Melina kept taking so long to go to and from concession stands... why she would bring back the wrong things... she couldn't read even the Spanish-language signs, and because she doesn't yet speak English, she couldn't communicate with the vendors verbally. The poor child was forced to randomly pick among a number of choices with no way to be sure that she was getting what she wanted.' Stuart swallowed and nodded soberly. He moved a little closer to the Ramirez family, and held out his hand to Teresa. 'Teresa... Juan... I hate to tell you just how many adult men and women, your age and older, here in the United States... are illiterate,' he said. 'You have nothing to be ashamed of... it's an epidemic all around the world, even in the wealthiest countries.' Teresa looked shocked as she clasped Stuart's hand. 'How do they graduate public schools, then?' she asked. 'They just... fall through the cracks,' said Stuart. 'No public education system is perfect... and few children get the special attention they might need. Some children have criminal backgrounds, even at their young age, and have been through the juvenile court system several times... and the schools pass them just to get RID of them and not have to deal with them anymore. You're trained in political science; I'm sure you know the old, 'Let George do it,' adagio. With private tutors, it's even easier for a child to fall through the cracks... the more you trust the tutor, the easier it is to overlook what the tutor ISN'T teaching your child... the more you take for granted.' All three Ramirezes were now in tears as Teresa lightly kissed the top of Melina's head. 'We don't want to fail her again,' Teresa said softly. 'What can we do?' Stuart smiled. 'My wife is a social worker, and she specializes in working with women and girls,' he said. 'Maggie's bilingual, and we live close by here... 40 minute drive, tops. She has access to resources that will ensure Melina is taught to read and write. Also, the fed has some staff members that still have Board of Education certification as qualified public school teachers... a few of them probably did teach at a public school for a little while. You're not the only ones in GW who have children... that's why we have these staff members on the payroll. After all... when we travel to another state, or another country, for our monthly Pay-Per-View specials, you're not the only one who's afraid to leave the kids behind with someone else.' Juan and Teresa couldn't help but chuckle lightly at the remark, though both chuckles were uneasy and shaky. Melina looked up and saw the small smiles, which sparked the ray of hope inside her. '?Estar? bien todo ahora, Mamacita?' she asked a little plaintively. (*Will everything be okay now, Mommy?) Teresa smiled and kissed Melina on the cheek. 'Estar? bien todo ahora,' she said reassuringly. (*Everything will be okay now.) Stuart took out his cell phone. 'I know you didn't have the chance to give Melina a celebration for her 10th birthday this past Friday,' he said. 'Let me call Maggie and we'll all go to lunch... I think you'll all like the birthday present Maggie is able to give your daughter. Does Melina like Burger King?' '?REY DE HAMBURGUESA?? Certainly not,' Teresa scoffed, as Melina wrinkled her nose distastefully in response to the translation of the words 'Burger King' to Spanish. Teresa shook her head and accentuated her point. 'None of us got to be in the kind of athletic condition we're in, by eating that kind of offal!' Stuart chuckled as he hit a number on his speed dial. 'I think we can accommodate that,' he said. 'Hi, Maggie, it's me, sweetie... fine, thanks, but are you busy? Okay, good... I'll be coming over with Juan and Teresa Ramirez, and their daughter... while we're on the way there, call Easy as ABC and have them send someone over to the center? I'll explain on the way there... speak to their children's party caterer too; we have a little birthday celebration to throw...' A,B,C! Easy as... 123! Or simple as... Do re mi A,B,C, 123, baby, you and me girl! A,B,C! Easy as... 123 Or simple as... Do re mi A,B,C, 123, baby, you and me! ABC, The Jackson Five, ? 1970
