Revenge: Watched

4 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 4, 2006 It was a boring job and that was exactly the way Ned liked it. All he had to do was sit outside this rich idiot’s door and protect him from whatever boogeyman it was he feared. The only unbearable part of the job was that he had been saddled with John as a partner. John was a lot younger than Ned and he was a big fan of wrestling, GLOBAL particularly, and he acted like a damn fan boy…all excited just to be sitting outside the door of one of his favorite wrestlers. “I still can’t believe it Ned,” John said with a smile. Ned looked over at him, the young idiot sitting on the right side of the door with a little kid smile plastered on his face. “Get over it already,” Ned replied, he felt a migraine coming on. “Dude,” John said as he pointed his thumb to the door behind him, “Mike Biddle is in that room right now. He’s ‘Mr. Main Event’ man, he’s a total legend and…” “Just shut up!” Ned scolded. Crestfallen, John looked down at his feet for a few moments before speaking up again. “Why do you think he hired extra private security anyway Ned?” Ned sighed; thank God his shift was over in an hour. “I don’t know,” he replied, “but it probably has something to do with the guy that murdered his girlfriend breaking outta jail last week.” “Yeah,” John said, “that’s what I thought too. You think he’s really going to try something?” Ned shrugged. “I don’t know. I just hope that if he does try something he does it when I’m not here.” “I don’t know,” John replied, “It sure would be nice to catch that asshole though.” “It’s not my job to catch criminal’s,” Ned said, “I’m only supposed to slow ‘em down enough for the real cop’s to catch ‘em.” John nodded and paused for a moment. “I hope he asks me to go down to the bar with him tonight,” he finally said. Ned sighed and shook his head, why would anyone in their right mind want to watch some over privileged idiot drink his ‘problems’ away? ***** Behind the door that Ned and John guarded, Mike Biddle sat on the bed terrified for his life. Despite the extra security that he had hired he felt as if Charles was watching him, just waiting to make a move. Biddle had never been this scared before in his life. He had spent his entire existence defeating turd snaps like Charles but for some strange reason he felt that this particular chapter of his life would spell out his end, and he was just too damn good looking to die young. The door opened and then suddenly slammed shut, Biddle was startled but he knew that it was only his personal bodyguard coming to check up on him. “Hey boss man,” The Brawler said as he entered the bedroom. Biddle acknowledged his bodyguard’s presence with a nod of his head and went back to his deep dark thoughts of impending doom. “Ya shouldn’t be so down boss man,” the Brawler said as he sat down on the arm chair next to the bed. “Shit, ya have a lot ta be happy ‘bout. Ya just won that title an ya get to kick Josh’s ass again next week.” The Brawler smiled as he thought about the upcoming match on Fusion, it was rare for him to step into the ring nowadays and he cherished every opportunity he was given. Biddle sighed, the big man had been trying to cheer him up every chance that he got and it was starting to get annoying. “Yes, it’s nice being the champ,” Biddle shot a glance at the International Belt that lay on the dresser, “but that doesn’t change the fact that I could die before I even get a chance to defend the damn thing.” “Aww, don’t talk like that boss man,” the Brawler said with a disappointed tone. “Well it’s true,” Biddle shot back. “Ain’t nothin’ bad gonna happen to ya on my watch,” the Brawler said. “You better hope so,” Biddle said an angry glare. The Brawler took this as his cue to leave. Once Biddle was alone again he began to pace around the room. He had beaten Charles so many times and yet he felt that this time Charles had the upper hand. All these dark thoughts that consumed him made him wish for a stiff drink. He went to the door and asked one of the guards on duty to go with him to the bar downstairs. The younger one was the most eager to follow him, it didn’t matter to Biddle either way he just needed a few drinks to help him to sleep. ***** Biddle had good reason to feel watched by Charles, for the scheming murderer was doing just that. In a small hotel room on the outskirts of Las Vegas Charles was hunched over a spiral notebook, waiting for Jimbo to come back. Charles had forced the former biker to get a shave and haircut and then sent him into Reno to obtain his guard card under a false name Charles had bought near the border. Jimbo was set to be hired for Biddle’s security team (the owner of the guard company Biddle had hired owed Charles a favor). Duke and Hawk were out buying supplies for the attack, leaving Charles alone in the hotel room. He had already planned his revenge down to the last gory detail and he laughed maniacally as he read over his checklist, once the members of his gang came back the plan would be half completed. “You’ll beg me for mercy you son of a bitch,” Charles said to himself as he closed the notebook. Suddenly Jimbo burst through the door, a smile on his rough features. “I got the damn guard card,” he declared. “Excellent,” Charles said. “And you already talked to our contact in Vegas about the job?” “Yeah,” Jimbo said as he grabbed a beer from the mini-fridge. “I start tomorrow.” The smile on Charles’ face grew wide. “Just make sure to keep notes of everything he does.” “Sure,” Jimbo said as he took a sip of his beer. “You positive he won’t recognize me though?” Charles nodded. “The man is an idiot and won’t even remember you, especially with your shave and haircut.” “Right on,” Jimbo said as he fumbled through his pockets for his cigarettes. Charles reached for the remote, the replay of last week’s Fusion was on and he wanted to see the fear in Biddle’s eyes yet again. He knew that it took all of Biddle’s so-called acting ability to hide the fear that had gripped him, but Charles could see through the disguise and it made him giddy to see Biddle squirm. Of course, the squirming he did in front of the camera’s would be nothing compared to what he’d do to Biddle once the main part of his plan was set into motion.
