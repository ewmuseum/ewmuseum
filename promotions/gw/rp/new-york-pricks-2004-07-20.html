New York Pricks

20 July 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
New York Pricks Sage Sabbot Global Wrestling Promo #1521 Date: 07/20/04 Location: The Big Apple... Driving down the streets of New York City. I loved it so much the first time, I thought that I’d make a guest appearance in the wonderful city. I mean, I’ve been to cities and all, but nothing tops the Big Apple. You have the faithful citizens sporting the Yankee’s, Jet’s, or Knick’s colors. Everybody is brought together, in this one place by a bond that nobody else can claim. They are all New Yorkers. I turn up the radio, just enough so I can hear it. I’m not like all the other guys, I don’t need my car to bounce when I’m listening to music. And I don’t appreciate hearing anybody else’s music over mine. It is very disrespectful in my opinion. All I need, is to have my music loud enough so I can hear it for myself. All around, you can see lights shine from all around you. You have people crowding the streets, waiting outside in front of buildings. Most of them with a beverage in their hands, socializing. Getting to know each other. I stop at a red light. I heard things about red lights here in New York. I heard to not even stop at them. That it is better to get a fine from that then to have some drunk key your car or worse off, shoot you in the head. …been there, done that. I nervously look around, nobody storming by my car with a key or nobody with a revolver in their hand. I take a deep breath of relief. POUND! POUND! POUND! I literally almost shit my pants. That wouldn’t be too good considering that they are over $250. I turn around at my passenger window and see a homeless man, a bum, staring into my car window pointing at a sign in his hand. It reads: ”Will Work For Food” I look at the red light, waiting for the damn thing to turn green. When you know that you may have seconds before you die, everything seems to last longer. …been there, done that. The light is still red. POUND! POUND! POUND! The damn bum keeps pounding on my window. I reach into my pocket and pull out the pocket, showing the dumbass I have no money. Or, showing the dumbass that I have my wallet in my other pocket. POUND! POUND! POUND! The asshole keeps pounding on the window and pointing to his sign. I give him the worldly sign for “I don’t know” by shrugging my shoulders and shaking my head side to side. I look at the light. …its green. I turn around to the bum and give him the worldly sign for “fuck you” by saluting him with my middle finger, then hitting the gas peddle and leaving him in a world of shit. I chuckle, I know I’m a sadistic bastard. But hey, the guy probably had money at one point in his life. But probably spent it on drugs or alcohol or something of that nature. I don’t feel sorry for guys like that. Instead, I salute them…with one finger. I drive down the road a couple more miles and start to realize what I’ve done. I remember that Cherish was once somebody like that homeless man. That she had to resort to prostituting her body to survive. She had to have sex to eat food, even scraps that some rich bastard like me left behind. Cherish was not a drunk or a drugbag, she was…remarkable. I pull into a McDonalds restaurant on the side of the road. I scroll through the parking lot, in hopes to find a place to park. I can’t find anything, so I take matters into my own hands. …I go through the drive-thru. I end up in a line of only a couple cars, which is probably a lot faster then waiting in line inside for 25 minutes. Fast food? Of course, the food is fast but you are also waiting the amount of time it would take to make and digest a steak. Fast food my ass. I drive up to the giant McDonalds sign. I swear, they get bigger every year and have more lights. An employee of the great fast food chain whispers sweet nothings from the speaker… ”Welcome to McDonalds, how may I help you?” I turn my radio down… ”I’d like to…” ”Would you like to super size that?” ”Actually, no, I haven’t even placed my order yet.” ”Okay, is that all, sir?” ”No, I haven’t even said anything. I’d like a…” ”Okay, pull up to the counter around the corner to get your order. Your total comes to $13.49.” I lean back in my seat and take a deep breath. A sigh of frustration. Sometimes, I swear, you feel like killing somebody. …been there, done that. I slowly press the gas peddle and drive up to the counter. I hope they at least gave me something I would have ordered. The fabulous McDonalds employee, opens the window… ”Would you like ketchup or mustard?” ”Sure, it might help eat whatever you decided to give me.” She throws a handful of ketchup and mustard into the brown paper bag sporting the McDonalds colors. Surprising it doesn’t have the Yankee’s logo on it. I look at the window and read a Help Wanted sign on it. It says: ”Night shifts available. Ask for an application.” She hands me the bag and I grab it from her hands before she screws it up anymore. I put it in the passenger side of the car and I hand her a $20. She leaves the counter and comes back shortly after. She hands me $4.23. I count the change out in my hand… ”I thought you said my order was $13-something?” ”No, that wasn’t me.” Oh, so I must be stupid then. I can tell that it’s the same person because she has the annoying and retarded voice, the one you have to attempt to understand through the chomps of her bubble gum. And by her heavy breathing into my food and into my face, I can tell its Juicy Fruit. …I smile at her. ”Oh, okay then. Can I get an application please?” ”Sure.” She leaves once again and returns with a paper with mustard and ketchup stains on it. She hands it to me and then licks the ketchup off her fat pudgy fingers. I throw it on the passenger’s seat. ”Scrumptious wasn’t it? Who in the hell would work at a dump like this anyways?” I don’t care anymore, I’m out of here. I hit the gas peddle and drive to the end of the parking lot and turn back to where I was coming from. I put the bag on my lap and look through it while driving…if you can call it driving. Well, we are in New York, so I guess it would be driving. I see two whoppers, both with pickles. I open one up with one hand while my other one is on the steering wheel. One eye is concentrating on the whopper and the other on dozens of cars flying past me. I lift up the soggy bun of the whopper and pick off the pickles and throw them to the side. I’m allergic to pickles. One bite of one of those little guys, I’d end up looking as big as Brick. I take a big bite of the…cold shit-tasting burger. I throw it out the window. I won’t even eat a bite of this damn shit. I place it in my passenger’s seat. I turn the radio up again…just enough so I can hear it. I stop at a red light… POUND! POUND! POUND! Not again, that piece of shit hobo. He is looking at me and he recognizes me. He points to his little sign again… ”Will Work For Food” I nod my head while wiping the mayonnaise from the shitty burger on my pantleg. My $250 pantleg. I roll down the window on the passenger’s side. The smelly piece of garbage is staring at my McDonalds bag of crap on the seat… ”Will work for food, huh?” The stinky bastard nods his head… I reach over to the passenger’s seat and I hand it to him. He grabs it and takes a step back. He looks at it, frustrated, but not as much as I am. ”They are looking for somebody for night shift. You can probably make $5 an hour. That’s called a job application. …you’re welcome.” I hit the gas peddle once I realize there is a green light. I feel good about myself for the first time in awhile. And who said that I was never a nice guy?
