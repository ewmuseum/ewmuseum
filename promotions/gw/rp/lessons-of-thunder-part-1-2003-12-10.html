Lessons of Thunder, part 1

10 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: December 10th, 2003 Two days. Yoshiko had a definite capability to amaze me, but this was beyond anything I had seen before. It was like she was possessed -- her injuries barely hampered her in the slightest, and two days after she woke up from a two-week coma, here we were in Japan. And we weren't taking it easy, either. Apparently, spending two weeks 'slacking' like I had was bad for me -- so Yoshiko worked out a training regimen for me that led right up to the day of SiT. I don't know how she managed to do that either. She must have been on the phone from practically the moment we left the hospital to the moment we got off the plane in Tokyo. But she'd done it, and now, here we were. I could feel the wooden floor, cold and hard against my knees. My hands rested on my thighs in a traditional meditative pose. Just a few feet away, Yoshiko knelt in the same position. We both stared forward, waiting for orders from our sensei. She circled around us, studying us quietly, but intently. Raven hair spilled down her shoulders freely, contrasting starkly with the soft rose hues of her kimono. The only sounds in the room were our breaths, and the soft swish of her kimono as she walked. Shiba Sakura, the Snow Phoenix. The first of five Thunders I would train with in the coming days, and easily one of the most esoteric. She had always claimed to have some kind of mystical control over her 'chakra', the energies within the body. It was because she could control her chakra, she had explained one night years ago -- when we'd both been drinking heavily -- that she could perform seemingly suicidal maneuvers in the ring, and spring back up like nothing had ever happened. Then she'd kissed me, and, well? it's a story for another day, but suffice to say her that stamina that night convinced me that more than a little bit of her story was true. And so, here we were. Waiting. It had been nearly an hour of Yoshiko and I -- and I still don't understand how she managed to sit like this with a cast on one leg -- kneeling on the floor, while Sakura simply observed us. I was beginning to wonder if she was going to teach us anything, or if this was just some kind of prank the girls had decided to pull on me. But finally, Sakura spoke. 'I want you to breathe,' she said, her voice soft and even, 'Just breathe. Do nothing else, think nothing else. Take in your breath, and hold it. Focus on that breath. Feel how it fills your lungs, how it gives you life. Concentrate on every sensation. Try to imagine the oxygen travelling out through your body, giving strength to every part of you. Your lungs should be starting to burn now. Focus! Do not breathe in again. Concentrate on that burning, your body pleading for air. Search within yourself for a way to cure it. If you look hard enough, you'll find reserves of cool blue strength, of energy and life, in the hidden places of your body. Draw on them. Grab that energy and pull it up, let it fill your lungs and quell the burning. Do it now.' I took a moment to clear my mind, and did as she said. First I forced all the air out of my body, then took the deepest breath I could. I let the air fill my lungs, puffing my chest out, and held it. Focusing only on the sensation of breathing, I was surprised when I found I actually could do what Sakura had described. As my entire being became more and more focused on the breath, I could feel the oxygen dispersing through my bloodstream, the sensation of it bringing life to my cells. I could fell them release their carbon dioxide into my lungs as well, and slowly, the cool sensation of a fresh breath was replaced with the burning of dead air. This then, was the moment of truth. I focused intently on that sensation, on flushing my mind and body of all other thoughts, all other feelings. I took the burning, and made it my world, my reality. In my mind, I could see it, a red haze in my chest, threatening to choke me to death. Concentrating, I let it spread, let it become a searing fire, reaching out through my body, looking for a water to quell it. And to my own surprise, I found it. In the pit of my stomach, I felt like there was a lake of cool strength, of vital force that could quench any blaze. Carefully, I tried to take hold of that force, dragging it through my body to my burning lungs. When it met the blaze, I nearly gasped in shock. The burning heat vanished in an instant, replaced only with cool peace -- like I had been breathing all this time, and breathing clean, fresh air at that. I marveled at this newfound strength, this power that had been within me all this time. How had I never found it before? I opened my eyes, and just then realized that I had ever closed them in the first place. There stood Sakura, looking down on the two of us appreciatively. I smiled at her, and then looked over at Yoshiko. She still knelt as motionless and serene as ever, her chest still and calm. My smile widened -- and then I lost it. The cool blue strength vanished, and I doubled over onto the floor, hacking and coughing. As suddenly as that strength had come to me, it was gone -- and it had taken even more than it had given. I tried to lift myself off the floor, and found it unbearably difficult -- until I felt hands on my shoulder and chest, helping me back up into the kneeling position. 'Tsk,' said Sakura, as she steadied me, 'not bad for a first timer, Rokku-kun, but you've definitely got a lot to learn about control.' 'What?' I trailed off, hacking and gasping again, 'what happened?' 'You lost your focus,' replied Sakura, sitting cross-legged in front of me, 'it's not surprising. You've always been rather? distractible.' She smirked knowingly at me, and I chuckled, finally managing to catch my breath. 'Okay,' I asked, 'but why am I so weak now? What's up with that?' 'Truly controlling one's chakra is a very delicate process,' said Sakura, 'it requires absolute peace and concentration. What you have to understand, Rokku-kun, is that everybody molds and uses their chakra on an unconscious level. When you use the Emperor's Right Hand, for example, you pour an incredible amount of chakra into the blow -- I've seen it.' 'Wait,' I said, scratching my head, 'you can see my chakra?' 'I can see anyone's chakra,' Sakura replied, 'it's an advantage of having trained at this for my entire life. I can tell exactly what you did just by looking at you. When you saw how well Yoshiko-chan was succeeding at the breathing exercise, you felt joy -- and that emotion caused you to overextend yourself, drawing too much chakra too quickly. You surpassed your current ability to consciously control it, and lost your grip on it entirely. It burned away, and took a lot of your normal energy with it.' 'So, emotions can heavily affect your chakra flow,' I said. 'Exactly,' replied Sakura, 'you learn quick. Emotion can be a powerful tool in channeling your chakra. Powerful feelings like rage and love can allow you to draw on incredible reserves of energy. But without the proper training, you'll just burn yourself out. I have to say, though, that both of you are naturals. It would take most people days to manage to channel their chakra for that breathing exercise -- the two of you got it on the first try.' 'Yeah?' I trailed off, looking over at Yoshiko again. She hadn't moved at all since I'd looked at her minutes ago -- she was still calm, still serene. 'Yoshiko seems to have a better handle on it than me, though.' 'She's incredible, I'll admit,' said Sakura, smiling. 'She seems to have a natural grip on her chakra, drawing out a slow and steady flow. She's practically in a trance state at this point, discovering the secret energies within her body. At this rate, she could go without breathing for an hour, maybe. I'm sure I'll be able to teach her a lot before you two go back to the States. If I can teach her enough, she should be able to heal those wounds and be back on her feet in no time.' I let out a long, low whistle. 'This is amazing, Sakura-chan. I've studied meditative techniques before, but I never realized there was so much power that could be tapped into like this. I guess I never had the patience before.' 'Yeah,' said Sakura, 'but now you have a reason, don't you?' 'What do you mean?' She simply smiled, and nodded her head at Yoshiko. 'How did you? I mean? we never said?' 'Like I told you, emotions can have a powerful effect on your chakra. I could read it all over you two when you first walked in here. And I have to say, it's about damn time. Did you know the Thunders had a pool going once for how long it was going to take you two to get together?' I laughed. 'That sounds like something Tsuneo or Chagatai would come up with. Is it still going?' Sakura cringed. 'I hope not. If it is, we all owe Kenta a lot of yen?' 'That's not surprising,' I said, with a knowing smirk, 'he's always had more foresight than any of us.' 'Yeah, and now that foresight's probably going to get him a new car.' I burst out laughing again. It felt good, even though my whole body ached. 'Well, Sakura-sensei,' I said, 'shall we continue? I want to learn as much as I can before the day is through.' 'Oh, don't worry,' said Sakura, 'I plan on cramming everything I can into that pretty little head of yours, Rokku-kun. Lets try an exercise for chakra regeneration next?'
