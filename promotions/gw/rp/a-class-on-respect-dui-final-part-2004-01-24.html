A Class On Respect (DUI FINAL PART!)

24 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/24/04 Hunched over the desk, she sits scrolling her eyes over the minuscule lines that would drive any dyslexic crazy. Hundreds of words compiled on top of each other, including some that seem to take up half the line. And it always helps to have a dictionary nearby for a little explanation for others. Franticly zipping from line to line, her eyes begin to disappear behind the wrinkled eyelids as time begins to wane against her. Tossing her head and neck slightly, her sight widens once more as she continues on at a rapid pace. But again, fatigue returns and her head dips forward slightly along with the drooping eyes. Instead of falling totally into the pages, she catches herself in time. Leaning into her hands, the lady gently rubs her face over them exposing only her nose. Gradually she pulls herself away and her right hand gently runs through her thick hair just above her ear. Rolling over the top of her head, she brushes her fingers down allowing the red strands to fall before her face, letting her notice where the red begins to turn gray. She knows age has started to come, but she didn’t expect it so soon. Collapsing back against the heavy cushion of her chair, Judge Ruth Holt sighs heavily and acknowledges to herself that her night is over. “I suppose that’s enough fighting crime for one day.” The chair lets out an screeching echo as she pushes it far enough to get up from her seat and away from the desk. As she turns from the chair, she flips her hand quickly, shutting the scattered tomes before her. Leaving scarce marks of pages in a few, she flicks down the large lamp on the edge of the desk and turns to pass across the large woven rug that covers the majority of her library. Judge Holt passes through the open archway into the well lit hallway as she heads towards her bedroom, a few doors down. Her eyes dangle quickly across her red carpet and down toward the crafted staircase nearby. For a long time, she has adored the house for what it has been and become over the years. However empty of family it might have been, it was fulfilled with memory and good times. But such is the life of the independent woman. Giving up the children and spouse for a fulltime career. As she passes through the doorway, Ruth quickly stops in her tracks and mutters to herself, “Stupid me. I forgot my jacket.” Rolling her eyes, she doubles back across toward the open study area as she is still in her the clothes she wore earlier throughout the day. Busy times for busy minds, as they say. Swiping the jacket as it hung on the edge of the chair, she flops it over her shoulder forearm as she heads back out. Heading toward the arch leading into the hallway, Ruth flips the lightswitch nearby. But as the lights in the library go out, so do the ones in the hall as they begin to flicker heavily before totally going dark. Crossing her brow, she flicks the switch repeatedly as nothing comes back on. With a shrug and a sigh, she shakes her head. “Must have burned out a fuse or something.” Pasting a “care not” smile on her face, she turns to walk blindly back toward her bedroom until her eyes adjust. But as she takes the first step, she hears a clatter coming from a nearby room. Frozen in shock, Ruth’s jaw drops as all she can move is her eyes. But they won’t do her any good right now. Slowly raising her hand over the center of her chest, she fights out a couple tattered breaths, trying to regain her composure. With her breathing mostly back to normal, thoughts start spinning around in her head at the many possibilities of what could be going on. But most of them coming up are of Ursula knocking over something of value. The first picture to come to mind is the sleek black cat lying on the fireplace mantle and nearly falling off, but the only thing to save her are those reflexes and the nearby vase. Shaking her head, Ruth turns into the hallway and sticks close to the wall for support through the darkness. However dark it is, the moon still shines brightly through the skylight beside the staircase. Quite the breathtaking sight for any romantic. But for the walking blind, its nothing to see. Suddenly, a loud shattering crash echoes through the house that could only be heard as a broken window. In an instant, Ruth drops to the ground and leans hard against the wall. She knows there’s an intruder around. With her eyes wide, she covers half her face with her hands in total shock. Questions flood her mind ranging from who it could be to why the alarms didn’t go off. Nothing coming for an answer. Ruth fights to snap out of her shock as she only has so long to act before the intruder finds her. The shatter came from a door or two down, so she must do something. She has to get help. Quickly Ruth recalls her phone being in her bedroom merely three rooms away. But there’s only one way to get there... Using the wall with as much support as possible, she rises up to her feet and leans forward. Its been a long time since her track days, but she still runs the occasional marathon for charity. Too bad they’re walking ones. Regardless, she has one shot. Rearing back, she takes it and bolts forward. Moving at what sees is the speed of light, she charges past the first door no problem. Using the door as the target, it’s the only thing she sees. She doesn’t feel any pain at all. Not even the heavy thump against the side of her ribs. Before she knows it, the door she was aiming at turns horizontal as she feels the first step hit her. The intruder sprung out from the second door across from the staircase and tackled Ruth hard enough to send her and himself past the first five steps. Catching his balance, the assailant stops a couple steps later and leans down to watch his target roll and tumble over each set of stairs. Rolling constantly, her momentum doesn’t slow down at all as she catches her back and neck several times on the way. Finally, she reaches the last pair of steps and finds the right side of her face slapping against the smooth flooring of the lobby. Lying motionless, Ruth eyes glaze over at the sight of her own shadowy reflection beside her. Her jaw hanging open slightly as a thin line of blood drips out the side of her mouth that might be from her tongue. Half of her face is puffed out and still shows a heavy blush from several possible rug burns. Her left shoulder sits awkwardly backward from her body as do the ring and pointer fingers of the same hand. But as she stares at her own shadow, she barely notices the matching one from her attacker. Letting out a low pitch moan, Ruth whimpers shortly before the pain is too much to shout about. But as she quiets down, her guest speaks up. “Tsk, tsk, tsk. Look at you.” Crouching down, the attacker scrolls his eyes up and over the prize. “Just look at you Miss Holt. All hurt and not so well off. Why, just a few days ago you were a gleaming bitch of a woman.” Lightly, he smiles behind his dark mask while Ruth lets out a few tears of senseless resistance. “But now...you’re this helpless heap of trash. Just lying flat down at the fault of your own vanity. I guess that’ll teach you to put a door so close to a flight or two of stairs.” With a loud crack, the masked man rises from his knees and approaches closer to Ruth’s view. Letting out a few muffled cries, Ruth tries to regain some focus as she looks up at him. “Aw, shut up. You’ll live. I made sure of that.” Letting out a devious chuckle, the attacker runs his hand over the top of his mask and rubs it roughly to itch. Looking at his handiwork, he notices the judge’s obtuse shoulder and leans back in pain. “Ooooooo! That looks like it hurts. Let me help you with that.” He reaches down and grasps the top and bottom parts of Ruth’s upper arm and jerks it back roughly. The judge lets out a loud scream of pain as the joint clicks back into place. Dropping the arm back against her side, he takes a couple steps back and stands over her head. “Is that any better?” His eyes pierce back down at her through his mask as hers are sealed shut with the pain. “Its amazing how you can still be conscious after all this. You must be a tough girl after all. Funny how some bitchy people can make it through shit like this, while tough guys would drop like a house of cards and be out for days. But I guess I’m not done yet either.” Strolling back towards the steps, he leans down and lifts up a thin, dark object by its base and grasps it tightly with both hands. Spinning it over a couple times, a white name rolls around and finally stops at the line “Spaulding”. Twisting his hands repeatedly on the taped grips, the man pushes the bat outward and takes a couple swings at the air letting out a heavy swoosh. “You see Ruthie, you pissed me off. Pretty damn bad, I would say too. I haven’t been that pissed in a while. I’ll have to congratulate you on that. You should be proud.” Ruth quiets down to a soft muffle as she fights to start moving away from where she’s at. But instead of getting to far, the bat connects with her right hip and quickly she stops in her tracks crying in pain. “Now, now. Don’t you run off on me. I came here for a reason.” Winding up again, the bat makes another soft swish through the air before making a thud against a pair of ribs. Ruth lets out a quick cry, that’s only muffled by two other smacks as the bat knocks against the rear portion of her ribs. The cries turn into heavy coughs as Ruth starts gasping and choking for breaths while her assailant stomps before her and gets down in her face. “You told me to learn a lesson on respect. Honey, I fucking know about respect. I’ve had to live and learn about respect my whole fucking life. What it seems to me is that you don’t know jack shit about it. So....that’s why I’m here tonight. To teach a class about respect. Got me”!” The eyes behind the mask widen as the man raises the bat over his head and repeatedly cracks it against Ruth’s ribs and back. Sickening thuds repeating over subtle cracks of bone until the cries and coughs grow silent. Breathing heavily, the attacker regains his composure and raises his right hand against his forehead as if to wipe sweat off his brow. Lowering down, the man puts a gloved hand against the jugular vein of her neck. Feeling a light, steady beat he rises back up and shrugs his shoulders. “I told you that you’d live.” Letting out a loud laugh, he sets a black folding cellphone about a foot in front of her and back up towards the staircase. “There’s your phone. Just in case you feel the need for a little help.” The heavy stomps of the steps echo throughout the entry as the attacker reaches the top of the stairs and pulls off his mask. Underneath it is revealed a heavy puff of dark hair and a bright smile from one Johnie Ringo. Laughing loudly, he heads back into the room he stormed out of and climbs out of the shattered window where he came in. A loud cough echoes back from Ruth as she starts to turn over but she can’t seem to move enough. Before slipping back into unconsciousness, she collapses with the phone in hand and her face nearby. But as she lets out a rough sigh, a soft purr sounds from nearby. Stealthily, Ursula trots over toward the fallen justice and pauses in front of her. Taking a few cautious steps forward, Ursula steps between Ruth’s arms and near her left hand until finally reaching where she wants to go. Innocently, she rubs her face against Ruth’s cheek softly, but enough to indent it a bit. Looking over Ruth again, the black cat leans her head forward and starts licking her face, cleaning some of the blood away from her mouth. FIN....
