Heat Of The Day

21 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Week Of 8-21-05 The Trip The desert wind carried omens of evil on it as the grit and sand hit his face along with the heat. The very power of the sun, which had long ago starved the land of all but the hardiest vegetation, was now trying to whither him away as well. He ignored such small claims on his physical body, knowing he would not succumb to the heat and dryness. He had been riding in the open air for most of the day, allowing the sun to ravage him as he cleared his mind. Everything was jumbled once again, and there was nothing like a ride on the Iron Horse to let him clear his mind and renew his spirit. Phone calls, emails and inquiries all went unreturned. Whatever reason for it, he had become worried about the disposition of his’friend? If something were wrong, then his worst fear was realized once again. The dark forces were working to remove anything and everything he could rely on, to isolate him and tear him down slowly. Such an attempt did not surprise him. Most of the work he had done was designed to isolate him on the path, but human beings were social animals. He had to have some contact with the world outside of himself, and now those last, few vestiges of his previous life were being ripped away. He should have seen this coming the moment he opened himself up to the world, but a lesson was never truly learned until it involved loss. He needed gas and something to tide him over against his fight with nature. He saw a roadside station ahead, and pulled off the highway. The station was a small one, probably owned by someone who had lived in the small town their entire life. He stopped right next to the pump, and noticed that there weren’t even credit card readers at the pump. Jake unscrewed his gas cap, and then began the process of filling his tank. The motorcycle had a small tank, and it was full almost instantly thanks to the quick pumps. He screwed his gas cap on and headed into the store. The owner looked like he had been left in the sun to dry up and wrinkle for far too long. They exchanged a small nod to each other as he headed over to the cooler. Jake grabbed three bottles of cold water, and then went by an aisle as he made his way to the register. Two Snickers bars and a package of peanut butter crackers were added to the mix. The old man rang up his purchase. “That’ll be twenty-one dollars and eleven cents.” He said looking up at the young man. Jake looked out at his motorcycle and then returned his gaze to the man, “What the Hell” How much is gas?” “Two dollars and ninety cents a gallon.” He explained. “Son of a bitch.” Jake handed over forty dollars and got change. “Take it easy old timer.” Jake walked out to his bike. He sat down on it and wolfed down the peanut butter crackers and the two Snickers bars. He washed it down with one bottle of water. The next bottle went over his head and onto his bare torso. Feeling refreshed, the final bottle went into the saddlebag. The heat was so oppressive he could see the asphalt reflecting and radiating the heat up into the sky, distorting the landscape ahead. He started up his ride, and kept on moving. The sun smiled down at him as their battle was renewed. Arrival Pulling up the long desert road he could taste the dust in his mouth. The home was an older one, but the structure was perfectly maintained along with the grounds around the home. Water conservation was ignored one hundred feet from the house as thick, luscious green grass surrounded perfectly maintained gardens and rose bushes. He pulled the motorcycle under the awning, and dropped the kickstand before stepping off. A shirt came from one of the saddlebags along with the blue wooden box. Jake pulled the shirt over his frame, and slowly buttoned it up. He walked up to the door and knocked. He waited a moment before knocking again more loudly. He heard footsteps inside, and the door opened up to reveal a slight man with a rotund belly and hair only on the sides of his head. He looked Jake up and down before speaking, “You are Mr. Hix I believe, correct?” Jake nodded, “I am. I came to see Corvus.” “Master Corvus is no longer here.” The man replied calmly. “Please come in.” Jake stepped inside the house. The entryway was huge, with granite flooring and rich appointments, but half seemed gone and plenty of boxes were packed against the far wall. “What’s going on?” Jake looked around, noticing through other doorways that the packing was going on all over the home. “Master Corvus is selling the home, and he has asked me to make all the arrangements. We are trying to finish the packing by next week, and then the home will be sold.” The little man explained. Jake turned to him, “Where did Corvus go?” “Master Corvus, or as he now likes to be called Eryc Morgan, left. He said he had some soul searching to do, and he would not return.” Jake shook his head as he walked around the room, “Unbelievable.” The man watched as Jake seemed to be working through the scene in his mind. “Is there anything else I can assist you with Mr. Hix” I really must get back to packing.” “Did he leave a note or anyway to get in touch with him?” Hix was beginning to feel the anger and frustration rise. “No sir.” The man smiled sadly, “He may get in touch with us for some final instructions on what to do. Otherwise, we have nothing to do except wrap everything up.” Jake shook his head as he walked up to the man. Jake handed the box over to him, “If he calls tell him I left this for him. If not, pack it with all his other possessions. I don’t want it.” Jake walked out of the house. The butler slid the case on the wooden box open. Inside the blue case was a bottle filled with amber liquid resting on golden straw. The Macallan 30 Year Old Scotch was one of the best and most expensive bottles of whiskey in the world. The butler nodded in approval as he stepped into the bar to pack the bottle away. Jake Hix stripped off the shirt as he stepped out onto the driveway. He folded it up, and stuffed it back into the saddlebag. Jake pulled out his cell phone from inside and dialed a number. Sensational Stephen Reed picked up on the third ring. “Hello?” “It’s Jake.” “Jake, you didn’t come back for training.” Stephen scolded him. “I know, I had to get an answer to something.” He explained. “Now I know I won’t have a tag team partner, and I am alone.” “Not alone, just on your own. I’ll fly to Vegas and we can train there. I think it’s time for you to remember why they call you the Big Dawg. Time for them to see the power and the fury.” Jake nodded, “See you tomorrow.” “See you then.” Jake hung up the phone. Jake climbed onto his motorcycle, and pulled out to fight nature once again, on many levels. Tijuana Old, rotting wood two by fours and nails held up the walls. Years of water leaking through the rust eaten tin roof ensured the shack would never stand forever. It looked like one of the thousands of tine shack shanty homes lining the streets of Tijuana where the definition of poverty was stark and frightening. The table stood out, too new and nice for a setting such as this. He was not unaccustomed to dealing with these types of surroundings. He sat on a padded folding chair on the far side of the table waiting for his guest to arrive. None of his plans had worked, and he would hold the man responsible for the lack of progress. He heard a car outside, and then the door to the tin shack was opened by one of his acolytes. Malum shoved Gilberto into the room. The high priest stayed outside with the other acolytes, closing the doors as the eerie creaking of metal on metal echoed throughout the room as the man stepped inside. Sid Hix indicated the folding chair across the table from him, “Sit.” Gilberto sat down across from the man he feared so much. Even sitting here, he could feel the waves of dark power wash over him just being in the man’s presence. He fought against the fear and terror gripping his soul as he dutifully waited to be addressed. “Tell me why my brother still stands strong.” Sid’s voice was angry behind the pleasant presentation, but the control over tone and pitch did not stop the power from shaking the man the comment was directed at. “I attacked him with everything I had. Nothing came to fruition.” Gilberto could sense he was babbling, talking fast to allow his fear to escape his body. “I tried to curse him, to slow him, to harm him and even to torture him. Nothing worked! I only was able to try and cause despair! I don’t even think that worked!” Sid leaned forward, “No, apparently it did not. I hear he got somewhat depressed, and then snapped out of it. Perhaps I chose the wrong person. I thought you were a powerful shaman.” “He is God’s right hand! What can I do?” Gilberto protested. “What you should be asking is what will I do to you and your family for failing me” I think if one of them is removed it would lighten the load on your time, and then you could spend more time serving me.” Sid sighed. “Pity for you, but I think a change must be made.” Gilberto realized he had made the right choice, “You cannot harm my family.” “Oh I can, and I will.” Sid’s face darkened. “No, you can’t. I killed them this afternoon.” Gilberto felt all the fear and sadness rush away. Sid stood up angrily throwing the table aside, “What!”!” “You would do far worse than me, drive them to suicide and despair. This way God will take them home.” Gilberto felt the power of the beast raise him into the air with unseen force. “YOU WILL DO AS YOU ARE TOLD!” Sid yelled, his voice and power shaking the very walls of the shack. “No.” “AHHHHHHHHHH!” Sid screamed as he closed his shaking hands into fists. He threw his arms apart violently, and the unseen force tore through the man he had chosen. Contained within the skin Gilberto’s insides had been ripped apart. Internal bleeding and damage would kill him in just a few more moments. Gilberto fell to the ground. Sid towered over him as he shook with rage and anger over what had occurred. “I will find every person you know, every person you love and make them pay.” Gilberto’s words came out along with blood. The fine spray came out touching his shirt. “El Dios me perdona?” Sid kicked the man, “Tell him I’ll be coming soon, and I’ll burn down the gates and cast him out.” The Dark Messiah kicked open the door to the shack. Malum and the acolytes ran over to see what was happening. “Burn it, burn it all down.” The acolytes went to burn the shack, but Sid held Malum back. “Burn the book on Gilberto. Everyone in fire, everything in flames.” Malum bowed his head, “As you wish master.” The massive figure cloaked in black walked off into the night as the fires behind him began building into the night sky.
