Get Right With The Lord

7 July 2005

Date: Week Of 7-7-2005 Tijuana, Mexico Black Sunday, July 31st In such an affluent area of town, the structure was strange to say the least. Even though it was several miles from any other structure, having such a cheap tin shack would be considered uncouth, but everyone knew the source of the man’s power and influence. He had erected trees on three sides to hide the view of the shack from all directions except the West. Left hand of the damned? Where the sun sets the devils dance? The neighbors ignored the structure, except to make sure that their children never approached it under any circumstances. The area was unholy, dangerous and full of magia negra that could peel their flesh from their bones and curse their souls for eternity. The heat was oppressive, but such days were the only days the dark spirits could be called. They were demons of heat, only listening to those disciples who would submit to the force of heat and flame. Gilberto led his two apprentices to the metal shack, and they gave one last apprehensive look back before stepping inside. The sides of the shack had metal shelves covered with glass jars filled with all manner of ingredients. The middle of the earth had been dug away, and there was a black, cast-iron pot hung over kindling and wood. The two apprentices went to the shelves and began collecting items while Gilberto lit the fire beneath the pot. The brought him the ingredients, placing them on the small table next to the pot and fire. The fire was dangerously hot, and the water was boiling away quickly. Gilberto dropped in the Guinea pepper, Chinese pepper and Indian pepper along with rock salt as the water neared the end. He mumbled words as he did this, calling out to the dark forces as the heat caused his body to pour forth sweat. When the water was all gone, the vapors wafting about his head, he grabbed the metal pail and turned it upside down making sure all of the cow fat and blood ended up in the hot pot. The rotten, spoiled flesh could make a man wretch, but this was not a problem for him since he had been around such things his entire life. Gilberto was motioning over the pot as he spoke in ancient tongues; forgotten languages of people long since overrun and conquered by the onslaught of the powerful. The fat slowly melted, becoming a bubbling mess of grotesque flesh rendered to liquid. The assistants stood on each side holding a small bowl filled with dirt. Gilberto spoke in Spanish now. As he spoke each statement he took a small pinch from the bowl dropping it into the pot, “With this unholy dirt from the resting places of one who cannot rest we curse the man. With the torment of the souls bound in fire we curse the man. With the cries that delight the dark we curse this man. With the sadness and tears of the victims we curse this man. With the pain of the living left destroyed we curse this man. With the shadow of the beasts moving in the darkness we curse this man. With the scream of the unholy birth we curse this man. With the wretchedness of the rotting soul we curse this man. With the power of the dark fires below we curse this man.” There were small bowls of dirt from nine graves of the most evil and foul men from the cemetery. They each were given the same treatment as he worked his way through everything to bring the pot to a boil. After the final pinch of soil, the fire became more intense, with red and black flames now dancing there and powering the heat in the shack. The assistants each poured an entire bowl of dirt into the pot, “The entrance to the cemetery marks his entrance into torment. The unmarked grave marks his place amongst the cursed.” The fires exploded, growing up over the pot and rising into the air. The heat was so bad one of the apprentices almost passed out, but he held steady. Gilberto knew he was being pushed dangerously close to exhaustion and dehydration by the heat, but the price he paid now would save his family. The flames slowly died back down, but they still stayed halfway up the sides of the pot. Gilberto grabbed the broken pieces of beehive, adding them to the pot. The wax was slowly incorporated into the mixture. He stirred it all with a huge wooden spoon. The end was almost near, and so he motioned to the two assistants to get the final crucial ingredient. They stepped out of the shack as Gilberto continued stirring. The apprentices didn’t know that the ritual required something else to curse the man. Normally, you wouldn’t require this, but the man he was attacking was not normal. He pulled out the small envelope from his pocket, and opened it up. He poured out he dried blood and hair into the pot. The souvenirs were from a battle his benefactor had been in with the target, and they would assure that the magic would find him wherever he was. The two apprentices returned leading the fat young calf. They forced the beast into the pit, and tied him down hear the fire. The calf was nervous, but Gilberto grabbed his head and pulled it up. He motioned to his apprentice for the knife. He pulled the blessed steel across the animal’s throat. He held it tightly as the blood hit the flames, pot and most importantly the content of the pot. He held tightly until the animal bled out completely. Dropping the beast, Gilberto threw the knife aside. “Put the wax into the nine molds with wicks, and take them to the unmarked grave tonight. Finish the ceremony on your own, and if anything goes wrong I will feed you to the flames.” The both nodded as Gilberto left. Dallas, Texas Monday, August 1st The lights on the streets outside were on, a glorious tribute to the running of the city he loved. There were always political struggles, but basic services such as street repair and light repair had improved tremendously. The lights were on, with orange glow sprinkling down around the building once beloved by wrestling fans. Behind the closed doors and drawn curtains the memorabilia was still there. Pictures, trophies, title belts and everything else collected by a roster that read like a who’s who in the now defunct league. None of that mattered. The tattered memories were now nothing more than dust in the infinite oblivion as the world moved on, leaving them behind. On the third floor, the gym was still in great condition except for the dust on all the equipment and trash on the floor. Footsteps in the dust, like steps through the snow, led over to the side where the column stood. The column was simply a huge, concrete pillar erected there long ago for the fighters to practice strikes and kicks on during training. Hands had been broken, fingers dislocated and shins rubbed raw thanks to the fanatical training seen on the floor as men tried to become monsters. The theory was simple: Learn to strike without fear of your own body. The man in front of the pillar now had been through that training. Harsh for the wrestling world, and dangerous for a mixed martial arts fighter the training had long ago turned his hands to stone. He looked out of place now though, standing in front of the pillar in dress shoes, suit pants and button-up shirt flying about as he had ripped it open, the white buttons scattered across the floor. Jab, jab, jab, straight right to the head, left hook to the body and right hook to the head. The pattern was familiar to him as he went through the hundred of series he had always used for boxing. He had dress shoes and suit pants on, and so he decided not to kick the pillar. Everything seemed to be falling apart, and he found himself ignoring everything he had relied on before in his life. The loneliness was something he swore he would accept. He knew the path would be difficult, but then it had all begun to weigh down on him that afternoon when he was making an appearance at the charity luncheon. He was helping Mission Arlington by donating a sizable chunk of money to their Women’s Shelter, and he was working to raise money from other local businessmen and celebrities. Everything was going well, and the money was coming in freely. At four o’clock, after the long luncheon, Jake was in the back with one of the Mission Arlington representatives Howie James. His wife and four children joined Howie to meet the professional fighter. Jake signed autographs and posed for pictures, but the entire time his heart was breaking. He finished up, and ran across town to a restaurant he enjoyed called Portofinos. Jake started in the bar while they prepared a table for him since he didn’t have a reservation. Portofinos was a strange little restaurant and bar. They always got fantastic reviews, but the prices were a little high considering the location was the corner of a strip mall where eight smaller shops had once been. The interior had been redone in creamy whites and rich, warm wood tones. The interior was the opposite of the exterior where the prefabricated buildings were always the same no matter where you went. He enjoyed the fresh yet inviting look of the inside. Jake enjoyed going to Portofinos because he liked the food, the atmosphere, the owners and perhaps most importantly they stocked his scotch. As Jake sipped his fourth scotch, everything he had lost began appearing before him, as though memories had become walking ghosts to haunt him. Every single night before he went to bed he remembered seeing the bloody, broken body of his nieces and nephews. He hadn’t been able to stop their violation, their slaughter or their mutilation. Thinking of them made him think of his newly discovered niece and nephew by Pitt, but he was denied any access to them because he would endanger them. Of course, the rest of his family was lost to him as well. Jake had traded his own access to his family for their safety. He had left their support, their comfort and their encouragement behind in the Big Easy. Jake had walked out with a smile on his face, knowing he had finally set his family free to enjoy life in secrecy, and with that concealment they had security. Unfortunately, as life became even more dangerous and hectic he found himself wanting to turn to his family. No one remained he could trust? And he didn’t want to endanger anyone else by bringing them into his confidence? Jake finished the scotch, and asked for another round of hard drink from the bartender. Another few chips of ice were added to his tumbler, and then a fresh round of the expensive liquor he preferred. The Big Dawg slowly sipped the scotch as he kept thinking about everything he no longer had in his life. Pitt was injured, and he was angry about that on more levels than he was ready to deal with. Jake wanted another shot at his brother inside the ring; Jake wanted to make his brother tap out; and Jake wanted to simply talk to his brother like friends again. Everything was lost to him now. The emotions seemed out of control inside of him. He didn’t know why his restraints weren’t keeping it all at bay. He had always controlled the emotions, at worst allowing the fury within to fuel his rage and power as he struck out at the world and his enemies. Now, those emotions were out of control inside of him, working against him. He couldn’t help but face all of the losses and the pain. The scotch, his friend at drowning his emotions for a long time, was now not working for him. Jake was sipping on the scotch when Julio came over, “Jake, we are still trying to work you into a table. We were just overbooked tonight, and there were no cancellations. We will have something for you within the hour.” “Within the hour?” Julio was surprised by the length of time it would take to get a seat. “Yes, but we will get you in.” He assured him. Jake downed the rest of his scotch, “No, don’t worry about it. I should have called, and there’s no reason to make anyone else wait. I’ll come back another night and have bite to eat.” “What about taking something home with you?” Julio offered. “I can have the chef’s fix it up right now.” “No, I don’t need it. Just call me a cab Julio. I need to be driven to the house.” Jake smiled as he took one of the slivers of ice into his mouth and crunched it loudly. “Of course.” Julio left to get him the cab. That had all led him back here to the former headquarters, and his personal stock of scotch in the bar. He had downed another half bottle before coming up here to test the pillar once again. So far, he felt like he was winning the battle as he had caused more than once crack in the formerly pristine surface. Jake continued the wild swings, as he wanted to break the pillar. He kicked the base even in his dress shoes, and he swore he felt the pillar move under the impact. A knee to the side did crack the smooth concrete. He let everything flow through his arms and legs as he tried to topple the pillar. The strength he had tapped into when he needed it before began coming out, and small bits of concrete flew off, pelting his body as he attacked the pillar. Finally, Jake ducked to avoid the imagined punch and then slammed his right fist into the pillar. The earlier cracks suddenly expanded and all ran together. Jake took a step back from the pillar as the middle slanted sideways and the top half fell off in two pieces away from him. There was a smug look on his drunken face when the voice cut through the darkness of the floor. “Did that make you proud of yourself?” The footsteps echoed through the empty training floor. “Beating up the pillar is something we’ve all done at one time or another.” Jake Hix turned around. He couldn’t see the source of the voice, but he caught movement off to the left. Jake pulled off his white shirt, throwing it aside. “Who is that” You boys finally decide to quit following me and come challenge me!”!” The voice came again along with the footsteps, but the man stayed in the dark. “I’ve been wondering how far gone you were. I knew you had lied to me, but this just means things are worse than I thought, or your sanity has slipped.” “Come on!” Jake screamed as spittle flew out from his maw. The man stepped forward until all but his head and shoulders could be seen. He looked sleek in the chocolate suit, and he was obviously a strong man thanks to the powerful build. “You seem like you’re looking for a fight here. You always were though. You always wanted to fight to prove yourself to the world, no matter who you had to go after. You even attacked your own family.” Jake had heard enough. He ran forward, and swung a right hook at where he thought the man’s head would be. The man blocked the blow, and flipped him over onto the concrete floor with a judo flip so easily that Jake wondered for a moment if the figure was a figment of his imagination. He scrambled up, and saw the man in the light now. His long hair was blocking a clear view of his face. Jake charged at the man, and he got flipped again, but this time the man held onto his arm. Jake hit hard again, and caught a glance at the man’s face briefly. “Reed?” He asked confused. The man dropped back locking Jake’s arm up in a Japanese Armbar. The hold was perfectly locked in as the leg was draped across his neck. “Sensational” Stephen Reed had his trademark submission locked in tightly. He felt Jake struggling against the hold, but he didn’t relent. “Give it up Jake.” Ignoring the words, Jake tried to roll into the hold to lessen the pressure and counter. Stephen raised his leg up, then caught Jake across the jaw with his heel kick. Jake fell flat again. The pain continued for several more moments, and then it was gone. Stephen let go of the hold and rolled off of Jake. He kept careful eyes locked on him as he wiped the dust off his tailored suit. “I’m sending you the cleaning bill for this Jake.” Jake Hix was rubbing his arm on the ground. He ignored the pain as he rolled over and stood up. Jake had dangerous eyes locked on the old family friend. He was still angry at the interruption and the attack. “What the fuck are you doing here!”!” Reed held up a warning finger, “You’re drunk Jake. I could stand toe-to-toe with you on a normal day before you took me down, but right now I’ll throw you around like a three hundred pound rag doll. Back off Big Dawg.” Jake thought about it, but then he sidestepped him and headed over to the remains of the pillar. He kicked a piece of the concrete aside as he looked down at the floor. “Why are you here?” “You lied to me. Pitt isn’t on vacation, and I found out most of the business is now in Hawk’s name for day-to-day. You all forgot as a partner I get notified of that.” Stephen looked Jake up and down, “Then when I talked to Hawk he let me in on some of what was going on. Then Julio called me because you looked in a bad way. He told me where you were headed. Jake came rushing at him, but Stephen moved out of the way easily. No contact was made as the big man staggered by him. Stephen shook his head, “What ever happened to playful banter” You used to be able to stand tit for tat in a verbal war. You’re off here. What is going on with you Big Dawg?” Jake Hix didn’t turn around, “Nothing’s bothering me. I’ve got a lot on my mind.” “You never could lie worth a damn when you were drunk, and it gets worse when you’re emotional. Your family is in danger, that much I know.” Stephen watched for a sign of what was going on in his friend. They had never been extremely close because Jake associated him with all the problems he had experienced with Pitt. None of that mattered now. Hawk told him when they spoke that Jake needed a friend, and Stephen could fill that role, at least for a little while. Jake whirled around with contempt in his eyes, “Don’t talk to me like you know anything! They’re safe! We couldn’t protect them before, but I bought their safety! They are hidden away where not even I can find them.” “So you are alone then.” “So what!”!” Jake stumbled sideways. The alcohol was hitting him now. “When haven’t I been alone SSR” Huh? When I came into the league you and the rest of The Syndicate cast me aside like some ugly stray dog you didn’t want dirtying up your pretty little place. I had to find my own way! I was alone then too!” “No, you weren’t?” “I was!” Jake was laughing now. The laughter was like an angry spite shot out at the man before him and the faded images of everyone else he cursed as he spoke. “I remember when I was sent down for development and Pitt said I wasn’t ready! He never thought I was ready! He never thought I could do it! I showed him! I showed you! I showed everyone!” Stephen let his voice raise a little, “Oh sure you did. You beat me, you beat Pitt, you attacked Pitt over and over to try and destroy him. You never even understood what happened. Even when you realized some of what happened, you never got it Jake. You’ve been living behind that little cardboard cutout of yourself you show to the world for so long it has become you!” “Shut-up!” Jake fell sideways again before catching himself. “Why” No one ever wanted to talk to you about this stuff. Pitt always said that you would come to see it on your own, or he would take care of it. Of course, now that he’s gone he can’t take care of it, can he? You need to crawl out of your dark little hole and quit wallowing there.” Jake walked right up to SSR. He peered down at the man who challenged him with those words, “So what, you think you can set me straight” You think you can put me in my place? Pitt couldn’t, no one else could and I don’t see a washed up little punk like you doing it.” “See, you don’t want to have to talk about this at all. You’re trying to turn this into a fight, but that’s not what this is about Jake. You’re family is slaughtered. You’re still holding onto all the old problems of the past, and now you need to begin seeing the truth. You need to begin using the emotions for your own good.” Stephen Reed responded. Jake backed away, “What do you know anyway old man?” “I know enough.” Jake Hix began walking away from the area. He crossed the floor to the stairwell. He took the stairs slowly as he headed back down to the bar they had utilized for entertaining many years ago. Johnny was some skinny little punk, likely a meth addict on a bad day. He actually reminded Jake of a strung out hair band guitarist in a bad video. The jaundice was a sure sign along with the bones pressing against his weak flesh, “I don’t know who you think you are, but I don’t take shit from anyone.” “You know, right up until this moment I don’t think I was sure who I was. His own foul breath was nothing compared to the odor coming off the man. Hair matted together and unwashed skin reeked of vomit and disease. “You think that little fucking pistol in the small of your back is going to do a damn thing” I’ll shove it and the hand holding it up your ass before I make you pull the trigger. You ever come back, and then we’ll continue this conversation under less hospitable circumstances.” “You don’t make the law here! I go wherever I want when?” Jake had heard enough. He wasn’t going to stand there and listen to anymore of this. He dipped his shoulder then sent the man into the air with a powerful uppercut. He flew back with his bag of bones body ten feet before slamming into the unclean floor. He was in shock for a moment, but then he began reaching for the gun in the waist of his jeans. Jake walked forward, and stepped down onto the man’s arm before he could ever pull it clear. He pressed down as the man screamed. “Let go.” “I let go! I let go!” Jake kicked the man over onto his stomach. The gun was there on the ground. Jake kicked it away then looked over to the bar, “Tony, this motherfucker ever shows up again then you call me because I’ll end his miserable fucking life.” “Whatever you say Jake.” Tony agreed. Jake kneeled down right next to Johnny’s ear, “You hear me Johnny” You just lost your pass, next time I exact a fucking toll like God used to in the Old Testament days. Heads on pikes bitch, heads on pikes.” Jake walked over to the bar. He pulled the folded bills out of his wallet and dropped two hundred down on the bar, “For the trouble.” He picked up the scotch, but as he looked at the amber liquor he remembered everything Stephen had told him. He needed to move beyond some of this shit. He needed to begin coping in some other way. Jake set the scotch back down and left the bar. The sun blinded him again, but he ignored it. He motioned a cab over. They pulled up, then a little girl came up to him. The girl was Vanessa from inside, “Hey man, thanks for that. Johnny’s a real sweet guy, but he hasn’t had a good few days.” “The guy’s a loser and a drug addict. Stay away from him.” Jake said sternly. “Maybe I could come with you.” Vanessa looked up at him with doe like eyes as she rubbed her lithe, young body against his. “I know how to treat you right.” “You don’t even know how to treat yourself with respect little girl.” Jake opened the cab door for her. “Get inside. Get inside right now.” Vanessa was used to following instructions, so she went right inside the cab. Jake leaned in and passed a hundred dollar bill to the driver, “Take her to Arlington. Take her to Arlington First Baptist Church, and behind there is Mission Arlington.” “You got it!” The cab driver had just made a huge tip. Jake looked at Vanessa, “You ask for Howie James when you get there. Tell him Jake Hix sent you, and he’ll get you a place to stay and get cleaned up. Take care of yourself.” He closed the door, and motioned for another cab. He slipped inside, “Portofinos in Arlington.” “Where is that?” The driver asked. “Collins and I-20.” “You got it.” The driver pulled away. Jake dialed Stephen Reed’s cell phone, “I got your note.” “Are you ready for some help?” Stephen asked. Jake sighed, “I am. I didn’t drink today. I went to the bar, but?” “No explanations needed Jake. You said you needed help, and that’s all you needed to say. No judgments anymore Jake. We’ll get you back on track with what you need to do.” “I’ve still got all this’anger inside. I just, I can’t control it anymore Stephen. After our talk it just keeps boiling up.” Jake felt the anger there again. Stephen laughed, “The best thing about your new profession is you can take out all of that anger and aggression, and the fans just love it a little more. I’ll see you at my gym at three.” “Good.” Jake hung up the phone.
