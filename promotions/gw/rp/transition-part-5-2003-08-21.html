Transition, part 5

21 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 21, 2003 All hell breaking loose happened kind of like this: Yoshiko launched a spinning heel kick at Danjuro, which he rapidly blocked, and followed by pushing her down to the steel. Danjuro swung a fist down at Yoshiko, but she rolled back onto her shoulders, dodging the punch by inches and leaving Danjuro with nothing but cold, hard steel to hit. He growled as pain lanced up his arm, then howled when Yoshiko kicked him in the face and sprang to her feet. The two of them stared each other down for a moment, and a glimmer of respect flashed in their eyes, before they launched themselves at each other again. That's all how 'Shiko told me it happened, anyway. I couldn't really see it, as I was busy with a whole hell of a lot of ninja. The black-clad throwbacks to medieval times had all converged on me, following their orders to the letter. With more fists and feet than I cared to count at the time coming at me, I had to let go of thought, let go of conscience, let go of the science of fighting. I had to become an animal, raw power in a man's skin, acting on instinct alone, and letting savagery reign in my skull. They wanted to hurt the Steel Lion? Fine. We'd see how they liked the Steel Lion when he was out of his cage. I lashed out savagely, driving my fingers into one man's eyes, my boot into another's groin. I felt punches and kicks slamming into my body, but refused to recognize the pain. I refused to let anything slow me down as I grabbed two ninja by their throats and chokeslammed them onto the steel. A maelstrom of fists and feet, I lashed out at anything that moved. Then, two of the ninja managed to grab my arms, and rushed me down the ramp, slamming my back into the edge of the ring. A third immediately followed, driving his shoulder into my stomach with a vicious spear. Again he slammed his shoulder into my gut, and this time I spat up blood. He came in for a third spear -- but this time I sprung up, leaving him to slam his head into the edge of the ring. As I came back down, I jerked my arms in, smashing the ninja who'd been restraining me into each other. Stunned, they let go, and I slid under the ring. One of the ninja was foolish enough to stick his head under the ring looking for me -- he got a facefull of ladder for his efforts. I sent the fifteen-foot ladder flying out from under the ring, catching a few ninja at their knees, then slid out behind it, snatching it up and swinging it in wide arcs, sending ninja sprawling. Roaring like my namesake, I rushed up the ramp, bashing more ninja out of my way with the ladder. Yoshiko and Danjuro had been brawling for a while now, and were definitely a bit worse for wear. Danjuro's jacket was gone, and his bare arms were covered in bright red welts from blocking Yoshiko's kicks. Yoshiko's hair had come out of its tight bun, and stuck to her sweat-slick skin. Blood trickled from her split lip, and she seemed to be favoring her ribs. The two of them circled each other slowly, watching for signs of weakness. It was then that I brought the ladder down, catching Danjuro's head between two of the rungs, and jerked it back, smacking the steel into his face. As he staggered back, I caught him in a reverse DDT, smashing his head into the steel ramp. 'Yoshiko-chan,' I said, between gasps for air, 'lets get the hell out of here.' 'Not a bad idea,' replied Yoshiko, wiping some of the blood away from her lip. I nodded, then turned, and threw the ladder down the ramp, catching several ninja who had been advancing from behind me. As Danjuro began to come around, I grabbed Yoshiko's hand, and we ran. *** We burst into the lobby of IWL arena, running for the main entrance -- and slamming into the locked and barred steel doors. We were locked in. I slumped against the wall and let out a long, slow breath. You already know this part. 'So, Yosh,' I said, as I spat blood onto the pavement, 'explain to me again how this was supposed to be a good time?' 'Well,' she replied, wiping sweat-slick hair out of her eyes, 'what I had PLANNED was a big surprise going away party for you with the rest of the IWL workers.' 'So,' I stretched my neck and heard a few pops, 'you knew nothing about the ninja?' Yoshiko gave me that look. You know the one. 'No, Lion. I didn't know about the freaking ninja.' 'Okay,' I said, rising to my feet, 'so we're locked into the arena with Genbu's crazy son and a small army of hired ninja goons. We have no way of calling for help, and fighting them all off isn't a viable option because, well, they're a small army. What's the plan?' Yoshiko just stared at me. 'What?' I said, shrugging, 'you always have a plan.' 'I've got a plan,' said Yoritomo Danjuro, as he stepped out into the lobby with the ladder in one hand. Three dozen ninja streamed out behind him, forming a semicircle around us. 'My plan is, I'm going to take this ladder, and beat the two of you with it until you're unidentifiable. Then, I'm going to drag your bloodied -- but still living -- carcasses back into Tokyo, and present them to my father as a gift. And then, I hope, he'll let me throw the two of you in a gutter somewhere to rot. And then I'm going to laugh. And perhaps have dinner.' I spat at Danjuro's feet. 'Danjuro,' I said, flashing a bloodied grin, 'why don't you just shut up and fight already, you insufferable little daddy-loving son of a bitch?' 'Fine,' shouted Danjuro, his face turning red with rage, 'if that's how you want it? RIP THEM APART!' Yoshiko and I fell into fighting stances, watching the ninja intently as they closed in on us. We were ready to go down fighting. But when we heard the roar of an engine behind us, we knew better than to stay in its way. As Yoshiko and I leapt away, the main doors of the arena burst off their hinges. A black van, now with a cracked grill and smashed windshield, skidded to a stop in the lobby, Danjuro barely leaping out of its way. 'Wow,' said Yoshiko, 'I never thought I'd see anything like that that wasn't scripted.' The driver's side door of the van swung open, and out stepped a hulking man who looked like he'd been chiseled out of solid rock. 'Wow,' he said, 'I never thought I'd get to do that unless it was scripted.' My mouth hung agape for the second time in a day. 'Hida Tsuneo. What the hell are you doing here, Stone Crab?' 'Well,' said Tsuneo, absent-mindedly grabbing a ninja that had decided to attack him and flinging him across the lobby, 'after Genbu forbid us to come to your party, we figured something fishy was going on. Thought you might need our help.' I laughed, and stepped over some rubble to clasp Tsuneo's hand. 'Who's 'we', Crab?' Tsuneo smirked, and rapped his hand three times on the van's side door. It slid open with a low rumble. Out stepped a shirtless bald man covered in tattoos, a portly man in jeans and an ill-fitting T-shirt, a pale-skinned beauty with long, jet-black hair, and a small, average-looking man in a gray business suit. Respectively, they were 'The Black Dragon' Hitomi Kenta, 'Khan of the Ki-Rin' Moto Yotogi, 'The Snow Phoenix' Shiba Sakura, and 'The Shadow Scorpion' Yogo Itoju. They were some of the finest competitors the IWL had to offer. And together with Yoshiko, Tsuneo, and myself, they had formed The Seven Thunders, the most dominant stable in the League's history. Though I couldn't really see their faces, I was pretty sure several of the ninja blanched. 'Damn it!' growled Danjuro, hauling himself up off the ground and brushing dust and bits of debris off his skin, 'it doesn't matter how many of them there are, just do your jobs and PUT THEM DOWN!' The ninja started to move in, but cautiously. I looked around at the faces of my six closest friends. 'Ready guys?' I asked. 'And eager.' replied Tsuneo, cracking his knuckles and wearing a predatory grin. 'It is we who need be ready, but they.' said Kenta. Yotogi belched loudly. 'Lets crack their friggin' skulls already.' 'The fortunes are definitely with us.' said Sakura, as she tied her long hair back. Itoju simply smiled -- unnervingly so. Yoshiko stretched her long legs. 'Lets show them what the Thunders can do.' she said. I smiled. 'Time to remind the management who makes them all their money, guys.' 'Bring it ON!' shouted Danjuro, holding the ladder out in front of him firmly. And we did.
