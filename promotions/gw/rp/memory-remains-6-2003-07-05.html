Memory Remains #6

5 July 2003

Date: 7503 (One minute you're on top) Next you're not watch it drop (Making your heart stop) Just before you hit the floor (One minute you're on top) Next you're not missed your shot (Making you're heart stop) You think you've won (And then its all gone) John Zero and Deacon Holmes cruise down the road in Zero’s black Mitsubishi Eclipse Spyder convertible, the top down, wind buffeting their bodies, as Hit the Floor by Linkin Park filters through the car’s sound system. Deacon is yelling over the music, something to the effect of the smooth ride and the tightness of the audio system. Zero just nods his head in agreement, having no idea of the inner workings of the audio system, nor the engine, but did know how much it cost for the installation and the maintenance of the systems. Zero dropped the car back down to fourth gear, giving him the extra get up and go that he needed to slide around a few cars in the way. John Zero liked to drive, it relaxed him, put him at one within his universe. It allowed him the rare moment of inner peace that occurred less and less these days. Not since Lily. Stop it. Don’t. Not here. Not now. Allow me this moment. You’ve taken everything else from me. Let me keep this one ounce of sanity that I have left. As the feeling passed, Zero smiled an even rarer smile as he slid the gearshift into fifth, idling back. As he drove, Zero looked around, catching a glimpse of an alleyway on his left and the happenings therein. “Hold on,” Zero said in a slightly louder than normal tone of voice. “What’SHIT!” came the reply from Deacon as Zero slammed the brakes, cutting the wheel hard to the left, the car spinning around haphazardly, other cars swerving to avoid a wreck. Letting off, and slamming the gearshift down, Zero spun out in a burst of smoke, passing by alleys to find the right one. Finding the one he was looking for, he came to a normal stop, pulling into a parking space on the side of the road. “The fuck was that all about”!”” exclaimed Holmes as Zero leapt out of the car, running towards the alley. “Fuck this, I’m waiting my ass here.” Zero ran down the alleyway to where he had seen two men assaulting another person. They were still there, and so was the victim. Both assaulters had their pants around their ankles, and knives in their hands, trying their best to remove the skirt from the woman they were attempting to rape. Zero came to a stop as both men looked up to see who was approaching. “What do we have here?” one of the men asked, who was loosing most of the hair on his head. “Looks like a genuine hero,” the other answered, who had lost most of his teeth somewhere. “Let her go,” Zero said quietly. “Or what?” the balding man asked with a sneer. “Or else. Were you expecting some other kind of threat?” Zero asked calmly. “Let’s kill him, fuck the girl, and rob “em both.” “Aye.” Zero pulled out both guns from underneath his sports coat, firing each weapon once, each bullet striking the ground in front of the attackers. “Let’s not and say we did, shall we?” Footsteps echoed throughout the alley. “What the fuck was that?” “Ah, Deacon. Impeccable timing. If you would collect the young lady over there and take her to the car, I will be along momentarily.” Deacon looked from Zero to the two men, their arms raised in front of their chests, pants still around their ankles, to the woman on the ground, crying quietly to herself. Deacon walked over to the woman, whispering softly to her, gathering her up into his arms and trotting back down the alley to the car. “Is this where you tell us never to do it again and let us go?” the toothless attacker asked, flashing a toothless smile. Zero fired twice, once through the toothless man’s mouth as he smiled and once into the chest of the balding man. “Something like that.” Zero took a few steps forward and fired a few extra rounds into each man’s body before holstering the two weapons and jogging back down the alley. The black Eclipse came into view, with Deacon behind the wheel and the girl in the backseat. Zero jumped over the closed door and landed in the passenger’s seat. “Go! Go! Go!” Zero exclaimed as Deacon popped the clutch, spinning the wheels as he merged into traffic. “What the fuck was that!” Holmes yelled as Zero turned to look at the girl. “You okay?” Zero asked the girl, who nodded slightly. “Sure, ask the girl if she’s okay, no mind to good old wheelman Deacon Holmes, who’s about to have a mother fucking heart attack over here.” “You did good, Deacon.” “Yeah, whatever I did. You shot those two, didn’t you?” Zero didn’t answer the question, but gave Holmes an address. “Can you find it?” “Yeah.” Zero looked back at the girl, who looked back at him with emerald green eyes. Lily’s eyes. I stayed with Lily in the hospital after work in the afternoon. When I got home in the evenings, I worked on my plan to kill Bryce. At first, I considered just breaking into his house late at night and getting him then. But after some careful consideration, the decision was made that Bryce’s family probably had beefed up security since my last visit. So that plan was out. Then I thought why not just blow the bastard up? Car bomb? Nah, too many innocent people could be around. Letter bomb? Nope, could be delivered and opened by the wrong person. After some thinking, I remembered a recipe for a generic bomb that I saw on the Internet. You evaporate a couple of drops of gasoline in a glass jar and then add some potassium permanganate to it, which is found in snakebite kits. To detonate this handy little device, just throw and break the jar. The only problem was getting Bryce alone. I kept close to Lily, watching her recover, helping when I could. She was determined to do it all herself. I was there to help if she needed it. I also kept an ear to the ground, listening for any information that might help me with the “Bryce” problem. Waiting for that one bit of info to set me off into my course of revenge. It turns out that Bryce’s parents were once the travelers. They have circled the globe on more that one occasion. Be it out of love or for old times sake, they decided to take off once again, which provided the perfect opportunity to act. Finding out the day of departure was not a problem. It was a well-known fact that there would be a huge party at Bryce’s after his parents left. It was scheduled to take place about two hours after they were gone. That gave me two hours to kill him. With about a week to go before Bryce’s D-day, I decided to try out my little homemade bomb. Driving out to the woods and walking a couple of miles in, I selected the perfect tree, one that was old and set aside from all the others. The bomb was already made, placed into a piece of foam in a cardboard box. It came out of the set foam with a sucking sound. I took aim at the tree and hurled the jar of bottled death. It sailed through the air and missed the tree completely. I was already backpedaling through the woods. It didn’t matter that I missed. The jar shattered against a rock. The air was filled with an artificial thunder. The ground shook. The tree had been knocked over. I imagined the jar working its magic on Bryce. The last week went quickly. I spent most of the time with Lily, helping her when she wanted it and just being there for her. The rest of the time I spent prepping, getting everything in order. I made another bomb, slightly bigger than before. I also got some bullets for my snub-nosed .357 magnum, just in case I needed to make a hasty retreat. I left my apartment early the morning of Bryce’s parent’s departure. The timing of this excursion had to be perfect. There was almost no room for error. I parked my car in almost the exact same spot that I had left it last time. I hiked through the woods, but didn’t venture any further. I only carried with me some old binoculars that were bought at a yard sale. I left the bomb in the car. Climbing a tree and settling into a high and sturdy branch, I watched the front of the house. A new looking silver Volvo was pulled near the front door, it’s trunk open. I watched a short, pudgy man make trips between the house and the car, pausing every once in a while to wipe sweat from his brow. I presumed that this was Bryce’s father. The man disappeared into the house and reappeared with a middle-aged brown haired woman, Bryce’s mother perhaps, on his arm. They walked down the steps, towards the Volvo, stopping halfway and turning to the house, saying words that I couldn’t hear. I focused on the door. There he was. The man of the hour. Bryce. They were probably giving him last minute instructions. Or threats. Maybe both, considering their son. I watched the couple get into the car and pull away, leaving Bryce alone. I refocused on him. A smile formed on his face. Or was it a sneer? If my grip on the binoculars got any tighter, the lenses would’ve shattered. I looked back for the car and saw it hitting the road in a scattering of dust and gravel. ShowTime. I’m gonna wipe that smile, that sneer, off of Bryce’s face. Permanently.
