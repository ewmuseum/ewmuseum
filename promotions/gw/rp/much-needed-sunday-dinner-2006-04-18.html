Much needed Sunday dinner

18 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-18-06 The past month and a half had been extremely stressful for Brick...to put it mildly. It all began at Reload with Jake Hix and his brothers, known collectively as the Texas Wrecking Crew,severely beating Brick after the end of the main-event. The first edition of Fusion saw Brick attempt to take payback against the Wrecking Crew who were facing his friend Jesse Briggs in a handicap match. Attempting to hit Pitt Hix with a steel chair, he instead hit Jesse. He was later involved in a huge brawl at the end of the show involving Hix, Mike Biddle, the Wrecking Crew, the Back Alley Brawler, Josh, Ken Partridge, Jr., and the Hammer Brothers. The following and most recent edition of Fusion saw Brick, after much prodding from his partners and a rather violent assault on Michael Cervantes, reveal the mystery of who he saw in the stands : The Scarecrow Jonathan Davis. On top of this, an angry Jesse Briggs came to the ring, confronting Brick on his mistake, blaming him for costing him the match. Their altercation in the ring led to Brandi Sights booking them in a street fight on the next Fusion. Brick still had the ten-man tag match to worry about. Now known collectively as Syndicate Rules: Extreme, Hix, Biddle, Brawler and the Crew assaulted the five men backstage before the match could begin. They won by forfeit. After all these events, Brick figured only one thing could ease his mind: Sunday dinner at his mother's house. Admittedly, Brick had been missing a few dinners. He definitely wanted to change that. The table was filled with food: roasted chicken, macaroni and cheese, lasagna, collard greens, candied yams, ham, cornbread, mashed potatoes with gravy. At the head of the table sat Coretta. Brick sat to her right. To her left sat Monica and Kyle. At Monica's right sat the twins, Mary Alice and Randall James, now 13 months old. 'How are you feeling?' asked Coretta. 'Better,' Brick replied while taking a bite out of a drumstick. 'Takes more than a bunch of punks like Hix an' Biddle an' them to keep me down. Of course, now, Hix has given me a whole lot more incentive to beat the mess out of him at High Octane.' Meanwhile, Monica was performing what seemed to be an exercise in futility: getting little R.J. to eat their vegetables. 'Come on, R.J.,' she said, trying to feed the little toddler to no avail. He merely pressed his lips shut and turned his head away every time she tried to get him to eat. 'That's the thing about little boys,' said Coretta. 'They're the most hardheaded.' She then turned to Brick. 'Speaking of hardheaded boys, Jamal, why didn't you tell us it was Jonathan Davis you saw that week?' 'Becuase I knew you'd worry if I did.' 'Of course, I'm going to worry. I've seen what Salaok did to you, what he put you through, and now his older brother, who you say is worse, comes back.' 'Is Jonathan Davis really worse than Salaok?' asked Kyle before taking a bite of lasagna. 'Who do you think taught Salaok all he knows? Then there's the matter of him putting me in a coma. That's what started the whole mess with the Davises.' 'What?' Kyle replied, nearly choking on his lasagna. Monica smacked him on the back to keep him from gagging. 'When was this?' 'A while back, before Monica met you. It was right after a match. He hit me in the back of the head with a crowbar...repeatedly.' Brick then turned his head to show Kyle a vertical scar down the back of his head. Kyle just sat, staring at Brick. 'Oh, man.' 'I was comatose for a couple of days.' 'And you don't want me to worry,' Coretta interjected. 'Look, if anybody needs to deal with Davis, it's me. Of course now, I got him, Hix and Jesse to deal with.' 'Yeah, I missed the first Fusion,' said Kyle. 'Did you really hit him with a steel chair?' 'I wasn't trying to hit him,' Brick said, defensively, after a sip of green tea. 'I was trying to hit Pitt Hix.' 'That makes it better.' 'Hey, the Hix brothers stomped on me while I couldn't defend myself. They need to be thankful I only had a steel chair.' 'So, now you're facing Jesse in a match,' said Coretta, 'never mind that this all could have been avoided.' 'I tried to apologize, but Jesse wasn't hearing it.' 'And why do you suppose that is?' 'Because he's a stubborn jackass, Ma. Always has been.' 'Watch your language in front of the babies,' chided Monica. 'Sorry.' 'Did Jesse ask for your help?' 'No, he didn't.' 'So, do you understand why he was upset?' 'I was only trying to help.' 'The road to hell is paved with what?' 'Point taken,' Brick shrugged, 'but like I said, Ma, Jesse's stubborn.' 'Reminds me of someone else I know,' Coretta replied, slyly. 'Anyway,' Brick continued, half ignoring his mother's comment, 'the only thing he understands is gettin' his ass beat.' 'Uh, language?' said Monica. 'Uh, feed your babies?' Brick replied. 'What do you think I've been trying to do?' 'Give him to me,' Coretta said, holding her arms out to take him. 'Now, let's just enjoy dinner. I wanted to forget my problems for at least tonight.'
