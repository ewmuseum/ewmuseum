The Cell: Happenings Within

11 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-11-04 Prologue: Jesse was seen pleading innocent in his last interview with the police. Unfortunately the incident only furthered the police to believe Jesse was the killer. He fell to his knees and cried as he told them to lock him away, for he’d never kill someone he loved. Now Jesse is in his cell, on probation for the acts he had done during the interview. He’s not allowed to leave the cell for a day, but that doesn’t stop Jesse from speaking out loud to himself. He’s got a lot on his mind and without a person to speak to or a piece of paper to write it down, his words float freely from his mouth. With their being a tour in the facility of how it runs Jesse is one of the prime examples of how the facility is used. Will Jesse be able to conduct himself at an appropriate level? Or will the Global Champion become constrained of all the stress he’s endured and burst out? Story Continues: ‘It’s been too long since I’ve seen daylight. Through these hollow walls my eyes stray to glimpse. Even a peak at the beautiful sunlight would leave me happy, yet these walls allow not. Beyond my reach is freedom, and though I can’t do it myself in here, I’ll find a way to grasp it. Freedom surrounds me in the ring, and when I come to evolution I’ll give them what I must to win my freedom. I can only foretell that the man who killed my wife waits inside a locker room hiding his guilt with a smile of nervousness. Behind his eyes will be guilt, and past that will be his sorrow, and finally when we’ve surpassed those will come his fight… The fight to survive when I tell him what she meant to me before he took her away… I’m no god, even if I am ranked as one. I feel the same as every man does. Pain is no substitute for pleasure to me, it’s a natural cause of weakness. But now… that pain will become my fuel… Not the fuel that drives me insane, or the fuel that tells me to get on with life, but the fuel to get revenge on him… Bitter sweetness of blood will trickle down my hands as I close them and deliver one punch after another… Burying my knuckles in his face I’ll keep giving them until it’s no longer visible. Then I’ll pick him up and… do as he did to her. Motionless he’ll lie on the ground as the paramedics load him into an ambulance. I’ll follow them as I did with her, and I’ll watch him be taped up and taken away. Then I’ll see him in the hospital be treated as she was… The tubes coming out from all over her body, the bags of blood and instruments lying in pans that were used to keep her alive. That’s when it’ll end… You’re body will stop simultaneously to the respirator and it’ll all go flat lined. The only difference will be that my tears won’t be of sorrow or fear, but of joy and excitement. Because whoever you are, wherever you are, I’ll find you, and I’ll…’ Jesse mutters this all in the corner of the cell but dares not to end his words, for one wrong word could make him the number one suspect. The hallways had been empty all day and Jesse had to deal with looking at the floor or ceiling to take his mind off of the loneliness. Jesse’s eyes were blood shot from the sleepless nights. He had no window, no television, nothing but the empty cell to occupy him. From time to time he’d do workouts. But besides them he laid in his cotton bed that had springs poking out in places just staring up at the wall and remembering the times he’d been okay. Finally however the cell would come alive, as a group of news reporters and cameramen came by during a tour. Jesse was a prime example of how the facility worked, for even though much the same as a rehab center it worked with potential killers or people who were danger and may still be in an open case. Jesse’s file spoke for itself. Running away from home at the age of 17 only to be busted and put in jail for 7 years for assault and robbery. Later on he’d be found as the person who killed his mother and father yet the evidence presented during his trial proved nothing and he got out of prison in 6 months. Finally his biggest crime Jesse committed was the attempt of suicide. Suicide was and still is a crime that can put people away. For eight long months Jesse spent his life in rehab fighting through emotional trauma. It was surely a case file that proved to be a good source for a murder suspect. Even if he’d had an alibi, his record told the police other things. ’The police didn’t want to hear the truth… They wanted to find things out themselves. Try and prove something to the people that they knew how to do their jobs. Nobody wanted to hear a killer was lose, hell for all they knew it could be the president of the united states… But nobody cares to interview him and see where he was on the date of the attack. Besides that, I’m intimidating… People like Ice, Josh, Train… and many more; they’d say I did it because they don’t want to face me. I’m some type of prodigy to Global Wrestling. They fear me, and the only way they can beat me is by the use of interferences or tactics they know will take my mind off the matter. I could’ve beat Josh… He’s nothing compared to Chris Storm, Patti Anderson, and Apocalypse. But my mind wasn’t on the match, it was on who killed my future to be wife. Given the time and date, I know it was a fluke, and that’s all that matters. When it comes down to it, I’ll just have to look at Train as the man that killed… Jesse stops himself from saying her name, because every time he thinks of her his mind begins to lose focus and he breaks down. Closing his eyes he lies their just staring up, not much really matters to him right now. Everything he’s done is only steps away from being taken away from him. A file and a stamp that says he’s guilty will erase Twelve years of hard work and dedication. How that’s possible is just as absurd a question as who’s free? Everyone is held back by the economy, and even though a man proves he’s done no wrong, he will be treated as he is until the ‘man’ says otherwise. Life can’t be simple. Their must always be a figure of power. That figure of power then must keep things balanced, and by blaming one person he can change the course of life for so many others. One word, could change a persons life as they know it… ’I learned this rule at a hard price. When I’d lost my first wife they told me they couldn’t find the killer, he was untraceable. However I did, and when I did, they said no. Then they blamed me… I proved them wrong, and in the end, they’re now getting me back for it.’ Staring at the wall Jesse dozes off to a much-needed sleep. The Tour Guy The clicking of well polished, fancy shoes awoken Jesse. A mass group of reporters and cameramen had been following a woman who was showing them a tour of the facility. Jesse rolled over and looked at them as they had began to move on with the tour. However one man stayed. For some odd reason he looked at Jesse with observing eyes. Jesse hadn’t noticed at first, but then heard him breathing and turned his head slightly to his right shoulder while looking down at the floor before saying, ’What’d Ya want?’ The man smiled, he had a pair of thick glasses on and wore a rather peculiar business suit that looked like it was from the 80’s. Pushing the glasses up his nose as they’d fallen down he cleared his throat and began to speak in a screechy voice. ’I wanted to get you’re thoughts on the Global Title Match that’s coming up. Would you mind?’ Jesse didn’t look interested. His eyes said he was awake but his body was exhausted. But Jesse turned and nodded. Sitting on the edge of his bed he stared at the floor with his hands hanging down between his legs near his knees, in a resting manner. ’Okay well, you’ll be taking on Blake Wolfson, Hessian, Todd Dream, Iron Lung, and Josh in an upcoming ladder match for your Global Title. Whats’ your strategy?’ ’… wait a second. Who are you? I don’t do interviews with anyone, especially someone who’s suppose to be on a tour of this facility and for some odd reason is here bothering me.’ Asserts Jesse with a bit of fire in his tone of voice. Still though he stares at the floor for his body screams tired and he just wants to relax. ’I’m a… new reporter for… P…TC! Yes that’s it. I just wanted to get some comments regarding the match Mr. Jesse.’ ’Well how about instead of the PTC match you ask me about the Global Wrestling singles match I have with Train this upcoming week?’ The man stares at him with a blank expression. Peering a hole in Jesse that shows the man doesn’t even know what he’s talking about. ’Of course, if you’re new then you’d have gather that information to ask me before hand. However by your non-existence of words I can tell you don’t care for the question nor know what I’m referring to. So shut up and listen to me.’ Standing up Jesse looks at the man and explains what he’s talking about. ’Train… a man who has a match that could make him the future star of the wrestling industry. He’s facing the best in the business come Evolution this Monday Night, yet doesn’t have a clue what he’s up against. I’ve seen his type a million times. His mouth says all the right things but his eyes will show fear when he steps in that ring. He doesn’t realize this is a shot of great magnitude. He’s got a shot to be recognized as the man who beat Jesse Jamester! The Global Champion! Sure, I’m not one to boast, but I hate it when people think they know everything about someone, especially about me. I’ve got more tricks up my sleeves than a magician and Train will realize that this tall figure of muscle isn’t just a walking zombie for him to knock over. Though I’m not going to say I’ll kick his ass… it’s not my style. I’m going to simply say he’s going to school. That’s all he needs to know.’ The interviewer stares at Jesse and notices his eyes flare with the words he speaks. Everything he says comes from the heart and now standing a foot away from Jesse with only steel bars separating them, the man feels the same intimidation Jesse described his opponent would. Slowly stopping the pen that he writes with a dot to conclude the notes he took down the interviewer puts his pen in his pocket. ’AHHHHHHH!’ are the only words that are heard as Jesse reaches through the steel bars and suffocates the reporter. As his body struggles to get free Jesse face becomes flustered with anger, before finally Jesse pulls the man face first into the steel bars and knocks him out. The man drops the notes. Reaching through Jesse takes the notes and extends his arm into the pocket to take the pen too. Smiling as he does so, ’thanks,’ is all he says as he goes back to his bed and begins to scribble words and thoughts onto the paper.
