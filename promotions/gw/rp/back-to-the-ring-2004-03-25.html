Back to The Ring...

25 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/25/04 The lock popped right open... Train had half expected the thing to be rusted shut after all this time. I might have been easier if it had been. That would've meant he didn't have to go in...to stare his past in the eye. No such luck. Chain slid the lock out of the steel link. He tugged on the chain, the steel clattering between the two metal handles on the front doors of the gym as Train freed them from objects that had held them shut up so tight for so long. Train wasn't exactly sure what he was walking into. All the windows of the yellowish gold building had been boarded and barred up years ago so there was no looking inside 'The Bottoms Training Facility'. Train had always said he'd arranged for all that so slick niggas wouldn't get no ideas. But the truth was...he didn't want to see what was inside. It only reminded him of what he'd lost. The steel of the chain clacked together as Train balled it up and passed it to Biggie standing right behind him. Train drew in a deep breathe, working his way up to the inevitable. He searched the key ring and found another, old, copper looking key and readied it between his fingers. He exhaled his breathe as the he slid the key into the deadbolt of the gym. The faded, weather beaten, wooden doors creeked as he put his free hand on them to steady himself...remember that this damn lock had always been a pain in the ass. He'd told Isaiah to get the thing fixed a million times. Isaiah's answer had always been the same... 'I'll get it fixed next week.' The answer always irritated Train about as much as the lock, but he always let it drop. He figured Isaiah would eventually have to find a week to carry out his promise. Who knew he was going to run out of weeks? Train flexed the muscles in his forearm and gave it a turn... Click. Hell... for once the thing opened right up. It must've been destiny for him to come back. That or some kind of damn conspiracy. Train wrapped both hands around the the handles of the door, placing his thumbs over switches that control internal mechanisms of the locks. He holds for a moment. Wondering if this is really necessary. It was just exercise though. He knew he did. Time to forget all the showboating bullshit he'd picked up being on tv, in front of those crowds. It was time to get back to brass tacks. Time to get back to what had gotten him there. It was hard though. Fuck it. The ancient metal hinges squeaked loudly as Train pushed the doors inward. A torent of sunshine flooded in, illuminating things that hadn't been seen by anyone in years. And, As the light entered, something else escaped. The air. Train took it into his lungs, detecting the odor of a time gone by. It wasn't just the smell of dust, but another familiar mixture... a musky coctail of the all the blood, sweat and tears that had been shed in pursuit of any number of dreams within these walls. Aside from dust and cobwebs the place was unchanged. Untouched. Like stepping back in time. The light illuminated the hallway entrance and flowed right into the gym. Everything about the place was stiff. The light colored, wooden floor boards creeked nearlly as loud as the door hinges as Train and Biggie stepped inside. The light reflected off the dust covered glass of the trophy cases lining both sides of the white hallway. Train slowly moves down the hallway, observing the cases. Train's hands run across the dirty glass, clearing paths for the light to penetrate, revealing the treasures inside them. Trophies from just about every sport one could imagine sit on the shelves, a picture of the victor who'd a trained here. In return for all the help they'd all brought their spoils back to the place where they'd learned. No, that's not it. They brought it back to the man who'd helped them. The same man who'd helped Anthony James become more than what the statistics said he would. Train and Biggie both hover in the hallway, looking at the pictures of the glory days. Happy, smiling faces, the extacy of victory lighting them up. But Train new he couldn't stay there forever. He slowly turns and looks down the hallway...to the stairs that lead down into the belly of the beast that turned out champions. That changed his life. He slowly strolls down the hallway, his footsteps echoing in the half darkened emptiness. The stairs that seemed so sturdy when he was a younger man now warranted caution, feeling like they may give under the new layers of muscle that had grown since he was last there. the hallway now opened into a large room, the ceilings going up a good 15-20 feet. As Train steps a few feet into the room the sunlight coming through the door reaches it's limit. The gym is safe to mauever though. The sun still pours down through a large skylight, the brush glass sitting in the metal frame reinforced with metal mesh within. Basketball court like, parkay floor was interuped in several places by large mats that clearly defined the borders of the different training areas. The racks of free weights and weight bench mark the strenth area... The heavy, speed and movement bags mark off the boxing. The square area with the circles painted on top trained your traditional wrestlers while the square area, in another corner, complete with tackling dummies was for your football training. It was interesting to see them again, but those spots hadn't ever meant much to him. The mats that were familiar to him lined the 18' x 18' square cut out dead center in the middle of the gym, under the massive skylight. The dust that's been stirred up by the new gusts of fresh floats and flicker in the beam of light as it illuminates his area... The ring. Train didn't even the notice the grin that quickly redefined his countenance as he stared at it. He could only half hear Biggie's footsteps as he cautiously made his way down the stairs into the gym area. With no conscious effort Train moves over the ring, feeling like he's trully come home again as he stares at it. Train remembers the days of the old school. He doesn't bother climbing the stairs to get on the apron, cuz there were no stairs. He just jumps up, his tip toes landing on the apron, his hands grabbing on to the middle rope to pull himself completely up. Just like old times. Train quickly eases through the middle ropes and strolls out to the center of the ring. His eyes squint as he looks up to the bright Chicago sky for a moment. He lowers his eyes and begins the survey the gym from a perspective he hadn't been in ages. The view took him back....
