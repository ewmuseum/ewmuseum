Lending a Little Truth to the Rumor-Mill

21 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/21/04 Sex. Looking back on it now, I could see just how meaningless it all was. My entire love life, didn’t amount to much, really. Casual sex? uneventful encounters, as exciting as watching paint dry. Not like it was with her. With her, I had the fourth of July? I had music, and summer nights? and that feeling of euphoria. The little things. Funny, really, when she was actually gone, that was when I realized how much I had missed her. She’d left a hole that nothing would fill, an empty ache. I chased her, but in the end, I let her slip away. One word, and I could have brought her back into my arms. Maybe I didn’t want that. Maybe I hesitated when I shouldn’t have. Didn’t matter now, she was gone. We sat in the back corner of the crowded bar, sharing a drink called loneliness, complete with little tropical umbrellas. The music was loud, the atmosphere chaotic, at best, yet for some reason, sitting here, looking into her understanding blue eyes, I felt like maybe, I could get over her. Or maybe, I was just fooling myself. ---- “Pain,” he murmured, as he swirled the contents of the glass, listening to the ice clink together in the sudden silence, “for me, it’s the catalyst.” Brandi Sights tossed her auburn hair over her shoulder, her eyes narrowing slightly as the smoke from the cigarette smoldering in the ashtray wafted in her face. “I’m not sure I follow?” “The Merchant of Venice” I remember, we read that play in high school. I guess everyone has a little bit of Shylock in them. I just wanted my pound of flesh. You see, Brandi, it’s like this. You hurt yourself. You hurt those around you. And then you blame someone else, that’s the way we’re made. Recrimination, and my self-loathing, that’s my pound of flesh? my burden. I’ve found, quite recently, that I really am not all that fond of myself.” He took a small sip from the glass before setting it down with a resounding thunk. The cigarette found its way to his mouth, and he inhaled the acrid smoke deep into his lungs, his intense indigo gaze seeming to look right through her. “Uh,” she hesitated, taking a small, delicate sip of her pina colada. “What’s not to like” I mean honestly? you’re successful. You’ve got a killer body. You don’t seem like as bad a person as they made you out to be? so I don’t get it, why aren’t you happy?” She looked away from that probing gaze, fiddling with a lock of her hair. Something about him made her feel uncomfortable? it was like he knew what she was thinking. What he was saying, made perfect sense, it was like he was reading a page from her own diary. “Yeah. I’ve got it all, Brandi, don’t I” An empty house, filled with empty rooms and the sound of silence. I have a bank account filled with zeros, and nobody to spend it on. I have things, Brandi. That’s all. Material garbage,” he thumped his fist against his chest, bitterness creeping into his tone now, “that does nothing to fill the emptiness I feel inside here. That’s what I’m talking about, Brandi. Do you understand” Money can’t buy happiness? or love? neither can success, although one would think it would place you in a better position to find that.” He tossed back the last of the contents of the glass, the last vestiges of the ice crunched away between his teeth. He leaned forward, resting his elbows on the table, interlacing his fingers under his chin. He looked at her, one eyebrow slightly raised, the hint of a self-deprecating smile on his lips. “I find, generally, that being honest like this usually drives the women,” he chuckled softly, “away.” He concluded, a wry smile turning up the corner of his mouth. She found herself drawn to him, for reasons she didn’t understand, and it wasn’t purely sexual, although? truth be told, he looked amazing. His dark hair was pulled back, secured at the nape of his neck with a length of leather, making him appear old fashioned. A snug fitting dress shirt covered his chest and abs, the white almost sheer enough to give a hint of the tanned skin beneath. He’d rolled up the sleeves, exposing the tattoos that encircled both wrists and the tribal design that stretched across his tight bicep. She was a sucker for tattoos, always had been. “You want to get out of here?” Straight and to the point, she never was one to beat around the bush. If it was on her mind, it would come out her mouth, that was one of her flaws. He threw his head back and laughed, clearly amused. “No.” He saw the crestfallen look on her face, and rushed to repair the damage done. “What I mean is, well?” he shook his head, a flush of embarrassment coloring his cheeks. That, coupled with the strand of hair that came loose from his ponytail, flopping over his eye, lent him a boyish quality that stole away some of the years that hadn’t been kind. Brandi didn’t understand his sudden mood change. She snapped her words with vexation, after draining the contents of her drink. “Well what” What? You don’t want me? You’re a virgin? What already?” “Well no, I just wanted to, well, you know,” he spread his hands, indicating the bar in general, “sing.” She blinked, utterly stupefied. “I’m sorry, what?” He repeated his words, this time more forcefully. “I said, I’d like to sing. Do you have a problem with that?” She smiled, laying her hand atop his on the table, giving it a light squeeze. “No, not at all. Funny, I never thought you’d be the karaoke type.” He shook his head, finally meeting her gaze. “And, what type did you take me for?” “The same as all the rest, I guess. You are a wrestler, after all” you guys aren’t exactly known for your tenderness or sensitivity. I mean, busting heads all day isn’t really a?” Brandi tossed her hair over her shoulder, hesitating, weighing her words. Jackson interjected, his expression guarded. “That may be true, but I’m not really a wrestler. I mean, it’s just a job. I wouldn’t call this a career. I do this for the money” not for the fans? not for the recognition. I do it for my own reasons? maybe even for reasons I don’t understand. But, Brandi, I would never call myself a wrestler. I am a man, nothing more, nothing less?.” ---- The rest was meaningless, really. Idle chatter, leading to the intevitable outcome. It was amusing, really, to discover that I was still able to play this game. I said things I didn’t mean, things I cannot recall. I awoke to the sounds of her leaving in the morning, the bed beside me still warm and damp, things better left unsaid. I would have called out to her, but what would have been the point? I grow weary of these games? and frankly, I’m sick of being used. My heart still belonged to her? nothing would change that. They say, that when you met the person you’re meant to be with, that everything changes, in the blink of an eye. They were right. She’d left a void in me that nothing could fill. Certain urges had been sated, but nothing more. Brandi wasn’t right for me. I still hadn’t found what I was looking for, or rather; I hadn’t yet found the nerve to go after that which I wanted so badly. Kitty. Someone once said that loving someone means never having to say you’re sorry. That, I didn’t agree with. I wanted to tell her now, just how sorry I was. I was a fool, and my punishment was to roam the Earth alone, yet another restless spirit without a destination. I could still feel Brandi’s warmth, her sweat on my skin, her taste on my lips, and it did nothing more than make me ache. A physical pain, pining for the woman I could never have. She was lost to me? Forever? That was something I needed to face.
