Tales from the Outside: Episode I

14 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 14/04/03 Tales from the Outside Episode I: [Insert Something Cool Here] 'When you're walking around the streets of the world, helping little old ladies across the road... and little girls get their cats out of trees, be sure that you remember one very important thing. You're not here to wrestle for money, you're here to wrestle for pride and honor. You're here to be powerful and full of courage... and to destroy your opponent using superior mat skills.' These words, as pointless as they may appear to the average person, were one of the first things that I was taught when my family decided I was going to become a professional wrestler. No, I didn't actually ask to walk into this business... or even get a say in the matter. Instead, everything was decided by the unfortunate bloodline that I happened to fall into. To wrestling fans and freaks alike, my bloodline isn't to be considered unfortunate or even a misfortune. For me though, somebody who didn't have a liking for wrestling... or really serious fighting in general, couldn't honestly feel all too glad at the prospect of things. 'Son, I know that you don't really appreciate wrestling all that much... but I really don't care. You're going to carry the family name through thick and thin, even if it's the ONLY thing that you happen to do with your life. The family will not miss a generation inside the ring and you're not going to leapfrog this hurdle. If you want to blame anyone though, you should blame your sister for being a girl.' ... and these words, as pointless as THEY may seem, were said to me at the end of my first day. Hurt and contemplating walking away, I was told that I had absolutely no say in the matter and that I'd compete around the world regardless. You'd think I could have walked away from training at the moment but considering the father actually had been a pro wrestler himself, catching me and forcing me to agree to this lifestyle really wasn't that difficult for him. 'You're going to face matches that you may have to work even harder to win. You're going to face opponents that you're not going to be able to beat on your first try. Don't think you've failed though because one loss is never the end of the situation. Instead, one loss can often lead to one hundred victories.' These were perhaps the smartest words that my father had passed onto me during my time in his training care. I didn't want to believe at the time and I didn't want to believe them when I walked through the doors into GW... but as this second encounter with J.C Diamond gets nearer, I'm beginning to wonder if one loss here will actually help me to progress and snatch victory at the elusive GW Pay-Per-View, Wrestlecade. Memories behind me now, the scene slowly begins to open in what could only be described as a busy street. Masses of people were everywhere, standing around, conducting their daily business... generally doing whatever they felt best on a night like tonight. I was walking up the roadway; struggling to get through the waves of people as they attempted to either get into a store or stop and insult me for one reason or another. '... Wow, you were right when you said that most of these people don't think too highly of you at the moment.' a voice said, coming from the person walking beside me. That's Jarvis Cocker, former front-man of some rather lame group called, 'Pulp'. I can't really remember the name of the group or anything btu apparently, they were really good. I can't say that I ever heard of them though, although it didn't help that I was always listening to the soundtrack from Final Fantasy 4. The man in question now was somewhat tall and looked like a real geek. At first, I couldn't understand my association with him either... but my father made a point of ensuring me that even ugly freaks were kinda cool. 'Unfortunately, since I abused a couple of black people on Evolution last week... with my tasteless references to 'Different Strokes' and such, they've just reacted in the only way that I didn't expect them to. It's a shame, I thought they would have treated me differently with you. I mean, Jarvis Cocker becomes J.C and you've got a totally black name.' I replied, looking at Jarvis with a look of seriousness etched across my face. He looked back with a confused look spread across his own face. I could tell now that he was obviously trying to comprehend the genius that it took for me to convert his name from white-boy Jarvis into black gangster JC, like JC Diamond. 'Rich, there is something of a problem with this whole angle that you're trying to play at the moment. Despite the way that you've been able to change my name into something that's going to give me a little more credit on the street, I'm still the same white-boy and you're still the same person who verbally abused a couple of black people on television.' Jarvis said, his voice carrying a sense of formality but I could tell that he was just another one of these people who was trying to make a fool out my genius-like mind. '... Of course you're completely black now. Even Deacon Holmes could probably testify to the fact that you've now become absolutely black. For example, how many white people do you know who have a first name of Winston?' I said with authority streaking through my voice. The two of us continued to walk up this road, people continuing to throw their random vulgarities in my face as I awaited the correct answer from Jarvis Cocker, the man who obviously didn't know a thing about being black. 'It's quite obvious that I'm not actually going to get through to you, isn't it? As for you very ignorant point, Winston Churchill was a white person and his first name WAS Winston. The man was alive during the World War... something I'm sure you know about.' Jarvis informed Rich, his voice beginning to sound snobby now that he was using high school education against the laziest athlete in the ranks of professional wrestling. 'What on earth are you talking about? Everyone knows that you don't get a white man called Winston... and you know that you'll never find a real white man called JC either. Amigo, you are completely black... and since you're my new black friend, you're going to have to say a few words to certain people about the terms that I used last week on Evolution. I'm almost certain that they'll listen to somebody of their own race!' Jarvis looked bemused and concerned, wondering if I'd ever been signed by that organization for really smart people. With every step, I could tell that he was in awe of my absolutely magnificent intelligence and ability to perform inside a wrestling ring. Granted, he'd not yet seen me perform in a wrestling contest... but with him knowing that I was some kind of super-genius, I could tell that it was safe to assume he thought other really nice things about me too. We continued to walk up the street, this time in complete and utter silence. I don't know what it was that forced him to become such a mute but after five minutes of this uneasy tension, I felt it was my duty to break the ice. '... JC, why aren't you talking to me? Are you in awe of my magnificent intelligence? Have I done something that has given you reason to believe that you're nowhere near to conversing with me? Don't worry if you feel insecure, many people in my time have often silenced themselves because they have been unable to hold conversation with me.' I said, trying to make him feel better but at the same time, making it known that there were few people that could hold an actual conversation with somebody of my stature. 'Rich, I really can't understand why you should happen to think that a white person talking to a community of black people is going to help your image or give you hope against JC Diamond on Evolution... whatever the Hell that happens to be in real life. As far as I'm concerned at the moment, you're a complete nutter who is paying me a small amount of money to walk up a busy street and tell a couple of people that you're not a racist and that you are actually quite sorry about these... remarks that you made.' Jarvis revealed, his lack of intelligence coming through with every spoken word. For a few moments, I felt like ripping him to shreads with my awesome knowledge... but, decided that doing such a vile thing would only further damage my reputation infront of these people. My father had brought me up with the intention that I'd be some form of super role-model to children and young adults all around the world and I was intending to keep to that intention. 'Jarvis, at any given moment, I could beat you up and throw you into a small Japanese car... but I'm supposed to be a role-model for young children everywhere, so I'm not going to take the usual kind of action against you. Instead, I'm going to ask for you to be politer and stop being so jealous of my advanced knowledge. It's not my fault that you just don't have the ability to comprehend my methods and thoughts.' I reminded the fading 'Pop' star, his eyes rolling as I finished what I was saying. I could tell that he was getting annoyed now and I was happy that such a reaction was being provoked. Eventually, after another 10 minutes of walking and another 10 minutes of silence, we arrived at our destination. The local was still the street but a large square had been boxed off and a whole host of black people were sitting on fold-out chairs, looking up to a vacant stage where there stood a sole wooden booth and microphone. Jarvis Cocker looked at the people with a look of horror on his face as I looked on with a smile across mine. I have no idea quite how he felt but I knew that I was getting closer and closer to being cleared by the people. I could sense that in the next five minutes, everyone that hated me was going to come rushing into my arms and parade me about like the ultra-hero I'd been raised to be. Walking up the aisle was never an easy task, especially when the mass amount of folk were instructed to stare at the two of us as he walked past them and stepped onto the large wooden stage. Jarvis Cocker, as unco-operative as he was, took center stage as instructed and waited for me to sit down on a wooden chair some way behind. I crossed my arms and looked ahead, smiling and waving to the people who'd turned up for this apology of the sorts. They didn't wave back at first or acknowledge my existence... but I had a feeling that would all change. Jarvis tapped the microphone a couple of times, a static buzz being given out all about. '... Well, I don't quite know what I'm doing here today, but apparently, I'm going to be apologizing to you people on behalf of this person from a wrestling place. His name is Rich Sherwin and apparently, he said some really bad things about us. I use the word 'us' because apparently, since my name became JC, I'm officially one of you black people.' Jarvis began, looking to get a reaction from the crowd with his opening, but recieving none what-so-ever. He waited for a few moments before pressing forward, '... So, I stand here before you all to apologize for the comments made to Brick and JC Diamond by this fellow called Rich Sherwin. I don't quite know what the word were but apparently they had something to do with 'Different Strokes' being used as a platform for verbal abuse. For this, he is sorry.' Cocker stopped again, expecting his words to smooth across the people like butter on a piece of crispy toast. I knew that he'd gone about the entire thing wrong, but I didn't want to hurt his feelings. After all, with the money that I actually spent on this fool, I think I was getting everything that I asked for... perhaps a little more if I really thought hard about what I'd obtained. The crowd though, remained silent as Cocker removed himself from the microphone. I looked toward the gentleman with a puzzled expression on my face before decided to do the deed myself. 'Yo!' I shouted, grabbing the attention of everyone, including the escape-attempting Jarvis Cocker. 'Yo! Hey Yo! Fo Shizzle!' I shouted again, hoping to grab the crowd with my use of slang and stuff. 'Before I talk about everything that's going down, I just need to tell you all that the guy over there... he think's he some kind of black man. He think's he goes to the same KFC's as you... and even gets to eat from the same chicken bucket that you do!' The crowd looked toward Cocker, anger spread across their faces like marmite on a piece of completely burnt toast. Inside, I was smiling, looking at the way that Jarvis had actually helped me in my cause to win back the hearts of all these people. He thought that I actually wanted him to apologize for me... when in actual fact, all I needed was for him to make a fool of himself and then for me to use HIM as the scapegoat, blaming my words and phrases on his influence. 'Jarvis Cocker, as he's apparently known, likes to call himself JC and parades around the house thinking that he is the distant brother of a failure called JC Diamond. Now, I know how much you kind people don't like this kind of situation... but the sad thing is, it's a truth that we are going to have to endure together. It's a fact that we are going to have to sit through and watch to the very end. Jarvis is a menace... he is the biggest influence of everything that comes from my mouth!' Finishing that last word set off a trigger in the minds of all these people. Blaming Jarvis Cocker for everything that I'd said was turning out to be one of the smartest moves that I'd made in a long time. As the coloured people all rose from their chairs, the scene slowly began to fade deep into black. I could have continued the story and told all about how I egged the crowd on as they destroyed and beat the living piss out of Jarvis Cocker but that wouldn't be very nice of me, would it? Regardless, with the first episode of my new tales thrown deep into the back of things, I was left to contemplate what would happen the next time I was about. Should I talk about something my father did and then go onto something with no connection... or should I do something interesting, like impersonate JC and publish my entire life on VHS and DVD? Who knows... and to be honest, who cares?
