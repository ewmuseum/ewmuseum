Beverly Hills: A Social Dinner

28 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 28, 2005 The upscale Los Angeles neighborhood of Beverly Hills had always been the home to Hollywood’s brightest stars and some of America’s most powerful businessmen. Over the course of time the residents of Beverly Hills had formed their own society, one that only the rich, famous, and powerful were allowed to be a part of. Those that were allowed admission to this social circle were expected to be decent upstanding citizens that regularly contributed to charity. There were two cardinal rules by which this social circle had always adhered to, the first was to not do anything socially unacceptable while in the public eye, and the second was to never be caught in any act that could cause a scandal. Mike Biddle had gained entry into the elite social circle of Beverly Hills due to the fortune he had inherited upon the untimely death of his parents. With the inherited fortune came his Father’s company and a spot within the annals of high society. Mike’s situation wasn’t unique; many had entered into fashionable society by inheriting vast fortunes and were considered to be exciting until they became married and dull. Mike was controversial amongst his peers due to his treasured hobby: professional wrestling. Wrestling had once been considered the sport of kings but in recent years had become taboo and high society now frowned upon the entire business. Despite his choice of employment Mike was still tolerated by his Beverly Hills peers due to his parents being highly respected members of the social circle before their deaths. Mike was not oblivious to the fact that his occupation raised a few eyebrows but he felt it made him interesting and occasionally made for good dinner conversation but only when those he spoke with had an open mind. Also his recent strides into the film industry had, for the most part, quelled any ill-will that his peers held towards him. On this night Biddle been invited to a small dinner party held by his esteemed neighbor, Nicholas Rumson. Rumson was a small, middle-aged, chubby man with curly gray hair and wire frame glasses. Nicholas hadn’t always been rich, he had been born into a poor family but he somehow married a rich woman and that is how he had entered into the social circle of Beverly Hills. Normally at these small get-togethers Mike had to defend his hobby but he hoped that with the release of Gilligan’s Island the conversation would turn to the more pleasant topic of his movie career. Even with that hope Mike still feared that he would have to waste an entire evening defending wrestling, especially after he had injured an invalid in order to win the Platinum Championship. Mike had arrived at Rumson’s home fashionably late and was immediately shown to the dining hall where Rumson and the rest of his guests were already seated around a long wooden table that had a great feast placed upon it. Rumson and his guests were far too interested in their discussion of the latest scandals to even notice that Biddle had joined the party late. The party was small but it was the people that Mike normally associated himself with. Rumson sat across from Mike at the other end of the table. At Mike’s right was his good friend James Loewen, the young black man that had inherited his Father’s chain of restaurants. On Mike’s left was a red-headed man named Charles that had always tried to compete with Mike, but Charles always found himself on the losing end. Seated next to Charles was Hillary, her hair was still dyed black and once every so often she turned to Charles and gazed at him lovingly’something that she did with more frequency once Mike had entered the room. Seated next to James was Rumson’s wife, a middle-aged woman that had maintained her beauty through countless visits to the plastic surgeon. Once the current conversation died down Mrs. Rumson turned to Mike and smiled. “How good of you to join us Mr. Biddle,” she said. “How have things gone for you on that television show you’re on?” An arrogant smirk crept across Mike’s thin lips. “Well,” Mike began, “I recently won another championship?” Charles interrupted Mike, “And you injured an invalid in the process,” the red-headed man said. “Oh dear,” breathed Mrs. Rumson. “How did you manage to injure an invalid Mike?” “He dropped the man on his head,” Charles stated, “probably broke his neck.” Both Mr. and Mrs. Rumson gasped and James let a small chuckle escape his lips. The reaction caused Mike to sigh deeply, he had hoped to avoid the subject of Myers’s injury but he had only himself to blame for bringing up his title win. “How could you do such a thing?” Mrs. Rumson half asked half scolded. Mike took a moment to search for an explanation that sounded reasonable and suddenly he remembered what he had told Lisa Jameson when she had questioned him about Mindy Mercer. “It’s all for show,” Mike began, “wrestling is fake. You see, the “invalid” isn’t truly an invalid he only plays one on television.” Mike finished his sentence with his most sincere smile. “So,” Mr. Rumson began, “you didn’t injure an invalid BUT you still injured a man?” “I didn’t really injure him,” Mike said, “that was part of the show too. He’s taking a leave of absence to attend to some family business and the writer’s usually have the character injured storyline wise just as a way to explain his or her absence.” “I see,” Mr. Rumson said, “well that certainly makes sense to me.” Mike turned to Charles and smile in triumph. Charles looked down at his plate in disappointment; Mike had defeated him once again. The conversation then turned to politics and Mike felt very relieved at that fact. ***** After dessert was finished both Charles and Hillary asked to be excused; they said that there was a play they wished to see downtown and that if they stayed any longer they would be late. Mrs. Rumson also excused herself; she liked to swim in the evening after her meals. That left only Mike, James, and Mr. Rumson. Rumson invited Mike and James into his study for a cigar and a stiff drink or two. The three men sat on plush leather armchairs in front of the fireplace, each man had a lit Cuban cigar between his lips and a stiff drink in his hand. The conversation started with Rumson talking of the first edition Charles Dickens books he had recently purchased, he brought them out from their places on the shelf over the fireplace and allowed his guests to gently flip through them. After Rumson was done showing off his prized possessions, the cigars were still lit and the glasses still full so the host turned a new subject. He turned to Mike and asked, “Tell me Mike, why is it that you choose to participate in wrestling” I’m sure there must be something else more practical that you can do with your time and money.” Mike took his cigar from his lips and set it down on the ashtray on the arm of his chair and took a quick sip from his martini. “Wrestling is nothing more than a hobby to me,” Mike said, “I happen to enjoy the adrenaline rush I get every time I step out into an arena full of people and put on a show.” “Surely you can get that same sort of satisfaction from acting in movies or plays?” Rumson asked. “It’s not the same,” Mike answered. “Now, most of the men my age around here go rock climbing, parachuting, or kayaking. Wrestling is pretty much the same thing; just I get paid for it.” Rumson leaned back in his chair and stroked his chin and as he soaked up what Mike had said. “I see where you’re coming from, but I still think there is something better you could do with your time though. I guess I’ll just have to watch one of your shows to actually understand.” James looked over and noticed that Mike had started to become slightly annoyed by Rumson’s choice of conversation and quickly came to the rescue. “Tell me Nick,” James interrupted, “what’s this I hear about you getting a first edition of War of the Worlds?” With that question the conversation turned back to Rumson’s book collecting, which was the subject of conversation for the rest of the evening. Once the cigars had burned out both Mike and James had asked to be excused. Once they left Mike thanked James for the change of subject and then the friends went out to the country club in search of some female companionship to help finish off the evening properly. Meanwhile, Rumson called his friend that worked for one of the local television stations and asked if there were any tapes available of Mike’s wrestling career. Rumson was told that he would have the tapes within the hour. The only question was, would Rumson finally understand Mike’s attraction to the sport of kings or would this somehow start some sort of downward spiral in Mike’s social life? -To Be Continued-
