The Inevitable Return IV: Loose Ends

11 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/11/2006 ---- FLASHBACK’Saturday, November 13, 2004 0613 hours, local time CID Safehouse, Amsterdam, Netherlands ---- His assailant chuckled, the sound equally as pained as his previous speech, and the wind kicked up with it, sending the heavy rain in Jackson’s face, each drop like a tiny needle piercing his numbed skin. “You don’t need to see to believe, friend” believe this, your life is now in my hands. My bow is steady, another arrow is already loaded, pointed at your heart right now? ah, even now, I can see the pulse racing, I can almost hear it. Lub-dub-lub-dub-lub-dub-lub-dub? the gallop of a horse running for his life?” “Spare me the melodramatic claptrap, my friend. If you mean to kill me, do it now. Conserve your energy, don’t talk to me.” Jackson let his head hang, conserving his own energy, pressing his fist against the hole in his shoulder. He was still kneeling on the cold, wet ground, between two corpses, and the fact wasn’t lost on him that he may very well become a third. Footsteps came closer, boots squelching in the mud, and then the voice was closer. “It is ever so nice to see you again, Bradley. Have you had time yet to appreciate the irony of this situation” It does seem that the bodies do tend to pile up in your wake, don’t they? And who are these men, pray tell? Friends of yours?” Jackson grunted by way of response, and then looked up, finding himself peering at a man whose face was mostly concealed by a dark hood. “Do we know each other” Who are you?” “No one of consequence. Now then, are you ready to die” Here, in the mud, like the pig you are?” The man in the hood chuckled, and then drew back the fabric slightly, making his face visible, revealing a black leather mask that covered the top half of his face, leaving only his dark eyes, and dimpled chin visible. Patchy stubble covered the only skin Jackson could see, but that wasn’t remarkable. The eyes, somehow, were uncannily familiar. He cast about in his memory, trying to connect the voice and the eyes with a name, or the rest of the face, and found that he could not. The man held the crossbow in his hands, his finger poised on the trigger, his lips curved into an icy smile. “Wait,” Jackson croaked, “please don’t. Whatever transgression you believe I have committed” I am truly sorry. Please, I beg of you, whoever you are, let me live.” The hooded man clicked his tongue disapprovingly, shaking his head slowly before replying with acidic scorn. “My ears must be deceiving me, did I just hear Bradley Jackson say he’s sorry, and then beg for his life like a dog begging for table scraps” My, my, how the tables have turned. It would serve me best to end this conversation now, and be done with you?” Jackson chuckled, the sound filled with bleakness. “Ah, yes, and then you can cut off my head, place it on a pike and display it on the front lawn for all of Amsterdam to see. Isn’t that, right” You’re not the first to threaten my life?” “Indeed. That is a sporting idea; after all, nothing quite says, “obey me” like a head on a stick. But I fear that would be too quick for the likes of you, I fear I killed you too quickly the last time, a mistake I will be sure to remedy this time.” “Killed me” You must be mistaken? I haven’t died, that should be painfully obvious to you, since we’re both here, breathing the same dank air.” Jackson met the stranger’s stare, his eyes burning fever bright with anger? and fear. “Drop the pretences, Jackson” there’s no one hear to listen to your lies. I know as well as you do, that you never should have survived that fall, yet somehow you did. I let madness consume me, and I played your games, but that is OVER! NO MORE! Now you will pay for what you have done!” Jackson blinked a few times, his expression dazed. “Dante” But, how can it be? Impossible, you should be...” “Dead?” He drew back the hood completely, revealing stark white hair, that was cut in a military brush cut, but still, the mask covered his face, concealing the hatred that twisted his features. “Afraid not, my friend. Another loose end, you always were so adept at missing the finer details.” “Impossible,” Jackson breathed, and tried to pull himself to his feet, only to sag back to the cold ground as he grew close to passing out. The man, now known as Dante reached around behind his head, and unsnapped the clasp holding the mask shut, letting it fall from his face. Bands of scar tissue puckered the skin, making him appear as gruesome as a Halloween mask. Jackson averted his eyes, revolted at the sight, appalled really, at this, his own handiwork. He spoke, the words serving no purpose but to fill the silence with idle chatter. “I have money, I will give it all to you, Dante. A house, a nice house, on the beach like you always wanted. Doctors, I’m sure my money will buy you a new face. All I have is yours, brother” take it! Please, just take it.” “SILENCE!” Dante roared, making Jackson flinch. “You can keep your goddamn blood money, and your godforsaken bribes. I don’t want your shit” I want to see you dead. You stole my family, you bastard! You interloping piece of shit! She was my sister, MY flesh and blood, and she loved me first. We were supposed to be together, always? she promised me that? and then you came along and ruined it all. You stole my love? my life.” Jackson shook his head, tears shining in his eyes as he raised his hand in supplication, the golden band on his ring finger sparkling in the first rays of sunlight as the dawn broke over the horizon. “You don’t understand, Dante. I didn’t mean for it to happen. Love isn’t something you plan, it’s unexpected. It’s a blessing and a curse; it’s a double-edged sword that cuts you to the quick, leaving you powerless in its wake. I could not deny her then, any more than I could now. I love her, Dante” if that were my damnation, then so be it, but I love her. She is carrying my child, she has taken my name? please understand, Dante, I never meant to hurt you. I never meant for this to end so badly. I?” He broke off, sobbing brokenly, his hand still raised as though to ward off the blow he expected. “That doesn’t change the fact that you tried to kill me. Twice.” Jackson’s voice was low, defeated as he kept looking down at the waterlogged ground. “You’re right. Tell me you wouldn’t have done the same, Dante, if you were in my shoes.” Dante’s eyes fastened on that golden band and he stared for what seemed like an eternity before he broke away, and steadied the bow. “Right, well then, are you prepared to die?” Jackson didn’t reply, lost in the depths of his own prison of misery, suddenly overcome with an attack of conscience. Dante’s finger tensed on the trigger, and that’s when a voice spoke from the darkness, muttering one word. “Crossbow.” The weapon vanished from Dante’s hands, and a moment later, one of his own metal arrows slammed into his stomach, spilling him backwards against the trunk of the tree. Another arrow sliced though his neck, pinning his head back, spilling his blood in great crimson floods, and then all was silent? ---- FLASHBACK’Saturday, November 13, 2004 0717 hours, local time CID Safehouse, Amsterdam, Netherlands ---- The red flashing lights painted the dawn with splashes of crimson, eerily reminiscent of blood where they glimmered in the puddles. The sky was a uniform shade of gray, the rain still falling softly, making the world appear soft and gauzy, like something out of a dream. He stood on the stoop, smoking one of his unfiltered cigarettes, his eyes hidden behind a pair of dark sunglasses even though the day was nowhere near bright enough. Smoke curled around his head, a makeshift halo as he watched the body being loaded into the ambulance, already zipped inside a bright yellow body bag. Dante was dead this time. That thought should have given him peace, but it didn’t. Instead he felt empty, and more than a little sick. A voice startled him from his reverie, and although the man spoke in Dutch, he understood every word. “You shouldn’t be smoking; it’s bad for your health.” Jackson whirled around, making eye contact with the stranger, who simply grinned, revealing stained teeth. “There are a great many things I do that are bad for my health,” he replied, in faltering but still flawless Dutch. “This being the lesser of those evils. I promised my girl... wife I would quit, but she is back home, and I am here. What she doesn’t know won’t hurt her.” The man smiled, and then held out his hand, offering his name. “Inspector Boersma. And you are, Agent?”” “Jackson.” He shook the hand, applying more force than necessary, grinding the bones together. “Ah, yes. Is that a first or last name?” “Both.” Jackson took one last deep drag from the cigarette and dropped it to the sodden lawn, where it sizzled for a moment before going out. He turned the full force of his gaze on the Inspector, using one finger to draw the sunglasses down his nose. “Care to tell me what you want or are you going to make me guess?” “We have always had longstanding cooperation between your people, and mine. I am here to take advantage of that. Who was that man we have taken to the hospital?” Jackson shrugged, feigning ignorance. “Who” The dead man?” “No. You know perfectly well whom I mean. The injured man.” The Inspector put a hand on his hip, drawing back the edge of his long trenchcoat, giving Jackson a nice view of his holstered gun, and his badge. Interpol. “Are you trying to intimidate me, Inspector” I have a gun too. And unlike you, I don’t need to hide behind my posturing to pose a threat. I can kill you in the blink of an eye, that is, after all, what I was trained for.” He shook his head, slowly, and then leisurely reached inside his jacket, withdrawing a crumpled pack of Camel cigarettes and his battered silver Zippo lighter, giving the good Inspector a glimpse of the twin Walthers he wore in hip holsters beneath the coat like a modern day gunslinger. He shook one out and lit it leisurely, pausing only to blow a stream of smoke in the Inspector’s face. “We have nothing more to say to each other, as you can see, this house is empty. The garbage has been carried away, and that means whatever agreement you had before, has been dissolved. Good day, Mr. Boersma. Now, get the hell off my porch before I have you arrested” or worse.” He stood there for a moment longer, saying nothing as Boersma met his challenging gaze. The Inspector’s eyes roamed over Jackson, noting the hole in the jacket he wore, a ragged tear above the breastbone. A smear of something darkened the edge of the torn leather, and Boersma was willing to bet it was blood. Before he could comment, Jackson stepped off the porch himself, disappearing into the morning deluge, leaving nothing behind but an amorphous cloud of cigarette smoke, and the coppery smell of blood.
