Who am I?#3 Background RP #3: Trust Nobody?

14 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 14/9/04 *Note* Although he is referred to as John Winters in this roleplay, by this point the still has not taken a name yet. John Winters sat in the stereotypical farmer house kitchen, absently wondering whether it was obligatory to own a plaid table cloth. He caught the farmer staring at him mumbling something undecipherable and grunting a lot. Since bringing Winters inside, the farmer had calmed down an awful lot ,and now he no longer pointed the shotgun at him, primarily due to the presence of his good lady wife, who busied herself in the kitchen, Winters wasn’t one hundred percent sure that he would’ve offered the same show of trust if their roles were reversed? “Want another cookie?” The farmer’s wife asked, wearing a pleasant smile and holding out a plate stacked high with chocolate chip cookies. “Thanks.” Winters replied, taking a rather sizeable one from the top of the pile. “Goddamit Peg,” The farmer shouted. “He ruined ma cornfield!” “What?” She said, developing an innocent expression. “I’m only showin” hospitality!” “But he’s a goddamned vandal!” “You got no proof of that.” “I don’t need no goddamned proof, I?” The farmer trailed off, hearing the heavy clunking sound of a car door being shut outside. He stood and made his way over to the window, gently pulling apart the curtains to take a closer look. The farmer smiled, walked over to Winters and took him roughly by the arm. “The police are here, come on!” Winters stood, then dragging his arm out of the farmer’s vice-like grip with considerable effort. “There will be no need for that!” Winters declared, as the farmer made his way over to his back door grumbling. Winters put the remnants of the cookie on the table and followed him. The whether outside was good. Hot, but there was a distinct wind that gently caressed his skin. It was good to be alive. “What seems to be the trouble here, Ned?” The Sheriff said, making his way over towards Winters; his deputy skulking behind. “Hey Pete,” the farmer began. “I caught this fool in a vandalised section of my cornfield. I’m thnkin” he be the one who vandalised it.” The sheriff eyed Winters up carefully. “This true, boy?” “I am unsure.” Winters replied, calmly. The sheriff’s lapdog of a deputy, poked his head over his master’s shoulder. “Unsure?” Winters eyed the little poodle up wearily. “Indeed.” The sheriff glanced at the farmer confused, who simply shrugged in reply, and then redirected his attention back to Winters. “What the hell do you mean you’re unsure?” “He claims to be suffering from memory loss?” The farmer butted in. “That true?” The sheriff asked. “Indeed. I have no memories before waking up in the cornfield. I can neither confirm or deny my involvement in this crime.” “I think he’s been on drugs?” The farmer added again. Winters shot him a rather distasteful look. “That is’doubtful.” “But not out of the question?” The sheriff asked. “No?” “That’ll do for me,” The sheriff said. “Johnson, cuff him!” The deputy stumbled out of the sheriffs shadow and fumbled about with the handcuffs attached to his belt. “Is this really necessary?” Winters asked. “Yes,” the sheriff said, his face holding a rather sadistic smile. “It is.” The deputy forced Winters? hands behind his back and then roughly slipped the cuff around his wrists. “What do you think, sheriff?” The farmer questioned. “I think that we can’t prove a damned thing,” The sheriff replied. “So, what do you want us to do with him, Ned?” “The special treatment.” The farmer said. “What the hell is the “special treatment?”” The deputy asked. “Shuddup, boy!” The sheriff demanded. Winters remained silent, however, he knew that this did not look good. He could only guess what the yokels meant by “special treatment”, but he suspected that his imagination couldn’t do what they had in store justice. The deputy led Winters away from the sheriff and roughly jammed him in the back of the police car. As he sat there wondering what was happening, he noticed the farmer hand the sheriff a rather large wad of notes. It didn’t look good at all. The sheriff strolled back to the car, grinning at me, as he opened the car door and uneasily got inside. Within a couple of seconds the motor whirred into life and soon after that the sheriff reversed out of the farmer’s drive way and sped off. They were driving for about five minutes when the sheriff finally broke the silence. “You wanna know what the special treatment is, boy?” “Indeed.” Winters replied, with a distinct tone of indifference in his voice. The sheriff did not reply. Instead, he slammed on the breaks’hard. Winters stopped himself from flying forwards by resting his foot on the metal partition (a means of segregating the front of the sheriffs car from the back) and easily absorbing the momentum. With this, the sheriff burst out of the car and quickly made his way around to Winters side. “What the hell are you doin”, Pete?” The deputy asked, similarly exiting the vehicle. “Get back in the car, Johnson!” The sheriff replied. “But, sir?” “Now, Johnson!” The sheriff aggressively opened the car door and dragged Winters out of the car. “What do you intend to do?” Winters asked, managing to remain calm. “I intend to give you the “special treatment”.” The sheriff simply replied, throwing Winters into the mud-lined ditch by the side of the road. “I don’t feel good about this, sir!” The deputy said, nervously taking a couple of steps back. The sheriff then rushed over to his deputy and grabbed him by the collar of his shirt. “Turn away now,” He warned. “You will not witness a thing!” “I don’t know, sir!” The deputy bleated. “Look,” The sheriff said, now in calm tone, unhanding his young understudy. “If we don’t teach these punks a lesson then we’ll get this type of behaviour all the time! Everything will be vandalised. Do you want that, boy?” The deputy hesitantly shook his head. “No, sir! But this isn’t a young punk. He looks about thirty’he looks older than me!” Winters observed the sheriff seem to think about this prospect for a second, obviously trying to come up with some kind of argument. Winters figured that Johnson, the deputy, wasn’t the sharpest knife in the draw’and he didn’t see the wad of notes the farmer handed to the sheriff before they departed his farm. “The same thing applies! If we let this outsider get away with who knows what we’ll find vandalised tomorrow?” “Technically you don’t know whether or not I’m an outsider?” Winters butted in. The sheriff turned around to face him, wearing a nasty scowl and landing a nice kick in Winters? gut, winding him severely. “Shut up you! I’ve never seen you before and that’s good enough for me?” “Damn yokels?” Winters mumbled, successfully getting to his knees with his hands still tied behind his back. “What did you call me” What the hell did you call me?” The sheriff shouted edging closer to Winters. Winter developed a defiant smirk. “You heard me.” “You see Johnson?” The sheriff said not taking his eyes away from Winters. “This is what these people are like. We have to stem the problem before it becomes a catastrophe.” “Okay’but I’m not watching!” The deputy finally conceded. “That’s fine,” The sheriff replied. “This ain’t for the weak of heart anyhow?” With that the sheriff proceeded to attack Winters, kicking and punching him, while still wearing the handcuffs. After a couple of minutes the sheriff was out of breath and so stopped the vicious attack. Winters lay in the ditch, beaten to a bloody pulp. “Ugh’does’that make you feel better” Beating a man while he’s cuffed?” Winters managed. The sheriff drew closer, developing a snug smile. “Hell yeah’and thanks for reminding me’I’ll take those cuffs back now.” The sheriff stood on Winters head, reached over and unlocked the cuffs. “I’ll be taking those back now’and remember if I ever see you around here again’I’ll do a lot worse.” “No,” Winters replied. “You will not’the next time you see me - if you see me coming at all - I will not be cuffed.” “I’m terrified I’m sure?” The sheriff said, walking confidently back to his car.
