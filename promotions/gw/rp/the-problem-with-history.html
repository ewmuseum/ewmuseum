The Problem With History

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Christian walks down the darkened streets. His head swivels around, scanning, searching, unaware of what it is he truly sought out. The air was cold, its caress chilled Christian’s blood. The darkness of the early morning sky is welcoming, Christian had always been a nighthawk, preferring the silver gleam of the moon over the golden rays of the sun. The streets are nearly empty, most of the bars have emptied, the city has finally died, retreating to sleep before doing it once again. Christian scoffs at the sight of these empty streets. How disappointing it is, that people refuse to acknowledge the true beauty of the early morning. I shrug my shoulders, maybe it’s just the drugs talking, I still haven’t come down completely, why would I want to? But, I am confused, I know there is something out here that has called me into these streets, there is something I must find. Christian continues his pace, slowly trotting down the sidewalk. His head continues its scan of the streets. His pupils, swollen and enlarged continue to focus and refocus as his neck turns looking for some unknown goal. His footsteps echo loudly across the empty streets. The streetlights, fully illuminated cast the shadows back into the alleyways, and keep the streets looking alive. Christian knows better, the city was like a ghost town, unmoving in the dying hours of the night. There is the odd crack or bang, surely coming from the nearby industrial neighborhoods. It’s so empty, it’s hard to focus on what I’m looking for. Hell, I don’t even know what I’m looking for. It’s a gut-feeling and instinct that has called me out into the night, not some great need or idea. However, I have always trusted my gut and now isn’t the time I’m going to stop, I must continue. Christian starts to cross the street, he doesn’t bother searching either direction, the silence of the night assures him there is no traffic to endanger his crossing. He continues to the other side of the street, and continues along his path, there had to be something to find, something to ease his mind, to calm his nerves. He usually wasn’t this worked up before a match, but this one was different. He had something to prove in this tournament, he couldn’t miss this opportunity. All his life he was a nobody, some punk kid who lost it all and turned into a scared little boy. Ever since I was born I got pushed around, there was always someone trying to hold me back. Then my parents died, and that set it all off, the bullies, the death, I turned into a hopeless soul, and I ran away from it all, in true Christian Darke fashion I ran from my troubles. I turned to drugs and the drugs turned me into nothing. Then finally, after being pushed around by everybody and everything, I finally pushed back. And when I pushed back, I started to like it? a lot. I started gaining some recognition in Toronto when I first started fighting, but that was nothing, I finally caught a couple breaks and made it to the DWO, my home for the past several years, and there is where I made a name for myself. I made a name for myself, I changed? I went from the kid who lost everything, to the man who took everything. And now, I’m faced with an opportunity to take even more. Christian looks to the side, he sees a light radiating from a rather large building up the block. He glances at his watch, nearly 4:00 am. Wondering what might be going on at this time of the morning, he begins to make his way down the street towards the light. Christian gets to his destination, he stands in front of the large steps leading to the front entrance of the building. On the limestone banner hanging over the door is an engraving reading “Library.” Christian smirks, a library open at 4 in the morning, this was truly an odd sight. Christian carefully begins to ascend the large staircase leading to the front door. The huge maple doors were closed, the gothic carvings engraved on them enthralling to Christian’s stoned eyes. He stares for a few seconds, becoming lost in the ripples and lines, then he closes his eyes and shakes his head, recovering from his zoned-out state. He steps forward and reaches his hand out, touching the door handle. He presses the small lever down and finds the door surprisingly unlocked. He pushes the door open the rest of the way and makes his way inside. His head swivels and his eyes scan as he sees the rows and rows of books, volumes, magazines and newspapers. Awestricken he moves towards the light, the same one he had seen from up the street. The light calls to me, drawing it to it. I feel like I might find whatever it is I’m looking for in its warm light. I make my way through the small tables and desks, and when I locate the source of the light, I see a man sitting at the table carefully studying. What could he be doing here so late? or early for that matter? Christian approaches the man at the table. Quietly he makes his way towards the source of the light, not carefully enough, the man quickly looks up from his novel and begins to swivel his head around. He notices Christian quickly, and becomes confused. Christian immediately speaks up, “I’m sorry to interrupt you, I was just drawn in by the light?” “That’s fine, I am always happy to have visitors, please come, sit with me.” The man was short, sort of chunky, his face was round, and the signs of age had taken a severe toll. Christian smiles, he would talk to this lonely man, he makes his way to the table and sits down. “Why are you here all alone?” Christian asks. “Oh, I spend much of my time alone now,” the man replies. “Ever since my wife died, I have been here many late nights.” A man after my heart? a man who has felt the same pain as me. A man who is hiding, a man escaping the harsh realities of life. A man who has suffered a great loss, I must know more? “I am sorry to hear that, I too have gone through the loss of loved ones. It is a terrible thing, but why a library, why come here of all places?” The man smirks, “it is the only place for me, these books, these volumes all of them contain history, a history that I love, for I cannot love my own history.” Christian’s heart sinks. This man could not speak words more heart felt, he feels exactly as I feel, his words sooth my pain; more than any drug ever could. He hides in history, because he cannot live with his own, he escapes his own pain by reading the lives and tales of others. “I know the feeling. When I was young, I lost my parents to a drunk driver?” “That is terrible, I am sorry to hear that?” Christian turns away as the man tries to reach across the table to console him. Christian was not a man searching for pity, but he was enjoying the conversation. I can’t believe I’m opening up to a stranger like this. I could barely kiss anyone as a teenager, I could never speak to one of the other users, I could never open up to anyone I loved, how can it be I open up to a stranger like this. Strange, how those we know the least, have the most in common. Christian begins to speak once again. “Yes, I too ran away after that. Hiding, like you are. But I found my refuge elsewhere, I found it in drugs, in pain, in hatred. I became hopeless, living” but a waste of breath, a drain on society.” “No, no, no my friend, no one is a drain on society. Even those who feel the most terrible at times, often make heroes. You should join me here, in these books. This is my refuge, these are my drugs. The lives of others enthrall me, it gives me hope to read their tales, and brightens my soul to know others have triumphed over the pain I have felt.” I can almost spit on him now, the corny shit coming out of this guy’s mouth sickens me. But I find myself still mesmerized by his words, his soft, concerned tone keeps my gaze, locks my interest. “How does this help you” I can find no comfort in knowing that Mrs. Sally Jacobs from Butt-fuck, Idaho overcame the death of her sun to invent the cure for world hunger!” “You are an angry man, my friend. Let go of your hatred for others, and embrace the history of the world. In it you will find stories of love, stories of triumph, stories of hope.” Christian stands to leave, he could not listen anymore. The more the man spoke, the more Christian’s mind was tugged back to his opponent; El Rayu Azul II. The man who he would meet in a mere amount of days and that was the last thing Christian needs. “I’m sorry, I don’t feel the same passion for books that you feel. I must go.” I can’t listen to his shit anymore, I’ve heard enough. I need to get out of here, I need to get back to the hotel, and I need some sleep. This history lesson is doing my no good at all. “Please my friend, do not abandon your hope. Things will truly get better?” Christian whirls on his heel, his voice fuming with anger and his body posture fully confrontational. “Do not speak to me of hope, do not speak to me of things getting better! You sit and you hide in these books, in these histories. They are all fake! They mean nothing to you, they mean only falseness and fear. You hide behind the histories of others, because you have none yourself!” Christian lets out a huff and a sigh and turns. He puts his head down and nearly breaks down in the middle of the library, he begins to walk away, he hears the soft sobbing from behind him. He had never felt so bad to inflict pain on another man. “I’m sorry?” is all Christian can manage to let out inaudibly as he walks out the door. The darkness immediately consumes him as he tries to forget his small encounter. He looks back and forth, deciding the best path to get back to the hotel. His words make me sick as they run through my head. His face makes me angry as I watch him in my head. His strength makes me weak. Weak because he can get by and I cannot. He speaks of hope and conquering above only because he reads stories of it. His lies will catch up to him. Me I refuse to live in those lies, there is no rising above, there is no hope. There will never be any more money in my life like the success story of Bill Gates, there will never be any greatness or glory in my life, like the war hero Napoleon Bonaparte. No? I am simply me. The lost, scared, confused little boy. Seeking out my next fix. My next way to hide and run from the pain. But, I will not confide in his lies, I will not rest in his hope. As Christian steps down onto the sidewalk, he takes a final look back at the library and lets out a soft sigh. He can murmur only one phrase as he turns his back and walks away to his hotel room? “I guess that’s the problem with history?”
