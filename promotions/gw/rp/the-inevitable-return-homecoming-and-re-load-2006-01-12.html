The Inevitable Return: Homecoming and (re)Load

12 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/12/2006 ---- THE PRESENT- January 2, 2006 0900 hours, local time Global Offices, Dallas Texas ---- Jackson stood on the sidewalk, shivering slightly in the cold, his expression a study in neutrality. The fear, however, was visible in his eyes as he looked up at the imposing building of glass and metal. This would be a turning point in his life, in more ways than one. In all his thirty, almost thirty-one years, he had never apologized, and until recently, he had never begged for anything. The times, as they said one in a song, they are a-changin?. He reached inside his battered leather duster, fishing out his ever present pack of Camels, lighting one with the absentminded precision of a long-time smoker, or a stage magician. He blew the smoke upwards, watching as it mingled with the mist from his breath. The air was unusually crisp, fitting perhaps since it smelled to him like a new beginning. “This is probably stupid,” he muttered to himself, “they’re not going to give me back my old job, and certainly not at my old pay rate. I’ve been out of the Global octagon for more than a year, hell, nobody in the locker room will remember me.” He shook his head, taking another long drag. “I don’t know why I’m doing this” except that I have this urge to set things right. With everyone?.” He flicked the remains of the cigarette into the brisk wind, and turned back towards the building, walking resolutely up the steps and into the future. The sunlight flared off his dark sunglasses, and then, in a flash of blinding light, he was inside. ---- THE PRESENT- January 8, 2006 2300 hours, local time Golden Gloves Boxing Academy West Palm Beach, FL ---- Jackson sat upon the weight bench, frozen in thought, his features etched with something akin to worry. Various weight machines adorned the room, from a treadmill, to a Bowflex. All this he’d set up in the loft of the old boxing academy he’d bought with his inheritance money. Daddy’s money, spent on something he would have considered frivolous. The thought brought a small smile to his face, carving a hint of a dimple on his left cheek. Sunlight slanted across the worn floorboards, warming the buttery stained wood. The room vibrated with the bass of the heavy metal that filtered from the surround sound speakers. He was slowly and methodically smoking one of his ever present Camel deadheads, his expression distant, fixed on a point in the distant horizon, out in the aqua depths of the water beyond his windows. Sweat poured from his upper body, making the tanned skin gleam in the sunlight. He’d maxed out at sixty reps of 350 pounds, and he felt like a million dollars. His body was pumped, and his thoughts were coming fast, not that that particular phenomenon was anything new. He was feeling pretty good, on a natural high, a smile still on his face. Just this morning, he’d been finally, actually, legitimately medically cleared to wrestle. His knees were back at 100%, or maybe even better, and his body was in better shape than ever before. He could return to the ring any time he wanted now? without fear of the consequences if management found out. Ironic, really, when he’d been moonlighting for months putting over the up and comers in WCWF, mostly as a favor to Larry Gowan. His smile grew brighter, as he thought about what the internet fanboys would say when he arrived in Manchester. Funny, how in the past long nine months of rehab since he’d begun this official medical leave, time had grown wings and flew past at a dizzying rate. It seemed like just yesterday that he’d walked away from the Global octagon, not quite the squared circle he was used to, but it was a place that almost felt like home. Oddly enough, he’d gone there after Kitty, wanting to tie up the loose ends and the feelings? and instead he’d fallen in love again, with her as well as wrestling. These last few months had afforded him more than enough time to think, alone with his thoughts and this unfinished renovation. It was funny, when you had nothing to do but work out, and think, just how much perspective you could get on things. Everything seemed to be in focus for him now. He stood slowly, pausing only to drop the remains of the cigarette into the Folgers can that rested beside the bench. He crossed to the mirror that lined one wall, and looked at himself. He was ripped. Zero body fat. Solid as a rock. His upper torso looked like a gladiator’s armor, perfectly defined pecs, a six-pack abdomen, the works? broad, muscular shoulders tapering down into a perfect vee at his narrow waist. He flexed his arm, nodding in approval as his bicep and forearm bulged rock-hard. He was more than ready to make his glorious comeback? now all that remained was the planning. Plans needed to be made. He was, after all, still a strategist. Hell, he supposed, half the damn roster or more didn’t have a clue who he even was? and he’d likely lost more than a few fans, if he’d ever really had them to begin with. He could count the people who remembered him on one hand, but then he’d have to add one more, since it seemed that Gabriel was still alive after all. He lay back down on the weight bench, and gripped the metal bar overhead. He’d added two more ten-pound plates, bringing the total up to 370. He took a deep breath, and pushed the bar off its pegs. His mind was quiet now; this was his spiritual and mental cleansing. Zen. He lowered it to his chest. Then he blew all the air out of his lungs in a rush, pushing the bar back up. Clean and solid. He could feel the burn now, turning his arms to fire. He did another, then another, and struggled through a fourth, his arms beginning to tremble, only slightly. On fifth, his arms shook from his effort to slow the bar as it fell, his part in controlling gravity. His arms felt as though they might give out. But he reached down inside himself, way down? and visualized that the bar wanted to rise, hell, it was growing lighter? gravity was working in reverse, steel into feather? all he had to do was work with it. Wax on, grasshopper, like the Karate Kid. His eyes squeezed shut, and he craned his neck, gritting his teeth and pushing with everything he had left, powering the bar back up, extending his arms for a split second and holding the weight steady before letting it crash back into place on the steel pegs. “Hooo-wah,” not really a word, more a sound as he sat up, breathing like a bellows. Five reps. Three seventy. Not spectacular, but decent enough considering the sleepless night he’d had last night. He’d trained to the point of collapse after endless hours of watching promos. He’d passed out from exhaustion, not a good sleep at all, and he could feel it now. He remained still a moment longer, lying there on the bench, breathing like a bellows. He shook his head slowly, spraying water from his longish bangs. “Respect. I’d like to think I’ve earned it. One would think so; after all I have given to this sport. The better part of seven years, filled with blood, sweat and tears. Filled with injuries. Seven years of my life... doesn't seem like much, but it is. I’m 30, getting closer to 31 now that the New Year is past. Seven years, just a drop in the bucket of the huge amount of time we’ve all been allotted in this life. But to me... it might as well be forty. It’s getting harder to keep doing this. Every week, it takes just a bit longer to feel that burn, and every week just a bit less time for me to feel the lag in the ring. Pretty soon I’ll be blowing up on the ramp. But these new kids will still be around, still going strong, a fresh crop popping up every day it seems. Fresh faced kids, hopefuls from the indy feds looking to make a big name for themselves. I’m not one of those guys anymore. I’m an old horse, maybe in a few years these rookies will be getting close to my age. Maybe just starting to feel what I feel. This business is hard. It takes a toll that people don’t understand. To me, this isn’t just sports entertainment. It’s reality. More than that, it’s my reality. It’s all I have?” He would have completed that thought, but he didn’t want to say it. Now that Kitty was gone, he had nothing else. The bottle of ice-cold apple juice was refreshing as he ran it over his forehead and chest before cracking it open and taking a generous swig. He’d been nearly living here, and it showed. His renovations, done himself out of sheer boredom were nearly done, and most of what had been an office and storage had been converted into an open loft apartment. He’d put his own home up for sale; his childhood home? and it had sold quickly, for more than double his asking price. It made it easier to pay off some outstanding debts. He had the money? he had a lot of money, and nobody really to spend it on, recently. It was all going into the bank. What he needed was? her. Someone he could lavish attention? and diamonds? and minks on. Someone to laugh at his stupid jokes? someone to tell him to pick up his dirty socks? he wanted to settle down, all the way. He’d tried, really, and they’d made a go of it, for a few months in the old penthouse condo in Chicago. But, he’d fucked that up too? one night; she’d just up and left. He was thirty, and some days, he felt twice that. He shuddered. That was too fricken old to still be a bachelor. His bare feet made no sound as he crossed the warm boards through the archway, pushing aside the plastic drop sheet that still served as his door. The smell of wood was strong in here, cedar and pine? and the smell of paint was almost fading. To him, it smelled of freedom? of new beginnings. He raised the bottle of juice, making a mock toast to the room in general. “Here’s to Global, and the wrestling ring” a place that still feels like home after all these years? just as much as this that I built with my own hands does. I guess it’s all about pride? about doing something by yourself? for yourself. I guess I had to learn that the hard way. But here I am. This is me? the real me for a change. No masks,” His eyes drifted to the newly stained floor that gleamed in the sunlight. “Like these boards under my feet, I stripped away all the old crap, all the accumulation of the years, and found myself under it all” just like I found this beautiful hardwood under all that crappy tile. I guess life’s like that? sometimes you need to take time out? and take stock of the big picture? see your life for what it is? or maybe for what it isn’t. I guess I sound a little bit like a weekend philosopher? but really, I’ve just been doing a lot of thinking. I know now, where I belong. I’m an athlete, an actor, a performer? and the ring is? well? is my family. It’s time to come home.” He closed his eyes, and finished off the last of the juice, wiping his lips afterwards on the back of one hand before raking that same hand through his sweat dampened hair, leaving it sticking up in sodden spikes. The image made him appear boyishly innocent. He set the empty bottle on the yin/yang coffee table, and then continued across the living room, around an elaborately decorated Chinese screen, and past his enormous four-poster bed, before coming to another door. The bathroom. He glanced over his shoulder with the hint of a smile on his lips, and this one reached his deep blue eyes, making them twinkle just a bit. The dimple was deeper now, bringing out his good looks, probably winning him fans with the female viewers. “Coming home feels good” Manchester here I come.” And with that, he slipped inside, closing the door behind himself as the scene faded to black?
