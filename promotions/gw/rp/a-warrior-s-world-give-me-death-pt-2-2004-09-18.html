A Warrior's World: Give Me Death… (Pt.2)

18 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 18th, 2004 Prologue In part one, Jesse had shown a new sign of gratitude towards his life. For once, he was beginning to think more confidently, and now trying to turn around his life. Though the loss of his father was tragic, it helped him move on. After seeing a therapist, Jesse was assigned to take an anger management class to help him move on with his life. Even with his will to move on, it is still hard, so the anger management class is to further that urge to do so. Now we will see how well Jesse accepts his criticism and the change he is to adapt himself to. Chapter 5 Tuesday mornings always seemed to be a drag. However, this was an exceptional one in the least. I’d beaten KPG, a man I’d never thought of in a sense other than that he was a hero to me. His talent and his admiration towards people in this business were above any I could serve up. Because he gave people what they wanted. Sitting back Jesse drank his latte. The sun shined through the window lighting up his booth. It was the perfect aftermath to a perfect night. Ryan and Jesse had partied all night long with the women they found and now Jesse was enjoying some alone time. Something he hadn’t gotten in a while, despite him always being alone. It’s weird how things work out. I spend half a week nearly killing myself to get sleep, and then last night I drank myself asleep at the party. It’s like if I try too hard, I don’t get it, but if I just relax and let it come, it does. I hate how that happens… rrr, can’t someone just get what they want once in a while. Finishing the latte, he sat there observing the room as if it were a painting. The light peach walls with flower borders gave the place some design and style. As the checkered tile floor in black and white were glistening from the sun reflection, it also showed to be stylish for a coffee shop. Quite commotion took over in certain areas of the room as college students waited to attend class. It was the perfect spot for an outing, especially for someone trying to get away from their problems to enjoy them self. Now I have a bigger task ahead of me. A four way for the television title… I’ve only won two titles in Global Wrestling, so I definitely want to add to that collection. Hmm… Rich Sherwin, Venneman Pendragon, and… ah who was that last person? He shook his head of the thought. Jesse knew he had to let go of the distractions he dealt with every day, as he was told during the anger management class, he had to relax and let the stress dwindle away. Dropping the empty latte in the garbage recycle bin on the way out, he strolled down the street in no hurry to get to where he was going. Chapter 6 “Now group, we want to help you, and so we need your cooperation. This isn’t a group where we will change you, but a place for you to help improve yourself. Think of it as constructive criticism. You are here to get help, and we are here to guide you to do that on your own. Everyone understand?” squeaked a woman in the front of the room. She was standing on a carpeted stage only two feet off the ground. A heavy sigh of ‘yes’ was the response, showing the delightfulness of the members who joined or were assigned to this class. Some of them were older than Jesse, and others were in their late teens. The woman's squeaky voice grew deafening as she moved off the stage and went through the seats of people. Coming nearer to him, Jesse began to feel irritated of her annoyance. 'You must accept change, and must allow it to come in, or it'll only be more of a hassle than help to move on. As someone once told me, don't do what you think is right, but do what you find is true in your gut. That was something I always used apart of my time in school and even now as an adult,' she squeaked as she passed him. 'It's not smart to make decisions your unsure of. However taking your instincts into effect is what makes you precise on your decisions. Does everyone understand where I'm coming from?' Half of the class was daydreaming, a fourth were trying to listen, and a fourth were like Jesse just observing those around them. Seeing this young kid in the very back, Jesse was wondering why he was their. The kid’s clothes were rags. The pants sagging down with the belt on, the shirt hanging down even further than it normally would go, and the kid’s hat tipped sideways. He kept positioning his fake silver spray-painted chains on his neck so they would stand out more. Jesse was not one to particularly judge others; instead he just took in the details and considered the possibilities. However, this kid looked like he didn't care what he was told, he was right and the world was wrong. The woman opened her mouth one last time before the meeting was over. It was nearly time to go for him anyway, and the anxiousness of the group was more than a notice of that. 'I'm going to pass out some papers, and I want you all to fill them in and bring them back next week.' After passing out the papers the people began to leave in a hurry. Some more than others, as their age held them back. Walking up to the kid, Jesse observed him for a split second before introducing himself, 'Hi, I'm Jesse Fristik.' The kid turned and took in what he saw. Staring at the six foot six, two hundred fifty nine pound, and one of the fiercest men in all of global wrestling today, the kid showed the least amount of fear. Whether it just is his acting, or possibly his tough attitude, the kid didn't give a sigh of worry. “Hey, I’m Jake Williams… My friends call me Jay Willie though,” softly spoken, the kid said, without raising his voice or showing any nervousness. “What did you want, I was just about to leave.” Staring at the kid Jesse’s face showed an expression of surprise. He hadn’t expected someone who dressed like a thug, to be so calm and collected. Sure he didn’t judge people, but appearance was definitely a huge consideration towards attitudes nearly ninety percent of the time. Though the kid’s attitude towards the anger management class had been of either disgust or boredom, Jesse truly didn’t expect the first impression that was given. “I was just wondering if I could give you a ride and talk to you, I sort of need someone right now to do that with. You able to lend me a few words?” claimed Jesse trying to not sound too forward and not scare the kid away like he was a pervert or something. Nodding his head, Jake and Jesse walked out the front door and then down the steps. Crossing the road they jumped in Jesse’s black dodge truck and pulled out of the parking lot steadily. Jesse knowing that the kid didn’t live far away if he hadn’t had a car to get himself to the class. I hate having to talk to strangers but that class just sucked. I hope this kid can understand me or maybe just learn something about life. I think I owe that to this world for all it has done for me… Correct one young life to satisfy the mishaps I’ve caused in others. It’s worth a shot. Driving off down the road the two chattered. Chapter 7 Pulling into a short dirt road that ended abruptly ahead of them, the two stared at a mere vision of heaven. The sun was setting, as the orange sun was mixing in with the gray clouds, and the red was distorting the middle of the sky with a purple haze following it. Like an angels tears had disrupted it’s perfect flow, the sky was a mixture of emotion to the men, and Jesse knew he found a friend when he saw Jake staring out the window with a smile of bliss. This is what makes me forget about the past, and this is what makes me realize I’ve got nothing to lose… Sure life is sweet, but dying is only another step. You only fear what you don’t know enough about. I feared death, because I knew nothing about it. Well, I’ve realized every time death has taken a part of misery in my life, I began to accept it. Now I feel nothing but comfort with the fortune. “It’s been good hanging out with you man. I’d never have thought that losing someone you love could cause you to feel so angry. Too me, I’d be too depressed about them going that I couldn’t get angry,” muttered Jake as the sun began to fade away slowly, “Its like life passes you by and you’re too pissed about it to forgive yourself. I actually think being as livid as you said you were with yourself makes you be able to channel your anger better. Just think of how many people get depressed and fall from the reality of this world.” The words the kid spoke were nowhere close to what Jesse had normally heard from people. Thinking hard he realized everyone had told him he was odd for being so irate with the death and himself, and that his sorrow must’ve been eating him away. Yet, now Jesse could see that the people didn’t understand what he was going through either, nor understand people for that matter. “How do you know this?” Jesse asked, almost unsure to believe the kid or to just have faith in him because of his age. “I’m studying to be a psychologist. However, I’m not going to be like all those thieves that do their job for money, I want to do it to help people,” the kid answered back. Jesse wasn’t getting it, and he quickly followed up with his next question, “Then why are you taking anger management classes?” “I want to study how people react to the teachings and see what approach others take on trying to get peoples attention along with how they direct problems. For example, that woman who was teaching you, oh she was horrible. She doesn’t realize that your not children, and so by teaching you like you are, she only loses your focus from learning to questioning her degree,” he pointed out softly, still not raising his voice even though indirectly showing he wanted to. Sighing Jesse took a deep breath in. “You’re twenty one, and you’re studying to be a psychologist, as well have all this knowledge about people, yet I still don’t understand…” “What don’t you understand?” Jake asked with confusion of what he presented wrong about the whole topic. Smiling, “Why you dress like that?” laughed Jesse as he turned and saw Jake laugh too. “It’s comfortable. It’s not that I don’t like dressing fancy, I just like to be comfortable, as I’m sure you do too.” They chuckled and laughed for a while. Jesse knew very well that he was nowhere near as smart as the young Jake, but knew also that his experiences in life were greater than Jake’s to a much higher degree. Sitting and talking until the sun faded away and the sprinkling of rain was upon them, the two sat there. It was a time neither one would forget in the back of their minds. Chapter 8 Sitting there under the light in the dining room of the apartment, Jesse stared at papers with biographies of the men he was to face. Rich Sherwin, Venneman Pendragon, and Trey Reed, the man he didn’t know oh to well. Pondering over the words of which described their past and their attributes, Jesse sighed. “Damn it… Paper does nothing for me. Reading may help you know what they were, but it does not prepare you for what you need to know,” frustrated he says as he swipes the papers off the table into the air and then seeing them float to the floor in a scatter. “But knowing my father was ill would have definitely changed things. If only…” His face shook red as he slammed his fist down on the table. He couldn’t stop himself from blaming the happenings of his father to be his own faults. Even with a therapist, an anger management class, and a psychologist’s help, he was blaming himself. “FUCK THIS!” Walking across the room and into the bedroom, Jesse walked straight for the balcony and without even thinking he jumped over it. Falling a good fifteen feet he hit the ground hard and laid their motionless. … … …
