Home Again

28 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/28/04 [Start Scene] [The camera fades in to show a gloomy May morning. The exact place could be anywhere on the eastern shores of the United States as the beautiful grass-covered and leaf-covered field to which the camera is focusing on ends abruptly with a cliff that goes down a good thirty feet and overlooks a sand-covered beach. Beyond the sandy shores is the immense and innocently-dangerous Atlantic Ocean, its blue waters being illuminated by the sunlight.] [The camera slowly begins to pan around to the right, focusing in on an amazingly lush forest, not so much a forest but a large grove of trees that spanned as far as the eye could see. As the camera continued to pan around, almost 180 degrees removed from the cliff, it noted a large and expansive red brick home. It looked as though a hand had plucked a cottage from upper Michigan, added a few levels to it and set it down in the middle of the field. The entire scene was very picturesque.] [The home itself was nothing fancy, large but with a very quaint feel to it, as if to step inside would be to step into your own little world but at the same time never feeling truly withdrawn from the rest of society. The side of the home in view was the back of it, with a solid red oak porch built out from one half of the home. There was no roof to the porch, but rather a single oak tree, not much unlike the ones outlying the field. The tree provided shade for half the porch and it was under that half that a table had been set up. The table was of glass and the chairs cushioned’four chairs to be exact but only three were empty.] [In the chair is a man who is recognizable across the world, but no more so than right here at this very moment in Core Wrestling. Even seated he has vibrations of power coming off of him and his dark clothes mimic the darkest regions of his heart. The black boots, black jeans, black button-down shirt and black leather jacket are all accentuated by the wraparound black sunglasses that cover his eyes. The avid viewer of Core should already know who it is, but if they do not then the large golden championship belt that lies across the table next to a glass of scotch should give away the secret just fine. It is Jason Hughes, the finest wrestler in Core Wrestling and its former Legacy Champion.] [Hughes is leaning back in the chair, half facing the camera and half facing the table as he reaches out and takes a slow sip of scotch before licking his lips lightly to let the taste linger before setting the glass down and letting out a small sigh.] This is the Core Wrestling Legacy Championship and it is one of the most prized trophies in all of this sport. It was on February the 22nd where I went through an “I Quit” Match and won this championship. I won this belt. I became Champion. Time passed on. On May 16th I went through a brutal twenty minute match and lost the championship, having only this replicated belt that is given to all former champions. I lost this belt. I was no longer Champion. Time passed on. May 30th’my shot at redemption’a two of three fall match to regain my Legacy Championship and be the World Champion for the third time. I could win. I could lose. But win or lose I have another match on the 31st and this one’this one has nothing to do with gold’only glory. This is when I return home, this is when Jason Hughes returns to Global Wrestling. [Hughes grinned as he picked up his drink, sipping slowly before setting it down on the table and leaning back again in his chair with his hands folded across his stomach as he continues to speak out at and towards his rival.] Now don’t get me wrong’I enjoyed Global Wrestling when I was there. I held the tag team titles with the late Shawn Corrick, was a member of the second Mile High Club with Jay Robson, the Price Brothers and Ace Cash, held the International Title for a short period and then helped to found the most prestigious group in GW history in the Upper Echelon. [Hughes slowly leans forward, pulling off his dark shades so that his cold eyes were visible. He tossed the shades onto the table as he picked up his shot glass and took a small sip of scotch, letting the taste linger in his mouth for a moment before swallowing and placing the glass back on the table. He leaned back again and just looked out over his large estate for what seemed like hours before sighing ever so slightly and speaking once again.] The reason I left though was the lack of faith in me shown by Global Wrestling. It seemed as though I was the man that they decided was just to big for his britches. I was the man who had beaten Brick and Sabbot and the man who had won tournaments that held Camero, Robson, Price, Stanley and others, but I was not the man who would ever hold that World Title. [Hughes? tone is matter-of-fact and genuine and the camera pans back slightly as Hughes leans forward and puts his dark shades back over his eyes. He takes a quick sip of his scotch as he stands up on the patio and, with his Legacy Title on his shoulder, makes his way down the patio steps and on to is expansive backyard as the camera follows closely behind.] [He slaps the Legacy Title across his shoulder once to add to his comment before gesturing outwards towards his lawn, his home and finally the magnificent view on the beach below.] But look at what I have become since leaving this federation behind. I am atop the mountain of a federation far more supreme then Global Wrestling and in a place far more interested in my abilities. I know that somewhere, somewhere McCoy is watching this and shuddering at the thought of how he let me slip through his fingers. Imagine the bouts that could have taken place. Jason Hughes Vs Ice, Jason Hughes Vs Josh, Jason Hughes Vs Jesse Jamester, hell, even Jason Hughes Vs John Zero, Jason Hughes Vs Deacon Holmes and Jason Hughes Vs Maverick Reno. I’ve been waiting a long, long time to come back to a Global Arena and show the world that when GW let Jason Hughes slip away it would be the most drastic mistake of the federation’s history. I’ve gone through so much since May of 2003 when I had what was my last match in Global Wrestling. I’ve held three belts, had a record of 22-7 with five of those losses due to interference and yes, I’ve even become engaged. But the fire still burns’the hate is still in me and the world will still see what I can do. [He sips his scotch again as he continues to walk calmly around his yard, the camera never more than five feet away from him as he moves in hopes of not missing a single comment from the Legacy Champion. Hughes does not speak but rather stands calmly near the cliff as a few minutes pass before he finally speaks out again.] The MOB’some force to be reckoned with? I’m not impressed. I’ve been in the mob’years ago, I was in the mob and this, what I see here is nothing to the brutality of its namesake. A mob is a family, not just three peasants thrown together because no fan can remember their individual names. A mob is a force beyond anything the likes of a wrestling federation will ever see. The mob yearns to end’end what? They yearn to end lives. The true mob of Global Wrestling, if its name deserves to be placed anywhere, is within the hallowed walls of the Upper Echelon. We are a brotherhood in the truest sense of the word and we will not settle for some Trifecta to walk around claiming to be what we truly are. The Upper Echelon has more blood on their hands then the MOB has ever seen and in just a few days that crimson glory will flow again’from their bodies to our waiting hands. [Having walked around the entire edge of his yard he begins to make his way back towards the patio. His voice was now holding a tone of hate, pain, determination and cold evil all mixed into one.] Pain’it’s what I do best and it is what makes me shiver in happiness when I cause it. I admit to enjoying it just a bit too much when I deliver that cold, calculating blow across the back of some unsuspecting victim. It feels good as the thud of the fist hits warm flesh. It is almost’almost orgasmic in the pleasure I feel as I hear someone’s pitiful screams still ringing in my ears. No man should ever enjoy the passing of pain as much as I do, but I cannot help it. There is something about seeing a man lying at your feet, broken and in agony’their body motionless except for the rhythmic rise and fall of the chest as their breathing slows, anguishing in their pain. There is something about it that turns me on’and oh, oh when I deliver that blow into the frame of some MOB member’mhmm, you got it’I will feel that pleasure and it’will’feel’good. [Hughes? face, as he reached the patio steps, was now overwhelmed by a sick and sadistic grin as he thought back to the brutal attack from a week earlier. He climbed the steps slowly, the camera following, and slowly sank back down into the chair he had raised himself out of moments earlier. He placed both the Legacy Title and his glass of scotch onto the table before leaning back in the chair and letting out a small sigh, still smiling as he spoke once again.] One night’one single night for the entire world to see. Maverick Reno. John Zero. Jason Hughes. The three men who will stop at nothing to get what they want have but three men standing in their way. Some MOB whose individual names are nothing compared to the canvas of gold and notoriety that the Upper Echelon has carved out. The Upper Echelon’the greatest ever shall truly be alive again on Memorial Day. That is right’on Memorial Day in front of a worldwide viewing audience three more men will be memorialized, three more men will be struck down in the tides of battle. They will be the MOB, fighting but in a losing effort at their final stand’one by one they shall drop until all that remains are their enemies’three victors amidst a pool of crimson. [Hughes smirks and slowly reaches forward, taking the shot glass in his hand and raising it up in mock respect towards the camera.] Here’s to the death of the MOB and long live the true mob of this world’T-U-E.
