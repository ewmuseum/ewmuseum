Memory Remains #4

27 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 062703 Flashback before Evolution John Zero and Deacon Holmes stand before a metal folding table with a black briefcase on top of it. Zero reaches out, keys in the combination and opens the briefcase. Inside is an odd collection of wires, small chrome colored cylinders, black boxes with red buttons, and gray blocks. “Where do you get this shit, man,” Holmes asks quietly. “Does it matter?” Zero returns. Holmes gives Zero a glare. “Look, this is your standard “Ask me no questions and I’ll tell you no lies” deal. You don’t wanna play? I’ll put it to use elsewhere.” “Naw, naw, shit man, I was just wondering. Thinking about, how you say, acquiring some of that shit for my own private collection.” Zero returns the glare to Holmes. “Nevermind.” “Alright then.” Zero picks up a gray block. “This is your standard issue C-4 explosive. Insert that little cylinder into the block, press the red button on the black box and piff, no more target.” “How many of those blocks you need, say for a car?” Holmes asks, rubbing his hands together. “One.” “And if more than one is used?” “You know what they say?” “No, what do they say?” “God hates a coward.” “Hell muthafuckin” yeah.” Flashback to end of Evolution Hiding is easy. Hiding in a crowd is even easier. Find the texture of the crowd, the coloring, and wear the same thing. Blending in. And at the last possible second? John Zero is seen in a crowd, mingling with people. He is wearing a black tee shirt with the word ZERO written in stark white with a pair of faded blue jeans. He stands amongst men and women ranging in age from early teens to middle-aged. The other people cheer, they scream, they shout. Zero moves about casually, staying mixed in the bunch, but not far from the edge of the crowd. Zero moves his head a fraction of an inch to the left, his dark eyes cutting over to check the main aisle. There is movement at the top of the ramp. He turns his head the rest of the way, looking directly across the aisle and into the eyes of Tyler Pierce. They both nod and leap over the metal railing, separating their respective sides of the crowd. Zero lands in a cat-like crouch in the on-coming path of Ben J. Pailthorpe, who keeps running despite the obstacle now in his path. Zero stays crouched, as Pailthorpe gets closer, closer, closer? A fraction of a second before impact, John Zero springs upward, his arm coming fully extended, his middle and fore fingers held together in a straight line. The fingers jam into Pailthorpe’s throat, just above his breastbone, slightly collapsing the windpipe. Pailthorpe’s hands reach up to his neck as he falls to his knees and then to his back. Zero stands above his fallen opponent and slowly shakes his head before turning towards the ring. The hurt. The pain. It all comes back so easy. So easy to create in other people. It’s like riding a bike, except you’re not the one who has to worry about falling down. The four member of The Upper Echelon stand, side by side, on the ramp to the octagonal ring. Inside the ring, the Nevermore waits, with Salaok, the half owner of GW, yelling at TUE via microphone. “Yo, shut the hell up, old man!” Deacon Holmes shouts into his own microphone. Salaok attention attained, he grins mischeviously. “You do not own US Salaok! We did not sign to GW under you! We signed with Christina McCoy! Ohh And as far as that ring is concerned” You can have it...” Holmes removes one of the small black boxes from his pocket, holding it high for everyone to see it before thumbing the red button. A big explosion rocks the arena as the ring bursts into flames. Zero leans over close to Holmes, whispering, “How much of that did you use?” “All of it.” Zero slightly nods, both to Holmes and himself. “Good.” The Upper Echelon stands at the ramp, watching the fire burn for a few moments before turning and leaving the arena. Over the next couple of weeks, I would see Lily one of a couple of ways. Either sitting pretty and all smiles or beat all to hell, saying Bryce really doesn’t mean it, that he really loves her, that he just gets jealous and it is really her fault. It reminded me of my mother. Blaming herself for my father’s drunken fits of rage. I took care of that bastard, leaving him crying in the hospital from where he and my Louisville Slugger exchanged words. My heart wasn’t set on killing him. Bryce wouldn’t be that lucky. I first thought a good threat was in order. Maybe scare Bryce away from Lily. Put the fear of God in him. That might work. It was worth a shot. Finding out where he lived wasn’t a problem. I just got Lily talking on one of her good days. Once she got into the steady flow of conversation, getting her on the subject of Bryce wasn’t hard. I found out that he played football, basketball, baseball, and wrestled. Just about your average sports hero. His parents are moderately wealthy and live in a plantation size house outside town. He had the whole third floor to himself. Bingo. I was practically there. “Dogs,” she said. “His family love dogs, and has several Dobermans.” No problem. Couple of pounds of hamburger and I would be man’s best friends? best friend. I decided to go pay my little visit to Bryce late Sunday night early Monday morning. Since Sunday isn’t a real big party night, I figured this would be the best time to go. The Saturday before, I drove to a city two-hours outta town to get my supplies. A set of black sweats, a ski mask, a butterfly knife, a roll of duct tape, some sleeping pills, some handcuffs, and a couple of pounds of hamburger. As an afterthought, I decided to stop by a hardware store and get a big suction cup and a glasscutter. I got to Bryce’s house without much trouble. I drove a little bit past it and pulled into a patch of woods. I walked as quietly as possible, listening for dogs. I made it almost to the house before I heard the first growl behind me. Reaching slowly into the black bag at my waist, I pulled out a hunk of bloody meat and tossed it. Before I left my apartment, I ground the sleeping pills up and mixed them in with the hamburger. The dog pounded after the meat and pounced on it. Soon, several more dogs were around me. I emptied my bag, tossing the beef in different directions. The dogs scattered and I continued on. Reaching the door, I took out the suction cup and glasscutter. Selecting a pane of glass near the doorknob and lock, I attached the suction cup and began to cut away a sizeable piece of glass to stick my arm through to unlock the door. Shutting the door quietly, I made way through the house to the staircase and up to the third floor. The place was huge on the inside. It looked like it would take forever to find Bryce’s room. But, there was no turning back now. It took less time than I thought. Luckily, Bryce’s room was the first that I looked in. He was laying face down on the bed, legs near each other and arms laced together. I did his ankles together first, without waking him. When I did his arms, he snorted, but still wasn’t awake. I smacked the back of his head, hard. He woke muttering, “What the?” That was all he got out before the tape was in place over his mouth. His eyes got big and he started to struggle, finding he couldn’t move much. I pulled out the butterfly knife and nicked his face, right below his left eye and he stopped. “Listen up,” I said, disguising my voice. “You hurt Lily, you get hurt. This is your one and only warning.” I stood and looked down at him. He was still bleeding and his bed sheets were damp looking. I guess I scared the piss out of him.
