Date: unknown .:: ::: ::. The estate of Leinad Soma Shadow sighed. She was an utter mess right now, blood sweat dripping down her face, skin under her sharp fingernails, and a mess of gore spattered across the front of her. You might say that her training had gone quite well’if you happened to be her, and not her opponents, at least. But training for what? That was a perfectly valid question? most people would have assumed that if Shadow said training, she meant training for Crossfire. After all, she was a former Wrestling Midwest employee, and certainly it meant a lot for her to step into the ring and defeat these people from the other side of it than from within the company. It meant a lot to her to make a good impression, and prove to them that she’d made the right choice by leaving. However, wrestling training, and what she’d just gone through were two entirely different animals. Wrestling training involved a ring, and practicing holds and it involved a mat and going over her aerial maneuvers often enough that she could do them in her sleep. It had nothing to do with dueling with beings that seemed to be her own kind hopped up on every stimulant and painkiller known to man. Training for a match did not involve ripping them apart with her bare hands, using powers that had come to her so recently she still felt like she was a novice with them. And then the rest of it? things becoming too much for her to handle, the arrival of the Crimson Knights? they had seemed to spring out of nowhere, threatening her with a death she was hardly ready to receive. She’d been prepared to take them on, but that was before she found out that Soma had trained each one of them? and had it not been for Soma making the save, she very likely would have died just now’just minutes ago. More than ever now, she was questioning Leinad Soma’s motives. Perhaps it was the nature of his training to keep her in the dark about what was really going on? but Leinad Soma was not a stupid man. Shadow knew that very well. He knew what these new powers of hers were like, he knew what sorts of tests that she could reasonably stand up to with them, and he had evidently just chosen to overwhelm her. Perhaps he’d done it to prove that he was still in control. Shadow looked down at her still-shaking hands, and then looked emotionlessly away. She didn’t know how much of the blood that covered her was her own, and she didn’t suppose that particularly mattered. What mattered to Shadow was that she felt like she’d failed him. She felt like she’d made a promise that she couldn’t keep. She hated being expected to live up to someone else’s expectations, but paradoxically enough, couldn’t stand the thought of letting someone down once she’d let them into her inner circle. For better or for worse, Leinad Soma had made the cut, and dealing with his aspirations for her was something that she should have expected to come along with the territory. Still, it all came back to the question of what, exactly, he wanted out of her. And that, unfortunately, was a question that Shadow didn’t know him well enough to answer. Memories flooded back of last year’s Crossfire’last year, when she had stood across the ring from Leinad Soma, and his then assistant or trainee or whatever he had been? Sage Sabbot. She’d been happy enough to just have the opportunity to compete, no matter whether it had been ridiculously stacked odds or not. Perhaps what had surprised her the most was that The Kindred, consisting of four people, had gone up against just two in Leinad Soma and Sage Sabbot? and lost. It was more than just a little frustrating to realize that you’d had the odds stacked in your favor from the very beginning, but had still screwed up so monumentally. Then again, perhaps that’s what led to her wanting to trust Soma from the moment she first spoke to him after signing with Global’memories of that one week that she had been forced to consider the presence of Leinad Soma on this earth had rushed through her mind? and she’d wanted to know him. She had wanted to know his secrets, his teachings, what made him so powerful, and what made him so drawing. There was a part of her that was telling her quite clearly that she was in over her head right now. That she didn’t know what she was getting into, that she’d trusted him too quickly? but her curiosity had been to blame on that score. More often than not, it was Shadow’s natural curiosity that got her into all of the sticky situations that she’d been in thus far, and her luck that got her out. She supposed that this would be no different. The only problem was the stakes. They were infinitely higher this time, because this time, she was being transformed into something different, a being of a higher plane than the one that she had occupied before. That was always her goal’the goal of Metamorphosis, ascending from her current existence to the next one up from it; according to what Shadow had always been taught, vampirism was not only a step above humanity, but a step below something else. That something else was the goal that she was taught to seek. Pointless killing was not a part of that. Slaughtering anyone she could get her hands on simply because they were put in front of her was not a part of that. Perhaps that was why this whole thing bothered her so much’because Soma was forcing her to push past things that she’d been taught never to do. Learning new lessons was one thing? forgetting all the old ones was another. One thought to her first teacher, Vaclav Ionescu, was all it took to remind her that she had more to do than just exist’she had to hold up the teachings of a dying breed of vampire intellectuals who were being overcome all too quickly by hordes of rabble in these nights. She was suddenly interrupted in her thoughts by the sound of approaching footsteps. Her green eyes seemed to slowly be coming back to earth, after being completely lost for so long. She sniffed the air’human. Most likely that new servant that Soma had hired to do? well, something or other. She really had no clue what the woman was doing, nor why she should particularly care about what the woman was doing, but evidently, this little Kine had some business to discuss with her, because she didn’t go away. Call it insurance that they’ll be too distracted to notice themselves getting their asses kicked.” “Come on, Chris” they weren’t going to beat us anyway, not with three of HG on the Global Team,” Shadow said, smirking at him again. Zenith shrugged, starting to smirk himself. “I know,” he said simply, “Global doesn’t need the extra insurance, but it’s a lot more fun to handle things this way than to just let this be a normal match, don’t you think?” Shadow arched an eyebrow skeptically at him. He certainly did enjoy his mind games. Zenith gestured back into the penthouse with his stein. “Now go put my toy up, Shadow, I don’t want you ruining my fun before Crossfire.” Shadow smirked, and laid the belt over her shoulder, pretending to admire it there for a moment, before looking back over at Zenith and Viv. “You know I’ve always wanted to be the top dog of a place that produces wrestlers who actually uses “gave them the cock” on camera and then expects to be taken seriously. Shame on you, Zee? ruining my dreams like that.” With a chuckle, Shadow stepped back into the penthouse to go hang it up. Of course, it hadn’t mattered that much anyway’what mattered was that things were different now than they were when she’d been in WMW. People were expecting to see the same old Shadow that they’d always seen, but what they always forgot to take into account was that Shadow wasn’t like them. Shadow adapted to fit each occasion. She rose to each challenge. That’s why she’d fared so much better than them in GTT4. That’s why she’d been the only WMW wrestler to ever win a PTC Title. And that’s why she’d taken such perverse pleasure in leaving a place that had made her so miserable, and then tried to tell her it was her fault. But the rules had changed this time around. It wasn’t “you do it my way or I’ll sink your chances” anymore. In fact, WMW didn’t know what they were getting into. Because as far as Shadow was concerned, the moment she set foot in the ring, they were playing by her rules. And her rules did not involve leaving WMW with even the slightest hint of its pride intact’her rules were as merciless as they’d been when they had tried to lay the guilt trip on her about why things were so disgustingly sexist, and why she was always treated like a joke. Maybe, just maybe, some of the people in that Crossfire Cup match would go toe-to-toe with her and learn something’they’d learn exactly what they had lost when they’d let Shadow and Dawn McFarlane walk out the door. They’d learn exactly what they’d lost when they’d said that Shadow and Dawn McFarlane were the people who deserved to be jeered as traitors for going somewhere that made them happier than WMW did. But whatever they learned? they’d be learning on Shadow’s terms. They’d be playing her game. Whether they believed in vampires or not? whether they just wanted to call her a whiny goth clich? or not? whether they thought she deserved anything she’d earned up to this point in her career or not? they’d still be in her shoes’playing by rules that they hadn’t made up. And Shadow had no doubt that WMW was not ready for a dose of its own medicine.
