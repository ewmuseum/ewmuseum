Going Hollywood: The Opening Scenes

27 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 27, 2005 Despite his defeat in the final round of the Gold and Glory Tournament, Mike Biddle was upbeat. Crossfire would provide him with the opportunity to gain his first chance at Global Wrestling’s highest honor. All he would have to do is defeat eleven other wrestlers, the way he saw it the odds were in his favor and all he’d have to do is sit back and wait to collect his championship. His upbeat mood was also drawn from another source, Gilligan’s Island was to start filming that day and the cast and crew were on location in Hawaii to shoot the film’s opening scenes. Mike sat in his trailer in front of a large mirror. His makeup had been done and he had already stopped by wardrobe for his character’s attire, a navy blazer with a white turtleneck and an old sea captain’s hat. He didn’t particularly care for the hat since it covered his precious golden hair and he had caused a small scene when told he was to wear it in all of his scenes, but he had lost the battle. He did have to admit that after putting on the clothes he did feel like he truly was Thurston Howell III. A light tap on the door startled him and he turned to see the Assistant Director in the doorway. “We’re ready for you now Mr. Biddle,” she said with a quiet voice. Mike could tell that she was nervous around him, probably due to his dashing good looks and large pocketbook. He rose from his seat and followed the young woman out into the hot Hawaiian sun and down to the docks that the scene would take place at. His arrival was greeted with a smile from the short and nerdy director, Benjamin Johansen. “Are you ready Mike?” Benjamin asked as he wiped the mist of his sweat off his bifocals. “Of course,” Mike replied with a confident tone. “Good,” Benjamin said, “we’re going to try to get this done as soon as possible so that you don’t miss your flight to Berlin.” Mike nodded his approval and made his way onto the dock, he was more than prepared for this simple scene. All he had to do was approach The Skipper, played by Patrick Warburton, and purchase his ticket for the infamous three hour cruise. Mike looked up at Benjamin and waited for the director’s signal that scene was to begin. Benjamin held his megaphone in one hand as he held his other hand the air and did a small countdown with his fingers. “Action,” he yelled. With the signal given Mike began to walk down the old wooden dock. He stopped when he came upon Patrick Warburton. The other man was busy making sure that his boat was tied securely to the dock, so busy that he did not notice Mike’s presence. Mike cleared his throat and caught the other man’s attention. “Excuse me my good man,” Mike said with a high society accent. “Would you know where I would be able to find the skipper of the Minnow?” Warburton rose from his crouched position and looked down at Biddle. “Of course I would,” Warburton said with his booming voice, “I am the Skipper. What can I do for you sir?” “Ahh, yes,” Mike said. “My friend at the club said that you give the most fabulous three hour tour and I was hoping to purchase passage for the next voyage.” “Good,” Warburton said, “that’ll be fifty dollars. The tour leaves in an hour.” Mike reached into his back pocket and handed Warburton a fake fifty dollar bill and shook his hand. “I’ll be back in forty-five minutes then,” Biddle said as walked off. “CUT,” Benjamin yelled as the scene finished. “Now everyone back in position, I want to do another take.” “What the hell was wrong with the one we just did?” Mike asked. “Nothing at all,” Benjamin nervously replied, “I just want to get a second print to be on the safe side.” “I don’t have time for a second print,” Mike shot back, “my flight to Berlin leaves in two hours and if I miss it I’m going to be a very unhappy man’if you catch my drift.” Benjamin gulped, he hated confrontations. “F-fine,” the director said, “t-take five everyone.” Mike made his way back to his trailer. When he entered the small shelter he found his manager, William Bastafar, and William’s grandson, Carlos Fraga, waiting for him. William was an odd old man that resembled Sean Connery in appearance and his portly grandson was an even stranger person. Most people would never guess that Carlos was related to William; due to the ancestry of the boy’s father he appeared to be Hispanic. The first thing Mike noticed about Carlos was the boy was no longer wheel-chair bound. “I take it that clinic I sent him too worked wonders,” Mike said as he sat down. “Yes it did lad,” William replied in his light Scottish accent. “Are you done with filming yet” The plane will be leaving rather soon?” Mike interrupted William, “I only have one more scene to shoot today and it’s going to start in four minutes, shouldn’t take too long.” William nodded his head and took a seat opposite from Mike; he leaned on his cane and rested his head on his hands as he prepared for the wait. Meanwhile, Carlos looked around the small trailer with wide-eyed wonder. “Hey Mike,” Carlos asked, “Is there something I can be in the movie?” Mike rolled his eyes in annoyance as he rubbed the bridge of his nose his thumb and fore-finger. “I can’t get you a role in the movie kid,” Mike began, “but I can get you one of the most important behind the scenes jobs.” “What’s that?” Carlos asked with an excited voice. Mike turned and looked the fat kid in the eye, “You can be producer,” he said. “What does a producer do?” Carlos asked. “They produce things,” Mike said. “You can start by producing me some coffee.” “Oh boy,” Carlos exclaimed as he left the trailer in search of coffee. After Carlos left the Assistant Director knocked on Mike’s door again. “We’re ready to shoot the boarding scene,” she said with her nervous voice. Mike nodded and grabbed the suitcase prop he was to use for the scene. “I’ll only be a moment William,” Mike said as he exited the trailer. The boarding scene was even simpler than the last scene Mike had shot. It would just be a long shot of the cast boarding the ship while the movie’s main theme played in the background. This scene would then slowly fade into the storm scene, which would be shot using stunt doubles. The four cast members that made up the passengers gathered at the end of the dock. When Benjamin yelled action they all filed onto the boat and were greeted by Gilligan, played by Jimmy Fallon. Once the scene was done Benjamin yelled cut. The director then called for the cast to meet by his big director’s chair. “Okay everyone,” Benjamin breathed, “the first day of filming is done. Tomorrow we’ll head over to the island and shoot the scenes that don’t include Mr. Howell. When Mr. Biddle is done with Crossfire he’ll meet up with us and we’ll go from there.” Benjamin nodded his head and the cast dispersed to their respective trailers. Mike sighed in relief, he was glad that filming was done for the day as he gathered up William and Carlos. They then headed off to the airport, toward Mike’s destiny. -To Be Continued-
