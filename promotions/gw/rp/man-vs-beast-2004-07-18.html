Date: 07.18.04 Man vs Beast MOTM V Qualifyer R2 International Championship July 18th, 2004 ? Evolution before MOTM PPV Soma ? v. Reno 'To insult someone we call him 'bestial.' For deliberate cruelty and nature, 'human' might be the greater insult.' -Isaac Asimov To really sit and ponder upon this quote, one must sit back and realize that it is based upon the nature of both the human animal and the “wild” animal. The wild animal, or beast, in this context, does everything it can for sole survival of itself, its family, and its species in general. That is “bestial” behavior. On the other hand, we have the human animal. The human animal is, to some extent, guaranteed survival in these times. Yet, we still kill our own kind. The human animal is guaranteed rights and freedoms, yet we still fight and argue with our own. The human animal is born into a family but has moved away from this unit in society. Where the bestial animal would relish in such a society where family units can provide safety and security, the human animal furthers itself from this unit; it even scoffs at the tradition! Atop all of this, the human animal is statistically more likely to hurt those of its own family than those of another. So let me ask you, when it comes right down to it? and you’re in a room with your own brother and they have a choice of fighting or handing you over. In which manner would you prefer he react? Human? or bestial? July 18th, 2004 ? Approximately 4:00 PM New York City, New York ? The document originally was written on parchment in an ancient dialect and has long since been forgotten. The story was passed down for centuries through word of mouth and arcane lore and finally written in an early Romanian dialect in or around 1250. This copy of the document, now arcane in our concept of time, has been kept and passed from dynasty to dynasty and government to government for centuries since its creation. It has been locked away in the Wallachia Palace, the Constantinople Palace during the reign of the Ottoman Empire. In slightly more recent times, the document has spent time in the Palace of Versailles, and the Tower of London. The document is as well traveled as the rumors surrounding it. This is quite the opposite of its initial manifestation considering that the original document is thought to have been locked and sealed away in an ancient Egyptian tomb soon after its creation. Until recently, the Vreme de “nţelepciune had been stored in Allerheiligenhofkirche (All-Saints Church) in Munich, Germany. However, the church did not legally own the document. It had fallen into German hands during World War II and due to its unimportance to informed governments, it was simply left where it lie. Through the years, it ended up in the hands of the Munich church. In May, however, an organization known as Geschichtlich Deutschland bought the document with the purpose of preserving German history. The document was sold one week later for a hefty 4,000,000 Euro to an unknown bidder. The organization set the document on its way by train through Germany and into France until it reached the port city of Marseilles. Once reaching Marseilles, the document was placed upon the America bound Le Mer Reine. Le Mer Reine would set sail carrying fine wines to a port in Boston, Massachusetts where a crew would be waiting to unload the French wine. A large crate marked “Das Manifest Das Mal” was removed and instead of making its way onto trucks and to the local importation warehouse, was placed into the back of a white van and put on its way into the hands of its newest owner: Maverick Reno. However, there was a small problem. The Romanian dialect in which the scroll was scribed had not been in pure form for hundreds of years. With this obstacle, and the fact that it was never considered an “important” documentation of history, there were no translated versions to reference. After all, what’s the point in translating ancient Egyptian Vampire Lore? Maverick Reno garnered the services of a small historical group that focuses on Middle Eastern anthropology as well as the associated ancient literatures. It took more than a month and a half to translate the semi-deceased language to Romanian. Once it had reached this point, translation to English took merely days.
