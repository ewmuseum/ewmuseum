Let the games begin

17 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Let the games begin Sergei Strakhlzhenski Global Wrestling Promo #880 Date: 2/17/04 Location: The Airport The meeting with Ms. McCoy hadn’t gone how he would have liked. She had told Surge that a requirement of his employment would be that someone be there to keep him under control. She feared that if he lost his temper, he could genuinely hurt someone. She would either hire a manager for him, or he could get someone of his own choice. In fact, Ms. McCoy’s favorite choice would be for his new bride to rejoin him in his career. Surge had explained his wife’s new career, but to no avail. He was going to have to get someone, and he didn’t want to break to his wife that his career was going to dictate what she would be doing. With that in mind, he opened his cell phone and hit the quick dial number that would put him in touch with her. The other side picked up after a few rings, and answered him in Polish. “Hello sweetie,” she said, with a slight English accent to her Polish. “How did the meeting go with Ms. McCoy?” “Not to well,” said Surge. “She is pretty much demanding that I have a manager or valet to keep me under control during a match. She fears I will hurt someone if I loose my temper.” “I’m afraid I would agree with her,” said Frankie. “In fact, I think she had a point. Would you like me to return?” “Absolutely not,” said Surge. “You have your own career to look after.” “Ah, phooey,” said Frankie. “I’m not doing to well among these homosexual prudes here in Paris. I’ve exhausted all of my avenues here. I’m going to have to go to New York anyway.” “New York,” exclaimed Surge. “Were you going to tell me about this?” “I would have gotten to it before the end of this call, sweetie,” said Frankie. “Besides, I kind of miss all of the action in the Wrestling world. Besides, I could work on a whole new wardrobe for you. You’re due anyway after traipsing all over the world after your brother.” “But…” Surge was speechless. He was expecting to have to go through a fight to get this done. He was expecting the worse. “Well, if that’s what you want, then I’ll have the plane sent for you.” “Don’t bother,” said Frankie. “I already have the tickets to New York. The trip had already been set up. We’ll just meet at the flat.” The flat was an apartment kept by the Strakhlzhenski family in New York. It was open to any of the family having an extended stay in New York, and worked as a permanent US address for Surge, since he was a dual citizen. “Very well,” said Surge. “I’m going to have to do some damage control with the CIA anyway. I kind of treated them rough when they came to meet me at the airport today.” “Sergei, what have I told you about that,” said Frankie. “You know they’re going to be waiting for you every time you enter the country. Its their policy. They have to know what your intentions are.” “I’m sure they know what my intentions are before I get here,” said Surge. “There’s absolutely nothing else I come to the United States for other than for Wrestling. They’re stupid, Frankie, and they disserve to get bruises for their stupidity.” “Well, just remember, they’re going to come with more force next time,” said Frankie. “I just don’t want you to get hurt.” “Don’t worry about me,” said Surge with a smile. “I know how to take care of myself.” “Alright,” said Frankie. “I hope you know what you’re doing. I love you.” “I love you too, and I’ll see you in New York.” With that, Surge turned the phone off and exited the building. Right on cue, Surge was surrounded by a group of men, all with their pistols pulled. “Mr. Strakhlzhenski, you will come with us,” said a commanding voice. “We can do this the easy way or the hard way. I would much prefer the easy way.” Surge noticed one of the men who he had beaten at the airport among the men. She smirked, and then turned to the commanding voice. “Vat do you vant vis me,” he asked. “I just vant to be left alone.” “We have some mandatory questions we have to ask you,” said the man. “Please, this way.” The man pointed at the limo Surge had rented. Surge sighed. “Ve take my ride,” he asked. “Since you were so adamant about it last time, I figured I would just ride along and conduct my interview there.” “Very vell,” said Surge. “Let us go.” The interview was nothing special. He was asked about his last mission, which he could give very few details. Most of it was mundane stuff about what was expected of him while he was in the country. When they arrived at the airport, the man looked around surprised. “You’re not going to be staying in town,” he asked. “No,” said Surge. “I go to meet vis my vife in New York. I am a citizen, you know.” The man smirked. “Yes, I do know you are a citizen.” “Very vell, zen,” said Surge. “I vill take my leave of you here. Driver, you vill take zis man verever he vishes to go. Charge it to my bill.” “You got it Mr. S,” said the driver. Surge got out and got his bags out of the trunk of the car. He walked into the airport and looked around. His pilot was waiting for him at the door. They nodded to each other, and walked out to the jet, where Surge crawled in and sat down, leaning his seat back and going to sleep. Before he fell to sleep, he thought to himself, let the games begin.
