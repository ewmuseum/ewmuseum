Preparing

20 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 20th, 2004 Midnight. A dark basement. A hanging bag. And me. I rest my hand on the top of the punching bag, and smile. It's been a long time, old friend, I think. Too long. Stepping away from the bag, I take a long, deep breath, and let it out slowly. The cool air passing through my lungs feels good. It feels right. When my fist first strikes the canvas bag, that feels right too. I start out working the bag like a boxer, switching up crosses, jabs, and body blows. Soon I'm running on automatic, making the bag dance to my tune. I hadn't realized how much I missed this. Me and the bag. The quiet and the dark. This time, this world of me and the bag and the shadows and the silence: this is mine. Just mine. And in all the craziness in my life, all the schemes and the plots and the madness, none of it can touch me here. My face slips into an easy smile, and I let my mind wander as drive my shin into the bag again and again, repeating the same kick over and over, trying to work out the kinks in my precision. The meeting went well. We're finally ready, I think. Ready to take on whatever they -- TUE, Ice, The Syndicate, whoever -- has to throw at us. The guys, they understand the risks, they know what we have to gain, and they're willing to take this chance. Most importantly, we've started to think like a team. It was a little shaky at first, but everyone's beginning to trust each other. No mean feat, considering who I've brought together. But they trust each other enough to watch each other's backs. And they trust me enough to lead them. Which is exhilarating and scary all at the same time. It's been a long time since I lead troops into battle? But I have to do it. The prize is too precious to pass up. Peace of mind. That's what this whole 'war' is about. Knowing you can go home at night and not worry about a TUE hit squad. That you can get into the ring, and compete without having to look over your shoulder for a sneak attack from The Syndicate. Never having to worry about a sociopath like Ice torturing the ones you love. And so, as I drive my heel into the bag -- where a man's head would be -- things come back around to Ice. I knew they eventually would. I've let him go on and on since the day I beat him, let him say his piece and get it out of his system. I had hoped, that after losing so much, he might find perspective. That maybe at rock bottom, his twisted soul could find peace. I was wrong. And he, along with so many others, can't be left to their own devices any more. Someone has to police them. Someone has to make them stop. Sadly, Christina McCoy has proven that she can't be the one to do it. Her father, Bubba, maybe he could have. But Christina's not strong enough. She has too little experience, and too much fear. And so here I am. Pounding on a sand-filled bag in a darkened basement, steadying my soul for the days to come. I chuckle wryly, realizing that, as much as I thought this was a sanctuary from the chaos of my life, I've let it creep in anyway. Looks like I can't hide from the world, no matter how hard I try. Not inside my own head, anyway. That's fine. The time for hiding is long passed. I kick the bag hard, and stagger several steps back, my breath coming in ragged gasps. I let my self lose track of time, as usual. Pushed myself beyond the limits, as always. I can see the first hint of sunlight creeping through the basement windows. Sweat glistening in the dawn's early light, I stretch out my body, taking a wide martial arts stance, and forcing every muscle I have to tense. Gritting my teeth, I begin to draw out chakra from every part of my body, opening gate after gate. Slowly, the energy trickles into my right hand. As I pour chakra into it, I stretch my arm back, and it feels like it's burning, veins rising and popping? I can hear each knuckle in my hand crack as each finger curls, one after the other, and with a sound like thunder, my open hand becomes a fist. With a roar, I blast forward, slamming my fist into the bag and skidding past it in an instant. All is still, an instant frozen in time. Then the second hand ticks by, and the bag explodes, unleashing a shower of sand in all directions. 'Sorry friend,' I mutter, 'it's all for the cause?' I'm ready now, I know it. Ready to lead them to war. Ready to be the Champion I always should have been. I'm ready for war. But war's not ready for me.
