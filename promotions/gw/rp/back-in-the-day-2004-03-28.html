Back in the Day...

28 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/28/04 July 14, 1992 There was a faint squeal of old metal as the breaks on the van engaged and it pulled to a stop next to the curb in front of the old gym in the heart of Chicago's south side. The hot summer sun beamed down, gleaming off the paint that would normally be white if not for all the dirt covering it. The windows, darkly tinted also reflected the sun, but not so bright that you could not make out the shapes of it's passengers. Or the meshed metal that lines the inside. The vehicle was boldly marked, not just by the graffiti like interpretation of a middle finger sticking up, cut out using the dirt on the back of the van as a rebellious medium. The State seal of Illinois emblazened on the side boldy proclaimed it's official nature. The string of letters next to the seal literally spelling out it's intended application.... CSD.... Department of Juvenile Justice. The engine shuts down and almost immediately the passenger and driver's side doors shoot up open. Two green and khaki clad Sherrifs, complete with the stars on their chests step down from the van and slam the doors behind them. Both sets of eyes are protected from the sun by your typical aviator sunglasses that reflect the picture in front of them. The coming from the passenger side pulls up his clipboard and pulls the pin from his left chest pocket. The driver dangles his hefty key chain as the passenger jots down a few notes. Finally the passenger, hefty with a gut and a mustache, not to mention the sergeant stripes on his sleeve motions over to his partner with a simple head nod. The second sherrif, much younger with few stripes and less body fat, moves over to the sliding side door and takes a moment to pick out the correct key. He slides it into the lock and turns. The door slides back an inch on it's own before he completes the process stepping to the side of the exit for the passengers, pulling the door along with him. 'Alright gentlemen!' yells the sergeant, establishing control right off the bat. 'File out!' With obvious reluctance 4 young men, all dressed it cheap denim jeans, a white t-shirt and blue denim overshirts, slowly file out onto the sidewalk. They are diverse in background, 2 black, one white and yet another of latin descent, they share the same uninterested and even defiant postures and mean looks, but they all seem to know the same routine. They instintively line up side by side in a straight line...though there seems to be a straggler. It's possible his relative lack of experience in the system is the reason for his slow pace. After all, he hadn't been there nearly as long as the rest of his traveling buddies. Just a matter of weeks. But of course, that's not it. The young man, who can't be a day over 15, his brown skin already begining to glisten with sweat in the heat, obviously doesn't care. Odd that the smallest amongst them, standing at least 2-3 inches shorter than the rest, maybe 150 soaking wet, would be the rebel. It's obvious to anyone that his wirey frame can't handle the weight of being out on a limb by himself. Obvious to everyone, but him. The short tufts of hair going every which way from his scalp don't move much even when the young sherrif behind him gives him a stiff shove in the back, finally putting him into his proper place in line. An irritated scowl quickly works its way across his face as he glances back at his assailant. 'Eyes forward.' the young sheriff states plainly. The rebel slowly turns his face forward as the sergeant steps forward towards the center of the line, the whole group distorted and small, reflecting in the lenses of his shades and his eyes. 'Alright,' he begins. 'Let's get some shit straight up front gentlemen. If it were up to me you little bastards would be locked down 23 and 1 just like they do upstate. But, unfortunately the bleeding hearts in the country don't see it the same way. Not only do they not see it the same way, but neither does our illustrious superintendant. Now I don't know how he met the man that runs this dump, but apparently they are friends. And, somehow or another, he's convinced the superintendant that he can just turn you boys right around if he gets a little time with you. Obviously this man doesn't know you like I do. I'm letting you know right now. There's not gonna be a whole lot of bullshit tolerated just because you're on the outside today gentlemen. You will do exactly as you're told, when you're told to do it. Anything else will result in an asskicking . No if's, and's or but's. I don't know why, but you junior achievement crooks have been extended a courtesy. You'd better be curteous in return, because if you're not I will make you pay for it. Now... Roll call, respond with 'here' when I call your name. Jackson, Leon....' 'Here.' 'Micheals, Randy....' 'Here' 'Rameirez, Julio....' 'Here.' 'James, Anthony....' The sound of the wind... cars passing on the street... quiet. Sarge slowly moves over and gets right into the Mr. Rebel's face. 'James, Anthony!' 'There's only four of us dogg, I'm right here.' responds the little man, his face turned away from the sergeant actively trying to stare him down. The surly sheriff stars down with a searing gaze, not quite hot enough to melt off the brass plating. Vice like, his grip quickly locks onto the shoulder of Anthony's shirt, yanking the kid half up on his tiptoes. Inside it has to phase him as the relative giant takes advantage of his size, but Anthony isn't about to let it show on his face. Still locked in a scowl, the rebel continues to avoid eye contact with the sheriff who's all over him now. 'I ain't your fucking dogg you little shit!' The sergeant yells. 'Jack...' the younger officer starts, trying to bring things back down a notch. 'Shut the fuck up Johnson!' the sergeant growls, cutting him off at the pass. 'You're a real tough guy aren't you Anthony?' 'Depends on who you ask.' Anthony responds steadily, using all his might to keep the fear from creeping out in his voice. 'Naw, don't be modest there tough guy.... I mean that's why you're here, right? Cuz you're so damn tough. What was the charge? Assualting a police officer.' 'Yup.' 'haha... You see this Johnson? This little shit attacks a cop and these fucking family court judges just charge him as a fucking minor............. They should've just let you head up to the pen like all the other tough guys. Yeah, those fucking cons would have had a lot of fun with your little ass.' 'Whatever man.' the rebel throws out. ' You know that cop you hit with that pipe? Well, keep right on pushing your luck cuz that cop you hit? He was a friend of mine. A good man...' For the first time something about the conversation causes the young man to break. But not backwards...straight forward. A hint of the man he could grow up to be comes out as he snaps his gaze square into the sergeant's eyes. 'Yo man put my boy Biggie in the hospital and he wasn't even doing shit! Doctors talking about he won't even be able to talk right after he hit him in the throat.... I don't care how good he is, FUCK yo man!' Bam It takes no time at all for Anthony to be planted back first against the van, his feet dangling in the air, not even close to touching the ground. The move shocks everyone, even sheriff Johnson has to jump out of the way to avoid being run over. The sergeant used both hands to pick Anthony up, but doesn't need them both to hold him up. He quickly and simply transfers one hand to the opposite shoulder, crushing Anthony against metal van with his massive forearm, his newly freed hand reaching for his night stick. Johnson doesnt' seem much help in the situation, freezing as if he doesnt' know whether to shit or go blind. It sounds like a sword being drawn from it's sheath as the night stick slides out of the metal ring along the side of the sergeant's belt. Anthony's body thrashes, but can't free himself from the iron grip. There isn't much his body can do, the only option now, preparing his mind for whats about to come. As the other boys interject their voices, yelling for this situation to come to an end, the night stick readies itself in the air. It's on the cusp of swinging when.... 'Hey!' yells a voice from behind. This voice is new...more forceful as it carries the weight of years. The sergeant glances back to the man behind him. The man's skin, the color of milk chocolate brightly reflects the sun's harsh rays off his clean shaven scalp. His face show's his age, likely somewhere in his 50's. However, his body doesn't seem to realize it's age, the new players stripped running suit not baggy enough to hide the barrel chested, muscular frame beneath it. The man keeps a steady pace as he quickly strides out of the two front doors of the goldish yellow building, emerging from beneath the sign above the doors bolding proclaiming this place 'The Bottoms Training Facility' in red and silver letters. 'This is police business sir, it doesn't concern you!' barks the sergeant. 'This is my property officer.' retorts the man, quickly closing the distance. 'Anything that happens on it is my business. And I'll always be concerned when I see a grown man threatening a child with a night stick...officer.' The man stops just outside the crowd up people, poised to take on whatever comes next. Anthony's scowl is still fierce, but it can't hide a slight sense of relief that someone is finally here to bail him out. 'I think the boy has learned his lesson.' 'Oh really? I don't think so. If he had learned any lessons he wouldn't be here now.' 'That might be true. But he's here to learn a lesson today. And I'd hate to tell your superintendant that you impeded that...the next time I play golf with him...or we have lunch downtown.' Johnson seems to finally have found his cajones again and moves over to his sergeant. Whatever he whispers in the sergeant's ear is hard to make out, but certainly it's something to the effect of not screwing up both their careers. Slowly the sergeant lowers Anthony back to the pavement, sliding the night stick back into it's holding ring even slower. The sergeant leans down menacing leans down and whispers into Anthony's ear... 'Do something stupid. I'm begging you.' Train jerks away, moving out from beneath the sergeant's shadow. 'Alright,' begins the sergeant. 'They're all yours.' 'Line up men.' says Anthony's rescuer. The boys regain their composure and quickly line up, even Anthony following suit. 'Alright. First off, my name...is Isaiah. And you? You're here for one reason and one reason only. You're here... because you're fighters. Now normally I'd appreciate that being a former fighter myself. Here's the problem. You boys lack discipline. You go around stupidly fighting every thing and every one, not having the good sense to pick your battles. Well... I don't plan to stop your from fighting. No, as a matter of fact I plan on teaching you how to fight even better. But there is one difference. See you ain't about to take what I give you and and fight in the streets. No, no, no... You wanna fight? Fight for something that's worth a damn for once. Fight for a better life. Fight to be a better person. The only way I'll agree to help any of you...is if you stop fighting in the streets and start fighting your way off of them. Stop fighting to be a bunch of statistics and start fighting to be champions. I got two rules. Education and discipline. If you are going to participate in this program you have two obligations, not just to me, but to yourselves. One? You will strengthen your mind. If you are not persuing your diploma or GED you are not in my program. If you are persuing your diploma or GED, but failing, you are not in my program. If you are in my program you will check your attitude and stop trying to play hard rock for every dumbass that talks about you. If you are caught fighting in any setting except the one I'm about to take you to you are not in my program. Now, what I'm about to teach you has taken me to every state in this country and around the world. But I grew up here in the south side. No matter how well I did, I couldn't just walk away from this place without give other kids who came up tough just like me the opportunity to make all the toughness a place like this grows count for something.' Isaiah opens his mouth to continue is motivational speech, but stops as he detects a chuckle. He turns, trying to find the source. He almost chuckles a little himself as he finds his detractor. Perhaps the sergeant had been right. The young man that was pressed up against the van a second ago apparently hasn't learned much. 'Something funny?' Isaiah inquires. 'Naw man... But I'm already making it count for something. And I'm counting in 10's, 20's, 50's and 100's.' says Anthony, bring a seperate round of chuckles from the other boys. The sergeant stiffens again, his body motioning another close encounter for Anthony. But Anthony waves him off as he looks into the eyes of the bold young man. 'I've got it.' says Isaiah. He's got heart, he'd give him that. 'I'm sure you are man. I'm sure you're clocking big money out there on the corner. Only question is are yougoing to be a leech for the rest of your life?' 'Leech? You callin' me a leech?' 'Well, you're slinging God knows what to people who are trying to kill their pain and making money off it. You're feeding off their misery. What would you call it kid?' 'First off, I ain't no damn kid, aight? And second of all, I call it feeding my family. I call it paying rent. I call it keeping the lights on. That's what the fuck I call it!' 'That's it...' growls the sergeant, quickly pulling his night stick. It was time to put this kid in his damn place. Someone was going to have to restore some order, before this got out of control. He cocked the stick back as he quickly stormed over in Anthony's direction. Suddenly, instead of gaining control, he found himself being controled, bolts of pain shooting up his arm. His wrist was twisted in a way that wasn't natural, trapped within the iron grip of Isaiah's trained hand. The night stick toppled to the cement with a clatter as a scream of pain escaped the the sergeant's lungs. Sheriff Johnson draws his nightstick in defense of his partner, but that's about all he can manage, frozen by his confusion as to what to do. All the boys are amazed at how quickly the menacing man had been nuetralized, including Anthony. 'I said I've got it sergeant.' 'Let me go!' The sergeant's free hand moves for his holster, but another quick jerk locks up his hold body, the pain making it nearlly impossible to do anything. 'Alright! Alright God damn it!' screams the sergeant. 'Anthony, right?' asks Isaiah. Anthony, as much in shock as anyone else just stares up at Isaiah, nodding in agreement. 'Anthony, this is called a top wrist lock. Applied correctly, you can easily nuetralize an opponent, even one larger than you. Now... I want you to understand, while this is one of the things I can teach you, this isn't the most important. Neither is any move you'll learn working with me. I admire the fact that you do what you do to provide for the people you love. But that don't change the fact that it's wrong. You're wrong for helping to flood this community with drugs, no matter what the reason is. You know why? It's the easy route. You're going to find out... The hardest thing you can think to do? It's usually the right thing. It's a pain, but that's life and that's what a man does. He does the right thing. I can teach your wrist locks, submission holds, suplexes, body slams and a million different other things. But those aint' the things that count. It's learning to do the right thing. It's learning to be a man. Now... You talk big, but like I said, the right thing is almost always the hard thing and working with me is going to be hard. So the question is for you...all of you... are your ready to learn how to be a man?' Perhaps some of the depth of the question put to him was sinking in. What would likely have been Anthony's usual brash response doesnt' come. He stares for a moment at the older black man, strong and up right controling the man that was controling him and he knew that what he did next was going to haunt him or help him for years to come. 'Yeah' Anthony said plainly. 'I'm ready.' There is a moment of silence in the agreement. Isaiah shoots his hands open, releasing the sergeant, who topples to a knee, but springs right back up again, holding his wrist. His anger seems to burn hotter to the son as he snaps around, turning to Isaiah. 'Thank you sarge.' Isaiah says. 'I appreciate you allowing me to demonstrate one of the basic wrestling manuevers on your for the guys here. I'll be sure to tell the superintendant how you jumped right on board with this program. I'm sure he'll be happy to know you were so...enthusiastic.' It's a hefty meal. The sergeant grimaces as he realizes he has about no choice, but to eat it. He simply reaches down, picks up his sword and sheathes it. 'Cork it up gentlemen!' growls the sergeant. 'If you're ready... let's get started.' says Isaiah, his authority making itself evident. The group moves in as a group, following close behind Isaiah as he lead the way to the gym doors and the whole crew disappears into the front hallway as the scene fades to black.
