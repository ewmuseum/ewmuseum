A Fallen Angel: Interview With A Murderer

13 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 13, 2005 With a depressed sigh he turned the key in the latch and entered his home. Despite the bright, sunny weather of Beverly Hills he felt dark and gloomy within. He grunted as he threw his gym bag on the floor and then he sulked into the concealing shadows of his unlit house. He entered the living room and sank into the leather armchair in front of the big screen television. He didn’t turn the television on; he didn’t want to listen to the news reports about how he had allegedly killed his ex-girlfriend. He rested his chin on his hand and began to wonder why he always had to deal with so much drama. Things used to be easy, his opponents used to tremble at the sound of his name but now they suppressed hysterical laughter whenever he approached the octagon. There was a time, not too long ago, that he was a perennial main eventer but now he was nothing more than opening match fodder...the guy that was supposed to go out there and make the new kids look good. He smirked as he realized that it had been that way ever since he had left Hillary. Back when she had been his valet he had been unstoppable but since he had parted ways with her professionally he had been nothing more than a caricature of his former self. Something was going to have to change, he just didn’t know what. Suddenly the sound of the James Bond theme broke his concentration; his cell phone was ringing. He wasn’t expecting a call so it was with a catlike curiosity that he reached into his pocket and pulled out the small device. He didn’t recognize the number on the display screen but decided to answer the call anyway. “Hello?” He casually asked. “Mr. Biddle,” a deep voice said, “are you in Los Angeles at the moment?” “Yeah,” Biddle asked, both annoyed and curious at the same time. “Good,” the deep voice replied. “I need you to come down the station right now for that questioning we talked about not too long ago.” “I’ll be there within the hour,” Biddle said before he hung up. Right now the last thing he wanted to do was answer Detective Hagen’s questions but he had no other choice. So with his “woe is me” attitude in full effect, Mike Biddle rose from his seat and left for the West Hollywood Police Department. ***** The Police Department’s questioning room was a small, dimly lit; impersonal room with no windows whatsoever. The only decoration was an intercom that hung up on the Westside of the room, presumably so the higher ups in the Department could listen in on the questioning. Biddle sat at a small wooden table, still clad in the gray sweat suit that he had worn on the short plane trip back from Las Vegas. On the other side of the table was Detective Hagen, the lines on the Detective’s weather beaten face had deepened from the look of concentration that had taken over his features. Hagen thoughtfully stroked his goatee as he thought of a way to open his line of questioning. Hagen took off his suit jacket and loosened his tie as he began the interrogation. “All right Biddle,” Hagen said, “I’m going to start off with a few questions about the background of your relationship with Miss Watson. I want to see if I can find a possible motive for why you or someone else would have killed her.” Biddle slumped back in his chair, he had already been there for five minutes and he already felt like a kid in a carpet store. “I thought you guys solved cases with science now,” Biddle said, “like on CSI. I mean, just the other day you guys took hair and blood samples from me. Isn’t that enough for your investigation?” Hagen smiled sarcastically. “This isn’t TV,” he said, “while the Department does use forensic methods to solve cases, with the number of crimes that happen in this city the CSI is a little backed up at the moment and hasn’t had a chance to check all the evidence yet. Besides, I prefer to use the old fashioned method of investigation, gut feelings, and deduction.” Biddle didn’t reply, he just waited for the Detective to start the line of questioning. The sooner Hagen started, the sooner Biddle could get back to lamenting about the pathetic state of his GLOBAL career. “Getting back to the reason we’re here,” Hagen firmly stated, “tell me Mr. Biddle, how exactly did you meet Miss Watson?” Mike leaned back as he thought back to that fateful night nearly three years ago. When he had met Hillary he had thought she’d be nothing more than a one-night stand, he never thought that she would become the most melodramatic chapter of his life. “I was at a party a few years back,” Biddle reminisced, “it was a boring party and I was trying to grab a date and sneak out. I had a particular woman in mind, I think her name was Crystal...but of course she doesn’t matter. I was about to approach her when I was cut off by one the waitresses...the one that was serving the champagne. I didn’t notice her until we had literally run into each other and I had champagne all over my good tux. “She immediately apologized and tried to dry me off while she picked up the bits of broken glass off the floor. At first I was furious, but once I got a good look at her I saw that she was a lot prettier than the woman that I had set my sights on earlier was. I helped her up and immediately asked her if she wanted to come home with me. At first she seemed offended by the invitation but after our eyes met she gave in. I asked her when her shift ended and she told me that she was supposed to leave in an hour but could talk her boss into letting her leave early. So while she went to talk with her boss I went to the rest room and dried off. I met her out back ten minutes later and we left for my place,” Mike finished the story with a wistful look on his face. He would never forget the night he had met Hillary, that night had been some of the best sex he had ever had. Across the table, Hagen took notes of what Biddle had just said. “And I take it this encounter led to a relationship between the two of you?” Hagen asked, he didn’t bother to look up from his notes. “Of course,” Biddle sighed. “How was this relationship?” Hagen asked, devoid of emotion. “Everything was fine at first,” Biddle answered. “But after a few months she became very clingy and annoying. I tried dumping her but for some reason she thought I was joking. I tried yelling at her and kicked her out of my house, but she sued me for palimony and started hanging around my house again after the judge threw the case out. So I had to try more desperate measures.” Hagen arched his eyebrow at those words. “Such as?” He asked. Biddle took a deep breath; the story he was about to tell wasn’t one he was particularly proud of. “I had called her up and told her to put on her best dress, I was going to take her out to dinner at a fancy restaurant. I picked her up and she looked absolutely stunning in her little black dress. I could tell that she didn’t suspect that I wasn’t really taking her out. So she got in my car and I drove to Mount Wilson and left her there.” “Did that help her get the hint?” Hagen sarcastically asked. “No it didn’t,” Biddle replied, “she got a ride back to my house from some trucker and was actually happy about it. She thought I was giving her some kind of message about how she needed to be more serious about our relationship.” “I see,” Hagen said as he wrote down some more in his notebook. “So how did you finally break up with her then?” “I asked her to marry me,” Biddle answered. “Excuse me?” Hagen said, looking up from his notes. “I said I asked her to marry me.” “I heard that part, but I don’t see how that would be a very productive way to break up with someone,” Hagen replied. Biddle smiled, even though the way he had broken up with Hillary had been extremely cruel it had been one of his most brilliant schemes. “We went to her parent’s house in Boston and started to plan the ceremony. It took a few months to plan everything out but the day of the wedding finally came. Of course, I wasn’t there for the ceremony. I left a note for Hillary that explained how the whole wedding idea had been an elaborate plan to break up with her. The plan worked the way I had wanted, I didn’t hear from her for months after that.” “But she showed up again, correct?” “Unfortunately, yes she did.” Biddle answered. “How were you involved with her in the last year or so then?” Hagen asked, his head rested on his hands as he waited for Biddle’s answer. Biddle formed the words in his head he ran his right hand through his blond hair, almost as if he were trying to jump-start his brain. “Hillary first came back to Beverly Hills right in the middle of my wedding ceremony with Lisa. Hillary came during the part about objecting to the ceremony. She beat me to the punch, I’m afraid. I was going to object to the wedding because Lisa was only after my money and I wanted to embarrass her in front of everyone.” “Okay,” Hagen mused, “but I take it your association with Miss Watson didn’t end there?” “No it didn’t,” Biddle replied. “A few months ago I became blacklisted because of my participation in professional wrestling and Hillary helped me cope with the situation and she helped me un-blacklist myself.” “I see,” Hagen said, “did the two of you become involved romantically again during this time?” Biddle smiled arrogantly and chuckled to himself. “Of course,” he said, “I had found out that Charles, whom she was seeing at the time, was responsible for me being blacklisted. So I crashed one of his parties and took her away from him. And the next morning I dumped her and boy did she get pissed off...I think she had finally realized how I felt about her that morning and I didn’t see her again until...well, until the day she died.” Hagen sighed as he finished writing this information in his notebook. “I see that the two of you had quite the relationship,” the Detective said. “Yes we did.” Hagen now looked Biddle directly in the eyes. “So tell me Mr. Biddle,” the Detective began, “what happened the last time you saw Miss Watson alive?” Biddle rubbed the bridge of his nose with his forefinger and thumb before he answered the Detective’s question. “I had just gotten out of the hospital and I needed to stop by Hillary’s house to pick up my lucky gear...you see I had a really big match coming up and I needed the gear.” “Go on,” Hagen urged. “She buzzed me in without even asking who I was and I found her standing in the hallway waiting. She got upset that I had stopped by and told me that she was waiting for Charles and that I better leave before he showed up. I explained to her why I was there and she agreed to give me back the gear. I followed her in to her apartment and she handed me what I had come for. I turned around to leave and after I walked out the door I looked over my shoulder to thank her but the she had closed the door behind me. I hadn’t thought anything was up until you showed up at my house and told me I was a suspect,” Biddle honestly stated. “All right then Mr. Biddle,” Hagen said as he finished his notes. “Thank you for co-operating thus far in the investigation. If I need you for anything else I trust I can count on you to help out?” “Of course.” “Good,” Hagen said, “you can go now.” Biddle rose from his seat and left without saying another word to Hagen. The Detective watched as Biddle left the room, up until today Hagen had no idea that Biddle really was the sort of man that he played on television. “That guy needs therapy,” Hagen thought to himself. Once Biddle was gone Hagen reached into his pants pocket for his cell phone and dialed a number. “Mr. Magee,” he said after a few moments. “I’d like you to come down to the station for some questioning.” ***** An hour later Harry Hagen found himself seated once again in the interview room. Only this time Mike Biddle wasn’t seated in the chair across from him. Charles sat in the chair with a very nervous air about him, he looked like a small child that had just been called to the Principal’s office. Hagen cleared his throat and grabbed Charles attention. “Okay Mr. Magee,” Hagen began. “I’d like a little background on you and Miss Watson. So I’m going to have to know how you met her.” Charles smiled nervously and ran his hand through his thick red hair and paused on the back of his head for a scratch. “Mike brought her with him to a party once. She was his flavor of the week at the time and I remember that the first moment I laid eyes on her I was instantly smitten by her,” Charles said. Hagen nodded his head and wrote the response down in his notebook. “Did you ever become romantically involved with Miss Watson?” Hagen asked. “Yes I did,” Charles replied. “When?” “We became an item over the summer,” Charles said, “after Mike was done filming that awful movie.” Harry nodded, he had seen the movie but he had found it to be delightfully stupid. “From what I understand you then took it upon yourself to get Mr. Biddle kicked out of your social circle and he repaid you by taking Hillary away from you,” Hagen stated. Charles looked down at the table, apparently embarrassed to have this particular event brought up. “I was trying to save her from him,” he quietly said, “Mike had caused so much pain in her life I thought it only proper that he not be near her. But for some reason she helped him get off the blacklist and then he showed back up and whisked her away from me.” A tear made it’s way down his cheek as he completed his sentence. Hagen could clearly see the jealousy in the young man’s green eyes. “Did you ever get back together with Miss Watson?” Hagen asked. “No,” Charles sighed, “but I’ve spent the last few months trying to win her back.” Hagen wasted no time in asking his next question, “When was the last time you saw her alive?” Charles sighed again, “The day before she died. I ran into her on Rodeo and asked her if she would like to get together and do something. She told me to come over the next day and maybe we’d go to a movie at the Chinese theater or something.” Two more tears joined the previous one, tracing it’s footsteps down Charles’s face. Hagen waited a moment for Charles to wipe the tears from his face. Charles was certainly more emotional than Biddle. “Just one more question Mr. Magee,” Hagen softly said. “What happened the day Miss Watson was murdered?” Charles took a deep breath and went over the events of the day in his mind, he still couldn’t believe that she was gone. “I went to go pick her up and I noticed that Mike’s limousine was parked in front of her building so I waited to see if she was going to leave with him. He came out alone with that damned arrogant smirk on his face and left. It looked as if he had stopped by to cause her more pain so I decided to come back later.” “What happened when you came back?” “She wouldn’t buzz me in so I rang Mrs. Duran’s bell and she let me in. Then I went up to Hillary’s door and spent an hour knocking. I knew she was in there, her car was still parked out front. But I gave up and went home. The next day I heard she had been killed,” Charles was too choked up to continue. “Thank you Mr. Magee,” Hagen said. “You’ve been in a huge help in my investigation, we’ll be in touch okay?” Charles nodded. “May I go now?” “Of course you can,” Hagen said as he wrote a few things in his notebook. After Charles left Hagen began to wonder why he was always the one stuck with the melodramatic cases. After interviewing the two men he was now certain he knew who the killer was. -To Be Continued-
