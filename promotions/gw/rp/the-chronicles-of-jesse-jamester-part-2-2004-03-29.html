The Chronicles Of Jesse Jamester: Part 2

29 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3-29-04 Prologue. Jesse Jamester proved that he’d been under stress, but to what extent? He drank some whiskey and took some pills, not enough to kill him, but definitely enough to have him pass out. To some it’d seem like this may be a very dangerous attempt at suicide, but the pills Jesse took were sleeping pills, and what he drank was only to get the pills to dissolve faster. All this information was acquired by the local hospital where Jesse was treated. As he’d only had the pills in his system for a good twenty minutes, the doctors made him regurgitate whatever may be in his stomach under his girlfriend’s orders. Jesse now has to put up with the two people who care for him the most, for they, as he does too, want to make sure Jesse is okay. Can Jesse withstand the pressure of two sets of eyes watching over him at all times? Can ‘the kid’ forget about his past doubts and dreadful memories? Will Jesse overcome his fears of failure? Or will he have no choice but to give in to the demons he’s fought for the last twelve years to reach this stage of his career? The Turbulence The sun shined through the windows of the living room into the chair where Jesse was found just last night passed out. Now Jesse’s back in the chair, and because the pills he took he’s being lectured by his girlfriend and his best friend. As if the pills didn’t kill him, their yelling may just do it instead. ’What the hell was you thinking man!’ yelled Ryan in his left ear, ’I can’t believe you did that! How… ah, I was scared to death, stated Nikki in his right ear. ’I’m sorry. I wasn’t thinking okay? I just thought they’d help me get some sleep, since I hadn’t had any rest in two days. I’m sorry okay! Damn it, it’s not like everything else in my life hasn’t stressed me to a boiling point, and now you too?’ boasted Jesse worried of his upcoming match. ’Jesse, we’re sorry but this is hard to deal with. What if we didn’t find you till sometime that evening? Would you have been all right? I’m sure the pills wouldn’t have killed you but you’d never be ready for your match in that state of mind, Nikki calmly replied trying to explain how it could have been worst. The three sat there for a moment, and then Nikki got up and left with a disapproving yet worried expression she wore. Shutting the door on her way out she left in a hurry, leaving Ryan and Jesse alone to talk. ’I’m sorry… I can’t stress how much I am, but like I said Ryan man, I’m already in a bind. With me going into this big match come Sunday that I’ve always wanted to be apart of, and now having that chance, I feel it’s more of my duty to be prepared than anyone can imagine. This is my chance, you know how I feel, and I just reacted last night without evening thinking about what would happen,’ Jesse rambled on as he too was explaining himself as Nikki did moments ago, ’and I’m going to start thinking more before doing things. This is my chance Ryan, and I want to make that chance a dream come true. Ryan stared at the floor for a few moments before looking up at Jesse and wondering what he’d thought the night before. In the head of Jesse this was his life, but to Ryan that dream of being a wrestler faded many years ago along with his ability to walk normally. Ryan stared at Jesse with cold unforgiving eyes, ’it’s not okay. Jesse you caused me to worry about you when you knew what you were doing and knew what the consequences were. How am I suppose to react to that? You scared Nikki into a dark place last night. She thought you were going to die!’ Jesse could only shake his head and shrug his shoulders because his words seemed to be nothing that Ryan wanted to hear. Though Ryan did care for Jesse, he had something inside that wanted Jesse to recognize how much tension that Jesse positioned on the two in so little time. It wasn’t like Jesse to be so imprudent thought Ryan. This was the man who’d taken his own cousin Julius dress up like him to wrestle Tristan Belmont for the world title in Lights Out Wrestling when Jesse went into major surgery. This was the same man that had taken a sledgehammer and scared the best by using it in ways never seen before. This was Jesse Jamester thought Ryan, even though he was really Jesse Fristik, the man was the same. Looking at Jesse as he seemed to not care Ryan spoke up, ’this… has to stop. You know you’re afraid and you use tactics like this to have people surround you to help, only to let you know that winning isn’t everything. But that’s not what you think; you just use us to feed you this garbage so when you lose you can feel okay about it. Well you want to know something Jesse,’ Ryan stopped for a moment and grabbed Jesse by the shoulder to turn him to position him face to face, ’You will not lose, and you will get no pity! Jesse, you must win, because this is your life. So get off your ass and get to doing something constructive.’ Ryan let go of Jesse and walked away, he could no longer look at this man as a friend. Jesse had become a maggot who’d feed off his family and friends to feel better about himself, but this time it didn’t work. Jesse sat there, looking at the floor, not sure of what to do. His mind drew a blank, for he’d never had this problem before. He’d been left to actually try, and as all the times before had succeeded, he was now at the point where he had to make a decision. What would that decision be though? The Holy Water Alcohol was Jesse’s holy water. From the time he first drank it at the ripe age of twenty-one to the current days of which his thirty-five year old body used it as fuel. Jesse could be classified as a regular, for he’d drink whenever his mind felt like nothing else was worth doing. Now was one of those times unfortunately. Only ten minutes after the talk with Ryan, Jesse had started to drink. The bar stool he sat on was his normal seat, far from the door but close to the bar tender. Jesse pulled out his wallet and dropped three fifties on the bar top before asking for some a beer. The bar tender smiled and took the fifty and got the change before serving Jesse his first of what would be many beers. The mind of Jesse Jamester worked in ways many wouldn’t comprehend. Jesse was a sensible guy, and loved what he did and the people he was around, but when pressure surrounded him, drinking along with other irrational things became his problem solvers. He’d never taken the problems into consideration for he as many couldn’t solve their own problems, and instead just stored them until they built up and exploded. For Jesse the ring was where those problems began to explode one at a time. Each person he faced he’d unleash his problems on sometimes even believing the person was the problem, and he’d fight them down and beat them until he saw the actual person and noticed what he was doing. Wrestling was a stress reprieve mechanism for Jesse, but not just that. He’d always taken his mind to new heights thinking of what he could do to put on a show, since acting wasn’t Jesse entity. He had thing journal he wrote in thinking up ideas to do in the ring, his finisher was one he thought of a long time ago. He’d been watching a movie called ‘twister’ when the idea of picking up a man above his head, then twisting them while they fall, while holding their neck that he’d definitely turn some heads. That’s exactly what he did too. But moves weren’t all that circulated in Jesse’s head. He’d made matches that seemed impractical and unbearable to effectively do in a ring without causing pain of unnatural proportions to both men. Yet, he did them, and he became known worldwide for some of his matches, one being a Taipei death match. Jesse was a wrestler, and a good one at that. Though it seemed near impossible for him to ever win a world title Jesse was focused on doing so, and if he could win the Global Title in a match with Chris Storm, Apocalypse, and Patti Anderson, he’d do one better than a world title. This was his dream, doubting it or not that he’d win, Jesse had to put his heart into it, and had to show why his name, and his families name, would never be forgotten. That’s how Jesse felt, and that’s why he would still wrestle in this condition of worry. Staring at the bar counter in front of him Jesse had only drank one beer. He looked down and noticed it wasn’t even empty, for all he’d done was think the whole time. Maybe it was his mind that had said no to adjust and think about what Ryan said to him. Either way Jesse was done drinking and he’d left a five-dollar bill on the counter as a tip for the bartender, as he got up and took his other cash he’d placed there earlier. Unforgotten Ones As he walked down the street the lights had become more vivid above him as the sky became dusk. Jesse had been nowhere near drunk when he left but for unknown reasons he looked it while he walked. Stepping left and right bumping into things almost as if he’d been hitting vodka for the last six hours. However he stopped and sat down on a bench that was the waiting place for a bus. Not a soul was out this late, only the cars that passed showed signs of life. Jesse looked tired, yet wanted to do something. His eyes searched up and down the street, until they stopped upon a payphone. He got up and walked over to it, then reached into his pockets and brought some change out for the call. Dialing the number he waited as the phone began to ring on the other end. Ring… Ring… Ring… Ring… Someone finally picked up. The voice an above average voice with somewhat a high pitch as Jesse stared at the ground and smiled at the sound of it, ’Hello? stated the voice on the other end showing it’s feminists. ’Hello, do you know who this is?’ said Jesse smiling as he held the phone to his ear. ’No, who is it?’ said the voice on the other line. ’It’s your father, Jesse!’ boasted Jesse while nearly crying as his little girl starting to yell ‘Mommy it’s Jesse!’ on the other end of the phone, ’How’s my little girl doing, huh Sammie?’ ’I’m fine daddy. I’m in the fifth grade now; I’m learning math, spelling, English, history, and science. How are you daddy?’ said Samantha as bliss as possibly could be, knowing that she hasn’t talked to her father in about a year. ’I’m fine honey. I’ve been traveling a lot and was worried about you. It’s been a long time since we last talked, I hope you aren’t mad at me for that. I would have called earlier an all, but you know how I’m so busy,’ rambles Jesse trying to explain himself. ’It’s okay. I know you’re busy, and you can barely find time to see me, but I still love you,’ Samantha claims with a calm voice. ’Well I have to ask you something. It deals with you and I being together. What would you say to living with me on the road?’ Jesse says now with a blunt expression on his face as he awaits her answer. As Samantha goes to someone else intercepts answer the phone and a new voice comes on speaking in a livid tone, ’You know you can’t do that! Jesse she’s in fifth grade, how can you even think of taking her out of school just to travel with you! You and I both know this is not acceptable!’ Jesse stare and blank expression spark at the voice and in only seconds does he respond to the words this woman speaks, ’I know what you mean Clare, but she’s got to see her father and I’m never in one place at a time… I’m just so tired of being without her, and you of all people know that.’ ’Yes I know. But I can’t let her think she can be with her dad when you yourself were the one who gave her to someone else to take care of. Jesse, I can’t let you toy with your daughter like that, nor mine. I’m sorry but I’ve got to go, please don’t call back to talk to Samantha tonight,’ Clare boasts now less livid but still upset of Jesse’s stupidity in trying to get his daughter to travel with him. The phone goes dead and Jesse stands there just looking down at the ground in stunned awe. He drops the phone letting it beep as he walks away down the street like a child who was forgotten by his drunken father. Wrestling Fans 101 Only a few minutes after Jesse was hung up on at the phone booth, Jesse began to walk down the street. His eyes a misty blue as the tears flowed down them. It’d been so long since he’d talked to his daughter. Almost too long for Jesse for his heart was sick of being toyed with. It ran through his mind many times, should I quit, should I just give it all up and settle down? Or should I take this challenge on and do whatever I must until I’m too old to take a fall? Many of the greats had kept going but Jesse didn’t consider himself to be the best, and that’s what he wanted to prove. But his challenge lied not in the ring, but outside it, in the real world. The movie “Dawn of the Dead” had just showed as the theater was being emptied by dozens of people. Jesse stopped and looked around; observing the couples, the parents and their children, and whom ever else left the theater. However one child had been a focus to Jesse. The child stared at the ground as he walked to Jesse in a way that showed he was intimidated. Though it wouldn’t be the first time for the bulk dimension of Jesse compared to average men was monstrous. Jesse smiled as the kid stopped, and looked up at him with his baby blue eyes. With a blank expression of awe on his face he showed Jesse a marker and Jesse new exactly what he wanted. Bending down and taking the marker Jesse signed the kids shirt and patted him on the back as the kid mustered the words, ’Thank you… Jesse sir…’ before walking away with an enormous smile on his face. It’d been a long time since Jesse had seen a fan so happy to see their favorite wrestler. Since his time with Global Wrestling all he did was travel, and never did have time to sign autographs. It was quite a relief to know that even if he wasn’t the best, that some day, somewhere, that child would remember Jesse. As the child walked back to his mother he had been grabbed by the hair and yelled at for leaving his mother’s side. When the child tried to explain himself and showed the shirt, the mother began fumigated and told the boy he’d be grounded. This wiped Jesse’s smile right off, for every time he’d seen these actions he’d remembered the nightmares he’d had as a teenager. Jesse wasn’t always a gothic good guy as some said he was, but his mind had changed at the age of fifteen when he’d first picked up a gun and shot his foster-father. It was by mistake but by doing so he killed his father and regretted it. Jesse then saw his foster-mother die do to a heart attack when he was eighteen. This was the reason why Jesse was so mean and disgruntled in the ring. He’d never stabilized after those happenings, and from that point on was scared to get close to people, even women such as his girlfriend Nikki. There was always this pattern Jesse followed, something Jesse would do because he felt it was the only choice. He’d fall hopelessly in love and then want to marry some woman, but then in the days leading to the wedding he’d go to Canada where he’d hide from her and try to forget her by training. It was a weird way to deal with life, no doubt, but since it happened Jesse had become more stable. Did it work? Somewhat, but everything had flaws and his memories proved to be just that. Crying the child walked away with his mother clutching his hand as Jesse shook his head in disagreement. It was a sad thing to see parents do such things as that to their children, but it was nothing new to Jesse. Life wasn’t kind to the younger youth, and would definitely never get any easier as they grew up with those memories in the back of their minds. Jesse walked down the street and crossed a few corners before he finally reached his place of being. There in the park as the moonlight had begun to shine deeply into the trees making the shadows of the trees look alive and evil. It was just that as well, for Jesse loved being in the core of darkness, it’d been something he’d always been able to withstand. As the moon rose above him he peered down over the edge of the bridge down into the water where he could see himself. A mere reflection of Jesse was one thing he could never get out of his mind. He hated knowing that people thought for him, and believed even though a big man you were always stupid. Just because you could pound some guys face in didn’t mean you didn’t know what the gravitational pull difference between earth and the moon was. That was a boggling thing about Jesse’s mind, he could easily make someone who’s trying to make him look daft, look completely ignorant for he wasn’t afraid to point it out. Like his opponents in the global title match for example. Patti Anderson had been on Jesse’s mind for quite sometime for she’d been saying things that made Jesse think she was doing some drugs. She kept boasting of how men were all alike, they all thought she was a speck in this match who had no chance in hell. Except unlike what Patti foretold, Jesse took every opponent serious. If he didn’t he’d never be in this match, and he’d by no means have been so known for being able to win big matches like this one. Jesse didn’t care if you were woman, man, animal, alien, or whatever, he took a hard stare at you and tried to find the way to defeat you. Just like Apocalypse being the biggest man in this match, Jesse would stare at tapes for hours watching Apocalypse wrestle, finding his weak points. So when the occasion came in the match Jesse would take the advantage of such situation and eliminate the immense man known only as Apocalypse. It was the same for Chris Storm as well, no matter what suplex or counter Storm could use on Jesse or any other opponent Jesse would know it. He took the matches of his opponents, the attitudes they wore to the ring, then the weaknesses they presented as opportunities that others didn’t notice, and combined them together and uses them to take down whatever person who stepped in his way. This was his way of winning. Not many others had that much patients to workout and then study their opponents, but instead worried about endorsements and money that they forgot about what wrestling was. The joy of being in front of those people and put on the best damn show you could possibly, along with winning if you could as Jesse wanted to do. That’s why Jesse, just like the moon, would shine on this night no matter how dark things were in the sky or in his mind.
