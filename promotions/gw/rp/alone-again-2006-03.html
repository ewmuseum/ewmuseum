Alone Again

March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 2006 Washington DC Airport The car pulled into the private airport at a slow crawl to lessen the impact of the large steel speed bumps as much as possible. As part of the updated security measures, a push of a button would send the sharp spikes up to puncture the tires of anyone attempting to enter or exit without permission. The driver followed the painted yellow line around the line of hangars until they came to Hangar 509. Standing outside the hangar in the cold air, he waited to see his friend. No one had told him what was happening until it was too late, and now he had to face a man he was learning he didn’t really know at all. He pulled the brown leather jacket more tightly around his body. The ravages were making him more susceptible to chills and hot flashes. He felt the rush of nausea attack him, but his strength of will forced it away. For the moment? The car stopped right in front of the hangar, and his friend stepped out. He looked at him, and thought for a moment of his own face in the mirror each morning. Here he saw a man whose tanned, toned flesh had been eaten away by something sinister. He had gone to ash, in the way guilt and sickness could do to the most powerful of men. The tired eyes that stared blankly into his own worn-out vision matched the gaunt features, drawn hollow from within his own soul. Weary windows into a soul that was tortured, “Sensational” Stephen Reed recognized the sadness of this situation. “Sensational” Stephen Reed had to admit he had not been “Sensational” in a very, very long time. His friends all assumed he was a recovering alcoholic, left shattered by the loss of those he loved long ago. They were wrong, more wrong than he could explain, and he didn’t want to explain it to them. He feared the slow, sad looks of pity that would accompany his truth telling. No, better to live on, and do what he could while he could. As Stephen looked at his friend, he realized he wasn’t looking much like the “Big Dawg” Jake Hix at the moment either. His friend seemed tired and run down physically, but there was a crushing weight he carried within himself that was wearing him down even more. Jake looked like his spirit had been broken, and now he was simply another abandoned pup hiding in the back of his cage. Stephen put all his thoughts aside for the moment as he nodded at his friend, “This was a quick trip.” Jake hesitated. His lips parted to speak, but then there was nothing. A cold wind blew across them, and he stared off into the distance. Finally, he shook his had slightly to throw off his own confusion before speaking with volume if not confidence, “The best business meetings don’t last long. So, are we ready to get going back to Dallas?” “They’re waiting on us to board.” Stephen let it go for the moment. He looked Jake up and down, noticing the small spots on his jacket and neck. He decided to wait, as the darkness let the evidence remain concealed. “Let’s go.” Jake quickly walked past Stephen. The six foot eight inch monster seemed small, diminutive inside the huge hangar where several private planes were kept. The image struck Stephen as he followed behind his friend. They boarded the plane. Due to the lateness of the flight, there would only be the two pilots and themselves on the jet. Once the plane was locked up tight they began to taxi out to the runway. Stephen grabbed a bottle of water and a linen napkin, passing them over to his friend. Jake took them, “I’m not thirsty.” “No, but they should help get the blood off your neck and sleeve.” Stephen eyed Jake carefully, wondering what his reaction would be to this news. Jake had kept him in the dark about this trip, and a phone call to Pitt was the only way he knew what was happening. Pitt looked down at his sleeve, noticing the droplets of blood there. He wiped them away with this napkin, and then had to add some water to get them off enough so that they didn’t show on the black material. He already knew he would be burning the suit to remove any possible trace of what had happened. Jake wiped off his neck, hoping he had gotten it all, and threw the napkin onto the table between them. Stephen picked the napkin up. He poured a little water on the other end and leaned across the space. He wiped away a few droplets of blood that Jake had missed. Jake’s entire body was tense as Stephen wiped away the evidence of’of his crime? Stephen leaned back, “That took care of it.” “Thanks.” “What happened?” Stephen’s voice didn’t lack any confidence or authority. He was clear-headed and strong, unlike his friend. “Look, there was just?” Jake couldn’t even finish. He had no words left inside him to offer here. Everything was still swirling in his mind. “I called Pitt.” Jake went on the defensive hearing the words. While Xavier and he seemed to see eye-to-eye, Pitt was beginning to drift away a little. His brother’while not going soft, he did seem more willing to ignore the situation whenever possible. “What did Pitt tell you?” “As I understand, you decided to meet with Veston.” Stephen looked down at the napkin, “What kind of meeting was it Jake” Because the last time we spoke about Veston, you said you were going to rip him into pieces. Now, even though as far as I knew he had vanished, you meet with him and come back bloody.” “I wouldn’t call this bloody.” Jake felt the corner of his mouth pull up for a moment in a half-smile, but he brought his cocky nature back under control. “Look, I just met with the man to understand.” Stephen looked out the window as they rolled to a slow stop. The pilots would be waiting final approval for their take-off. Stephen knew what Jake was looking for, and he remembered when Jake was intimately familiar with such a question. Of course, that was often just the role being played out on the screen. “You want to know why he betrayed you.” Stephen said bluntly as the jet engines powered up. “Betrayed us!” Jake corrected just as the jets reached capacity to release the break and send them down the runway. “No, you can say us all you want, but you just want to know why he betrayed you.” Stephen felt himself pushed back into the plush leather seat. “I remember who you are a little better than you do sometimes Jake. You want to know what YOU did to cause HIM to betray YOU. Sure, he betrayed all of us, but deep down there’s still that selfish, lonely man who always thought the world was out to get him. That’s what you’ve been fighting against for so long.” “Do I need to remind you about the stakes we’re all playing for?” Jake was flabbergasted. “No, I know what they are.” “They want to kill each and every single?” “Right! Everyone and everything!” “And all I’m doing is?” “You aren’t doing anything right now!” “Fighting everyday to make it through and?” “Just keep making yourself the martyr!” Jake leaned even further forward than the angled position of the plane required to make his point as he shouted, “I didn’t make myself a martyr! He made me a martyr! He made me what I am!” Stephen watched as Jake was breathing heavily, the emotion causing him to look as though he had run out of breath, and now he was forced to take huge gulps of air to relax. “Who is he Jake” Who is it that you blame at the end of the day?” Jake looked confused for a moment, “Sid, it’s all about Sid.” “I don’t think so.” “Really?” Jake was blind to whatever point Stephen was trying to make to him. The man had always been a little too elusive for his liking, and now he seemed to be enjoying the whole damn thing. Jake wanted to reach out and slap him, draw blood from his mouth as he reminded him whom he was dealing with. And yet, the entire idea made him sink even further into the depression hanging all around him. “And who is it you think I’m angry with?” “Oh, clearly deep inside you are angry with God.” Jake rolled his eyes. He looked around the interior of the plane, “This is fucking G4 jet Reed. Does this look like the place for a fucking Bible lesson” I’m not mad at God, I’m mad at Sid.” “No, not really. Sure, you blame Sid, but then deep down it isn’t fighting against him and what he does that makes you angry. You’re mad at what you see as the ultimate betrayal. You think God betrayed you when he chose you to fight Sid.” Stephen was sure he knew the truth, and spoke with the confidence of a man who was holding all the cards. “Jake, what do you remember about Samson from Sunday School?” “If this is a hair joke you can bite me.” Jake laughed, still amusing himself at the se comments. He ignored the sudden rush of emotion and questions springing up from within. He couldn’t handle this right now; he didn’t want to face this. He would put on the bravado he was sow ell known for? And get past this. “Samson is an interesting study. Most people, like you, just remember the hair and the whore Delilah. You see, no one ever seems to realize it was actually Samson who begins to free Israel from the Philistines. Everyone thinks of the other Judges such as Samuel and Saul, but people forget the fist strike for freedom because of the other colorful anecdotes.” “What is the point?” Jake could feel the anger edging into his voice. “You see, God chose Samson before he was ever born. He told his mother she would have him, and that he would be set to serve him for all his days. Samuel was not perfect, as no man ever is perfect before God. So he did all sorts of interesting things such as laying with whores, showing wrath and killing. He did all of this while serving God though.” Stephen smiled at his friend. The edges were beginning to crack, and he could see the muscles tensing in anger as he kept speaking. Jake had too many burdens to maintain everything for the moment, he would be forced to face this here and now. “Eventually, through his own folly, he removed himself from God. He could rip down the entire structure of the gates of Gaza, and yet he turned to a whore for comfort. Then, later we see a whore betray him to the Philistines.” Jake Hix pulled the rosary out of his pocket. He gripped it in his hand tightly, feeling it cut into his flesh. “The interesting thing is, God never turned away from Samson. Even when he had been beaten, subjugated and imprisoned God was always there with him. God had chosen him, and when the time came the strength he needed was there. He returned to him to allow him to deliver the blow against the enemies of God.” Stephen leaned over. He grabbed the bottle of water and unscrewed the cap. He downed what was left without ever taking his eyes off his friend, “So, tell me what happened during those moments you thought God had abandoned you tonight?” The darkness of the memory came rushing back to him, and he couldn’t fight it off no matter how hard he tried. Hyatt Dulles Earlier That Evening The car pulled into the back parking lot, and stopped hidden from any view of the street. The driver watched the rearview and side mirrors, making sure they hadn’t been followed. Satisfied after a full minute had passed, he gave the nod to his passenger. Undercover work was not something he would ever be able to do in any line of work. He was a modern monster, built by the science of weight training and cross training until every muscle in his body bulged out. He was born big, and the training accentuated that without the need for chemicals or enhancements despite Internet rumors. Of course, a few phone calls had ensured the security cameras were down for scheduled maintenance today, so his easily recognizable form would not be caught on camera. Even so, he knew behind the suit he was easy to spot among the crowd. The contact would take him in the back way, up the service elevator and into the hallway outside the room the bastard had lodged himself into for quite some time. So every base he could think of had been covered, but still’he had the gaping hole in his own strategy. He didn’t know what he planned on doing when he walked into the room. The man had betrayed everything he ever believed in, and more to the point he had endangered Jake’s family. Jake quickly hopped up the short stairs to the back door. He had no idea what he was planning on doing, but he would figure it out. Thinking on the fly seemed to personify a lot of the planning lately, so no reason to fly in the face of what had worked so far. The security guard standing at the back door looked like anyone else. Slightly better built maybe, but not much else. The blue suit hung limply off his body, “Mr. Hix?” “Yes.” Jake eyes the man carefully. Betrayal could come here if it were a set-up. “Mr. Johnson?” “Yes. Here you go.” The security guard handed over the small, padded package. Jake knew what would be contained inside, right down to his specifications. He tucked the package inside his jacket and followed the guard into the labyrinth of the hotel complex. The security guard led him down the back way to the service elevator. “Just come out the same way.” The security guard walked away. He was a good Catholic, and when the Bishop asked him to do something he never asked why. The church was still reaching out, trying to save a world growing ever more sinful by the day. He recognized this man was holy because the church told him he was holy. That was all he needed to know to arrange all the help he could. Jake Hix hit the call button, and he dutifully waited for the elevator. Just a little space now existed between himself and the traitor. The elevator came down into the service area, and the dirty doors spread apart revealing the worn interior. Jake stepped inside, turning on the floor of the elevator with grooves cut from movement of furniture and other heavy items. Once the doors closed, he ripped open the padded package. He pulled out the .45 caliber pistol and the clip. Checking the action, he was satisfied as he slipped the clip inside. Along with the weapon, he had been given a keycard that would work on any door inside this hotel. Everything was set. Nothing stood in his way in the world. Jake pressed the button, and his stomach lurched when the car began being pulled up into the high rise hotel. He knew it was more, much more, than the simple action of the elevator playing havoc with his insides. The entire time he had his friends and associates looking for him, he had always hoped he would never see him again. He didn’t know what he would do, but the beast within was clawing at the bonds restraining him, and then he would crash through the bars of his cage unrestrained by any of his own moral doubts. The elevator stopped, and the doors came open into the storage room and work area for the floor. Jake stepped out of the car, and grabbed one of the white towels from the shelf. He draped the towel over his arm, making sure it covered the pistol. He had the cardkey in his other hand, and he looked at the door. The white door was dirty, given to all sorts of abuses in this kind of environment. He walked up to the door, and stopped. He listened. He should have been listening for someone on the other side who might see him coming, but he was listening for someone to tell him not to go. He heard nothing, and so he opened the door. Jake stepped out into the hallway. Each step seemed to echo with a thud in the empty hallway. He could feel the reverberations through his entire body as he came closer to the room. There had never been doubt like this inside his soul before, and there should not have been any doubt now. The man had tried to kill his family, the man had tried to ruin the entire world and if he had been there at the revelation of his treachery he would have put him down without a thought. Time was working against him. During the long break between the pre-holiday Japan visit and today he had been wrestling with the issue. They had been meeting every betrayal as a matter of life and death, and he realized he was betraying at least one of his own beliefs. Men could so easily fall into the logic of an eye-for-an-eye that they turned blind to the newer lessons? Destruction was easier than creation? Vengeance was easier than forgiveness? The pattern they had fallen into hadn’t alleviated any of the danger they faced. If anything, as they searched in every dark corner for danger, they had escalated the threats by coming closer to defeating his dear brother. Jake had been coming to look at their efforts in much different lights. Perhaps by showing forgiveness they could come closer to ending the madness. The angel on his left shoulder whispered as much in his ear. He needed to focus on the forgiveness of his enemies to not only defeat them, but turn them around from their wickedness. Then there was the angel on his left shoulder, the one holding a sword in one hand and noose in the other. He advocated clear-cut destruction, razing to the ground anything standing in their way. He was the advice from the righteous anger that had delivered those into pillars of salt. Such a bloodlust cried out to him as well. As Jake walked forward, clutching the steel in his hand he didn’t know where he was going on this path. He walked up to the door, and waited. He heard the television on the other side, low and soft. Jake slid the card key into the slot. The light changed from red to green with a light click, and he pushed the door open. Inside, glass broke on the floor of the room as someone scrambled out of bed. Jake turned the corner, his arm outstretched as the white towel fell away and the barrel of the pistol lined itself up with the scrambler. He froze. There were no weapons he could defend himself with, but even if there were he was not sure they would work against the man before him. He had seen the man withstand punishment only God had exempted him from. Jake said nothing, as he looked him up and down. The man stood there frozen in his sight, his ebony skin in sharp contrast to the yellowed and dirtied boxers, which were the only clothes on him. His hair had grown long, unkempt as it seemed to shoot out and away as though it wanted to escape. The man’s beard was beginning to extend as well, the gray of his years of worry showing there most of all. He reached his free hand out while never moving the pistol away from his target. Jake turned the television off. The acid rising from his stomach came out from his tongue, “I have never laid my eyes upon a man who would willingly betray everything he ever was for no reason at all. Now, I look upon that feat, and the only thing I can think is you are slowly becoming the twisted, ugly creature you should be after what you did.”” The chest muscles jumped up and down as a deep laugh came rumbling out of Veston’s body. “Is that the best you can do?” Jake simply stared him down until his laughter, so haughty and proud, died away with the false confidence he could no longer display. “No, I can do far better than that, but you shouldn’t be worried about that. No, what should be sparking in your memory is how much worse than that I can do. You know what I’ve done in the past when they’ve forced my hand. Knowing that full well, you still decided to betray us.” “Betray you?” Veston slowly sunk down to the carpeted floor of his hotel room. He looked up at the monster that had finally after these long months appeared before him. “Why is everything always about you Jake?” “Tell me what it’s about then.” Veston roughly ran his palms across his face, smoothing out his beard hair. Then he pulled his hands back across his hair trying to return the unkempt mess to something more presentable. “Betrayal.” “Well, for once we might be in complete agreement Veston.” Jake hadn’t wavered at all. A simple squeeze could end it all right now. “You fool, you still don’t understand do you” You think you being chosen was a good thing still, don’t you?” Veston shook his head. “You see, sometimes God comes to you in the middle of the night with the warmth of his light of being. When he does that, you feel special. You finally understand your place in his world, your part in his plan. So finally, everything else is set aside. Nothing matters but Him and your relationship with Him.” “Of course, then he goes away. You can never live up to the promise He sees in you because as His creation, we’re flawed. So when you don’t live up to the promise, the light begins to fade, and the warmth is replace by nothing more than cold cruelty. So you work harder, trying to appease whatever insult you gave. You try and atone for your sins, but no matter what we do it all depends on Him deciding to forgive you.” “When I was a young boy, I remember the white priest coming to our little fishing village. He said I was special, and he explained it to me. Then, when I was caught stealing some fish he explained God’s forgiveness to me. He explained it to me as a child, forgiveness does not depend on feelings, or on forgetting or excusing the other person's sin. God forgave me completely. God forgave me immediately after I confessed my sins to him. God forgave me without asking for guarantees that I wouldn’t do it again, and God gave me the grace to forgive others.” Veston coughed, a sound of phlegm from deep inside coming up, and then he spat the large spot of phlegm down onto the floor. “Forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who trespass against us. I remember saying that over and over as I was trained as a good little Christian soldier. When I say Christian soldier I mean it to, because I was taught the Santeria, the divine and the dark to combat against all the unholies in the world. So when I said these words I knew them to be true more than anyone else, but when I asked for forgiveness I was also looking at the cold dead eyes of my father who abandoned me.” “He turned away from me, and even when he forgave me, he looked upon me with such disappointment that I could not live up to standard he had set for me. Every time I tried to find that warmth I felt the first time he called me out, all I found was a God who felt disappointed in me. He couldn’t forgive me, not really. He said he did, but in the end he had never let me forget that I was not living up to his standards. He just kept moving further and further away from me.” Jake let his arm lower to his side. The pistol may not have been aimed at Veston’s chest, but Jake was still gripping the pistol angrily. He looked down into Veston’s eyes, seeing the sad reality of what this man had become. Veston seemed locked in his own pity and depression. Could he end up the same man he saw seated here on the floor, “So I should pity you” I should feel bad that you couldn’t live without feeling that special warm feeling inside like you did when you were a child? No one ever finds that sense of wonder like they did in the beginning.” “You’re as thick in the head as you are in the chest.” Veston looked up, eyes filled with self-satisfaction with the knowledge Jake would never come to have. “Don’t you understand” I could have lived without the feeling, but then you begin to realize that you’re not loved at all by God anymore. You aren’t the favorite son, and you aren’t even considered by him anymore.” “You see, I was presented with His own special appointment. I was to put an end to the threat posed by your brother. I did, I brought together the covenant to stop his ruinous reign.” Veston stopped, “Doesn’t that just roll off the tongue” Ruinous reign of Sid Hix. I brought Robert back into God’s fold! I brought the Sioux back onto a Holy path instead of indulging in their heathen Hell! I brought your brother Pitt to help in this, and do you know what happened?” “You won.” Jake smiled, remembering all the emotions of that day. “We saved Xavier, and my family was made whole once again.” “Always about you isn’t it?” Veston rubbed his left temple, trying to massage away the anger and hatred he was feeling. “You see, it was supposed to be me who saved the world. I had worked since I was a child to serve Him, and in the end He turned his back on me to choose some redneck hick!” Veston pushed himself up onto his feet, “Pitt was chosen to subdue Sid, and then from within his heart we witnessed Xavier come forth saving us all as Sid was banished back to hell! None of you thanked me! No one saw what I had done to make this happen! Everyone spoke of the man who was touched by evil, by God and then was born anew! Xavier was the story of the day, and I was only a footnote!” “That night, I cried myself to sleep. I was alone, and I found the first taste of the cold ash in my mouth from separating myself from God. I had committed such a simple sin. I had let my pride dominate me. I had become Lucifer, separating myself from the Lord. So I prayed, and I confessed my sin. Except, deep down I don’t think I had ever truly apologized. I was going through the motions.” “Sid came back, and I stood in the church awaiting my appointment as his righteous avenger. I was ready for all the glory of the Lord, and it never came.” Veston looked down at the floor, ashamed of the memory. “Now I know that when Sid returned, if I hadn’t turned away from God he still would never have chosen me. He had turned away from me long before, and as far as he was concerned I could serve, but never be chosen again. So when Sid is back, and you get chosen to face him I know it’s over for me. I can serve?” Veston smiled, crooked with his yellowed teeth showing through the black and gray beard. “The truth was always simple. It became an old philosopher’s question to us all. Was it best to serve in Heaven, or would it be better to reign in Hell. All I had to do was make a phone call. Serving was warm and inviting, but in the dead and dark night I found something I actually liked.” Jake narrowed is eyes, “What was that Veston?” “I began to like the taste of cold ash.” Veston could feel the water in his eyes. “So I made my choice. Hell comes here thanks to me. What I never expected was that after I made my choice, I didn’t expect the regret. I realize being separated from God is Hell to many, but being separated from God and being burned by the fires of Hell instead of welcomed by them leaves you in a state you can never describe in words. You can never explain what it’s like to be isolated from everything?” Veston looked off in the distance, “When forgiveness cannot be asked for because it may not be given, you are finally alone before God himself.” Jake stepped forward. He grabbed Veston around the throat. He lifted him up off his feet, and saw the smoke rising off of Veston’s flesh as he held him aloft. His black eyes met Jake’s deep blue, “You have betrayed us. I came here to make you pay for the pain you have caused.” Jake brought the barrel of the pistol up against the side of Veston’s face. He saw vengeance right there, ready for him to take it. Ready for him to show his fealty to the Lord, but he also knew this was all he had known for far too long. “I’m not here to judge you or forgive you. I won’t become as empty as you are Veston.” Jake tossed Veston to the side. He hit the nightstand and bounced off. Jake stepped into the bathroom and grabbed a hand towel. He wiped the pistol down and threw it on the floor. “You made your choice before, and now you have to make it again. You can ask for forgiveness and disappear, or you can make your own peace.” Jake turned around. He took a deep breath, and then began walking back toward the door. A thought came into his head from somewhere he didn’t know. Passion and shame torment him, and rage is mingled with his grief. As Jake walked to the door, the reverberations of each step shaking his body as he walked he heard the noises behind him. He knew what was happening, but he had made his choice. He knew Veston had taken the pistol in his hand, and now was pointing it to his back. “Did you really think you could just leave?” Jake didn’t turn, “It’s like you said. I was chosen, and I have things to do. Everything we do is a choice, and I decided that vengeance would only lead to more darkness. We were delivered from darkness into the light. If you want forgiveness, all you have to do is ask him for it. Even now, he hasn’t abandoned you.” “I abandoned him.” Jake turned just as Veston pulled the trigger. The pistol was pushed up under his chin, and this time Veston turned his pain completely inward. The bullet tore through his head, exploding out of the top of his skull. The blood sprayed out through the room, landing on Jake as well. Jake’s mouth was open, and when he closed it he could taste the metallic linger in his mouth from the blood. Washington DC Airport Jake looked his friend over deeply. He couldn’t admit it to himself, let along his friend. Yet, he felt compelled to answer the question. “God never abandoned me, and I never abandoned him.” Jake said flatly, “But the time is coming when He won’t forgive me Stephen.” “God will always forgive you.” Jake shook his head, “No He won’t. He only forgives those who ask for forgiveness.” “Exactly.” Stephen was smiling. “See, when you don’t think you should be forgiven for all the dark deeds you’ve done, you can’t ask for forgiveness Stephen. So even though tonight I was on the righteous side of God, I know some of what I have done I can’t forgive myself for, let along ask for forgiveness on. And when I look ahead to the darkness stretched out in this ugly world, I know there are too many dark deeds left for me to do. I can’t refuse to do it, but I can’t live with what I have to do either.” Jake let go, and as the rosary fell the short distance to the floor of the plane, there was a crash heard through the heavens. Ethereal tears fell as one of the chosen walked his path alone.
