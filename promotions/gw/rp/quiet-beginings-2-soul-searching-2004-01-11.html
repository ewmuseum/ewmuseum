Quiet Beginings #2: Soul Searching

11 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: jan. 11th, 2004 As I watch Salaok leave, I cant help but be a little bit surprised. A whole afternoon without an explosion, or angry words from The harvester of Minds. It was refreshing to say the least. That, however, is the end of my pro’s list. Salaok is troubled and I’m not sure that its is my place, or within my power to help salve his tortured, burning soul. He has come to me for help. He has come for guidance’but can that be provided from within these walls? And if not, do I dare leave them to help a man so full of hurt and anger and sorrow that his own soul remains a stranger to him? Some are sadly beyond redemption. Some are so far gone’so lost in the call and the dance of the void that no amount of light can bring them back, or show them the way. In our time together, I never pretended to be as depraved as Salaok. I had never reached the depths to which he had delved. The Void consumed him, and drove him’whereas I was the master of my own personal void. The lord of a hell of my own devise. It never drove me as it drives him still. It never haunted me as it does him’and it surely never controlled me as deeply as it does him. I took strength from it, yes?.but I was not lost in its current. I revelled in its freedom and its power, yes’but I was not blind in the dark. My eyes were open enough to see the light when it was shone. My skin was not cooled enough to ignore the warm rays of the Lords love and forgiveness. I fear that Salaok is dead to the light. Dead to the ever-warm embrace of the Lord Jesus. And, worst of all, I fear that he is dead to himself. He knows not how far he has fallen’for if he did, it would not be my counsel that he would not seek out. Sometimes, to seek out help is to ignore the brevity of your situation’he needs to help himself if he truly seeks what strength I have to offer him. I wish to help him’and yet’and yet I cannot. I cannot leave these walls. Temptation lurks around every corner, beyond every door and in every dark place, he waits for me. Beyond my sanctuary lies his kingdom; one of tragedy and remorse’deep and dark and full of sorrow and rage. I have not the means to face that world’and I fear I have not the courage. My might is great, yes. My strength and size and skill unparalleled in most circles?.and yet’yes’I am afraid. I am afraid that if I leave this place’if I leave my home and my salvation, that he will find me. I fear that should I cross this threshold that I will not return’and yet, I must, mustn’t I? “.I do not know the answer to that question’My heart screams that should I turn my back on those who seek my aid, that I am welcoming him home more readily than facing him outside the walls of my resolve. My mind reels at the thought of leaving this place. I have fought and strived and worked so hard here. I have laughed here, and I have cried here’and in two years I have not stepped beyond the walls of this compound” As I stand on the safe side of my gates and watch him walk slowly away, I feel my heart sink. Despite his failings, despite his depravity and his malice and his hatred’he is not afraid. He is not torn. There is no hesitation in him’only the strength of a madman. A strength I once possessed. And it is not jealousy that pangs me so’it is a true and deep sorrow for the man. He dose not even know the dangers that he faces in the quiet places of his mind. He does not know of the silent battles waged, and the quiet wars won in that place between wake and dream. But I do. I know all too well that one morning’perhaps soon’he will wake up, and find that he is gone. Salaok will be no more than a name attached to an empty vessel. Dead eyes will stare back at him from every mirror, and no sadness or joy will touch him again. The world will dull, and life will become grey and distant’and he will become what I once was. The Void will bow to him as it once did to me, and he will embrace it so fully that he will come to forget that there was ever a different life for him. “I need guidance, Lord” I step away from the gates, and lock them as I go. Moving slowly through the courtyard and past the doorsill into my home and my sanctuary, I begin the slow walk to the sanctuary of the Church of the Redeemer, seeking the wisdom and the patience that I need, and that I can only find in Him. Pushing open the large double doors of oak, I stride into the massive sanctuary that was once nothing more than a Ford factory processing room. I slowly walk past the rows and rows of empty pews. Past the stained glass windows, and their saintly visages. Past the communion bench and to the alter. Kneeling before the cross, I dip my head in prayer’seeking out my inner voice, and calling out with all the strength and conviction I can muster to the Lord my God. He hears me. I know he does, and I feel peace flooding through me like a mighty river. It douses the fires of doubt and quenches my thirst of regret. I am at one with the Light now, and I prey. Long moments pass’becoming hours, and yet I am not aware of the passing of a single second until a tap at my shoulder rouses me. “Forgive me for interrupting you, Father’but there is a package here for you. The courier made it seem rather urgent, else I wouldn’t have disturbed you.” The young woman has been a good help here, and a strong preacher and leader in this community of broken souls. Her name is Shauna King, and I have come to love her deeply although she does not know it. “Thank you, Sister. Leave it in my room, please.” “I’m not sure that I can, Father. I think perhaps you should attend to it now.” She shifts uncomfortably from foot to foot, her gaze no longer meeting mine. I bow my head in prayer and briefly fall back into myself, thanking the Lord for hearing my pleas, and giving me the answers I sought. Rising smoothly, I walk from the sanctuary, following Shauna. As we cross through the halls, she becomes more and more agitated, and my concern grows’what could possibly have upset her so? As we round the final corner and enter the main lobby of the Church of the Redeemer, I see that the courier is still there, holding a small package. The courier looks vaguely familiar; powerfully built and tall, but I dismiss him, my eyes locking onto the package in his hands. He looks at me coldly’with a distain not worthy of a stranger, but I ignore it. Trembling hands take the package from him, and open the lid. Inside are two tickets. Plane tickets to Las Vegas, Nevada. Its time to leave this place.
