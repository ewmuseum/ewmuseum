Evil, the root of all money

28 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/28/03 “The reports you asked for Mr. Davis.' The young dark haired woman hands a handful of papers to Salaok who wraps his hands around them knowing that within these pages of worthless typing was the answers to the federation's lack on money. 'Thank you Liz.' He replies while trying his best to give a warm smile but all that showed on his face was a look of bemusement and a face that screamed out that he was hiding things. But what would he need to hide from this woman? A woman who was at his beck and call. A woman who was at the offices more than he was these days? She isn't his wife, but is his secretary. He turns his back on the dark haired beauty that Salaok had actually hired for her skills this time and not for her looks like most of the others who worked for him at the many other offices. It had been one of the smartest moves he had made. Sure it meant leaving behind his other operations to be run by other people now, but GW held two points of interest unlike the others, which only held the one. Sure, he was interested in the money side of GW as well, just like the other investments, but with GW, he was also able to control those who had annoyed him for so long. The likes of Brick, Tragic Hero, Robson, Frost and Pailthorp. These were the people he bought GW for, not himself. What fun would it be owning a fight federation that had no one in it in whom he hated? There would be none. But with GW, he had people in it with whom he didn't like and therefore, he was able to make their life hell while earning money for himself. For Salaok, it simply couldn't get any better than this. Or at least that is what he had thought. Sure since buying the federation the market value of the place had almost doubled and the television stations were beginning to pay attention and had even granted GW a second weekly show which is something that the stations had originally taken away from the federation. He had made the place good again, and yet now it would seem as though the federation's income was stalling. Sure it wasn't dropping in value, but it had stopped rising which annoyed Salaok. As his hand gripped the papers, his gothic rings began to rip into them accidentally. It was always hard wearing the rings which his brother had given him upon being giving the title of Harvester of Fear. Most of his fingers were covered by finger armor and those, which went, had rings of skulls and spikes which made one punch from his fist a devastating blow. He would never have gotten into the ring with them on his hands, but in the street, the rules were different and that is why he wears them. The offices and the homes had been controlled for a long time now, but the streets were never able to be controlled. It was those on the streets in whom had nothing to lose and therefore The Lair was never able to control them through fear like the others on this planet. He begins to flip through the papers one by one, scanning each page as he does so. Looking for what exactly, even he didn't know. But he was sure that he would see something that he wouldn't agree with in the books. Whether it be companies not paying the correct amount, people being paid too much per show, or there being no income through the sale of seats. And as he went through the books, he found examples of each thing. Budweiser hadn't paid for the banners which had been put up above each of the door ways in the arena and that along was $50,000 which the federation could be using but instead it lays unpaid. That would be dealt with quickly. The Lair was always good and finding out why people hadn't paid their debts and were even better at getting the money owed. Then there were people who were being overpaid. There contracts say one thing, but the amounts which go into their bank each week is significantly higher than what it should be. He sits there looking at the list of people who are receiving more than what they should and he runs his finger down the page reading them out loud as he does so. 'Ben J Pailthorp. Jay Robson. Frost.' Salaok's head shakes as each of the names is read out. However he seems to miss the fact that Quartz, Sage Sabbot, Kullypso and himself are also in the list of people being paid more than what they are meant to be. He shrugs and is willing to let it be for now as he soon spots something that annoys him greatly. Knowing that whenever he arrives at the arena, the sign out the front always says 'Sold Out' and yet the seats sales which now sits in front of him tells another story. 'LIZ!' Hie's voice rings out deep and purposeful as the dark haired beauty quickly enters the room with a note pad already in her hand and a pen poised ready to take any notes that Salaok is about to let out with. But upon eye contact with the co-owner of the business, she understands that this isn't going to be a memo taking moment. 'What can I do for you Mr. Davis?' 'How about tell me why the fuck we have 'Sold Out' shows when we still have about one thousand seats without asses on them each night? Do you know how much money that costs us each other. What if there is one thousand people wanting to get in to see a show and the signs all say sold out but in fact they are. We've just missed out on around $50,000. What is the fucking deal with that?' Liz tries to calm the mood by walking up to Salaok's desk with her hips swinging more than a 1970 disco. She sits herself down on the char opposite to Salaok and begins to talk to the enraged man. 'Well, this is something that has apparently been happening for a number of years now. Fact is that people see we are sold out and then think that something really good is happening within the walls of the arena and therefore go and buy tickets for the next week instead.' Thinking for a mere second on this practice, Salaok's head begins to shake before he has even thought about it fully. 'Sorry, but make sure it doesn't happen anymore. Send a message to the people who do the signs and make sure that we are only seen to be 'Sold Out' if we are in fact sold out. Think about it. Those people could get into the show instead of thinking it's sold out and like it so much that they buy tickets for the next weeks show anyway. That way we've just doubled the money we would have otherwise gotten out of them. ' 'Yes sir.' Not bothering to reason with Salaok while he's in this frame on mind, Liz is happy enough just to take the message and to forward to the people and see what happens for a month. 'Anything else Sir?' 'No Liz. Make before the people from the accounts department leaves today, send them in to see me.' Liz nods before adding 'Shake I get the Termination of employment forms ready then?' 'Now that you mention it, yes. May not need them but you can never be sure these days.' Again Liz nods before getting out of the chair and heading towards the door. Salaok is about to stop her and say something but it slips from his mind and simply doesn't bother to say anything. Still not happy with the money situation in the federation as he still hasn't worked out a way of getting more of it, he puts the papers down. After hours of looking at the pages full of number, he rests back in his chair, placing his hands behind his head and thinks to himself about nothing. But it is within this state that an idea seems to drawn on him. What the federation had always been lacking was a place where it could groom it's new recruits. What GW was needing was its own school facilities. But this was completely perfect as The School of Pain would work well in that situation. Not only would it bring funds to the federation, but it would give Salaok to get to know the recruits before Bubba ever got to talk to them. A grin spans Salaok's face as he knows that this will make him money on both sides. The School's enrollment fees would be able to be raised because of this and the federation would have a good flow of new talent coming into the federation which would always sell tickets. Suddenly the office phone brings to ring, snapping Salaok out of his dream like state and he quickly moves forward and presses the button to activate the speaker phone. 'Yes?' 'Mr. Davis, there is a man out here who wants to speak with you. Say's his name is McCue.' A voice can be heard in the background 'It's Jeremy McCue.' A smile appears on Salaok's face again as this visit may just be the answer to the problems that he had been having within the federation. Salaok leans forward to the phone 'It's all right Liz. Let the commissioner in.' Pressing the speaker phone button again in order to hang up the call, he looks at the door and grins as it opens. Although not huge in size, this man seems to scream power with his appearance alone. His suit nicely pressed and his hair tied back. McCue returns the smile from Salaok as Salaok stands up to welcome him into the room. 'You're just the man I was hoping to see Jeremy. Take a seat, we've got a lot to talk about.'
