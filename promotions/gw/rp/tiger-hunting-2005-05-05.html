Tiger Hunting

5 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/05/2005 Risin' up, back on the street; Did my time, took my chances... Went the distance; now I'm back on my feet! Just a man and his will to survive... Ramirez Home; Cochello, CA May 5, 2005; 1120 hours 'El... ga... to. El Gato,' said Melina proudly, looking up at Margaret LaPortia from the reading exercise she was practicing. (*The cat.) 'Bueno,' said Margaret, lightly giving the child a hug. 'Excelente, Melina.' (*Good. Excellent, Melina.) 'How's she coming along?' asked Teresa, coming up behind the two and lightly touching her daughter's shoulders as she kissed the top of her head. 'Beautifully,' said Margaret. 'She'll easily be reading on her grade level within a few months.' She smiled at Teresa and clasped her hand. 'Now, listen.' Teresa tilted her head and listened as Margaret turned back to Melina. 'Hello, Melina. How are you?' she asked. Melina's pretty young face crinkled into lines of concentration. 'Hel... lo. I... am f... fi... fine, think... you.' Teresa smiled wide with surprise and lightly applauded. '?Maravilloso, Melina!' she said, hugging the child. She didn't correct the slight mispronunciation of 'thank.' Just that Melina was starting to learn English at all was wonderful. (*Marvelous!) So many times, it happens too fast; You trade your passion for glory... Don't lose your grip on the dreams of the past, You must fight just to keep them alive! Stuart came into the living room, carrying some grocery bags. 'I think this is everything we need,' he said eagerly. 'This, and what Juan's carrying.' Juan walked in on Stuart's heels, also carrying some grocery bags. 'All authentic Mexican ingredients, for a real Mexican Cinco de Mayo meal,' he said with a measure of pride. 'And with that,' said Teresa, 'it's time to hit the kitchen. You'll love how dinner is going to turn out.' Margaret stood up. 'Mind if I help out?' she asked a bit eagerly. 'My kids like Mexican food, and maybe I can learn a little from you two.' Teresa looked at Juan, who nodded and looked at Margaret with a small smile. 'I'll get an extra pair of gloves,' he said, before turning to Melina and adding, 'Qu’dese con Se’or LaPortia, preciosa.' (*Stay with Mr. LaPortia, sweetheart.) 'S?, pap?,' Melina said, returning to practicing her lesson. Margaret went with Juan and Teresa into the kitchen, looking with approval at how well-equipped it was. Both Ramirez spouses seemed to like to cook, and knew their way around a kitchen. She listened diligently to their instructions, following their lead, and making mental notes in her head. It's the eye of the tiger, It's the thrill of the fight! Rising up to the challenge of our rival... And the last known survivor Stalks his prey in the night, And he's watching us all with the eye... Of the tiger! 'A little too spicy?' smiled Teresa as he watched Stuart's face wrinkle up. 'A little, maybe,' Stuart admitted, 'but more than anything else, it caught me off guard. Mexican-American restaurants almost definitely go easy on the spices when cooking for American customers.' He took another bite of the dish, this time managing it more easily. 'A bite of the Tex-Mex pizza will help better than a cold drink,' said Juan, in an almost instructor-like voice. 'Cold liquid provides only temporary relief, but then the bite comes back full force. Follow the liquid with a bite of bread, and then the bread is what eases it up.' The mention of the Tex-Mex pizza made Margaret turn to look at Melina. 'Teresa, you've got to come over for dinner at our place a few times,' she said almost jealously. 'So my kids can watch Melina eat the vegetables on that pizza and I can give them a lecture on it.' She watched Melina take a bite of the pizza-- baked special as a treat in celebration of how well she was doing in learning to read, and to speak English-- and smiled in a way Juan and Teresa knew all too well. Although not really able to follow the conversation, as she still only spoke a very few and limited words of English, Melina seemed to understand the point of what Margaret was saying, and smirked complacently to herself. She picked a piece of broccoli and a jalape’o pepper off the edge of her pizza and ate them with every evidence of enjoyment on her young face; almost as if inviting Margaret to take a snapshot to show to her own children. 'You have to start when they're very young,' smiled Teresa. 'Once they start to develop habits, they're very hard to break. And although it might be okay as an occasional treat, health-wise, be careful about bringing them to a fast-food restaurant. McDonald's and Wendy's don't spend so many millions of dollars on advertising and toys for nothing. It's like smoking-- you try it a few times, and then suddenly you're hooked, and the kids want to do it again and again. By that time, the thought of eating any vegetables is simply alien to them. Children don't crave variety the way many adults do.' Juan lightly ran his fingers through Melina's hair with every square inch of his face showing a look of fatherly pride. The child simply smiled at him and returned to her meal. Although eating more slowly than, and not as much as, the adults, it was clear Melina would have little trouble finishing. 'So Jos? won't be returning until this weekend?' asked Stuart. 'That's correct,' said Juan. 'He always spends Cinco de Mayo with his family back home in Mexico. We often joined him, but we had to be here because of Evolution tomorrow.' Stuart nodded as he stood up to help clear away the dishes. 'I came prepared,' he said. Teresa looked at him curiously. 'I'll explain in a minute,' said Stuart. 'Let me help with the dishes, and then I'll get the tapes out of my duffel bag. While I'm doing that, get your encyclopedia... the one with the articles on bull fighting.' Teresa raised her eyebrows. 'Bull fighting?' She smiled slightly. 'Bull fighting is quite a bit different from professional wrestling, Stuart.' 'I know,' said Stuart, smiling back. 'But I'll explain the anecdotes so they make sense.' Face to face, out in the heat! Hangin' tough, stayin' hungry... They stack the odds still we take to the streets, For the kill, with the skill to survive! Juan shook his head. 'A bull fight is not a hunt,' he said in a decidedly negative tone. 'There's a rich tradition surrounding the pageantry involved. It's an art form, and a dance. A dance of death, perhaps, as the matador's goal is to slay the bull... but there is a definite difference.' 'Does the matador stalk the bull at all?' asked Stuart. Teresa shrugged. 'Depends on how you define stalk,' she said. 'There's no place for the matador to hide, so he doesn't sneak up on the bull... and to try and hide would permanently stain the matador's valor. He walks with the bull in a manner that is similar to stalking, but they are always within sight of each other. Even if they are not always facing each other directly, the bull always sees and keeps its eyes on the matador, even as the matador always keeps his or her eyes on him.' Stuart paused in thought. 'He times his strikes with the sword, right?' he asked. Teresa looked at Juan, and they nodded in unison. 'Yes, to strike at the bull aimlessly is to invite death,' said Teresa. 'The bull can take much more physical punishment than a man or woman... and it can deliver blows with far, far greater force than a matador's slim rapier-like weapon.' Stuart smiled. 'That's the similarity... that's how it ties in,' he said. 'Hunting Grounds is all about comparing a wrestling match to a hunt... and even victory is not as important, in the end, as the hunt itself.' Teresa gaped at Juan, who shook his head in seeming disbelief. 'That sounds more like a cockfight than a bullfight,' said the Crimson Comet, an edge of distaste coming into his voice. Stuart raised his eyebrows. 'Isn't cockfighting illegal even in many parts of Mexico?' he asked. 'Exactly,' said Teresa. 'That's the point. Bullfighting is an art form and a pageant and a dance... this 'Hunting Grounds' you describe takes the metaphor of hunting and makes it completely base... on an animal level. There's no grace, no art, no beauty. Just sheer animal savagery.' 'I think you just hit the nail on the head,' said Stuart, pausing to rewind a section of the tape he'd been showing them, and playing it again. It's the eye of the tiger, It's the thrill of the fight! Rising up to the challenge of our rival... And the last known survivor Stalks his prey in the night, And he's watching us all with the eye... Of the tiger! Teresa watched the sequence and grimaced. 'If my dear pap? was made to fight like that... that's why he lost that cantina fight. He was far too proud, and too honorable, to sink to such base tactics. How he fought was so important; a win was not worth the victory if it was not gained the RIGHT way.' 'That's the exact train of thought that Hunting Grounds would call a sign of weakness,' said Stuart with a knowing look. 'They'd say that this is why you're in the current slump you're in, because you're preoccupied with making an art show out of your wrestling.' Juan snorted derisively. 'Hmmph... and who says that their boast, proclaimed so haughtily from the peak of an ivory tower, is the RIGHT way, or that the art form that is Lucha Libre is the WRONG way?' he challenged. 'To a member of Hunting Grounds, the only proof they need is another check-mark in the W column,' said Stuart. 'Nothing, but NOTHING, justifies their means like that W... and nothing proves someone else wrong like a check-mark in the L column. They see things in very rigid black and white, without even a whisper-thin trace of gray area in between. A 'W' is right, and strong, while an 'L' is weak and bad. It's that absolute for them.' Risin' up, straight to the top Had the guts, got the glory Went the distance; now I'm not gonna stop Just a man and his will to survive Juan and Teresa looked at each other for a long moment. Their brows knit in thoughtful silence. Finally Juan looked at Stuart again. 'Imagine you're a jungle cat for a moment, Stuart,' he said. 'Like a jaguar in Mexico... or a lion in Africa... or a tiger in Asia.' Stuart blinked his eyes in puzzlement, but stopped to consider Juan's suggestion. 'Strong hunters,' he said about them. 'What else?' asked Juan. Stuart thought, but the point was lost on him. He shrugged. 'What?' he asked. 'Cats only see in black and white,' said Teresa. 'They don't see any colors. It provides for sharper detail, but they miss much of the bigger picture. Even if a cat had human-like intelligence, and could think and reason... you can't explain blue or yellow or brown to them. It would be beyond their understanding, much like it would be beyond the understanding of a person that has been blind since birth.' 'And the relation to Hunting Grounds is...?' Teresa and Juan both smiled in unison. 'They focus too deeply, and too narrowly,' said Juan. 'They miss more than they see, because they are too single-minded in pursuit of a narrower goal. If you soften your focus, and open your eyes to the bigger picture... you are ABLE to shoot for and hit a goal that their tunnel vision cannot see. Sometimes, that goal is a more vital spot on the target than the only one they see with their narrower vision.' Stuart nodded, giving a small smile. 'Sounds like you're talking about going tiger hunting,' he said. It's the eye of the tiger, It's the thrill of the fight! Rising up to the challenge of our rival... And the last known survivor Stalks his prey in the night, And he's watching us all with the eeeeeeyyyyyyyyyyeeeeee!!!!! Of the tiger... Teresa smiled. 'Tigers are the largest of all jungle cats... and jaguars can be the fiercest,' she said. Maybe their own philosophy will work against them... and you know that we of Lucha Libre are all about artistry... the POETIC justice of seeing their own preaching trap them in their own net is as beautiful and artistic as it can get. Hunt or be Hunted, is it? What do you wish to be, predator or prey? I think they will find we can be quite the predators if we wish.' Juan nodded and touched the side of his fist to the side of Teresa's fist. 'Predatory indeed,' he said. 'Even the most skillful jungle hunter can be entangled in another's snare.' The eye of the tiger... The eye of the tiger... The eye of the tigeeeerrrr.... Eye of the Tiger, Survivor, ? 1982
