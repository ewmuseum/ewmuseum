Changing Family pt 4

6 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3:6:05 I don't need your 'go ahead' to go ahead. No, I know no one said it was gonna be easy. ---- I was released from the hospital this morning. The doctors said I'll be fine. I just need to stay off my feet for a couple weeks. What the fuck do they know? Fucking quacks. I have to defend my tag titles. I have to win the International title. I have to talk to the lawyers, the police, my family, and my tag partner. What gives those doctors the right to tell me I can't go in to my matches at High Octane, in my home town, and wrestle. Some people in this world just need to be eliminated from the gene pool. 'Why do you insist on going to the lawyer right now?' asks my wife. 'Because, I have to find out what caused Johnny's car to explode. I can't go on wondering why my parents were taken away from me,' I reply. 'I need some sort of closure.' 'Can't you just call the lawyer's office again and tell them not to wait on us? You heard Dr. Mott, you need to rest. I don't want you to further injure yourself. And I really don't think you need to be working. Call Bubba and tell him what the doctor said, he'll understand.' 'Cindy, I can't do that. This is my chance. Right now, I'm in a do-or-die situation. If I fade out now, I'll never get any further with my career. Then I'll be stuck doing odd jobs or working on cars for a living. No offense to my father, rest his soul, but I don't want to have to fight for things the way he did early on. My father was a great man, a self made man. You remember what I told you about my childhood. We were struggling to get by, with Mom working at a grocery store and dad doing tune-ups and tire changes. My father spent six years building a car, his 56 Bel Air, only to sell it for enough money to buy his own garage. That garage turned everything around. After he started that up, he started making money. He could afford better equipment, professional paint tools, and he started getting a good client base. After that, everything was different.' 'Well, we've already got enough money to live very well for a few years, and we could even sell our house and get something smaller, and we could live comfortably for a long while.' 'Yes, I know we could. But I don't want to do that. Cindy, you and our son deserve the best. I'm not going to give you any less,' I tell her, putting my hand on her thigh while she drives. 'But you can't give us anything if you're permanently injured. You're beaten enough right now, and if you go back to work so soon you will only get worse.' 'All I have to do is get in there, win both matches, and I can take a week or two off. I'll defend my titles as little as possible and I can rest up six days a week.' 'Which exit do I need to take to get us to the Attorney's office?' asks Cindy. '376,' I reply. ---- Sometimes I wonder how different I would be right now if my parents hadn't died. Of course, as I ask myself that I'm sitting in a lobby, my eyes closed and rolled slightly back into my head. If my parents were still alive right now, I wouldn't be in this lobby. Cindy, Damon, and myself would be at my parents for our Sunday lunch. My father and I would be out in the garage working on his latest toy, the 72 Chevelle Super Sport he picked up from a junk yard for about $450. We would be rebuilding the carbuerator, replacing the cams, or swapping in some light weight axles. Things would be peaceful, happy. Not dreary and fucking depressing like they are now. Every day since the explosion has rained. Not the pretty rain that comes late at night with lightning and soft thunder, making for the tranquil setting idea for a great night of sleep. No, it's been the light rain drops, like the ones you would expect to find in Seattle or London, not Detroit. The weather has been warmer than lately, creeping in to the mid 40s somedays. Every night it falls below 30, making everything freeze from the rain. This is the first Sunday in years that my brothers and I havent been to our parents' house for a meal and some sporting event on television. The first Sunday in years where I spent the morning in the hospital, and the afternoon in our lawyer's office. The first Sunday in years that I could not look in the mirror and say I liked what I saw. Regardless of that, I'm still here. Nothing I can do will change that. ---- Finally, the lawyer steps out of his office and motions for us to follow. We get in and sit down in the chairs opposite his desk. He extends his hand, so I accept it and shake. Cindy does the same. 'Thank you for meeting us on such short noticem Mr. Busik,' I say. 'Please, call me Phil. I knew your parents for a number of years, and I owe you at least this. Anyway, what may I do for you today?' replies our lawyer. 'I need to know what caused the explosion. I don't care about law suits or justice or any of that. I just need to know what caused this.' 'Well, I feel you deserve to know, so I will tell you what I know. The police have gotten back to me recently, and so far they are saying that it was as simple as a fuel line that had a small puncture in it, and a wiring harness that was rubbing on the exhaust headers. Over time, the insulation rubbed off of the wires, and unfortunately with the construction being done along I-75 at the time of the accident, the bumpy road caused the wiring harness to move excessively, and the bare wires grounded out on the metal of the headers. They suspect a spark from this ignited the fuel left standing in the casing that holds the battery is what caused the explosion.' I feel nauseated. My head spins, eyes drying up one second, watering the next. My stomache churns and I exhale. I don't know if part of me was hoping it was an intentional attack, as unlikely as that might seem, but at least then I could have done something. I could have taken some agression out on somebody. A grounded out wire and a leaky fuel line? How am I supposed to get vengeance against a couple of car parts? Cindy puts her hand on my shoulder, causing me to snap back to reality. 'I'm terribly sorry for your loss. My prayers are with your family. I can only imagine how hard it is for you right now,' says Phil. I stand up and lunge over his desk. My hand shoots out and I grab him by the throat. I lift him up out of his chair as I feel my wife's hands on my waist, trying to pull me back. I drag the attorney up and onto his desk, scattering the stacks of papers he had there. I lean down and whisper in his ear, Cindy still trying to pull me off of Phil. 'You have no idea how hard this is for me. I was feet from my parents as they burned to death. I passed out from the smoke and smell of my mother and father's burning flesh. I was helpless to save them from the scorching inferno of that car explosion. Just like that, my parents were ripped away from me, and you have the nerve to say you can imagine how I feel? You don't know the fucking half of it!' I release my grip on the man's throat and storm out of his office. Cindy comes out a few seconds later, yelling at me. 'What the hell are you doing Derek? That man did nothing to you and yet you slammed him onto his desk and started choking him! What the fuck is wrong with you? Get back in the car, I'm taking you to the hospital so we can have a psychiatrist check you out. I don't want you near our son until I know you're not a mental fuck up.' 'I....I was choking him? Cindy, why didn't you stop me?' 'You mean you....You don't remembet what just happened?' 'No. I know we were in the office, and he told us about the fuel line and stuff, and next thing I know I'm standing out here, and you're yelling at me. I don't remember anything about yelling at Phil or choking him,' I say, running my fingers through my dark blue hair. My wife and I leave to go back to the hospital. I'm examined again, then sent home, problem free. That night, I have another dream. This time, my father's dismembered head is floating around. I yell at it and tell it to go away, to leave me alone. It laughs, then fades in to the darkness.
