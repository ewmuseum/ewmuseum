Date: ??//??//?? Footsteps. They echoed off the cold cement walls with an almost ungodly sound. I hated hearing them, because every time those footsteps came near me I was either going to be beaten, or yelled at. I couldn’t remember how many times the prison guards had taken me to the hole and beaten me with clubs, I could remember them all yelling at me calling me a pervert and that all those women didn’t have to die. It’s not like I killed them, I had sex with them? and then they mysteriously disappeared. I had never actually killed a single one of them; they were all alive when I left them, buried in a wooden box six feet under ground. It was a game to me; to see how many hookers I could rape and trap underground before I got caught? My magic number was thirty-two. Thirty-Two local prostitutes killed in a matter of one month. That was a whore a day by my count, except one weekend I picked up two twins that were whoring themselves out for tuition money, I suppose those two didn’t have to die, but they made the mistake of getting my car. It took the cops weeks to find me, I left them clues to my whereabouts and identity in the form of newspaper clippings, the classic psychopaths trademark. I hadn’t been able to grip sanity since I had been expelled from the Freedom Alliance for killing an innocent man in a bar fight. I told Chris Davis that his neck was in that position when I started talking to him, and that I didn’t really do it, but of course, he didn’t believe me. After my expulsion from Freedom I went into a suicidal mindset, drugs and partying were my main priorities in life, I made my money by selling crack cocaine on the streets and lived wherever I could find shelter from the rain. My mind had been destroyed by the drugs I was doing and I fell into insanity in a matter of weeks. I started offering money to hookers because I was so deprived, and then I killed them and took my money back because I was in an accepted position of poverty. I had enough money in my bank account to buy twelve cars, the work I did for freedom had paid off very well into my pocket book, I was only homeless and hungry because I chose to be. Why I chose to be, I will never fully understand. The footsteps drew nearer and each one sent a shiver down the back of my spine. I could feel something terrible coming to me and I knew I had somehow, deserved it. The cops had a hard time putting me in the cell I am currently in, the clues I left them no longer made any sense in my mind let alone the hands of the worlds top investigators. I left them in the purses of the hookers I had killed, and then thrown the purses back onto the streets, the funniest part is, they still have not found a single body. My charges were based on the suspicions, the accusations and then finally, my own admittance. I had completely blocked out my previous lifestyle of wrestling and the glory of the spotlight. I admitted to them that I had killed 32 prostitutes but I didn’t remember where I had put the bodies, the judge assumed that the local waste management office had picked them up out of a dumpster and thrown them in the landfill, that was all they needed to drop the evidence factor. Two years later my body is back into the shape it was when I joined freedom, and I am rid of my cocaine addiction. The fact that I still had a few hundred thousands of dollars in my bank was the only thing keeping me from snapping again, the fact of financial security for when you make bail or fill the sentence really keeps you going in a time of need. The footsteps were only a few feet away and my fore head beaded with sweat the anticipation of pain was on my mind. I chuckled to myself as a single guard walked to the door and looked at me with a very disgusted face. “Gothic, Get the fuck out of bed you lazy piece of shit and get the fuck up to the bars!” he shouted. I didn’t say anything in retort as I walked to the bars with the chains that connected all of my limbs to my neck I, was just inches away from the guards face, just waiting for a wad of mucus filled spit to come streaming from his ugly face. “Well shit Chris, for some fucking reason, the gods have looked down on you and smiled today. I have no fucking clue who, how or why it happened, but you made bail” He said as he slid the cell door open with a thunderous clang. “You are free to go Mr. Gothic” All sarcasm and hatred had dropped from his voice as he put the key into the padlock on my ankles. The chains fell off quickly and I felt my body begin to rise, it was a good feeling being back down to normal weight again. As the guard stood up I stared deep into his eyes, half of me wanted to grab him by the teeth and drag him across the room a few times, but the other half of me didn’t want to go back into the cell I had spent so many sleepless nights in. He led me down the hallway towards the offices, every other prisoner in every other cell had an unspoken respect for me, and I knew that if any of these guys saw me in trouble they would help me with asking any questions. I was fearless to most, and a fucking psychopath to the rest. I looked each one of them in the eye on my way out and gave each one of them a simple nod of my head. The guard stopped me at an office door and said, “When you walk through this door the first person you are going to see is the person who made your bail. I don’t know why he did this for you, but for some fucked up reason you are free to go. Behave Mr. Gothic, because if you have to come back here, you are never going to leave.” The door flew open and he shoved me through to the other side, I was blinded at first the sudden fluorescent hue coming from the ceilings had initially pained my eyes, but faded quickly. I was looking right into the eyes of the man who bailed me out. He was no stranger to me, to me he was a god amongst men, a real class act. The top dog of the world I chose to live, a guy just like me. His name is Saloak Davis, and just like me, he is a fucking psychopath, I had a good feeling about what was going to happen in the near future, but one thing confused me. If I had expected another favor from the Davis family, it would have been from Chris Davis himself. I looked at Saloak for a few moments and asked him, “Why didn’t your Father bail me out this time?” The guard looked at me with shock, as if there was something I didn’t know. Saloak looked at me with a smile, and two word’s that I will never forget escaped his lips, “He’s Dead” he replied, as we walked out of the police station into a bright morning sun. Like I had noted earlier I had an excellent feeling about this, I knew something bad was going to happen.
