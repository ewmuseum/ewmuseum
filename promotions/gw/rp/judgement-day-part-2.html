Judgement Day (part 2)

undated

In complete silence, Salaok walks out of the throne room leaving his fellow Harvesters behind knowing full well that he must now do something big enough to gain their approval once again. Not stopping to shut the door, Salaok just keeps on walking through the halls of the massive building in which he had spent so many of his years either training to become, or being one of The Harvesters and now his position seems tainted. What right do they have to doubt him after all he had done? What right do they have not to doubt him though, for all he had done? His footsteps, echoing off the blue stone floor and walls, alone. It had been a long time since he had walked the halls of this great building alone for always another Harvester was around him but not anymore. Their faith in his mental state had diminished, and yet for some reason, that was not what was controlling his line of thought. It had been something Quartz had said earlier that was now his meal ticket, or so he thought. Earlier that day, Quartz had mentioned something about taking care of a Contract, which in their world meant only one thing. Quartz had been talking about a hit, and from the sounds of things, the hit was a major one for The Harvesters but who was it? One name came to mind for the younger of the two Davis brothers and it was a name that he never wanted to speak again after the lies and games he played with their minds. Salaok’s hand reaches down into his pocket as he stops walking and pulls out a packet of cigarettes as he always has done so. A snap of the wrist and the top of the packet swings open before he bring the entire packet to his mouth and pulls out a single cigarette out between his lips. As smoothly as the packet of cigarettes were opened and removed from the pocket, they are shut and returned, only to be replaced by a matchstick. Beginning to walk again, Salaok presses the match against the blue stone wall and drags it across the rough surface before flicking his wrist and igniting the match. The bright red and yellow dancer sits neatly on top of the match as he brings it to the end of the cigarette and begins to burn the tobacco. Once upon a time, he had vowed to give up these disgusting cancer sticks, and yet he still buys a packet per day and doesn’t think twice about making sure the last piece of the cigarette is gone before tossing it to the side or putting it out on the bottom of his boot. The motions of smoking the cigarette took no concentration, as his mind was so intent on finding out if the contract that Quartz had spoken about was the one in which he was meant to have taken care of ten years ago. That was all that consumed his mind at this point in time. Alone his footsteps sounded out into the silent air. Alone he was. Always alone these days. No family to take care of and protect of his own. The only ones who were left where the ones who had caused him so much pain and misery over his life. His father and mother still haunted him, not to mention his brother in whom words were heard before and then there is his sister who married Salaok’s worst enemy but still hope one day the two will see eye to eye. Something that just doesn’t happen with Salaok. Seeing something from his point of view is something that not many people wish to do, and his views on life in general have been disturbed so greatly by the influences of The Lair and his fellow Harvesters, that a normal man could never relate to the words in which he spoke. Settling a dispute with a silent hit and dropping the body in the water was the usual for those involved in The Lair, but for the average man, it wouldn’t be seen as the norm. Reaching the front doors to the massive building, Salaok puts his hand out in front of him, readying to grab the door handle with a hand grabs him on the shoulder and stops him from leaving. “It’s been a long time Salaok, things have change.” “Have they Sage” Have they really? Or has everything simply stayed the same while you have changed?” There is silence from the Harvester of Tears as he stands there with his hand still planted on Salaok’s shoulder, waiting for what he knows is going to come. It didn’t matter what Salaok said anymore, there was no reason for Sage to look up to him as a mentor, as they were equals now. Sage had earned his place, just like Salaok had done so all that time ago now. If anything, it was Sage now in a higher position than Salaok for Salaok had fallen from the higher ranks due to his house-bound nature for the past year. Salaok begins again, knowing that Sage is waiting to hear what he has to say. “We both gave Lisha our word Sage on her death bed. I gave her my word to never forget her and I haven’t. You gave her your word to look out for her boys, being Darrell and myself, but have you” Darrell is dead Sage and you stayed away the entire time, how is that staying true to your word Sabbot? Tell me old friend, how is ignoring the situation that my family was in looking out for Lisha’s boys?” “You don’t want to go down this road Salaok, I did what I could, just like you did. Only thing is, I wasn’t seen, but that doesn’t mean that I wasn’t around. His voice calming and attempting to stop the hostility in which fumed from Salaok with every breath and word that escaped his lips. “I know what you are about to do Salaok, I know what you are going to prove to Quartz and Johnathon. For your own sanity, I beg of you not to! This will only make matters worse I fear.” “Sage, there is no need for your concern here as I have everything under control as I always have and there is no way that what I have to do tonight is going to make things worse. If the contract on my father’s life had been completed ten years ago like Quartz was paid to do, then none of this would have come about.” Salaok’s voice is cold and holds nothing back but is disturbed by Sage’s once again. “Then I’m coming with you Salaok.” “I think not Sage.” His answer was sharp and had a hint of bite to it as Salaok now slowly begins to turn around and face The Harvester of Tears, staring him straight in the eye. Shaking his head, Salaok continues, “I have to re-prove myself to the other Harvesters and having you with me will prove nothing to them. This is a quest in which only I can take with no back up. For too long I relied on the back up of The Lair but tonight will be different. Tonight I show my old friends that I am still one to be feared and that should they no longer want me in The Lair, then I will fight on my own against them like I have done before.” “You fight against us Salaok and I will have choice other than to deliver you to the rest of your family.” Sage’s words are surprisingly cold but his eyes were more so as they return Salaok’s stare. Sage turns and begins to walk away, and as he does so, he speaks back to Salaok. “You want to do this on your own Salaok, then fine. If you want to die a lonely mentally disturbed man, then have it your way.” Salaok turns away from watching Sage walk away and then pushes against the door, opening it up to the outside world again. A world which is far more cruel than the Inner Circle of The Lair and twice as deadly. However, today, none of that mattered anymore, for he had but one task to take care of and then his personal war can finally end. The only question that now ran thought Salaok’s head was how he was going to take care of business. The office? The personal driver? The hotel room? That final thought brought a grin to the normally blank face of Salaok as the cigarette moves back up to his lips and he inhales another lot of smoke, only to let it trickle out of his nose as he walked forward today a date that would change his world forever. ---- He stands at the foot of the hotel’s back wall, looking up at the building in the diminishing daylight, knowing that his time to get inside and plan how it will all unfold is running out fast. He had used all of his known contacts just to find out which hotel and what room Chris was staying in, now the problem was getting inside but it really wouldn’t prove too much of a problem the more he thought about it. Chris was a lazy man, never checked anything in his life that didn’t have to do with money and knowing that his hit has a major problem with heat, Salaok smiles to himself as he looks up at the window of the room. From his vantage point, the window looked to be slightly ajar which caused The Harvester of Minds to shakes his head at the foolishness of this man. Within seconds he had begun to scale the fire escape without a second thought about whom may see him and the closer he got to the floor that Chris was staying on, he could see for sure that the window was indeed slightly open, which only made him move faster up the metallic structure. The only problem now was the fact that the window in question wasn’t connected to the fire escape and would require Salaok to move along the small ledge under the windows, but that didn’t bother him. Being afraid of heights is something Salaok never was as his life had seen him having to fly all over the world and looking down out of the window of an airplane is a sure way of testing out your tolerance for heights. Not wanting to be seen of course, Salaok grabs a hold of the ledge and hangs on, dangling him body into the air and moving his hands along the ledge slowly, holding on with all he has. The last thing you want to do when going to perform a hit, is end up dead via your own mistakes before the job is done. Inch by inch he moves along the ledge until finally he reaches the window in which is needed and so pulls himself up by his arms, and holds himself there while he pushes the window up and then lets himself drop down again as to wait to see if opening the window draws any attention. For most people, a minute in your life isn’t a long time, but when you are hanging five stories up in the air by your finger tips, waiting to see if you are going to be found out or not, it seems to last forever. Slowly the time passes and no one comes to the window, in turn giving Salaok the all clear and so he again pulls himself up onto the ledge and lets himself fall through the open window. This was it. This was the place where it must happen. The hotel room of Chris Davis, a man who had been looked up to during his time in The Lair, and then was seen as their greatest tormentor as the next generation got older. A man who was meant to have been killed some ten years ago and yet paid his way out of the situation, letting everyone believe that he had indeed been murdered, including his family. To let your family believe that you are dead for ten years is an act so cold and heartless that it could never be forgiven but he did it without a second of thought. In fact, it was his idea. Life away from those you love is better than death for some people but it would certainly be a hard thing to do? But what would be harder is letting that family then know that you are still alive after those ten years, only to vow to destroy them. Salaok stands up, looking around the room with the eyes of a starving hawk circling an area for prey. What would be the best way to do this and to make sure that nothing was going to go wrong? The room was clean, but not due to Chris Davis’s efforts, he wouldn’t clean a thing if he had to, no this was the work of the hotel staff and therefore, Salaok straight away puts his hands into his pockets and pulls out two leather gloves. The room was spotless and any traces of him that were left behind would soon be found. No, this had to look like suicide if Salaok was going to get away with things. His eyes set on the bar fridge, which if knowing this man well enough, he would have made sure the hotel keeps it well stocked. In his younger years, Chris Davis? major downfall was his ability to drink himself nearly into a coma, and then manage to take out his anger on his two sons. As he pulls the gloves onto his rough hands, he walks over to the bar, opens the door and just as expected, it’s full of scotch. Did Chris have a drinking problem? Who knows. More importantly to Salaok, who cares? He no longer did. Perhaps when Chris was someone he respected and looked up to he might have cared about it, but not any longer. The only thing that Salaok cared about now was ending this man’s life as a means of proving to his peers that he was still the same man he was before the loss of his wife and son. The side of his mouth curls up and his lips part ever so slightly as he eyes set on the bottle of Johnny Walker Green Label. Wanting ever so much just to take a small swig of the bottle himself, but having to restrain for the want of not leaving any evidence of himself behind. Grabbing the bottle, he moves it onto the bench in a place that will be easy to grab later on without having to take his eyes of his prey. The door handle begins to turn. Salaok had made it inside only just inside and now it would be here that it happens. The sound of drunken mumbling from the other side of the door and still the door handle turns this way and that, but not opening. The sounds of keys clanking into the key hole but none of then working. A burst of laughter from another voice, a female voice at that strikes Salaok as strange and then he hears her say through her shrieks of laughter. “It’s not even our fucking room!” Both people on the other side of the door burst into laughter and Salaok stands still, like a cat in the headlights of a car no knowing which way to run, as he listens to their voices trail off down the hallway. His heart now beating faster, much faster than normal and the ideas of what he was about to do strike at him. It had been a year since he had performed his last hit, but it had been a long time since he had performed a hit this close to his heart and to those around him. Never before had he done a hit this large on a personal scale so this was uncharted waters for Salaok. Moving his attention back to the bar, he finds a short glass and places it next to the bottle, making sure everything is set up to make things easier. And then the final piece to the puzzle as Salaok pulls out a silencer and rests it in the glass, again for easy access. A suicide this may very well be, but it’s not going to be one that draws people’s attention straight away. No, this act of false self destruction would be silent so that by the time Chris is found dead, Salaok will be on the other side of America attending to his casinos for the first time in a while. There was no way that Salaok ever could have committed such a crime from the other side of the country. Again the door knob begins to turn and only this time it is for real and Salaok knows it as he bolts over to the door and stands behind it waiting for it to open. His heart beating like a teenagers at some all night rave after taking speed. The key enters the whole and turns. Click. It unlocks and the door knob turns again and this time the door begins to open. This was it, the moment that should have happened ten years ago was finally about to be completed by the man that Chris Davis had managed to cause the most problems for. For it was the son of Chris Davis who had short Lisha that fatal night at Salaok’s home, killing her later that night. If Chris Davis had been killed when he was originally meant to be, then Mani Shigeru never would have found out about his father and his hatred for The Lair that he had turned his back on. The door opens fully and Salaok presses himself flat against the wall as to make sure the door doesn’t hit him and give him away. He watches as Chris’s hand reaches around the door and shuts it behind him without looking at it. He was now shut in the same room as the man in whom he had to kill. The man who had played such a large part in the destruction of his life. Clad in his expensive suit and his short blonde hair combed back. His stance full of arrogance. The arrogance that Salaok had learned to hate and the arrogance that would be his downfall in the end. Chris’s head tilts to the side as his eyes fix on the bottle of scotch and the glass, but more so the black item in the glass. “Your drink is waiting for you on the bench old man.” Salaok voice rings out, low and cold which instantly turns Chris Davis around and the two lock eyes, much like Salaok and Sage had done only hours before hand which resulted in a death threat. However this was different, this wouldn’t result in a death threat, but death itself. The two men with eyes that are identical as they are both nothing but white orbs. Many people say to a child that they have their parents eyes. “Hello son.” Chris’s voice isn’t as surprised as Salaok had hoped it to be but never-the-less, now was the time. “Hello father.”
