The Uninvited (Evolution Promo)

19 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03-19-05 “A failure; a disappointment will never find true success.” ~*~*~*~*~*~ A fog hung low around her; her vision became blurry. The dew ridden grass slid effortlessly through her toes as she made her way across the grassy field. A scent ascended to her nostrils, the scent of impending death and decay. A churning ignited in her stomach and she arched her body, using a tree for support. The awful aroma overpowered her and she became ill. This would not stop her. Upon finishing, she made her way through the field of fog, the dew quickly fading from the grass. The darkness around her lifted as she stepped out onto black pavement. The sun’s warm rays beat upon her pale face. Aware of her new surroundings, she turned to find a busy street. The fog and the dewed grass vanished. Confused, she turned back to the parking lot, lined with rows of apartments and cars. The sounds of children playing and distant televisions floated to her ears. She glanced at her watch, but the numbers were out of order and the hands did not make any sense. She scrunched her face in confusion and let her arm fall back to her side. A breeze blew a veil of her raven hair into her face and in aggravation she delicately pushed a few strands behind her ear. As she pulled her hand away, she felt a liquid between her fingers and brought her hands to her face. She gasped; both hands were covered in blood. The wind blew her hair back into her face and she pushed it away, only seeing the black of her hair. Once pure black, there were now multiple rivers of blood, criss-crossing and oozing in and out of her hair. She took a step back and shook her head. The blood disappeared from both her hair and her hands. Utterly confused, she looked around once again and began to make her way through the parking lot. A noise made her stop. Suddenly very alert of something behind her, she turned to find a little girl smiling at her. The closeness of the child evoked fear and she took a step back, gasping for air. The little girl’s long blond hair flowed in the breeze, her white sundress doing the same. Her little toes wiggled on the warm pavement. “Hello, Aradia.” The child smiled up at her, warmly, but there was suspicion in her eyes. Noticing this, Aradia raised an eyebrow and stared the girl down. “How typical of you.” The girl’s words fell from her tongue like fire. Intrigued, Aradia responded. “What do you mean?” The girl cocked her head and giggled. “It’s simple. Only you would keep a heart of ice, even to an innocent child, instead of keeping a loving stature.” Aradia squared her eyes and shook her head. “Excuse me, little girl, but I don’t know you and I don’t even know where I am, but I know I didn’t come here to be insulted by a child.” Offended, Aradia started away from the girl, back to the main road. She figured that if she stepped back onto the road, it could possibly take her back to the fog and the field. At least there she felt more comfortable. The tugging on her black tank top stopped her thought process. The little girl was following her. “Please, Aradia, I did not mean to insult you. Just stop and listen to me, I want to tell you something and I don’t have much more time to play. It’s time soon” Her small eyes appeared frantic; Aradia was compelled by their intensity. “Alright, child.” Aradia cautiously took the girls extended hand and followed her back to the parking lot. She stopped suddenly, at the first row of cars. An obvious thought crossed her mind and so she looked at the girl inquisitively. “Time for what?” The girl shook her head, her blond hair shifting wildly and in slow motion. “That is not important, Aradia. You will see soon enough.” The girl’s innocent, anxious face suddenly morphed into pure evil. “Then again, I suppose a failure like yourself may not see what I’m trying to show you.” Anger immediately overtook Aradia and she stepped forward. “What” What did you say to me?” The little girl merely smirked and leaned against one of the cars. “On what grounds do you think you can insult me” You’re only a child! I’m the Cruiserweight champion?” Aradia paused, taking this fact in for the thousandth time. It still did not feel right. “I am not a failure.” Aradia turned away and stared into the distance. For miles ahead, there was nothing but open parking lot. Irritated, the girl stepped in front of Aradia, not frightened by the woman’s height and size. “That’s exactly the problem. Everyone underestimates the child. As if we don’t understand.” The girl paces, her small feet pattering on the pavement. The stench of decaying flesh fills Aradia’s nostrils again and she fights off a dry heave. “We understand so much more than any of you will ever know. I understand more, I know more. I know you...” The girl stops pacing and turns to Aradia, menacingly. “You are a failure, you cannot win and you can not hide. A failure; a disappointment will never find true success.” “Big words for such a small girl,” Aradia says, sneering. Aradia looks around again, trying to find a distraction, something, anything to get her away from this brat. Again, the girl pulls at Aradia’s tank top. Aradia peers down at the smaller form and almost feels sympathy. As the girl tugs, she can almost feel her sorrow, the pain she endures. Images of bruises and fright pierce Aradia’s being. She cringes and backs away a step. The sorrow she feels on a day to day basis is more than she can handle, let alone the pain and suffering of this child. “So you see it” Good. We have something in common, a few things, actually. You just took a different path.” The girl begins to pace again. “I understand you, Aradia, but you cannot hide. They will find out. All the money in the world will not hide the truth. Someone will see. The memories plague your existence anyway. It does not matter what life you lived before then or what life you live after; it happened to you and you will never forget.” Aradia stares at the girl in horror. “What are you talking about?” The only response the girl offers is a smile. Suddenly finding herself scared of the girl’s mystique and knowledge, her imagination runs wild. Could this girl know what has happened in the past? What she’s done? It all happened so long ago? before this brat was even born. How could she know? Aradia bends down to the girl. “Who are you?” she asks, a mask of anger covering her fear. The girl stops pacing again, her back to Aradia. Turning slowly, the girl frowns. “That is not the important thing. The question is, who are you” You cannot hide who you were. That doesn’t matter anyway. Who are you now? That is the answer you must seek. Until then, you are a failure, destined to disappointment and doubt.” The girl begins to walk away. “That is all I can say. Do not forget what I have said.” Filled with confusion, fear, and uncertainty, Aradia starts after the girl. “But wait! What do I do” Where do I start? Come back! WHO ELSE KNOWS!”? The girl does not stop. She starts into a run, her little arms flailing back and forth, her little form quickly making its way across the hot pavement. “You must find that out, time is up!” Aradia saw it again: the pain, the blood, the bruises. She heard the words and threats, the punches and throws. She looks down at herself and sees the bruises, again, all over her body. Tears well in her eyes. “Wait, please come back!” Aradia started after her again, the girl needed her help. There was something wrong. Then they were there, the red tail lights, the piercing red eyes of the devil. The girl did not see them, or perhaps she did. Aradia screamed in horror, dashing after the girl, but the fog took her over. Frantic, she tried to push through the fog, trying to find the girl. The fog finally cleared and she found herself before a gray headstone. At a loss of breath, Aradia knelt down to study its surface. What she saw made her stomach churn. Aradia Xiomara 1996-1997 Lost to Failure, Hate, and Murder Her eyes grew wider, her chest heaved, and her mind clouded. Distant screams impaired her hearing while mists of fog disturbed her vision. She found herself sprinting through fields of black; an inescapable abyss of thoughts and emotion. Silently she fell, her breath short, memories encompassing her body. Violently she shook, convulsed, and trembled, her arms wrapped around her knees; her knees buried in her chest. She coughed, a dozen screams of agony caught in her throat. Then, suddenly, it was over and she was thrown from the couch. She brought herself to her knees and ran a hand through her raven tresses. Peering down upon her hands, she noticed the blood; it was streaming down her arms. A wave of fear washed over her. “Not again” she whispered, as she jumped to her feet. The blood formed a puddle around her feet; her black jeans were stained. Swiftly, she ran to the bathroom and turned on the shower. Not even stopping to remove her clothing, she hopped into the cold water. She closed her eyes and violently began to rub at her arms. Her eyes opened and found the blood gone, all traces of it vanished. She looked at her pants, the stains were invisible. Confused, she looked around her to find not one trace of the crimson liquid. “What in the world is wrong with me?” she asked herself, as she stepped out of the shower, grabbing a white towel that hung above the toilet. She dried her hair as she stepped into her living quarters once again. Calmly, she peeled off her wet black tank top and threw it onto the floor. The shirt hit the stone tile floor with a loud thwack. She used one of her arms to conceal herself as she made her way over to her duffel bag. Opening it, she pulled out the first shirt her hand could find: a blood red tank top. “How appropriate” she muttered. Next, she pulled out a pair of black leather pants. Slinging them over her couch, she looked over at the television as she removed her jeans. As she toweled off, she studied her last match furiously. Each move she missed, each move executed upon her was another arrow to her gut. By the end of the match, she was fully clad in the red tank top and pair of black leather pants. She sat on the couch and began to pull on a pair of heeled boots, watching as she was announced the winner. Instead of feeling victorious, she felt a pang of anger with each mistake. There was a passion that filled Aradia, down to her very core. It did not matter how the match was won. She needed to fight; she needed to win. She needed to conquer. She needed to be perfect. Winning the Cruiserweight Title, while a major accomplishment, only felt like a consolation prize for her failure. She shook her head. “Snap out of it you halfwit,” Aradia mumbled to herself. “You’re the champ for Christ’s sake?” She ran her hand through her black hair one more time, allowing the wet locks to fall down her shoulders. Standing, she made her way to the mirror above her television. She gazed at her large brown eyes and her full blackened lips, at her pale complexion and her self-loathed features. A beautiful woman stared at her, but the woman standing before the mirror was anything but beautiful. She turned away, ashamed. Her physical attractiveness could never make up for her failure. Anger surged through her veins. She grabbed an empty vase and threw it at the mirror, watching as it shattered into a hundred pieces. “Damnit” she exclaimed as she bent over to begin picking up the pieces. She peered down into a thousand mutated reflections of herself. An eye here, half a mouth there. This was more like her. Every day, inside and out, her reflection is mutated. Little could change how she feels about herself. Sighing, she threw the pieces of glass in her hands back onto the floor. Maybe the next inhabitant of this room will have an easier time picking up the pieces. Angry with herself, Aradia made her way to the door of the hotel room. Flinging it open, she was greeted by a man holding a bouquet of flowers. Brilliant red roses caught her eye and she gasped. Nearly two dozen of them, intimidating her. “Aradia Xiomara?” the stout man inquired, smiling at the taller, fit woman. Aradia nodded, dazzled by the flowers in his hand. “Wonderful. Whoever sent you these beauties must really have a liking for you.” The man looked Xiomara over and smirked. “I can’t say I blame “em” He handed the flowers to her and walked away, leaving Aradia holding the roses and staring after the man, scowling. “I’m not your type!” she called after him. Slamming the door, Aradia catches a glimpse of herself and Adam Myers climbing the ladder. The DVD had started over again. “I really need to turn that off,” Aradia mumbled as she looked at the roses again. Grimacing, she shook her head. “Who the fuck would send me flowers?” She said this to herself as she went to the coffee table grab the vase, but found herself grabbing at air. Then she remembered her bout of aggression. “Fucking wonderful.” She looked over at the shattered vase mixed in with the bits of mirror. Sitting down on the couch, Aradia sighed. She studied the roses thoughtfully. Who would bring her flowers? She brought them to her nose and smiled, she loved the scent of fresh roses. Studying them again, she noticed their brilliance and beauty. Her stomach turned slightly. Although deep red roses were beautiful, she preferred them black. Then she noticed the card. She pulled it out slowly to look at its words, scrawled in red ink. What she saw made her eyes wider and her heart stop. “You cannot escape. We know.” She dropped the card in horror and looked around her. Her lower lip quivered as she brought her knees to her chest and held them close. Eyes darting wildly around the room, she found herself back at square one, images of demented blond haired girls, crimson liquid, and forewarning gravestones flashing through her head. She put her head down into her knees, breathing heavy. Silent sobs rose from her throat. Through her fear and confusion, she looked up and sighed. She shook her head. She had business to attend to. Tonight marked the fifth year. Nodding, she walked to her coat rack, grabbed her leather duster, and quickly walked through the door to her apartment. “Why does life haunt me?” were her only words.
