Will the streak continue?

24 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/24/04 Brick’s Wrestlecade streak. It's become something of a small legend For the past three years, there has been a Wrestlecade, and it has been the most important card of the year for Global Wrestling. For the past three years, Brick has competed at every Wrestlecade that was held. And for the past three years, Brick has walked away in victory. It began at the first Wrestlecade in April of 2001. Brick was in a triple-threat match for the now-defunct United States title with then-champion “The Ladies Man” Jay Robson and Griven. The match ended when the lights in Madison Square Garden mysteriously went out. When they came back on, all three men were mysteriously left laying unconscious in the middle of the ring with note left by an individual then known only as the Jackal. However, Brick lay on top of Griven. When the referee saw this, he immediately began the count. One three count later, Brick was once again United States champion. In April of 2002 came Wrestlecade II. That year was a double victory for Brick. The first was his singing group DMAK making their national debut singing the National Anthem. The second came when Brick faced Jay Robson yet again. This time, Brick was the one defending a title. In this case, it was the International championship. One Bricklayer near the end and victory came yet again. But with Wrestlecade III came the most personal and gratifying victory of all. Brick’s feud with Salaok originally stemmed from his animosity with Salaok’s brother Jonathan “Scarecrow” Davis, who had put him in a coma in January 2001. Since then, Brick had been looking for payback and received it when, on an edition of Overload, he defeated Davis in the ring. In November of 2001, Brick had won his first ever shot at the GW World title. The following week, the match was supposed to happen, but it never did. Near the time for their match, Brick’s then-partner Top Dawg was being assaulted by Acid and Shale, members of the Nevermore. When Brick came out to his friend’s rescue, he was ambushed from behind by Salaok and Quartz. It then escalated into a Nevermore assault on Ruckus Inc., ending with Trista Romano, Brick and Top Dawg being driven through tables. Brick was robbed of his first-ever shot at the World title. Brick had fought for two years to win that shot and it was taken away. Making matters worse was Salaok sudden departure from the company at Showdown In Tokyo 2001. It would be months before Brick could get close enough to take payback. Their feud would re-ignite in the summer of 2002, when the Harvesters reunited. Brick had shown absolutely no fear in dealing with them. Then, they kidnaped and tortured Brick’s childhood friend Aaron Davis. It enraged Brick and led him to wage a personal one-man war against them. Then came the spring of 2003. Salaok was revealed to be half-owner of Global Wrestling. He used this power to make life hell for anyone who either had stood against him or would stand against him. High on this list was Brick. Salaok placed a stipulation on all of Brick’s matches. If Brick were to lose any match, he would be suspended indefinitely until such time as Salaok saw fit to reinstate him, which would never happen. It was in a triple threat match that Brick would finally lose due to interference from the Harvesters. Finally, Brick got tired of Salaok’s games. On an edition of Evolution, Brick challenged Salaok to a match at Wrestlecade III. Since theirs was a particularly brutal feud, there was only one type of match that would fit: Hell in a Cell. Salaok agreed to the match, but only if Brick could defeat the returning Chris Camero. Brick won when Camero unexpectedly laid down for him. Since that victory, Salaok tried to put up roadblock after roadblock to ensure that he and Brick would never face off. But Brick wouldn’t be denied. To further drive this point home, Brick attacked both men during a match for the World title, which Camero held at the time. But the night of Wrestlecade III in Texas, their match almost didn’t happen. Brick was delayed at the airport and desperately trying to get to the arena. Salaok went down to the ring, more than ready to declare himself the winner by forfeit, but again Brick wouldn’t be denied. He drove into the arena in a black Lincoln Navigator right to the ring and the match would go on. That night, Salaok wasn’t the only person Brick would have to face. He would also have to face Quartz and Mark Kullypso who attempted to interfere on Salaok’s behalf. Brick would dispatch both men before finally hitting the Bricklayer on Salaok and picking up the pinfall victory, continuing Brick’s undefeated streak at Wrestlecade. So far, Brick’s record at Wrestlecade is 3-0. Many insiders have said that it could potentially be a winning streak of epic proportions. That can change this coming Monday in the Memorial Stadium at Wrestlecade IV. Again, it’s personal. The two men he faces at Wrestlecade are two men who have gone out of their way to antagonize Brick. One has done so for about a year. The other is more recent. Brick will be in a cage with Deacon Holmes and Train, fighting for the Platinum title. Three men with great animosity towards one another, locked in a cage, with virtually no rules to speak of. It will not be for the faint of heart. This time, more than a title is at stake. This time, it’s about pride. Brick has been looking to continue his streak and make it 4-0 this year. Many insiders are considering him an underdog in this match. Most have said that the match would come down to Holmes and Train. Brick is basically seen as a non-issue in this match. But one thing Brick has become famous for is beating the odds. Wrestlecade IV may just be the place where Brick beats the odds once again.
