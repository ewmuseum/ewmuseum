Downfall: Not who I seem

10 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/10/2005 For the last several years, darkness had become commonplace in the McFarlane estate. The virtual parade of night creatures had warranted some changes to ensure their security; Heavy blinds to keep out the sun, thick drapes to ensure no prying eyes could see what went on within the halls and dark carpeting over hardwood floors, to ensure those who walked at night would make less noise to disturb the living. The only place that this was not true was Dawn’s master bedroom on the fourth floor. The d’cor had remained as she wished it to be, with light, gauzy curtains that let the wind come and go as it please’much as she wished she could do. The result of these modifications was that night had ceased to be a time of peace or solitude in the estate, rather it had turned out to be one of the busiest times of the day as its various sleeping guests roused themselves from torpor to greet the moon. For Dawn the lack of peace brought by the night was a source of frustration, one that often drove her outside to seek solace and solitude away from the prying eyes of any guests. The first light of dawn, however, was another story entirely. Though now able to walk in the day as Lia did, Virgil still sought to avoid the suns rays for as long as possible and Alex had long ago retired for the evening. Even her strangest guest, Cyrus, had ceased his prowling through her library and sought out the relative shelter of his guest room. For a few blissful hours, the McFarlane estate would know peace and quiet’giving Dawn time to reflect on the state of things. She slipped out of her bedroom, slowly easing the door shut behind her, so as not to disturb the massive “dogs” that had taken to sleeping outside her door while Lia was awake. Dawn favored Damek and Jirina with a fond smile, her own love of dogs allowing her to see past the strange modifications Lia had made to her hellhounds some time ago. Her long robe whispered of heavy silk as she slipped between the sleeping animals, and her slippers made less then a whisper against the polished hardwood of the hallway floor. Though the temperature had dropped outside, the windows on the fourth floor had been left slightly open and soft breezes rustled Dawn’s robe around her ankles, or blew strands of errant hair about her face. The sleeping robe was a new addition, purchased during her latest outing with Alexis into Toronto. Since admitting her feelings to Lia, Dawn had found herself less and less willing to spend her night sleeping in worn boxers or an old tank top, regardless of how comfortable either garment was. A tight, sleeveless black shirt with a high collar had replaced the old tank top, and a set of black silk underwear had replaced the worn boxers’both choices that required a much warmer cover when venturing out into the cold night air. Dawn paused for a moment at the window, closing her eyes in rapture as the soft rays of moonlight cascaded about her. Even before her association with Vampires, Dawn had been something of a night creature and she found the soft light of the moon much more preferable to the harsh rays of the sun. With a sigh she let her eyes fall open again, reaching to undo the latch on the window. Cold air rushed in as she cracked the window wide open, forcing her to tug the heavy silk of her night robe firmly around her slender frame. She shivered in the cold for a moment before setting one foot into the windowsill and grabbing the edges of the frame with her hands. As she tried to pull herself up, something closed heavily around her ankle like a vice, holding her back from the edge without causing any pain. Looking back over her shoulder Dawn sighed at the sight of Damek latched firmly around her ankle, his sharp fangs pressed so lightly against her skin that she hadn’t even known they were there. “Damek.” She soothed, stepping from the sill. “I’m not doing anything to hurt myself you silly goose. I just need to go outside for a while and collect my thoughts, I need to be alone.” The hellhound whined deep in his throat, releasing Dawn’s ankle and sitting back with a bemused expression on his unusually intelligent face. Dawn crouched before him, taking his face in both hands and ruffling his large ears, scratching the space just behind them with expert fingers. “I won’t be gone long, and I’m not going to do anything rash alright” You can wait right here if you like, I’m not going anywhere but the roof.” Since Lia had left, Dawn found herself having these strange conversations with the dogs more and more. Though they had something of a common ground, Dawn still found it very difficult to relate to Virgil and imagined that he found himself in a similar state of affairs, either unable, or unwilling, to spend much time with Dawn. Even Alexis had retreated a ways, leaving Dawn to spend most of her home moments alone or in the company of Lia’s dogs. She looked over Damek’s massive shoulder, making out the dark outline of Jirina standing nearby, her eyes shining eerily in the darkness. “You tell your brother I’m going to be alright. If it will make you feel better you can both wait right here.” She straightened slowly, wincing a little as the aches and pains of recent matches protested to the prolonged crouch and the sudden movement. She wasted no time, hopping up into the windowsill and pulling herself up so she dangled half out the window. Reaching up she grabbed the side of the chimney purely from memory, pushing off the sill and dragging herself up onto the gentle slope of the roof with a grunt of effort. The wind howled about her as Dawn stood up, holding her arms out for balance as she walked delicately along the roof to a small spot between where the top of the roof met the gradual slope. An error in construction had resulted in a small, flat patch about three feet across and three feet long, just perfect for one person to be sitting in. Sometimes she licked to flatter herself to think that her father had left it this way for her, so that she could climb up here whenever she wanted to collect herself’like tonight. Plopping down on the cold shingling she drew her knees up to her chest, hugging her arms around her calves and resting her chin on the point of her knees. Even from here she could make out the sparkling lights of Toronto, the winking and flashing that showed a city that was wide awake, even at this lonely hour. Shivering a little in the wind, her mind wandered to Lia, wondering desperately where her lover was at this time of day and praying, to what entity she didn’t know, that she was keeping herself safe. “Lia.” She spoke her name without hesitation, almost without thought, the word rolling from her lips like she’d known it since birth. In truth sometimes she felt like she had, as though there had always been this delightful squirming sensation in her stomach whenever they were alone or the shiver she couldn’t suppress at the other woman’s cool touch. Dawn sighed, tucking her chin to her chest and resting her forehead on her knees, banging it softly against the bone there in frustration. “Why why why” Why does it always have to be like this? Why can’t we just’be together’why does it all have to be so goddamn complicated?” It was maddening. With one of the largest matches of her career looming on the horizon, all Dawn could think about was the absence of her lover. This had never happened to her before, she had never been separated from anyone she cared for by any great stretch of time’but Lia’s note had been quite clear that she didn’t know when she was going to come back. The unspoken desire to be left alone was almost as fresh in Dawn’s mind as the actual wording of the letter itself. At no point had Lia forbidden Dawn to come seeking her, but she couldn’t shake the thought that her lover would be terribly disappointed if Dawn didn’t let her work through this on her own. Dawn slapped one palm down against the roof in frustration, a growl slipping its way free of her throat. “I can’t just sit here and do nothing’I can’t just let wander all over hells half acre without any kind of lifeline’goddammit?” The last word was hissed through clenched teeth, her jaw aching as it clamped shut almost as tightly as the hand she held squeezed into a fist at her side. The worst part of it all was seeing Lia at Evolution’knowing that she was there, potentially only feet away at any given point’but that she might as well be in another country as far as Dawn was concerned. She couldn’t even say hello, trusting in a simple glance to convey all the though, all the worry and all the care that went into each moment. And on top of all of that’Emoticon again. The soul of the poet had left her alone since Whiplash, living up to the note he had slipped under her door on perfumed paper. But even Emoticon could not control the booking, and this week she would see him again’in a match that might have stood out as one of her worst nightmares. It was a virtual neon sign of Global’s top talent, all of them strong superstars that were trying to, or already had; secure themselves a spot near the top of Global’s Elite. But of all of them, only Emoticon had ever pinned Dawn’s shoulders to the mat. There were men and women there she had never faced before, but in this case the fear of the unknown was drastically overshadowed by the fear of the known. One. Two Three. Just like that Emoticon had taken from her the title she had taken from Lia. He had taken from her what she had won in a contest she had never wanted’and in so doing had thrown the first chink into Dawn’s armour. “If he beat me then?” She mused, her breath pluming around her face. ??would he have beaten me at the end of Masters of the Mat? Do I deserve the honour of that title?” The fear was maddening, worming its way down into Dawn’s soul and undermining everything she tried to do. Fear so deep’it was getting the best of her, making her into a person she couldn’t stand to see in the mirror anymore? Fear of losing Lia. Fear of admitting her feelings to Lia. Fear of losing to Emoticon again. Fear of not being good enough’Just’Fear. She whimpered a sound most unlike the woman people had come to call the Steel Rose. She imagined that no one would recognize the 2005 Mistress of the Mat if they were to see her now, curled up on her roof in her underwear and whimpering at the thought of stepping into the ring again’of failing AGAIN. Another sigh wrestled its way free of her lips, escaping into the night air to blow away on a plume of steam. She raised her head to watch it go and found a new set of lights moving down the highway towards the estate. Cocking her head quizzically to one side, Dawn watched it for a long moment as the vehicle slowed, obviously trying to find its way. “Now who the hell would be visiting me at this hour?” She grumbled, “It’s not even morning yet?” The words stuck in her throat as a small glimmer of hope leapt into her mind. Maybe Lia had come home’maybe all of this maddening waiting was about to come to a blissful end. She pushed herself away from the roof, dashing along the shingles before dropping to a crouch above the window she’d slipped out of. Snagging the side of the chimney with both hands she swung adeptly through the window, letting go of the chimney at the last moment to land in a crouch on the window. Damek and Jirina watched her with anxious expressions, each dog whining a little as she dropped back into the hallway and shut the window behind her. “It’s alright guys.” She whispered, “We’ve just got a late visitor.” She scratched each dog’s head as she walked by, an unspoken command raising them from the floor to fall into step at her heel, flanking her as she took the main stairwell to the main floor, hoping to get the door before the knocking disturbed any of her slumbering guests. Dawn pulled the front door open as a white SUV rumbled in through the front gates, tires crunching over some lingering gravel from the highway before rolling onto the paved driveway of the estate. The hellhounds sat at either side of her as the unfamiliar vehicle rolled to a stop in front of the door, belching out a final plume of exhaust before powering down, the engine pinging and ticking as it began to rapidly cool. The passenger side window rolled down and a bright face peered out at Dawn, a slow smile creeping across her face. “Well now there’s a sight for sore eyes, you’ve filled out awful well Dawnie.” She punctuated the comment with a low whistle, grinning and winking one mischievous blue eye. Dawn’s jaw hit the pavement as she stared in wonderment at her guest, struggling for words. “Caitlin”! Caitlin fucking Valentine?! What’how’what are you’how did you?” Caitlin laughed, throwing open the drivers side door and stepping out with a groan, stretching her aching muscles and adjusting the beige cowboy hat on her head. She came around the front of the car and took the steps two at a time, reaching the top in time to embrace Dawn with a powerful hug. “Why don’t you collect your thoughts and form a complete question Ah can answer darlin?”” Dawn couldn’t help but laugh, the sound practically forced out of her by the strong embrace. “What are you doing here” I havn’t seen you for YEARS! I thought you’d stopped talking to me after we’after?” Dawn’s voice trailed off weakly, her eyes looking pleadingly at her childhood friend. Caitlin shook her head and leaned forward, pressing a quick kiss to Dawn’s lips before taking a step back. “No no, Ah just needed my own space is all. Ah’m still only interested in Cowboy’s though’it just seemed like kissin” you was the right thing to do.” She winked and Dawn blushed, a heavy scarlet creeping up into her cheeks. “You should have told me you were coming, I don’t have anything ready for you.” “Oh Ah don’t want you to be makin” no fuss over me darlin??I’m here to be makin? a fuss over you. Ah’ve been keepin? an eye on you Dawnie, and I figured it’s well past time Ah came up here and took some hand in it myself.” Dawn blinked in confusion, her head whirling from the suddenness of it all. “Took a hand in it?” Caitlin’s bright face fell a little for a moment, becoming temporarily serious as she gazed up at the slightly taller Dawn. “Ah know you’re hurtin” girl, ya can’t keep that kinda thing from me. Ah know it’s been a while since we saw each other, but Ah can’t leave my best friend by herself when she needs me.” “Caitlin?” Dawn whispered the word, fighting back the roiling tide of emotion that Caitlin’s words brought on. “I’m’I’m?” Caitlin stepped up close again, wrapping her arms around Dawn and pulling her close so her head was pillowed against Caitlin’s shoulder. “Yer not fine, so don’t you think for even a moment that Ah’m gonna accept that load of horse shit. If you want me to go away that’s fine’but Ah’m here to help you sort you out.” For a long moment Dawn debated telling her childhood friend to go home, that she was fine and she didn’t need help. But the chink in her armour felt a little wider, and Dawn couldn’t help but wonder if maybe she really did need someone else’s help this time.
