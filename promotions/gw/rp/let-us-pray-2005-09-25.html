Let Us Pray

25 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09-25-2005 “Fahgive me fatha fo I have sinned.” Casinos, brothels, and strip joints. Chances are, a Las Vegas citizen has frequented one if not all three of the structures mentioned. It actually seemed a bit out of place in a city filled to the brim with vice to find oneself inside a place of faith and healing. In Las Vegas, a church was a mirror of truth, useless in a city where no one wished to see their true visage. Tonight would be a different night however, as the church confessional would be filled by a strange occupant in this case. To say he had strayed far from the path of God was like saying Hurricane Katrina was merely a “drip” in Louisiana. There sat the Back Alley Brawler with his head pensively looking down, his 300+ pound frame trying to find any sort of comfort possible in the tight space of the confessional. It seemed even tighter when he thought of the priest across from him, his face distorted by a screen of tiny sequential holes. The Brawler wished for the same comfort with God, and the confessional, that the priest had become accustomed to; but it was a pipe dream to wish for a feeling he would never know. With as much bloodshed and broken bones that the Brawler had left behind in and out of the ring, it took an even stronger man to accept and attentively follow the path of God. At the very least, the priest was comforting. His voice reflected an uncanny wisdom, the type that had experienced all that life had to offer and survived to tell the tale. For all the Brawler knew, the priest could have been some 19-year-old who developed bass in his voice too soon, but it was a negative thought he would have to suppress at that moment, given the gravity of the situation. Any situation that would drive him to the church instead of standing in front of the pulpit of Reverend Jack Daniels had to be serious. “Speak my child. What is it that God will forgive you for tonight?” The Brawler could not help but snicker, rubbing his stubbly chin as he thought of the laundry list of pain and suffering he caused. Coming to the conclusion that an all-powerful entity like God probably wouldn’t want to be held up for weeks with the confessions of the Bronx Barbarian, he decided to keep it simple with recent sins for the attentive priest. “Fatha, I want da Lord to fahgive me for a lot’I do. But my momma, bless her soul, told me not to be a greedy bas”.I mean boy.” The priest took in a hearty laugh, picking up on the stumble of the Brawler’s words. He admired the candid demeanor of his temporary guest in the confessional, especially given that his house of God usually never saw too many guests to begin with. “Fatha, I wany God tah fahgive me for being a terrible fatha and a husband. I love my little girl and I STILL love my ex-wife to dis” day’but it is a sin dat I left them to the wind the way I did and I know it. I refused ta realize it till I got slapped with court orda’d visits and child support?.but at least I realize it. I hope God and the both of them find it in their hearts to fahgive me?.I need all three of dem more then eva, padre.” He could hear a slight breath by the priest as he adjusted his seat in the confessional. The holes were wide enough to catch a glimpse of his aged blue eyes. For a moment, they caught the onyx pupils of the hardened New Yorker as the priest made his reply, his voice having a certain command of the situation that no fist to the face could ever hope to capture. “I had a little girl”.once.” “Once?” “I was driving drunk’rainy night”.dark road?.you put the pieces together.” “I’m sorry.” “Every waking day, I wondered WHY...how could I have stayed alive” What did I deserve to still walk the Earth while my little girl was taken? Had she committed some great sin in 4 years on Earth that deserved her life? I asked myself these questions for quite a while my friend?.questions that led me to the path of guide. I can’t replace my daughter. I know that she will never return to my side....but I think she forgives me because I learned my lesson.” The Brawler’s face contorted with sorrow for the priest’s loss. He did a great job of holding back the pain in his voice, but the Brawler was able to notice the saddened cracks in his speech as he spoke aloud. It reminded him of the nights little Carmen had fell ill and the Brawler would have to call Karen, the gruff New Yorker speaking to his wife (at the time) as if he felt the touch of death “No need to feel sad for me, sir. I’ve shed my share of tears over it. What I intend to ask of you is this” What are you prepared to do to prove you have learned your lesson?” His body stiffened. The Brawler had never truly thought of repaying the debt of immorality to his two loves in the manner the priest had presented it. What could he do to prove that his little angel and his lost love had every reason to have faith in Joe Balboa once more? What could he do to prove that his lust for the ring over a family was not all for naught? “I hear you’re facing Michael Mercer for the TV title”.I would suggest you start making it up to them then.” A smirk forced its way onto the Brawler’s face as he looked to the holed screen once more, wishing he could see the face that went with the eminent voice. “Wrestling fan are we, padre?” “On occasion. I am not always in the house of the Lord you know. I pay a mortgage of my own. From the sound of your plight though, it seems like you have never given your family a real reason to believe this dream of yours is worth chasing. Make them believe”.win or lose. This opportunity is a God send. You came here for God to forgive and bless you’with a match like this, maybe he already has.” The Brawler’s eyes had not known such brightness since the days of his childhood. This priest had taken a wall and broken it down with words, with enlightenment waiting to join the Brawler on the other side. For tonight, he was happy that he didn’t choose the Church of Broads and Booze. If the decrepit relic that was the Brawler could muster up the willpower to seize the TV title from Michael Mercer, it would meant the world to his family back in New York to see that he had made the dream come true. A title had never enwrapped itself around the Brawler’s burly waist since he was 20 years old, his mentor dying in the ring as he pinned him for the NYWA Empire State Title. If he wanted to repay the anguish he had placed on the women who loved him’the drought would have to end. “Do my words make sense, or perhaps you should seek Buddah?” Only three words could fit the bill for this situation, a fresh vigor filling the Brawler’s soul. “Amen padre, amen.” As much as he would have liked to take credit for calling up the passion inside him for this bout, he could feel the guiding hand of something higher. The Brawler could only pray that it would guide him to all that glitters.
