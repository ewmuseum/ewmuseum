The Prophecy, Part II: No End in Sight

Date: various .:: ::: ::. The past It was not the thunder that awoke Lia from her sleep so much as it was the darkness. By all rights, as she looked around her room in groggy half-observation, she felt as though she should still be asleep. But there had to be a reason she was not. Half entranced, she slipped out of bed, her feet fumbling for purchase on the deep pilings of the carpet. It was almost like the sun was still burning away’she could never find her bearings to do anything when the sun was still out. Most of her kind lived that way, which is why it was absolutely necessary to sleep most of the day’day was vulnerable, and Kindred could not be vulnerable. Gently and slowly, she unfastened the heavy tie that bound the curtains, pushed them aside aside, and peered down Chartres Street towards Jackson Square. Fear seized her heart as the wind pushed the curtains further back, tossing rain in through the open window. It was still daylight out, and that was what frightened her’but as she looked up and down the street, she gathered that the rest of the city of New Orleans was rather frightened as well. Up and down Chartres Street, the colorful salmon and avocado buildings had black shutters pulled-to, bolted against the outside world. It was a fearful storm outside. The live oaks in Jackson Square tossed back and forth violently, their leaves blowing across the street. Lightning flashed down from the sky in a jagged streak, cutting knife-like into the mighty Mississippi, churning viciously seaward. Thunder rattled the foundation of every building in the French Quarter shortly afterwards. The silence following was one of the tomb, and she could sense, rather than see that almost every resident was cowering in an isolated room, fearful of the black clouds that engulfed the city. Lia did not find the storm much comforting herself. She considered ducking back inside, and attempting to sleep away the uneasy churning of her innards, if that were even possible. Daytime or not, this was something of a din, and a spiritually disturbing one at that for the hundreds-of-years-old vampire, who had always been sensitive to that sort of thing. Movement’human movement’in Jackson Square caught her eye, and her eyes immediately gravitated to it. A group of men, features inscrutable, carried something through the gates towards St. Louis Cathedral. Then another group followed? Groggily, her mind came to, and she realized what they were carrying. The bodies had been lying unburied in Jackson Square, by orders of the Spanish governors of the city, for nearly three weeks. Rebellion wasn’t looked upon too kindly by the pompous Spanish, in their boats out by the river because the townspeople would have slain them to a man if they’d come ashore. New Orleans was unabashedly French, and the townspeople weren’t planning on letting the Spanish forget it. Three weeks ago, tensions had come to a head. Governor de Gama had attempted to come inland, to put his foot down on the necks of the obstinate French? that hadn’t worked well at all. Governor de Gama had barely escaped with his own neck intact. His guardsmen, troops of the Royal Spanish Army hadn’t been so lucky, and had been slaughtered nearly to a man. Retaliation from the boat out in the river had sent nearly two dozen men to the gallows’and worse, they were not to be allowed Christian burial, but were to rot in the middle of Jackson Square. For the deeply Catholic French, this had been a grievous wound, one that cut deep’and in fact, their deeply Catholic Spanish governors had intended for it to. There had been talk about the town of defying orders and burying their dead since it had happened, but they all knew what that meant’more French deaths. It was a heavy cost to consider. Were they to incur more senseless death? And if they did not at least try to bury these men, what sort of penance would they pay when they themselves died? What would happen if they could have prevented these men from going to Hell simply by braving the wrath of man on earth instead of tempting God to smite them? The group of men swelled as the third and fourth corpses passed out of Jackson Square. Lia’s eyes widened increasingly as she watched them, led at the front by the local priest, who was called P’re Antoine. The lightning crashed down to earth again, in concert with the thunder, and the wind whipped P’re Antoine’s cassock so that it tangled about his ankles. He turned back to them, his thinning brown hair pushed messily into his eyes. “Venez!” he called to them over the din, “D’p’chez-vous!” (“Come!... Hurry up!”) And come they did with him’despite the storm, despite the danger to life and limb. Pere Antoine turned into the wind and pressed into the alley on the right-hand side of St. Louis Cathedral. BOOM! Again, the thunder rang loud and long, shaking every building within range. And again. But there was a sound drifting into Lia’s ears over it. Kyrie?. Christe Eleison?. Her face changed expression, almost as though she’d been slapped. But the haunting sound would not fade from her ears, though it got softer as the priest and his entourage ran towards St. Louis Number One, the cemetery on Basin Street. There, they would lay their brave companions to rest on sanctified ground, but not without the holy rites being sung over their bodies’no matter whether they had to sing those rites while running through the sopping wet French Quarter streets or not. Kyrie? Christe Eleison? Something stirred within her soul at the sound of the familiar Greek, which had been adapted into the Roman Catholic Church’s rites. The phrase meant “Christ have mercy.” There was no other reason that could explain how the Kyrie made Lia feel at this moment’no one had ever pronounced those words over her. They would not have even if her original fate’burning at the stake for heresy and murder’had been completed instead of aborted by her interloping rescuer. Christ’s mercy did not extend to her. She was among the Damned. The tocsin at the Cathedral sounded slowly. CRACK! This time it was Lia’s own thunder that shut out the noise of the Kyrie as she slammed the window shut forcefully, tying the black drapes back in place to shut out the light that would no doubt return abominably soon. .:: ::: ::. Shadow had never quite understood the mindset of the significant number among her kind who claimed that they were completely unaffected by human events, no matter of what sort they were. She had always been different. A raging whirlpool in New Orleans had done more to alter her world than just cause her passing concern or pity’in the back of her mind, she wondered how many havens had been consumed, how much property owned by her kind destroyed, how many bloodless monsters floated, torpid, under the surface of the mucky grime of Lake Ponchartrain. More evident, more pertinent than any of those questions was the human suffering’the abject misery and raw pain of those affected. She wondered if most of her kind would have really called her so ridiculous for taking notice of these goings-on. It was a macrocosm to her microcosm. When she allowed herself to think about it, she knew deep down that it would only be a matter of time before her own barriers broke, before the stream of frustration inundated her personal life. She feared for those around her’she feared what they’d think of her if she didn’t put them first. These were all things she wasn’t supposed to worry about’at least not if she was the person that Emoticon said she was. “Answer me now,” he had said to her, in so many words, “Nevermind that you don’t let me down because you’re concerned for my feelings, I want my answer, I WANT you to shatter me.” But she couldn’t do it. Just like she couldn’t say no to Dawn’couldn’t bring herself to look into those pretty green eyes and deny her what she needed. Nor could she turn away from Virgil. One moment spent in his arms made her realize how much he needed her’how much she needed him. She couldn’t forget about her responsibilities to Soma, either. Week after week, the words that he’d said pressed closer and closer into her mind. The longer his absence, the more keenly she felt that she was neglecting her duty, the more harshly her mind grated, shouting at her to do as she should. It was the same with Hunting Grounds; call them friends though she might, in the end, it was just all more responsibility, expectation, and sublimation of self. What she wanted didn’t matter. And hadn’t Emoticon said the same to her? “Shadow, it’s not fair, keeping us all in the dark like this,” his eyes had begged of her, “Why can’t you just make a choice” Why can’t you just leave the majority of people unhappy and pick one, end this ridiculousness for good?” Since coming to Global, she’d bent over backwards to not ruffle any feathers, no matter what her own wishes had been. It had thus far been a balancing act of epic proportions, one that might have been impossible for someone who didn’t have the same resolve to deny their own needs in favor of the needs of others. To be called essentially selfish over it was unthinkable to her. It was one of the reasons that she was right now caught between hating Emoticon and wondering if he was right. All it had taken was that one question’that one attempt to push buttons and force her to answer to another set of expectations. This would be the first time she’d ever directly denied someone their request, the first time that she had ever looked someone in the eyes and said “no” when they asked her to give of herself. And she felt terrible for doing it, but she couldn’t force herself. She couldn’t force herself to come down from the pedestal she felt she’d been put on and choose one person above all the others. None of them deserved to be shunned, and she could not bring herself to do it. .:: ::: ::. McFarlane Estate Early in the week The sunlight glimmered off of the deep blue of Virgil’s eyes almost obscenely. The intrusion of their gold rays into the placid, shimmering lake of his irises tinted the color slightly, illuminating tiny lighter threads of cerulean amid the depths. Three months ago, the light gleaming into his eyes would have caused him blind terror. Now, to the eternal fright of those who took solace in the idea that vampires only came out at night, said light was just a slight inconvenience to his sight, something that made him squint myopically as his eyes watered slightly with blood tears. He forced a small sigh through clenched teeth as he turned from the majestic sunrise that was sweeping over the countryside of the McFarlane Estate to look back inside the bedroom. There, the majestic ivory landscape of his nude paramour, stretched drowsy-lidded across the black satin sheets as though across some artist’s canvas, almost made him forget why he’d gotten up so early. His boots crushed into the thick red and black piling of the rug noiselessly as he strode to the edge of the bed, his pale fingers looping around the smooth wood of the tall, carved bedpost. His other hand reached to her, and his fingertips found purchase stroking gently across her soft cheek. She closed her eyes fully, her eyelashes making a heavy black fringe along her well-defined cheekbones. “I have to leave soon,” he said simply. The corners of her lips turned ever-so-slightly downwards. “Do you have to go today” It’s going to be disgusting down there.” What she’d really meant by that was “dangerous.” He could read that much in her tone. He smiled slightly. “I’m afraid I don’t have much of a choice. Kindred or not, I’ve no desire to step into the middle of a city governed by martial law and get inconvenienced by a bullet to the forehead. Best that I go take care of my business now and get it over with.” She yawned softly, reaching her fingers out to brush his forearm. “Be careful.” He pressed a soft, cool kiss to her forehead and started towards the door. Feeling that jerky spasm in the pit of his stomach, he turned back to look at her one last time, and found her eyes tilted all the way open, boring a hole into him. “I love you.” “I love you too, Lia,” he said, somewhat surprised. Their spirits seemed to be connected in ways that were rather uncanny sometimes, but it wasn’t something that he questioned’more something he appreciated for its novelty. Novelty was something to be appreciated when one was as old as he. He smiled at her one last time, his eyes flashing softly sad before he stepped out the door, feeling the rope between them tighten fast, as though trying to yank him back into bed with her. He pushed past it? business was business, and he had to go tie up his loose ends to prevent them causing problems later. Much as he hated the idea of leaving Lia behind, he hated even more what he knew he would find when he got to Louisiana. .:: ::: ::. McFarlane Estate Late in the week The deep black eyes of the Kiasyd focused on her, somewhat unclearly. “Am I to assume that Miss McFarlane knows of my presence?” Lia nodded. “Yes, of course. It would be terribly inhospitable of me not to tell her that I was keeping someone in her guest room.” “Especially someone who is rather dark blue in hue and over seven feet tall.” She smirked slightly. She hadn’t intended on bringing that up, but it seemed that Kiasyd were always well aware of their own freakishness. She supposed it would have been impossible for them not to be. Cyrus? gentle chuckling brought her back to the moment. “Leave it to creatures such as us to worry about propriety when there are emergencies at hand.” Lia smiled. That had always been something she liked about Cyrus’they both very much valued the sanctity of personal space and courteous propriety surrounding it. That meant that they very rarely encroached on one another’s boundaries. Cyrus? black pupil-less eyes fixed on her nearly emotionlessly’another trait of his kind that she rather enjoyed. Nothing messy about a Kiasyd’s interest in someone’it was always purely business. “How much did she tell you about the Prophecy?” “She” of course referred to Lilith. It surprised Lia slightly that Cyrus would even want to talk about this, considering that Lilith had very nearly put him into Final Death a month ago. Lia shook her head, pressing her hands to either side of her head in the mimed motion of clearing her hair out of the way, but really, all it did was show her frustration. “I guess she told me everything. You know, about the whole mothering of a new race, giving up everything that surrounds me to become some princess on a pedestal with my scepter, lording it over all the little kine below me” I don’t know if I can handle that.” He could tell how much it bothered her. For that reason, he measured his words. “She may not have told you the truth.” Lia’s eyes flashed momentarily with hope, but she tried hard to suppress it. “What do you mean?” Cyrus shifted uncomfortably. He might have been awake? but he wasn’t well. He winced. “Lilith is a jealous creature, and may have told you something to drive you away from Soma” which is exactly what she wants.” Soma? There was a blinding white flash of light. Lia’s fingernails clenched into her temples, in pain. Dawn was creeping over the Estate. Shadow could feel it in her bones, something she’d gotten used to’she no longer felt the inexorable pull of sleep coming on, but she still had a tendency to shy from the sunlight. Virgil looked at her through terribly clouded blue eyes’he hadn’t had the benefit of Soma’s blood infused into him, meaning he still had to while away the daytime hours in near torpor. He was fighting sleep like a tiger, but she knew it wouldn’t be much longer before he was forced to give in to instinct over want. His arms cradled her almost gently, their deceptive power laid to rest for the moment in the peace of the moment. She kissed him softly as she customarily did before he slept, both of their bodies still covered in a sheen of blood sweat that mingled as they tangled together into one form under the sheets. “I love you,” she whispered, closing her long-lashed eyes momentarily. Virgil’s eyebrows pressed downwards for a moment, as every second, the malaise pressed down harder, the confusion that would eventually envelop him and steal him away from her until the daylight passed away outside. “I don’t want to leave?” he trailed off, a trace of his gentle Iberian lilt coming through when he took no conscious effort to suppress it. She pressed him closer, a moment of quiet sadness coming to her. More than anything, she wanted this eternity to share with him. She only knew one way, but sharing her blood with him now that she was a part of Soma’s lineage? she wasn’t sure if it was wise, or if he would approve. He blinked, his eyes nearly shutting for that last time’and without thinking, she acted. “Then take part of me with you?” She pressed his lips softly to the skin just above her breast. With barely the energy to distend his fangs, he pressed them into her skin, the blood rushing immediately to engulf his lips. He swallowed softly. “Lia?” Cyrus? question sounded like it came from so far away. She wanted to answer him, but she couldn’t. Rage, rage, that’s all there was. Rage, and the subtly African-featured face of Leinad Soma. She opened her mouth to speak, but all the red rage surged forward and engulfed her. She couldn’t think, couldn’t speak. It was like being trapped under heavy lava, ton after ton after ton. His face was all she could see. And then she heard him. He spoke only one sentence. “What gave you the right?” With a gasp, Lia snapped back to the present, her eyes wide. She shook her head. “Soma?” Cyrus? lips curled into a humorless smirk. “I would imagine he’d want you to remember him at a time like this.” Her adrenaline racing through her veins at breakneck speed, she glanced at him’she wasn’t “all there” and he could tell it. “Why” Do you think? do you think that I sold him short?” Cyrus shook his head. “That I don’t know” but I think that you shouldn’t write him off.” “Why?” “That’s something that I can’t answer for you” you’ll have to find out for yourself whether or not you can believe in him.” Numbly, she nodded, and fell into a brief reverie’brief because she heard the opening of the front door, and it dimly registered in her head that Virgil was supposed to be coming home from Louisiana today. His footsteps trod lightly up the stairs, for his size, and down the hall to what the occupants of the house now referred to as “her” room. There, they paused, but only for a moment to observe that she was not there? and then he started towards Cyrus? sickroom. The door slowly opened, and both of them looked up in concert. Virgil, looking timeworn and exhausted, squinted slightly as he looked at them. No doubt all the sun had given his eyes a good bit of strain, but perhaps it was more than that’perhaps the deep strife had affected him as well. Cyrus started’Lia almost wouldn’t have noticed had it not been for the short gasp of pain afterwards. But he bowed his head. “Senor” You will forgive my rudeness, but my condition prohibits me from leaving my position. It is a pleasure to see you again.” Virgil had been about to speak, but he paused. It was obvious that Cyrus knew Virgil? and it was equally obvious that Virgil had absolutely no clue how. For Lia, it was another note in the symphony of discord that her soul had turned into as of late’another knot in the story to be untied before anything could make sense. The only thing that truly registered right now was that she was a long, long way from knowing the truth of it all. .:: ::: ::.
