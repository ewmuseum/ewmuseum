Vengeance (part 1)

17 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 17, 2004 Revenge. What would you do to get it? What lengths would you be willing to go, to seek retribution for your dead parents, or for the girlfriend that got raped? These questions could be asked to anyone on any day. The idea of vengeance is enough to blind a man, to make him so blindly stupid he would rush into even the craziest of things. But, once the deed is done, does it truly ever make the pain go away? When Little Billy grows up into Bill Smith and finds out that the man convicted of murdering his parents reads in the newspaper that the man is getting out of jail today on good behavior. Does it make him feel better, when he rushes to the hardware store after work gets a gun, and kills the man? Is the pain and suffering he will have to bear in jail for the next twenty-five years of his life be worth it? Or was it truly worth it for millionaire Bruce Wayne to invest all of his money into coming up with cool gadgets and vehicles to stop crime in his city and wear tight spandex all the time? I mean sure he got the chicks with the tight rubber suits but millions of dollars later, is revenge really worth it all? The time? The effort? The money? The sacrifice?” Ichiro and Jushin walked down the long streets of downtown Tokyo. It was their first visit back to the city since they had reinstalled their uncle Gohei, in charge of their fathers Yakuza Empire here. They walked down the streets, lost in the busy shuffle of the rush hour foot traffic. It was eight in the morning and people were rushing around, all of them in a desperate hurry to get to work on time. The two brothers walked in opposite pin striped casual suits. Ichiro dressed in a white suit with a black pinstripe, he wore matching white dress shoes, and it was complimented nicely with his semi-see through sunglasses. His brother however, was dressed in a black suit with white pinstripes, with black dress shoes and very dark Armani sunglasses. The brothers had come back here to relax, but they had found themselves in the centre of more trouble then a vacation was worth. After they were set-up by The Consiliu and the rest of The Echelon they were put into jail. They got out the next day, Christina McCoy had sent her best lawyer to bail them out, and they made sure that they were secure to leave the country for a few days, and back to their roots they came. As they walked the busy streets of the downtown core the bright morning sun reflected of the clear glass windows of the skyscrapers. It was sort of assuring, even though many things between downtown Dallas and downtown Tokyo were the same, it was refreshing to get away from their usual routine. The black Mitsubishi was a sight for sore eyes to Gohei. He was having a shaky time here, and the view of the car pulling through the security gates, that he viewed from his massive bay windows brought a huge smile across his face. “Yeah, uh I’ll call you back later on today,” he spoke into his small cell phone, before he flipped it and put it into his pocket. He then made his way towards his front door to greet his two nephews. As he opened the door he saw Ichiro and Jushin climb out of the small sports car. His two nephews smiled and gave a slight wave before they made their way towards their uncle. He immediately embraced both of them, forcing a smile as he did so. He was happy to see them, but nothing could make a man more uncomfortable then when hugging another feeling two pistols, custom crafted for expert killers digging into one’s side. He backed away slightly and smiled before he spoke. “It is good to see you two. What brings you here” Pleasure I hope?” Gohei spoke softly but confidently. The two brothers smiled and nodded in unison. It was good to hear a family members voice, they needed to make a plan and they needed to relax, the reason they were here was strictly pleasure. However, if business needed to be done, then they would gladly revisit their roots and deliver some good old fashioned business etiquette to all of Tokyo. Gohei lead his two nephews inside. The three men made their way inside and sat down in the well furnished living room of the mansion. Gohei pulled out some glasses and poured some drinks, he knew the brothers weren’t drinkers so instead of the gin which he poured himself, he filled their glasses with water. The two brothers reached and quickly drank their drinks, then they put their empty glasses back down on the mahogany table and began to speak. “How is business, uncle Gohei?” asked Jushin. He was excited to hear how things have been going with their father’s business since they had last been in Tokyo. “Well Jushin, I cannot lie things did very well at first. People were scared, my enemies respected me, there was no trouble. But, lately things have beginning to change. There is an outcry, there is more violence in the streets then ever. People are beginning to die, and even though it is not my men, it is not my men who are doing the killing either,” replied Gohei. “Well, what do you think it is?” asked Ichiro, piping up for the first time. “Well, I cannot yet say for sure. But I have heard many rumors,” Gohei said, his voice dwindled off at the end. It was enough to make the two brothers more then a little uncomfortable. They looked back and forth at one another, before looking back to their uncle. He was now seated in silence, and was staring down into his gin instead of at them. “What rumors have you heard?” asked Jushin, finally breaking the silence that seemed to have lasted forever. “I do not wish to trouble the two of you with such things. You are here to enjoy yourselves, to not be bothered to meddle in the affairs of the business, I’m sure things will get better,” Gohei spoke softly as he tried to avoid the question. The tint of doubt in his voice made the brothers think that he did indeed want their help. “What rumors have you heard?” repeated Ichiro. “Well, the last I heard it was an international affair,” started Gohei. “An American is beginning to push his way into the market. But from what I have heard, he is no mere street arms dealer. He has a large financial backing, not to mention enough manpower to occupy a small city.” Ichiro’s eyes focused and his mind began to race. He couldn’t believe it, could it be true? No, there was no way. It was a mere coincidence, it meant nothing at all. How the hell could the Echelon ever force their way into Japanese Yakuza territory. They were ruthless businessmen, powerfully lead, and financially backed, but what would possess them to enter a competitive market where death is often the cost of doing business? Why would they put themselves in such a mess? Ichiro did not know why, but as ludicrous as it sounded, he would have to make sure. It looked as if this would be more business then pleasure. Jushin saw the look in his brother’s eyes and it brought a smile to his face. Jushin knew what his older brother was thinking, he knew because he was thinking the same thing himself. How and why would TUE get involved in Tokyo? He did not care truly. The details meant nothing to him, they were more of his brother’s style. What he cared about was strictly business and taking care of business was what he and his brother did best. Jushin smiled as he saw a slight look of discomfort grow on Ichiro’s face. Ichiro never did like going into battle when it could be avoided. But such was not the case with Jushin, Jushin lived for it. He lived for the chance to deliver death. The chance to strike fear into the hearts of an enemy, before striking the death blow. They had come here for pleasure, but that was soon going to change. The Souls of the Black Lotus would soon be working the streets of Tokyo once again. To Be Continued
