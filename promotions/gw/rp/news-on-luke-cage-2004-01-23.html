News on Luke Cage

23 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/23/04 As was normal for Brick on a slow afternoon, he would surf the Net. Recently, he was concentrating on one of his current pursuits outside of wrestling: acting. He had taken a few acting classes here and there, and showed promise. One of his teachers gave him high praise after a scene reading from “Raisin in the Sun” where Brick played Walter Lee. Lately, Brick had been looking to snatch the male lead in a live-action movie based on the Marvel Comics character Luke Cage. Wandering onto the Comics Continuum web site, he found the news that may make him the happiest man alive. LUKE CAGE MOVIE Kevin Feige of Marvel Studios told The Continuum that a live-action Luke Cage movie is making progress at Sony and might be a 2005 release. 'Ben Ramsey turned in one of the best drafts that I have ever read,' Feige said during The Continuum's visit to the Marvel Studios offices on Tuesday night. Ramsey wrote and directed Love and a Bullet and also wrote The Big Hit. 'It's going to have a totally unique flavor from all of our films,' Feige said. 'It's totally contemporary Cage and a helluva lot of fun.' Feige said Cage's signature 'Sweet Christmas' is part of the film, but this isn't a retro-movie. 'All of the movies we've done take place in modern day, but it's about finding a hip and contemporary way to get in some of those classic references,' Feige said. A director or star for the project have not been announced yet. Look for more from The Continuum's visit to Marvel Studios all this week. This was good news to Brick, indeed. The chance of there being no star attached was music to his ears. This meant the possibility of the part being fair game. Of course, the article said that a star hadn’t yet been announced. It was a gamble, but Brick was known for beating the odds. Immediately, Brick reached for the phone and dialed a number. “C’mon, Aaron, pick up, man.” “Yeah?” “Aaron?” “Yeah.” “Listen, man, log on and then go to comicscontinuum.com.” “I’m already logged on. I’ll go there.” Brick waited a few seconds. “OK, Brick, I’m there.” “OK, now click on Wednesday, January 14, 2004 “Luke Cage movie?” “Yep.” “Man, are you still trying to get in that movie?” “Yeah. Why do you say it like that?” “Look, you’re my boy and I’ve got your back, but you’ve never really done any acting.” “I’ve done acting before.” “Just those classes. Besides, they’re probably gonna be looking for rappers to play the part, like LL.” “Maybe they are, but that don’t mean I’m just gonna give up.” “Fine. Good luck, man.” As Brick hung up the phone, he went to one of the closets in his apartment. Underneath a pile of clothes was a cardboard box. Brick opened the box and pulled out what appeared to be a comic book. It was a near-mint copy of the first issue of Luke Cage: Hero for Hire. “I gotta get this part.”
