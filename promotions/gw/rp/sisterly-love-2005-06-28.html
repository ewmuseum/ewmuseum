Sisterly Love

28 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 28, 2005 June 24, 2005; 1042 hours Ramirez Home; Cochella, CA Teresa's skull still rang from the chair shot. She placed a plastic pouch filled with a gel, suitable for freezing into a flexible icepack, onto her forehead as she lay back on her sofa. It would be another hour or so before Noi arrived... the first interview they'd granted the reporter, in their own home. They'd flatly refused to allow a cameraman, or even a camera of any kind, to be brought into the house... but they were agreeable to Noi writing out a description of the living room, even though they claimed the right to proofread said description before the article was published. The icepack gently cooled Teresa's forehead, and she sighed with pleasure. Twenty minutes later, she switched the icepack from her forehead to the back of her head... using a towel over the armrest of the couch to keep it clean and dry. She'd be on top of her game when it was time to square off in the Triangle Tag Match at Crossfire... for now, she was glad for the silence, and the cooling effect of the icepack. Her only regret was that the Enforcers had won their tag-team match with the Syndicate. It was Michael and Terelle who were responsible for the beating-- and the chair shot-- that made her need this icepack right now. Yes, they might have beaten down the Enforcers in order to come out to wrestle Teresa and her husband, Juan, that night on Evolution, but Ken Partridge Jr. was a former GW World Champion... he could fight his own battles. Teresa was only interested in exacting vengeance on the beating she and Juan had received... the chair shot that made her skull ring even now. Ring! The Syndicate would get their day of reckoning... it just wouldn't be at Crossfire. Winning the tag-team match had granted the Enforcers the right to square off against La Herencia de Herederos in the Triangle Tag Match... Michael Mercer and his new tag-team partner, Michael Ryba, as the new Absolutely Merciless, making up the third tag-team in the match. Teresa respected the ring prowess of Michael Mercer's cousin, Mindy, but she was glad not having to face her at Crossfire. Mindy's addiction to sugar and sweets was something Teresa hoped wasn't catching... especially to Teresa's young daughter, Melina. 'Melina would be 15 pounds overweight within a month if she ate like that... her finger would become too big for her favorite ring--' Riiiiiing! Riiiiiiiing! Teresa's head snapped up and she quickly sat up on her couch. Had she drifted off to sleep? Riiiiiiing! 'Teresa? Is anyone home?' 'I'll be right there!' Teresa called back hurriedly. Fortunately she'd placed her wrestling mask on the coffee table within easy reach. No need to run back to her room for it. Pulling it over her head, she tied the laces loosely and went to the door. No bad hair days with the mask on, she thought to herself. Only the last six inches or so of her long dark hair came out from underneath the mask. It made the need for hair spray non-existent. Teresa opened the door to her apartment and smiled politely at Noi Tsunami. 'Come in,' she said. 'Juan and Melina are due home any minute. They went to pick up some groceries.' Noi nodded pleasantly in return and stepped into the living room. 'Nice neighborhood, Teresa... friendly people here. And you have a nice place. Like I promised, I'll have the description I write, ready for you and Juan to read and approve within 24 hours. Nothing goes live until you agree.' Teresa was grateful for the reassurance. 'Please, sit,' she said, gesturing to the sofa. 'Real leather,' thought Noi as she sat. 'These two just might be Americanized, yet.' She pulled out her notepad and her favorite pen. 'So... I was wondering if we might--' The sound of the telephone distracted them. Teresa smiled apologetically. 'I'll turn the ringer off,' she said. Going to the phone, she glanced at the caller ID. 'Oh... it's Juan. Hang on a moment, please.' Teresa answered the phone. 'Hello?' 'Hi Teresa, it's me.' It was Juan's voice. 'We have a flat tire. I made it to the auto-shop, and a spare is being put on. I'll be home in another twenty minutes. Has Noi arrived?' 'Yes, just now,' said Teresa. 'You can start without me, then, if you like,' said Juan. Teresa shrugged to herself, and nodded. 'Okay,' she said. 'See you when you get back.' Teresa clicked off her phone and deactivated the ringer. Turning back to Noi, she crossed her legs and collected her thoughts. 'What would you like to know today?' she asked. 'As the fans get to know the wrestlers, inevitably questions arise about their families... what family life is like away from the ring,' said Noi. 'Some fans are interested in knowing whatever the wrestlers are willing to discuss, about their upbringing and what their families were like... how the families are affected by the wrestlers becoming wrestlers.' Teresa nodded. 'I'm always happy to tell more about Pap?,' she said. 'His legacy is the focal point around which the tag-team and our own story revolves.' 'And it's a very fascinating one, at that,' agreed Noi, smiling lightly. 'But to see more of the bigger picture, today I'd like to see another spoke in the wheel.' Teresa's brow furrowed slightly, a slight smile on her lips... but the smile died in a heartbeat at Noi's next words. 'Tell me a little about your sister,' Noi said. 'When you said you needed to wish her happy birthday a little while back... that was the first time you really opened up about her. I'm sure the fans would like to know a little about her.' Silence. Teresa seemed to be staring a hole right through Noi's body... even the royal blue mask didn't hide the look in her eyes. Her breathing stayed at the same, steady rhythm, but her fingers were clenching together; her left hand becoming a fist. 'Curious... why is this such a touchy subject with her now?' Noi was a bit puzzled, although her face didn't show it. 'Why start openly speaking about her sister before, only to get annoyed when she's asked to tell more?' When she answered, Teresa's voice was carefully controlled; almost as if keeping a rein on herself. 'What would you like to know?' 'Well, to start, what does she look like?' asked Noi. 'Did she go to college, like you did? Does she have a family of her own?' There was something about Teresa's voice, the way she answered the reporter... Noi couldn't quite put a finger on it, but even though she really knew nothing about this sister of Teresa's... 'She's hiding something from me,' Noi knew. 'Almost as if she's... afraid of saying the wrong thing.' 'She's in a relationship,' Teresa answered. 'She holds a degree in sociology. As to what she looks like? A little more like Pap? than I do. She's just a bit shorter than I am... she has the same dark hair as Pap?, though she wears it longer... the same golden tan to her skin. The only thing keeping women's hearts from fluttering at the sight of her smile is that she's a woman herself.' She chuckled. Noi smiled politely, though a bit mischievously as well. 'So she smiles like Juan?' she teased. Noi caught herself at the sudden stern look in Teresa's eyes. 'Can't she take a joke?' Finally Teresa let herself give a soft chuckle. 'Juan smiles like... Juan,' she said. 'I'm married to the man; wouldn't it be only fair that I be a bit prejudiced in favor of him?' Noi chuckled in return. 'It seems that almost everything that made your tag-team, La Herencia de Herederos, what it is today... everything that led to your father's downfall... revolves around when that drunken-- what's the word you use? Gringo?' Teresa nodded. 'When that drunken gringo made a pass at your sister,' Noi said. 'And yet your sister didn't go into the wrestling business... so what's she doing these days?' Teresa exhaled a breath in a careful, slow rhythm. Again, there was that curious control over her voice... as if she was walking on eggshells when she answered. 'What is she afraid of?' Noi was asking herself. 'My sister's calling was... different,' said Teresa cryptically. She seemed ready to follow up, but then closed her mouth again. 'You likened your sister to your father,' said Noi, 'and it sounds like your father had a real fire in his belly... a real fighting spirit. So your sister doesn't? Is that why she isn't wrestling?' Teresa's jaw dropped slowly. Her voice was simply incredulous. 'Hear this story, Noi... and remember it well. Because I won't ever again let you challenge the notion about just how much a fighter my sister really is.' June 24, 1989; 1428 hours Mexico City 'Damn it, not again!' Teresa's sister ground her teeth as she saw the three girls forming a line at the school courtyard's gates. It was painfully clear to her, and any other girls milling about the courtyard, what the intentions of this threesome were. The leader of the three girls smiled sweetly, but condescendingly. 'Come here, La Zorra Azul... pay your toll to go through the gates and be able to go home.' The other two girls giggled at the taunt as Teresa's sister glared at all three of them. 'Out of the way, Rosita,' she growled. 'Who died and appointed you gatekeeper?' Rosita tapped her cheek as if recollecting. 'Ah, yes. I think it was Mr. Del Paso, three weeks ago?' Again the other two girls giggled. Mr. Del Paso was the custodian of the building, who none of the girls at the school really liked because he was old and homely, and he smelled bad because the water in his one-room apartment wasn't suitable for washing clothes... and he couldn't afford decent detergent. Four months ago he cut himself on a broken pipe he'd been trying to replace... and it got infected. He died three weeks ago, and the school probably wouldn't have adequate heating even come wintertime. 'Is that so?' Teresa's sister sneered. 'Show me a copy of his will, then?' 'You're hoping he left you something?' Rosita grinned sardonically. She turned to the girl on her left. 'Maybe a nice fancy mink stole and a diamond ring? Why couldn't he leave ME that, Nilda?' Nilda snickered. 'That's why we're collecting tolls,' she answered. 'Like from La Zorra Azul here.' All three girls gave that same infuriating giggle again as Teresa's sister took a few steps closer. 'I get the diamond ring, Zorra,' said Rosita. 'Now hand over your toll.' A frayed, dirty green backpack clattered lightly to the floor as Teresa's sister let it drop from her shoulders. 'Out of the WAY,' she warned the girls again. Rosita stepped forward and pushed her. 'This is MY area,' she said. 'I decide who goes through.' She pushed Teresa's sister again. 'And I say you can't pass.' She pushed her again. 'Now are you going to give me what I deserve?' She pushed her again. Or not, as her intended victim's hands snaked out with devious agility; her body twisting to the side as she grabbed Rosita's arm and threw her weight forward onto it. Both girls landed on the pavement, with Rosita's arm taking the full brunt of the impact as Teresa's sister landed on top of the forearm; holding Rosita immobile with a simple wrestling hold. Quickly the girl pulled back on the arm. 'Is this what you deserve, Rosita? HUH?' Teresa's sister demanded. 'Is this what you deserve? Are you getting what you deserve, HUH?' Rosita's plaintive cry was barely heard over the shouts of all the other girls in the courtyard who quickly organized into a semi-circle around the aggrieved combatants, cheering for a fight. 'Get her, Nilda! Get her, Carmen!' 128 pounds of weight came off of Rosita's arm as Teresa's sister clambered to her feet, readying to meet the oncoming rush of two more assailants. Leg muscles tensed like coiled springs; sleekly powerful calves propelled her into the air as her body twisted. A flying dropkick planted one of her feet right into each of the two girls's faces; Nilda and Carmen crying out harshly as the soles of their intended victim's sneakers raked across their mouths and noses. None of the girls in the semi-circle were cheering for Teresa's sister; although Rosita, Carmen and Nilda were the only ones brave enough to fight her... and them, only in a three-on-one. Many of the others had tried to pick fights with the daughter of Mateo Ramirez before... the girl who had left such a stain in the world of Lucha Libre... and they'd all been sent home crying, dirty and bruised. Teresa's sister vaulted to her feet, her dirty skirt swirling all the way up to her hips; giving a rather indecent view of her underclothing. The school couldn't afford to provide the girls jumpers, so the uniform consisted of a simple white blouse, a knee-length plaid skirt, white knee socks and plain but serviceable blue sneakers. She bent her knees slightly as Carmen came at her again, while Nilda tried to pull herself to her feet. Teresa's sister drove a stiff knee into Carmen's stomach, doubling the girl over with a harsh cry. Seeing Nilda clamber to her feet on the other side of Carmen, Teresa's sister bent backwards, laying her back across Carmen's and doing a vaulting roll over her body; her two feet arcing down into a double mule-kick that send Nilda right back down to the ground with a bloody nose. Teresa's sister landed on her feet behind Carmen, and she grabbed the squat but short girl around the waist, swing her up into the air and then down again; jamming Carmen's tailbone onto her knee. An angry shriek whirled Teresa's sister around; a second too late. Rosita grabbed her by the front of her blouse and swung her around in a circle, before flinging her against the gate. Rosita and Nilda were upon her a second later; each of them pinning one of their opponent's wrists against the gate with one hand, while pounding her viciously about the face and stomach with the other. 'Are you getting what YOU deserve now, Zorra?' Rosita screamed, as all the girls watching the fight cheered her on. 'Is your father coming to protect YOU now?' Calling Teresa's sister 'Zorra--' feminizing the word zorro, Spanish for fox, was insult enough... but this was just intolerable. It was a slip of the tongue that Rosita, Carmen and Nilda would regret for months afterward. Through the haze of her pain, through the increasingly queasy feeling in the pit of her stomach as it was punched repeatedly and unmercifully; Teresa's sister felt a crossbar in the gate graze her writhing fingers. Straining her pinned wrists upward, she grabbed hold of the crossbar in a death-grip. Shrieking with effort and pain, she used the crossbar to haul her body straight up; her legs arcing around and inward in a double inside-moon kick that bashed both assailants upside their heads... her calves connecting with their heads with such force that their skulls were knocked against each other's with an audible CLUNK. She didn't dare aim at Rosita's neck... she still had to pull the blow, and aim it across her shoulders and collarbone. The running clothesline still sent Rosita to the ground with a painful grunt, where she lay dazed. Carmen and Nilda were peppered with punches, kicks and knee strikes as the crowd tried to cheer them into a rally. But there would be no more fight left in Rosita's henchwomen today, as two dizzying judo throws pitched them in opposite directions like rag dolls; Carmen knocked completely cold by the impact of the gate against her back and head. Teresa's sister kneeled on top of Rosita, turned her over onto her stomach, and hooked her into a wrestling hold... a brutal bow-and-arrow that was magnified as Teresa's sister rolled onto her back, suspending Rosita in the air across her knees. 'Say it,' she growled. The cheering of the other girls watching the fight was quickly losing intensity. Everyone knew that the girl they all hated even more than Mr. Del Paso, wasn't going to be 'saying it...' even though a few clung to a fading hope that Rosita wouldn't, either. Rosita was crying... more from pain than rage. Her body quivered in her tormentor's brutal clutches. 'Say it!' Teresa's sister demanded again. The crowd was nearly silent as Rosita's face turned red... Carmen and Nilda were out of the fight... Nilda was crawling backward, toward where she'd left her book bag; clearly scared and trying to sneak away. She's Rosita's best friend in the whole school, and if SHE'S abandoning her... Rosita's plaintive wails were met with only a furious shout from Teresa's sister. 'SAY it!!' Tears of bitter shame trickled from Rosita's eyes as the pain from the hold she was locked in surpassed her will to fight. 'T’a Maria!' she half-yelled, half-sobbed. T’a Maria, or Auntie Mary, was the universal cry of surrender in the schools in this part of Mexico City. Boys in the boy's schools would yell T’o Jos?, or Uncle Joe. Relief flooded into Rosita's body as she was released from the wrestling hold. Teresa's sister got to her feet and spat disdainfully on the ground about a foot away from Rosita. A button or two had been torn from her blouse in the altercation. Mateo and Lupe couldn't afford new buttons, so she scanned the courtyard carefully to find them. At least, they had some thread, so the buttons could be sewed back on. Not one of the girls in the courtyard, including Rosita and Carmen, dared to interrupt the search... Nilda had crept away and was nowhere to be seen. Finding the buttons and putting them in her backpack; Teresa's sister put it back onto her shoulders and trudged through the gate to the school onto the street; heading on her way home. Tears of anger welled in her eyes, because she knew what would happen tomorrow. Ramirez Home June 24, 2005; 1110 hours Noi blinked her eyes in puzzlement before a look of recall came to her face. She remembered Teresa's aggrieved description of her father's trial. 'So, the girls who picked the fight with your sister, ran to tell their teachers and the principal that your sister started hitting THEM for no reason... and they bought the story with no questions asked. Your sister got punished and they didn't.' 'You catch on quickly,' said Teresa in a bitter voice of complaint. 'She was kept in detention at the school for two hours the next day after it ended... and then suspended for three more days. The other girls were just comforted and reassured they'd be 'SAFE' now... although all the girls in school knew that the only reason they'd be SAFE was because none of them dared antagonize my sister again.' Teresa's chin lifted noticeably as she made this proclamation; a look of pride etched all over every inch of her face that wasn't covered by her royal blue wrestling mask. It shone in her liquid brown eyes; radiated from her voice. 'Interesting that you would tell me this story now,' Noi mused as she jotted down more notes. 'Today's June 24... that means it's 16 years ago, to the day, that this altercation happened.' Tears suddenly came to Teresa's eyes... she turned her face away and sniffled. 'Perd’name...' she said weakly. Noi leaned forward suddenly. 'Teresa, I'm sorry,' she said, feeling suddenly regretful. 'I didn't mean to--' Teresa made a small gesture. 'It's not that,' she said. 'My sister got back home from that fight just in time to see our dear Pap? being arrested.' Noi swallowed a lump in her throat. 'I'm sorry,' she said again. 'Okay, I think we can call it a wrap... I'll just ask one more question and we'll close the interview.' Teresa nodded and wiped her eyes. 'What is it?' 'The one thing you and Juan never volunteered about your sister,' said Noi. 'What's her nam--' '¡Mamacita, nosotros hemos vuelto!' called Melina cheerfully as the door was unlocked and opened. (*Mommy, we're home!) Teresa got up and smiled as she kneeled down to give her daughter a warm hug and a kiss on the cheek. Melina and Juan each carried a grocery bag in their arms; Melina put hers down to accept the hug from her mother. 'Welcome back,' said Noi to Juan as he closed the door again and put his keys on the coffee table, next to Teresa's geode. 'I'm afraid you missed the interview... we just wrapped for now.' She grinned. 'You wore that mask to the SUPERMARKET?' Juan chuckled. 'I put it on just before we came into the house,' he said. 'Nobody at the supermarket would have recognized me for who I was.' Noi nodded and put her notepad in her tote bag as she watched Juan go up to Teresa and give her a peck on the cheek and a hug. Her brow furrowed slightly. 'I've given more passionate hugs and kisses to boys on our first date,' she thought. 'So you got all the information you were looking for?' Juan asked conversationally. 'Yes, we were just talking about your sister-in-law,' Noi said matter-of-factly, starting for the door. A clatter of metal cans perked up Noi's ears, and she saw that Juan had dropped his grocery bag. 'Darn,' Juan muttered. 'I'm sorry. That was clumsy of me... fortunately, nothing broke. There were no glass containers.' Noi chuckled uneasily. 'Well, thank you for the interview, Teresa,' she said. 'I'll let you and Juan look the article over before it's printed.' Juan nodded a little curtly. 'Thank you,' he said. Noi exited the house and closed the door behind her. As she walked toward the gate-- Juan and Teresa lived on the ground floor, so they had a small patio in front of their house-- she heard the sound of raised voices. Noi carefully eased toward the window and glanced through it... the draped had been opened to let in the sun. She could see Juan and Teresa arguing... Juan was angrily accusing Teresa of something, and she was trying to defend herself. 'What's THAT about?' Noi's brow crinkled as she walked through the gate toward her car. 'What's going on with the two of them now?'
