Date: Week of 6/17/2005 An Ounce Of Prevention Is Worth A Pound Of Cure -Benjamin Franklin The Internet anonymity was an amazing thing to behold. Everyone knew some of the popular stories about drugs sold over Ebay in plain view, sexual encounter and everything else. Of course, with such great anonymity there was also the opportunity for great evil to take place behind the shades identifications of screen names. Such was the message board that arranged all of the weapon deals Jake used. Thanks to the more liberal use of the Patriot Act, any suspicion could quickly reveal who he was. Whenever dealing with this particular dark corner of the cyber world, Jake used a throwaway computer using an open Wi-Fi connection that so much of corporate America didn’t secure. If the heat ever came, he would disappear, never known. The same information went for the money he used. Always untouched by his hands, there were no fingerprints or D.N.A. that could be used to track him down because he had intermediaries collect cash from one of the clubs. The first email contained the price, which of course ange5red him beyond anything else. They were demanding a premium, but he decided it was better to pay the premium and not be bothered. The weapons would be delivered to a drop point while the money was held by a runner he used through yet another intermediary. The young man would wait for a call from a prepaid cellular phone before he handed over the cash. Everything was very selective, secretive and overprotective, but Jake valued his security procedures. Ten miles off the main road in the back woods he moved over the broken landscape in an F-150 that was left in Dallas by a friend. Between the two trees he spotted the packages. He parked beside the larger tree, looking around at the landscape to ensure no one was monitoring the scene. A mask over his face hid his face from recognition. Satisfied there was no remaining surveillance he walked over to the three boxes. He opened the first carefully, and found two automatic pistols inside. They were 9mm for him. The extra clips were there along with the ammunition. The second box contained two sets of throwing knives, and a machete in it’s sheath. He pulled the machete out and looked it over. As requested, the weapon was silver plated and had some symbols carved into the metal. Finally, the last box contained a few special items for him. There were even written instructions for the flammable items. Jake loaded the three boxes into the aluminum storage chest in the truck. He locked the chests down, ensuring no one could inspect or steal his new gear. Jake returned to the truck, and left the scene. Before reaching the road again, the mask went away too. He had one more stop before he would be ready to get back to the house. Maybe it was the CSI effect. Jake had read an interesting article about some court cases around the country. Apparently, jurors were less likely to convict on circumstantial evidence when there was no physical evidence. Mainly, they were expecting an experience where the crime lab could and would find anything to put the criminal away. Real life was infinitely less complex. Officers finding someone at the scene didn’t process everything like on the show. Shrinking budgets meant not every test possible was run, and so the real life crime labs were never as good as the ones on television. Jake wasn’t taking any chances though, and so his stop was at a car wash. Jake paid for the Deluxe Package, and walked inside as his truck was immediately put into the wash thanks to no one else being around at this time of day. Jake paid in cash, and also bought a Diet Dr. Pepper to sip on as he watched the machine erase any and all physical evidence that might have been on the outside of the car. He might have something on his shoes, but he was about to head to the bathroom to fix that. Gulping down the last of the beverage, Jake walked into the bathroom. He brought his first foot up to the counter by the sink and cleaned off the bottoms. There really wasn’t anything on them’but no chances. He repeated the procedure on his other shoe then dried them both off. Satisfied, he returned to the waiting area to watch the workers hand dry the truck. Jake dropped a five-dollar bill into the tip bowl as he returned to the truck. A short trip later, the boxes were in his trunk and his friend’s truck was back where it belonged. No one had seen him, but of course he didn’t know if that was the case. He was just being careful, because he had to under the circumstances. . . . . . . .
