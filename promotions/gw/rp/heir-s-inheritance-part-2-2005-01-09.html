Heir's Inheritance, Part 2

9 January 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/9/2005 (OOC: All dialogue translated from Spanish unless otherwise noted.) May 8, 1988 1025 hours 'We don't need you anymore, Ramirez,' said the promoter, although Mateo knew damn well what he meant. 'None of us ever TOLD anyone in that fucking cantina who we, or I, was!!' shouted Mateo to the promoter's face in desperation, making the promoter involuntarily cringe back, knowing that he could not stand up to the now-former All-Star Light-Heavyweight Champion in an altercation, at least not without using the same 'never hit from anywhere other than directly behind' tactics as those four gringos who beat Mateo to a pulp in the cantina fight. 'It doesn't matter,' said the promoter, completely unsympathetic. 'He said he recognized the fighting style of your VALETS.... as if you had any business teaching your two girls how to WRESTLE in the first place, in a complete violation of the code of machismo! Those drunkards said they would have backed off and left as soon as they were done, all those two chicas had to do was keep your dirty little secret instead of playing LADY Galahads and none of this would have happened. Thanks to THEM--' 'You mean thanks to YOU,' growled Mateo. 'You truly expect me to believe that my daughters ASKED you to give me the can?? Or how about that stupid reporter who I will see in COURT--' 'The reporter doesn't matter either,' shot back the promoter. 'You know the media types, they print what they see. Are they sharks? Damn right they are... so it's up to YOU to hide what you don't want them to learn, such as the fact that this handsome man's two daughters wrestle just like El Zorro Azul. Once that was made public, along with the photo of your face without your mask, the fans now walk out of the arena whenever you wrestle, and that's if they come to the arena to watch any other matches! The fans stop paying us because they don't want to see YOU anymore. What would you have us do? Clean out your locker, Ramirez, and don't call us... we'll call you... maybe.' June 24th, 1989 1456 Hours 'The job has been filled.' ::click:: BUUUUZZZZzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzz.............. Mateo had gone through the classifieds for three entire newspapers... in just one day. It was getting to be a tiresome routine for the handsome ex-Lucha Libre star. 'Lupe... come here, please.' Mateo's wife balked at approaching him. He'd changed in the past four months... he'd started to drink. His behaviour was more erratic; more abrasive. It wasn't his fault, but it was making him harder to live with. 'Damn it, come HERE woman!!' Mateo snapped. He grabbed up a fistful of crumpled letters and telegrams all containing an assortment of legal threats. 'PAST DUE,' read one. 'COLLECTION IMMINENT,' read another. 'YOUR SERIOUSLY DELINQUENT ACCOUNT CONCERNS US,' read a third. The Ramirez family had exhausted their supply of lenders and any other means they had accessed to pay the regular minimal costs of living, with zero income. Her lip trembling, Lupe walked over to Mateo gingerly. He thrust out the phone to her and tapped the final ad with a perspiring finger. 'Call them,' he said tersely. 'Pretend you are applying for the job.' Swallowing a lump in her throat, Lupe dialed the number. It rang twice... three times... 'Hello?' answered the voice of a somewhat middle-aged-sounding man who graduated from the school of hard knocks. 'I am interested in applying for your position, sir,' said Lupe, squashing down her trepidation into a lump at the bottom of her stomach, as hard as steel. The man's voice sounded like he was smirking. 'YOU wish to apply for this position?' he said dubiously. 'You sound too pretty to spend your day shoveling burro manure--' 'My children are hungry,' Lupe snapped. 'Either I SHOVEL burro shit or stop my children from fighting over who gets to EAT it just to fill their stomach so they won't be hungry.' The man sounded like he was wincing. Poverty in Mexico City was that oppressive for some people. He could tell that this woman was no shanty dweller or squatter; just on the basis that she was able to call him on a telephone... but her voice carried enough desperation that it seemed she would end up there soon. 'I am sorry, Miss--' 'Juarez,' said Lupe, giving the man her maiden name. 'Maria Juarez.' This wasn't a lie either, since Maria was her middle name. 'Address?' Lupe gave him her mother's address. Paychecks could be sent there if necessary, and while almost everyone now knew the street address of Mateo Ramirez, almost nobody knew where his mother-in-law lived. Lupe and her mother were never overly close but tried to be there for each other when truly necessary... and this would work to their advantage in giving out an address on job applications. 'Date of birth?' Lupe froze. Her mouth opened but she was silent. 'Date of birth, Miss Juarez?' It was their downfall. This was one thing Lupe had to tell the complete truth on. 'February 11th, 1947,' said Lupe, again crushing her trepidation down into the dark recesses of her body until it formed a steel-hard lump. A brief silence before the man's voice asked to be excused for a brief moment. Lupe could hear an older man's voice speaking to the man she had been talking to, telling him something, but she couldn't make out exact words, both because they were talking in hushed tones and apparently the man was covering the phone's transmitter with his hand. The last part of the conversation she could make out clearly, however, and it was painfully obvious this was intended on the part of the people she was talking to. 'She's desperate,' said the younger man's voice. 'So are these people,' said the older man's voice. 'And they came here in person.' 'I apologize, Miss Juarez,' said the younger man to Lupe, in a tone that didn't sound sorry at all... or at least the apologetics in his tone were as phony as iron pyrite. 'The job has just been filled; I don't know what to--' 'You are a LIAR,' hissed Lupe indignantly, understanding every bit of anguish and desperation her husband was experiencing. A slight sound told Lupe the phone was being grabbed away from the man. 'The job has been FILLED, Miss Juarez,' shouted the older man, 'and I CHALLENGE you to prove otherwise to any judge's satisfaction. Good Day.' ::click:: BBUUUUUUUZZZZZZZZzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzz Mexico's population wasn't that big to begin with... not many of its residents could boast married couples where husband and wife were born on the same day, date, and year... February 11th, 1947. Mateo and Lupe Ramirez were both born on that date and year. Add to that the timing of Lupe's call, and the employers had caught onto the ruse. Mateo spat out a series of Spanish vulgarities and threw his half-finished bottle of tequila against the wall. Several roaches scurried away from the impact. The Ramirez family once lived in a beautiful apartment in one of Mexico City's trendy spots. As their financial situation became worse and worse in the year and one month since Mateo was fired from wrestling, so did their living arrangements... until now they were staying in a two-room slum-like apartment with a leaky roof... an unpleasant insect problem... and water they didn't trust for drinking or bathing unless it was boiled. Grabbing up his frayed summer windbreaker, Mateo stormed out of the apartment and started down the stairs. As he reached the middle of the lobby he felt an unwashed hand drop down onto his shoulder. 'Where is your RENT, Mr. Ramirez?' said the landlord/slumlord in an irate tone. 'No more excuses, if I do not have all 50 pesos by the end of the month--' The excited yip of a Chihuahua broke through the dialogue before it could truly begin. The dog grabbed Mateo's pants leg in its mouth and started to shake it. The chuckle of the dog's six-year-old owner drifted in from a few feet down the hall. 'What, you don't hide behind a skirt now?' taunted the boy. Children in Mexico treating their elders in such a manner was unspeakable... and Mateo was not going to let this dog ruin this pair of pants. A blood-curdling yell issued from the anguish-stricken man as his hand clamped around the dog's neck and he yanked it off his pants leg, swinging it up in the air until it was level with his neck. The dog snarled and twisted its jaws, trying to bite Mateo's hand... until a pitiful yelp followed the sound of the heel of Mateo's other hand slamming into its nose with frightening viciousness. Mateo flung the wounded animal against the nearest wall and stared at the landlord with a look that made him scramble back out of reach. The six-year-old boy wasn't as wise... children so young seldom are. 'Pepe!' shrieked the child, as he rushed the haggard man who had done this deed, raining punches and kicks on his shin and thigh. The blows of a six-year-old on the body of a 42-year-old man, still well-conditioned from the wrestling career he once enjoyed, tend to be the same as hitting him with a pillow... but the injury to his pride was far more severe. A sudden shove with Mateo's left hand flung the child back... he tripped over a loose floor tile and fell down... not hurting himself badly, but of course, young children crave attention when they feel even the slightest pain. This one was no different, as attested to by the crying and carrying on he commenced at once about the 'terrible monster that hurt him.' Even Mateo himself would never dream of speaking half the profanities spewed out at him by the child's mother, chasing after him out onto the street as he proceeded on his way to a bar. Then the mother's eyes alighted on two tall men coming around the corner. 'Police! Police!' Like most of the people that the Ramirez family had to deal with over the past year, these officers hated Mateo... hated the insult to the code of machismo he had committed; hated the dire insult of he being photographed without his wrestling mask... hated that their young children mourned so bitterly that their hero was now a pariah. Anything and everything he said, that wasn't drowned out by the young mother's anguished screeches, fell on very-much deaf ears as he was shoved to the ground and handcuffed... and touched twice with a cattle prod when his resistance to arrest became a bit too energetic for comfort. The officers scooped him up and radioed for a police car to haul him down to headquarters. 'Papa! Papa!' Mateo could hear a young woman's voice screaming to him. A girl in her late teens was running toward him, tears in her eyes and her school uniform scuffed up with a button missing from the blouse... one of her eyes had a slight bruise mark on it. The other girls in the school had probably picked another fight with her and found out the hard way just why she had been able to fend off four adult drunkards in the cantina side by side with her older sister. 'No, stay back, my child,' groaned Mateo. 'Papa!' cried the girl again, before a harsh look from one of the officers halted her in her tracks. His hand almost casually drifted down to his baton, and there was no doubt he would use it-- on her if he was given an excuse. Mateo's daughters were no better-liked in most parts of Mexico City than the former Zorro Azul himself. The car pulled up to the broken curb and the officers roughly loaded Mateo in, before getting in themselves to bring the mother's statement in for processing. The plaintive cries of Mateo's daughter merely echoed and re-echoed in the distance, competing with the car siren's unmerciful wail on the city street that was only one step above the lowest depths of despair found in the shanty towns. 'Papa! Papa! Paaaaaappppaaaaaaaaaa!!'
