A Sheepish Prophecy

6 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 6, 2005 He had underestimated her and ended up being embarrassed. The only comfort he had from the tragic loss was the sweet revenge he had obtained after the bell had rang. He would have a chance at redemption this week and victory would be even sweeter when he stood above the three bodies of his competitors with Global Wrestling’s Television Championship raised high above his head. He would not allow a single one of them to win; he needed this more than they did. His need was what had attracted him to the abandoned dojo where he had once trained under the eccentric Mao. The dojo was dark, dank, and highlighted by the sounds of Mike Biddle grunting as he punched and kicked at an old punching bag that dangled from the ceiling. He had been at it for hours and his entire body glistened with sweat and his precious blond hair was damp and matted down to his skull. The rusty chain that connected the bag to the mildewed ceiling began to squeak louder with every hit until it finally snapped from the force of a stiff kick from Mike. The kick sent the bag sailing a few inches in the left, causing a collision with the wall that sent plaster dust everywhere. Mike stood in place with his hands placed upon his knees, his mouth wide open as he desperately gasped for air. He was covered in white dust from the collision, making him appear as a ghost. As he stood there collecting himself he didn’t notice a mysterious figure that stood behind him. The figure cleared its throat, a sound that startled Mike. “To what do I owe the pleasure of this visit Michael Biddle?” Asked the raspy voice of the figure. Mike turned around and came face to face with the figure; he rolled his eyes when he noticed that it was Mao himself. Mao was clad in blue silk robes that were covered in various Asian characters that Mike’s brain could not even begin to comprehend the meaning of. “I’m just doing some training before the Television title match,” Mike began, “what the hell do you want?” Mao thoughtfully strokes his gray Fu Manchu mustache as he took in Mike’s question. “I think I might ask you the same question Michael Biddle,” Mao said, “it seems to me that you claim to not want my guidance but yet you always seek me out in your moments of doubt.” “I do not,” Mike said with a defensive tone. “Oh I’m not going to play your childish games Michael Biddle,” Mao said with a smile, “I know that you’re here because you fear losing yet again.” Mike’s brow furrowed in anger. “I came here because I wanted a quite place to train,” Mike impatiently said, “I figured you’d be dead and wouldn’t bother me.” Mao laughed loudly at Mike’s comment. “I know why you are here Michael Biddle,” Mao said, “come, follow me and I will show you what your future holds.” Mao motioned for Mike to follow him; Mike rolled his eyes and followed. Mao led Mike to a small room in the back of the dojo. Inside the room was a circle of lit candles and within the circle was a small altar on which a dead lamb rested. Mike watched in both amazement and disgust as Mao kneeled before the altar and began to cut into the animal in a ritualistic fashion. Mao made a cut into the gut of the dead animal and pulled out the slimy intestines. He then placed the intestines in a circle and squeezed blood from the animal into random places within the circle. Mao set the animal down then began to look at the mess he had made on the altar and smiled as he saw what lay within. “What the hell did you do that for?” Mike asked with an incredulous voice. “It’s the only way to tell the future,” Mao answered in a bemused tone. Mike sighed heavily; he had come here for training not for a cheap carnival sideshow. “What does it say then?” Mike asked, he hoped to speed things along so he could leave and put as much distance between himself and this freak as possible. “It says that you are the master of your own destiny,” Mao answered, “and the only way you will lose is if you defeat yourself. You must enter the match with confidence, not cockiness.” Mike rolled his eyes; it was the same drivel each and every time he met with Mao. As Mao turned to look at him Mike faked a polite smile. “Thank you Mao,” Mike said in an appreciative tone, “I’ll be sure to keep that in mind when I’m at the arena. Now, if you’ll excuse me I have stuff to do at my house.” Mike didn’t wait for a response; he turned and left Mao alone in the room. Mao smiled as he watched Mike leave. “I hope for his sake that he takes my advice for once,” Mao said under his breath. When Mike left he knew that the match was his to lose. He was the closest thing that Global Wrestling had to a Television Champion and he wanted it to stay that way. He was happy since he knew that it was now only a matter of time before he regained what was once his. -The End-
