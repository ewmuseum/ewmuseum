And Thus it Begins

9 January 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1-9-05 A distant clock strikes twelve; its booming tolls announcing the slumber of a town. The ringing reaches a small park, barely illuminated by the moon above. The moon shimmers in a creek, which slowly creeps through the park. In a particular area, a quaint wooden bridge stretches across the creek, connecting two separated halves of the park. A lone soul occupies this bridge. As the rest of the town sleeps, she watches the water move over the smooth stones; she listens to the noises that only a creek could make. Her body is tired, she is physically exhausted. Yet her mind races, a surplus of thoughts passing through her mind. A woman of such a small town would never be expected to have such thoughts. Women of her stature are not even expected to think. She bent the rules thousands of times, became a social outcast, and spent less time in this town as time went on. Now she had a chance to leave. This is the freest time she has been able to manage in the last few days and she plans on taking advantage of it. Her last few hours in town may as well be in her favorite spot, at her favorite time. A cool breeze brushes against her pale face; she closes her eyes to embrace the breeze. The clock finally ceases its disturbance and the woman opens her brown eyes to take in the world in which she lives. A world she has grown to despise with every fiber of her being. Tomorrow will mark the first day of her new life; she will leave her family and town behind. She will leave behind their “love” and “care”, without remorse. Everyone has lost respect for a woman of her situation. The woman moves over the bridge and into the grass. She hugs herself to shield from the cool breeze. The tight red tank top and the sheer black netting she wears barely protects her from the cold. If she had known she would be coming to the park at such an hour, she surely would have brought a jacket. Today marked her last day of training before she moved on to something bigger and better. Her coach has been pushing for her to graduate for months now, stating that she has been more than ready to leave and has definitely surpassed the others in her class. At this point, she could easily train her peers. She is fully prepared to take on any challenge that may come her way. Her coach has given her so much advice, that it is hard to keep it all straight. Therefore, she has resorted to repeating it in her head constantly. A crash of thunder announces an oncoming storm. The woman glances up at the once star-filled sky in disbelief. She slowly begins to trudge back to her car, her exhausted form too lazy to move any quicker. A light rain begins to fall upon her. She runs a hand through her raven hair, pushing it from her eyes. As she nears her car, the rain begins to fall harder. In mere seconds, her clothes will be drenched. She can already smell the wet earth all around. Completely drenched, the woman sighs as she retrieves a key from the back pocket of her black cargo jeans. She slips the key into the lock and unlocks her car door. Looking around her one last time, a faint smile crosses her blackened lips. She snaps back from her thoughts and enters her car. While starting the engine, she looks back out at the bridge and smiles. This is her last night in hell. This rain must wash her away from the past and into her future, because tomorrow, she will finally achieve her dreams. She will even leave her old name to let the world know her as someone else entirely, Aradia Xiomara. All the stages were set. Tomorrow begins a new life and new recognition. Sitting in the blackened car, Aradia looks around her again. The rain splatters its drops onto the car, falling in a million streams around her. The wipers begin their rhythmic beat, wiping away the streams of tears. She breathes in this silence and exhales one more inner demon. She opens her blackened eyes to the world once again. Glancing to the mirror, she takes in her new appearance, smeared with beads of rainwater. Her blackened eyes drip down the side of her face, mixing with the red blush on her cheeks, and then swirling into a blood colored mess that drips from her jaw. The black shade of her lips parts to a sea of white, and she laughs before turning on the radio. Aradia pulls out of the parking lot, wishing the bridge, the park, her town, and ultimately her life farewell. The rain begins to thump against the car as she pulls out onto the main road and away from the park. Her car becomes a mere dot and as far as the sleeping town is concerned, she is dead. Aradia Xiomara, however, is just being born.
