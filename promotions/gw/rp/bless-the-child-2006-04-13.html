French Manor 798 CE [conversations in the past are assumed to have been conducted in Old Czech or Old French] A white rose withered in the moonlight as the dark shadow of a black bird passed over it. For a few moments, it wholly disappeared, and then the moon shone down again. It looked the same as it had before. Just an illusion of the oppressive night-time. Lia Radilu Vlaszy-Martel peered out the window of the manor-house. The child shifted in her stomach, as a cold fear gripped her. She remembered- it seemed so long ago. She had been back on the family manse in Zdice, sitting on her grand-mama’s knee. Jalena Vlaszy had told her grandchildren all sorts of fantastical stories, but Lia was the one who loved them the most. She would go to her grand-mama, still surprisingly smooth-skinned after all these time-worn years, and beg for her to tell stories, over and over again. Jalena had told her grand-daughter of a legend. A legend that had never left her. “But grand-mama, why would the crow do something like that?” Jalena shook her head, the tight steel-gray bun not moving as she did so. “The crow is a trickster, little one. He seems as though he would not hurt us, but that is why he is so feared.” “But mama never fears the crows in the garden.” Jalena smiled at the obstinate child, looking down into Lia’s little wonder-filled eyes. “Ahh, but mothers have the most to fear of all. There is a legend in Zdice that any mother who looks into a crow’s shadow when she is almost to term with her child will experience nothing but misfortune afterwards until?” “Until when grand-mama?” Jalena paused, not wanting to scare the child. “Never you mind, Lia. Go play outside, hm” Grand-mama has to go help mama and the servants with the cooking.” Lia shuddered, remembering the story. “Until the child dies.” A coldness overtook every fiber of her being, but then there was something that she couldn’t afford to ignore- her water broke. Her stomach churning, she stumbled for the door of her bedroom, hoping Aric was within hearing distance in the sprawling house. “ARIC!” One of the servants quickly ran down the hall at hearing his mistress yelling so loudly. He looked at her, leaning in the doorway with such wild eyes, one hand on her stomach. “What’s wrong Mistress Lia?” She panted, looking at him. “Go get Aric, Jacques” hurry.” “Is it?” “Yes, hurry.” Jacques ran down the stairs as fast as he could, calling all along. “MASTER ARIC! IT’S TIME!” .:: ::: ::. French Manor 805 CE Lia smiled out on the large manor-yard, watching the children play. The large dogs she had brought with her from Zdice, Damek and Jirina had recently produced a litter of pups. Angela was frolicking on the grass with at least two pups chasing her, while one of the smaller pups was relentlessly licking Brennan’s face. The nursemaid with them was giggling, as was Brennan. Angela’s shrieks of delight permeated the air. Lia turned her head back down to her work. At the moment, she was chopping up vegetables for a stew- for once, she decided to give Chantal a night off from the kitchen and do the work herself. It didn’t sit well with her forcing the servants to do everything- back in Zdice, her family had helped alongside the servants often. She presumed it was because the women of her family simply did not like being idle, but she didn’t know what the real reason was. In any case, she had to keep reminding herself that this was France, it was not Zdice, and things were done differently here. Out in the yard, she heard the cry of one of the children and then some accompanied sniffling. “Angela,” she thought to herself. She dropped the knife hastily, cutting her thumb in the process. She winced, but simply pressed it into her apron as she slipped out the side door into the garden. She darted over to the nursemaid’s blanket, where Angela’s big green eyes were filled with tears. “Appolonie, what happened?” Lia reached over, cradling the little girl in her arms. Even at her age, Angela still occasionally allowed her mother to hold her so. “It’s nothing, Madame, just a little scratch. She got too rough with a puppy, and Jirina bit her.” Angela held up her little hand to her mama, and Lia looked it over. It was red, and bleeding a little bit, but Lia smiled. “You have to be careful with the babies, Angela. Jirina doesn’t like us to be too rough with them. She’s scared we might hurt them.” She kissed her daughter’s thumb, and Angela’s sniffling started to die down. The little girl coughed slightly, looking up innocently at her mama, and as Lia looked down at her in her little white dress, her heart almost stopped. A red splotch rested on the front of the chest, still moist. Her grip tightened inadvertently. Angela squirmed. “Mama, I want to go play again.” Lia let the little girl go. “What is that spot on your dress Angela?” The little girl looked down at the spot and shrugged, impatiently wiggling in place for a moment. Her mother looked away and Angela took that as a sign that she was free to play again. Lia looked at Appollonie, alarmed, and for a few moments, there was a fearful silence between them. “I’m sure she just wiped her thumb on her dress, Mistress Lia.” Lia nodded. But for a first-time mother, that was empty assurance. She feared the worst. .:: ::: ::. Where have all the feelings gone? Why has all the laughter ceased? Why am I loved only when I'm gone? She tried to move, but felt simply rooted to the spot. She forced herself forward towards the front door of the house while even Aric was frozen in place. “NO! Not my baby!” The cart-man turned from the door. His eyes misted slightly, and he tried not to look down at the little child in his arms- seven years old and dead of plague. The woman rushed towards him, her black hair spilling about her shoulders in a way that was most undignified for a woman of her station. Her husband looked down, the bridge of his nose pinched in between his thumb and forefingers. Lia’s eyes overflowed with tears as a sudden desperation overtook her. She clutched his arm, looking into his eyes with a wild look, half in the real world, and half drifting around somewhere else. “Please, not Angela, not my baby.” The cart-man looked away. “There isn’t much to be done for this little one anymore, Madame.” “But there has to be” there has to?” She lost her control. She let go of the man’s arm, and simply wept. The little Angela he held in his arms was not the Angela that she knew, not the Angela that she had given birth to. The plague had taken her so quickly that it had seemed like only a split-second. P’re Almandie had come to give her the Rites only two days ago when the family physician had said there was no more to be done. Agony split her in half. How was she supposed to let go? It was her fault, all her fault. If only she had done more for Angela? A strong pair of hands took her shoulders from behind, pulling her gently away. The cart-man left, waiting until the door of the manor-house was shut before laying the child’s body gently atop the many more plague-dead he had collected today. Lia looked up at Aric, her lower lip trembling, wondering why he had pulled her away. But she saw the futility in his eyes. And then she wept, letting herself go as he wrapped his arms around her. It was as though there was suddenly a great gulf between them, as though the little soul that had just departed this earth had been the most concrete object holding the two of them together. .:: ::: ::. Lia stood in the family graveyard, dressed all in black, her thick black hair pulled soberly away from her face. She clenched the rosary in her fingers as the priest intoned the Kyrie in a wavering baritone. She felt like every word of the Mass was tearing at her sanity. This was impossible. She was never supposed to go before Angela did. She was supposed to see Angela’s children born, supposed to sit Angela’s little girl on her knee and tell her all the old Czech and Magyar legends that Jalena had told her. “Kyrie eleison” Christe eleison?” Her stomach turned. Why should anyone be begged to have mercy on a little girl? Why shouldn’t God have had mercy on her when she was still alive? The reluctant attendants put their hands to the ropes that would lower the tiny carved wood casket into the ground. They were all family servants- family servants with tears in their eyes. The little coffin descended slowly, and Lia’s heart was rent in two as she thought of the little figure inside with the flowing blonde hair and the green eyes just like her mama’s. A cold hand squeezed her heart in a vise-grip. “Ashes to ashes” dust to dust?” This was the end, wasn’t it? She couldn’t concentrate, but found herself instead in the grip of a relentless terror that pulled her in first one direction and then another. It was only the cruel thudding of the dirt on the casket that snapped her out of her reverie. A scream welled up in her chest, and she barely trapped it- it came out as a strangled sob. She wanted to throw herself into the grave- someone had to keep Angela warm at night? someone had to take care of her. It was Lia’s job. Her muscles failed her. The mourners were starting to walk away and Lia’s knees collapsed under her. She reached towards the grave, but it was as though there was now a yawning gulf between mother and daughter. It dimly registered that Aric’s brother had placed his hand gently on Brennan’s shoulder, and had started guiding him away from the grave. Brennan was crying, his little blue eyes filled with confusion. Lia clawed frantically at the ground with one hand, clutching her rosary with the other. Chokingly, she prayed, the beads pressing painfully into her skin. “Zdr’vas Maria, milosti pln”, P’n s tebou; po’ehnan? tys mezi “enami, a po’ehnanŷ plod “ivota tv’ho, Je’i”. Svat? Behind her, Aric’s ragged voice interrupted. “Lia, we should go.” She didn’t want to go. As she pushed herself up from the ground, she looked down to see her hands bleeding from the hard imprint of the rosary beads. .:: ::: ::. Where have all the feelings gone? French Manor December 806 CE The hardest thing for Lia was knowing that she stood in the small graveyard with the purpose of saying goodbye. She had pulled a flower from the tree nearby, and had laid it numbly on Angela’s grave. Tomorrow she would go back home with her kinsmen, far away from France, far away from Angela’s grave... far away from Brennan and Aric as well. Aric had been drinking away his sorrows since Angela’s death. Lia had barely been holding herself together. And then the letter had come from her kin, from the powerful Vlaszy of Zdice. Her duty called, and she had to come home to serve on the estate. It was time for her to take the blood oath- she had put it off much longer than most of the other servitors in her clan, but now her domitor had made the decision that she was to come home and prove her fealty. She had no choice, and telling Aric of it would have been ludicrous- he never would have believed her. She looked down at the grave of little Angela almost hatefully. She bore no malice towards the child, but perhaps the anger that seethed within her was directed at a more heavenly source. The church’s God had cursed her, and the life that she now lived was nothing but a dim spectre of what it should have been. Anger surged, and she tossed down the rosary. It tangled as it hit the ground and she turned away, stalking towards the gate of the graveyard in the direction of the house; she was going to see if her servants were done packing her things, and if Damek and Jirina were prepared for the trip. A hacking cough interrupted her reverie, as a feeble watery voice called out to her. “Madame, you dropped this.” She spun around, her eyes narrowing. And old woman, one of the serfs that tended the graveyard, was holding up the rosary to her. How long had the horrid old beast been watching? She shook it off, speaking up in her heavily Czech-laced French. “No, I meant to leave it there.” The old woman stood and looked at her for a moment. Lia felt as though the woman had looked straight into her soul. “You can’t have given up hope so early in your life.” Lia shook her head, folding her arms over her chest and attempting to keep back the angry tears. “I’ve nothing left to hope for.” “Perhaps if you pray to God, he will show you how you can still believe.” She shook her head. “There is nothing to believe in. God has taken everything which was important to me and left me with nothing.” The woman shook her head, as though she could fully understand that Lia was completely exaggerating her claim. She thrust the rosary at Lia. “Without innocence, the cross is only iron, hope is only an illusion?” Lia bit her lip and took it back, her hands shaking. She didn’t want to hear that right now. She turned and bolted from the graveyard. .:: ::: ::. Zdice 807 CE The old house seemed unwelcoming now. Its eyes peered out on her as though she was a wayward child, scolding her for ever leaving. Her head hung low, and her body jostled with every rocking motion of the carriage. The door was opened by a familiar looking family servant- Jarek. “Welcome home, Miss Lia.” The moment surprised her. Were they not even going to acknowledge that they had forced her to leave the husband that she had been so in love with, that they had forced her to leave her baby boy? Weren’t they even going to acknowledge that she wasn’t a Vlaszy anymore? That she was a Martel? “Lia! Come in the house, we have much to discuss!” Her father’s head poked out of a window as he saw her reluctance to step out of the carriage. Damek and Jirina romped with the other dogs as though they had never left. The three pups were grown almost to full size now and were submitted to the scent-check by the other dogs of the Vlaszy manor. Everyone seemed impatient to get back to business. Lia bit her lip and resolved not to show her sadness. She had a duty to fulfill. The Lord had called her home, and like it or not, she had to prove her fealty and serve as it was her duty to do. All that changed however, when she stepped into the house. Jalena Vlaszy stood a few steps back from the rest of the family, watching her grand-daughter. Lia looked around, dizzied by the assortment of people standing around to welcome her home- her younger sister Anezka, her twin brother Lazlo, cousins, servants, her parents, and of course grand-mama Jalena. That was when Lia lost it. She ran to Jalena first of all, nearly bowling the old woman over with the force of her embrace. She was suddenly engulfed in tears, and the family was deadly silent. Through her sobs, she tried to speak. “Grand-mama” I saw the crow’s shadow? it was just like you said?” Her sorrow started to overwhelm her, and some of the servants looked away in respect or discomfort. Lazlo’s eyes were glued to his sister. Her grandmother gently stroked her hair, and the family waited patiently. She could not be presented to their Lord in this state. “Shh?” Lia could suddenly think of nothing but Jalena’s story. Only the legends were wrong- Angela might have been dead, but the shadow of the crow’s wings still tainted her life. She had to wonder how long their deadly shadow would stain everything- if she had only known how cursed she was, she might have availed herself of the opportunity to fling herself into Angela’s grave. .:: ::: ::. With a concentrated shove of the back of her hand, she wiped off her face and licked off the blood. A scowl graced her lips. She slowly pulled herself out of bed, shaking off the disorientation that came every evening when she woke up. With motions just as deliberate, she walked over to the mirror, looking herself over, looking deep into her eyes. There was nothing within their icy greenness that suggested weakness, or even humanity. If there had been, she quite likely would have attempted to purge it with an ice pick to the face, after a daymare like that. She cleared her hair away from her face- and that’s when she saw it. Or rather, her. In the mirror was a flash of blonde hair. Out of the corner of her eye once again, she saw the flash of wheat-colored hair, almost fading into the wall. Shadow, with lightning quick reflexes, flung the dagger at the wall, where it hung in the plaster. Childish laughter was the only answer to her attack. Shadow’s face twisted into a mask of fierce anger. “Show your face, you little bitch! I’ll cut you in half with my bare hands!” Nothing.
