'Consequences and repercussions: the truth will not set you free.'

22 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 22nd, 2005. Consequences and repercussions: the truth will not set you free. ---- 11:11 AM. July 22nd, 2005. SoHo, NY. The two college students sat at a table in 'the cellar door' coffee shop, enjoying a lazy summer afternoon with a frappuccinno and sweet reminiscence of the school year, as they enjoyed the last days of a hot July in SoHo. 'The cellar door' was a classy place, or as classy as a coffee shop can be. It prided itself on great coffee, great service and great notoriety. The notoriety came from celebrities that frequently visited the cellar door for a quick cup of joe, before returning to their busy lives. But it wasn't just the celebrities that this quaint little coffee house managed to snare, but members of all different walks of life. The majority and the minority, their customer base was far and wide, ranging through many different races and creeds, a shining example of the melting pot that America is. With a cozy interior of wood floors and brick walls and gentle acoustic guitar riffs playing in the background, the cellar door cast a warm and inviting face towards the newcomers and served to please the returning customers as well. As Jeremy - the emo kid on the right, decked out in a pair of canvas black and white checked vans, tight Ralph Lauren jeans and a 'Vote for Pedro' t-shirt, his outfit complete with poindexter glasses - took a sip of his iced chai tea, he smiled over at Gregory, his longtime friend and school chum. Gregory - sporting his emo roots with a 'new found glory' t-shirt, faded jeans and black flip-flops, topped off by big bulky earring in each ear - returned his friends smile, his fingers curled around the cup of his frappe, drumming the sides thoughtfully as he listened to Jeremy speak. '...and then mother was like: Jeremy, if you don't get a haircut you can't come to grandmas for thanksgiving! You know what I told her? I told her that grandma sucked anyway.' , Jeremy said with a giggle, vainly playing with his shoulder length blonde hair. 'You said that grandma sucked? That's hilarious!', Gregory chuckled back, a smile covering his face. Both of these young men, while incredibly vain and self-absorbed had a lot of potential. Each of them was handsome in their own strange way, although their silly emo fashions and Gregory's dyed red hair probably didn't help things, yet in spite of their shortcomings they were both quick witted, intelligent and rather observant, perhaps that's why their eyes caught the man at the counter as he turned away from the long marble top and walked towards a table, where he took a seat. The man they observed had the tendency to stick out in a crowd, due to the nature of his appearance. Shaggy black hair, ending at his collar, streaked with shades of silver and blue, his eyes were a brilliant shade of bluish gray, alive with a twinkle of mischief and a dazzling spark of rascality. His physique, while short and fairly thin, was well built, a solid 200 pounds of muscle stacked onto a 5 foot 9 inch frame, adorned in ragged jean shorts, black etnies, a white t-shirt featuring the 'greyhound' logo on the chest, a slight rip in the left sleeve and a silver earring in each ear. He had the perfect choice of accessories in a white 'Nintendo: know your mushrooms' wristband on his left arm, and am i-pod, the earbuds plugged firmly into his auditory apendages. 'Hey, not to cut you off, dude, but is that...', Gregory raises his brown eyes to the table, which our snickers mocha drinker had taken a seat at, directing Jeremy to turn in his chair and take a look for himself. 'You're right, it is...', Jeremy answered, a wide eyed expression of shock covering his face as he smiled in surprise. 'American Youth' Josh, minus the gold but with all the glory, sat at a table facing the emo boys, they were a bit surprised and excited to see the famed star of Global Wrestling in such close capacity, and being longtime fans of the Wrestling industry they were without a doubt; starstruck. ---- 'Cruise the strip, gotta look real cool! Turn up the base, act like a fool!', screamed the lead singer of Calibretto13, his voice breaking through the earbuds of my i-pod, with the simple press of a thumb I paused the song, muting his artistic expression for the moment. 'Cruise the strip' by Calibretto13, is a great song but at the moment I wasn't in the mood for punk, I wanted something with more of a bite to it, more kick, more edge, something along the lines of Subseven, Project 86, Underoath, or everyone's favorite Swedish Christian rock band; Blindside. Scrolling through the list of artists on my gleaming i-pod - the lights reflecting from its silver base - I searched for Blindside, yet as I sat there in silence I heard my name mentioned in an excited tone, I was all ears from that point on, retiring my i-pod to the table and leaving it on pause, as I focused intently on what was being said about me. Oddly enough I love hearing what people have to say about me when they don't think I'm listening. 'Dude, he's not that big in person...', said an unimpressed emo kid with a shock of artificial red hair. 'He's not.', his blonde friend with nerd glasses commented, before chipping in: 'He kind of looks like a jeans model, skinny and cute.' 'Did you just say he was cute?', the redhead chuckled, teasing his friend. 'For a guy! I mean c'mon, don't you think so?', the embarrassed colleague responded. 'Whatever you say, wiener wrangler.', nerd # 1 - the redhead - answered. 'Please, I didn't mean it like that. I meant that he has that look that girls dig, a weird rugged sexuality about him, even you could pick up on that Mr. Homophobe.', nerd # 2 - the blonde - retorted. 'I'm surprised he's so small, no wonder they put him in matches with women!', the first nerd laughed, taking a sip of his coffee, my eyes raising slightly to study the unusual duo. 'Yeah, I'm sure he'll have a lot to brag about, first he brags about holding the title for 184 days, now what's next? He beat two women to win the Cruiserweight title!', the second nerd chuckled, obviously enjoying the smug feeling of breaking down a celebrity, all the while thinking that I couldn't hear him. 'Maybe we should go talk to him?', the redhead suggested, his actions saying more than his words as he rose from the table. 'Yeah, maybe we'll get an autograph...and then we can eBay it!', agreed the geek in poindexter glasses. I took a sip of my cold snickers mocha, which couldn't have tasted any better on this lovely July afternoon, the temperatures soaring well into the hundreds, resting at a toasty 102 degrees. As the dark liquid rushed past my lips, over my teeth and soaked into my tongue before sliding down my throat I couldn't help but smile, my taste buds rejoicing at the visit from the brilliant concoction. As I sat my cup on the table I noticed the emo boys standing in front of me, I glanced up and removed the earbuds from my i-pods to acknowledge their presence. They were slow to speak, the redhead taking the lead, as he broke the silence. 'Hi, I couldn't help but notice that you're Josh and my buddy and I just wanted to say hi.', he smiled, before throwing in: 'My name is Gregory.' Hi.', was my only response, accompanied by a nod. 'Dude, don't forget me, I'm Jeremy.' poindexter boy added. 'Good for you.' I said with a nod. 'Yeah, we're fans.', Gregory said, his uncharacteristic smile spreading across his face from ear to ear. 'Really?', I said in sarcastic shock, my features sporting a visage of mock surprise. 'Yes, really.', Jeremy added. 'Oh, and we like Ken too.' 'And Gunner.' Gregory chuckled as he spoke. 'We like Gunner a lot.' 'A lot.' Jeremy confirmed, sharing a smile with his partner. 'So dude, can we have an autograph?', a beaming Gregory inquired. 'What's the going rate for a Josh autograph on eBay these days?', I asked, a mischievous smile playing at the corners of my mouth. 'What are you talking about?', Jeremy asked, trying to look surprised and innocent and failing miserably at both. 'Look, I heard your conversation. I'm not giving you an autograph because quite frankly I don't have the time for you, but allow me to give you something even better.' I began in a blunt manner, their eyes opening wide with anticipation. 'Advice. That's what I'm going to give you both.' Their eager faces suddenly turned to an expression of dismay as I began my lecture. 'Grow up, get rid of the nerd glasses, the big earrings and the silly t-shirts. Listen to some decent music and please, please...stop whining. I'm sick of emo, the world is sick of emo.' I ranted, targeting them with my left index finger for emphasis. 'Throw away all of your Dashboard Confessional albums and listen to something that doesn't suck, stop posing and deal with things as they come. Sure, life sucks sometimes and I have some crazy ways of dealing with things, but wearing clothes, acting metrosexual to impress girls and listening to whiny little girl music isn't the right response.', their eyes widened to a bizarre width as I spoke. 'Get over life, get over yourselves.', I ended my speech, lowering my hand onto the table. 'Dude, don't hate us for being different!', Gregory protested, seemingly shocked. 'I don't, because you're not different, you're nothing more than a carbon copy of a million other emo kids who dress like geeks because they saw Coheed and Cambria do it. Grow up, both of you.' I stated bluntly. 'Screw this, jerk.' Jeremy snapped, spinning on his heel and walking away. 'I hope you get your butt kicked by one of those girls...', Gregory mumbled as he retreated quickly. With a victorious smile and the flaws of the emo lifestyle exposed I returned to the task at hand, which was finding a good Blindside song, and as I reached Blindside on my artist list, I selected 'Sleepwalking'. 'Wake up she called, screaming like an alarm! Are you trying to wake me up? Now whats this you're trying to pull, is it my arm? I do believe that you're trying to wake me up! Now could it be maybe, you saw me sleeping, could it be maybe you awakening... As the lead singer paused, the guitar kicking into the chorus, I felt a hand tapping my right shoulder. Pausing the music I jerked the earbuds out of my ears and stood up, spinning around, snapping at the annoyance before I had even fully turned. 'I thought I told you to go find a life?', I snapped before turning to face...a uniformed member of the NYPD. 'Actually, you didn't, but we have a few questions to ask you about yours.', the boy in blue said with a smile, staring at me over a pair of aviator glasses, I didn't like him already. He had a square jaw, blonde hair, cut in a short flat top and a heavy gun on his right hip, the holster unstrapped. 'You have questions for me?', I asked, rather surprised at the sudden shock of seeing a police officer in front of me, and another flanking him. 'We do.', he said with a smile. 'Can we step outside?', I asked, depositing my i-pod into the left back pocket of my jean shorts and scooping my drink up with my right hand. 'Why?', the second cop, a chubby mick with a round face and a black buzz cut inquired. 'Because it's easier to run outside?', suggested the first officer. 'No, I've already had enough problems with my dirty laundry being aired in the eyes of the public and I would rather avoid any more details of my personal life leaking into the media, you know how they twist things.', I stated, turning my eyes to the door. 'Did they twist that story about you knocking up Rosa Padilla and then running out on your love child?', the second officer asked with a grin, safely remaining behind his partner. I twitched, just barely flinched in anger, the veins in my neck bulging as I bit my tongue. Turning I took a step to the door, trying to compose myself. 'Please...lets just go outside.' I led the way and the fuzz followed me, step for step, until we were on the sidewalk outside of 'the cellar door'. Glancing around, I took a seat on a bench in front of the coffee shop, as the officers remained standing. 'You have a question?' I spoke in more of a statement than am inquiry as I looked up at them. 'Questions, plural.', the mouth second cop retorted. 'Great.', I remarked sarcastically. 'Do you know Howie Branner?', the first cop, the older and obviously more professional of the two asked. 'The radio guy? Not really, I did one show with him, it didn't go so well and I haven't talked to him since.', I said, half of the statement true and the other half a complete and total lie. 'It didn't go well at all, in fact...you tried to beat him into a bloody pulp, didn't you?', the mouthy cop sneered. 'Is this legal?', I spat out, glaring at the second cop, as he was beginning to get under my skin. 'Very.', my least favorite cop responded with a smug grin. 'I wonder if my lawyer would agree to that?', I asked, as I extracted my cell phone from my right pocket and flipped it open. 'Wait, there's no need to get any lawyers involved, we'll just ask a few questions and then be on our way, okay?', the first cop said, attempting to diffuse the situation. 'Fine, I'll cooperate.', I sighed, glaring at the annoying member of this legal tag team. 'I was going to lay Branner out, yes, but I never even touched the guy.' 'Someone did.', the first cop spoke gently. '15 days later, someone laid more than a hand on him, they played the drums on his mug, messed him up pretty bad.' I shrugged. 'Yeah? I heard he was mugged or something, resisted and got slapped around.' 'His wallet was still on him, he wasn't wearing jewelry, the only thing they wanted to take was his life.', the second cop blurted out, his voice blunt and cold. I shot a piercing glare in his direction. 'It wasn't me.' 'Oh? Well, we have reason to believe otherwise.', he stated, looking into my eyes as I prayed that he couldn't somehow tell that I had lied through my teeth. 'Howie Branner got knocked around and messed up, he was in bad shape for quite a while, actually spent the better part of a week and a half in a coma.', the first officer said, a slow scowl covering his face. 'Gee, it's too bad I didn't know this sooner, I would've sent him a get well soon card.', I smiled sarcastically at the blue as I said those words. 'Howie woke up two days ago...he only remembered a little bit about the incident but he says that you were the one that attacked him.', the second cop, spat out in accusation. My expression was one of fear, yet thanks to the mastery of mind games and charades I've managed to learn as a professional Wrestler, I turned that look into one of shock rather than dismay. 'What? That's ridiculous!' 'I don't think so. You've got a grudge and a history of being violent, when you put two and two together...', the second cop, stated accusingly, before I cut him off. 'That's theory, where's the proof? I gasped in anger. 'Josh, we just want to ask where you were between the hours of 10:30 PM and midnight on June 26th.', the reasonable policeman asked in a soft tone. 'In bed.', I answered without a second thought. 'You were in bed between 10 and 12? Please, everyone knows that you're a party boy, I don't think anyone is going to buy into the belief that you were in bed between 10 and 12 on a Sunday night.', said the 'bad cop' a smug smile over his ugly face. 'Yeah? Well, jackass, who said I was in bed alone?', I shot back violently. 'In that case, we'll need the name of your alibi.', the 'good cop' stated politely. 'Her name is Casey.', I said softly with downcast eyes. 'Casey? Come on Josh, help me help you...what's her last name?', the kind boy in blue asked, placing a reassuring gloved hand on my left shoulder. Glancing up at the second officer, I smiled, smugly, arrogantly. 'Branner.' His eyes opened in shock, as I repeated myself. 'Casey Branner.'
