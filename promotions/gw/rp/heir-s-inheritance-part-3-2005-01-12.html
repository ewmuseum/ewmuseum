Heir's Inheritance, Part 3

12 January 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/12/2005 (OOC: All dialogue translated from Spanish.) I am just a poor boy, Though my story's seldom told. I have squandered my existence For a pocket full of mumbles, Such are promises... March 14, 1992 1820 hours 'Teresa!' called a young woman's voice from the front doorway as it opened. Teresa looked up and smiled at her sister as she came into the rented one-bedroom apartment they shared. The younger Ramirez took off her coat and hung it up, leaving her clad in her waitress uniform. This job, plus Teresa's job as a receptionist for a small moving company, paid their bills and let them live in somewhat better conditions than they had had to endure for three months prior to their father's arrest. Both Ramirez sisters were bitter that this arrest, and prison sentence imposed on their beloved father, was what allowed the two of them to finally obtain work that paid for a clean and sanitary apartment, even if only a small one-bedroom model. They clocked in at the required time, clocked out when allowed to, and came straight home to cook and eat dinner, and then either watch television or dance to some salsa music on the radio, until it was time for bed. They didn't socialize with anyone from work, or with anyone in the neighborhood. Both of the young daughters of the once-beloved Mateo Ramirez, although both drop-dead gorgeous young women, lived as near-recluses together in their shared apartment, and outside of each other, both had very few friends. Of course, those few friends were very close, and one of them was VERY close to Teresa... All lies and jest! Still, a man hears what he wants to hear And disregards the rest... 'Madre de Dios, where did you get that???' stammered the younger Ramirez sister she looked on the new ring Teresa was suddenly wearing. 'Is that---?' Teresa's boyfriend came in from the kitchen where he had been hiding, and put his arm around Teresa, smiling warmly. 'Yes, it is,' he said. 'Your sister has agreed to marry me.' A stifled cry of gushed elation was all the girl could manage as she threw her arms around Teresa first, and then her fianc?. 'This is wonderful news! When?' 'October,' said Teresa, and it was crystal-clear why this month was chosen. Mateo was due to be released from prison on the first of September. By holding the wedding in mid-to-late October, there would be time for father and daughters to get re-acquainted and for Mateo to meet his future son-in-law... and for Mateo to walk his beloved Teresa down the aisle. In the excitement, the younger sister had forgotten to go through the day's mail, which she carried in her hand. 'Here's the mail,' she said, leafing through it. 'Electric bill... advertisement... and THIS goes right into the waste bin.' Teresa didn't need to ask her sister what she meant. 'No, give it to me,' she said. 'I don't know why, but tonight I'd like to read it. I won't answer or acknowledge it, but I'd like to read it.' Teresa accepted the envelope from her sister and slit it open with a butter knife. Reading over the letter, she suddenly frowned. 'She knows,' she said, looking pointedly at her fianc?. When I left my home And my family, I was no more than a boy In the company of strangers, In the quiet of the railway station, Running scared... 'I swear I didn't write to her,' said the handsome young man, shaking his head in a decisive negative. 'I have never even met your mother, much less told her I was dating you.' Faced with the prospect of finding herself forced into such utter depths of destitution that she and her children would be living in a cardboard shack on a street of mud in the squatter settlements on the outskirts of Mexico City, begging for food and finding their only water source muddy and unclean, Lupe had divorced Mateo shortly after he was sent to prison, and married a rich, arrogant, elderly man who looked on her as little more than a piece of ass. It was clear he did not truly love her, and wanted her only for her body and to flaunt another 'possession' under the noses of the 'wealthy' society. Lupe knew this very well, but was entirely unsympathetic to anyone who condemned her over her marriage to him... including her two daughters. The Ramirez sisters had moved out of the mansion six months later, finding work wherever they could to support themselves in a small but clean and comfortable apartment. They had not spoken with their mother in eighteen months; leaving her to enjoy the money and gifts she was given as 'payment' for the sex she gave her husband when he was in the mood. As far as the two young girls were concerned, their mother had sold her soul for money, and was living the life of a full-time, live-in whore... something they wanted no part of. 'With the money she has now, she must have contacts,' said Teresa scornfully. 'Maybe even one of our friends.' 'It's very possible,' said Teresa's fianc?, who himself was only a blue-collar chef at a restaurant that made a living but was nothing truly outstanding. 'If it's someone I know from work, you can be certain I will be looking for a new job the next day.' Teresa responded to this affirmation with a grateful hug and kiss on the cheek. There was a reason she loved this man. He loved her for HER, despite her former status as a social pariah simply due to her birth and family. 'Can you believe what she's saying in this letter?' she asked incredulously. 'She wants to know if we're getting married soon... if I want to have my wedding dress pre-paid for and delivered... and if she's going to be a GRANDMOTHER soon. Like the PIMP she shares her bed with doesn't have many of his own, from just as many girlfriends.' All three of them laughed. 'Okay, how about we have ourselves some supper?' asked Teresa's fianc?. 'You're going to love my arroz con pollo and the delightful flan for dessert.' Laying low, Seeking out the poorer quarters Where the ragged people go, Looking for the places Only they would know. September 1, 1992 1119 hours 'This is our home, Papa,' said Teresa proudly as she opened the door with the key and led Mateo in. 'Just a little over a year now, we have been living on our own in this apartment.' The smile on Mateo's face was but a fragile husk of the vibrant smile he flashed for the women fans as El Zorro Azul... the killer smile that could melt the heart of any woman... before the news article that made any woman's heart freeze cold as ice just at the mention of his name. 'It's very nice, Teresa,' he said genuinely, proud of his two children for the accomplishment, but barely able to show the enthusiasm his dear children remembered him for just a few years ago. Teresa's smile looked almost mournful as she gazed into her father's still-beautiful brown eyes. 'You can stay with us as long as you like, Papa,' she said, trying to make him feel welcome, at home... something he surely had not felt in over four years now. It was a feeling he desperately missed, a feeling he hungered for voraciously deep in his heart... a feeling that had been ripped out abruptly and then denied him most cruelly until now. Teresa wasn't sure he even remembered how to take that feeling back into his heart now that it was finally being served up to him in a heaping portion, after so long a period of deprivation. Asking only workman's wages I come looking for a job, But I get no offers-- Just a come-on from the whores On Seventh Avenue... The light toot of two party noisemakers turned Mateo's head toward his younger daughter, and his soon-to-be son-in-law. 'Welcome home, Papa,' said his younger daughter, the other half of his pride and joy... if only he hadn't trained them how to fight as well as he could. 'I am so honored to finally meet you, Mr. Ramirez,' said Teresa's fianc?, shaking hands with gusto. 'Teresa and I are both eager to see you walk her down the aisle at our wedding.' Mateo's other arm was filled with his younger daughter, who hugged him like he was a long-lost doll treasured over all other dolls in her collection. Her eyes told in no uncertain terms the unbridled joy she felt just being held by her father again... and she knew with a little tender-loving care, he would soon be a vital, robust man and caring father once more. Teresa looked at Mateo, almost appraisingly. 'We saved many of your clothes,' she said, pointing to the nearest closet. 'You look like many of them will still fit you. It will save you from having to buy a whole new wardrobe. I've talked to my fianc? and he says he will try to see if any of his contacts will consider hiring you. Hopefully within six months you will be back on your feet, and we will all be a happy family again.' Mateo smiled weakly. 'Yes... happy...' I do declare! There were times when I was so lonesome, I took some comfort there. They all knew that Mateo was still missing Lupe, even though she walked out on him to sell her body into what her two children considered live-in prostitution in order to keep herself out of the squatter settlements. Part of his heart still belonged to her, even if she shut hers to him. 'We will let you nap if you like, Papa, before dinner,' said Teresa. 'We've been planning a big dinner to celebrate today and your being in all of our lives again. It means so much to us.' 'I am happy to be able to see you again, Teresa,' said Mateo genuinely, hugging both of his daughters again. There was pure love in his arms... but something else as well; something his two children couldn't quite put their fingers on... They led him into their bedroom and plopped his duffel bag onto Teresa's bed with a heavy plop... unusually heavy. 'Have a good siesta, Papa,' said his younger daughter. 'We'll wake you for dinner.' As Teresa closed the door, her fianc? hung up Mateo's light summer windbreaker. As he placed the hanger onto the closet's pole, a piece of paper fell out of the jacket's pocket. He picked it up curiously and glanced at it. 'Teresa...' Then I'm laying out my winter clothes And wishing I was gone, Going home... Where the New York City winters Aren't bleeding me, Teresa and her sister looked at the tired, haggard handwriting on the paper... the sweat stains darkening it... their eyes widened in terrible fear; heightened by the firecracker-like noise that reached their ears. A neighbor's sister was a new mother, and the neighbor liked to discharge small firecrackers for celebrations... 'That was no firecracker; it was a GUNSHOT!!' Teresa's fianc? was racing like a madman for the bedroom door. Leading me, Going home... Mateo lay in a small pool of blood at the foot of Teresa's bed, a .25 pistol in his hand. A single bullet hole under his jaw dripped dark droplets of blood into the slowly-growing pool. The bullet had gone straight up into the center of his brain. The screams of his two daughters as they threw themselves onto his limp body were completely unintelligible; their sobs, dripping salty tears to mingle with the blood, were choked with immeasurable grief. On the floor of the living room lay a sweat-darkened rectangle of plain notebook paper with the following written on it: In the clearing stands a boxer, And a fighter by his trade. And he carries the reminders, Of every glove that laid him down Or cut him, till he cried out In his anger and his shame! 'My dear children, Don't make the mistakes I made. Never forget family always comes first, and sometimes the best way to say yes, is to say no. I didn't say no to you so many years ago. I pray you follow my words, not my example. Love, Papa' The words of the late Mateo Ramirez were no consolation to his now fatherless children, sobbing inconsolably over his cold, stiffening corpse, holding and hugging him tightly as if this would somehow keep the life force trapped inside it. 'I am leaving, I am leaving!' But the fighter still remains... Ly-la-ly! Ly-la-ly-ly ly-la-ly, ly-la-ly! Ly-ly-ly-ly ly-la-ly, ly-ly-ly-ly-ly! ...... The Boxer, Simon & Garfunkel, ? 1970
