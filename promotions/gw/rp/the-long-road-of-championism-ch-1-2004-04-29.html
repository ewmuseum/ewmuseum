The Long Road of Championism (Ch.1)

29 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-29-04 Prologue. It’s true in the wrestling world that gold will make a difference upon how you act and are seen in others eyes. Beyond the logical nonsense of rambling that people did backstage before events, a wrestler had a lot on his plate to display as a champion. When you saw the World Champion of a company, take Global Wrestling for instances, you thought of that person as the top contender of them all. It wasn’t as if the man would go to the ring and be shot down by some rookie as easy as 1-2-3, no he would fight and show why he was the champion. That’s why wrestling was found to be unreal in the fans eyes. It wasn’t normal for one person to always win, and when they did it became boring to watch. So companies began to throw in some stories and told their members to do this and that, because why? It drew ratings. The technology of today has made wrestling a whole knew profession, something many would watch for the entertainment, instead of the athletic performances. Although it was watered down by the entertainment, the wrestling of today had not lost all of it’s roots. Some of the most EXTRAORDINARY men still wrestled with their hearts. One of those men was Jesse Jamester, ‘the kid’ as he was formerly referred to. He proved it when he won the Global Championship, a match that didn’t have a determined winner or some whacked out story leading into the big match. It was simply four of the top best wrestlers going at it, and Jesse proved he was the best when he pinned Apocalypse after hitting the Jwister. Yet deep down, he ached, not because of the match, or because of the pressure that was now on his shoulders, for those two things he could handle. Yet this aching pain was deeper than both of those things together. It was deeper than his personal problems of being a on-and-off alcoholic, and even deeper than the blood in his body could travel. This aching came from the fans… Whether they were the most loyal in the world or not, he felt their position on the matter of Jesse’s next title defense. Not a single one of them thought he’d win in the six-way ladder match. With names like Josh, Hessian, Blake Wolfson, Iron Lung, and Todd Dream, the odds were definitely stacked. Yet was this not the man who proved he could beat Chris Storm, the proudest technical wrestler of this generation? Or how about the 7’ monster Apocalypse who dominated in power and size over Jesse? And though not the biggest, strongest, or technical wrestler, he also defeated the woman who proved to show the wrestling world she could hang with men in the squared circle, Patti Anderson. So what was all this bull shit of doubt about? Maybe it was a lack of respect, or just motivation on Jesse’s part to feed the match to the public. However one thing would definitely not be forgotten when in his mind… That determination he used to win the title. That’s all he needed… so he thought. Story begins. It’d been a beautiful day in Calgary, Alberta, Canada, one of the finest in months if my memory served me correct. The sunlight burst on my face like fresh water hitting dirt as I stepped out of my black dodge truck that had four by four drive. Walking across the newly paved street road I entered a gym that had a title ‘The Canadian Dungeon’ which was famous for it’s big time lifters who took immense amounts of pills to get ahead. However, I wasn’t there to lift, I was going to do an autograph signing for some local fans. Being my hometown I was a large asset to the communities overall view to the United States and the public eye of Canada. As I entered the locker room I set my stuff in my locker and locked it as I went back out to the table that’d been set up for me to sign autographs. Large piles of pictures with my face on them were in boxes under the table and a pile on the table with a box of permanent black markers. I sat down and as the doors opened the fans poured in. From the place I entered the building, you could never imagine that so many people were waiting outside. The first person was a little teenage boy with brown hair that went down past his ears and was growing longer. I leaned over and shook the kids hand, he was about thirteen and looked to be in the brightest of moods as his eyes sparked open when he saw me. ’Hello, who do I have the pleasure to be talking to?’ The boy was shy at first, maybe instinct held him back from speaking, but within moments his mouth opened and he finally mumbled his name, ‘Aaron.’ ’It’s nice to meet you Aaron. You want to become a professional wrestler?’ Nodding his head yes, the boy was sure of it, as a smile of agreement overcame his frail face. I signed an autograph and then told him I’d take a picture. Stepping to the side of the table the kid handed a camera to one of the assistances at the gym. Taking a good two snaps the kid was happy and as he shook my hand he looked into my eyes with the most blissful face I’d ever seen. ’You’re my hero Jesse,’ he whispered before he turned and left. My eyes fixed a gaze on the small boy as he walked out of the gym where he met his father outside. He didn’t know it but those words had made my day, and possibly made me forget everything that had gone wrong in my life up to this point. I could tell Aaron was happy because as most children do, he pestered his dad showing him all the things he got and explaining the whole experience in detail. I sat back down and the next person came up to get an autograph. It lasted all day, I’d been there for near an hour and forty-five minutes of meeting people so I stood up and told one of the assistants I’d be taking a fifteen-minute break after these few autographs. Sitting back down I smiled as four teenager boys walked up, all four being over the age fifteen and definitely showing a passion for wrestling as their clothes displayed such. Leaning over I shook their hands, and smiled as they one by one said their names ’Jake, Dave, Mike, Alan,’ as I signed their autographs for them. ’You’re going to annihilate those other six man, I’m sure of it!’ stated Jake as he shook Jesse’s hand. ’Yeah, no way will those five punks beat you,’ followed Mike who shook his hand following Jake. The other two agreed, and I just smiled, because honestly I didn’t know if what they said was true. A ladder match with six guy in it was like trying to fight someone who was invisible. You didn’t know where they were at, and so anything from a cheap shot to an unexpected strike could take you out. Nodding to the four as they left I stood up, my legs were getting really tiring from sitting down for so long, and so I stood up and stretched them as the assistants told the people I was taking a break. Though disappointed I knew many wouldn’t leave, because something like this came by only a small percentage of times in somebody’s life. I knew the feeling, because I use to be one of those fans. I’d be walking through that line just hoping to see my idol as I peaked closer and closer to the table where I’d eventually meet him. It was an adrenaline rush, you could never really tell what was going to happen until you met him, and then it ended so quickly and you relived the moment in your head as if you recorded it in your head to remember. I went outside to get a breather, for being in that musty gym got to you. I was bored, even though the sky was beautiful and the streets were active as they usually were in Calgary, I was bored of just standing there staring at the ground. Seeing the newspaper machine near the telephone pole I decided to grab something to read for I hadn’t been caught up on the latest news of Canada for a few months now. Being an active wrestler in the states had it’s perks, but as I was Canadian by naturalization, I missed the stunning scenery that was across the Canadian-United States border. The main news wasn’t all to great but then I saw the sports and the Canadian Football teams. I was a big football fan, and being that the Rams were my favorite U.S. team I always compared them to the Canadian teams. Of course though, Canadian football was much rougher, being in the blistering cold and tackling people onto ice would do that to players though. Turning the page I looked to see the stats but my paper flew away and one of the four kids I saw had it caught under his shoe. As I walked over to get it I heard my name being mentioned in the conversation and quickly hid behind the telephone pole trying to look as casual as possible. ’That guys a joke… Jesse has no chance against Blake Wolfson and Hessian, he’s going to be slaughtered like a pig and left to hang by his neck when they’re done with him,’ chortled the kid Mike, ’Just look at him. He’s all big headed about winning and thinks he’s going to be able to take down the monster Hessian after he was man handled by Apocalypse last month. Heh, yeah right!’ The four of them snorted in laughter without knowing I was there behind the pole. In my own little world now my heart was being torn apart for it was these four teenagers who I worked my ass off to be the best for and now they were talking behind my back with such disrespectful things to say. Even though I half expected some to do such, I didn’t expect four kids who shook my hand and said I was talented to flip the words they said moments before in a completely negative way. ’It’s all Blake in this one guys. He’s got the XUW behind him and it makes sense that he’ll win since he’s got the talent to. The fans will be saying, Jesse Jamester who after this match,’ snickered Dave as he pounded fists with Alan for props on his smack talk. ’If I were Jamester I’d go bury the title so they couldn’t take it from him when they climbed the ladder, but then again, he’ll probably no-show anyway because he’s afraid of the best, Blake Wolfson! Can only be one champion and it’s definitely not going to be Jamester after Sunday Night,’ Alan claimed thinking he was a tough man while trying to impersonate Blake Wolfson. I couldn’t hear it anymore. Ten minutes ago I had the impression that these kids had idolized me or believed in me to win the match, and now they were saying Blake Wolfson was going to demolish me. Falling apart piece by piece inside I turned from my side as I had been laying up against the pole to hear them and walked the opposite direction of the four. Not caring that I’d told the people inside I’d be back in fifteen minutes, I just wanted to get away, this wasn’t the same as what I expected. I thought being the champion meant getting respect, but now I knew different… No matter how good you were the respect you earned from some would never be fulfilled in all. Inside me, as I walked down the sidewalk my body began to ache, not only in my gut or in my chest, but in my arms and in my neck and all the way up in the back of my head. It wasn’t pain or a headache, it was like a mental void I could not get rid of with pills or a glass of water. It was a puzzle I’d have to unscramble… But with the time constraints, and the desire to prove everyone that I wasn’t a one hit wonder, I questioned whether I could do it. Could I defeat the odds? Could I beat myself in a game of chess? Or would the aching only get worst, and would the power of me inside begin to crumble? This was a test… the ultimate test… and now I had to face it… …
