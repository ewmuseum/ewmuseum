Date: 03-06-05 A plate of spaghetti sits before her and she pokes at the tangled mess with her fork. Somehow, hunger has slipped past her and now, here she is, sitting alone in a restaurant, poking at some foreign substance that wants to be spaghetti. A long, exaggerated sigh passes her lips as she throws her fork down and, disregarding the stares of other diners, leans back and stares up at the ceiling. The lighting in this place is minimal, mostly lit by the candles on each table. Other than the intimate glow, the place looks like any other low-income Italian eatery. She sits alone, in a booth, next to a window which looks out to the wild nightlife of the city. Well, the nightlife is usually wild, but tonight it appears to be dead. This proves to be rather disappointing for Aradia, since she practically feeds off of the night. Instead of being at the warehouse, which is inconveniently closed for renovation, or at some party, she sits in this diner, poking at a plate of slop. Resting her elbow on the table, she gently begins to massage the area between her eyes, hoping to relieve the tension in her head. A moment later, her glass of wine crashes to the floor. Her head jolts up, her raven hair falling in a frenzy around her face. She quickly pushes it away to find a man standing in front of her, staring at her. Sitting up completely, she looks the man over once and arcs an eyebrow. Short and peculiar, he sports a white dress shirt with a Garfield tie and brown slacks. His glasses are perched on the bridge of his nose; they had apparently fallen there when he knocked over her glass. In his hand he grasps a notepad, pen, and voice recorder. There is no doubt that this man is a reporter. Startled, Aradia presses herself further into the plush booth, but upon regaining her senses, leans forward again and gives the man a half snarl. “Excuse me, I’m sorry” he says, as he leans down to pick up the glass. Rolling her eyes, Xiomara grabs for his arm and latches on, looking him in the eye as he turns his head to look at her. “Leave it. That’s what the bus boys are here for.” They both straighten up and Aradia sighs. “What are you doing here, anyway?” The reporter clears his throat and then sticks out his hand, a goofy smile plastered on his pale face. “I’m Jonathan Brookes, field reporter for”.” Aradia stares at his hand, making her disgust apparent. The man stops speaking, catching her look, and drops his hand. There is a moment of awkward silence before Aradia finally speaks. “How did you find me?” She looks at him inquisitively, sitting up straight in her booth. Her hands are folded neatly in her lap as she gives the reporter a look of contempt. “I was told I could find you here?” he responds, nervously. Aradia nods and smiles a little, looking back down at her spaghetti. Neither of them notices that the bus boy is in the process of cleaning up the spilled wine. She looks back up at the reporter, her brown eyes sparkling mischievously. They draw him in and he stands, in awe, studying her flawless face. After a moment of heated tension, Aradia laughs menacingly, causing the reporter to return to reality. Blushing, he looks around and notices that their episode has caught the attention of the entire restaurant. “What can I do for you, Mr. Brookstone?” “That’s Brookes?” “Yeah, whatever, same difference, what is it that you came here for?” He swallows dramatically and nods his head. “Uh, well, you see, I came here in hopes that I could get a few words with you?” “Regarding what?” she asks, suddenly very annoyed. “Well, you, to be honest. We know nothing about you” well other than the fact that you’re an extreme talent and? quite beautiful?” he lets out a distorted, geek-like laugh which pierces Aradia’s ears. She stares at him blankly and he swallows again. “Do you mind?” She nods her head and then begins to stand. “Yes I do, actually.” She reaches for her black leather duster and slowly puts it on. A coy smile crosses her lips as she looks at the man with satisfaction. “What do you have to hide from everyone” You can’t even answer a few simple questions?” His appearance suddenly very aggressive, he steps forward and grabs her arm. Taken aback, she shakes it loose and looks at him with confused fury. He sneers and his eyes gleam with knowledge. Suddenly afraid of what he may know, she looks away and begins to hurry for the door. He calls for her just as she begins to push it open. “They’ll find out” sooner or later? they’ll know. You can’t hide forever.” She stops in the doorway and slowly turns her head halfway back. Black hair curtains the hatred on her face. Then, she walks out, leaving the man behind. As she makes her way past the remaining windows of the restaurant, she makes sure to not look back. What does he know, or does he know anything at all? Was he just playing the old reporter’s trick, or does he have information? Or could he know something that she does not? She unwillingly shrugs it off and continues down the empty street. There is no time to worry about that right now. In only a few days, she has another chance at victory; another chance to make a name for herself. If she spends her time worrying about the past, she may find herself defeated once again. This chance is too important to throw away. Exhaling loudly, she runs a hand through her black hair and stops, leaning against a cold brick wall. “It needs to die, it just needs to go away” she says to herself as she leans her head back. The night sky stares back at her, each star blinking with its own unique beauty. She tilts her head to the side and admires the view. Memories of lying in the grass and staring at the star-filled sky run through her head and she smiles. Those times were so simple, so happy. Where did they go? How can she find happiness? She looks away from the sky and shakes her head vigorously. “Damn it” she murmurs as she rummages through her pockets and pulls out a pack of cigarettes, a lighter, and a hair clip. She lights her cigarette, and then inhales. Exhaling quickly, she watches as a ring of smoke rises over her head. Placing the carton back into her pocket, she holds the cigarette with her blackened lips as she clips her hair out of her face. Random, loose strands of hair hang in front of her face, but she disregards them as she inhales again. More thoughts of happier times run marathons in her mind as she tries to enjoy this small amount of free time. Her joy falters, however, when the silence is penetrated by a blood-curdling scream for help. Opening her eyes quickly, she looks around crazily, alerted by the call. Another scream shoots through the night. Aradia looks to her right, realizing that they are coming from the alley next to her. Stealthily, she spins to peer into the alley. What she finds bewilders her. No more than thirty feet from her, a man pins a woman to a chain-link fence. He has one hand wrapped around her torso while the other is pulling her brown hair, causing her head to tilt to the side. His face is buried in her neck and she shrieks again. He punches her in the gut and she coughs and then tries to struggle, but he only presses himself against her more. She tries to kick, but he knees her, causing her to cough once more. Aradia shudders and then looks down around her feet, looking for some object to use against the man. Determined, she silently moves forward, as to not disturb the man and make matters worse. Finding a metal pole, she grabs it and raises it to her shoulder, holding it like a baseball bat. Slowly, she creeps towards the man, being sure not to make a sound. As she draws closer, she notices that the girl has bruises on her face. Her tear-filled eyes are shut tight. The girl opens her eyes and catches Aradia. She stops in her tracks. The girl? her face so familiar? her brown hair with red streaks, and even those brown eyes were hauntingly familiar. Then it clicked. It was the girl she beat several weeks prior. The bruises on her face, now a pale yellow, were beginning to heal and a small cut is engraved above her right eye. All were marks of Aradia. Finding that her breath has stopped, Xiomara shakes her head. This poor girl has been leading a life of tragedy: first, Aradia’s beating and now an attempt at rape. Still stunned, Aradia does not notice that the girl’s glance has caught her own. Before Xiomara can signal for her to be quiet, the girl shrieks again. “Stay away! Oh my god, someone help me!” The man looks up, but sees that the girl is no longer focused on him. He spins quickly, following her stare, and finds Aradia with the pole raised above her head. She strikes down, but he dodges, throwing himself and the girl to the ground. Aradia loses her footing, the pole disturbing her balance. The rapist takes advantage of this and kicks at her feet, watching as she falls to the ground. Landing face first into a mud puddle, Xiomara coughs as the man stands and comes closer to her. The girl lies on the ground, momentarily incapacitated. Now standing above Aradia, the man raises a foot to stomp on her and forcefully brings it down. Aradia is too quick, however, and spins to catch it in midair. Taking advantage of his lack of balance, Aradia pushes him back. He stumbles as she springs to her feet. As he regains his balance Aradia raises her leg high enough to kick him in the jaw. He stumbles back again as she comes forward and grabs him by the face. She slams it into the fencing several times as he falls to the ground. She turns; her mud covered duster trailing in the wind, and grabs the pole once again. As the man tries to get to his feet, she quickly turns back and clocks him in the head. He falls over just as the girl starts to rise. Aradia drops the pole and looks down at herself. Her black cargos are torn in several places and her black tank top is shredded at the bottom. Everything is covered in mud. Sighing, she pulls her muddy hair out of her face and ties it behind her head. The girl looks at her, afraid and shocked. Blinking several times, she looks at Aradia and shakes her head. “You just saved me?” she says meekly, unsure of Aradia’s actions. Aradia chuckles lightly as she wipes the blood from her forehead. “Think nothing of it.” The girl shakes her head again and responds. “No” I can’t help but think? I mean? why? You beat me senseless several weeks back? and now you save me from being raped? I don’t understand?” “No one understands me, dear.” Aradia interrupts the girl’s confusion. She thinks for a moment before responding: “Consider this an apology. Go get the cops; I’ll stay here with him.” The girl nods, still not completely trusting Aradia’s words, and hurries away, seeking the nearest officer. Aradia uses her hand to wipe some water from her nose. She looks down at the unconscious perpetrator and shakes her head. “You couldn’t just hire a hooker like a normal guy, could you?” She bends down to look at his face. The wet ground has caused dirt and grime to cover his tanned flesh. Blood covers the side of his head where she hit him with the pole. She had not planned on being quite as violent. Sighing she stands and looks around once again. A few moments pass and she beings to become impatient. If the girl had not been so shaken, Aradia would have gone to get an officer herself. She leans against the fence and goes into her pocket for the cigarette carton, hoping to actually enjoy this one. She is interrupted. “You deserved it.” The voice was more than familiar. The look on her face changes from pride and aggravation to utter fear. She turns and looks back at the man, still out cold. Looking all around her, she tries to find owner of the voice, but she tries in vain. It rings through the alley again and she cries out, throwing herself against the wall. Where is the voice coming from? She looks at the rapist, and covers her mouth. She recalls the scene in the alley only moments before and she gasps as that other memory overtakes her. ~*~*~*~ She was eighteen, a beautiful girl, arguably the most beautiful girl on campus. None of this fazed her, however, since she did not care about her beauty or her reputation. Unlike the other girls, she had an exotic feel about her. With her hair dyed black, heavy eyeliner, black lips, and mesmerizing brown eyes, most men could not help but watch as she walked down the sidewalk. In seven years, not much has changed. She dressed the same then as she does now. The same rebellious attitude still defines her personality. She was one of those people that you either loved or hated but she did not care either way. Not that she was selfish, but she had lived in an area that taught her to act on survival. These lessons made her cold. Now, planted in the middle of a small town college, where her ways and looks were anything but orthodox, she could not help but be the stereotypical rebellious teenager. It was no particular day when she stepped foot into that building, prepared to meet with her professor. She stopped in front of a glass display case, admiring its interior. Quietly, she smiled at her reflection. It was raining outside, but the world was still a beautiful place. Each drop of rain felt like another cool mark on her soul. She tucked a few strands of black hair behind her ears and studied herself. Simple but beautiful, she stood in a black tank top and black leather pants. Her hair fell over her bare shoulders, blanketing her tanned skin in black. Then another form appeared in the glass. Standing behind her was the professor, a look of anger on his face. Confused, she set her books down on the countertop in front of the display case and looked back at his reflection. “What’s going on Mr. Harris?” she asked, the words coming out with force and annoyance. “Why did you call me here?” It took him a moment to respond. He shook his head in disapproval. “You just love that image, don’t you?” Unsure of his intentions, she looked over her shoulder at him. “What do you mean?” Mr. Harris continues, uninterested in her question. “I’ve called you here to discuss your actions lately.” She looked at him with confusion, but somehow knowing where he’s going with this. “I’ve heard from several sources that you believe this “rebellious teenager” act is suitable and that the school deserves to have someone such as yourself gracing its halls.” “I didn’t say it quite like that” but yeah, for the most part.” She arcs one of her slender eyebrows and shifts her weight. “Well, Miss Xiomara, this is a problem with not only me, but the administration. As one of your professors, they’ve asked me to be the one to have a talk with you” to possible reverse your thoughts and feelings about your actions.” Aggravated, she looks away and shakes her head. “That’s what it’s all about, isn’t it” Conformity?” she pauses and then looks back at him, squaring her eyes in hate. “Of course you’d be the one to come talk to me. You’ve hated me since the minute I walked into your classroom, you smelled difference in me. Your first statement to me was “Do you think that attire is appropriate, Miss Xiomara?” There is no room in this world for conformity.” “You think its okay to look the way you do” to dress that way?” He laughed, hatefully, before continuing. “Do you even realize what you do to men?” “No, I don’t. But there isn’t a problem with the way I dress. I’m not exposing myself to anyone. Perhaps if you left this closed-minded, small town, you’d realize that this is how people dress in a lot of places.” Mr. Harris let out another chuckle. “It’s not a matter of what you wear; it’s your God-given features that drive men wild. You use it to your advantage.” He looked at her, crazed, and took a step forward. Her confidence dwindling with the rise of her fear, she took a step back, pressing herself into the counter. “What the hell are you talking about?” “Don’t raise your voice to me, little girl.” Anger rang through his words. “Excuse me?” She stood tall, knowing better than to let him intimidate her. “You’ll learn to step down?” He grabbed for her arm, but she pulled away. “Don’t touch me!” she screamed, staring him in the face, daring him to move again. He moved for her arm and she moved away. Then, one palm cracked across his face. Time stood still as he cradled his cheek in his hand. Turning slowly, he raises a fist which slammed down hard onto her jaw. Falling backward, she held onto her mouth and howled in pain. Coming down on top of her, he began to pull at her tank top. Afraid, she kicked at him, nailing him in the gut. Grunting, he retaliated by punching her again. Feeling light headed, she struggled as he tore at her shirt. “You’ll learn. You deserve what you get, you little hussy. This will teach you to tease men” this will teach you to challenge?” He was interrupted by her spitting in his face. Outraged, he lifted her head and slammed it into the ground. The room began to spin as he punched her again and then proceeded to wipe her saliva from his face. The world grew dark as she felt her shirt lift away and his greedy, chapped hands run along her body. She awoke in a classroom, lying behind a desk. Everything was sore. Groggily, she sat up and studied her surroundings, trying to remember what happened, but she could not. It was as though everything was blocked out. Looking down, she noticed the tears in her tank top and her loosened pants. Confused and afraid, she stood, straightened her shirt, and zipped up her pants. The pain in her groin was unbearable. Her eyes filled with tears as she looked around her, trying to find help, or anyone that could tell her what happened. All she found, however, was empty desks and a dark classroom. Leaning against the wall, she slid down slowly and tucked her legs up to her chest. Tears fell as rivers down from her brown eyes and she began to rock back and forth, unsure of what to do next. ~*~*~*~ Aradia came to, standing in the alley, the man still unconscious before her. Taking her hand away from her mouth, she quickly wiped the tears from her eyes. For the first time in seven years, she could finally remember the events that lead her to a silent decline. She heard the muffled voice of the girl as she spoke with the police, hurrying back to the scene of the crime. Unprepared to deal with the situation, she scaled the fence and jumped down on the other side. Hiding behind a few crates, she listened as the girl and the officer hurried into the alley. Their footsteps stopped. “She was here just a minute ago?” the girl said, astonished. “She must have taken off,” the policeman said, harshly. “Did you do this?” There was silence for a moment and Aradia imagined the confusion and hurt on the girl’s bruised face. “Of course not! He tried to” he? and then she saved me? but now she’s gone?” “So there were no witnesses?” the policeman asked a state of disbelief in his voice. “Just her?” said the girl, defeated. “Well, we’ll take him in, but we’ll need to take you in for questioning.” The girl began to sniffle and the officer spoke once more. “Come now, maybe she’ll show up at the station. Maybe she’s already there. Don’t get worked up, come on; let’s get you to the station?” Aradia listened to their words intently, knowing she would have to show up at the station eventually. She couldn’t let this girl live with anymore hardship or tragedy. She owed it to her. Right now, however, she had her own problem to tackle. For years she struggled to remember the full events of that day and now they have flooded her being. The fragmented shards of memory haunted her and now the full picture stalks her. Shaking her head, she slowly gets to her feet, regaining her senses. This can’t break her, not now. Her most important challenge to date comes in less than twenty four hours. All of her energies and all of her focus must be on this obstacle. Then, she can take care of business. First, the police station. Then, this match. And after all that is through, there is someone she must see. Someone who will help her. Now that she knows the truth, now that she can remember everything, she can finally take care of the problem. There will be no more fear. There will be no more haunting. Only confidence and determination. Retribution is at hand.
