Quiet Beginings

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I squint as I shield my eyes from the glare, biting winds chilling my skin. It has been too long since I’ve been outside. How many days now? Four? Five? Its beginning to become difficult to discern the flow from one to the other. I step from my meditation chambers here in my Church, and into the courtyard. Snow and fallen foliage scatter the court yard. Flint is cold this time of year, and my hands quickly begin to chill against the winter breeze, my knuckles white on the broom handle. My chores here, though self appointed, are becoming too much to handle. My mind seems clouded, as if there is something at the edge of consciousness clawing to be let in like a dog at the kitchen door. It is hungry, and wants to feast. Hm’A disturbing thought. And one for later, when its not so cold and I’m not so tired. My eyes slowly cross over the virgin white clearing of stone and snow, my silver and black hair blowing and billowing in the slight wind. My modest garb seems to present little in the way of protection. I’ll have to send someone into town for more materials. I’ll need to make myself some appropriate attire when I get the chance. That will have to be added to the bottom of an ever growing list. I have to wonder: will that chance ever arrive? Time management does not seem to be my strong suit. I hurry to finish my sweeping, clearing the freshly fallen snow from the carefully laid paths and benched areas in the garden sanctuary. The cold has killed many of the trees and the grass is grey-brown, but they both will grow back. Stronger and more lustrous. Such is the way in nature’such was the way with me: I was dead, cold and lifeless inside, a shell without a man inhabiting it. A car with a sleeping driver at the wheel. I had the power to stop my course, but was unaware of the great destruction I was causing. However, I was awakened by the spring of the Light. The warmth of the Lord filled me, and I grew from the darkness of death, and returned to the world. I thank him for that every day. Normally twice. Today, I thank him an extra time for good measure. I have found peace: I am content, and I am helping others find that same contentment. My life is not one full of the same heady exultation as my former existence, but instead it is to the brim with a quiet joy and a contented dignity. I am at peace. I have worked hard to find this life. It was not something that happened overnight, and I am pleased with the growth of those around me and of that which is within me. Much I had lost over the years returns to me: Compassion, Love, Faith’Strength. Not strength of the body, though that too has increased through my training, but strength of my core’my Kree-Cho-Chee’my life line, and my centre of being. If I close my eyes, look deeply into my core, I can see it there. Three points of light running through my metaphysical core. The Trinity of my strength. The Father, The Son, and the Holy Spirit. My Kree-Cho-Chee. My Crutch. My core. My life. Stepping back inside the monastery, I brush my feet on the hand woven rug at the entrance way and move inside. Looking over everything here, I cant help but feel proud. I know that’s wrong, but this is mine. My creation, and my legacy. One of drastic opposition to the life I have led. One of compassion and faith. Everything here has been made with my own two hands. Every stitch, every nail, every coat of paint was my doing. And now it fills up. Its own life growing; calling out to others, and I feel pride. And no shame enters my mind. Only sadness. Change is coming. It is a feeling that I cannot shake, and one that I do not exactly greet with any sort of giddy excitement. I have spent long hours in prayer. Long days and nights in deep contemplation with little more company than my own thoughts and the flickering dance of a candles flame upon bare walls. The church has grown and they missed my presence at mass, but the nature of this stir in me is too disturbing to ignore. Shades of black and red cross my mind as a moth across the face of a lamp; fleeting but hard to ignore. Flashes of Crimson enter my thoughts when I’m alone at night, and I know he’s coming. I know that he’s struggling to reach me, and I know that he is the dog at the door. But I wont let him in. Not yet, my walls are not yet strong enough. My church can not yet stand, and my soul is not yet whole again. His pain is great. His mind torn and broken, his life taken from him as mine was from me’I pity him, but he has no place here. He is not ready for what I have to teach him, and if he sets foot here, I fear that he will destroy all that I have created. Crossing through the halls of this place, feeling my slipper clad feet padding silently along the floor. My large frame and long legs carrying me through my home and my salvation quickly, but with a fighters grace. I reach my quarters and pull open the door. I ease myself into a deep, hand crafted chair and let myself fall into a quieted meditation. He will be here soon. And I will greet him. Outside’I will not have him inside these walls with the burden he carries. He is not ready to be healed’but perhaps I can help him yet? Sitting back in my chair, sinking into its depths and turning my eyes inward, I seek out my unwanted guest, and I feel him. As soon as my eyes closed, I felt his upon me. Cold ice blue, filled with such hatred and such unfocused rage. Heavy lids behind a mask of blood red and ebon black, he stares at me. “You are not welcome here,” I hear myself say, my tone calm, my voice clear. “You were asked to leave once, forced to leave a second time, and today I simply refuse you admittance to my home. You have nothing to offer me anymore, and are little more than a stranger to me. A shadow of a thought.” Behind the darkness of my lids I can hear him pace back and fourth inside my small room, his long black jacket trailing across the floor. “I need not your welcome, Son of Weakness” he says. His voice full of a rage barley checked. “These were once my halls. This was once my home, and you were once my slave. I was the ruler of this place, and you were naught but a quiet voice not worth noting. Such as it will be again. I am here to re-claim my thrown. To take up my crown and my seat at the head of Church of the Void. Step aside, Son of Weakness. Your master would have you prepare for his return.” The darkness inside my mind is becoming oppressive. But I will not allow this. I am not helpless. I am not weak or broken. Struggling for the calm I need, I speak again. Simply. Plainly and without anger. “You are not welcome here. You were asked to leave once, forced to leave a second time, and today I simply refuse you admittance to my home. You have nothing to offer me anymore, and are little more than a stranger to me. A shadow of a thought.” My ears ring. His scream is one of rage and of fear. His place is gone in this world, and his power is all but departed. I bow my head, and feel his fist explode across my face. The force of the blow sending my large frame careening across the room, toppling my simple dresser, and scattering the few items that had been placed across its top. I hit the ground. Hard. Struggling for calm, I open my eyes slowly’and am greeted by his baleful stare. Standing up slowly, not relieving his gaze I straighten my seven foot frame. “You are not welcome here. You are weak and you have nothing. You are not welcome here.” He screams again, but I barley hear it. I watch him slink away into the darkness, and put the mirror back onto my dresser. As I place the looking glass back upon my chest of drawers, I see the Dog at the Door and look into his eyes. Unlike the master of the Void who had just visited my mind, these eyes were not my own reflection. The Harvester of Minds was here. I head slowly across my haven, and unlock the gates he had found barring his way. Moving from the lock, I enter my large kitchen and pour myself a large glass of water and set myself to wait for the rain to pass us by. He would not try the gates again, and I do not expect that he shall leave’his pride is great. As is his potential. It pained me to hear of his loss, but from such pain men can grow strong. Stronger than before. I hope that this is the case. I hope that he wishes to leave the Kingdom of tragedy that we created, the two of us. The rain is pounding him outside. It is a shame that he is too sure that the gate remains locked to check its latch. I hear the torrent lessen and I remember my chores left unattended. Moving to a large hand woven wicker chest I remove a pair of gardening gloves, and from a dark oak drawer, I remove a pair of sheers and move out into the cold Michigan air. Seeing Salaok again really does bring back memories that I had wished to leave buried. Memories of pain and humiliation brought upon others. Memories and shame. I wonder if he too has changed’and if so’has it been for the better? I nod to him and go about my business. The gate is unlocked. It is only a matter of time before he testes it again. I hear his voice over my shoulder, it too is full of anger and’madness? Let us hope not that. 'Gabriel, why do you not talk to me now? Why do you make me stand behind these gates during the storm, only to give me a nod and then ignore me? Have you forgotten who I am? I haven't forgotten who you are, Forever Knight!' How much he misses while blinded by rage. How much he fails to see. 'It has been a long time since anyone called me that, however I didn't make you do anything Salaok. You were the one who chose to stand there. And if you had tried the gate again, you would have found that I unlocked it not long after your first attempt at opening it. You've always been too fast to make up your mind Salaok. However, I see your ability to wait for what you want has grown. Come inside and we will talk. Why have you come to see me, old friend? Why have you brought the burden you carry to my gates and to the Church of the Redeemer?”
