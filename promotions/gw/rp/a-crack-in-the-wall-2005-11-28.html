A Crack in the Wall

28 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/28/2005 Strange things had been happening in the mind of Virgil Ambrosi as of late. Perhaps motivated by the sudden upheavals in the world outside of his head, a certain uneasiness was worming its way through the cracks of his usual calm exterior. Anyone allowed into the realm of his confidence would know precisely what was behind this, but this would presuppose that there be someone in his confidence that was not currently gone. More weeks had passed, with little sign of his paramour, save her somewhat sedate appearance at Global shows. His patience was beginning to wear rather thin. Waiting further seemed to become more loathsome by the second. His surroundings had some bearings on this loss of patience. While not aesthetically disagreeable, the walls of the McFarlane estate seemed more and more like the drab brick and mortar of prison walls with each passing hour. He knew that the time to leave was coming, but in contrast to his previous ability to cut and run at his leisure, he now found himself being presented with a problem. Aside from the obvious concerns that arose when he considered following a woman into the deserts of Nevada with all knowledge that she had the ability to hide her tracks well, with no clue as to her location, there was something else, something new. Alexis. Of course it was weak of mind to want to protect her. At his very core, it pulled at his predator’s sensibilities to care about anyone, but the isolation-bred instincts had been dulled dangerously as of late. After the Den, the organization to which Alexis used to belong, had sent a group of armed soldiers to silence her, Virgil had felt an odd need to protect her from the unfairness and cruelty that she would have been subjected to. He found it most bizarre, like a lion protecting his pride from a pack of hyenas. Though he found Cyrus to be quite respectable company, Virgil found it unlikely that he would stand up to protect Alexis, were the Den to make another attempt on her life. Virgil could not say there was no sense in this. After all, with one escaping, it was likely that if there was another attempt, they would be prepared for their usual query of vampires. Cyrus could not be expected to place a human that he had had little contact with before his own personal safety. It certainly posed a problem. There was only one other being that Virgil trusted enough (aside from Lia) to take up such a task. Truly, Rune, his Norse pilot/smuggler would have been included, but Virgil realized that his abilities would be better suited to flying planes. That left, as Lia was preoccupied, for lack of a better term, only one. Virgil pushed open the large doors that led outside to the balcony that stood outside the bedroom he shared with Lia, and flipped open his rarely-used cell phone, pressing down a key until speed dial reacted. ? ? “Geia sas, kirie Ambrosi.” (Good day, Mister Ambrosi) “Xerete, Niko.” “No formality today” To what do I owe this most rare of honors?” Virgil rolled his eyes. His lawyer, Nikos Patridis, was perhaps the only man he knew that could be as overtly condescending as him. “I would ask a favor of you.” “Ah, Mister Ambrosi, have your underlings gotten you into a skirmish with the law?” “No, fortunately. Business has been slow enough that I have little use for underlings.” “A blessing and curse in the same space, I see.” “One could say so.” A pause. “Forgive me, kirie, what favor would you ask of the humble Nikos?” Virgil’s lips creased in a smirk. “I have an acquaintance that requires your expert protection. Certainly, you’ve been behind a desk for long enough, and need some exercise?” “What acquaintance worthy of you would require my protection?” “A human, Niko.” “For some horrid purpose, I assume.” Virgil’s eyebrows turned sharply downward. “Nothing of the sort. She is not to be tortured, killed, interrogated, drunk from, or injured in any way. I have no plans for her.” “I see. Forgive my forwardness.” “Forgiven.” “What, pray, am I protecting this woman from?” “An inept, yet well-financed group of hunters. Nothing your skilled evasions cannot outmaneuver.” “Ah. I understand” there should be little problem, then, provided I may relocate her, if necessary.” “Yes, you have my permission to do so. Although, if you have plans, do notify Cyrus.” “Ah, the Kiasyd?” “Yes, the same. I won’t be needing you to drop everything you’re undoubtedly busy with and come to Toronto immediately. We will be leaving for Tokyo, and will not return for a fortnight or so. I assume you will be able to perform this task for me?” “Certainly, I believe I owe you a boon somewhere or other. I await your further command.” “Thank you, Niko. Of all my associates, you are the only one I trust with such a job.” “I’m most honored.” “I will be in touch. Adiosas.” “Adiosas.” He slid his phone back into his pocket, and as he stepped back inside the mansion, he was gripped with a sudden despair. Far from being another effect of staying more or less alone in such a large house, it was something more profound. He attempted to push it back long enough to make an attempt at packing his old suitcase, but it seemed as though every piece of clothing he stuffed inside only served to remind him of her. It seemed as though even the most insignificant of things did, these days. He knew that it would be nearly impossible to shake this grim feeling, this sense of loss that was inescapable. Alexis was not in the house, Cyrus had holed up in the carriage house with an unexplainably large remote library, and Caitlin, whoever she was, was not very intent on speaking with him, but that last matter was one Virgil preferred. Emotion had never been particularly useful for him, throughout the last thousand years or so, or at least the patchy memories of them that he possessed. Of course, for the majority of those memories, he’d been alone. There was no-one to cause rifts in his usually calm existence, and no-one to reveal any kind of emotion to, even if he was to have such a thing. Personal shows of emotion were useless to him when he had nothing to be emotional about, but what he was presented with now was difficult for him to deal with. He latched the bolts of his suitcase, and spun the dials on the number pad, and suddenly found himself wanting to drop to the floor and sob uncontrollably. Instead, he pulled the suitcase off of the bed with a definite sense of urgency, and threw open the bedroom door, practically storming into the hallway, the scowl on his face an attempt to mask the weakness affecting him so deeply. The door slammed behind him, and he heard some sound of reaction from another part of the house, assumedly having disturbed Caitlin. He tore down the staircase, trying to remember if there was anything he could have left behind in his attempt to escape this wretched house, this place that reminded him of everything he couldn’t have. The front door was treated slightly more gingerly than the less ornate bedroom door, and he stepped out onto the patio, with his car in sight, but suddenly found himself blocked. whine, whine? Virgil stopped, and turned around to view the interloper, a pair of slightly horrendous dogs staring at him through glowing yellow eyes, wagging their tails. “Hello, Damek, Jirina.” They scampered toward him excitedly, bouncing on their front feet. He dropped down to scratch both of them behind the ears with his pointed fingernails, sending them somewhere in the vicinity of heaven. Through low grunting noises that he assumed were happy, he was struck full on by the futility of his situation. Everything he loved and desired was far from him now? and what was worse, there were so many things that were unexplained. The absent look in her eyes that he’d heard people mention when they saw her at shows, the way she simply disappeared afterward without a word to anyone? There had to be something happening to her. That itself worried him less than the fact that she always disappeared more or less without a trace. Jirina whined at him again, as though sensitive to his despair. He didn’t even know where to start? Frustration, loneliness, anger, confusion, despair, and desperation coalesced into being, in the form of a crimson tear escaping the iron walls of his eye, and rolling down his cheek, followed by a mirror image on the opposite side of his face. And now that his feelings were completely obvious to any that could see him, Damek dropped his manly exterior, and whined as well.
