The Inevitable Return II: Confusion Will Be My Epitaph

10 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/10/2006 ---- The wall on which the prophets wrote Is cracking at the seams. Upon the instruments of death The sunlight brightly gleams. When every man is torn apart With nightmares and with dreams, Will no one lay the laurel wreath As silence drowns the screams. ---- FLASHBACK’Sunday, November 21, 2004 2300 hours, local time backstage, Global Arena ---- Anger. A pain pounding in my head, behind my eyes, surging through my veins like fire. Rage. White-hot poker in my belly? a pain that was similar to the other, accompanying the stitches the arena doc was currently putting in my temple. Another bump to add to the collection. Another scar, this one dangerously close to doing me in, according to the doctor. Zenith’s handiwork. I hadn’t told the doctor how wrong he was, that I’d already seen my death, and it wasn’t for years to come. People already considered me a freak; I didn’t need to feed more fuel to that particular fire. “Ok, you’re all set,” the doctor patted his shoulder, startling him from his reverie. “Just try, for once, not to tear this open again.” Jackson stood slowly, grabbing his blood-soaked t-shirt from the table, flipping it over his shoulder. “Yeah, whatever. I know the routine.” He took one step, then another, and then his knees buckled, spilling him to the cold floor. The antiseptic smelling room faded away in a wash of white that bled to darkness? ---- FLASHBACK’Saturday, November 13, 2004 0555 hours, local time CID Safehouse, Amsterdam, Netherlands ---- Jackson paused before the steel door, taking a deep breath. In one fluid motion, he clicked off the safety on the gun, and poised his finger above the trigger, rearing back on one leg. His booted foot slammed into the door, splintering the wooden frame and as the door swung inward, he stepped over the threshold. He was oblivious to the chain that was still wound around his fingers, the charm dangling against his palm, resting comfortably beside the textured pistol grip. He paused in the silent room, his gaze flicking across the barren kitchen. He remembered this room well, the well-stocked pantry; the late night snacks. The sounds of his own breathing were oppressively loud, so he held his breath for a moment, breathing out slowly, bit by bit, slowing his respiration. He crouched down beside the butcher block, listening to the silence. “Come out, come out wherever you are,” he muttered under his breath, reaching beneath his coat to withdraw the second, almost identical Walther handgun. He hesitated one more fraction of a second, and then rose in a fluid motion, crossing the moonlight silvered room. He eased from that room, into the next, making his way to the back of the house, still troubled by the fragments of the dream. “Loooooocy!” he bellowed, in a terrible Ricky Ricardo parody, “I’m hoooooome! You got some “splainin to do!” Silence answered him, and it was a silence that seemed to be mocking him. He could smell the blood in the air, that sickening hamburger smell, and he knew without stopping, that he would find nothing but gore in the room beneath the basement stairs. He hadn’t known that Gabriel even knew about the torture chamber, which had been a throwback to a darker time in the order’s history. Judging by the white light filtering up the stairs, he was certain he did now. The door lay ahead, spilling out onto the wide back veranda, leading to the sheds, and beyond, to the field. He could see, from this vantage point, the barren apple tree, gleaming white like bleached bones under the moon. He reached for the doorknob, and that’s when he felt something shift in the air. He knew without turning around, with the certainty that you knew night was going to follow the day, and vice versa, that Gabriel stood behind him, at the top of that same stairwell. “Hello, brother,” Jackson murmured, tension evident in the line of his shoulders beneath his jacket. He started to turn around, but Gabriel’s sharp reply made him hesitate. “Ah, ah” not so fast, Jackson. Your guns. If you would be so kind?” Gabriel remained still, backlit by the glow from below, nothing more than a velvety black shadow amid the darkness. Jackson held up his hands, letting the cartridges fall from the guns as they dangled from his fingers. They clattered to the floor, spilling bullets. Seconds later, the guns followed, and then Jackson turned around, still holding up his hands. “Well then, Gabriel. It seems that you’ve caught me at a rather large disadvantage.” Gabriel stepped into the light, and his appearance made Jackson gasp. His cheeks were sunken, his hair hanging in grayish clumps, thinning and sparse. His eyes however, were the same, burning with a cat’s ferocity, gleaming in the moonlight as he closed the gap, pointing the curved blade at Jackson’s chest. The necklace in his hand grew warm, growing almost hot as Gabriel stepped into his personal space, stopping only when the blade was mere millimetres from piercing Jackson’s skin. He took a deep breath, trying to stop the sweat that was pouring down his back, trying like hell to stop the trembling in his legs. It would do him no good to show fear now. He swallowed the lump in his throat, and managed to look at the smaller man, looking past the dark blade. He could hear the whispers in his mind, he had, after all, been the first to touch that forbidden relic, a mistake he would carry with him for the rest of his life. Now, in this moment, he thought of Kitty, of her impossibly green eyes. He thought of the life growing in her belly, probably still unbeknownst to her? and somehow, he drew strength from that. “Take my life, then, Gabriel. If that is truly what you wish to do. But tell me this first, when I am dead, on the floor, nothing more than an empty shell, what purpose will you have?” Gabriel blinked, and then chortled with lunatic glee. “Trickery, and how can you say the opposite of what you mean at a time like this” Don’t you know, brother, I know your mind? I can hear your thoughts, as clear as day.” Jackson gazed at Gabriel, his eyes filled with nothing, strangely cold and dead. “You lie, brother. I know you cannot. If you could, you would have known I was here, long before I announced myself. I think, Gabriel, that sometimes you forget I was playing this game when you were still in grade school. Do not try to con the master con artist, and do not try your tricks on me. They won’t work. I won’t tell you anything.” “Oh, but you already have, brother. I know everything!” His voice had taken on a wheedling tone, but the blade was ever steady in his hand, still barely touching Jackson’s sternum. “I know you have sired a child with that bitch you were supposed to dispose of! I know why you sacrificed us all, for your pitiful cause. I know now, why you ran away” I know all about the masks you have chosen to wear. Brad Jackson, that was, now going by Jackson Nameless Darkness? Brad Jackson the wrestler,” he sneered, spitting the last words with contempt, “and then Brad Jackson the happy fianc” and future father. Oh tell me, brother, when was the big day planned for?” Jackson blinked, saying nothing for a moment, and then he raised his hand, showing the simple gold band he wore on his left ring finger. “Two weeks ago. Private ceremony. Terribly sorry you weren’t invited.” “You lie! There was nothing in the annals about that!” Jackson’s lips curved up in the ghost of a smile. “I wouldn’t lie to you, Gabriel. Look in my eyes, and tell me that I don’t speak with veracity. Well” look.” He widened his eyes, saying nothing more, nor did he have to. Gabriel could see the love in Jackson’s eyes; he could see no hint of falsehood. He took a step back, and that’s when Jackson finally moved, bringing his right hand down, pressing the talisman in his palm against the dark blade. There was a sizzle, and a crackle of light, and then the blade fell from Gabriel’s hand as he shouted in pain. Jackson could smell burning flesh, and a moment later, as he pushed out into the night, he realized it was his own. The hot charm had burned against the inside of his right wrist, leaving a mark. The night air was cool on his skin as he hurried across the gravel, his walk rapidly becoming a full out run, and then a gallop. The tree, he was headed for the tree? the skies overhead opened up with a mighty rush of icy water, and he was soaked to the skin in seconds, freezing? ---- FLASHBACK’Sunday, November 21, 2004 2300 hours, local time backstage, Global Arena ---- The water hit him in the face like a hard slap, leaving him sputtering, and gasping for air. He’d returned, it would seem, to the present, and to the world around him. The doctor stood before him, a worried expression on his face. The cup was still in one hand, an opened vial of smelling salts in the other. So, that was what accounted for the bitter smell filling his nostrils. He coughed, and tried to sit up, finding that the slight movement made his head pound even harder. The room tilted crazily, spinning in and out of focus, and the doctor’s voice sounded a million miles away, heavily distorted like the wonking of Charlie Brown’s teacher. “Jackson” Can you hear me? Brad!” The doctor waved a hand in his face, and after a moment, Jackson seized his wrist, grinding the bones together as he snarled, water still dripping from his hair and face. “Yeah, I can hear you. Shut the fuck up, you’re giving me a headache.” He let go of the doctor’s arm, and then let his head fall back against the padded examination table. How he’d gotten up here was a mystery that was best left to another day. His eyes drifted closed and then a sharp slap across his face brought him back to reality. “Wake up, Jackson” think you have a concussion? probably a pretty bad one. Not surprising since Zenith damn near knocked you into next week. You can’t sleep now? much as you want? dangerous.” The words faded in and out, like a badly tuned radio station. Jackson opened his eyes, glaring at the arena doctor, before reaching up with one hand to grab a handful of his shirt, dragging him closer. “Do me a favor, jerk. Tell Zenith to go fuck himself” and tell that doctor the same thing. I don’t need people telling me what to do. If I want to sleep, I’ll damn well sleep.” Jackson growled, his eyes narrowing to dangerous slits. “In my mind, that bastard has just made one hell of a mistake. Mark my words, Zenith, this means WAR.” He slid off the table, for the second time in the evening, nothing but angry adrenaline keeping him on his feet as he shouldered past the bewildered doctor? ---- Confusion will be my epitaph. As I crawl a cracked and broken path If we make it we can all sit back and laugh. But I fear tomorrow I'll be crying, Yes I fear tomorrow I'll be crying. Lyrics credit: “Epitaph”- King Crimson
