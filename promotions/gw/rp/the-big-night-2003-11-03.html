The Big Night

3 November 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 3rd, 2003 Akodo Rokku couldn't remember the last time he'd been this nervous. He glanced down at Yoshiko nervously as she straightened his bow tie and smoothed out his lapels. 'Are you sure everything's ready, Yoshiko-chan?' Yoshiko rolled her eyes and smiled. 'Of course it is, Rokku. You think I'd let anything go wrong here? I know how long you've been dreaming of this day. Everything's perfect, I wouldn't settle for anything less.' Yoshiko finished straightening up Rokku's tuxedo, and looped her arm around his. She smiled up at him, and the Steel Lion couldn't help but appreciate her as she was one more time. Her hair up, resplendent in her white silk gown, decorated with diamonds? Well, this was it. Taking a deep breath, Rokku pushed open the double doors in front of him, Yoshiko at his side. Almost immediately, flashbulbs began to go off, and Rokku was thankful he had decided to wear sunglasses. He let his gaze sweep out over the paparazzi -- it was about as good a turnout as he could have hoped for, the guardrails and security keeping everyone in check. Slowly, he let his breath out, and pulled the microphone from his pocket. 'Welcome!' Rokku bellowed, and his voice issued out of the building's external speakers. 'Welcome,' he repeated, 'friends, guests, ladies and gentlemen of the press. What you're going to experience tonight is the result of several weeks of hard work -- and a LOT of money -- giving birth to something I've dreamed of for a long time. That said, I don't see any good reason to delay things any longer. So please, follow Yoshiko and myself inside. And welcome? to the Lion's Den!' Smirking knowingly, Rokku vanished back into the building, Yoshiko less than half a step behind him. *** Immediately upon passing through the front doors the crowd was set upon by the sights and sounds of a rave. Minus the ravers, anyway. A DJ waved from the back of the club, spinning albums with reckless abandon as people streamed in. Multicolored lights flashed throughout the club, and bars could be found on the three remaining walls. A smattering of tables were set up around the central dance floor, with more tables set up on the balcony above. 'This is the first floor, obviously,' said Rokku, spreading his arms wide, 'and probably my favorite part of the Den. I've borrowed bits of all my favorite Tokyo dance clubs and put them into the design here. And then there's the music. I'll be selecting the starting rotation here myself, but over time it'll become a mix of my personal choices and popular requests. There'll be plenty of American hits of course -- but also some of the top music coming out of Japan. If the Den does well, I'm hoping to get some of my favorite Japanese groups over here to perform live. Questions?' 'Just one,' said a female reporter near the front of the group. 'Is this it? You do have a rather nice club here Akodo-san, but is this all you called us out here for?' Rokku's lips split into a wide grin. 'Not at all, ma'am. In fact, if I might direct everyone to the stairs at your left and right, or the elevator in the center of the building, I'll show you all the second floor.' Murmuring with curiosity, the crowd split in three, the majority heading for the staircases, but a fair share -- including most of the GW stars who'd decided to RSVP --followed Rokku to the clear round elevator shaft. 'Nice place, Rocks,' said Ben J. Pailthorpe, leaning non-chalantly against the elevator as it ascended, 'but what else've you got?' 'Oh, I think you'll appreciate the second floor, Ben,' said Rokku with a smile, 'I think you might just love it.' *** The plexiglass elevator doors slid open just as people were pouring out of the stairwells, and Rokku strode out of the elevator confidently. 'Well Ben,' said the Lion to the Youngblood, 'what do you think?' Ben's eyes took in the myriad of machines with their brightly colored flashing screens, and a tear nearly came to his eye. 'I think I love you, mate,' said BJP. Rokku's grinned again. 'As you can see,' said Rokku, turning to address the reporters again, 'the second floor of The Lion's Den is dedicated to a video arcade. I'm doing my best to stock this place with a mix of the hottest new games straight out of Japan, as well as copies of old classics. I think anyone who's ever laid hands on a joystick should be able find something they'll enjoy here.' 'Rokku,' said a gray-haired man with a notepad in his hand, 'so far you've shown us two great places for the teens and twenty-somethings to go for a good time. But what about those of us looking for a quieter night out? Does the Lion's Den have anything to offer us?' 'I'm glad you asked,' Rokku replied, 'because your question leads us directly to the third floor. If you would ladies and gentlemen, lets all ascend once more.' Everyone split off towards the stairs and elevator again -- except for one Ben J. Pailthorpe, who looked longingly at the SvC chaos machine. 'Hey Ben!' shouted Rokku. Ben turned, and Rokku flicked a plastic card his way. 'Eh?' said Ben, catching the card, 'what's this?' 'Unlimited game time,' replied Rokku with a smile, 'also, your key to the roof. Swipe it through a the card slot on the elevator control panel and meet us up there later, but for now, knock yourself out down here man.' Ben returned Rokku's grin. 'Thanks, mate. I'll do that!' *** Those expecting more of the party theme that the first and second floors held were quite surprised when the reached the third. With serene biwa music wafting through it an a series of pale white shoji screens splitting it into rooms, the third floor had nothing in common with the first and second. 'As you can see,' said Rokku, his footsteps echoing on the hardwood floor, 'this level of the Den is designed to replicate a traditional Japanese sake house. Basically, it's a restaurant, specializing in traditional Japanese food and drink. I decided to take it a step further though, and have retained the services of a handful of classically trained geisha, for those who can afford their skills.' 'Prostitution?' said one reporter, raising an eyebrow. 'That's hardly legal, Akodo-san.' Rokku scowled. 'I said they were geisha, not prostitutes. If you knew anything about classical Japanese culture, you'd know that a true geisha wasn't a whore, but a versatile entertainer and conversationalist, who slept with her clients very rarely, and only after months of wooing, at the least. The women I've hired are of that caliber, and are in no way expected to perform sexual favors for money. Any other questions?' 'The first two floors are pretty noisy,' said another reporter, 'are we going to have to endure that if we want to get to this floor? And for that matter, where is the noise from the first two floors?' 'I had this floor thoroughly soundproofed to maintain the ambience,' replied Rokku, 'and to answer your first question, there's a second elevator on the side of the building that comes straight up to this floor, for those who want to skip the first two. Now, all this talk of floors has me wanting to move on again. Please everyone, up a floor. The guards at the staircases have passkeys that will open the doors to the roof, and I have one for the elevator.' As the crowd parted for the last time, Rokku smiled. Things seemed to be going just his way. *** 'Welcome,' said Rokku, 'to the Crane's Perch, VIP area of the Lion's Den.' Through thick plexiglass windows, the crowd looked out at the Dallas skyline. The roof was set up somewhat like a home -- there were a few tables with chairs around them for eating, a couple giant flat-screen TVs with couches and chairs facing them, and a variety of other things, all suggesting that this was a place to relax. Rokku let the crowd take everything in for a few minutes, then cleared his throat. 'I don't think I need to explain this floor,' said the Steel Lion. 'If you have a passkey to get up here, this is basically a sanctum where you can unwind. Food and drinks can be delivered here, as well as just about anything else, through the same elevator that goes straight to the third floor. Now! Everyone back downstairs. Those of you who already have keys for this floor can come back here whenever you like -- everyone else? well I just wanted to give you a glimpse for your reports, and motivation to earn your way here.' Rokku grinned, as the crowd started filing down the stairs again, then beckoned to one of the security guards. 'Let them know that everything on the first three floors is on the house tonight,' Rokku told the guard, 'I want them all to have a good time, and know that this is the only place in Dallas they can have a time that good.' The guard nodded, and joined several people taking the elevator down. Finally, only Rokku and Yoshiko remained on the rooftop. Letting out a long breath, Rokku collapsed onto one of the couches. Yoshiko followed, cuddling up to him. 'So,' asked Rokku, 'how'd I do?' Yoshiko smiled, and kissed him softly. 'You did great, Rok-chan. This'll be one of the hottest spots in the city soon, I guarantee it.' 'I sure hope so, ' said Rokku, 'I sunk a lot of the money dad left me into this place? I feel kind of like it's his legacy as much as it's my dream.' Yoshiko smiled, and was about to say something when her cell phone rang. Frowning, she reached into her handbag and plucked it out, flipping it open. 'Mosh moshi,' said Yoshiko. 'Yes?' she continued, 'no? no we hadn't heard. We've been so busy with the club? yes, I'll tell him. I'll tell him right away.' Rokku raised an eyebrow in confusion. 'What's wrong, 'shiko-chan?' 'It's? it's Star, Rokku,' said Yoshiko. 'She's been in the hospital since you accidentally speared her, and?' Rokku's smile melted, a look of concern taking it's place. 'And what, Yoshiko-chan? Is she alright? Tell me. So she told him.
