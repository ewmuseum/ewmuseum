A Warrior’s World: Give Me Resolution (Pt.3)

19 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 19th, 2004 Prologue. The fear of disruption, confusion, and organization of life is what makes those who seem so sane, flicker like a light. The thought of what is and what isn’t becomes one, draining logic from theory. Friends become foes, and rivalries heat to a boil. It’s the tension of lost trust building up in the wounds one suffers. Lights out maybe the resolution, but to the ones who try, the ones who seek their faith, their purpose of life, and believe in their own destiny… are who will survive. For all the rest, they will suffer a death of agony and distraught. Not realizing that it’s a part of life, to die… Chapter 9 His body lay motionless on the ground. A small whimper of pain is heard before he pushes his palm into the ground and flips his body over from his stomach to his back. Sighing heavily as he rubs his chest he begins to laugh. Okay… I understand now… My destiny is to die… I will live a life I was destined to live, and in the end my time will be up and the omega of life will take place. Death is the end, and acceptance is the only way to lose the fear of that. Maybe it’s a load of crap, but at least I believe in something rather than the fear of it all now. Sitting up right his face stared into the night sky, it was an okay night, not depressing or moody, just a blank dark blue hazed sky with few stars and a bright moon. The trees lurked over him as shadows of their limbs drew back and forth due to the wind. He stood up holding his knee due to the fall, soreness had taken the place of what was his kneecap and he looked down at his knee noticing the mud stain on the pants. I’m so stupid for jumping over that… What the hell was I thinking? Oh that’s right, I wasn’t! Eh, well I better get some ice on this or I’m not going to be winning any matches… Including the television title bout with Pendragon, Sherwin, and Reed. He limped up around the apartment building complex. Not many people jumped out of their balconies here, but for sure, many had been locked out of their apartment. Realizing he was one of those people who was stupid enough to go out and forget he locked his door. Instead of waiting around though, he walked down the street and looked for a rite-aid. Instead of finding one he came upon a Dairy Mart. Walking in he gave a heads up to the cashier whom he knew quite well, and grabbed a bag of ice in the back. Bringing it to the counter he plopped it down and threw down two-dollar bills. The cashier slips it into his hand and flicks the cash register open putting the two-dollars in. Giving Jesse the change of five cents, he smiled and told him to have a good day. It was normal for Jesse to get a heads up from people. He was a huge wrestler in the circuit. Maybe one of the best in Global Wrestling, but he’d never say that about himself. As a wrestler he believed he should never congratulate himself or build an ego. It wasn’t a smart or a good thing to do when you’re wrestling the men you do in the Global ring. Egos are the inflations of what men lack. When a man has to push his ego with every little thing he can, just to get satisfaction, he’s hiding something. Me… I’ve lost interest in hiding the truth. Whether you the almighty God himself or the evil of which is Satan, I can tell you straightforward I don’t give a damn. I’ve played those roles and seen those attitudes too many times to know that you hide something that is more than what you present. Heh… It’s funny how you can learn this little bit about people from the experiences you have in life, that have nothing to deal with them. Smiling as he walked out of the Dairy Mart he turned to go back to the apartment when he noticed a phone. Shaking his head in thought of the decisions, he finally picked the phone off the receiver and placed the coins in to call. Ring… Ring… Ring… “Hello?” started a voice. “Hey, it’s Jesse. Whats going on man?” he replied in an anxious tone. A sigh of boredom was the first noise heard in a static state before the voice answered back, “Ah nothing, just sitting here watching some TV. What about you?” “I got locked out of my apartment by accident. Left my keys in and went for a stroll. Forgot that I locked it… so now I’m calling you from a payphone,” he laughed knowing it was a stupid lie. The voice chuckled as well, almost not believing from the sound of the chuckle, but words spoke louder than expression, “Oh… Well you want to go do something?” “Yeah. Pick me up at the Dairy Mart on 132nd street. I’ll be near a bench with some ice. We’ll pick up some beer and decide what were doing from there. Sound good?” Jesse replied with a note of confidence that the voice would say yes, and it did. Hanging up the phone Jesse sat down on the bench and smiled, for some odd reason the person he called was like his subscription to a depression. Whistling wind hurled by as he sat just staring up at the sky, not worrying about his father’s death, or his global wrestling match, or even the time he’d have to spend working out had he wanted to win the television title. All he cared about was relaxing. Anticipation is my strong point. I’ve always wondered why I was good at wrestling, and now it just hit me. I know how to anticipate a move, or how to react to somebody’s second wind. I don’t fall from my perch just to attack, I plan and plot the move in my head seeing it through till the end, and then executing it as I saw it. A perfect resolution to my matches though, is when I know I can win with what I see in my head. Yet I know Monday, it’ll be a lot harder to plan and plot those moves. Four men in the ring at once, it’s like a zoo taking the lion, the panther, the jaguar, and the tiger and sticking them in a cage to see whom is the greater species. Well… I will do my best, and that’s I all I can state. I know winning is the focus, but it’s not my plan to win. Surviving is that key. And in the end… the result will be my strength over powering their weakness. But then again… A white dodge truck pulls up, almost identical to Jesse’s besides the special design of a tribal tattoo like design on the side near the door extending to the back tire. Jesse hops in the front seat and the two take off, not waiting for anything but their own sense of plans for the night. Chapter 10 After a long night of talking, drinking beer, and hanging out at the apartment of his friends, Jesse stands up and places a hand on the man’s shoulder. “It’s been cool man… But I have got to go. I’m going to go do something before this buzz wears off. See you later Ryan,” stuttered Jesse as he turned and open the door showing only the red eyes of a drunken man, and none of the other effects. “Later Jess, call me when you want to do this again,” said Ryan in a calm voice too as if he hadn’t been drunk. Then disappearing into the parking lot, Jesse left the apartment complex in which Ryan lived and walked down the street. Ryan hadn’t lived too far from Jesse. In fact he was about six blocks from him if you walked. It’d been only last week on Evolution that Ryan was seen hanging with Jesse and the two got drunk and partied. Since the death of his father, Jesse had lost himself in alcohol as he did in the past, but now he had control of his drinking. Ah, Ryan, what a friend… Been buddies since we started wrestling. Both thought the circuit sucked but dealt with it and stuck in there. If it wasn’t for his knee problem he’d probably still be wrestling. Sucks so much he isn’t their now as my tag partner or something. We were the shit back in the day… It was history repeating itself in Jesse’s mind. A highlight reel of the good ole’ days was a better way to say it, like the rerunning Brady Bunch episodes on TV. Jesse sighed as he knew then life was bad but he had friends to depend on, and now he just had friends to hang out with. Walking up to the dairy mart he sat on the bench again. The ice he had was forgotten at Ryan’s apartment, but it didn’t matter because his knee was fine now. A temporary soreness that came and gone with the drinking of some good ole’ healing juice, alcohol. Falling asleep was the last thing Jesse wanted to do, but beyond ability to stop his self, he did, and would later regret doing so. Chapter 11 A small cloud of gray transparent air passed by Jesse’s face. Immediately coughing he sat up wiping his eyes of the sleep. No hangover showed, but the bars in which were in front of him implied he did something wrong. “Where… where am I?” he muttered as he leaned against the rough brick wall and placed his feet on the cement floor. A laugh came from what seemed to be a far distance, “You’re in a police station buddy. We found you sleeping on the bench at diary mart with no ID. Since your intoxicated you’re here… So when you feel like explaining yourself, I’ll be waiting here.” Jesse sighed in frustration and tiredness. He’d just had a good night and now he was regretting the whole thing, and becoming depressed because of how stupid he was for locking himself out. “I’m Jesse Fristik, I live in the apartment complex on 130th street. I left my keys inside when I went out for ice, and so I called my friend Ryan and we had something to drink. His phone number is [detail withheld],” Jesse explained as he stood up and then fell back down on his bench. “Okay… I’ll call him, and tell him to come pick you up. You have a $100 fine for sleeping in a public area,” replied the officer giving Jesse the bad news like it meant something to him. All Jesse could think of was his stupidity of being so irresponsible and dumb to do what he did. It’s not like I don’t have a record. If these guys were smart they’d keep me, but I suppose it wasn’t too big of a deal that I slept drunk on a bench. Eh, so what, still stupid. Damn I hate losing myself in that poison of a drink. I vow from now on I’ll never drink again! Upset with himself he laid there on the bench for an hour before getting picked up by Ryan. Taking him home Ryan sat in Jesse’s apartment with him as the two talked in what seemed to be the first real conversation between the two in ages. “Ryan, thanks so much man… I’m sorry you had to pay that fine. Once I get the time, I’ll write you a check and pay you back. Cool?” he said in an apologetic form still mad at himself about being so brainless. A sigh was Ryan’s first expression as he waved Jesse off, “Man, it could have happened to anyone. You haven’t drunk in a while and forgot about what you were doing. Don’t worry about it. Anyway, I know you’d have done the same for me.” Pushing his hair out of his face, Ryan stood up and headed for the door. “Uh Ryan, I have a favor to ask you…” muttered Jesse so silently that the wind outside was almost louder. “What was that Jess?” Ryan said now as he opened the door and was prepared to leave for work. Shaking his doubts off Jesse walked towards Ryan and stared him in the eyes. Ryan was only six foot three inches and weighed about two hundred thirty pounds, but looking at Ryan was like staring at a navy official, he looked solid to the core. “I want you to come be my partner in Global Wrestling… WAIT! Before you say no, I want you to realize you’ll be my manager, and if you manage me and help me win you’ll earn good money,” said Jesse, but looking at Ryan’s facial expression toward the thought he knew he wasn’t hooking him in. “Just think about it okay? Please! I need someone who can keep me out of trouble and push me that extra distance. So… just think about it man, please.” Ryan nodded without saying anything. Patting Jesse on the shoulder and shaking his hand he smiled and walked out the door to his truck. Jesse sat there wondering what was running through his head. I may be nuts, but I know he wants to wrestle. If I can only get him to manage me for the four way, it’ll be the perfect set up for me impressing him enough to try and get him to come back. I just know it! He has to have that desire of hitting the Bladez Cutter once again… I know I never could leave the ring… not for the time he has anyway. Smiling as if he knew the conclusion, he went over to the fridge and opened it up seeing what he had feared to come upon, alcohol. His smile faded and standing their dumbfounded by the bottles and cans of beer, Jesse could only think of one thing to do. Lifting the bottles out of the fridge with full arms, it took him two trips to get all of it out. Opening one of the bottles of Jack Daniels, his smile came back. Lifting it up over his head he opened his mouth… Laughing as he poured it down the drain and knowing the alcohol would have no effect on his abilities any longer. Bye bye alcohol, hello, television title… Pendragon, Sherwin, and Reed, you’ve never met a man so determined as this. Oh boy have I got a surprise for you come Monday night. Oh boy do I… The laughs faded as he opened up the beer cans and dumped all of them into the sink at once. Fading away with a new source of light for Jesse’s future, one that would contain none of the poison. Conclusion After all the disruption, confusion, and loss of organization in ones life, they can still change their ways. Whether drinking, doing drugs, or plain doing wrong, life can be altered for the better. Fear is only an epitome of the lack of self-confidence you have in yourself. So get the courage to stand up… and shut up those fears that haunt you. It’s the only way, to be true, to yourself. The End.
