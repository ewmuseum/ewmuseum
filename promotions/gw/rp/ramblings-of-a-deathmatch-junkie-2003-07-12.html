Ramblings of a deathmatch junkie

12 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Ramblings of a deathmatch junkie Ronnie Long Global Wrestling Promo #414 Date: 7/12/03 Location: In a car, on the way somewhere “This is what I’ve hated… always hated… about GW.” Ronnie Long, recently, has managed to get his hands on the card for Overload. And something seems to be bothering him. “I go out there to fight a guy named Mike McKendrick. McKendrick can’t do the GW the courtesy of showing up at all during the week. Then, in time for the match, he manages to drag his lazy carcass out to the ring. Meanwhile, I get jumped – and upstaged – by Tony Stecchino. The man who gets hired repeatedly when he throws guns out into the audience… threatens to shoot people in the head… man, this always happens. Always upstaged by some ego driven, maniac fed, fed backed…” He trails off into moody silence. “Maybe, instead of messing around with McKendrick and Asylum, I’ll just go straight to the top, and take down Stecchino. I mean, I came back to fight the Nevermore, but hey, taking him on, that’s bound to get me some of that recognition I’m aiming for around these parts, y’know?” Ronnie Long and Deanna D’aimore are seated in a car. Deanna’s car. Ronnie in the passenger seat, his arm braced against the window, his head resting on his hand. Deanna can only spare him a quick glance, as she’s driving. “Ronnie, it’s bad enough that you’re turning into a Deathmatch junkie, you don’t need to go running after Mafiosos who try to shoot people. You could wind up hurt… injured again… and I know being injured doesn’t bother you…” “I’ve been injured so often I’m used to it.” Ronnie mutters under his breath. But Deanna doesn’t hear him, and she continues talking. “… but it bothers me, Ronnie. I can’t stand to see you in the hospitals, with tubes stuck in your arms and your eyes glazed over from painkillers…” “Mmm… painkillers…” Deanna slaps Ronnie’s arm. “That’s not funny!” Ronnie offers up a wan grin. “When I’m all serious like, and I talk about school shootings and the dark side of humanity and all that junk, you say I’m creeping you out, and that I need to lighten up. Then when I do, you tell me I’m not funny. Now, which is it?” Deanna doesn’t answer. As bad as Ronnie’s sense of humor might be, it’s better than what he was like when she – and GW(F) – first knew him. Ronnie’s debut promo, he cut from a closed down school, at night, in the rain. “Ronnie, I just want you to be… well, you. No posturing… not as some freak of the night, not as the almighty hero, not anything in between. It’s…” To understand Deanna’s problems, we have to go back, to the start of Ronnie Long’s career. The first time he stepped into a Hardcore match. Against former GWF World Champion, Gambit. This was back in 2001, before he’d even met Deanna, before he’d fallen out of the good graces of the higher ups. It was in this match… Long’s second title shot opportunity. Just fresh off losing the Television Title… and he and Gambit were fighting. He could still remember the end of the match. The two men were each armed with kendo sticks, and were fencing with them. Long had just managed to deflect Gambit’s stick out of the way, and then he brought his down in an overhead swing. Gambit, just barely, got his stick up in time to block. Long’s shattered. Gambit was quick to react, swinging his stick, catching Long across the jaw, and spinning him to the ramp. Long, however, wasn’t going to be stopped by this. Grabbing the broken halves of his stick, Long lunged to his feet. Crossing the sticks, he jammed them into Gambit’s neck, throttling the man. Gambit staggered backwards… grabbed Long, and fell off the edge of the ramp, holding onto Long and pulling him down with him. The two men crashed into a pile of tables and electrical cords. Gambit was lucky enough to land on top, and he won the match. But the end… It was such an adrenaline rush. Blood flowing, lungs burning… it appealed to the darkness that he used to cling to. The darkness that impressed even Salaok. And since then, he was addicted… to pain, and to bleeding. As for Deanna’s problem with it? It’s just like she said. There’s nothing more horrifying than watching the one you love, lying in bed, suffering, in one case, unconscious, close enough to death that he was hooked to a machine that monitored his heart rate. This happened while Ronnie was in the independents… suffering from broken ribs, he insisted on wrestling anyway, and in the end, got a punctured lung. Deanna doesn’t want to have to go through sitting at his side, praying for him to recover, ever again. And that’s why she says this. “Ronnie, PLEASE. Don’t do anything rash. If you try, if you keep your mind on this, you can make it to the top of GW, just like you did in IWA. But there’s no reason to go after a gun wielding maniac…” “Except the glory.” “Ronnie…” “Deanna, don’t do this to us. Our turn’s coming up, you don’t wanna miss it.” The car pulls into a parking lot as the scene fades.
