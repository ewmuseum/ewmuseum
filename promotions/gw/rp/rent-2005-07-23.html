RENT

23 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07.23.05 The eyes of Ken Partridge, Jr. burned with dryness as he stared blankly over the contract that was laying in front of him, the balck words all running together on the sheets of solid white that were reflecting in his dialated pupils. He had lost intrest in reading it over a few minutes earlier when pure exhaustion had finally taken him over. His eyes were hurting beyond belief, as it felt as though someone had taken sandpaper to them. 'Ken,' said KPJ's lawyer, making sure that he was still with him. 'Are you okay?' Ken shook his head a bit to knock out some of the cobwebs before looking up and realizing who had just broken the silence. He gave him a half-hearted smile before looking back down at the contract that was drawn-up for him and then back into the curious eyes of his lawyer. 'I'm sorry, Rob,' Ken began, 'but I'm exhausted. Could you just give me a rundown of what this all about and I'll sign wherever I have to sign?' Robert McDonald held a mixture of pity, sorrow, and annoyance in his face for the request made by his most prized client. 'Out partying, again,' asked Rob. 'Hardly,' replied a completly zapped Ken. 'I wish that were what the problem is. Then I could just say screw it, go home, and go to bed when I wanted to.' And odd look of both curiosity and embarassment over took the lawyer McDonald's face as he posed his next question. 'Then... well, what is it?' 'This PR,' ranted Ken again, 'it's kicking my ass. I'm filming the Superman movie and helping develope the next one's script. I'm in the studio helping Ceci record and on the horn talking to clients for Nameless Records. I'm going from Burbank for Leno, to New York for Conan, to Atlanta for Larry King, back to New York for Stern, and then the overseas publicities. I'm running The Elements and putting together the premier party for the movie. Then, you factor in the one thing I've ever wanted to do with Master of the Mat on us, and I don't have time to close my eyes, let alone sleep.' Rob's eyes had been getting larger and larger with each sentence Ken formed. How he longed for that type of life. He didn't know how anyone could make it sound like there were any type of burden involved in it, such as Ken spoke. 'Don't get me wrong,' the Superstar continued, seemingly reading his lawyer's mind, 'I wouldn't trade any of it for the world. I would just like a moment to breathe. Some time to enjoy my new home and spend some time there with Gunner and with Josh. Have some time to bring Ace and Bill and Ceci over to grill out on a grill I haven't even gotten to get dirty yet. I'd like to lay and sleep in my own bed with the woman I love. I'd just like a moment to, you know, be Ken.' Ken broke his ramblings with a yawn as he stretched his arms over his head, causing the black fringed short-sleeved button-up to rise a bit, showing his fresh new naval piercing just above his charcoal washed Oakley jeans that fell over his black flip-flops. 'So, please, Rob, just tell me what the contract says.' Robert, with a new care in his eyes, pulled the papers nearer to him and began to sort through them. 'They're just your basic legal documents, Ken,' he began. 'Stating the ammount of money you'd be earning and so on. Now, the money's right, in fact it's a touch more than what you were looking for, but there's one catch along with it.' 'What's that,' asked an irritated KPJ. 'They want you to do three episodes instead of two,' Rob finished. 'That's the catch,' asked Ken with a new vibrance. 'Hell yeah, I'll do an extra episode of Will and Grace. Are you kidding me?' 'Okay,' concluded Rob. 'That means you just need to sign right there,' he said as he pointed to a line at the bottom of the last page with an 'X' sitting on it. 'There are a couple of other things,' contuned McDonald as Ken etched out his signature on the assigned line. 'Angela Robinson got in touch with me about--' 'Wait,' said Ken, cutting of the man that was pretty much acting as his agent as well as his lawyer. 'Angela Robinson? As in, the writer for 'The L Word?' 'Yeah,' shot Rob. 'You watch that show?' 'Religiously,' resounded Ken enthusiastically. 'Ceci and I don't miss an episode and I have the first season on dvd. Kate Moennig is amazing. Shane is my favorite character.' 'Really,' responded a half-interested Rob. 'Well, Ms. Robinson metioned her name, actually. It seems she's quite a fan of yours, as well. Ms. Moennig and Ms. Robinson have requested a lunch with you to talk appearances on upcoming episodes in Season Four.' 'Absolutly,' responded Ken, waking more by the moment. 'You have my shcedule, Rob. If I'm not wrestling or shooting the movie, shift to convenience them, but put it together.' 'Okay, well, that took care of itself rather effortlessly,' smirked the lawyer. 'Also,' he continued, 'Michael Grief--' The new Superman broke his lawyer's words with a girlish super-scream. 'Michael Grief,' question a now shouting KPJ. 'The director of RENT?' 'Yeah, and I know you're a fan of it. Well, they're doing auditions and he heard about you singing talents and that you were pssionate about the show. He wanted me to let you know that they want you in the part of Roger if you'd like to come sing the part for them.' A single tear strolled down Ken's face in glee. The role of Roger had been a dream of his since the Tony winning Broadway musical made it's debut in 1996. Jonathan Larson's moving play about a group of friends and lovers living a year in a life plagued by AIDS and love had been one that immediatly captured the hearts of Ken and Todd, his best-friend since the age of six taken from the world by way of the AIDS virus. 'Tell him that I would be honored and moved,' responded Ken solemnly and calmly before picking himself up from his chair and making his way out of the office. 'Wait,' yelled Rob before Ken could walk out of the door. 'We have more to talk about.' 'Later,' said a smiling Ken. 'This has been a good day that's finished on a wonderful note. I'm now going home, to my new house, and laying in my bed with my girlfriend before I have to be in Vegas tomorrow.' 'Well,' continued Rob somewhat baffled, 'what should I tell the media?' 'Tell them,' paused Ken, contemplating the best words ofr use. 'Tell them that there's No Day but Today,' he said, deciding on the theme from RENT. 'Tell them nothing more. The true fans will get it.' With that, Ken exited the office with a smile on his face, closing the door behind him. Rob couldn't be certain, but he thought he'd heard a shout of glee in the hall shortly after the door had clicked closed.
