Coming Out (Part One)

13 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11.13.05 'Coming Out' (Part One) To understand the future, you must first reflect upon the past. Vincent High School was an odd sight as it stood surrounded by snow for the first time in ten years. The weather in that part of Alabama just doesn't ever seem to permit a white Christmas, regardless of how cold it may get and, seeing as it was late October, the snowfall seemed especially odd. Things on the campus were calm and those that had chosen to spend their time before classes began participating in snowball fights and making snow angels had since retreated to the school halls once the bell had rang. The parking lot now looked something like a car filled ghost town as a light blue 1985 Dodge LeBaron with a white soft top pulled in going far too fast considering the slickness of the pavement. It pulled into a parking space near the football field, which had a sign hanging above it's entrance reading 'Harris Stadium,' and the door flew open as a pair of feet hit the ground hard and immediatly forced themselves into a run as the car door slammed. The sounds of a bookbag filled far too heavy hitting against a back echoed through the concourse before the seventeen year old opened on of the side entrance's door and scooted inside. The blonde headed young man checked his watch, which read a far too acurate '8:28.' 'Damn,' escaped from his lips as he took off in a silent powerwalk through out the school's bottom hall. The boy, dressed in a pair of Guess label blue jeans that fell above a pair of black immitation Prada tuxedo shoes and a red Ralph Lauren sweater, finally came to a halt in front of a door at the opposite end of the corridor. He cleared his throat and checked his hair before gentley turning the handle and slowly opening the door. 'Nice of you to join us, Ken,' smiled the bargain dressed teacher in a white oxford adorned with an awful perriwinkle blue tie and a pair of khakis somewhat sarcastically. 'We were just discussing what Khafka was trying to relate in his work Metamorphisis. Perhaps you'd like to take your seat and enlighten us with your opinion.' 'Yes, sir, Mr. Mehaffey,' Ken exhaled before grabbing his desk behind a very attractive bruenette girl dressed in a pair of jeans and a grey Calvin Klein 'CK' sweatshirt. He sat his bag next to him as the awkwardness of a room full of eyes watched over him pull out his Psychology book from his overstuffed bag, followed by a spiral bound college-ruled notebook and a paperback of Franz Khafka's works. 'Well, then,' Mr. Mehaffey continued, 'now that were finally good-to-go, Mr. Partridge, why don't you explain to us the relevance of the apple stuck under Khafka's character's wing once he had fully transformed into the cockroach.' 'Well,' Ken began after clearing his throat, 'to be perfectly honest, I don't think that you can even begin to delve into that until you realize why he felt the transformation happened in the first place.' A smile spread across the teacher's face. 'Fine, then... indulge us.' Ken returned Mehaffey's smile with one of his own before he began on his analysis. 'Khafka felt useless as an adolescent, which spun around issues with his parents, namely his father, whome he was never good enough for. His dad treated him like he was a lower form of life... a cockroach, if you will, which is the grounds which his own low self-esteem were based upon. He'd been treated like cockroach for so long that he began to feel like one.' 'Very good,' Mr. Mehaffey commended, 'but what has the apple got to do with any of that?' 'Quite frankly,' he continued, 'the apple doesn't fall far from the tree. He felt that the reason his father made him feel this way is because his fathere viewed himself the same, which is why the object used was that particular piece of fruit. You could spin other meanings into it, like that the apple was the fruit given to Eve and later consumed by, and choked on, by Adam to make man less than perfect and begin a lifetime of sin. One may say that Khafka blamed and cursed God for making his family the way it was and thus was the relevance, that the entire chain could have prevented if only he'd not made that single apple. This opinion does hold water, and could very well contribute to the overall effect of the stuck apple drying out his body, essentially killing that part of him... killing his shell and making him vulnerable.' A silence was in the room as the eyes of Ken's classmates tore away from him and, with smiles on their faces, turned upon Mr. Mehaffey, smiling himself and leaning forward on his podium to the left of the blackboard. 'Well, then,' the teacher said, breaking the quiet, 'I'm glad at least one person, late as he may be, paid attention to the heppenings in the book and in the lesson.' Before he could say anymore, he was interrupted by a voice booming over the intercom system mounted above the blackboard in the center of the wall. 'Mr. Mehaffey-' 'Yes?' 'Principal Martin would like to see you in his office, please.' A look of inpatience fell over his face as he answered a short 'fine.' He then turned his attention back to the already bussing class before he exited. 'I'll be back before the end of class,' escaped his lips before he looked at Ken and nodded with another smile on his face as he walked out of the door. The talk began immediatly when the door shut and the young lady sitting in front of Ken turned to face him with a satisfied look on her face. 'Late again. Tsk, tsk.' 'Har Har,' he replied. 'I couldn't get myself out of bed this morning thanks to the cold. On top of that, the pipes were frozen and I had to go to my grandparents place to get ready. I thought your dad was gonna' roast me when I walked in half an hour late.' She just smiled as she reached back to hold his hand. 'Believe it or not, you're the first guy I've dated that he's actually liked.' 'That's because he's the first guy you've dated that wasn't four or five years older than you, Megan,' came a voice from Ken's best-friend in the desk to the right of her, prompting a laugh from her boyfriend. 'Very funny, Todd,' Megan expressed with a half-amused and half-annoyed expression on her face. 'But, maybe it's because Ken's intelligent, funny, and good-looking.' 'If Lloyd approved of me because I'm good-looking, we may need to have a talk with him,' Ken laughed. Megan and Todd both laughed along with him before Ken's best-friend was taken from the conversation by a new girl, named Ashley, that apparantly had a crush on him. Ken knew that Todd wasn't out, but he blew his mind how anyone could think that Todd wasn't gay. 'So, you're gonna' tell him?' asked Megan, taking Ken by surprise. 'Tell who what?' 'Your step-dad. You said last night when we left the movie that you were gonna' tell him.' At the mention of the movie, Ken let a sneer pull the left side of his upper lip into an arch. 'Tell me again why I spent ten dollars on Speed.' 'Stop it!' Megan smiled as she hit him playfully on the arm. 'You're avoiding the question.' 'Avoiding or not, that movie sucked. It was Bill and Ted under a bus. We should have gone to see Pulp Fiction' 'Last week we went to see Ace Ventura because you wanted to,' Megan explained. 'Last night I got to choose. Now answer the question.' Ken let out a deep sigh of anxiety before replying with, 'yeah, I'm telling him tonight.' 'Telling who what?' asked a pretty blonde headed girl to Ken's left, dressed similar to Megan, but wearing a white VHS Seniors sweatshirt. 'I'm telling William that I'm bisexual, Tina,' Ken whispered. 'You're WHAT?' she exhasperated. 'William's a red-neck. I know that he's taken care of you like you were his own son for the past eight years, but think this through, Ken. He goes deer hunting, you go clothes shopping. He wears camoflauge, you wear sports coats. He listens to Garth Brooks, you listen to Barbara Streissand.' 'I do NOT listen to Barbara Streissand,' Ken quickly corrected. 'Yeah,' laughed Megan, 'he listen to Cher.' 'Don't forget Madonna,' chimed in Todd, apparantly back in the conversation. The four laughed before Ken pointed at Todd, dressed in a pair of black Dockers with black Timberlands and a maroon button-up by Bugle Boy. 'You do, too, hetero-boy.' 'Blah blah blah,' laughed Todd. 'I don't SAY I'm straight,' whispered Todd, 'I just let people assume it.' 'Jesus, Todd,' laughed Megan, 'how many people do you think actually ASSUME that you're straight? Come on, it's 1994. I think it's pretty safe to say that people know a queer when they see one... no offense.' 'None taken,' smiled Todd 'Ken,' Tina continued, 'just think about it, first.' Ken's face went from laughter to seriousness in seconds. 'Trust me, Tina, I have. i came out to my mom, and she's okay with it now, especially since Megan and I are dating. I just don't want to keep it from William anymore. I'm out to everyone but him. Sure, I may get lude comments and hateful looks in the hall from time-to-time and yeah, there were a group of guys that tried to get me kicked off both the wrestling and the football teams, but overall, evrything's been okay.' 'We just don't want you to get hurt,' Megan said with concern in her tone and on her face. 'What?' question Ken in disbelief. 'When we discussed this after watching that train-wreck of a movie last night you were behind me one hundred percent.' 'And, I still am,' she reiterated. 'That doesn't mean I'm not concerned. I just... I really wish you'd let me be there when you did it... you know... to hold your hand... to be your support.' 'Trust,' Ken said, 'I'll be fine. The worse thing he can do is kick me out, and my grandparent's have already told me I could live in the apartment they have in their basement this summer before I go to college. I'll just move in a little early. Not that it's even an issue. I mean, I doubt very seriously it'll come down to that.' The conversations in the room came to a quick halt as the door opened and Mr. Mehaffey entered the room again. He closed it behind him and assumed his position at the podium. 'It seems that we don't have much time left, so I'll make this quick. Tonight, I want you all to write a three to seven page paper on your psychoanalysis of the empty nest syndrome in the Khafka house and compare it to the happenings in your own right now. Seeing as you'll all be leaving for college after this year, I'm sure there are a few experiences worth putting on paper.' He finished his last sentence in just enough time before the bell rang. He attempted to finish through the rustle and hustle of the class putting away their books and exiting the room. 'On my desk by the end of class tomorrow.' His word fell mostly on deaf ears as Ken and Megan, hand-in-hand, walked by him. 'Ken,' Lloyd called, 'may I have a quick word?' Ken simply shrugged and dropped his bag back down to his feet. 'See you in Trig?' Megan asked. 'Yeah,' Ken replied as she gave him a quick kiss on the cheek. 'Bye, Dad,' she waved as she exited the room, leaving only Ken and Mr. Mehaffey to discuss before Lloyd's next class filled the room. 'You need to go talk to Mr. Martin,' Lloyd said in reference to the high school principal. 'Apparantly, some guys are trying to stir some things up again.' 'What do you mean?' asked Ken curiously. 'Well, apparantly, two guys told him that you said you were making a list and were gonna' start checking off names. He told me because he knew that you and I were able to go outside of the student-teacher relationship and into friendship.' 'That's ridiculous,' Ken laughed. 'He doesn't believe them, does he?' 'Of course not, but this is serious, Ken. It's something we have to look into. I mean, this could prevent you from graduating.' 'What?' Ken blinked. 'It's a rumor. A couple of homophobes, probably Cameron Keller and Jim Bob Roper, are just trying to get to me again. How can that prevent me graduating?' 'It's a serious offense, Ken, especially in light of the school shooting last year in Grayson, Kentucky. Your scholarships to AAMDA, NYU, and UK could be in jeopardy, as well.' 'I can't believe this,' Ken stated with anger building in his face and sorrow in his voice. 'Don't do anything drastic, Ken. I'm behind you and so is Mr. Martin. You know that Mrs. Roland, Mrs. Estill, and Mr. Williamson are behind you and Coach Harris, Coach Brooks, and Coach Garrett won't do anything but sing your praises. It's just something that has to be completely examined.' Their conversation was cut short by new students entering the room for English 10. 'Just go see Mr. Martin. I'll let Mr. Williamson know that you're going to be late for class.' 'Yeah,' Ken said somberly while nodding his pale face and pulling his bag back over his shoulder. 'Okay.' 'Don't worry, Ken,' Lloyd said to him as he was exiting the room. 'We'll make sure everything's okay.'
