motm2-misfortunate coincidences [of two].

21 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Monday - july 21st, 2003 Cut into the history. I’m with Jay. We had a big party the other night; alcohol, drugs and women flowed. Today, Jay Robson introduced me to another side of the wrestling world. A place I’ve never been nor had to deal with before, it’s what I guess you could call the dark side. Murders, drug smuggling, and money laundering ? oh my. He told me that this is the life people in Global Wrestling live. Did I mention he was leading me on? Yeah, that’s Jay Robson for you folks. Always the prankster, you could say. He had me chew down on a piece of wax (aka LSD on blotter paper) and then start twirling around a gun. What do you expect to happen when I’m hallucinating and carrying around a deadly weapon? Bad stuff. Anyway, I’m sitting here in the car with Jay. It’s the SL500 Roadster, sheathed in black. It’s a little after noon right now. He’s laughing hysterically. 'Are you serious?' 'Yep,' he replies, barely able to get the word out. 'The entire thing is...a joke?' 'What? Did you really think that guy was a criminal? HAHA!' 'Heh,' I laugh along. 'Yeah. Funny stuff, very ha-ha Jay.' 'Come on Reedy,' he says and pops the thing into first. 'Drinks are on me.' I sigh and sit back in the seat, relaxing as much as I can. That was too close to happening. ---- 'Are you sure, man?' I ask, looking across the way. 'Trey. You wanted to learn, there’s no turning back now.' Where we are at is Broad street, it’s around the heart of Southern California. We are sitting in the SL500 Roadster, passing off binoculars between each other. We’re looking across the street, where two brick apartment structures meet to make a small back alley. There isn’t much to it, a couple back doors with windows higher up on either building, along with a dumpster. What we’re looking at in particular is the old man that stepped out of the left building’s closest back door about a second ago. He had a garbage bag in each hand, and was heading towards the dumpster. No idea of us. No worries in the whole world. 'All I’m saying is that he doesn’t look like who you were talking about. How could he even stand a chance of doing that? Look at him.' 'That’s him.' 'I...I don’t know, man.' 'This is do or die time right now, Trey.' Deep breath, step out of the car and tuck the gun into my pants. Take a step off the street and onto the curb, walking faster and faster. Briskly, sneaking up behind him. Reach into the back of my pants, pull it out. It’s a Glock .357, small but effective. Two steps. One step. Directly behind him. Reach out with the left forearm, sweep it across his neck and pull him back tightly. Twirl the gun once in my right hand, flick off the safety and drag it to his head. 'Don’t think about moving, Frank. It’s over.' What I’m doing right now is holding a gun to the temple of Forceful Frankie, a man who’s killed hundreds with his bare hands. He’s turned into an old man now, since he went into hiding after he became a snitch for the FBI. However, he’s massive just like Jay had told me, about 6’6 and upwards of 250. Let up on my neck hold for a second, to throw him down against the wall. He fell into a slump, coughing, and I pressed my foot onto his chest, keeping my eyes above the barrel, aiming it directly at the forehead. 'Frank?' He questions, scared and sick. 'I don’t know who Frank is.' 'Funny. So I guess I should call you by one of the many aliases you’ve collected up since then?' 'I’m just dropping off my garbage. I live upstairs in that apartment. Please...' Press my shoe harder into his chest and he coughs out again. 'Enough jokes. You hid out, you tried to get away with heinous crimes. It didn’t work out. It’s over now.' Cock back the hammer; begin to pull on the trigger. 'Is that REEDY?' A voice yells out. 'Yo, I think it is,' Another voice responds. 'Trey, mon!' Let go of the trigger and turn to see whom it is, keeping Frank down tightly. '...Jack? Rico?' Jack Marley and Rico, to fill you in, are people from my wrestling past. If you’ve heard of JD Lawson, then you might have heard of these two. They are what he calls his lackeys, for lack of a better term. They live with him, hang out with him, do his bidding. I know them from FWF, I used to hang out with them a lot backstage. Jack looked up to me a lot, for various reasons (such as my weed addiction). 'Dude, Trey, what the hell are you doing mon?' Jack asks. 'I’m...handling business.' 'That’s some weird business, mon.' 'No, it’s...I have to get rid of this guy. He’s a former mobster, he’s wanted. Bounty on his head.' Jack turns to Rico, and tries to whisper, but ends up talking loud enough. 'Hey mon, I think I know what’s going on.' 'What is it?' Rico replies. 'Reed’s so broke dude, he’s gone insane. He must have spent all his money on pot, and now he’s robbing hobos for money.' 'Oh, yeah. That makes sense.' 'Yeah mon. What should we do?' 'Well, JD has money.' 'Yah! We could bring Reedy to JD and like, he can ask him for money.' 'Let’s do it.' 'Rasta style mon!' Then Jack turns back to me. 'Reedy, mon. You gotta forget about this guy and come with us to meet JD.' 'JD’s still around, eh?' 'Yah. He’s totally rich too, he could help you out.' 'I’m not brok-' but I stopped. Seeing JD again, one of the richest men I’ve ever known. He’s also always nice to me. Shrug. 'Sure, but I can’t right now. Can I get at you guys another day?' 'Yah mon. Here.' Jack says and hands me something. Address, phone number, whatever. 'See you around Wheedmon. And stop the robbing bums thing, mon.' 'Oh, he’s not...' Never mind. Wave them off. Talk about a coincidence, highly unlikely. So, back to him. Lift gun back up. 'Please, no...' 'Sigh.' Set gun back down. 'I can’t do this.' Leave the guy, walk back to the car. Jay’s laughing. 'What’s so funny, man?'
