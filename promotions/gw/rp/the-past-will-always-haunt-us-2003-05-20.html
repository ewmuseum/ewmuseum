The Past Will Always Haunt Us

20 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5.20.03 May 16th, 2003, The Adolphus, Dallas, Texas. Fading from black, we hear the blasting of Eminem’s The Eminem Show. The music blasts from behind a closed suite door at the end of a luxurious hallway. A large portrait hangs on the wall about four feet from the door; a light fixture hangs upon the opposite wall. Our view does not change although we do hear voices coming from behind our location. The frame bounces up and down with the pulse of the music, the glass inside the frame rattling. An employee of the hotel, dressed in a bellboy uniform, runs towards the source of the music from the other end of the hallway. He attempts to hold the portrait flat for a moment, before rushing to the door and knocking on it. He waits a few seconds and begins screaming and pounding on the door. He continues screaming and pounding, growing more and more agitated. We can barely hear his words above the powerful bass coming from the hotel room. “MR. PIERCE! PLEASE TURN YOUR MUSIC DOWN; YOU’RE BOTHERING THE OTHER GUESTS! MR. PIERCE!” The music stops suddenly but the hotel official continues to bang on the door, screaming at the top of his lungs. Only one other patronizes this floor and that person steps from their door, dressed in pajamas and wearing a pair of ear muffs. “TURN THE DAMNED MUSIC DOWN!” The last phrase has caught the attention of everyone around and a couple of person on the floor above and directly below. The door opens as he continues slamming his fist on it, Tyler Pierce steps into the hallway dressed in a towel marked with the hotel’s insignia. The man straightens his own jacket and clears his throat, attempting not to look into the eyes of the silent Tyler Pierce. Finally he speaks, “sir, I” we? I was wondering if you could turn your music down. We’ve had quite a few complaints from the other guests.” Tyler says nothing, simply staring down the attendant. Silence fills the hallway until the sound of a door closing is heard from behind us, the other patron having left the area. A step is taken backwards as the official attempts to walk away. Tyler smirks and turns, closing the door. The attendant quickly grabs his cart and begins walking away, glancing over his shoulder at the door closing slowly behind him. He speaks, attempting to salvage a little of his pride and the victory in this situation. “Thank you for cooperating, Mr. Pierce.” A laugh is heard from behind the cracked door as it fully closes. We catch a look into the face of the attendant who continues walking towards the elevator with his cart of materials. He presses the button on the elevator console and folds his hands before him, waiting for the doors to open. Just as the doors open, the Adolphus attendant steps onto the elevator with his cart, closing the doors behind him. As the doors begin have almost fully close, the sounds of Eminem’s 8-Mile blast through the hallway causing the attendant to spin and stare down the door. He attempts to exit the elevator but trips over his own cart and another hotel guests foot and falls face first inside the elevator. He lands so that half of his body is inside the elevator and the other is on the outside; with the doors attempting to close, they keep hitting his sides over and over again, not stopping long enough for him to pose a good chance to getting up. Although the doors can’t hurt him, the scene is rather humorous as he attempts to pull himself away from the metal doors, squirming and kicking as the reason he came to this floor in the first place continues at full strength. The guests in the elevator, prim and proper attempt to hide their snickers as the attendant fights to get free of the door. He clumsily struggles up just to fall again. As we watch, we fade to black. The scene fades up on a Western European atmosphere, the room designed like an old English Inn. A four post bed is placed against the far wall with the room designed around the posh bed. A towel has been tossed onto the foot of the bed haphazardly, the color being the only part of the room that doesn’t flow perfectly. A few moments pass and the booming sounds of 8-Mile cease. A young woman, dressed in a thigh length white silk robe walks towards the bed. Her long brown hair is wet and has soaked the top part of the robe, causing it to stick to her flawless skin. She takes a seat on the bed, carefully as to not to show anything beneath the robe, and reaches back onto a nearby night table, picking up a brush and running its teeth through her hair meticulously. She closes her eyes, smiling as she continues what would be to most, a dull and monotonous routine “Hey, where’s my cell?” Tyler’s words ring forth in the room and the young, beautiful woman looks up and off-screen. “I don’t see it, Ty.” With her words she stands and places the brush on the bed before walking around the bed and looking into his duffle bag. Within seconds, her hair cascades down the side of her face. She reaches up, brushing it back behind her ear and then reaches into the bag again, pulling out a small flip phone. “Tyler, I found it.” Her voice calls out towards the opposite side of the room. Tyler makes his first appearance inside the room, dressed in a pair of carpenter jeans and no shirt. His hair is dripping of water and has been combed back, the water rolling down and off the tips to land on his upper back. He reaches for and takes the cell from the hands of the beautiful brunette before him. A smile crosses her lips as she steps in, kissing his cheek and running her hands down his chest; she leans in, whispering in his ear. A grin crosses his lips as he presses a button on the now opened cell phone. He continues to gaze at the cell phone as she works her way from his cheek to his neck, kissing. His eyes however seem to be focused on the cell phone but one hand remains around her, rubbing her upper back. She lowers off screen and then comes back up, sliding against his chest as she does so; she looks up at him however, noting his lack of interest and sighs, stepping away. “Sorry” she utters as she returns to the foot of the bed and picks up her brush. Tyler however seems oblivious as he continues reading the text on his phone’s screen. Suddenly, he flinches and brings the phone to his ear. “You called?” The voice on the other end is inaudible but unmistakably there. Tyler walks back towards the other side of the room, our view following him this time. He picks a shirt up from a desk and glances down at it for a moment as he listens. “Roswell huh” Sounds promising.” The voice continues as Tyler listens, growing weary of this conversation. Tyler’s the type of person who prefers to know what’s going on but only the blunt explanation; he then takes lead and does what needs to be done. Tyler lifts an eyebrow and glances back at the young woman seated on his bed. It’s as if her actions from before are just now taking effect in the way he looks at her, his eyes moving over the entirety of her body. The voice on the other end of the phone ceases and waits for a response from Tyler. Tyler continues to gaze at the woman whose advances he just turned down. “Yeah yeah yeah, Roswell, Area 51, hydrogen balloons and shit. Gotcha, I’ll call you, don’t call me.” With his quick words, Tyler places the phone on the receiver and approaches the brunette from behind and runs his hands around her. At first she ignores him but upon kissing her neck, a grin comes over her face and she shoves him to the bed. We fade to black as she climbs atop him and throws her robe back towards the camera. May 20th, 2003, Logan International, Boston, Massachusetts. The sky is dark, the clouds opaque, and the view is limited. Lights shine through the night, illuminating the runways of Logan International, one of the Eastern Seaboard’s premier airports. The view pans slightly, allowing us now to see a limo parked in a specially marked zone about twenty feet from the end of the runway. Although the area is lit for the most part, the limousine is still, for the most part, covered in shadows and therefore partially hidden from our eyes. A few moments pass, the airfield busy with departing and arriving flights from various locations. Emblems mark the tails and sides of the Boeing 747’s: American Air, Continental, Delta, US Airways, and many more. The limo sits at the end of an airstrip set apart from the numerous other strips with a pair of helicopter pads nearby; but only one of which is occupied. A few more minutes pass when a blinking light of an incoming plane appears in the night above. The difference in this and the many others that have landed in the past few minutes is that this one is coming towards our location and slowly descending. The driver’s side door opens and the sounds of a speakerphone can be heard from inside, complete with static. “This is the plane now. Mr. Pierce should be on the ground shortly.” A man, dressed in a full Chauffeur’s uniform topped off with the clich? drivers cap steps out and closes his door. With a quick step, he walks around to the opposite side of the car and stands next to the truck. Moments pass and the plane touches down, a jet black Piper Saratoga II with some type of insignia on the side like all the rest. The difference between this and the others is that this insignia is not visible due to this being a darker area. None of the airfields lights seem to capture the wording on the side of the jet until the last moment when an elegant “T” can be seen. This only last for a split second however as the plane rolls past this point on the air strip and comes to a halt some 20 yards from where the limo waits. The wheels screech and smoke spirals and flows from the back, filling the strip with smoke as it slows to a stop. The engine cuts off, the propeller slowing to a stop as the side door unhinges and lowers to the ground, forming a set of steps. Movement can be seen of the doorway as the interior of the plane is lit. Since light can not be seen through the windows on the passenger part of the plane, it can only be assumed that even the windows of this luxurious personal aircraft are tinted. Moments pass and Tyler Pierce exits from the jet, duffle bag in hand. A moment later, a man in a black business suit exits the plan, carrying a suitcase in one hand, a briefcase in the other. He walks a few paces behind Tyler, who is dressed casually adorning a pair of jeans, a “Prodigal” Pierce t-shirt and a thin brown leather jacket. He walks towards the limo and the chauffeur quickly takes the duffle bag from him and opens his door, allowing him to enter before going to the back and popping the trunk. He places the duffle bag inside and then the suitcase, followed by the briefcase. The chauffeur nods to the gentlemen from the plane and he quickly walks away. With this, he returns to his seat and closes the door. Within moments, the limo has departed and well on its way towards the exit. The scene fades to black. Harbor Side Inn of Boston, a premier hotel located near one of Boston’s most historical sites, the home of the Boston Tea Party, the Boston Harbor. The night has set its toll on the city, the area’s nightlife booming but the hours putting many citizens in their respective homes. The Harbor Side Inn of Boston is much similar, even the clerk is nowhere to be seen when Tyler and his Chauffeur enter the hotel. The chauffeur, of course, carries the suitcase and duffle bag that arrived with Tyler on the plane and Tyler carries the briefcase. Upon reaching the elevator and pushing the button, the doors open with little to no wait suggesting that very few people remain awake in this luxurious hotel. Pierce and his driver make their to the suite door, push it open and step inside. With the flick of an index finger, the room is brought into view and fully illuminated. The walls are made of brick and the room is luxurious although, sustaining its rustic appeal. The European look gives the room a grandeur that many hotels lack; you’ll note that Tyler has a superb taste when it comes to the establishments he stays in. He has this upon request in Boston, The Adolphus in Dallas, The Millennium Broadway in New York, The Hotel Oceana Santa Monica for his stays in Los Angeles, and The Ritz-Carlton Lake in Las Vegas. Tyler Pierce is not exactly one to stay in a Holiday Inn. The main room is lavish with three rooms spurring off from the main, a master bedroom, a second bedroom, and a washroom. Pierce says nothing at first but begins his walk towards the master bedroom, his eyes dead-set on the bed in the room before him. At the last moment, he glances over his shoulder the driver. “Don’t bother driving back, just stay in the other room tonight. Trevor is expecting us in the morning at 11. Then my plane leaves tomorrow at 3 for Dallas” my match with Roswell is in a couple of days.” Our scene fades to black. The scene fades in again but now, we see a sign outside of a veteran business in Boston: Alan Rouleau Couturier Tailor and Design. The sign is as elegant as the profession and as elegant as the black limo pulling up in front of the storefront. Tyler steps out and glances around the city, his eyes capturing many of the places he’s visited in his life; this tailor being one of the many. “So many memories. It’s almost nostalgic to even visit this city” as if I’m walking in the shoes of a man who’s already been buried.” Tyler walks steadily towards the entrance to the tailor, his eyes still searching the street around him. “This city is no longer my home, though” but it will always be special to me. Heh, whether I like it or not. The Tyler who used to live here is gone? he’s grown up and passed on; I’m his replacement.” Pierce, dressed simply in a pair of dress slacks and a dress shirt and tie, steps into the tailor’s store. The scene pans back, viewing numerous people walking in both directions up and down the street. Tyler’s voice can be heard over this as we watch: “This city is no longer my home. The Tyler Pierce who lived here was different” much different than I am today. I just wear his memories, nothing more.” The scene fades to black.
