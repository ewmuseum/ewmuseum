Back to the Begining...

24 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/24/04 'How'd I get back here?' That's what the voice in Train's head asked him as he sat in the passenger seat of the custom Denali. He wasn't sure how long he had been sitting there...just staring at it. It had seemed like years since he'd been back home to Chicago. And it seemed like decades since he had set eyes on this place. It made sense that time seemed so distorted. It might as well been a life time ago. The Train that sat in the truck on this bright, sunny Chicago day wasn't the same one that had left here two and a half months ago. The life he lead then, running, or more aptly, ruling the streets dead and gone. Well...gone anyway. Train hadn't really changed, just the way he made his money. That taste for violence? He could still taste sickly sweet flavor on his lips. But at least now it didn't leave him with a new face burned into his psyche to haunt him in his dreams. He could finally sleep at night without the nightmares...or the 12 guage next to his bed. He'd finally accomplished a dream. But, more importantly, he'd finally fulfilled his promise. Close to anyway. That was the only reason he was here now, looking at this building for the first time in years. 'The Bottoms Training Facility' read the faded red and silver letters on the weather beaten sign above the double doors that had been chained shut for ages now. The place hadn't always been like this. It hadn't always been tattered and old. Train remembered when the yellowish gold paint on the outside didn't looked like it had been tarnished. He remembered when the grass was cut and groomed immaculately, even in the midst of a neighborhood that looked like something you might scrape off your shoe. He remembered when the place used to tell folks to stop worrying so damn much about where you at and focus on where you wanna go. It drew people like a beacon. You might not be the next big thing when you walked out of there, but you knew whether you fought in boxing gloves or a business suit the ghetto couldn't keep you prisoner unless you let it. Of course it wasnt' so much the building that delivered the message as the man who owned the bulding. It was all Isaiah. Train had to keep his promise to him. After all, Isaiah had held up his end of the bargain. Looking back on it, he probably shouldn't have bothered. It's not like anyone would have blamed him if he hadn't. Why would anyone take time out of their life to try and talk some sense into some smart ass little punk who thought he was bad? Thought he could conquer the world with his bare hands? Or by sheer force of will... Of course, maybe that's exactly why Isaiah had done it. Maybe he thought within that scrawny, loud mouthed kid, there was a warrior waiting to come out... a will that wouldn't be conquered... the heart of a champion. Whatever. It didn't even matter why now. Bottom line? Train owed the man. He told Isaiah he was goign to be the best. Isaiah lived up to his end, damned if Train wasn't going to do the same. Of course, it's hard to do all that loosing to some drunk ass whiteboy like McNasty. Son of a bitch... Train wasn't sure how he'd gotten so far off his game that he could get beat by some carrot top looking muthafucka he'd beat down twice before. Something was wrong with that. The game plan, was getting as scattered as his focus nowadays. Brick... McNasty... Makonie. Naw, the shit was getting too complicated. He was so busy trying to conquer that world he forgot to fight the battles in between the victories. He had to get back to basics. Cant' get no more basic than going back to where you started from. One problem with the starting line though. Now matter where you ended up...when you go back to the begining? Those damn ghost are always right there...waiting on yo' ass. 'We gone do this?' inquired the gruff, raspy voice coming from the driver's seat. Train glanced over. He caught his own reflection in the silver chrome lenses of Biggie's shades as he glanced at Train. Train couldn't help, but notice how flushed he looked. He was glad his boy was there for support. He'd done a lot of things in his life that should've intimidated him and didn't. It wasn't like that this time though. He needed the back up. The big man, all 6'10', 365 pounds of him had been sitting back quietly... waiting on his friend. Train hadn't even realized he was swinging the pad lock key around his finger until he'd been jarred out of his trance by the deep, gravely voice. It was hard for Train not to feel like a punk. Sitting here. Outside a building he owned. A building he hadn't been in since that night. But there was no more holding out. He had to remember how he'd gotten to where he was. He had to start from the begining if he was going to get any farther. 'Yeah,' Train responded in a humble tone that he had probably not spoken in since he was a child. 'Let's do this.' The passenger and driver door swing open at virtually the same time and close in unison. The two men step up onto the sidewalk in front of the gym and stand for a moment. Finally, Train forces himself forward. The two close in on the front doors as the scene fades to black.
