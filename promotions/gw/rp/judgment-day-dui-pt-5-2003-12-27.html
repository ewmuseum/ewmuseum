Judgment Day (DUI pt. 5)

27 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/27/03 When most people are brought into a courtroom knowing that they've done something wrong and are going to be found guilty of it, a sense of imminent fear rushes over them. Sweat pours from more glands then they even knew before. Heart rate rises to an immeasurable pace. Even their stride becomes a slight jittery as their knees begin to shake uncontrollably. Such it is for most people. Still some are exceptional and can handle it. Two large walnut doors turn forward opening a path through the aisle of the audience. Escorted under both arms, Johnie is nudged past the small crowd and toward the defendant's bench on the left. Strange how people still find the law so interesting. Even mere unnamed cases can draw a moderate crowd of onlookers. Almost as if seeing the law in action is some form of entertainment. Much like when a person was sentenced to hang and half the town came to see it just to say they were there. Walking confidently in stride, Johnie stares forward at the judge's bench still empty as his critic has yet to make his appearance. Letting out a small smirk, he glances over toward his own bench as his lawyer, Percy, fumbles through his papers going in and out of his thin briefcase. Surprised he even showed up, he chuckles to himself and steps forward. As Johnie approaches the gate dividing the gallery from the courtroom floor, a young child runs in front of him causing him and his escorts to pause. A small girl, not much more than five or six years of age, skips forward as she plays with her hand-me- down red shorts. The innocence still gleams over her darkly tanned face as she has yet to meet the world with any real insight. Her spirit still not broken. There's still hope for her. Although instead of just running by she turns her deep brown eyes towards him, looking up in awe at the size of Johnie. She raises her right hand up to brush some fallen strands of her matted black hair from her eyes. Yet instead of staring blankly up at him, she turns her head to the side slightly and giggles loudly revealing a staggered set of baby teeth. 'Rosanna!' her mother chants causing the child to whip her head to the left. Johnie follows suit and glances over at her mother sitting in the third row bench, cradling a wrapped up newborn over her chest. 'Come back over here and sit down.' Turning back toward Johnie, Rosanna takes a step closer to him and slowly extends her palm up toward him. Able to nudge his right arm free from the bailiff, Johnie returns the greeting as the chain from the handcuffs dangles between his wrists as he accepts greeting from the little sprite below him. 'Nice to meet you sir,' she manages to mumble out staggeringly. Johnie can say nothing, but not in humble gratitude. As she retracts her hand, she smiles brightly muttering a quick reply of 'Good luck!' before running off to tackle her mother's left thigh. Rosanna's giggling echoes throughout the auditorium-like court as Johnie is continued on shuffling through the swinging gates to his bench. Percy turns to him quickly before hopping up in his chair a bit, startled. 'OH!' he shouts as he shuffles through his papers, nudging them over to the opposite side of the table to allow room for his client to sit down. 'Oh my. I didn’t expect you to be here so soon.' Johnie leans his wrists straight out allowing the officers to remove the manacles from him. In doing so, the officer on the right scowls up at him angrily. As he finishes unshackling him, Johnie gurgles back and spits in his dark face. The saliva drips down over his nose and down his left cheek as it flinches and flexes in anger. Jumping back, Johnie watches as the two officers struggle against each other as the one tries to charge at him while his partner holds him back. Being shoved back through the gate, the black cop yells back at Johnie. 'You'd better watch yourself, you little prick!' Leaning himself backwards raising his arms in the air, Johnie smugs back cockily. 'Hey, maybe you should work on your manners. Its not polite to stare at people.' Laughing loudly as he watches the two policemen fight their way though the rear doors, he swaggers over to sit next to a nervous Percy. 'So what do you have, Sparky?' Stammering over his tongue, Percy flips his papers into order and stacks them to the right of his chewed up leather J.C. Penney briefcase. 'My name is Percy, Mr. Ringo. And I would appreciate it if you would respect that,' he mutters in a soft tone. Johnie puckers up his face, leaning back in his hard wood chair causing it to squeak loudly. Raising an eyebrow to his lawyer he busts out laughing. Percy stops shuffling and looks over at his client. 'When..ah...when did you decide to grow a backbone to me?' Johnie replies as he tries to compose himself. 'Suddenly you want me to respect you? Dream on, pencil dick.' Reclining back in his chair, he crosses his arms over his chest looking around the ceiling at the wooden archways at the front of the room awaiting the arrival of the judge. 'So what are we looking at then, verdict wise?' Sighing deeply, Percy reluctantly obliges the question with a response. 'I worked it out pretty well for you. I got the obstruction of a criminal investigation wiped out for lack of evidence. The open alcohol container was easy to shake. It was a minor charge and there were only empty bottles in the vehicle at the time so there was nothing that could totally stick. As for the minor charge you can’t shake it. That's going through along with the Driving Under the Influence. Just be glad you're pleading guilty because that lessens the penalty.' Johnie leans forward and turns toward Percy raising his brow. 'So what is the penalty?' 'Well...' As he tries to clear his throat and continue, Percy is interrupted by the booming voice of an officer near the witness stand, causing the gallery to jump to their feet. Johnie lacks behind by a couple seconds, but eventually rises. 'ALL RISE! Criminal court is now in session. Judge Ruth Holt presiding.' As the bailiff's announcement ends, a large, fire-red haired woman enters the room with her dark robe brushing over the flooring. She turns sharply and bounces her way up to her podium at the top. As she leans over to sit down, Johnie’s jaw drops slightly. 'You didn’t tell me the judge was a chick,' he whispers over to Percy who quickly adjusts his cheap gray tie. Shrugging back, Percy doesn’t reply as Johnie can only sweat under his breath. 'You may be seated,' declares Judge Holt as she pulls up her chair and begins to read over the docket for the day in a monotone demeanor. 'Ok. Let's get down to business. First case, The People vs. Johnathan Ringo. Charges state: Driving Under the Influence and Consumption of Alcohol by a Minor. How do you plead?' Still standing Percy clears his throat and answers in a kind tone. 'My client has pleaded guilty on both charges, your honor.' Nodding solemnly, the judge continues. 'Excellent, a quick case counselor. So you’re saying your client readily accepts judgment and is willing to agree to the delivered punishment?' 'Yes he is, your honor.' Percy answers in a bright smile as he sees the end in sight. But Johnie isn’t as responsive. He feels his heart sink a bit as he awaits the verdict as sweat begins to perspire on his brow. 'Terrific! Fines are set at $750 and $265. I will also add surcharge fees of $300.' As each dollar sign was stamped by his name, a little piece of him seemed to be sold off to the lowest bidder. 'Johnathan Ringo,' continued the evil wench. 'I suspend your driver's license for no less than ninety days along with thirty hours of Alcoholic Anonymous meetings. And finally, I sentence you to forty-eight hours of community service.' As the word comes down, so does Johnie’s spirits. His confidence he once had has been shot to hell once again. His jaw hanging low and letting his mouth sag open a bit as he can only stand in shock. 'Case Dismissed!' shouts the wrinkled lady as she bangs her gavel down on the block as the case ends. As the hammer strikes, so does Johnie. 'You’ve got to be shitting me, your honor!!' Before he can even think anything intelligent to say, the first thing pops out. 'Community service?! Why the hell should I deserve that? So just for having a couple empty beer bottles in my truck, I get stuck babysitting some old fart for who knows how long? No fucking way!' Before he knows it, Johnie is standing before Judge Holt’s stand, shaking his index finger uncontrollably at her. His eyes nearly bulging out of the sockets as the rage builds to an uncontrollable meltdown. Percy runs up to Johnie and tries to snap him back into reality. Grabbing him by the shoulders, he pulls his client backwards little by little. As Judge Holt looks down at the troubled youth, she leans forward and smiles sympathetically at him. 'I'm sorry, Mr. Ringo. I didn’t know that my sentence would trouble you so much. I thought I was being light on you and letting you off with no jail time. But I guess I was wrong.' Johnie begins to calm down slightly as he hears the poetic mutters from the judge of above. Little by little, he regains composure over his senses. Crossing her fingers together, the judge leans back and frowns as she continues her heartfelt compassion. 'I’ll tell you what, Mr. Ringo...how does seventy-two hours of community service sound?' That did it... 'BITCH!!' Johnie shoves over Percy and tries to go toward Judge Holt’s steps, but instead meets up with three police officers and its carted backwards. 'Take him out of here,' is the last thing Johnie hears as he’s carted back to the cellblock in the rear of the building. Two officers holding his arms and two more on his legs. Writhing and thrashing, he tries to break free, but he can’t control himself enough to get there. He’s lost in his own anger. The doors close behind him as they swing shut, leaving the courtroom in the same peace it was before it was graced by Johnie Ringo. 'Next Case...' TO BE CONTINUED...
