More Excitement Reginald Atkins Global Wrestling Promo #2975 Date: 1/30/07 Location: Farmington, CT & Dallas Yancey Jetters is an old man, but some things never stop working, even in old age. Mr. Jetters has always been a shrewd businessman, his vision only focused on what will get him the most money and respect. This hasn’t changed since he was a hard working high school student, and it certainly won’t change now just because there are liver spots on his skin and his hair is nearly all gone. Mr. Jetters is also not the kindest of men, and this may go hand in hand with the previous statement. Men who are driven and focused on their goals of attaining wealth rarely operate outside of their shrewdness. For most businessmen, there is a daughter or wife, or some object of affection that centers them and keeps them from becoming a complete animal. Yancey Jetters has no such center. So, by that ruling, he would be a complete animal. But even animals break when they’re being tortured. Yancey’s arms are tied behind his back, and there is a streak of blood running down his left cheek. The man standing over him is holding a harmless-looking sock that is filled with harmful-looking doorknobs. A classic beating device, in the eyes of James Eugene Parker. Parker smiles and sits back on the balls of his feet, looking up into Yancey Jetters’ blood-speckled eyes. “How you doing, old man?” Tapping Jetters’ cheek in a playful manner, then grimacing as he pulls away blood, Parker breaks into a smile to correctly play the role of sadistic torturer (something he has had much experience with over the years). “Hitting those black books lately?” James Eugene Parker is also a shrewd businessman, but he has something Yancey Jetters has always lacked: backup. Even with all of his money and supposed power in some realms, Mr. Jetters is a weak man, especially now as he gets closer to sixty. But Parker – no. Parker’s body, resourcefulness, and unbreakable habit of always carrying a nine millimeter on him has always given him the backup to exude all the confidence he wants. “What do you want?” cries Jetters, rhetorically, predictably, helplessly. Parker smiles, stands up, and drops the sack for the moment; no more beatings, at least for right now. “I want what’s mine, Mr. Jetters. More specifically, I want your sons in the same position you are right now. And I want you to help me find them.” Yancey Jetters hasn’t seen Jett or Atkins in months. They haven’t returned his phone calls, although it may be more appropriate to say phone call, as he only tried them once, and that was because he was missing a few thousand dollars from the personal safe behind the Renoir in his third story. Ignoring this small digression and getting back to the main point, Yancey Jetters has no idea where his foster sons are, as they couldn’t taken that letter of recommendation he gave them anywhere. But as was previously stated, he is a shrewd businessman. And he hadn’t stayed wealthy for that long without knowing how to lie his way out of a sticky situation. “Sure, I’ll lend you a hand. They owe me some money anyway.” Previously, Parker’s laughs have been forced, almost canned. But this last statement draws a booming chuckle out of the stout man, so much that he actually grabs his belly to contain it. Recovering, he smirks at his prisoner. “You too, huh?” ---- Thousands of miles away, Reginald Atkins is locking an antique ivory box with three different locks. Giving suspicious glances back and forth, he takes the box and opens a large book with The Endangered Species of South Africa: A Full Catalogue And Study Guide in gold print. Opening it, Reginald places the box in the large hole where the bulk of the book would ordinarily be. Closing the book and putting it up, he hears footsteps, which hastens his action. Looking up and seeing Lyell come into the room, he sighs. “Oh, it’s you.” Stopping, Lyell raises an eyebrow. “Wer sonst würde es sein?” The situation here is that Lyell understands English and Reginald understands German, but neither is willing to bend and converse in the other’s language. This makes for an often fast exchange understood by very few people. Luckily for the confused, a translator will be used for the duration. “Who else would it be?” Reginald shrugs. “Well, Bozzy’s been poking his head in without knocking too much for my tastes. And I’m trying to keep a little bit of a low profile here.” Lyell laughs. “Wirklich? Ist das warum du wringst? Oder warum du Nathan Boswell genau erklärtest, warum wir nach Dallas liefen?” “Really? Is that why you wrestle? Or why you told Nathan Boswell exactly why we ran to Dallas?” Reginald clicks his tongue and sits down, raising his feet up on the table. “Oh, come off it. We’re in the clear. No one can find us, we didn’t tell Yancey where we were going, and we’re more than equipped if Jimmy Gene wants to send the army at us. I’m pretty sure we’ll be safe, at least long enough to undergo our next venture.” This statement catches Jett by surprise, and he shows so by cocking his head sideways and once again arches his eyebrows of the universal gesture of “huh?” “Huh?” That needs no translation, obviously, and Reginald laughs, clapping his hands together. “Well, I’ve been stuck with a bit of a quandary lately. I mean, we get our kicks by kicking staff members around, right?” Lyell nods, prompting Reginald to continue. “It’s a gas and all, but it’s only once a week. And we can’t exactly start sticking people up here in Dallas.” Lyell rolls his eyes. “Ja, ein niedriges Profil.” “Right, low profile.” “Exactly. But I’m feeling empty when I’m not at the shows, because I can’t have any fun. So I’ve been thinking of a few ideas to get my kicks, and get some more firepower while I’m at it.” Reginald smiles, waiting for his German friend to connect the vaguely placed dots. When he doesn’t, Atkins nods his head over in the direction behind the house. “Bozzy, man!” Lyell scratches his head. “Warum Boswell? Ich habe ihn gern.” “Why Boswell? I like him.” Reginald nods, sighing. “Yeah, I do too. Well, I used to. But the bloke’s gone soft, mate. Won’t even come to the show to rough up some buggers with me. He’s got his face so far in that blind kappa-slappa’s minge that he’s like an eighty year old man. No wreckage, no pain, not even kicking a beer back with us. She’s making him about as bad as she is.” “Ich weiss nicht,” says Lyell, shrugging his shoulders and continuing to look in the direction of their neighbor’s loft. “Sie ist immer so nett to me.” “I don’t know. She’s always so nice to me.” “That’s because she doesn’t understand German and she sometimes thinks you’re Nathan. Anyway, it’s going to change. If we have to deal with what we did in Connecticut in a few weeks, I’m more than ready to bring it on. But when it happens, I want her out of the picture.” ---- Nathan Boswell used to be a monster. Men of such imposing size usually find it hard to control all of the power they hold, and for a long time Boswell was no different. He was a beast, laying apart everything in his path. In fact, he was the one who began the practice of hurting staff members for fun. When he met Lyell and Reginald, they enjoyed the game and picked up on it. But all of that changed one day when he went for a walk. He saw a girl in the middle of the road, wildly trying to avoid cars honking and people staring. Not knowing what else to do, he leapt into the road and grabbed her, then took her home. That was three years ago, and their relationship had come quick and unexpected like a tornado. Within three weeks they started living together in the same loft they live in now. Soon after, Nathan Boswell quit the National Wrestling Council and instead took a steady job running a gym in downtown Dallas. He tried to wrestle a few more times, but couldn’t bring himself to leave his girlfriend for more than a few days. He used to be a monster, and now he’s a regular guy. And Reginald is not the only person who doesn’t approve. Nathan stares at himself in the mirror, not sure if he likes what he sees. The same face is there, but it has never been this inanimate. In older times he growled, gnashed his teeth, yelled, and roared in victory. There’s nothing more to roar about these days. Which was why Nathan got excited, just a little, when Reginald asked him about coming to Global. It seems like it would be fun, and it’d be only set in Dallas. But something keeps holding him back. Still, the letter he had received that day excited him. Dear Mr. Boswell, Thank you for your application to Global Wrestling. I have reviewed your resume and the tapes you sent me, and I am impressed. Your combination of strength and agility is a commodity we value here at Global Wrestling. If you are still interested, I can arrange a meeting with one of our personnel to discuss options. Please call back to request an appointment date. We look forward to seeing you in Global. Steve Gordon VP of Talent Signing Nathan hadn’t sent in the application; Reginald had put it in with his name, because Reginald wanted more destruction on the show. But Nathan wasn’t completely unhappy to hear that they were interested. Maybe he could get back into the game. There was only… “Hey, honey, where are you?” Arianna softly walks into the room. Even without sight, there is a grace about her entrance. Nathan smiles and holds her to him. He doesn’t know if he could leave her to wrestle. She wouldn’t want him to get back into the game; she is afraid his monster will come out again. But going in for a tryout wouldn’t hurt. Arianna reaches to hold his hand, then feels the letter he has been holding. Grabbing it, she sweeps her palm across it slowly. Looking up, she asks, “What is this?” Nathan sighs, then kisses her forehead. Sometimes having a blind girlfriend wasn’t so bad. “Just a bill, babe.”
