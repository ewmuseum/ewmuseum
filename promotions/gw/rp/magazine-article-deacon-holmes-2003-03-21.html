Magazine Article: Deacon Holmes

21 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/21 WARNING: this is about to turn R- rated real fast. There will be lots of (bleeps) and %*&#s. No, the rating isn’t because I’d discovered a story in Deacon Holmes that’s unsuitable for children under the age of seventeen, well, actually I have... but that’s not the point. And thankfully, it’s not because of nudity either... It’s just that while doing this story I found out that I was dealing with someone who is who he is, says what he says, acts how he acts, and doesn’t go all Nick at Nite for your sake. Not that the self proclaimed “New Face of Global Wrestling”, “Poster Child for Don’t Try This at Home”, “Ruler of your universe, Rocker of your world, and Reason you beathe”, as well as nineteen year old prodigy needs a Parental Advisory stamp. He isn’t a walking Marshall Mathers LP, although his foul mouthed antics do sometimes rival one, and he’s definitely not a living, breathing high body count action movie, although sometimes the explosions he causes in the ring with his unorthodox suicidal blend of innovative high flying and all around elusiveness. He just speaks candidly on whatever the subject may be, his vocabulary unabridged and unchecked. Or as he explained to me, he just says whatever the %*&# he feels like. Because if he can’t be himself, why bother being anything? “If you know me at all.” Holmes says, his silky southern drawl languidly dancing through the air as he sits, now transformed into a puddle of sweat by his uncle atop a weight bench, “I’m a straightforward mother(bleeper).” Consider yourself warned... Holmes is who he is whatever the circumstance or situation, whether he’s being worked within? an inch of his life by his uncle and only known relative, CW, or ravenously devouring a load of protein in the form of a tray full of “Triple X” wings from “Kathy’s”, a reporter’s tape recorder on the table. He wears his pants baggy, his dreadlocks down just a little past his shoulders, and loves talking #@%!, all of which makes Holmes Global Wrestling’s most loved yet most fiercely hated star. He is by far the favorite villain of opposing fans. “When I’m going up against Brick everyone boo’s me when I come out, they all hate me. But then as soon as I do something crazy they all go for their cameras and cheer. Shit, sometimes people even throw beer cans at me. I do don’t care, I’ll pick up the can and start drinking it... I love it.” And so do the fans, rather they openly admit it or not. The gloved hand of Deacon’s own megalomania more often than not being the force holding them back from doing so. But the scariest thing about him is that for all his ranting, raving, and proclamations of his own greatness, one thing remains clear. His grandeur is no delusion... Although he does indeed have a propensity for shooting off his mouth, it’s all true, he’s just as good, if not better, than he says he is. His will and determination has been sharpened to the point that it can split atoms, amongst other things... Such as amass an impressive collection of accolades at the highest stage of performance. At the tender young age of eighteen he entered Global Wrestling, and now at nineteen, with less than a full year’s experience, he as held all but one of the titles Global Wrestling has had to offer. Some of them multiple times. He became the youngest world champion ever at eighteen, and the final primestar champion when he merged it with the platinum title by beating Brick in a unification match. At the age of nineteen this young star is already steeped in Global Wrestling’s history. At the last pay-per-view event they held he also went on to become one of only a handful of people that have the luxury of calling themselves triple champions. Yes, he is THAT, good. He proved all his doubters wrong, and continues to do it on a weekly basis in front of a sea of millions. Before matches he fills up on silent rages, maniacally pacing back and forth in his own personal locker room, his girlfriend of late, Rhaiza, watching in awe. He doesn’t talk much before matches, and if he does it’s a grunt or a growl or a get the (bleep) out of my way. Even Deacon’s, or as his closer acquaintances call him, Deac’s locker room even screams obscenities. Posted up on the wall is a picture of Holmes, both fingers flipped. That’s basically his message to anyone who questions his ability. At six feet one inch, Holmes is far from the tallest, as some superstars tower so much as an entire foot over him. And at only one hundred and eighty four pounds he is by no means the heaviest or most powerful. It’s his intensity, as much as any other of his factors that give Holmes his edge. He keeps himself wound to so that he is ready to explode with every ounce of ferocity in his blood once he steps in the octagon. He flies around the ring like a bat out of hell, all hell itself more often than not breaking loose hot on his heels. Deacon’s four flat speed, forty six inch vertical leaping ability, and uncanny ability to bounce back from even the most devastating of maneuvers. His pure lack of respect for anyone’s safety, including his own, propels him to the top in every match. Some might call him warped, some might call his antics suicidal, but greatness comes with a price... one that Holmes pays gladly, maybe even a little too gladly. In just about every match not only has Deacon taken himself and his opponents to new levels, but he’s also pushed the limits of the envelope so far that no one knows what he may do next. More often than not the length of Deacon’s matches stems not from Holmes finding it particularly difficult to put an opponent away, but more often than not from the amount of showboating he does. Holmes tramples sportsmanship with reckless abandon and rarely looks back. On these rare occasions in which he does it’s just to see who it was he’s stepped on this time. This isn’t his ego going out of control, this is him. He actually believes everything he says about himself. When asked to describe himself with a single word Deacon quickly responded “Confident.”. Surprised at the expedience of his response I asked for another, at which point he spat out “Very.” with a predatorial scowl developing on his face. It’s the same scowl he gets right before beginning yet another work out with his uncle, CW. An ex-wrestler who can still throw four plates on each side of the bar and bench more than four hundred pounds. You should see the two of them, the wrestling prodigy and the former star turned self taught trainer. They work on more track and strength inspired techniques rather than just conventional weight lifting and sparring. Though Deacon has a shot coming up to avenge his recent loss to “American Youth” Josh, the two aren’t out for revenge. They wouldn’t want to give anyone the satisfaction. It’s more like a guilty pleasure. CW’s smile beams while standing over Holmes, who is sitting on a weight bench panting heavily. He informs Deacon that he’s still got another set to do, then when asked if it could wait he shoots back “If I wait one more minute you gon” be waitin? on an ambulance!”. With a huff and a rolling of the eyes Holmes goes right back under the bar and begins his final set of lifts. What’s the bible passage Holmes reads before every match? Philippians 4:13: “I can do all things through god Christ who strengthens me.” That’s Deacon... Strong. Determined to get what he’s earned. Not even those close to him, of whom there are very a few, really know him. The Global Wrestling roster doesn’t, his girlfriend doesn’t, his own stable mates of T.U.E. barely even know him. Even John Zero, whom he lives with for the majority of his time, knows very little of him. Only family gets a true peek, and due to a rather tragic youth, the word family consists of only one man. “I don’t trust a lot of people,” he says. “People are sneaky. I like people to be one hundred percent straight up.” You’re straight with him, he’s straight with you. Does he think he’s the best cruiserweight in Global Wrestling? “Yeah,” he says, “I do.” You can down right down the list of contenders in the division. Holmes is a fan of them all, but he attaches asterisks. Roz Blayze “Good, but relies too much on power alone.” Global Wrestling’s current cruiserweight champion Josh “Too easy to get inside his head, too slow. He’d do better as a heavyweight.” KPJ, Seymour Almasy, Jin Akiyara? Thumbs up, but not much more. “No,” he says finally. “I’m not going to say any of them are my equal.” This could be wrestling suicide, of course. Locker room bulletin boards were invented for this stuff. When asked if he worried about his comments being printed Deacon shrugged and said “I’m not scared to say it,” with a smirk. “Why be scared if it’s what I think?” The attitude didn’t come by accident. Holmes is good, and he’s not afraid to tell you just how... Deacon’s meteoric rise to fame in Global Wrestling has made him the only wrestler in history to be given his own shoe as he just recently inked a deal with NIKE. He is also the only wrestler ever to appear in this magazine. Apparently we weren’t the first to ask for a moment of his time though, as he informed me he’d turned down several offers from other magazines. In order to speak with him and his uncle I had to fly down to Florida where he is currently working with his uncle to improve his speed. Huh? Deacon was never exactly poky... Prior to returning to Florida to “sharpen his edge.” claiming he’s never lost it, that it just needs to be sharpened a bit, Holmes ran a four point one five forty yard dash. That wasn’t good enough for CW... In response to Deacon’s “slipping” time in the forty, CW instituted a six day a week schedule featuring all sorts of track-inspired training methods. Holmes ran down hills, tugged sleds, was pulled along by a bungee cord attached to CW’s El Dorado. And then he did fast-twitch muscle drills, did one hundred mat downs, and burned out on weights. “Pure hell,” says Holmes. Chances are good that few other wrestlers put themselves through that type of daily grind. Chances are even better that fewer still pore over as much videotape as Deacon and his uncle. The two break down film of Holmes? past matches and upcoming opponents three times a week, or whenever CW can find time to break away from working Deacon into a sweat puddle. At random points during the week CW will induce an unscheduled ten minute workout for Deacon right there on the spot. CW cranks the treadmill to max speed and Holmes does four sets of runs, each lasting for about thirty five to forty seconds. Next, he does one hundred incline pushups with a forty five pound plate on his back. “Just a little maintenance program,” CW says “Wouldn’t want him goin” soft.” he continues with a smile and a puff of cigar smoke. After training for the day is complete it’s time for dinner. Tonight, CW’s specialty, and Deacon’s favorite, fried fish and grits. Vegetable? Hot sauce. Dessert? Little Debbie cakes. Holmes doesn’t think twice about the routine, to him it’s all worth it. Minor aches and pains are nothing compared to the reward... Having an entire sold out arena standing in their seats hooting and hollering at the top of their lungs and every klieg light in that arena pointed directly at him. Finally, I asked him how he views each match. In a moment of relaxation and clarity Deacon waxes philosophical and leans in. “It’s kind of like an art, man,” Holmes says. “It’s like I go out there and paint a picture.” And what does that picture look like? He leans back, stares up at the ceiling, then shakes his head and laughs. You sense a (bleep) coming. “It’d probably be all (bleeped) up,” he says. “The canvas torn in half, black and blood, all that stuff. A (bleeping) mess.” You were warned.
