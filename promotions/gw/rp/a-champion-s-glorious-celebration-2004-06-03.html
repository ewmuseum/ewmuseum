a Champion's glorious celebration

3 June 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
a Champion's glorious celebration Jonathan Drachen Global Wrestling Promo #1376 Date: 6/3/04 Location: Dallas, Texas Jonathan thought of the beautiful and reassuring thing that lay next to him on the couch upon which he was seated. He thought what he had been through to get close to being in a federation like GW. He thought of how he’d never imagined he’d find something like he’d found in GW. He thought he’d never be happy with his wrestling career. He thought he’d never be pleased with the way in which it was progressing. He’d thought he’d never be a champion in a federation he truly respected, admired, enjoyed and had begun very quickly to love. He’d thought he’d never have bouts with honorable and great opponents and have great matches that caught the eye of fans who were worth impressing and not just out to see blood. He thought he’d never have something to show for his hard work and his dedication and the heart that he spread so thickly into his work. He looked over at the lovely and wonderful thing that lay perched adjacent to him, silent and wondrous. He remembered what had been there next to him just a week earlier. It hadn’t been nothing at all. He looked at the belt, and sighed. Not the defeated kind. Not the dismissive kind of sigh. The broken kind. “Why can’t it be her?” ---- In Mexico, he’d been doing some light physical training every morning for about 3 hours, just to keep himself relatively ready physically for the match. He’d followed the advice of his teacher in not focusing on the physical, more the mental aspect of the match. But that in itself required him to think a lot less about things and to feel while acting more. As he’d gone from his hotel room on the last morning before the day of the match where he would be awarded his golden waist-trophy, he’d gotten a bad feeling. This wasn’t the nerve-racking insecurity of thinking he might be defeated that he’d been used to for the previous few days. This was something entirely different. This was something foreboding and haunting. This was something that reminded him of earlier days. This reminded him of when he could see without vision. He’d looked at her, immediately, after this feeling had come. He didn’t like that. It seemed as if he’d been directed to do so. It seemed as if the feeling had selected her as a primary “flashpoint”. He’d looked into her eyes, and something terrible was there. It was something he had not seen yet. But it wasn’t just a flash. It wasn’t just a glimpse. It was a clear sight. Something cold had entered her eyes. Something forbidding had entered her glance. Perhaps she just didn’t know that he was looking and was dwelling on something else. For she turned to him, and it dissipated almost completely. He dismissed it, but still felt odd. He walked over to her. “I’ll be back as soon as I can, ok?” She smiled and nodded, warm as usual. He lightened his grip on the world. Her smile had always made him do that. “Take your time. I know you need to get ready.” ---- He didn’t want to remember this. He didn’t want to remember any of it. He didn’t want to know that any of this had happened to him. The memories were something that he couldn’t fight any longer. He’d tried to thwart them. But they’d thwarted him. He didn’t want to know any of it. ---- It was cold. Her kiss, it was cold. Something in it had been cold. Perhaps it just felt cold and there was really nothing at all. He looked at her, his crystalline blue eyes pleading. He hadn’t meant to show her this. But it didn’t seem to matter. She looked away, betraying a smile that seemed …false. But then one that seemed real and she’d genuinely wished him a good day. This discontinuity disturbed him. He did not know whether he should leave now. He was suspicious. ---- His morning workout routine wasn’t helping to curb his suspicion or fear. So he quit early, in hopes that he would return and things would be changed slightly. He had hoped that a new situation had emerged. He’d hoped they were just a morning episode of mental disrepair. Perhaps he’d woken her up too early. She had seemed a little jittery for the whole trip though. Maybe that explained it. Maybe the travel and the incessant heat were bothering her. He made up a thousand excuses as he journeyed back to his hotel from his workout. If only one had been true. ---- He’d tried to ignore it. It had hurt massively. He’d been gasping on the floor for hours afterwards. He had no idea how he’d picked himself up and dragged himself to the arena for workouts the next day. He had no idea how he’d carried himself in his match. He had no idea how he’d come out on top. He’d planned the whole time to put on a show for the crowd, but he hadn’t even heard their cheers as he’d emerged victorious. The only time he’d been thinking of the match was while he was in it. And then, he’d only been thinking of it. He supposed thinking of wrestling was how he’d survived that day or two in hell. ---- He’d come back two hours early. He’d wanted to surprise her and tell her that today they’d go out and enjoy each other. He thought that might have been a better solution. What he’d found was not pleasant upon his entry. It did not seem she’d enjoy the change in plan from appearances, whatsoever. ---- He remembered the scene and didn’t want to anymore. He needed to step outside. He needed some air. He didn’t need the confines of this thought palace he’d created any longer. It was more like a dungeon now for him. He stood up, not looking at his now prized possession. He no longer looked at his now only object of affection. For he couldn’t separate the memory of it from the memory of her. He felt desperate for air as he’d felt for a day now. He stood and walked quickly over to the door of his apartment. Before he reached for the doorknob, he paused and looked down at his carpet and feet for a moment. He was trying to let a bad moment pass, one would guess. But who knew as he looked back over his shoulder at the title on his couch. He looked like he was going to smile for a moment. But he needed to leave. He shrugged his shoulders, opened the door and left. He walked down the hallway to a stairwell. Amazingly, he started feeling like he might be able to breathe again. ---- He looked into her eyes as she was packing her belongings into the single bag she’d brought along with her. She hadn’t noticed he’d entered at first. She was bustling to leave, it seemed. He’d felt defeated before he’d even seen her. He knew the feeling. He didn’t say a word as she stopped when she finally noticed he was in the room. A look of shock and self-directed anger spread across her face that turned whiter than its usual ivory tone. He looked at her and sat down on the bed she had placed her suitcase upon. She didn’t move, fearing what she had not expected. He just looked at her with a plaintive glance. There seemed to be no emotion at all in his eyes other than curiosity. He knew she was leaving. And he knew it meant she would never come back. He knew that from the moment he’d seen her. He was just glad he’d seen her before she left. He tried not to ruin that with getting upset. For this, he had to become emotionally dead. Her head and upper torso fell against the suitcase. This startled him a bit, but not much. He heard sobs muffled by hands. He looked at her, very objectively and with more curiosity than anything else, somehow. He knew why. He knew why the second he’d opened the door and let her in. He knew she hadn’t forgotten. He knew she hadn’t forgotten him leaving her for a year. He knew the pain he’d caused the second he’d started to cause it. He knew he deserved this from the moment he’d seen her again. But he’d ignored it, because he’d seen her again. He couldn’t be angry with her. He had no right. So he was not. He leaned in a little closer to the distraught and hurting girl he’d thought of as an angel. He felt disgusted with himself for bringing her to such evil deeds that he knew she would hate herself for later. He touched her, knowing he did not deserve to do so. He caressed her upper shoulders gently. She bolted her head upwards at him. He knew this was a catlike angry reaction and retracted himself as much as possible. She opened her mouth and venom filled her angelic face. He couldn’t bare the sight. So he spoke to her softly: “I know. I know.” Her eyes softened a bit as she realized he did and was not angry. “I deserve every bit of it. I know I did. I knew you were leaving. Deep down, I knew it when I kissed you this morning. I knew it when I let you in my door. I knew you had to have your revenge. I knew you deserved it. But like then, now I know one other thing than that.” She questioned him with desperate eyes. She couldn’t mouth any words. “I knew. I knew that I needed to see you again.” He stood, and smiled at her. He would never know how he had the strength to do this later. He fought tears like he’d never fought in his life. But the fact that he did not deserve to cry them gave him just enough strength to avoid them until later. He stood, and walked out of the room. He knew then, that that would be the last time he ever saw her. Even broken like she was over what she had to do, she was still the most beautiful and wonderful woman he’d ever seen. Somehow, he was thankful, in a way. ---- He didn’t watch her leave. He didn’t need to, He didn’t return to the hotel room. He didn’t want to feel what had been her presence. He paid some boy to go in and pack his bags. He slept on a cot in the arena that night in the locker room. He’d done that before many times when he’d started out. He always knew to bring a pillow while traveling because of this. Although not the most “cushy”, place to stay. It felt the most at home of all the places he could have stayed. And thinking about the match, he didn’t need to think about her. Yeah right. He tried not to break down. It was difficult beyond difficult. This was more trial than he knew he would ever have to face in the ring. This was by far the worst injury he’d ever known in a lifetime of injuries. The worst part was that it was self-inflicted. ---- He tried not to wonder where she was. He tried not to hope she was happy. He tried not to hope she was miserable. He tired not to wonder what her hair smelled like at the moment. He tired not to wonder, think, dream, hope, nor despair. It was next to impossible. He couldn’t stop. It didn’t matter as much though now. He was outside and free from confinement. It wasn’t as bad, for sure. It was hot and stuffy outside on this Texan Summer afternoon. It helped alleviate focusing on anything other than trying to avoid heatstroke. This was just what he needed at the moment. He bought an overpriced bottle of water from a street vendor. Cliché as it may seem, they made him think of vultures with their priorities fucked up. He walked about downtown and drifted into the arts district, with no real purpose in mind other than to roam aimlessly until his mind was put at ease. He felt like he could be here for years. It didn’t matter, he loved this city. He realized he looked like well-dressed and fed homeless man with his sightless stare and downcast demeanor. He knew that someone might approach him eventually, but that would still be better than letting what had happened be a burden on his mind. Being arrested and thrown into a cell for vagrancy was better than thinking about that. Getting mugged and beaten, maybe even stabbed seemed better than living with this grief. He didn’t know how long this would plague him, but he sensed a while. He had tried to think of good things that had happened recently in a haphazard attempt to count his blessings in the wake of the atom bomb. Bu these good things only led to thoughts of her and to old depressing love songs that he thanked the heavens he could not actually hear right now. He thought of the ticket in his pocket for the seat he’d reserved for her ringside. He felt it in all its untorn and unused malicious virginity. He felt it, but could not touch it. He still tried to think of good things very bravely. He tried to be valiant like the opponent he would soon face. He thought of winning the title and how amazing it hadn’t felt because he knew he couldn’t go home and… NO! He couldn’t think of that. No matter how magnificent it was that he’d succeeded in becoming a champion under such heartwrenching self-loathsome emotional distress hadn’t even occurred to Jonathan because of .. NO! He couldn’t think of her. He was trying not to hurt as he took a few steps toward a bench and then placed a hand on the corner of it. He rested slightly on one hand, feeling weathered not only from the heat… Wow, he hadn’t noticed how hot it had gotten. He felt a little dizzy. Maybe he should have drunk some more water. Maybe he wouldn’t have… fainted.
