Downfall: Prologue

25 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/25/2005 For the second time in as many weeks Dawn’s knees went weak and she could feel the familiar burning of unshed tears trying to force their way to the front. Her hands twitched and shook, and it was all she could do not to tighten her grip on the piece of paper held tenderly between her hands. She shook herself like a cat does to rid itself of something unpleasant, forcing her eyes back to the paper. Dawn: As I write this, I know without a doubt that you've been terribly harsh on yourself. I don't have ESP, but I do know you well enough to know about how you beat yourself up whenever something goes awry. Don't. That's all that I ask of you. Do not blame yourself for this. To tell you the truth, my need for a time of solitude came from no one place. An Emoticon jab here, a Josh Brand jab there. What really made me realize it was Emoticon's attempt on Virgil's life. I don't know whether he sees it as such or not, but what matters is that it was, and it scarred me more deeply than I feel he will ever know. He broke my trust-and now I understand why you distrusted him so much yourself. I'm not running to get away from you and Virgil. I'm in no state of mind to love anyone right now, because I don't love myself. I am convinced that I am a selfish being who needs to seriously re-evaluate practically everything she has done in the past six months. I don't know if it was anyone's intent to provoke that reaction, but again, intent is not the question-only the result of what was said. When I have resolved my mental state, I will return. Do not think that I would ever leave you; I would not, after what you have been through, both what you have told me, and that which I gathered from Eryc some time back, dim sense of things though it was. I will not abuse you as you have been abused in the past. I want you to trust that I will return. Hopefully, I will have reconciled myself to a few things, namely finding a way to cope with being myself. If I can't do that? Well, I'll just have to, there's no question. I haven't been the same for two weeks now. That's why it's better that I be gone for a little while. I left you something, though-check your right ear. I hope that you like it. La tienne, L.R.V. Dawn’s mind reeled and the room spun around her, a wave of sudden dizziness forcing the Steel Rose to her knees. The carefully handwritten letter fluttered gently to the ground a few feet away, it’s flowing text staring unmercifully up at her from the dark surface of her carpet. Her fingers dug into the deep pile of that same carpet, nails aching from the strain as she clenched handfuls of carpet into her fists. She shook her head slowly from side to side, her voice trickling out like a tiny stream over a dry riverbed. “Oh no?” She gasped her voice uncharacteristically harsh. “No no no no?” Shaking hands went to the side of her face, snaking up into her hair where they threatened to tear large handfuls of the dyed black strands from her head. “Not again?” She gasped, her shoulders shaking and her breathing ragged, “Not this again?” The woman turned away, a silvery tear sliding down one cheek to splash onto Dawn’s fevered face. “I have to go sweety, or you’re going to get sick again. When I go you’re going to be all better, and you’ll stay that way forever.” “I don’t want you to go mummy’I don’t want to be better!” A small hand came into Dawn’s vision, grasping weakly at her mothers arm. Gently her mother freed her arm and took Dawn’s small hand between both of hers, pressing it up to her own cheek. “You have to get better Dawnie, I could never live with myself if I knew you were sick because I stayed. Once I go you’ll feel better, and then?” Her voice caught, a choked sob sticking in her throat. ??And then someday you’ll forget’me.” “NO! No mummy I don’t want to forget! I won’t EVER forget you!” Dawn flung small arms around her mother, squeezing tightly. Dawn’s hands tightened on her head as the tightness in her chest increased, a feeling of pressure that got progressively tighter until she could hear her own blood roaring in her ears, drowning out the sound of even her own heartbeat. She could still feel the touch of Lia’s skin on hers, the feel of cool hands soothing her feverish skin and the loving touch of another’s lips to her own’gone. “NO!” Dawn practically screamed the word, shoving her hands into the carpet and struggling to her feet. Throwing open the doors to her walk in closet Dawn dug into the pile of boxes at the back until she found what she was looking for, a backpack she’d used while camping with her father years ago. As she pulled the container free another large object came with it, looped limply through the backpack straps and dangling drawstrings down to her bare calf. She tore the object free to toss it back into the closet before a slow realization dawned on her. “Daddy’s duffel bag?” She whispered, turning the worn green container over in her hands. A Canadian flag had been lovingly sewn into the side and her father’s name was stitched near the top of the bag, the thread worn and fading from years of misuse. “Lt. James McFarlane.” Dawn stared for a long moment at the bag, testing the strength of the old cloth between her fingers. She nodded, as if in agreement and cast her eyes briefly to the sky. “Thanks Dad.” Both bags went onto the floor near her bed as she began tearing through her closet, digging out shorts and pants, t-shirts and turtlenecks, jeans and vests. “I am NOT going to just sit this one out and lose her?” She growled as she tossed clothes onto the bed, throwing some heedlessly back into the closet. When the pile on the bed had nearly reached the top of her canopy bed she slammed the closet doors shut, striding purposefully across the floor and digging into her drawer. “I can’t just let you go.” She muttered to herself, tossing underwear and rolls of socks over her shoulders, “Not this time, I can’t let you face this on your own.” Folded bras joined the rest of the underclothes, followed by a hair brush, hair elastics and a travel bag. Snagging one of the elastics Dawn wrestled her unruly hair back into a short foxtail, wincing as her rebellious hair caught stubbornly in the metal of the elastic and threatened to foil the entire procedure. From the top drawer she dug a red and white ball cap, settling it into place and poking the foxtail out through the hole in the back. “I won’t let this happen again.” Dawn’s voice had taken on a desperate edge as she crammed clothes into the duffel bag, stuffing them in without any concern for folds or wrinkles. She grabbed a pair of blue cargo pants from the pile; jumping awkwardly into them and sliding them up over her sleeping panties and up over her hips until they settled comfortably. She tugged the duffel bag shut and set to filling the backpack, discarding any clothes she didn’t think would fit to the floor. “I know you’re not happy but dammit I can help with that’I can make life happy for you.” Dawn seemed lost in her own world, muttering dazedly to herself as she stuffed clothes into the backpack until it wouldn’t take anymore. With the bag as full as it was going to get she turned back to the closet for footwear, striding purposefully across the room again’and pausing as she passed the mirror, an unfamiliar sight catching her eye. The morning sun glinted off silver in the mirror, producing the small gleam that had caught Dawn’s eye. High in the cartilage of her right ear was a small earring, the silver setting winking cheerily at her in the dawns light. A brilliant opal stone had been placed in the middle of the silver setting, a ring of deep blue that surrounded a core of green, the colour of the sea after a storm’and the same colour of Dawn’s eyes as she peered at it in her mirror. She reached up to touch it with a hesitant hand, her fingertip slipping along the slightly warm silver and the smooth surface of the opal. “Opal?” She whispered, “For October’my birthday. Oh Lia?” Dawn squeezed her eyes shut against the tears that threatened to spill again, sliding hotly down her cheeks to splatter against the dark wood of her dresser. “I do not think that gift was given to make you cry.” The sound of the voice from her door was familiar, but only in that she had heard it before. Dawn looked up quickly from her dresser, eyes settling on the handsome form in her doorway. “Virgil.” Her voice was equal parts annoyed and relieved; Annoyed at the reminder of his presence, but at the same time relieved to see someone who was likely as affected by Lia’s disappearance as she was. “Si. May I come in?” His posture was relaxed, leaning against her doorframe, but not so much of an inch of his person had intruded into her room before asking permission. “If you like.” She replied, waving to a small chair near the door. Virgil slipped in quietly, settling into the chair and taking in the whirlwind of chaos that seemed to have blown through Dawn’s bed chambers. His gaze settled on the full bags by her bed and one slender eyebrow rose a little in amusement. “Planning a trip?” “I’m going after Lia.” Came the short reply. “You can come if you like.” He snorted, shaking his head. “I assure you, if I had thought chasing out after her with my head on backwards would have accomplished anything’I would already be long gone.” Dawn’s head snapped up and around, fixing an emerald glare on her guest. “So you’re just going to let her go then” With Lilith’s assasins after her?!” Virgil shook his head slowly, leaning back against the stiff chair. “You’re assuming far too much. I said I wasn’t running out with my head on backwards, but I assure you I’m not going to let her face the rest of the world unaided.” Dawn took in the bandages still wrapped around his arms, only able to wonder at the damage Emoticon had caused Lia’s paramour. “What can you do then?” He bristled a little at her tone, raising his eyes to hers. “At least as much as you, I can guarantee that. At least I had the sense to get dressed.” Dawn blushed; looking sheepishly down at the black tank top she usually slept in’and had forgotten to remove while she had been shoving clothes into bags. She wiped her hands self consciously down the front of the shirt, looking back up at him. “I’d have gone after her in my underwear if I thought it would have accomplished something.” Virgil sighed, raking his fingers back through his hair. “Yes I don’t doubt it, but now is not the time for rash actions. Chasing after Lia without any other thought is a sure way to place her in the kind of dangers you were hoping to avoid, my contacts can find her with much less risk and much less hassle.” “She could be anywhere.” Dawn fired back, “You don’t even know where to start.” “On the contrary, I think I know Lia better then anyone else’including you.” Dawn flushed at the rebuke, opening her mouth to retort before getting cut off once again. “And my web of contacts could find anything I wanted in North America’after all, I found you.” Dawn did a sharp double take, looking up at Virgil with questioning eyes. “Found me” What the hell do you mean?” “Oh come now, did you really think it was simple coincidence that you found a trucker willing to go where you were headed” Or that Christian Zenith and his significant other found you scant minutes after you arrived at your sisters home?” Dawn’s opened and closed like a fish out of water as she floundered for words. “That was you”! You told them where to find me? How? Why?” Virgil waved his hand, indicating it was a relatively small matter. “The how is not important, suffice to say my contacts are as reliable as I have told you. As for the why’because Lia wished it, your disappearance caused her no small amount of grief.” Dawn turned slowly away, presenting her side to him as her mind warred with itself. “And you think you can find her the same way?” “I don’t think, I know. After all, you didn’t have a vehicle and I found you just fine. I can find her, you mark my words.” “Vehicle’she took the vet?” Dawn queried, remembering she’d given Lia a spare set of keys. “She did not. She took your motorcycle, which is another reason pursuing her on foot would be a futile endeavor’that machine is capable of some extraordinary speed.” Dawn looked back at him incredulously, her eyes wide. “My bike” But my bike was trashed when I crashed it over a month ago, and then it was stolen from the crash sight. Are you saying she fixed it?” Virgil inspected something on his nails, indicating a lack of interest. “Actually I had it fixed. It was simple enough to find the original builder and have him do some work on it for me, it was supposed to be a gift for Lia to give to you.” Dawn gaped, finding herself without words for the second time in the conversation. “W-why?” Virgil looked up again, making eye contact and holding her gaze. “Because Lia wished to see you happy about it again, and frankly that machine is a work of art. It would have been a shame to allow such a piece to languish away in your garage in a stat of disrepair.” Dawn fell silent again, mulling over Virgil’s words as he pushed himself up from the chair. “Suffice to say, my contacts will find Lia. And when they do, only then, it will be appropriate for us to go and see her. Now if you’ll excuse me, I have some people to call.” Virgil sketched a small bow and left the room, leaving Dawn alone with her thoughts. She stared after him for a long moment, her right hand clenched into a fist so tight that blood started to well up around her nails. “Dammit I’m helpless again?” “DAMMIT!” She punctuated the shout by hurling her now useless backpack across the room.
