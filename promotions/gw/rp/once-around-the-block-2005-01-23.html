Once Around the Block

23 January 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/23/05 'I'm so stupid. I should have expected something like that to happen. Why couldn't it all just be perfect? I tried so hard to make my debut a success, and that man ruined it for me. That... that bastard! I should have watched myself, I should have a title shot, I should be having a beautiful dream and not a nightmare and a bad headache to go with it. It's not fair, it's just not fair!' It was a day after Reload. Michael Mercer was sitting in his New Mexico home. It was a little bit after noon, and Mindy remained in her bedroom. In fact, Michael didn't remember seeing her leave the confinements of such all morning, which was weird all on its own; Mindy was usually the first person awake in the house, partially because of her quickly falling into the position of 'nanny' at the Mercer household, and partially because all the good cartoons came on in the morning anyway. Although Michael wasn't bothered enough to go crazy about it, he certainly noticed Mindy's absence. How couldn't he? It was actually the first time he could truly remember that he was able to hear the television in the living room without having to blast the volume in order to hear over his cousin's grating mouse-like voice. 'But if something bad happened to her last night, she probably didn't deserve it. And she probably doesn't deserve me hounding her about it.' Michael and Jaden sat in silence. Michael simply stared into space, as if beyond the material in front of his face- as if he was trying to stare into the octagon Mindy had wrestled in last night and see if he could figure out what was wrong. Jaden didn't pay no mind- as long as it wasn't her husband in that octagon, she was happy with whatever he did. And if she wasn't happy, Jaden would be more than susceptible to Michael's scrutiny. After all, Michael made a promise not to wrestle again, and he was going to keep it. That wasn't to say he couldn't help his cousin get off the ground, though, and it didn't mean he couldn't train, either. He was going to do whatever it took to get his edge back- he hadn't forgotten about his former 'friend'. All those thoughts rang through his head at that moment, as he glanced over at his wife who worked quietly across the room. 'Jaden, are you gonna pick up Gabi from school today, or shall I?' Jaden looked up from the large book in her lap, and batted her long red hair away from her face. 'Mindy told me she would pick Gabi up today.' 'When did she tell you that?' Michael inquired. 'This morning?' Jaden shook her head in dismay. 'Sometime over the weekend. She offered to pick Gabi up if I ever needed her to, so...' She trailed off then- it was at that moment when she became aware of the look Michael gave her. 'What's wrong?' Michael need not say a word, as the expression on his features was all the indication of the problem. He took one glance at the closed bedroom door in the distance, and blinked in stoic form. 'I don't think she's going to be doing much of anything today.' 'I'm so stupid, I'm so immature, I'm so stupid, I'm so immature...' 'Do you think something bad happened last night?' Jaden questioned, to which Michael smirked and lowered his head slightly. 'I'd say that's a safe assumption, yes.' Jaden gave a flash of a frown, and then went back to her work with little more of a shuffle in her seat on the couch. Michael looked once more in his wife's direction, suddenly curious as to what she was doing. With a faint smile, he slid closer to Jaden and leaned forward, looking at the clutter on the coffee table. 'What's all this?' After a moment of gawking, however, he soon answered his own question. A plethora of photos, most including him and his wife, or the two of them with every person they had ever had the pleasure of knowing. They were in the midst of being assorted into one large photo album, one that showcased the rare times between he and Jaden- the times when they were truly happy. 'Ooh...' 'Almost hard to think we were like that once, huh?' Jaden asked with a slight laugh. It was a legitimate question- Michael hadn't remembered the last time he and Jaden were actually able to have a few days where they could just sit back and smile. But those moments were long gone.. any moment of clarity in their recent days of living would quickly be ruined via thoughts of angst, vengeance, work... 'Mikey was right telling me that being a wrestler was tough. I can't believe I'm such a dreamer that I refused to listen...' 'Too bad life can't just be a picture, eh?' Michael debated, as he leaned forward and picked up one of the photos. He stared into it with a deep gaze, observing every speck of color in the picture. 'Just take the stuff we want to see, and judge everything off of that. No words, no complications. Just pictures.' Jaden looked up at Michael, and smiled. 'That's not like you,' she stated. 'How so?' 'When's the last time you talked to me about life without mentioning the F word?' 'I dunno,' Michael mumbled with a shrug. 'When's the last time I've talked to anyone about anything without mentioning the F word?' 'Good point.' Michael smirked in his small triumph, before he stood up and stretched out his arms. 'I suppose I'll pick Gabi up myself. I need to spend more time with her anyway. I'm sure she secretly thinks I still wrestle.' Jaden wrinkled her nose in disgust of the remark. 'Ugh, don't give me that thought, please, Michael James Mercer.' '...You used my middle name.' 'Yeah.. so?' Michael and Jaden exchanged looks for a few seconds, and then Michael finally turned away towards the front door. 'Nevermind.' He moved to the door and grabbed his leather jacket from the nearby coat rack. However, before he could grab the doorknob or so much as throw on his New York Yankees cap, something else caught his eye down the hall; something that came with the accompaniment of the bedroom door that had creaked open. There, beyond the door, was Michael's cousin- bed hair, pink bunny decorated jammies and all- standing amidst a stream of tears that rolled down her face. Before Michael could so much as have his voice heard, Mindy ran and clung herself to her bigger cousin, bawling noisily against his shoulder. 'Mikey,' she whimpered, 'I'm not cut out for this, am I?'
