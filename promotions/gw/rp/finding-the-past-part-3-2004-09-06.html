Finding The Past (Part 3)

6 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09.06.04 'I can't believe what all he's going through. I feel so bad for him.' 'I know. He's had a very difficult few months. I just wish there was something I could do for the poor Dear.' The two older ladies chattered back and forth in a whisper on their way up to the beautifully arranged flowers adorning the gravesite of Lola Faye Tumlin. The floral patterns atop the hill were magnificent of colors of every shade graced the frshly dug burial plot. Behind a podium atop the sunlit hill in a solid white suit adorned by a snow white overcoat, both by Giorgio Armani, with matching white shoes by Prada stood Ken Partridge Jr, the grandson of Mrs. Tumlin. As the final friends and family arrived to pay their respects, KPJ began to speak, hiding his tear filled eyes behing a pair of black Tag Hauer sunglasses. 'I stand in front of you all today a lucky man,' began Ken. 'A few months ago, my life was nearly taken from me and several of my memories were erased from my mind. I'm lucky because, of all the memories I lost... none of them were of my grandmother... my Nanny Faye. I still remember all of the times that I spent with her, and the smile she held which never had any troubles warming even the coldest room.' Sobs continued to flow from the many in attendance as Ken paused to look at his father, his sister, his neice and nephew, and his mother. Tears quickly began to roll down his face as he continued. This had been the third time in less than a year that he had been asked to speak at a loved ones funeral... and this was definatly the most difficult of the three. 'You see... our grandmother... she lived her life. She had no worries, or reservations, because she knew that no matter what happened, she would be okay. I know that she's up there now, looking down on all of us, trying to figure out why we're all down here crying instead of rejoicing. I also know that if she were able to, she's smack each and every one of us and tell us to cheer up. She's the happiest she's ever been. She no longer hurts... and she's still always smiling... with our grandfather.' Choked up, Ken makes his way down from the stand prematurely, unable to finish his words. What he left everyone with, though, was good enough. As the Brother Walker, the pastor of his grandmother's church for as long as Ken would remember, walked up and began a prayer before carrying on the services, Ken found his seat between his father and his sister. His sunglasses couldn't disguise the tears anymore. They had been blessed with a radiant day. The sun shined and there was a slight briskness in the early Spetember air. His grandmother would have been more than happy. She was a beautiful woman who deserved beautiful things. Ken only hoped that she realized how amazing and lovely she always was in his eyes. His grandmother was the eternal optimist... and one that could make you believe in the best as well. She truly was a magnificent woman. He no longer cared if his tears were seen or not. There were things that he didnt remember, and was sure that some of those things he didn't care to remember as well. But he had what counted now. Everything that these terrible months had brought him were nothing more than a blessing in disguise. It was the ultimate slap in the face to get him back on the right track again, and he planned on getting full-steam on that track as soon as possible. His tears were his strength.
