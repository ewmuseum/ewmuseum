War Within: The Scavenger

10 January 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/10/2005 ---- FLASHBACK’Saturday, November 13, 2004 0523 hours, local time Amsterdam, Netherlands ---- The night pressed against the windows, inky black? I could feel the nights chill seeping into my marrow; the narrow backseat of this taxi was less than warm, perhaps a degree above freezing. The chill however, was quite bracing indeed, and it was doing much to keep me awake. I felt the dream; still, tugging at my sleeves the way an eager child would, wanting to drag me down. We were still a good thirty minutes from the destination; the CID safehouse was miles away from the airport, and even further still from the home I had just left. Blood filled the creases in my fingers, staining the calluses? even after washing. I felt a little like MacBeth’s wife, even as I found myself rubbing absently at one of the darker smears that marred the beginning of my lifeline. The silence was grating, but I didn’t feel like talking to the man behind the wheel. My Dutch was rusty, I could still understand it well enough, but I was hesitant to speak it. I guess you could call me shy, I preferred to consider myself a perfectionist. I abhorred flaws? and weaknesses of any kind. I felt my eyes growing heavy, and then, everything grew? ---- Light. A million points of light, dazzling on the indigo backdrop, glittering like precious stones on a jeweller’s tray. The hawk wheeled through the sky, floating effortlessly on the wind, wings spread majestically to maximize lift. He looked down, surveying the landscape below, oblivious to the chilled air. Fields and houses rushed by, unimportant to one so high above it all. His keen eyes took it all in, missing nothing even as it rushed past, the only sound the soft beating of his wings as he flapped against the sudden downdraft. He felt the weight of the air pushing against him, beating his wings didn’t help; he couldn’t turn against the cold air pushing him down. On the rapidly approaching ground, he saw something of interest, there amid all the traces of humanity, there, in the middle of a barren field, was a body, staked out. He fought the wind currents, winning the battle, gliding in a lazy circle overhead, and trying to get a better look. He could see the glimmer of blood, staining the naked torso. This wasn’t a fresh kill, and the thrill of the hunt wasn’t in it, but something about this strange scene drew his attention. Perhaps it was the dark shape, huddled on the man’s chest. A scavenger? a crow. The crow sat on the man’s chest, talons digging into his flayed chest. His beady eye was fixed on the shiny object that lay around the man’s neck. He reached out with his cracked beak, pulling at the chain. The twisted metal pulled against the man’s neck, jerking at his hair, startling him from his stupor. The man groaned, twitching, startling the ragged crow. The bird squawked, flapping its patchy wings, sending a few bits of feather flying, and then it settled back on his chest, glaring now at the partly opened eye with malevolent intent. The man lifted his hand, intending to shoo the bird, and failing miserably, sending the short chain to rattling, and not much more. McLeod was still too weak to manage more than a blink or two of his eye. The one that wasn’t swollen shut, that is. All he could taste was the coppery, bitter tang of blood, and his mouth didn’t seem to be working. He could feel a runnel of drool trailing down his cheek, the wetness like ice on his feverish skin. He tried to close his lips, failing miserably as pain slammed through his nerve endings at the slight movement. It was dislocated, perhaps broken even, judging by the pain, and the numbness in his chin and cheeks. He felt the bird move again, and that slight shift in pressure drew his attention. The scavenger had inched closer, dangerously close even, within the range of pecking out his eyes. “Nevermore, quoth the raven,” he thought to himself, greatly amused at the misuse of one of Poe’s greatest works. This wasn’t a ghostly visitor, or a weaving of fiction? the claws digging into his chest were real, and so was that hooked beak. The crow made a sound that made his skin crawl, it was so close to a laugh that it was uncanny, and it darted its head forward quite suddenly, again seizing the chain in its beak. The amulet hanging from the chain bumped against his chest, heavy and warm in spite of the cool night air. It was a trefoil, the Celtic knotwork depicting trinity? his own personal talisman. It would seem, after all, that it was quite ineffective? but even as he groped with that random thought, another came into his mind. Where was he? How had he gotten from that dank dungeon slaughterhouse? to this place, in the middle of nowhere? He rolled his head around, seeing nothing but one almost dead tree, directly behind him. Upside down, he could see another bird, perched in the lowest branches, peering at him with the same fixation as the crow. He pulled against the chains that were bound to the stakes in the frozen earth, trying to move, unable to do more than irritate his dark visitor. The crow chortled again, fluttering its wings, digging in with its talons for purchase, and tearing open more wounds. He felt like Prometheus, chained to the rock, forever tormented when the bird came every day to take his liver. “Take the damned thing,” he muttered, the words coming out all wrong from his mangled jaw. “It’s not doing me any good. Take it, and then kill me, you bloody shit-hawk.” The bird simply stared, for a long moment, still holding fast to the silver chain. It pulled back, and the chain dragged across the back of McLeod’s neck, abrading his flesh. Still, the bird didn’t relent, scrambling back, tearing open ragged gashes in the man’s chest as its talons scrabbled for purchase, and then, with a loud twang, the chain snapped. The bird tumbled back, startled, squawking angrily in a flutter of feathers. It sat there for a moment, almost as though collecting its thoughts, peering pensively into McLeod’s glazed eye, much to the irritation of the incapacitated Highlander. “Bloody hell, begone with you. Stop looking at me. Both of you. I am not dead. Get on with you, scavengers. Bunch of bloody vultures. Christ, next thing I know I’ll be talking to the birds. Go on, get out of here” what do you want from me?” He didn’t expect an answer, so when the crow perched on his abdomen replied; he was rather startled, but not totally surprised. “It’s not so much that I want something. I’m just waiting for the inevitable. I am waiting for you to die, like your companion here.” McLeod craned his neck, looking at the bird with a puzzled expression. “Companion?” The bird, instead of replying, lifted off his chest in a flurry of musty feathers, alighting instead on the body staked out beside him. The body of Marcus complete with bullet hole in his forehead, a familiar, if not wholly unwelcome sight. The bird, somehow, strangely, seemed to lose cohesion, its dark shape bleeding into the chest of the dead man. McLeod watched with detachment, feeling nothing but the coldness of death creeping over him in a numbing wave. Surely this was a dream? or a hallucination. The dead man sat up, pulling the stakes from the earth with ease, sending the chains to rattling in a way that would have made Jacob Marley proud. He wiped blood and brains from his eyes, and turned to peer at McLeod, the eyes as jet black and uncaring as those of the crow. “Ah yes, this is much better.” “You’re dead. This can’t be happening.” The dead man nodded. “Perhaps so, but a more tactful person would have at least beat around the bush about it. Still, you are perilously close to death’s door, and have lost a considerable amount of blood, so I am willing to make allowances. Yes, I am dead. But then, so was Jackson at the beginning of the entire mess.” McLeod sneered, or at least made a move to sneer, not doing much more than curl his swollen lips, making the cracks begin to bleed anew. “Fuck Jackson, who the hell are you?” The dead man shook his head slowly, his thick, blood matted curls brushing against his face with a wet sound that made McLeod feel ill. “Who I am, or consequently am not, is of no bearing on this conversation. Suffice to say, you were looking for Me” and now you have found Me.” McLeod shook his head, not noticing the pain in his smashed jaw. “I wasn’t looking for anyone but Gabriel Delacroix. And you’re not him” or he’s not you.” “On the contrary,” the dead man stood slowly, swaying precariously on legs that should not have supported him. The knees seemed to be hinged backwards, as the former CID lackey bent over McLeod, reaching out for the fallen necklace. “I am he, and soon you will be with us, together.” McLeod laughed, a sound full of darkness, “Aye, and I am the walrus” goo goo cachou. Save your smoke and mirrors for someone who cares to play your games.” “Fool. Insolent fool. Do you not recognize me” Can you not feel my power? Tremble before my greatness? beg for mercy, and perhaps I shall grant it.” McLeod threw his head back, roaring with laughter until spots danced before his eyes. He could feel his breath growing shorter. Life was fading away. “That was rich. Who do you think you are, the great Zeus himself” Perhaps the Duke of Wellington? Or Lord Byron? I bow to no one? and I cowtow to no man. You do not frighten me. Off with you, crow? you scavenging spirit. Let me die in peace.” “You anger me, and for that you will” GAAAAAHHHHH!” it was at that exact moment, that the hawk perched in the tree above decided to make its move, diving down and taking a swipe at the dead man’s face with its deadly talons. The dead man fell, hard, and the crow rose up from the body, leaving the cold, dead corpse behind. The two birds circled each other in the air, one majestic, the other bedraggled, light and dark. Polar opposites. The hawk screamed, diving at the crow, hitting it broadside, knocking it from the sky in a flash of darkness and a cloud of feathery bits. The scavenger fell to the earth, and at once, the hawk was upon it, digging in those lethal claws, being as merciless as any natural born hunter. The crow’s blood seeped into the earth, mingling with that of the dead man’s body. The crow stared, unblinking, a dark, radiant light gleaming in its eye, even is its body shuddered in the last paroxysms of death. And then, that too was extinguished. The hawk shook itself, and looked one last time at the dead crow. Justice had been served, or so it seemed as the silvery wings spread towards the heavens. One last time, the hawk wheeled back towards the earth, scooping up the broken bit of chain, flying away with McLeod’s talisman. It hadn’t been more than a few seconds, but to McLeod, it could well have been a year. He watched the hawk glide away, until it was nothing more than another light speck among the stars? ---- Jackson jerked awake, sweating and trembling, feeling oddly elated. He felt chilled, as though he’d been out in the chill wind. He felt something cold against his wrist, and looked down to find that he was clutching a length of broken chain in his hand, from which dangled a dented and bloodstained pewter charm. A trefoil. At once, the dream came back to him, mingling with the reality of the scenery that flashed by the windows. McLeod in the pasture beyond the safehouse, staked out? the crow? and the hawk. But how? had this come to pass. He didn’t have time to muddle it over, as the cab ground to a halt in a squeal of brakes. The man turned, and muttered in broken English. “We” are? here.” Jackson nodded, and stepped from the back of the conveyance, dropping a handful of guilders through the slit in the window, too much money, but he didn’t care. He slammed the door with purpose, and looked up at the building. Home, at last... yet it was a homecoming that didn't feel at all welcome, and he expected that his host would go to great pains to make him feel anything but. He shrugged one massive shoulder, smoothing out his long jacket, feeling the comforting weight of his trusty P5. Somehow, he knew this would turn out badly... it usually did. But then, at least life was predictable. Zen Buddhism... shit happens... ramalama ding ding. He suppressed a shudder and crossed the wide lawn, tracing the same route Bruce had earlier. He didn't fear death. In fact, he welcomed it, with open arms... at least it would be something different... a challenge. And if nothing else, the man once known as Bradley Jackson had never balked at a challenge. He drew the gun, clicking off the safety, and then he kicked in the steel door, stepping over the threshold and into his destiny with the bits of broken chain still twined between the fingers of his left hand. ---- To Be Continued?
