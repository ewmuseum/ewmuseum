Hey Baby II: The Hunt

19 February 2006

Date: 02/19/2006 ---- Saturday, August 13, 2005 0333 hours, local time CID Safehouse, Amsterdam, Netherlands ---- The cab idled at the curb, the cabby more than content to wait inside his hack, waiting for the mysterious man in black with the American accent. He’d been paid with five crisp hundred dollar bills. He would wait all night if bidden to. The American leaned against the back bumper, oblivious to the wind, and the brisk wind in the air. The wind buffeted the cab, making it rock slightly, whistling against the poorly insulated windows. He’d been out there for a long time; smoking cigarette after cigarette, his presence only known by his restless shifting that caused the small car to rock. The cabby glanced in his mirror, noting the man’s presence for the umpteenth time, and then returned to the videogame he was playing on his cell phone. Modern technology at its best. Outside in the wintry chill, Jackson muttered an epithet, and gazed into the gloom, his eyes narrowed. “Where the fuck are you, McLeod?” As though summoned, a tall man slipped from the shadows, crossing the street with a marked limp that made him seem menacing on a night like this. By all accounts, he should have died that morning in the field behind this same estate, but he hadn’t. He stuffed his hands deep in the pockets of his jacket, and stopped in front of Jackson, his breath steaming from his mouth. The night was unseasonably cold for August, more like fall than the tail end of summer. Both men stood there on the curb, staring at the empty building with its boarded up windows. A for sale sign stood on the vast lawn, another pasted to the iron fence surrounding the property. Both seemed to shudder at the same time, different memories, spawned by this locale. ??Tis a fine night for real estate shopping, did you ask me here for my opinion? Don’t buy the place, too many spooks and haunts in those walls?” McLeod accepted the proffered pack of smokes, and paused to light one, shaking his head slowly. “Aye, this is a place of evil. I can feel it in me bones, I can.” His brogue was thicker tonight, as it usually was when he was agitated. “So can I,” Jackson murmured, looking at the covered windows, thinking it only made the place seem more evil, as though it was watching from behind veiled eyes, plotting secretly. “I don’t ask you here about that. I asked you here for a favor, Bruce. I need you to help me” I don’t know who else to turn to.” McLeod chuckled, “Then you know the price. You owe me, Jackson, I saved your life, or are you forgetting that?” “I seem to recall that debt being paid before it was incurred. It was my blood that brought you back from the brink of the abyss, my friend.” Jackson flicked his cigarette into the wind, and turned to look at McLeod, his indigo gaze boring into McLeod’s, his eyes shifting back and forth slightly, as he tried to see more than what was visible on the surface. “Semantics, nothing more. If that is the case, I still owe you nothing. You placed me in that situation in the first place.” Jackson shrugged, looking away for a moment. “Fuck. You’re right, what the hell do you want me to say, Bruce” It was my fault, I dragged you into that mess and for that I am truly sorry. All of my actions have had terrible consequences. Innocent blood has been spilled too many times, wouldn’t you agree, Bruce?” “Enough. Out with it, I’m getting tired from running in circles with you. What do you want?” Jackson took a deep breath, trying to steady himself, but McLeod heard the catch that gave him away. “I” it’s Kitty. My wife? she? she’s-? “She’s what?” McLeod let the cigarette fall from his fingers, forgotten, his attention riveted on Jackson’s face, and the naked agony he saw on those usually stony features. “Missing, Bruce. She’s missing.” McLeod laughed, thinking this was a setup for a punch line. “Missing, huh” Meaning she left you, or what? Isn’t that a matter for the police?” “No,” he shook his head vehemently, grabbing McLeod by the shoulders. His voice was strained, filled with earnest pleading. “They don’t understand, Bruce. Just listen” it was Gabriel, he kidnapped her?” McLeod’s features sagged in sympathy. “I’m sorry. When?” Jackson shook his head, misery on his face now. “I don’t know” six months? Maybe more? I can’t really remember. Time’s slippery now, Bruce.” “She’s likely dead, y’know?” Jackson bowed his head, saying nothing more as the wind tore at their clothing. “I know that Bruce, that’s what the police have been telling me. They’ve said I should wash my hands of her, have the marriage annulled and have her declared legally dead.” “Aye, seems sensible. So why haven’t you” Do you know something they don’t?” “A son,” he murmured, swiveling those tormented windows to his soul back up to meet Bruce’s eyes. “She was pregnant with my child. The doctors said it was impossible, after her” injuries? but? it happened. I can’t let this go, Bruce, don’t you see? Not when there’s that question? I could be a father.” McLeod snorted, “The chap could be anyone’s” she was with other men in that fighting octagon. The snake, that pirate with the eyepatch, no? He could be the father?” Jackson grabbed a handful of McLeod’s shirt, shoving him back against the cab, hissing in his face. “Fuck you, McLeod, that baby is mine. I don’t fucking care if someone else sired him, I love him and that’s all that matters, goddamn it!” McLeod raised a hand, looking sheepish. “Cripes, calm yourself down?” He ran a hand over his brush cut hair, heaving a sigh. “Fine, I’ll look into this for you. But I expect to be paid for it.” Jackson withdrew his wallet, counting out a stack of crisp hundreds, ten of them to be exact. “That’s your front money, Bruce. Find her and I’ll give you ten times more.” He pressed the money into McLeod’s hands and walked around the cab without another word, pulling open the door. He hesitated there, looking up at the building with one last look of trepidation. McLeod cast a sidelong glance at the larger man, sizing up Jackson, surprised to see fear shining in his eyes as he looked up at the vacant building. “What are you afraid of, Jackson” What you will find in there? Or what you won’t find?” Jackson jerked as though slapped, and swiveled his tormented gaze to McLeod. He chewed on his bottom lip for a moment before answering. “Perhaps a little of both. Love has changed me, McLeod, in ways I really don’t comprehend. I fear for her life. I have no regard for my own. We may very well be walking into a trap. I don’t care. I want answers, hell, I need answers.” “Then why in the hell are we standing out here freezing our arses off?” McLeod shook his head, clearly disgusted. “Aye, the longer we linger out here, the colder the trail is getting, my friend. Let that be your motivation.” “Yeah,” he slammed the door, getting a look from the cabby. He flicked his cigarette into the dark, watching the tiny orange spark spiral away into oblivion, finding himself distracted. He was exhausted, and it was starting to get to him. He squared his shoulders, unbuttoning his jacket as he shouldered open the iron gate. He put his hand on the butt of his pistol where it rested against his hip like an old fashioned gunslinger. Comforting. McLeod followed, no less cautious, a dagger pressed against the inside of his wrist inside his jacket, ready to throw if need be. They both paused on the threshold, exchanging a look. Jackson cocked an eyebrow, asking a silent question to which McLeod replied, in a voice that was barely a whisper. “Aye. “Tis safe.” The rooms were dark, the silence deafening. They snapped on lights as they went, until the night was flooded with light. The front yard was lit up like a ballpark, with light spilling from every window. Every room was undisturbed, every room except one: the room beneath the stairs. It had been a butler’s quarters when the house was built at the turn of the century, now it was more of a study, or it had been. Now it was transformed, into something a tad more unsettling. An examination table was set up, complete with stirrups. The wax paper covering the tabletop was wrinkled and torn, dotted here and there with dark droplets. Jackson crossed at once to the table, drawn there almost unbidden. The drops were deep brown. Blood. Or perhaps iodine. He was betting on blood. He bent his head, smelling the paper, drawing the scent into his lungs. He could smell stale sweat, the stink of fear, and the subtle hint of patchouli oil. Kitty’s perfume. He looked at McLeod, standing in the doorway, his face a mask of misery. “She was here alright.” He touched the paper, feeling the dryness, feeling it crackle beneath his fingers. “It’s dry. He must have brought her here first.” “Aye, but where have they gone now” Can you determine that? Has the connection faded?” Jackson shook his head, “The connection was severed when I took your mark. I can control the power, I can resist the Reaver now, but I cannot feel Gabriel. I cannot feel the darkness any longer.” “Is that really such a bad thing?” McLeod pulled out his dagger, using it to clean his fingernails while he commented idly. “I would hardly think you’re in a position to complain. You could have been dead, with your soul trapped forever in that infernal scrap of vorpal. I would think this is a wee bit preferable.” Jackson wasn’t listening; instead he was bending over to look at something on the floor, pressed against the edge of the table, almost invisible. A scrap of paper, with something glittering beside it. He pulled both objects out, and felt a relieved smile spread across his lips. The shiny object was one of the tiny hoops that Kitty wore in her ears; the catch was bent, and covered in blood. That sight alone made him angry, turning his stomach to ice, and making his skin feverish. The paper was crumpled, covered in letters. It was the cover of a matchbook, the name of the place called the Sand Witch in Albuquerque, New Mexico. He held it up, triumphantly. “A lead, McLeod, we have a lead...” ---- Monday, August 15, 2005 0101 hours, local time somewhere in New Mexico ---- The tires hummed on the asphalt, the music blaring loudly as Jackson hunched over the road map spread out on his lap. He could feel her. Like a compass needle is drawn to true north, he was drawn to her. Maybe it was his imagination, or maybe it was some grand cosmic peculiarity, but it almost seemed that Kitty's earring was leading him. 'Turn left here.' Jackson grunted, speaking absently to McLeod, who sat behind the wheel, expertly steering the midnight blue TransAm through the dark, desolate streets. “Turn left here.” McLeod cranked the wheel sharply to the left, turning down the nearly invisible road in a spray of gravel. The car fishtailed wildly, nearly losing control on the wet stones. McLeod exhaled sharply, and after getting the car under control, glanced sidelong at his companion, and employer. Jackson was slouched in the seat, his eyes rolled back in his head, breathing shallowly, Kitty’s earring dangling from his left earlobe, in place of his usual silver stud. The fingers of his left hand were twisting and turning the small hoop, in an almost disturbing manner. McLeod shook his head slowly, and returned his eyes to the road, flicking on the high beams to cut the gloom that pressed in around the car. “I suppose there’s no sense in asking how you knew to turn down this road when you weren’t even looking?” McLeod paused, sighing loudly, “You know what though, I’m getting really sick of getting screwed over here” I mean? are you planning to drag me around the bleedin? globe on this wild goose chase? Why? This is pointless-? Jackson’s hand lashed out, lighting fast, grabbing the front of McLeod’s shirt. His eyes snapped open, the blue orbs flickering in his skull. His voice resonated in the small confines, filled with quiet authority. “Shut the fuck up and drive” your pointless chatter is distracting me.” He glared into McLeod’s eyes, until The Highlander looked away, gulping as he turned his attention back to the road. Brad nodded once, perfunctorily, and returned to his slouch in the passenger seat, the map sliding to the floor unnoticed. The night zipped past the windows? and for the fiftieth time, McLeod wondered what the hell they were doing out here in the middle of nowhere. Following a long shot hunch, and a scrap of paper? Foolishness. Jackson hadn’t told him a goddamn thing about what the hell was really going on here. He sensed there was more involved than just his wife being taken, but Jackson wasn’t about to spill the details. It was fucking creepy, the whole thing. McLeod looked again at Jackson, who now stared forward through the windshield, his jaw slack, a runnel of drool dripping down his chin. McLeod stomped on the brake, swerving to the shoulder as safely as he could. He nudged Jackson’s shoulder. “Jackson?” Brad?” Nothing. No response. Not even a flicker of acknowledgement. He leaned over, peering into Jackson’s face, growing concerned at the glassiness of his eyes. He passed his hand in front of Jackson’s eyes, still getting no reaction. Smoke curled up from his hand, the half smoked cigarette still smoldering in there, ashes dropping on his pants as the stub burned into his fingers. Quite suddenly, Jackson blinked, drawing in a shuddery breath, and then looked at McLeod, his expression angry. “Why the fuck did you stop” It’s another 2 miles.” McLeod shook his head, peering at Jackson still. “Where did you go, man” Jesus, don’t do that to me? I don’t get paid enough to go on rescue missions into the afterlife? or wherever in the hell you slipped away to.” Jackson snarled, masking his puzzlement with anger. “Do what” What the fuck are you flapping your gums about?” McLeod shook his head, not believing what was happening, wondering if Jackson had finally, totally snapped. Since they’d met, it had always seemed that Jackson was walking a razor’s edge between madness and genius. The scales appeared to be tipping in the extreme towards the former. Realizing that discretion is ALWAYS the better part of valor, McLeod grunted, and resumed driving down the narrow country road. The trees were barren, gleaming in the wash of headlights like bleached bones. Death loomed close in this neck of the woods. He half expected to see cow skulls along the sides of the road, or perhaps the entire skeleton, animated like a Hallowe’en party trick. Jackson bent forward, pushing in the cigarette lighter just as the car rounded a sharp curve. McLeod shouted an epithet and slammed on the brakes, breathing hard. In the glow of the headlights, they appeared to be in a tree lined natural cul-de-sac with no exit. The trees pressed in close, their shadows reaching towards the car, shifting, almost like the talons of some monster. McLeod turned to look at Jackson, his expression filled with exasperation, turning to anger as he watches Jackson idly light another cigarette, taking a long drag. He was the portrait of calm, if not for the wildness in his eyes. McLeod on the other hand, was about three seconds away from going nuclear. “What the fuck, man! Why the hell am I driving down a dead end road” This cannot possibly be their route, ye bloody arse. There isn’t a way out!” Brad exhaled in McLeod’s face, a wry smile twisting his mouth as he responds, amused as McLeod splutters, coughing and blinking away the searing smoke. “McLeod, the road isn't dead, but your career might be if you don't get out of the damned car and move those sticks out of the fucking way! This is what I pay you for, asshole. Move, soldier!” Jackson points out the windshield, at an enormous pile of deadfall that looms in the headlights, his eyes gleaming strangely. McLeod looks, and then looks back at Jackson, his expression one of disgust. “My career, I think not. I think perhaps it’s your goddamn wrestling career on the line now, Jackson. You forget who you’re talking to. I can kill you, as easy as breathing.” Jackson simply stared, his expression unwavering, radiating authority. “What the fuck are you waiting for”! MOVE YOUR WORTHLESS ASS!” McLeod opened the door, a longsuffering sigh escaping his pursed lips as he stepped into the night. Silence was overwhelming, even the purr of the high-toned engine seemed to be swallowed by the heavy stillness. The preternatural silence was downright eerie, and he shuddered as he crossed in front of the car. Not a branch moved, not a leaf fluttered, not a fly buzzed. It almost seemed to be an alien environment, utterly devoid of life. McLeod approached what had seemed to be a deadfall of brush. Upon closer inspection, it did not appear to be a natural formation at all. Under the loose branches was a pile of large logs, fully a foot in diameter and ten feet in length. McLeod judged that each would weigh over three hundred pounds. “Great?” He paused, looking back over his shoulder at the car, squinting in the glare of the headlights. He hoped to ask Jackson for help, but what he saw made him reconsider. Jackson’s head was cocked to the side as if he was listening to something. McLeod saw his lips move as if he were answering an unheard question, his hand gesturing as he drove a point home. No cell phone was present. Great, the man was arguing with himself. Wonderful. Could the night get any better than this? With a string of curses that would make a sailor blush, McLeod turned to the pile, grabbing the loose sticks and branches and tossing them aside, where they clatter loudly on the dry earth, making him flinch at the sudden burst of noise. Sweat was already running down the back of his neck as he bent to move the first log. He muttered to himself as he wrapped his arms around the log, using his weight to drag it backwards across the clearing. “Sticks my arse. Why the fuck do I have to do all the work?” His anger began to swell? fueled by his irritation at the entire situation. Why had he agreed to go on this fool’s quest? It was filled with nothing but danger, his senses screamed at him when he’d accepted, but the money had been a great incentive. Along with Jackson’s empty promises. One by one, the pile dwindled? until only half of them remained? perhaps six or seven logs. McLeod collapsed in a heap, gasping and wheezing, sweat stinging his eyes, his hands raw and bleeding from the bite of the rough wood. As he crouched there, wishing he were anywhere but here, he heard the car door open? footsteps crunching over the dry ground until they stopped beside him. A black, highly polished combat boot rested beside his face? it took the last of his energy for McLeod to flop backwards, so that he was staring up at Jackson. Jackson’s eyes were unreadable in the gloom as he returned McLeod’s tortured gaze. McLeod couldn't help but notice how the light didn't seem to reach Jackson through the trees, but yet, everything else was well lit. It was as if a cloak of night adorned the Dark Horse. Exhausted, he watched as Jackson effortlessly picked up the remaining logs one at a time, and threw them into the woods. It had been all McLeod could do to drag them to the side, but Jackson treated them like so much kindling. He should enter one of those lumberjack contests, or those strongest man competitions where they have to drag around buses, McLeod thought, watching with exhausted distraction. In a few moments the way was clear. Jackson returned to McLeod’s side, kicking him hard in the ribs. “Do you want a fucking engraved invitation” Get up, you worthless Mick! Get in the car and drive, you lazy pile of shit!” Black dots danced before his eyes, and for a few heartbeats, the world lost focus. When the color bled back into the night, he pulled himself shakily to his feet, sweat still dripping from his brow. McLeod hadn't yet noticed that Jackson had returned to the car and taken the wheel. It was just as well, because he doubted he had the strength to hold the wheel, let alone remain conscious. He didn’t get paid enough for this shit. Sliding into the passenger seat, McLeod barely had time to get his legs in the car before Jackson floored the gas and sent organic detritus spraying from the rear wheels. Slewing through the forest at an extremely high speed, Jackson seemed to know exactly where he was going, his knuckles white where he gripped the wheel, his jaw clenched in sheer determination. In the distance, McLeod saw a break in the treeline. As they came up to it, Jackson slammed on the brakes, sliding the powerful car sideways in the loose dirt. A triumphant smile crossed his lips, manic glee gleaming in his eyes as he turned to McLeod, his voice deafening in the stillness as he crowed. “Here! This is where it happened! This is the place!” Jackson leapt from the car, crossing the narrow paved road that lay before them, his hand outstretched as he crossed the blacktop, his movements slow and hesitant, as though he was being drawn there. McLeod realized Jackson’s destination, his eyes pulled towards the massive, gnarled tree whose branches stretched out towards the car, almost as though beckoning. Scars marked the twisted trunk, a pale gash that stood out in the glow of the headlights. He could barely tear his eyes away; as he found himself opening his own door, leaving it open in his haste to reach the tree. Dead brush and scrub grass crackled underfoot as Jackson crossed to the base of the immense tree. A presence loomed in the air, and they could both feel it, prickling the skin? a cold wind, nipping at his flesh like the bite of an icy December wind. Slowly, Jackson pressed his hands against the tree, gingerly at first? then he clasped his palms to the wood, feeling the stickiness of the wound on the tree. It was fresh, the sap still flowing, the lifeblood of this massive tree. A shiver of pain coursed through his hand, running through his body, and ramming into his head with the force of a Mac truck. Images accompanied the onslaught of pain? and he could smell the burning rubber? he could feel her fear as she saw the tree looming? and then? the pain? as she was thrown from the car, coming to rest against the tree? there? his eyes found a dark smear on the pale slash. Blood? Kitty’s blood. McLeod reached the tree just as Brad bent his head. What the hell was he doing? McLeod stepped closer, and what he saw stopped him in his tracks. Jackson was licking the tree, lapping at a dark smear like a cat would lick up a saucer of milk. He grunted and then sagged to his knees, face upturned, his eyes fastened on McLeod. He was panting and the gleam in his eyes was almost feral. His lips were stained reddish, and so were his teeth when he grinned, revealing something that suddenly seemed all wrong. Too even, too large. A low sound of terror burst from McLeod’s mouth as he turned to run, only to be roughly hauled backwards by the collar of his leather jacket. Jackson blinked, his eyes flickering? the irises seemed to whirl, colors swirling. His lips thinned as he glared at McLeod, bending the younger man’s will. His voice was filled with understanding as he whispered, his gaze returning to the tree. “I know” now. She was here, with him. They had an accident; she took the wheel, and pulled it from his grip. He was too weak to fight her. His power is waning? the blade hasn’t fed. He wants to kill her; I can feel it in the air. He wants to destroy something beautiful. He covets that which he cannot have, but the blade refuses to do his bidding. It cannot as long as it bears the mark. She is safe, for now, if we can find her in time?” “And the child?” McLeod found himself saying, almost regretting his words as Jackson flinched visibly. “I” cannot say?”
