Heir's Inheritance, Part 1

6 January 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/6/05 Out in the West Texas town of El Paso I fell in love with a Mexican girl. Night-time would find me in Rosa's cantina; Music would play and Felina would whirl. April 22nd, 1988 2236 hours Mexico City, Mexico Life was good... and as he walked into his favorite cantina with his two daughters on his arms, Mateo didn't see how it could get much better. He was tired, and his sides and arms were sore, and his throat was parched... but he had his family, an adoring public, and a career that had just rocketed into the stratosphere like a comet burning bright... Mateo Ramirez was on top of the world right now. 'Hay una mesa vac’a, Pap?,' said the elder of his two daughters. 'Llamar? a Mam? y la avisar? estamos aqu?.' 'There's an empty table, Dad. I'll call Mom and let her know we're here.' 'Muy bien, mi chica,' said Mateo. 'Tan pronto como ella llega, nos tendremos una peque’a celebraci’n.' 'Very well, my child. As soon as she arrives, we'll have ourselves a little celebration.' 'D’gale venir hambriento, Teresa,' said the younger daughter to her sister. 'Los olores que vienen de la cocina son divinos.' 'Tell her to come hungry, Teresa... the smells coming from the kitchen are heavenly.' Blacker than night were the eyes of Felina, Wicked and evil while casting her spell. My love was deep for this Mexican maiden; I was in love, but in vain, I could tell. Mateo's younger daughter smiled at him as they sat down at the empty table and Teresa went to the pay phone to call her mother. The girl smoothed out her dress as she hummed along with the music playing, absent-mindedly tapping a foot along to the beat. The tapping opened up a short slit on the side of her dress, revealing a few extra inches of leg and sheer tan stocking to a rough-looking, unshaven, somewhat inebriated gringo from Arizona, who was down here on business... business he detested. His own wife had run off with some handsome Mexican dance instructor and was suing for divorce-- and he had to come down here to get the paperwork finalized. He was sad... he was depressed... he was lonely... and right about now, he was ready to blame his slowly breaking heart on any Mexican male between the height of 5'5' and 5'10,' weighing between 130 and 180 pounds, with medium-short, straight dark hair and a smile that women would die for. Mateo Ramirez fit into all of those categories very nicely. One night a wild young cowboy came in, Wild as the West Texas wind. Dashing and daring, a drink he was sharing With wicked Felina, the girl that I loved. The man got up and stumbled over to the table where Mateo and his daughter waited for a waitress to take their order. Teresa was just finishing up her phone call. The man flashed a lopsided grin at the younger daughter and touched her shoulder in a rather inappropriate way as he addressed her in somewhat broken Spanish. 'Ven ac?, batata dulce,' he said in an unbecoming tone. '¿Que tal un baile lento?' 'Come here, sweet potato... how about a slow dance?' It was the wrong thing to say, the wrong time to say it, and the wrong man's daughter to say it to. Mateo was in the man's face in a heartbeat, recognizing that he was from north of the border and refusing to honor him by speaking to him in the Spanish language, even though the man's Spanish appeared to be fluent (even if broken from his intoxication), and Mateo's English was poor. So in anger, I Challenged his right for the love of this maiden. Down went his hand for the gun that he wore. My challenge was answered in less than a heartbeat; The handsome young stranger lay dead on the floor. 'Hands off, gringo,' he warned ominously, 'Before you have to wear cast on it eight weeks.' The gringo sneered at Mateo, clearly viewing him as if he posed no threat. Almost casually he pushed the man aside. 'Not talking to you, flea,' he said dismissively. 'How about we let the lady decide?' Just for a moment I stood there in silence, Shocked by the foul, evil deed I had done. Many thoughts raced through my mind as I stood there; I had but one chance- and that was to run! Even before Mateo could react, the girl was on her feet, lashing out with a back-handed slap right across the jowls of the drunken gringo. 'Pig!' she snarled, also in English. The inebriated man grabbed the girl around the waist, breathing beer-reeking breath into her face. 'Full of fire, eh, chi--' Out through the back door of Rosa's I ran, Out where the horses were tied. I caught a good one, it looked like it could run. Up on its back and away I did ride, He didn't get to finish. A deft grab of his wrist by Mateo spun him around into a vicious right cross that sent the gringo flying into the table he had been sharing with three newly-made friends. The friends were all a little tipsy as well... and now their drinks were spilled all over their shirts. Needless to say, they didn't like that much... and now there were four men all advancing threateningly on the handsome man who was defending his daughter like a gallant. Just as fast as I Could from the West Texas town of El Paso Out to the badlands of New Mexico. Back in El Paso my life would be worthless. Everything's gone in life; nothing is left. It's been so long since I've seen the young maiden My love is stronger than my fear of death. Mateo proved to be brilliantly talented in fighting arts... flinging the men about with deft judo throws like they were near-weightless and stunning them with flurries of surprisingly powerful blows. But when every second or third punch, kick, or throw is answered with a brutal punch directly from behind into the small of your back over the kidneys, it adds up... and a couple of chairs busted over his head, also from directly behind each time, laid him out on the ground in a slightly bloody heap. I saddled up and away I did go, Riding alone in the dark. Maybe tomorrow a bullet may find me. Tonight nothing's worse than this pain in my heart. Teresa had returned from the telephone niche... and she didn't waste words. With her sister by her side, she waded directly into the middle of the group, picking up where her father left off. Suddenly the gringos were taking every bit as much punishment as before... but their new opponent had a partner watching her back, defending her from all sides that she personally could not. It was now two against four, instead of only one against four... and those two, even if both young women not properly dressed for a fight, operated together like two parts of a single well-oiled machine, co-operating for attack and defense purposes with chilling effectiveness. Where one of them wasn't strong enough to throw the heavier attackers for a loop-de-loop, both of them together were... if one punch wasn't strong enough to double one of the men over, two were. If one kick didn't knock one of the men down, a double kick from each of them would. Teresa and her sister stood disheveled but almost completely unscathed, in a protective back-to-back formation over their fallen father, as the four drunken gringos pulled themselves out of a jumble of splintered table wood; their clothes torn, their bodies covered with sweat and bruises. They looked at each other, then looked at the women fearfully, and ran out of the cantina for dear life. And at last, here I Am on the hill overlooking El Paso; I can see Rosa's cantina below. My love is strong and it pushes me onward. Down off the hill to Felina I go. The battle over, Teresa kneeled over her stricken father, tears forming in her eyes. '?Pap?! “Pap”! Hablame papa, “est’s bien?' 'Dad! Dad! Speak to me Dad, are you okay?' Mateo's eyes opened slowly, groggily, and a thread of blood trickled from the corner of his mouth. 'Teresa...' Off to my right I see five mounted cowboys; Off to my left ride a dozen or more. Shouting and shooting, I can't let them catch me! I have to make it to Rosa's back door! Teresa looked at her sister, who was near hysteria, and rested a firm hand on her shoulder. 'Ay’deme, “lo hemos de obtener a un doctor!' 'Help me; we've got to get him to a doctor!' With her sister's help, Teresa picked Mateo up, and both daughters put his arms over their shoulders and helped him out of the cantina as everyone watched on silently. Mateo's wife was just pulling up to the parking lot, and her face froze in horror as she looked at what had happened to her husband. She helped her daughters put Mateo into the car, and with all four of them inside, they sped off to their family doctor's office. Another man also got into his car and drove off in the opposite direction... toward the news offices of the newspaper he worked for. His 35mm camera fresh with a whole collection of photographs. He recognized the fighting style of both Mateo and his daughters... just wait until the public read about THIS. Something is dreadfully wrong, for I feel A deep burning pain in my side! Though I am trying to stay in the saddle, I'm getting weary, unable to ride. April 23, 1988 0702 hours Morning rush hour was beginning in Mexico city; the working class of citizens hurrying out to get to work, whether in business for themselves or working for others. Some people bought the morning newspaper, looking for something to read while on the bus, or at the office during slow periods. Headlining the sports section was a large photo of Mateo lying on the floor of the cantina, his two daughters standing back to back over him as they fought off the attackers; the photo showing a clear look at Mateo's face... and accompanied by a close-up inset photo of him from just before the altercation started. The headline and first paragraph of the article read as follows: El Zorro Azul protected by women? Patrons at the trendy Maria's Cantina on East Burro Avenue were witness to the most shocking altercation that might have been seen in years, when Lucha Libre sensation 'El Zorro Azul,' who recently won the Mexican All-star Light-Heavyweight Championship, was overcome by a couple of drunken patrons from America... and had to be protected by his two clearly adolescent daughters. But my love for Felina is strong and I rise where I've fallen, Though I am weary, I can't stop to rest! I see the white puff of smoke from the rifle, I feel the bullet go deep in my chest... Although El Zorro Azul has never been seen without his mask before, the fighting style exhibited by the handsome man shown in the photos was unmistakable... and the two young women seen defending him exhibited an identical style, modified to work for two partners in a tag-team. 'I was shocked when I was told,' said one witness who had not realized the man was El Zorro Azul. 'That he had to have his daughters come to his rescue... it was shameful. I won't be watching his matches on TV anymore.' 'It was an insult to machismo and the spirit of Lucha Libre,' said another patron who regularly took his kids to watch local shows. 'He should step down as champion immediately. I will be boycotting all further shows until then.' El Zorro Azul was reportedly treated by a doctor and sent home. His family was not available for comment. From out of nowhere Felina has found me, Kissing my cheek as she kneels by my side. Cradled by two loving arms that I'll die for, One little kiss, and... Felina, gooooood-byyyyyyye. (*OOC -- News article snippet translated from Spanish. Zorro Azul means Blue Fox.) El Paso, Marty Robbins, “1959
