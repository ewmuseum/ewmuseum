Another lazy weekend afternoon

12 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/12/04 Usually, on lazy weekend afternoons such as this one, Brick and his best friend Aaron play a little one-on-one in the park or out getting lunch and discussing business with DMAK. Not this afternoon. The two have spent a considerable amount of time reading through Brick’s comic book collections. Specifically, they’re reading through the comics featuring Luke Cage. Ever since it was first announced that plans were in the works for a live-action Luke Cage movie, Brick has been looking to get involved. He has looked to branch out into acting and he wants this movie to be his first starring role. “Tell me again why I’m here,” Aaron sighs. “I need your help,” Brick replies. “With what” This whole Luke Cage business is your thing.” “Look, I’m getting ready to take a big step in my life. I’m getting ready to go into a new area that I’ve hardly ever been in before, and I’m trying to get attached to a project that I believe in. I figured if anybody would help me, it would be my best friend.” “But we’ve been at this all day. You read the comics, you know the character. This isn’t necessary.” “You’d just better hope they give Cage a different look for this movie. I know you don’t want to dress up like this,” he says, pointing to a cover on one of the comics. Granted, Luke Cage’s original look was very dated. The open-chested yellow shirt with butterfly collar, the chain belt, the metal headband (which a lot of fans call “the tiara”), the yellow buccaneer boots. The whole look definitely screams cheesy. “Yeah, I figure ain’t nobody gonna get behind a cat dressing like this nowadays. I mean, they changed the X-Men’s entire look for the movie. Singleton’s a fan, too, but I don’t think he’s that big a fan that he doesn’t know what does and what doesn’t look good on film, and the original Luke Cage outfit does not look good on film.” “Hell, it don’t even look good on printed page,” says Aaron. “Back in the day, a costume like this might have gotten by.” “I doubt it. Man, Bootsy Collins “Yeah, but who could have played Luke Cage back in the day?” “I always saw Jim Brown playing the part. If you are going for bad-ass black man, then Jim Brown is your man.” “Really” I always saw Fred Williamson as Luke Cage.” “Black Caesar?” “What’s wrong with Fred Williamson” Tell me you don’t think the artists made him look like “I have to go with Carl Weathers. He had the build, the height and the look back then, plus enough acting ability.” Aaron then reaches into his pocket. “Oh, I almost forgot. I printed this up from the Internet.” “What is it?” “It’s an interview with Avi Arad.” “The head of Marvel Studios?” “Yeah, read down a few pages.” Brick reads down to the following. CC: Let’s talk Luke Cage. Now this is a property that’s been floating around for some time now, right? AA: Yes. Power Man’Luke’he’s one of the properties that’s kind of been in and out of limbo since the 80’s’both in terms of comics as well as film development. But we’ve made some progressive steps. He’s come a long way. CC: I’ll say. He doesn’t wear that silver tiara anymore, so I’d call that a step forward. AA: Yes, well’we will be updating his look. It will be contemporary. We’re not going the Starsky & Hutch route. CC: Yeah, I guess it would suck to have to share billing with David Soul & Paul Michael Glaser. Anyway, seeing as how it’s hard to pass yourself off as macho while wearing a canary yellow disco shirt, are there any definitive ideas on how to craft his image? Will he have some customized Sean John regalia? AA: (quizzical look) Sean John? Ahh? CC: You know, P. Diddy’s fashion line’he’s probably available for wardrobe consultation... AA: (laughing)?I just don’t think we’ve gotten that far yet. CC: What about his origin? Is he still the ex-con who gets beat down by Mr. Charlie? AA: Mister Charlie? CC: You know, The Man. Whitey. AA: (vaguely embarrassed look)?No, not quite. There will be some elements of his background in there, where he had a troubled youth. But Cage kind of gets maligned, a little unfairly, I think. In conversations I’ve had with Stan, he related that his original concept was to do a modern-day version of the Count of Monte Cristo, by Alexander Dumas. He updated it for the modern, urban world, and yes, there were certain movies that were popular during that time. We won’t get into that, but... CC: The Shaft comparison? Shut your mouth! AA: (brief sigh) Yes, well, anyway (smile). Back to my point, Power Man was pioneering in the sense that he was the first black protagonist who was given his own book from the get go. Eventually, teaming up with Iron Fist, which I think was the first black/white buddy pairing in a regular series. CC: One thing that some fans are disappointed about is that Cage and Iron Fist are being developed for separate movies. AA: That’s understandable. A lot of fans grew up reading the team-up books. Still, to be fair they did start off separately in their own books, and we think they’re strong enough to star in their own features. CC: So, in the future, we could eventually get to see a team-up movie with Luke and Danny? AA: Well’anything’s possible. When characters are juggled at different studios, that’s kind of the main obstacle’but you know, co-ventures still happen every so often, so it’s not totally far-fetched. CC: In the comics, Luke always had these sanitized expletives like “Sweet Christmas”, and “Spit!” Can we look forward to some of the same wacky slang in the movie? AA: Ah? I believe it will deal with some of Luke’s catchphrases, in some manner. We want to do a PG-13 movie, so I’m thinking we’ll avoid heavy cursing. In the comics, you had to be creative about it. In movies, you have the freedom to swear, but that gets you an “R” really quick (smiles). Luke Cage is a knight of the urban world, but it may explore an international side as well.. I think he can work well in diverse locales.. CC: Let’s see’when it comes to casting, do you have anyone in mind, something to get the fanboys chattering? AA: To be fair, we haven’t sent out official invitations to anyone yet’but I can see a number of people. LL Cool J, Vin Diesel, The Rock. CC: Really? That’s interesting? I guess it’s a good thing it’s not a period piece, then. AA: Oh? CC: Well yeah’all these guys are more or less shaven bald, and I’m thinking they’d look terrible in an afro wig.. AA: (chuckles)?you might be right. But like I said, casting hasn’t been done, so we’ll see what happens. I think fans will like it. “Well.” “OK, LL I can see. But Vin Diesel and the Rock?” “What’s wrong with them?” “Neither of them look like Cage and Diesel can’t act worth a damn!” “Well, it’s not like you’ve got that impressive an acting resume.” “Well, I’m better for the part than those two.” “Then maybe you should let Arad know this.” “You know you’re right. I gotta get ready.” “And how do you plan to do that?” “By doing what I’ve been doing.” And with that, they resume reading comic books. “Oh, and Brick?” “Yeah?” “Even if you don’t get the part, you’re still a Black hero.” Brick smiles and resumes reading.
