Path to redemption. Part 2

1 July 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/1/04 The trip to Flint was one that he had never enjoyed making, but this time there was more of a reason behind it than before. This time he was driven by purpose and a goal. A quest if you will to rebuild the army of The Nevermore. But was he fit to lead such a group? He was going to explain that he was far from in control enough to lead this army into battle once more when in truth, it was nothing more than another game. A deception like most other things in his life that he had bargained for. And yet, Salaok would go to The Master and speek with him, asking for guidance in his troubled times. The void had controlled Salaok's life for over a year after the death of Lisha and then Darrell, so seeking out the man who was once in control of such a thing was the perfect thing to do. But would it truely be hidding under deception that Salaok would be seeking out such a man, or was there some truth to his search for guidance? Even Salaok didn't truely know this anymore, as it would seem as he would these days take his own mind games so far that he could not tell the difference between reality and his games. Many things had changed in Salaok's life since the passing of his family, including his state of sanity, or lack there of for that matter. The Council of Harvesters thought that they had picked up on it until he had proven them wrong by taking control into his own hands again. However, the question wasn't, can he take control again, but should he allowed to in the state that they knew he was in. The sound of thunder rolling around the clouds over head gave yet another grim reminder of exactly the whether situation in this town as Salaok pulls his black leather trench coat around his body, tucking his hands under the opposite arms, in hope to stay warm. Although he had only been here once before, he knew his way like the back of his hand, almost as if he was being drawn to the place. And that he was. A world in which a crimson void once controlled such a tragic kingdom, now seemed to be pulling at Salaok. Haunting him. Reminding him of his past and that which he has done to others. He knew the man he was going to see was no longer the man in whom he stood against for so long before due to his beliefs and actions, but instead was a man in whom no one could ever invision him talking to. And yet he must. He must walk into the church just like he had walked into church for the funerals of Lisha, Darrell and most recently, his own father for the second time. Not many people get the chance to have two funerals, but Chris Davis did. And Salaok couldn't have been happier to see his father put into the ground for the second time during his life. And as Salaok rounds the corner, he sees the building. The tired old factory with Gabriel had bought. A factory in which with his own bare hands he had turned into a church. A church once of Void, but now of God. Even though Salaok had never set foot in the place, it's power strikes at his heart at first sight of the structure. Unsure if it was the chill of knowing what he was about to step into, or the extreme cold air which seemed to filter around the area but Salaok suddenly found himself in a shiver. ---- 30th of September 2004 Standing in front of the audiance, he shivers as he looks down at his father, remembering the past between the two. Calling the group of people who had gathered for this event an audiance for most people wouldn't be the correct discription. However, this was a funeral, and for Salaok to be getting up in public and doing the speak about a life gone by. A life which in Salaok's mind had gone on for many years past what it ever should have. A life in which had found itself not only alive but willing to taunt his sons all over again. Again Salaok looks down at his father but this time, a grin forms on his face but quickly he tries to hide it. For while he is up on this stage, he had to put on a show for his audiance. 'It was a day that will forever be planted in my head, the day that I found outt hat my father, Chris Davis had been murdered. His life, although longer than some, just wasn't long enough for us to fully be able to thankful for what he has done.' Salaok looks up from his piece of paper at the stone faces of those in the church. Looking around the holy building he doesn't see a single tear in the eye of any of those who had come to pay their respects. Or lack there of them. It was at this stage that Salaok crumpled up the speach in which he had written for his father in the car ride over to the church and began to speak freely. 'Who am I trying to fool. Look at us, we all came here to make it seem like we cared about Chris Davis when in truth, most of us couldn't stand the site of the man, and the rest of you merely disliked everything about him. Chris Davis, to me and my family died with I was just a child and strangely enough, much like this death, he was murdered then too. If one man can die twice in a lifetime and both were a result of murders, you've got to wonder what this man was doing wrong.' It was as soon as Salaok had said those words, that a voice inside of his head seemed to speak to him. 'The only thing he was doing wrong was breathing. But you put an end to that now didn't you brother.' Instantly Salaok looks around the room for the man who speaks inside his mind. The man in whom had killed his wife over a year ago. He looks for Mani Shigeru. It wasn't until his eyes reaches the second level of the church that he sees a man sitting in the shadows with his feet up on the pue in front of him. There he sits, relaxed and in control once again as laughter begins to echo from his mind into Salaok's. 'I'm not going to say that Chris Davis deserved to die in such a manner, but the mere fact that not many people in the world care if the man is alive or dead. Chris hurt a lot of people with his fake death all those years ago, so to have him return only to cause more pain and suffering for his family, then you ask why did we let him back in? Family is family no matter what.' Salaok says while looking straight up to his younger half brother. 'No matter what your family has done, you give them the benifit of the doubt that they will do their best in order to right the wrongs of the past.' ---- Righting the wrongs of the pasts is the only thought that Salaok had in his mind as he walks closer and closer to the giant metal gates which keep the church safe from those who would do it harm. One of those people were always deemed to be Salaok. But not today. For today Salaok sought out guidance and advice from a man in whom he respected for what he has done in his life. Stopping, Salaok looks up at a window on the second floor of the building and sees a figure, a large figure watching his arrival. Although only a dark outline gives the man away, Salaok feels instantly who it is as there had always been an aura emitted from this man that called out to Salaok, gaining his attention whenever it was possible. It was this man who originally convinced Salaok to leave the haunted grounds of The Lair and go to The Tragic Kingdom as it was known back then. Finally stepping up to the gates, Salaok puts his hand on the metal structure and pushes. It doesn't move. Straight away Salaok looks up to the window, as if looking for an answer but the figure is gone. Could Gabriel have known of his lies in which he brings? Could his knowledge of Salaok have seen past the deception in which he was about to create? It had been too long and even with the help of his lord there was no way that Gabriel could have known that Salaok has no intention on talking the truth. Or at least the truth the way most people would have seen it. But alas, for five minutes Salaok stood at the gates, waiting for the giant monk as he now was to come and greet him and still nothing. The drip, drip, drip of rain began to hit his head and shoulders and soon the small drops had been followed by billions of their friends. The lightning opens up the sky for a mere second only to then be followed instantly by a crack of thunder proving that the storm was right over head now. Knowing that standing outside the church in the rain was going to get him no where, he understands that turning and walking away was the only thing that he could do if he wanted to get out of the storm. However, Salaok was too stubborn to just give in and walk away from such a thing that had taken him hours to arrive at. Instead of seeking shelter from the storm, Salaok simply stands there looking at the church made from an old factory in front of him. His hair now staturated falls down over his face and water runs off his trench coat in rivers to the bottom of the trail before forming a mini waterfall to the ground. For what seemed to be hours, Salaok stood there, in the rain. Struggling to even have a cigarette as the rain drops would wet the paper and ruin it unless he managed to shield it with his hand which only exposed more skin and thus made him more cold. Growing increasingly tired of the storm, he looks up to the sky for a sign of hope and it was right there. An end to the storm clouds was about to move over head and although it wasn't clear blue sky with a warming sun to dry him off, it was a relief from the storm none the less. Finally the rain stops and it was at the exact time that the final drop of rain hits the ground that the front door to the church swings open and out walks the giant man looking like any old monk. In his hand a pair of gardening gloves as his work on the grounds was never ending, much like his work with God. Walking up the path, Gabriel sees Salaok and nods his head in acknowledgement of his arrival, then turns to the garden and kneels down on the edge of it. Seemingly stunned at the almost shun treatment that he has received from the monk, Salaok grabs a hold of the gate and calls out to the man he once knew as Master of The Void. 'Gabriel, why do you not talk to me now? Why do you make me stand behind these gates during the storm, only to give me a nod and then ignore me? Have you forgotten who I am? I haven't forgotten who you are Forever Knight!' Salaok's tone is angry at least, which clearly doesn't bother Gabriel as he continues going about his grounds work while he answers back in a calm and clear manner. 'It has been a long time since anyone called me that, however I didn't make you do anything Salaok, you where the one who chose to stand there. And if you had tried the gate again, you would have found that I unlocked it not long after your first attempt at opening it. You've always been too fast to make up your mind Salaok. However, I see your ability to wait for what you want has grown.'
