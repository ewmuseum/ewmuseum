Gravedigger

18 September 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09.18.03 Gravedigger... As we sat the casket down onto the plot, it was already beginning to rain again... both in the cemetary and in our hearts. I had done well to hold in my emotions all day, though I have been a volcano ready to erupt on the inside. I'd not once lost my nerve or temper, save when the funeral director actually had the spaldings to ask me for my autographs as the service was about to begin. However, now there was a different type of pain inside of me, and it was finding it's way out. It was when I looked into Gunner's face that prompted it all. She looked back at me with great sorrow on her face, trying her best to fight a warming smile through it... for me, so that I would feel better. Unfortunantly, I could see the pain in her eyes. She didn't know Todd all that well. In fact, I believe they only met once, so I knew the pain came solely from seeing me hurt. I'm so grateful that she came with me today. It's hard enough putting your best friend in the ground. Let alone doing it apart from the one you seek for comfort. When you dig my grave... Todd and I had been best friends since we were six years old in the first grade. Twenty years is a long time to be that close to someone. We had so many good times, and he was one of my biggest supporters when I was getting into the business. He'd seen every one of my matches and was there with my family when I won my very first World's Title. Now I have to put him in a grave when we're twenty-six years of age... all because of a damn drunk driver. He wasn't old enough to be taken from us... not yet. And if I ever see that driver again, he'll be the guest of honor at his own funeral sooner than later. God... I can't imagine what his mother must be going through. Could you make it shallow... Jackie has always been like a second mother to me. In fact, I even call her mom and she introduces me as her other son. Hell, she helped raise me, just as my mom helped raise Todd, what would one expect. She thanked me earlier for agreeing to speak at the end of the graveside service. The honor, though, is truly mine and I am thankful for the chance to do so. But I never thought it would be this hard. I crack up up just by looking at Jackie, and I'm not even up to speak yet. The tears in her eyes and the sorrow on her face are almost too much for me to bare. As I look at her all I can think of is how angry I am at the driver that killed Todd. How could someone be that careless... destroying this many people's lives all for a few drinks. Then I look at Gunner... and the tears come. So that I can feel the rain... When I look at her face, I think of Ice. How he's threatened her physically and mentally... only to get to me. About the blood, his blood, smeared on her face. I think of what I'm going to do to him when I finally get my hands around his neck. I think of his vicious attacks on myself and on Gun. Of how I'm going to break him... Break his body, his mind... his spirit. Break his pride. I think of his blatant betrayal of our friendship... and how precious friendship truly is. The minister breaks my thoughts. Gravedigger... It's my turn to speak. I make my way past the other pallbearers and look down on the casket, inside of which my best friend now permanantly resides. Gravedigger, When you dig my grave, Could you make it shallow, So that I can feel the rain. Gravedigger. I miss Todd.
