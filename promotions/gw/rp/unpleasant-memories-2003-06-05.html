Unpleasant Memories

5 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/5 This is me, actin bad, seems like from birth. Standing here naked, huddled up against the frigid tiles of my shower with my newly acquired Platinum Title serving as my final fig leaf as it were while I cower in the back corner in an attempt to shield myself from the icy water pouring from the shower head. It’s meant to sober me up but fuck that, it’s freezing. My evasion techniques work to no avail though as the water beats down on me in a bedlam of droplets before gurgling around and then down the drain. A party to celebrate my championship victory rages on outside this room. Deaconpalooza they’re calling it. Too bad I can’t enjoy it, once again I’ve over done it. My head is pounding. No more than thirty feet away massive speakers pump the soundtrack to the thundering pandemonium going on all throughout my house, complete with ear splitting lyrics and bass that rumbles across the floors before rattling every window in the house. Amidst all of this the only thing I hear is myself murmuring words I spoke much earlier in the evening. “Two more shots and it’s all over.” I said. Now fifteen shots later I’m all over this wall tryin not to fall over, shivering all the while as I clutch my belt. I fully open my squinted eyes and unroll them from the back of my head for half of a split second in order to take a glance at my latest accolade. Partially just to make sure it was still there. I still haven’t gotten used to all of this yet. “Deacon Holmes, Global Wrestling’s own Platinum Champion.” I mumble to myself unintelligibly. It brings a smile to my face and a slight chuckle escapes my lips as I think about that night. Leviathan had just beaten Jay Robson to a bloody pulp and pinned him in the middle of the ring in order to retain his title as Primestar Champion, the one he beat me for. No one expected what happened next. Who would’ve thought I’d dive right on top of Robson while Leviathan’s back was turned to score the second pinfall? Certainly not him. Hell, most people probably didn’t even expect me to come away with a title. That’s what I like though, bein a surprise. The wildcard, the hand up Mona Lisa’s skirt, no one saw me comin?. I had sharpened my appetite to the point that you could split an atom with my desire, an no one was gonna stop me from walkin? outta there a champion. As I slump down slowly into the pale cold porcelain tub I feel my thoughts drifting off someplace they usually dare not wander. My grandmother: Hattie May Jackson, and the years I spent with her. My childhood. Not that I’m all that old now, eighteen to be exact. I lived with her because unlike most children in the neighborhood I had no mother, she died giving birth to me, which I think is part of the reason she was so hard on me. And like most other children where I came up, my dad was missing in action. Standard story of unwanted children, doped up on ritalin by age ten, at sixteen a fiend. All those horrible days confined to the space between her front door and the gate at the end of her small yard under the hot Florida sun while she sat in the air conditioned darkness of our HUD home nurturing a weed habit with a bottle of gin, watching old black and white reruns of The Andy Griffith Show, Gomer Pyle, and The Twilight Zone. I wasn’t allowed to leave the yard, so I was unable to play with the other children. Not that things would’ve been much better had I been allowed to. Always smaller and almost a full year younger than many of the kids in my grade, I was constantly bullied. They’d push me down and steal the few toys I had, throwing them back and forth over my head. Around my outstretched arms, just inches beyond my reach until someone tripped me, sending me falling to the ground again only to kick dirt in my face. It only got worse when I got home, as grandma would then beat me for coming home dirty, and anything else she could think of in her drunken stupors. Of all the kids that picked on me there was one that really stood out. The school bully, Andre Jones, I lived in fear of him through my first four years of schooling. Apparently he was unaware of the fact that there’s one thing you can count on: if you push a man too far, sooner or later he’ll start pushing back. It was Friday recess and AJ threw rocks at me while all the other kids watched, and most days I would’ve let him. But the little warrior in me roared to like and knocked AJ to the ground, held his head against the dirt, and punched him so hard my knuckles swelled. He almost looked like he was wearing war paint as he lay there crying, but I was the warrior. And I chanted It’s a good day to die! It’s a good day to die! All the way down to the principal’s office. That day when I got home grandma beat the shit out of me in the backyard with the water hose. Come to think of it Andre is my garbage man now, I still flick him a bird when I see him. We were poor, so toys for me were few and far between. One day at lunch I had an idea. I pulled the fire alarm, little bastards runnin everywhere hollerin and screamin, all the while I was fillin? my pack with butter cookies which I later sold to the junior high fools for a quarter a piece. It was golden, until grandma found the fifteen dollars I’d made. When I couldn’t come up with a satisfying enough answer to where it came from, she beat me... then took my money and bought alcohol with it. In middle school I picked up a basketball for the first time and made my first shot. No, actually I missed. Missed the basket completely sending the ball into the sawdust and dirt where I had sat just moments earlier, but it felt good. The ball in my hands, all those possibilities and angles. It was mathematics, geometry, it was beautiful. The following year I learned to huff rubber cement out of a paper bag. It made life bareable, before that everything looked like a noose if I stared at it long enough. My ears rang, my mouth was dry, and everyone seemed so far away. But it felt good, that buzz in my head, all those colors and noises. It was chemistry, biology, it was beautiful. I made good grades all the way through thinking maybe she’d have a problem beating me if she was proud of me. I was wrong. If anything it just made her more creative. I used to have to stand arms spread, palms up balancing a school book in each hand while she whipped me. Lord help me if I ever dropped one. What made it worse was that even after she was done whipping me I’d have to continue balancing the books indefinitely. I stood alone in the corner waiting for the punishment to end, I’m still waiting. When it came time for High School the one near me was severely overcrowded so I got rezoned to go to the next closest one. It was in the suburbs and filled with over privileged children with loving parents and real nice lives. From the boys? bathroom I’d listen to the voices in the girls? bathroom. Nervous whispers of anorexia and bulemia. I could hear their forced vomit, a sound so familiar and natural to me after fourteen years of listening to my grandmother’s hangovers. I distinctly remember one day telling a girl that she should give me her lunches if she was just going to throw them up. Though it had no effect on the number of beatings I received, I made straight A's all the way through sophomore year and stayed out of any serious trouble. Although I was somewhat of a class clown. That was also the year my grandmother drank a gallon of vodka a day, started two hundred different quilts but never finished any, and beat me more savagely than ever before because I always looked guilty she said. I probably did, I was living in fear. I always looked as though I had just hit and run someone. That was also the year I ran away. One day before turning sixteen years old, during a particularly violent beating involving an extension cord, I pushed my grandmother away from me, ran out the front door, and didn’t stop until my lungs felt as though they were going to explode. I don’t know about angels, but it’s fear that gives men wings. I spent the next few months wandering aimlessly. Squatting in abandoned houses, stealing from convenience stores, hot wiring cars and driving them as far as the gas left in them would take me then sleeping in them, whatever it took to make sure I’d never have to go back home. The only thing worse than having nothing was having my grandmother. As I worked my further north I got wind of these illegal cage fights held in an old warehouse. I began attending, at first because it was someplace warm to stay for a few hours each night. Eventually I began fighting, it felt good. I was finally able to let it all out. After about a year of damn near killing myself every night for little more than enough to afford a shit hole room to stay in and barely anything to eat I got a break. I got an offer to do a year long tour of Japan, I jumped at the chance, the further away from her the better. Although I doubt she ever looked for me before she died. The matches were harder, but the money was better. I ate well, and for the first time in a while had a bed to sleep on. That was where I honed my skills, competing in grueling death matches night in and night out. Often performing with multiple injuries. I kept my head down and the time flew. One day a letter arrived for me from Global Wrestling. It was an offer for a contract, which brings me back to where I am now. Stone drunk, shivering in the shower wearing nothing but my title belt, drudging up old memories of the worst years of my life. Time is in no way elegant, it has a tendency to corrupt beauty, even if there was never any there to start with. Another glance at my belt reminds me of just how much things have changed for me lately. A few days ago I hadn’t proven myself. Now after doing so at Dead Zone I’m faced with a new challenge: doing it every show at the cost of my title. The ride is only gonna get bumpier from here on out. My first challenger will be Christopher Canada. He probably thinks he’s getting a huge break and considers himself lucky to be facing a GW champion in his first match. At Evolution I’ll show him that he is sadly mistaken and that by joining Global Wrestling he has done nothing more than signed a contract to get his ass kicked. I can feel the bile rushing into my throat while that bitter salty taste you get right before throwing up accumulates on the back of my tongue as I slip away into the arms of sleep. Ah, the wonders of alcohol abuse.
