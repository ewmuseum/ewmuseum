Homecoming

21 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Feb 21 After times of great loss, one finds himself with an empty feeling inside. A feeling of worthlessness and selflessness. One cannot live like this, and thus the world has changed for the individual. They must learn how to deal with the loss. There are many ways that one can deal with the loss. They often turn to drugs, the call of the needle or the bong are often a strong opposition to the tears. However, often that is a turn for the worse. Soon the drugs do not combat they emptiness inside, they create it. They begin to pry what little remains in a man’s life and rob him of everything. Then the man must go on, relying on pills and syringes to get him through the course of his normal day. A horrible way to live. Then there is those who turn to cults and the church. But, what is the true difference anyways. If God loved one so much, why would he steal the life of a loved one away? The church is not a salvation, it is a joke. Those who turn to the church are often the most desperate, those who have lost the most and have no sense of direction in life. The cults are a mere step lower then that of the church. Those are the crazy, those who have lost not only all sense of life, but all sense of reality itself. Those with nothing to live for and so they seek the cult to create their world for them. Thirdly are those who do nothing, those who travel the world wearing the horrible mask of denial. Completely unaware of the effects of the real world, they often lose sense of what is important. They are often depressed and alienated. They wander the world lonely and uncaring, there life to them is meaningless. They try to ignore the loss like it did not happen, but it does not make it better, it can only make it worse. Finally, there is acceptance. Those who can accept what has happened and bounce back. Those who find out what happened, and do something about it. They are often seen as bloodthirsty, the vengeful, the killers, the ruthless. But they are not the ones with a problem, they are the ones who solve their problems. Often, in the only way they know how. That’s how it had been for the Nakazuras. They needed not to poison themselves with drugs, nor throw their lives away. They took matters into their own hands, and now they had dealt with it. Ichiro remembered the moment like it had happened seconds earlier, he had relived it so many times in his mind since it had happened. He closed his eyes and pictured it. Ishuzu on her knees, trembling and defenseless. He had sensed a great burden that day, one that he felt was instantly lifted from his shoulders as his lead bullets pierced the skin of his enemy. As it pierced the skin and spilled the blood of his father’s murderer. But since then a same sense of burden had come back to the brothers. It seemed that the death of Ishuzu was only a temporary relief. It seemed to both Ichiro and Jushin, that the Lotus was telling them something. Almost warning them, that their fathers death had not yet been avenged. But how could that be? Why would an informant clearly lie right to Ichiro’s face. It was obvious in his tone and voice as he spoke about the Souls of the Black Lotus, it was obvious the informant did not know that one of the two Nakazura sons was sitting directly across from him. And if he did, why would he make such a statement, if he knew one thing about the Nakazura sons, it was they were not afraid to kill. But, what if the informant was not lying, what if he was telling the truth? How could it be, there was no way that there could be two truths to one matter. Ichiro could no longer sit in silence, he watched as his brother sat playing the Playstation 2 on the small color television set. He was playing a Streetfighter Game, one of his favorites, but that did not matter now. Ichiro broke the silence. “Did you feel a pressure on your shoulder’s when we were inside Ishuzu’s?” Jushin looked at him inquisitively. “Well sort of, but it just sort of disappeared once we were done.” “Almost as if as soon as our bullets left our guns?” asked Ichiro. “Yeah, yeah, almost as soon as I heard the crack of the gun shot, I felt instantly relieved,” answered Jushin. “Me too, but ever since we got back in the United States, I feel as if there is a strong weight back upon my shoulders,” furthered Ichiro. “It has always been there for me, almost as a constant reminder of things that need to be done,” replied Jushin. “But what do we need to do now, now that we’re back in the United States?” asked Ichiro. “It probably has something to do with GW, I sometimes wonder what it is though, like what makes it happen,” offered Jushin. “When we were still at the church, I had told our master about this,” Ichiro stated. “He said that it was a natural ability, to those strongly gifted by the Lotus. He said it was a reminder of things that must be done, and things that have gone amiss. Almost as if a form of spider sense, I don’t remember exactly what he said, but I do know that the Lotus is trying to tell us something.” Jushin looked confused, he turned off the Playstation 2 and looked at his brother as he rose to his feet. “What the hell is it trying to tell us!” I can’t stand these small mind games, sometimes I feel as if I can’t take it.” “Relax brother. Everything will be fine, I must meditate, hopefully my meditation will prove valuable and allow us some insight. I shall find out what has happened, and then we shall deal with it, no matter what it may be,” encouraged Ichiro. Jushin smiled at his older brother, there was something comforting about his words. It had always been there, something in his face. Something about the way his eyes gleamed and his smile faded, something that made his words sound so calm and soothing. Jushin smiled at his older brother, he was all he had left, and to be truthful was all he had ever had. Jushin had never gotten along extremely well with the others at the temple. He was ridiculed by the others for being special, tortured by their jealous fits he was saddened and often got in fights, while Ichiro had been the opposite and had gotten along with everyone. It seemed that since he was the older brother, he was meant to be stronger, even if since he was 12 he could beat most of the sensei at the temple. But, since he was younger he was some strange freak to be so strong. However, he had seen many a fight where Ichiro would fight his good friends, often several at once, just to stand up for Jushin. It was strange, but Jushin felt safe whenever his brother was around. “I am going to work out,” said Jushin. He reached for his small gym bag and headed out the door. Ichiro was not surprised often when confronted with a problem Jushin would ignore it. He figured in his own way, Jushin avoiding the problem with the gym was his way of contemplating. Ichiro nodded. “If you need me when you get home, you know where you can find me,” said Ichiro as his brother brushed by him. “The usual spot?” asked Jushin. “The usual spot.”
