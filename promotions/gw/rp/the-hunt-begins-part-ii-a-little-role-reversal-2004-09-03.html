The Hunt Begins: Part II, A Little Role-Reversal

3 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/3/2004 Books. Thousands upon thousands of moldering tomes, stacked to the ceiling, filling chamber after chamber in our annals, pages turning to dust beneath even the gentlest touch of my fingers. I had read them all, and still wasn’t any closer to finding answers. I was immortal now, yet I still had no inkling as to my true nature. And in every book, there were references to him? a phantom, a face without a name? a face with many names. Vampyre. Murderer. Evil incarnate. I sought to understand, now that I was on the same side of the fence, so to speak, just what made these creatures tick. A lust for blood, surely, but it was a necessary evil. As surely as mortals needed to feed to survive, so did they. Anne Rice had made it pop culture, and the world has dismissed the nightwalkers as nothing more than fiction? perhaps we were not so different. My society, versus theirs? we were both best kept in the shadows. Hunters and watchers? was there really such a difference? Their society killed by action, mine through inaction? same ends, different means. The blood of countless innocents stained my hands as surely as it stained their lips. Perhaps, I should seek out this ancient being, and question him? perhaps he can shed some light on my condition. But first, I shall return to the place I once called home, this body was beginning to smell of sweat and blood, the stink of humanity? and I was in desperate need of a change of clothes. I wasn’t used to this so-called “dirty work?” revenge was a messy business indeed. ---- Gabriel walked slowly up the walk, shoulders hunched against the rising wind, hands thrust in the pockets of his dark trench coat. The condominium rose impressively from the sculpted lawn like a marble sculpture? rising towards the heavens like the biblical tower of Babel. It was neither large, nor small, rather adequately sized for the big city, but the neighborhood was nice, and as such, the purchase price of one of these apartments would gag even a sword swallower. He never understood how Jackson had afforded these sumptuous accommodations. They had never really run in the same circles, and although they had both been part of the elite, they rarely saw each other outside the council meetings. He had a feeling that Jackson had money from somewhere, old money, and that perhaps it was best not to ask where it came from. A chill crept up his spine as his feet rang out in the sudden silence, slamming down atop the faux cobblestone walk with more authority than he felt. The place was small enough to not require a doorman, which, Gabriel supposed, was a good thing. The small foyer was deserted, but then he knew it would be. A small door laid to his right- the stairs? but he chose to take the small elevator instead. He waited before the beveled glass doors, trying to avoid looking at his distorted reflection in the glass, trying to pretend he was normal. At least his long hair covered the pointed tips of his ears, making him passably human, on a cursory inspection. If one looked harder, however, they would notice those strange eyes? the amber irises with the slightly elongated pupils, so much like a cat’s. He could have worn corrective lenses for that, but they irritated him, they made it so he couldn’t see clearly. Without his senses, he would be nothing. Moreover, he would be useless to them. The doors opened with a muted sound, and he slipped inside, already tugging at the fingers on his gloves. He stood there, before the buttons, his bare right hand outstretched, at first feeling nothing. Almost as though being jolted with an electric shock, he suddenly stiffened, his eyes growing wide as he saw the afterimage of Jackson’s clouded aura. Black with flecks of red, hovering around the button for the top floor. Thirteen. The button read fourteen, but Gabriel knew, as surely as he was standing here, there was no silver disc inscribed with the number thirteen. Superstition. And Jackson had always scoffed at silly superstitions. It should have been simple logic to deduce what floor he lived on. Gabriel steeled himself and touched the button as the doors whisked closed, expecting to feel the lingering impression of Jackson- the slippery, slimy feel of pure evil. Instead he felt nothing but a stirring of unease. The walls seemed to close in on him, making the elevator seem smaller and a faint, rather unpleasant odor lingered, perhaps from the last occupant. Gabriel began to sweat, as he stared at the numbers, willing them to light up quicker. As the fifth numeral flickered to life, he realized that he was gasping and wheezing as though he’d just run a marathon. The air seemed to grow thicker, choking him now? and he knew that these physical manifestations were simply masking his underlying fear. He wasn’t a spy? nor was he the clandestine, sleuth type. However, the duties had fallen to him when the rest of the council had died, or rather, been slain by the man he now hunted. He imagined any manner of possible catastrophes? from the collapse of the building itself, to the cables snapping, plunging him in the little metal prison to his death. Or perhaps, worse yet, Jackson would be waiting on the top floor, with his gun pointed at the closed metal doors, awaiting Gabriel’s arrival. He shuddered, and pressed himself against the wall, sheltered behind the buttons, his hand going to his belt, where the pistol was stuffed down in his waistband. He hesitated, and then, feeling like a fool, withdrew the revolver and held it in one trembling hand. The doors opened with a muted ping, revealing a narrow hall, carpeted in muted pastels. Deserted. He let out a breath he hadn’t known he was holding, and stepped out of the cab, his feet making no sound on the plush floor. So this was what the upper class lived like. Warm light spilled from the walls, oozing from behind decorative, fan shaped light fixtures. There were only two doors on this floor, the penthouse suites? one to the left and one to the right. He hesitated again, unsure as to his destination. He stopped beside a low shelf, built into the wall, and touched the leaves of the potted fern with his bare hand. An image slammed into his head with startling intensity? a premonition? a girl, her features blurry, screaming soundlessly as Jackson’s teeth sunk into her shoulder. He shuddered, and dropped to one knee, his hands going to his head. He hadn’t had a vision of this intensity in God knows when. A single drop of blood fell from his nose, spattering to the dusty rose carpeting, spreading like an exotic flower. He was obviously headed in the right direction? and the sliver of light coming from the door before him was further evidence to support that conclusion. “Goddamn fool,” he ground out between clenched teeth as he groped his way back to his feet. What was he doing here? What was he looking for? ---- “answers. That’s all I really wanted. All my life, I have felt like that’s what I was looking for. A never-ending quest. First it was the meaning of my own existence, and to a lesser extent, to make my father proud. That was a failure, it didn’t take a genius to see that the harder I tried, the less he respected me. In my desperation, I couldn’t tell that he wasn’t able to give me that which I desired most of all- love. They say love makes the world go around, and I had always scoffed at that. Some idiot once said, it was better to have loved and lost than to have never loved at all” I would gladly take the ignorant road, for loving her had led to my ruin. I would not be here, if it hadn’t been for her. And still, I wondered, now that I was above it all, if I would do it again. Knowing what I know now, would I still bare my soul to her? Would I still give her everything? Would I have thrown it all away for one night in her embrace? Perhaps I would. Every man has a weakness, an inherent character flaw. I thought myself above her power? I had been able to resist her species in the past. Sensual women were my weakness, and I had conquered them all? that was until her. She’d been like a poison, slowly stealing my will, with her insouciant smiles? with her dimples, and those jade green eyes. I longed for her embrace, even now, my Judas. She had betrayed me? she had corrupted me? she damned me. And still, goddamn it, I wanted her. I wanted to feel her beneath me? I wanted to feel the press of her lips. Perhaps. Just one last time, I would look for her. I couldn’t leave this place without seeing her. One. Last. Goodbye. For old time’s sake, of course. ---- Gabriel eased over the threshold, and stopped, listening, the door standing open at his back. The pistol was still clenched in one hand, in a white-knuckle death grip. The wind whistled against the windows, the central air-conditioning whispered through the vents, otherwise silence reigned. Tomb-like stillness. Maybe instinct was warning him, perhaps a hint of his sixth sense, or maybe it was simple paranoia, but he felt as though this silence was a mask. This was not an empty silence, rather it was a pregnant pause, filled with menace, something deadly, like a coiled cobra, waiting to strike. He let his eyes return to the door, and with something akin to reluctance, closed it firmly, locking the deadbolt. He didn’t want the prying eyes of the neighbors, nor did he want to facilitate any exits other than his own. He felt like a fool, as he pressed his back to the wall, holding the pistol up, pointing it at the ceiling, and as he inched towards the low archway, he almost laughed at the ridiculousness of his posture. Acting this way, made him feel like an extra from Law & Order, full of police drama, or perhaps Agent Mulder from the X-Files, since he was apparently afraid of a dead man. Perhaps the slaughter at the council meeting had been a clever ruse. Maybe Jackson was really dead, maybe what Dante had seen had been a mistake. And maybe if he kept trying to fool himself, he would actually start to believe it. The dead didn’t walk? nor did they carry semi-automatic weapons. Still, he was loath to waltz from room to room without some measure of caution? dead man or not, he was afraid. There was the truth. Evaded before, now acknowledged. He felt better having faced that fact, but he still felt the stirrings of the same dread that had plagued him in the elevator. The claustrophobia and the panic had been nothing more than attempts to deflect himself from the true terror. The fact that Jackson was not just an animated corpse? he was something much more frightening, and considerably more powerful. He wondered what Jackson would do if he found Gabriel here, walking among his possessions, an interloper in his private sanctuary. He passed a room that had a light burning within, illuminating the circle of candles on the floor, marking out the points of the pentacle drawn on the hardwood in chalk. He shook his head, and passed by the place of power, feeling the electric tingle of the energy there. He hadn’t known Jackson had practiced magic, but then, only Dante knew him well. Jackson had been a private man, cold and remote, always aloof and sarcastic. His demeanor kept most at bay? to all but the elite- the council? he was intimidating. In the living room now, silence poured out, as though a cauldron of emptiness had been upended, sucking away all the sounds. Books littered the floor, littered every stick of furniture. Ancient volumes, some of the covers swollen and split with age, some not even in English. Cyrillic, Arabic, even German and Dutch? even to the end, Jackson had still been a scholar. Gabriel recalled, there hadn’t been a time in a meeting, when someone had broached an obscure subject that Jackson hadn’t spoken up, his eyes lighting up with that peculiar gleam as he regaled them with his knowledge. He’d known at least five languages, perhaps more, judging by the books scattered in this very room. In the center of the mess lay a bejeweled sheath, filthy and tarnished with age. A strange glow seemed to emanate from it, and Gabriel found himself drawn to it. It was empty, but he recognized it just the same. Klinge der Schw’rzung. The Sword of Darkness. The Soul Reaver. It had many names, and Gabriel had always believed it to be a myth, until he had gleaned the truth from Dante’s shattered mind. Jackson had the Reaver. “Fuck,” he muttered to himself, his expression one of sheer vexation. That didn’t bode well? if he had used it, he would be virtually unstoppable. He backed away from the low table, his features pale and drawn, making his eyes seem more otherworldly than usual. Before he could lose his nerve, he continued into the next of the adjoining rooms?
