A talk that teaches?

24 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-24-05 February 20th, 2005 Time: 1:15 AM Finally. Yes finally I can say that I am living my dream. A dream that took me to heart several years ago when my uncle, Aaron Johnson, told me about his dreams. His dream was also to be a professional wrestler. Now as I enter my federation, I look to do more then he did. I look to spread my wings and to spread my eyes as I try and do what he did. And that was win a World Championship. But as I sit here looking out at the night stars that have come I can’t help but ask myself. “Why am I really doing this?”. Am I doing this for me? Am I doing this for my dreams? Or am I doing this for the family that I have always loved to be like? So many different reasons that I could say but the only one that really seems right is that I am just doing this. The stars looked bright tonight. Lighting up the ground and the oak wood porch that I was standing on. Taking a long drink from the glass of milk in my right hand, my thoughts and dreams took a hold as a loud sound came from the barn that my family had on their porch. *CRASHHHHH* “What was that?” It almost sounded like something fell. I sat that glass of milk down and began to run all the way to the closed doors on the barn. What could it be? No one was up at this time. Hell the only reason I was, was because I couldn’t sleep. Whether it be excitement or whatever, I just couldn’t seem to rest my head for some reason. I opened the door of the barn. To revel the face of a man that I had known all my life? “Uncle Johnson!” I said before running to his aid. He looked bad. Bleeding from the left arm as he hit the table. I grabbed him by the shoulder and helped him lean up against the hay that we had laying around the barn. “Elia,” Aaron said. “Why aren’t you asleep?” “I could ask you the same question but I think I already got the answer.” Oh how I knew the answer and I didn’t like how he was. I took off my outer button up shirt that I had on and began to rub the blood that he had running down his arm. God how I hated him when he drank. He was always so violent to everyone, even to himself. Though he hadn’t drank in several months. Why was he drinking now? Why was he drinking so late in the night? Getting most of the blood off, I tossed the shirt aside before sitting back against the hay myself. “What is wrong my little precious?” Aaron said, rubbing his eyes to get focused on what was around him. “Nothing,” Nothing? Did I just say that? Oh how I thought it was nothing but something had been bothering me since joining Global Wrestling. I just couldn’t figure it out. The horses in the barn began to get a little rattled. Letting out different noises as the two of us sat there in the middle of the night. You see, ever since my dad passed away, I lived with my uncle. Growing up he was my dad to me when my real dad was away. I learned a lot from him and still do. “Come on, lets get you up.” Grabbing his right arm and pulling with all my might . He Didn’t budge but rather just laid there. “Come on Aaron.” Laughing out loud, Aaron looked up at me with his golden blue eyes and wiped his hand through his short brown hair. “You can’t pick me up little lady. Didn’t I teach you that?” Aaron said, falling down face first right into a pile of hay. Did he just say what I think he said? I can’t do something? That is one thing that I never got taught by him. I got my strength from my mother and grandmother. Leaning down and taking hold of his two arms, I pulled with all my might, getting him from the ground to a standing position. He was heavy. About two hundred and fifty pounds heavy. “God you weight a ton. What did you eat?” Crying towards him as I struggled to toss him into my open arms. Turning to look at the open door, I began to drag him towards it. Listening to the soft grunts of the old father I was so proud that he was. You see, my family told me that. I would never be a wrestler. I was to small. I was to weak. Well my father never was alive to see how far I got in the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament Four. The fourth round. I got to the door, seeing an old wheel barrel right next to the door. “Ok, get in this you old bag.” Falling forward with my words as I tossed him right in the wheel barrel. “Old bag” If I wasn’t so drunk I’d show you how good I still am.” Laughing with what motor skills he still had, Aaron just laid there and looked up at the big bright stars. I was never worried about Uncle Aaron when he was teaching me. I knew that if I made a mistake that he was tell me. But now that I’m no longer with him, I am worried. What if I make a mistake against this Itigaki Freeze? I froze for a second and stopped pushing him towards the house. “Elia?” “Elia?” I couldn’t get the thought out for some reason. I just stood there for a moment before answering. “Yes?” “Are you ok?” Aaron said, with a worried look on his face as he did. “Have you ever been scared to enter what you were trained to enter?” My lips were shaking and the cold was making my legs freeze seeing as how I was in my favorite white jeans. I looked down at him, seeing him wipe his eyes to keep the focus on me. “Baby, I’ve never been scared. I’ve always knew?” Pausing for a moment as he wiped his eyes once again. “I’ve always trusted my inter self and you should to?” Aaron looked over at the ground, leaning over and the most awful sound came out. Oh that was so grouse as he began to puke up all the alcohol that he drank. I turned my head away. Trying to get the image of it from my head. And what seemed like seconds turned into a minute before I turned back around. “I have no?” Looking right at Aaron who was sound asleep. I picked the wheel barrel up again, struggling to get it up the ramp to the door as I began to thing. Inter self? Maybe he had a point. But maybe it was to late to get inside that part. Maybe I was going to lose. Or maybe I was just going to follow like my father said, a loser. Either way, the ride is here and I’m going to ride it.
