'On Air'

27 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/27/05 It felt like home. The sounds of hip hop filled the air of the studio as the “Hot 97' sign glowed on the wall of the studio. Even turned down the speakers that hung from the ceiling by brushed steel brackets vibrated a bit, the bass thumping as I-20 let’s the world know it’s time for him to get his cut now that he’s made his return to the streets, “Break Bread” flowing out over the airwaves and into the medium sized room. Train let his eyes drift across the black and gray paneled walls as he sat casually behind the large, dark, maple wood table. The walls are liberally sprinkled with the promotional posters and signed pictures of the steady flow of hip hop and R&B artists that have filtered through here, the station more than any, considered the launching pad of superstars. He took a deep breathe and sat himself up in the seat as he adjusted his head phones. Train couldn’t help, but glance at the large, hung in the air before him, attatched to the flexible metal arm that held it aloft. He couldn’t help, but feel a bit out of his element here. The studio was far more confining than the arenas he was used to. However, he looked and the mic and remembered this was nothing new. He’d been on many a mic many a time letting folks know what was what. And besides... for once, the only ones that were listening this time where his folks... the hood would be listening. Hell that was the whole point. Train spun as chair as he heard the heavy footsteps behind him. The studio was empty when he’d been lead in and set up at the desk ago by an excitable young intern who obviously recognized him just a few moments ago. Now instead of his normal position of being the admired he’d grown accustomed to Train found himself in the place of the admirer. Funkmaster Flex had his name and face plastered all over the game and the media. A cross over sensation. Train didn’t much pay attention to that. He knew him cuz he’d been listening to his mixes since he was in high school, by mix tapes cats were selling out of their trunks. Now Funk Flex stepped into the room like he owned the joint and he pretty much did. Train stood to his feet, now suddenly a fan more than anything else and the two men slapped hands to greet eachother. “Choo Choooooo!” bellows as their hands slap together, the sound of the impact a good impersonation of a clap of thunder. “What up man” It’s good to meet you.” “Yeah man,” Train responds with an uncharacteristic grin. “It’s good to be here dogg. A brotha’s just kinda buggin” a little is all man. Up here chillin? with Funk Flex man. Been listening to your stuff since back in the day, you know what I’m sayin?”” “Hey, it’s all good man, cuz I been a wrestling fan since back in the day,” Funk Flex replies with an uncharacteristic grin of his own. He motions for Train to have a seat again as he settles himself back down in front of the console board, computer screen and the other mic. The dj slides his headphones back on as he sports his own grin. “So we just gonna get into this. Gonna ask you a few questions about how you got into the wrestling game and what you up to now. That cool?” “I’m ready for whatever.” Train says, lounging back into his seat, setting his arm up across the back of the chair. “Bet.” Flex says simply. His finger effortlessly flipped the switch on the console board and the “On Air” sign hanging over he desk began to glow bright red. Go time. “Hey yo,” Flex growled emphatically, his fingers finding the volume sliders on the console, pulling down without ever having to look to find them. “Yall know, the deal! Break me off my cut! Yeah that was I-20 with “Break Bread” and I had to play that in honor of my guest here tonight. Yall know I’m a wrestling fan so tonight I’m having a good ol? time cuz in the studio with me I got the realest cat in the wrestling game today as far as I’m concerned. We got Global Wrestling’s bad boy... That THUG... Train in the building! What up Train?!” “Hey,” Train began, the adrenaline pumping as hard as for any match he’d ever had. “You know what Flex” Man, I’m up in Hot 97, the biggest hip hop station in the world... hangin? with Funkmaster Flex... Feeling like I’m surrounded by the streets up in here... Man all I can say is it’s all hood! You know what I’m sayin?” It’s all hood.” “And you know this... Man!” “Damn right.” “Aight...” Flex uttered somewhat quietly, changing gears letting everyone including Train know it was time to get started. “First off, on the real, welcome to the show. It’s good to see you.” “It’s good to be seen.” Train responded, a small glint of enthusiasm in his voice. ? Right, right... Now first of all, everybody knows I’m a wrestling fan. Have been since I was little. But I got to say, except for may 2 or 3 cats that I can think of in the wrestling game that even remotely seemed like they was representin? the streets. You know... Junk Yard Dog might be in there.... Ahmed Johnson was in there you know... Even a couple of dudes you had or have beef with in GW like Brick and Deacon Holmes... But on the real I don’t really think there has been any cats in the last 9, 10 years that have been more real in reppin? that grimey... down and dirty street thing....than you man. I been saying that for awhile you know? As far as wrestling goes don’t nobody remind me more than the brothas on the street than Train.” “Hey, I appreciate that dogg. I mean the bottom line of it is I’m just being me. I ain’t putting on no show, ain’t trying to play no games. I’m just doing what I been doing for most of my life. I’m going out to get mine. Anybody who’s standing in the way of that” I’m droppin? em. Period.” “No doubt, no doubt... Now I don’t know if too many people really know too much about personally outside the ring. First of all, you’re a midwest cat, right?” “Oh yeah. Chicago man, Chi-town... Southside...” Train says as he pushes his sleeves back revealing the Gothically styled “S” he has on each forearm, revealing exactly what they stand for to Flex if no one else. “And proud of it. Whoever don’t like it cuz it aint” New York or L.A.... Hey, all I got to say to you is we ain’t “The Big Apple” or “The City of Angels”... but we are the Windy City so watch yo? mouth. Cuz you start running off at the lip in the Windy City you liable to get yo? punk ass blown away. Please believe it.” “Word...and that’s real people. See that’s the streets talking right there. And for all those that think it’s fake... I mean especially all them that wanna say, “Oh, he’s a wrestler. He trying to talk some game for his character so he can sell some t-shirts...” I’ma just make sure of something here. Now Train, I know some cats that know some cats that know some cats that say they used to know a cat in Chicago by the name of Train. They was saying this dude was on point, you what I’m saying? Like this cat didn’t just have his block, but like the entire south side of the city on lock. Now... are you that Train?” “Whoa, whoa, whoa...” Train responds with a chuckle, holding up his hands to slow down the segment. “Flex... dogg... I’m not sure what you mean dogg. I mean they way you say locked down makes me believe that the Train you talking about might have been involved with some kinds of illegal activities or something. A brotha like me wouldn’t know nothing about that type of thing. You know what I’m saying?” “Hahaha... Yeah, I think I know what you sayin” man. You saying the cops might be listening.” “And there it is.” Train responds with another laugh. 'There’s some stuff you don’t be talkin? about in public cuz there’s some stuff that ain’t got no statute of limitations on it.” “Word, I feel you on that.” “I’ma just say I ain’t no stranger to the block and leave it alone.” “Gotcha man,” Flex says with a grin and air of levity. “Now let me ask you some stuff folks probably don’t know. Like first off... in the ring people know you for having nice hands, but really people know you for just throwing cats around in there. And I mean these are some big dudes too. Like you was fighting this dude the other night...what’s his name” Oh, ahhh... Hessian! Yeah, Hessian... Ok for those of yall out there that don’t know... Train... you like, what? 5'9'... 5'10'?” “Yeah, something like that.” “And how much you weigh?” “Ahhhh... depends... I guess I come in about... any where between 249 to about 255... some place in there, you know.” “Ok... So imagine some dude like 7 feet tall... way over 300 pounds... Now imagine that cat” that’s 5'10' and 250 picking up ol? boy that’s 7 foot, 320 or something and droppin? him on his damn head. That's like me body slamming Shaq right there!” “Hahaha.... Hey, gotta do what you gotta do, know what I'm saying” He would've done the same stuff to me. hahaha...” “Yeah man, that stuff was crazy man. I mean this dude is just huge and you picked him up and dropped him a few times like he wasn’t there. I mean I know scrappin” on the block teaches you to handle yourself, but I mean damn.. That stuff is just crazy dogg. Where’d you learn to do all that?” “Naw, that took more than time on the block to do that man. I mean I learned to fight on the block, but I learned to wrestle from the best... a cat by the name of Isaiah Walker.” “Ok, now who is that” Is he a wrestler cuz I don’t think I’ve ever heard of him....” “Yeah, yeah man... Yeah, he was a wrestler, but I ain’t surprised you never heard of him really. Isaiah came up in the wrestling game way back in the day, you know what I’m sayin?” A black man in the game that wrestled and boxed coming up through high school. You know he made a run at some of these promotions in the states, but it was like the 50's and 60's man... It was a different world back then, you know what I’m saying?” “Oh yeah man... Brotha still had to come into the arenas through the back door.” “Exactly man, that’s exactly it. Not only that, but back then you didn’t have no agent or nothing to deal with the promoters for you and all the promoters were white. You know what I’m sayin” they wasn’t try to give no ni.... excuse me, brotha....brotha....hahaha...” “Hahahaha... right, right, I feel you...thank you for checkin” yourself there. A brotha like myself hates hearing from the FCC.” “Right, right... I got yo” back man, I got you... But ahhh... Fact is Isaiah was a brotha that knew what he was worth, you know what I’m sayin?” So when the crooked ass promoters was offering him like a third of what they was offering the rest of the roster...and he knew he was better than two thirds of them cats he said the hell with. He saved up and took his butt to Europe and started cleaning up, built a big career over there. He was just mauling cats to the point they nick named him “The Black Bear”, you feel me? And ahhhh... he won a lot of titles and tournaments... even won some world titles over there.” “Word, that’s hot. So the cat that taught you was really blazing a trail.” “Most definitely...” “So Isaiah came up in the game way over in Europe. How is it he got hold of you and taught you the ropes?” “Well... it’s kinda funny... you asked me whether or not I did dirt back up in Chicago... Well Isaiah was from Chicago. Grew up like 3 blocks from where I did. And when he finally retired he decided he needed to give back to the neighborhood. Turned out he knew some cats on the city council and whatever and so he suggested to them that he do a little program for ahhh... what should I call it” Wayward youth.... In other words bad ass kids.” “Hahaha... Right, right....” “Well...haha... at the time I was on lock down in juvenile hall and...” “Oh, so in other words you was a bad ass kid!” “Hey, hey... I was not bad... persay... Aight, yeah I was bad. hahaha...” “Uhuh... you can’t hide nothing! Hahaha...” “I know, I know... but on the real I was locked down as a kid and he came up with a program where he’d take these kids and train them how to be wrestlers, but there was conditions to it. First off you had to get back in school, go for your GED or whatever... and you wasn’t allowed to use what he taught you to be fighting outside the ring. You messed up, you got dropped.. Now at the time that meant I got to get out of that hell hole I was in for a little bit every day so I was all about out. Plus, you know I thought I was real slick, right? Real cool, tough cat. That lasted for about 5 minutes until I got in the ring with some dudes who knew what they was doing. I thought I was real smooth until the started through my little ass up and down the ring, you know what I’m saying? I was mad, but Isaiah... as much as I wanted to quit he wouldn’t let me. I don’t know what he saw in me, but whatever it was I’m glad he stayed after me to keep working. He taught me a lot and I am where I am today mostly cuz of him.” “That’s what’s up. That’s a cool story...that yo” man would come back and give to the community like that. So is Isaiah still trying kids like he did you?” The world stops for a moment. Isaiah. He wasn’t a subject train normally covered when he talked to interviewers trying to get a glimpse into the anatomy of a champion. Some would think it was a lack of appreciation of what the man had done for him, but those people didn’t have a clue. There was nothing but respect. The problem was every time he had to talk about his life he had to talk about his... Damn it. Train froze for a moment, trying to think how to broach a subject that ached like an open, infected wound on his soul. This was now and likely would always be the last part of the story he wanted to tell. But he’d already opened his mouth... Question was Flex going to be one of the insistent ones. One of those who just had to know what happened. Was he going to have to tell the standard lie to avoid telling the truth... to avoid telling the world Isaiah had died in his arms, his hands stained with the blood of the man who’d been like a second father... his life leaking from a hole left by a bullet that, despite having Train’s name on it, had been delivered to the wrong address. “Ahhhhhh....” Train started and stumbled, still fighting his way through a natural inclination to stay silent. “Naw, naw... Isaiah ain’t training no more. He... passed a few years back. But ahhh... I still own the gym where he trained me and one of these days I’m gonna get it up and running again. Let it do for other kids what id did for me, you know?” Was that going to be it? “Well I’m sorry to hear about yo” man, but the fact that you gonna keep that legacy going on, helping cats persue their dreams, so that’s what’s up right there. Now let me ask you something else...” Train’s soul wiped away an invisible bead of perspiration. “We talked about hip hop and the streets earlier,” Flex continued, never realizing how easily he’d let Train off the hook. “And we talked about how they didn’t really seem to be all that represented in wrestling...which don’t really make all that much sense when you think about it being a culture where, one way or the other, you always fighting to come up and survive.” “True.” Train responds, happy to move back to brighter territory. “So the question is... are you the realest cat doing it” Is Train the realest dude in the wrestling game?” “Dogg, I might be the realest cat in the game, period. Don’t matter what game you talkin” bout.” “Word...now you talkin” big now man. I mean how did the streets fit in when you was trying to persue a career in the ring?” “Hey.... I persued a career in the ring, but the streets pursued me, you know what I’m saying” It’s like Pacino said it... Just when I thought I was out...” “The pulled you back in.” “That’s it man... look, on the real, in the last year I’m just coming back into wrestling cuz I decided it was time to live up to the promise I made to Isaiah before he passed and that was I was gonna change my life. I told him I would and just like the streets taught me to be I’m a man of my word. But see, when I came back to the ring I promised myself I wasn’t gonna come in kissin” no ass and trying to make everybody like me. I promised myself that I wasn’t gonna be nobody other than who I’ve always been and that’s Train. You feel me?” “I feel you on that.” “The bottom line is I’m Train and I don’t care where I am, I keep it gangsta”... Ya? heard me? I see something I want I cop it. Somebody else go at it, I take it. They wanna fight about it I hurt “em. Only thing that changes is circumstances, the game remains the same.” “And that’s real right there. That’s definitely gangsta”! Hey yo, so tell us about what’s going down in GW right now man. Let us know when we can see you get down.” “Yeah man, we doing big things right now. We got out pay-per-view High Octane coming up on March 6th, check your local listings. And every Monday night on HBO we got Evolution coming to you live. And you got to check it out this week cuz we got a mystery main event and knowing Bubba McCoy’s crazy ass that thing’s gonna be bananas so you got to tune in and see what’s gonna go down there... Even the cats that are gonna be fighting, which I’m pretty sure I’m gonna be one of “em, don’t know what’s going down so you need to check that out.” “And that’s what’s up man. Hey, yall heard the man! High Octane, March 6th! Evolution tomorrow night, you know it’s gonna be off the chain! Train, thank you for coming out and hanging with us.” “It ain’t no thing man.” “Like I said you been keeping it real so all I got to say is keep on keeping on.” “Damn right.” The two men raise up out of their chairs, their hands slamming into one another with an audible clap as they clutch. “Aight people, you know how it is... we got to pay some bills, but don’t touch that dial! Train... do yo” thing man.” “That’s what gangstas do.” “And you know this... Man!!! Hey yo, we’ll be back... Holla!” “On Air” sign fades to black.
