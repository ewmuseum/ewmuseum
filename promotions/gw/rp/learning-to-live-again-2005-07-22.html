Learning To Live Again

22 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/22/05 When posed with the question, “what would you do if you had a second chance to relive your life?” How exactly would you take to it? To know that something so trivial as being able to do things like breathe air or have a fancy meal to watching the sunrise with a beautiful woman would be at your finger tips. What would you be willing to do for those little things that are simply lost on most people? Would it be worth a price? I have to ask myself this constantly as I have been undead for well over a thousand years. I’ve seen and done things many mortals could only dream but those things were limited to where I could not be able to sustain myself with anything other than the life essence of what I once was. I was confined to darkness where I watched as life evolved and became advanced more than anyone could ever imagine. I did the best I could through the years with what I had but there was always that want for something more. It wasn’t until I was given a second chance myself that I realized how hard things could be. A bird whose wings have been clipped for so long and then given a new pair to fly with is a feeling that is uneasy to process mentally. . . . . Corvus could not remember much about the previous night’s events or the fact that Jake Hix had brought him back to his loft apartment and helped him out of his clothes and helped him into bed before he left to continue taking care of business concerning his brother Sid. The room was completely dark and the air was staler than usual when Corvus sat up suddenly from the middle of his slumber to gasp for breath. His hands clutched his chest quickly choking for air, wondering what was happening to him. Every part of his body felt like it was aflame and nothing would be able to extinguish it. As he rolled out of bed he hit the floor with a hard thump. No one was there to help him; his brother found a friends place to stay and Rhianna went back to her club. Corvus himself was now living in a building he renovated for his brand new record label/recording studio. Slowly he crawled towards the bathroom struggling with every inch he covered before laying there motionless for another three minutes or more. The feelings in his body were alien to him and his stomach felt like it was going to explode. He brought his arm up and clutched the sink with a weakened grip before bringing up his other arm to slowly pull himself to his knees at first. He found it odd that the feeling he normally had when he woke up was no longer there ... the thirst for blood that sustained him throughout his unnatural existence was abesent. It had been replaced with an empty feeling, his insides were hollow and yet he craved something but it was not blood. There was something odd going on and he knew it. Was it a dream? It couldn’t have been as he tried with all his might to wake himself up but the realization that he already was began to dawn on him. He was now on his feet and slowly raised his head and looked towards the mirror that was on the medicine cabinet. “What the ... fuck!” He staggered backwards and fell through the bathroom door onto the bedroom floor as he couldn’t accept what he saw just then. It was hard for him to deal with seeing his reflection in the mirror after so long. He could waste energy to do it but it wasn’t really something he worried about. “What did that damn dart do to me” Am I dead ... am I in hell?” He looked around to see if anything was amiss but the entire room was unchanged. “I couldn’t be in hell, this is too convenient; my lungs are pumping air into my body ... this is way too sci-fi for me.” Corvus began to roam his hands over his body and felt that he was not ice cold in temperature. His right hand moved along his chest over where his heart is and could feel it beating once more. “This is fucking impossible!” He exclaimed as he picked himself back up and went back into the bathroom to give himself another once over. His hair pitch black was no longer. Instead it was more of a darker blonde color, the same as before he was embraced into a creature of the night; his eyes had gone from their grayish hue back to the more soft blue that he was familiar with in his mortality. “This is unreal ...” He continued to muse over the fact that he’d somehow metaphysically reverted back to his once mortal form. A jumble of thoughts flitted into his mind about what to do next. Should he tell anybody or just let it go because it could do more harm then good? His instincts were to let it ride for the time being and try to adjust to his new found mortality and have a little fun. He walked back into the bedroom and sat on the bed and placed his hands on his knees. The clock radio illuminated the time was somewhere close to 8 p.m. More of the urges that “I could use a drink ... I need to get out of here.” He moved along to shower and put on some clothes which he took a while going through his closet to see what might look better on him. He chose to go with pants from Jhane Barnes: a pair of white Kuro jeans. He complimented it with a plain white tank top and unbuttoned white sleeveless shirt. It was one of the few times he wore all white since he was accustom to wearing black as that was his mood practically all the time. Corvus checked himself out in the mirror one last time before heading out the door to go to wherever struck his fancy. There was not an actual plan in mind for where he would go to get his drink but he would let his instincts guide him on this particular mission. . . . . . . His motorcycle had recently been washed and detailed to the nines with a full gas tank. He had paid a trusted neighbor of his to wash it for him; it sparkled so beautifully against the lights of the strip as he cruised down the street. One of his favorite places to go was the Hard Rock Caf?. He was a musician after all ... well he was at one time before he decided to switch his career to the wrestling scene. Some of the memorabilia there had been items he had seen up close when they were with their original owners that made them famous. He enjoyed the simple atmosphere that it produced. Loud music played, beautiful females walked around ... it was a virtual devil’s playground. A waitress came by his table and after he had browsed the menu slightly, he thought to take a chance and ordered a New York Strip and a glass of Merlot. Every last part of his meal he savored without trying to make a spectacle of himself while he ate as people might have found it odd to see him appear to orgasm over his food. The steak was cooked to perfection (medium rare), the merlot was exquisite and it tasted much better than that of vitae he was forced to drink every night for the past century. Delicately he patted his mouth with a cloth napkin and set it down next to the empty plate. Even if he had not been able to live on human food he still remembered basic etiquette. He ordered a few more drinks and they started to get to him but he was unaware that it was the alcohol starting to make him funny. A familiar face spotted him and thought it polite to come over and speak to him. “Hello, Corvus ... it’s been a while.” He looked over and saw Detective Van Buren standing there but she was dressed a little better than he had seen her before when he had been taken downtown when he was suspected of being a murderer. “Good evening, Detective. I would never in a million years have expected to see you here; especially when I was not on the most wanted list.” The sarcasm in his voice was prevalent but unnecessary. “You’re look gorgeous, what do I owe the pleasure of your company?” He smirked as he pulled out his cell phone while speaking. “I’m appreciative. Maybe you wouldn’t mind if I took a picture with my phone to preserve the memory?” The detective bit her lip wanting to say something just as sarcastic back to him but she couldn’t find it in her to do it. “If it would help you sleep at night, sure. I’m off duty right now so you can call me Katherine.” Corvus smiled and motioned for her to have a seat if she so desired. “I’m off duty too so call me Eryc.” She chuckled and sat down across from him. “The city has been somewhat quiet lately, have you been out of town?” “Yes, I’ve been very busy with work and traveled out of the country for a little bit because of an event.” He said with a smile as he leaned back a little in his seat. “Is something different about you” I noticed your hair was a little different. I like it. But aren’t your eyes gray?” For a moment he forgot that his eyes had changed color when he became mortal again. He hesitated and quickly came up with an answer. “I wear color contacts for appearances; I just forgot to put them in tonight.” He noticed it was time for a refill so he waited for the waitress to come back around. “Since I did say that I was going to buy you a drink when you were off duty, what would you like?” Of course, that was the best way to change the subject by offering her something to drink. She looks through the menu a bit and decides to have an Appletini. When the waitress comes he orders another beer and her drink and tells her to add it to the bill. They continue to talk and have a few more drinks and it is evident that Corvus is getting blasted off of his ass. Unfortunately for him one of the owners of the establishment comes over to talk to him. He excuses himself to Katherine and steps aside to talk with the owner. After a few moments he sits back down and she raises a brow wondering if they were going to throw him out for having too much to drink. “Is everything okay?” She enquired. “Of course, the owner just wanted to talk to me about possibly playing at the club some night.” Katherine took another sip of her drink casually and played it cool. “I didn’t know you were a musician.” She acted as aloof as she possibly could. Katherine had been a fan of his music but did not want to elaborate on that given their past relationship. “If you would excuse me again for a minute, I will be right back.” Corvus stood up and swaggered a little bit and finally found his way to the bathroom. Yet another interesting re-introduction to mortality and one he wished he could do without. He conducted his business and came back nearly falling into the table. “I think maybe you should get back home ... if you don’t mind, I’ll take your keys and drop them by tomorrow.” She had never seen the man like this before. Normally he was so in control of himself and never seemed to give into anything. “Maybe just one more drink.” He said as he raised his arm to get the waitresses attention. When the waitress comes the beautiful detective speaks up and tells her that she just needs the check brought to the table and the service was wonderful. Corvus did not agree with her but some rational thought popped into his head. “Perhaps we could go for a cup of coffee and then you can take me home, what do you say?” He gave her the most charming look she had ever seen and smiled. “Sure, I know just the place.” He placed several large bills on the table that covered well over the amount and then some for a tip and the two left together after she managed to get his motorcycle keys from him. . . . . The Las Vegas strip was only a small part of Vegas, and for the locals there were plenty of scenes off the tourist traps. Down a set of stairs some maniac was crazy enough to build a basement in the desert. The stairs descended to a set of double doors like something out the high school gymnasiums. She opened the door for him, owing to his current state on inebriation. The coffee house had the hipster charm of the rebel forties and fifties before the sixties flower power had invaded. The low lights, throw back decorations and attitude all fit. The hipsters flocked here, those too deep and introspective for a simple Starbucks or Coffee Bean to house. Cold air was the sign of affluence and accomplishment in Las Vegas, where the energy bills to chase away the heat was always outrageous. They walked past a small group of young men resembling beatniks from back in the day. The leader had a small, black soul patch and short hair. He was puffing on a sweet clove cigarette. He eyed Corvus as he walked by him. Corvus looked at the man, 'What is that?' 'What?' 'What are you smoking?' The man laughed, looking around at his band of friends, 'That's a clove daddy-o.' Corvus hadn't heard that term in a long time. The guy had the lingo down, 'Think you can spare me one?' The man pulled out his pack and popped one out for Corvus. He took it and brought it to his mouth. Corvus picked up one of the candle jars from the table and lit it up. The sickly sweet smoke filled his lungs, and then he started coughing. Corvus pulled the clove cigarette out and looked down at it as the men laughed at him. 'This really stinks ... I can't even smoke like this.' He pushed the lit clove down into the ashtray, and then turned to Katherine. 'Let's grab a table.' Sitting at the table, they were facing the stage where the focus was squarely in the middle where a bright spotlight shone down on an empty stool. The last person had just finished, and soon another endeavoring giant of coolness would try and wow the crowd with rekindled rambling reflections trying to touch their souls. They had a poetry slam going on at the moment. The most recent person that was on stage just finished up. They had recited a composition about flowers or something but Corvus really didn’t pay much attention to it. “You’re pretty creative aren’t you Eryc?” Katherine said to him with a bit of enthusiasm and caught him off guard. “You’re not trying to get me up on that stage are you?” She smiled while continuing to encourage him. “Sure, why not” I’m sure that whatever you have would be better than the last guy.” “Anything would be better than the last guy.” Corvus added with a chuckle. “I suppose I could go up there, what harm would it do?” He watched as she placed her hand on top of his but he slowly pulls it away. “What the heck, here goes nothing.” Corvus stood up and finished the cup of coffee he had in front of him and offered himself up to public scrutiny by getting onto the stage and taking the microphone; sitting on the stool before closing his eyes so that he couldn’t see the people watching him; it was bad enough he could feel their eyes. Wrestling and music was one thing but something as intimate as this was another. He was very private with his literary works more than he was with everything else. He took a very deep breath before beginning. It's at arms length Temptation that cuts deep Burning down inside They make it that way for a reason If you didn't want If you didn't need They wouldn't find a way... to make you bleed From the mouth or the heart it is always damned from the start. It felt like I was falling the one I loved was hidden From the distance I heard her calling My arms couldn't reach; she was forbidden I need to grow I need to heal I have to find out how to be real and not something I dreamed up in my sleep. Corvus sets the microphone down in silence and opens his eyes to the sound of people clapping. He stood up and walked off the stage to quite an ovation by the small crowd that was congregating that night; even the beatniks that had been laughing about his ineptitude to smoke were among the adoring crowd. He returned to the table while the detective gazed at him with awe. “That was very powerful.” He shrugged his shoulders not feeling comfortable any longer after going on stage. “I think you should take me home if you don’t mind. I’m a bit drained.” “Sure.” Corvus left money with a tip for the coffee and the two went to her car. “I really enjoyed your work, and your company.” She smiled as she unlocked the door on his side first and walked to her side. “I had a good time as well.” He said it with enough convincing that she didn’t second guess his words. There was little conversation during the ride back to his place. He just looked out the window and contemplated things and she kept her eyes on the road. When they arrived she hesitated but got out of the car with him and walked with him to the door. “I hope we can do this again sometime?” She offered suggestively. “If I can keep you off my doorstep professionally ... I wouldn’t mind.” He chuckled. She moved closer to him but he was still a little buzzed to figure out her motives. “That all depends on you.” She pauses for a moment. “Don’t be a stranger, okay?” It was more than obvious she wanted him to invite her in or at least kiss her but she wasn’t going to get him to budge. He must have had some really emotional issues going on or he just didn’t like women as much as he seemed to. “I’ll keep in touch don’t worry. Maybe I’ll make a lewd call to your work.” He chuckled as he took out the keys to the building and put it in the first lock. She refrained from giggling and was reminded by the keys that she had the one to his motorcycle. She pulled it out from her purse and handed it to him before she forgot but could have kicked herself for it as she would have had another reason to see him again. “Here’s your key Eryc. I thought you might go looking for them in the morning.” “I appreciate you remembering; thanks again Katherine, I had a good time.” “Goodnight.” Katherine spoke as she walked down the few steps that were in front of the building. “Goodnight.” He said as he opened the door and closed it behind him after he was inside, locking up for the night. He leaned against the door thinking for a moment before walking to the elevator to go up to the penthouse. . . . . . . During the night, dreams seemed to encompass him and to some degree haunt him. He had fought hard to forget as much of his past as he could but there was one constant thing that he had in his mind and until it was resolved it would never let him rest fully. Once upon a time he had been courting a lovely lady named Dawn McFarlane, but like most romances within the workplace it fizzled out much like a roman candle at its greatest height. A lot of things were left unsaid and that was something he needed to get closure for whether he wanted to admit to it or not. He kept it in his head that when he woke up he would call her up and set up somewhere that they could go and talk things out. It was the least that could be done. His head was a jumbled mess from the moment he woke up. The effects his body was going through were still working themselves out and he felt ill every now and then. There was absolutely no food in the house so he had to figure out what he was going to do for breakfast since what he had in the fridge would only make him even worse off than he already was. His stomach was in knots as he picked up the phone. There was no other way to escape it, he just had to call her and set up a meeting between them to get it out of his system. All of that human emotion was really burning into him now and there was no telling if it was guilt playing tricks on him or something else entirely. His fingers traced the buttons on the phone and started to put it back on the charger but went through with his intentions; dialed the number and waited. The soft voice on the other end picked up and he hesitated for a minute and didn’t want her to hear that he was breathing. He poised himself and then spoke. “Dawn ... it’s Corvus, I’m going to be in your area and I was thinking that we should meet.” He listened as the surprise and reluctance in her tone mixed together until she finally acquiesced with his request. It was a bit of a lie he was telling her about being in her area. He was simply going to call and have his jet take him there on a moment’s notice. Those were just things he had the luxury of doing when a whim came about. “I was thinking that we could meet in Toronto ... you know that park we went to that one time?” He paused for a moment as she remembered it. “Yes, that’s the one.” A slight smile formed on his lips as he continued setting things up. “Ok, I will see you tonight then after sunrise; I look forward to it. Goodbye Dawn.” He ended the call and quickly dialed the number to the airfield where his plane was located and made arrangements to grab a quick flight out later in the evening to Toronto. Corvus quickly took a shower and packed a few things so they would be ready for him to leave and then went out to have a bite to eat at a little restraint a little ways away from his building. . . . The plane arrived in Toronto around 7:30 where he stepped out feeling very odd about the encounter. The Dawn he was going to meet with was probably nothing like the one he had spent time with just over a year ago but then again he was not the same person from back then either. He walked out of the airport and fetched a cab telling him exact direction on how to get there. Upon arrival he paid the fair and took his bag with him and walked around while a few memories of the past began to filter into his head. Like a scene out of a movie he stopped when his eyes spotted a familiar bench in which the two sat. Moving closer, he placed his bag down and touched the wood and the particular evening he had spoken of on the phone came spiraling back. This was the closest they had ever been and for him it was the closest thing he had ever felt towards true love. He had taken her there after going to the theater and seeing and independent film she had been buzzing about for several days. It was a clear night just the same as the present with the moon fully alight in the sky. It was not overly windy that night either as they sat and spoke in soft tones so only the two of them could hear; passers by had admired what they saw of the couple. Corvus sat down as the memory continued to play out in his head of them close together and their faces closing in towards the moment of perfect bliss and shared a passionate kiss that could have stopped time dead in its tracks. The moment faded away as he heard footsteps which were unchanged. He folded his arms across his chest as his eyes met with hers and it was a struggle to even speak a greeting. “Good evening.” Dawn seemed more and more like a creature of the night herself these days. Her recently re-dyed hair hung just past her shoulders, jet black locks streaked through with the occasionally strawberry blonde of her natural colour. Her trademark longcoat was away for the hot summer, replaced by black cargo pants and a dark blue tank top. A black leather bracer took up the majority of her right wrist and forearm, and a black silk choker was fastened securely around her pale neck. She regarded him suspiciously, green eyes flitting over him, like a laser scanner at the checkout counter. 'Hello Corvus. You wanted to see me?' It was obvious that seeing her this close to him for the first time in as many months seemed to make him a bit nervous not so much as uncomfortable. He clutched his arms tighter to his body before he replied to her. “Yes, I did ... and we can be a little less formal; call me Eryc, please?” He motioned for her to take a seat and kept his things between them so she wouldn’t feel so uneasy about sitting down. There was no plan for him to make any moves on her as that wasn’t what this was all about to begin with. She hesitated for a moment, one slender eyebrow raising at the gesture...before she slid from the shadow of the tree she had been standing in and slipped onto the bench. There was a moment of silence before she turned to look at him again, this time more searchingly, as if she could divine his intentions from his expression. 'Eryc huh? I haven’t called you that for over a year. Why now?' “Time changes things and people along with it; there are a lot of things about me that I don’t think you ever had enough time to discover and even more now than before.” His expressions explored more of a pained state as he kept his head lowered a bit and every now and then would only take a glance when she was going to speak. She cocked her head to one side, looking quizzically. 'How so?' “The reason I came here was to come to terms with what happened to our relationship and I think ... I know that you felt that I thought of you as more of a prize than anything else.” He stopped for a second trying not to let all that sadness take over him and show weakness in front of her. “I wanted to prove to you otherwise.” It was Dawn's turn to look away at that point, casting her gaze out over the park. After a long moment she turned her head back to him again, struggling to keep her expression unreadable. 'Alright. You've got my attention at the very least.' Eryc tried to cover up that he took a deep sigh before he resumed speaking. “Out of anyone I have ever known personally, you were the one that pieced everything together.” The demons of the past continued to dig at the wound. “I was stubborn quite a bit of the time and unbearable to deal with and I can’t blame you for being so distant with me now.” “I think now because of the things that have happened that I wish I could have given you more. I’ve never loved anyone quite like I loved you.” He ran a hand through his hair as he continued to make his confession. “Even after our departure from one another you were always in my dreams and you were what helped me to conquer my fears and ultimately led to my best success in Wrestling Midwest.” Dawn chewed on her lower lip for a moment, worrying it between her teeth while she mulled over what he'd said. She opened her mouth to speak and then stopped, trying unsuccessfully several times before settling on what she wishes to say. 'I admit I don't really know what to say about that...you'll have to forgive me, but it's difficult to believe you.' He seemed to be affected by her words and nearly stood up to walk away but it would not have solved anything and only labeled him a coward in her eyes. His hand extended out to her and if she took it she’d feel that he wasn’t cold as stone. “Then would you believe my beating heart?” Dawn raised a skeptical eyebrow. 'Eryc, your heart hasn't been beating since long before I met you.' Regardless she put her hand in his, noticing immediately the warmth that came from his normally cold flesh. “Maybe I couldn’t have done anything as an uncaring monster ...” He squeezed her hand lightly in his as he looked at her. She would have realized the color of his eyes were different and resembled pools of the ocean. “But I can try and mend things as a human being.” Dawn narrowed her eyes, glaring suspiciously through emerald slits. She took her free hand and laid it against his chest, pausing for a long moment as her eyes wide as saucers. 'H-how...how is this possible?' “It is quite a long story and I’m not sure I understand all of it myself but I’ve been given a gift, a second chance to make things right.” He closed his eyes for a moment feeling her hand against him and cherishing the moment. “I don’t know if this is permanent or what will happen but you were the first person I needed to see when I realized what was going on.” He did all that he could to make his words sound justifiable to her and to dispel the skepticism she was feeling towards what was happening there and then. “I needed to let you know how I really felt. I’d be content not to wake up tomorrow just as long as I knew that you knew deep down in your heart that I loved you as a creature and more so as a man.” Dawn closed her eyes for a long moment, an expression akin to pain sliding across her pale face. She took a deep breath before opening them again, gazing into his eyes for a long moment before speaking. 'Eryc...I appreciate that you wanted to tell me these things, and it means a lot that you'd think of me. But I...for me that all came at one of the worst periods of my life...I can't honestly tell you how I feel, because I don't know. Whatever's real is caught up in all the hurt and the bitterness...and maybe the hurt is what's real, I don't know.' He slowly let her hand go and understood her point as he knew what a rough time that was for her. What could he possibly do to assure her that it was all over? Absolutely nothing and that could have struck him dead right then and there. “That is why you need a chance to let old scars heal and let new memories, better ones, take over.” It also applied to him as well. There were things he had to let go of from the past too. “If anyone knows more about time it would be me and whether this is going to be how I live out my days or if I go back to what once was ... I will wait. All I wanted was to give you what you needed back then and your eyes won’t deceive you. Part of you wants it still.” Maybe he was trying to convince himself blindly that she needed him just so he had something more to hold onto. 'You can't just walk back into my life...' She said softly, 'Not after the way we parted, and not after the things that were said. You replaced me, and in my heart I found a way to move on from that. We run in very different circles right now...this is going to take time, we can't just dive right back into trusting each other.' He nodded solemnly. “I know we walk on different sides of the road, I just don’t want to be enemies ... I’ve let too much of that ruin me in the past.” Eryc stood up and picked his bag up and looked at her one more time. “Anyone else in my shoes might regret what I did tonight, but I don’t. Maybe one day you will trust me and until then I’ll live with that loneliness welled up inside me and put it to good use.” Dawn looked up at him rather than stand, conceding a small bit of defense as a point towards a common objective. 'As long as you don't regret coming here tonight and you honestly want to make things right...then maybe someday it'll happen.' She offered out one hand. He took her soft hand into his and once again holding onto it for a second not knowing when the chance would arise again if ever. He brought it closer and placed his lips against it softly before letting it go and slung his bag on his shoulder. “Til anon ...” Dawn nodded. 'Safe drive. I'll see you again at Evo.' “You will ... and be well, Dawn.” And that was it. He walked through the park covered in darkness as was usual for him and in that moment he never felt so alone. There wasn’t enough reasoning for him to stay in the city let alone torture himself or drown himself in alcohol which was just a cheap way out of dealing with the hurt. He couldn’t blame her for not trusting him as he couldn’t trust himself either. The path he was on now was paved with uncertainty. What was to become of him now? He was just as much of a target as a mortal then he was as a vampire, which meant he would have to keep a low profile. These were all questions that needed an answer but for the time being he could have cared less ... he just needed to go somewhere to brood and that gave him an idea for what he wished to do next as a human that he couldn’t do before. Eryc decided to make a u-turn instead of going back to Las Vegas and went to California, taking his acoustic guitar (that he had stored on his private plane) to Long Beach where he had laid out a towel on the sand to play as the sun began to rise. His fingers strummed against the strings gracefully with a clear tear running down his cheek due to the pure beauty of what he saw before him as well as lamenting over what he felt was no longer his to have.
