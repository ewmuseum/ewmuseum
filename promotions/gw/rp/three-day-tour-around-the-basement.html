Three day tour around the basement

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Day 2 Dark, cold, decrepit, leaking. This dungeon I live in is seeping, searing and endearing with it’s creepy night music. Up above the cage I rot away in, the music of hell itself (Yanni with Organ Grinder solos) blasts through a great system. Through the concrete and floorboards it is muffed into a little voice that can creep up the back of my head. Otherwise, there is never any noise down here, a total thicket of solitude to keep me oppressed from freedom. This is my hole, and I dug myself into it. At the cellar door comes a scratching, clawing, and gnawing sound from the creaky door hinges. I sit up tight from my dreamy slumber of days gone by, and check for the light from above the stairwell to see if a figurine comes to attack. It is, to think, that sports agent Dennis Winston who has come to pay a visit to this slug infested nuke shed. “This isn’t working.” He speaks with sighs and gestures of undeniable ready-to-give-up-ness. But faith in a plan is all that I can ask for, and it is this with what I wish to find out how to return to who I was. I smiled gravely and could tell be my tone that he could tell I was a failure to modern plans of mistakable entry. What, oh what could I do to deceive this man from taking me away from this hell I have sent myself to? “It is though Dennis, it is! I am here and I roar like the clouded raindrop of wind.” He sucked up what I said like a whirlpool would an infant, and drained the lifeless words into a huff and a puff! But that wasn’t enough, he ran around dungeon with his arms waving in the air and his knees kicking up like a marching band, all while staying in the same place. A sight, what a sight to see this man be. “Are you even listening to me” We need to get you out of here and back to reality.” That’s right! Dennis that menace of a sports agent was so correct in his timing to be like that, because I needed to get out of this dungeon pronto and in haste too! Soon, the dungeon master will be here to take out his unholy blade and dissemble my body to the floor, and I would be no more. So sad, am I that I now longer worry what could become of me! “So, Dennis, please,” I beg of him, “Please oh please unlock this cage and let me roam, roam to the countryside where I will be forever free from the oppression of this horrible Dungeon master. A scary man this man who held me back from being free to do what I want was, a slayer of his kin and daggers flying truly from his eyes. “I will die; just die if he stabs me with his eyes!” Dennis scratched his chin like a philosopher, unsure of just how bad I was in this, trying to comprehend the bend of my situation. “What...can I...do...to help you?” “You must take up my gun and fire the bullets to the lock, cracking it to pieces and freeing me from my hole. If I was free, oh what I could be, so happy to be not-slain.” My smile lay goofy and reassuring, but inside I was yearning, to just get my way. Dennis shrugged and pulled out my gun, which was so big and made of gold that it was powerful enough to make some run. What I mumble to make him shoot, is so mumbled I don’t know what I said but is sure to make some heads turn. He shot the golden gun and it burned, burned, burned through the lock and set me jumping back with shock! I looked left and right, and up and around, it was now time to follow the hellish sound. “Wait, please!” The man in the cell next to mine said. He was laying in various pieces, I thought he was dead. His body was disembodied, but his arms still moved to the beat of his speech. “You cannot defeat this man, you cannot defeat him yet. BUT if you find the golden key than you will be set.” “The Golden Key” Is that some sort of spiritual thing? If I find this will I be like a Lord of the Ring?” “It’s not like that, no. He resides in a room that hides in the loom of a lock, inaccessible without the key.” “Oh,” what a disappointment to me it was. I’d prefer the thought that I could find all the power in the world in the form of something that easy. But no, I would have to fight this man alone, with a gun and my will to live. It was true, and the sad reality in it that I would have to rely on myself to defeat those in my way. Maybe since the Master of the Mat tournament I was not so confident in this ideal. “..Oh.” And that was my reality. Back and by and bye.
