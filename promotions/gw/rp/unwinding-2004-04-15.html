Unwinding

15 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/15/04 As always, there was a great deal of energy in the Dark Horse saloon. The sounds of “Ramblin’ Man” filled the air. People were dancing, laughing, drinking, eating, just having a good time. The doors had swung open and in walked Brick, Ely Braxton, Jesse Briggs and Logan Creed. It was a rough night for all, or at least, three of them. Ely and Jesse had lost their tag match to Black n White. tag match to Black n White, partially because Jesse didn’t feel right hitting women and was trying to be somewhat chivalrous. His chivalry was wasted on them, as they both dropkicked him in his knees. Brick, in addition to losing his three-way battle to newcomer J.C. Diamond, had been involved in a three-way brawl with Deacon Holmes and Train. Now they had come to the Dark Horse saloon to cool off. Brick, Ely and Jesse had each taken a bar stool. Logan went to a specially reserved bar stool. On the shaft of the stool, there was a button. Logan pressed the button and the stool lowered down to him. Once he sat, he pressed the button again and the seat rose back up. “Hey, guys, what’ll ya have?” “Ginger ale,” Brick replied. “The usual for me,” said Ely. “Just a beer,” said Jesse. “Make it two,” said Logan. “Okay, that’s a ginger ale, a Texas mudslide and two beers, coming right up.” “Hell of a night, huh?” “No kidding.” “Hey, Jesse, how’s your knees?” “Still a little sore.” “I warned y’all not to underestimate women.” “Hey, I didn’t underestimate any damn body. That was all Jesse.” “Yeah, what the hell was that?” asked Logan. “Sorry, fellas. Don’t know what came over me. Maybe I underestimated them because they beat Bishop up so easily.” “You kiddin’ me? My little goddaughter could beat Mark Bishop up an’ she’s barely one!” “Funny, though. Never thought I’d be the one gettin’ all chivalrous. Especially after all the crap I did to Gunner.” “Yeah, I ain’t always been the poster child for chivalry, either, all the stuff I did to Christina. Speaking of which, what’s the deal with her and Gunner? I was watching Overload and she seemed pretty pissed off about her being there.” “I dunno. Somethin’ about a guy, I think. Shocked me, too, because those two were like sisters.” “It wasn’t you, was it, Ely?” “Look, Christina an’ I were just friends, okay?” “Bullshit!” shouted Jesse. “Excuse me?” “Sorry, E, but I’m with Jesse on this one.” “Me, too. You and Christina used to act a little more than just friendly.” “Aw, whatever!” The bartender then returned with their drinks. “OK, one ginger ale, one Texas mudslide and two beers.” “Thanks, Jack.” “Anyway, did you guys watch Overload? What the hell got into Gunner? She just kept throwing herself at Spivey the whole show.” “You know, Brick, if I didn’t know any better, I’d say you were jealous.” “I’m not jealous, ok? I’m concerned.” “I figured you’d be happy considering what she said about you.” “You mean when she was licking her lips? Suddenly, “Take My Breath Away” started playing as many couples started slow dancing. “What the hell? Who punched that song in?” “Don’t know. Didn’t even know it was in the jukebox.” “OK, let’s forget about Gunner, forget about Christina, forget about Black n White. Hell, let’s forget about women in general, right now. What are you gonna do about Holmes an’ Train?” “Yeah, I know. That brawl was just the beginning. I can feel it.” “Well?” “When the time comes, I’ll handle it. Hey, I thought we were here to unwind. Let’s leave the shop talk in the office.” “Amen!”
