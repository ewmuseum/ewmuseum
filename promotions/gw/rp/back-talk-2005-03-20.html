Date: 3/20/05 Darkness opened to a rectangle of light. Train hardly came in this part of building any more. When he’d converted this old warehouse slash factor into his new Dallas home he’d chosen it for several reasons. One, growing up Train had learned to not like people very much. In the hood the muthafuckas were everywhere. The system had folks stacked on top of eachother, packed together like sardines. You barely had room to breathe. And forget owning nice shit. If you had that you might not have it for long once some muthafucka came climbing through yo? window to take it. Having people around was a pain in the ass. This place was slap in the middle of a concrete wasteland who’s industry had long since died and left a bit of a black hole. Fuck neighbors, there were none to worry about around here. Secondly he could get away with more here. In your normal residential area people might have a problem with you erecting a 12 foot high, iron rod gate around your house with a bit of razor wire wrapped around the top for more than just visual effect. Something like that might be just a little too intimidating for the neighborhood kids. The could also object to the set of 4, 200 watt, halogen lamps attached the each corner of the place. The ones will snap on at night with even a hint of movement within 75 feet of the place. That type of thing could ruin somebody’s blissful slumber. Oh, and the neighborhood birds might not appreciate the unadvertised custom option on the iron gate. The push of a button near the bed or the front door and it’s suddenly coursing with 50,000 volts of electricity. Damn neighborhood parents, the cops wouldn’t be happy about that shit. But out here, nobody who wasn’t trying to rob you would give a fuck. And if they were trying to rob you who gives a fuck about their opinion? But it was the last that might have been as important as any other. Storage. Train had decided that this opportunity in GW would be the final shift of his life. He’d decided he’d be done with the streets. Which meant, where ever he went to it had to have plenty of room. He had a whole life to pack up. Plenty of room to store things. One thing about a warehouse... there would always be enough dark corners to hide darker impulses in. The normally pitch black room Train stood in doorway of was now flooded with light. The illumination cut a large pillar shape in the darkness, skinny at it’s base, widening out as it progressed across the room. The darkness only persisted in the corners of the room, obviously full to the brim with boxes and metal shelving, in the corners and the wall that contained the door itself. Train’s shadow cut another dark spot in the room, his silhouette taking the same shape as the pillar of light. The dust that had long since settled in the room is whipped up by the sudden air flow and dances in the light. He stands quietly for a moment, surveying the seeming haphazard organization of the room. He slowly steps strolls through the room, twisting and contorting his body to keep from running into errant cardboard boxes scattered through the room, finally ending up in front of the far wall. His eyes trace the set of metal shelving in front of him, apparently looking for one of the random items stored on it. But appearances are deceiving. Train grasps the side of the shelves that easily way upwards of 400 pounds and takes advantage of the small, basicly invisible, furniture slip pads he had work men put in when he first moved in and started renovating. The gargantuan shelf that runs from floor to nearly ceiling slides over as if it were on wheels. On good shot from his boot knocks off the baseboard of the wall and Train stoops down and digs his hand into the dark gap that’s no longer hidden. A moment later a harsh scraping sound fills the air as Train pulls a long, black, metal case from it’s hiding spot. A hiding spot just accessible enough to be practical, but isolated enough to suggest a desire that whatever is placed in it will never be seen again. He pops back up to his feet and lifts the seemingly heavy case before effortlessly sliding the shelving unit back into it’s place. A moment later the room goes dark as the door slams behind him. The kitchen table rattles as the black case drops harshly across it’s face. Train’s hand reaches out to unclasp the locks, but freezes in the air. It suddenly occurs to him once he opens this pandora’s box there may be no going back. A part of his life he said he’d left behind would be resurrected. Pop... Pop... The locks of the case pop open, he flips the cover up, revealing the contents. They didn’t shine the way they used to. Two chrome plated Berretas.... Specially made and etched, chrome plated, 12 guage Mossberg pump action shot gun... 2 black Mac 10's with extended clips and vented, compensating silencers. There hadn’t been a presence like these in this place before. Sure Train had a a shotgun hidden in the door frame of the house for possible emergencies. A quick fix in a tight spot. But these? These were different. . These were instruments of war. Nobody in Train’s recent history would know anything of them. They could count themselves lucky. But there were several men back in Chicago who could attest to just what the purpose of these things were. To kill. Pure and simple. When you crossed him... when an example had to be made... when there was a cancer to be cut out... these were for the surgery. But they’d been out of circulation for a while. The chrome was tarnished and dull from time and lack of care. But that was only the outside. Train knew the inside still functioned. They’d roar to life a moments notice if need be. Which is why they were still around. And, considering what he was about to do... considering the doors he was likely going to have to open to keep his good name... he might just need them again. Train walked over and dug into the one of the drawers on the kitchen counter and pulled out a white rag. He returned to the table and slowly dropped down into one of the chairs before pulling one of the Berretas from it’s cushioned holding spaces. the drags the rag across the smooth, metal surface of the gun, the rubbing slowly begins to return the gun to it’s former luster. Suddenly a shrill toll cuts through the air. Train puts the gun down and digs into his pocket and pulls it back out with his cell phone in hand. The caller ID displays the 212 area code. He doesn’t recognize the number, but he knows the area code. The call was from New York. The call was the one he’d been waiting for all day. A man he’d never even met had run his mouth about Train. That meant a man he’d never even met before had to be put and check. And if he didn’t take the hint. If he decided he just had to be a fucking tough guy and wanted to take it to the next level and start a real beef. Train knew, despite the fact he didn’t want to... he might have to settle it. “Sup?” Train asked bluntly, his mind still focused on priming his weapons. “Hi... Train?” a pleasant female voice, over flowing with an unmistakable New York accent inquired. “That’s me.” “Hi Train, this is Hot 97 in New York called for the interview we had scheduled.” “Cool, cool... I been waiting for yall.” “Great, great... Alright I’m going to put you on hold for a moment and then you should hear Angie Martinez, the dj on air right now, speaking. When you do you’ll be on the air, ok?” “Sounds good.” “Alright, here we go.” “Yeah...” There is a short series of clicks before the sounds of hip hop echo in his ears. It’s obvious he’s hearing what’s going over the air waves at Hot 97 where? this controversy had all started. Train lets go of the phone, holding to his ear with his massive shoulder. His hands now free he returns to the task of polishing. Suddenly there’s a voice over the music. “Hey yo, welcome back!” the brassy voiced latina exclaims. “I’m Angie, but you already know that and that was the new one by 50 Cent called “Piggy Bank” off his new joint called The Massacre... That track has sparking up all kinds of major beef, 50 taking a shot at Ja Rule, Fat Joe, Jada Kiss, Nas and I think even Mr. Rogers up in that joint! Hahaha... But anyway, speaking of beef I got a guest on right now that, cuz of something he had to say right here on this station has a beef of his own right now in the rap game... and he aint? even a rapper! Ladies and Gentlemen, please welcome... From Global Wrestling... World Tag Team Champion... Train!!! Train, you there my man?” “Oh, I’m right here baby girl.” Train answers with a faked pleasantness meant to keep his anger from boiling over until the proper time to release it. “I’m glad to hear that baby boy. How you doing?” “I’m doing me girl. I’m chillin.” Train responds as he pulls a small brush from the black case and proceeds to scrub out the barrel of the Beretta. “I hear that baby. Now I hear you shining down there in Texas nowadays man.” “Hey, when you carrying around 12 pounds of gold on your waist you can’t really do nothing, but shine.” “Hahaha... I heard that! Congratulations on busting out those tag team titles recently. “Oh it ain’t no thang girl.” “Well let’s talk about something that is a thang. You and Lil” Flip. As you know my man dissed you just a few days ago right here on this station. I mean I don’t get it. Do you even know him?” “I ain’t never met ol” boy before in my life.” “So what’s the deal with you and him then?” “All I know is this” Some little punk we was up on his album cover dressed as the Lucky Charms Leprechaun up on his first album, talkin? like he’s a gangsta has the nerve to get his panties in a bunch when I tell him I’m as real is they come.” “Right, right...” “Well I’ma make this quick and simple. I done earned my stripes on the streets of Chicago. I locked that bitch down. I ran it up in there, you heard me” Now I got important things to do in GW like becoming the world champion... but if he thinks just cuz I ain’t gonna go dissing him on some mix tape that he aint? got to worry about me and he can just disrespect me and that stuff’s just gonna stand he’s lost his damn mind. You tell him, that Train said... he’s a bitch... his boys are bitches and his whole clique is ass.” “Oh!” “You can also tell him... that I told you... that if he don’t like me or what I got to say and he’s feeling froggy” Houston ain’t nothing but a hop, skip and a jump from Dallas ! Get at me dogg.” “And that’s what’s up.” “Damn right.” “Alright Train, it was good having you on and hopefully you come back...” “Any time.” “And as for this thing with Lil” Flip and you... I hope yall squash that cuz there’s way too much beefin? going on nowadays... but if he don’t I hope he knows hope he’s been paying attention in the gym cuz that could be an ugly fight for him. Train, thanks for coming on, I’ll talk to you later.” “Peace.” Train pulls the phone from his ear and flips it shut, before slamming it down on the table. His full attention returns to the gun in his hand. The gleam of it’s silvery surface tells him it’s presentable again. He grabs the grip of the gun and it nestles itself into his palm as if crafted just for him. It seemed like years since he’d held the trusted friend in his hand. It also seemed like yesterday. The muthafucka wanted to talk shit? Let him talk. The only thing that little trick was ready for was a war of words. Train was ready for a war of a whole different type.
