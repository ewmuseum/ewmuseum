Closer

12 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04-12-2005 The world around her hung low. Every branch, each sideways glance, and even the clouds in the multi-colored sky above seemed to hover just below her, all of them judging her for the current state of her realm. Creatures of the night possessed her thoughts, pulling and tugging at her mind, stretching it like taffy and then letting it go, permanently damaging its once perfect structure. Invisible demons covered her flesh, burning with each step and letting the blood flow with every mouthful. As hard as she tried, she could not shake off these creatures and demons so instead she allowed them to chew at her soul. She felt lost even if she had walked this road a thousand times. The road of despair runs its own course and any unlucky soul who runs along this obstacle course of rotating knives knows that all personal control loses itself to the power of the night. The backlash of the past two weeks seemed, to her, like an open wound, oozing its crimson life and scabbing over to leave a permanent scar. Losing to Mercer, while maddening, could not compare to the other troubles of the past week. With Tommy missing and her father presumably dead, any other problem seemed quite miniscule. The mystery of her father’s disappearance worried her the most. The police had contacted her only several minutes after she evacuated the crime scene, only to inform her that her father’s office had been broken into. The man in the overcoat, the same man who had abducted her several weeks earlier, had been shot between the eyes. What shocked her more than this fact, however, was that her father could not be found anywhere. When asked to observe the crime scene, she did notice that his briefcase was missing, but did not mention this fact. She had been clear in the fact that she had not seen her father in over seven years and when asked why her fingerprints were found on the door knob, she very calmly replied that she had been in to visit earlier that day. The local police were forgiving, knowing that with her best friend already missing and with her father missing too; it was likely that she was going to breakdown? “if she had not already. So here she was, standing on the outskirt of an alley, watching as people bustled back and forth, going about their everyday life. How do they not feel this pain” Why can they go about their everyday lives without understanding the anguish of every other person walking this cursed planet? Exhaling loudly, Aradia brought her cigarette back up to her blackened lips. She took a drag before blowing out a puff of gray smoke. Her brown eyes turned up to watch the smoke dissipate into the air, which was heavy with twilight. Running her tongue along her upper teeth, she snickered before bringing her face back down to the masses. They, like the smoke, were dissolving quickly. Probably returning home to their wives, husbands, two and a half children, and dog just to sit down to a dinner full of laughter and hidden animosity she thought, smiling to herself. Unlike most other individuals, Aradia possessed the ability to see right through even the happiest moments. To Aradia, everything had a second meaning, a double entendre, or a suppressed negative reality. It was only one of the many burdens she required herself to carry. She shook her head with another smile and took another drag of her cigarette. People watching, while amusing, was not one of Aradia’s past times. Her motive for standing in the alley was more than just watching these mindless androids shuffle past. In fact, her motive for being back in this hell of a city meant more than she would prefer to admit. Nathaniel she thought, shaking her head in disbelief. It was so like her father to use code at such a crucial time. If he was prepared to die that day, then why wouldn’t he just out and tell me what was going on? This thought crossed her mind a thousand times, daily. She looked to her right, a blank look of thought on her face. There is something more to this? there has to be. That man was shot in the head? and dad is missing. There was a reasoning for using our most infamous code? fuck him for being so damn confusing! Yet another thought that had plagued her for a week. Bringing her cigarette to her mouth once again, she realized that it was just about gone. Groaning in frustration, she flicked it to the sidewalk and proceeded to use one of her black combat boots to smash its flame and spark into the warm cement. Shielded light illuminated her body in a muted orange glow. She stretched to relieve some of the tension in her body and in her mind. Looking down at herself, she took in the sight of her tight leather pants and black tank top and chuckled. These morons probably assume I’m some street walker, dressed like this standing in an alley? The thought pleased her, mind games and make-believe being two of her favorite hobbies. Taking a hair clip out of her pocket, she pulled some of her long raven locks out of her face. When is this asshole going to show up? I don’t have all night? She looked around cautiously. The sun was setting rapidly and the street was beginning to become barren. I really wish my father would have explained this whole Nathaniel thing to me in greater detail? but then again I guess he never thought he’d actually ever need to use that shit. She was frustrated with herself for letting things get to this point. For the past few years she had everything under control. The occasional abductions, the rendezvous with lackeys, and even threatening letters and phone calls were merely a job. She knew how to handle these situations. But now, the game had been taken to a new level and she had been caught off guard. Strands of her hair fell into her face and she pushed them away, angrily. I need a fucking haircut? Her thoughts were broken by a whisper in the alley. She held still as her mind fell silent. For a few moments, only the sounds of the city surrounding her landed upon her and so she perked her ears, hoping to catch any extra background noise. That’s when she heard it again. “Come to me, child” Her heart stopped fast. The voice was vaguely familiar, but in a hushed whisper there was no way to pin point its owner. Scrunching up her face, she began to turn her head but was greeted by a breath on her neck. She froze in fear, not knowing how to handle this new situation. What the fuck? The breath was heavy and she assumed that it belonged to a man or a really large woman. Someone was standing directly behind her and she did not know how to take it. Is this the person I’ve been waiting for? What if he’s trying to mug me? Shit! Her eyes darted left and right, trying to find a means of distraction, but the whisper beckoned her once more. “Do not be afraid, Radie. Just step into the darkness.” Paralyzed by fear, Aradia’s jaw shook as she began to whisper back. “Who the fuck are you and how do you know my” name?” She stumbled on this last word, knowing that this intruder not only knew her name, but her nickname. The breathing continued, but was interrupted by a hushed cackle. Suddenly full of adrenaline, Aradia spun to meet the figure dead on, but instead found nothing but the darkened alley before her. Both confused and afraid, Aradia took a step into the alley to investigate. What the hell? Where did it go? She bobbed her head from left to right, attempting to scan the entire alley. Defeated, she turned to step back into the lighted sidewalk, but instead found herself face to face with a man dressed in black. A shriek trembled on her blackened lips, but the figure quickly covered her mouth with his hand and pushed her further back into the alley. She tried to struggle, but her attacker was able to avoid being hit. Letting her go, the figure stepped back and observed the beauty before him. Angered, Aradia began to question her assailant. “What the hell do you think you’re doing, you crazy bastard!”” The figure only brought a finger to it’s darkened face and kept silent. “Answer me!” Aradia said, louder, hoping that this increase in volume would cause the man to speak. “My god, Aradia, do you always have to be so feisty!”” Her irate expression morphed into shock. That voice was much too familiar to disregard. She backed up against the wall, her face full of doubt. Her lips trembled as she reached out to touch the man’s face. “Daddy?” The figure nodded and he dropped his hood, revealing the face of her father. Terrified, Aradia began to shake, unable to take any more surprises this evening. “My dear, you look frightened of me?” She shook her head and forced a laugh. “Of course I do! I thought you were dead?” Her father nodded, understanding. “I see, but yet you were convinced Tommy wasn’t dead?” With a sigh, Aradia quickly responded. “I saw Tommy disappear, I knew he wasn’t dead.” She pauses before responding. “But I heard the gunshot and so I had assumed?” Her father shook his head, not believing her. “First of all, our code should have explained that I would be okay. Secondly, when you went back into the office with the police, you saw the other man’s head wound. You should have known me better than that, Aradia.” Still in shock, Aradia looked her father over before continuing. “Well, it’s been seven years, dad, what was I supposed to think” Half of the codes you used were just precautionary and after I left home, why would I need to remember them? I’m lucky I remembered the one about Nathaniel?” she pauses and looks at him in question. ??which, by the way, you owe me an explanation for. What the hell is all this?” Staring at her blankly, her father tried to gather his thoughts. How could he approach this without sounding insane? He gave it a try, besides, what did he have to lose? “I’ve told you since you were a child, that if there were ever a problem in the office, that I would tell you to go see Nathaniel. As you already know, that meant to come to this alley three days later for advice. What you didn’t know was that I used this to protect you. When you’re in the line of work that I’m in, my dear, you have to understand that these people will come after the ones you love. Telling you that you could meet someone who would help you, someone different than myself, keeps you safe. I would eventually show up here and explain these circumstances to you?” Aradia shakes her head and quickly interrupts her father. “I’m sorry dad” but what the fuck? After all those years of helping you? and? just damnit, dad! What if you would have died?” He nods and continues. “In the unfortunate event of my death, I would not have shown here, but one of my associates would have. He would have explained the situation.” He pauses, seeing the hurt and anger in his daughter’s eyes. “Look, Radie, I can understand why you’re upset, but you need to get over it right now. We both are in danger and if you want my help then neither of us can let this pettiness get between us.” He glances at her blank face and then down at his watch. “Damnit, Aradia, I don’t have much time. Do you want me to help you or not?” She looks around before nodding. “Yes, yes I do” she says in a meek and timid voice. “Good, else all of this planning would have gone to waste.” He smiles and so does she, appreciating that he can joke in such a crucial time. “Now listen, my dear, you are in a lot of danger. I hope you are heeding my advice to stay within the federation” and to rarely stray from its sight or property.” She nods and responds. “I have. I’m partially there for the protection it offers, but also because I enjoy doing it.” Her father shakes and lowers his head. “I’ll never understand that?” “Oh give me a break,” she says, disappointed. “What more could I ask for than the ability to perform and kick ass?” “Yeah, I suppose it is the best place for you?” he chuckles and then continues with his warning. “Here’s the deal, Radie. Your part in this little game is over. From here on out, I will be taking control of this affair. Whatever Mr. Johnson wants done with you is now my business. I already have a few leads on his location?” She looks at him, rage in her marbled orbs. “Now you wait a damn minute,” she says, interrupting his monologue. “I have been dealing with this for several years now, dad. He’s out for my life at this point” and he has something to do with Tommy’s disappearance. How can you ask me to just step back and be completely uninformed? You’re asking me to back down.” She laughs. “You know I don’t do that easily.” Nodding, her father looks at her intensely “I know you too well, Aradia, and I knew this was the response you’d have. But you have to understand that if meddle in this any further, you’re going to put yourself in more danger.” Catching her look of contempt, he further states his claim. “You’ll be putting Tommy’s life at danger as well.” Defeated, she drops her head, knowing that he is right. “That’s the way, my dear. You know that I can handle this. It’s my job. As far as Mr. Johnson is concerned, I’m dead and gone. He had instructed that man to dispose of me after I died. He has no clue that I’m still alive. Then again, it’s not as though he’d suspect I’m dead, either. I’m just missing. He’s left to assume that I’m dead and that one of my associates killed the man you saw in my office.” Aradia stood still, feeling overwhelmed. Her life was turning into one of those terrible action movies where everything is one big secret and the characters have to run around and solve the puzzle in order to kill the bad guy. What was different, however, was that this was not a movie. This was a bad version of her real life. “Alright, dad” she states, looking up at him with frustration. “I’ll stay out of it. But you have to promise to stay in contact to let me know everything is alright. Otherwise, I’ll be after you.” She lied through her teeth in order to appease her father. In no way would she ever consider letting her father run after this on his own. She had to be involved somehow, but in order to make him happy, she would let him think she would follow his orders. “I promise, although I can’t guarantee how often I’ll be able to be in touch.” Aradia nods and looks around, noting that night has befallen them. “Also, Aradia, I think it would be beneficial for you to start getting the fans” approval. You’re going to need all the backing you can get until this matter is taken care of.” The record in her head came to a screeching halt. Get on the fans? good side? He’s asking me to be? to be? fan-friendly?”” How am I going to pull that off? “Alright dad, I’ll try.” “That’s my girl,” he says with a smile. “Start making friends, you’ll need them.” He places the hood back over his head, preparing to leave. As he turns, he makes another statement. “Use that title of yours to your advantage. It’ll help you. Don’t lose it.” She smiles, knowing her father has no idea how the world of Global works. “I’ll try not to,” she says, trying to give her father hope. She watched as he hurries back out into the street and quickly loses himself in a crowd of clubbers who stampede by. Never were one for goodbye, were you dad? Although rather shocked and afraid, she could not help by smile at her father’s forced professionalism. He was sacrificing a lot for the welfare of his only daughter, but she was starting to understand why. He wants to make up for the mistakes he made. I guess I have to respect that? She screws her face into disgust at the thought of respecting the man and then heads back out of the alley. Dusk had long since past, she found herself stepping into the barren and empty night. Taking in a deep breath of air, Aradia let a nervous laugh escape her. What the hell is happening to me? As she made her way down the deserted sidewalk, Aradia took note of the trees, their leaves just beginning to bud. In several areas, tulips were standing at their peak, proud to be on the earth. At least someone is she thought as she trudged further down the sidewalk. Thoughts of her upcoming title defense and what kind of leads her father actually had passed through her until a new, more important one, occurred to her. Stopping dead in her tracks, Aradia found herself paralyzed once more. That whisper in the alley? that voice that chilled her bone to its very core? that voice had not belonged to her father. The voice was of a higher tone as compared to her father’s more baritone timbre. Her eyes widened in astonishment. Someone else was there? Her mind raced, knowing that the voice was familiar and that it had known her name. Who did the voice belong to? Worry engulfed her, but she knew there was no way of knowing if the voice was even real. She had not seen anyone else in the alley and her father would have surely seen another figure when he was there with her. It was almost as though he had a sixth sense. Bringing a hand to her forehead, she massaged the area between her eyes, hoping to relieve tension. Her life was falling apart around her and there was no way to pick up the pieces of shattered glass. The only way to begin trying was to take her fathers advice and stick to Global? at least for awhile. Once things began to fall back into place, she would begin an investigation of her own. Satisfied with this decision, Aradia resumed her walk, desperately trying to remember where she parked. Swearing to herself that this would be the last time she concerned herself with the situation, she lets out a long, deep breath. Finding her car, she reaches for her keys as she clears her mind of everything weighing her down. Friday’s match would be an important one and her mind must be cleared. Yet, her father was still on her mind. Everything would be alright; she just needed to calm down. Sighing, she shook her head and began to get into her vehicle. As the engine started and she put the car into drive, one last thought crossed her mind before she wiped the slate clean: Be careful, daddy.
