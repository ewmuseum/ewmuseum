The tales of Xavier Crysto.

30 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 30th, 2004. 'Xavier?' I called out, seeking my tutor in the Romanian inn. Yet he was nowhere to be found, the rooms were empty, barren save for their beds and ancient tapestries. The attic is now where I lingered, searching for Xavier. 'Xav?' I asked again, truly perplexed by his disappearance. Glancing around the attic I saw no sign of him, even the small bed in the attic was devoid of his presence for he had vacated this bed in the early hours of the morning. Everything about the room seemed the same only something was different…something that I couldn't quite place my hand on. The spider webs, which sent chills of arachnophobiac fear running up and down my spine, the crystal ball that gleamed as beautifully as I had ever seen the sacred orb shine. The leather bound, tear stained journal, the small window at the top of the attic in which light made its entry casting forth the morning glare throughout the entire attic. Something wasn't right about this…something was here that hadn't been here before. And suddenly I placed my finger on it…it was the journal! Never before had mine eyes been lain upon this book. Making slow steps across the floor my Chuck Taylor's softly patted their canvas sole against the hard wood, making no noise as I reached the table. Admiring the aged leather binding of the book I ran my fingers across it, greatly intrigued by its presence. 'What's this...?' I mused aloud, to myself. Wiping the seat of my static style jeans I sat down upon the oaken stool that faced the table. Lifting this book into my hands I carefully examined it. My eyes picked up everything…tearstains upon the forefront, a frayed stitch in the binding and a scratch upon the leather of the back cover. Pulling my left foot up, I rested it upon the bar beneath the stool, my right foot remaining on the floor. Eyeing the book cautiously my mind began to race. 'I wonder what could be in there…' I said in a tone barely audible to even myself, as my bluish gray eyes carefully scrutinized the book. 'Don’t touch it…' Whispered the voice of common sense and logic. 'But don't you want to know what is in there?' Replied the voice of curiosity. Battling myself the thumb on my left hand moved across the book and almost as if by it's own choice, opened the cover of this journal. The first page was almost blank save for a name and date. Xavier Crysto. October 26th, 1492. My eyebrows leapt vertically as I read this; I knew that Xavier clamed to have been cursed by unwanted immortality, yet…could this be true? I had dismissed his claims as crazy ramblings from a psychotic being, yet perhaps there was valid proof in this book. 'I must go on…' I told myself, curiosity taking place of common sense. Flipping over to the next page, the brittle and yellowed paper fluttered to a page full of black ink, tear stains and scrawled hand writing that was barely interpretable due to the pain and sorrow that this page had no doubt been written in. Water marks indicating tears of centuries ago left their impression upon this page, smearing the ink in a few places and showing the unbearable agony that the writer had been in when he penned these words. Brushing my shaggy black locks from my eyes I allowed the book to rest upon my left knee as I began to read this pained writing of days gone by. My God, It seems as if thou hast forsaken me O' Lord. Such bitter wailing's rise from within and I, as a human, have not the ability to silence the demons screaming within my soul. Only thou, sweet Father can silence my breaking heart. Screams of pain emit from within, echoing throughout my being and projecting through my appearance. Mine eyes have shed tears until no further tears could be shed, my cheeks swelled with the pain of sobbing. My heart has been poured out before thee O' God. Thou knowest what evil has occurred here today, for when I returned home from laboring in the field I found my father, my mother, my brother and even my sister, still in her youth, slain at the edge of a sword. O' Father, it is my fervent prayer that you Lord allow me to seek justice upon my behalf. For it is my solemn vow that at my hand, the murderers shall suffer the same violent ending that fell upon my loved ones. The first two paragraphs stained with tears comprised almost half of the page, and at the bottom two fingerprints of blood ended the page. My eyes widened as I turned the page…only to be interrupted by a voice. 'In most cultures reading the diary of a man such as myself would be considered taboo.' Snapped Xavier as he stepped from the shadows causing my heart to leap into my throat as I gasped in shock. He bore a sober expression on his wrinkled face as his gnarled fingers encircled the walking stick in his right hand. His attire was his ever-traditional black cloak. 'In the American culture it's just considered downright rude.' I retorted in shallow breaths, as I slowly began to regain my composure. 'Then what is your excuse my child?' Xavier asked me, lifting his lips into a cruel snarl. 'Curiosity?' I replied with a shrug, feigning ignorance. 'You know what they say, curiosity killed the cat…and you don't have nine lives.' Hissed Xavier, a cold and chilling tone in his voice that made my skin crawl. 'What are you suggesting?' I inquisitively asked, lowering my left foot to the floor in a more stable position, almost worried that I may need to defend myself. Xavier smiled, his white teeth flashing, as his cold blue eyes seemed to penetrate my soul. He didn't answer, but avoided the question entirely. 'If you seek the truth about my life, who I am and what I am…then perhaps you should ask Me.' 'Ask you? You've been a living enigma since the day I met you…' I countered defensively. 'Do you really want to know the story behind those words?' Xavier asked, pointing a bony finger at the book I held in my hands. 'Yes…' I responded hesitantly, almost fearfully. 'Close your eyes.' Xavier said, his voice indicating that this was an order and not a suggestion. But…' I began, but was cut off before I could finish. 'Close them.' He ordered once again. Bowing my head, my vision crossed my orange button up H. Hilfiger shirt and then danced over the pages of the diary one last time as I lowered my eyelids and slipped away into a world of darkness. ---- Beyond my eyelids the world opened anew, only it was a far different place at a far different time. I had left reality and the present only to travel back into centuries long expired and a culture that was only seen in Shakespeare plays and history books. I seemed to almost float in the air as my panoramic view perfectly made out the vivid details of the field in which a young man toiled tirelessly. He was dressed in clothes dating back to the late 1400's and appeared to be a peasant, and a hard working one at that. Dirt and grime had settled on his face, and his brown pants and white shirt were also soiled with sweat. His heavy boots were no doubt hot and itchy yet this hard working peasant continued on in his labor. With a grunt he pulled back his sickle and swung it violently slicing through the golden wheat in this field, and as it fell to the ground he smiled merrily. Kneeling down he procured an armful of wheat and deposited the crop in a brown burlap sack that hung from his shoulder, extending to his waist. The young man was obviously a hard worker yet he looked as if he had great potential in all facets of life. His smile was contagious as it spread across his handsome face, a face marked by shining blue eyes that couldn't be dimmed by the dirt surrounding them. His hair was blonde and straight falling over his shoulders and across his face. Keeping his sickle in hand he swung again and the blade sliced through the wheat once more, and again the young man flashed his charming smile as more wheat fell. He was no doubt hard at work, as he went about his fathers business. After all it was never easy to put food on the table when you lived as a poor farmer, yet by this young mans athletic frame it was quite obvious that he was not malnourished, nor did he appear to be a failure at raising crops. With his jubilant smile still adorning his handsome features the young man took what I had witnessed as his third swing, yet by his sweat stained appearance it had obviously been a much longer day than that. As he swung the wheat fell for the third time and once more the young man leaned down, letting the sickle dangle in his left hand as he scooped up the wheat and dropped it into his bag that was now on the verge of spilling over with abundance. 'I guess I'll need a new bag.' The young man proclaimed joyfully, apparently satisfied with the progress that he was making on this blistering day of late summer. It was a short walk for the young man as he turned on his heel and began his journey towards a stack of burlap bags filled with wheat. The bags were on the outskirts of the field and next to these bags grew plentiful green grass, grass that a large gelding horse had decided to consume. The horse had not been tied up, but rather left to graze on his own accord. The horse was truly a thing of beauty, brown with white hooves and a solitary white stripe beginning at the base of his head and racing down his nose to the large nostrils at the end. The horse was unsaddled, yet he wore a bridle showing evidence that he had recently been served as transportation. The reigns of the bridle hung loosely and drug along the ground near the horse's head. The horse munched the grass, at ease and relaxed not knowing that the world as he knew it was going to be changed forever. The horse raised its head at the approach of its master and in turn, the smile of the young man grew even wider brightening his sweat streaked face. His big blue eyes met the large brown eyes of the horse and both beast and master seemed to share joy in seeing one another. The young man grunted slightly and the smile vanished from his face as he dropped the heavy bag of wheat next to the stockpile of similar bags. Patting the brown bag gently, the young man brushed his golden locks from his eyes and smiled at the horse lovingly. 'Tobias…you may very well be the dumbest creature God ever created but I still love you!' Said the young man, a joyful grin on his face, as he stepped up to the horse and softly rubbed it's nose. The young man seemed to derive great pleasure from the horse as he leaned his head against the horse's front flank letting the solid muscles of the horse cushion his skull. Closing his eyes momentarily a strange smell wafted through the air before crossing the young man's nostrils…smoke! His eyes suddenly lifting and jerking his head up alertly the young man scanned the horizon looking for any sign of smoke…and there he found it, to the north, through the trees. 'No…' The young man gasped in disbelief, dropping the sickle that had still been in his hand. It seemed to fall in slow motion before it hit the grass softly. To the north, beyond the trees and hills lay the only existence this young man had ever known. It was the village of Broken Willow, a town that was miniscule by any standard. It served as home to a population of two hundred and eighteen people, most of them hard working farmers and laymen, all of them honest and vigilant, strong in the Catholic faith and patriotic to a fault. At this hour it was common knowledge that all the men of Broken Willow would be at work while the women worked around the house and the children played in the dusty streets. Most of these laborers were at their farms leaving the town unprotected, save for the few merchants and elderly men who kept watch over Broken Willow. 'Spare them Father…' The young man gasped in audible prayer, already assuming the worst had happened. Leaping onto the back of his horse he slapped it across the front left flank and in response the horse lifted his head. Snatching the reigns in both hands the young man kicked the horse and instantly the horse took off into a trot, yet another kick, this time harder sent the horse into a gallop and a third kick served as motivation for the beautiful animal to break into full stride. The once joyful expression on the young farmers face had transformed into a wide eyed, panic stricken look of terror as all of the color had drained from his cheeks leaving him pale and scared. Staring straight ahead the bareback rider navigated his horse through the trees as the animal's hooves rained down upon the well packed terra firma. This young man already feared what he would find once he escaped these rural surroundings and reached the village of Broken Willow. Would his fears be confirmed? Large hordes of bandits were notorious throughout the land yet, surely they wouldn't attack a village such as Broken Willow! Broken Willow was unlike the other towns that had been raided, it was much smaller and laden with poverty, the farmers all lived off of the crops they grew and money was rare. The town's economy was a fragile ecosystem that could never survive a raid, yet it wasn't the economy of this town that concerned our young hero on horseback, but rather the populous of Broken Willow and the danger that they may be in. Thankfully for this young man his house was on the edge of town, he would reach it at almost any second, and in his mind he could already picture the damage that had been inflicted upon the village…the damage that he prayed hadn't taken place. Breaking the crest of a hill the young man saw flames licking high in the air as they shot up from the town…Broken Willow was almost in sight, and the sight was sure to be an ugly one. The stout hooves of Tobias, the horse, tattooed the sod as the beast raced on, finally crossing over the last hill that stood between Broken Willow and the wheat field. As Broken Willow came into view, tears struck the eyes of our young farmer as he saw the destruction that sat before him. Reigning in his horse, the farmer dismounted quickly as he leapt from the horses back and landed on his feet, running from the very second he landed. The house of his family stood before him in a shambles. The sodden shack was in an order of disarray, the oaken door had been kicked off of its hinges and the thatch roof made of the best straw was burning uncontrollably. 'No…' He gasped, tears streaming down his face, mingling with the sweat and dirt. Rushing forward he stepped over the door that had been torn down and raced into the house. He fell to his knees in stunned horror as he saw exactly what he had feared… On the floor lay the mangled body of his crippled father, a dagger driven into his heart. His father had been easy prey due to the right hand that he lost, long ago in battle, yet despite his distortion he had no doubt struggled until he fell to a ghastly death. 'Father…' The young man cried out, a teardrop hitting the wooden floor. Fearing the worst, the young man turned his weeping eyes yet again to see the body of his mother in a corner. In her frail hands she clutched a broomstick, she had fought them off as long as she could yet the deep cut across her throat had proved she was matched against a superior warrior. With his heart crushed and his body shaking with deep sobs the young man turned his eyes once more to the disastrous scene that surrounded him. 'Katherine!' Screamed the young man, crawling across the floor to where he saw his sister lying on her back her gentle blue eyes still wide open with terror and her face pale with death. 'Katherine…' He sobbed as his eyes examined the bloodstains in her beautiful blonde hair, bloodstains that had been put there her cranium was introduced to a mallet. Pulling his sister, Katherine, into his arms the young man sobbed. Underneath her fingernails was blood, signs of how she had fought against the attackers, trying not to keep them from stealing her possessions but rather from stealing her life. Locking his arms tightly around his beloved sister the young man squeezed her close, burying her lovely face in his chest. Weeping uncontrollably the young man fell to the floor with Katherine still clutched in his arms. 'Who did this?' He asked rhetorically. 'Who did this?!' He asked in repetition, his voice now filled with both anger and sorrow. Letting Katherine slip from his arms the young man stands to his feet and turns his imploring eyes to the burning ceiling of the ramshackle hut. 'Father…help me to find the men who did this…' ---- 'That is my story…I am the young man you saw.' Xavier softly said, snapping me back to reality. 'Xavier…I…' I tried to say something yet words failed me at this moment. 'And thus is the beginning of a hero's life…' Said Xavier, a lone tear escaping from his eye and running down his cheek.
