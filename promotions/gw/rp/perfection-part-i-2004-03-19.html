Perfection [Part I]

19 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3.19.04 Perfection is something that we've been told for years is impossible to attain. That no matter how hard we worked, or how hard we tried, we'd never reach that level. Why? Because 'nobody's perfect'. I'd heard that quote at least a couple of hundred times, and I still thought it was bullshit. For my entire life, I'd been a finely tuned machine of sorts that was set with a goal to achieve. Once that goal is achieved, I move on to the next one. I was hell bent on achieving that perfection that I'd been told for so long I could never have. I finished off my set of decline bench presses, and set the bar back on the rack. I sat up on the bench and wiped at the sweat that beaded down my forehead. I felt that ever familiar sensation of a mucle being worked to it's brink. There's just nothing that can compare to that. It's better than sex. I got up from the bench press and began to walk around the basement. I had several fitness products in that basement. It was my own personal gym. My own world. I walked over to a bar I had suspended from the ceiling that I could do pull ups on. I pulled my chin up over the bar, and saw a piece of paper I had scribbled a quote onto taped onto the wall. 'A man can be beaten, but not defeated.' Ernest Hemingway had written that. I had put that there so I would read it every time I worked out. The quote was pure inspiration for my quest. It was a constant reminder of what it was that I was training for. I did a set of chin ups with ease, and thought better of my initial idea of doing another. They were too easy right now. They wouldn't do me any good. Instad, I opted to go upstairs. Jameson greeted me at the top of the stairs with a freshly filled water bottle. His very well tailored black suit fit him well, and he physically looked like a very professional and responsible elderly man. His eyes told a different story, however. There was a certain grayish clouding to them. He smiled weakly as he raised the water bottle to me. He always seemed to know the perfect timing for everything. 'Thanks, Jameson,' I said. 'Exhilerating workout, Mr. Idol?' Jameson asked as he handed me my water. 'Nothing extraordinary. Any new calls?' 'Yes, Mr. Idol. Just one.' 'Yeah? Who?' It wasn't too often that I had any calls other than business, but this was a different encounter. Usually, I was far too busy working out and training to be bothered with such unimportant things such as personal phone calls. 'A miss Julie Wing, sir,' Jameson said. His eyes blinked a few too many times. He knew that I would be upset. 'God damnit, Jameson!' I roared. 'I told you not to answer her calls!' 'I'm deeply sorry, sir.' 'You damn well should be!' I pushed by Jameson with my water bottle now in my hand. I couldn't believe he had answered her call. He should've known better. If he hadn't done so much for the Idol family over the years, I'd have fired him a long time ago. I walked up a second flight of stairs quickly. I entered the upstairs bathroom, and turned on the shower. Looking in the mirror, I studied my reflection. I'd always been told I had a far too serious face for my age. My face was incredibly sharp, and great for profile. Had I stuck around in Hollywood as an actor, chances are that I'd be a movie star. I had the All American look, and I lived the All American life. Why did I choose wrestling over acting? I honestly never could figure that out myself. I chose wrestling because of this burning instinct inside of me that told me to pursue it. No. Not just pursue it, but dominate it. I let out a sigh as I checked to see how hot the water flowing from the shower nozzle was. With the water being at a good temperature, I removed my clothes. Right before I stepped into the shower, I remembered the call from Julie... and realized I'd have to return it.
