Heavenly Shades of... Madness

6 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/06/2004 The chill of October was in the air, the crescent moon looming large in the sky as he crept across the well-tended lawn. He knew where he was going, but even if he hadn’t, the moonlight-silvered glow would have been adequate to light his way. He paused against the brick fa’ade of the guard shack, casually lighting a cigarette. The shack was empty, not surprising since this place hadn’t been used in years. The iron bars of the fence were rusted and warped, casting eerie shadows, providing little protection for the items stored within. For some reason, only the hopelessly insane came here, the cast offs of society. Homeless. Vietnam vets. Shattered minds. Perhaps those were the only souls who could stand this bizarre chaos. The fragments of shanties and gaming booths stuck up from the spongy ground like broken teeth overgrown in places with a creeping darkness. Moss. Mildew. Darkness. Twisted bits of metal lay strewn everywhere, sheer chaos, and what had once been a roller coaster was now a tumbled jumble of tracks. Amid this desecration, was the leering clown’s face, still oddly intact. The funhouse. This is where he paused, briefly, looking up at the sky, his hair tossed about by the wind, caressing his face the way a lover would, if he had one who could bear to look upon him now. He stooped, stepping between the clown’s parted crimson lips, muttering to himself. “Beware the Jabberwock, my son” the jaws that bite, the claws that catch.” A snippet of nonsensical poesy, made ominous just by that leering, almost demonic face. The mouth seemed to swallow him whole, the darkness closing behind him, rendering him invisible. The night was utterly silent. Even the wind dared not blow in this place. ---- This place was familiar, not as disturbing to me, perhaps as it was to you. I had played here as a child, and come here as a teen to seek silence and solitude. Now, I found myself drawn to the place like a moth to the flame. I could feel the pull, as strong as it ever was. As a child I had feared that clown, its eyes were so much like my fathers. Icy blue, filled with madness, a sort of maniacal glee. Now, I could see it was nothing more than old paint, paint that over the years hadn’t faded. Even as I’d aged, and become something more, this place was much the same as I had left it. I could see the buildings beyond the fence, peeking above the hedge that grew along the backside of this property. It should have been leveled sometime ago, but then, I had squandered my inheritance on this place. It was mine, one of many such expenditures that kept me entertained. My father would have killed me for such a foolish waste. Ah, but he was dead now, so it mattered not. I walked along the moldering carpet, but what I was really doing was retracing my old steps. I passed by the homeless sleeping amid their boxes and lousy mattresses, taken no doubt from the neighboring university’s garbage. They paid me no mind, to them I was invisible. An outsider, who meant nothing. Most of them were too far gone to even care? looking for Thanatos to cash in their tickets early. But then, nobody had told these poor souls that only the good die young. They would waste away forever here, amid these dark walls, where the light never reached. Some things were better left to the dark. It was easier to imagine something more pleasant. Dante wasn’t dead; he was here, the lunatic king atop his throne. Yes, I had come to pay a visit to my erstwhile brother? ---- Somewhere in the dark he felt a quickening, his pulse pounding just a little louder, his blood surging a little faster in his veins. Rivers of red. Flowing, ever flowing? never stopping. His eyes were wide, and staring from amid his dirt caked face, opaque with the glaze of madness. Once, he had been handsome, a pretty boy really? now he was a sad sight indeed. He scrabbled across the room on all fours, muttering in a singsong, the words growing in volume. ??beware the JubJub bird, and shun the frumious Bandersnatch?” He clambered across the filth on the floor, a grin splitting his chapped lips, blood flowing over his chin into his wild beard. He laughed, and clapped his hands together in childish glee, and that was when he heard the footfalls, coming ever closer. After a moment, they stopped, silence closing in around Dante. He could hear nothing but his own respiration. Inhale. Exhale. Inhale. Exhale. “Hello, brother.” Jackson drew deeply on the cigarette in his mouth, making it flare orange. For a moment, it illuminated his impeccable features, his expression was hard, his face carved from granite. Dante chortled, and held out his hands like a child begging for a treat. Jackson flicked the cigarette into the darkness, and then snapped on a small penlight, pointing it at the floor. For a second, it bounced off the silvered walls, distorting the image. Mirrors. Hundreds of them lined this space, a maze of madness, each reflecting some twisted parody of the two men. Fat. Skinny. Tall. Short. Dante held out his hands still, waving them now, his puppy dog eyes fixed on Jackson, the sores that covered his skin now visible in the feeble light. Weeping gashes covered his arms, his body streaked with filth that was best left unmentioned. How Jackson could stand the stench was a mystery, but he did, looking down impassively at the ruin of his brother. Dante’s white hair was matted and ragged, huge patches missing where it seemed to have been torn right out of his scalp. “I brought you something, Dante. A present. Would you like it?” Jackson’s voice was carefully neutral, his eyes fixed on those of his brother. “Yes. Yes. Yessss. Please.” Dante’s words were sibiliant, a lisping, hissing tone of madness. Jackson withdrew a black trash bag from the inside of his jacket, and held it out, tantalizing. “Then you may have it, if you answer me one question first. What did you tell Gabriel?” “Tell” Tell? I told him nothing. He took. Took. Took. No tell.” Jackson crouched down, placing a gloved finger beneath Dante’s head, tilting his face up, looking him in the eyes. “What did he take, you little fuck?” “All of it. Everything. Couldn’t stop him. Killed me, he would have. Why won’t you” Peace. Peach. Just want that. Sweetness. Death.” “Goddamn it. I knew letting you live was a mistake. You’d be better off rotting in hell, serving my master. An error I should correct right now. Give me one reason why I shouldn’t, you miserable little worm.” Dante snickered, the sound disturbing. “You need me. You need me. You need me. I keep him away from Reaver. I keep it happy for you. Without me, chaos.” Jackson kicked Dante hard in the chest, sending him skidding back against the pile of broken glass that served as his makeshift throne. “Something I would gladly risk to be free of this bargain. Don’t forget that, brother. You are expendable.” Dante shook his head, a split second of lucidity flashing in his clouded gaze. “Not expendable. You would have killed me already if I was. Were. Not.” Jackson shook his head, refusing to admit the truth. He tossed the bag at Dante, and watched impassively as the broken man tore at the plastic wrapping, salivating like a starved dog. Inside was a charred animal. It looked like a rabbit, or perhaps a medium sized chicken, impossible to tell in the sketchy light. The stench of overdone flesh filled the small space as Dante tore into the offering with gusto, smacking his lips and growling like a wild animal. Jackson turned away, leaving the place of madness behind, his expression revolted. He had the answer he had come for, he didn’t need to linger. He turned in the doorway, casting one last look at the ravenous madman as he reached inside his jacket. He flicked something through the air, and smiled as the sound of something landing came to his ears. Twin plinks. Two coins. ---- I wondered then, like I usually do after seeing the ruination that I had wrought upon those I loved, just what awaited me on my judgment day. Would I be judged accordingly? Or was I above those laws? I had committed sins, and I had exacted revenge. I had killed. And I had spared. Balance. I sought only to bring order to the chaos around me, nothing more. It all came back to her. It pained me to feel that empty ache. I stepped back out into the wind, letting the air touch my face. It was a soft touch, something that made my skin prickle. Once she’d touched me like this. Kissed my eyelids. Run her fingers through my hair. Was I destined to be alone forever? I wanted to hang on to the past. Was that so wrong of me? I had known love, and in some ways, I knew it still. ---- Beyond the funhouse was something else. A source of light. Broken strands of tiny bulbs flickered in the gloom, almost as though beckoning him. The carousel. At one time it was a thing of beauty. A grand spectacle that dazzled children and adults alike? and now, it was nothing more than refuse. Time marches on. Its warped, twisted frame invokes an overwhelming feeling of sadness and a creeping sense of wrongness. Even Jackson is forced to avert his eyes for a moment, feeling that yawning abyss of depression that looms around its edges. He cannot, however, keep his eyes away for long. It had a way of doing that. A subtle power. The entire contraption was leaning to one side, almost drunkenly? almost like a hooker thrusting out a hip. “You like what you see, baby?” the words seemed to echo in the night air as he took a few more steps, breathing shallowly now. It was precisely how he remembered it. The mirrors and painted scenes that once decorated the middle are grim parodies of their former beauty. The mirrors are twisted, pitted, Jackson’s reflection grossly, darkly distorted, the visage of some sort of demon from the blackest pits of hell. Sometimes the truth hurt. He tore his eyes away from the mirror, and instead focused on the pastoral meadow painted beside it. The once bright, wildflower-filled meadows in the paintings were faded to varying shades of brown and black. What should have been charming scenes appear instead to be the horrific wastelands left behind after a nuclear holocaust. Children that in a lifetime long ago frolicked with joy with their heads thrown back in silent laughter now seemed to be screaming in unbridled horror. Their bodies seemed twisted, horribly mutated. Jack and Jill fell down the hill? screaming all the way to the bottom. Twisted and broken strands of lights hung from the rotting canopy, swaying lazily even though no breeze was present. The night is deathly still, as though it is waiting for something. He walked slowly around the desecration, looking for something. Several of the rusted poles were barren, their noble steeds and colorful giraffes long since vanished. Burned for firewood by the madmen who roamed these relics. All were gone, save one. The one horse that still remained was at one time one of the grandest sights to behold. Hand carved from fine wood and stained to a deep chestnut color, a glorious, magnificent beast. He was majestic; his mane tossing backwards towards the rider as though blown back by a strong wind? proud head held high, his strong shoulders rigid and defiant. One leg rose as though prancing haughtily, forever frozen in a pose of youthful rebellion. But the crowning achievement of this beautiful creature were the glass eyes. Once a lovely and soulful shade of deep brown, these eyes captivated and enthralled women and children alike. For somehow the eyes appeared to radiate some light, as though the horse was truly alive. If you listened hard enough, you could hear the snort and nickering? you could almost feel the wind that rippled through that glossy mane. But alas, the years had taken their toll on this proud stallion. The once glossy wood was cracked and faded with age. The once proudly raised front hoof was broken off, leaving a weatherworn stump. But those eyes, those eyes, the portals to the soul? hadn’t changed. In the twilight, those eyes still glowed with life. Faded to a dull blood red, those eyes still shone with a light that was chilling indeed. But the once beautiful and proud gaze was something much more sinister. The once placid expression on the horse’s face had turned to a horrific grimace. And still those eyes shone with that red light, drawing his attention. “Hello, old friend,” he whispered as he reached out to touch a switch on the ground beside him. “I see the years have been as kind to you as they have to me.” Suddenly, the lights flickered and a few more bulbs came to life, shining weakly in the lengthening shadows. The calliope music started as the carousel began to revolve slowly. As the carousel rotated, gaining momentum, the scratchy track also gained speed, until it turned into a sound not unlike the scream of a thousand souls burning in hell. Grating. Piercing. The sound is overwhelming, but the most horrible part of this spectacle is that of the lone horse. That lonely, sad, rider-less horse of a long forgotten era? and those eyes. The red pools reflecting nothing but madness and decay. As the horror-go-round continued to revolve, everything blurred, everything save those demonic eyes. Jackson made a small sound, low in his throat, and turned his head away, muttering to himself. “Heavenly shades of night are falling, it’s twilight time. They say the last hours before dawn are the darkest” perhaps the last hours of our lives are the darkest of the soul.” He pushed the button again, and now that the contraption was slowing again, the music was recognizable as the tune he had just mentioned. He shuddered as though suddenly chilled, and rubbed his arms through the leather jacket he wore. He spoke again, absently, his words torn away by the wind as it rose again, nipping at his exposed flesh. “They called him “Dark Star”. In an era almost forgotten, people traveled from far and wide to see the beauty of the hand carved stallion, the crowning glory of one of the world’s most beautiful carousels. And hand carved he was indeed, made with passion by a man obsessed with his work. Everyone commented on the lifelike quality of the horse’s soulful eyes. Local legends say that the creator used magic to capture a real soul within the horse’s wooden frame, thus the reason behind the life-like eyes. And it was those eyes that eventually drove its creator to madness. Those haunting eyes. As the years passed, those eyes became increasingly frightful, as though every evil wrought in the world had found a resting place within this shell of wood. Eventually children stopped wanting to ride on the proud stallion. People stopped coming here altogether. Those eyes. Chilling aren’t they? No longer filled with the dancing laughter of happy children. No, forgetfulness has caused decay to seep in to every fiber of his being. And eyes that were once filled with joy and peace are now the baleful gaze of a raging lunatic. Look. Take the image in. For this is the root of the lesson here. Ignorance and denial can lead to only one end, madness.” He stepped forward, laying a hand on the rippling mane. His shoulders trembled slightly, his head bent as he stood there, simply breathing for a moment. He could feel the cold of the wood beneath his hand, cracked with age, old and dry? but he could also feel a heat. There was something else inside, even after all these years. The horse seemed to radiate darkness and decay. He chuckled softly, and withdrew his hand, continuing his monologue in a voice filled with rebuke. “At one time I was in self-denial. That era has come to an end. At one time I believed myself to be above average, remarkable, notable even. An up and comer” and who was I kidding. The best and brightest? cut down in my prime. Perhaps that was true, but it doesn’t have to be. It is, after all, all about perception. I should have listened to those voices long ago, for they were always speaking the truth; I was just too caught up in myself to realize it. But, unlike our chilling companion here,” he thumped the horse with one hand, causing it to wobble and wiggle on the rotted pole. “Denial. Delusion. Unlike him, I’m unwilling to continue along that route. Ignorance and forgetfulness lie on a road that seems like the easy path, but leads to ruin. At the end of that road is an end I’d rather not come to terms with. No. I have been there, ignorance may seem like bliss, but it isn’t. It’s a lot more like those funhouse mirrors, distorting reality. I thought I was in love” I lived and breathed her presence? and it was the death of me. And still, I cannot let her go. Madness! I will not deny it any longer. She has a hold over me, and I am powerless to break it. I will not fight it. I’d rather accept my darkness, my true nature, give in to the power it has over me, and continue to tread the rough road, the one that walks a fine line between sanity and madness. And that’s the simple truth. Too bad he couldn’t have realized it sooner.” He stood there, looking down at the pitiful remains of the once noble steed. For a moment, he was utterly silent, still as a statue. Quite suddenly, with a primal yell, he reared back, and kicked the horse with all his might. The figure broke away from the brittle pole and flew backward towards the rotten center of the carousel, breaking into several pieces as he goes. The body smashed through the support of the center spindle, and the entire roof caved in, falling in one crash. The head of the horse, however, narrowly missed Jackson, who ducked at the last moment. It landed behind him, and he turned his back on the collapse, turning his eyes to the stallion’s face. The twisted visage seems to soften, the snaggle-toothed grimace softening into a peaceful smile, the evil light seems to fade, and suddenly the horse is beautiful again, the face again that of a noble steed. Jackson turned around, walking back towards the fence. He picked up a gasoline can, and returned to the site of the destruction, liberally dousing the shattered remains. He stood there, breathing the heady fumes, his hair whipping around his face. A muscle in his jaw jumped, betraying his tension, and then he reached into his pocket, withdrawing his crumpled pack of Camels. He shook one out with practiced precision, and lit it with a flourish of his Zippo. He smoked it silently, his eyes fixed on the pile of debris. Without a further word, he flicked it, and the entire pile went up with a loud whoosh, the heat searing his eyes, making them ache. The entire place would burn. Let the madness here consume itself. He smiled fleetingly, and for a brief moment, his face appeared handsome, although impenetrably cold. ??and the mome raths outgrabe? goodbye, Dante? give my regards to Charon. I paid your toll, brother. Remember that, above all else? this I do out of love? out of remembrance of the way things were. Forgive me, that’s all I ask.” The words were muffled, his voice strained as he looked away from the inferno, trying to hide the tears in his eyes. And with that, he simply walked away; turning his back on the chaos his hands had wrought. Sometimes chaos was necessary to restore the order of things. It was, after all, all about balance.
