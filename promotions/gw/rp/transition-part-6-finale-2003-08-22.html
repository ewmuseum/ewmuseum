Transition, part 6 (Finale)

22 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 22nd, 2003 I made a beeline for Danjuro, as the other six Thunders engaged his hired goons. 'Just you and me,' said Danjuro, leveling the ladder at me, 'exactly how I wanted it.' 'Sure you did,' I said, as I sidestepped a thrust of the ladder, 'that's why you brought the pajama boys.' 'They were just meant to be a distraction. You belong to me, Rokku.' Danjuro swung the ladder down, hard. I did a back handspring out of the way, and it hit the concrete floor with a resounding clang. 'Yeah, well,' I said, as I ran up the ladder towards him, 'they didn't do so well, did they?' He let go of the ladder, but too late. I sprung off it, catching him with a shining wizard. He crumpled to the ground, but pulled himself back to his feet, groggily. 'Look around, Danjuro. Your boys aren't doing so well at all.' Dazed, Danjuro looked around the lobby, and saw what I'd seen. Many of the so-called 'ninja' were out cold on the floor already, taken apart by the Thunders. And all around us more fell: Kenta delivered a palm strike that sent one ninja flying into another. Yotogi had a pair of ninja in a bearhug, slowly squeezing the life out of them. Tsuneo held one ninja by the legs, and used him like a club to battering others. Sakura launched into a suicide plancha off the roof of the van, smashing three of the ninja into the concrete floor -- then sprung to her feet, ready to continue the fight. Itoju struck with precise, vicious blows to nerve clusters, leaving those who didn't black out to wish they did. And Yoshiko was like water, moving from enemy to enemy with the grace of a dancer, and smashing them into the ground. Danjuro paled. 'This is over, Danjuro,' I said, placing my hand on his shoulder, 'Walk away. Your father might be crazy enough to order something like this over business, but you don't have to be crazy enough to be involved in it.' Danjuro sighed, and his shoulders slumped. Then he spun around, his face a mask of hatred, and slammed his fist into my jaw. 'You know NOTHING about my father!' he screamed. Danjuro charged me, ready to spear me into the wall -- but I dropped, sliding between his legs, and yanking them out from under him. Toppling over, Danjuro slammed face- first into the ladder. Sighing, I started walking away from him. 'Damn, man,' I said, shaking my head sadly, 'I gave you a chance at the easy way out. You are REALLY dumb.' 'Sh-shut uuup-p-p-p?' stuttered Danjuro, trying to haul himself to his feet again. 'No, really,' I continued, 'you are seriously dumb. You're not even smart enough to stay down so I don't have to hit you any more. It's a new level of stupid.' 'F-fu-fu-fuc?y?' 'Sorry kid. Game over.' I turned, and ran towards Danjuro again. He swayed, keeping himself up through sheer bull-headedness. I centered myself, and leapt at him, putting all my frustration, all my power, everything I had into a very angry Emperor's Right Hand. My fist smashed into Danjuro's nose, and he crashed to the floor, lying motionless. As I shook out the numbness in my hand, I looked around. The 'ninja' were almost all disposed of, the last few of them being chased off by Tsuneo. And though there were some bruises, scratches, and whatnot, all in all, the Thunders were in pretty good shape. And I had an idea. 'Hey guys!' I shouted, beckoning the lot of them over to me, now that the fight was done, 'I think it's time we go have a talk with the boss.' *** 'Afternoon, Satomi-chan,' I said, smiling brightly. Satomi smiled back, at first -- but when she looked up from her desk, her smile melted away. Maybe it was the fact that I was a little roughed up. Or maybe it was because I had six angry-looking wrestlers with me. Maybe it was because I had her boss' son's unconscious carcass over my shoulder. Who knows? 'Is Genbu-san available,' I asked, 'we'd really like to talk to him.' Satomi looked on in horror as blood mixed with sweat dripped off Danjuro's forehead, and onto her desk. 'G-G, Genbu-san requested that he not be interrupted for the rest of the day, Rokku-san.' 'Oh,' I replied, frowning. 'Well, we hate to disturb him, but we've really got to go see him right now, okay? I'll be back in a bit, if you want to go get pocky or something with me on your lunch break.' Whistling happily, I strode off down the hall towards Genbu's office, the rest of the Thunders falling in line behind me. 'Rokku, you dog!' muttered Tsuneo, grinning. 'What is she, twenty-two?' 'Nineteen,' I replied, as I knocked on Genbu's double-doors. 'Not bad, man' said Yotogi, 'not bad at all.' Sakura and Yoshiko managed to roll their eyes in synch. Itoju smirked, and Kenta showed complete disinterest in the conversation. I knocked on Genbu's door again. Still no response. 'Tsuneo, he doesn't seem to be coming to the door. We should go in and see if he's okay.' 'Yeah,' said Tsuneo, taking a few steps back from the door, 'we definitely should.' Tsuneo rushed at the doors, shattering the lock with a powerful kick. The doors burst open, and there was Yoritomo Genbu, with a look of absolute shock on his face. 'Tsuneo,' he shouted, 'what the hell is the meaning of this?' 'My idea,' I said, stepping out from behind Tsuneo, and tossing Danjuro's bulk on the floor. 'I just wanted to have a little talk, and return something of yours.' 'You're insane! I'm calling security!' I tilted my head towards Itoju, and he was across the room in an instant. He jabbed Genbu's wrist with two fingers, and the pig's hand went limp. The phone tumbled from Genbu's fingers before he could even start to dial. Still smiling, I strode across the office and sat on Genbu's desk. 'Genbu,' I said, 'buddy. I'm not the one who hired thugs to attack a former employee, so lets not throw words like insane around, okay? Now listen up. I'm tired of your shit. So you're going to cut it out, or we're going to make your life a living hell.' 'I very much doubt that,' said Genbu, scowling, 'because by the end of the day, all seven of you are going to be in jail.' 'Great idea,' said Yoshiko, smiling sweetly, 'have us arrested. Then we can tell the police about all the crap you pulled. To say nothing of how your employees, or the industry as a whole, would react to what we have to say. I'm sure most of the roster would be glad to come forward with the crap you've pulled on them, if we took the initiative.' 'You wouldn't be able to hold a show in your own backyard by the time we get through with you,' added Sakura. 'Face it man, we've got you by the balls!' Yotogi punctuated his statement with a hearty laugh. 'When faced with an end, change,' said Kenta, looking into Genbu's eyes, 'and having changed, pass through.' 'In other words,' growled Tsuneo, 'shape up or ship out.' Itoju simply smiled. 'We'll be leaving now,' I said, standing up from the desk. 'I have a trip to America to prepare for. But these six,' I gestured to the other Thunders, 'will be keeping an eye on you from now on, Genbu. If you want to stay in business, I recommend you follow their suggestions from now on.' Together, we strode out of the office, leaving Genbu alone with his thoughts. And his unconscious son. *** Three days later, I was standing at a United Airlines terminal in Tokyo Narita International Airport, ready to board a flight to Dallas by way of Los Angeles. None of us had heard a word from Genbu since we'd confronted him -- either he'd decided to heed our advice or he was licking his wounds while trying to come up with a new way to get the roster back under his thumb. Whatever. I wasn't going to be a part of it any more. The boarding call for my plane sounded again. 'Well,' I said, looking down into Yoshiko's eyes, 'I guess this is it.' 'Yeah,' she said, looking away from me for a moment, 'I'm? I'm going to miss you, Lion.' I smiled. 'I'll miss you too, Crane.' We just kind of looked at each other for a moment. Then Yoshiko flung her arms around me, hugging me tight. I returned the favor. 'You'd better kick some ass over there in America for me, pal.' said Yoshiko sniffling a little. I smiled, and kissed the top of her head. 'You'd better kick some ass for me over here,' I replied. 'Like you have to worry about THAT.' she said. I chuckled, and slowly, we broke the hug. The final boarding call for my plane sounded. 'Well? goodbye, Silken Crane.' 'Goodbye, Steel Lion.' I turned away from Yoshiko, and handed my ticket to a pretty red-haired flight attendant. 'Thank you for flying with United,' said the stewardess, smiling brightly at me. My spirits started to rise as I boarded the plane and found my seat. I was going to miss Japan and the people I knew here, for sure. But a more important thought was in my head. I wondered if that stewardess was interested in joining the mile high club. Halfway to Los Angeles, I found out she certainly was.
