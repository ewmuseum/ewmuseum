A Fallen Angel: The Funeral

6 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 6, 2005 It had been the worst week of Biddle’s life. Some moronic detective had accused him of murder but that had only been the perfect start of a perfect week. Not only had the GLOBAL committee refused to recognize him as the rightful number one contender to the International Title, they had forced him into a match with Jake Hix to determine the contender and he had lost. Surprisingly, the loss was not Biddle’s fault. Hix had cheated and won by having his family interfere and help him win. That fact alone proved that Hix feared Biddle just like all the other turd blossoms in the GLOBAL locker room. To make matters worse the committee had refused Biddle’s request to miss the next Evolution taping and had booked him in a match against Blaise McClancy. His opponent’s name was a familiar one, McClancy was a fellow 4 Winds Wrestling alumni. Biddle knew that McClancy would use the match as an excuse to air the dirty laundry of Biddle’s departure from 4WW...the McClancy’s were immature idiots in that respect. Biddle wouldn’t stoop to that level though, why give the son of a bitch the pleasure? Biddle would simply steamroll over the talent less hack, no questions asked. Besides, it would be good therapy after the events of the past week to bash the little poser’s head in. For the time being Biddle had to put the stress of GLOBAL to the side, for today was Hillary’s funeral. He truly didn’t want to attend the event; he never actually cared for Hillary and was actually glad to see her go. His bodyguard, the Back Alley Brawler, had advised him to attend the solemn event. The Brawler had told him that if he attended the funeral it would tone down the suspicion surrounding him. So there he stood, studying his reflection in the full-length mirror in his bedroom. He wore a black suit, dark sunglasses covered his blue eyes, and he’d be damned if his blond hair didn’t look good. Satisfied with his appearance he looked over at the clock on the wall, it was ten o’clock...the funeral would begin in half-an-hour. With a weary sigh Mike Biddle left his room and went downstairs to meet the driver. ***** “Do you really need to go?” Delores whined at her husband. Harry closed his eyes and sighed, she always acted like this when he was in the middle of an investigation. “Of course I need to go sweetheart,” Harry said. “But you don’t even know her,” Delores said. “So?” Harry replied. “I’m not going for the funeral; I’m going to keep an eye on Biddle and that redheaded kid. The way they act at the funeral could be essential to my investigation.” Delores rolled her eyes in annoyance, couldn’t Harry just stay home with her for once? “What if they don’t show up?” She asked. “Oh they will,” Harry answered. Delores sighed in defeat, she knew that her husband’s mind was made up and that there was no longer any point in discussing the situation with him. “Just be back in time for supper,” she said as she hugged him goodbye. “I won’t be too long honey,” he said as he bent down to kiss her. As he walked out to the car he wondered if the funeral would be helpful to his investigation. ***** The Kirby funeral home was a dark, depressing place that was set up to look like a chapel. The only part missing from the set up was the various religious artworks that would normally be scattered around a chapel. The Kirby funeral home was designed this way on purpose, without any obvious religious affiliation the funeral home could host the funeral of any man or woman no matter his or her religion. Today the funeral home was packed with the friends and family of one Hillary Watson. They had traveled from as far as New England to come pay their final respects to the young woman that had died so mysteriously. Hillary’s parents had decided to bury their daughter in Los Angeles instead of her native Boston. Despite the extreme depression caused by the circumstances Hillary’s parents were glad to have the opportunity to meet the people that had had the pleasure of calling their daughter “friend”. One of Hillary’s friends had been exceptionally pleasant to the grieving parents. Charles Magee, the redheaded man that Hillary had told them about over the phone. He was kinder and gentler than their dearly departed daughter had said he was. Charles had been at the funeral home when they had arrived and as they sat and waited for the other mourners to arrive they sat and discussed their fondest memories of young woman. With the wealth of memories shared with the young man it made it easier of Hillary’s parents to put on their strong faces when the mourners began to arrive. The first mourner was a tall Germanic man that Charles instantly recognized and brought over to meet Hillary’s parents. “This is Detective Hagen,” Charles said, “he’s the man that will find out who was responsible for this despicable crime.” Hagen smiled as he shook the hands of the parents. He saw that Hillary had been a carbon copy of her mother in appearance. He also noticed the angry scowl on the girl’s fat father’s face, Harry decided then that it would be best not to let the parents know that he thought the killer might show up at the funeral. Instead, he assured them that he would find the murderer in a timely fashion, which seemed to put the father at ease. Hagen then went to the back of the room where he had a very good view of who came and went through the door and who went to stand next to the closed casket. Normally a funeral for a young person would be open casket, but in this case that would be impossible. The bastard that had killed Hillary had shot her in the face. As time went by more and more people came to the funeral room and Harry had observed nothing out of the ordinary. Of course, the person that he wanted to observe hadn’t shown up yet. That was, until exactly five minutes before the service was set to start. Biddle had tried to enter the room unnoticed, but was unable to do so. The moment Biddle entered the room Hillary’s father walked up to him, a look of anger painted upon his fat face. The older man grabbed Biddle by the arm and dragged him out of the room, an action that caused everyone in the half-filled room to look over their shoulders and wonder what was going on. Hagen quietly rose from his seat and stood near the doorway, he couldn’t see what was going on but he could hear the words exchanged by the two men. “What the hell do you think you’re doing here?” Hillary’s father asked in his thick New England accent. Biddle sighed audibly. “I’m here for the same thing you are Don,” Biddle said, “to pay my last respects to Hillary.” “That’s Mr. Watson to you punk,” the fat man said, “and you never respected my daughter in the first place so you have no right to be here.” “Never mind the fact that I almost married her,” Biddle replied with an annoyed tone. “And you left her at the alter you son of a bitch,” the fat man said. “So” That doesn’t mean I can’t attend her funeral,” Biddle said. “Oh yes it does. You broke her heart, you don’t deserve to be here.” “She’d want me to be here,” Biddle said. There was a long silence after that, and then there were footsteps heard coming towards the doorway. Hagen went back to his seat, figuring that Biddle had gotten his way. Hagen’s assumption was correct, Biddle walked through the door and took a seat in the second row. Hagen watched Biddle carefully throughout the whole service and it appeared that Biddle was truly saddened by Hillary’s passing. The only question was whether or not Biddle was using his acting talent to fool everyone. As the service ended everyone rose and formed a line to walk past the casket and leave anything that they had wanted to give to Hillary. Hagen made his exit at this time, next would be the burial and Hagen felt that Hillary’s family and friends should be left alone for that part of the ceremony. ***** The service had been more boring than Biddle had predicted, so boring that he had wished that he had given into Hillary’s father and left. Of course, that would have been a bad decision especially with the detective keeping such a close eye on him. Thankfully Mike had managed to avoid any further confrontations with the other mourners. During the final viewing of the casket Mike quietly slipped out the door and into the cool morning air. As he made his way to his car he noticed that a woman was standing next to the vehicle. As he got closer he recognized her as Alice Martinez, the reporter from the Beverly Hills Star. Alice had followed Biddle around during his relationship with Lisa. “What the hell do you want,” Biddle asked as he impatiently tapped his foot. “Just some information,” she smiled. “About what?” “Is it true” Did you murder Hillary Watson?” Biddle noticed that she reached into her jacket pocket, presumably to turn on her tape recorder. A mixture of shock and anger came over Mike’s face when asked this question. “Who told you that?” he asked as he seethed with anger. “A very reliable source,” Alice nervously replied. “I didn’t kill her,” Biddle said, “now get the hell out of my way.” Alice stepped to the side, she now had the quote she needed for her article. She watched with a smile as Biddle sped off. To her it didn’t really matter if he had killed Hillary or not, but she would be disappointed if he had...he had always been a good source for stories to run with for her gossip column. -To Be Continued-
