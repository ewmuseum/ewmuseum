motm1-misfortunate coincidences [of me].

27 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: sunday - july 27th, 2003 It has been a few days now with my head under the water. Last weekend, I was partying with Jay Robson. Listening to Marvin Gaye, drinking Hennessey and “gettin laid” to coin the ladies? man. Snorting cocaine, smoking pot and puffing cigars. It was back to the old Reed, the one that Bubba McCoy used to know back in EGWA. It was the guy that I didn’t think I’d ever bring back out. You see, with every sip of alcohol, every bong rip, and each line - a part of me dies. My conscious rots... ...and I had forgotten how good that feels. Last Monday I was learning with Jay Robson. I was tripping on lysergic acid diethylamide, which might explain why I thought I saw a gigantic Russian communist in the middle of a ghost town. I was twirling around a gun, which might explain the bullet holes in the extra t-shirt I brought. I was supposed to take care of an old man in a back alley that had supposedly been stirring up trouble, which would explain the tears the earth cried that night. That night’s over now. It’s a Sunday morning, no party last night though. Pull my head out of the water and grab a towel. Step out of the bathtub, dry off completely. Catch my breath, pull the drain. Walk over to the mirror. Hair is matted down, still locked in spikes...still dyed blonde. Push hair up, run fingers through. Hair stands tall, but it’s still not me. Slick hair back. Different attempt, same result. Pull towel back to head, ruffle the hair every way apart, still isn’t Trey Reed. Look to the right; grab the cold steel sitting on the counter. Pull my fingers tight on the smooth black handle. Bring it to my head, looking at the mirror. No, that wouldn’t be right either. Pull the possible weapon from my skull, rub finger over the mark it made on my temple. Twirl the thing around my finger, pose with it in front of the mirror. The right look seems to be like a lost cause by now, and it has put me in a bad mood. I grip the handle tight, and pull it back. Aim it at my throat. Fire the weapon into my jugular. Crash, boom bip bow. No blood flowing. Cock the weapon back again, thrust it to the vein. A SHATTER!. Blood trickles from my cheek. I set the scissors (my weapon of choice) down and step back. When I shattered the glass of the mirror, a piece of it flew right into me. Looking at the blood slip down, I smiled. It still wasn’t me, but what the hell does it matter, right? We all know Sean Price isn’t a Mafia man, so I can play my role as whoever I want to be. ---- Opening the door, I waved goodbye to my Hotel California, the Best Rest Inn. Yeah, I know what you’re thinking. Still in Cali, what? I told you I didn’t party last night, but Friday night was another story. After going to the finals for the International title, and advancing in the Master of the Mat, and the Youngbloods walking away with the tag titles, Jay said we had to celebrate. So, I decided to stay here for the weekend, and then leave for Dallas tomorrow morning. Shut the door, lock up, and walk down the hall. Through the elevator, past the lobby and out to the parking lot. There she is. My pride and joy, a 1971 Volkswagen Beatle. Reaching her, I ran my fingers across the Baby Blue shell and I bought her two days ago from a man moving to Tennessee for a mere five hundred dollars. Can you believe that he would let get of such a masterpiece for such a small price? Unlock the door, hop in, put keys in the ignition and start it up. No more walking to the gas station for a pack of cigarettes, to the market for a bag of bread, et cetera et cetera. Right now, I’m on my way to visit an old man. To be more specific, it’s the guy that I instilled terror into last Monday thanks to Jay Robson’s little ploys. But, that’s not the thing I want to be thinking about right now, or hopefully ever again. I sorted everything out with the guy, and was able to find out that he was a wise and interesting man. So, like yesterday I’ve decided to visit him, soak in some information he has to tell me. He turned out to be a former wrestler, one that I had heard of a time or two from my father. Pretty cool I thought, the stories he tells me remind me of my own attitude on the sport. I stop on the way to his place by the coffee shop. It’s eight, so he could probably use a wake-up call. The shop’s three blocks away, so I opt to lock the Beatle up and take a walk from there. A cigarette shop is on the same strip mall as the coffee house, so I’ll pick up a pack when I walk back. Carrying two coffees on a Sunday morning in Dallas isn’t the same kind of experience I thought it would be; kind of disappointing. Reach a small back alley in between buildings and fumble to open the door without a spill. Four fleets, two hallways later, I’m at his door. 'Come on in Trey,' his warm voice welcomes. Hand him the coffee and walk over to the couch, take my seat. 'How are you doing today Jack?' 'Oh, I’m okay.' He takes a seat across from me, sipping up the black liquid. And then he told me a story. No need to get into the far details, but it was about his feud with a Shawn Prince from twenty years ago. Shawn was an impressive athlete with a load of charisma and a big bank account. Shawn had all the momentum in his corner, while Jack was fairly new to the promotion and had more enemies than he knew names. Jack tells me that Shawn wasn’t even focused on him leading up to the thing, so he had to take drastic measures to get noticed. He said he used mind games and funny-little tie in stories to his advantage, to put Prince on lock down. And it worked. Anyway, we didn’t chat for too long, maybe an hour or so. I bid my goodbyes to Jack and went on my way back to the hotel. Maybe I’ll come out here another time and listen to some more of his stories, because he’s a pretty cool cat. Walking down the blocks back to the strip mall, I’m feeling better. I’m myself again, or at least it feels like this fits. Subtle little me, trying to throw things together just right. Maybe people will get it, probably most won’t. I kind of like that though, I’ll feel for the response. 'Wait a second.' Something’s not right, something’s not right at all. I’m back at the strip mall. They charged me three dollars and ninety-nine cents plus tax for a pack of cigarettes; mind you, but not what I’m talking about. Searching, looking, not finding. Freeze frame, zoom out, circle the world. Start running, running through the parking lot. Not there. Shot of me grabbing my hair and screaming no. ---- 'Are you serious?' Ronny says on the other end of the phone. He seems a bit distracted, caught up in something else. 'Yeah, dead serious.' 'Wow man...I don’t know what to say. That really sucks though dude.' I just don’t feel like he’s putting the type of care into this that I have. 'So, where’d it happen?' 'I was at some strip mall.' 'Oh, what part of town?' 'Somewhere around east Market Street.' 'Ah, yeah. Eastside of Market St. Bad place, have to be careful in that neighborhood.' I just lost a nineteen seventy-one Volkswagen Beatle! That thing is classic, man! We're talking vintage Baby Blue, baby. Whatever, I guess. No clue what could have happened, no leads. No ideas at all. 'I guess I might as well just fly in tonight then, instead of taking the drive. I’ll see you sometime tomorrow then I guess, bye Ronny.' Hang up. So what happens now? Pull out my equivalent of three dollars and ninety-nine cents plus tax, pack them down. Strike a match, breath in some chemicals. Much better.
