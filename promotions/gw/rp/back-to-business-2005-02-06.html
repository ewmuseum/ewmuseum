Back to Business

6 February 2005

Date: 2/06/05 What the hell am I doing out here? As he stood at ease it was the only question really occupying his mind. The night sky was over cast, the air crisp and cold. The south was supposed to be warm, but obviously winter didn’t care much from the reputation of the south in general or Texas in particular. The wind, that might normally have been considered gentle at any other time of year, bit with cold fangs. It wasn’t exactly the type of evening that invited people to venture out and partake in it. That’s what made being here so odd. Even Train had to admit he had no real idea why he was standing out here. His only guess was a simple one... or perhaps more profound than he’d fathomed consciously. He figured... He just needed to look. Train hadn’t really bothered sparing the time to really re-absorb the ambiance of his only semi-new surroundings again. When he made his abrupt, but hardly unceremonious return to GW just the night before the goal hadn’t been to survey the scene, but to make it. It couldn’t have been much more spur of the moment, though it didn’t have to be that way. It would have been easy to make the big return with the flashing video packages, “Train is BACK!!!” flashing in the center of the screen, but he knew that was the wrong move. Tempting, but wrong. It wasn’t about the pomp and circumstance of it all... the roar of the crowd. Naw... That was the last go around. Train’s last run in GW had been about building the name so he could get the big pyros in his entrance and getting the fed to cough up the licensing fees for the good music. Last time had been about showing off for the crowd and proving he was a true to life superstar for the rest of the boys... intimidating those who were impressed and shoving it down the throat of the rest. The last time had been about having a little fun. However... this time around? It was all about business. There wasn’t going to be a hell of a lot of time to sit around soaking in the scenery. Last night was the purest example of the way things needed to go this time around. He hadn’t made an appearance on Evolution or any other GW programming or walked through the backstage in months. So many new faces and stories floating around... and yet he hadn’t stopped to look at any of that. There were a ton of old stories hanging in the air, just waiting to be plucked out, rolled over and reminisced about, but he hadn’t bothered with that shit either. His return had only been about one thing... Picking his spot. What would have been the highly anticipated return of a former champion, had anyone known it was coming, was only about doing the maximum amount of damage in the shortest amount of time to reminded the broadest range of people he was the last one to be fucked with. The last one to be underestimated. With that as a goal what time had there been to just stop? To just look around? To soak in the fact that he was back where he really wanted to be? Those were easy questions to answer really. There had been none. Train chuckled to himself a bit as he let his mind drift back to the night before. He hadn’t even had the time to learn the names attached to the asses he’d decided he was going to stomp when he finally saw “his spot” to make his presence felt. If he couldn’t manage that simple task surely there had been no time to really sit back and soak in the fact that, despite being dropped like a hot potato a few months before, he finally had a chance to come back and make a run at the only title he gave any kind of a damn about...the GW World Heavyweight Title. Train drew in a deep swallow of cold air and released it, the heat of his body producing a thick puff of steam. The smallest semblance of a grin creeped across his lips as he stood looking at it. It was almost hard to believe he was here. Realizing that he finally determined that his initial suspicion had been correct.... He was just here to look. He hadn’t had the time to just stop, look and appreciate the night before so now he was compelled to make the time. He withdrew his hand, protected from the cold by the black, leather glove clinging to it, from the pocket of his matching black, leather jacket that protected the rest of him and pulled the zipper of the jacket down a bit. He had no intention of allowing the wind any more access to him beneath his armor than necessary or for any longer than necessary. Train quickly dug into the jacket inner pocket and retrieved the recently re-rolled, chocolate flavored, Swisher Sweet cigar and placed it between lips before quickly zipping the jacket. He quickly placed the hand back into the outer pocket of the jacket, but pulled the other gloved hand from the opposite pocket, this one clutching a silver Zippo lighter. On quick swiped down across his leg popped the lid of the lighter open. Another quick swipe in the opposite direction sparked the flint and brought the flame to life. The wind attempts to stifle it, but to no avail. A few pulls of air over the flame and the cherry of the hand rolled cigar comes to life allowing the intoxicating smoke to drift into his lungs and the air. It was only the light from the flame from the lighter that replaced the glimmer of lights from the building he watched so intently now. And that was only for a second or two. Placing the lighter back in his pocket Train let his mind refocus even as the cigar lifted his consciousness. He’d forgotten how long the process had taken, but somehow he’d convinced himself that he would never lay eyes on the place again. But here he was..... Just staring at the place. Global Arena hadn’t really changed. The lights of the building glowed the same way they always had. The gaping emptiness of the black top parking lot was sprinkled with the litter of a crowd the same way it always was the day after an Evo taping. That same sign with the thousands of small, white bulbs still sat on the same concrete post hoisting it up into the air as the lights pulsated with the same grand promises of “Unbelievable Action!” as an invitation for the same messy crowd to return. Train marveled at the scene, his mind swam a bit with a sense of dejavu. He leaned back casually against the grill of the black Denali as the truck sprawled indiscriminately across several spaces etched out in white on the surface of the ocean of black top. It was like time had decided to take a break the second he left this place. Of course the sense that time had stood still while he was gone that confused him. It shouldn’t have been. The sense that he’d been here in this same position before? Hell that was easy... he had. Yet somehow it seemed like a contradiction. He stood eying the arena intently, noticing everything was just the same as he’d left it... but as he looked at the place so familiar to him he could help thinking... It felt completely different. How could that be? If it was all the same what could have changed? The roster? Most definitely. Train had made it a priority to watch Evolution over the last few weeks to make sure he was up on the goings on. Who hated who. Who rolled with who. All the things he’d need to know in order to only tread on territory he was ready to step into. Everything he needed to know not to be used as some kind of pawn in a game he wasn’t prepared to play. As he watched it astonished him just how many new faces there were. Yet somehow they were as familiar to him as if he’d met everyone of them and shook their hand. It was the gleam in their eyes that was so familiar. The look of young lions. It said that they were ready to fight at the drop of a hat to make sure they got their fill of the kill. The look of young men who’d just hit the big time like he had not so long ago. They weren’t that different... hell looking like them was like looking in a mirror so it wasn’t the new faces. The game? Hell no. That was the first thing he’d learned hustling in the streets. A lesson that was only reinforced when he made the shift from the streets to the ring. The players... the alliances... the enemies... the pay offs... the pitfalls... those things change to varying degrees.... The game remains the same. You may change for it, but damned if it’ll change for you. Whether it was unloading 40 or 50 bricks of coke a week or winning titles that encompassed the world the concept was the same. Don’t trust anybody... Watch your back... Don’t take anything at face value... Make sure they know you’re not to be played with... It’s ok if they come for you as long as they thought twice about it... You pull it out you pull the trigger... Get your money up front... When you get yours everybody’s gonna want a piece of it... Fuck them. Wrestling..... Hustling..... it was all the same. So what was it that had changed in the equation? There weren’t a great many variables that made up this problems, but it was obvious he must be missing one. And that’s when it dawned on him. He’d forgotten to factor in the biggest variable of all...himself. The scene that lead to his departure from this place was far from pretty. Train had stormed out of this arena months ago, his blood boiling, heart pumping venom. He’d burned with anger of what he’d seen as a complete injustice... just when his opportunity to prove he deserved to be considered part of the promotion’s top tier they’d snatched the rug right out from under him. One day the next mega star...the next on the street with nothing, but a canceled contract in his hand. Just the thought of it put him close to frothing at the mouth. He’d vowed never to step foot in the building he’d been staring out in the cold, dark, dead of night, but yet here he was. And not only was he here, but all the anger and bitterness... It still swirled a bit beneath the surface, tinging the water red just a little bit like a few drops of blood in a bucket of water, but a few months ago you wouldn’t have been able to make out water for the blood. The anger...the rage... Somehow it had dissipated. And standing there in the cold, dark, dead of night something completely off the world occured to him. Maybe... just maybe... they’d done him a favor. GW was hardly Train’s first brush with the ring. He’d done short stints in little independents and a few Asian tours over the years, every time hoping to hit it big. To find his out of the street game. Every time heading into the engagements with dreams of superstardom. Each time, no matter how many men he left laying in the center of the ring, finding nothing, but a few new aches and pains along with a few old dreams that never were. He’d resigned himself to his life. The one that made it hard for him to sleep at night. The one that made him feel like a performer in that Scottish play, in his mind yelling out damn spot as he washed his hands. It had been like a light from above shining down on him when he got the call from Bubba McCoy, who somehow, someway had come up with tapes of some of Train’s old matches in the no names that he figured squared him up for a shot at the big times. And that’s exactly what GW was to him... the big times. That opportunity to change his life once and for all. Train made another vow before the one not to return. A vow that he would never leave and he’d die before somebody ever forced him out. When he’d threatened Christina McCoy... Got in Bubba McCoy’s face... made an enemy out of just about everybody... beat the man that would be heavyweight champion when nobody gave him a chance in hell of doing it... when he pushed another human being off the top deck of the parking garage in the trunk of a car... everyone took him for evil. For having no regard for anyone. They were wrong. He had every regard for himself... for his family... for the man that trained him... He had been determined to prove that he had what it took for the big time and he’d take down anyone or anything that said otherwise. He wanted to prove to everyone he had what it took to be big time. No.... Scratch that.... He wanted to prove it to himself. And he was right there. Right on the verge of proving it. When they dropped him. It was like a bullet in the back. He might as well have been lying in the street bleeding to death when he heard the news. He’d never admit it to anyone, but... some of that rage... was really fear. Fear that, that was it. Fear that despite putting his life on the line to break away from the old life there was no new life. Fear it was over. Fear the dream was dead. Fear he’d never make good on the promise to the man who sacrificed his life so he could make it. That was as ugly as it got. In his head he knew those fears weren’t really founded, but they felt real. That’s when WMW came calling. Train had proven everything he set out to prove and he knew it because within a week he was on live television again doing what he did best... hurting people. Within two weeks he was working with former GLC Champions. Within 2 months he was headlining pay per views. He could go anywhere. He could stand with anyone. He could stand with the biggest names and hold his own. He had nothing left to prove. At least as far as having the goods were concerned. He had no more fear. And there it was. That’s what had changed. He’d been freed. There was no more anxiousness or anxiety about proving himself. It was done. It looked different because it was different.... He was different. Now that the world knew he could make it anywhere, now all he had to do is make in the one place he wanted to more than any. Back in the place it all started. Now that he knew he could make, all that was left to do... was to do it. It was on. The hard vibration and the sound of buzzing snapped Train back from his deep contemplation and Train reached into his pants pocket, retrieving the Razor cell phone. As he flipped it open and put it to his ear he didn’t bother checking the caller ID as he normally would. He knew who it would be. He’d been waiting for this call. It meant the Train station was officially re-opened for business. “Alright, it’s done.” pronounced the harried and nasally voice on the other end of the line. “The contract is signed sealed and delivered. You are officially a Global Wrestling Superstar once again.” “Good job Jerry.” Train responded flatly, his eyes still locked on GW arena. “Sure, I don’t know WHY you’re a Global Wrestling superstar again, but hey,” Jerry, Train’s fidgety and slightly sleazy agent, pausing specifically at that moment for dramatic effect. “Who am I to judge, right?” “Jerry,” Train responded in a plume of smoke after taking another puff from his blunt. “I don’t feel like arguing with you about this again.” “Arguing”!” Jerry squawked in his high pitched, passive aggressive tone that mean he was going to do nothing, but without calling it for what it was. Train just leans back more heavily on the truck, bracing himself. Sometimes the best thing to do with Jerry was just to let him get it out. Perhaps if if he did that he’d finally shut up about what he sees as a grave injustice in the agreement with GW. Or, more accurately, what he sees as a hit to his 15%. “Who’s arguing” Look, Train... Tony... My man... I’m not arguing with you here, ok? I... I... I just need you to help me understand again, ok? I just need you to explain it to meeeee... One more time. All you been saying to me is we gotta take care of business, we gotta take care of business, we gotta take care of business... Now, ok... You still with me? Good... Here it is, ok? WMW? They offer you 50K more a year to stay. They offer you 10% higher share of your merchandise. They offer better medical... So... why are we in GW again? Exactly?!” There is a short pause before the answer comes. Train was only half paying attention, still concentrating on the symbolism of the arena as the smoke from the blunt starts to do his work. The pause in the tornado of words alerting him that it’s his turn to talk again. “I told you Jerry, business ain’t always about money.” “Yes... yes you have said that. But if I can just ask one more... teeny, tiny, itty bitty question... What the hell kind of business isn’t all about money”!” Train just leans back, relaxing himself, knowing he’s got what he wanted. Another grin forms on his lip, the image of the GW World Heavyweight Title sparkling in his eyes even brighter than the lights of the building now. “The unfinished kind.”
