Past Life

21 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/21/04 What would you do if you happened to forget everything you know? You forget what a Christmas morning feels like. You don’t remember how boxed pizza tastes. You don’t even remember what your parents look like, or, even if you have any at all. You start all over, back to the drawing board. You have to start from scratch, a homemade recipe on the spot. A dose of patience with a dash of initiative. A tablespoon of luck and you have yourself a beginning. Another, new, beginning. When you are 24, or at least you think you are 24, it makes life difficult. You wander around for a job, because you don’t remember if you had one or not. So, you apply for a new job instead of sitting around, waiting for a call saying, “Where were you on Friday night?” Hoping to startle your memory. Then you say, “My grandfather died, I had to attend his funeral. Work was the last thing that came to my mind. It won’t happen again.” You make up any excuse, just to save your ass. Just, so you can have your job back. Your life back. But that doesn’t happen. You end up sitting around, burning a hole in your front pocket on frozen fish sticks and microwavable meatloaf dinners with a side of instant mashed potatoes and dried corn. You apply for a new job. You apply, for every job. When you hand in your job resume, it looks like a blank piece of paper with the accomplishments that you’ve accomplished in the past two weeks. Congratulations, you’ve paid your electric bill. You’ve watched the entire M*A*S*H marathon on TBS. You’ve fed the dog. The only job you get a call back from is the late night shift at Subway Express making minimum wage, or the diner down the street that is a great job for high school teenagers, looking to get into the business world and save up enough money to purchase their first car. You choose between a roast beef sub, extra cheese, everything, with extra oil and vinegar on the side or mingling with the 10th grade folk down at Sheldon’s Lunch. You take a deep breath, because it’s the only thing that assures you anymore. Work is work, or at least that’s what you tell yourself. The paycheck you earn each week just might be enough to pay for your house payment. The house that you own or rent. You only know this, because you woke up in it this morning. Sheldon’s Lunch called, they are looking for a part-time dishwasher. You kick up your feet on the bed in front of you, watching Oprah Winfrey? Dr. Phil? Jenny Jones? And the rerun of the Oprah Winfrey show you just watched three hours ago. But for once, you don’t care. You don’t mind watching the rerun about “change” in your life to make yourself happy. Perhaps, that’s what I need. Nothing seems to be permanent in my life, perhaps happiness is something I’d want to start with. A call from God put the same episode of Oprah on today, not NBC. I think, I need some excitement in my life. I need, some adventure. Subway Express called, they said I have the job and can start training on Monday. I told them that Uncle Leonard passed away and that his viewing is on Monday, so that wouldn’t be possible. They said to call back when I get back on my feet. There are many roads I can take in my life. If you aren’t happy with something, why settle for it? Why settle for second best when you deserve the gold medal. The 1st place trophy. The platinum record. Change, it is something I need in my life. I’m starting all over again, a new beginning. I need something, different? February 1, 2004 I talked to Bubba McCoy yesterday. He signed me as a professional wrestler for Global Wrestling. Wow! That one word means so much to me. An expression that takes your breath away. You can breath now, our troubles are behind us. A paycheck, of a decent size, is exactly what I need in a time like this. And the excitement, of wrestling in front of thousands upon thousands of people is remarkable. It is the definition of what I need in my life. I’ve never done anything like this before, but you know what they say, it never hurts to try. Well, unless I get slammed on my head or socked on the jaw hard enough. Thrown threw a table. Or even a one on one meeting with a steel chair and my face. Perhaps, trying can hurt a lot. But, it is a risk I am willing to take. A risk I need to take. You see, ever since the day I can’t remember, I’ve been seeing doctors and specialists about my problem. Twice a week, I meet with the same exact doctor. Oddly enough, I don’t remember anything past one morning where I woke up in a bed. I suppose it was my bed, inside my home. I was diagnosed with “long termed memory loss”, but to me, they are just words compiled together. It makes no sense. I ask what could have caused it and they tell me, “a traumatic experience or damage done to the brain.” But I don’t think that happened, if it did, I don’t remember it anyways. I look at my body, blanketed with scars. I have one on my kneecap. One on my shoulder blade. One on my elbow. One by the temple on my face. And so on. I look like a human pincushion that has been tortured time in and time out. A never-ending cycle of pain and anguish, that I don’t remember. I asked the doctor for copies of my medical records, hoping to clear something up about my “injury” or to find out more about how I got these horrible scars. The doctor said that there weren’t any medical records past the day that I can’t remember. You are sitting at home, thinking about how impossible this is. Well, welcome to my world. Take a deep breath, and try to accept it. I ask him if he remembers seeing me, he says no. The owner of Subway Express called, giving her condolences for Uncle Leonard’s death. I thanked her. Told her, he was going to die anyways. He was old. She mentioned that she was in her late 70’s and I then gave her my condolences and reassured her that she can go any day now and not to take things for granted. She hung up. I’m 6’8, a giant compared to the size of the normal man. I weigh approximately 310 lbs, of pure muscle. You see, I always wonder if I was a juicer in my “past life”, you know, a glutton for steroids. Bigger, is better. Or, I could have been a championship weightlifter. A fitness model, or even a normal man who loved to keep in shape. Sheldon’s Lunch called, they could really use a cook. I tell them, “I’m taken” while smiling as a hang up the telephone. You see, perhaps I don’t know these things for a reason. Maybe these scars were caused by a knife. A stab wound. Or, somebody shot me from close range. If these scars were caused by something so horrible, would I even want to know? The last thing I think about before I go to bed at night is if I have enemies out there who want to kill me. And how about my size? Was I a drug-induced freak who was a glutton for steroids? Was I so addicted, so close to death, that God had to give me a second chance? He helped me forget all the bad things in my life. Hell, I don’t even have withdrawals if I was on steroids, because I don’t even know if I was or not. The only thing you can do, when you step in my shoes, is hope and pray for the best. You honestly want to find out that you were a healthy man who had a steady job that was an avid weightlifter. Or, the scars that cover your body, they are from an accident from playing football when you were in high school. Your scar on your shoulder was from a party when you were hanging out with the 10th grade folk. Enjoying your childhood. But what if’that always gets to me. If I found out that these things were caused by something bad, I wouldn’t even want to remember them. If I knew, I would want to forget they ever happened. And that is what happened. God, gave me a second chance? Still, the curiosity makes me wonder about my past life. Was I ever in love? Did I ever save somebody’s life? Did I ever do anything so amazing, I’d want to remember? I go to the courthouse and research my criminal history, hoping that it would be clean? “I’m trying to research a criminal record.” The man behind the counter lifts his head? “Of whom?” I hesitate for a moment? ??me.” The man rolls his eyes and snickers? “You serious?” I nod my head, embarrassed. “Yes.” The man lowers his eyes back to his paperwork in front of him? “And’your name please?” He says with a smirk. I take a deep breath, this is the hardest part? “I don’t really remember.” The man looks at me once again? “How can you forget your own name?” The ignorant man laughs at me as I turn my head and walk away with tears in my eyes? If only you can walk a mile in my shoes each and every day, you’d see what it’s like. It’s not easy, I can tell you that much. Try going on a date with a lady. You ask about her life. Where she works. If she has a pet. Where she grew up at. And when the questions start to fly at you, you just shake your head side to side saying, “I don’t recall. I don’t remember. Boy, that was too long ago. Do I have a bad memory or what?” If she only knew. You take a deep breath, because it seems like the only thing permanent in your life. You try to explain to her about your problem. Quite frankly, women these days don’t want to hear a flaw. Especially on the first date. If there is something going on in your life, they don’t want to be involved. They strive for perfection. And who can blame them? “Hey, get back here, sir.” Some women tell me how strong I am, right before they leave. They wish me luck. They tell me I’m brave. They call me a real-life miracle. I laugh. I’m a miracle of God. I’m God’s little “halo”. I suppose, that is where my nickname came from. Halo, at least something sticks now. They thank me for a great night and a glorious time. We exchange phone numbers, knowing that she’ll never call me. And when I call her? “Hello?” Good, it’s her voice on the phone. “Hey! How are ya?” “May I ask whose calling?” “It’s me, Halo.” “Oh, I’m sorry, you must the wrong number.” The next thing I hear is a click. The sound of the phone slamming into the receiver. How embarrassing. You take a deep breath, and hang up yourself after two or three minutes of hope. Hoping that she’d come back and apologizes. But that never happens, does it? “Mr’whatever, please come back.” But I don’t turn around. How can a man seem to help you if he is too busy laughing in your face? Instead, I take go to get fingerprinted. Perhaps, they can match my fingerprints with the fingerprints of me, from my past life. They can pull up the record of that man, and there you have it. I have my real name, real address, real date of birth. I know, everything that I need to know? “Would I be able to get my fingerprints taken?” I speak to the woman behind the counter, chomping on a piece of gum? “You some criminal or sum’t’in?” She says while pushing her glasses up on her nose a little more in her New York accent? I chuckle, to lighten the mood? “No, I just want to find a match to my fingerprints.” “What, you forget your name or sum’t’in?” The woman giggles. I know if I tell her the truth, she’ll laugh as well. “No, no, I just want to see if anybody has the same fingerprints as I do.” “Beat it kid. Go take a high school science class and learn that nobody has the same fingerprints in the world. I learned that in the 9th grade or sum’t’in.” I nod my head’and take a deep breath. “They stopped enrolling adults in their classes after Billy Madison graduated?” Lightening the mood once again, with a smile upon my face, before I turn around and leave the building. Nothing breaks a bad mood like an Adam Sandler quote. I shake my head, side to side, before I open the double doors and leave the building. Before I leave my problems? You see, this is just a normal, average day in my life. Trying to figure out my past, or, at least my name. And when I ask somebody about my past, who should know, they tell me they don’t. Welcome to my world. Where another person shares the same fingerprints as me. I am the one and only in this world. You can call me Halo.
