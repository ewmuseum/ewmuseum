Amor Vincit Omnia

27 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 27, 2005 -------------------------------------- The McFarlane Estate August 27th, 2005 -------------------------------------- An ancient, hand bound book rested on the bed next to Virgil, the gilded lettering that once graced the cover long ago lost to the ravages of time, along with the whiteness of the pages. It had once been 'The Art of Courtly Love', and perhaps still was, though the age of the book perhaps betrayed the obsolescence of the concept itself. In truth, he'd forgotten many of these old methods and signals, along with, curiously enough, nearly everything he'd ever experienced, leading up to around the time he'd met Shadow in Cleveland, under the name of Gideon de Medici, a name which, he realized, he could also not remember the origin of. Curiously, everything since Cleveland was still very fresh in his mind. The flames tearing into the sky from the falling walls of his mansion in Napa Valley, facing down numerous Chinese vampires in 'business' dealings, when a compromise was not easily reachable... this all stood out as though the previous day had contained each experience. He was aware of existing for quite some time previously to this, and it seemed as though his practical knowledge had survived whatever had happened to his memory intact. He still spoke several languages with native fluency, still knew the lore and workings of his kind, and his firsthand knowledge of history was still untouched, and even included brief glimpses of himself playing roles in said history... though now, he couldn't entirely trust them to be real. But the past didn't concern him much as of this moment. It was, after all, obviously not important enough to come searching for him when his memories had left his mind. A thousand years? history had crumbled from where they stood into nothingness, but there wasn’t much that he could do to remedy that’so he decided that building a new life where over a thousand years had stood to be more important. That, of course, did require some work... which was where this ancient tome came into play. The last few weeks had been trying ones. Though he had learned to co-exist reasonably peacefully (or at the very least ignore) living with Dawn McFarlane for the moment, it seemed as though his patience was always to be tested, as the thin, latex-clad Casanova with the bizarre name of Emoticon, had begun to move ever closer to intruding upon his personal life. In fact, quite recently, he'd been very successful. His name meant nothing at all to Virgil; as far as Virgil was concerned, all of the man’s self-righteous airs meant nothing as well. As much as he wanted to play at being chivalrous, Virgil could tell what the nature of his game was. He was just a simple human, like the billions in the world, and Virgil could definitely remember being convinced long ago that love was very rarely possible among humans in these modern nights, at least for its own sake. Modern love was fueled by hormones, by sex, by base instinct. It didn't take much for Virgil to see this coming from Emoticon as well. For after all, were his cajoling to be successful, what rights would he then exercise? The answer was quite obvious. Virgil could remember a time when sex did not play into love as heavily. His beliefs had changed with the nights, but so long ago, the first day he had set eyes upon his betrothed wife, his first thought was not of pressing the time forward to the night of their wedding. In fact, he recalled being quite stricken by her beauty, and considering himself quite lucky to have been graced so by fate. Idly, he pressed the book’s pages apart, gently for a man of his stature, scanning the first line. 1. Marriage is no real excuse for not loving. My, how many modern men could have used that lesson. Shadow reminded him of that day, of that feeling, what it was like before being jaded by time. But as much as he remembered feeling it, he couldn't remember ever telling her so. Did that mean he actually hadn't? Their connection, to Virgil, was obvious, but it had not seen its full strength. Suddenly, Virgil had a reason as to why. -------------------------------------- Somewhere in Prussia, near the North Sea January 27th, 806 ---------------------------------------- Virgil's black horse shifted underneath him, made as nervous as some of his men by the tension that hung thick in the air, as the two armies faced each other from across the valley. His hand found its way to his long sword, hanging at his side solemnly, and he could hear the regiment of archers next to him begin to nock their arrows, as the battalion across the valley began to slowly advance forward. His thoughts, however, were very independent of his actions, because all he could contemplate was going back home, to return to his beautiful wife... whose name was completely lost, despite the sudden clarity of this memory in his mind. He'd left her with a few freshly cut red roses, and a promise to return unharmed, before mounting his horse, and joining his lord's army, with the strange feeling of a string being pulled taut between them as he moved farther away, ever dragging him back to his home, to her. “Love and courage, my angel,” he’d said to her, handing her the red roses with a gentle touch. He’d taken care to pluck every last thorn from their stems, for his love for her had no thorns’and he’d chosen the color with care too, to represent just what he wanted to say. “I don’t want you to go.” The desperation in her voice had been obvious. The tears trembling full in her eyes would have made him give up his duty just to stay there, no matter what the consequence would have been? but she spoke again. “I know you must. But promise that you’ll come back to me?” Gently, he kissed the soft, warm, white skin of the top of her hand. He closed his eyes for a moment, relishing the feeling? but time was slipping away. He relinquished her hand; she never was one for emotional farewells, and he did not like for her to prostrate herself at his feet. He smiled. “I leave with reluctance and return with the swiftness of the wind.” Reluctant though he may have been to leave, he had to. He would never shame her by being the type of husband who did not respect the duties that his station called for. Then, he’d mounted his horse. The last thing he remembered seeing was her slim form standing in the doorway, her long dark hair loose around her shoulders’it was improprietous, but impossibly beautiful. The wind had ruffled her hair, and she had pressed his flowers closer to her breast. The Polish troops that had been making forays into Prussian and even French and Spanish territories on scouting missions were rather close now, and all that was needed to attack was his order. They had little chance, as a scouting party was no match for a full three regiments of specialized warriors, but Virgil feared that several of his men would not be returning. However, orders were orders, and carried with them practically the same weight as a decree from God. He drew his sword, and the piercing whip of bowstrings snapping broke the uneasy silence. His beautiful wife painted in portrait across his memory, Virgil hefted his heavy iron shield to a ready position. He narrowed his sea-blue eyes, and clenched his jaw. This battle, pointless though it might have been for him, would be won in her name. As his swordsmen charged down the hill to meet the aggressor, only one thing resonated with any truth’he had made her a promise. He would keep it. --------------------------------------- The Present, once again -------------------------------------- 2. He who is jealous cannot love. Virgil winced slightly. Perhaps he’d been ignoring that a bit too much lately. Perhaps he’d been so concentrated on wanting to kill Emoticon that he had forgotten Lia’s very real emotions. The episode just hours ago while she was tending Cyrus seemed to tell the truth of this. More than anything, whether she felt the need to talk about it or not, Lia needed to feel loved. She needed to feel safe in someone’s arms at night, and she needed to feel as though she brought light to someone’s otherwise dark existence. Deep down, he realized, he’d known exactly what she needed. Why he hadn’t provided it was a mystery to him, but it was a mistake that could be fixed. He wondered exactly how much else he knew about her, just by instinct. 8. No one should be deprived of love without the very best of reasons. She felt isolated, and alone, he realized, his confidence in his own gift of intuition increasing. She should not have had to feel that way, because once, she had not. Once, she had meant the world to someone. Virgil didn’t know who that someone had been, nor what the first event was that split the two of them apart, but specifics didn’t matter. What mattered was that he could right the wrong. What mattered was that he could step in, in place of the Corvus’s, in place of the Gray Brand’s, in place of everyone else who thought that Lia should always give of herself, and receive nothing in return. They were wrong. What she deserved was a love perfect enough to make up for the one she had lost, and everyone in between that time and the present who had tried to make her feel like she didn’t deserve another chance at it. The stream of his memory shifted again’now, he recalled these verses without having to crack the tired old spine of the book. 12. A true lover does not desire to embrace in love anyone except his beloved. His conviction grew. Emoticon was too fickle. Even in his pretensions to courtly airs, he didn’t really understand what he was promising. Either he loved Dawn, or he loved Lia. Loving both was an impossibility, and playing at games like that put his fa’ade at danger. Virgil could crack it easily enough, simply with loyalty. He had journeyed across America trying to track Lia down. He’d practically gone insane at the thought of never seeing her again’what could Emoticon offer to counter that? 30. A true lover is constantly and without intermission possessed by the thought of his beloved. The heady scent of flowers normally would have been enough to nauseate him slightly, but this evening, the perfume was a positive joy. Perhaps his mindset had really changed that much that he could actually feel joy at such things again’and he owed it to her, or rather, his revelation about what kept them from each other. He paused in front of a row of plants, the ones which seemed to produce the headiest perfume of all. Yes... he had been a true knight. He had known chivalry first hand, and practiced it happily, not this farcical nonsense that Emoticon was constantly portraying. Perhaps more fortunate than that, certainly his dearest Lia would have some memory of the same, or at the least, some buried subconscious reaction to it, as her memory seemed as strangely warped as his. If it came to defending his love with blows, then he would not shy away from combat. In fact, since deciding to amuse himself by joining Global Wrestling some time ago, he'd become quite fond of physical tests, and apparently was rather skilled with them as well, considering the strangely wrought piece of metal that signified his prowess. He realized a clash with Emoticon was inevitable. They were, despite their differences, two men possessed of devotion to a cause. But Virgil was quite certain that his cause was more valid, his love stronger, more genuine, and perhaps the deciding factor, he was much more able to back up his claim with the fervor of combat. Virgil smiled’and for once, it was genuine. Adeptly, he used his fingernails to slit the stem of the blossom in front of him. Its red radiance glowed even in the low light, and perhaps even more spectacularly under the sunset. For a moment, he regarded the flower clasped between his fingers. Love? respect? passion? courage. One bloom signifies simplicity, gratitude, and above all, love. Lia understood these things. She would know once she held this betwixt her fingers’she would know everything that he had not had the words to say until this point. The words, of course, had been right underneath his nose, and from an assortment of everything he’d known as the lord of the manor, or the commander of his regiment, or the dutiful husband who never once broke a promise that he made to his wife? 26. Love can deny nothing to love. And he would not deny it any longer. Perhaps the past was lost to him, but the present held just as much promise. It was up to him to show Lia that she was fully deserving of what she had lost’and up to him to prove that he was the only one who could give that to her. “Amor vincit omnia,” he said, smirking, “It would seem Ovid was correct.” The sun was still dying on the horizon, but had sunk lower still during his long moments of contemplation. The time to contemplate was over, once he shook off the haze of memory. He had to get to the Global Arena in time to do what was required of him. Naturally, a knight owed it to his lady fair to let her know that his daring gallantry was inspired by her grace and beauty, that the spoils of battle were nothing compared to the gift that was her love. -------------------------------------- Quotes from Andreas Capellanus? “The Art of Courtly Love”
