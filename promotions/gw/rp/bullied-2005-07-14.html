Bullied

14 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 14, 2005 That son of a bitch. How many times had the bastard robbed him? Robbed him of pride. Robbed him of peace of mind. Robbed him of blood and sweat in an attempt to garner his tears. The last had never worked, there was no way in hell that he’d have ever given that bastard the satisfaction. Doesn’t mean he hadn’t been tempted though. It was like those days back on the play ground in elementary school. The bully always had to choose the victim that he figured wouldn’t fight back. The kid he though would just stand and take it because for bully, no matter what they said, it was never about even competition. No matter the crap that came out of their mouth, all the fighting and picking wasn’t about proving their were tough. It was always about self preservation. Who’d dare try to devour you if you at least appeared to be the top of the food chain? Competition is about respect. What bullies did? It was all about fear. It was only about singling out those they considered weaker than them. Embarrassing the weak link, so that no one else would dare challenge them for fear the same would happen them. That’s why the call bullies cowards, not searching for challengers, but victims. And there was an even worse part about having bullies in the world. The fact that... no matter how much you hated them... no matter what you said about them... the truth of the matter? They were smarter than people gave them credit for. It’s a sickening thought, but as true as true can be. It’s been said more than once that this is a dog eat dog world. And like any food chain that has ever existed the fact is always it’s always going to be the big dogs that devour the smaller ones. Bullies? They were the big dog. Doesn’t matter if it was by hook or by crook. The dirtier the tactics used to earn the rep, the uglier the impression left. Doesn’t matter whether their heart matched their bravado, everyone else was too busy being scared witless to smell his fear. Doesn’t even matter if their ass could cash the check their mouth was writing. Nobody had the balls to try and cash it. Executed properly it was a hell of a scam. The problem was just about every bully would run into the same problem that would blow it for them. Themselves. It was only a matter of time really. After extended periods of having people kowtow and quake at the site of you it’s easy to forget that in the end your are still vulnerable. Still human. That just like they you wouldn’t take that kind of shit of someone, there had to be somebody out there that wouldn’t take that shit off you. The great irony of that was that the signs of a rebel was usually right in front of their face. Of course it is somewhat understandable how they would have missed it as they were usually sent in a format they were very familiar with... Tears. After seeing them so often it’s not so hard to see how a bully could misinterpret. A bully naturally assumes tears were about only one of a couple of things. Fear... Pain... Anxiety... Humiliation... But their was a possibility they always for got about. Rage. The type that, no matter how above it you considered yourself to be, welled up from the pit of your soul. The typed that bubbled up and rolled out and over everything in it’s path like molten rock from the belly of th Earth. The tears weren’t about fear or humiliation. They were about having no other way to express what you felt. That is until every collected ounce of pain and anger detonates, resulting in an explosion of a kind of hatred most who have it happen never really knew they were capable of. As Brick looked back he could only think of how close he’d come to his own explosion on the only bully that had every been stupid enough to think he was man enough to push him around.... Salaok. “That son of a bitch.” Brick muttered to himself out loud. His voice was only barely over the racing engine of the Jag as it did it’s best to glide down the bumpy road. The veins on the back of his hand bulged as he inadvertently gripped the wheel as if he could choke the machine to death with enough force. He’d have felt guilty had he consciously realized it. Brick loved the car. It was the man that was running laps around his head who’s still-beating heart he wanted to feel pounding in the palm of his hands. And that was exactly the type of thought that he lived in dread of. He hated it because when he had thoughts like that he understood just how close the bastard really had come to winning. It was never about wrestling with Salaok. Titles and win-loss records meant nothing. The bastard had always made about more than that. There had been no one else that had ever involved his family. Laid their hands on his family. From the beginning the bastard made it personal. Brick understood Salaok’s goal from the beginning. It was about bullying him. But not like your average bully. Your run of the mill bully wanted to take your pride and break your spirit. No... with Salaok, it was always more insidious that all that. Salaok never wanted to break a spirit. Salaok wanted to corrupt it. The man was the personification when of evil when it came to GW and as one of those who’d stood as the unwavering pillar of the promotion Brick was the prime target for Salaok. He’d wanted nothing more than, through the incitement of rage, make the incorruptible sink to the same depths of depravity that had become his home. Brick would have to live with the rest of his life regretting just how close he came to giving in. Looking back Brick had a hard time believing it was him. Looking back on it he understood that, at least for a moment, Salaok had stifled his sanity. For a while Brick honestly thought being forced to team with a man like Train was the most unsavory thing that could happen to him in his life. He knew who Train was. What he was. They say the camera add ten pounds, but the truth was that, the gleam of the television lights could actually wash away blood. They had for Train. Most in the company figured the whole “Train” thing was just a nice nickname. A nifty marketing plan to make some bucks hocking t-shirts. Most in the company were fucking clueless. Being forced to work with him was nothing, but ugly. It only got uglier when Brick almost turned into him. Brick had known from the moment he’d layed eyes on him. Train was a killer. It really wasn’t surprising that he’d know that before anyone else did. Birck had an advantage most others didn’t. Brick had Brooklyn. He’d seen Train’s type a million times grew up and living life in the hood. Guys like Train weren’t hard to come across. Cats that were willing to do anything to get all the cash they thought they were owed. Didn’t matter who they had to hurt. Didn’t matter who they had to kill. They all had that same coldness in their eyes. The same coldness Brick saw the moment he looked into Train’s. The same coldness Brick had made the conscious decision never to develop no matter how tough he became. That is until that son of bitch Salaok told the world he’d had sex with Brick’s sister. That had been it. The final straw. Salaok had long sensed crossed the line with Brick, but never like that. Never enough to ask Train for the number of a couple of hard hitting pipe niggas that more than likely weren’t strangers to either a pair of pliers or a blow torch. Fuck medieval. When he asked Train for the number of his boys JJ and Street... He’d been aiming for just plain evil. Thank God the very person Brick was looking to defend talked some sense in him. He didn’t know how the hell his sister knew what he was thinking, but she had and it was the only thing that stopped him from taking that next step. She was the only thing that kept him from stepping into that darker side of himself... that kept him from becoming the exact thing he loathed... that kept him from allowing himself to be bullied. Maybe Salaok had sensed that. Maybe, somehow, he’d looked into Brick’s eyes that he’d faced down his darker instincts and refused to turn to them. Maybe that’s why he did it. Maybe that’s why he quit. That son of a bitch. Just when Brick had come back to himself. Just when he’d found his way back to the strength that made him the man GW fans and the company itself had come to depend on as a constant the man he wanted to unleash on the most just... disa-damn-ppeared. Once again, the bully was always smarter than anyone wanted to give him credit for. With renewed dedication and unclouded judgement Brick was more dangerous than ever. That meant Salaok was in more danger than ever. That meant Salaok would be showing more wisdom than he’d ever shown to get the hell out of dodge. The bastard had turned tail and run. He’d abandoned the battle. Brick had won. So why the fuck did it feel like he’d lost? Brick silently wondered it to himself, but fact is he knew the answer. Yeah... Salaok had lost, but only in part. He’d chosen his time to leave. He’d chosen the terms under which he did it. He may have run, but he did it when he knew he couldn’t win and did it in a limo. It was supposed to have been when he least expected on a fucking stretcher. “That motherfucker....” Brick muttered to himself again. He couldn’t help, but think that Salaok was somewhere laughing at him. Sure, he’d been the one to run, but Brick would be the one who’d stew. That made it one of those moments he was tempted to let the rage take over and explode. That’s why he’d been in the car, for the last hour. The sun had sunk below the horizon about 30 minutes ago, but still Brick pushed the petal towards the floor. His body bounced in the seat a little until the strip the car rolled on gave up any attempt to disguise itself as a paved road and transformed into the dirt road it had pretty much been since he’d pulled off the highway. Brick only drove a few more moments before he pulled to a stop, the gravel crunching beneath the tires as the dirt itself floated in a small cloud around the car. He was careful to turn the key as to turn the engine off, but leave the headlights on to illuminate his way. The dust of the Las Vegas desert flew up from beneath his boot with every step. Brick hand wondered about moving out here. He loved the place out in Texas, but it was a long flight to Dallas and back, at least every week. He’d had real reservations about it early on until realized even in Vegas and the thousands of tourist buzzing about that feeling of isolation wasn’t hard to come across. All he had to do was set out down the road a little ways and he could find a place just like this. Nothing but rocks and dirt with the occassional cactus or some semblance of grass dotting the landsscape. He was nothing, but alone. Brick quickly reached back into the open driver’s side door and reached for the latch on the underside of the console, finding the switch he was searching for. With a quick pull he hears the lock on the trunk pop and the metal flap slowly starts to raise towards the sky. It only takes 3 steps with is large gate to wind up behind the car. He quickly helps the trunk lid reach it’s apex then reaches into the trunk, retrieving the 5 plywood boards. Maybe it was the cool, night time, desert air or just the drive itself, but Brick could almost feeling himself calming as he used his strength to burry the metal posts that were bolted to the backside of the wooden boards deep into the fragile, dry desert floor. Brick walked the 50 feet or so back to the car, wondering if he even needed this kind of release anymore. As his hand wrapped the handle of the shotgun, the raised diamond pattern roughly grinding against his skin he thought he was just wasting time and effort with this, his rage already having drifted away. Leveling the barrel of the shotgun in the direction of the back, man shaped target tacked to the front of the plywood board in his sights, the figure illuminated in the harsh, blueish gray glow of his halogen headlights, he thought to himself that he didn’t really need to do this. That’s when black target opened it’s mouth with it’s obsidian smile... the same enraging, mind twisting, soul twisting grin as the man that had escaped his grasp when he’d become his most powerful... when he’d finally found himself a gain. The explosion of gun powder illuinated the desert and the sound echoed through the desert like a clap of man made thunder. The head of the target tore open, spewing as spray of sawdust that, just for a moment gleamed red in Bricks eyes before splattering onto the desert floor. Nope. That felt damn good. ***** ***** ***** Brick found himself captured and held by the rolling of the flames as they rolled across the face of the wood sticks that were being consumed in the small pit surrounded by stones. The moon itself added light too as it hung in the sky above. Brick swished his Budweiser around in the bottle a bit before brining it to his lips. The alone time had done him well. The ability to release his anger without someone’s skull being knocked in was always surprisingly helpful to him. However, a few things had changed. As many people as he considered friends, he’d never invited anyone besides his brother in law to keep him company on these little make shift therapy sessions and the brother-in-law was only invited cuz his sister would be pissed if she felt like her brother had snubbed her husband. He wasn’t a bad guy or anything and neither were his friends, but he just preferred not to have them there... to see him like that. Before, with others around, he’d always had to hold back... to hold in and not release all. He knew in his heart they cared, but he knew even more they’d never understand. Seeing that kind of anger flow out of him. It would have scared them. No one was ever willingly invited before because there was no one who would really have understood what it was all about before. Brick was drawn out of his trance by the pounding of music that was growing louder and louder by the second. Brick leaned back and reached into the cooler pulling out two more Buds as the black Ukon Denali pulls up next to the Jag. From anyone else’s vehicle it might have been hard to identify the music, but not this time. It must have been an old school kind of night, “Triumph” by The Wu-Tang Clan pulsating through the vastly enhanced sound system. It only takes a moment for the truck to shut of and the silence to return to the empty desert, lit up by only moon and fire. The driver takes a few moments before sliding out and down to the ground. The dark figure reaches back into the truck, retrieving what looks like some kind of long, fairly thin case and closing the door behind him. As the light of the fire flows outwards it doesn’t take long for it to wash over the only man that could keep Brick company in this mood. The only one that wouldn’t bother being frightened by something he’d seen in himself a million times. The figure stops right behind Brick, staring out at the tattered targets in the difference. Brick doesn’t bother to turn around, simply opening his new beer and taking a sip. “How many times you blow Salaok’s brains out?” Train inquired, more interested in the firing pattern than the symbolism of all the holes that had been blown in the wood. “I lost track after the first box of shells” Brick responded calmly, taking another sip of his beer, the smooth ripple of the flames started to recapture his attention. Train just chuckled a bit as the kneeled down, setting the long metal case down in front of him. Brick only took minimal not of the clicking locks as Train popped them open. He could even here as Train reached into the case and pulled something free, the metal of whatever the object was chiming a little as it’s pulled free of the snug housing inside the case. “Hope you saved a few shells for Los Hernandez.” Train mumbled, as he slide the clip in. “La Herencia de Herederos”, Brick responded half amused by what he figured was a genuine misstating of their opponents name. “And I don’t need a clip for them. They’re warriors. I respect that. No doubt they think we’re just a couple of bullies, but that’s our advantage. No doubt they think we’re all talk. They don’t think we’ll come straight at them or respect them cuz we’re cowards like all bullies.” “Most bullies.” “Huh?” “I said most bullies. Not all bullies are cowards. Some cats are bullies cuz they’re just plain mean. ? Train stated in correction as he levels the Mac-10. “And unfortunately for their little midget asses...” The shotgun was thunder, but if it had a sound the Mac-10 would be lightning were it to have a sound. The sound of the automatic fire even snaps Brick out of his doldrum. He looks back just in time to see the target be cut in half. “I’m a warrior... But I got a little bully in me too.” Brick chuckles as he hands Train his beer. They sip in unison, more on the same page than ever.
