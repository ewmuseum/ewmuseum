A Longing and A History

16 June 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 16, 2004 [It’s a dazzling summer afternoon, with a full, blinding sun far up in the clear blue sky, and large white fluffy clouds stretching over the skies for miles in every direction. Underneath the clear heavens there is a small park setting. At one end of the park there is a lofty swing set with four seats attached to the top. Below each of the four swing seats there is a ditch where children have been digging their feet into the sand, for pure fun or to stop or slow the swing. A few feet from the chipped ruby red swing set structure there is another structure. This structure is based in the very middle of the playground; it has monkey bars, multiple different slides, a cargo net for climbing, a fort like tower, a tunnel, a swinging bridge, and many other attractions to occupy the children. Another point in the park has a colorful merry-go-round, there’s also a tire swing, and teeter-totters. There are children crawling all over the playground, laughing, smiling, and having fun while their parents sit on benches around the playground. Some of the parents are playing with their children on the playground structures. A few yards from the playground in this park there is a shelter area. There are a bunch of picnic tables all gathered under the shelter’s roof. There, leaning against one of the shelter’s four corner posts is a woman. Shoulder length brown hair, full of curls, faded blue jeans, and a camouflage t-shirt, cut off at the mid drift. Legs crossed, arms crossed, she’s gazing thoughtfully at the playground, at the children, and the parents. Moving out of her relaxed stance, she steps away from the sheltered area. Little by little she begins to wander aimlessly towards a bench closer to the playground. There is a faraway look in her eyes; clearly her mind is completely clouded with thoughts, and memories.] ?Mackenzie?? [A hand touches Mackenzie’s shoulder. Suddenly she’s torn from her thoughts, as another woman is standing in beside her, looking at her wonderingly. This woman is about the same height as Mac, glasses, and darker brown hair, completely straight and shorter. This other short woman is wearing a blue sundress underneath a black blazer jacket.] “Mackenzie, why did you want me to meet you here? Come, let us sit.” [Shaking her head slightly, Kenz clears the mist of lingering thoughts from her mind and follows the woman to a near by bench. They sit down next to each other; they both cross their legs. The other woman lays a yellow tablet of paper on her lap, and then she pulls a golden pen from her jacket pocket.] “This was an unusual request, indeed; I wouldn’t have complied to it with many of my other patients. However, after only having a few sessions with you so far, I felt you would have a good reason for such a request.” [Mackenzie thinks over this statement for a moment. It’s true; she had only been to see Dr. Bower, her therapist, three or four times at the most within the last month. Of course, the first two or three sessions had been more or less completely about Doctor Bower trying to gain her trust, as all therapists ritually begin their sessions with new patients. In these two or three sessions the real issues of the patient are rarely brought up.] “Doctor Bower, at our last session you were really reaching into my past, into my childhood. So I wanted to bring you to a common child’s childhood activity. One of the activities I scarcely received as a child.” [Dr. Bower, jotting down a few notes on her yellow tablet, nods at Mac’s explanation. From previous sessions Mac knows to continue when she nods as she is writing.] “I just wanted to be in an environment that might bring back some more of my memories.” [Pausing momentarily to look over to Mackenzie, Dr. Bower smiles at her intelligent. Mac returns the smile while folding her hands together in her lap.] ?Now, why do you think it is you rarely got to visit a playground or park like this in your childhood, Mackenzie?? [Mac half smiles, letting a very small chuckle escape her throat, vaguely remembering one of her first time she was taken to a playground just like this one.] “I’m pretty sure, almost positive, in fact, that it was because I was a little terror. I didn’t listen, I refused to obey, and I would throw a fit rather than share anything with anyone.” [Meditating on her childhood thoughts she pauses and glances up at the playground; there are many kids on the center structure. Playing a game of tag, enjoying each other’s company. Watching, she continues explaining herself to her therapist.] “I kicked everyone off anything I wanted to play on, either by threatening or by actual violence. When my foster father told me it was finally time to go I just ran away from him, and made him catch me. When he finally did, I threw a fit and he had to drag me into the car in the end. I screamed my head off the whole way home, and when I was grounded to my room, I laughed in my foster mother’s face about it.” ?How old were you when you exhibited this type of behavior?? ?Three and a half? Four?? [Dr. Bower makes a small “Hmm” noise in her throat, and scribbles down more notes. After a moment she looks up at Mac, pushing her glasses back up her nose a little.] ?Why do you think it is you demonstrated such angry and violent behavior at that early an age?? [Kenz takes a moment to ponder this question; with her hand she sweeps her curly brown hair back behind her ear. She then crosses her legs and folds her hands back together in her lap. Dr. Bower is jotting more notes down on her tablet now.] “After experiencing life outside those foster homes I’ve realized most of my actions were uncalled for. Since this realization I’ve racked my brain thinking back to my bratty ways, and there is only one logical explanation I’ve been able to muster from all this thinking.” [A calm silence surrounds the both of them; Bower lifts her eyes to Mackenzie’s meditative face, stunned and overwhelmed at the deepness of her thoughts and examinations. She nods at Mac and puts her eyes back down to her paper, where the pen begins to write like crazy. Clearly, Dr. Bower feels no need to ask the obvious question, and Mackenzie feels this, knowing to continue.] “I was annoyed. This was my second foster home. I went through my first when I was two, and I made it there longer than any other home, mostly because I was young and stupid. When I finally got booted from that home I was in a group home for months before going to this second home. No one would explain to me why I was with these people who were not my parents, and no one would tell me where or what happened to my real parents.” [Mac let out a sigh, rubbing her palms on the knees of her jeans. Glimpsing up at the playground again she watched a mother push her little girl around on the tire swing. After a moment Dr. Bower looked up and followed her gaze to the tire swing.] ?Mackenzie. Do you have any memory at all of your biological parents?? [Mackenzie’s response is an unhurried one; it doesn’t take her long to think of it. In fact, it practically spills out her mouth as if she was about to say it before Dr. Bower even asked. However, the slowness is in the reply itself. It’s as if she’s savoring the words, reliving the goodness of the memory.] “One. When I was a baby, I remember looking out of my crib and seeing my mother looking down at me like I was the most precious thing she’d ever seen. But, it was so long ago; I can’t even remember her face now. Although, I remember her voice, she was singing to me. It was beautiful, I wish I could remember the song, or her face for that matter.” ?And your father?? “Nothing.” [Nodding again Doctor Bower sets her pen down and turns to Mackenzie.] “Kenz. You’ve come a very long way from your childhood and teenage years.” [Mac forces a smile, pushing the memory of her mother and the lack of memory of her father to the very back of her mind. Dr. Bower gives her a moment in silence before continuing.] ?By the way, did you ever get the knowledge you were seeking about your parents?? [Kenz is silent for a long moment before shaking her head from side to side.] ?I was told nothing for seventeen years of my life, and when I turned nineteen I jetted so fast they didn’t have time to tell me if they wanted to. Don’t you have records, Dr. Bower? Doesn’t my folder say anything about them? Names? Anything?? “I’m afraid not. I’m sorry, Mac. I only have the information they sent to me from your previous therapists in Colorado. Unfortunately, the records, they end when you’re eight teen..” “When I refused to see anymore therapists.” [Dr. Bower nods, and concludes.] “However, they do not go back before foster care began. I have no records of you before you were two. I’m so sorry.” “Don’t worry about it.” [Mac shifts her weight as she moves to another position on the bench. She put on a slight smile for the Doctor.] “It’s been a long time since I was nineteen. I’m twenty-six now, and I’ve accepted the fact I will never know where I came from before I turned two years old. I’ll deal.” [Dr. Bower, picking her pen back up, writes down a few final notes before checking her watch. She smiles at Mac and shifts in her seat as well.] “Well, Kenz. It seems our time here is up. I’ll see you in about a week and a half? Back in my office.” “Right. Thanks for coming.” [They both stand up from the park bench. Mac shakes the Doctor’s hand. After another goodbye Dr. Bower walks away from Mackenzie, leaving her watching the children at play once again. After standing there for another minute, listening and watching, Mac beings to stroll away from the playground. She heads for the parking lot where her Durango is waiting, leaving behind the smiles and laughter of childhood.]
