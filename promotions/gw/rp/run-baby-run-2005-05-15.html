Run Baby Run

15 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05-15-05 In a sea of pure black satin, she lay without movement, soaking in the eternal waves of nocturnal beauty. Her pristine features are calm; they had not been so for months, even years. Raven hair cascades down around her solemn face, outlining her flawless features. Nevada heat pours through the open window, a breeze pushing past the black curtains. Only the thin satin sheet covers her bare body, the heat her enemy. As usual, she sleeps void of all clothing, keeping herself at one with the world around her. Tonight, not even the heat can disturb her. Weeks of journey without reward had drained her of all life. Back alleys, park benches, and shrubs, her most recent temporary beds, could not compare to this blackened heaven which awaited her. When the floorboard creeks, she does not notice; she does not stir. Still crystallized in an entranced slumber, she would not hear such an intrusion to the picture-perfect environment. Moonbeams filter through the open window, casting a pale yellow light to illuminate her body, specifically her face. The perfect calm of her visage is set for the night. Nothing will break this tranquility. Not the click of the gun or the finger on the trigger. Moments passed, etching an eternity into the distance between the pistol and the bed. A shadow cast over her face, where the gun aligned with her forehead. Waves of excitement passed through his being; this would be the moment they all had waited for. She would be taken care of. His finger began to pull on the trigger, adrenaline pumping through him. In a moment, this game of cat and mouse would be over and he would be victorious. The last ten years would finally come to an end. The mechanisms inside of the gun began to function, the bullet lodging, ready to fill its duty. A smile crossed his lips as he pulled it further, slowly, as to not make noise. The sheets rustle. Startled, he puts the gun down, stepping back into the shadows and watches the sheets intently. A pair of yellow eyes stares at him from the mound of satin, the eyes of Satan himself. Disoriented, he tries to comprehend the situation. As the black cat leaps off the bed, he curses under his breath, angry that he would let such a detail pass his perception. The cat was easily camouflaged in the mountain of black. If he had not been so excited to complete this deed, then he would have taken note. The sudden change in pace leaves him veiled by the shadows. No reason to let another missed detail ruin this agenda. Besides, the cat’s movement may have stirred the beauty lying before him. It had. She stirred only slightly at first, but within a second her eyes were open, groggily looking at the world around her. Black walls and red velvet stare back at her, the same four guardians that protect her every evening. Yet, something was different, there was something wrong. Still laying still, she scanned the room, her brown orbs blurred but alert. Only the walls and shadows appeared, all without movement. Her heart calming, she let her eyes shut once more, knowing she needs this sleep. He only had one chance. He had to make this quick. Stepping forward, he made certain not to let the floor squeak again. Her content and peaceful face appeared before him in the moonlight. She had changed in six years and the time had been gracious. Her features, still flawless, had matured greatly. He could not suppress the stirring in his loins, but this time his agenda would not be interrupted by his natural desire. This time, the job must be done. Quickly, he cocked the gun once more and wasted no time pulling the trigger. Crimson drops painted the black walls, barely visible in the moonlight. He smiled. The fog fell and then lifted. The bridge spanned into the horizon. Cities sprung on either side, but they were too far to conceive journeying to. The fog began to dissipate more and she stood at the edge of the bridge, staring down at the water below her. Weeks had passed, she knew that. They all knew that. Now, standing on the bridge between the two halves on one city, she looked at the tattered nightgown hanging loosely from her shoulders, flowing effortlessly in the high winds. The city lights began to go, dawn quickly approaching. Blood stained her gown; it oozed from her wrists and upper arms. She could still feel the pain below, even though it had been a week since the repercussions were spent. The pain was a constant reminder; the physical pain would pass, but this mental pain would stay forever. She had no other choice. She glanced to her right, staring at something? someone? dead on. Then, with a swift movement, she toppled over the railing and fell to the river below. He sat up, disillusioned and unsure of what had just occurred. All he knew was that everything he had witnessed was rooted in truth. It always was. Quickly, he reached into the inside of his suit jacket and pulled out a cell phone. For a moment, he stared at it. Using it would be a risk. It could put them both in danger, but he had no other choice. Rarely were his notions incorrect. His daughter was in danger. He hit call. Her number was the only in the phone. A lifetime passed before the operator connected the call. The ringing began. Across the country, she sat up in bed, the emergency phone ringing across the room. He waited impatiently, praying he would hear her voice. The phone continued to ring. Even through three time zones, it was definitely late and even as a nocturnal creature of the night; his daughter would surely have been sleeping long ago. But why was she not answering? She slowly got out of bed. Was she dreaming? Why would he call at this hour, and for what reason? Was there a lead? Did he realize that the biggest milestone of her career was coming in less than a week? He cannot expect her to lead two lives in two worlds so different from one another. She groaned as her feet hit the floor, or the cat’s tail, rather. With a pained growl, the cat sped into the next room. She quickly wrapped her sheet around her body and made her way to the dresser where the emergency phone is placed. Only a few more rings and the phone would click into voicemail. Where the hell is she? Then the cold metal pushed against the back of his head. “Hang up the phone.” The voice was deep and instantly recognizable. He had been caught. She picked up the phone and hit the call button. Aggressively pressing it to her ear, she let out a grumbled hello, but received only silence on the other end. Baffled, she listened more intently, swearing she could hear whispering. Then, a loud thud followed by static caused her to jump back a step. Suddenly, the phone cut out and the call ended. She looked at the phone, one eyebrow arced high. He looked down at the phone smashed underneath his boot. He would rather die then let this creep get one step closer to his daughter. The cold metal still pressed to his head, the man behind him pushed him out of the alley and into the city street, understandably deserted at this hour. He had been found, but that did not make sense. He had been more careful than ever. A million possibilities flashed through his mind as he was lead to a black limo, the door being held open by a familiar man in a black tuxedo. Grumbling to himself, he hesitated to enter the vehicle before finally being forced to do so. The man with the gun nodded to his servant before entering the limo. Before the door shut, he managed to say, “your daughter should be more careful, Mr. Taylor.” She stared at the phone longer, knowing that something was amiss. Unless her father had accidentally hit call, there was something terribly wrong. Without proof or a way to contact him, however, there was no way of knowing. Sighing, she placed the phone back in its rightful location and turned back toward the bed. Making her way back, she caught a glimpse of herself in the mirror, wrapped in black satin with her raven tresses falling around her in waves of eternal twilight. She cocked her head, staring at the woman so different from herself liked to taunt her in the mirror. It was inconceivable how the woman staring back at her differed so much from the original copy. “Get with it,” she said as she found her grasp on reality once more. Her father was always careful; there was no way he had fallen into a trap. There was nothing to worry about. Right now, she needs to focus on the match next weekend. Her biggest one to date. She could not lose. She could not fail. She could not? “the limo pulled off into the distance, the man in the tuxedo laying face down on the cold pavement, only a streetlight illuminating the crimson puddle he lay in. Indispensable, like the rest, this one was a known accomplice and spy. Nothing could ruin this sudden switch in power. The tables were turning. In a week, the match would not be the only thing on her mind. In less than a week, she will be defending more than her title. She will be defending her life.
