A return to action

16 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A return to action Sergei Strakhlzhenski Global Wrestling Promo #877 Date: 2/16/04 Location: The Airport Sergei Strakhlzhenski had found his brother. His brother who had been feared dead, and who had a WWII war criminal with him when his plane went down somewhere in the South Atlantic on its way to Brazil, had been found in the Middle East. What was bothersome was that the war criminal was nowhere to be found now, and Igor, Sergei’s brother was keeping a tight lip on it. This angered Surge, as he had converted to the Jewish faith when he moved to the United States in the late 80’s. Surge had gone through many things in this quest to bring the Butcher of Lublin to justice, including a run-in with the Mossad. However, since the case was cold, and his brother, the chief of the Polish Intelligence Service for the region of Lublin, was not co-operating with him. He had decided to leave it alone, which is what Polish Intelligence had wanted from the beginning. However, Surge had also let something slip that had kept him sane over the years. He had ventured into the world of Professional Wrestling, and over the last months, as he tracked down his brother, he had left that world behind completely. Now, months later, he was ready to return. Surge had gotten married in the interim to his valet, Francine West, known as Frankie to all of her friends. Frankie had hit it big in the fassion industry, working on setting new European trends. It was Frankie that had designed most of Surge’s outfits during the part of his career that she was around. That had paid off for her, in that she had been taking correspondence courses with the money she got from being his valet in wrestling. Now that she was into a career of her own, Surge was happy to return to his, but this would mark the first time he had wrestled without a valet or manager. Before Frankie, Surge’s manager had been Frankie’s father, Quinten. Quin was a CIA agent who was currenty assigned to Iraq. He had been there for some time, only getting leave for his daughter’s wedding. He had helped Surge track down his brother in the Middle East, and he was also the one that talked Surge into dropping the whole affair. Somehow, Surge thought, the CIA wanted this man alive as much as Polish and Russian Intelligence. Surge knew what reason. Surge and his brother had been products of this man’s work. They were both genetically engineered human beings, who towered over the rest of the world at over 8’. Igor had been singled out by the KGB during the Communist Era, but Sergei had been spared due to his father’s political influence. Instead, Surge had gone to Polish Intelligence, only to rebel and defect with the help of Quin. All of that was history… water under the bridge. Now was the time for Surge to return to what he enjoyed. He had called and secured a meeting with the McCoy family, and asked to come back full time to Global Wrestling. He didn’t know what capacity he would hold, whether that of janitor, or participant, but he did like the fact that he was getting back into the business. He didn’t know what has been going on, because he hadn’t had time to keep up during his hunt for his brother, but he was ready to make his comeback and make his mark on the business. As the plane landed in the US, he wondered if he would go through the usual debriefing with the CIA. It seemed that every time his private jet landed there, someone from that agency was waiting with a few questions for him. He scanned the surroundings with his eyes as the jet taxied to a stop outside the private airport terminal. So far, he couldn’t see anyone suspicious looking, but he hadn’t looked hard enough yet, either. After shut down, the pilot stepped back and opened the hatch, letting Surge out. Surge walked into the terminal, and waited for the pilot to bring his baggage to him. It was then that he saw them, a small group of people in dark suits hovering around an SUV. Behind the SUV was his limo. His driver was standing there talking on a cell phone, an angry look on his face. Apparently, he wasn’t going to get his fare today. Surge shook his head. This had to quit. He was going to have to talk to his brother or Quin about it. For now, though, he would have to endure it. Upon seeing him, one of the men spoke into his wrist, and then approached Surge. “Mr. Strakhlzhenski,” asked the man. “Vat do you vant,” asked Surge, in a gruff voice. “I have no patience for zis today. I hired zat limousine, and I vill be using it.” “Please, sir,” said the man. “Don’t make us get rough with you.” “Rough vis me,” asked Surge, in a threatening tone. “Little man, I vill crush you vis my bare hands if you try to delay me. I have business in zis country zat has noting to do vis intelligence. I am on permanent leave, and vil remain so until my career in vrestling ends.” “We’re not so sure of that,” said the man. “So if you’ll just come along with us, we’ll make that determination.” Surge sighed, then grabbed the man by the collar, lifting him into the air. “Little man, I varned you, now I am telling you. I vill be going vis my limo driver to zi arena in zis town. You are free to follow me, and ask me questions vonce I speak vis my employer. Until zen, you vill have to suffice yourself vis tracking me.” Surge tossed the man away into the wall, brushing his hand and walking over to his baggage. He picked up his bags and walked to the limousine, where there were three other men waiting. Surge sighed again, giving his bags to the limo driver. “Put zeez in zi car,” he said. “I vill be going vis you.” “Excuse me, sir,” said one of the men. “I believe you are scheduled to come with us.” “You can believe vatever you vish,” said Surge. “I am going vis zis man, and zere is noting you can do about it.” Surge stepped up to the door, but one of the men put his hand on it and put his other into his jacket. Surge looked at him angrily, going into action quickly. He smashed the man on top of the head, knocking him out instantly, then whipped around, smashing the second man in the mouth with the back of his fist. The man slumped to the ground unconscious. He then turned to the third man, who was backing away and pulling his pistol. Surge opened the door and stepped into the limo, closing the door behind him. The driver, who had had the since to duck into the car earlier, looked in the mirror and smiled. “Where to, sir,” he said. “Zi arena, and make is qvick,” said Surge, as he the car jetted out around the SUV. The remaining two men chased the limo, pulling their pistols, but neither bothered to fire. Surge chuckled to himself as the car sped along towards its destination. “I told you I vas going vere I pleased.”
