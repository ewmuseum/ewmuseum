Audition

20 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/20/04 The set is a mockup of a courtroom. It has been used in several TV shows like soap operas and courtroom drams and in movies. Thanks to a favor from a friend, Brick is using it now. Brick needs the set to film a sort of audition tape. He’s filming an audition tape to send to the producers and director of the Luke Cage movie in development. Actors are there, playing viewers of the trial and various family members and friends of Carl Lucas a.k.a. Luke Cage. “Luke Cage audition tape, take one!” “And action!” Judge: Members of the jury, have you reached a verdict? Foreperson: We have, your honor? Judge: What say you? Foreperson: On the charge of possession of an illegal substance with intent to sell, we find the defendant Carl Lucas…guilty. Brick’s face registers shock, as do the faces of the actors watching the trial. All except for one, the actor playing the role of Willis Stryker, the man who framed Lucas. Brick looks back at all the other actors. He then sees the actor playing Stryker. He sees the smug look on his face. Brick: It was you… Brick then lunges at “Stryker” and grabs him by the throat. Brick: IT WAS YOU!!!! Judge: Bailiff, restrain the prisoner! Actors playing bailiffs grab Brick and pull him away from “Stryker”. Brick: You son of a bitch, you set me up! I know it was you! Judge: Bailiff, remove the prisoner! The actors playing bailiffs drag the struggling Brick away. Brick: I’ll get you for this, Willis! I’ll get you! Director: And cut! Beautiful, just beautiful! Hours later… Brick is now on a set, designed to look like a prison cell. Here, Brick sits with an actor playing Dr. Noah Bernstein. “And action!” “Bernstein”: So, Lucas, why don’t you tell me about yourself? Brick: I grew up on the streets in Harlem. Back then, my best friend was a dude named Willis Stryker. We used to run in gangs together when we were kids, stealing purses, robbing liquor stores, stuff like that. Then, times changed and I wanted out. I worked a few odd jobs here and there. Willis stayed in the gangs, though, lookin’ to make a name for himself. But we were still friends. Then came Reva Connors. We both loved her and for a while, she was with Willis. Then, he got hurt and we just started getting’ closer. That didn’t sit too well with Willis, so he planted heroin in his apartment and tipping off police, which o’ course, lands me here. It was bad enough, all the brawls and the beatings from Rackham. Then I heard that Reva Now I'm livin' for one thing, and one thing only -- to get out! Get out an' get Willis! “Bernstein”: Lucas, I believe I may have the key to your freedom. You see, I’m conducting an experiment in the electro-biochemical stimulation of human cell regeneration.” Brick: Electro-what? “Bernstein”: If this process is successful, it will slow down the effects of disease and aging. If you agree to volunteer for this experiment and it’s successful, then I may be able to arrange for you to receive an early release. That is what you want, isn’t it, Lucas? Freedom from Seagate? Brick shrugs his shoulders. Brick: What have I got to lose? “And cut! That’s a wrap, folks! See you all tomorrow!” Brick and the actors all begin to leave the studio. He’s weary from all the takes that they have had to do, but it’s worth it. Brick is looking to accomplish his goal of playing Luke Cage and he will do so, however he has to.
