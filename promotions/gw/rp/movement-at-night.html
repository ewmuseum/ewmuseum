Movement At Night

Date: Post Katrina To The Big Easy There was a new storm building in the Gulf. Not yet a hurricane, the forecasts thought it could become one easily. After the destruction of Katrina, everyone feared the new storm could cause another round of devastation. He didn’t have time to worry about such concerns’at least not for the moment. Stephen Reed had chosen a jeep for their travels. The vehicle had been outfitted with a snorkel in case they ran into any deep water, and the tires ensured they would find traction wherever they might need it. Louisiana was essentially a barren land, where people were fleeing out of instead of into unless they were part of the emergency relief crew. The truck loaded down with tons of supplies had been sent ahead hours earlier due to the slow rate of travel. They would catch up easily speeding down the road at what officers of the law might call unsafe speeds. Safety was such a relative term. They were loaded down with protein bars, water and some other select foodstuffs for the trip. They knew the search could take a long time, and they couldn’t rely on finding supplies in the ravaged parishes they would be visiting. They needed to be self-reliant, right down to the box beneath the supplies filled with weapons and ammunition. Stephen pulled into the drive of Hawk’s country estate and moved up the paved road. Half a mile later he pulled under the awning to the front door. He honked, not relying on time to be on their side. The front door opened, and there was Hawk looking strange as far as Stephen was concerned. Instead of a suit, something Hawk always wore, he was dressed in khakis and a long sleeve button-up along with a pair of good hiking boots. He was out of character, but perfectly dressed for their endeavor even as he leaned on his cane. “You look ready to go.” Stephen said as he stepped out. Hawk shrugged as he handed his bag off to the younger man as he approached, “I am. We’ve got things to take care of, and not enough time to do it. Any word from Jake on location?” “No, he’s working on it.” Stephen opened the door and wedged Hawk’s bag into the back of the full jeep. “If Sid can figure it out, Jake says he can do it. He just isn’t quite sure how. If he figures it out he’ll let us know.” Hawk shook his head as he placed his cane in the back, “This just gets stranger and stranger.” “Hey, I never thought I would spend my older years doing this stuff. I thought I would be fat, happy and surrounded by the kids. Not everything goes as planned.” Stephen laughed, but as he walked past the rear of the vehicle his face broke as the pain overcame him. Touching old emotions always loosened his controls. He pushed it away, and resumed his brave face. Stephen Reed slipped back into the jeep, “Okay, let’s get going.” As the car pulled out Hawk continued the conversation on planning, “So we’re meeting up with the truck?” “Yeah, we’ll escort it in past the troops and then take off on our own. All the paperwork as relief workers is in order, and no one should give us any hassle about it. We’ve made some generous contributions, and the water and generators are appreciated.” Stephen liked this part. Working the system to get what you needed was like a game of chess to him, and he was a master chess player. Hawk leaned back against his seat, “Wake me when we get there.” Meditative Revelation Everything in the universe is connected. From the atoms making up all of the structures of people and things to the unknown connections God had strung together between his entire creation everything is connected. Ashes to ashes and dust-to-dust meant more than a simple return to earth. The return included every part of the physical being returning to the cycle of life and creation. Energy had a different trip to take in passing. Within that connection, there were paths people could use to find anything they are looking for, if they have the power. Of course, very few people had such a power. Even those who did could usually only find connections to someone they were extremely close to, like twins knowing when their counterparts are in danger. Learning to maneuver through those feelings and connections was a difficult ability to master. Without any kind of guide to teach him how, he had been left to his own devices. His home came with a sauna, and he had the temperature up as high as he could. Jake Hix looked through the door into the sauna, knowing the red wood would be his home until he found what he was looking for in Louisiana. The bathrobe was thrown off, leaving him in a pair of black boxer briefs. He opened the sauna and stepped inside as the hit air hit him, constricting his skin and throat. He walked over to the wooden bench and sat down. He tried to relax, entire body slowly going limp and peaceful as he waited to see what would happen. The arduous journey would not be easy. He had to think only of Xavier as he set all other concerns and idea aside in his mind. As the heat in the room ravaged his body, he continued to focus on the image and thought of his brother. He felt himself begin to pull away from the physical stimuli as he began floating in his own mind. Finding his brother was the most important thing he could do at the moment. Through The Gates Of Hell Faces marked the passing of time in years and in experience. Tragedy aged people more than the simple pace of living along with other environmental factors. People who lived in the sunniest regions often had deep facial lines from the constant squinting along with rough, leathery skin from the constant exposure. You could tell a lot about people by looking for the signs of what they had experience in their lives. Driving past the long lines of refugees still escaping the state, the lines of aging were profound. People had lived through a tragic experience, made worse by the ineptitude of their leaders and elected officials. People’s faces were drawn and wasted, from emotions spent and nutrition unquenched. Everyone had been deeply affected by the tragedy, and everyone was showing the strains of their experience. Stephen drove by, wondering what people saw in his face. Would they see the quickly changing face as it began to pale and grow hallow thanks to the decay eating away at his insides? Did they know he was on the road to the end? Stephen had been lucky so far. All his friends were too focused on the problems at hand to notice the change in him. He was old, and people could attribute many changes to the ravages of aging. Of course, for Stephen that was not the problem. He had a large amber bottle filled with white pills to control the pain his own self-control had long ago been unable to fight against anymore. No one knew’yet. Finding the Hix family had given him something he missed. He had a purpose once again. Driving along the broken, poorly maintained rural roads he stared off into the amethyst and gold sunset as the night advanced on the world like an enemy robbing the people of the serenity of sunshine even as it brought the relief of cooler temperatures. The day brought hope, whereas the night still brought fear of what might lay beyond the circle of vision. Such concerns didn’t matter. They needed to find the family, and they were running out of time. There were other predators in the darkness, and they weren’t looking to rescue the family. They were looking to devour them. The Black Water Spoiled by the currents churning up the messy, murky mud from the bottom of the bayou, the chemical contamination channeled into the streams and of course the simple debris of destruction the river and bayous had changed. What was once fairly peaceful churn had become fast and violent in some area as the floodwaters found new escapes. The water was no longer dirty and mossy; it was now simply black and cursed. Driving the boat through the river was difficult, slow going work to ensure you didn’t hit anything to large. Such items included cars and houses even hidden beneath the mess. Malum was not concerned. Any of the disciples who disappointed him on this trip would face the wrath of their master. The three boats knew they were in the right area, but finding the exact location had challenging. They knew the Hix family was in a boat of their own, and they were constantly on the move. They were getting closer and closer. Soon enough, he would capture the bright shining light of hope, bind him in chains and present the prize to his master. Unfortunately, between his grand designs and machinations Malum was also running over to the side of the boat and hurling his stomach contents into the water. Boats did not agree with him, and he found it difficult to keep up his brave fa’ade. One of the men came up to him, “Sir, we have the Dark Messiah on the phone for you.” “Okay.” A horrible sound of muffled cries and strange guttural groans came out along with the sudden onset of dry heaves. “I’ll be right there.” Malum wiped his mouth and headed back to the enclosed bridge of the small boat. He took the cell phone from one of the men, “This Malum my master.” “Have you located them?” Sid’s voice was not patient or friendly. Malum collected himself before responding, “We are in the area, but they are moving around in the boat. We will find them soon my Lord, and then we will bring them to you as promised.” “Do not fail me Malum.” Sid’s warning also included a twinge of pain in the base of Malum’s brain. Just another ability he had over those he had empowered, “I will not tolerate failure Malum.” “Yes my Lord.” Malum said just before the line went dead. Malum walked over to the man leading the mission. His name was Ashok Assudani, and he had been one of the first recruits to the cause. He was very proficient at his job, which just happened to be violence and killing people. “Ashok, have you found them yet?” “Not yet. We thought we had them just up here, but they were gone. We will have them soon enough. They are only four people, and we have the advantage in forces, weaponry and surprise.” Ashok smiled evilly, his dirty, yellowed teeth showing through his thick black beard. “Good, very good.” Malum returned to the outside, where his stomach told him he would remain. No More Running All the power in the boat was off, and it was parked next to a home covered in water to help conceal the location. They were looking for movement, signature or even power signs. None of that would be made available as Pitt, Alexandria and Bella watched the group of three boats pass them by. Pitt had been paranoid ever since Xavier had died for those few brief moments. He knew that Sid would know what had happened, and then their hiding place would be out in the open. He didn’t like the idea at all, and so keen eyes had peered out in all directions throughout the day looking for anyone who would try and come after them. Three boats moving swiftly didn’t seem right in the area, and so they had deftly avoided them for several hours. Night’s onset and the quickly dwindling area for maneuverability were ruining their plans. They needed a new strategy, and Pitt wasn’t sure any of them would work. “Three boats, with plenty of crew on each boat.” Pitt pursed his lips together as he stared through the night vision binoculars. “This doesn’t look good at all.” “We can’t keep running.” Bella checked the maps as she spoke. Alexandria looked over her shoulder, “What about somewhere to land” They still think we’re on the river.” “If I’m right, they’ll know wherever we go. Sid and Xavier are connected, and without the masquerade of holy ground we’re screwed ladies. We need some help to get us out of this situation.” Pitt pulled the binoculars away from his eyes. A weak voice spoke from the edge of the cabin, “Then let’s not run anymore.” The three of them turned to see Xavier leaning on something in the doorway. He staggered forward slowly, and then picked up his makeshift cane. They saw it was a shotgun, “If they’re after us, then let’s make sure they can’t keep coming. We take the three boats out, and then get out of here.” Bella walked over to her man. She put her arm around him, “I don’t think you’re up to that.” “She’s right Xavier, we’ll find another way.” Alexandria agreed. Xavier gently removed his love’s arm, “No. If we run, we die. If we fight, we have a chance. They will never stop chasing us. We have to put a stop to them here and now.” Pitt looked his brother up and down. He should have been in bed, resting after the ordeal in the house. He should have been happily retired, living a life he deserved with Bella. None of that was meant to be though, and Pitt couldn’t let it end here. “We packed everything, right Xavier?” Pitt asked. Xavier’s nod was full of strength his body didn’t have, “Down in the hold.” “I agree with Xavier, if we keep running we’re all dead. Before the sun goes down tomorrow we’ll run out of room, and they won’t just kill us. Sid will want his prizes. We fight now, and we stand a good chance of making it. We were handed plenty of weapons to protect ourselves, and we brought them.” Pitt shifted his vision, staring hard at Bella and then Alex as he spoke. “What weapons?” Bella didn’t know about them. “Let me show you.” Pitt led everyone down into the small cabin where the sleeping quarters were. He unscrewed a section of the wall, and exposed an arsenal. The girls looked at the weapons, and a plan was quickly formed. Bella pointed to a weapon, “We can take out one boat with that, but we’ll still have two left we have to deal with.” “We ram one of the boats, and then reduce it to one-on-one combat.” Xavier had a twisted little smile. “I sent him to Hell before, and when we send him back we’ll make sure all his little buddies are there to keep him company.” . . . Ashok continued his grid search. He thought he had seen a brief flash of light, but then he couldn’t find it again. They continued on, slowing only long enough for him to dismiss his suspicions. Normally, Ashok was busy handling the blackmail and intimidation for on behalf of the Dark Messiah. Maintaining power was part of the key to keeping the worldly authorities from interfering in their work. Ashok studied his map, wondering what else they could do to find these renegades. He needed to get the glory and the recognition for all his hard work. He would capture the shining star of the campaign, and then perhaps he would be most favored in the Stone Court of his master. Ashok was still studying the maps when a sudden illumination of yellow light hit the cabin. He looked up to see the streaming flash of light, but then the explosion brought daylight to the entire cabin. The RPG had sent the explosive round across the darkness into the third boat. The shot was perfect, and the initial explosion was followed by a second series of explosion when the generator, gas tank and secondary gas tanks all exploded in succession. The boat flew up out of the water, and landed on a patch of land. Alex started the engines, and she pushed the throttle to full. She had a line directly at the second vehicle, and she wasn’t planning on letting up. They had to take the second vehicle out of the equation to stand a chance. Pitt had done as promised with the RPG, and now Bella and Xavier would get their chance as soon as she connected. Alex held on tightly as she approached the turning boat. The bow slammed right into the side of the boat, pushing it up slight and back before together they hit the bank of land. Bella and Xavier stood up. Each of them pulled the pin, and tossed the grenade as hard as they could. The grenades went through the glass windows of the boats. They each ducked back down and crawled to get more protection from what was coming. The two explosions were near simultaneous. They sent shrapnel and fire through the entire cabin of the cruiser. The men inside were ripped to pieces, and no one was left to fight back. Inside their own boat Alex tried to reverse course, but the rudder was smashed on some of the debris and not moving anymore. She kept trying back and forth, but they were stuck. “We’re not moving!” Alex yelled. Pitt came inside, “Get down below and stay there!” Alex ran down into one of the bedrooms. Pitt checked over the AK-47 as he was walked out onto the deck of the boat. The third boat was turning back toward them. He took aim and pulled the trigger. Illegal though it might have been, the fully automatic weapon was needed at the moment. The bullets tore into the boat, and also into the men who were trying to come out from inside with their own weapons “Out!” Pitt yelled as he dropped down behind the wall of the boat. Xavier and Bella came up. He was using the shotgun while Bella preferred the automatic pistol. Their shots were less frequent, but somewhat more accurate as they were able to hit two of their enemies. They were soon out, and the other boat began flooding the deck with men. They ducked back down knowing the plan would be working. “No, take cover!” Ashok yelled at his men. He saw Pitt standing back up with the banana clip loaded into the assault rifle. He took cover behind the wall of the cabin, knowing his own men had just fallen into the trap. Pitt Hix had the weapon on semi-automatic now. He took his time, using steady if fast breathing to help remain as calm as possible while his heart threatened to explode out of his chest. The bullets were grouped more tightly together, and he was able to take down three of the five men on deck before dropping back down. He had bullets left, but he didn’t have any more clips. He wanted to conserve his ammunition as much as possible. Return fire came now. The boat was riddled with bullets as the men tried to take revenge. Pitt looked down at Xavier who held the last of the grenades. He nodded at his brother. Xavier pulled the pin and tossed the grenade blind at the other nearby boat. The grenade hit the deck and skidded across. The disciples saw the grenade, and stood to run for cover. They were almost to the side of the boat; ready to jump off when the explosion sent them flying. Even inside the cabin, Ashok was tossed aside as shrapnel hit his shoulder. Malum was still cowering in the corner. Pitt Hix stood up. He aimed down at the bodies in the water and continually pulled the trigger as he finished off the disciples in the water. He ran out of rounds, but he still saw two of the figures moving. Pitt drew his sidearm from his hip holster. He took slow aim, and ended the final two. “Clear here.” Pitt said as he looked around for any movement. Xavier looked at where the boat had been rammed, “Clear here.” Bella joined them, still holding onto her pistol tightly. Inside the cabin of the final boat, Ashok stood slowly. He pointed to the broken window, “Go, we must escape.” “I’I?” Malum was still stuck in the corner, unmoving as the fear gripped him tightly. Ashok grabbed Malum by the shoulder, “Get up and run. Run as if the Dark Lord’s breath were at your back. We must get out of here!” Malum still seemed stuck, but the stout Ashok picked him up off the deck and tossed him through the window. He turned to the boat where the family was and began firing his own weapon. Pitt, Xavier and Bella all dove for cover. The shots continued coming, destroying the boat even further as they tried to regroup. “Guess we missed someone.” Pitt laughed. Bella shot him a horrible glare, “You find this funny?” “Beats sitting on my ass rebuilding a house.” Pitt dropped the clip out of his pistol and slid another into place. Xavier was ignoring the conversation as he loaded the shotgun with new shells. He had a snarl on his face as he jumped over the side of the boat into the water. Pitt didn’t know what was going on, but he popped up enough to shoot a few rounds at the man in the cabin. As the sudden quiet descended on the scene, Ashok stepped out. He was looking for a shot, but Pitt had only been lying in wait for the man to expose himself. He shot with deliberate aim, nailing him in the left shoulder and chest. The man dropped his weapon, and stumbled back to the edge of the boat. He fell over into the water. Xavier was already on his way, swimming over as he kept one arm above water where he held the shotgun. As Ashok made his way to land holding onto his wounds, Xavier’s feet found the soft soil underneath the water. He began stepping slowly as his weight sunk into the mud. He slowly emerged from the water, his bottom half covered in the thick black mud. Ashok had barely made any headway as the pain and blood loss was sending him into shock. He looked out at the broken path through the vegetation where Malum had run, “Go! Tell the Dark Lord! RUN! RUN FAST YOU FOOL!” Malum heard the words as he ran through the darkness. Xavier was marching toward Ashok. He kicked the man in the side of the head, flipping him over onto his back. Xavier pointed the shotgun at the man’s head as he loomed over him. “The morning light of hope. Keep running, we’ll find you soon enough. Our Lord is prophesized as the Dark Messiah.” Ashok laughed, enjoying the end he knew all along would come one day. He had given his life for his master. Xavier’s words were harsh and cold as he spoke. “No more running.” “Tell yourself whatever you like, but you’ll run. You’ll always run, and we’ll be there.” Ashok was looking into the man’s eyes, but now he wondered if he were wrong. Xavier pointed the shotgun down at Ashok’s knee. He squeezed the trigger, and the shotgun tore through the leg, severing it completely at close range. “No more running.” Xavier pumped the empty shell out. He pointed the shotgun at the other knee and pulled the trigger. Another leg was gone. “No more running!” Xavier pumped it again, and then shot the man in the crotch. “NO MORE RUNNING!” Another pump was followed by a shot to the belly. “NO MORE RUNNING!” Xavier followed up with a shot to the chest. “NO MORE RUNNING!” The Fallen Angel, long on the run, hiding from the world and his own tormented memories of three lives shared was staring down at the long dead man with a dark fury in his eyes. He squeezed the trigger again as the round obliterated the man’s face. “NO MORE RUNNING!!!” Another click, but no shot went into the decimated skull. “NO MORE RUNNING!!!” Click. “NO MORE RUNNING!!!” Click. “NO MORE RUNNING!!!” Light In The Hazy Darkness Within the confines of his mind, he kept looking for some sign he could latch onto. Nothing was appearing for him, and he needed to find direction. All his thoughts were on his brother, and sometimes something seemed to be coming into the light’only to evaporate as he reached out to it. Suddenly there was a flash of light in front of him. He saw his brother Xavier’s face for a brief moment, and then another flash of intense white light blinded him completely. He kept staring into the light, and saw Xavier now. He was standing over a body in the real world, but here he saw Xavier as he truly was. Huge black wings hung overhead as he held the shotgun over the man’s body. Jake saw his brother as he reclaimed himself for what he once was. The nickname had deeper meaning than simply being a catchy moniker. Xavier Black a.k.a. Seth Hix a.k.a. a little of Sid Hix was truly the Fallen Angel once again. The Ascension was once again at hand. Jake was thrown out of his vision into the real world. He fell forward onto the red wood boards of the floor. He crawled over to the door, energy drained and spent. He unlatched the door and pushed it open. The sudden rush of cold air onto his skin almost sent him into shock, but he fought it off. Jake crawled over to where he had laid his cell phone. He opened it up and hit redial. The phone was picked up immediately. “This is Stephen.” “SSR, I know where they are my friend. You have to get to them. Are ready for coordinates?” Jake was breathing erratically, but he had found his family. The Scene The coordinates had been perfect. They led them directly to where the family was’well, where they had been a few hours before at least. Hawk and SSR were looking around the battle scene with weary eyes. If anyone came up it would look strange for these men to be looking through the wreckage. Hawk was inspecting the boats that could only be reached through a short swim. They knew which boat belonged to the family, and Hawk started there. The boat taken some damage from a collision with another boat, some explosion damage and enough bullets to make his heart sink as he inspected the hull and deck. SSR concentrated his attention on the terrain near the water and the boat where several corpses floated around in the water and there was more gunfire than any other boat. Stephen wondered who had been here, but the scene on shore still haunted him. The clothes marked him as one of the disciples, but he was pulverized by repeated blast from what must have been a shotgun at extremely close range. The action played out in his imagination, but mostly he found himself proud of his friends. No matter the outcome, they had put up one Hell of a fight. They had taken out plenty of these damned disciples, and one more soul sent back to Hell where it belonged didn’t burden his heart one bit. “Stephen!” Hawk hollered as he walked back out onto the deck. Sensational Stephen Reed looked over, “What is it Hawk?” “I don’t have any blood here.” Hawk held his arms out wide, “Nothing. This was their boat, and there’s plenty of damage to the hull, but it doesn’t look like anyone got hit. The one boat is gone, just a blackened frame. That boat was hit with some kind of explosive, and then bullets. I think everyone there was dead.” “Same thing here. Someone got away, but someone else who tried to escape to shore didn’t have such good luck. I think our friends are safe.” Stephen nodded as he looked around. “What do we do now?” Hawk wondered aloud. Stephen jumped off the boat onto the shore, “If they’re on the ground now, then they’ll work to blend in. We need to find the shelters and evacuation centers around here. We have their pictures, and we’ll just pound the ground.” “Who’s calling Jake?” “I’ll do it.” Stephen pulled his cell phone out. “I finally deliver SOME good news. His family is alive.”
