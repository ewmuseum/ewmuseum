'Demons', part 1

29 September 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 29th, 2003 Police tape is meant to keep you out of a crime scene. But it didn't stop him. It never could. This was his home, and nothing would keep Akodo Rokku from it. Not now. He had a mission, and he'd be damned if anything kept him from it. Glass from his shattered bay window crunched under his feet as he walked slowly across the living room. He studied the damage that earlier he had barely registered. Everything felt so much more real now, more visceral -- earlier, it had been like walking through a dream. No, not a dream. A horrible, horrible nightmare. He slid his fingers over the arm of his couch, feeling the deep cuts in it. Some of them were clean and even. But a few? they were rough and jagged. More like tears than cuts. Like they weren't made by blades, but claws. The claws of an animal. Or a beast in the skin of a man. There, where the coffee table was smashed, was where he had found him earlier -- the battered and barely conscious man the police had later taken away. Over there, was the television, its screen smashed in, blood coloring the jagged glass. All over the house were the signs of battle, belongings broken and structure damaged. It was obvious she had given her attackers a hell of a fight. But here, where the wall was broken in, and blood was spattered on the floor? That was where she'd made her last stand. That was where she had fallen. Someone with great strength must have slammed her into the wall here, knocking her unconscious. Someone stronger than a normal man. Slowly -- as if he was absorbing everything around him, the pain and chaos that the ruined rooms radiated -- he walked through the living room again, and ascended the stairs to the second floor. He walked into the his bathroom, flipping on the light, and dropped the box in his hand on the counter. He opened the box and pulled out the tie that held his hair back, letting it fall free. Quickly he went to work using box's contents for their intended purpose. An hour later, his once-blonde hair was a dull black. Leaving the mess where it lay, Rokku slapped the bathroom light off, and went to his bedroom. He didn't bother to turn the light on this time -- he knew exactly where what he wanted was. First, he stripped off his clothes, letting them fall to the floor. Then, he reached into his closet and pulled out a heavy steel case. He heaved it onto his bed, and for a moment, just stood there, staring at it. For years he had wondered if he would ever open this case again. He had hoped he'd never have to. It reminded him of dark times in his past. Times when he had done bad things for bad people, when he'd made good people suffer, just to get by. Times he never wanted to have to think about again. But he would have to remember them. He would have to be that man again, to do what needed to be done. The law couldn't save her -- they could never touch the person who had done this. Rokku took a deep breath, and opened the case. Inside was a stack of clothing, all in black. First, he pulled out a T-shirt. He slid it on, then slipped the kevlar inserts into the pockets covering it. Next was a pair of heavy black cargo pants. He donned them as well, then pulled several tools -- and other, less legal objects -- from the case, and slipped them into the various pockets. After that, he pulled the heavy black boots from the case and put them on, tying them tightly. Next were the gloves -- supple black leather -- which he slipped onto his hands. Following that was a belt, with a pair of adjustable loops attached to it. He put the belt on, then lifted his swords from the stand at his bedside and slid them into the loops, making sure they closed tightly around the saya. Then there was the coat. He pulled the heavy black duster from the case and held it up, looking at the golden lion mon stitched into the back. The coat had been a gift from the yakuza boss he'd worked for once -- a reward for exemplary service on the day they had parted company. A shiver shot Rokku's spine as he thought of that day. It was on that day that he'd first seen this symbol, which he would eventually make his own. And it was that man who had said he was, 'bold and unfailing, like a steel lion.' Why had he chosen to make those things part of who he was now? Immediately he chided himself. Why ask yourself a question if you already know the answer? It was because everything he had ever done was a part of who he was now, had helped make him The Steel Lion. Allowing himself a small chuckle, Rokku put the coat on. Finally, there was only one object left in the case. He lifted it out with a delicate touch, and stared at it. To some, the red steel mempo, with its intricate etchings that made it look like the scowling maw of a demon, would have been a work of art. To Akodo Rokku, it was a symbol. It was the line between the good man he had struggled to become and the demon he had long ago been. If he put this on, there was no turning back, and it would not come off until she was safe and all who had hurt her were broken or dead. Rokku slipped the mempo on, covering the lower half of his face, and buckled the straps that held it on tightly. Swiftly, Rokku left the room and darted down the stairs. In the distance, he could hear sirens coming closer -- one of the neighbours must have alerted the police to someone being in the house. Shooting out the front door, Rokku leapt into his car and peeled out of the driveway, blatantly disregarding legal speed limits. The last thing he needed now were the police, telling him again that they'd do all they could, that he should leave everything to them and they'd call him if they found something? No. Rokku knew exactly who was behind this, and knew the police couldn't handle him. At first he had thought it might be Soma. But when his shock ended and his head cleared, he realized it wasn't likely. This wasn't Soma's style at all, to attack so blatantly and openly. He preferred to play mind games and pick apart your psyche before moving in for the kill. No, Soma wouldn't do something like this. But his father would. The monster in his father's skin had professed so much hatred that Rokku didn't doubt for a moment that he would do something like this. He only hoped that he hadn't killed her. Rokku realized that he was relying on his father's sick perversions -- that he would rather keep her alive and torment her for a while, that he was maybe even using her as bait to draw him into a trap. 'I'll find you, Yoshiko,' whispered Rokku, as he took the corner hard, nearly rising onto two wheels. 'I'll find you, I swear it. And I will make that monster pay.'
