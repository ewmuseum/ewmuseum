Signposts: Chapter 1 - Siren

10 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/10/04 Winters jumped off the back of the truck and less than gently slapped the side of it, producing a loud metal clunking noise, signalling to the driver to carry on. The truck had stopped at a god-awful service station on the outskirts of Kansas state, it was the last stop on the truck diver’s list and now he would be turning around to head back into the heart of Kansas once again, something that Winters wished to avoid at all costs. The driver of the truck informed him that trucks leading out of Kansas state passed through there on a regular basis, so getting out of the state shouldn’t be too much trouble. Winters stood at the edge of the road, wearily eying up the three buildings that made up to be the service station. The dingy-looking buildings looked inhospitable to say the least, and Winters found himself hoping that the information gained by the driver was at least marginally accurate. The service stations consisted of only three buildings: the first, a grubby-looking diner, which had an unhealthy-looking, rusted, metallic exterior; the second, a small gas station, with an adjoined shop with only three aisle’s worth of products; and the third appeared to be a make-shift garage of some kind, which didn’t appear to be too busy at the moment, as the mechanic was sat out front, in the sun, sipping what appeared to be iced tea. Winters examined the car park on the right of the service station allotment. He couldn’t see any trucks as of yet, but there were a number of cars. He figured that most of them would be owned by the various workers employed in the diner, or the gas station or the garage, but some of them had to be customers. Perhaps one of them would be able to transport him out of the state. However, he very much doubted it. Winters suspected that nobody would help him for nothing and he had very little to offer anyone. He still had the sheriff’s wallet, but that only had about fifty bucks or so in it; not nearly enough to purchase sufficient passage. Winters forced the thought from his mind and decided to worry about that problem when - or indeed if - it happened. Winters made his way to the diner, hefty open the thick glass doors and chose a cubicle right next to the exit. He had no idea why he had the impulse to do such a thing, but he figured it was instinct and if nothing else the incident in the small Kansas town had taught him to trust his instincts without question. Winters eyed the room up. There was a group of students at the far end of the diner, probably on a road trip of some kind, but he figured that would be no room in their mode of transport. A couple of cubicles down, sat a family of three emanating the glow of safety’no chance of help from them. Winters slipped into the cubicle seat, instinctively grabbing a poorly laminated menu from his grubby plastic holder. From his position he could see no more people, however, the diner turned off to the right ahead and there could possibly be more people around the corner, but it wouldn’t do him any good to get up and take a look. Winters figured that it wasn’t worth worrying about. Hell, he was sat right by the one and only exit. If there were other people around the corner, they would have to pass him to leave. Winters had only read about half of the menu when the waitress arrived. For some strange reason he felt a buzz about his senses, as he felt her presence. That was strange; it’d never happened before. Winters glanced up at the beautiful brunette waitress and instantly felt a wave of brief familiarity sweep over him like an out of control tidal wave. Was that a memory? Was it a brief recollection of the past? Was this waitress a person from his past, or did she merely resemble somebody from his past? “What can I get you?” The waitress asked, with a rather distinguished glint in her eyes. “I’ll just have the special’huh”.this is going to sound strange, but’have we met before?” Winters asked. A smile swept across her face. “You wish, huh?” Winters visibly deflated. Perhaps the sight of the woman merely jolted up a memory of the past? Perhaps he didn’t know her in his past life. “I’m sorry. Stupid question I guess. My memory’s bad, but I thought that you might’ve been from the past.” Winters added with a sigh. “Who’s to say I’m not?” The waitress replied. “You know, they never thought you would remember me right away. They said that you would need to see me a few more times before it kicked in.” Winters hopes expanded just as quickly as they were just deflated. He stood up and got face to face with the woman, but she gently put her hands upon his shoulders and forced him back into his seat. Winters couldn’t find the power to resist her actions. “You know who I am’you know where I come from’what happened to me?” Winters asked, obviously getting excited at the prospect. “What can you tell me?” The waitress developed a concerned look. “I can’t tell you anything. I am only here to guide you to where you need to go’to what you need to do.” “I don’t understand. If you know who I am, why not just tell me.” Winters feebly responded. “Because you do not want to know,” She replied. “Believe me, when I tell you that.” “Why the hell should I believe you?” Winters questioned. “Because I knew you before all of this happened,” She gently reached for his hand. “Believe me when I tell you that.” “So where are you guiding me” To do what? Will I remember my past at the end of this?” Winters asked. “I cannot answer any of those questions now, but take these,” She handed him set of car keys. “It’s the black one in the parking lot. Go and see what’s inside. It will all become clear from there on.” She turned to go, but Winters grabbed her arm to pull her back. “Will I see you again?” She smiled. “Of course. I am your guide.” With that she left the diner and disappeared, leaving Winters sat there trying to wrestle with a number of emotions that were set to overcome him. He had found a link to the past’but his guide had brought up a good point: What if he really didn’t want to remember the past? He shook the thoughts from his head and hoisted himself out of his cubicle. There was only one way to find. He left the diner and went to search for the black car.
