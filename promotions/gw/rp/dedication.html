Dedication

Train always hated these places. There was something about the hospital that put him on edge. Didn’t even matter when there really wasn’t anything wrong with him. At least nothing serious anyway. If there were never anything wrong with him he’d never step foot inside one. Maybe it was the smell. The antiseptic tinge of the air didn’t smell like any other place in the world. Didn’t smell real in a way. Could have been the flourescent light that flooded about every inch. There was no personality in it, just a cold glow that seemed altogether unnatural. Gave him a headache really. Of course it was more than lack of ambience that put him off. He wasn’t much for superstition, but as far as these places went for him they might as well be a pack of black cats crossing his path as he stumbled under a ladder because he slipped on the shards of the mirrors he’d dropped. Hospitals were bad luck. He hated to think of himself as childish, but really he knew why he didn’t like the hospital. It went back to when he was a kid... all the damn doctors. Sure they always had a lolipop for you, but that was after they’d poked and prodded you with something... stuck something cold on you to listen to this... shooting something in you or sucking something out. It never failed, seemed like they weren’t there for anything else. Yeah, he was old enough to know better, but that’s life. With some folks once you caught a bad rep there was no inoculation, piece of candy or apology that was going to make it any better. Of course... way, way, way, way back... in the back of his head... he knew why he focused on those things. The childish stuff... it was way easier to deal with than the other shit. The real shit. In the same part of his mind he knew that somewhere out there hospitals were really the places of healing they were all touted to be. Problem is that was always in somebody else’s neighborhood. The real of it was... he knew a lot of folks who’d made it to the hospital. Just not a lot who made it back out. His brother.... Isaiah... two handfuls of friends over the last couple of decades... all they’d managed to get from there trips to the healing party was a reservation for their very own slab in the morgue. Unfortunately though Train had always managed to choose the wrong professions to stay away. Roll on the streets long enough and there was no avoiding them. Punch, kick, cut, stab, puncture, bullet... didn’t matter the wound, you’d wind up with one if not all of them eventually. Running the ring was no different. Hell, even though it shouldn’t even be a thought, when you dealt with the crazy bastards walking the hallways in an arena you can’t even rule out catching a bullet hole or three. Most would laugh at that notion, but most have never worked in a company with some nutcase dressed up fucking pink bunny toting around an AK-47. Oh well... There wasn’t a damn thing he could do about any of that today, but suck it up. Train wasn’t one to complain about every little ache and pain, but he couldn’t deny what had been eating away at him for the last two week. That little fuck Biddle had taken a bat to him a couple of weeks back all so he could earn a shot at the International Championship that was offered up on a silver platter by that big fuck Zenith. And, of course, just as his luck demanded, the son of a bitch had even nailed the shoulder that had just been scoped. The one that kept him out for over a month. All that and busting his head open, then leaving him in laying in a pool of his own blood. Again, Train hated to be childish, but he just had to take issue. It was only the opportunity to hand out the first in what he was determined to make a series of ass whuppins for both Zenith and Biddle that prompted him to step into the ring with his partner Brick at the last Evolution against both conspirators in a tag team match. His doctor hadn’t been happy about it, told him that he was risking permanent damage by going back in the ring with barely a week to heel, but Train was either too brave or too stupid to use his injury as a reason to opt out. And there was no doubt... when he was in that ring slamming his fists into the side of both their skulls it definitely seemed worth the risk. And when he was doing it the injury didn’t seem like any more of a problem than any of the other little nicks and dings he’d fought his way through. Unfortunately the next day he knew the truth. Adrenaline is one hell of a drug. It dulls all pain. It strangles all fear. Too bad that’s just for the moment. It never seems to have the same effect when you wake up the next morning stiff as a board, un able to raise your right arm above the level of your navel. Where the hell is that no pain stuff when the inadvertent brushing of your shoulder against anything harder than a feather sends a wave of pain reverberating up and down the right side of your body? And why is it that this stuff always comes the week before some super secret coven of Masons running your promotion suddenly decide to give you the rematch for the world title you’ve been asking for weeks and weeks, but don’t get until after they realize there’s a pretty damn good chance you’re injured? Train had the questions, but he had the answers too. And the answer was? It didn’t matter one way or the other. The bottom line was there was no breaking his this appointment today, despite every instinct to the contrary. He had to know if his shoulder would hold out. It didn’t matter that he thought the new little board in charge made a crap decision waiting till right now to give him his shot. Honestly, he knew what they were thinking. If Train was really a champion it wouldn’t matter if he were hurt. And in the end? They were right. Hurt or not Train was going to bring everything he had to the ring and use it to knock Mercer’s head off into the front row. He just had to know how much he was going to be able to bring. As for preferring not to be near any hospital, today of all days... he knew that it wouldn’t last forever. Besides, for all those he’d lost he took a deep breathe and satisfied himself with the thought that just before he found his way back to being the world champion, he’d be able to resurrect one of them. Their memory anyway... and that would do just fine. Train sat at the edge of the exam table thinking of the future. He ran the words of his speech through his mind, making sure that he’d set it to memory despite the fact he had his note cards in the car waiting for him. He was certainly dressed for the occasion coming up. He’d left the , the coat of his black Armani suit draped over the back of the driver’s seat of his Navigator, his official Chicago ride. The rest of his outfit, his black slacks, black, gator styled shoes, light and dark blue striped, silk tie and heavily starched, white shirt all clung to him the way any well tailored outfit would as sat trying to distract himself from his surroundings. Any other day he would never have wasted time on a day this important sitting in this place, but one part of his ensemble demanded he come. The rather ugly, pale blue sling draped over his shoulder supported the arm that hung from his swollen shoulder. As much as he hated having it there was no getting rid of it today. Getting dressed today had been close to agony. Any time he raised his arm more than a few inches sent jolts of pain surging through every part of him. Unfortunately it was a necessity he couldn’t escape from. Kind of like being here today. The words he’d spent hours writing over the week finally finished filtering through his mind just as he heard the door knob turn. A jet of cold air was pushed into the room as the door swung in and the doctor finally arrived. Train liked doctor Miller. He even trusted him... as far as doctors went. The wrinkles on his face and the thinning of his silver mane placed at around 60 and distinguished him with experience. He always had a little grin on his face, the seemingly perpetual cheerfulness even able to win over a guy like Train. He wore the stethoscope draped over his neck like rappers wore their oversized diamond charms, its presence an obvious status piece that he loved for the world to see. Train had never questioned whether the man loved what he did since the day he’d been introduced years ago. “Well, well, well,” Dr. Miller began with his normal upbeat voice that was thick with the Chicago accent he’d picked up growing up in the city. “Look what the cat drug in.” “Sup?” Train asked mustering as much pleasantness as he could being in pain and in a place he hated to come. Dr. Miller stepped confidently into the room and marched over to the light frames that hung on the far wall of the room, his white lab coat and conservative striped tie blowing in the breeze he created with his movement. With a quick upward swing he slide the x-rays in his hand into the clips of the light frames and flipped on the lights. The pale white glow lit up the black and white pictures or the swollen shoulder Train had done his best not to move for the last week. “What’s up is you’re a lucky S.O.B.” the doctor stated with an extra dose of his normal grin in his voice. “I thought for sure after what happened to you on Monday that I was going to see a fracture. At the very least I was prepared to see a few bone chips floating around in there, but instead I don’t see anything. No indication of ligament damage either. How you managed to escape serious injury I’ll never know.” “Escaped, huh?” Train groaned as he rolled the swollen joint. “It feels seriously injured to me.” “I’m sure. I’ve heard being attacked by a crazy bastard with a bat will do that for you. And then when you don’t take time off to let the damage heal properly I have to imagine it’s going to continue to hurt like hell.” “Uhuh... how long before the swelling goes down funny man?” “Hard to say really. I would expect it will take a couple of weeks before it’s back to normal. Then again, considering we just did surgery on this same shoulder not too long ago it could be a month or so before you can operate without any pain.” “Damn.” Train said, mostly to himself as his eyes shot up in the air. “Do I have to tell you I have a shot at the World Title this week?” “Tony I’ve only been following you and your career on and off for close to a decade now. What do you think?” “I think I need to know if I’m gonna be able to raise my damn arm above my waist anytime soon.” “Now that month prognosis is dependent on that shoulder not taking any more punishment,” the doctor added. “However, since that obviously isn’t going to happen I’m going to advise you keep constant heat on it for the next few day. That should let the blood flow more freely and help reduce the pain. I figure you’ll be able move the arm more freely by the time you get to Monday.” “That’s all I need to know.” “Not true. What you really need to know is that even if you can move it more freely that’s not the same as saying you’ll be able to use it as normal.” “Doc, do we have to have this argument today” I’ve got stuff to do.” The doctor could only lower his head and shake it from side to side as he chuckled. For him it was like going back in time. Train was no different than the man that had introduced them. He was the same kind of stubborn, bull headed individual that Isaiah was. No matter how many injuries he’d nursed the man through he never paid any regard to his well being. It was all about fighting to the bitter end and leaving it all in the ring. The old physician looked up back into the eyes of his patient. As annoying as it used to be and still was, when comparing the determination to the prima donna set of athletes he treats nowadays it was rather refreshing. “You do understand with that shoulder injured, whether you can move it better than you can today or not, you could wind up getting hurt more than you already are?” “Well if I do get hurt I’ma make damn sure somebody else feels my pain.” The doctor takes a deep breath and lets out a sigh. It only takes a glance into Train’s eyes to know there’s nothing he can say to talk him out of it. “Do you still have those anti-inflamatories I prescribed for you?” he asked trying to make sure the joint would be as healed as it could be before his patient stepped into the ring. “Yeah.” Train responded with a grunt as he gingerly hopped down from the exam table. “I’ll keep icing it down too, get rid of all the swelling.” “Good. You want to heat it too to combat the bruising and pain.” “Thanks doc,” Train said as he used his good arm to straighten his tie and the doctor did his best to maintain his trademark grin even as he resigned himself to the reality Train wasn’t going to sit the battle out. After brushing out any potential wrinkles in his outfit Train turned back to the doctor and the two shook hands. Train locked eyes with him for a moment before asking the only question that honestly mattered to him today. “You’re gonna be there, right?” “Wouldn’t miss it.” Dr. Miller responded, widening his grin. Train gave him a nod and moved towards the door. ***** ***** ***** Isaiah must’ve been smiling down. It was all Train could think as the sun finally peaked out from behind the clouds that had kept the Chicago skyline gray most of the day. Now the light from the sky bathed the crowd that had gathered for the dedication of the building, the sun glimmering off the new planes of glass mounted in the brand new building, while the new, granite face of the building glowed with the warmth of the day The clicking of camera shutters and whirring of video cameras filled the air as the press pool stood facing the new gym and community center. The pack of community children added excitement to the day, all of them obviously anxious to see where they’d be spending most of their afternoons after school. Several south side aldermen and women stood proudly in front of the “Isaiah Walker Community Center” as a large red ribbon stretched across the doorway. The place had been a dump not too long ago, a fact Train had been ashamed of. When he died Isaiah left the place to his star student. The one he’d died protecting. Train knew he should have made sure it stayed up and running. New that he should have made it a priority to keep the doors open for the dozens of kids just like him who needed a place to take refuge just like he had so long ago. It should have been a point of pride really. It only made him ashamed. Up to a few months ago he could barely stand to look at the place. All it did was remind him that the man who built it should still be alive. That he would have been if not for Train. However it turned out that the experts were right... time did heal all wounds. Or at least allow them to scab over so you’d stop losing blood and regain your strength. Alderman Nate Jackson, slightly pudgy man with salt and peppered hair and beard stepped to the mic set up in front of the front doors. “Ladies and gentlemen, boys and girls... thank you for being here today,” the alderman began. “I’ve been at my post, serving the south side of Chicago for a long time. The great thing about public service is that when you serve long enough you always have the opportunity to come in contact with extraordinary people. Isaiah Walker was one such individual. “The Black Bear” as he’d been nicknamed in his career, grew up right here on the streets of Chicago. Grew up not far down the street from me as a matter of fact. In truth were good friends and I’m proud to say that I knew him. And it’s not because of his distinguished career as a wrestler in which he won title after title around the world. No, it was what he did right here in his old neighborhood that makes me proud. If Isaiah had wanted to he could easily have stayed a world away and forgotten all about where he came from and the hardships he endured here. Instead he refused to forget. Moreover he refused to allow any child go through the same cycle of ignorance and violence that threatened him so many times. Instead of staying away he came right back to this neighborhood and opened his gym. Over the years he trained many young men who might have otherwise been swallowed up by the streets of this city. And don’t make any mistake. Even though he trained athletes in just about every sport you can think of what he did here wasn’t about achievement on any field. It was about giving the young people of this community guidance on how to live their lives. If you want proof of that then you need to look no further than the man standing right next to me. His star pupil... another south sider. A man that not only learned how live and fight from Isaiah, but learned how to be a champion from him. Ladies and gentlemen... boys and girls... I give you the man who is singlehandedly responsible for breathing new life into this much needed asset to the community... Former Heavyweight Champion of the World... TRAIN!!!” A roar of approval echoed through the air, those gathered from the surrounding neighborhood and the kids who stood to benefit directly showing their appreciation. Normally Train wasn’t the type to smile. Sure he grinned a good bit, giving the world his calculated, measured response that kept him from giving away too much of himself. Today he couldn’t help but beam brightly as he shook hands with the alderman and moved towards the microphone. “Thank you,” Train responded with a smile, humbled for perhaps the first time in his life by the response. “Anyone who’s seen me work knows I’m not one to do a whole lot of talking and today ain’t gonna be no different. But I will say this. Anyone who looks at me who thinks because I’ve built this place back up I must be so charitable and giving has got the wrong idea. As many who knew me when I was coming up in the streets of Chicago or seen what I do to my opponents in the ring can tell you I am not Mr. Nice Guy. I didn’t do this out of any sense of charity. No. This isn’t charity... it’s obligation. I made a promise to the man who this place is named after that I would change my life. And really there’s no doubt that I’ve done just that. But now it’s about more just changing. Now it’s about living up to him. It’s about being worth of the sacrifice he made for me. And if I want to do that I have to first make sure that his legacy lives on. And that means this place lives on. And that is why the “Isaiah Walker Community Center” will be here for this community from here on out!” Another round of applause rings out as Train takes the large set of shears from the alderman. The blades glide through the silky ribbon and it fall to the ground unblocking the way. It was a moment that normally Train would have responded to with a calculated, measured response. Train smiled like he hadn’t done in a long time. ***** ***** ***** The collective gasp made Train smile. The moment he pushed the large, maple, double doors open revealing the rotunda added on at the back of the new building. The circular room is lined with pictures and plaques. Most of the pictures are black and white, some with the beige tinge of the years they’ve managed to survive through. Gold and silver championship belts hang and several spots along the wall, mixing in with the pictures to give the an outline of a career spent winning. At the center of the room stands the winner. Isaiah Walker, looking not much older than his 20's, occupying the center of the pedestal in his old school singlet and one of his many championship belts over his shoulder. He was set in bronze, his legacy now secured for future generations. The kids and press slowly circled the room taking in all the history on display, most hearing for the first time about a man who everyone should have known long ago. A man who changed his life. As the rest moved about, exploring the room Train could only move to one place, magnetically drawn to the spot. He stood at the base of the statue, staring up at the unmoving copper eyes. He knew the statue couldn’t speak... wouldn’t speak... still he couldn’t help wanting something. Anything. He snapped to as he felt a hand clutch his good shoulder. Train turned to peer of his shoulder, Dr. Miller suddenly appearing behind him. Isaiah’s old doctor from back in the day only seemed able to stare up at his old friend the same way Train did. Slowly he just turned back to his mentor and the two men stared together, silently hoping to hear a word from him just once more. “He’d be damn proud.” Dr. Miller said breaking their mutual silence, despite the room being abuzz with activity. “Yeah,” Train replied far softer than is his unusual method. “I think he’d like the place too.” “I wasn’t talking about the building kid.” The doctor said with his normal grin. Train quickly turns to look at him and is greeted with a smile. Dr. Miller pats him on the shoulder and strolls away to join the masses. Train turns back to the statue, wondering if that were really true. Wondering if Isaiah had it all to do over again whether he’d give up his life to save Train’s. It was a question that had haunted him from the day it happened. It occupied his mind to the point where he didn’t even notice as the 6'7', nearly 300 man moved himself to stand parallel to him. “You ain’t gonna put up any of your stuff?” Brick asked even as he was pulled into locking eyes with the man that stood at the center of the room. Train whipped his head around as the voice of his tag team partner pulled him back into the here and now. The two share a short glance before Brick and Train both turn back to Isaiah. “I haven’t done what he’s done just yet.” Train responded with as transparent an answer as he’d ever given to anyone. “You haven’t” I see a lot of belts, but I don’t see any world titles on the wall.” Train could only chuckle at the man who he’d recently bonded with in the way only being champions together can cause. “When I say I ain’t done what he’s done it ain’t about titles man. It’s about saving somebody’s life. Like he saved mine.” “Well... now that you got this place up and running seems to me you’re gonna save a lot of kids.” “I hope so. Now call me back in a decade or two and we’ll see about putting my stuff up in here.” Brick slowly turns and looks down at his partner, knocked a bit off kilter by a level of humility coming from him that it never even occured to him that Train had. Train turns and returns the glance. Brick just shakes his head with a grin and turns back to the statue, Train quickly following suit. “I feel you man,” Brick says, suddenly privy to a new insight into the man that fights next to him week after week. “I feel you.”
