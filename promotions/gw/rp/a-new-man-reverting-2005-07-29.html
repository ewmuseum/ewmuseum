Train’s feet were planted in the only spot that wasn’t littered with debris. Nothing in the room had escaped his wrath. A good portion of the tiled floor was covered in the fine, white powder the wall sprayed in the air when his fists punctured them. What had once been a black leather couch now stood leaning against the wall, tipped over in the corner with it’s guts spilling out from every tear and strewn across the room. A glimmering constellation shined up from the floor, the bits of glass for the broken mirrors around the room twinkling like stars. His chest heaved as his black threads of hair hung down over his face, every now and then being blown out of place by his hard breathing. The sweat of his exertion dripped down his body, though he only remembered half of it. As he stood trying to catch his breathe he couldn’t quite recall when he’d even arrived at his dressing room. He might have assumed that the mess was left for him as some kind of message from any number of enemies, but the specks of white plaster stuck to his striking gloves said diffently. It didn’t take a genius to figure out this was the reason he was breathing so heavy. Despite not remembering it was perfectly obvious what happened. A bomb went of here... he had detonated. It was a bit disturbing that he couldn’t recall doing it though. He let his mind track back in time the sequence running backwards in his head. He vaguely recalled the hallway... the long stretch stacked, white cinderblocks and white speckled tile... it was hazed over... dreamlike... he could feel the eyes on him... every set of that had an owner who could use with him within a distance that didn't require binoculars locked and focused on him... all of them full of everything he didn’t want to see... surprise, shock, amusement, satisfaction, out and out joy.... pity... He continued to retrace his steps, the universe somehow stuck in reverse. Train knew this had to be his trip on his way to his own, one man riot, but that’s not where he was going now. Instead he stepped backwards up the steps... across the steel grating... then back through the curtain. The crowd was a bit distorted, but even with their voices looped backwards their cheers were impossible to mistake. Train turned on his heels and his fists are sucked from his sides into the air above his head. The crowd’s warped cheer grew even louder and continued to echo through the arena all of the few moments his fists floated above him. There condolences only lasted a few moments though. Train turned again and stomped backwards down the ramp... down the aisle... he turned a quarter turn and his feet magically rose from the floor, his body parallel with the mat before rolling under the bottom rope. When he stopped he wanted to be on his feet, but that's not how history went down. He assumed the position he last recalled vividly... flat on his fucking back. He hadn’t seen where the little bitch had come from. He had no clue how she’d hit him so flush and how he’d never seen it coming. Of course it didn't really matter now. The past was written and there was no way to change it. Mercer was on top of him. In his heart he struggled to escape... 3.... 2....1. Unfortunately it was over before it began. How the hell could she beat him? Walking into the match Train was convinced his game plan was perfect. He knew she was faster than him. Hell, what choice did she have at about a 150 pounds lighter than he was? She would have had to strap a set of pig weights to her ankles not to be. In his head that meant he'd be stupid to expend all that energy chasing her. In his head laying back would force her to come to him. In his head he figured she’d get in a few punches or kicks, but for every 10 of hers one swat from him would do the same amount of damage to her. In his head it was only a matter of time before he caught her with a shot that knocked her cold. In his head he never saw himself walking away as anything, but the world champion. Unfortunately you don’t live your life in your head. The past dispersed in a puff of smoke and Train was standing in his dressing room again. His breathing had slowed, his heart no longer threatening to pound its way through his rib cage. He looked around and took in the destruction he'd unconsciously caused. He stood in what used to be the extravagantly decorated dressing of the world champion. He stood there as the man that used to be the world champion. He stood there naked. The glass crunched and cracked beneath his boot as he slowly stepped over to the couch. He reached up and took hold of the end nearest the ceiling and gave it a yank. The tattered, leather clad frame fell back to the ground with loud thud, the impact blowing a jet of plaster dust and foam stuffing across the room. Train sank down onto the torn cushions, his head sinking into his hands right after. It wasn’t often he’d been left without words, but he was truly speechless. He’d been so ready... so anxious to prove to the world that he deserved his spot. He deserved to be the biggest thing in the company. He deserved to be the biggest thing in the game. He’d only proven the point of those who said the opposite. Train took a deep breath and raised his head. His eyes were locked onto the wall, but his mind floated in space even as hands were able to instinctually tear away the velcro straps of his gloves and pull themselves free of the leather and foam protection they provided before throwing them to the side. The gloves landed with a slight thud on the counter top that used sit below the large mirrors that used to be attached to the wall, only a few, long dagger like pieces still hanging. “FUCK!!!” Train growled from the pit of his stomach as he shot to his feet from the couch, swing his arms in front of him with clenched fists. He paced the room, the crunching of glass and debris bouncing off the wall with every step. He wandered the room in his rage tinged trance for a little while when he finally caught a glimpse of himself in the remains of the mirror, his reflection filling up the longest and most jagged piece. Train closed the difference between him and his reflection. He stood there staring at the man on the other side and found himself right back where he’d been yesterday morning. A day before he’d caught a stranger staring back at him through the looking glass. The man had his face, but Train had no idea who he was. It damn sure wasn’t him. At least it wasn’t the “him” that he remembered. It hadn’t been hard for Train to note the lack of fire behind the man’s eyes, the same fire that had fueled him for so long in his life. Now here he was. No fuel. No fire. No title. No way that was a coincidence. Somehow he’d managed to blow enough smoke up his own ass that he actually believed that lack of fire was a good thing. That it meant he'd grown... matured... become a man. He'd manufactured that conclusion from the advice of a man who'd guided him even when Train refused to be guided. He thought back to a time when the man who’d shaped him into what he was in the ring and given him the advice that he was so quick to accept as an explanation. He hadn’t lost his fire, he’d just learned to control it... learned to only use it at certain times. What he’d done was skip the rest of the story... avoided remembering the rest of it. Problem is the rest of it was how he became the man he was everywhere, not just the ring. It was where he got the fire from. Try though he may...
