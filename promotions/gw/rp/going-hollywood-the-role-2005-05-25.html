Going Hollywood: The Role

25 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 25, 2005 The Beverly Hills offices of William Bastafar were silent and had been silent for quite sometime due to the recent slide in the career of William’s star client Mike Biddle. In this sort of situation most managers would cut their losses and go in search of “The Next Big Thing”, but not William. Mike was the son that he never had and he’d be damned if he ever gave up hope on the brash young man. Even though Mike had never before in his career experienced such hardship William knew it was only a matter of time before Biddle turned things around and everything would go back to normal. For this very reason William sat in his small, hot office and waited patiently for something’anything to happen. His black mahogany cane that was tipped off by a golden hand in the shape of a fist rested against his desk beside him. William wore a white Armani suit with a black turtleneck sweater and he patiently stroked his grey beard with his right hand. Many had said that William bore a very close resemblance to the world famous actor, Sir Sean Connery which was a statement that William took as a compliment. William had made a few phone calls to the movie studios in Hollywood in hope that he could find a role for Biddle. William was of the opinion that if Mike could land a starring role in a Hollywood blockbuster it would give his career the boost that it so desperately required and William could fatten his own pocketbook in the process. The weary manager looked up at the clock that hung over the office doorway and sighed deeply when he saw that it was a little after five o’clock, which meant that he had been seated at his desk for well over nine hours. He could go home but that would mean that he had accepted defeat. He stared at the phone with his grey eyes and began to wonder if the damned thing was broken. He picked up the receiver to see if there was even a dial tone. “Bloody thing,” he muttered with his light Scottish accent when he found that there was no dial tone. “Hello?” called out a confused feminine voice from the phone. William’s eyes lit up at the sound of the voice, it meant that the phone wasn’t broken after all. “Yes,” he said into the mouthpiece, “this is William Bastafar, may I ask who is calling?” “Mr. Bastafar this is Amanda from Miramax,” the voice on the other end began, “I’m calling in regard to your client Mike Biddle.” “I take it the studio has found a role for him then?” William asked. “Yes,” Amanda replied, “one of our directors, Benjamin Johansen, is beginning a new project and he believes that your client would be perfect for one of the starring roles. In fact, he’d like to meet with your client tomorrow afternoon if possible.” “He’ll be there,” William said before he hung up the phone. William rose from his seat and grabbed his white bowler hat from the hat rack next to the window behind his desk and rushed out of the office. “Hold all my calls!” He said to his receptionist, Miss Moneypenny, as he passed by the front desk. “Nobody calls you anyway,” Miss Moneypenny said as she rolled her eyes in annoyance and went back to filing her nails. William didn’t bother to admonish her for her statement; he was in much too big a hurry. He had to tell Mike the good news right away. ***** When he arrived at Mike Biddle’s Beverly Hills mansion he carefully made his way up the front steps and knocked on the door with the tip of his cane. When no answer came William pressed the end of his cane against the doorbell and kept it there. He knew that the loud buzzing of the doorbell should at least attract Mike’s French maid, Belle. William’s theory proved to be correct when the saucy brunette answered the door. He said to her, “Hello Madame is Mike in by any chance?” “I just saw him go upstairs with some rope,” Belle stated with her thick French accent. “Why would he need a rope?” William asked with a perplexed voice. Belle shrugged. “I don’t know. I asked him but he did not say a thing to me about it.” William became worried; he knew that Mike was very distraught after his tragic loss at Wrestlecade and the fact that the lad was acting so strangely caused William great concern. “Tell me Madame,” William said with a serious tone, “was he up last night drinking by any chance?” Belle nodded her head. “When I arrived he kept mumbling something about ending it all.” William’s eyes became wide with fear; he couldn’t allow his star client to do something stupid. With a rushed voice the old manager spoke, “Then there isn’t a moment to waste! If you don’t mind Madame I need to come in and speak with Mike right away!” Belle stepped aside and William hobbled into the foyer and hurriedly made his way upstairs. He knew that there was only one room that Mike would go to at a time like this, the upstairs library. William rushed up the stairs the best he could and once he reached the top of the stairs he took a moment to wipe the sweat from his forehead. He pressed on down the hallway to the last door on the right and turned the knob and rushed into the library. The scene that William stumbled upon once he entered the room both startled him and strangely enough relieved him at the same time. Mike stood upon a leather ottoman with one end of the rope Belle had mentioned wrapped around his neck and the other end loosely wrapped around one of the wooden rafters on the ceiling. Biddle was clad in red silk pajamas, he was unshaven, and the blond locks that he took much pride in where unkempt. His dark blue eyes were bloodshot and the strong stench of alcohol emanated from his body. Biddle noticed William and looked at his manager with a sad look upon his handsome face. “There’s no use trying to stop me William,” he said with a strained voice, “I can’t take this anymore’this is my last resort.” William stepped into the room, careful not to make any sudden movements that would startle the younger man. “You can’t take what anymore lad?” William asked. Mike cleared his throat, “The constant losing’I-I’ve lost my touch William. I couldn’t win a match if my life depended on it!” “Just two weeks ago you defeated that vile Count Dracula wannabe,” William stated with a soothing tone. Mike took a deep breath, a single tear trickled down his cheek as he thought of what his last victory had led him to. “And the very next week I was defeated by that damn Bubble Boy. Not only did I lose a championship’I lost millions of dollars. I tell you William, there is nothing left for me to do. That is why I’m going to end it all,” Mike said with a cold voice. Before William could say anything else Mike kicked the ottoman out from under him and dangled from the rafter by his neck. William would have jumped into action right then and there if he hadn’t of noticed that the rope had started to come undone from the rafter. Within seconds the rope could no longer support Mike’s weight and became undone, an action that caused him to fall to the floor with a loud thud. William knelt beside the fallen man as Mike held his head and groaned in pain. “I can’t even do that right,” Mike said with disdain as he removed the rope from around his neck. “Perhaps it is a sign that you should not give up,” William said as he helped Mike to his feet. “If you had succeeded then I would not be able to tell you about the meeting I set up for you tomorrow afternoon.” “What meeting?” Mike asked as he sat down on the leather chair he had pulled the ottoman from. William proudly cleared his throat before he spoke, “Today I received a phone call from Miramax Studios. It appears that one of their directors has a part for you in an upcoming movie.” “What sort of part?” Mike asked. William sighed, in his rush to tell Mike the good news he had forgotten to ask what the part was. “I forgot to ask, but I assure you that it is just the sort of thing you need right now to pull you out of your misery.” Mike nodded his head; at this point he was willing to try anything to end his recent string of failures. “Fine,” Mike said, “I’ll be there tomorrow afternoon. What time though?” “I’ll have to call you and let you know,” William said apologetically. “I know that you have a lot to brood upon now lad, I’ll leave you to your thoughts and see you tomorrow afternoon.” William turned to leave the room. “William,” Mike called out. “Yes lad?” William asked as he turned back around. “Don’t tell anyone what just happened here,” Mike said with a red face. “Of course not lad,” William said with a smile as he left the room. ***** The Next Afternoon The sun shone brightly through the white clouds and palm trees as Mike Biddle drove his silver Ashton Martin V12 Vanquish towards Miramax Studios in Hollywood. Despite his suicidal episode from the day before Mike wore a grin on his face and his usual air of arrogance surrounded him yet again. He turned into the studio’s entrance and was immediately stopped by the small, nerdy security guard that guarded it. “E-excuse me sir,” the guard said with a nervous voice after Mike rolled down his window. “I need to see some identification.” Underneath Mike’s dark sunglasses his dark blue eyes rolled in annoyance. “You don’t need my ID junior, I have an appointment,” Mike said with an irritated voice. “I can’t just let you in,” the guard said, his left hand rested upon the company issue stun gun attached to his belt. “What if you’re some sort of terrorist and you blow the place up” I’d lose my job, and my Mom wouldn’t be very happy if I lost another job!” The guard finished with an angry tone. “I’m here to see some guy named Benjamin Johansen, go ahead and call it in Deputy Fife,” Mike said with a superior smile on his face. “Fine,” the guard said as he inched his way back to the guard shack, careful to keep a watchful eye on Mike. “Don’t you try anything funny now,” the guard said as he entered his little shack. Mike waited patiently for a few moments; the guard emerged from his shack with a defeated look on his face. “They’re waiting for you in the second building on your left,” the guard said. He pulled the lever beside his shack and the gate rose, Mike drove through the gate and into the parking lot. “I have my eye on you though!” The guard called after him. Mike found the office right where the guard said it would be. He entered the room and found William seated at a small table along with two other men. One man was a fat bald man with a ponytail and beard. The other was a short scrawny man with thick glasses and greasy hair. William motioned for Mike come and sit down. Mike shook hands with the two men and learned that the fat man was named Peter; he was the writer of the movie. The other man was the director, Benjamin Johansen. “Mr. Biddle,” Johansen said, “you’re the guy we picture as one of the stars of our next movie. And we are prepared to offer you a lot of money to play this role.” “What is the movie about?” Mike asked. “It’s a comedy,” the writer said, “its funny’trust me, I wrote it so I should know.” Mike grimaced slightly, he had hoped for an action movie. “What kind of role am I getting?” Mike asked. “You’ll see when you get the script,” Johansen said. Johansen reached over to the small table next to him and produced a pen and piece of paper. “If you sign this contract now you can go home and read the script, your lines are the highlighted ones.” He didn’t know what to do so he looked over at William, the old Scotsman nodded his approval so Mike shrugged and signed his name on the dotted line. Johansen handed him the script and Mike once again shook hands with the writer and director. “When I need you on the set I’ll give you a call,” Johansen said as Biddle and William left the room. The door closed behind them and Peter looked over at Benjamin with a grin, “It’s about damn time we found someone stupid enough to take that role,” Peter said. The two shared an evil laugh. Meanwhile, outside the office Mike stood next to William. “Are you sure this will make things better?” Mike asked with an unsure tone. “Of course I am lad,” William stated, “you’ll get so much mainstream publicity that you’ll be able to smite your opponents on name recognition alone.” -To Be Continued-
