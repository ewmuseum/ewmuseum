5 reasons not to judge people.

30 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: june 30th, 2003 Things were going good. I mean, I beat Tyler Pierce, got another wad of cash. Now I’m officially on the payroll though, and the money will be coming monthly instead. Now I have finally found a place where I belong, a place where I can rebuild my career. I’m comfortable and relaxed. I moved away from Tinseltownhell, Texas and into a nice apartment in Dallas fairly close to the arena where Overload and Evolution are held. The fans gave me support last week, they’re impressed by my ability to go from the top rope to the mats to the outside, and take it to the best of them. If I’m not mistaken, I beat the life out of the current cruiserweight champion. So I should be happy with everything, right? Right. I was. But then again, that was five minutes ago. Five minutes ago, in walked Ms. Carmen. “Don’t people knock anymore?” She giggles at my comment. I was serious. “I wondered where you were hiding at.” “Why.” I’m definitely going to need a cigarette now. She stands there with one hand on her hip, staring at me as I sit in my recliner. Waiting for me to ask her to sit down. “Why are you here.” “I’ve missed you,” she says removing her hand and taking a step towards me. “We haven’t talked in months, you know.” She looks around the walls at my posters, twirling her brown curls as the curves on her hips bounce. “Yeah. When was that last time we talked?” I scratch my chin, pretending to think about it. “Oh! That’s right, when you ruined my life.” She gasps. “You don’t really mean that, do you” Come on now Trey, you take things so personally.” She moves her finger from her hair and bites on the nail, a small grin. This is Miss Carmen. Her hair falls down past her shoulders, dark blonde locks of beauty. Her body isn’t firm; she’s never worked a muscle once in her life. The skin is as white as the nail she bites into, as soft and smooth as mink, velour and silk combined. Her stomach feels like jelly, it doesn’t hang out, but you could rub your hands across it for hours. And her legs, I won’t go there. There is a fragrance around, probably her shampoo or some kind of perfume she applied. It’s hard to describe, but every time I catch scent of it, I feel like a dog that caught whiff of a warm pie cooling on a windowsill, only to find out it’s booby-trapped. It turns me into a wolf putting on grandma’s dress just to get to the little tasty girl. And it’s happening again. I shrug it, and notice she decided to seat herself on the couch next to me. “You must have heard I’m back on a contract.” I pull the ashtray close to me. “Did you already run out of money?” This is Miss Carmen. I first met her six months ago, did you hear about that? Yeah, it was a week after I retired from wrestling. It was a week after I got the scar across my chest. It was a week from when I entered the hospital; it was when I was leaving the hospital. I had a run-in with her during my checkout. There’s something about the rain that makes scenes so much more romantic, and she was standing in the rain. The thing about Miss Carmen is that she’s a big shot, she’s a pro. She played me six months ago, and then three months ago she sued me for my estate and savings. Miss Carmen is who sent me from greatness to the street, and she is also the reason that I’m in Global Wrestling. Waiting for Monday. Waiting for Whiplash. Waiting for Judgement. Waiting for the fans. “Waiting for Ronny to come back with the cigarettes.” “Hmm...”” She asks. I mumbled the last one. “Wow, this is a comfortable couch, you seem to be doing good here Trey.” Miss Carmen stretches her body out on the couch, her legs reaching onto my coffee table. She looks me over. “Have you lost something?” My eyes, they are focused on the poster on the wall; it’s of me this time. “You have,” she says with a laugh. A giggle. “You’re still wrestling, but it’s not the same for you anymore, is it” Look at yourself; you’re not in peak physical condition anymore. You’ve lost weight; you’re not nearly as strong. I wonder if you’re even good at sex anymore.” I’ve lost 20 pounds since I was in NEW. Muscle weighs more than fat, and my skin weighs little. “Well, you weren’t that great back then, anyway.” She gets off the couch, and walks over to where I sit. She falls back onto my lap. “And that is when I wake up,” I explain. “And this has been going on for the last three nights?” Yeah. Three nights. Friday. Saturday. Sunday. It’s Monday morning-slash-afternoon. I’m telling Ronny my dream. “Well...I don’t know what to say dude.” “Yeah, no one does. This is just like the one I was having about Diamond.” Jade Diamond, the man on the wall, the man I dreamt told me I would fail. The man I dreamt I killed. “Yeah.” “I just wish it wouldn’t end so abruptly, I’d prefer if I could kill her real quick before it’s over.” “So, what do you think” Psychologist?” No. “Shrinks are overrated. Everyone from wrestlers to actors to six year olds try their problems there.” “How are we going to deal with this?” “Well. I’m going to wrestle tonight, put Judgement back on the shelf, come back here, and get sleep. If I don’t have the dream, then it just had to do with me worrying about the pay per view, I guess. But if it happens again tonight...” Hmm. “...I’d better kill her.” Yeah, that sounds good. Ronny sighs, “I guess there’s not much choice then.” “Nope.” Lightbulb. “Ronny, tell me, how come in the dream you never return with the cigarettes?” He sighs, and grabs his keys.
