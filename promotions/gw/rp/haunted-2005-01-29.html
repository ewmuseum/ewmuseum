Haunted

29 January 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1-29-05 A shower of black falls over a crouched, awkward form. The form shivers, causing the black to ripple. Silent sobs creep from behind the wall of black, as the form quietly nestles deeper into its corner. Somewhere in the distance, soft and haunting music plays. The music floats into the room, seizing the form, controlling its actions. A long, weakened sigh makes its way past the fragile black wall, warbling as the form fights to catch its breath. Slowly and with force, a pair of feet emerges from the wall of night. The wall pulls back to reveal the sullen face of Aradia. Tussles of her raven hair fall as a black waterfall around her blank face. Rivers of black run down the contours of her sunken cheeks, sore from endless chewing. Her eyes are red and puffy from a night of remorse and reminisce? a lifetime of pain and hurt flashing past her broken shell. Weary, she nibbles on her bottom lip, her eyes slowly scanning her surroundings. Brown orbs move slowly in their white pools, barely floating, ready to rise up into their intoxicated despair. The room is dark, illuminated only by the light bulb hanging above. Her eyes turn up to this disturbance of the dark. She hates it; her face contorts into disgust. She contemplates its removal, but finds herself afraid of its absence. Without it, she is lost. Begrudgingly, she stands, her hair taking reign of her face once again. She shivers, falling victim to the cold draft that circulates the room. Eager for warmth, she hugs herself close, rubbing her bare arms. Her tight fit black t-shirt barely protects her from the winter air that drifts in through her window. Still holding herself close, she stalks to the window and slams it shut with a jolt. The street below captivates her, catching her attention for a few seconds. A group of local teenagers creep by, shrieking and laughing, oblivious to the life or harsh world around them. A gray tabby cat slinks past the group, slowly turning its small head to stare as it prances along. A car flies past, music blaring from its interior. She surveys the scene, momentarily distracted by its chaotic beauty. Once again, she finds herself overwhelmed by her current state. She draws the blind and returns to her darkened room. The room is practically empty, consisting of a single leather chair, a dark oak end table next to it, and what appears to be an animal’s bed on the other side of the chair. Upon the table rests a black book; silver words scrawled across its cover. In the center of the book is a roughly sketched silver eye. The eye peers out at the room, filled with so much emotion and so much memory. Tonight, the eye stares at Aradia with judgment. Aradia stares back with guilt. Turning away, she finds herself looking at the closed window, sheltered by the black blinds. Closing her eyes tightly, she almost screams, but resists temptation. “I thought I’d find you here?” Her eyes burst open and she spins quickly on her heel, taken back and startled by the voice. A deep voice, filled with anger, hatred, and lust. A familiar voice, equipped with daggers and knives, all aiming for her heart. Aradia is surprised to find no one behind her, only her gray tabby cat prancing happily into the room. It purrs as it approaches its owner, ice blue eyes full of love. She backs away, not wanting the cats affection. It stares at her, conveying some kind of emotion, probably sadness. Meowing loudly, it turns slowly and sulks over to its bed. Aradia watches as it nestles itself into a content little ball. It looks at her one last time, lets out a friendly meow, and closes its eyes to rest. None of this fazes her, however. Not her cat’s affection, not the scene two stories below her, not even her victory a few nights earlier. Instead, she finds herself plagued by memories that will not leave her to rest. She finds herself envious of the feline; it rests so easily, forgetting any of the grief it felt throughout the day. If only she could do the same. Instead, she is haunted by the same voice, the same presence, always lurking right around the corner of her mind. It presents itself at any chance, in any form. Through voice or apparition, he haunts her reality and her slumber. He reminds her of the life she once lead and the life she can never have. He reminds her of lost light and her fall to darkness. He reminds her of lost innocence and muffled cries in the dark. He reminds her of death. She feels him everywhere, she smells him in every bar, every club, every restroom. The more she fights, the more he follows. She imagines his presence, convinces herself that one day, he will return. That he stalks her and follows her at night. Sometimes, she even finds herself staring out of open windows, certain that he is just across the street, watching as she goes about her daily life. She imagines that he watches her with anticipation as she strips from her clothing. Sometimes she draws the blind before doing so, afraid of his gaze. Other times, she enjoys the imagined attention and takes her time stripping away her clothing, letting him get another peak. Then, she quickly shuts the blind, reminded of the pain. Tonight, the black blinds separate her from the man that haunts her. If not for the voice, she would doubt his presence, but he must be near. The voice was much too close, but so far away all at the same time. Her mind races and she finds herself breathing heavy. Hatred and anger pump through her veins. How can she be so stupid? How can she allow the memory of that man follow her for all these years? Is she not better than that? Can she not move forward with her life? Twisting her face in rage, Aradia lunges for the table and grabs her book in one swift movement. She finds herself staring at the eye on the cover, daring it to judge her further, to make any other assumption about her current state. It only stares back at her. Frustrated, she rips open the book and flips through its pages, staring at her own handwriting. Words that have haunted her for years now make her feel worthless and stupid. The voice that lodges itself in her memory breathes heavily into her ear; she can almost feel his breath. Shuddering, she throws the book across the room, watching blankly as it smacks against the brick wall. The cat looks up from its slumber and Aradia stares back, blankly, tears forming behind her eyes. She wipes them away before they have a chance to fall. She won’t allow herself to cry again. There must be another way, another way to release. There must be another way to feel. Sullenly, Aradia makes her way to a mirror that hangs beside the window. Cracked into a spider web formation, the mirror gives her an askew vision of herself. It gives her the self she sees in any other mirror she peers into. She stares back into her own eyes and she feels hate and guilt. She hears her own voice and cries and she hears his screams and anger. All she wants is for the pain to leave, so that she can live her life. She continues to stare into her eyes, noting that the shard of glass where her eyes appear is looser than the rest. Through actions that are almost possessed, she reaches forward and pulls it out of its resting place, her eyes now missing from her reflection. In its place she sees only the black plastic that once held the shard in place. Eyeless, she steps back and takes in her new form; her new, blind form. There must be another way to release. Another way to feel. Quickly and without second though, she takes the shard of glass and runs it along her arm, slashing her perfect, pale skin. Quietly, she watches as the crimson quickly pores from behind its barrier, trickling down her arm in red rivers that branch in various directions over white flesh. She feels nothing and slashes again, forcing herself to scream in false agony. She raises the shard one last time and cuts deeper into her arm, amazed at the red liquid that gushes down her arm. Overtaken by the reality of her blood loss, she strips her t-shirt off in pain and quickly covers her wound. Anything is better than the thoughts of him. She secures the fabric around her arm and contemplates her next move. She’d forgotten his face. For a few moments, she’d forgotten his face. She’d tried to forget his voice, his actions, and his hate, but those memories were still lodged in her brain. For years they lurked silently in the back of her mind, until tonight, where they now found themselves parading at the forefront of her delicate mind. How could she let them return, to haunt her, to overtake her being? To allow her to manipulate her body, all because she just cannot forget him. Why should she feed herself more pain? Why must she be the only one to feel this pain? Other girls, they wake in the morning to thoughts of what dress to wear and whether or not today will be the day that prince charming whisks her off her feet. No, she is not one of those girls. Instead, she awakes wondering if today is the day they will cross paths again. She cannot allow herself to be frightened any longer. She cannot cause herself unnecessary pain. No, she can’t let him win. If she must endure these memories, then so will everyone else. In one way or another, everyone will feel the pain that possessed her being. Applying pressure to her arm, she turns to look back at the mirror, her eyes still removed from her face. A sick, twisted smile turns at the corners of her mouth. She exhales slowly and turns to walk away, retiring to the next room. No longer will she be the only one to feel the pain. If she must feel it? “then so will everyone.
