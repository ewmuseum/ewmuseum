Lessons of Thunder, part 2

11 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: December 11th, 2003 'Perception.' Itoju's voice echoed out from the shadows, its source uncertain. I peered out into the dark, but could make out only more darkness. 'Perception becomes victory, Rokku. Perception is a deadly and powerful tool. It is my control over perceptions, what I see and what others do not, what I let them see and what they do not, that has made me the Shadow Scorpion. It is this -- more so even than my skill in the martial arts -- that has made me deadly. It is this that made me a Thunder.' I scowled at the darkness. 'I get it, Yogo,' I said, as I tried to ferret out his location, 'that's practically martial arts 101. Learn to read your opponent's body, to see what they're going to do before they do it. I learned that when I was twelve.' Suddenly, a fist slammed into my jaw, sending me staggering backwards. I swing out with a sharp kick, but struck only air. 'You do not 'get it'', replied Itoju, 'you have only the basest understanding of what I mean. Reading your opponents face and muscles in a fight is just the beginning. You must also find that which they wish to hide, and hide that which they wish to find. You must learn to understand the tells in every action of they take, and then stifle those tells in yourself. To master the art of perception and it's counter deception, that is to win a fight before it even begins.' 'Fine,' I said, 'then stop dancing around in the shadows and teach me!' Itoju's laughter echoed throughout the room. 'What do you think I'm doing? This is a crash course in discovering that which is hidden, Steel Lion. The darkness hides me from your sight. The acoustics of the room create echoes, stifling your hearing. And my atemi strikes can be light enough to evade your sense of touch. If you can find me, and stop my attacks, you'll have taken the first major step. If you can't? well, I'm sure you'll be able to move again by the time your match with the Canadian rolls around.' Before I could react, I found myself swept off my feet, slamming hard into the floor. Itoju's laughter echoed through the chamber again. 'What's wrong, Lion? It's a simple game of hide and seek!' 'Maybe to you,' I grumbled, hauling myself back to my feet. 'Well, I am the teacher,' replied the Shadow Scorpion. 'I'm already very good at this.' 'No kidding,' I growled, trying to find any hint, any sign of where he might strike from next. 'Here I come,' he said, chuckling, 'ready or not!' Immediately I swung a kick out to my left hoping to catch him as he advanced -- but again, I struck only air. That was when my right arm went limp and numb. 'I warned you,' said Itoju, 'you know how extensive my knowledge of pressure and chakra points is. And if you learned anything at all from Sakura, you learned that when the chakra flow is cut off to a part of the body, it becomes useless. You've only got three limbs left, Steel Lion. Which one should I take next?' 'I'd rather you left them all, thanks,' I replied. 'I wish I could,' said Itoju, 'but then what would you learn?' I felt a feather-light brush, and suddenly my left arm was as useless as my right. But now I had an idea? 'You're getting sloppy, Scorpion,' I taunted, 'I actually felt you that time!' 'Really?' asked Itoju. 'Huh. I will have to be more careful. Another mistake like that and you might actually catch me! Then I'd have to pay up on my bet with Tsuneo. Do you know he actually thinks you can beat me here?' Without warning, my left leg gave out, and I dropped to one knee. 'He's smart, for a big guy,' I retorted, 'because I've already got you beat.' 'Really,' said Itoju dryly, 'and how's that? You're down to one limb. There's no way you can block or dodge my next attack. I'd say you're about to spend the next few days a quadriplegic, my friend. In fact, I don't think you'll be able to move a finger again before your match. It's a shame, because I'm sure you wanted to take Yoshiko-chan out for a night on the town at least once before going back to America. Ah well. I'm sure I can take care of her for you, eh friend?' I said nothing. I had stopped listening, my eyes closed in anticipation of Itoju's next attack. 'What's up, Rokku? No retort? No famous Steel Lion witty rejoinder? Very well then. Get ready to learn how Christopher Reeve feels, Lion!' There was no sound, as Itoju attacked. I expected as much -- he was as good at remaining silent as he'd always been. But I'd noted that he'd also grown rather good at talking as well -- and I'd made sure he talked as much as possible. If he hadn't, I might not have been able to block his attack. I could imagine the expression on his face when I caught his strike, though I couldn't see it -- but I didn't have time to dwell. Immediately I swung my boot up into his ribs -- not letting go of his wrist as I did so -- and sprung to my feet, following the kick with a hard right cross that sent him flying. I heard him hit the ground with a thud. For a moment, there was silence, and I fell into a ready stance, prepared to fend off another attack to the best of my abilities. Then the lights came on. Itoju was sprawled out on the floor, the widest grin I'd ever seen on his face -- and after a moment, he started applauding. Chuckling, I gave him a quick bow, then went over and helped him up. 'Excellent work, Lion,' said Itoju, 'you're a quicker learner than I'd expected. Now, how did you find me?' 'Well,' I began, 'first of all, I played off your chatter. I figured you were trying to keep me off balance with all your talking, knowing that I hardly ever let a good taunt go without retorting. So I kept you talking as long as possible. That let me think.' 'Continue,' said Itoju. 'First of all, I noticed a slight change in your voice when you attacked -- it went up ever so slightly right before you struck. I confirmed that as a pattern after your second blow.' 'So why didn't you stop me then?' 'I wanted to limit your options. By letting you disable three of my limbs, I left you with only one target -- limiting where and how you would attack.' 'Smart,' said Itoju, 'so you know when, how, and where I would strike you. But how did you manage to restore your limbs to working order?' I chuckled, a grin spreading across my face. 'Come on, man. I had a marathon training session with the Snow Phoenix yesterday -- you know our resident chakra expert? -- and I knew I'd be training with you today.' Itoju furrowed his brow, but just for a moment -- and then a sly grin that matched mine decorated his features. 'You made her teach you how to unblock blocked chakra paths. I'll be damned, Lion. You've got a good head for tactics -- not to mention that you pulled off a pretty advanced technique for a beginner.' 'Yeah,' I replied, 'well it's a good thing I did it right, or I might have blown out my muscles. Sakura really didn't want to teach me that so soon, but I figured I'd need it to have a chance of getting through training with you.' 'You were right,' replied Itoju, 'and you'll probably need it again before the day is out. Now come on. I'm hungry, so lets go get breakfast, and I can cram your head full of knowledge while we eat?'
