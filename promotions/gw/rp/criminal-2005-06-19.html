Date: 19-06-05 She left the door ajar, silently watching Jake pull out of the abandoned parking lot. The door silently clicked shut and she locked it effortlessly. Peering from the window, she gazed on as Jake made a u-turn and slowly drove past, presumably satisfying the idea that she was now safe at home. While the idea may have set will with the man behind the wheel, it was not a comforting thought in her body or soul. As she ascended the stairs leading to her abode, those penetrating brown eyes fell to the black t-shirt which covered her as a cloak may. A small smile replaced that permanent frown which haunts her visage, cursing her to absolution. The evening had been full of shock, on more accounts than one. As she peeled the black fabric from her moist and sticky skin, she grimaced at the sight beneath: dried blood and pieces of flesh had attached themselves to her body in a disgusting constellation of death and destruction. Upon reaching the top of the steps, she stopped to remove her combat boots. The smell of decaying flesh rose from the worn black leather. Aradia frowned, disappointed at the thought of throwing away her favorite pair of boots. She tossed them down the stairs and shut the door, hoping to trap the stench in the stairway. As she entered the apartment, she was careful to not brush against the walls or furniture. She learned years ago that blood stains are forever. Dying for release, she stopped at a desk and turned on the lamp, dully illuminating the room. Black walls absorb light, reducing the amount of light in a room. They also added dramatic effect. None of this was a concern to Aradia. At that moment, she was concerned with shedding herself of these blood stained clothes. After turning on the stereo, she made her way across the room to a closet. Inside, she found herself staring at coats never worn and boxes still left to unpack. Opening one of the boxes, she removed a pure white cloth and winced at the sight. Then, she removed the katana from her belt and proceeded to wipe away the blood and flesh. Then, she placed the weapon back in it’s box, where she left it for the remainder of the evening. Aradia folded the cloth and tucked it through one of her belt loops. She stretching, sensing the pop and crack of each vertebrae. As she made her way to the bathroom, she snapped her head from side to side, feeling the relief. Once in the bathroom, painted the same as the rest of the apartment, she opened a closet and removed a roll of black heavy duty garbage bags, used for throwing away clothes used in battle. One of the many perks of being a Global star was the money to buy new gear. Before her days spent in the profession, she had to spend days in clothes covered in the remains of battle. Days were spent hiding in allies, staying clear of mortal sight. Evenings were almost always spent in hunt or in battle. Now, a new life granted her the material wealth to simply dispose of ruined clothes and purchase replacements. After stripping herself of the soiled clothes, she placed them in the bag, along with the white cloth that she used to clean the katana, and then tied the bag shut, trapping the death and odor inside a plastic shell. She tossed the bag back out into the living room, certain that it would remain undisturbed. She watched in amusement as even her own disturbed and crazed cast was not curious enough to go near the bag of death. Aradia turned to the mirror, positioned neatly above the sink. Picking up a lighter, she lit a few black and red candles, to enhance the relaxing mood. The flames burned, their shadows bouncing on the walls around her naked form. She leaned forward, using the sink as a point of balance. Brown eyes peered back at her, through the dirty mirror. The flames? shadows began to play upon her face, dancing and bouncing, playing connect the dots with the small drops of blood spattered across her face. Closing her eyes, she recoiled away from the mirror, thoughts of failure surging through her weakened body. The clan leader managed to flee before she could even reach him. For a moment, she wanted to blame Jake for the beast’s escape, but she could not bring herself to such an accusation. No one was at fault, they had both fought for their lives that evening. Even the surprise appearance by Corvus had taken the beauty off guard. There were too many factors and twists to consider in order to even begin pointing fingers. Besides, it was not as though this were the first time he had escaped her. He would be found again. A man like that cannot hide for long. She opened her eyes once more and found herself looking the reflection of a confident smile. It quickly faded, however, as she realized that the time for pride and confidence had passed. Determined to rid herself of the last few weeks, she began to tug at the band holding her ponytail in place. It snapped, letting her raven hair fall gently around her bare shoulders. She studied the image of her broken self in the mirror, admiring her beauty but rejecting her flaws. The game of cat and mouse she has been playing for seven years has become tiring. Pushing her hair away from her face, she turned to the shower and let the hot water flow. Stepping inside, she felt the warm water cascade down her bare body, washing away the blood, flesh, and tissue. The water around her feet began to run pink as she moved slowly, letting the warmth envelope her body. Twenty minutes later, Aradia stepped out of the bathroom and around the bag with her old, discarded clothes. Wrapped in a towel, she walked through the living room towards the bedroom, water dripping from her body. Several minutes later, she reappeared, sporting a black tank top and black leather pants. A new pair of combat boots shined from beneath the flare of her pants. Her hair was air drying rapidly and she had decidedly left it down. She looked around herself, first glancing at the black garbage bag, then at her black cat curled in it’s bed. A sigh escaped her; she would not be able to relax in this environment. There was too much to remind her of the last three weeks. It was time to start living again. Her tongue rolled off of her lips, blood still present. Unfortunately, her taste buds had become immune to the crimson liquid over the years. Unlike her enemies, she did not thirst for it, but she did not find herself spitting it away either. The taste would wear away eventually, only to be replaced by it once again. At least the substance had not entered her mouth during battle. Not only was that dangerous, it was also quite disgusting. She cringed at the thought. She quickly ran a hand through her hair, rustling her tangled locks. Her body was sore, so sore that it was practically numb. Every muscle called for release, or action, anything to divert the pain. A vain attempt at shrugging off her discomfort resulted in her decision to leave the apartment for the evening. Besides, staying in the apartment after the night’s events may be unsafe. It was not uncommon for the creatures to track her down and attack her in the night. Especially recently, after the events of the last few weeks. It was as though his lackeys were in rare form, tracing her every footstep and stopping at nothing to seek vengeance on her guilty soul. A chill ran down her spin and she shuddered. The events of the last weeks ran through her mind in disturbed and interrupted segments, much like the broken wheel of a film projector. The hunt, the games, the fear, the death, and the partial retribution. Her head fell in shame. She did not serve justice. This was no longer about the hunt or the rape. This was no longer a personal vendetta. His life must be traded for the one he took? and the one he still holds captive. A sigh passed her lips, as she realized how desperate her social life was becoming. A night out would be ideal. Recently, she had learned of a club about a block away from her apartment. Interestingly enough, she had never heard or known of the place. From the description she received, it could not be much different than the club she frequented back in Pittsburgh. A jolt of excitement rushed through her as she thought of the new environment to explore. Other than a good fight, nothing could thrill her more than a trip to a dark spot. The music, the atmosphere, and all of horny men to put down could not please her more. So, with that thought in mind, she went back into the bathroom and began to apply her make-up. Tonight was going to be about letting it all go and giving herself a break. She cannot let her life get to her. If she were to break? what a sight that would be. Aradia Xiomara, break? Such an unthinkable event. After years of building the hard shell in which she lives, breaking would be improbable and impractical. She had too much pride for that. Too much going on to let herself fall apart. And who would be there to catch her when she fell? No one. But that is nothing more than common. Her life has been spent alone, with no one to lean on. She shook her head, putting the finishing touches on her make-up. Black eyeliner and mascara and dark red lipstick, the same shade as the blood which once covered her body. Then she remembered the bag in the living room and the boots at the bottom of the steps. “Shit.” she muttered as she turned off the light and exited the bathroom. “What am I going to do with this crap?” A few weeks earlier, the dumpsters had been taken out of the complex, mainly because no one was living in them. She needed to get these clothes out of her apartment, though. Otherwise, they will be sensed and she cannot have hoards of the enemy crowding her apartment. She simply did not have enough food to entertain, let alone any decorations to improvise with. Aradia, contrary to any belief that may exist, is no Martha Stewart. As she walked from the bathroom, she grabbed the garbage bag and made her way for the door going back down to the steps. The black cat, nestled in it’s bed, looked up at its departing owner and let out a small mew, letting her know of its disappointment in her departure. “Stop it, Athena.” Aradia spat back, shooting the cat a look. “You know how hard I work. I just want a night for myself, alright” I’ll be back tomorrow, I promise.” The cat gave her the same, mournful look. Or perhaps it was a mocking look, as if she were saying “foolish human. Cat’s do not speak.” Either way, the cat was still staring at her. “Why the hell am I talking to a cat, anyway?” she said aloud, as she opened the door. The odor from her discarded boots smacked her in the face and she scowled. Frustrated, she slammed the door and headed down the steps, scooping up the boots as she went. She opened the front door and then exited the apartment. She used her foot to kick it shut again, too busy scanning the area to turn her back on the door. What am I going to do with this shit? She walked towards the road, praying that no one saw her wandering through the empty parking lot with her garbage bag and blood covered boots. It was just her luck that a large dump truck passed as she turned onto the sidewalk. Smiling, she waited for it to pass before throwing the bag and boots into the back of the truck. As the truck kept moving, Aradia smiled slightly, knowing that the scent and items in that truck would lead the enemy further away from her. With that taken care of, Aradia began down the sidewalk, making her way towards the club. It only took a few minutes of searching; her informer’s directions had been quite concise. Basically, she had to look for the alley with the red markings on the outside and through that, she would find a side entrance to the club. For reasons unknown to her, the club insisted on being out of the public view. There were no signs to indicate that its frequent visitors were at the right spot. In fact, she was not sure the place even had a name. Apparently, many of it’s frequents referred to it as “The Pit.” She chose to not think too deeply into that one. She opened the door to the club and was pleasantly surprised with what she found on the other side. Although the club was hidden from the view of street-dwellers, it was definitely well known amongst the underground crowd. What she saw before her was the largest crowd of night crawlers she had ever witnessed. She was accustomed to large crowds in Pittsburgh’s best kept secrets, but what she found there were usually posers, hippies, Goth wannabes, and college students. A very disappointing crowd to a true night crawler. But this place was amazing: it seemed as though every person in the place had a seal of authenticity stamped on their forehead. It made a devious smile cross her, to know that such a place existed, and not more than two blocks from her apartment. The pressures and guilt of the last three weeks would never penetrate her here. There was no way. Not with the sight of pale skinned romantics twirling and gyrating on the dance floor, their black garments floating and spinning around them. Black lips, smeared eye make-up, and barren, wasted hearts were the essential ingredients of this wicked place, equating it to the exact needs of her cold and twisted heart: a diversion to the guilt she would not allow herself to feel. Rather, the guilt she knew was present, but was fighting through denial. Guilt that she was forcing to melt through the adrenaline and aggression of battles of retribution. Battles that were more for herself than for the life that had been taken. She needed the diversion. Somehow, she could not help but blame herself. Aradia stepped further into the establishment, soaking in the dark and miserable vibes emitting from the bodies twirling before her. A smile was perched on her lips, barely making its presence known; her game face was stretched to its limit. The mask of deceit was worn to hide the awareness of self-denial. And when it is all over, she’ll only have the headache and the sore muscles to remind her of such a tragic evening. I didn’t see you there,” the woman says, a look of regret forming on her pale and beautiful face. “Perhaps I’ll pay better attention next time.” Aradia shook her head, realizing that the woman had taken her remark the wrong way. “No, no.
