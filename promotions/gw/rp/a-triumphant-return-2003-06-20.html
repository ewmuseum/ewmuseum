A triumphant? return

20 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A triumphant? return Ronnie Long Global Wrestling Promo #330 Date: 6/20/03 Location: Long's apartment in Miami, Florida “For I have changed for the worse, and for that I thank the lord…” Ronnie Long claims that he is “The Dark Side Of Humanity”. Not that he’s evil – he’s quick to correct anyone who would tell him that. What he is, he claims, is one who can see the truths that are forbidden to the race of Man. He sees past your eyes, into your soul. A tortured wreck of a human being himself, he has only recently finished saving the mind of a young girl in the Innovative Wrestling Alliance. This girl, Heidi… does she mind the fact that he lurks in the shadows? Or that he’s more comfortable sitting in an empty grave, then he would be on a velvet sofa? No, she doesn’t. To her, Ronnie Long is a hero. “What I am… is a savior. It has been said, that some in this world are born to suffer. And I was one of the ones cursed with that. But at the same time, I was given a precious gift. I sit alone in the dark, but I can heal. If there were more of me, I could save the world.” Ronnie Long isn’t evil. Scary? Yes, indeed. Twisted? Yeah, you could say that. Sinister? Oh yeah. But evil? Hell no. “Evil is nothing but what you make of it… I tried being evil… it was a struggle in futility. But I was saved, saved by an angel. Only this one didn’t come from heaven, only from a cheap club in downtown Denver. But at any rate…” And we finally open up on the scene. The living room, of the apartment of Ronnie Long and Deanna D’aimore. It’s small, and rather battered, but it’s kept relatively clean. Female influences, and all that. The only light comes from a television set, against the wall, bathing the room in its grey blue glow. Directly opposite the TV, there’s a couch, with some sort of a throw rug over it. Other than that, there’s an old, scratched coffee table with a few cans of beer sitting on it, a Lay-Z-Boy (broken), and a window with plastic taped across a shattered spot on the lower pane. An air conditioner rattles, but it is doing very little to relieve the heat. On the couch is the man of question, Ronnie Long. The blue-gray light seems so much a part of him, he nearly blends into the couch, perfectly camouflaged. Black hair hangs in greasy rat tails over his forehead. His upper body is bare, glistening with sweat. A pair of ragged jeans covers his legs. He half reclines against one of the sofa’s armrests, holding the television remote with one hand, supporting himself with the other. “Nothin on…” he mutters under his breath, continuing to flick through the channels. If you live in the slums of Denver, you don’t really get much in the way of good programming. Well, actually, you can’t get good programming anywhere, these days. All that runs are stupid sitcoms about people who you’d rather beat the hell out of soon as look at, more of those “reality TV” shows (and that’s a whole other rant, there)… et cetera et cetera. But it doesn’t stop Ronnie from looking for something to watch, especially during hot, sticky summer evenings when there’s nothing to do and nobody to do it with. He’s almost glad when there’s a rap at the door of his apartment. With a large sigh, he heaves his 6 ft 5 frame to a standing position, then pulls a black Metallica T-shirt over his shoulders. Heading to the door, he opens it. As soon as he does, a look of bitter disgust crosses his face. It’s the landlord. A wizened little man, of indefinite age. His hair is unnaturally black and sleek, like he soaked it in ink, and then gelled it into place with superglue. And he wears a threadbare tweed suit, with pleather pads at the elbows. He reminds Ronnie of one of those sitcom morons who you sometimes hallucinate about mutilating and disemboweling, while pouring acid on his eyes. “Your rent is due.” “I would never have guessed.” Ronnie speaks so dryly that the little man can’t figure out an appropriate response. “It’s $250 this time, and I expect you’ll be paying it soon…” The man is persistent. Long looks down at him. “I think we’ve been through this before. Deanna works at the night club, and she’s the one who’s bringing home the checks, seeing as I work so little in IWA that I don’t make any money worth speaking of.” There’s nothing to be gained by being unpleasant to the man, no matter how much Ronnie would like to do more than beat him to death with his own spine. Thus, the civilized conversation. “Well, Mr. Long, I still need $250 for this months rent.” “And I told you, Deanna will pay when she gets back from work, if you’ll just be patient a while.” With a harrumph, the man nods. “And when will that be?” Ronnie glances at his watch. “Five minutes… ten if she hits traffic. She doesn’t work the night owl shift, not since the shooting. Here, unless you wanna go somewhere else and then come back, you might as well come on in. I’ll make ya a cup of coffee or something.” Leading the man into the small dining room (nothing more than a table with 2 chairs and a shelf against one wall), Ronnie walks into the equally tiny kitchen and begins pouring two cups of coffee. He works slowly, deliberately hoping to stall. And luck is with him. As he’s pouring the second cup, the door slams. By the time he makes it out, Deanna D’aimore is sitting at the table. Her raven hair is in a sort of halo, where it isn’t stuck to her neck, a product of the oppressive heat. Like Ronnie, she’s wearing as little as she can get away with – in her case, a pair of black denim shorts and a cherry red halter top. And she’s filling out a check – presumably for the $250 rent payment. A bit later, the ink & gel haired man leaves, without a single polite word. And Ronnie sits down in his place. Not because he wants to, he’d rather be channel surfing. But he knows that look in Deanna’s eyes. It’s the one that says “I have to nag you, but please initiate conversation with me so you can’t complain about it later”. You probably know that look. “So…?” Ronnie’s no conversationalist. In high school, when he should have been learning the art of conversation, he was instead kneecapping the captain of the football team with a Cougar Magnum. So it’s up to Deanna to set the tone, and well, everything else. “Ronnie, listen to me. I don’t make enough money at the club to support us like this. This month’s rent was two hundred fifty dollars. I make five hundred a month. Not counting what you bring in from your odd jobs, that leaves us two hundred fifty dollars to live on, for an entire month. And since winter started, you haven’t even been doing anything. Just sitting in the house, absorbing money.” Ronnie takes a brief glimpse out the window. Even in the apartment, there’s that smell of sweat and soot that only a hot city produces. The thought of going outside sends a chill down his spine. “So what do you want me to do, then? I’m assuming that you’ve got some kind of plan…” Deanna smiles, in that patient way women do. “I thought you’d never ask. Here, take a look at this.” Deanna reaches into her pocket book, and tosses a piece of paper onto the table. Ronnie’s eyes widen, as he realizes what it is. It’s a wrestling contract. For the GW. “Ronnie, this is the only way we’re going to be able to make ends meet. I called up GW, tried to get your job back. They weren’t exactly thrilled about the prospect of bringing you back… apparently, they were quite fed up with your lackluster work rate. But they did, in the end, agree to sign you…” She pokes the paper with a long red nail, for emphasis. “This is a contract for GW. It’s for 3 months, and it’s bringing you $60,000 per year. That’s five thousand a month, minus taxes of course.” Ronnie looks at the paper, hard. He’s thinking about it. But he doesn’t have to think very hard. “And for these three months, I get paid, so long as I don’t get busted for breach of contract? As in, not coming through like last time?” Deanna nods. Ronnie heaves a sigh, then stands up. “Guess I better get started training, then…” he says, leaving the room. Deanna… called by Ronnie an angel, called by her enemies “The She-Wolf Of Murder City”, watches him go.
