A Warrior’s World: Give Me Life… (Pt.1)

12 September 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9-12-04 Prologue This is a new chapter in a long series of books of the legend of Jesse Jamester. For the longest time has Jesse struggled for something to hold onto in life. Whether that something be his father, his daughter, or wrestling, it has always fallen short of his grasp. However now the Jackal will release his grasp upon many things, some being deep secrets, and others being family. It will be a new dawn for the man who never was, a new beginning of something… positive. For once, Jesse will become what he was not, a man, above all else, a man true to himself. Chapter 1 I’d been lost in this world I’d seen in my dreams, but never gone to in reality. It was my echoed paradise, with a few exceptions. For so long had I consider it to be my own world, a world in which no others knew about. However those hopes were shattered as my alarm went off and I awoke from my deep sleep to go to work… Beep. Beep. Beep. The alarm sounded as Jesse rolled over in his bed and slammed his hand down on the sleep button, however instead of turning it off he only made the noise louder by hitting the radio button. Reaching down beside the bed, his face buried into the white cotton pillow, he blindly pulls out a cord. The alarm dies a silent death as he rolls over and blinks his eyes while rubbing the sleep out of the corners. It was three A.M. and he’d only gotten fivers of sleep, but he knew he couldn’t fall back to sleep and so arose from the bed and placed his feet aside it as he stared into the dark silhouette of a room. It’s been a damn week since I’ve gotten a full eight hours of sleep, what the hell is wrong with me. I knew death could play a toll on your mind, but I barely knew the man, so why would it strike me as hard as it is? Cracking his neck from side to side he pushes his hair into a ponytail and removes the ponytail on his wrist with his fingers to hold it up. Eyes still shuttered close to an extent, he staggers up and across the room as he reaches a night light on top of his dresser. Twisting a knob the light dims on, and then a second twist gives it’s full brightness against the wall and corner of the room where the dresser sits. I guess I’m just not use to losing someone I care for. I thought by now, after two lost loves, a mother who I saw die before my feet, and my best friend’s death witnessed as well, that I’d have some sort of relief when it came to this feeling. Hell, I’m all bent out of shape though. This really is something I have to see therapy for, or I’ll never get the sleep I need. Flicking on the bathroom light, he walks in and immediately turns on the faucet. Splashing his face with a cupped hand, he looks into the mirror. The reflection showing tiredness, and his eyes a blistering red because of the sleepless night as he rubs the corner of his eyes trying to see better. Shaking his head he snapped himself out of the sleepy trance an began to finally get ready as he planned to. This can’t be happening to me… First thing I’m doing when I get done with this funeral is calling a therapist. It’s the only way to get myself straightened out. Staring down after brushing his teeth, washing his face off, and cleaning his unusually long goatee, he was a man with a lot on his mind, yet nobody to tell it to. A loneliness feeling of desperate heart aches, and long gazed thoughts were what Jesse spent all these days as he sat alone in the dark staring out his bedroom window up at the stars. For everything he’d gone through, he couldn’t find any happiness. Whoever said money bought happiness, sure was delusional, cause Jesse couldn’t buy a moment of joy with his four-figure bank account. Let’s just get through this day… Get it over with and let it be apart of the past. Shutting off the light he walked out and got dressed. Being apart of the Global Wrestling organization he had perks. He got his clothes tailored to fit him, and he got everything at a discount. Sliding on a perfectly new pair of black dress up pants, and then a white undershirt, he slowly slipped on his fancy clothes, a site not many see of Jesse, and before long he is looking like a James Bond replica without the fake charming smile. Standing up after putting on his shoes he stares at his pants and wipes away the creases, showing his uncomfortable state in the dress clothes. Shaking his head of the self-conscious thoughts he had, Jesse grabs his keys and walks out the door of his hotel room and into the hallway where he walks to the end and steps into the empty four thirty elevator. Hitting the down button, the doors gradually close, losing site of Jesse as it does. Chapter 2 The door to the funeral home opened gently, as a teenager would do so when sneaking into the house late at night and knowing of the trouble they are about to face. However this unhurriedness of the opener was not because of the trouble they were to bear but the fear they would soon see. Stepping through the doors and up the short three stepped stairs, Jesse was gulping his air in and breathing deeper. His eyes stared at the floor ahead of him as he walked. It was now six A.M. and he was about to face his fears for what he hoped to be the last time. Rounding the corner into an unoccupied settlement of a room. Near the left side of the room laid a bed with a man lying in it. The bed not of the normal statue, for it was at the height of Jesse’s abdomen as he came up close to it. Staring down he saw what made him feel so uneasy over the past week. It was his father. So lost I felt without him… The time we spent was precious, even if he didn’t pay attention to me. He never looked at me, but I felt that it was cause he didn’t know me well enough I tried to be the best son I could, and I believe I achieved that in his eyes, at least achieved more than he had. He told me not to give up, and not to care for the little mistakes, because they were apart of life. Yet I still feel responsible for this. If only I’d… hmm, I just needed more time. Putting his hand over his fathers he closed his eyes and thought of what it’d be like with him again. The days they spent together in GW, the first time they met in the locker room, and of all of the things, the day he told his father he hated him. I can say I’m sorry now, but I know it will mean nothing. He is gone, and he doesn’t even know how I really feel. This is my fault, and however I look at it, I’ll always think that… People may say no, but in my heart I know it’s the truth. I caused this, and I’ll be forever denied the possibility of righting that wrong. Removing his hand Jesse took an envelope from his coat and placed it inside his father’s coat. On the inside is a pocket and so Jesse gently places it in and turns leaving the room. Inside of him he’ll always place the blame on himself, but at least he can’t say he did have other feelings and at least deal with them better than he had in the past. For now though, he was to walk away a victim of his own silent guilt. Nobody would understand how he felt, and that’s just how it was. Leaving the funeral home he looked back once and then not again, knowing it was now a piece of the past, and no matter what, he couldn’t change it. Chapter 3 Walking into the room Jesse took a seat on the bed and stared up at the ceiling as the woman stared at her sheet and took in his information. It was definitely not a new situation for her professional degree to handle, but it was odd to see someone so calm come in and in her opinion look like someone who had nothing but worries wasting their money. Ever the less, she took her dollar as it was given and flipped the page over so she could write down the information she would soon receive. “So Mr. Fristik, how may I help you today?” she started hoping that this would be easier than other patients yet somehow knowing just by looking at him that it would be. Jesse sighed, “I have just gone through a life altering change. See my father died, and well we never really knew each other up until a few weeks ago. He caused a lot of problems and bar-none we had our disagreements. But now I feel guilty for his death because of what I last said to him.” The woman observed the writing she just took down and read it to herself over once and then again just to make sure she had it down right and knew what she was answering to. “And what was the last thing you told your father?” stating those words almost as if she already knew what the answer was, but saying it anyway for reinsurance. “I told him I hated him and that he was never a true father,” he exclaimed, showing a deep regret for telling his own feelings but also realizing it did take off the pressure he had on him as he now began to relax a bit. Putting her pen to the side she pursed her lips as she asked the next question, “Where do you work Mr. Fristik?” Almost like it was a second home Jesse answered with specifics, “Dallas, Texas at the Global Wrestling organization. I’m one of the many talented superstars on their roster.” “So you’re a wrestler. Very demanding sport wrestling is. Do you often get stressed when you work?” she asked as she wrote down all the information. Hesitant to answer, “At times yes.” “How many times in the past month?” she demanded without raising her voice, and keeping her control on him as she showed no fear in any frustration the beast of a man he might have over her extruding of his mind. Sighing, “A lot, eh more than normal I suppose. But as I said, it’s because my father and I weren’t getting along.” “Are you wrestling anytime soon?” questioning again, this making what must have been the tenth question on her list as Jesse sat up a bit and answered her, “Yes, this upcoming Monday on the thirteenth. I face Ken Partridge Junior.” The woman scribbled the last words down on the paper and stared up at Jesse while placing her elbow on the clipboard. “It seems to me that you are going through a period of grief depression. You feel guilty for what you said but also are mad that you couldn’t correct it and say what you really meant during your fathers final hours. Along with that you’re lonely because now you can’t spend your time with anyone since nobody is around.” Staring up Jesse looked at her shocked, “I paid you to tell me what I already knew? What the hell is this, some joke? Look lady, I know I have those issues, but I want to know how I can fix them. So… how can I?” She smirked at the question almost like it was too easy of a job to handle, “look, I’m going to sign you into an anger management course. Now don’t get me wrong you don’t seem to have a lot of anger but this group also helps in giving up the past and getting along with your life.” He nodded as he cooled down a bit. Sure he had a temper, and though he knew that yelling at her may have triggered the reason for the group, he thought to himself, any kind of help was good at the time, even anger management. Standing up he nodded and told her thanks as he walked out the door. Looking down as he walked out he noticed the date on the note, the day of his return match, he would have to attend the class she assigned him. Eh, I have to go to anger management on the day I have to face a guy I respect yet have to prove I’m better than… Joy. Chapter 4 Staring into the dark night Jesse looks up at the stars. His eyes build up water from inside; as the tears begin to silent descend his face. He knows he has to live with the guilt, but it’s almost unbearable at times. The hurt it causes makes his body shiver with fear of what his death may be like. I can’t believe I’m crying. It’s been a week since he died, and a day since I’ve seen his face at the funeral home. Either I’m just messed up or this is seriously effecting me in ways I don’t understand. God why don’t you just make your damn mind Jess! Hitting himself in the side of the head he realized he had to put up with facing a man on the same day he had anger management. Who knows how much his head would be yo-yo’ed on the day of his return match. It’s like I’m being told I can’t wrestle when the only thing I feel like doing is wrestling. Not that anger management is a disturbance, but I know it’s going to make me think about things I never have before, making me lose focus of my match with KPJ. Wiping the tears from his eyes he stared at the floor in disappoint of going to the therapist. Sighing heavily he got up from the chair and walked to the edge of the balcony as he looked out at the evening sky not caring a tad if he fell over and died at that very moment. Whether the Global Wrestling superstars like it or not, I’m back, and though I am being pulled from side to side, I know they’ll see the change and realize that I mean business this time around. Swallowing his pride and the rest of whatever he thought, Jesse headed to bed earlier for the first time in a long time. The night breeze swept through the room only once that night, and it didn’t wake him up at all. Concluding Notes With his meeting with the anger management group hours before his match, will Jesse be able to focus, or will his mind be in other places? He promised he’d be back and back to bid his business, yet what does that mean? Could the Jackal’s second face, one not seen yet, be discovered?
