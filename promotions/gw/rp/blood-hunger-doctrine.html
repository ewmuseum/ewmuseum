Roughly 55 miles east of Madison, WI Alexis shivered slightly, her exhausted senses on fire. She had no clue where she was, and the surroundings were getting slummier as she passed farther east. The shiver didn’t come from the cold, but from nerves- even the stars seemed dingy, hanging over the cracked and dirty asphalts of the streets. She wrapped her arms around herself, rubbing the thinning muscles of her upper arms. Alexis looked a far cry from the Alexis Key that she had been back in Cleveland- the brutally-muscled sharp-minded Den novice hadn’t had a substantial meal in weeks, and she walked with a pronounced limp that came from too many times forcing herself to go on when she had no energy or strength left, all in the name of making it to Napa Valley. She’d been following the interstate up until this point, planning to ride it out as far west as it could go. But, she confessed, she was pretty exhausted by this point, and had all but lost her way. She’d come into this little town, whose name she couldn’t even remember, in an attempt to find a place to sleep and think over how her plan was going to work from here on out, but her pace had slowed to a crawl this past week, as exhaustion, frustration, and most of all fear had begun taking over. Alexis Key had never been so afraid in her entire life. There was nothing she could do, nowhere she could go for help, and nobody she could call without giving herself away. After she had joined The Den, her contact with those outside it had ceased almost completely. Lost in her thoughts, Alexis stumbled, scraping her knee against the concrete. She heard the resounding rip of the leg of her jeans, and she cursed quietly. She had nothing else to wear, and people already looked at her suspiciously. Moments later, however, her appearance was the last thing on her mind. A light scuffling on the concrete caught her attention, and as she looked up, inches from her nose was a pair of shoes- beaten up hiking boots. To the left, another pair. As well to the right. Drawing in a sharp breath, Alexis? blue eyes widened. She pushed herself quickly to her feet, adrenaline coming in to serve where energy could not. And when she stood up so quickly, she bumped into a very solid figure behind her. She spun around quickly. Her eyes met a chest- she looked up about six inches into the grinning face of a young white male. He was probably about her age, dressed in a puffed jacket and baggy jeans. Alexis backed away from him, and then realized that there were five of them, knotting around her in a ring, and all dressed similarly to the first. And it was in that moment that Alexis realized how suburban and weak she looked- and how much she hated it. “Well” we don’t get to see girls like you too often,” said a voice from behind her, “what, you get lost comin” from the mall or somethin?”” She gritted her teeth. “No, I didn’t. I was just looking for the interstate-? “For the interstate?” asked the same voice. She spun around to look at whoever was talking to her, and was met with a fairly stocky sable-skinned youth, with several gold teeth, evidenced by the grin on his face. He was the only dark-skinned boy in the group, and seemed to be conducting himself as a leader of sorts. He shook his head, taking a step closer to her, as she backed up. “Come on, baby, the interstate’s miles from here, I know you gotta have another reason for bein” around here? you lost?” As he said it, he leaned closer to her, so close that Alexis could smell the faint scent of fruit-scented cigar on his last puff of breath. Nausea surged through her, especially as she realized that as exhausted as her muscles were, the only chance she could have possibly stood would have been essentially going into adrenaline overload, and bolting. Suddenly, his hands wrapped around both of her upper arms tightly, squeezing her into immobility. “I hate it when sweet lil” girls like you get lost. Why don’t you let me and the boys show you around our “hood?” She struggled in his grasp, panic threading through every vein. .:: ::: ::. Alexis? feet pounded against the pavement as she bolted- on the run again. “Somebody get that bitch!” “Dude, I ain’t touchin” her, she broke Darry’s fuckin? neck!” The sounds of varied arguments for and against pursuing her echoed behind her, some rather indistinctly, that is, until the sounds of an argument ensued. “Go!” “Naw, I ain’t doin” it!” “Look, Ima solve this problem straight out, Ima just cap the bitch!” The sound of gunshots rang out behind her, but Alexis was already too far ahead. They’d be in their cars soon, though, so Alexis had to find a place to hide. She rounded the corner of the neighborhood, and saw off in the distance the spires of a church. Ignoring the blood all over her palms, she lowered her head and ran towards her only hope. “Alexis” what have you done?” The question slammed up against the front of her forehead with the veracity of a freight train. She tried to push it out of her mind. “No” if you think about it right now, you’re going to give up, and you can’t give up, not right now? keep going, dammit!” She forced herself forward, trying to keep from taking breaths that were too heaving- a sound that would give away her whereabouts. Every breath sounded like a hacksaw. And before she could help herself, the memories of what had just happened came flooding back. She drove her knee up into the young man’s groin, as desperation started to kick in. He cursed, and leaned forward- but he let her go. She sensed the option to escape, and leapt over his prone body, bolting down the street. Amid a few yelled curses, the other stunned members of the gang picked up the chase. The first to catch up was the lanky boy that she had run into. He grabbed her by the back of her shirt, but she spun around quickly, catching him in the solar plexus with a hard palm blow that knocked him back. In his shock, he was weak, and Alexis was desperate upon hearing the orders given to the rest of the gang by the leader, who was starting to pick himself up from the ground. She had to do something. Playing a quick power move, she palmed the young man in the nose and pulled his head into a headlock. The gang was fast approaching, as the young man’s blood dripped in a river from his nostril. She placed her hand on the other side of his head, her eyes taking on a wild and desperate look. “Lex, don’t do this” you can’t do this?” A split-second after the thought ran through her head, she became a different person. Her eyes steeled as the other four boys approached. One of them laughed. “What you gon” do, honey? Let Darry go.” She growled under her breath. “I’ll” I’ll snap his fucking neck, I swear to God. Back the fuck off.” At that, Darry’s eyes widened- far be it from him to feel the grip that she had on his neck. He reached down to something tucked in at his waist- a gun. “Oh my god, a gun?” She panicked. Snapping her hands sideways, she heard a sickening crack, as Darry’s hands fell limp at his side, and his entire great weight weighed down on her tired arms. “What did you do, Alexis”!” But there wasn’t time to think about it. “Don’t scream, don’t scream” just go.” She bolted. Darry’s body slumped to the ground, limp, falling like a giant redwood crashing to the forest floor. .:: ::: ::. Alexis stumbled towards the church door, the blister on her foot having been rubbed to bleeding by now- she could feel that much, as the red, sticky fluid began to drain into her sock. The recent memories were weighing heavily on her now, though they had happened a scant ten minutes ago. Had she been on auto-pilot? She had to have been? she never would have done it otherwise? never. She stopped short of the door and threw up into the bushes. The sounds of Darry’s neck snapping echoed in her head over and over again, a sickening cacophony that she doubted she would ever be able to wipe away. Panting heavily, she forced the creaking door of the small church and stumbled into the sanctuary. The room was dark, and smelt of must, as though the church must have been quite old, or perhaps as though all of Alexis? senses were in hyper-drive, and everything struck her more strongly than it should have. “You’re a monster.” Alexis fell to her knees in the center aisle halfway up to the altar. All those weeks of Den training came back- when they first began, after the initial rituals of commitment, they had started on a course of physical training. As she recalled, the summer they had learned had been brutally hot, and the training had been more difficult than anything she had ever done. Luckily for Alexis, though, she was more resilient than some of the others. A good portion of the novice recruits had been lost those few weeks, but Alexis had been proud of herself for sticking it out. And now what had it gotten her? She looked down at the blood on her hands, and a wave of nausea swept through her, sickening, intensifying with every passing second. Her disgust intensified as she crawled, inch by inch towards the altar. “You promised” you promised?” The words assailed her, and she whimpered as though she was going to go insane at each knell. She had promised herself? she’d promised she would never use those powers that The Den had given her ever again. The Den was behind her now, and that meant that the days of fighting like a cold-blooded killer were as well. She never thought she would have had to use those skills- at least not until she’d been through The Den’s orders, and was out on mission. She’d never meant to hurt anyone? never. She was supposed to be snapping vampire necks, not fellow humans. Finally, for the first time since she’d fled Cleveland, Alexis Key burst into helpless tears. She fell upon the soft, velvety carpeting of the altar, her tears crushing down the fabric like aquatic giants crushing tiny fiber villages. One blood-soaked hand reached heavenward as she tried to pray. But her words came out only jumbled nonsense. “Monster” monster? monster?” .:: ::: ::. April 2005 Undisclosed Location A stone-cold look was in Lia Vlaszy’s eyes. The look held nothing of care for anyone- looking into those eyes would have lent you nothing of sympathy. Her jaw was set in a hard line as she sat at her desk, her taloned fingernails tapping on the ebony wood. Such was the only noise breaking the stoic silence of the study. Plans were formulating in her mind- the time had come where plan-making was essential. Certainly, the impact was over now, and her blood debts were all paid, but there was part of her still clinging to what was recently passed. Part of her desperately wanted to bathe in the blood of that one great enemy who, a few scant months ago, was desperate to have his love back. The typical smirk was absent from her face at this moment, this moment when plans roiled, unhindered by the thought of a narrow plot of revenge. After all, she had had her moment of revenge- she had made him pay for what he did to her. Still, some part of her wanted more. More blood? enough blood to coat the entire earth, and the gods damn whoever stood in the middle of such a battle. She craved it, hungered for it, the sanguinary thirst overwhelming practically every other sense that she might have possessed, even her reason. Yet, the reason still had a strong hold- he was as elusive as could be these days, and certainly would not lend himself easily to embarrassment. That was the shame- his own ego always got in his way. He didn’t want to look the cuckold, so he ducked her at every opportunity. As much as it was out of plan, she had to admit that it was satisfying to know what he was doing- to know that he feared her. He didn’t have to say so- avoiding her was an outright acknowledgement that he considered her power something that was not to be trifled with. An admission that he had never once made to her face, though she had seen it boiling behind his glances many a time, even when he was in full rage. She sighed, her brow furrowing in intense thought- so what was the order of the day then? She could turn her attention somewhere else, and easily make a go of it. After all, the entire world was at her disposal, now that she had nothing to worry about. Never again would she be expected to be simply an accessory, or a lap pet. She brought her fingernails up to her forehead, pressing them against the taut white skin stretched across the well-formed tiny bones of her face. Thoughts began to cascade in a rushing river. But suddenly, she jumped. The sounds of scrabbling and ferocious barking interrupted her reverie. Angrily, she shoved the chair back from the desk and walked out into the hallway. Several feet from the dead-end of the hallway, Lia’s hellhounds, Damek and Jirina stood barking. The hair was raised high along the bony shoulder ridges that she herself had designed for them. She had to yell to make herself heard over their snarling and barking. “Jiri! Damek! Stop it!” There was no change in their behavior, and irritation surged forth- if her own hellhounds didn’t listen to the call of their mistress, then something was certainly amiss in the house. But she closed her eyes a moment, calmed herself, and surveyed the situation- here they were, barking at a wall, with no intruder in sight. Tricks. Someone was up to their bullshit again. The irritation surged forth afresh, but she shoved it back down her throat, shaking her head. “Ridiculous?” Within moments, she had made her way down the stairs, pulled on a long trenchcoat, and stalked out of the house. .:: ::: ::. Three hours later, and she was no better. The world continued to weigh upon her, mocking her. And the only outward sign of it was a haughty sneer, written across her face in unmistakable lines. People crossed the street to avoid her. The world before her was mottled and shifting- nothing was as it seemed to be. Every statue in the public gardens had twisted into something tenuous, not of this world- a substance that seemed not entirely real. A sculpture of a father holding his daughter twisted into the clothing of the Long Night, and the man became Aric? the little girl, of course, morphed into Angela, but only out of the corner of her eye. When she turned her eyes back, the illusion was completely gone. She’d left the gardens in a flurry of motion, and almost plowed into a young couple walking a stroller along the street. Her eyes were inexorably drawn into the apparatus, and no matter how hard she tried not to see, her mind forced her to look. Inside, a little child sucked its thumb, possessed of wheat-blonde hair, and sparkling green eyes. Her snarl startled the child so that it began to cry. Everywhere before her was that infernal little face. Every child on the street turned into a blonde-haired cherubim, taunting her with sweet little smile and laugh. It took all the strength that Lia could muster to keep from enacting a scene of complete slaughter. Every single time she turned her back on the street, she could hear it. “Mama?” The tiny little whispered voice, now singing with laughter and mischief, taunting her to chase. Memories of French fields cascaded through her mind, chasing the little child with laughter written across her face, and Mama’s image scrawled in her expressive green eyes. Suddenly, her eyes widened. There was one course of action she could take- only one, one last resort. But she would go to any lengths to purge this from her head. She wasn’t the person that she used to be, and concern for mortals was entirely beyond her- especially concern for dead mortals. All a dead mortal was to her was evidence of a satisfying meal. And she would prove that, even if only to herself. .:: ::: ::. France seemed to squirm under Lia’s touch, and that was something that she took glee in. Long ago, the weeds had grown over the home that she had occupied with Aric, the servants, and her two children so long ago. There was not even a whispered memory of it here. She didn’t care a whit for that in any case- mortal memories, no matter how they haunted her, had long ago ceased to matter to her. Instinct and the past guided her like an unseen hand towards the place she intended to visit with her deathly cold hand of vengeance. It helped, of course, that there was a beaten path leading to the spot- someone had been here recently. She sneered. If she had to guess, she’d say it was that coward whose name she dared not even speak in her mind, for fear that her disgust would overwhelm her and she would tear her own hair out by the handful. With eyes as cold as glass, she strode across the tall-grown field, finding the night-time scent of lilacs suddenly quite sickening. But that was fine with her- soon, the lilac smell would be entirely overwhelmed by that of brimstone. Crack! The tip of her boot smashed brutally into a tombstone that was old and cracked with age. The letters had long since worn off of it, but she remembered what they said, as though she’d carved them herself. Aric had spent enough time mooning over what to carve on the blessed thing that she guessed that it would be well engraved into her mind for eons to come. She knelt down, her eyes caressing the blackened stone coldly. She didn’t know how it had possibly survived all this time, save for some farmer’s interest in preserving the history around here- the French were absolutely mad about themselves, so of course they preserved whatever they could for posterity. That, however, would be something for them to grieve over this evening. She bent her head, as though in prayer. Ah, my girl, my little girl? You’ve been harassing my damned soul to come back to you for years upon years now, all irritated by that spineless father of yours. Oh yes? despite how much you seem to love him, Papa is quite useless to me now. Your dreams of once again having the perfect family are, I’m afraid, quite impossible, all things considered? but I’ve still come here to see you. An emotionless smile twisted across her face. You and Mama shall have a talk, you see? because as much as you seem to enjoy playing your tricks, I still don’t believe that you quite understand the situation. Innocent souls cannot, after all, fathom the movement of devils, and I dare say that you long ago ceased to understand Mama for what she was, and what she was born into. You were born into it too, you know, though you were never old enough for me to teach you what you had been given. Half-Vlaszy? I am surprised that the plague took you at all, with my strong Revenant blood running through your veins, but you must have been an exceedingly weak soul to allow it to do so? And that is why I’m here. For too long you have blamed me, Angela. For too long you have attempted to make Mama human again? but Mama is dead, Angela. Mama has been dead for quite some time, and what you are attempting to convince only looks like Mama on the surface. As unfathomable as that is, I’m afraid that I’m not quite so merciful as a mother might have been, and I shan’t suffer you any mercy now that you’ve dragged me all the way back to wretched France and its wretched memories. Shall I do my business with you now? She stood from the grave, pulling out the small rosary that had been torturing her so the past few weeks. She tossed it emotionlessly on top of the stone, where a quiet clinking noise indicated that it had hit its mark. And then she turned away, as she had so many years ago after flinging her rosary down upon the stone to go back home to Zdice. The koldun’s power rose within her this time, though, and she was finally able to do exactly what she had wanted to do for eons, but simply had not the stomach to attempt. She turned to face the grave for a moment. With a face completely blank of emotion, she made a small gesture with her hand, and the smell of acrid smoke began to pour through the air as the fire began to rise from grave-stone itself. Another hand motion ensured that the leaping flame grew? and as it began to spread, she turned from her work. Preternatural speed carried her away across the field, away from the fire and the Red Fear that it would inevitably bring when it turned into the blazing inferno that she had envisioned in her head before casting her magic. When she was far enough away, she paused to listen- and she thought she could hear a young girl’s ethereal screaming. Her cruel crimson lips twisted into a sensuous smirk. .:: ::: ::. We bring forth monstrous birth To the world’s light .:: ::: ::. That satisfied-cat smirk suddenly crossed her face again. As she walked down the street back towards the house, she flipped open her phone. Not caring at all that it was three in the morning, she dialed in the number of her lawyer in Montreal. The phone rang several times before he answered, half-yawning, half-speaking. “Eric G’nette.” She put on a silky smooth purr. “Eric” what a pleasure to speak with you again.” She heard the sound of rustling- she could imagine the little puppet sitting up in his bed and forcing his slim, wire-rimmed spectacles onto his nose, while running a finger through his tousled sandy-blonde hair. “Mrs. Chalemette” what a? surprise.” The way he said the last word meant anything but one would have normally expected it to mean- perhaps a surprise in the sense of finding a horse head in your bed in the middle of the night, but certainly nothing pleasant. She went on airily. “Eric, I’m terribly sorry to have woken you, I know I must have, but you know all my pertinent business concerns always end up happening at night somehow.” He sighed on the other end of the phone. “What is it this time?” He sounded annoyed. That made her smile. It was never much of a challenge to make him angry. “You do still have my divorce papers, don’t you?” “Of course” they’re in the safe at the office.” She made a sound of faux-delight on the other end of the phone. “Oh wonderful! Would you be a real love and file them tomorrow” I don’t think I’ll be wanting to hang on to them anymore.” “Are you SURE this time” Last time, I was about to file them when you called to get me to hold off.” She paused for a moment. “But” of course I’m sure, Eric. I mean, I’ve just had a discussion with Mr. Chalemette, and we’ve decided that it’s just not going to work out? so let’s file them tomorrow, okay?” He sighed heavily on the other end of the phone. “It’ll be taken care of first thing in the morning.” The smile that graced her face on the other end of the phone- well, let’s just say that it was a good thing that Eric couldn’t see it, otherwise, he would have been direly alarmed. “Good” you’re a love, Eric, you know that?” “Can’t you call me during normal hours?” he asked, finally exasperated. She made a clucking noise with her tongue. “Remember who pays you, angel.” She clicked the phone shut, pleasure crackling over her face like flames licking. That would take care of that, then- there was no sense hanging on to the papers anymore now that there was no game to be played. If Corvus wanted to pretend like nothing mattered, then he could. She certainly didn’t give any more of a shit about him than he had ever pretended to give about her. It had all been about the game- for so long, he’d led her around on a leash, that it was her turn to do so? and for a good few months, it had been a fun game, but now she was tired of it. Now, her preference ran towards something entirely more sanguinary in nature. Looking rather self-satisfied, she raised one hand to the other and cracked her knuckles resolutely. She wasn’t sure exactly where she would start- but as far as she was concerned, now that old ties were cut, the world was hers, waiting on a platter- and she intended to pluck it up between her talons and squeeze it until it ran dry of all its blood. It was time for the last of the Vlaszy line to claim her birthright, and rule- and so the line would rise from death and claim dominion over all in an apocalyptic hail of blood rain sneaking in with the moon-drenched night. .:: ::: ::. song lyrics: 'Blood Hunger Doctrine' by Dimmu Borgir .:: ::: ::.
