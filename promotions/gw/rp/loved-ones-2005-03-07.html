Loved Ones

7 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/07/05 'Don't even think about it, Michael. You promised me and your daughter that you would stay here.' 'You promised me and your daughter that you would stay here.' 'You promised me and your daughter.' 'You promised.' The words continued to ring through Michael's mind, but he couldn't let them sink in now. 'They're just words,' Michael thought to himself definitively, 'words that shouldn't be allowed to dictate the way I choose to live my life. I don't deserve to sit here and twiddle my thumbs. This... I can't stay happy doing just this.' He watched his cousin wrestle every week, and he felt something he hadn't felt in a long time. Even when Mindy told him about what her father said, it only made him angrier- he was jealous. He needed to get back into the ring- if not for the World Title, it was just to prove he still could do it. That his title win wasn't some fluke. That... and something else. Before Mindy could add any more to her diary, she was surprised to find her bedroom door thrown open. Whirling around in her seat, she was surprised to find Jaden standing in the doorway. 'Mindy, come out here, please, I need your help. It's Michael...' Mindy stood up quickly at Jaden's sentence. 'Mikey? What's wrong?' 'Just head out there and talk to him, he's not even paying attention to me now. Maybe he'll listen to you.' 'Why?' Mindy persisted. 'What's he doing?' Jaden shook her head, either out of frustration, sadness, or a mix of the two. 'He's going to leave again.' With that, Jaden turned at the heel and bounded off to her daughter's room, presumably to keep Gabi from getting up again. Mindy wasn't left with much of a choice. 'Mikey...' When she wandered out of her bedroom, she caught Michael just as he grabbed his leather jacket and made his way to the front door. 'Michael... Michael!' Michael threw the door open, before he slowly turned to face his cousin. 'You called me Michael.' Mindy couldn't help but at least crack a smile from the remark. Still, the situation wasn't one to be giddy over. 'What's going on? Jaden says you won't listen to her and that you're gonna leave...' 'Well,' Michael began as he put his jacket on, 'I guess that isn't too far from the truth to be honest with you, cousin.' 'But... but what about what you said to me earlier?' Mindy inquired. Michael tilted his head at the question. 'Remind me what that exactly was.' 'Sleep on it,' Mindy recited, going as far as to say it in Michael's tone of voice, 'give yourself some time to think before you go doing anything else. Remember, Mikey?' Michael sighed and pushed back his messy black hair. 'Eh... yeah, I remember.' 'So, maybe you should take some of your own advice? You can't be feeling that bad... just talk to me, okay?' Michael stared blankly at his cousin- he wasn't in much of a mood to talk, not any more. 'No,' he stated coldly, 'it's not okay.' Before Mindy could cry out in protest, Michael was out the door. Michael sat behind the wheel of his jet black Porsche 911, gazing out at the empty road ahead which was bathed in night sky and dim street lights that barely worked. It made him wish he was in New York again. It was the wish alone that got him to the airport to buy his way onto a plane. At the stroke of midnight, Michael Mercer was travelling across the country. In some ways, he preferred it that way- the sky was more beautiful to him at night than it was during the day. Good morning, sunshine awake when the sun hits the sky Look up the sounds that surround the day you died She waits for me outside a hole in the ground In this one way thinking you might get the upper hand Dear Newo Ikkin how's Apollo been treating you? Has he been a good boy since the day I left? Give him my love and a sweet kiss for his head 'Cause I won't be coming home- when you get this, I'll be dead It was more or less half past seven when he landed, and it wasn't too long after that when his cell phone started to buzz. With a sigh, he answered it tentatively. 'Yeah?' 'Are you going to come back soon, Mikey?' The voice alone was enough for Michael to want to choke out a kitten. He gritted his teeth as he stormed out of Laguardia Airport and into the always busy streets of New York. The neon lights were still bright enough to blind the toughest of eyes, but the sun was on its way up, and then it would already be time for Michael to do what he needed to do. He had to see New York, yes, but he already grew upset with the sight of it; the knowledge that his home was back in New Mexico was not pleasant in the slightest bit. And his thirst for the answer to his problem would not be quenched in a simple manner. 'Of course I'm coming back,' Michael groaned in what could have been considered his biggest lie. He was way too tired to argue with his cousin at that moment to give her the obvious reason of his departure. 'Why did you call me now?' 'Because I don't want you to run away all upset and stuff,' the girl cried into the phone, 'you're too good for that, Mikey!' Michael rubbed his forehead with his free hand as he walked out to the street in hopes of finding a cab. 'May I ask you something, Mindy?' 'Sure, cuz,' Mindy answered ever so cheerfully. 'Anything you want!' 'Okay... what made you want to become a wrestler?' Mindy's voice didn't surface with an answer for a good couple seconds. When she did find her response, though, it was mentioned with her perpetual sense of glee. 'Because you were the greatest wrestler in the world, and you were a role model to me. I wanted to return the favor and be a wrestler just like you!' 'Well there you go,' Michael said softly. His comment left Mindy confused. 'What? Whaddya mean, Mikey?' Michael frowned and closed his eyes. 'You're doing it for fun... it's not fun for me anymore, cousin.' He grimaced, almost as if in physical pain, although truthfully, it was only his heart that ached. 'I don't know if it ever was,' he continued, 'no.. I don't think it was much more than something I did because I could do nothing else with my life. I think that's why I did it.' Mindy's tone of voice soon changed into one of sorrow. 'What are you saying?' she asked. 'What is wrong? Did I do anything to make you upset?' Michael's brown eyes opened and scanned the area. A yellow taxi waited idly for him. The conversation had to end. 'Why did you come to me, Mindy?' he inquired, and his voice had also changed into one that pleaded for an answer. 'Why me... of anyone else you could have gone to... why did you have to choose me?' Mindy hesitated, and gave Michael enough time to walk to the cab and throw the back passenger side door open. 'I knew it was my fault,' Mindy informed coldly; she sounded as though she was crying already. 'I don't like it when you say things like that, Mikey... you could have just asked me to leave you alone!' Michael winced in sadness. Maybe what he told her wasn't the best thing he could have said, after all. 'No, I didn't mean it in that se-' Too little, too late. Mindy hung up on him. 'Well... maybe that means she won't miss me anymore.' Michael climbed into the car and closed the door behind him. '110 Bronx Park East,' Michael mumbled, which prompted the middle-eastern cab driver to turn around and glance at Michael with a bewildered look on his face. 'Quite the trip, my friend.' 'Yeah,' Michael replied slowly. 'Yeah.. that it is.' It took close to an hour for Michael to reach his destination, but he was happy in a bittersweet way with the wait turned nonexistent. He was home- finally, truthfully home. He gave the driver the money he needed to pay him off, before he slid himself out of the cab and eyed over his house. It hadn't changed one bit, still as average a house as it always was. ~*~ 'You like the place?' Chris asked his nephew. Then, a thirteen year old Michael Mercer slowly nodded his head. He hated the house; his parents' apartment on the other side of the neighborhood was home, not the untidy two story house that he found himself in. However, living with an uncle who was a near seven foot tall wrestler, he wasn't going to argue with him. A nod of the head would be enough. 'I know it's not much comfort, but I sorda know how you're feelin' kid,' the thirty year old man said, giving Michael a comforting pat on the shoulder. 'I lost my parents when I was young, too... you still have people around that care for ya, you know.. just because they can't be called 'mom' or 'dad' doesn't mean they can't be there for you, alright?' Michael sighed and nodded again- he was too nervous to do anything else. It was actually the first time he'd ever met his uncle Chris. Of course he had watched his matches on television, and had thought of him as his idol.. but he was also careful not to piss him off. After all, he had seen how dangerous Chris was. 'Yeah...' Michael stammered, no louder than a whisper. Chris wasn't going to rag on him for being so quiet- he hadn't a reason to blame him for it, either. He didn't want to talk to anyone when it happened to him, either. But Michael's case was different... so much different. ~*~ Michael walked up the front steps, his ragged Converse All Stars making a more than familiar clack clack as his feet smacked pavement. He turned the doorknob, and was happy to feel the comfort of the doorknob turning in his grasp. He pushed the door open and sighed in relief. ~*~ A teary-eyed Michael knocked helplessly on the front door- how he managed to carry the weight of his two duffel bags over each of his shoulders was unknown to him. His friend Eddie had helped him carry two of them half way to Chris' house, but he soon had to go to school, which left Michael to trudge through the corrupt streets of The Bronx all on his own. But then again, he couldn't feel much of anything physically- his emotional burdens were enough to concentrate on. 'Please be home,' he whispered through choked sobs. He never believed in God and he never prayed, but that moment had to be an exception. He felt that only a prayer would get him to go on in his life- he wished his parents were around. He didn't think he'd need them so much, but now they were gone. He had no one to go to, except his uncle. Three more knocks, but no response came. 'Please!' Michael yelled, hoping the door would open. He gripped the doorknob with a firm right hand and turned it- locked. 'Come on, be home!' Few people glanced at the boy as they passed, but each person was too enamored with their own priorities to care. No one had the courage to step up and ask if the young teenager needed help. Eventually, however, they would not need to. A wave of cold air passed from the whirl of the door opening, and in its place was the one person Michael expected to be on the other side. They both stared at eachother for a long moment- words could not express either of their feelings. Chris hadn't known what happened- it had only taken place an hour before, so how could he have known? However, by the tears in Michael's welled brown eyes, he knew that whatever it was that sent the boy to his doorstep, it was something important. Lowering his head, Chris stepped back and allowed Michael inside. Michael's eyes swiftly hit the wooden floor as he made his meek entrance to the home. ~*~ Michael gently closed the door behind himself before he made his way through the living room. Some of his things were still there, unpacked and where they always stayed- his uncle's couch, the bookcase, the cherry wood coffee table. Other stuff, though, was boxed up as if it would be out of the home any second. The place did feel emptier than it used to, but it was still home. He hadn't even expected much- if anything- to be left, so he was grateful. There was another thing that remained that he didn't bother to notice at first, but when he saw it, it made him want to tear his heart out. A picture hung up atop a hollow entertainment system, which was now reduced to nothing but empty shelves. The picture was of Michael and his best friend Eddie when they were ten, along with both of their parents at a Fourth of July picnic in 1990. It was the one moment from his childhood Michael remembered in which he was smiling, and he was in disbelief it was still there. ~*~ 'Hey, Eddie,' Michael said with a smile at the sight of his friend who had stepped into the doorway of his room. 'Ready to walk today?' Etienne was thirteen just like Michael, although older by a couple months- his birthday was in eight days at the time, the twentysecond of February. 'Eh, why are ya askin' me that?' he replied with a slight French acccent, wild purple hair in front of his face. 'Parents lost their licenses or somethin'?' 'You're a funny man,' Michael mentioned sarcastically. 'Really, though, I think my dad's got some shit at the bank to deal with.. he's been on the phone for a good hour now.' 'An hour, shit... woke ya up, didn't he?' Michael nodded his head as he finished tying his sneakers and stood up. 'Man, I woulda been pissed if my dad did that.' 'When did I say he didn't piss me off?' Eddie chuckled and shook his head. 'Your parents do scare me sometimes, dude. Your mom offers me breakfast every morning when I come here, and when she's too busy she apologizes like I'm gonna smack her and tell her to get in the kitchen.' Michael snickered; he felt guilty laughing at such a thing, but he couldn't help it. His parents were too nice- sometimes too much for their own good. 'Eh heh.. she's always like that. And you should know, me and you've walked to school together every day since we were what, eight?' 'Well technically we got rides to school when we were eight, but whatever, Mikey.' They both laughed, as carefree as can be. Michael grabbed his bookbag that rested next to his feet, and slung it over his shoulders. 'Alright, let's take off.' Both teens were ready to do so, until Michael's father had barged into the room. The bedroom door almost got taken off its hinges as a result. 'Michael, I want you to pack as much of your stuff as you can, you're going to your uncle's house today, alright?' The order left young Michael dumbfounded. He'd never been to his uncle's house once. Why now? 'But w-' he started, but was quickly cut off. 'Don't question it, just do as I say.' By the time Michael finished packing his bags, Maggie and Joe were nowhere to be seen. Michael would never see his parents again. ~*~ Michael sighed and made his way into the kitchen. To his good fortune, there was a kitchen knife left on the counter top just next to the sink. All he needed to do was put it to use... 'I never questioned why I was the only one left behind,' he murmured to himself, 'but now I have no other choice. I fought through everything... I wrestled through it all, in hopes it would ease the pain, but I just wanted to find the people who took my parents from me... that was all I wanted.. and then you died, Chris... you fucking left me to do what? I'm still here and I'm nothing... why? Ugh.. well, I guess I'm not going to be here much longer.' He could feel the cold of the knife graze his skin. Just one slice... I've spent so long sitting down here Paper cut my heart in half and discard the evidence When it's yours, come send me the last half Doused in kerosene in a torched, blazed blood bath When boy sets fire, God knows you've lost at a cost that has no price When you've purchased guilt Stand at attention and make sure you know your lines and yourself You'd say I'll be home alone again, waiting... 'Shit.' His grip on the knife loosened, and the weapon hit the tiled kitchen floor with a loud clang. Soon enough, Michael found his head buried in his hands. 'God dammit...' He sat there for the longest time, thinking to himself. Could he really just go and kill himself? Was it really that simple? Would Mindy really just shrug it all off? No way... she wouldn't- she couldn't. 'Why does this have to be so fucking difficult?' 'Who the hell's down there?' a voice called from upstairs. Michael's eyes widened in disbelief. 'Oh, God.' 'I'll kill ya, motherfucker!' the voice continued. A female voice erupted to argue with the male one, but it quickly disappeared as footsteps could be heard clammering down the flight of stairs in the living room. Michael wanted to run- he could have if he wanted to... but there was no real point. Sighing, he sat there on the kitchen floor, looking out into the living room to see the person who threatened his life. At first it was just some random stranger in a white tee shirt and plaid pajama bottoms with a really pissed off set of bed hair. Purple bed hair. That was when the French accent from the man sunk into Michael's head. As the man approached him, it became more and more clear as to who the man with the baseball bat was. Etienne stared down at Michael for a second, bat armed and ready for attack, and then... it dropped to the ground. 'Michael?' Lyrics: 'Junesong Provision', Coheed And Cambria
