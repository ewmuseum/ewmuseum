The Dawning of the Paint

11 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 11, 2004 - A GRAVE SITE SOMEWHERE IN HIROSHIMA - .:We can see a dark figure standing amongst a number of grave stones. The figure appears to be dressed in a long trench coat, with the hood of the trench coat draped over the figure's head. A thick night fog is forming just above the ground, and around the feet of the figure as the moonlight spills from the night sky:. VOICE: I look around, and what do I see? I see a world that turns it's back on the weak and deprived, and embraces the strong and wealthy. I see a planet full of violence and betrayal, a world that is slowing walking down the path to destruction. Yet, despite all of these follies, all of this pain, I can't help but feel it necessary to inflict even more pain on those who stand around me. .:The figure crouches down in front of a pair of marble tombstones side by side, and begins to run his hand over the smooth objects. As the figure runs his hand over the stones, we see the name 'JETSUI' placed in between the connecting stones:. VOICE: Why do I feel this urge to hurt other individuals? Is it powered by revenge? No. Am I angry at the whole world in general? That is certainly not the reason. Then why? Why would I take something away from a man that means so much to him? What reasons would I have to inflict pain of one of my fellow creatures? Well, the reason is not as simple as I would like to believe. .:The figure stands up after feeling the tomb stones in front of him, and the pulls something out of his pocket. The item appears to be some sort of emblem attached to a chain-linked necklace. As the man looks down at the item in his hand, he proceeds to keep speaking:. VOICE: You see, when I was a young boy, maybe six or seven, I witness the murders of my mother, my father, and my younger sister. I watched a group of men slay every single member of my family. But as the blood flowed from the victims, I did not feel a sense of revenge. As I watched my family's blood hit the walls of the room, I did not ask myself 'what should I do?' No, instead I asked myself 'why would my father let this happen?' I asked myself 'why did this happen to MY family?' And I now realize that this happen to my family because they were weak. And ever since I witnessed this act of the natural order working itself out, I have devoted my life to ensuring that others do not make the same mistake. .:The figure throws back his hood, and places the chain around his neck. Upon removing the hood, we see that the figure is indeed Takahashi Jetsui. Takahashi's face is now painted with a Crimson Red base coat with a large Japanese symbol painted in black in the center. Takahashi's long black hair flows in the slight wind that wistles through the grave sight, as he pulls a cigarette from inside the trench coat, and lights it:. TAKAHASHI: It is shame, however, that people in this world do not learn the error of their ways simply by a man preaching words of change to them. I cannot help but think what have become of me if I had not had my family taken away from me. My family had studied the ways of the Budhasei for generations. My great grandfather was the first in my family to begin studying the peaceful ways of the Bushasei, and what did we learn from his murder? Not a single thing. Instead of realizing that it is human nature to kill, or be killed, my family continued to be followers of the Bushasei. And they kept their beliefs all the while, until it became my turn. .:Takahashi begins to walk away from his parent's grave stones, and lets out a couple puff of his cigarette before continuing:. TAKAHASHI: Now that I am the last line of blood in the Jetsui tribe, it is my call as to what direction I go in, and I refuse to make the same mistake my father and my ancestors before him made. I refuse to allow myself to be ignorant to the natural ways of the human species. And I refuse to be surrounded by love and peace, like the love and peace that swells within the walls of Global Wrestling. .:Takahashi continues to walk through rows upon rows of tombstones representing the deceased in Takahashi's home town of Hiroshima. The moonlight follows Takahashi with every stride that Takahashi makes through the thick fog along the damp graveyard grounds. Suddenly, as Takahashi approaches a fencing around the graveyard, he begins to remove what appears to be a dagger from the trench coat. With a quick leap, Takahasi bounds over the raught-iron fencing with the dagger-in-case clinched in his hand. Once Takahashi is on the other side of the fence, he proceeds to enter what seems to be a thick forest. Takahashi walks into the forestry, the glow of the cigarette in his mouth guiding the way. Takahashi finally stops:. TAKAHASHI: Here we are. .:Takahashi stops in a spot in front of a tree, and begins to slowly remove the dagger from the case in his hand. Once the dagger is out of the case, we can visibly see what appears to be blood stains lining the large knife. Takahashi then throws the cigarette from his mouth and approaches the three before him:. SCRAPE! SCRATCH! SCRAPE! SCRATCH! SCRAPE! .:Takahashi finishes carving a message into the tree, and blows the layers of wood shavings, exposing exactly what he carved in the tree. Takahashi then rams the dagger into the tree, just above the lettering that he scracted into the tree:. SLAM! .:The message reads:. LESSONS IN LIFE ARE HARD AND THEY'RE ABOUT TO GET HARDER T x J x 04 .:Takahashi then removes the necklace that he placed around his neck, and hangs the necklace on the dagger that was put into the tree. The emblem on the chain features a series of what appear to be Japanese symbols. Takahashi then begins to walk into the damp Japanese nights, creating a trails in the lingering fog that remains above the cold ground:.
