Date: 090503 Things started to pick up at the hotel. We didn’t have many problems to be handled, but every once in a while there would be one. I would go to the room, talk to the guy, suggest that bad things could happen if he kept it up. One of a few things would happen. The john would nod, leave and never come back. The john would nod, come back and be good. Or he could continue the abuse, or try to get rowdy with me. Either way, it was nothing that I couldn’t handle. Over the weeks, I thought of ways to make mine a safer occupation. Latex gloves to keep fingerprints from getting on the body. Soft shoes for sneaking up on people. The garrote. It made handling reoccurring problems easier. Less physical confrontation, not that it I couldn’t use the workout. It just made things quicker and less painful. Clean-up was a lot quicker too. The women were happy and more money came in. The more money came in, the richer I got. Plus, the news of my new position had apparently hit the right crowd, because I was having less and less work to do. Didn’t bother me as long as Hector kept giving me my share. I convinced Hector that we needed to improve my job a little bit. If we bought small cameras and placed them in the rooms, I could watch several rooms at once rather than listening with my ear against the wall. Also, we could start a small time blackmailing scam if worse came to worse. Hector had some connections and we were able to buy enough lipstick cameras to place in every room. Because of the small size and installation, it cost quite a pretty penny. But, it would be worth it in the long run. One particular gentleman sticks out in my mind, more than likely because he was the worst case I had to deal with. Watching my monitor with the divided screen to show the different rooms shortly after they were installed, I saw my cue to go to work. Getting up while still watching the screen, I watched as the man pulled back and knocked the woman in the left side of her face with a closed, solid fist. Not an open palm slap but a full, solid punch. And he did it again, striking the other side of her face. A new rage boiled inside me. I had seen the aftermath and I had listened to the act, but I had never seen a man beat on a woman before. All I had done to protect these women, my women, all being spoiled by the actions of this man. No, no, no this won’t do. Won’t do at all. I didn’t even wait to listen at the door to see what was happening inside. My blood was too hot to do that. The flimsy door gave way easily to my shoulder. He was standing over her crumpled body, staring at me, framed by the open doorway. “Well, well,” he said. “What do we have here” A hero, perhaps?” He spoke with the tone of someone full of hate, an endless rage. Like Bryce. I did one of the worse things you could do to a man prepared to fight. I rushed him, flat out, running and jumping at him. He caught me, and using my forward momentum, threw me into the wall behind him. I laid there for a second before getting up. Reaching into to my pocket, I drew out and flicked open my butterfly knife. “Oh, I’m scared now,” he said. “You should be,” I told him in my best, flat tough guy voice. He laughed. “You ever dance with the devil, boy?” He drew out the last word in a thick accent. I pondered his question. I am the goddamn devil. “Let’s dance.” I scored first blood, swiping my knife at him, slicing his tan naked chest. He swung his fist at my head, which I easily ducked, and connected a second shot to my gut, knocking the wind out of me. While I was doubled up, he drove a knee to my face. I thought I felt bones crunch. Falling to the floor, I dropped my knife. He came up and kicked me in the gut. I rolled a few feet, coming close to the bed. “You think you can get the best of me” Do you know who I am? I’m Georgio Vedici. I practically own this town,” he laughed. I saw the answer to my problem, sticking out from between the mattresses. The handle of a gun sticking out, the red dot of the safety button staring at me. “And now you. . .” his last statement grew into a scream. “You bitch!” I grabbed the handle and pulled, drawing out an automatic pistol with silencer. I rolled towards Georgio, his back to me with my knife sticking out of his shoulder where the woman had stabbed him. She lay whimpering on the floor. “Psst. Georgio,” I said. “What?” he asked, turning to me. The first bullet hit the right side of his chest, the second on the left, just below the heart, and the third about where I thought the heart was. Georgio fell to his knees, a shocked look on his face before slumping forward, dead. I dropped the gun. “Thanks,” the woman said. I nodded and leaned back onto the floor. “What?” Hector’s voice came over the phone. “It’s me. Does the name Georgio Vedici mean anything to you?” “Why?” “He’s dead and he apparently thought that he was someone important.” There was silence over the line. “What room?” There was a hint of concern in Hector’s voice. “Two A.” “Stay there. I’m coming over.” Hector came in and stared at the dead body on the floor. “Not good. Not good at all,” he kept muttering. “What’s the big deal?” “The big deal” What’s the big fucking deal!” I’ll tell yeh. Yeh killed Georgio Vedici!” he screamed. “So?” “So” So you just killed the only son of Allesandro Vedici.” “Okay,” I said. “And you’re not worried?” “Should I be?” “Well, considering you’ve just killed the son of the most prominent mafia don in this city, yes, you should be worried.” I stood there looking at Hector. “The son of a mafia don?” Hector nodded. “Yup.” I thought about it some more. The thought of waking up with a horse’s head in my bed crossed my mind. Not a pleasant thought. “Shit.” “My sentiments exactly.” Hector left me in the room with my thoughts and my problem. MY PROBLEM. One thing was for certain in my mind. If I was going down for this, I would be the only one going down. It wasn’t Hector. It wasn’t the girls. I was me. If I had to, I would be the one to pay the piper. Me and me alone. Because of the stature of the victim, extra precautions would have to be made for disposal. Using a hacksaw, I cut each finger off of both hands. So much for fingerprints. Taking a sledgehammer from the maintenance room, I went to work on Georgio’s face, breaking the jaw in several places and knocking out a bunch of teeth in the process. So much for dental records and picture id. The last thing to go was the clothing. Shirt, pants, and underwear could be burned and I could add the jewelry to the collection in my room to be taken to a random pawnshop out of town. The body would be dumped in the river after midnight. The current would take Georgio a couple of miles away before being discovered. By then, all other traces of him would be forgotten like all of the others before him. You would think that the disappearance of the son of a man like Allesandro Vedici would at least make the six o’clock news. It didn’t. I watched, expecting to see the famed mobster issuing a blood oath, vowing to find out what happened to his beloved son. Nothing. A week passed. Two weeks. I was in the clear. John Zero lifts the cell phone to his ear, waiting on the call to connect. Finally, he hears Deacon’s voice on the end of the line. “Where the fuck are you?” Zero yells into the phone, getting his voice to carry over the whipping wind, as he drives down the road in his Mitsubishi Eclipse Spyder convertible. “Yeah, yeah. I’m on the way. I’m dropping Dink off?” Holmes says, Zero smiles and slides into a ninety degree turn, heading down a tight alley. “Where are you?” Holmes asks. Zero laughs into the phone as he zips past Holmes and his Porsche. “Sonofa Bitch!” Holmes yells into the phone as Zero hangs up.
