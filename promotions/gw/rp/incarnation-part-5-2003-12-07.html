It’s mine! … … … … … … f. a. t. e. … Story Continues… As things became more complicated and the walls started to close in, Jesse made the choice that would change his life for sure. He called a doctor, more specific a scientific doctor who researched mental and physical disabilities or problems that affected a person pass normal constraints. So on the following morning Jesse hopped on a bus and traveled to Louisiana, which is known for its variety of prisons and rehab centers due to the small population it has. Currently two governors for the state of Louisiana are running for office, and for both it’ll be the first in history. A woman or an immigrant will be elected as governor for the state of Louisiana. Jesse was fascinated by the state and always found it a great place to live, but not in the way he would be soon enough. Riding in the bus Jesse turned on his CD player and listened to the lyrics of “How You Remind Me” by Nickelback letting his mind flow with the lyrics and his ideas go free. Snoring away the bus keeps going to it’s destination of where Jesse shall spend much of his time at. *** *** *** The afternoon sky was fading away, and soon nothing would be left but the darkness of night. As the doors opened a drenched trench coated man came walking in the medical center. Almost ten o’clock and the rain began to down pour like crazy. “Sir may I get you checked in here?” asked the woman behind the desk as the man pulled back his hood and looked at her with his tangled brown hair that came down past his shoulders and was hidden within the trench coat. ”Yes, my names Jesse Fristik. I have an appointment,” responded the man who is known as Jesse. The woman logs onto the computer and processes a sheet, then hands it to Jesse as he looks around at the empty facility. Understanding that it’s so late, it’s no wonder it’s empty, but a lot of the staff seems to be missing as well. Seeing down the long narrow hallway, there appears to be very few lights on, therefore making the hallway lit only scarcely for those who are walking around. “Mr. Fristik sir, Dr. Lehman will be seeing you very shortly. If you would be so kind to take a seat over there for the time being, and he’ll be out to get you,” says the front desk lady, and Jesse listens to her. Taking a seat he looks out the window at the distraught weather. Rain pouring down as the thunder cracking the heavens wide open, and dark clouds blocking the sun from existence; it is without a doubt a night of complete and abrupt chaos. On nights like this Jesse can only remember the past, some good some bad, but on this specific moment, a generous moment he had with his daughter. The day before his big match, the night he was suppose to face off against Tristan Belmont for the World Title of Lights Out Wrestling in a Triple Tier Iron Man Match. The exact same match that had happened in the first World Title match of LOW’s history and crowning it’s first ever champion. However this night would be much different, this night would allow him to go up against a man so much more talented than all the rest. On this night Jesse was going to battle a man who had given him a lot to hope for a little to respect. Belmont was a champion, and was strong, but Jesse was stronger. This night was going to be the biggest night of Jesse’s life, and he thought he would share some time with his daughter, a seven-year-old blonde headed girl who was born from his now deceased wife Julie had given him a lot of hope after a run in with drugs. She was a blessing in disguise, and Jesse realized that. Samantha is her name, and with everything Jesse went through she was like the holy water that cleansed him, so in what Jesse hoped was not his final day, he was to spend some time with her. “Daddy, why do you wrestle?” said Samantha on that night, and that’s when Jesse had to lay it all out. “You know that’s a big question. I guess I can say it to you now that you are old enough. Sammy, I love to wrestle. It was given to me as a reminder of what my father was. When I was young, my father wrestled. Though he was Jewish, he found a way to wrestle, and was a third generation wrestler. In his time, he wrestled really well, and now I’m going to do the same in his honor,” replied Jesse. “Why though? Why not be an artist or a cop?” asked Samantha with her bright big blue eyes glaring at him. “Well, it’s all I know my father of. Wouldn’t you take after mommy if she was still around?” responded Jesse but quickly tried to reorder the question for his wife’s death was not a pleasurable memory to him. ‘Yes…” claimed Samantha, who now was wondering about her mother. (Interrupted) “Sir,” stated Dr. Lehman, “We need to go to the back Jesse, it’s important.” Jesse follows the doctor back to a room, the door steel, no window, no name on it, just plain steel, and inside the room is padded. Jesse looking around begins to look uncomfortable, but the doctor reinsures him with a seat as he pulls one up as well. A camera in the far right corner records everything as Jesse is about to talk. ”Jesse, you’re in a dangerous state. I know because I have diagnosed what you told me on the phone, and from what it seems, you’ve got a… well it’s not that hard to explain but it’s more complicated than what the stereotype of your condition is. It’s called a split personality, however, it’s not that simple. You don’t have two people living in you, it’s the fact that you have two personalities that control you in complete opposites. Such as, if you were very sensitive, then you would be kind and calm, as well caring, however if you started to feel groggy, or uncomfortable, soon you’d become angry and very emotionally raged with anyone around you. To be honest, it’s not uncommon, but in your case it’s slightly more aggressive. You told me Jesse, that it was uncontrollable and that you didn’t remember doing anything, however you’ve seen evidence of your terror afterwards. So in that case, you’ve got a blackout going on. This is a general term I use with people because it expresses the problem more clearly. You fade out when unfocused and become enraged, not fighting yourself, but losing yourself, you’re REAL self. In this case, I have only one way for you to survive…” says Dr. Lehman. Dr. Lehman leaves the room where then six police officers that were waiting in a room walk in and take custody of Jesse. He doesn’t fight, he doesn’t kick or scream, or even punch, but takes his hands and places them in the cuffs, and leaves with the escorted police officers. (To Be Continued in the New Series…)
