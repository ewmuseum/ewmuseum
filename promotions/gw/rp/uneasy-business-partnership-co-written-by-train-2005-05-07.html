Uneasy business partnership (co-written by Train)

7 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/7/05 Chapter I: Just Shoot Me Brick found himself once again storming through the hallways of the Global Arena towards Bubba McCoy's office. As usual, it was anger and frustration that drove him to it. Frustration over the fact that his friend and tag team partner Donny J. McNasty was placed on the shelf indefinitely. Frustration over the fact that Bubba McCoy, Global Wrestling's owner, was now forcing him to team up with Train, a man he generally despised, and whom he suspected of being behind the attack on Donny. And, as usual, he was going to let Bubba know how he felt. 'Excuse me,' said the receptionist, 'you can't go in there. Mr. McCoy--' As usual, Brick ignored the receptionist and burst into Bubba's office. 'May I help you, Brick?' Brick immediately reached into his pocket and pulled out a gun. Bubba began to rise out of his seat. Brick then placed the gun on top of Bubba's desk. Bubba looked down at the gun and then at Brick. 'I'm supposed to do somethin' with that?' Bubba asked. 'Yeah,' Brick replied. 'Shoot me.' 'Excuse me?' 'You heard me. Shoot me right now. 'Cause you might as well kill me, teamin' me up with that muthafucka Train.' 'Brick,' Bubba sighed, 'I don't feel like hearin' you bitch about this again.' 'Yeah, well, too damn bad, cause I'm gonna bitch about it an' you're gonna hear every damn bit of it! Plain and simple, I don't like the son of a bitch and I damn sure don't trust his ass!' 'I think you can trust him not to screw you over at High Octane, or on any other night as long as you're partners. He had his chance last Monday night. He coulda screwed you over with Salaok an' Frost, told the both of us to go to hell. But he didn't. He's got as much invested as you do.' 'Yeah, well, I ain't gonna trust him just 'cause you say so!' 'Oh yes, you are! If you like working for this company, you are.' 'Don't go there with me, Bubba.' 'Oh, I'm goin' there. Like I said last Monday night, you two lay a hand on each other or don't perform, I'll suspend the both o' ya, indefinitely. You two may be big draws, you may put asses in the seats, but neither one o' you is irreplaceable.' 'I don't like gettin' idle threats.' 'Good, 'cause I don't make 'em.' 'I don't make 'em, either. You think this is the only wrestling company in the business? As much as I love Global, as much as I've given to this company over the years, I will be able to move on. You put me out, there are other companies out there. Core, Four Winds, Lights Out, WCWF. Hell, if there's another Crossfire this year, I might just head to the Midwest.' The office got deathly silent. Brick and Bubba glared holes into each other, the desk between them. 'You do what you think you have to an' so will I. Now, as much as you hate Train, I know you hate Salaok a lot damn more. And High Octane ain't that far away. The Tag Gauntlet is how I'm kickin' things off. I suggest you get your mind on that and th' Tag Titles.' Bubba was right. Brick did hate Salaok more than Train. For almost as long as Global Wrestling has been in business, there has been a great deal of animosity between the CEO of Ruckus Inc. and the Harvester of Minds. The previous Monday, during Evolution's main event, Brick was more than willing to kill Salaok. He had his hands firmly clasped around Salaok's throat and nearly squeezed the life out of him, the only thing stopping him being Corvus' interference. 'Look at it this way. It might not be forever. Once you find proof Train either was or wasn't behind Donny's attack, all bets are off. You can beat the hell outta each other to your hearts' content...in a sanctioned match. Brick, look, I need you to think about what's best for the company.' 'Fine, I will.' Brick then started to walk out of the office. 'But I ain't gonna like it.' 'Oh, Brick?' 'What?' 'Ain't you forgettin' somethin'?' Bubba asked, pointing to the gun Brick left on his desk. 'Keep it,' Brick replied, walking out. Chapter II: The Workout The air reverberated with the sounds of battle. The hard, clattering chime of metal as it scraped across and banged into more metal pulsed loudly through the open space. Only the unrelenting echo of leather slamming into the leather could even attempt to drown it out. The heavy bag shifted violently with each blow. Train had set up the work out space on the bottom floor of his converted factory home with the intent of having everything he needed at arms length. Today he was getting his money’s worth out of hundreds of thousands he’d invested in the place. Today Train needed to hand out a beating. Someone... Something... Who gave a shit? As long as something felt his wrath all would be right with the world... eventually. But for now there was still work to be done. He planted his feet and popped his hips with each punch, allowing the maximum amount of power to be delivered in each shot. The blows slammed into the bag at a jackhammer like pace with sledgehammer like ugly intent. Train’s fists bore deep into the innocent, black leather sack currently serving as scapegoat as if into an imaginary set of ribs that he seemed determined he would actually hear crack if he could just deliver one more strike. His trademark dread locks whipped around frantically as he continued to pound, his mind dead to the world or any other task, but that. Sweat dripped down every inch of his face and torso. Once the capacity for conscious thought returned to him Train tried to recall how long he’d been at this, but drew a blank. The only reason he’d even thought to ask was the ache in his hands. The red, leather, open finger boxing gloves girding his hands could apparently only provide cushioning for so long. How long he’d been pounding beyond the gloves capacity to protect him was unclear. It’s been said that in moments of danger severely injured men have managed amazing feats of strength and endurance with adrenaline carrying them every step, no amount of pain able to stop them. It was obvious now rage had an identical effect. It hadn’t mattered that he hurt up until now. He’d felt no pain as he pounded his enemy, the bag having made the unfortunate mistake of impersonating the wrong man. The bag having made the mistake of impersonating Bubba McCoy. Every single, solitary, ounce of bad intent behind every single vicious strike was meant for him. Anybody watching would likely have assumed that Brick was the man being beaten in effigy, but Train was a different man these days. The world computed in a different manner than it ever really had before. Months ago Brick likely would have been the target of his anger, but now Brick was simply a means to an end. Mad about Donny? Tough shit. Tried to take Train’s head off with a bat? Couldn’t get the job done. And, more importantly, whether he liked it or not? Brick was going to help Train put gold over his shoulder. Would he like doing it? Nope. Would having no real option, but to help Train get under his skin? Yup. But that’s life. Like it or not Brick would have to act like a professional just like Train was going to do. He didn’t like the idea of having a man who hated him in the back “watching his back” in the ring, but that was neither here nor there. What was done was done and now they were left to deal with it. He may have hated Train, but at High Octane, in the Tag Team gauntlet Brick would do what he always did. He’d go out to the ring and do his damndest to wreck some shit. He’d do it because he was addicted to the crowd and just had to hear them cheer for him. He’d do it because, as much as he was loyal to him, Brick knew McNasty had never been anything, but an anchor. He’d never have had a legitimate shot at the tag team titles lugging him around. McNasty being taken out was the best thing to happen to Bricks floundering little career in a while and deep down in places he didn’t like to acknowledge existed? He knew it. No, there was no more malice for Brick than there had always been. It all belonged to McCoy. Train wasn’t the type of guy that appreciated being used. McCoy was trying to use him. This new found interest in putting over the tag team titles was recent. He’d been running a series to determine the fate of the Platinum Title for weeks now. Had the man many called the greatest Platinum Champion of all time been involved? Had he called Train in on it? Not even a fucking whisper. A shot at the International? Hell no. And who’s headlining the show? Some fuckin? pretty boy holding the strap fighting some little punk who hasn’t proven he has what it takes to stay on top for any real length of time. And where was Train? Back to jerking curtains at the top of the show?! Son of a bitch. It was all a game to him. It was all checkers and McCoy wanted to control all the pieces. Problem is he knew controlling Train wasn’t really an option. At least until he could half pin this Donny McNasty bullshit on him. Yeah... the muthafucka wouldn’t let Brick and Train work it out like men because Brick couldn’t prove Train had touched McNasty. Oh, but he won’t tell Brick to get off his dick because Train couldn’t produces video tape or eye witnesses to prove he didn’t.. Kinda like most of the roster wouldn’t be able to do either... And it hadn’t slipped past Train that Bubba didn’t seem to be doing much investigating on his own to find the culprit. Hell, why would he? It was all too convenient for him to jeopardize with the truth. Why cut Train loose for some shit he couldn’t prove when he had just the excuse he needed to justify putting him on a leash. To allow the dogg roam just as far as Bubba felt comfortable? Why put him out when you could just use him? Use his name to draw a crowd while keeping him out of the hunt for anything real. Naw, don’t do something like that...just stifle him. Give him the spots that he wanted him to have...that he thought was safe. That’s all this Brick shit was. Bubba playing a game of social engineering. This was his version of some fucked up Big Brothas program... setting up Brick as some kind of Uncle Tom ass chaperone to make sure the head strong nigga stays in his place. Damn redneck. Train hopped back and lunged forward again delivering a parting shot into the face of Bubba McCoy, super kick that left the impression of his combat boot tread pattern clearly visible in his mind’s eye. The heavy bag pitched violently as it dangled from the end of it’s chain. Train tore off the gloves, tossing them aside before messaging his aching hands. He could see the small scuffs and bruises on his knuckles, but it didn’t concern him. If they didn’t hurt while handing out an imaginary beat down they damn sure wasn’t going to hurt when stepped in that ring to put gold over his shoulder. Besides... he felt better now. Train sat his hands on his hips, standing silently as the sweat rolled down his body as the sounds of battle faded away. He stood for a few moments, watching the heavy bag gently sway back and forth...like a body from a noose. Damn... why couldn’t that be Bubba? No matter. It would be soon. Fuck what McCoy thought. Gold was gold and gold got you consideration. Tag team or otherwise. Brick and gold were big enough stepping stones to work with. No doubt Bubba had checker board locked down, but there was one problem and he’d find it out in good time. Denzel said it best... It’s chess... not checkers. Chapter III: The Fallen Man It was Valentine's Day. The Nomad Donny J McNasty entered the arena, instead of to 'Not Falling' by Mudvayne, to KISS's Disco song, 'I was Made for Loving You'. He went out to wish the fans a happy Valentine's Day. At one point, he called out his girlfriend Alexis Steele. Instead of music and flashing lights, the entire Global Arena went dark. As the lights came back on, Donny was left lying in the middle of the ring, bloody and convulsing, spitting out blood from his mouth. Laying next to him was a steel chair, but there was no sign of who did it. The Global Wrestling Medical Crew and Alexis Steele rushed down to the ring to tend to him. No one knew what happened or who did it. The current suspect is Train, given his history with Donny. But whoever was behind the attack was in for a world of hurt the minute the lights went out. Because Donny is the partner and good friend of one Jamal James Randall, known to Global Wrestling fans as Brick. And Brick doesn't take kindly to people hurting his friends. Place: Altoona, PA. [Brick walks into the hospital and up to the information desk.] Nurse: May I help you? Brick: I'm here to see a patient. [The nurse turns to a computer.] Nurse: Name of patient. Brick: Donald McNasty. Nurse: Ah yes. Mr. McNasty's room is on the third floor. Room 316. Brick: OK, thanks. [Brick walks towards the elevator and presses up. The elevator to his left immediately opens and he steps in and presses 3. Within less than a minute, he reaches the third floor.] Brick: 312, 313, 314, 315...ah, 316. [Donny lies in his hospital bed, bandaged up almost all over his head and face. His neck is in a brace and his body is wrapped up in bandages. Whoever attacked him did a number on him. Sitting next to the bed is Alexis Steele.] Brick: Hey. Donny: Hey. Brick: You look like shit. Donny: Maybe, but I feel fine. [Donny shifts in the bed some and groans loudly.] Alexis: Will you stop moving? You're in bad enough shape as it is. Donny: Don't you just love this woman? Brick: Look, man, I'm sorry for this. Donny: Why? You didn't do this to me. Brick: Yeah, but if I'd been there, this might not have happened. Donny: Hey, it was so dark in the arena that night, neither of us could have seen what happened. It might have been the both of us laid up. I'm just sorry this happened when we were getting so close to the Tag Titles. Brick: Now, I gotta team up with Train at High Octane. Donny: Yeah, that's what I've been meaning to ask. What the hell was Bubba thinking? Brick: I don't know. I think he just wants to test me. Donny: Hey, at least it won't be long. Just prove whether or not Train attacked me. Brick: To be honest, I do have some other suspects. Donny: Like? Brick: Salaok. He knew we were gonna challenge him and Frost somewhere down the line and plus you're my friend so he probably would have done it just to get to me. Donny: Does sound like him. Brick: Don't worry, Donny. I'm gonna find whoever did this and I'm gonna see that they have hell to pay. [Alexis casually looks at her watch.] Alexis: Oh my God, it's almost 1:00! Donny: So? Alexis: All My Children is coming on! Brick: Oh man, you're right! Turn to ABC! [Brick rushes over to a seat next to Alexis and quickly sits down as All My Children starts to come on.] Donny: You gotta be kiddin' me. Alexis: I am so glad this baby switch story is over. Brick: I know. Nine months o' that mess. Alexis: You know Edmund is dead, right? Brick: Get out! Alexis: Edmund can walk. Brick: What? Alexis: Yeah, he's been hiding it. Plus he set up Maria and Zach. Locked them in a shed and watched them on a monitor to see if they'd turn to each other. Brick: Oh snap! [Donny sits back and sighs.] Donny: I'm in hell. Brick/Alexis: SHHH!!! Chapter IV: One Phone Call The smoke hung in the air, wafting languidly through open space inside the truck. It morphed and shifted shape as it escaped from the end of the “hand rolled” Swisher Sweet cigar and blown free of Train’s lungs. The smoke was for more than painting pretty pictures in the air. It’s intoxicating smoke pushed Train’s consciousness up into the atmosphere, allowing it to hang as weightlessly as the smoke. Judging by the sullen feelings he’d been carrying all day Train decided he’d spent too much time letting this Brick, Bubba bullshit consume him. Tonight was a night to get right. No more being pulled down by those that wished they could be half of what he was. He sat reclining in the plush leather seats of the Denali listening to the music of the club, the beats thumping so loud from inside it was fairly clear even as he sat in the parking lot. Train had been here a few times, the club owner a big GW fan...or at least a Train fan. The door was always opened to him as were his tab and the women. The dude seemed pretty cool so Train always had enough respect to get blown out in the truck before heading in. Add a little Moet and a few shots of Hennessy and the buzz would last the evening. Train took another pull off the cigar, the additional fire burning it down to official roach status. He expertly held in the smoke while dabbing the cherry out in the ash tray before pushing it shut and billowing out a plume from his nostrils. Train sat up in the seat a bit adjusting his attire. It was much different than earlier today. The Adidas warmups were replaced by a set of black slacks, baby blue button up and a black leather jacket. The smell of sweat and rage replaced by the aroma of Cool Water and chocolate tie. He leaned over and reached into the glove compartment, his dreads shifting as the hung in a pony tail, and retrieved the spare bottle of Cool Water and gave himself another couple of spritzes to cover the distinct smell of the cigar. The bottle quickly goes back into the glove box and Train rights himself. Train quickly scans himself in the rearview, primping the threads for quality assurance purposes. Seeing that all is right he reaches for the door. Suddenly his hand freezes in mid air. The music of the club is drowned out by Jay-Z’s “99 Problems” as it blasted out of his jacket pocket. The hand shifted from the door to the interior pocket of the jacket, quickly retrieving his Moto Razor, flipping it open with a flick of his thumb before pressing it to his ear. “Sup?” Train asked cool, calm and relaxed as he’d ever been. “I should be asking you playboy,” the man on the other end of the line responded. Despite it’s familiarity Train had a hard time placing it for a moment. There was a second or to of silence until the tone and inflection recalled a name. J.J. Back in the day he was just a soldier, that run and gun cat to settle up debts and lay down shots if need be. J.J. wasn’t actually his name. Fact is he was always a skinny brotha, reminded everybody of J.J. Walker on good times. Not to mention that, in order to prove a point to prove a point to cats he didn’t like, he took to his name sake, throwing sticks of dynamite through the living room windows of cats who though they could disrespect the organization Train had helped build. At first he’d had a hard time recognizing the voice because the phrase he heard from him most often was “It’s done.” However it was a new day. he was set to move up threw the ranks and apparently he’d leaped up pretty far to be making a social call. “J.J.”” Train asked, trying to confirm the identity he already knew as he was the last man he’d expected to hear from. “You know it man.” J.J. replied “What up man?” “Bigs just wanted me to call and check up on you man.” “Check up on me?” “Yeah son. We all saw that shit this week. That mu’fucka coming at with that damn bat. Nigga was trying to take you slap out dogg!” “Shiiiiiit... You mean Brick” Man yall don’t need to be worried about that shit. I got a handle on his monkey ass man. And what’s up? Biggie can’t call a brotha? himself?” “Hey man, Biggs just looking out for you dogg. You know how these mu’fuckin” feds be these days. Yo? shit shows up on the phone records and next thing you know you got a mu’fucka up on yo? do? step trying to serve yo? ass with a subpoena or some shit like that. You need that type of drama. But on the real dogg we worried about you son. That damn Brick nigga try cave yo? head in and that redneck mu’fucka makes him yo? partna?! What the fuck is that ol? bullshit man?!” “I told you dogg. I got this. When it comes time to bring the hammer down I’ma sweep up all these muthafuckas that don’t seem to know who I am.” “Shit you ain’t got to wait on that shit man. Biggs gave the word that we could lay it out for this nigga man.” “What you talkin” man?” “I’m talkin” bout we know this nigga lives up in Harlem with his kin. We got cats in New York dogg. This mu’fucka think he can just fuck with our peoples. That shit’s a disease, but we can cure all that. Have a couple of cats run up in his crib with a few doses of “Act Right”. Know what I mean?” “Oh, oh...” Train bellowed, cutting him off at the pass. “You talkin” bout subpoenas. I’m on my cell dogg.” “Don’t even sweat that, I’m on a pay phone up here. Unless somebody tappin” yo shit it ain’t no thing.” “Aight, aight... Look man. I appreciate all that man, but I got this. Trust me. Tell Biggy I said I’m an expert at the game. Don’t think just cuz I ain’t in running the streets that I done forgot how to handle mine. I’m Train nigga”. Sit back, relax and watch me work.” “Word...... Aight man. Well look. If you need it. Don’t be looking for them mu’fuckas fool you. You need somebody to watch yo” back... You know who to call. Family.” “How I’ma forget nigga” I’m the one that taught you.” “Hahaha... Word man. Stay up son.” “One.” Train flips the phone shut and sits for a minute. Reminiscing... Oh for the days when the dudes watching his back actually had his back. Train chuckled to himself. Everyone one was so convinced Train had be painted into a corner. That it was just inescapable. If they only new. If they only new he could be out any time he wanted. If they knew all it took was one phone call. Train just chuckled to himself again as he tucked the phone back into his pocket before opening the door to depart for the front doors of the club. Chapter V: Act a Fool The VIP room was filled with plush upholstery. The overhead lights weren't so bright as to blind, but also weren't so dim as to completely obscure the room. 'Confessions Pt II' by Usher played in the backgorund. Sitting at a table were Brick and his singing group DMAK. The group had an engagement performing at the club. They had just gotten through performing one set. They were taking a break in the VIP room until the next set. Damian and Andre (the group's two tenors) sat to Brick's left. Keith and Marcus (the group's baritone and bass, respectively) sat to Brick's right. They were all decked out in champagne-colored velour track suits. However, despite the atmosphere, Brick was barely in the mood to relax and enjoy himself. 'Bubba wouldn't change his mind?' asked Damian 'No, he wouldn't.' 'That's ridiculous, man,' said Keith. 'Forcing you to team up with a cat you hate?' 'Yeah, man,' added Marcus, 'why didn't he just put you up with Salaok while he was at it?' 'What can I say? He's the boss. Best thing I can do right now is deal with it at High Octane. Tag Titles are up for grabs and me an' Train are just gonna have to get over it. Tonight I'm just gonna concentrate on you guys.' 'Speaking of which,' said Andre, 'we've been thinking. We've enjoyed the success we've had so far. We figured we could try something different.' 'You mean solo projects?' 'Exactly.' 'Well, to be honest, fellas, it's a risk, but hey when have I ever played it safe? We can work on finding new songwriters and producers for each project and--' 'Uh-oh,' went Damian. 'What?' asked Brick. Damian pointed to the cause of the 'uh-oh'. Brick turned around to see Train walking into the VIP Room. 'Aw, shit,' Brick grumbled. DMAK all fixed their stares on Train as he walked towards a table. 'Stop starin' at him,' Brick muttered. 'Y'all might start a fight.' 'So what if we do? We're not afraid of him,' said Damian. 'Yeah,' added Andre, 'there's five of us and only one of him.' 'Be that as it may, I'd just rather not get into any shit tonight. I just want tonight to go off without any drama.' Unfortunately, it couldn't be helped. Train noticed their stares 'Y'all mu’fuckas got a problem?!' Train growled. 'I don't know,' replied Marcus. 'Do we?' 'Nah, 'cause y'all niggas is over here, starin' me down like y'all wanna start some shit. An' I know none'a y'all want any o' this.' 'Train, not tonight.' 'Oh, hol' up,' Train said, taking a step back. 'Hol' up. I thought I recognized y'all. Y'all Brick's little songbirds. What, y'all gettin' tired o' the fake-ass Boyz II Men image? Y'all wanna try an' be hard now? Well, y'all couldn't have picked a worse nigga to try that shit on.' 'We ain't afraid of you,' said Keith. 'Yeah? You should be, nigga! ' 'And, for the record, if it wasn't for this rule Bubba set up, Brick would be whuppin' your sorry ass.' 'Y'all think y'all hot shit 'cause y'all singers an' whatnot? I heard y'all niggas sing before. I ain't impressed. Brick found y'all in a subway station? He should'a left your asses there!' Just as DMAK started to advance towards Train and Train began to advance towards them, Brick stood up and stepped into the slight gulf between them. 'Awright, that's it! Fellas, go get ready. You go back on in a couple of minutes.' 'Brick, are you sure--?' 'Yeah, I'm sure, Keith. Train and I are just gonna have a little chat.' The singers looked back at Train and then at Brick before going on their way. 'You need to school your boys. They liked to get they soft little asses beat down. See how good they sing then.' Brick then turned towards Train and leaned in towards him. Train returned the glare Brick was shooting him. 'Let's get something straight. Like you said, we got bigger fish to fry, so I'm gonna do things Bubba's way because it's in my best interests. At High Octane, I'm just gonna squash and we handle our business. But, make no mistake, I'm still gonna look into what happened to Donny that night. And if I find out you had anything to do with it, it's your ass. Believe that.' 'Nigga, whatever. Go handle your little songbirds.' Train then turned away and walked back to his table. Brick, as much as he would have liked to, didn't raise a hand to him. He knew that to do so would be to jeopardize his future in Global and there was too much riding on his partnership with Train, however reluctant and forced. Besides, he had clients to tend to.
