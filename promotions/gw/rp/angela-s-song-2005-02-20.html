Angela's Song

20 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/20/05 Straddle my hope and make me pray To a God I've never seen, but who I've betrayed. To the people who live the earth, the life, In a place I'll never be 'til I'm crucified ? Oasis * * * He sat in the same corner, holed up for two days without nourishment. The energy was seeping out of him slowly and it showed. His will was less then it normally was and when he didn’t answer any of his calls it began to worry some of the people who knew him well. The events after he had fed on that young woman whom he saved in the back alley of the Mandalay Bay resort tore a hole right through the very fabric of his immortal being. He tried to analyze it every way possible but the facts seem to be very clear, he was being haunted. Revenants usually do not bother those who are dead, only the living. That was the thought spinning in his head over and over. It was a thousand years and more since the plague had stricken his daughter and led her to an early death. He refused to let himself believe that he was just feeling guilty because he was still walking, talking, but also at the same time damned to the very core. He did not have any inclination to go out into the real world, even to sustain himself; it was not something he was willing to do after what happened the other evening. The little energy he had left was not even enough to manage his way to the kitchen, where he had bottles of blood in the refrigerator. Corvus began to slump even more down the wall ... his energy was fading now at a much faster clip. The security alarm in his mansion tripped but there was nothing he could do. He had not feared the end, for he knew that one day it would come for him. His eyes closed and he laid fully on the floor. “Corvus, are you here?” The voice was faint to him; he could barely even move his fingers. Deacon Black found him in the Master Bedroom and used his strength to pull him up and get him into bed. He hurriedly went into the kitchen and grabbed a pitcher, filling it up with the bottles of blood in the kitchen. Deacon brought a glass with him and poured it on sight, giving it to the menace of GW. He gulped it down and requested more, which was given to him immediately. His energy seemed to be coming back with each glass. He then sat up and took the pitcher from Deacon and devoured the contents and when he was finished, he wiped his arm. “Well aren’t you glad that I decided to stop by for a chat” It looks like someone hasn’t been keeping themselves nourished.” Corvus put the glass into the pitcher and set it upon his nightstand (or was it daystand?). The foggy feeling he had gained from not having enough blood started to wear off slowly as he began to remember everything that had happened. “I haven’t been feeling well. I went out and found a mortal and brought her back here, and then after I finished ... I had these weird visions and voices. I saw my daughter, Angela, instead of the girl I had drunk from.” Deacon listened to him tell the story about having the hallucinogenic type visions which he knew a little something about. He had connections and when the help that Corvus had called to come take the body away, they informed Deacon that there had been some drug paraphernalia “I just don’t understand it ... usually only the living get haunted and as you well know, I’m not one of the living. Am I just putting this all in my mind as one big guilt trip to myself?” Deacon brought his hand up to his chin, taking a moment to try figuring out what it was that had made such a vicious man seem almost afraid. “There is a possibility, but before you try to make more out of it than need be, you should know that your kill was under the influence of narcotics.” “I should have been able to see the signs, but when you haven’t lived that kind of lifestyle for at least twenty years it tends to become forgotten. You know the stories; I really need not repeat them to you.” He of course was referring to a time, not long ago, but long enough to be just a faded memory for most. To truly be able to get the kind of fix that would make a vampire feel like mortals, it took the passing of blood from the user to the vampire. Often, Corvus would choose two to three a night that had boozed and drugged themselves up enough to give him what he craved. He craved it like a junkie until he realized that it would get him nowhere, except easily killed by a cunning hunter or another of his kind that wanted to make a name for themselves by claiming they had done what no one thought was possible to do: put the mighty Corvus to rest. “I’m not sure what possessed me to even go out to hunt, and on the strip no less. You can say I have learned a valuable lesson but it still does not explain why I was had visions of Angela.” Deacon removed his top hat and walked around the large bed that Corvus slept in. He brought a chair around and placed it directly across from the brutish demon. It was evident that Corvus was smart, but sometimes he too needed guidance. “Perhaps after all this time, you are still having problems with coping with the loss. There may be some guilt underlying being that both you and Lia, and also your son ... were to become creatures born into darkness. She is the only one left of your family and your souls can never be united.” Corvus grumbled before slightly giving in to the facts that were there. “Maybe ... I’m still not sure I want to believe that it is coincidence.” Deacon nodded and spoke again, “If you like, I know someone who might be able to help you.” The monster stood and waved him off. “No thanks, I am going to deal with this on my own.” Deacon nodded, “Have it your way.” He stood up from his seat and put his top hat back upon his head. “Now that your vigor has been restored, I see no other reason for me to be here. It would be a shame to waste a perfectly good evening, so I bid you adieux for now.” Corvus rose from his bed and silently escorted Deacon to the door so he could reset the security system after he left. He did not want to be bothered by anyone, he just needed to collect his thoughts now that the drugs that were in his blood stream had worn off. Deacon stopped at the doorway, tilting his head as he looked at the massive being. “I don’t think putting yourself in eternal sleep is the best thing for you right now, you have history to make and a Platinum Championship to win. Keep that in mind before you do anything irrational.” Corvus displayed an agitated look towards Deacon when he realized that the man, who compare to him, was like comparing an ant to a human ... had been reading his thoughts. It was one thing for an enemy to try doing this, but someone he considered an ally? It was like a slap to the face. “I’ll see you later, Deacon. Thank you for stopping by.” The caustic tone of his voice could have cut through the toughest metal. He didn’t want or need to be lectured on what he wanted to do least of all by a priest who became the one thing he fought all of his life ... which was evil. The door closed behind Deacon as he left the mansion. Corvus knew it was time to start focusing on defending his Television Title. He drew two of the most respected men in Global Wrestling. One was the current Cruiserweight Champion and the other a former World’s Champion. This was his chance to prove that he was one of the best, it was also to show that he was not a one match wonder, like so many had written him off as in WMW. * * * Driving across town was a pain in the ass. If I didn’t need to look for a certain book, I wouldn’t have even bothered. Who was to say this shop would even have the damn book anyway? A magic shop in a little strip mall, how ambiguous. The cover of being just a shop for people who performed magic acts on stage at the various different resorts was very clever, when in fact it contained many artifacts for not only good magic but also those of the black arts. The clerk was completely unprepared for me as I stepped through the threshold. That was the beauty of being a public shop, I could come and go as I pleased and didn’t have to be invited in. That little problem is also one that is likened to a rash where the sun doesn’t shine. “How may I help you, sir?” The clerk said as he slurped on a big gulp from the 7-Eleven just a few doors down from the shop. He studied the tall, dark, nature of the man who browsed a few of the things in the shop. I nearly felt like I was clearly at the wrong place but I knew that the information I was given by the resources I had wouldn’t be wrong. I wasn’t looking to pull coins out of people’s ears or rabbit’s out of hats ... I needed something much more than that. Maybe I should have asked if there was some secret password for the goodies I was looking for. “Since there is no one in the store right now, I feel that I can freely ask you if you have texts that deal with the occult and restoring souls.” The punk gave me one of those retarded dog looks as he cocked his head to the side. I knew this was going to be an interesting conversation. I anxiously awaited his reply as I continued to look around absently. I could smell some cheap incense burning which was much more offensive than you could imagine. “Sir, this is a magic shop. Does it look like we sell books on the occult” Really, I think that is funny ... maybe you’ll find what you are looking for at the library.” The kid chuckled to himself even though he knew the truth was that there was more to the shop than met the naked eye. “Listen kid, I’m not in the mood for games. I guess I don’t have the magic word to get you to show me what I’m looking for so I guess that would leave me to my only other option ...” It only took a matter of seconds before I was pressing the kid against the wall, my hand against his throat. I could smell fear but that wasn’t all I could smell. He knew that I wasn’t playing around. I could have easily gutted him like a freshly caught fish before he could even say “abbra caddabra”. “Ok ... ok, what are you looking for?” The young clerk knew he needed to comply, this man was unusually strong. Out of the corner of his eye he looked in a mirror that was not gimmicked and only saw his reflection. “Jesus ... you’re one of them!” “You’re a damn genius, you know that” Maybe you should put in a nomination for a Pulitzer in Rocket Science, Einstein! I’m looking for a book(s) on soul restoration ... so you better tell the truth because I can tell if you’re lying.” I released my grip on him to give him a chance to at least give me what I was looking for. I was already in the mood that if he tried something, I would have a snack that wouldn’t drive me towards madness. This guy looked like he had been listening to way too much Simple Plan or something. The punk rock look just bothered me for some reason, I don’t know why. “I’ll be back in a second or two. I have to look up what we have, if anything.” The clerk hustled off into the back of the store and was there an estimated fifteen minutes or more. When he returned, he had two old looking books and placed them on the counter. “This was all I could find Sir, there should be a few different passages about the topic you were looking for inside.” I studied the books for a moment; they certainly looked old and ancient. The price tag that came with them certainly wasn’t cheap but they were cheaper than I thought it would be. I dug into my pockets and set down a wad of money and slipped it to the clerk. He gave a sheepish grin that took everything I had to force myself from punching his teeth in. “Here’s a little extra as a tip, go yet yourself another pair of pants ... it smells like you could use them.” I turned and walked away; as I reached the door I could hear him tell me to come back again. His voice was cracking which meant that he was somewhere in puberty limbo. I’m sure that my appearance in the shop gave him something to tell his Dungeon’s and Dragons group the next time they gathered. “I met a vampire; he was in the magic shop.” Imagine all the oohs and aahs that would come along with that. It reinforces my opinion that mortals are such morons. The rest of the night was going to be very exciting. My idea of a good time is going through ancient books trying to figure out just how I can restore the soul I lost when I was embraced only for the purpose of being able when I die to once again see the daughter that was taken from me oh so long ago. The sound of it just seems corny but everyone knows vampires are damned creatures. It just happens that some don’t choose the life they are given, some feel that they can contribute and others it is just forced upon. There are some who have bits and pieces of the humanity they gave up but that is never enough even when you reach the time of your final death. Once you’re done, it’s down to hell you go ... * * * Corvus returned to Gotham Manor and holed himself up in the library with the books he had bought. He looked through them diligently for any answers to his question of whether or not a soul that has been taken can be restored upon ones death. It was his last attempt to be able to be united once again with his daughter. He dared not speak of it to his wife (if in fact she was still his wife as he hadn’t heard from her lately so he wondered), as she would deem him daft in the head. To him once his body was of no use to him, he at least wanted a chance to make up for lost time with the little girl he’s been without for over a millennia. These books are worthless. It doesn’t say anything about lost souls being returned. Maybe I’m just looking in the wrong place for it ... it’s foolish to think that you could restore a soul to a vampire. That’s a bit too Hollywood to be possible in reality. It would be like every vampire’s mortal essence was trapped in a bottle in some type of storage house in the ether. That’s a good one, very funny. I think I’m just going to find somebody to read this for me that will know how to deal with this sort of thing. Maybe Deacon’s resources might be able to decipher this. I’m a violent, unconscionable man ... I don’t bother with books unless I have to for my own enjoyment. This is just too much bloody work. He was in a foul mood as he heard the buzzer from the front gate being sounded. Someone wanted to get in that much he knew. He walked to the intercom and pressed the button to speak to the visitor. “What the hell do you want; don’t you realize it is after 11 p.m.” I dislike people coming to my home without having a good reason, so if you don’t have one I’m going to be out there in a second with a with a hot poker to brand your ass!” A heavy breath could be heard before a rattled voice spoke to tell him what they were there for. “I have a special delivery for Corvus ...” “What is it” If you can tell me, then slip it through the damn gate and get the hell out of here!” The delivery guy hesitated a little again and then pressed the button to speak. “It has a seal on it; I don’t know what it is. I was just sent to deliver it ... I’m sorry. I’m leaving it through the gate like you asked. Have a nice night, sir!” Corvus watched from the front door as the messenger got back on his little moped and sped off as quickly as he could. He was afraid of the place, and had heard rumors about things going on in it which creeped him out. After the delivery person was gone, Corvus opened the door and stepped outside. He walked toward the gates that kept his home secluded from all the others in the residential area. As he walked down the drive he sniffed the air for any sign of something that could lead him to believe that the messenger could have set a trap. His senses prove to him that nothing out of the ordinary is going on. The lights that lined the driveway illuminated a manila envelope that had been slipped through the steel iron that was a part of the security gates. He bent down and picked up the folder and carried it back with him into the house; returned to the library where he could look at it from the confines of his leather reading chair. The envelope could have been a contract that someone was looking for me to carry out. It seemed discreet enough, and also had a good security seal on it. I used my nail to slit it open to quench the curiosity that I had about it. I tipped it upside down and a huge stack of papers that were stapled together came out into my lap. Another sheet of paper also came out after it and I looked at that first to see if it helped in giving me the answers I sought. It was a note from that director, Wes Craven. He said that he had heard about me through someone who was a big wrestling fan and wanted me to peruse out a script he was working on that had me in mind for the lead. I was honestly impressed with the fact that I was being recruited to do a motion picture from a top horror director but that really wasn’t for me. It was good that he was under the impression that I played a “character” but what very few knew was that I wasn’t living a gimmick, that my gimmick was how I really was ... a psychotic, vicious, soulless creature that fed on the blood of humans to survive. Our kind has been keeping our origins secret for eons and that was the way we were going to keep it. I would hate to see what happened if the lid ever blew off of this and we were discovered with our hands in the cookie jar of the world if you would call it that. Maybe I should send Mr. Craven a letter of recommendation to cast Brick as a bounty hunter for one of his movies. The guy was obviously good at being an actor, going into some club full of simple-minded neonates looking for me. Most of them had no idea where I was and as far as they were concerned, I was a myth. He killed them but he was only pissing off the upper echelon that had their progeny destroyed. Good luck when they come after him, the damn moron! After having a good chuckle with the note, he wrote down the address and planned to send a letter that regretfully declined the offer to appear in the motion picture. He tossed the folder into the fireplace and let it burn before he took it upon himself to kill the fire. The evil entity needed a little time to brood as that was his thing to do because all of that hatred and rage needed to go somewhere when he wasn’t smashing people to pieces. He stepped into his bedroom and grabbed his acoustic guitar; then walked to the stairs nearby. He took them all the way to the third floor which leads him to the room where the balcony resided. He walked over to a chair that was on the balcony and sat down. Corvus checked out the guitar to see if he needed to tune it any to play the song he had been working on most recently. It was rare that someone as vicious as he was and the type of life he lead would show that he was a talented musician and had some kind of feeling. His fingers began to go to work, playing a dark and moody sound from the instrument which had an angst-filled aura about it. The intro was a little long but that was just because he thought it sounded good. He began to sing, his raspy voice was very chilling yet there was no one there to listen to him play. I woke up alone in the darkness echoes filling inside my head. The feeling was hot like the burning sun On innocence I wish I hadn't fed He was becoming entranced in the moment, playing this song that he had written that had come to him when he started having these visions of his daughter, after he had taken the life of the girl whom he did not know was a drug addict. You point out all of my atrocities When will you realize that is me? He knew that if his daughter was watching him, and he had a good indication that she was ... from somewhere that she disliked the life he went onto and how he became a creature that was damned for all eternity. His fingers continued to move fluidly on the guitar as he moved into the chorus of the song. Sweet little angel, you've gone away Left me here with a bitter taste Too man things words are unable to say These are the memories we can't erase His eyes where closed while he sang, his fingers playing out of memory, the chords that made up the song. He paced himself as he neared the second chorus. I have accepted the villain that I've become The years have washed away the guilt Out from the shadows is where I come, to stock pile the screams that I have built You try to show me when I am wrong My dreams have been haunted for way too long He repeated the chorus of the song for a second time, hitting the point home that he had really been affected by the tragedies that had befell him and brought him to where he is today. Maybe one day he’d become resigned to the fact that there was a reason for his fall from mortality. Corvus transcended into the bridge of the song, he had really thought to himself that this was one of his more personal songs that hit home. He’d play it one day for Lia, but when he didn’t know because it was hard to share things like that with anyone ... especially her. Always lurking where I can't see ... Stop all this torment so I can be FREE! There was a part of him that wanted to be released from the pain that came along with the burden that his daughter’s memory provided for him. It wasn’t one of those things where he could say, “Ok, I can let go.” It just was not that simple. He repeated the chorus for the third and final time before he had one last small verse before the end of the song. You've left me here with a bitter taste. These are the memories we can't erase. The words “we can’t erase” were repeated several times before he played the last few chords. When the song was finished he sat with the guitar on his lap for a few minutes. He listened quietly as the sounds of the night replaced the melodies he had provided. Corvus sat there and looked out at the views from his balcony for a while, quietly thinking to himself.
