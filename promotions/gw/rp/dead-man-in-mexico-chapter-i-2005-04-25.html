Dead man in Mexico: Chapter I.

25 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 25th, 2005. Dead man in Mexico. ---- Juarez, Mexico. April 25th, 2005. 2:11 AM. It was another busy Friday night in Juarez for the local policia, but lieutenant Eduardo Ruiz had more than sufficient time to kill. Flicking the switch on his radio to turn the volume down he kept a close eye on the black Lexus in front of him. Such a nice car was rare in Juarez and only the rich were privileged enough to afford such luxuries, the man in front of him made a suitable income but was hardly rich. His famed protege? That was an entirely different story. Eduardo made sure to keep his distance, watching the taillights of the Lexus as they cut through the streets of Juarez. Lt. Ruiz looked down at the digital clock in his dashboard, 12:13 AM is what the little green numbers said, he knew that it would just be any moment now and he would be rewarded for tailing the Lexus. He smiled cruelly, his thin lips twisting into an image that even Satan himself would despise. Eduardo glanced into the backseat of his cruiser at the black briefcase that rested there, his smile grew as he thought of the contents inside. $20,000 dollars in American money, Judas Iscariot had settled for far less. Eduardo watched as the Lexus approached a large red sign, bearing the word: 'Alto', on it's face. Bracing himself, Eduardo waited for the driver to discover that his brakes didn't work, he didn't have to wait very long. The driver of the Lexus made the discovery a little late as he pumped the brakes violently to no avail, with a loud crash the Lexus was introduced to a heavy garbage truck as they two vehicles met head on. The garbage truck didn't budge as it showed it's superior strength. The Lexus? Amidst animalistic screams from both human voices and twisting metal, the shattering of glass and the sickening sound of tearing flesh and breaking bones it came to a sudden stop, it's frame much shorter than it had been mere seconds ago. Eduardo sighed, it was done. He flipped on the lights in his cruiser and pulled up behind the car. He put the cruiser in park but didn't bother to turn it off as he stepped out. He raised his left index finger to the driver of the garbage truck, it was the signal to leave the scene, the unharmed truck driver willingly obeyed as he kicked his vehicle in reverse and turned into a dark alley. Eduardo walked slowly to the Lexus wondering what might await him there. When he stood next to the car and looked inside he grimaced. The long black hair of a woman was plastered to her face with blood, her husband, the driver was slumped back in his seat his eyes wide open, staring at the ceiling. The man wasn't moving or breathing, the woman on the other hand was breathing in ragged gasps. Over the gasps for oxygen, Eduardo could hear gas spilling out onto the streets as it drained from the vehicle. Eduardo took a step back and reached into his pocket extracting a pack of cigarettes and a book of matches. He paused momentarily to look at the bikini wearing blonde on the matchbook cover before stepping away from the vehicle and lighting his cigarette. He didn't bother to blow out the match but took another step forward and tossed it over his shoulder. It hit the street and suddenly flames shot up behind the local policia, singing the hair on the back of his neck. He calmly took a puff of his cigarette as he walked to his cruiser. Throwing open the door, he grabbed the radio, turned the volume back up and spoke in a calm voice. 'Car accident on Rosemary street, single vehicle, a black Lexus with two people inside. It looks like the driver ran the stop sign, the automobile is in flames, requesting emergency assistance immediately.' Eduardo paused, a flicker of static danced across the frequency as he spoke again. 'There are no survivors.' ---- Seattle, WA. April 25th, 2005. 7:57 AM. I rolled over onto my stomach again, sick of trying to sleep on my back or sides. It seemed that I just couldn't get comfortable anywhere, which would explain the small amount of sleep that I had managed. Then again, couches don't always make the best beds. Closing my eyes I thought of my girlfriend lying in the center of our queen sized bed, covered in sheets and blankets, hugging my pillow tight and getting some of the best sleep of her life. I on the other hand had spent the night tossing and turning, complaining about the lumpy couch and promising myself that I'd buy a hide-a-bed the next time I decided to restock the furniture. I had also realized that the worst part of fighting with my girlfriend isn't trying to duck the things she throws at me but rather sleeping on the couch. If I had actually done something wrong I would have apologized, though somehow it seems that wouldn't get me off of the couch. A week of sleeping on the couch due to her insecurity. At least I had won my match last week for CW2, otherwise I'd never hear the end of how I blew it. I knew that I had to beat Firoe this week, in fact I had to win the tournament or she would never stop harassing me about choking in the tourney and of course, informing me how much further she could have gone if she has been accepted. 'If.' Pulling a pillow over my head to block out the sunlight that had began to creep in through the windows of my apartment I groaned. Sleepy and cranky wasn't fun and I already felt sorry for anyone who crossed my path because it wasn't going to be a good day. With the pillow pulled over my head I noticed that the sunlight wasn't visible and the rays of sunshine were warming my body to the point where I had stopped shivering beneath the single sheet I had for bedding. Finally! I was going to get some sleep, it had been long overdue. Dropping my heavy eyelids over my eyes I smiled as I answered the call of Morpheus and began to slip into his realm. ...and then the phone rang. No sooner had I dozed off the piercing sound of my cordless phone, conveniently placed on the end table a foot away from my head jerked me back from the Sandman's grasp. With an angry groan I pulled the pillow down harder over my head as it rang again. I didn't know what time it was and I didn't care but whomever had elected to call me at this hour would have to be content with leaving a message because I wasn't going to give my sleep up to talk to them, a third ring didn't change my mind and neither did the fourth. After the fifth, my answering machine kicked on, belting out it's familiar message. 'Hi, this is Josh, I'm not available at the moment but if you'll leave your name and number I'll catch up to you as soon as possible, thanks and have a great day!' The recorded voice sounded so happy and well rested, if I could have chased down the digital version of me I would have choked it to death with a shoelace. A Nike shoelace, I hate Nike. An annoying beep was the next sound to break through my pillowed defense and into my ears as a voice spoke rapidly and breathlessly. 'Josh? Please pick up, please...it's important. If your home...' Spinning around at the word 'important', I snatched up the receiver and pressed the talk button. 'Hello..?', I asked in bewilderment, unsure as to whom was on the other line. 'Josh! Thank you, Jesus!' Between the thick accent and heavy pronunciation of the name 'Jesus' I quickly deduced that the man on the other end of the line was of Spanish origin, other than that I was still clueless to his identity, then again in the dawning hours of daybreak I could hardly identify myself. 'Someone better have died...' I growled into the receiver, I hardly knew the irony of these words as I spoke them. 'Josh, this is Juan Padilla, I'm afraid I have bad news.' His voice bore a grim tone, he didn't even need to announce the bad news I could already tell it in his voice. 'Juan Padilla?' I asked in confusion, still attempting to discover exactly whom I was speaking with. His tone was rapid, he sounded as obfuscated as I was. 'You may not remember me, we've trained together before, I - much like yourself am a former student of Javier Castillo.' 'Juan, Juan, Juan...' I repeated his name three times, taking a moment to spend in thought as I attempted to remember him, then it came to mind. Juan Padilla: promising young Wrestler, at the age of 25 he was 2 years older than I am, he had toured Japan from time to time and gained a decent amount of fame in Mexico as a star of Lucha Libre. 'Lucha Loco, Juan Padilla?' I asked, recalling the name bestowed upon him for his daring maneuvers. 'You remembered.' He said without humor. 'I haven't heard from you since our last tour of Japan, what was that 2 years ago?' I inquired of him, backtracking down memory lane to my last tour of Japan as I rubbed my eyes sleepily. 'You have a good memory Josh, however I'm afraid that I did not get in touch with you because I missed your company.' His voice was sincere, lacking any sarcasm that one may have been expecting to trail that statement. 'Well, if you were waking me up to tell me you wanted to have coffee then the answer was a definite no, what's up Loco?' I asked, fully awake at this point. 'I hate to be the bearer of bad news but Javier is no longer with us.' The voice of Juan Padilla was now flat and honest and his words bore the additional weight of a chilling edge. 'Wait, are you saying that...' I stopped myself, refusing to accept what I thought was about to be said, I didn't want to hear it and most of all I didn't want to believe it. 'Last night as he was returning from a Juarez Wrestling venue he was in a head on collision.' There was a brief moment of silence as Juan paused and waited for me to say something, I didn't, he did. 'Josh...he's dead.' I felt my heart sink in that moment as a hard lump of emotion rose in my throat. My hands lost all feeling and became numb and wooden, it was all that I could do to hold onto the phone. 'But...' I tried to say something and failed, failed miserably. I wanted to point out that he was too young to die, to healthy, to full of life. He wouldn't be killed by something as simple and common as a car wreck. This was the man that had trained one of the greatest Wrestling stars of this generation, he could not die! I had always seen him as invincible, believing that the only way he could ever be killed is if God Himself came down to earth and crushed Javier with His mighty hand. Yet now...he was dead. 'The funeral will be in two days, you need to be there.' The tone of Juan's voice informed me of how important it was that I made an appearance, what he said next showed me that it was more critical than I thought. 'The family has asked you to deliver the Eulogy.' 'Juan, my Spanish is far from perfect, it's a struggle for me to keep a conversation going in Spanish much less deliver a speech!' I said in a combination of shock and protest. 'I'll translate, Josh, even though he never said it; we all know that you were his favorite student, not to mention his best. He would have wanted it this way.' Juan's voice was kind and dignified but demanding nonetheless. I didn't argue, I knew it was pointless and wrong to argue this point. Javier would have wanted it this way whether he died young or old, I would grant his wish regardless of what inconvenience it served to me. 'Okay, I'll be there.' I sighed, conceding defeat. 'When?' Juan wasted no time in asking. 'Tonight.' I responded. 'I'll catch the first flight to El Paso and drive down from that point.' 'I'll meet you at the airport.' Juan insisted. 'Thank you. I guess I'll see you tonight then?' I probed, in an attempt to verify that I would indeed have a ride to Juarez. 'I'm staying with Javier's family, call the house as soon as you arrive and I'll be there to pick you up.' Juan confirmed. 'Normally I would say I'm looking forward to seeing you, Juan, but to be honest...under these conditions? Hardly.' I choked on the lump of emotion as I spoke. 'He was a great man.' Juan said with a sigh, letting me know that he didn't enjoy the cause of the reunion either. 'That he was. I'll see you tonight Juan.' I punched the talk button with my index finger as I hung up and fell back on the couch my eyes clouded with tears, my mind filled with memories of a great man. Javier Castillo, a trainer, a mentor and a friend. He had been like a father to me. He was an amazing Wrestler, an even better trainer and a true Picasso to the art of Lucha Libre. The ring was his canvas and he left it marked with the grace of his high flying skills and the blood of his opponents. Dead at the tender age of 34 years old, he had so much more to offer the world...yet everything had suddenly been taken away in the blink of an eye. 'Rest in peace, my friend.' I gasped, the hot tears now rolling down the sides of my face. ---- Seattle airport. April 25th. 12:12 PM. I had stopped crying and recessed into a numb, cold state of mind. I sat in my comfortable first class seat on the Juarez bound jet with Raki seated next to me. I'm sure we struck the world around us as an interesting pair. My shaggy black hair collapsed over my face, even falling into my bloodshot eyes, which were covered by a pair of black shades in an attempt to cover up the evidence of my sorrow. My look was completed by a pair of ragged Italian jeans, black Chuck Taylor's and a jade t-shirt reading: 'I was the longest reigning World Champion in Global Wrestling history and all I got was this lousy t-shirt.' One foot rested on the floor and the other on my seat, my right knee pulled up to my chest, my chin resting on my hand as I peered out the window. My eyes were locked onto the concrete of the runway but that was hardly where they were focusing, instead they had targeted an old path...memory lane. My aforementioned girlfriend was seated next to me, looking as beautiful as ever, in Lucky jeans, a long sleeved white tee and a pair of canvas Vans. Her hair was braided on the sides and she wore a strange shade of blue eye shadow today, something I had hardly noticed. Most of the time I was affixed by her radiance yet today she didn't even seem to exist. Granted, we had been fighting for the better part of a week and a half but today my mind was so preoccupied with Javier's death that she was a distant figure in the shadows of my concentration. 'I'm sorry.' Raki blurted out, snapping me back to attention. I looked over at her, not bothering to lower or remove my shades as my bluish gray eyes, hidden behind the sunglasses, met her big brown eyes. As our eyes met I arched my brows, showing a sign of confusion, I didn't bother to respond to her verbally I knew her well enough to predict that an explanation was forthcoming. 'I'm so sorry, Josh.' Raki sighed as she reached across the armrest and took my hand in hers. My hand was bigger but at this point it felt lighter, weaker, it felt lifeless and we both noticed. She gave my hand a tight squeeze as she leaned over and kissed me on the cheek. 'I know that things have been a little rough lately but we don't need to fight. Not now, not like this.' She shook her head as she spoke, squeezing my hand even tighter, I squeezed back. 'I love you, and I know that you love me. I think that I overreacted when I found out that I wasn't allowed to participate in CW2, Josh, I know that you only want the best for me and...you know what? I'm willing to wait until you say I'm ready to step into the ring. I waited for what seemed like an eternity for you to admit that you were in love with me, so for something far less important? I can wait a little longer.' Raki spoke in a tone of humility and sincerity, looking into her eyes I could see the pain, I knew this wasn't easy for her. I also knew that she was being honest, she wasn't trying to make me feel better about Javier's death she was just being honest about how she felt and that made the apology twice as effective. 'I love you.'. The words came in a hoarse whisper but in my emotionally distraught position it was the most I could force from myself. 'I'm sorry if I've ever failed to make it anything but blindingly obvious, Raki, I love you.' She smiled at me, I smiled back, it was the first time I had smiled today. Reaching over the armrest she locked both of her hands around mine. We ignored the loud 'ding' that echoed throughout the 747 as a 'fasten seat belts' sign flashed at us and continued our conversation. 'I can't even imagine what your going through. Josh, are you sure you're okay?' Raki asked with genuine concern. I answered with a smile. 'I am now.' She looked down at the notebook and pen in my lap, it was the beginning of a painful eulogy. 'How's that coming along?' Raki politely asked, diverting her eyes to the notebook to identify 'that', as she had put it. 'I don't know. What can you say about a legend?' I asked, turning my focus to the red lined paper 'Don't talk about the legend...talk about the man that the legend was, and more importantly, the man that the legend influenced.' Raki stated as she rubbed the back of my hand tenderly. 'That's a good idea.' I admitted, picking my pen back up with my free hand. She watched as I began to pen the words. 'Javier Castillo, My mentor, my friend...' I stopped, looked up at her and smiled. Returning to my paper I crossed the word friend out and in it's place put one simple word...'brother.'
