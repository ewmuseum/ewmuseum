Sequitur quinta pars .:: ::: ::. “Thus I descended out of the first circle down to the second, that less space begirds, and so much greater dole, that goads to wailing?” The Italian Gentleman held up a hand as the two of them reached a burnished wooden door, as though he was making sure they would be safe before continuing on. That made her more than just a little bit nervous, but then again, what was the point of being nervous? This was already about as bad as it could possibly be. He knocked softly at the door. “ENTER!” The voice boomed forth from within, a commanding baritone with thousands of whispered echoes of all timbres. Chills ran down the center of her spine at the sound of it, and she looked at her guide most uncertainly. But he seemed unbothered by it, opening the heavy ebon door and stepping through it, into what looked to be nothing short of a plush-furnished office. A row of mini-blinds lined up against what appeared to be windows on the back wall of the room, the cords writhing in what was no doubt an artificial breeze. Bookcases lined two other walls, but the predominant figure was a sprawling, ornate, and finely wrought desk that sat in the middle of the room. Its bottoms, instead of being rounded rollers, were delicately carved claws. It was these that her eyes caught on, and she was examining them so closely that she scarcely heard the squeal of the leather desk chair turning round. “Ah, Signor!” came a rather cheery voice.. for hell, “Pardon the theatrics, I simply had no clue I was to be paid a pleasure visit today. I’d damn the secretary, but you know” I’m the secretary, and I’m quite afraid that I’m already damned.” The Italian Gentleman made no response, but simply nodded his head, the glowing orbs of his eyes focusing on the figure at the desk. “So” tell me, how’s Limbo, my friend? Not bad, eh? But not so great either?” At that, the figure at the desk slapped his knee and laughed’more of a cackle, really. That caused Lia’s head to jerk sharply upwards as she observed him for the first time. His figure was undoubtedly that of a man’surprising among the demons and shades she had thus far had contact with. His hair was a chestnut brown in color, pulled away from his face, and beneath his well-formed forehead danced merry brown eyes. He wore a suit, tailored to fit him just so, of black, with infinitely small red pinstripes running down it, and between his fingers was poised a cigar, the smoke wafting scentlessly up towards the ceiling and disappearing. A creaking sound drew her attention, and she looked down to see the claws on the desk pausing in their support work to stretch tired muscles. Her eyes widened. “My Father, how rude are you going to be, Signor! I didn’t even see you’d had a guest with you.” And suddenly, he jumped to his feet, extending a hand to her. “Welcome to my humble abode, bella.” Gently, he pulled her fingers to his lips, kissing them with very familiar courtly grace that made a scarlet blush run across her cheeks. He leaned closer to her, pulling her inadvertently into the desk, which she tried unsuccessfully to avoid touching. “To tell a family secret” well, if I could be said to have family? I usually like to do judgments in private. But? in the case of Signor, here, I like to make exceptions, as he is a personal friend.” He shook his head. “To think, though, I’ve got to judge such a lovely woman. I” oh wait, I didn’t even catch your name! I feel like such a boor, what is it again?” She bowed her head. “Lia. Lia Vlaszy.” He smiled widely. “A lovely name for a simply lovely woman. I, in case you didn’t know, am Minos. You might perhaps call me” Satan’s personal analogue assistant. Or his secretary, if you will. The souls that are to be judged? all the ones that aren’t stuck in Limbo, that is? come here. I choose where they go, and then I send them there. It sounds easy, sure, but it’s not always quite that cut-and-dried?” He felt like he was losing her, so he stepped around the desk in a fluid motion and smiled at her, his eyes holding a fire-like gleam somewhere deep within the pupils that she found quite hypnotizing. “I’m afraid that I never get unattached to the beautiful women I see walking through my door.” He sighed gustily. “It’s a shame they get to stay for such little time before I have to send them on their way. I’m afraid this job never gets any easier?” “You can shut up any time,” the Italian Gentleman said, in tones filled with a tense unease. A chair picked its legs up, and began inching towards him slyly. The Italian Gentleman, as always quite aware of his surroundings, kicked it viciously. A growl issued forth as it stood on claw-points. “Karekla!” Minos snapped, “Cease that infernal grumbling!” Something like a disappointed sigh came from somewhere within the wood’or perhaps skin’of the apparatus. She couldn’t prevent herself from staring. A groaning sigh issued forth from the desk; when she looked up, the master of the office was seated on it, clenching his cigar between his teeth, and rolling his eyes. “This stupid desk never gets tired of overdramatizing everything. You’d think I beat it every day or something, but no. Eternity can make a whiner out of anybody. Although perhaps I should beat it every day.” There was a hushed whimper, and Minos looked up to his guests, grinning. “You see?” He took a puff of his cigar, glancing between them, and then looked at the Italian Gentleman with eyes half closed. “What is it that brings you with this little lost soul, hm” Even the pretty ones don’t usually get escorts to judgment.” The Italian Gentleman rolled his eyes. “If you would shut up for two seconds, I could tell you” the lady is not to be judged. She is a traveler.” At that, merry eyes lit up, and she could see them burning with fire once again as he leapt off his desk. “In that case” let me show you around! Come, the view is simply breathtaking from my window.” As Lia approached the window with the Italian Gentleman, she could feel that there was no wind, as she had suspected’rather, the curtain strings were writhing of their own volition. A quiet hissing issued from them as Minos pulled them up. Almost immediately, she was assailed with the feeling that she was about to be burned alive. She could nearly feel singeing heat emanating from without’but perhaps it was not heat at all, just the impression of it, a residual nightmare that came back to haunt her in situations such as this. What she did see was a hellish wind, carrying about wailing shades of every description, some whose faces she could see, and others she could not. Clearly, they were all in agony, but for what reason, she could not easily discern. Minos sighed, looking out into the tumbling dervish of souls. “I could give you the typical spiel about it, but I prefer to make things simple” This is where those who are dominated in their lives by lust go. As their lust whipped them from place to place and lover to lover when they were alive, so will they spend their eternity.” She wanted to comprehend, but a blinding flash of light obscured all vision, all reason. That silly mask grinned at her still. It was only a silly mask, though, because no one could really comprehend what it meant. Even she didn’t do such a good job of that, but what she knew, all she knew was that whatever the mask said didn’t matter, and the person under it did. The sofa was soft underneath her, inviting to visitors, inviting to a lovers? tryst. Smiling at the man behind the mask, she pushed his mask away, and drew his lips to hers, kissing him slowly, passionately at first, then deeper, her teeth pressing gently into his bottom lip, causing him to sigh wistfully and lace his fingers through her hair. But it was as though all of a sudden, all of that faded away, and she found herself looking into a reproachful pair of green eyes, staring at her from across the kitchen. She knew that she’d hurt the young woman. In all honesty, she’d hurt herself, but they were both being so selfish, so ridiculously selfish, not admitting their feelings for each other. She couldn’t stand this anymore. A few steps across the room found her standing inches from Dawn. And she reached forward, letting everything flow forward’all the jealousy and competition from WMW days, all the wary mistrust, all the repressed desire. And she bent Dawn’s face to hers as she wrapped her arms around her. Moments later found them sprawled across the bed in the master bedroom, her cool lips tracing patterns on Dawn’s stomach as her earthly lover writhed in delight underneath her. But then it wasn’t Dawn’s room at all, but her own room with Virgil, and it was she who was writhing. His lips set her on fire, as her fingers twisted the silken sheets beneath her lithe, pale frame. And it was in these brief moments that she knew. She knew what everything meant, what it meant to have one’s soul in line with celestial and cosmic forces. Virgil growled, throwing the covers aside, pushing her knees up so that they nearly touched her shoulders? and she sighed, arched her back, and gave in. Minos? somewhat sympathetic eyes were cast upon his lady visitor. “Hmph. I’ve never had my tour be presumed that boring before. “A guilty conscience can do wonders,” the Italian Gentleman murmured, twisting the blind cord. At this, there was a choking sound, and Minos looked down, most displeased at what he saw. “Oaf! Stop killing my blind cord!” His eyebrows shot downwards across his face, a clear picture of irritation. He shoved the woman, only now breaking out of her trance, towards her guide. “Out! Out both of you, I’ve work to do, and I’ll not have you standing here ruining my office. Go downwards.” And with that, they were ejected, into a still-deeper chasm. .:: ::: ::. “New torments I behold, and new tormented around me, whichsoever way I move, and whichsoever way I turn and gaze. In the third circle am I of the rain eternal, maledict, and cold, and heavy?” She shivered, and that seemed to break her from trance. Rain poured down from every conceivable angle so that she could barely see what lurked around her’wary of where she stepped, she put a foot forward cautiously. She knew that with this much rain, rain that seemed as eternal as it did inconceivably heavy, there must be mud everywhere. “Back to your senses, I see.” The slightly sarcastic voice came to her from somewhere close by, and she reached for him, for her stoic, unemotional Italian Gentleman, who would guide her through these trials. She found a forearm, and he did his best to bear her up to her full height. “We must go on” I apologize for Minos? rudeness.” She nodded, trying to speak, but feeling like she had to yell to be heard over the rain, despite his voice not seeming to be any louder than usual. “It’s quite alright. I” I suppose he had every right to be rude.” The Italian Gentleman rolled his eyes. “If you give him an inch, he’ll take a mile.” A sudden barking split the sodden darkness, and her jade-green eyes alertly picked up the distance and direction. She squinted through the rain, attempting to see the beast, and from whence it came. But there was no panic this time’not lupine, just dog. “A” dog?” He shook his head. “Not exactly. Cerberus. He guards the souls who find themselves here.” Through the pouring darkness, she saw a pair of glowing eyes? then another, then another, all at relatively the same level. They approached slowly, warily, and over the noise of rain, she became aware of a low, threatening growling. She attempted to shy away from the huge beast approaching through the darkness, but the Italian Gentleman grabbed her elbow in a vise grip. “If you run, you will only give him reason to chase you. I would presume that your experience with dogs would have allowed you to know that simple rule.” She gritted her teeth as the creature came into view. Just like the mythology always said, the beast had three disgusting, snarling heads. She shied away and it was, just as the Gentleman had said, as though he could smell her fear. Three sets of jowls raised, baring fangs that were razor sharp, gore caught in between them in some cases. The breath caught in her throat. “O” brave new world? that has such? creatures in it.” The Italian Gentleman gave her a slight incline of the head. “Indeed?” He sighed, rather gustily, reaching into the pockets of his pants, and pulling out a handful of cakes. The dog growled ferociously at them, but the Italian Gentleman tossed a handful of cakes in his direction. With a hungry half-yowl, the dog pounced on the pile of cakes at their feet. Lia paused for a few moments to stare blankly at him, gorging himself on the cakes. “Honey cakes,” her guide explained, pulling her somewhat forcefully, “If you do not feed him, you’ll never get past.” It took much of his strength to pull her down the proper path. Below her now, she could tell that they were walking on something a bit more disturbing than mud? wraiths. Shades. A living, writhing floor of them. Terror gripped her so that it was difficult for her to continue moving forward. “Come?” He ordered her, and she did her best to obey, her stomach lurching at the shifting ground beneath them. “Here,” he commented, waving his hands in all directions, “Gluttons. Those who could not control their appetites while they were on the earth.” Again, there was a flash of white, and she staggered, nearly falling into a dead faint, which the Italian Gentleman was ready to receive. It was all a darkness around her? but it seemed to be something of an alley. Brick walls lacing either side of her, a brick wall behind her, and no sign of escape anywhere. A lone white spotlight snapped on, and underneath it stood a small girl, seemingly blending into the shadows behind her. Her face was framed by fringed black hair, her eyes large, dark and luminous. “Who are you?” The words came out so quickly, so sharp and rude, that she hardly felt like she could have said them herself. The girl scoffed, visibly beginning to sneer. “What, you don’t remember me?” Something about her manner made Lia unable to speak. She simply shook her head. The girl pushed aside her fringe of hair, tilting her neck into the light. Lia’s stomach lurched at the sight of a gaping wound. It was plain what had happened here. “Hmph. I thought you’d recognize your own handiwork. November 15, 1992. A little side street in Eufala, Alabama. Thought nobody’d care because I was just a transient, but I can tell you one thing” you created quite a stir in that town dumping me in the ditch for a couple of day-laborers to find at five o’clock in the morning.” There was a pause in the relentless diatribe, the endless spilling of words from the girl’s lips. She felt as if she wanted to clap her hands over her ears and shut it all out, but she was rooted to the spot, unable to move. “And that wasn’t it, either” No? no, not at all.” The girl held up her fingers. “A priest, Dos Passos, 1972. A family of four, Apple Hills, 1980. A hunter, Las Vegas, 2005. 1942” a little girl, Altadena Pass. A little girl, for Christ’s sake.” The girl’s eyes narrowed in disgust, but she continued on, her voice devolving into a hoarse hissing that matched the grievous wound on her throat. “That’s not even it. I could list for hours and you know that I could, because the list is endless, and as long as you’re alive, it’s only going to get longer. You could say it’s for your sustenance” but it’s not. You’ve never been one to take only what you need. You get to a point where you enjoy the ritual, the violence? you live for it. You thrive on it?” Lia opened her mouth in protest, but there were no words, and her head began to bow, in acceptance of the truth. “Disgusting?” Shuddering, she came to, and found herself face to face with a shade, writhing in the mire. A scream came forth from deep within her being, matched by a half-disembodied wail from her counterpart. She struggled up to her feet, violently flailing her limbs this way and that, and staggered right into the strong chest of the Italian Gentleman. Stinging tears came to her eyes, and she clung to him far more closely than she meant to, hoping against hope that he wasn’t some vague supernatural light show like everything else here seemed to be. But he was corporeal, wrapping his arms tightly round her and holding her as she shook violently. Moments passed innumerable there, until she realized where she was, and felt like she had violated some sacred bond. She stood, pulled away from him, and passed her hand over her face, wiping away the tears that had started down the hollows of her cheeks. “Is it always so difficult?” she asked. She needed clarify no more than that. He seemed amused. She could almost see him smirking, save that even so close to him, there was still not a face to be seen underneath his hat. “It is always this difficult,” he affirmed, most stoic, “Always.” Steel gripped her innards, as the Italian Gentleman stepped away from her, downwards across the writhing streets that let to the next destination. “Come,” he said, “We are not finished, and you must go on.” As always, there was no way to question his ruling. A sense of despair began to sink down upon her as she trudged after him, trying to ignore the slippery writhing floor beneath her. The rain poured a little harder, it seemed, just for her. .:: ::: ::. “Round in a circle by that road we went, speaking much more which I do not repeat; we came unto the point where the descent is?” .:: ::: ::.
