Masques

4 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/4/2005 April 4, 2005, 1305 hours El Serpenti's apartment, Las Vegas Watching Melina play checkers with El Serpenti, a contented smile on the child's face; Juan and Teresa were profoundly glad for the Spanish-Mexican warrior's friendship. In Serpenti, Juan and Teresa found a kindred spirit, a sometimes-playmate for their still-shy daughter, and a good friend with whom they could pass time in conversation about the history of Lucha Libre and its effect on all three of them. The husband-and-wife Luchadores, and their daughter, had grown comfortable enough, and on friendly enough terms, with the Spanish-Mexican fighter that they had even come to his apartment at a penthouse in the Treasure Island resort in Vegas... normally the last place they'd even consider bringing a child two and a half weeks away from her tenth birthday. Indeed, they wouldn't let Melina even let go of either of their hands until they were inside Serpenti's apartment, away from all the chaotic hub-bub of the resort proper. The curious part was that neither Juan nor Teresa knew what Serpenti actually looked like; for in the true spirit of Lucha Libre, he never let himself be seen in public without his mask. Normally Juan did not do it either, though on rare certain occasions such as the interview with Noi Tsunami in Rome the previous week, he broke the rule simply because he would have drawn too much attention to himself, dining in a public restaurant with his mask on. There was little need to discuss masks with Serpenti, as both he and Juan were steeped in the tradition of it. The wrestling mask was as much a part of Lucha Libre as the high-flying speed maneuvers that characterized it. The designs on Juan's crimson red mask were based on different fundamentals than Serpenti's, but no less ornate. So it was a bit of a surprise to the husband-and-wife Luchador team when their friend turned to them pretty much out of the blue after finishing his game of checkers with Melina. 'So, tell me, amigo... why do you not wear a mask as well?' The Ramirez spouses turned to look at Serpenti with no small bit of surprise, especially since it was clear who he was talking to. Jos? Sanchez, mentor and trainer to La Herencia de Herederos, was not with them right now, and there was no need for him to be masked since he was not wrestling. Teresa Ramirez was taken aback, and had to push through a bit of a sheepish chuckle just to give some kind of response at all. Collecting herself together again, she looked at Juan before answering Serpenti. 'I've never worn a mask,' she said, blushing lightly. Huummmm... You know, in my time here in America I have heard this saying...' Serpenti tapped the side of his head, trying to think. 'Ah, yes. 'Do not knock it till you try it.' Yes, I believe that is how he said it.' Serpenti gave a nod and a smile, and let out a small laugh after a thought. Do’a Teresa, you know the mask... It is not uncomfortable at all.' he added with a slight chuckle. Teresa chuckled in return. 'Yes, I know, else my husband would have never entered the business. But me, wear a mask? Not very ladylike, is it?' Serpenti shrugged. 'Maybe not ladylike, but it is warrior-like... Lucha Libre-like. When you enter the ring, even if you are Do’a Teresa, are you not a warrior first?' Serpenti spoke with a passion in his voice... the passion of a true Luchador. Teresa had to admit she had never thought of it that way, even if she believed it with her whole heart. She WAS a warrior even before she was a woman... but then again... 'They won't even let me wrestle at all in traditional Mexican Lucha Libre... so the idea seemed rather moot,' Teresa said, hanging her head down. 'If a woman can't wrestle in Lucha Libre, is there a reason for her to wear a mask?' Serpenti walked over and looked out the window of his apartment. Silence filled the air and Juan comforted Teresa. Serpenti turned once more and looked at Teresa. 'Do’a Teresa, No disrespect. But look out this window. This is not Mexico... This is America. No one can tell you, here, that you cannot. Here, the masses that woefully prevent females from entering the sport of Lucha Libre, cannot keep you from it. Many women I have seen in Japan don masks that they cannot wear in Mexico! They are not shunned. No, many are cheered and admired for there skill in combat... Yes, it is the way of our homeland.... but Teresa, this is not that place, and you are a fine warrior... a fine Luchador.' Juan and Teresa were taken aback again, knowing that Serpenti might have been hinting at Herencia's opponents for the upcoming Evolution... Sada and Nariko, a.k.a. Pure Thunder. 'They're... Japanese,' said Teresa. 'They were raised on different fundamentals. Japanese wrestling doesn't have the same traditions... the mask doesn't have the same meaning there.' 'Japanese, perhaps, but they trained under one of the finest Lucha Libre showmen... the great Luis Salvador,' Serpenti said, looking closely at the Ramirez spouses. 'It was Luis who presented Nariko with her wrestling mask... the mask of a Lucha Libre warrior.' Serpenti stood, looking once again out of the window. He sighed and shook his head. 'Please forgive my outburst, my amigos. It is not my place. Lo siento.' Serpenti said heading to the door. (*I'm sorry.) Serpenti stopped and looked down at the young Melina, who grabbed his hand as he came closer to her.. Serpenti knelt down to eye level with Melina. 'Paseo, ni’a. Volver? despu’s de un tiempo. “Querr’as que m” devolver alg’n helado?' Serpenti said with a smile. (*I am going for a walk, child. I will return after a time. Would you like me to bring back some ice cream?) Melina smiled as well. '?S?, se’or; por favor!' Melina said eagerly. 'Adorar’a alg’n helado. Mamacita, “puedo tener alg’n helado?' (*Yes, sir! I would love some ice cream. Mommy, may I have some ice cream?) Teresa smiled and nodded, and Serpenti stood up, looking at Teresa and Juan. 'Again, I am sorry. But I could not contain my thoughts. I shall return after a walk. Hey at least here in Las Vegas, I do not look like I am a bank robber,' Serpenti said with a cheesy smile on his face. The three laughed a bit, lightening the mood in the room. Serpenti bowed and turned on his heels, leaving out the door. Juan and Teresa looked thoughtfully at one another after Serpenti's departure. They were no strangers to the name of Luis Salvador. 'Luis... he trained them,' said Teresa in low tones; her voice carefully controlled with a sense of near-awe. 'Juan--' Juan nodded soberly. 'Mateo worked with Luis before,' he said, recollecting to the stories on the media coverage of Lucha Libre in Mexico. 'Many people said that if either of them were interested in pursuing a tag-team championship together, they'd have been unbeatable.' 'Their styles were rather similar,' said Teresa, thinking back to the tapes she'd viewed with her family when she was young. 'Anyone who thought Luis was just a showboat was left dazzled and on their backs... finding out the hard way that as much flash and flair that he gave in the ring... it worked in actual battle.' 'Do you think it is fate that some of Luis's pupils would find their way here to America, and GW... and enter the ring with students of Mateo Ramirez and Jos? Sanchez?' answered Juan with a small smile. 'It could be,' said Teresa, starting to feel the spirit of the Luchador flow through her... the thrill of battle. She turned to Melina and smiled softly. 'Melina, por favor, traigame parte del papel all? sobre la mesa, y uno de los l’pices.' (*Melina, please bring me some of the paper on the table, and one of the pencils.) Juan looked at his wife as Melina brought over the paper and pencil. 'Teresa...?' Teresa simply smiled and started drawing some rough sketches on the paper. Every so often she'd pause and tap her cheek with the eraser before continuing. Each new sketch seemed to compound upon the previous one, as if refining the detail. Presently, Serpenti returned, carrying a large paper bag which contained several plastic dishes of butter-pecan ice cream, and several plastic spoons. He noticed the designs Teresa was sketching and glanced at her, showing interest. Do’a Teresa blew some eraser dust off of the final sketch and showed it proudly to her husband and her friend. 'It will be blue,' she said, 'in memory of my father.' Serpenti smiled warmly at the intricate Mexican art designs on the sketch of the wrestling mask Teresa had drawn. 'A couple of Mexican tailors work in GW's costuming department,' he said. 'Bring this to them early tomorrow and they can have it ready for you in time for Evolution.' The ice cream was distributed with everyone getting their own plastic bowl and spoon. Serpenti raised his bowl and offered a toast. He spoke in Spanish so Melina would understand. 'Para la memoria de Mateo Ramirez... y el esp’ritu de Lucha Libre,' he said. The bowls were touched together with an almost ceremonial flourish; Melina having to stand on tippy-toes to reach high enough. The child was not overly interested in fighting spirit, but she understood who her grandfather was and what his legacy meant to her family. She was a little sad that she'd never get to see or meet him... but looking into the eyes of her mother, she understood that the sentiment was that his spirit was here in the room with them all.
