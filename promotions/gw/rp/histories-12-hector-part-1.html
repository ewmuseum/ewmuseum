Histories #12 -- Hector Part 1

The Numbers Man. That was a name that I hadn’t been referred to in years. But, back then, no one really knew it was me. Threats were made, orders were issued, assassinations were committed. It was all my job. And I did it well. Hector. He was my first boss. Not so much as a boss as a partner. He was the man that got me started on it all though. He helped me develop my taste for blood. And Vengenace. ***** I drove for hours, only stopping for gas. Even then, I was scared shitless that an APB had been issued and that my face had been plastered all over the TV. The pumps had the pay-at-the-pump options, but I couldn’t risk the use of credit cards. I went into the store, tossed some bills onto the counter and mumbled the pump number. The clerk nodded and took the bills. If he recognized me, he didn’t show it. I went back to the car and continued my journey to nowhere. When the sun began to set and the first stars began to shine, I decided to bring my journey to a close. I was approaching the outskirts of some city. The landscape was dead - treeless with brown grass. Abandoned houses promising eerie ghost tales to why they were like that. Men standing next to the street doing late night business. In the background, the black city backdrop against the dark midnight blue sky. Up ahead, I could see a hotel. It looked like one that wouldn’t remember names or faces. It would have to do. I pulled into the bare and near-empty lot. Grabbing a handful of cash from the duffel bag, I made my way to the office. Behind the counter sat a short, squat man with a bad comb-over. He was staring blankly at a TV set. When I came in the door, a small bell chirped and he jumped. The man stared at me, trying to pass judgement based on looks. “I need a room,” I told him. “A room, eh?” he asked. “And fer “ow long would yeh be needin” it fer?” “A night. Maybe two.” “I think I “ave just the thing fer yeh.” He tossed a room key at me. “Room Four B. Upstairs, at the end.” “Thanks.” I turned to leave. The man cleared his throat. I looked back at him. He was making the universal sign for money. “Fifty a night.” I peeled off a hundred-dollar bill. “A night for sure. If I’m gone tomorrow, keep the other fifty.” “Fine.” I started to leave again. “Oh, and one last thing. What name shall I put down fer yeh?” I looked at the man again. “John. John . . .” “Doe?” “Zero. Johnny Zero.” The building was your standard cheap hotel design, a two-story building splattered with dust and dirt from passing traffic that all faded into the abysmal brown color. The parking lot was old and in need of repair, gigantic potholes threatening the undercarriage of any car. All of the rooms had outside doors, and everything was accessible from the parking lot. I climbed the stairs with my moneybag in hand and went where the man indicated. I could grab the rest of my stuff later. The numbers on the door were crooked. The inside wasn’t much better. Old yellow carpet adorned with stains. White walls filmed with tar from cigarette smoke. Tired sheets on the bed. A battered TV set on a broken dresser. A closed door that led to a bathroom. I wasn’t ready for that adventure yet. I set the moneybag on the bed and emptied it out. There was a lot there. I had just started to count the money when there was a knock at the door. I pulled the butterfly knife from my pocket and flicked the blade open. Pressing my knife hand close to my leg, I went to the door and creaked it open an inch. “Hi,” a blonde wearing a fur coat said. I opened the door the rest of the way. “Hector thought you could use some company.” She opened the coat to reveal her almost nude body. Somewhat clean white lace lingerie covering a scarred and bruised body. I looked at her face. A dead stare met my gaze. There was no smile or twinkle in her eye. Just a woman doing a job to put food on the table, and taking no pleasure in it. “Thanks, but no. Maybe some other time.” I offered a smile. Her lips curled slightly up. She shut the coat and walked off. I shut the door and collapsed on the worn bed, next to the money. The springs in the mattress groaned. I finished counting the money and placed it back in the bag, tossing it onto the floor next to the bed. Through the thin walls I could hear moans of pleasure and screams of pain, from both men and women. Sleep would come eventually, I hoped. I awoke the next morning, stiff and sore from the night’s long sleep. Harsh light shone in from the gaps between the blinds. I got up and stretched. Joints popped and muscles complained. I looked for the bag next on the floor. It was gone. “Did I bring the bag in?” I asked myself. Leaving the room, I went outside to search the car. Reaching the ground floor, I looked around. The car was gone. I rubbed my eyes and looked again. The car was still not where I left it. I ran around the hotel, keeping distance from the homeless looking men with their arms stretched out, begging for money. I reached the front of the hotel. Still no car. I headed for the office. The pudgy man was still behind the counter, still zoned out to whatever was on TV. He jumped again when the bell chirped, announcing my entrance to the office. “Ah. Di” yeh “ave a good night?” he asked, winking. I thought about what he said and the wink. “Hector?” “At yeh service.” “My car’s been stolen. Plus, I think someone came into my room and stole one of my bags from the room. “That’s a shame. Once the city eats a car, it’s long gone for sure. As for the bag, I would have to say that Meredith took it. When she left for the night, she had a small black bag with her.” “That would be it. You have any idea where I can find her at?” Hector shook his head no. “I surely don’t, my boy. The girls come to me for work. I don’t ask too many questions. That way, if anyone comes lookin” for them, like yehself . . .” I nodded in understanding and thought about the money in the bag and the car. The money was going to be my livelihood. Now all the cash on me was it. Plus, no car. How was I suppose to run now? And I couldn’t exactly call the police either. Screwed on two counts. “Is there a diner somewhere around here that I could walk to?” “Across the street.” “Thanks.” I turned and walked slowly out of the office, feeling defeated. The diner reminded me of one of those from the late fifties - a row of booths and tables against the wall of windows and a bar with stools separating the kitchen from the dining area. I stayed in the back corner booth, with only the waitress stopping by every so often to refill my cup of coffee. I only had five hundred dollars, less the cup of coffee. If I stayed at the hotel and got some food, I could survive a couple of more days. But, the question was what to do when the money ran out. I could hear footsteps walking down the linoleum floor of the diner. Could have been the police for all I cared. “This seat taken, honey?” a female voice asked. I looked up and saw three women staring expectantly at me. One was a blonde in a black miniskirt and what looked like a painted on red haltertop. Another was a brunette in a leather jacket, and the third was a black woman in a leopard print outfit. I gestured and they sat down, the brunette and black woman across from me and the blonde next to me. The brunette had a large welt under one eye. “What happened?” I asked. “Same shit that happens every week,” the brunette started. “Some guy comes in and asks for a girl. He starts to get too rough and is soon smacking her around.” I thought of Bryce hitting Lily. “Don’t you have somebody to ensure your safety while you’re, um, working?” I asked. “You mean a pimp,” the black woman said. I nodded somewhat sheepishly. “Hector,” all three spoke at the same time. “Him?” “Yep,” it was the blonde speaking. “He said he warned some of the guys, but they just laughed at him. That was weeks ago.” My threat to Bryce. I looked at the brunette, my anger starting to swell, and the monster inside stirring. “Hector warned the guy?” My voice was getting stern. The three women nodded. Bryce exploding, nothing left but little clumps of useless flesh. “I’ll take care of it.” Hector jumped when I walked into the office. Nerves, I guess. “What can I do for yeh?” he asked. “I got a deal for you, Hector.” “A deal?” “Yep. You pay me a percentage, say twenty-five percent, of what the girls bring in and I’ll take care of the guys that get too rough with them.” Hector looked at me. “You’re serious.” I nodded. “Take yeh shirt off.” “Why?” “Just do it.” I pulled the shirt off over my head. He studied my naked chest for a moment. Coming around the counter, Hector circled me. He really was a little, fat guy - about as wide as he was tall. He stopped behind me. I jumped when I felt his hands grope the inside of my legs. “What the hell you think you’re doing?” “Shhh?” was all he said. His hand went down my legs, first one, then the other. He then circled around to face me again. “Lean yeh head down.” I bent over and felt his pudgy, sweaty hands run through my hair. He gave a little grunt of satisfaction and returned to his seat behind the counter. “What was that all about?” “I was checking to see if yeh was wired.” “I’m not.” “I know that now.” Hector appeared to be thinking. Reaching under the table, he pulled out a small notepad and consulted the scribbling on the small pages. “One of our more regular “problems” will more than likely show up in a coupla days. Most of the rough guys never come back, but some hang around. If yeh take care of him to my satisfaction, yeh got yehself a deal.” “Just let me know when he shows up, and I’ll take care of him.”
