Untitled

4 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11.04.05 It was cold inside that box they had us working in. The whole place smelled of cold blood and refrigerated meat. The front of my apron was covered in blood but I was used to it at this point. Since I broke my arm I’ve been working here at the meat processing plant. So, for almost a couple of years now, every day I’ve come home, I check the messages on the machine while I take the meat that I got from work and stuff it in the freezer. I suppose that was one good thing about working there, I know I’d never go hungry. I had it down to a routine after so long. I’d get up at around 5 in the morning so I could lift weights for an hour before heading off to work. There was no use in taking a shower in the morning, simply because you’d only come home smelling of the plant. I spend all day in the meat locker and only get out once in the morning for a smoke, then for lunch and once more in the afternoon for my last smoke break. Other than that, there were no breaks in that place. It was a non-stop operation where if you slowed down, you had the boss breathing down your neck faster than you can mention the word “union.” That was the thing about my boss, he despised the Union and he let you know it from the first day you’re hired. Jack was an older man, in his late 40’s. His balding salt and pepper hair always seemed slicked back with some kind of strong smelling hair grease. Overweight, he usually struggled to make his rounds without stopping to catch his breath. Jack too, was a smoker and had been since he was 11. Rarely was he seen without a fat cigar in his mouth and a clipboard in his hand. What he had written on that clipboard is a mystery to all. When he did yell at you for screwing up or having mention of the Union, he would sit you down and scold you in a way that you could make you feel like you were back in Kindergarten. That is if your Kindergarten teacher had a shotty voice and had a thick New Jersey accent. For whatever reason, he always seemed to have liked me. I could tell because he didn’t yell at me as much as he did everyone else. I almost think he yells at me just so the other employees don’t jump me during break. I recall him speaking to me after I had been here only a year. It was basically a lecture on how I should’ve went to college instead of fooling around with the wrestling. “You know it’s all fake, right?” He would say to me. It wasn’t fake to me and I proved it to him when I rolled up my sleeve to show him where the bone punctured through the skin. It was a nasty scar that has faded a bit. When it was fresh it was about three or four inches long. There’s titanium fused to the bone just to make sure that something like that never happens again. Jack looked in awe at my scar before slapping me in the back of the head and going on about how it was stupid to jump from the top of a barn like that. I mean, I know it was stupid, but it was my passion. It still is my passion. Anyway, upon leaving work at 6:30 that night, with no less than twenty pounds of pure lean beef. Jack usually made us take home at least that much because he “takes care of his employees.” Honestly, I think it’s just something he does to stick it to his brother, who happens to own the plant. Whatever though, I’m not complaining, because at least I know I can have a hell of a cookout if I ever manage to get a grill. I arrive home at around 8:30 usually. It sucks having to use public transportation, but it’s the only way I have to get around. I lost my car to some dick who decided he needed quick wheels. He wrecked it running from the cops and I’m left without a vehicle because I only had liability on it. However, I have met quite the few characters of people while taking the bus home. Apparently I don’t look too threatening because I’ve had long discussions with elderly women about what it was like when they grew up. The bus driver as a result of it, thinks that I’m, “such a nice young man.” The first thing I do when I walk through the door is check the messages on my machine. I’m still waiting for that one good offer that will let me leave this meat-beating job and get back into the ring where I belong. I’ll wrap the meat up and throw it in the freezer. My apartment is a small studio place, the light colored walls make the place look bigger. Of course, I have hardly enough furniture to fill the place. A bed, a couple of couches, an old oak coffee table and a dresser that I must’ve gotten at the Salvation Army or something. Actually...now that I think about it, I’m not sure where that dresser came from. I have a 13-inch television that only gets four channels because at this point, I don’t feel like picking up a cable bill. The hardwood floors of the place are cold in the winter, but then again, that’s why I bought slippers. The heat in here works, just not too well. It’s either way too hot or way too cold. This place holds heat like a compulsive gambler holds cash. After my shower, I do what I’ve been doing since my arm healed, looking for a wrestling audition. I’m bound and determined to make it back into that ring. Last week I was placed in a dark match at the bottom of a pay-per-view event called Dark Rituals. I lost horribly because as I found out, I wasn’t mentally prepared for my opponents. I was out of it shortly after my mind gave in to being overpowered. It was a bad scene. However though it seems that Global wants to try me out again. So this coming week, I have yet another dark match at Evolution. I’m sure of myself this time, I’m sure that I’ll come out on top. Where does that put me if everything happens to take off? Some up and coming wrestler living in a broken neighborhood surrounded by people you couldn’t trust to watch your pet for the weekend because they might sell it to a Chinese restaurant so they can get cash for whatever habit they’re supporting. I wouldn’t plan on staying here, I’ll say that. If this ever takes off, being a wrestler, I guess I’ll have to change my lifestyle. I won’t have to work in that factory and I sure has hell wouldn’t have to put up with this place. ~FIN
