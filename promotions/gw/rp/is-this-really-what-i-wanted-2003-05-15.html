Is This Really What I Wanted?

15 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/15/03 Distant from everything and everyone You think this is easy on me? When all that ever was will ever be And what could always be - is what we Build up and destroy together One another forever My family My friends My love My heart My home Distant from everything and everyone Yet I’ve never felt so close to anyone -Another Days Gain He stands outside his home in China. He looks around the farm, recognizing the hard work he has put into it throughout his whole entire life. His father shed his own blood working on this field, trying to make a living’a decent living for his family. He would work day and night and night and day on these fields, harvesting everything possible. He passed it up when he passed away, leaving the labor to his son. Is this what he really wanted? To be thankfully handed his father’s lifestyle and pass it down from generation to generation, hoping it will change sometime in the future. Is this what he really wanted? He searches his eyes throughout the whole entire field. The grass, cut by him. The crops, grown by him. The dirt, plowed by him. His career, created by his father. Giving up his dreams and hopes, is this what he really wanted? “Leaving this field. Leaving the sweat and labor. Leaving the tears and cuts. Leaving the blood shed from working here. Is this really what I want” I should smile, realizing my dreams may come true. Becoming something in the Global Wrestling, oh, for it will be a lot more marvelous then sitting back waiting for things to grow out of the ground. But on this ground, this ground supports the most beautiful trees with the most satisfying fruits to the taste buds. The grass, greener then any other grass around the world. The memories grew from this soil, memories of my father and mother’chasing me around this field. The memories of my father teaching me how to do certain things. But I never paid much attention because my attention was on being around my dad, just knowing’realizing that he loves me. And my mother, the memories galore, her sitting in the kitchen, making Sunday dinner’smiling away while wiping the glisten of sweat off of her face. She made Sunday dinner because she loved her family, not because she had to. She smiled because she had something that nobody had, the love of my father and I. The grass is greener and the fruits taste better because they are my fruits. And that is my grass. Those are my trees, and these’these are my memories. Is this really what I want? Leaving the memories, because they are all I have left.” He pauses, not knowing what to do next. Confusion plagues his mind, thinking whether to smile for what he has once had, or to shed a tear for leaving it all. Instead, he turns around from his situation and walks around the house and up the grassy hill to the top, where an old oak tree stands. He stops in front of it and looks down towards the ground. A stone pallet lies on the ground reading: Won Lo Tse Ling October 11th, 1922 ? July 22nd, 1981 Forgotten By Nothing Remembered By All He looks up at the tree, the tree that his father built, the same tree that they would run around and play. So they buried him there, where he was remembered for the good memories. He looks at each leaf, starting to blow with the silent wind as it calls his destiny. If only he wasn’t tone deaf, he would hear what it is saying. A single tear falls from his cheek. Nothing more, nothing less. “And this is all I have to offer you. I have managed your land for years, and now I bow my head and wave goodbye for my own greed. Innocent dreams have taken the place of my innocent life. But now, those dreams feel innocent, for I am letting you down. Is this really what I want” Are these the memories I wish to keep? Letting you down for the nothing you have done?” He pauses and takes a deep breath and pushes every word he can out of his throat, escaping from his heart. “Father, I won’t let you down.” Is This Really What I Want?
