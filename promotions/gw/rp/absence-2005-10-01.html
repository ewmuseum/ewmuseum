Absence

1 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/01/05 The McFarlane Estate 10/1/05: 4:12 pm - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - Cyrus had made a dramatic recovery while in Lia's care, and had, for some time now, been able to converse as normal, his mind having returned to its usual razor-sharp state, regardless of the relative disrepair his body still remained in. There was little to do now that would help what injuries he had, save giving them enough time to recover on their own. Virgil had been spending the majority of time speaking with Cyrus about various bits and pieces of history and scholarly pursuits, which had constituted the majority of their interaction, until Virgil's curiosity had gotten the best of him. 'Cyrus, do you recall your past well?' Cyrus nodded, looking somewhat puzzled. 'Of course, we Kiasyd are rather precise about archiving our unlives. Why do you ask such a question? Have you not done the same? After all, you must have more years than myself.' Virgil shook his head, looking over his sharpened fingernails, looking for imperfections. 'I do not know the majority of them.' Cyrus' brow furrowed in thought, giving him the look of a curious scientist... albeit, a rather inhuman one. 'Hm. Interesting...' Virgil turned toward him, expecting him fully to go on without any encouragement. 'It would seem that you were not spared from the bizarre amnesia that seems to be widespread along the midwestern and eastern Americas. Apparently it does not discriminate with age.' Virgil raised an eyebrow, peering at Cyrus, who seemed to have retreated into the realm of hypothesis, his eyes quite dead to the outside world. However, Virgil was not planning on letting this go without any discussion whatsoever. 'There are others then, who have had their memory taken from them?' Cyrus snapped out of it, and nodded. 'You should ask your paramour about such things when she returns. Certainly you remember the long journey you had to set upon to return her to her true personality? It's only fortunate that you were able to do this before your own amnesia took effect. Had you not, there is little telling who either of you would be tonight.' Virgil's face twisted into a frown. 'How could the memory of an entire demographic of Kindred be affected? The kine are not stumbling about with no knowledge of themselves or their pasts.' Cyrus' face remained expressionless. 'It is a rather queer occurrence. Unfortunately, the nature of the danger in the area would make it impossible to document anything. Though, if the humans are not affected as you say... then there is something strange at work. Perhaps a disease of the blood.' Virgil raised an eyebrow. 'Or perhaps treachery of said humans.' Cyrus shook his head. 'Virgil, don't be preposterous. Humans are cattle, and are only slightly more ingenious. Were they to create a device or serum to cause amnesia, they would likely occupy themselves with using it on each other.' Virgil could hardly disagree with that assertion, but it still left entirely too much to the imagination. A blood-borne pathogen could possibly cause memory loss, he supposed, but why did it only affect vampires? Certainly, deteriorated though it might be from lack of use, they had the same internal structure as the humans they once were. Why, then, were they not affected? He didn't doubt Cyrus' library-like knowledge base, but with the thought planted in his mind, there was little he could do to erase the thought, no matter how 'preposterous'. - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - Two hours later: Gone. She was gone once again, and once again it was his time to sit in the McFarlane estate house, attentions directed at nothing in particular, a vicious sense of ennui descending further upon him every second. He lay on the bed that only a couple of weeks ago, he'd still been sharing with Lia, everything properly placed in his un-life that was, if one were to believe Kindred common thought, supposed to be completely and utterly miserable and without emotional stimulation. Perhaps it was for the best that he slowly found himself changing from what had been the norm for what may as well, to him, have been eternity. He couldn't remember what happened twenty years ago, much less whenever he happened to be sired. Kindred lore, history, archaic forms of multiple languages, all of his knowledge remained intact. Although, when he looked at the various books he'd collected on the topics, as he did now, the bindings worn from years of existence, he couldn't manage to remember when or where he'd laid hands on them. He knew that he had at one point. The bond between a hoarding vampire and his possessions was strong indeed, which puzzled him all the more about the ring that Lia had left with him. It was difficult to discern whether the attachment came from simple possession, or something more. After all, with no memory, it was impossible to tell the difference. He twisted it between his fingers, poring over the minute details of the small silver ring. He did know, at least, that it was in fact likely hers, due to her rare aesthetic aversion to gold, and the very slightly uneven ways the metal was fluted and cut signified that it had been done by an imprecise hand tool, which at the least made it predate 'modern' nights, and considering a husband that could afford good silver in times before modern would have had the connections necessary to find a jeweler with precise hand tools, even before 1600, it meant that she was likely much older. Thus, the origin of her power (even before consuming Leinad Soma's blood) was explained, and considering his own matched hers, he was assured that his life had extended far past the reaches of his memory. This was as close to a life as Virgil Ambrosi often came, these nights. He smiled into the mirror, somewhat wryly. 'Yes, even your name is false.' Ambrosi, an Italic name that meant 'of the gods', was only a pseudonym, created to fill only a piece of the hole that had been left where his life once was. He had grown past being frustrated about his misfortune, for it did, after all, prevent anything productive from being done. While it did indeed frustrate him to no end to have no knowledge of his past, there were always more important things to think about. Namely, the sad state of being alone. As he could hear Alexis somewhere downstairs, and knew that Cyrus was still in another room, he supposed that he really wasn't alone, in the literal sense of the word, but even with another of his own kind in the same house, he still felt as though he'd been living in a tiny cave for years, devoid of any kind of contact whatsoever. Perhaps he'd talk with one or the other of them at some point, but now he'd be almost useless company, unless they had designs on talking with him about Lia. He let the ring drop to the chain around his neck, and shuffled out the back door, into the last rays of the dying sun. Damek and Jirina, the excessively large dog-like beasts that Lia kept as pets bounded excitedly up to him, and Virgil managed a smile, as the bounced up and down next to him, begging for attention. They'd been acting rather skittish since Lia had left, and Virgil felt rather sorry for them. 'Nazdar, vy dve. Would you like a walk?' Underneath their terrifying exteriors, they were, of course, still dogs, and few dogs would turn down such a proposition. They walked ahead of Virgil, not really requiring a leash, considering they were on private property, and Virgil kept pace behind them, as they continually looked back at him, tongues lolling out of their mouths. He smiled again, as the ring continually thudded against his chest. It seemed he would always have to surround himself with things that reminded him of her. He'd become so dependent and weak lately, but somehow, it just seemed right.
