Training, Reflecting & Rebuilding

28 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Week Of 8-28-05 Training Las Vegas There it was, just inches away the finger as he stared at it. The heat wearing down on him as his face twisted into something between pain and frustration. The drops of sweat poured down onto the arid soil like salty raindrops. The momentum had ended, but he wasn’t giving into the inevitability. He continued trying to grab hold of the small flag hanging on the wooden poll. No matter his determination, the science worked against him. The bungee cord pulled back, snatching his three hundred pounds off the floor of the earth like it was brushing off a leaf from the tree in the fall. He fell, twisting on the ground as he looked like a bad action stunt from the movies. The flag was too far away. His mind told him that, over and over again. He couldn’t fight enough to overcome the laws God had instituted over his creation. Pride and arrogance continued telling him that a new technique, more strength and pushing himself harder would gain him the flag. “Don’t you believe in physics?” The voice asked from beneath the shade tree. He was there, sitting on the lounge chair as he peeled the fruit, throwing the rind off into the grass where he would let the birds and insects attack it for whatever sustenance it offered them. The dirt was foul in his mouth, and he spit the dry material out first, then the next spit had some saliva. The desert was too hot to allow him much in the way of moisture since he had been breathing through his mouth, drying out the membranes. Massive mitts brushed off the sandy soil as he stood, “I can do it.” “No you can’t.” A sweet, juicy sliver of orange flesh went into his mouth. “I designed this so you wouldn’t be able to reach it. I wanted you to get close, but you’ll never make it. No matter what you do, this is a matter of fact. This is something greater than you are.” The muscles were burning, a testament to the continued attempts and failures. He looked down the way at the post. The yellow flag hung there so easily, “I can do it.” “Can you storm the gates of Heaven and win?” The man asked as he pulled off another section of the orange. “You mean the gates of Hell.” “No, I mean Heaven.” He sighed, wondering where he would be going with this conversation. He bent over, resting his hands on his knees, “Of course not.” “Because God would not suffer it. He would not allow you to do that, just like you can’t break his rules of physics. You need help, and you aren’t very good at asking for help.” He continued eating the fruit as sticky sweet juice rolled down his chin. “What’s your point?” He kicked some dirt away from him. “I don’t do this whole mysterious insight into the world.” “You need help against Sid, and I am offering my help.” The man’s smile was as smooth and charming as it had always been. He dropped down to his knees, resting as he considered what the man was saying. “You know what it could mean, right?” Stephen stood up, “I know more than you do I think. I never thought I could take on the whole world and win. Look, I’m here to help you with two things. First is against Sid because it is more important, but next is about the octagon.” Jake Hix nodded, “So what about the octagon?” “I think you need to quit holding back. You used to be a fucking wrestling God, and now you’re not cutting it. When are you going to snap out of it?” Jake laughed as he stood up, “I don’t know. Come get me out of this thing, and I’ll figure out the answer.” Stephen stood up, and felt the sharp, intense pain inside his gut. He walked over without showing anything to the man he had offered his help to in the greater battle. Sensational Stephen Reed unbuckles the harness, allowing Jake to be freed of the device. “As your trainer, go run a few more laps, and then we’ll talk. Do five miles, you need the conditioning work.” He smiled. “No problem.” Jake took off to the track. Stephen pulled pills out from his pocket, and the two white pills went in with the orange, swallowed down with the acidic fruit. Reflecting On The Bayou’Location Unknown The porch was finished. They had replaced every single board, all of them rotted out or attacked by the elements. They had since moved inside the house, but he was out on the porch looking out on the bayou as the sun set behind the twisted trees. The bayou was beautiful. A setting where earth, sky and water all seemed to fight together to destroy each other slowly. However, within that battle the beauty of the colors and creatures and plants fighting for their survival were sublime, something peaceful in the battle played out over decades where there would never be a clear winner. He sipped the beer slowly as he rested his foot on the railing, looking out as he saw an alligator swim bye pursuing his own needs. The day was hot, and he had long ago cast aside the shirt to allow his body to cool down more quickly. The hair on his chest and belly had returned, now that he didn’t care about shaving or waxing for television. He heard the screen door open and then footsteps behind him. He recognized her steps easily, and enjoyed her caress as the arms wrapped around his torso from behind. “You look sad my love.” Xavier smiled with turning toward her, but it was a sad little smile. “I am a little. I just?” Bella leaned in next to his ear, her warm breath as always teasing and relaxing him at the same time. “What is it?” “I’m just’I’ve been happy.” Xavier took a sip of his beer. “And every time I smile or feel good, I keep wondering how he’s handling it all. I feel guilty for being happy Bella. I feel like I should be out there with him, fighting beside him. He’s fighting my battle.” “You fought your battle baby. We were all there, and you won that fight. This is something else, and Jake was chosen.” Bella tried to ease his mind, but she knew it wouldn’t work. Xavier took a deep breath, held it as the rage of emotions kept exploding inside his soul, and then let it out. “No, he was left. The fight was mine, and I don’t know if I can ever rest until this is over. Every time I think of some petty little annoyance, I think about what he’s facing.” “You can’t know what he’s facing right now.” Xavier took his foot off the railing and turned around. There was a vacant, scary look in his eyes as he remembered all the horrors in the memories locked inside his mind. He finished the beer quickly and looked at her as a single tear fell onto his beard, “I do Bella. I know’I know more than I can ever explain.” Xavier walked past her, and down the stairs into the sparse grass of the yard. He needed to walk off the memories, forget for a little while longer. Rebuilding On The Bayou’Location Unknown The pill bottle was right there on the side table, just a few feet away. The invitation of the pink darvocet wanting him to say hello once again, to let the feelings be washed away by the numbness. All he had to do was take one, but he didn’t want to. He hadn’t taken any medicine in weeks. The pain was horrendous, and he always found himself craving the relief the bottle offered. None of it mattered though, because he had a goal in mind. He wanted to heal up enough to leave the plantation, to rejoin the battle. As patriarch of the family, he couldn’t stand the idea of his little brother facing everything alone. Physical therapy was a daily occurrence for him, along with spiritual therapy in the books he read each evening. He was reconstructing himself from the ground up, rebuilding his soul piece by piece. The machine could be put back together, and he had all the tools he needed. A long time ago, the Path Of Destruction had been a good hook for the fans. During the period, he had become enamored with the idea that they all walked the Path Of Destruction. He thought the only question is how you took each step, what you accomplished as you came closer to oblivion. Now he knew there was more truth in those words that he thought. He refused to be a cripple wheeled about as everyone else chose their own path. He was going to walk on his own, run toward the future and never be beaten again. He owed that to the family, and he wanted it for himself. Of course, taking the steps to the pill bottle wouldn’t help him. He would only be slipping back into sloth and ruin. Pitt walked over to the wall. He leaned back into the sheetrock, and bent down into the wall sit. Something so simple ripped at the slowly rebuilding flesh. The doctors said he wouldn’t walk, then they said his strength would never return. He had proven them wrong, and he would continue proving them wrong. He sat against the wall, feeling the fire in his thighs. Mind and body were one, but he ignored the pain racing through both. He kept seeing the face in his mind. The same face he saw every single day, but different. More gaunt, and with darker eyes. The face was hard, mean. Remembering what had happened, he knew what real evil was when he looked into the face of Hell that night. The face disappeared as he crashed into the floor. His knee hit hard, swelling up at once. He ignored the pain as he forced himself back up to his feet. Pitt Hix walked back over to the wall, and repeated the wall sit once again. He was rebuilding himself. He needed to become strong again. He could fix all the parts, slowly and surly. The path he saw ahead of him required that he rebuild. The Texas Wrecking Machine had to return. He was back to seeing the face?
