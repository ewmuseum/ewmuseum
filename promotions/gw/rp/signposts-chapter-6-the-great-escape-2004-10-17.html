Signposts Chapter 6: The Great Escape.

17 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 17/10/04 Mr. Grey dragged Winters to the back of the room, with considerable ease. Fundamentally, the bodyguard was built like a brickshithouse and if he wanted you to go somewhere, then you sure as hell went with him. Because there was no choice but to completely adhere to the laws of physics. The bodyguard clicked a special button - which was attached, somewhere, to the bottom of the opulent, white fireplace - the resultant of which caused a section of the wall to smoothly slide to the side, essentially creating a back door out of the place. Winters struggled, managing - finally - to wrestle and squirm his way out of the Grey’s vice-like grip. “Sir,” He said in a firm, but respectful tone. “WE have to get you out of here!” “But we can’t leave her behind!” Winters exclaimed, with a little more feeling than he originally tended to show. “We have to, sir.” “But she is the only one who knows about my past”.what if she dies out there!” Winters sentence was over shrouded somewhat by the sound of gun shots in the other room and Mr. Grey had done enough arguing. He grabbed Winters by the arm and hoisted him through the secret back door, closing it behind them as he went. “She is following orders. She accepts that.” The bodyguard muttered, dragging Winters through the tunnel way. “You’re talking about her as if she’s already dead!” “Well,” He said, coming to the end of the corridor, opening the window exit at the end. “She might be by now.” “Really sympathetic of you’thanks.” Grey fumbled Winters through the window, whereupon he fell onto the black metal fire escape with a thud. Grey followed in suit, the big man finding it increasingly difficult to fit through the tight gap. When he finally did make it out, he simply glared at Winters who was still lay on the floor. “What the hell?” He exclaimed. “Get your ass up! We’ve got to get?” Grey cut his sentence short and for the longest time Winters didn’t know why. Then he saw the bullet hole in the mans chest, the faint trickle of blood, begin to stain the man’s white shirt. Must have been a silencer. “Shit!” Winters cursed, trying to avoid the giant’s lifeless corpse as it headed south. He failed and the rather large frame of the man fell on top of him in a heap. Below him he could hear the clanking sound of footsteps beat against the metal surface of the fire escape drawing ever closer. Feeling the pressure of the situation, he produced a hefty heave, managing to lift the limp body of the giant up and off him in a surprisingly easy fashion. Without wasting a moments time to look down to see where his pursuers were, he made his way up the fire escape, quickly finding himself on the roof of the building. Where to go from here? He couldn’t go back inside the building, because he would undoubtedly run into some kind if resistance and he couldn’t go back down the fire escape. His quick thinking led him to but one conclusion’and he didn’t like the thought of it one little bit. From where he stood he could see the next building a long. It was close to the building he found himself on top of, but was about fifteen foot smaller. That meant a fifteen foot fall if he actually went through with the crazy notion. Could his body stand it? He heard the footsteps of the men draw nearer. Now wasn’t the time to stop and think about the consequences of his possible actions. Now was the time to act, and act he did. He ran to the edge of the building and jumped. The transition through the air felt, to him, like he was flying, which inevitably gave him a sense of power. Of course, his delusions were soon brought to rest as he impacted with the unforgiving concrete of his landing surface. Except, it wasn’t unforgiving. He managed to land perfectly on his feet and he felt no pain. Was that normal? Since waking in that little town in Kansas, he’s been constantly discovering more and more strange, yet oddly familiar, aspects of his capabilities. He put this down in that list and made his way to the edge of the building. He heard the brief glint of metal impact with his concrete surroundings. He was being shot at. He jumped form the side of the building and didn’t look back.
