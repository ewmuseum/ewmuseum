The Dream and The Funeral

8 May 2007

The Dream and The Funeral Nathan Boswell Global Wrestling Promo #3107 Date: 5/8/07 Location: New Jersey He had the dream again. It’s been one of the staples of his since he was 19: Huge physique, full head of auburn hair, and having the same disturbing dream over and over again. For the past six months, the dream had gone away, leading Nathan Boswell to believe he was cured. But a lot of things have gone wrong since then. [FLASH!] “LOOK AT YOU! LOOK AT THOSE TEARS! YOU’RE A SEVEN FOOT MONSTER, NOT A CRYBABY TART! WHAT THE FUCK IS WRONG WITH YOU?” [FLASH!] And now the dream’s returned. The dream is bearable; on some nights he even finds himself enjoying it. But it’s symbolic: the dream represents the fear that Nathan has had since that time, the fear that Reginald Atkins has tried to prey on. His fear that he might lose himself again, to the anger, to the hatred, to the rage. Even now, the dream floats in his head… ---- Nathan steps forward, and an exhausted Ross Boswell falls forward, his eyes rolling around lawlessly. A wide tooth-exposing smile fills Nathan’s face as he bends down, getting in his father’s face. “Hi, dad. Had fun fucking with me recently? Had fun playing with my head? I might play with your head a little tonight. Soccer, anyone?” Nathan steps back and kicks Ross in the head, sending him to the side a few inches. Ross rolls over and looks up, hatred filling his eyes. “You do realize,” sputters Ross, blood staining his teeth, “that if you kill me, then that is it. I have already won.” Nathan shakes his head, growling. He takes the knapsack off of his back and unveils a knife. “So you won? Congratulations, dad. I hope they throw you a big old party in hell.” “Nathan, the charade is done. Come on, you can stop playing now.” “Sorry dad,” Nathan says, his grave face pointed down at the man who breathed life into him. “but the games are done. You’re the one who played the games, and now I’m done and I’m playing for keeps.” Nathan walks forward with the knife, growling, as Ross begins to slide against the asphalt, trying to escape. He sees a pair of headlights a few hundred yards in the distance and waves, signaling for help, but Nathan kicks his arm. “Please son, put down the knife. I’m sorry about everything, just put down the knife.” Nathan looks at his dad for a second and stops, then nods. “You know what, dad? Okay. I’ll put down the knife. “He drops the knife on the street, where it makes a clinking noise that is quickly overshadowed by Ross Boswell’s sigh of relief. “But only because it is so much better to kill you with my bare hands.” He steps on Ross’s throat, bringing more blood to the surface, which has nowhere to go but on Ross’s face. Nathan lets off a little, but keeps the foot over his father’s neck. He hears a car screech in the distance, but refuses to look up. Nothing is stopping him now. “You’ve been taking this path, Ross. You’ve been going down this road where you know I am supposed to walk down, and you keep throwing things in my way. You’ve tried to stop me using every object you could find, but you still couldn’t. And up until now I haven’t been able to get ahead in the path and stop you from putting things in my way. But now I have, and you can’t go down this path anymore. I’m your road block, and you can’t walk a fucking step.” Nathan sighs loudly. “Now, you’re the most proud fucker I know, so you most likely won’t admit defeat, even under my boot. You’ll probably look for a way to keep walking the path. But this time, it isn’t happening.” “This path is going too far, Ross. If you decide to stay on this path, then my foot may decide to stay on its path right through your trachea.” Nathan’s foot dances over Ross’s throat as we see his eyes dance about in complete and utter fear. Ross shakes his head, his eyes still trembling. And this is what Nathan always wanted to see. He had wanted his father to feel how he always felt with Ross messed with him. To finally shoot it right back in his face. He never knew what it was like to be on top. And now he knows. And it feels so fucking good. “So, father of mine, what’s it going to be? Are you going to run away and never look back? Or are you going to die knowing that you finally got the lower hand, and that the last thing you remember is that this was the first time a plan blew up in your face?” Ross Boswell looks up, his eyes still shaking in fear. “You wouldn’t kill your…your own father. Even…even if you did…cops would be all over you…you’re too high-standing for that…” “Police? I don’t care. All I care about is that the menace would be gone, and I could finally rest easy. So, pops…” Nathan breathes slowly, his eyes looking exactly like Ross’s did so many times before. “Are you going to run…or are you going to die?” “Fuck you…you little bastard…” Ross gasps, trying to regain his breath. “I’ll see you again…in hell…” “I’ll be sure to wear shorts to my funeral.” And the foot presses down. “WAAAAAAAAAAIT!” ---- His uncle had stopped him, and Nathan always wonders what would have happened if his uncle had been a second too late. He hadn’t seen his father since, so it probably wouldn’t have made much of a difference. Uncle Louie claimed to have saved Ross for Nathan’s sake. He thought that if Nathan had fully given in and murdered his own father, there would be no stopping the transformation that would take place. But it happened anyway. And THERE’S your symbolism. This dream shows Nathan the devil that he could be. It shows the unfeeling, uncaring, villainous monster that he is capable of created. And it also shows that when this monster is created, there are no lines of loyalty: family, former friend, tag team partner. Nathan Boswell had the dream again. But on this night, he wakes up smiling. ---- ---- The service for Arianna was short and anything but sweet. Nathan stared at the ground for most of the service, ignoring the looks from her family. He didn’t want to see their eyes blame him for the tragedy. It was something he’d be able to take if it weren’t for the fact that he knows he’s responsible. He brought her back to the business. Without really meaning to, she was wrapped up in it. And she became a casualty of the game. Reginald Atkins had finally stepped out of the silly persona that everyone – including Nathan – pigeonholed him into, and it was a doozy. Even here, at the funeral, Nathan acknowledges that Reginald is more dangerous than he thought. But that does nothing for the pain. The anguish. And those goddamned staring eyes. To his credit, one man actually decides to use his mouth for the purpose everyone is too scared to do. “I’ll never forgive you.” He is Bryan Riley, Arianna’s brother. Growing up with him, he was the only member of the family who knew who Nathan was, and he thought it was great because even though Nathan did some terrible things in the ring, Bryan thought he understood that it was all just an act. Now he knows it’s not. Nathan doesn’t reply. “It’s a selfish thing, you know?” Bryan continues, staring up at the much larger man with newfound courage. I guess that’s what happens when you turn 20 – you think it’s your turn. “To bring her around as an arm-piece? To use her for sympathy? As a half-baked plan for a wrestling comeback? You’re sick.” Having said his piece, Bryan starts to walk away, but is held by a mammoth hand grabbing his arm. His courage used up, Bryan’s eyes look up at the angry giant in fear. Nathan growls something, but Bryan can’t understand it. “Excuse me?” “I said don’t you ever…ever…talk about something you don’t know about. Not in a political debate, not in a public forum, and especially not to a wounded man who loved your sister more than anything in the world.” Now it is Bryan’s turn to not reply. Nathan lets go of Bryan’s arm and continues, “Never claim to know my motives. And never think that I only tried to use her for anything. She was my world. She was everything. And if you think that this attempt at venomous words is going to make me feel sorry for loving her, you better come up with something better.” There always an innate ability to tell when some order is being disrupted, and Arianna’s parents, George and Harriet, walk over to save their son from what they assume is an unprovoked verbal onslaught. “Come on, Bryan, let the sinner alone…” Nathan’s eyes widen and he shouts, “Oh no you fucking don’t!” causing the rest of the attendees to look around. “Don’t you dare villainize me. Don’t give me the sinner’s brand just because of what I do.” George Riley shakes his head and tries to walk away, but his wife is headstrong, which is another way of saying stupid. “You’re a monster. You ARE a sinner. You fed our innocent daughter mindgames, and then you killed her. She would do anything you said because she was blind and didn’t know any better.” “I NEVER SAW A BLIND GIRL!” Boswell shouts, and the entire family moves backwards from the force. “That’s what kills me about you! All of you only label her as blind! She’s your own daughter, but because she has a disability she’s less of a person to you. She wasn’t stupid, she wasn’t innocent, she wasn’t some doll that didn’t think for herself. “She was a person. A beautiful, wonderful person. And I made some mistakes, and I’m atoning for that in my head, but don’t you DARE come up to me on your high horse when you only used your daughter as a sign that you had bad times because you had to raise someone who couldn’t see.” Nathan looks at Bryan, who is now looking at the ground much like Nathan was a minute ago. “And that is the definition of selfish, Bryan. I’m out of here.” And as Nathan leaves the service and makes his way back to his car, back to the two day drive back to Dallas, he feels the last shred of Arianna leave him. He defended her well to her family, who never understood her. Now all that’s left is defending her to the man who brought about her death. And that one, for sure, will be a little less civil.
