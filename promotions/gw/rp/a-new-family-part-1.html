Date: 3.28:05 Broken down in bars and bathrooms All I did was what I had to Don't believe me when I tell you it's just what anyone would do -- Taking Back Sunday ---- I'm not crazy. I'm not insane. I'm not nuts. I'm sparked. I'm out for my own cause. I have to find peace of mind. I need to find a way to keep my father's dismembered head out of my life. He comes back three or four times a day. I can't do anything without being haunted by it. It's really starting to scare me. I think there's something wrong with me. What am i talking about? If more people suffered through what I've suffered through, they'd probably be having these fucked up nightmares. It's a normal thing. It will just take time to get over. I hope. In the past week, I haven't attacked anybody at random, I've managed to keep my mind without totally losing it. I've been rather quiet, but I've done nothing out of the ordinary. Deep down, nobody knows the hell I'm enduring. At night, I can't sleep. I'm afraid to. I'm afraid that I will continue to see some crazy shit. During the day, I'm a slave. I'm a slave to my desire not to appear crazy. I don't go out with any of my friends. I sit in my huge arm chair watching tv, sometimes staring through it instead of at it. Damn I love that chair. Other times I'll work on the Chevelle my father and I were to rebuild together. I'll take Damon out and lay him in the crib I made for him on top of the tool bench. It's not my father working with his son. Its my son watching his father work. Except the time where my father's floating head comes back and is giving me suggestions on how to best restore the classic car he loved so much. It was the floating head that told me to put a 5th generation Corvette rear differential on the Chevelle to increase traction. Without my ghostly mechanic, I would be completely alone in my garage. I seem to be alone a lot more lately. Cindy has been busy with a lot of things. She's been helping her parents move in to her Grandfather's old house. The house isn't really big, but the styling of it is extraordinary. The shutters and canopies are designed to look like a Venetian villa, very unusual for being in the suburbs of Detroit. Bobby has been on edge ever since his father was gunned down. Sound familiar to anybody? You'd think he and I would be able to relate on this, both losing a parent, or in my case both parents. Instead, my Father-in-Law and I have not said a word to each other in weeks. Just being in his presence anymore is creeping me out. He seems so calm and collected, almost as if he were invincible. I find that eerie because I've always been level headed, and lately I've been very messed up. No sleeping, conversing with my father's decapitated head, ignoring my wife. My wife. She's been so supportive through all of this. She stays patient with me, never yells when I space out, never screams at me when the floating head comes around, and has not told me to seek help. Another thing I find strange about my father. Damon isn't scared by him either. I know if I were a one year old, I would be scared to death of a talking head with no body. Not my son though, he finds nothing unusual about it. ---- I'm sitting in the passenger seat of Cindy's Lexus IS300. We're going down the on ramp to the highway, on our way to her parents' new villa-esque manor. Cindy has the stereo on at medium volume, with a cd playing. 'Leaving Through the Window' by Something Corporate. She sets the cruise control and pulls her foot off of the accelerator. She looks over at me and puts her hand on my thigh, then looks back at the road. I stare in the mirror to my side as my hand finds its way to hers. After about twenty silent minutes like that, we're pulling in the driveway of the new Cruse family estate. We get out and walk towards the doors, with their elaborate carvings and sculpted handles. After a knock, my Mother-in-Law, Marie Cruse, opens the door and hugs her daughter before taking her grandson's car seat. We step inside, and the Italian stylings are continued on the walls, curtains, and the highly detailed chandoleirs. Marie seats us on the couch in the living room. There is a fire crackling underneath the intricate mantle. Bobby comes in before long, his face pale and concerned. Before he can say anything to us, we hear tires squealing outside, and an engine rumbling its way down the street. Bobby rushes to the window, motioning for everybody else to stay seated. He turns back with a look of disgust. 'What is he doing here? I told him not to come back to this house until he was ready to accept his responsibility,' he says. 'Who are you talking about, Daddy?' asks my lovely wife. Before she gets an answer from her father, he opens the front door to reveal none other than his brother. Johnny Cruse. JC rushes past his brother and shoots a glance toward us. He spins around and points in his older brother's face. 'Damnit Bobby, I told you not to get them involved in this.' 'Involved in what? What is going on? Daddy, why did you not want Uncle Johnny here? He's your brother, this is his parents house too. And Johnny, what are you talking about?' Bobby sighs and bites his lip, before continuing. 'Cindy, Derek, what I'm about to tell you will change everything you thought you've known about our family.' I'm puzzled. My wife however... her emotions might as well be on a flashing neon sign. 'Bobby, don't do it. They don't need to know. Their lives have been affected enough, don't do it to them too,' says Johnny. 'Yes they do. It's Cindy's heritage, and Derek is part of the family. They both have to know the truth. Cindy, your grandfather wasn't mugged by a teenager wanting money, or by a junkie looking to pay for his next fix.' My wife and I stand there totally lost in thought, or shock, or some deep mixture of the two. To be continued.....
