The Chronicles Of Jesse Jamester: Part 3

29 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3-29-04 Prologue. Life could be tainted with so easily. From the straight hard facts that death is around every corner to the simple things such as nobody is what they appear to be... It’s odd isn’t it? As many times as a man could, he’d try and find a way to know his future, from either cheating, to the furthest point of must doing, fortunetellers. It’s not unusual for someone to witness death and life firsthand for its everywhere. Jesse was someone who had seen these two things and though he did, he didn’t let them stop them from going on as he has for so long. With the time winding down, only few can wonder what will happen when Jesse Jamester faces the best there is in the wrestling circuit, Chris Storm, Apocalypse, and Patti Anderson. All three unique in certain ways, and all three ready to do what they’ve trained for their whole wrestling career. Jesse showed his times of stress, followed with a period of rest, and now he has to feel it. The only thing left to do is to be confident in him and to be sure he has this match as his top priority. It’s do or die time for ‘the kid’ and we’ll see exactly how he copes with the state of mind he is in only half a day before the event. Needed Comfort. It’s always the person’s mind that will drive them insane first. Never does their body stop them from doing things before their mind. When a person believes they are too weak to do something or not in the right mood to do something most likely they won’t. Such a thing happened to Jesse. He believed he couldn’t do something and his body began to break down because of it. But with a slight breather from the world of wrestling he quickly realized he couldn’t give up or give in to the stress. And that’s when the real Jesse began to shine. Being that life was definitely a struggling game for the one Jesse Jamester, he’d always seemed to be dealt the same cards, the losing hand. Occasionally he’d gain some strength and move up in the chain, but never ever could this man that many believed in could win the big one. He always fell short, but now he’d began to feel it, yet needed that extra motivation. Who can comfort him at a time like this? Only he will know the answer to that question. Now only twelve hours before the event Jesse has time to get prepared for his big match. He has to be ready or else he wouldn’t win. Above all else adrenaline will be a key factor in winning and Jesse needs this key because without it he’ll never overcome his foes. Sitting in the park from the night before Jesse stares up at the sky as the dusk sky begins to fade. The days of pressure in the sky just as it was in Jesse began to let out, and rain commences. His face is covered by it as he just gazes at the stream of water that he’d been staring at earlier. His long brown hair a tangled mess as the water mixes with it, but he doesn’t care though. Jesse could sit there in the park till the next millennium for it was a time of peace for him, he had let out everything and now he was taking in the vicinity around him. Not sure of whether or not he was ready for the match he’d be going into, Jesse just wanted to be by himself where no one could bother him so he could think. Passing through his mind were many things at this point. Things that would begin to be thought out loud so that Jesse could hear them, and believe that this was all real. ’It’s bizarre. Twelve hours separate my dream, and me, only twelve hours. It’s… unbelievable. I’ve seen it all now, and I’ll fall to my death before I lose that match... Or will I?’ The downpour surrounded him like the headstones did at the cemetery. Yet they didn’t stop people from walking in and looking for anyone as such one particular person did in this very park. A male in his late years of age with a black hat, cowboy like, but round edged, and had a black trench coat on covering up mostly all of the mans body appeared walking up the sidewalk to where Jesse sat. He treaded over the water puddles at a typical pace not in a hurry, yet had some reason of being there in the park. The closer he got the more visual he became to Jesse, and then the man finally sat down next to Jesse. Not bothering Jesse but just far enough away to be another person in the park wanting to see the scenery. Jesse tried not to look at the man but his appearance seemed to be so familiar. It was as though the man was a piece of Jesse’s past that had been erased or shattered. The man stared straight ahead as his mouth opened and he spoke in a voice of calm and yet needed emphasize that it struck Jesse with astonishment, ’Hello son.’ The eyes of Jesse opened wide and his face froze as he turned his head so very slow at the man that he could not believe what he was hearing. The man’s face looked near absolutely the same as Jesse’s except the eyes and the nose, as well the wrinkles that were of obvious reasons that they were there. ’Wha- wha- what did you say?’ mumbled Jesse still not believing what he heard. The man stared back at Jesse staring at him as if it was a disappointment to have to repeat and stated, ’Hello Jesse, my son.’ Focusing in Jesse could see the resemblance but he was almost stunned to the extent that he couldn’t believe it was his father. ’Dad… is that really you?’ Jesse replied trying to make sure he wasn’t just seeing a ghost as he stared at the man in the same way that the man was staring at him. ’Of course it’s me. Who else would call you son?’ smiled Jesse’s father before he began to laugh. ’It’s… oh my, it’s you! I haven’t seen you in so long it was almost as if you were just a hallucination,’ boasted Jesse feeling sort of stupid for questioning his father. ’It’s okay Jess. I understand completely. It’s been a long time, and I have almost forgot what my son had looked like. That is until I saw you sitting here just gazing up at the sky with that silly expression your mother always put on. How could I ever forget such a funny face,’ gloated Jesse’s father now observing Jesse trying to take in the whole appearance of him. ’So why are you here dad? I mean how bizarre is it that we meet here in this park where it’s raining and NOBODY knows I’m here?’ Jesse said questioning his father on such an odd appearance as this one. ’Oh boy didn’t you’re foster parents teach you to ever just hush and let someone speak! Listen, I’m here because I know about your wrestling match. I also know that you’re having a hard time getting into the right state of mind,’ started Jesse’s father now more serious than before, ’I’ve got some things to say so just be quite and listen, because I’ve got limited time.’ Jesse nodded and sat still as his father stared at the stream with Jesse and began to speak. ’I know how you feel going into this. You’ve got all this pressure on you in the back of your mind saying you won’t win. Well, you won’t if you just let it go. Wrestling is in your blood. I myself was a wrestler, your grandfather was a wrestler, and even your grandfathers’ father was a wrestler back in his day. You were born as my son, and I tried to guide you out of the wrestling world, but to no avail you ended up where I least wanted you. It’s just as if this is where you were meant to be, and fate can’t be changed,’ claimed the man, ’see here. You could go a whole lifetime worrying about what will happen if you don’t let it go. So this pressure you feel, replace it with the power of what you know you can do and what is positive. No matter what that voice says, you have to think positive.’ Turning his head to Jesse he was sure he had to make his son believe or else Jesse would never be able to accomplish his dreams. ’In that ring don’t think of what you have to do to not lose, but think of what you must do to win. When you play not to lose, you end up losing because you’re not there to win. This is what you’ve worked for Jess, this is your life, and I respect that. So do whatever it takes, whether it be using a cheap shot, to grabbing a woman by her hair and throwing her into the crowd, just make sure you walk away with the win. It’s the only way you’ll guarantee yourself this win,’ continues Jesse’s father as he extends his arm and lies it on Jesse’s shoulder making sure he is paying attention. Turning Jesse to face him he look into Jesse’s eyes and stares at him with a passion that shows he loves him as a son and wants him to achieve his dream, ’I want you do win this for you. Don’t do it for me, do it for yourself, and you’ll make me happy.’ With that he pats Jesse on the back and the two stare at the stream for a bit. The rain still pouring down as it had been for the past half an hour, yet the two didn’t care, for this was a perfect day to them both. Turning to say something to his father, Jesse sees nobody. He sees only the coat and the hat that his father wore that now lay on bench beside him. Looking down at them he is confused and thinks could it just have been an angel, or was it real? Shaking his head he stays focused with the words of his father running through his mind as he gets up and grabs the black trench coat and throws it over his back pulling it tightly on as he buttons it up. Picking up the hat he placed it over his damp head. It fit Jesse quite well, almost perfectly. He walked down the same sidewalk that he saw his father walk up fading away as he turns past the corner. Fading with the rain and trees he departs from the park with confidence building as he got into his cab that’d been waiting. The Gates to Glory. The sidewalk was the same as the park, a huge puddle of water. The boots of Jesse take a step out of the cab and plant firmly on the sidewalk before taking two steps forward and his head glances up at the building. It was the arena Jesse would be wrestling in only a few hours. With his bag over his shoulder and his eyes glaring up at the building with a smirk on his face he looked like a man on a mission. While his eyes gazed up he turned to the billboard that stated, ’PTC Global Title Match, Chris Storm (Champion) versus Jesse Jamester versus Apocalypse versus Patti Anderson,’ and an even larger smile prevail over his face. He was contented that he’d gotten his shot, and now it was time to prove it once and for all that he was ready to take home the gold, and win the match that many doubted he could. ’It feels like home,’ stated Jesse still smiling as he glanced around, ’feels just like home…
