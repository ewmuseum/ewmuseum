Genesis

9 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/9/03 Nantucket, Massachusetts, March 2002. “Fuck you, man.” A Caucasian male with light brown hair staggered from the convenience store with a brown paper sack in hand. The top of a long neck bottle protrudes from inside the bag and from the demeanor of this slovenly dressed man; we can only assume that he’s had more than just this one. Although sloppy, he’s not a poorly dressed man; his wardrobe consists of a black leather jacket with a blue dress shirt underneath, pulled from his dress pants. A tie hangs about his neck, disheveled and loose. The only piece of attire on his body that is not completely lacking in cleanliness is his black Armani wingtips. He staggers towards the gasoline pumps, barely able to stand on his own two feet without swaying to and fro. Another man, also Caucasian but with black, charcoal colored, hair follows behind him. He’s dressed similarly in dress slacks, Armani wingtips, and a white dress shirt. His tie is neat and he’s obviously fairly sober. A black limo waits in the parking lot, a man in a chauffeur’s outfit stands outside of the vehicle, watching the two men. The sober man speaks aloud to the other less-sober individual. “Why don’t you get in the car and go home, Tyler” You’ll feel better after a good nights rest. In the morning, we can talk through all of this.” The drunken man turns, hurling the sack and bottle towards the head of the other, barely missing him but not due to aiming, due to lack of sobriety. He staggers around, walking away from both the sober man and the limo and heads into the street, screaming at the top of his lungs. “Go fuck yourself! What kind of brother are you, man” You think ?” The voice trails off as his speech slurs. He stumbles and lands on his hands and knees, attempting to vomit on the asphalt. His heaves are heard as he attempts to relieve his system of some of its alcohol. The second man, now partially identified as the first’s brother, approaches and kneels beside him and places his hand on his shoulder. “You’ll feel better in the morning, Tyler. Please, just come home?” “Why can’t you just ? wheeze ? leave me alone, Scott” I don’t want to fucking come home. Fuck home. Fuck you. Fuck daddy! Fuck all of it.” The second grows angry, losing the patience he had earlier been displaying over his obviously disoriented brother. He shoves him over, the drunkard already unable to sustain his balance but now with this new display of anger, now unable to even kneel on the pavement. “Who do you think you are” Dad does everything for you! He’s taken care of you for years! You go out, you party, sleep with any girl you want. You have never worked a damned day in your life! And you disgrace the Pierce name every fucking time you leave the house! You spend the money but you show him no respect! You fucking bum! I can’t sit back and take this anymore. You don’t deserve to be called a Pierce!” With this the man stands and kicks him once in the gut, causing the drunkard, now known as Tyler Pierce, to buckle. The sober man, Tyler’s brother, straightens his jacket and turns walking back towards the limo. The chauffer quickly side steps him and opens the back door of the limousine. “Your door, Mr. Pierce.” As the limousine pulls away, Tyler Pierce lies on the asphalt staring up at the stars. He clenches his eyes, tears welling up at the crease of the lid and his cheek, creating small puddles of salty water atop his eyelids. The miniature puddles grow until streams flow down his cheeks to disappear on the pavement on which he lies. His brother’s words have struck him deeply and his heart yearns now for comfort, the comfort once offered but now taken away. “Scott” what have I done? Please, come back?” Boston, Massachusetts, April 2003. “Right this way, Mr. Pierce.” The same man from before, now sober, steps into the lobby of a large building in downtown Boston; he’s dressed in a blue business suit with a white dress shirt and blue tie. His hair is in place and neatly arranged; a complete change from the last time this man was seen. He quickly runs his fingers back through his hair and removes a pair of oval mirrored shades from his handsome face, tucking the sunglasses into an inside jacket pocket. The man leading the way is also dressed in a suit but by his demeanor and subservient mannerisms, it’s obvious that he’s of a lower status. Tyler follows the man quickly through the lobby and into an elevator. They both turn, facing the lobby again as the doors close them off from the other visible patrons. Moments later, the doors reopen but now on the top floor of this fifteen story building. The two men, with Tyler Pierce tailing just slightly behind, make their way around a maze of corners until reaching a large door with a secretary seated a few feet from it. She smiles towards the unidentified man and Tyler and presses a button, opening the doors automatically. The two men then walk in with the John Doe announcing Tyler once inside. “Mr. Pierce, your son is here.” John Doe gestures towards Tyler as the large chair placed behind a large oak desk turns towards the two. Once we see the third man, Mr. Pierce, a large man whose hair that remains is silver although for the most part, his receding hairline has won out with time. The assistant now takes his leave as Tyler approaches the large desk. The older Pierce does not look particularly pleased to see his son although, greets him with a handshake over the desk nonetheless. “What brings you back, Tyler?” “Father, I’ve been doing a lot of thinking. I’m spent the last year getting my life straightened out. I know I have been distant but that’s what I needed. That’s what the family needed also. I’m still the same guy. I will always love a party and I will always be what I am, but I don’t want to disgrace the family anymore. I’ve decided that if I’m ever going to respect myself, I have to do it on my own.” The father stares at his son, examining the situation and the words that were just spoken to him. He grimaces and takes a seat, his eyes turning to his desk before returning to the son before him. “Those are strong words, Tyler. Those are words that can’t be thrown around lightly and they can’t be ignored. If you want to do this, you’re going to do it right or not at all. Now you decide right here and now, young man, what is your decision” Are you going to be a man or a mouse?” The question had been laid before Tyler before and he had always buckled in his execution of becoming a man. That had always been his answer, “I will be a man, sir.” Or father, or whatever he called him at the time. Tyler had thought of this question however and this time, he knew what to say to his father. “Dad, I’ve lived on your work for years. I love you for everything you’ve done for me and I always will. But if I’m ever going to respect myself and become the man I wish to be, then I not only need to this but I have to do this. I’m moving to Texas, Dad. I’m not going to be a mouse any longer. Nor will I be just any man; I’m going to be a Pierce.” A smile crossed the lips of the old man. His son had made him proud. Dallas, Texas, May 2003. A dust ridden old gym is the location. Only three or four men are inside, three that we’ve never seen before and one that we have: Tyler Pierce stands off to the side in a pair of Adidas black and white pants, boots, and no shirt. His hands have been wrapped in white tape and from the looks of him he appears to have been roughing it lately. His five o’clock shadow looks more like a small dense forest. His hair has grown out slightly and it’s obvious that he’s been intentionally going without certain amenities. He slams fist after fist into the punching bag, sending it reeling and shaking. He continues throwing punches, showing no signs of needing a rest. As a final movement, he jumps and sends his heel against the bag, causing it to jerk harder than before. He takes a step back, brushing a few strands of light brown hair from his forehead. His thoughts begin to pour forth as if a switch had suddenly been flipped on. “They’re going to be proud of me. If it kills me, they’re going to be proud of me. Maybe I can’t change who I am or what I’ve done but I can change what they think. I can and will prove that I’m deserving of the Pierce name. All I have to do now is bust my ass to get to the top.” Pierce drops to the mats below and begins doing single handed push ups, his eyes clenched shut as he focuses. The thoughts seem to stop at the moment as beads of sweat begin to glisten on his forehead. Another man steps into view but only from the waist down. He stands beside Pierce wearing black slacks and black dress boots accented in silver. Pierce doesn’t seem to notice him at first, his eyes remaining closed as he continues to work. Suddenly, he speaks aloud to the man: “Can I help you” I’m trying to work here, bud.” The man says nothing but places an envelope atop Pierce’s duffle bag and begins making his way towards the exit. Tyler rolls over and sits up, gazing at the back of the man as he lifts the envelope up, gazing at the writing on the front: “To Tyler Pierce II, Esquire” Bah, another businessman trying to weasel towards my father through me. Esquire? That’s rather unusual. “Damned Texan tycoons, probably trying to get on my good side.” He tosses the letter towards his bag again and stands, stretching his arms high into the air. “I don’t have time for Pierce Foundation business right now. Too much work to be done.” Tyler quickly opens his duffle bag and shoves the unopened letter inside, not intending to even open it. He grabs a white shirt quickly and pulls it on before snapping a watch about his wrist. “Hmm, I’m going to be a little late.” Tyler grabs his things and begins towards the gym exit. As he steps outside, a limo with heavily tinted windows pulls off down the street. Pierce gives a smug response and shakes his head before walking around the building and to his car, a cherry red 2002 Corvette. With a quick gesture, the doors unlock and he pulls the handle, opening the car. He tosses his bag inside and climbs in, closing the door. Seconds later, the convertible top lowers and the car begins moving out of the parking lot. Dallas, Texas, May 2003. The sound of squeaking can be heard coming from a closed door at the end of a hallway. The howls and moans of a woman echoing beneath the cracks as the room comes into view; the walls are somewhat old and uncared for as well as the embellishments, all toppling and rusting. The floor is an old wood, last varnished a dozen or so years before this night. The squeaks come to a stop as do the moans. Heavy breathing can now be heard in substitution. A woman speaks between gasps for air, “Tyler, that was” “She’s cut off as the sound of a phone ringing is heard and then foot steps. The ringing ensues and now a voice filters in, it’s the voice of Tyler Pierce. “Yeah yeah yeah, I’m coming!” A moment passes and the ringing stops. “Yeah, what is it?” The view of the door remains the same but the sounds are as if in the middle of the action. “I’ve been booked, huh” Against who?” Mumbling is heard as the person on the other side of the phone speaks. “Chaz Bryant” Sounds promising. Looks like I finally get to kick someone’s ass.” There’s another pause filled with the muttering of the person on the other end before Tyler once again speaks. “No, I don’t need anything. I can beat this and any ass you guys want to throw at me. No challenge at all, Creed. Stop bothering me! I’m busy.” The mumbling continues again and Tyler groans himself. “FINE! I’ll be at the arena in thirty.” There’s another pause as the sound of a phone clicking on the receiver is heard. More footsteps, and then one simple line, “OK, Teresa, get out. I’ve got to get ready.” There’s a pause as the shock of the statement hits home before, “WHAT!”” A female voice is now heard, shouting. “You think you can take me out to dinner, fuck me, then throw me out on the street” Who the hell do you think you are!”” There’s a moment of silence before his response is heard. “I’m the best you’ve ever had. I’m Tyler Pierce. Now get out” and be back here in four hours.” The statement is blunt and completely asinine. There’s another pause before the door opens, a blonde beauty, perfectly tanned and endowed steps from the apartment. Once out the door, the door closes against her back and she leans against it, quickly pulling a shirt on over the towel wrapped around her upper body, already wearing a pair of jeans beneath that. She drops the towel and leans back against the door and smiles; she glances at the door and speaks aloud “See you in four hours, Tyler!” After this, the scene fades to black. Dallas Texas, Later that day, May 2003. The Global Arena stands before us in all her glory. Legends have came and fought here, many have failed to meet the bar and some have hurdled it with the greatest of ease: Salaok and Quartz, Roz Blayze, Jean Claude Dafoe, Brian Welch, Tragic Hero, Silent Wind, and many more once met fist to fist in this ring. Now, it was Tyler’s turn. Tyler wasn’t about to let himself fail. Our scene fades to black as Tyler, now shaven and cleaned up, in a pair of jeans and a black t-shirt stands above the ring, gazing down into it with awe. Another figure makes its way towards him from behind but before they are revealed, the scene fades to black.
