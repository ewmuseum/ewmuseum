Date: Week of 5/5/2005 Last minute flights caused all sorts of headaches. Not wanting to keep draining the money, private planes were becoming less of an option. He had plenty of money, and he could use corporate money if he needed to, but he didn’t feel right about it anymore. Airlines always seemed to overbook, and then you added in the customers trying to cram on through standby because meetings ended early and you were in trouble. So the registration put him on standby status for First Class, and that had become a Business Class ticket if he wanted to go anytime soon. So the ticket went into the gate attendant’s hand as he walked down in Group One for boarding the American Airlines flight to New York from Dallas. Pretentiousness was not the motivation behind his distaste. Instead, he was simply being practical. The 737 aircraft was a nice sized aircraft, but the three seats on each side of the aisle were not roomy. He was six feet eight inches tall and a wall of three hundred and twenty eight pounds of muscle. No matter what he did, his shoulder and leg hung out into the aisle. The person seated next to him would not be comfortable. Notoriety was another issue. Jake would be recognized, and he personally was not a happy flier under the best of circumstances. Normally, paperwork or the computer would distract him. However, in Business Class he would never be able to access his computer. So he would try and sleep without offending anyone who might be a fan by ignoring them. Luggage was easy as Jake just had the carry-on. He had packed some clothes along with one of the journals for his reading. Study was never his favorite thing to do in school, but now he found himself a ravenous beast for the words of the personalities. His brother had become a very strange, almost unholy trinity. Waiting in line, there were several businessmen returning to New York City. They seemed in normal moods for businessmen, tired and happy to be going home. Luckily, Jake had swung a seat in the second row of the back on the aisle. He waited for his turn, and threw his bag into the overhead compartment wheels first. Jake took his seat, but didn’t fasten the seatbelt. There was no point’he would be letting people into the seats beside him. Quickly, a very small woman no more than five feet came up. She had the window seat. If he was lucky then someone the same size would sit next to him. Then the space would be a little better. He saw him. The man was large, two hundred and fifty pounds or so and over six feet tall. The weight of inevitability drug him down, and he guessed his luck had simply left him forever. No doubt, the man stopped in front of him. “I’ve got the middle.” He said as he threw his bag overhead. Jake simply nodded. He got up, and let the man sit down in the middle seat. He returned, and their arms and shoulders collided jockeying for position. Jake turned to the man. “This is gonna suck.” He nodded, “Yeah.” The small woman spoke, a trace of English accent coming out with the words. “Perhaps one of you would take the window seat and I can sit between you?” “That would be very polite of you.” Jake smiled. “I’ll sit in the window for the space.” “Okay.” The man agreed. They switched out quickly. The push of the rounded wall of the plane was worth the extra space. He buckled up, secure and happy. “You are awfully big young man.” The woman said. She was likely in her early fifties. “Yes ma’am. I’m a professional wrestler and shoot fighter, so size is a good thing.” He explained. “I bet it is.” Jake put a baseball cap over his head. He hunkered down, content to go unnoticed if possible. The sleep came at once. He needed the rest, and the flight would go bye quickly this way. . . . Landing at LaGuardia was always a fun experience. The landing was sharp, and it always looked like you might hit the water if you were looking out the window. The pilot was experienced though, and Jake barely woke up when they touched down. He shook it off and went right back to sleep. The plane taxied to the terminal, and he finally woke up when the flight crew asked everyone to remain seated with their seatbelts fastened until the captain turned the seatbelt sign off. Like any experienced traveler, Jake ignored the instruction and unbuckled along with half the plane. They waited until the door was opened and the light was turned off. Getting out was nicer since he was near the front of the plane. LaGuardia always felt old to Jake. Of course, it was old. The airport property had originally been an amusement park. The city broke ground in 1937 building the airport that would remain a New York City fixture. What always struck him, was when you left the terminal past the security station, you took stairs down to the transportation and baggage level. There was a single elevator as well, but no escalators. The airport construction costs to truly modernize were monstrous, and so not done. A man in a black suit stood with his name on a small, dry erase board.
