Ice vs. The Monster....

17 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/17/04 Warning: This Roleplay contains, what some might consider, unusually offensive content. While it's not my habit to apologize for any creative choices I make in my work, I do want to emphasize that this content is meant to help define a new character I am introducing. It is in no way meant to be hurtful to anyone. You'll know what I'm talking about when you see it. Please read it in the spirit in which it was written. A creative choice meant to illustrate the bigotry of a character in my work. The gunshots rang in Train's ears.... It surprised him how the explosion of gunpowder from the barrel of the gun even managed to drown out the stiff Chicago wind. Even as it howeled like a banshee from ancient mythology, the wind could not overshadow the explosion Train felt in his hands as his finger angrily squeezed the trigger. Train could only shake his head and chuckle a bit. No matter how long ago it was...as Train stood at the mouth of the raked alley, lined with trash cans overflowing with garbage, loose paper whipping to and fro in the viscious wind, Train could only feel one thing. He could only feel like he was there again. There on that night. That night where Train...not Anthony 'Tony' James, but Train had been born. Even though his hands were warming themselves in the pockets of his leather trench coat, the wind blowing it like a cape, Train could still almost feel the gun in his hands. Remember what it was like as the bullets had left the chamber and slammed violently into their target. The rage, the likes of which, he had never felt before th night he caught his first body. The night he killed a man. The chuckle that bubbled up from the inside was a result of the irony. Train had known another man named Ice years before his match on the next Evolution was even thought of being scheduled. That's why he was back in the place. Ever since he heard about the match being booked this alley had been calling him. Beckoning him to return. The Ice he knew then and the Ice he would face now had a lot in common. Not the least of which was their reputations. Of being cold. Of being heartless. Of being monsters. Now, for the sake of he future he had to come back here. To remind himself that he knew how to slay monsters...because he was a monster. Wrestling was a funny business. Because all the competitors have big muscles and are physically gifted they somehow get it into their heads that, because the can string a few punches and kicks together that they should be feared. Not respected...but feared. Ice...GW's world champion was one of those. Walk around with big muscles and a nasty look on your face and suddenly you're the Devil's own. Like hell. The other Ice, the one Train had met in the streets of Chicago...he had feared him a hell of a lot more than this new one. This one with the nifty tights and bad attitude. When Tony had tangled with the old Ice he'd feared for his life. After Train finished with him, they had found his bloody corpse rotting not 20 feet from where Train stood. Train had known since that night he'd watched the life drain out of the old Ice and trickle down a sewer grate that he had become a monster. Not because he'd pulled the trigger. The motherfucker had deserved it. He'd taken too much from Train. He'd taken too many people Train cared about from him. No...it was the fact that as he watched the man die...deep in his heart...he'd loved it. It's not that Train didn't have every reason to want him dead. His brother Leon, Tony's best friend...Tony's rolemodel after their father had died, died himself from a bullet put in him from Ice. Or at least that is what he and about everyone suspected, but could never really prove. Leon and Ice ran with the same crew back in the day. Something the much younger Tony didn't really comprehend back when Leon had introduced him to Ice. Leon had just said he was a friend. A buddy...a pal...a bastard that stabbed him in the back when Leon climbed through the ranks up to a captain's position on the streets. Ice was next in line and he didn't want to wait on a promotion. No one had seen Ice pull the trigger so he was too far up the ladder to just take out on mere suspicion. However, he was the only one with a real motive. There had been a long standing truce with all the other crews in the city at the time. A truce that Leon had helped negotiate. Even cats from across town regarded Tony's brother as a good guy. No one had anything to gain by his death. But, in the end...with no witness that could call out the trigger man neither the police or the crew leaders couldn't just snatch him up. But, Biggie, Tony's neighbor who ran with Leon's crew felt it and so had Tony. He knew from the second he'd layed eyes Ice he wasn't to be trusted. But what could he have done then? Simple...nothing. Hell, he was just a kid. He didn't even really understand why Leon had sooooo many 'friend', and, being older now, he knew that was how Leon had wanted it. He never wanted his little brother to have to do the things he did. He never wanted his little brother to get the same harsh reaction from their mother that he had gotten. There mom was a stand straight and tall, church going woman. Not all appreciative of a gang banger in the family. But it was life and Leon did what he felt he needed to. When Leon died it crushed their mother. And what happened to her...having to deal with the death of her child... Back then the symptoms of clinical depression wasn't tossed about on every morning and pop psychology talk show. Tony had no clue why his mother could do nothing but spend half the day asleep and the other half in tears. But he did know one thing. He knew not to answer the phone. The bill collectors never had anything nice to say. He knew to look through the peep hole because the building super was a broken record with his demands for rent money. Train also knew with a father dead and a brother the same he was the man of the house. He knew a baby sister and a mother who could barely get out of the bed wasnt' going to provide what the family needed. He also knew if he didn't put food on the table no one else would. But Leon was gone now. He knew that with Leon gone he could talk Biggie into putting him down on the set to make some cash. And that's just what he did. He probably should have regretted the decision, but how could he? If he hadn't done it, he wouldnt' be on a collision course with the world heavyweight champion at Evolution and he wouldn't be on the fast track to becoming the GW Platinum Champion at Wrestlecade. Because if he hadn't, he likely never would have met his mentor Isaiah. If everything happens for a reason then his decision to hit the streets to support his family was the right one. It had given him the chance to be great. Of course it had cost Isaiah his life. Train's feet slowly trudged deeper into the abyss of the dark alley, bits of discarded paper and plastic crunching softly beneath his boots as the wind continued whip around violently. As he slowly trudged deeper into the darkness of the past he became consumed by it for a moment. The pure aggressiveness and venom that had emerged the night he chased Ice into this alley had returned. Perhaps it was because he was facing another Ice... Maybe because Tony had returned to the spot where Train was born... whatever the reason, the visciousness that was Train was returning to him like it handn't in a long time. Train hadn't gone to sleep since the night Tony had turned into him, but he wasn't as on point as he was in this place. That's why he needed to be here. He needed Train to be reenergized. He needed the true monster to return full force in order to face a man who deemed himself one. The echoes of the past were ringing so loudly in Train's ears he didn't even hear the footsteps behind him. He was so busy reliving the night he earned his street name he didn't feel the return of an old nemesis. The other man's beige, JC Penny brand rain coat also billowed in the wind as he slowly and methodicly creeped up behind. The loud red tie and the off the rack suit furled in the breeze as well. His face was old and weathered, his hairline not so subtley beating a path of retreat, as the gray marched boldly forward, even in his thick mustache. Everything about the figure trodding out of the darkness seemed a little low rent. All except the jewelry clipped to his belt. All except the Chicago Police Department Detective's sheild that glowed brightly beneath the mixture of moon and street light. 'Back to the scene of the crime, huh?' inquires the detective, only a few words of it escaping his lips before Train whips around to meet him. Their eyes meet and there is an instant recognition. An instant hatred. 'Well, well, well...' Train says, his face instantly contorting into something half way between a smirk and a sneer. 'Detective Johns. Long time no see. Guess that's why I haven't had a headache in such a long time.' 'Awww...I'm hurt Train,' Det. Johns quickly responds, feigning an emotional sting. 'I especially expected better of the big wrestling superstar. I mean, I was hoping for a more civilized greeting after all we've been through together ol' buddy, ol' pal.' 'What the fuck did we go through together Johns? Seems to me I was the only one of us getting harrased.' 'Just doing my job. I mean, I'm a cop, you're a crook.' 'Oh really? Seems to me if I was such a crook you would've been able to prove it at least once.' Train exposes a ching in the detective's armor that seems to be composed of a mixture of smugness and bravado. His irritation at an apparent inability to back up any of his assumptions of Train's criminal life creates the hint of a scowl. But the lapse is only momentary. His confidence seems to return quickly, accompanied by a smirk. 'Yeah... Yeah you would think that wouldn't you?' 'Uh-huh.... What do you want Johns?' 'Oh.... Just wanted to talk. Like I said, it's been such a long time man. I mean, I had a feeling I'd be able to find you here.' 'How you figure that?' 'Because... You know I been following your career while you were gone...just like I did when you were here. Yeah.... GW's newest superstar. The Train. hahaha... Yupper... You know I been telling everybody in the precinct about us being such good buddies. Oh yeah... Me the old cop, knowing you...the bonafide celebrity. hahahaha... Woooo boy... I got to tell ya, this country never ceases to amaze me. I don't care how much all you niggers, chinks, wetbacks, spics, towel heads and gas pumpers complain about it... You all get your fair shot. I mean look at you! Just look at ya'.... One day you're a lying, theiving, murdering drug dealer... The next you're a glorified superstar! A God damn role model....' 'One? I ain't never done no pissing and moaning about the man or the system. Fuck 'em both. If I need something I'ma go out and get it. And, as you well know, ain't neither one of 'em gonna be able to stop me. Two? I ain't never claimed to be nobody's role model.' 'hahaha... Please...save me the song and dance Mr. Barkley.' 'Whatever. Three? You still ain't answered my question. Why the fuck are here?' 'Hey, don't be so touchy. Like I said, I just wanted to talk and I figured I'd find you here. I told you, I've been following your little career my friend. You're doing big things nowadays, going up against the heavyweight champion, right? Yeah....what was his name again? Ohhhh...I remember... Ice. Yeah...there's a name, huh? A real blast from the past, isn't it there Train?' 'Whatever do you mean detective?' 'I mean you're a lucky bastard. You should be in jail, using up your death row appeals my friend. I mean I had you dead to rights. I had three witnesses. All of them put you at the scene a block away. All of them saw you roll over the curb with your overgrown buddy. They all saw you chasing Ice down the street and around the block. I mean hell, you slammed through that whole line of people waiting to get into the club that used to be there. You even threw that one guy through the plate glass window trying to get the son of a bitch. From what I understand, that's how you got the name....Train. I mean you just ran those people down to get to him. And I'll admit it. I underestimated you. I had my witness and I didn't bother having anyone sitting on them. I thought it was a slam dunk. I'm still not sure who you sons of bitches paid off to find out who they were, but then again I never figured you had the brains to try to find out. Or the ability. Little did I know you boys were trying to turn that pack of street trash you ran with into an actual organization. I had no clue that was the beginings of the Southside Syndicate. Next thing I know I got three witnesses recanting three afidavits. What can you do, right? Anyway... I just figured you'd come back to this spot. I figured you'd need the ego boost.' 'Mmhmmm... You know I've always thought you was a funny cat man. I mean you constantly letting any ol' bullshit come out yo' mouth, but you can't never seem to pull any evidence out yo' ass.' 'hahaha... Yeah, you're a funny guy too. But you're right though. I never could nail your ass to the wall, could I?' 'Nope.' 'Yeah...I guess it's just lucky that I get anothe crack at it.' 'Excuse me?' 'Oh yeah, didn't I tell you? Yeah, I got a call from the Dallas office of the FBI. Yeah seems you've picked up some pretty interesting running boys since you...'left the streets'. The Greenisos....wow. You know I laughed when I saw you hook up with this M.O.B. thing ing GW. I figured it was just some cheap marketing scheme. Oh, but that's till the guys from the FBI and the Dallas PD gave me the low down on these two. These two are legitimate heavy hitters. And now, since you and I have such a history they've asked me to consult on the special task force they've formed to investigate 'criminal influences' in Global Wrestling.' Train face crunches into a grimace as his head dips, swinging back and forth in a display of disgust and disbelief. 'Yeah... Isnt' it great?' Detective Johns inquires, stepping into Tony's face, making no attempt to hide his animosity. 'I get another chance to nail your black ass to the fucking wall.' There is a pause in the conversation, their eyes locked in a combat of wills as the wind continues to howl. 'Yeah,' Train begins, his cold eyes nearly peering into the detective's soul. 'But don't the shit twisted. You seem to think this is some whole new begining type shit. But I'm here to tell you...you being on my case again? It ain't nothing new. You understand? Nothing's changed. You gonna keep chasing, but I'm still gonna be the gingerbread man. You still can't catch me. Fuck you. Fuck the FBI. Fuck the Dallas PD. Fuck Ice.' 'Which one tough guy?' Train brushes roughly past him and out of the alley, all the determination he was looking for now reignited. The monster shaken from his slumber. 'Both of 'em.' Train disappears around the corner as the wind continues to screech and Detective Johns can do nothing, but stew as the scene fades to black.
