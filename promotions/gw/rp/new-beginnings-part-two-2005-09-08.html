New Beginnings (Part Two)

8 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09.08.05 The tragedy that had struck Ken's life in recent weeks had been uncanny and obscene to say the least. Shortly after the funeral of his dear friend, Anna G, he was faced with the unearthing fact that most of the Gulf Coast, which he used to call home, no longer existed. The only calming effect that he'd gotten was that his numerous friends and family that lived in New Orleans, Biloxi, and Mobile, had all been heard from and were safe. However, the damage was sufficient and the devastation harsh. What he'd been allowed to see of Louisiana and Mississippi was horrific. The Creole Coast was nearly completely under water and fires were breaking out due to riots, with black smoke billowing into the sky towards the helicopter was flying over in. It looked like something out of an apocalyptic blockbuster motion picture and, looking down over it, he was curious how the projected ten thousand deaths in The Big Easy alone wasn't higher. Somehow, in his mind, no amount of money, shelter, or food could salvage anything that had been lost. People's entire lives had been ripped away from them in one swift swoop and millions of dollars didn't seem to be the type of thing that could ever give that back. His friend, Leslie, had lost it all... her house, her possessions, and her memories. However, the fact that she was still with her family and that they were all safe kept her moving. She was strong, Leslie... stronger than Ken could have ever hoped to of been. It was amazing seeing a mother shine as a beacon of light to the husband and two children that are lost in the gloom of fear and desperation. Still, she held her head high, put a determined smile on her face, and took great strides forward into the unknown of life. She knew that there was very little, if anything for them to go back to, but her optimism of a new day kept her moving onward. Her home in New Orleans was completely underwater and she'd been unable to salvage much of anything when they allowed them back into the city briefly on Monday. Now, as it stood, they wouldn't be allowed back for at least three months, if not longer. The contamination and toxicity of the water alone was enough to keep them away for a very long time. Ken had offered her and her family a place to stay in his home, but she declined. Leslie was smart. She'd saved up more than enough money to build a new home, much less to accommodate them for the time being. Her husband had done the same and Ken found some solace in the fact that they would be okay. As his eyes peered back down over the damage to what used to be the coast of Mississippi, he caught sight of nothing but rubble and wreckage. Stray boards and uprooted trees were laying scattered amongst the debris as volunteers worked diligantly to clean up what they could. It was at this time that he realized that his friend John's life had been narrowly spared, but there was absolutely nothing left standing for him to return to. John, who'd evacuated to Tampa, Florida, had moved to Biloxi a little less than a year ago for a sportscasting position with ESPN Radio, moving his mother down near to him shortly thereafter. His house was right on the gulf, which Ken had loved when he visited him earlier in the year for a bit of gambling and to go to Mardi Gras, something else that didn't look likely in the near future. As Ken peered down at the Casino Royale, which was now on the opposite side of the street than it's original foundation, he realized that he'd had a lot of good times in Biloxi, as well. Unfortunantly, now it was nothing more than a wasteland. An area of sorrow and depression was all he could see out of the chopper's window. Tears began to fill his eyes. 'Over there, sir?' shouted the pilot over the headset. Ken looked through the opposite window to see a small tent city erected in a nearby clearing and nodded to the pilot, who responded with a thumbs up before taking the helicopter down about fifty-five miles inland. As it landed, Ken opened the doors and jumped out, meeting Ceci with a hug as the chopper lifted back into the air. 'You ready to go,' he asked her with a forced smile on his face. 'Just waiting for you,' she responded with a true one on her's. With her guitar in hand, the two began to make their ways towards the center of the new village as people emerged from the tents. What was there wasn't much, about fifty tents and cots, some fresh water, and some food... but it was enough to get those that were there through the night, before they were transferred somewhere more secure. Ceci sat on a chair that had been placed in a cleared area and began to strum her guitar. Soon she would be playing, bringing smiles to those faces that had seen nothing but sorrow for the past two weeks. It was an amazing thing to see... how joy could come across the face of somebody who had nothing left was a wonder to behold. But they saw hope... they saw a future... they saw a new beginning.
