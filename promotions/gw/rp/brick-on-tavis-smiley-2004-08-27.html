Brick on Tavis Smiley

27 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/27/04 (Fade in on a close-up of Tavis Smiley.) Tavis: Born Jamal Randall in Brooklyn, NY, Brick has been, for almost five years, one of the most popular superstars in Global Wrestling. He has held many of the company's titles, including the World title. (Dissolve to a montage of Brick's career. Dissolve again to Brick and Tavis sitting in loveseats, facing each other.) Tavis: Brick, welcome to the show. Brick: Thanks for having me. Tavis: How have you been? Brick: I've been good. Tavis: First, let's talk about the name Brick. How did you come up with the name? Brick: Yeah, that name comes from my childhood. I got it from my uncles because I was a heavy baby. I weighed a little over nine pounds. Tavis: That's not too heavy. Brick: Yeah, tell my mother that. (Tavis laughs.) Tavis: Now, you were with Global Wrestling since pretty much the beginning when it used to be the Global Wrestling Federation. Brick: Yeah, when I came in, Global Wrestling originally came from two promotions: Unlimited Class Wrestling Federation and Freedom Wrestling League. There were cats there that already had built a history. True Evil, Justin Marks, Jonathan Davis, the Youngbloods, Skee, the Welch brothers, Bobby Brawler, Moneytime. I came in there without any history of my own and looking to build one. Tavis: And you've built a history, alright, brother. United States champion... Brick: Four times. That's a record that's never been broken. Tavis: Tag team champion. Platinum champion. Explain to me what the Platinum title is. Brick: That's the 3rd highest title you can get in Global Wrestling. Tavis: International champion. World champion. You've just about done it all. Brick: I've been blessed. There's no other way to say it. I've been blessed. That, in spite of all I've gone through, I've managed to stay where I am. Tavis: Now, not only are you one of the most decorated, you're also one of the most popular as I said before this interview began. Brick: I do. As an athlete and as an entertainer, I understand the need for positive role models, especially for Black youth. I try to be as good a role model as I possibly can. I'm not perfect, and I don't pretend to be. I mean, I don't walk on water and I don't turn it to wine, either. Of course, if I did turn water into wine, I'd probably have a different fanbase. (Both laugh.) Tavis: You've also been considered something of a pioneer in Global Wrestling. First African American to hold many of the titles that you've held. (Dissolve to footage from the World Title match at Whiplash, June 30, 2003. Brick is kneeling on the floor of the Pit, cradling the World Championship belt close to him, tears in his eyes.) Brick: Winning the World title was a definite highlight in my career. That was a moment I had worked four years for. All the blood, the sweat, the tears, and the pain, it all paid off. (Dissolve back to Brick. Cut to Tavis and then back to Brick.) Brick: As far as me being a pioneer, I don't like to brag, but I think I made some headway for some of the other African American The next person to hold the World title was Deacon Holmes, another African American. So you had two Black superstars hold the belt consecutively. That says a lot. Tavis: One of the most talked about matches of your career happened this past February at High Octane. You defended the Platinum title against a man named Mark Makonie, possibly the most controversial wrestler ever to be on your roster because of the racist views he held. Brick: I knew, going into that match, that people would talk about. I knew that pretty much the world would be watching and I knew I couldn't lose. If I had, it would I actually got a lot of calls from people like Julian Bond, Cornel West, Kweisi Mfume, Jesse Jackson, Al Sharpton, Bill Cosby. All of them let me know that they were going to be watching the match and that they were rooting for me. Tavis: Now, before we close, you wanted to promote Global Wrestling's next pay-per-view event Whiplash. Brick: That's right. It's taking place August 31st at the new Global Arena in New York City. Tavis: Folks, you can catch Brick at Whiplash on pay-per-view. Call your cable or satellite provider and don't forget to catch him on Global Wrestling Evolution, every Monday night on HBO. Brick, thanks for stopping by. Brick: It's a pleasure. Tavis: That's our show for tonight. As always, you can catch me on the radio on NPR. I'll see you back here next time on PBS. Until then, thanks for watching. Good night from Los Angeles. Keep the faith. Announcer: For more information on today's show, visit Tavis Smiley at PBS.org.
