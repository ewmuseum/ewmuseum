The Memory Remains: #34

14 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 31404 I remember the day I met Lily. I remember the day I met Bryce. Lily. The sweetest flower of a woman I have ever seen. I met her in my Chemistry Lab in college. We were lab partners. The moment the professor pointed to both of us and said, “You two will be working together” my heart leapt up into my throat. Here I was, partnered up with the most beautiful woman I had ever laid eyes upon. Sweet smelling auburn hair that fell past her shoulders and emerald green eyes that could melt someone’s heart. At the time, it was summer and she wore shorts that showed off her shapely tan legs. During the first class, we made the normal small talk. “Where are you from?” “Do you work?” “What type of degree are you working towards?” Things like that. Class let out, and we left together. I met someone soon after that would change the course of my life. “What the hell you think you doin”, boy?” The voice came from behind me and Lily. She stopped and winced. “I said what the hell you think you doin”, boy?” Something pushed against my shoulder. I turned to see the modern era “Three Stooges.” Larry, with his frizzy brown hair spurting off in all directions. Moe, sporting long straight black hair and sad, tired eyes. And Curly, the bald, mean looking bastard wearing a letter jacket with all kinds of sports pins on it. “Why you hangin” around this loser? This zero?” It was Curly talking. I started to speak. “You shut the fuck up. I’m talkin” to her.” “Jesus Bryce. We’re just lab partners,” Lily said. “Yeah, right.” Bryce rolled his eyes while Larry and Moe snickered. “Let’s go,” he spoke, grabbing her arm and pulling roughly. “See you next week,” Lily said. Next week was not soon enough. The days passed by slowly. My thoughts kept returning to Lily and the way Bryce pulled her away. Finally, it was time for lab. I walked into the half-filled room and looked for Lily. Seeing she wasn’t there yet, I crossed over to our workstation. She entered the room a couple of minutes later. Walking slowly, Lily drifted through the room to where I waited. Her hair covered her face and she wore dark sunglasses that hid her eyes. I tried to start a conversation, but she remained silent. Through the entire three-hour class, she never removed her sunglasses. When we were done with the assignment and walking out the door, I reached and grabbed her by the arm. She screamed and fell to the floor, crying. “What’s wrong?” I asked. “What did I do?” “Nothing,” she said. I knelt down to help her back up. Staring into a black pool with emerald centers, realization of what was wrong swept over me. “Bryce?” I asked. “Did he do this?” She nodded. “I think we should leave separately. He gets extremely jealous when he sees me with other guys.” I nodded and she left. That was the first time I seriously felt like killing someone. Over the next couple of weeks, I would see Lily one of a couple of ways. Either sitting pretty and all smiles or beat all to hell, saying Bryce really doesn’t mean it, that he really loves her, that he just gets jealous and it is really her fault. It reminded me of my mother. Blaming herself for my father’s drunken fits of rage. I took care of that bastard, leaving him crying in the hospital from where he and my Louisville Slugger exchanged words. My heart wasn’t set on killing him. Bryce wouldn’t be that lucky. I remember the day my life changed. Arriving at lab in the afternoon, I saw that Lily wasn’t there. Starting and working on the project alone was not a problem. But when she didn’t arrive, I began to worry. Leaving lab and walking the campus, I passed by some of Lily’s friends. I heard her name mentioned and the word hospital, so I decided to stop and ask where she was. “Didn’t you hear?” a lanky blonde asked. “She’s in the hospital,” a chunky brunette said. “Bryce lost control on her and put her in the hospital,” the blonde added. I felt the rage in me begin to swell. I thanked the ladies and left. It looked like I would have to follow-up with my promise to Bryce. I hurried to the hospital and rushed to Lily’s room. She was laying in bed, one eye swollen shut and her arm in a sling. Her one good eye was open and alert, watching the news on TV. When she heard me enter, the eye jerked over to the doorway. Lily couldn’t move her head due to the neck brace. She started to tell me the story of what happened, but I had already guessed some of the parts. Some guy had decided to ask Lily out on a date. Who could blame him? However, he did it in front of Bryce. Regardless of the little threat I had made, Bryce lost it. He started smacking the guy around. When Lily tried to pull Bryce off the poor shmuck, he turned on her, cussing and beating her. This was the end result. “It’s over between me and him,” she said. More than she will ever know. Forever. I got in the car and drove to Bryce’s driveway. Partially down the driveway, I started honking the horn to get him to come out. Pulling to a stop a safe distance from the house, I carefully pulled the glass jar from it’s casing. By the time I was out of the car, Bryce was out of the house. “Who are you?” he asked, an edge of anger on his voice. He started coming down the stairs and stopped halfway down. “Don’t you remember?” I said quietly, my voice a couple of notches above a whisper. Oh yeah, I can hit him from here and not worry about the blast. “I’m the one that left you handcuffed in your own blood and piss.” “You don’t look so tough without your knife, pal. I owe you a lesson.” “Well, before you teach me that lesson, lemme ask you this. Remember what I told you about Lily?” Bryce’s look of thought reminded me of how a deer looks when caught in some car’s headlights. “Nope, I don’t,” came his reply. “Figured. I told you that if you hurt Lily, you get hurt. Well, you hurt her pretty good a while back. And now it’s time to pay your dues.” “What” Little bitty you gonna put me in the hospital?' He was mocking my size. I’m scrawny and he’s built. Hell, I would make fun of me if I had his size. “Nope.” “Then what?” “I’m gonna kill you.” “With what army?” “With this,” I said, holding up the jar. Again with the deer caught in the headlights look. “Think fast.” I threw the bomb at him and started backing up, just in case. Now there is a reason that Bryce was a sports god, especially during football season. Homeboy could catch. And that’s exactly what happened. He caught the jar. Apparently, I threw it a little too well and it went right to his chest and his natural football instincts kicked in and the bastard caught it. “What the hell is this gonna do?” he asked. My mind raced. What now? I had failed. I gotta get outta here. No wait. I can’t let Lily down. I can’t let him torment another woman. How? How can I win? How? And then it came to me. “Explode,” I said, answering his question, while jerking my pellet gun from its holster at my back. It came free easily. I pulled it up, quickly sighted, and squeezed the trigger. The small, pointed pellet zipped through the air and slammed against the jar. A bright flash of light blinded me and the roar deafened me. I was knocked back by the wind of the explosion. I got up slowly. Most of the front stairs leading to the house was gone. And so was Bryce. I looked around. I saw nothing but blackened earth and the occasional clump of bloody flesh that I assumed was some part of Bryce’s anatomy. My job was done. I needed to go. I went to the car. There were a couple of flecks of blood and a gruesome hunk of Bryce on the windshield. I tossed the hunk off the windshield and got into the car. I turned on the wipers, mixing blood and windshield washer fluid and sped home. Once in the door of my apartment, I ran into the bathroom and vomited. I started to shake. I looked into the mirror and stared at my reflection. I looked into my eyes. A killer looked back at me. The zero had faced the jock and won. I was one step closer to what I was to become. Jumping into the shower, I let the hot water run over me, scalding my skin red. Using soap and a washcloth, I got most of the dirt and grime off of me. Maybe there was some of Bryce in there too. It slid off of my body in a brown tinge of water with the occasional glint of red. Throwing on some old clothes and running outside, I double-checked the inside of my car for any incriminating stains. Aside from a little dirt on the floorboard, it was clean as a whistle. I went back inside and collapsed on the couch. I was exhausted both mentally and physically. It was at that moment that the phone rang. If I were any more high strung, I would’ve jumped up to the ceiling. “Hello?” I said into the receiver. “Wanna come over?” Lily. Suddenly, I wasn’t so tired. “Sure. I’ll be there in a sec.” It only takes the brain one moment. One moment to see something. To process it. To understand it. And for you to register is. In most cases, that moment only lasts for what it is. A moment. As soon as the door swung upon, the moment turned into an eternity. Vic was strapped to a rack, in a standing position, slightly leaned back. Her shirt had been stripped off and electrodes covered her upper body. Her face was beaten and swollen purple. Two men stood in the room. One next to Victoria, the other behind the controls of an unknown device. He hit a switch and Victoria’s face scrunched up in pain as another scream escaped her lips. They were sending voltage through her body. It only took a moment. The guns leapt up, guided by my hands, fingers working the triggers as the world turned bloody. The two men’s bodies twitched as the bullets sunk into them and they both collapsed. I ran into the room, dropping my guns, working the straps that help Victoria in place. She fell into my arms. Her skin was clammy, her pulse racing. She strained to see me through battered eyes. I pulled her close to me. “I love you, John,” she whispered into my ear. Then her body went limp.
