A Change In The Wind

18 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 18th, 2004 The blade slips free of its saya with little more than a whisper. I kneel, saya in one hand and sword in the other, staring into the steel. I wonder, does this have the answer to my questions? Can I find what I need here, in the gleam of the blade? Slowly, I rise to my feet. Tossing the saya aside, I grasp the hilt of the sword with both hands, and hold the katana up. The sunlight bounces off the blade's polished surface, and for a moment it looks like I'm holding onto a shaft of blinding light. I shut my eyes, and I begin again. Lunging forward I begin the dance I've danced so many times. I go through the motions of fighting, the movements and the styles. Imaginary enemies come at me from all sides, and each strike, each twitch of the blade, is angled to fell one. I guide the sword through patterns I know all too well, cutting away at the air as if I can find my answer by slicing through the layers -- that maybe, if I dig deep enough, I can find peace of mind. Scowling, my grip tightens. My strikes become hard and vicious, as I try pry out the answer to the question that gnaws at me. What do I have to do now? I fly into a furious kata, chopping at ghosts as my frustration mounts. And still, there in the back of my head is that question. What do I have to do now? I had thought that defeating Ice would bring a measure of peace to things -- that just maybe, if I could put that monster away, push past him and climb to the place he'd held at the top, that things would be better. But I was wrong. Things were only getting worse. My eyes snap open, and I roar out loud. With a furious swipe I slash at the ground beneath me, sending blades of grass spiraling into the air. 'Well,' says a familiar voice, with a hint of amusement, 'if we ever need someone to kill the lawn, I know who to call.' I turn back towards the house and Yoshiko is there, smirking. She steps off the porch, leaning lightly on her cane, and crosses the backyard, coming towards me. 'It's not working,' I say with a sigh, plopping onto my butt and laying the katana across my raised knees. 'What's not?' asks Yoshiko. She's standing over me now, looking down at me with an eyebrow raised. 'This,' I say, holding up the sword and looking into its blade again. 'Usually, when I have a problem I can't wrap my brain around, kendo practice helps me think. It helps me find peace, and from there, an answer.' 'But it's not working,' Yoshiko says. 'It's not working,' I reply, 'and I feel like I'm running out of solutions. Enemies are surrounding us on all sides 'shiko-chan, and I don't know if I can deal with them all.' I flop onto my back, letting the sword fall to my side. 'I thought after I beat Ice things would be different. That the Echelon had finally been put away -- but they've only regrouped, gotten stronger, more focused. Ever since I won the title in Tokyo, things have only been getting harder. Everyone's targeting me, even complete strangers! I feel like the world's just waiting for me to slip, to make a single tiny error -- and then it'll all be over. They'll tear me apart, and that'll just be the first.' Chuckling, Yoshiko releases her cane and lowers herself onto me, straddling my hips. She leans down, letting her hair hang down into my face, forming a curtain of white that blocks out the world, and leaves me to be absorbed into the deep blue of her eyes. 'Lion, Lion, Lion,' she says, chiding me, 'did you ever think that maybe, you're trying too hard?' I narrow my eyes curiously. 'What do you mean?' 'You're trying to force things,' she says, 'trying to barrel on through like always, to force your way to an answer. I don't think that's the way to do things. I don't think it's going to work this time.' For a long moment, I simply look up at her in silence. And I realize that things have been so hectic the last few weeks that I haven't really taken the time to appreciate her. So I let everything soak in, her beauty? and her wisdom. 'Okay,' I say, 'so what do I have to do?' Yoshiko smiles down at me. 'For one thing, you need to realize that you don't have to do this alone. You'll always have me, you already know that. And there are more people out there who will help you, if you ask. You're not the only person trying to forge order out of the chaos. Believe it or not, there are some people who respect your accomplishments enough to subdue their lust for gold. And if you talk to them, they'll listen.' 'So you think I need to rely on others?' I ask, incredulous. 'No!' With a huff, Yoshiko lifts herself off me. She grabs her cane and leans on it again, then reaches down with her free hand. I clasp it, and she pulls me to my feet. 'Look,' she says, 'you've called yourself a samurai before, right? You respect what they once were.' I nod. 'Well, Lion-chan, the greatest samurai were more than fighters. They were commanders. Generals. Leaders. So if you want to be like them, if you want to try and get a handle on things before the spiral completely out of control, start acting like the champion you are and lead. Even if it's only by example. I believe you can do it. Do you?' I stare at her in silence, just absorbing her words -- the words of this exceptional woman, who has faith in me even when I don't. Finally, I look down at my katana, lying in the grass. I reach down, lifting it from where it rests. Holding it lightly I take a few short practice swings, then hold it up in front of myself, studying my own reflection in the blade. 'I guess,' I say, 'we're going to find out.' I turn my head and smile at Yoshiko, and she smiles in return -- we both know that words would be wasted now. I look away from her, and hold the sword out in front of me again, but I can hear the crunch of the grass as she walks back towards the house. Again I stand alone. I draw a long, slow breath and let it out just as slowly. My body relaxes, arms falling to my sides, the tip of my blade pointed at the earth below. I let my breath come slower and deeper, reaching for the cool blue strength within myself, scooping it up and letting it wash over my body. With my free hand, I touch the bandages around my midsection. Slowly, I begin to unravel them, freeing skin to feel the air it hasn't felt in weeks. I toss the bandages aside, and draw another deep breath. The pain that was once there, in my ribs -- it's gone now. Another breath, and that cool blue strength -- chakra, Sakura had called it when we were in Tokyo -- floods through me, suffusing every part of my body. At once I feel alive, connected to everything around me -- the air crackles like lightning on my skin, making every part of me fresh, new and alive -- and almost immediately, I launch into a kata. But this time, I let the sword guide me. I release my will to it and it flashes through the air, dancing in patterns of its own volition, taking me along for the ride. And as I let the blade guide me, I realize that I'm tracing a pattern, creating a single symbol over and over again, as part of this kata. The kanji for Wind. I smile, as everything starts to come together in my head, and I finally find my answer. My grip on the katana tightens, and I begin to add my own interpretation to the dance. And I laugh out loud as together the sword and I create a new kata, a new way. Together, we make things different. Together, we can bring about a change in the wind. And nothing can stop us.
