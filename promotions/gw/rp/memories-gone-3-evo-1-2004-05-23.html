Memories Gone #3 (Evo #1)

23 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 052304 This is how your day goes. You wake up, in the same spot that you fell asleep, back aching from sleeping in a chair, neck throbbing from sleeping at an awkward angle, still holding onto your love’s hand. You squeeze it, hoping to feel the familiar return squeeze but get’nothing. And then you remember. And you go through a wide range of emotion. Hurt. Sadness. Anger. Rage. Hope. Despair. And you sit. You wait. You watch. Hoping. Pleading. Begging. And if you are a god fearing man? You pray. And if you are not? You sit and wait some more. This is your day. This is the life you live. Other than the revenge you plan to extract. ***** The bed jerks slightly. I almost didn’t notice it at first. But then it happened again. I raised my head slowly, squinting my eyes at the harsh sterile light of the room Reno had prepared for Victoria. Across the bed, a medical technician was moving Vic’s arms and legs in a mock exercise to ensure that her muscles didn’t deteriorate. “Good morning, Mr. Zero,” the technician muttered softly. Was it already morning? My neck creaked loudly, protesting the movement as I craned around to look at the clock on the wall behind me, which reading ten a.m. Guess so. “Morning. Any good news?” “No,” the technician replied. “No extra good news. Just the same ole same ole. Everything looks good. They are both okay. It’s just a matter of time before she comes out. We just have to be patient.” I nodded my thanks to the technician, getting up slowly, stretching, getting the kinks out of my system. I wandered aimlessly out of the room, weaving a little bit due to the earliness of the hour, and found my office. On the back counter of the office, next to the wet bar, a coffeemaker sat, looking at me. After several minutes of fumbling with the maker, I finally paged one of the secretaries on duty to come in and fix a pot for me. Less than five minutes later, I had a fresh cup of hot steaming coffee in my hand, working its way into my system. I leaned back in the plush leather chair behind my desk and settled back for a moment. An odd thought crossed my mind. I picked up my cup of coffee and returned to Vic’s room, where the technician was still checking her over. I watched him carefully before speaking. “Repeat for me what you just told me.” He jumped slightly, unaware of my presence in the room. “What was that, Mr. Zero?” “Do you remember the brief conversation we had, not ten minutes ago?” “Yes sir.” “Do you remember what you told me?” “Yes.” “Humor me. Repeat, word for word, everything that you told me.” He sunk everything that I had said in before starting to speak, which he did very slowly. “You asked if there was any good news. I said no, just the same ole same ole. That they’re both okay and it’s just a matter?” “Stop.” I interrupted. “Back up.” “To which part?” he asked me, confused. “After same ole same ole.” I closed my eyes, listening to him carefully. “That they’re both okay?” “Yes.” I opened my eyes, staring at him. “What about it?” he asked, still confused. “You said “they’re” as in “they are.”” “Yes. What about it?” I gave the technician an annoyed look. “Why would you use a plural word to describe a single person?” “Well”.Um?.” “Spit it out boy, before I cut the thought out of your head and look at it myself.” Now I was starting to get angry. “It’s just” she’s not just a single person anymore.” “What do you mean?” “She’s’umm’she’s”.well?” “Say it, goddamnit! Just fucking say it!” “She’s pregnant, Mr. Zero.” I stood there, looking dumbfounded at the technician, then down to the woman that was to be my wife, still locked away in her coma. Pregnant. New hate. Pregnant. New rage. Pregnant. New wrath. Pregnant. New red. Pregnant. ***** The coffee cup sat stagnant on my desk. By the time I picked it up again, the liquid inside had grown cold, forcing me to pour it out and fix a fresh cup. As the steam rose from the cup, I let my eyes gaze into it, losing all focus in the surrounding reality. Why hadn’t she told me? Was she keeping it from me? Or did she not know? The thoughts twirled around in my head, moving faster and faster at cyclone speed. I reached up with both hands and let my head fall into their waiting grasp. These two hands’they held the weight of the world. I stood up slowly from behind my desk and moved to the closet, removing my battle harness and several boxes of ammunition. A box of fresh, empty magazines came with me. One by one, I started loading the bullets into the magazines. The bullets were special loads. Hollow points. Hollow points that had been filled with mercury. Even if the shot didn’t kill you, the poison contained within would. I’m not sure how much time had passed. I stayed locked away within myself, moving only mechanically. Remove one bullet from the box. Insert bullet into clip. Remove next bullet, insert into clip. If clip becomes full, set aside and retrieve an empty clip. Remove bullet from the box, insert into clip. A loud noise, like a book striking the ground startled me. On instinct, a clip slammed home, a bullet cocked, locked and ready to rock, gun pointed at the origin of the noise, my muscles tensed. I relaxed when I saw Reno sitting in a chair across from my desk. “Morning, Mav. Didn’t hear you knock,” I said somewhat flatly, returning to the bullets. Maverick slowly raised his wrist to eyelevel, consulting his watch. “More like, good evening.” My eyes left my work for a moment, giving a puzzled expression. “You’ve been here quite some time, John. Is everything okay?” “Not really, Mav. Not really. Have you ever felt like your whole world was starting to fall apart?” 'Your world is what you make it. It's the situations that you lose control of... what's bothering you, John?' “It’s Vic?” “Has she taken a turn for the worse?” “No” it’s just?” “Are the technicians not doing their job properly” I instructed them to give her their upmost attention?” I held up a hand, motioning to Reno to be quiet for a moment. “No. The technicians are doing a wonderful job. I really appreciate all that you have done for us these past few weeks Maverick.” “Then what is the problem, John?” “She’s pregnant, Mav.” “Oh.” “Yeah. Oh.” 'So you're expecting the pitter patter of liability in your future.' I grunt a response to Maverick, returning the guns and clips to the harness and locking them away in the closet. 'I apologize. What do you plan to do?' Reno asked. I circled around the desk, and leaned against it, still facing my friend. “I don’t know. Until all else fails, hope. That’s about all I can do.” “Hope” Hope for what?” “When I get that figured out, Mav, you’ll be the second one to know.”
