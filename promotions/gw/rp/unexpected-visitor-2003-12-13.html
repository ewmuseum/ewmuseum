Unexpected Visitor

13 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/13/03 The longest night of his life is over. Or so he thinks anyway. Lying there on his rack, Johnie stares up at the cold concrete above him. Too much on his mind. The thin light from the hallway filters into the cell and over his upper torso but is sectioned through the bars. Folding his arms behind his head he clenches his eyes shut and shudders. Still cold from his morning shower. Or at least it was considered that. Nothing more than cold water and three thin jets spraying out in different directions. And the hard block of soap wasn’t lathering anything at all. The taxpayers money going to the extent of the sheer comforts for its convicts. Johnie gradually lets his eyes readjust to the light again. Red as can be from a restless night of thought and anger. No point in sleeping when you know that your life has turned to the worst. He still lies there on his rigid slab they call a cot. Thoughts still rolling through his mind. Contemplating what can be done now. “Fuck you, Johnie!” Jesus. She could have at least had the decency to say something better to me. Is this what I’ve become? The designated family black sheep. Terrific. I’m now stuck with dealing with that for the rest of my life. What did I do to deserve this? He rolls onto his right side and tilts his head on his palm and finds something different to stare at: the opposite wall. Same concrete, just different scenery. Across from his is an identical cot latched to the wall and hung slightly by chains on the head and foot. Still with a thin mattress, but blank from vacancy and neglect. Behind the rack protrudes a prehistoric porcelain sink. It stands on a thin and unevenly sculpted stem and rises up to an oval mouth holding gray and rusted faucets. Not quite the Hilton of all prisons. Johnie raises his left arm up toward his face and glances over his Timex. “10:23” it says with digital rigor. Sighing to himself, he drops his arm to his side and grumbles hard. It has been 8 hours since his last cigarette and the fix is starting to get to him. As well as his temper. How long am I supposed to stay in this hell hole? Do they really have to keep me here longer than necessary on purpose? That’s how it works, I guess. Take your time pushing papers and make the guy sweat it out. Pfft. Like this will teach me a lesson. It sure will. I just won’t drive through Texas ever again! That’s about it. I didn’t even do anything. I declined to take a fricking breathalyzer. Is that such a crime?! Whatever. I can’t stand legal shit like that. UGH!! This is totally going to fuck up my life now. A legal trick is going to keep me from driving for a long time. I don’t believe this! How am I supposed to do my jobs now? What? I have to get someone to drive me there? Have them wait while I go bust someone’s ass and then they drive me back home? Great! That’d look great for my business. “Gee. I’m sorry. I can’t pull that job. Right now, my driver’s out sick and I can’t go without him. Maybe next time.” His green eyes are slowly surrounded with red as frustration builds within him. Flexing his jaw, he bites down hard on his bottom lip until it starts turning from red to a bright white. His cheek twinges slightly as he balls up his left fist and rears it back. Twisting himself off the cot, he leaps forward and rushes towards the other wall and swings firmly against it as he kneels on the in front of it. Punch after punch connects with the barrier, going from his left to right and back again. His fists switch from a white to a purplish red after every jab as he wails in ferocity echoing throughout the hallway. Sweat begins to trickle down his beet red brow and his breath starts to waver. Taking a last exhausted swing, he pops at the one crack that he put into the concrete and slumps himself against it. Leaning his head and arms across the wall, he pants and huffs for relief. The air satisfies the need, but not the rest he wanted. “I don’t think that wall did anything to you.” a familiar voice rumbles from the corridor outside his cell. Johnie gasps slightly more as he hesitantly tilts his head towards the origin. Fatigued, he raises his eyes toward the hall and attempts to focus on the two figures outside. “You’ve got yourself a visitor, Ringo.” the orderly officer grumbles hoarsely. “And you’d best not be messing up my block, neither. I don’t want you to have to stick around and fix up any holes you put in my walls.” Johnie glances past the gray-haired officer and views the man behind him in a silver suit. He can only see the blurry outline of him out of the corner of his eye, but Johnie already knows who the visitor is. “Fine...” he rasps faintly. Slumping himself over, he turns and sits back on the cot staring down at the floor. The officer motions to the visitor to step forward as he reaches to the side of his belt for the keys. Surprisingly, Johnie didn’t hear him coming with all the jingling he’d produce. Flipping through the series, the old man finds the right key and unlatches the door and pushes it to the right. The door rattles open as the visitor steps in. All that Johnie sees of him is the bottom of his trousers and the high gloss of his black shoes. Still slumped forward, Johnie starts to breath normally and hacks hard at the ground. “I see your health hasn’t improved all that much since I last saw you.” smugs Mr. High Class. He stands about three feet away from Johnie near the cell door as it gets slammed shut by the orderly. “Aren’t you going to talk to me, son?” Tilting his head up slowly, Johnie snaps his neck from side to side, cracking it. Gradually he looks up at his guest and gives a slight wince towards him. Glancing over the collar of his buttoned up pallid shirt and silver striped tie, he looks over the muscular definition of his neck and shoulders. And above them hold a clean-shaven visage with a ridged brow. But the rest of his face is a near copy of Johnie, straight to the shade of green in his eyes. “......” Johnie grumbles and tilts his head back down, shrugging to himself. Undaunted, his visitor stands proud and folds his arms over his chest and leans himself backwards a bit. “Is that how it is, Johnie. You’re just gonna shrug me off. I doubt it.” “What do you want me to say, JACK?” Johnie blurts out as his flips his head back up and looks his father in the face for the first time in years. Unflinching. Fearless. Frustrated. “About time.” Unfolding his arms, Jack Ringo paces to the far side of the cell and steps back towards Johnie. “I’m hearing that you’ve started your own little operation. Running around, bumping people off, that sort of thing.” Watching every step, Johnie never loses a beat and replies with ferocity. “So?” Jack stops in his tracks as his son rises to his feet and takes a step towards him. “What is it to you” Suddenly you’re gonna want a cut. Is that how it is?” Jack never flinches a minute. He merely steps toward him and shrugs him off. “No, that’s not how it is. He walks gingerly around his son, tilting his head up and down inspecting him. “Its just that I don’t want you getting into the wrong kind of work for you is all. You were supposed to be going to college and getting away from all this bullshit.” Johnie spins around in mid-assessment and stares his father right in the eyes sternly. “Well that didn’t work out now did it, Jack?” Turning the tables, he now walks around Jack and mockingly looks him up and down. “You don’t see me toting around any books do you” Bringing home any report cards. Getting some dumbass education. No! It didn’t happen. So now I’m just doing what I learned to do best, alright?! I do my job, I get paid. Simple as that.” Taken aback, Jack tilts his head down and raises his brow plainly. Brushing through his combed back hair, he chuckles lightly. “You sound a little touchy there, son.” Snapping his head up, Johnie grabs Jack by the collar and pushes him up against the bars of the cell. Pressing his temple into the face of his father, he clenches his face hard as he speaks. “Why shouldn’t I be”! HUH?! You leave me hanging there in that house for five fucking years, and you expect me to be cheerful?!” Johnie pushes away from him and shrugs himself off while Jack readjusts his jacket and tie. “I mean, you leave me with that...that THING you called a wife and had me suffer with her until now.” “I couldn’t help anything with that situation. Fuck.” Jack rubs his hands over the sides of his neck and paces in front of the bars. “She robbed me of everything I had gained over the years and then she threatened to turn me in. I had to get out while I could. What did you want from me?” “Why didn’t you take me with you?” Johnie counters with rage and distraught as he whimpers out a slight wail. “Huh?” Regaining his drop in composure, he continues on. “Why’d you just leave me there?” Jack shakes his head towards him and shrugs him off. “Enough of this. You have more shit to deal with than this.” Johnie shudders himself slightly and turns away from his father, stepping toward the back sink. “You got a goddamn drunk driving charge.” “So what are you gonna do?” Johnie grasps the lip of the sink and leans himself forward. “Post bail and get me out of here?” Rubbing his chin, he sighs heavily. “No, that’s not why I’m here.” Defeated, Johnie droops his head and runs his hand through his hair. “Then what are you here for if you’re just here to see me in a cage?” “I came here to make sure you stay out of this shit.” he utters. “This line of work to too big for you.” Spinning around, Johnie takes a few steps forward. “Too big for me”! To hell with that. Guard!” Johnie blurts out as a desk chair squeaks and a loud jingling echoes throughout the corridor. “Get him outta here.” The old guy jabs his key into the lock and slides the door over letting Jack step out from the cell. Turning around, Jack shouts back toward his son. “Gladly. Oh, and Johnie...” Johnie goes to the door as it slams shut in front of him. “I pulled some strings so that you can get out of here sooner. So your court case is tomorrow. Good luck.” “Whatever.” Jack turns and is lead out by the roundly shaped old man. Staring back at his father stone faced, Johnie mumbles under his breath. “Good luck yourself.”
