Coming Out (Part Three)

11 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01.11.06 The audience in the New York Nederlander Theatre hit their feet as 'no day but today' is sang out and the character, Angel, runs back on stage just before the curtain draws. Ken Partridge Jr, playing Roger and holding Antonique Smith, portraying the role of Mimi, in his arms, is finishing up the final performance of his Broadway debut in RENT. They all quickly shuffle off of the stage as 'Seasons of Love' begins to play. Two at a time, they walk out to take their final bows, Ken being last with Matt Caplan, the gentleman that has been playing the role of Mark. As they take their bows, Stevie Wonder's rendition of the show's title song brings a rousing chill through the theatre and a tear escapes from Ken's eye. This has been his dream of ten years, and now, it's over. 'It was amazing working with you,' expressed Marcus Paul James, a taller black man with dreadlocks that had played the role of Tom Collins, as he threw his arms around Ken for a hug once the final curtain call had occured and they were once again backstage. 'Likewise, Buddy,' retorted Ken. 'This was incredible. You've no idea what it's like to work with you after seeing perform that role to perfection some years ago.' 'Hey,' chimed in Matt as he jumped into them and turned it into a group hug, 'don't forget about me.' Matt and Marcus were both cast members that Ken had seen before in the national tour, and both had done stellar jobs in their roles. Working opposite Matt was breathtaking for him considering that he'd actually gotten the opportunity to befriend him after seeing him in Atlanta. The same went for Marcus, which Ken had met during a Birmingham performance. 'So,' Marcus continued, 'what's next for you?' 'I catch a plane for Manchester,' replied an emotional KPJ. 'But not until we throw a monster party, first,' proclaimed Justin Johnston, the thin young half-white and half-black man who had been playing the part of Angel. 'Thanks,' said Ken with a smile, 'but my flight leaves in three hours.' 'Well,' shouted a beautiful young brunette that looked of some Italian ancestory and went by the name of Ava Gaudet, 'we'll just have to start the party now.' Ken laughed as Ava, who had played Maureen, handed him a flute of champagne. 'A toast,' screamed Matt, as he and the rest held their flutes in the air. 'Wait,' came the voice of Antonique, a beautiful young half-black and half-latina woman that we were introduced to on stage as Mimi. She rushed up and threw her arms around Ken, embracing him in a very meaningful hug. You can't spend two months portraying lovers on stage and not bond a close bond. She delivered a kiss to his cheek and whispered a very gentle, 'you have to wait on me.' She pulled away and gazed into his eyes and, for a split second, KPJ was no longer Ken Partridge Jr... he was Roger, staring into the beautiful moonlit orbs of his love, Mimi. Ken quickly shook himself back into reality and looked away sheepishly, causing Antonique to do the same. It was no secret that she had formed feelings for Ken, and, while Ken may have formed some for her as well, they were nowhere near as powerful as the ones that he had for Gunner. Noticing what was happening, the just arriving Kenna J. Ramsey, a strong black woman who had played Maureen's lover, Joanne, was quick to break the silence. 'To us,' she shouted as she raised her flute. 'To Jonathan,' chimed Ken in salute to the shows writer and creator that had died on it's opening night. 'To Jonathan,' cried the rest as they tipped back their champagne. ---- 'So,' asked Ken, now aboard The Elite's private learjet 60, 'I'm sorry we had to take such a long pause, but there's plenty of time between here and England, Mrs. Alberdeane. Where were we when we last left of?' 'You were going to meet with Lloyd Mehaffey in regards to the investigation,' retorted Kelli Alberdeane, the well built, raven haired publisher on Ken's autobiography. She was sitting to his right, and Gunner, on his left. 'Ah, yes,' he replied, 'Well...' 'Anything to drink, Mr. Partridge?' interrupted a dark headed young man that had been shadowing Ken as of late and was currently playing the part of in-flight steward. 'I've already told you, Chance,' replied Ken without looking up, 'stop calling me Mr. Partridge. Call me Ken, okay.' 'Very well,' replied Chance. 'Would you like anything to drink?' 'Orange juice, please,' Ken replied with a sigh. 'Gun, would you like anything, Sweetheart?' Gunner looked up from her copy of Nora Roberts' Red Lily and delivered a beautiful smile to Ken and then to Chance. 'I'll just have a Perrier, please,' she responded before delivering a quick kiss to Ken and then entangling herself back in her book. 'Kelli?' Ken asked. 'Gin and tonic with a lemon and a lime, please,' she answered as Chance nodded and scurried off to fill the order. She looked at Ken to explain her choice of alchohol as a beverage. 'Flying makes me nervous.' 'No worries,' he retorted with a million dollar smile, his baby blues piercing directly through her. In this light, she thought, he looked just like the perfect mix or James Dean and a young Marlon Brando. In the front of the jet, Josh, Bill, and Ace all sat playing cards at a table. Bill and Ace were both partaking in a Sweetwater Exodus Porter and Josh was knocking back a Dr. Pepper. 'No Mountain Dew?' Ken asked as he noticed his little buddy's soda of choice. 'Nope,' Josh began, 'didn't you hear? That Kris Kanton's the official spokesperson for them again. No way I'm supporting that.' Ken laughed along with Josh and the rest as Kelli pulled out a notepad and an MP3 recorder, setting up for the details she was soon to receive. Chance soon returned with their drinks and Ken began. ---- 'You wanted to see me,' asked the seventeen year old KPJ as he stepped into the now empty classroom of Lloyd Mehaffey. 'I did,' the teacher responded. 'I was told that they're going to be doing an investigation.' Ken sat his bag atop one of the desks and walked over to Lloyd's podium, leaning on it as he responded. 'Yeah, they're doing an investigation. They're supposed to be coming to the house tomorrow to look for anything suspicious and to ask some questions. It should be interesting.' 'Just be careful, Ken,' Mr. Mehaffey warned. 'Principal Martin and the Superintendant of Schools will both be there, and you know that they're on your side, but these other guys, they're looking for anything they can find and manipulate into this turning into a full-blown issue.' 'It already is an issue, isn't it,' Ken asked, looking down at the podium with a stern look on his face and not raising his blue eyes once. 'I may not get to graduate and I'm being forced into coming out to my step-father tonight instead of when I'm ready and prepared. I'd say things are pretty damned full blown.' Lloyd left his desk and walked over to the now frowning Ken. he placed his hand on his student's shoulder and offered comfort. For a man that rarely showed true emotion, other than concern, he was truly offering it. 'Things are going to be okay, Ken,' he started. 'William's a good man and he's taken to you genuinely. He takes pride in calling himself your dad... not your step-father, but your dad. He's proud of you, I see it in his eyes.' 'I appreciate that,' KPJ said, still without raising his head, 'but he's also from a different upbringing, Mr. Mehaffey. The way he was raised was that gay is wrong and anyone that isn't straight is going to hell. Worse than that, he's very prejudiced against that type of thing.' 'But,' Lloyd interrupted, 'he loves you. That's what matters, Ken. Trust in that.' 'I hope you're right,' he responded as the bell rang and the students in the hall began to shuffle, heading to their final classes of the day. 'I really do.' 'Just keep you head up,' Lloyd finished as the first of many began entering his class, already chattering quietly under their breaths at the sight of Ken. 'Thanks, Lloyd,' Ken said as he grabbed his bag from the desk and began out of the door. 'Thank you for everything.' 'Hey,' he said, 'you're my future son-in-law. It's nothing. Now, get to Government and Economics before Sam has my head for making you late.' Letting out a small laugh and a slight smirk, Ken nodded and waved before heading out the door and towards the stairs to the second floor. Lloyd quickly stuck his head into the hallway and shouted his final words to his prized pupil. 'Ken... good luck tonight.'
