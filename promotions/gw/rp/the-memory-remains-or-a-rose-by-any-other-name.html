It was a dark and stormy night, the perfect clich”, with just a hint of foreshadowing. Rain slashed against the window, lightning painting its flashbulb, paparazzi glow across the girl's face as the nurse checked her vitals. Sadness reigned in this room, etching its stain on her furrowed brow. The nurse frowned, brushing an errant strand of greasy hair back from Kitty’s parched brow. She was still burning up; her body wracked with fever? some unknown poison eating her up inside. The heart monitor beeped, slow and steady? background noise as the nurse sat down in the chair beside the bed. The energy in the room was reflected in the turbulent sky outside? something was about to happen. It had been touch and go from the start, when Doc Bailey had brought the comatose girl to the emergency room, and she still hadn’t regained consciousness. They didn’t even know her name, besides being nearly naked, she hadn’t been carrying any identification, nor had anyone come forth to claim her. She didn’t match any missing persons reports, either. She may as well have been a ghost. The nurse bent over her, tsking softly to herself. “With a face as pretty as yours, you’d think someone out there would be missing you,” she straightened the light sheet that covered the girl, and turned down the light that burned on the bedside table, making the brightness of the pure white walls less oppressive. Thunder rumbled ominously overhead, and the rain rattled the glass in the window frame as the wind growled and huffed at the window, sounding like a hungry predator? ---- Sunday, August 29, 2004 0333 hours, local time Gold’s Gym, Dallas, TX The bench under her back was soaked with sweat, the same sweat that was running down her face, dripping from the end of her nose as she struggled to do just one more leg press. She muttered to herself, wiping the hair back from her face with an absent swipe of her hand. Her eyes were fixed on the window, at the rain that trailed down the glass in silver streams. Rain. Tears. “Shed enough of those to last a lifetime,” she mumbled as she let the footrests crash back into place, looking down at the faded scars on her body. Here, dressed without the usual black mesh, the marks on her stomach were visible still, faded to nearly white, still a few shades darker than her pale skin. They almost looked like letters, in fact, if one looked closely, they could read them. She didn’t have to. Mine. Just one word, and to think of it now, made her feel equal mixtures of anger and depression. Once he’d loved her? and more recently, he’d tried to destroy her. The bruises had healed, the scars were fading? soon the marks would be invisible to the naked eye? but she knew they were there, that would never change. She would carry the scars that nobody could see, for the rest of her life. She could never bear children? not that she wanted to at this particular moment in time? but the doctors had told her not to get her hopes up. He had destroyed that? for that she hated him, above all else. She hadn’t even known she was pregnant? until they had told her? the worst of her injuries? they said she’d had an abortion? the less than legal kind, but she didn’t remember that. All she could recall was him on top of her; forcing himself inside her? feeling his hot breath on her neck? feeling the pain as his teeth sank into her shoulder, tearing apart the flesh. Pain, no longer mattered. No pain, no gain? that’s why she was here, alone. All the better to think- to prepare. She had a big match coming up, her first real GW match? her first Pay-Per-View? and she planned to go out there and give it her all. She owed herself that much? hell; she was Kitty Mac the wrestler now? a fighter? a survivor. Everything else was in the past, and it would stay there, where it belonged? ---- Friday, July 29, 2004 0601 hours, local time “somewhere in Dallas” The EKG had been flatline for over a minute. “Sir, should we call it?” The pretty nurse asked as Doctor Garrett bent over, sweeping his long, blond hair back from his face. He scowled down at the wreckage of what had once been a pretty face, and then barked at the nurse, “No! Goddamn it, we’re not giving up on her yet! Get me a syringe filled with epi, NOW!” The team exchanged a glance, but nobody moved to get the requested epinephrine. They’d shocked her heart back into rhythm three times upon entering the surgical suite, and each time the pulse had only lingered longer than several seconds. They knew Garrett was planning something more invasive than an IV? and they knew it was a futile attempt at best. The stab wounds in her chest had likely either ripped a hole in the girl’s aorta or bisected her heart. Either way, she was already gone, that wound, even ignoring the numerous others, had been fatal. “She’s gone, Rex,” the nurse said gently as she placed a hand on his shoulder. “You did all you could,” “Get me the epi,” he repeated, his sapphire eyes still fixed on the waxen face of the girl. The team exchanged another glance, doubting his sanity at this point. The situation was beyond hopeless, the body before them too mutilated to survive. Garrett growled in frustration, and shoved past them to the code cart, rifling through the supplies until he came up with a syringe full of epinephrine. He walked back to the girl’s side, squirted a bit of the clear liquid into the air, and then plunged the needle under her sternum and emptied the ten cc’s directly into her left ventricle. He glared at the monitor, defying it to disobey him. He was a surgeon, goddamn it! He would not lose this patient! “Beat, goddamn you!” For a long moment, which may have only been a few precious seconds, there was just that dead hum? the sound of eternity? that buzz of an open line? and then? there was a blip. Beep. And another. Beep. Garrrett pumped his fist in the air, and then glared at the nurse. “Still want to call it, Nancy?” She averted her eyes, and instead, busied herself with handing him the required instruments, now came the hard part? inspecting the damage. He cut away the remains of the shirt, and grimaced at the sight. She looked as fragile as a porcelain doll, laying there naked on the gurney. Her skin showed the impact of hands, fists and worse. Some type of bite mark, certainly not human, was on her shoulder? a ragged, weeping hole of torn flesh. Various cuts, scrapes and deep gouges festooned her body like the work of a sadistic tattoo artist. By far, the worst was what he saw when he tried to clean away some of the blood that caked her abdomen. There, as if someone had been chiseling an old woodcut, he saw the word “MINE” in an ancient script, cut cleanly into the flesh of her stomach. That sight alone was enough to turn his stomach, and he had to avert his eyes for a moment. “Jesus, Mary and Joseph,” he muttered to himself, “what sort of sick monster would do this to a girl?” He wasn’t expecting an answer, but Nancy replied just the same as she handed him more sterile swabs. “The kind that I’d like to come across in a dark alley” I wouldn’t hesitate to pull the trigger, I tell you what.” ---- Sunday, August 29, 2004 0721 hours, local time West End Motor Inn, Dallas, TX She sat on the edge of the lumpy mattress, in the place that had become her home and thought about her mistakes. The things that had happened to bring her here, to this place. She withdrew a cigarette from the fresh pack she’d just bought, and placed one between her lips, feeling a little like a rebellious teen. It had been ages since she’d smoked, but she needed it now? if nothing else then to calm her ragged nerves. She hesitated, and then finally flicked the lighter. Once. Twice? and on the third try, it lit. She barely managed to light the cigarette, her hands were jittering so badly, and when she did, she nearly choked on it. She waited out the coughing fit, and tried again, this time inhaling slowly, quashing the urge to cough even as it rose. At once, she could feel a pleasant sensation overcoming her, something akin to euphoria, and she found it wonderful? and totally welcome. Ashes dropped to the floor as she sat there, holding the cigarette awkwardly between thumb and forefinger, the way a pothead would hold a joint. She wanted so badly to erase the past? but now, she wasn’t sure if she could do that. She felt so alone? and so scared. Maybe she should just walk away now, leave before she made a fool of herself? but one thing kept her from picking up the phone and handing in her resignation. Where would she go? She had no family? no friends? and no life, other than this. She still couldn’t remember the past? she couldn’t remember what had happened prior to his attack. Just bits and pieces, two-dimensional Polaroid snapshots? her memory was holier than the fishnet shirts she wore. She remembered something about a chopper? something about a woman named Jade? and she remembered seeing a diamond ring falling, mingling with snowflakes? that was all. That was what kept her from picking up the phone. The desire for answers. Something told her that they were here. Here in Dallas? here, in Global Wrestling. She stood without realizing it, and crossed to the window, snatching aside the thin barrier, peering out into the morning, watching the storm move in. She pressed her hand against the window, still idly smoking the cigarette, feeling the cool glass beneath her sweaty palm. Across the sombre gray clouds, lightning followed a jagged course like cracks in a china plate... bright flashes of purple against the charcoal clouds. The skies opened up, the rain pouring down with relentless fury. She could understand that kind of intensity, her entire body vibrated with a rage that she could scarcely control. In the unsheltered parking lot outside the motel, the parked cars glimmered briefly with hard-edged reflections of the storm’s light, and she watched as the road-grime was wiped clean from her battered old Toyota. An anonymous car, for a ghost. Kaitlynn McIntyre had never existed anywhere other than on paper? but it was a name as good as any. A rose by any other name? The wind gusted, whipping the trees. Rain beat with sudden and intense fury against the glass, then streamed down the windows, blurring the view beyond, which was just as well. She let the curtain fall back into place, and returned to the bed, dropping the unsmoked remains of the cigarette into the can of flat, piss warm Coke that rested on the nightstand. It went out with a sizzle, and Kitty wasn’t far behind as she stretched out atop the still made bed, fully clothed, giving in to the waves that crashed in her head, dragging her under. Migraine headache. Tension. Overdoing it at the gym. Probable cause. She sought solace in sleep? escape? peace? but before she could even close her eyes, the cell phone on the bedside table began to ring. “For fuck’s sake” can’t a person get any sleep around here?!” She swore as she groped for the battered Nextel i90c, almost dropping it on the floor in her haste to grab it?. “Hello?” The voice on the other end of the line was like a slap in the face, or maybe a bucket of cold water over the head? she was instantly awake?.
