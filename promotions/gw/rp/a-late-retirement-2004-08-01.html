A Late Retirement

1 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/01/04 I pat him on the back, I really do. He won the Master of the Mat and defended his title in the same night. He is the longest reigning Global Wrestling champion, ever. Truly is an amazing accomplishment. He pinned me, fair and square. Not many people have done that in my career, but he has. And I give him props, because he deserved it. He is one of the best wrestlers I have seen in my entire life. He lives up to the name of champion right beside guys like Christopher Camero and Hans Kruger. He is the future of Global Wrestling, leaving me to wonder? What am I? “bowing my head towards the ground, I know that I’ve lost a tremendous battle. I look up and see Josh with his arms up in the air, proclaiming victory. What hurts isn’t that Josh defeated me. I’m truly honored to even be in the same match with him. But what hurts the most is that I don’t know if I can take this pain and punishment anymore. I’m getting old, I truly am. I look at these young guns stepping into the ring for the first time and running circles around me. I admit, when I step into the ring, I’m nervous. I am afraid to lose. But I suppose these fears are what makes me tick. I missed worrying about a match. And if I won that match, it feels as if I accomplished something extraordinary. It feels that I cheated death itself’again. While I was gone on my so-called leave, I missed getting body slammed. I missed getting punched in the jaw. I missed getting kicked in the stomach. After you’ve wrestled for quite some time, you develop a thirst for it. And as I slam my fist to the ring mat in anger, I question if my thirst has run dry” Josh’s entrance music hits the system and the crowd erupts for him’and not me. I have to face the facts, I’m not the top dog in this business anymore. I’m starting to get washed up and old. It hurts to even think about it now. Sometimes I have to face reality, this loss is a reality check. And maybe I should check myself into retirement. I remember back when I first came here, there was a different batch of wrestlers I faced every week. There was Mani Shigeru, the Silent Wind, the Tragic Hero, Roz Blayze, Jay Robson and Derek Frost. I had a family in The Nevermore. Don’t get me wrong, Leinad Soma has been a great friend, but nobody compares to the relationship that The Nevermore and The Harvesters had. The way that Salaok and Leisha brought me into their lives and treated me like I was their brother. The way that Grendel and I could sit back and discuss our lives and how we relate to each other. The way that Johnathon Davis and I could relax and have a Sunday dinner, like a family. These are the things that I miss. Now, when I walk around the locker room, there isn’t a familiar face like that anymore. I occasionally walk past Brick and we look each other in the eye. Without saying it, we give each other a sign of respect. We have been through a lot in this business and our bodies show it. I know what he has been through, and he knows what I have been through. And I hope he realizes I give him a pat on the back for sticking it out. Everybody else has left, said goodbye, and retired. But not Brick. Not I. Maybe we’re too stupid to realize when to call it quits. Or maybe we are just too damn stubborn. I stand to my feet and roll underneath the ropes, the audience is on their feet, glorifying the name of Josh. I lift my head up towards the ramp and slowly make my way back to the locker room. Maybe this will be the last time I lace up these boots and walk down this ramp. I have nothing to look forward to anymore. I lower my head, ashamed of myself and walk up the ramp. I stop at the top and turn around and look at the fans, all of their attention towards Josh, who is still celebrating. I’ve done my time in this business and the adrenaline will forever always pump through my veins. Until my one last gasping breath, I’ll bleed GW blood forever? “forever “and ever. <hp> “It was just a loss, Sage.” I lift my smothered head from the bosom of her comforting shoulder? At least she is trying to help? “It’s not about the lost, Cherish. It’s not about losing the Master of the Mat. It’s about having the ability to survive in a ring with the future of the GW. And I don’t think I can do it anymore.” “You can’t just give up. You worked your whole entire life for this. You gave up so much to move to America and wrestle here. You don’t want to make that pointless now, do you?” She doesn’t understand. If she was my age, and in my shoes, I’m sure she could see where I’m coming from. But she can’t, she is in her 20’s, approximately half my age. She doesn’t understand. She just doesn’t understand? “That was the past Cherish?” Anger and immaturity control my words. I take a deep breath, smelling her rich perfume through my nostrils while she runs her hands through my white hair. White from stress and old age. I begin to think before I speak? “I suppose you’re correct, honey. I’m just not thinking clearly. Perhaps by the morning, I’ll start to work this out?” “Good idea. Let’s give it a rest and we’ll talk about it tomorrow?” I take another deep breath and press the gas pedal with my foot and slowly drift out of the parking lot? <hp> Lying in my bed the whole entire night, my whole entire life and career runs through my mind. I begin contemplating retirement. And I begin to ask myself, “what if”. Curiosity killed the proverbial cat, but if you stand your ground, it never hurt to wonder. Maybe I should have stayed at home and taken over the land, it may not be what I wanted to do, but at least my family would never have abandoned me. Or I would have never abandoned them. What happened if I wrestled in the small circuits in China, so I could even stay home. There are so many things I could have done with my life, why did I pick Global Wrestling? It’s not that I regret coming here, but maybe I should have quit when I got killed. Maybe I should have accepted my fate instead of fighting it. The Bible reads that there is no such thing as pain in heaven, I guess that I would have missed that too much though. What happened if? What happened if? What happ? My eyes grow heavy and my eyelids close. It was merely impossible to keep them open any longer. Hopefully I’ll dream about something worthwhile tonight to ease my troubles? <hp> I can hear my breathing pattern in my head. I keep my eyes closed. I clinch my fists and stretch my arms out over my head. My legs straighten out and I let out a small yawn. I roll over on my back and open my eyes a little. Soma stands in front of me, like he has been watching over me throughout the night.... “Morning, Leinad.” “Greetings.” “What can I help you with?” “Perhaps the question should be, “how can you help me?”.” Without asking, I know what he is talking about? “Cherish came to me last night after we returned and said that you weren’t feeling yourself lately.” “I don’t know anymore, I’m just having one of those bad couple days in a row I suppose.” “A loss is a loss, Sabbot. I know how bad you wanted the victory, but you didn’t achieve it. It is just something you are going to have to deal with.” “No, it has nothing to do with Master of the Mat. It’s just, I don’t know...” I don’t want to tell him, I know he’ll look down on me for even considering retirement? “You don’t know, what?” “Leinad, I’m thinking about retiring.” “Retiring?” “Yeah, I just don’t know if I can take it anymore. I mean, these young guys are really giving me a challenge these days.” “You prefer an easy victory?” “No, but it was different back then. I wasn’t hurting so much every night. And I don’t know, everything was different back then.” Soma nods his head? “Things definitely have changed since then. Nobody is going to give you an easy victory, Sabbot. Everybody on the roster wants that win, just as much as you do. This is a way for them to earn a paycheck, for guys like you and I, we do it because we are too stubborn to stop.” “Well, maybe it’s time to grow up and face the facts, Leinad.” “Alright, alright, Sabbot. Go ahead and quit. Retire.” “What?” It sure was weird to have Soma agree with me on something of that matter? “Retire. If you really want to, I can’t stop you, right?” “I suppose so.” “I just find it ridiculous that you are going to do something that you have never done before. You have never quit or backed down from a challenge. Do you remember back when we faced The Kindred at Crossfire” Remember when I asked you if you were up to the challenge?” “Yeah?” “What did you tell me?” I pause? ?Sabbot, what did you tell me?” “I said that I was up for it.” “And you gave me the idea that you weren’t a quitter. That you never gave up, Sabbot. And look what you are doing now, you sicken me.” “God dammit, Leinad. You dazzle in your immortal realm. You grow old, but your bones don’t weaken. You don’t even know what the fuck it’s like. You don’t have to worry about dying every fucking time you go to sleep at night. You haven’t seen the horrors and the hell of this world, let alone experienced them. How the fuck can you judge me, brother” How can you judge me?” I scream at my only friend. I scream so hard, tears begin to fall from my eyes. I expect Soma to punch me in the thick skull, but instead, he reaches his hand down towards me’and I accept it. He pulls me inside and gives me a brief hug’for our brotherhood. “Follow me?” Looking into his eyes, seeing my own reflection being cast away by a tear? Wherever he leads, I shall follow?
