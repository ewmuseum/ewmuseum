The Gray Area: So Many Funerals, So Little Time.

8 June 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06.08.04 'Jushin's passing had taken priority in the days before. To say that events leading up to it weren't important would be foolish if not outright insanity.' - Maverick Reno On the day before Jushin's death, Maverick Reno and cohorts, Aiden and Tobias de Sarret, attended a funeral. The funeral of Gohei Nakazura. The Tokyo Temple in Tokyo was the site of this great funeral. Members of the funeral party consisted of family friends, Yakuza guards and officials, Routakai from umpteen lineages, and business associates. The funeral had gone on for hours... I had watched but only had been half there. By this point, chanting had occured, messages of condolence, as well as the offering of incense. That however, continued... members of the Yakuza and friends of the Nakazura family gave offerings of incense to Gohei Nakazura. My mind seemed distant, the funeral bringing to surface memories that I had dared not think of in quite some time. Gohei Nakazura's funeral continued and thus, continued to trigger memory after memory. The priest chants in remembrance of the fallen Buddhist... my mind finds meaning in a lifetime's ending. A funeral. Every life has its beginning and its end. The alpha and omega. The alpha is the birth and the omega, the death. The birth is marked by a party; marked by happiness and great joy. The death is marked by a funeral; the gathering of family and friends, by sadness and tears. Every culture has a final ceremony although they differ greatly between beliefs. In the end, they're all meant for the same purpose: A final farewell. Memories. Gone. Those who have inhaled their final breathe; some due to my sickle. As I stood there, watching the ceremony I came to realize that this was not simply in memoriam of Gohei Nakazura. This represented every life I had taken, every funeral that I had helped plan, and never attended. Aiden and Tobias were with me although, neither of them paid as much attention as I. The Buddhist ceremony was one to truly consider as respectful. Not only were the family members close to the arrangements, but close to death as well. On the day before, they had helped in the crematorium ceremony in which the body is burned and then the bones are picked out with chopsticks. Gohei Nakazura met with the most honorable ceremony possible. My mind flashed to an American Mortuary; I imagined one by one what those funerals must have been like. Anthony “Cicero” Stecchino had met with a death more violent than most. He was abducted and drugged before being wrapped in plastic. Then, his body, complete with his plastic shell, were taken to the nearest river and dropped in. We made sure he was awake for the entire thing. I had not attended his funeral the following month, after authorities had found the body during a routine clean up. What could it have been like? An Italian family, circling their deceased as he lay in a closed casket. It was definitely a closed casket; the water plus the fish that live in the river. I’m sure that he was badly ripped apart, warped and decayed. Not to mention eaten? no, I had not attended the funeral. I did send a wreath though. I didn’t let my mind rest here long. The funeral before me continued, the priest speaking to the crowd before him. Most of the group were Routakai’s from random Yakuza families. For a less respectful crew, this would have been prime-picking for anyone wanting to damage the Yakuza. My mind began to drift again. This had become a common problem in my off hours. Not only did my mind enjoy wandering, but it enjoyed the most twisted of thoughts; anything that could cause me pain, my mind loved to ponder. Ryan Farmer. His wife. My god. His daughter. I received a letter announcing his death; it was from an old college friend. They held all three funerals at one time so that their families could get on with their lives. So many people. So many tears. A garden of flowers and a veritable Niagara of tears. I wonder what was said. “They were so young.” I wonder what was thought, “He had it coming.” I wonder ? I wonder how many of their family members swore to kill the man who did this. God, the pain they must have suffered. Losing an entire branch of their family trees at one time. Amber, Ryan, and their daughter Lillith. Reno clenches his eyes tightly. My. God. How could I have done that? So cold-heartedly slaughtered them? I swung my sickle and ended three beautiful lives! I wonder? I wonder what could have happened to that family. Would Lillith have grown to become someone loved? Did I, indirectly, slaughter even more? She would have gone through school. Hated high school until she met that perfect guy? then that would have ended. She would have graduated and majored in psychology, just like her father. “She’s following in her daddy’s footsteps” they would have said. She’d fall in love? she’d graduate and get her masters? and then her doctorate. She’d get married to a great guy. They’d have. “Maverick.” Maverick turns slowly, glancing at Aiden who now stands about five feet from him. Reno forces himself to remain stern and stable, not showing his weakness, even to his allies. “What is it, Aiden?” “I just thought you should know that John just called. The last of the Greensino’s have been taken care of.” The news strikes Maverick bluntly, his face showing no joy over the news. Maverick simply nods and turns, his mind returning to the topic but this time, with distance. ? I didn’t attend. I did send a wreath. ? the funeral is soon to end. I must focus on this; I must keep my eyes open. Stephen Perdue. Lois Houlihan. The funeral wasn’t covered by Stephen’s insurance; it was a homicide-suicide case. Actually, Lois? was but Stephen’s wasn’t. I fronted his funeral cost; it made me appear as the mournful friend. I wasn’t able to attend the funeral but I sent my condolences to their son of 17. I told of how much I would miss Stephen and Lois. How they were truly my dearest and closest friends. I had of course, lied. Stephen betrayed me. I feel no guilt for this. My sickle would slay neither of these people. Stephen shot his wife and then himself? not without my influence, but in the end, that’s all that really matters. ? I atleast sent a wreath. Poor, Gohei. He’s only the beginning. Only the alpha of a line of dishonors. Jushin ? he will be my next victim. But not before the complete dishonoring of Gohei and the brothers alike. Luckily, Yamajin is already taking care of that? Tokyo Prefecture ? Japan “So what did he die from that was so dishonorable” The scene had changed completely. This was a meeting house of some kind inside of a large home on the side of a mountain somewhere in the Tokyo prefecture of Japan. Three men sit on one side of a table, three others on the opposite side; the table is at traditional Japanese height. Ocha has been served to every guest, this being a casual meeting of sorts. At this meeting, only two are dressed in the traditional kimono; the others are adorned in more western apparel. The question comes from one of the four men we’ve never seen. Two we have seen before, they are: Yamajin Routakai Daimura Routakai Sitting with these two men is another high ranking member of the Yamajin lineage. “Betrayal.” Responds Yamajin Routakai. There’s a moment of silence before he continues, allowing the others in the room to wonder as to what kind of betrayal is so dishonorable that it permits the complete exclusion of a Yakuza lineage. “Betrayal from within is what killed the Routakai Nakazura. Gohei’s own nephews, the last remaining of the Nakazura blood lineage, placed a bounty upon his head” it was carried out brutally at our meeting. Those of us who were there were poisoned; they had gotten to the sake that would be served.” A moment of silence passes in the room as the Yakuza members think through this explanation. “They used a powerful sleeping herb. We woke up in another room after the deed had been done!” Yamajin repressed a smile, his story being fully believed by the other members of the Yakuza who sat before him. The story would be passed from Routakai to Routakai until it was simply “understood.” At that point, he nor any of the other Routakai would have to explain the situation again. “Then we must take the nephews down. For betraying their uncle, they have also betrayed the Yazuka! They can not be allowed to live.” one of the men exclaims, slamming a fist upon the table. Yamajin holds a hand up, calming his ally. “It is being dealt with. Our American associates, as a sign of their respect for us, have sworn to slay the Nakazura brothers.” Daimura Routakai speaks, his words building and strengthening the foundation of Yamajin’s lie. “Now let us drink in remembrance of the great Gohei Nakazura and to our new, Western, Allies, The Upper Echelon.” Daimura lifts his glass and is quickly followed by the other men. They hold their glasses their to observe a moment of silence “in memoriam” of Gohei Nakazura. Everything was in place. Blood truly is thicker than water. -I guess being caught red handed isn’t always a bad thing. Near Tokyo Temple ? Tokyo, Japan Reno’s mind is much like a Rubicks cube, the longer it shuffles, the wilder the pattern. Reno’s mind no longer thought to the funerals of those he had slain. Instead, it felt like only yesterday? October 21st, 2002 ? Approximately 1:00 pm Stitz Mortuary ? Los Angeles, California The sky is clear, birds chirping high above. The Californian sun stares down, burning a hole in the Earth. The grounds of the mortuary were nicely kept featuring more trees than you’d see on a normal lot in Los Angeles in this decade. Or for the last five for that matter. Commercialism and Californication; the sun may rise in the East but it’s settled in a final location. Cars fill the parking lot, a limousine and a hearse waiting at the side chapel door. A busy mortuary parking lot; another symbol of death. A black Porsche Boxter slowly pulls into the lot and parks. The door opens and Reno steps out, adorned in a black suit, blue dress shirt and black tie. Upon his face, he wears a pair of dark sunglasses. His hair is shorter than its current state but still to his jaw-line. His face is clean shaven; Reno stands before us a different man. He walks slowly into the chapel, intermixing with the entering crowd. Reno walks into the chapel, his eyes scanning the room. Pews line the sides, filling quickly with people. Three caskets adorn the altar, bouquets and wreathes of flowers adorning the caskets and surrounding areas. A sign has been marked near the entrance: “In Memoriam, Brittany, Jessie, & Alicia Pierce.” Reno stops at the door and takes the pen in hand, signing the book of mourners. He reaches up with his left hand and removes his sunglasses, uncovering his brilliant jade-green eyes. Reno places the pen down and glances at the next person in line, granting a nod. With this, Reno walks around the corner and fully into the chapel. The faces come into view offering smearing makeup and hidden tears. Throats filled with proverbial lumps and hearts weighed down with the weight of several worlds. All three caskets were closed; no need to sadden the world even more. Reno walks forward slowly, the funeral not yet underway. He stops at the altar and reaches into his pocket to remove a white rose; he places this upon one child’s coffin, and a second upon the next’s. He reaches Alicia’s casket, and places a hand atop it, his eyes closing briefly in a moment of silent mourning. An eternity passes. A set of well informed tears well up in Reno’s eyes, his hand still in place. The picture says more than an art gallery could do if filled to the brim. Maverick reaches into his jacket and removes a set of roses; one white and one yellow. He places each upon her casket and turns, walking to a pew and sitting. In front of him sits the Pierce family. Another moment passes and a priest slowly walks to stand behind the altar, his eyes moving over the gathering. Words form in the air, the gathering coming to a silence. “Ladies and gentlemen. Truly today is of the saddest of days. We gather here to remember the lives of three beautiful creatures. Alicia Leah Pierce lived a full life. She was born here in Los Angeles; married a loving man gave birth to two beautiful children. Alicia and her children were and still are God’s children. They have ascended our plain and have joined our Lord, God Almighty, in their home eternal. They rest in his arms and in his graces, protected from the sins that plague our world.” A pause finds the lips of the priest as he speaks, the family and friends crying at the words being spoken. It’s always been a question: Does a funeral provide closure? or more grief? And yes they continue to somehow, deliver both. He continues? “Alicia, Brittany, and Jessie. These three souls were lost to us on this day but their journey continues. They simply walk with our Lord for the rest of their path. For this, we must not mourn” but rejoice! But let us, not cry for our late family? our late friends. Let us bow our heads and enjoy a moment of silence in memory of those that we love.” Everyone lowers their heads and closes their eyes, tears still finding a way out. Time passes. Heads are lowered, some in prayer, some in silence, some complete with thoughts of the lost friends and family. Finally, the priest speaks again, his words ringing forth over the lowered heads. “Now, Mr. Reno has requested to deliver a eulogy from both himself and Scott. They have spoken in the hospital and Scott has sent his words through Mr. Reno.” With this announcement, heads turn and whispered words are spoken. The majority of the gathering is wondering just who this Mr. Reno is until he stands and slowly approaches the altar. He turns slowly and glances out over the gathering, his eyes finding Deborah Pierce’s, tears smearing her makeup. He nods his condolence and opens his mouth, words forming as if they were rehearsed, “I’ve been trying to decide what I was going to say ever since Scott asked me to deliver a eulogy on his part. I know that there are so many that seem like better choices. Tyler, Kyle, Mr. Pierce. But the fact remains that Scott has bestowed this honor upon me” when I came in, I placed four roses upon the altar. Three white and one yellow. White roses stand for innocence and untamed love. It is with a sad and sorrow filled heart that I place these roses here, knowing that innocence has been lost. A yellow rose stands for friendship and peace. With this yellow rose, I offer my undying friendship to the three souls. I know this seems like very little, but please, allow me to explain.” Reno glances down and then up again, the congregation staring at him, “I have known Alicia for four years. I was there on the day that she and Scott were married. I stood in the waiting room with a few others on the night she gave birth to Jessie. She led a beautiful life and left memories that no tragedy can tarnish. She may not be on this Earth to speak with us, to hold our hands. But she is with us in every way, in our hearts and our thoughts. She is Scott’s guardian angel. She is her families guiding light. Alicia’s spirit is in everyone in this room” and with that spirit, Alicia remains. She remains by each person’s side? and by her sides, her beautiful children. What has happened is a tragedy, but know that Alicia is still with us and still loves us. She still helps us along our paths? and guides those of us who wish for her assistance. Know that Alicia’s heart ? has never stopped beating because of the love in this room.” Reno reaches inside of his coat and pulls out a letter, his hands unfolding it carefully. He waits, allowing his words to sink in before he speaks again. “Scott has asked that I say these words on his behalf. But I believe that they are suited for a more private audience.” Reno slowly turns and kneels before the triad of caskets. He lowers his head, reading Scott’s words to his family who lay before the congregation. He speaks loud enough for everyone to hear. “I don’t know how to begin this. I don’t even know what I wish to say. I wish I could be there. I wish this had never happened. I wish that I didn’t have to write this. But wishing, it only gets us so far. Or so I thought once. I wished for a life worth living. I wished for a reason to continue living my life. I wished for success and wealth. To the first, God granted me Alicia. To the second, he granted me Jessie and Brittany. To the third, he granted me knowledge that my family and I were happy. These are my wishes. These are my hopes and my dreams. You lie before me, awaiting God’s graces. I have been graced by God; I was blessed with three angels to share my life. And with this, I feel ashamed for wishing for more. But I must. I must wish for individual happiness in Heaven for my family. My wish for Alicia. Grant her the garden that we were unable to have here in Los Angeles. Grant her a green thumb and a good harvest. Grant her peace ? and an eternity beside our beautiful children. My wish for Brittany. Grant her a pony. I was unable to give her one in life but we tried our best to let her experience them every so often. Once or twice a month we’d take her to the Clideston Ranch and let her ride her favorite pony. Her name was Horsey” her name was Yvette, but Brittany had problems with that so we just called her Horsey.” The crowd, in tears, still find a bit of humor in that. The smile more saddening than any tear. “My wish for Jessie. My first born son. May he never lose his talent or love for art. May he get every chance to wish and dream, draw and imagine. May the world be his canvas and his imagination his paint. May love be his muse. Now, Lord, I ask for a fourth wish. A selfish wish. I know that you choose the length of time that I must spend here on your Earth, but please, make it as short as you can. For every day that I spend in this hospital, remembering my family, I lose more than anything else, my hope. Dear Lord, may this wish find you compassionate as I ask you to take from me the breath that you gave so that my hopes and dreams, my love and my muse be reunited with me.” Reno stops here and closes his eyes, tears streaming down his cheeks. He speaks again, a lump forming tightly in his throat, “Now, I must speak to my family. I have no idea what to say to you. I want to tell you what you mean to me. I want you tell you how much I love you. I want to tell you how much I’ve missed you. I can’t tell you anything that I haven’t already. I speak to you every moment of every day. With every beat of my heart, another thought goes out to you. To all three of you. To Alicia, my loving wife” to Jessie, my first born? to Brittany, my little girl. I love you all from the bottom of my heart and I wish I were there with you to hold your hands. I wish that I could hold you in my arms one last time and tell you that I love you. I wish? Reno pauses again and kneels his head before continuing “I wish” that it were me instead of you. I am glad that you did not suffer. I am glad that you are together? and I count the minutes until I am at your side. I will love you always. To my wife. To my daughter. To my son. You are my love and my guiding light. You are my way home and I will follow until the day that we are reunited. I love you.” Reno stands slowly and turns to face the gathering. Tears soak the faces of so many people. Tears flow like Niagara; enough thoughts to compile a book. Reno stands in the center of the chapel, the three caskets and floral decorations behind him. The only sounds are sounds of sobbing and weeping. Reno holds the letter in one hand, his head lowered as streams of water stain his face. The priest even appears to be choked up behind Reno. Finally, Reno summons the ability to speak again, “I didn’t know if I’d be able to read that in full. When I read it the first time, I couldn’t imagine reading it here today. But what I just read is truth. It is a father’s word. It is a husband’s word. Scott loved his family. He would do anything for them. What I have read to you is a testament of that. What I have read to you” I hope never has to be said by another for as long as this Earth revolves. May God grant everlasting peace and blessings upon Alicia, Brittany, and Jessie. And, in the knowledge of Scott’s wishes, may God grant his request so that the happiness he once knew can find him again. Thank you.” Reno does not return to his seat. He simply walks straight out of the chapel, his eyes downcast the entire time. As he reaches the door, he puts his sunglasses back on and simply walks out? the scene transitions “Alicia” Scott? Tears again stream down Reno’s face. Tobias stands up and nods to Aiden who speaks calmly to Maverick. “It’s clear, Mav. Want to go down now” Reno turns and glances at the temple, noting the lack of people. He nods slowly and descends slowly down a fire escape without a single word. Aiden and Tobias quickly follow My ability to express this pain? is the same as my ability to fill the entire ocean into but a single glass. My ability to know this pain? is much more difficult. Through osmosis and conversation, one can only understand so much the pain to lose your entire family, including a wife and two young children, in a single stroke. To know this pain is to be baptized in blood. To know this pain is to stare the devil in the face and never look away. To know this pain is Hell. Scott is going to Heaven for he has already done his time in Hell. “Follow me. I don’t care if they see us.” Reno spouts off suddenly, a hand moving up to adjust his sunglasses and wipe any stray tears from his face. This had not been your average Japanese funeral as many western techniques were used; specifically, the body had already been cremated and placed into two separate urns before the funeral day: one for each of his remaining family members to do with as they wish. The Buddhist priest continued his sutra chanting as the final members of the funeral offered incense in honor. The priest would then slowly make his way from the temple grounds where the ceremony had been held. The Nakazura’s had long since gone. The scene was now silent, the small crowd having diminished. Three men in black suits slowly approach from afar, nearing the spot where the funeral for Gohei Nakazura had been held. Flowers, mostly lotus?, were placed in remembrance of the fallen Routakai. The three men appear as a bad clich? as they stop at the site. The two men on the sides are dressed in similar suits with minute differences: Aiden wears a black suit, a common blue dress shirt underneath and a blue tie with a black pattern. On the other side, Tobias wears a red suit of the same type, a black turtle neck in place of the blue dress shirt. Both Aiden and Tobias are silent, a pair of Armani shades covering Aiden’s eyes while a pair of round shades, tinted red, cover Tobias?. In the center stands Maverick Reno, in a black dress slacks and a black Mandarin shirt, complete with a silver clasp at the neck. The coat has been replaced by a light black trench coat; upon his face, a pair of dark sunglasses. His hair has been tied back into a tight pony tail that cascades down the back of his neck. Reno stands, his hands clasped behind him. The view circles around them, showing every angle as Reno kneels before the site. He speaks softly as he withdraws a black and white rose from inside his trench coat. On either side, Aiden and Tobias withdraw a red and blue rose. The four roses are placed on the ground in unison, Reno’s voice stating their purpose, ? In memoriam.” In memory of Routakai Gohei Nakazura. In our world, history is written by those who live to see it pass. Those who are killed do not get to argue their side of the situation and it is with this light that we remember Gohei Nakazura ? a man whose fate was brought down by he who he loved so dearly, a man whose on nephews condemned to death by execution. The death was not even an honorable one. “We should get going, Mav.” Tobias speaks, his eyes shooting around. Maverick stands slowly and starts walking towards the road. Tobias speaks up again, this time his topic a little less immediate. “I didn’t see them today.” “You didn’t have to. They were here and you know it. That’s all that matters. After all, I didn’t come here because of them today.” Reno states as he walks. Aiden pulls a beeper from his side and presses a page button quickly and puts it away. The three men stop at the edge of the road as Reno continues, awaiting their pickup. “I came to pay my respect to Gohei.” A stretch black limousine pulls up in front of the men and a man steps from inside to open the door.
