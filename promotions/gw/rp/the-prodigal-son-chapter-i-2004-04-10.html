The Prodigal son: Chapter I.

10 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 10th, 2004. The prodigal son. 11:And He said, A certain man had two sons: ---- GW Headquarters, Las Vegas. Dec. 17th, 2004. 12:24 PM. Without knocking I turned the knob and shoved the door to Bubba McCoy's office open and stepped through pulling it closed behind me. I never bothered to knock, after being World Champion for 7 months one of the privileges you earn is the ability to step into the owners office without an invitation or even knocking. Bubba didn't seem to mind the fact that I hadn't knocked, but when I left his office today there would be something troubling him quite deeply. 'Chief, we need to talk.', I said bluntly, dropping into one of the leather chairs across from his desk, not waiting for an invitation. A surprised Bubba McCoy pulled his boots off of his desk and blew a puff of infernal cigar smoke out, as he removed a stack of papers from his lap and dropped them on his desk, looking up at me with legitimate concern in his eyes. 'What's up, sexy?', Bubba asked with a chuckle, attempting to coax a smile out of me. It didn't work. Folding my hands I placed them on my lap, allowing them to rest on my worn out Levi's. Staring Bubba in the eye with a stern look on my face I ignored his failed attempt at humor and gave him a point blank answer. 'I want out.', came my monotone response. 'Wait...', Bubba paused and scowled not bothering to finish his sentence as he waited for me to elaborate on what I had just said. 'I can't do this anymore, Bubba, I can't.', I admitted, shaking my head. 'What are you talking about, Josh? Explain, please...', I didn't look Bubba in the eye as he spoke, opting rather to stare at his neck and the top button of his white shirt, which was left open, I couldn't look him in the eye at this point. It would hurt too much. 'Listen, it's nothing personal. You've been like a father to me. More of a father than my very own father, but I can't do this anymore...', I sighed, my voice trailing off as I clasped my hands firmly together, wringing them in distress. 'What are you trying to say, champ?', Bubba asked, his tone serious and concerned. The term 'Champ' made me feel much lower at the moment, he had taken to calling me champ after my infamous record-shattering title reign, suddenly this situation became a lot more difficult. 'I want out, that's what I'm trying to say.', I choked the words out in spite of the ever rising lump of emotion growing in my throat. 'Out of what, Josh?', asked the befuddled Bubba McCoy, grinding his cigar out in the ash tray on his desk. 'Everything. Global...Wrestling...Life.', I gasped, clawing at the arm of my chair, clamping down on it in frustration with myself. 'Okay, explain to me, why you of all people, would want to walk away and leave all of this?', Bubba asked, his voice sympathetic yet convincing at the same time. 'Josh, you are quite possibly the most beloved competitor of all time in Global Wrestling! You are the greatest World Champion of all time!' Bubba punctuated his sentence by emphatically slamming his fist on top of the desk. 'It isn't about the fans.', I replied, shaking my head, refusing to look up. 'Is it the money?', he asked, reaching into his shirtpocket and pulling out a blue inkpen. 'If it's about money, then let's talk, what do you want? How many zeros would you like to see on your next paycheck?' Shaking my head I turned the offer down, 'Money isn't an issue either.' 'What's eating you, kid?', asked Bubba, returning his pen to the pocket of his white shirt. 'I just got a call from my family yesterday. My family...the family that disowned me when I decided to pursue my dreams of Professional Wrestling.', my knuckles turned white as I clenched the armrest of my chair a little harder. 'Is that good...or bad?', the patient Bubba McCoy asked, interlocking his fingers and resting them atop a stack of papers on his desk. 'It was bad.', came my abrupt answer. 'They've only talked to me three times in the past five years, since the day I left home. The previous two calls were to talk me out of my quote-unquote, silly dreams.', I couldn't resist adding sarcastic quotation marks with my fingers as I mentioned this. 'But yesterday they called to inform me that my grandfather had died, he was my hero Bubba.' 'Josh, I...I don't know what to say. I...just...wow, I'm sorry, buddy.', a stunned Bubba McCoy said, his tone of voice telling the truth regarding his concern. 'That isn't all.', my bluish gray eyes were shining with tears, I closed my eyelids over them in an attempt to keep the tears from running, it didn't work, as a tear slid from beneath the canopy of my eyelid and rolled down my cheek. 'They called to let me know that he had died, and to remind me that...I wasn't invited to the funeral. My entire family has made a request. They've requested that I stay far away from everyone until I get my life straightened out.' 'Straightened out?', Bubba reacted in surprise. '...and their right.', I said tearfully. 'I don't see how you can even begin to believe that their right. Josh, you're a great kid, don't let anyone tell you any different, okay?', Bubba's tone was firm, yet contrastingly gentle and reassuring at the same time. 'No, their right. Bubba, look at me. I'm a tattooed freak that doesn't own a pair of jeans without a freakin' hole in them. What kind of role model am I? I chose the name 'American Youth', because I thought I represented the youth of America...but now, I pray that the youth of America doesn't turn out like me.', my tears were no longer contained as they slid down my face, dropping from my cheek and falling onto my sleeveless white 'Marvel' shirt. 'What in the..?', Bubba paused, his face twisting into a mask of obfuscation. 'I can't believe this. The guy that held the World title for 7 months, smashed someone's brains in with a kendo stick wrapped in barbed wire, the very same guy that spit his own blood into his best friends eyes...is crying?' Bubba shook his head in surprise, stunned disbelief. 'You don't understand, Bubba. All my life, I've tried to get my parents to accept me, yet for whatever reason they never would. I was never perfect like my brother, I was always the kid who screwed things up no matter how hard he tried to do it right. Eventually they stopped caring and so did I. I made my decision to become a Professional Wrestler against their wishes and they disowned me. Can you even begin to relate?', the words came out in a tear-blinded emotional blur. 'Josh, you can't do this. If you quit Global, then yes, we will lose arguably the biggest star of all time, but you'll lose out even more. There are thousands of fighters, clawing for your spot, wishing they could be half as good as you are. Are you really willing to walk away and let someone try to take that spot? Are you going to allow a lesser competitor the chance to soil the great reputation that you built for the World title? Think about what you're doing Josh. Don't think about what you're doing to Global...think about what you're doing to yourself.', the ring of Bubba's voice was truthful and heartfelt, yet my mind was made and he knew that as I stood to my full height of 5'10, looking across the desk, my bluish gray eyes blurred with tears. 'Thank you for the chance you took on a rookie, a year and a half ago, Bubba.', I choked out, reaching across the desk for a handshake. He stood, took my hand and shook it. He didn't stop there, pulling me in and throwing his big, powerful arms around me, squeezing me close with a hug. 'You weren't just another Wrestler to me, Josh...you were like a son.', Bubba nearly crushed me in his arms before releasing me. 'I hate to see you go, but when you do decide to come home, let me know. I'm going to call my lawyers and have them terminate your contract today, you're free to go.' I sighed, it felt so odd to walk away from what I had worked so hard for the past five years. 'Thank you, Bubba.', was all I could manage as I turned away and walked away. I pulled the metal door open once again and tugged it shut behind me. Letting out another painful sigh, I wiped my eyes with the palm of my left hand. Looking back at the nameplate on the door, I felt my heart grow much heavier as I took a step away from the door. ---- 12: And the younger of them said to his father, give me the portion of goods that falleth to me. And he divided unto them his living. ---- GW parking garage, Las Vegas. Dec. 17th, 2004. 12:42 PM. Glancing up in the rear view mirror of my black Jeep Wrangler I noted that my tears had subsided, leaving a pair of red eyes as the only evidence of my weeping and broken heart. I stared in the mirror a little harder, examining the person responsible for touching so many lives in far different ways. The man in the mirror has been called many things. A role model for teenagers. A hero for children. A mentor for the up and comers of this business. A loyal friend and partner. A great athlete and inspiring competitor. But those were just the good things, the sweet nothings whispered into my ears by an adoring fanbase, co-workers, friends, partners, journalists, and corporate enthusiasts hungry to make a buck off of my marketable name. Then there were the things I had been called by my enemies, the anti-Wrestling portion of the media, and jealous competitors. A loose cannon. Reckless inside the Octagon. Too daring for my own safety. A poor example of how to remain injury free in this industry. But even those things were nothing, I had never been wounded by those words. I laughed or shrugged them off. I had faced many challenges on the road to success and defeated them all. Their dares had simply been rocks kicked in my path and it was up to me to use the rocks for stumbling blocks or stepping stones, I chose the latter. Yet I wasn't invincible, no matter how many times I had stood in the ring and proclaimed that I was Superman, or invincible I knew, deep down in my heart that I wasn't. Yet at the same time I had no fear of my vulnerability because no one knew my Achilles heel. Anytime someone had found a secret that they believed to be my kryptonite I had always managed to overcome it and show them that they would need to find something that worked. I had been called a thousand things, and had ten thousand things said about me, it never mattered unless it came from the point that truly was my kryptonite...my family. They had called me numerous names that I had taken to heart. Stupid. Worthless. Useless. Disappointment. Failure. Lazy. Slacker. Degenerate. Good-for-nothing. Somehow all of their lies had managed to cake to the surface of my heart hardening it, and making my life so much more complicated than it should have been. The last time I had seen my father, he was on the floor courtesy of a right cross to the jaw that I had delivered to him when he tried to 'slap some sense into me'. It was no surprise that my family didn't want to see me again, even at such a somber occasion as my grandfathers funeral. But my mind was made...I would change. I would change to make them happy; even if it meant walking away from everything that I loved. 'Goodbye Global.', I said painfully. Reaching into my pocket I extracted a gray Cingular cell phone, I selected the name 'KPJ' from the list of contacts and punched the appropriate button to dial, a second or two later it was ringing. I had dialed his home phone in New York with the knowledge that he probably wouldn't be home. I wanted to let him know, yet I didn't have the heart to tell him that I had just dropped everything I loved. The phone rang a second time, then a third time and an eventual fourth before the answering machine kicked in. 'This is Ken, I can't come to the phone right now, but be sure to leave your name and number and I'll get back to you, the first chance I get. Thanks!', Ken's recorded voice ended and the machine beeped, eagerly awaiting my message. 'Ken, this is Josh.', I sighed, stating the obvious. 'I...well, I just wanted to call you and tell you, since I thought you should be the first to know...', I swallowed hard, trying to keep my voice strong. 'I just quit Global. Don't worry, I left on good terms. I'll explain later but right now, life is kind of crazy. I'm sure you'll understand.' Pressing the 'end' button on my display panel I dropped the cell phone onto the seat next to me, throwing my head back against the neck rest I slouched in my seat allowing my shaggy black hair to fall over my face. Turning the key, ever so slightly I reached for the dial on the radio and flipped the power on, music entered my ears, blaring through my sound system. '...with the plastic eyeballs, spraypaint the vegetables, dog food skulls with the beefcake pantyhose, kill the headlights and put it in neutral, stock car flaming with a loser in the cruise control, babies in Reno with the vitamin D, got a couple of couches, sleep on the loveseat. Someone came saying that I'm insane to complain about a shotgun wedding and a stain on my shirt. Don't believe everything that you breathe, you get a parking violation and a maggot on your sleeve. So shave your face with some mace in the dark, saving all your foodstamps and burning down the trailer park. Yo, cut it, soy un peredor, I'm a loser baby! So why don't ya kill me? Double barrel buckshot. Soy un peredor, I'm a loser baby! So why don't ya kill me?' My eyes widened as I realize the song that had begun to play. 'Loser' by Beck. Before it could continue even further, I switched the radio off, shaking my head. 'Was that a sign or something?', I asked, voicing my concern to The Almighty. He didn't reply and I didn't blame Him for His silence. I hadn't been overly appreciative of His graciousness as of late. It seemed I only talked to Him when I needed something or I was a little upset about the latest predicament I had caught myself in. What kind of friend only comes to you when he needs something or he wants to complain about the latest thing you've done? Not a very good friend, that's for sure. Somehow I couldn't blame Him for not answering right away, if the role had been reversed I wouldn't have answered either. ---- 13: And not many days after the younger son gathered all together, and took his journey into a far country, and there wasted his substance with riotous living. ---- Josh residence Jacksonville, FL. Dec. 18th, 2004. 3:18 PM. My palms were sweaty, hardly something I could blame on the 65 degree Florida weather as I parked my black Cavalier that I had rented from Thrifty, as soon as I stepped off of the 747 from Vegas. As I switched the engine off I didn't step out of the car immediately, instead I glanced around, looking at all of the other vehicles parked nearby. Two cars of various sizes, two mini-vans, a looming truck, and a mid sized SUV, were parked throughout the driveway and in front of the house, the house was packed. My eyes traveled around the vehicles and I cringed as I spotted an Astro with Nebraska tags, that meant only one thing...my parents, the people I hadn't seen in the past five years; the people I didn't care to see again. 'Help me out here, God.', I whispered towards Heaven as I jerked the keys out of the ignition and stepped from the car, closing and locking the door behind me. I took a moment to stare at my reflection in the window of the car, it had certainly changed since yesterday. My face had been cleanly shaven for the first time in two weeks, my normally scattered hair was only slightly mussed and my attire had pulled a complete 180. Gone were the torn jeans and comic book t-shirts, in their place I was adorned in a pair of khaki slacks, and a short sleeved button up shirt of a gentle brown shade. On my right wrist I had covered up my remaining visible tattoo with a leather cuff. For the first time in memory I had abandoned my Chuck Taylor's and opted for a pair of brown Doc Martens. I was almost saddened to see myself in this manner, yet at the same time I intended to look presentable for my family. It wasn't about me this time, it was about them. I was about to begin my journey toward the house before I spotted something out of the corner of my left eye: My eyebrow ring. With a scowl I began the task of removing it and within a matter of seconds I had pulled it free, I dropped it into my left pocket before stepping away from the car and onto the sidewalk. For luck, I kissed the cross pendant that hung about my neck as I proceeded up the walk with a host of butterflies fluttering around in my stomach, playing marco polo in a pool of stomach acid. The heels of my Doc Martens clicked on the sidewalk as I walked towards the looming white house before me, my mind grasped in wonder at what might lie in wait behind the curtained windows and closed doors. Dismissing these thoughts I stopped at the door and raised my hand to knock, I stopped as I noticed the doorbell, it had been far too long since I had been here. The exterior changes had been minimal. A lawn gnome or two had strayed from their old spots and apparently been relocated, standing amidst a flowerbed, surprisingly dead, killed off by the gentle frost of a Florida winter. With a flicker of hope in my eyes, a cloud of doubt in my mind and a heavy load resting on my heart I raised my hand, I pulled it back to knock on the door...and stopped. 'I can't do this.', I stated fearfully, turning on my heel to walk away. I had only taken one step when I turned back to the door, staring at the large metal frame. '...but I have to do this.', I decided, talking only to myself and the chubby little robin that had landed in my grandparents lawn and set to work on uprooting an earthworm from the yard for a nutrious lunch. Raising my hand again I curled it into a fist as I prepared to knock and then I froze; again. 'No...I cant...I just can't do this.', I cried out softly, forcing myself to remain calm so that my muscles wouldn't tremble. I closed my eyes, shook my head, I knew it was time to turn and walk away...then I saw his face. ---- His face was weathered but not overly wrinkled and as usual it bore a smile. Glasses were perched on his face and a shock of white hair had fallen over his eyes, he wore a white short sleeved dress shirt, a pair of khakis, and brown shoes. His smile was warm and inviting. I was 19 years old, perched on the top turnbuckle of a Wrestling ring in Hogknee, Arkansas, Wrestling for a cheap redneck promoter by the name of J. Pape. The man was atrocious, potbellied and cross-eyed he wore Sneakers and rustler jeans with a suit jacket and Cowboy hat, he cheated at cards and was infamous for skipping out on the paycheck and leaving early for shows. To top it off he would on occasion get in the ring himself and stink it up all the while booking himself continually against the champion, even worse, popular rumor had it that he stole his ideas from the internet. But it didn't matter that Pape was a redneck, what mattered is the fact that he would take a chance on this rookie in his sixth professional match in America. It wasn't easy to get booked when you were just starting out and as wet behind the ears as could be. Pape took a chance on me, and I took a chance on my supper; knowing quite well that a paycheck was in question. That night was just like any other night on the independent circuit and similar to any of the 3 or 4 occasions I worked for Pape's federation, Showdown Wrestling Incorporated. The payoff was small and so was the crowd, the fans crowded into the high school gymnasium would top out at 200 every show, but tonight the other 199 fans didn't matter, just my grandfather sitting in the front row his face alive with a proud smile. Looking at my grandfather gave me courage, and I quickly leapt from the top turnbuckle throwing my body into a somersault landing on top of my larger opponent with a 450 splash! I hooked the leg with my left arm, using my right to clutch my ribs in pain. The referee dropped to the canvas for the cover... One... Two... Three! It was over, it was all said and done and I had came out victorious. Shoving myself to a vertical base the referee took my hand and raised it into the air signifying my victory, as soon as he released my wrist I dropped to the canvas and rolled out of the ring directly in front of my grandfather. I didn't say a word, neither did he as I reached over the guardrail and threw my arms around him in a tight hug. He ignored the sweat dripping off of my body and returned the warm embrace, squeezing me tightly in his arms before letting me go. As we released one another I turned to slowly walk back to the curtain and at that tender young age I suddenly felt more alive than ever and something inside me told me that I would make something special of myself in this business. ---- Releasing a deep breath I raised my hand to the door and paused, balling it into a fist I closed my eyes in whispered prayer. 'God, I know we haven't talked a lot lately, but I think You and I are going to be speaking a lot more often now, so hey, would You mind helping me out here? It'd be greatly appreciated.' I opened my eyes and rapped my knuckles rapidly against the door three times. I paused, waiting for a response, behind the door I heard a chain being unhooked and a lock being turned over. A hand was placed on the knob and it turned slowly making me wish that I was anywhere but here at the moment. Mustering all of my courage I anxiously awaited to see who would be standing behind the door. As it opened...there she was, my grandmother. Her eyes were red, her face showed signs of stress and her graying hair was a mess yet when her eyes met mine everything changed as a smile stretched across her features. 'Josh!', she cried out as she threw her arms around my neck and kissed me on the cheek. 'I knew you would come.' 'Are you okay, Gram?', I asked as I returned her hug. 'I wasn't, but now that you're here...I am. This family isn't the same without you, Josh, welcome home.', she said softly and sincerely, kissing me again on the cheek. I sighed softly, a sigh of relief, I was a place that I never expected to return to...home. To be continued. All scriptures were taken from the KJV 1611, Luke Chapter 15.
