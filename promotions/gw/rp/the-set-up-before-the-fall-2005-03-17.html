The Set-Up Before The Fall

17 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/17/2005 Car services we a great thing to be able to afford. Not as prissy and pompous as driving around constantly in a limousine, but much better than utilizing the taxi services which were not always reliable like they were in New York. He let the driver stick his suitcases in the trunk, then slipped into the backseat of the Lincoln Town Car. The drive through the city was fun, getting to catch the early morning before there was so much traffic it marred the landscape. He was looking forward to a series of interviews and appearances. Old friends had been happy to hear from him, and the arrangements to do talk shows and appearances were quickly arranged. There was still a market for a man like himself, and he would take advantage. The best place to hide was in front of the world, where no one looked for you. He would continue the search for the truth about his brother, protect his family and everything would be covered by the new job. Memories of the past were calling to him more and more now. He remembered the good times now, thinking about his mother and father who had passed, their love linking them in death as well as life. Some connections were so strong they were unexplainable to most. Jake knew they couldn’t live without each other, couldn’t be sustained without the love of each other and so the funeral of both of them dying on the same day was natural to him. One died of heart disease, and the other died of a broken heart. His brothers were the youngest, purest of them all. They had that connection which all twins seemed to have between them. They were almost one being instead of two, but they also had their own identity quirks. Melancholy doesn’t suit me? He shook it off, and opened the Zero Halliburton briefcase he carried with him in the back of the car. The briefcase had the best lock in the business, and he used it to protect his information. He pulled the journal out and began reading where tragedy truly unfolded. Who would have though Vernon would play a role? . . . . . . . . . . . . . Vernon, Texas was a small town with plenty of history behind it. Originally, cotton farmers who wanted a place away from most of the world to live however they chose settled the town. Unfortunately, the town chose to settle as slave owning farmers who wanted to make sure they could be as profitable as possible without regard to humanity. After the civil war the slaves were set free, and most of them remained in the small town. Vernon was another town that ended up with strict rules of conduct, racial separations and a lot of racial tension. The oil boom saw an increase in money for the town when oil was discovered. The jobs changed the rules as a black family owned the land the oil was discovered on. The racial rules changed as the population began being divided by means of money instead of race, but the tension was always there. Several of the residents of town came from the family lines of some prominent Texas families. The town eventually settled down into routine existence that allowed the population to live together peacefully without flare ups. When Xavier was young he would go visit his older family in Vernon. Xavier always remembered joking that Vernon was where the old people came from. Xavier’s great grandfather was a wonderful man, and he owned a farm in Vernon. The farm had been successful, but eventually the family did not want to live or work on a farm anymore. Upon his great grandfather’s passing the farm fell into a period of no use. The family tried to sale the farm, but by then the market was bad and no one wanted to risk buying the farm. Eventually, the farm became a family get away. They occasionally held family reunions at the farm, and overall it was used for retreats away from the rest of the world. Xavier called his family to make sure he could borrow the farm for a vacation. The family had no plans, and so Xavier was cleared to use it. Xavier drove up to Vernon from Laredo, a trip that took twelve hours even as fast as Xavier drove. Xavier stopped along the way at some of his favorite roadside restaurants, including the Jim Bowie Restaurant in Bowie, Texas. Xavier’s family had always stopped at the home-cooking establishment whenever visiting family in Vernon. Xavier had his traditional meal. Chicken fried chicken, mashed potatoes, green beans, fried okra, sweet potato rolls and homemade chocolate pie with homemade whipped cream for dessert. The meal was heavy, and Xavier felt sated when he finished it all. Bowie wasn’t much of a town, named for a famous Texan yet with nothing there except a few thousand residents. Xavier only used the restaurant and the Texaco across the highway for gas and refills of his drinks and snacks. Xavier enjoyed drinking Coca-Cola, Chili Cheese Fritos, Ho-Hos, and the usual favorite snack Twinkies. Xavier loaded up on the snacks and continued on through Wichita Falls up to Vernon. Xavier took the Main Street exit off the highway and headed right away from the town. Xavier’s great grandfather’s house was thirty miles outside of town, past anyone and everything because none of the land East of Vernon was being actively worked by any farmers. Xavier realized the location was perfect for the meeting that was being scheduled by the Cartel and Mexican Mafia. The North Texas area they were in was not large enough to be controlled by either organization. Wichita Falls was the biggest city, but it was ruled by the independents who bought the product wherever they could get it from. There would be no one around to listen in to the meeting, nothing around to draw interest, and most importantly no one to interfere or hear the gunshots if the meeting were to turn sour. The old factory would serve as a graveyard for everyone if the meeting failed, and Xavier’s plan was to make sure the meeting absolutely failed. Main Street became the Rural Route he was looking for, and Xavier was soon at the turn for the estate. Xavier pulled through the open gate and made his way up to the homestead almost a full mile up the gravel path. The farm had been partially converted to a ranch long ago. There were three hundred acres surrounding the estate. Two hundred acres were once used to grow cotton, the favorite cash crop of the area. Seventy five acres accounted for the grazing land converted to support the longhorn cattle that were raised for sale and slaughter. The large home was a two story dwelling with a porch that ran all the way around the house. The porch was often used as a social area when the home had been filled with family, filled with life. The home had ten bedrooms, built for a wealthy family that once counted four generations inside the home. There was an additional home built behind the main house that was a one-story dwelling with three bedrooms. The second house was built for more room when it had once been needed. There were two well-sized barns on the property. One had been used for storage more than anything else, and the other had been sued to house the horses. Xavier parked between one of the barns and the homestead. Xavier made the rounds to check over the buildings. The homestead was maintained by one of the local families. Once every quarter they would come out to check over all the buildings, clean up any debris, and keep the entire area in general order. He looked under the frog in the desolate garden next to the main house and found the keys. Xavier unlocked the padlock on the bar, opened it and walked in. No livestock had been kept in the barn for many decades, so there was no smell of the beasts inside the structure. Xavier walked up and down, seeing the stalls now filled with weight machines and benches to serve as a gym. The machines were covered with tarps to prevent unnecessary wear and tear from the elements. The back end of the barn had been paved and carpeted which was unusual to say the least, but it had been made into a small living area with couched, chairs, a table, chairs and some end tables. There was a refrigerator, pantry and microwave to serve as a makeshift kitchen inside the barn. Xavier looked everything over, opening the doors to find an empty pantry and warm refrigerator also empty. Xavier looked down and saw the refrigerator was plugged in, but not running. There was a light switch on the wall beside the refrigerator. Xavier turned the switch on and the refrigerator began humming to life. Xavier saw another set of switches near the stairs on the right side of the barn. Xavier turned on all the switches and the fluorescent lighting on the ceiling of the barn came on. Xavier walked upstairs to the top level. There were six simple beds with a nightstand and lamp beside each. Xavier saw the entire area was bare, but he checked a closet on the wall, finding sheets and towels. He pulled one sheet out and smelled it to be sure. It smelled a little old, but it was clean. The entire place was ready to be inhabited. Xavier was surprised by how warm the barn was. Xavier walked over to the wall of the barn to check it out. The outside of the barn was painted red like so many barns were, but the wooden planks had been covered with sheet rock as if it was a house. Xavier assumed that there was plenty of insulation between the sheet rock and the wood. Xavier was satisfied with what he saw, and he believed it would help with the subterfuge they would need to pull off as well. He also knew that there was plenty of items he would need to pick up in town. It was seven o’clock, and the sun would go down very soon. Xavier would go get some food for the barn, make sure to pick up some personal items, and then come back and make his phone calls. Xavier stepped out of the barn locking it behind him. Xavier got back in his Excursion and left the farm. Xavier was making a mental list of what he might need, and the list would be a long one. Xavier remembered the town finally had gotten a Wal-Mart. The store was not as large as the ones he was used to, but it would fill his needs. Xavier quickly made it to town and found the Wal-Mart. He parked and went inside. Xavier got a basket and took what he felt would be the best approach. Xavier made his way up and down each aisle looking for anything he might need. The basket was filled quickly by all the goods he bought. They would need plenty of food able to be cooked in the microwave, eaten from the pantry or that could be made by adding boiling water. The food would be nothing like what Bertrand had cooked in Austin, but sometimes you were forced to suffer for the greater good. Xavier added some items he wanted for himself such as toiletries, snacks and books to read. Xavier took the overstuffed cart to the front of the store and waited in the check out lane. The Wal-Mart opening was a controversial event in the small town in North Texas as it had been in many small towns. Wal-Mart stirred emotions because many saw the encroachment of Wal-Marts on the small Texas towns an affront to the family values and core of beliefs that gave small towns their own life. The Wal-Mart had many good previously unavailable in small towns at affordable prices, and they also had lower prices on most regular consumer goods. All of these were points in Wal-Marts favor. The stores brought much prosperity in material possessions to the town, it allowed for the town to save money on cheaper goods and provided jobs to many people in the town. All of this was a small token compared to the toll it took on a small town. Wal-Mart was a chain store, and the chain meant it could provide a variety of goods and services it previously took multiple stores to fulfill. The Wal-Mart Pharmacy put the local pharmacy out of business with lower prices on drugs and cutthroat competition. Some of the local grocery stores would be put out of business because Wal-Mart would take the dry goods business. The local video stores lost revenue due to Wal-Mart was selling videos more cheaply so even their pre-rented copies no longer were as appealing. The list of effects went on and on and on. Wal-Mart had become a terrible bane on the small towns, and there did not seem to be any way to recover from the attack. The appeal of the store was quite seductive, and it could not be dismissed. Xavier got to the checkout counter and loaded his goods onto the conveyer belt. The clerk was a young woman who scanned every item quickly and efficiently. Xavier had done quite a bit of shopping, and the total was almost four hundred dollars. Xavier paid the bill in cash and took the cart filled with plastic bags to his vehicle. Xavier loaded everything up and returned to the homestead. Xavier had unpacked everything and put it away. Xavier sat down on the couch with his cellular telephone. Xavier dialed the number he had memorized. Sean picked up after two rings, “Hello?” “Hey Sean, it’s Xavier.” He responded. “Good to hear from you. Where are you?” Sean asked as he put away what he was working on. “I’m in North Texas just outside a little town called Vernon.” Xavier explained as he leaned back. “I’m very familiar with the area and the location of the meeting. I need you to bring Bulldog and Bertrand and get up here as soon a s possible.” Xavier rubbed his forehead as he spoke. Sean was excited to hear the final play was about to be made, “Sounds good. What do I need to bring to Vernon with me?” Xavier thought about it. He hadn’t really considered the plan of attack, but he was familiar enough with the factory that he could quickly figure out what would be needed. “We’ll need weapons of course because this is going to get ugly, and I would like you to bring some toys because we may want to reduce the entire scene to rubble. Bring it all man, so you may have to caravan up here.” “Got it.” Sean made mental notes, “I’ll get everything together and we should be up there in two days or so. We just need to make sure we have everything.” Xavier shook his head, “See if you can make it twenty four hours Sean. The meeting is soon, and we need to get together to map all of this out. Light a fire under Bertrand’s and Bulldog’s asses to get it done, get it done now and get up here.” “You got it. What else is there?” Sean was already looking around to figure out what special items to bring with him. Xavier sighed, “No, that should take care of most of it. Bring some money from what we took in Austin if we haven’t spent it all. We never know what we could need while we’re up here.” “Okay.” “Later.” Xavier closed his cellular telephone. Xavier needed some rest. His mind was not as sharp as it should have been, and he knew it. Soon they would all be together to make the final plans. Bulldog, Bertrand and Sean arrived in three separate vehicles. They were all surprised by the emptiness of the land and the structures. Everything seemed to be abandoned, although the condition of the buildings was in top shape. “Are we in the right place?” Sean asked as they walked around. Bulldog looked down at the directions he had written down, “We followed the directions I got from Xavier down to the letter.” Bertrand looked around with them. He enjoyed the look of the house, especially the porch. The entire set-up was something that he could imagine would lead to a calm life and joyful existence. He was jealous of the house and what joy it brought to the owner, “This place is beautiful.” “Beautiful?” Sean looked around. “This place is an abandoned shithouse!” “I think this is what they call a country estate isn’t it?” Bertrand countered. “This is the kind of place I would enjoy retiring to.” “Not me man, I’m a city boy.” Bulldog said to both of them. The barn door opened and Xavier came walking out. He yelled across to them, “Gentlemen!” They all turned and saw him. Xavier came over to them and they all shook hands, exchanging pleasantries. Xavier pointed to the far barn, “You’ll need to park the vehicles in that barn after we unload them. We have to make this place look uninhabited in case they check things out.” “If we’re in the house won’t they know there’s people here?” Bulldog pointed out. “Yes, that’s why we’re not staying in the house.” Xavier was smiling, knowing Bulldog wouldn’t like that answer. “I’m not going camping.” Bulldog said tersely. Bertrand laughed, “What” No sleeping bag and tent for you Bulldog?” “He snores bad enough indoors, the great outdoors would make the snoring even worse.” Sean joked. Everyone but Bulldog laughed at the comment. Xavier pointed to the near barn where had come from, “That barn is converted into a bunk house for lack of a better term. We’ll be staying in there. It’s self-sufficient although we’ll be living off the microwave instead of a stove. Sorry Bertrand, but the stove would draw attention.” “Your loss. No more good cooking for you.” Bertrand shot back. “Maybe I’ll finally drop some pounds!” Sean laughed. Bulldog was looking around the area suspiciously. “I did a check last night, and if the lights inside the bar are on it can only be seen from the side. If the lamps inside the upstairs area are on it cannot be seen from the outside, so your personal bed space is where we will live once the sun goes down.” Xavier was giving a run down. “How long do we have before the meeting?” Bulldog was back to business. “From today we have six days until the meeting commences. We need to recon the abandoned factory, make plans for handling the meeting and then set-up anything we want to put in the warehouse. If we wrap that up in two days we should be absolutely safe time wise.” Xavier walked everyone back to their vehicles. “Bring the vehicles over and we’ll unload.” Xavier walked over while the men all drove over to the barn. Xavier started with Sean since he was most likely bringing the most hardware. Sean was driving a Ford F-150 that was only a year or two old. The extended cab truck had all the amenities anyone could want, and the truck bed had a vinyl soft-top that was covering something. Xavier and Sean took the cover off exposing the boxes and bags beneath. Xavier and Sean worked to unload the hardware. They put the metal boxes, ammunition boxes and other containers inside the barn next to several wooden work tables. It took ten trips to get everything Sean had brought inside. “Planning to invade a small country Sean?” Xavier said as he put the last metal ammunition box down. Sean checked over all the equipment, “Well, I figured it would be best to be overprepared for the final operation. I don’t want us to lose out because we didn’t bring something, and no offense to this place but I didn’t think we would find everything we need in BFE.” “I can agree with that.” Xavier walked back to help Sean bring in his three personal bags. Once again Sean wasn’t sure what would be needed, so he had over packed. Bulldog had brought in his personal bags and then jumped on his cellular telephone. Bertrand had brought in his own bags and the computer equipment they had been using tot rack the data. They would use the computer to help formulate strategies and planning for the ambush style assault. Everything was in the barn. Xavier noticed Bulldog seemed very intent on his phone conversation, “Hey Bulldog!” Bulldog put his hand over the phone, “Yeah?” “Throw me your keys and I’ll move your car for you. After that we’re going to look around for a while to see how things are looking. You okay here” Xavier shouted upstairs. Bulldog walked over to the railing and tossed his car keys down to Xavier, “I’m fine, see you after while.” Sean, Bertrand and Xavier all moved the vehicles into the second barn where Xavier’s own Expedition was already parked. There was plenty of room as the barn was empty. They closed the barn and Xavier locked it with the chain and pad lock. “What do we do for fun while we wait around for the meeting?” Sean chided Xavier for the boring location he had chosen for them to hole up in. “There’s a wide variety of board games.” Xavier thought about it a few moments, “And besides, you’ll be working on some surprises soon enough.” Bertrand looked over at Sean, “Yes, and that always seems to capture your imagination.” Sean thought about the explosives, and he knew he was going to be kept busy while he got ready for the assault. “Well, I want to get a lay of the land before we turn in. Bertrand, want to see the surrounding area with me for a while?” “Yes, this would be helpful. We need to know what we’re going to be up against.” Bertrand followed Sean as they began walking the property. “Well, I need to grab a couple of things from the barn. Go check out the West side of the house first since won’t be operating much over there, then I can show you how the East side looks.” Xavier offered. Xavier headed back to the barn. Xavier slipped past the open door slowly and walked inside. He could hear Bulldog’s voice from upstairs talking on the telephone. Xavier walked as silently as he could toward the stairs as he listened into him. “I know that. I know all of that, but this has gotten too far! What are we going to do when you arrive in the next few days?” Bulldog’s voice was edging from anger to rage as he talked on the phone. Xavier went immediately on the defensive, knowing that Bulldog could only be talking about the operation. No one outside of the four of them, a Congressman and one of Xavier’s close friends knew about this operation. Xavier crept over to the table beneath the upper level to a bag he had left beneath the strong wooden structure. “Look, you and I agreed I would keep watch over Xavier while you operated on the other side. He’s coming too close now, and if we don’t change things then he’ll run right into you X. Is that what you want?” Xavier stayed under the second story, far back as he heard Bulldog walking across the wooden planks toward the railing. Bulldog was being very cautious. “Do you think I’m a moron X” He’s out with the other two doing some work to see what the grounds look like. If you don’t cancel or postpone the meeting then why not move it somewhere else? After the meeting this will all be over anyway. Just let this one go so we can stop this before it gets out of hand. You know where this is leading don’t you?” Xavier knew the pistol was loaded and ready. Xavier carefully turned the safety off as certain things began coming into focus. Bulldog had guided him away from the Russian Mafia on several occasions to keep him focused on the Cartel. Bulldog’s lifestyle was beyond the means of a former police officer, and Xavier’s mind had to know that even though his heart did not want to believe it. Bulldog was using Xavier for something, but he didn’t know exactly what for. “Okay, I know Xavier. He wasn’t all for this effort at the beginning, so I did what you said. I turned him into a fucking animal. He’s ready to take you out, them out and everyone connected to it. He knows doing so will start a war, and he’s aiming for that. Are you ready to face that down?” Xavier stayed in his spot, knowing the stairs would give him away if he tried to climb them to the top level. He knew that this was not going to be pretty, but he knew exactly what he had to do now. The path had no fork, and Xavier only had one place to go. “Fine, but when this thing comes apart don’t blame me!” Xavier heard the phone close. Bulldog cursed and then there was silence. Xavier slipped his pistol into the small of his back in his waistband. His mind raced, adrenaline flooded into him giving him small shakes and he took deep breaths to try and remain calm. A memory came flooding back into Xavier’s mind unbidden. Xavier remembered when he was a child he had decided to run away from home, but he couldn’t remember why. Xavier had no clue where to go, so he ended up going to his Uncle Bob’s place. Xavier hadn’t called Uncle Bob by his nickname Bulldog back then. Xavier had camped out on Uncle Bob’s back porch until he came home after his shift. This was during one of Bulldog’s separations from his wife because it had been a very rocky wedding. Uncle Bob had found Xavier on the back porch and let him come inside. Uncle Bob didn’t make a fuss about the incident. He explained he had to call Xavier’s parents and let them know he was alright. Uncle Bob had taken the time to cook a good meal for them. Uncle Bob’s culinary skills were limited, so he cooked breakfast for their dinner. Biscuits from a tube, scrambled eggs, bacon, hash browns and fresh squeezed orange juice made a nice meal. Xavier and Uncle Bob talked for a long time about everything. The discussion put Xavier at ease while he kept eating. Xavier stayed the night on the couch while Uncle Bob watched over him. Everything had been made better, and the next day Xavier returned home. The creaking of the stairs broke Xavier from his deep thoughts. Xavier looked over at the stairs as Bulldog descended them. Bulldog turned the corner to see Xavier looking at him angrily, fire and damnation obvious in his face.. Bulldog froze. He didn’t know how long Xavier had been listening in on the conversation, but by the look on his face he knew he would never be able to hide the truth anymore. The world was about to spin out of control. Xavier’s voice quivered as he spoke, “Why Bulldog?” Bulldog dropped the phone into his pocket, “That’s the million dollar question so to speak isn’t it my young friend?” “I’d say it’s worth a Hell of a lot more to me than money Bulldog.” The venom laced words had a seemingly physical force behind them. “What have I been doing this whole time Bulldog?” “You’ve been doing what you wanted to do!” Bulldog yelled back, but there was no passion in his voice. “You told me you wanted to do something about the flow of drugs into the country. You wanted to fight the drug trade, and I helped you do that.” “You’ve been working for them!” Xavier’s muscles were all tense. Bulldog leaned against the wall, “What is it you want from me” Do you think we’re going to be able to stop the flow of drugs? They call this a drug war Xavier, a drug war! That means we’re fighting against our own friends and family because that’s whose using this stuff!” Bulldog looked over at Xavier with empty eyes, “I fought against this entire thing my whole life, and the only lesson I took away was the choice would be that you can either keep fighting against it and lose or you can work with it and make some cash.” “I was being paid squat for my effort, and I wanted to provide for my family. I had kids who needed a mother at home with them, I had to pay for college and I wanted to provide a comfortable lifestyle for everyone. So I picked a side, and don’t act all high and mighty with me.” Bulldog looked down at the floor, not letting Xavier see his eyes. “The Romanoski Family has been slowly moving into Dallas for thirty years, and I’ve been working with them since I was a rookie fresh out of the academy. The choice was easy, and the payoff was good. I’ve dealt with independents, the Italians, the Mexicans, the Japanese, the Chinese and the Russians. They’re all the same, and I decided to make sure that I was taken care of.” “I provided security, I fixed evidence, I worked my way up to doing deals as an advisor and then I became the top fixer for the family. Internal affairs never knew about it, and I was set for life. I retired from the business when I retired from the force.” Xavier looked at Bulldog with something beyond contempt, “So now you’re setting me up to get killed. I want to know what’s been going on, and I want to know who Mr. X is because you obviously know who it is.” “You’ll find out when he wants you to find out, but I’m not going to tell you shit.” Bulldog had finally found some steel for his voice. Xavier edge was harder, colder and crueler than it had ever been before, “You taught me about justice bulldog. You used to tell me about law and about justice. Well, justice demands I do whatever I have to do to get the information required.” “What are you going to do” Beat me up? Torture me?” Bulldog’s laugh was filled with bitter irony. “You don’t have the stomach for it and we both know that. “Wrong, I didn’t have the stomach for it, but you gave it to me.” Xavier was screaming now to shock Bulldog. “I was a little non-committal, but you made sure that I would see things your way. Every time I was in doubt and unsure, there was Bulldog to remind me of the commitment we had to the world. You played me, and I guess I let you play me.” “It wasn’t that hard.” Bulldog gave a little laugh that made his chest bounce. Xavier wanted to rush forward and smack Bulldog, but he didn’t. “What was that?” “I said it wasn’t hard.” Bulldog wasn’t flinching away, “You’re such an idealist! The world is not black and white. You want it to be black and white, but it never has been. You want to talk about what’s best for the world, but what we’ve done isn’t a good thing.” Xavier rolled his eyes, but Bulldog kept pressing forward. “What did you think was going to happen at the end of this entire affair?” Bulldog waited for an answer that wasn’t coming. “We’ve been attacking people. We’ve been stealing from people, and no matter what type of people they are we have taken away their possessions. We have assaulted people by attacking them. That includes not just some physical altercations Xavier, it means we have tied people down and tortured them. We’ve broken into private homes, we’ve kidnapped, we’ve done so with the consent of duly elected officials such as your Congressman and we’ve committed murder.” “What was the moral justification there” You wanted to get drugs off the street?” Bulldog was beginning to enjoy himself now as he lectured Xavier, “You wanted vengeance Xavier. There was no moral justification for this. It all started because one of your friends, who was dealing drugs and using them, was killed as a matter of course in the business he had chosen to be part of!” “He was an addict.” Xavier offered weakly. “So what?” Bulldog was rolling his eyes now, “That’s an entire religion now. As a culture, we look for the next excuse to escape our own personal responsibility. The whole thing now is that an addict has an addiction, which is a disease and so they aren’t personally responsible for their own actions. That’s a load of bullshit!” “That then brings up the entire notion of why they may be addicts. They may have been beaten, or perhaps their parents didn’t love them enough, maybe their neighbor took them into the bedroom for abuse and maybe they lived in a broken home. All of it is simply designed to let people get away from their own actions.” “I made a choice a long time ago. I made the decision to work with the Russian Mafia. I accept the responsibility for that decision. I’m not making any excuses, and while I may explain why I’m guilty for a lot of crimes. So don’t give me any of that bull about problems that pathetic piece of shit you once called a friend went through that led to his death.” “I make decisions every single day, and I always accept the responsibility for the actions I take. So I won’t make any more excuses, and I betrayed you. I betrayed you, I betrayed myself and now what are you going to do about it?” “That depends. Who is Mr. X?” Xavier asked bluntly. “You have no idea how dangerous a question that is. I won’t tell you who he is because I can’t.” Bulldog said plainly as though quoting a statistic in a book. “Well, since I live in black and white your refusal means you have to be made to tell me.” Xavier raised one eyebrow. “It won’t help.” Bulldog was looking down at the floor again, “The gray area is where I live. I took the actions, and I will pay the price for them. If I betray the promise I made to Mr. X then my children pay the consequence. He’ll kill my boys, and that is something I can’t live with.” “He knows you’re coming for him, he knows it and he wants it. He will be ready when you strike, and in the end you’re going to die because you’re not ready to handle it. You’ve played into his hands the entire time.” Bulldog didn’t look up at all. “Really, this is what the Russian Mafia wanted” A war?” Xavier didn’t believe that. Bulldog still looked down at the ground, “You’re so na’ve. That’s exactly what they want. The war is going to play into the hands of the Russians. They are better prepared, better armed and better organized. They are ready to do things that make even me sick. The truth is there’s a lot of very powerful politicking going on in the hierarchy of the groups. This is simply an opening for a new order.” “The war is going to bring on more violence and bloodshed than you’ve ever seen. The drug dealers will be slaughtered, the users will die for being there when it happens and everything will be awash in sanguine wine for everyone to drink. Explosions, gunfire, torture and all other manner of evil will flood the world. You will cause all of that.” “The entire time you’ve been acting all high and mighty you became the best weapon for the Russians! Together, we’ve ensure a war will come into the fold and destroy everything. Now the only question left is how will this little play of ours end, and how much blood can you stand to be on your hands before you have to stop?” Bulldog was dismissive of Xavier now with his tone and gestures. Bulldog knew how it would all end even if his friend did not. Bulldog didn’t want to go through this anymore, “I’ve got to play this one way Xavier. Listen to me when we both know your name is Seth. You play the role, I play the role and now here we are in this fucked up situation.” “I remember when you were a kid Seth, and you ran away from home to my house. We spent that entire night talking and just having fun, and I remembered how much I loved my kids and wife because of that. I got back with them all right after that, and we haven’t been apart since. Do you remember when we did that Seth?” Xavier Black, Seth Hix to his closest friends, was looking at Robert “Bulldog” Hargis, Uncle Bob a long time ago, with eyes filled with sadness, “I remember that.” “Well, now I have to protect what I have remaining. You’re part of my family too Seth, but right now it’s got to be this way to protect you and my kids. No matter what happens remember that day Seth, because things will never be the same after this.” Bulldog said. “What are you doing Bulldog?” Xavier asked, on the defensive. “I’m going to give you the black and white options you’ve been looking for.” Bulldog’s eyes were filling with tears as he spoke. “I have to make this happen or else I know what you’ll do. I turned you into the animal we needed, and now you’ll do everything you can to make sure this is finished. I can’t let you finish it the way you want to.” Xavier could feel something was changing in his friend’s demeanor and stance, “What are you doing?” Bulldog took his jacket off, letting it fall to the floor. Xavier could see the shoulder holster Bulldog had on. The well-worn leather was the same he had used for so many years as a cop. Bulldog held both his hands up, “I’m going to reach behind my back for the pistol on my back hip.” “No you won’t!” Xavier pointed his pistol in the air, “You sit down and we’ll talk this through! It doesn’t have to end like this!” Bulldog’s face was suddenly overcome with tears streaming down his face. The large drops of salty liquid went over his dry lips, “This is the way it ends. You were always my favorite in your family Seth, and now you do what you have to do. I don’t blame you, I blame myself and always will old friend. Now, I’m reaching for my pistol.” Bulldog’s hand went behind his back. Xavier pointed the pistol right at him, “I will shoot!” There was a half smile that came out through the tears and sadness. Bulldog looked at Seth with’pride. Bulldog’s hand didn’t stop at all, “I know.” Xavier saw the silver metal as Bulldog tried to bring his hand around. Xavier aimed high and shot Bulldog in the upper left shoulder. The bullet bore into Bulldog’s body, through the muscular chest and slammed against the clavicle. The bone was broken by the bullet. The force of the bullet strike spun Bulldog around into the wall. Red blood streaked on the wall as he forced himself to turn back around. The silver revolver was closer now and more dangerous. Bulldog was intent on making Seth finish this game. Seth watched as Bulldog’s tears continued falling as small drops of blood fell to the floor. Xavier squeezed the trigger again, the bullet leapt out as if a fiery demon bursting out from hell as it slammed into Bulldog’s ribs. The bullet was ricocheted by the bone it chipped as it flew up into Bulldog’s lung. He dropped down to one knee and began coughing. Bulldog could feel the warm, salty blood come up as he coughed. The end was so close, and as the pain clouded his vision he smiled again, knowing he had won what he wanted. The time for playing the Janus faced killer and friend was over. Bulldog tried to bring the gun up, and Xavier would not allow that to happen. Xavier knew that he could not stop now, and the time had come for him to finish the game Bulldog had refused to leave alone. As the silver revolver was aimed at Xavier’s belly the black pistol was aimed at Bulldog’s forehead. Xavier hesitated for a brief instant. He was seeing his Uncle Bob instead of a man holding a gun at him. Xavier let the memories flash through, but he squeezed the trigger a final time. The bullet smashed through the thin layer of Bulldog’s skin and muscle before cracking right through his sternum. The bullet angled down, taking out the heart as the bullet bored through and exited at through the back. The bullet flew out into the wall along with flesh and blood. Xavier watched the smoke come up from the barrel of the gun as Robert “Bulldog” Hargis fell sideways onto the floor. The body hit hard, and blood began pouring out from his back as he flopped several times, dropping the gun. Xavier watched for a moment, then kneeled down beside Bulldog. Xavier took Bulldog into his arms as he tried to breathe, getting more blood than air. “Not’yourgggg’faugglt.” Bob coughed spitting blood out onto Xavier’s arm. “Igggg”.love you’Sggg’Seth.” Robert Hargis stopped struggling, and the remainder of his life flowed out onto the floor as his breath exhaled one more time. Xavier was still holding him. The images of a lifetime rushed through his head as he felt the entire weight of the world bear down on him. “Ahhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhh!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!” Xavier/Seth screamed into the air as though eh himself had been shot. Xavier was rocking back and forth, holding Bulldog like a favorite rag doll eh never wanted to let go of. The tears began streaming down his face as he shook violently with tears and wailing, pained cries he had never experienced before. Bertrand and Sean came running into the barn with weapons drawn, eyes were sweeping the room analyzing and leaping on everything they saw. They were shocked at what they saw. Xavier was sitting on the ground in the living area holding a bloody Bulldog in his arms as he cried and wailed. Xavier didn’t even look at them as they approached. Xavier was still shaking, not knowing what he should do. Bertrand kneeled down to check on Bulldog, ignoring the blood as his knee soaked it up from the huge pool spread out before them. There was no pulse in the wrist as Bertrand expected, but he had to be absolutely sure. He looked up at Sean and shook his head sadly. “What happened Xavier?” Sean asked gently, not wanting to enflame the situation although he wasn’t sure it could be avoided. Xavier didn’t respond as he held onto Bulldog tightly. Bertrand looked over Xavier. He was pale, there was sweat on his brow and he seemed to be in shock. Bertrand looked up at Sean. His raised eyebrows explained it all to Sean. Sean spoke even more firmly, Xavier, what happened?” There was still no response. Sean looked around, but there was no indication of a struggle. The furniture was okay, and there were no broken items or debris on the floor. Sean walked around until he was directly in front of Xavier. He kneeled down to look right into Xavier’s eyes, “XAVIER!!!” Xavier was finally broken out of his haze. He looked over at Sean in confusion. “Xavier, what happened!”!” Sean was being as forceful as possible to ensure he got a response. Xavier hugged Bulldog tightly for the last time, “Bye Uncle Bob. Goodbye?” Xavier let go of Bulldog and stood up. Xavier looked over at Bertrand and Sean, “He was working for the Russian Mafia.” Xavier walked over to the kitchen area and opened the refrigerator. He pulled out a glass bottle of Mountain Dew from the cold interior. Xavier twisted off the top. He gulped down over half the soda in one upturn of the bottle. Bertrand and Sean looked down at the body of the man who had hired them, and then to the man they had worked with in the field. They were unsure of what they should do and where their loyalties lay. Bertrand found his voice first, “Xavier, what is going on?” Xavier finished the Mountain Dew and threw away the bottle. Xavier looked over at both men, “Bulldog told me everything after I overheard him on the phone with someone.” “Who was it?” Sean was confused, trying to make sense out of things. “I don’t know who he is, but we call him Mr. X since we have no idea who this person is.” Xavier opened up the refrigerator and pulled out another Mountain Dew. He twisted top and sipped this one more slowly. “Bulldog told me he has been working for them for thirty years, and he has been working for this Mr. X for a while. Mr. X knows we are going to crash his meeting.” “Then we have to call it off.” Sean made the cold observation. Xavier pointed at Sean with the top of his bottle, “No.” “No?” Sean pointed at Xavier with his finger while putting his weapon back into his holster. “If he knows we’re coming then he’s going to be ready for us. We can’t go into that kind of ambush! They’ll cut us to ribbons!” “No.” Xavier’s tone was calm and straight forward. Sean was about to explode, but Bertrand held up his hand to calm him. Bertrand looked over at Xavier, “Why Xavier?” “Mr. X wants us to come. He wants to face us down, and so he is going to be overconfident, and he’s also going to want a battle to take out the Cartel. We’re going to show up, we’re going to kill everyone who gets in our way and in the end they’re all going to pay.” Xavier’s anger was returning. Xavier wiped away the sweat from his brow with the back of his forearm. The sweat was replaced by streaks of blood from his arm where he was holding Bulldog. Bertrand wasn’t sure what to think. Here he was with two men on opposite sides of the fence, and he was sure that these two men both had good reason to believe their own positions were proper. Bertrand looked the two men up and down trying to decide what to do. “You want vengeance?” Sean asked bitterly. “This whole affair was about vengeance, so yes that’s what I want.” Xavier didn’t even flinch away from Sean’s angry stare. “I don’t want to confront Mr. X because I want vengeance this time, but I do want to confront him because this has to be seen through to the end. You have to decide.” Xavier looked over at Bertrand, “Both of you, to decide if you’re in this until the end. This is the last move, and we may not all make it. I’m going no matter what, even if I simply run in with guns blazing trying to take them all out myself.” Bertrand had his own motivations that no one except for the dead man on the floor knew, “I will join you. I will see it through.” Sean walked away from them to the open area. He was looking out the door at the land spread out before him. Sean wanted to finish, but he didn’t relish the idea of sacrificing his life needlessly when the outcome might not be guaranteed. Xavier finished his drink and dropped the empty bottle in the trash. Xavier walked past Bertrand and stopped next to Sean. The tension was thick, but each man had a deep respect for the other. Xavier looked out the door with Sean for a long time before speaking. “This is it, and I just need to know which way you want to go. I’ve made my choice, and you have to make your choice based on what will be best for you. I got into this for revenge and to do something that will help the world. I don’t know why you got in this, and I want to know why?” Xavier was still looking out the door as he spoke. “I’m a mercenary. I do what I do because people pay me. I’m the man you go to when there’s something to do you can’t do yourself.” Sean couldn’t look at Xavier. “That was all?” Sean looked out into his past as he saw more than the land in front of him, “Once I fought for something more. What’s left that we can do” We’ll be slaughtered by them because they know we’re coming. That’s not what I want to have happen.” “I remember when my good friend once told me you have to be ready to walk into hell with nothing but spit to hurl at the devil. That’s the level of commitment you have to have to see something through until the end. I’m going to walk right into Hell and spit in his face, but the satisfaction I will feel in the end is worth it.” “I need to be able to count on a few men to come with me into Hell. If you have what it takes then come with me. If it takes cash to guarantee that then I’ll pay you, but I think there’s something else inside you that drove you here. If you still have that drive, then walk through the gates.” Xavier walked forward out the door. He walked around to the side of the barn and fell to his knees. He had killed his friend, and the fact he had to kill Bulldog for himself to live did not help him at all. Xavier was weeping, weeping like a widow on the side of the barn. Sean was still looking out the door, but he had made his decision already. “Let’s take care of the body.” Sean spoke just loud enough for Bertrand to hear. Bertrand nodded, “Yes, we can put the body inside garbage bags and throw it into the other barn for now.” “We could bury it.” “What if they make a check of the grounds” We are right next door.” “Yes, that’s what I would do. I would check out everything as much as possible the day before and the day of. I would check the perimeter.” “So will they. We need to make sure we have zero residual presence then.” “That won’t be that hard. I need to tell Xavier I’ll be there.” Bertrand went into the kitchen and began gathering bags and ties, “I will begin to prepare him. This is just like the kitchen, so I am used to this.” Sean walked out, passing through the memory he was fantasizing. Sean kept seeing his sister on that fateful day. The empty look in her eyes, the hopelessness, and the despair as the huge revolver was pressed against her chest and she pulled the trigger. Sean couldn’t dwell on that. He walked around to the crying form of Xavier Black. Sean spat on the ground beside him. Xavier looked up at him and saw the steel in Sean’s eyes. “We go in to Hell together.” Sean said. Xavier simply nodded.
