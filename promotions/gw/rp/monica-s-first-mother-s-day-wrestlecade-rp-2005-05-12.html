Monica's first Mother's Day (Wrestlecade RP)

12 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/12/05 Mother’s Day is a special day. The second Sunday of every May, millions of women around the world are given their due, honored for their roles as nurturers, teachers, givers of life. On this particular Sunday, Brick, his longtime friend Aaron Foster and his brother-in-law Kyle Fontaigne are treating the special women in their lives to a special Mother's Day brunch buffet after church. Brick treats his mother Coretta. Aaron treats his mother Marguerite and his wife Felica. Kyle treats his mother Lillian and his wife (and Brick's sister) Monica. 'This is so expensive,' says Marguerite. 'I wish you boys hadn't...' 'Sshh, let them spoil us,' interjects Lillian. It’s especially happy when a new mother celebrates her first Mother's Day. At least, it should be. But, for Monica, it isn't. What should be a joyous occasion has been marred by a dark cloud. That dark cloud: Salaok Davis. A few days ago, on Evolution, the last before Wrestlecade, the Harvester of Minds made a shocking claim while taunting Brick: that he and Monica had a tryst sometime before he met his late wife Lisha. He followed it up with a claim that he had videotaped evidence of the alleged dalliance, which he would show at Wrestlecade. It was yet another volley in his war with Brick. 'Monica, is something wrong? You haven't touched your omelet.' 'I guess I'm not really hungry.' 'Wait, Monica not hungry? There's a first.' 'Jamal...' 'I'm just joking.' Suddenly, Monica rises to her feet and walks away from the table, her right hand covering his eyes. 'Maybe I should...' 'No, Ma,' says Brick, as he rises out of his seat, 'this is your day. Just relax. Besides, I think I have a pretty good idea what this is about.' 'I'll go with you,' says Kyle, rising out of his seat. 'No, this is my mess. I'll deal with it.' 'I'm her husband.' 'I'm her brother.' Brick pauses for a moment. 'We'll both go.' Brick and Kyle go off together to follow Monica. When they catch up to her, she is in the ladies' room. They can hear her sobbing. 'Monica, baby, it's me.' 'It's me too, sis.' 'Guys, I...I just need to be alone, right now.' 'Look, this is your special day. You should be upset. Let us help you.' 'Don't let what Salaok said bother you,' says Kyle, putting his hand on her shoulder. 'It does bother me, Kyle. He dragged my name through the gutter on cable TV. In front of all those people.' 'Listen,' says Brick, 'you know he's lying. I know he's lying. That's all that matters.' * * * 'She's just been so upset after what happened with Salaok,' says Coretta, looking off to where. 'Kyle and I talked about it,' replies Lillian. 'He was so angry.' 'And Jamal has been just livid. As if his match at Wrestlecade wasn't enough, this happens.' 'Believe me,' adds Felicia, 'if there's one thing I know, it's just how deeply Salaok can hurt people. I still remember what I went through when he kidnapped Aaron. Then when Aaron wound up in a coma...' 'The man is a plague,' adds Aaron. 'I talked to Brick about forgiving him, but I would not blame him one bit if he killed the son of a...' 'Aaron...' interjects Marguerite. 'Sorry, Mom, but that's how I feel.' * * * 'My taste in men before Kyle was questionable, but it was never psychotic.' 'I believe you.' 'I do, too.' 'Good, because you know there is no way in hell I would let that piece of trash touch me and this tape he claims to have is fake.' Brick then takes his sister by the hand. 'Listen, this thing with Salaok, I'll take care of it. I took care of Deshawn Riley when he dogged you at the prom. I took care of Cedric when he was beating on you. I'll take care of Salaok at Wrestlecade.' 'Yeah, well, don't forget you still have Train and Frost to deal with.' 'I know, and Frost better pray that Train beats him or Salaok beats me. Because I got something for him, too.' 'What about Train? I mean, you two are partners.' 'Not at Wrestlecade, we ain't. We can't. But, to be honest, if I don't win the titles, I'd rather he won them than Salaok or Frost. But, let's not worry about Salaok or Wrestlecade or any o' that mess. Today is your day.' 'Come on, let's head back to the table.' Brick, Monica and Kyle return to her table. 'Is everything all right, Monica?' asks Coretta. 'It will be.' 'Good, because this is a special day. As the Bible says, no weapons formed against us shall prosper. Doesn't say they won't be formed, but they won't prosper.' 'Thanks, Mom,' Monica replies, smiling. Just then, Shirley Caesar's version of 'No Charge' began to play. 'Oh, now this is a Mother's Day song,' says Aaron. An idea then pops into Brick's head as he begins mouthing the words, eliciting chuckles from Coretta. My sister's little boy came into the kitchen this evenin' while she was fixin' supper, And he handed her a piece of paper he'd been writin' on. And after wipin' her hands on my apron she read it, And this is what it said: For mowin' the yard: five dollars; And for makin' my own bed this week: one dollar; An' goin' to the store: fifty cents; An' watchin' little brother while you went shoppin': twenty-five cents; For takin' out the trash: one dollar; Gettin' a good report card: five dollars; And rakin' the yard: two dollars; Total owed: fourteen seventy-five. Before long, Aaron joins in, causing laughs from both Marguerite and Felicia. Well, she stood there lookin' at him expectantly, And a thousand mem'ries flashed through her mind. So she picked up the pen, an', turnin' the paper over, This is what she wrote: For the nine months I carried you, Growin' inside me: no charge; For the nights I've sat up with you, Doctored you, prayed for you: no charge; For the time and the tears, And the cost through the years, there's no charge; When you add it all up, The full cost of my love is no charge. And now, Kyle can't help but to join in the silliness as well. For the nights filled with dread, And the worry ahead: no charge; For advice and the knowledge And the cost of your college: no charge; For the toys, food and clothes and for wipin' your nose, There's no charge, son When you add it all up, The full cost of my love is: no charge. Well, you know when I think about that, I think about the day that Jesus went out on Calvary and gave his life as a ransom for me. When I think on the words, 'If any man be in Christ, he's a new creature,' I like to think that the very minute he shedded his blood, my debt was paid in full. I want you to know today: When you add it all up, The cost of real love is: no charge.
