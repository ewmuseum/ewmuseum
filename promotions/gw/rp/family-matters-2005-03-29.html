Family Matters

29 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/29/2005 (Sorry for the lateness peoples) (OOC: All dialogue in brown translated from Spanish.) October 15, 1990 2017 hours Maria's Cantina, Mexico City, Mexico The handsome young man that had invited Teresa and her sister to join him at his table for a drink, so they wouldn't have to wait for an empty table, nodded sympathetically. 'Okay,' he said. 'You're grown women... you can handle yourselves, and don't need anyone. But if you please, I don't think you look like the kind of ladies that don't want anyone around them, either.' Teresa was taken aback, but answered coolly. 'Not all the time, no,' she said. 'What makes you think we want to be around anyone now?' The young man shrugged. 'Because you're here?' he answered. 'Maria's cantina isn't exactly the most popular place for people trying to stay away from others.' Teresa looked at her sister, who snickered lightly and gave a small smile. 'He likes you, sister.' 'Oh, is that so?' Teresa raised an eyebrow. 'Have we even met?' 'Allow me to remedy that,' said the man politely, as he stood and introduced himself formally to the two Ramirez sisters. Finally Teresa started to smile as she shook hands, introducing herself and her sister to the young man. 'And no, we're not accustomed to being invited to share a drink. We're not used to people smiling at us.' 'First time for everything,' said the man. 'I won't lie to you, Miss Ramirez. I know about your father. My parents took me to Lucha Libre venues when I was younger. I know how you and your sister were treated all because you wanted to help your father. I'm not one of the people who will treat you that way. I think you've been forced to pay far more than whatever debt the Lucha Libre world thinks you and your father owes it... don't you?' They missed their father very much, and Mateo had almost two more full years to go on his sentence. The two Ramirez sisters were no longer hated pariahs; their beloved father having been almost forgotten in the aftermath of being sent to jail. Life went on for the citizens of Mexico City, and the Lucha Libre federations... even Mateo's dear children. Not that Teresa or her sister liked that one bit. Mexico City had seemed to have forgotten, or at least forgiven, the two young women for their role in Mateo's disgrace... but the two sisters hadn't really forgotten or forgiven the rest of Mexico City. It was THEIR father all this had happened to... never mind that the officers who had arrested Mateo were starting to draw their batons, ready to give a beating to the 17-year-old girl that Teresa's sister was back then, if she hadn't halted dead in her tracks on seeing the two burly officers zap her beloved father with a cattle prod in order to complete the arrest and get the handcuffs on him. Neither of the two young women knew if they would be able to find jobs as street cleaners or pot washers, or scullery maids, when they moved out of their stepfather's mansion five months ago. The time they tried the previous December, was a most unpleasant and heated argument with their mother. December 2nd, 1989 1745 hours Mexico City, Mexico 'We're LEAVING, Mama,' said Teresa curtly. 'We don't want any of his money, or our own car, or our own CD players.' 'You really think so?' responded Lupe indignantly. 'I am your MOTHER, young ladies! You will do what I tell you to do!' Teresa smiled in a way that made Lupe have to flinch, no matter how much authority she fancied herself as having over her children. 'Care to make me?' she challenged. 'What will you do if I don't do what you tell me... throw me out? That's kind of a moot point, isn't it, Mama? You'd be doing us a FAVOR... that would be a reward, not a punishment.' 'You want punishment? You will have it,' retorted Lupe flatly. 'Go to your rooms, both of you... you want dinner tonight, you'll have to cook it yourselves... after my husband and I, and the maid have all gone to bed.' Teresa started to take a step forward, but was stopped by her sister's hand on her elbow. 'She's right, Teresa,' the younger girl said. Teresa turned around, completely aghast as she watched her sister quietly turn and start up the stairs back to her room. Teresa could hear her mother clucking her tongue in triumph. 'You'd best follow her, back to your own room, if you know what's best for you, young lady,' Lupe said. Teresa turned and handed her mother a look that could melt lead... but her sister's capitulation had suddenly sapped her will to resist. Her nostrils flared wide as she found herself fighting to keep from fidgeting under mother's suddenly much-more-confident stare. 'You won't--' 'Get away with this?' Lupe sneered sardonically. 'Looks like I just have... doesn't it? Now are you going up to your room, or are you--' Teresa's sister was coming back... panting slightly. 'Yes, Teresa... you should. You forgot one of your bags too.' The younger girl was coming back down the stairs, carrying another shoulder bag which she strapped to her hand truck. Her innocent smile filled Teresa with new resolve, and she snickered with infuriating complacency. 'I'll be right back,' Teresa said, hurrying to retrieve the bag and attach it to her own hand truck. 'Now what are you going to do, Mama... call your husband to paddle us?' she quipped on returning. 'How about I call the POLICE,' Lupe snapped, throwing down the gauntlet before her children. 'You want to be put into Juvenile Detention? SEE if I don't!' 'Juvenile?' Teresa laughed scornfully. 'You forget how old I am, Mama... I am of legal adult age now. I do as I PLEASE.' Lupe blinked from the blow... Teresa was right. Lupe had no legal recourse against Teresa now that she was 19. The two sisters smiled in triumph as Lupe's indignant expression started to falter. But Lupe landed back on her feet. She knew that the two girls were strongest when they were together. 'And remind me, Teresa...' Lupe said suddenly, lifting her chin in defiance, 'how old your SISTER is?' The tables turned in a heartbeat. Once again Lupe had the upper hand. 'That's right, young lady,' said Lupe, staring down her younger daughter. 'Last I checked, you were still seventeen years old... and you will be for another five months. YOU, I still have full legal authority over... and I can have you put away until your eighteenth birthday. Shall I USE that authority?' Lupe knew that Teresa would never abandon her sister... and she'd never let her sister be arrested the way her father was. The two young women looked as if they had steam pouring out of their ears, though the shadow of their mother now loomed well over them, even though Lupe was short even for a woman, and her two daughters tall. 'I won't let you do that to her,' growled Teresa. 'Then I suggest you get up to your ROOM, Teresa,' Lupe snapped again, 'and don't come out until my husband, the maid and I have all gone to bed. Or do you suppose to challenge me for legal CUSTODY of your sister in family court? Do you know how long it will take just to get a hearing? During which time I can easily have an injunction passed to have her taken into the custody of the city to await the hearing.' Lupe didn't point out that before the case was decided, her younger daughter would turn 18 and be of majority age, but the girl knew it well and good. Teresa did too. The courts probably wouldn't even grant a hearing at all with only five months remaining before the issue became moot. The two girls steamed with the pain of their defeat as they started up the stairs, dragging the hand trucks behind them. 'And as I said... only after the rest of us are in bed do you come out of your rooms. Then you can cook your own dinner and eat.' Maria's Cantina, 2250 hours 'I'm sorry to hear about it,' said the young man, as Teresa finished telling him about the argument. A couple of tequilas had made the two young women more talkative... as had their host's easygoing manner. 'What kind of work have you been able to find? You both look too attractive to be scullery maids.' The two young women smiled again at the wry compliment. 'I wait tables at a diner near our apartment,' said Teresa's sister, showing that she found no embarrassment in it. 'Teresa's a receptionist for a small moving company. Our apartment is nothing compared to what we lived in while Papa was still wrestling in Lucha Libre, but we came dangerously close to the kind of living arrangement that would make our current apartment seem luxurious by comparison.' The man nodded soberly. 'I can't do anything that would make up for what you've been through,' he said realistically, 'But perhaps I can offer you the hand of friendship today? Maybe it will make tomorrow more comfortable.' Teresa and her sister didn't really have any friends... largely by choice, although deep down, they believed nobody would want to be friends with THEM, either... but here was this gallant young man with a heartwarming smile and the most charming manners they'd seen since hearting stories of Lupe's courtship by Mateo, offering to be their friend, because he believed it was right. They were taken completely off-guard, to say the least... and while they were largely content to go straight home after work, with only each other for company until bedtime, the Ramirez sisters knew they couldn't shut the world completely out... they DID have to live. They had to work, they had to shop; they had to go out into the sunshine sometimes... 'But,' said the young man suddenly, 'I need to get some sleep and be up for work tomorrow bright and early... so if you wish to pass up my offer, I shall be on my way.' Teresa glanced after him with a sharp look as he nodded pleasantly to her and her sister, and started toward the register to pay his bill. Well, if that wasn't-- 'Wait!' she suddenly called, hurrying after him. Teresa's sister grinned as the young man turned around coolly. 'Yes?' he asked Teresa. 'I'll be here tomorrow night... 8:30?' Teresa slowly began to smile-- the first truly warm smile she'd offered up all evening. Some would have even sworn it was flirtatious. The young man was one of them, and he responded by gently lifting up Teresa's hand and kissing the back of it. 'I'll be here,' he said. 'I look forward to seeing you again.' Teresa bent her knees in a small curtsy as the man bid her good-bye. No sooner did she return to the table with her sister when she suddenly looked puzzled-- what had made her so impulsive so suddenly? Her sister seemed to know, although she wasn't telling. Neither was the cantina manager, who'd seen the little exchange and quickly ordered the band to play his favorite romantic song for the cantina patrons. March 28, 2005 Regina Hotel Baglioni, Rome (Italy) 1952 hours 'Sounds like a delightful story,' said Noi Tsunami, jotting a few notes down to go with her miniature cassette recorder which was recording the impromptu interview. GW was doing a story on La Herencia de Herederos for their monthly eZine and Noi, ever more curious about the Mexican Luchadores, had agreed to conduct the interview. 'I'm a sucker for romantic tales.' Juan and Teresa glanced at each other with a curious sense of unease at Noi's words. 'I wouldn't call it love at first sight,' said Teresa, her words carefully controlled, 'but that's how and when I met my husband.' 'And how does it feel to be here in Italy now?' asked Noi. She knew that the Ramirez family had never set foot outside of Mexico prior to signing the GW contract, and now suddenly they were traveling around the globe. Dallas, Las Vegas... then Hawaii... now Europe. 'It certainly makes the world feel much larger,' said Jos? Sanchez, the team's mentor and trainer. 'I'd been in the States several times before bringing Juan and Teresa over for GW... but even I've never been outside of North America.' 'I must admit it makes us most apprehensive,' said Juan, suddenly reaching to his side protectively to pat Melina on the shoulder and lightly stroke her hair. 'Teresa and I feel guilty sometimes, keeping such a tight rein on Melina, even though it is only because we just cannot control an overwhelming fear of being separated from her... she and us being lost in such a large, foreign city, and not knowing how to find each other again.' Melina glanced up at her father's touch, but then continued eating her dinner. Because the interview was being conducted in English, the child had no interest in it... and she was more than happy not to speak to Noi, anyway, as the reporter was very much still a stranger to her. Every so often, as Noi would organize her notes, Teresa or Jos? would exchange a few words with Melina to reassure her that she wasn't being ignored. 'There's no sense of excitement at being in a major city you never imagined you'd be in, until now?' asked Noi, smiling slightly at the seeming sense of wonder Juan and Teresa had about Rome. Teresa chuckled softly. 'Of course there is,' she said, nodding slowly. 'I understand what you're getting at, Noi... and yes. To be in ROME-- home of the ancient Colosseum-- don't think we intend to go home without seeing it. I can't begin to describe the thrill of walking through the small marketplaces, seeing the lights, watching a show in the theatre... of course we're excited. And we'd have just died if we asked Melina to stay behind with Jos?, or Stuart. Frightened as we are of her becoming separated from us and alone in a strange city, we have no right to shut our own daughter out of her own family because of that fear.' Noi jotted a few more notes. 'I could almost imagine the pay-per-view event, Clash of the Titans, being held IN the Colosseum,' she said with a wicked grin. 'That's what it was for, after all-- gladiatorial competitions, and the Clash will be one for the record books. To be live in front of an audience here in Italy--' 'Don't remind us,' said Juan. 'Far as we're concerned, we have the night off.' They knew the Clash would have only two matches, because the Rumble was going to be a long bout. Everyone in the fed was expected to participate, and they'd never refuse an order from senior management... but following this order made them outright furious. It showed in their expressions, the cutting tone of their words, every breath they took while being made to even mention the Rumble. 'You're not going to just exit the ring a few minutes after your entrance and forfeit, are you?' asked Noi. 'I wouldn't expect to see that from Luchadores with the fire in their bellies that I see you have.' 'And that fire is exactly what you'll see at the Clash,' said Teresa a little curtly. 'But we don't like it. We'll be doing what we're expected to do at the Rumble, because it's our duty. That's it. We're not there to try for a shot at the GW World Championship, we're not there to try for individual glory... Juan and I are a TEAM. We'll do our duty, but if one of us wins-- what we WILL forfeit is the shot at the champion at Wrestlecade. There's only World Championship belt, and we won't let it come between us. We are here to share a title, as co-holders... not become divided by a title that has room only for one.' 'What if it comes down to the two of you... whether at the end, or because you're the only ones in the ring at any particular moment?' asked Noi seriously. 'What if you have to face each other in the ring... as opponents?' Noi was not surprised at the extreme discomfort this confronting question caused the two Luchadores... not that she'd have held it back from them; interviews of this sort, for GW's eZine, demanded the wrestlers be able to answer the hardest sort of questions. It took hard men and women to face such hard questions and maintain their composure in the face of them. She noted with a bit of interest, however, that they didn't join hands, as they often did for mutual support... instead they both joined hands with Jos?... their trainer. 'We'll burn that bridge when we come to it,' said Teresa stiffly... though it was Juan who added the afterthought, 'Not before.' Knowing the Ramirez family's overprotective nature, Noi paused for a few seconds to make a respectful gesture and smile politely as a preface to her request. 'Would you be okay with my asking Melina a question? A few fans are interested in what it's like balancing family life with the life of an entertainer... and as much as GW is a league of sportsmen... mixed fighting... we do provide entertainment by competing in that sport.' Teresa set her fork down and gently rested a hand across Melina's shoulders, prompting a quick glance from the child as she took another bite of her meal. 'You can ask,' said Teresa, 'but I can't promise that Melina will want to answer you. She doesn't feel comfortable around strangers, and she doesn't know you yet, even though you've interviewed Juan and I before.' Although she didn't understand the conversation being carried on, Melina did know what her name sounded like... and she'd heard it spoken several times during the interview. She looked up plaintively at her mother and fidgeted awkwardly. 'Mamacita, “hice yo algo malo?' (*Did I do something wrong, Mommy?) Teresa quickly reassured her daughter and explained the situation. Melina frowned uncertainly, snuggling a bit against Teresa's side like a baby bird going underneath it's mother's wing, but then nodded her head in assent. 'Ask her your question,' said Teresa, nodding to Noi. Noi avoided looking Melina right in the eye, because she felt it might make the child nervous. She kept her face carefully neutral, but her voice gentle. 'Hi, Melina,' she said, 'My name is Noi. I'd like to ask you how you feel about traveling all around the world with your family, and watching your parents fight in the GW competition.' Jos? translated Noi's question into Spanish for Melina, and the child knit her brow thoughtfully for a bit. She seemed to stare at Noi for almost a half minute, as if wondering whether to speak to a stranger, even with her mother's permission. Finally she turned to Jos? and spoke to him in a soft, almost musical voice with a pleasant but guarded tone. Jos? turned to Noi. 'She says she tries not to watch the fighting, because she gets frightened if she gets a simple nosebleed,' he said, translating Melina's answer to English for Noi. 'She'd be happier if Juan and Teresa weren't such public figures, and she could go to a public school like most other kids... but she trusts her parents to do what they feel is right.' 'No sense of adventure from traveling?' asked Noi curiously. Jos? translated, and the child's tone in answering showed she thought the question was downright silly. 'She says she has enough adventure at the local playground near where she lives,' said Jos?, translating back to English for Noi, and both Juan and Teresa laughed. Juan ruffled Melina's hair affectionately and kissed on the top of her head. Melina smiled complacently and resumed eating her dinner. A waiter came up with dessert menus. 'Would you care for dessert?' he asked everyone politely. He handed a children's dessert menu written in Spanish to Melina. He understood the family was from a Spanish-speaking country, and since Italian and Spanish were very similar due to mutual roots in Latin, there was little difficulty producing Spanish-language menus for the occasional tourists from Spain. Juan, Teresa, Jos? and Noi glanced over their menus quickly and gave their dessert orders to the waiter in quick summary. The waiter nodded politely, jotting them down, and then looked at Melina. 'Signorina?' he asked. Melina stared at the menu in silence. She fidgeted a bit, not wanting to look at the waiter. Teresa stroked Melina's head and smiled at her. “No querr’as alg’n postre, preciosa?' she asked. (*Wouldn't you like some dessert, sweetheart?') Melina looked uncomfortably at her mother and then nodded a bit tentatively. 'S?...' she said. Teresa tapped the child's menu and smiled encouragingly at Melina, but the child just stared at it, and then looked down at the table, a glum expression on her face. Teresa put down her wine glass and looked at Melina with concern. 'Melina...?' Finally Melina looked up at the waiter and spoke in a nervous voice barely more than a whisper. '¿Alg’n flan de la vainilla?' (*Some vanilla custard?) Vanilla custard wasn't on the menu. Before Teresa could react, the waiter briefly touched her forearm and smiled reassuringly. 'The pastry chef knows how to make custard,' he said quickly. 'I think he'd be willing to make some for her, if it will make her happy.' Noi smiled at Teresa as the waiter went to bring the dessert orders to the kitchen. 'If you don't ask, you don't get,' she said. 'She's a smart young lady.' Juan and Teresa smiled politely but very briefly at the compliment. Noi had a point, but it wasn't a point that applied to their daughter... they knew her better than Noi did; Melina was too shy around strangers to be asking for what wasn't offered. Juan suddenly wanted the interview to be wrapped up. 'Is there anything else you'd like to know?' he asked, in a tone that showed his feelings plainly. Noi started to ask another question, but she caught the emphasis in Juan's voice, and her voice died in her throat before it was uttered. 'I think we can call it a wrap,' she said, not wanting to offend the two Mexican spitfires. She was too fascinated in every part of their backstory that they told her, to risk being cut off from being the one to break the next scoop on them. 'Thank you for speaking to me again. I'll let you know when the eZine goes live on the website.' She turned off her recorder and put it and her notepad back in her purse. Juan and Teresa smiled gratefully as dessert arrived. Truthfully though, they were eager to finish the meal and get upstairs for some quality time with Melina. A look in Juan's eyes told Teresa he was making plans to read her favorite bedtime story to her, and Teresa nodded approvingly. Teresa made a gesture to Jos? to let him know that any other plans for the night would have to wait. Family came first.
