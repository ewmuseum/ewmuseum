One Step At A Time

11 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/11 He sat by himself in the corner of the Global locker room. He didn’t know anyone. After all, he hadn’t been in the sport for a long time. He wore a white singlet and white boots, functional yet basic ring attire. It wasn’t anything fancy. Then again, that fit him, because he never was. Winning wasn’t on his mind tonight. Accuse most wrestlers of not wanting to win, and you would get angry rebuttals. Tell Tim Walden that he wasn’t looking to win the #1 contendership to the Platinum Championship, and he’d tell you that you were absolutely right. Tonight, his goals were far simpler. A hand reached around behind his neck, feeling the scar tissue there. Neck surgery was no small deal, and the quality of his surgeon would be put to the test in, of all things, a battle royale. Battle royales were dangerous matches. Most people would say cages and hardcore brawls were worse, but at least in those, you only had one opponent. You knew where he was coming from. In a battle royale, there was always a multiplicity of competitors. You could be facing one man and doing well, only to be blindsided by another. That was how injuries occurred. Injury, at this point, would be utterly catastrophic to the career that he was trying to relaunch. It would essentially mean that there was no comeback. That everything would be for naught. Tim Walden was many things. He was a technician. He was a proud Canadian. But he was not a miracle worker. Winning a match with nineteen people in it was difficult. There were too many variables. Too many people. So while everyone else was satisfied with their rhetoric and promises to rip people in half, Tim Walden was more realistic. He would give it his best shot in his return to PPV. If he won, great. If he didn’t, ah well. There would be more chances. After all, he was a technician. Technical wrestling’s not much good in a battle royale. By Walden’s side was a duffel bag, which he reached into. Tim came out with a roll of athletic tape, which he began to unroll and wrap around his wrists. The familiar tightness came back to him then, and he smiled in spite of himself. Slowly, he stood, walking over to the other side of the nearly-empty locker room, to the mirror there. From a distance, he saw himself in the mirror, and couldn’t quite believe it. His reflection stared back at him, in a singlet and well-tied boots, with tape around his wrists. And in that instant, he knew what he was. He wasn’t destined to sit at home collecting a severance package from his former employer. He wasn’t designed to watch television all day. The good Lord had made Timothy Walden to wrestle. And, starting tonight, wrestle he would, for as long as he could.
