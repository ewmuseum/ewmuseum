'Consequences and repercussions - the airplane confessionals.'

21 August 2005

Date: August 21st, 2005. Consequences and repercussions - the airplane confessionals. ---- 10:48 AM. August 16th, 2005. High above the lone star state. ---- There I sat, mute, numb, and admittedly; a little scared as the plane roared through the pale blue Texas sky on its way to Juarez, Mexico. The last time I could remember being this scared was when I woke up in an Italian hospital with a gut full of rohypnol and an IV in my veins, a doctor standing at my side and telling me in broken English how lucky I was to be alive. Then again, I couldn't remember an occasions as big as that, before this appeared on the horizon. Dear God, I was so scared, so terrified. Taking a slow sip out of my bottle of Aquafina I screwed the cap back on and returned it to the holder on the back of the chair in front of me, turning my bluish gray eyes to the window next to me I stared down at the mountains far below us, those gigantic mountains resembled mere hills of dirt from this vantage point. At this point an interesting thought came to mind... God saw the world in so many ways, and this was just one of His many vantage points. Somewhere I could imagine Him sitting on this gleaming golden club-foot throne, staring down at the insignificant specks of dust littering His creation with their sick desires and strange perversions. God saw it all, but the thought that drew me in was rather simple, yet altogether complex in the same breath: how did God see me? God saw that time when I was 4 and stole a micro machine from one of my older cousins, I didn't like him anyway. God saw that time when I was 14 and had my first taste of liquor. God saw me when I was 16 and slit my wrist for the first time, it certainly wasn't the last. God even saw me when I overdosed in Italy before the 2004 Clash of the titans show, to this day He's still the only one who knows the truth surrounding the Rome incident. God saw me each and every night, He knew my sins, He knew my downfalls, yet strangely showed grace in spite of my deep and numerous weaknesses, I would never understand Him. God had seen every single moment I had spent with Rosa Padilla, from the first moments we spent in sweet adultery until we conceived a child together. Unplanned or not this had been God's gift to her; His curse to me, or so I was led to believe. I still couldn't believe I was doing this, flying across the F'N country to bring a child into my world, to enter myself into his world. I shook my head, the irony was bitter, so were my questions. Would he love me? Would he hate me? Would I love him as a father loves a son? Would I hate him for the painful memories of his parents? What was his mother thinking as she sat next to God, watching us all from heaven? I sighed, retrieved my bottle of water, screwed off the cap and took another thirsty gulp of the cold liquid, a few drops spilling out on my white Anberlin t-shirt, the image of a head made of clay across the chest. 'You know, that is a peculiar habit...but strangely very healthy.' The voice said, breaking into the world I had secluded myself too. 'Uh...what?' Was the most I could force out, a bit caught off guard by the intrusion. 'Water.' Said my lovely girlfriend - Casey Branner - seated next to me on the plane. 'I think its odd that you drink water non-stop when you're stressed out.' I shrugged. 'I guess I never really noticed it.' She dropped a hand onto my leg, patting my left thigh, encased in a pair of $186.00 paper, denim & cloth jeans, leading down to casual black flip-flops. 'You aren't denying that you're stressed out.' Casey observed as she squeezed my thigh. 'Yeah, I'm not going to lie to you Case, I'm pretty messed up right now.' I sighed, turning my gaze away from the window and staring directly into her big brown eyes. 'Don't worry...be happy.' Casey said, punctuating her sentence with a dorky grin. 'Thanks.' I smiled, appreciative of her support and silly little jokes. 'I just wish it were that easy.' 'I know, I feel for you Josh, I really hope things work out for the best.' Casey whispered to me, leaning her head against my shoulder, her beautiful blonde-brown streaked hair falling across my shirt. 'Yeah...' I whispered back, allowing my head to rest against hers. 'I just wish I knew what was best.' 'That can be the hardest thing to understand sometimes, sweetie.' Casey replied, showing maturity for her twenty-eight years. 'What do you think is the best?' I asked, my voice cracking with emotion as I closed my eyes to keep anyone else on the plane from seeing my tears. 'I don't know Josh, I honestly don't.' Casey answered, trying to give me the fairest response she could manage. 'It isn't fair Casey...' I felt butterflies in my stomach as I spoke. 'To that poor little boy, to his parents, to anyone. How could I do such a thing?' Casey moved her hand from my thigh to meet my fingers, where they interlocked with hers. 'Josh, don't blame yourself, okay? You made a mistake, yes, but you didn't mean to get Rosa pregnant.' 'But still...' I stopped, unsure of what to say. 'No, Josh, please don't torture yourself over this. Espen will be happy no matter what. If he grows up with you I know that you'll make a wonderful father, but if not? I think things will still work out for the best.' Casey said, her sweet voice taking on a tone of reassurance. 'But Casey, we still don't know what the best is...' I choked, tears slipping out from underneath my closed eyelids. 'Don't worry Josh, we will.' Even though I couldn't see her, I knew she was smiling as she spoke. 'Thank you Casey.' I whispered, my heart heavy but my soul warmed by her beauty. There was a brief moment of silence, her head on my shoulder, my head leaning against hers as her beautiful hair splayed across my shirt in an unpredictable fashion. 'Hey Casey?' I broke the momentary silence. 'Mmmm?' She meowed back. Are you okay with...' I paused, stuck for words. ...this?' Another pause. 'The whole Rosa, Espen thing...' 'It's okay Josh.' She reassured me. 'I'm not going to hate you for a mistake you made in the past, I'm not going to hold you to yesterdays sins.' I soaked her words up like a sponge, reveling in her graciousness before posing my next question. 'Even if it was a life-altering mistake?' 'Josh, you have no accountability to me for the mistakes you made before we met.' She giggled lightly before speaking again. 'We all make mistakes, after all...I married Howie Branner.' Her humor seemed to lighten the burden, the albatross hanging so heavily around my neck, as I laughed. 'I guess you do have a point.' 'That was definitely one of those things where you sit back and ask yourself what you were thinking afterwards.' Casey said, a smile in her voice. 'Wow, you married a guy with a mullet...' Her smile was contagious, I noticed this as it crept onto my face. 'It wasn't about his looks.' Casey stated softly. 'Truthfully? It was about his money. I was a starving model and he had connections.' 'Wow, maybe I should get into the model-pimping business.' My tears began to dry as we continued the playful banter. 'No, I don't think so.' Casey said, a joke lingering in her voice. I took the bait. 'Why not?' 'Like I said, it wasn't about love or even lust, it was all about the money.' Casey chuckled. 'Boy, you would be in the red so fast it would make your pretty little head spin.' 'Case, I...' I tried to respond but was cut off by a voice over the loud speaker. 'Ladies and gentlemen.' Crooned a soft female voice. 'Your captain has requested that everyone please put their seatbelts on and turn off all electronic devices as we prepare to land, we will be touching down in El Paso in approximately ten minutes.' I opened my eyes at the announcement, the moment of truth was at hand. As soon as we left the El Paso airport it was thirty minutes in a taxi to dirty Juarez, Mexico to determine how my son would live his life. I glanced out the window again, aiming my eyes vertically this time. 'I want the best...for all of us...' I whispered in my heart, where only One could hear me. ---- 10:48 AM. August 16th, 2005. Lucha academy of arts. 'Pick up the pace Estrella!' Snapped Lucha Libre instructor Juan Padilla, standing on the outside of the ring in a blue tank, white shorts and Nikes. Estrella was one of the two men squaring off in a wide ring, decorated with gold ropes and a red canvas. He was the shorter of the two, but faster. Dressed in neon yellow tights, boots and a black mask with neon yellow decor he was aptly named after the Spanish term: 'star'. The other man, a few inches taller, holding maybe a ten pound weight advantage and dressed in black tights, and a half-white/half-black mask was known as Roderick Torres, nicknamed: 'el bandito cheato'. Roderick circled the grappler simply known as Estrella in the ring, looking for a chance to open their spot, both men fully aware that the keen brown eyes of Juan Padilla were upon them, and each man eager to gain their instructors seal of approval. Estrella stretched his right hand out and 'el bandito cheato' accepted it with a second of hesitance before fully locking up hand on hand, Estrella lifted his left hand and again Roderick accepted the grapple, as soon as he took on the challenge; Estrella dropped to the canvas and sunk his boots into the gut of Torres flipping him overhead with a monkey flip, their hands breaking apart. As Torres flips over he gracefully turns the maneuver into a full somersault, landing on his feet. Roderick Torres wastes no time as he blitzes towards the ropes, hits them, rebound and drills Estrella dead in the face with a low dropkick as the yellow-clad trainee begins to rise! 'Perfect, way to go Rod!' Juan applauded from ring side. Estrella rolled over, trying to regain his bearings before Torres pounced on him again, yet as he rolled over the sound of the gym door opening stopped everyone in their tracks, all eyes rising to the beautiful sight that had just stepped into the Lucha academy of arts. She was 5'8, maybe a hundred and twenty pounds, smoking hot in a pair of white Capris and adidas sneakers - matching a vintage orange 'Reeses' t-shirt, dark shades pulled over big brown eyes, and streaks of blonde-brown hair falling across her face. Her face itself was a masterpiece, she had striking cheekbones, fine lips, a cute little nose and a 'girl next door' type of innocence in her smile; enter Casey Branner. Even Estrella couldn't help but smile through his mask, never mind the fact that his jaw has just been realigned by a pair of size 10 boots. Torres dropped to a knee, taking a breather as he soaked up the looks of the sweet little thing at the door. Even the business minded Juan Padilla was drawn away from the task at hand, his eyes drinking in the beauty of the soon to be ex Mr's. Branner. As the three men gawked, a fourth entered the room, hot on Casey's tail; in more ways than one. The man behind Casey was instantly recognized in his rockstar like dress, crazy hair and stunning features: 'American Youth' Josh, world famous Lucha academy of arts graduate. 'Josh.' Padilla was the only one to speak, his voice jubilant as he smiled through a set of crooked teeth. 'Juan, my friend.' Josh returned his smile, walking across the room to meet Juan half-way and throwing an arm around him with a big hug as the two old friends embraced. 'Juan Padilla, I swear you get uglier every time I see you.' 'Yeah?' Juan sneered playfully before ribbing back. 'You get shorter every time I see you, got any cartilage left in that knee of yours?' 'Hey, that's a low shot you smelly whore-hounder.' Josh retorted, smacking Juan across his chest. 'Look who's talking.' Juan grinned, setting Josh up. 'Aren't you with a different girl every time I see you?' 'Hey, this time I'm at least dating a girl with class!' Josh shot back defensively. 'Shall I run down the list of tramps?' Juan teased. Josh fought back with a chuckle. 'As fun as it is I didn't fly all the way to Juarez to have you brag about how many girls I've had sex with in, in comparison to the three hookers in Tijuana that you tapped.' 'Then why are you here, Josh?' Juan inquired. Josh paused, his Adam's apple bobbing deeply as he gulped. 'I'm here for my son.' Juan's expression was somber, he blinked slowly, a hard line appearing across his lips. 'Josh...' He placed a hand on my shoulder. 'Are you sure?' I nodded slowly but vigorously. 'Yes, I'm sure.' 'This is a big decision Josh, as big of a decision as you'll ever make.' Juan warned sternly. 'I know that Juan, my God, don't you think I know that by now?' I gasped in exasperation. 'My name has been smeared all across the tabloids, the dirt sheets, dragged through the mud and kicked around, and I'm here to change all of that.' Juan looked deeply into my eyes with piercing black orbs. 'Is that what this is about?' 'What..?' I was slightly confused and made that fact evident. 'You?' Juan's voice was sharp. 'Is this about you Josh? About your name? About clearing up the once pristine image of 'American Youth' Josh?' 'No, not at all...' I responded a bit surprised at Juan's cutting tone. 'It had better not be, because Josh, there is so much more to this than you. In fact, you aren't even the person I'm most worried about in this situation, a little boy is involve, an innocent little boy and I don't want to see you screw his life up.' Juan's eyes were cold and accusing. 'I'm not going to screw his life up!' I protested loudly, prompting the two luchadores to exit the ring and even the gym itself, seeking out the sanctity of the dressing room. 'Are you going to change?' Juan asked. 'Is it going to be the same thing night after night? You come home in the early morning hours with a different woman, your a little banged up, bruised maybe a black eye or swollen lip. Josh, that messes with a little kids head!' 'I've already changed, Juan.' I moaned, raising my hands, palms up. 'What more can I do?' 'Josh, did I tell you that I decided to retire when my wife had our first child?' Juan asked earnestly. 'No.' I shook my head. 'I did, and when Javier died I was asked to take over the school so I leapt at the chance. Josh, I love Wrestling every bit as much as you do...every bit.' Juan's voice was tired, showing the stress he had been under as head trainer of the lucha academy. 'I grew up in a Wrestling family and when my father came home with a different injury every night it killed me. I cried myself to sleep on the nights he was Wrestling too far away to come home at night, I didn't know what had happened to him, I didn't know what was going to happen to him. I spent my childhood living in fear, terrified that my father would be hurt and never able to walk again.' The strain was visible and obvious in Juan's dark complexion. 'I don't think it would be wise to choose the life of both a father and a Wrestler at the same time, Josh...its one or the other.' I set my chin, my resolve growing. 'This is my life.' 'It's his life too!' Juan yelled back at me, showing deep concern for Espen. 'Take me to my son, Juan.' I spat out stubbornly, as Casey stepped up next to me and squeezed my arm gently in an effort to keep my stress level from reaching its breaking point. 'Josh...' Juan slowly began with a scowl. 'Juan, please...take me to my son.' I begged, with pleading eyes. Juan glanced over at Casey, seemingly as if he was awaiting her approval, she nodded slowly and Juan returned the nod. Sighing slowly he raised his hands in defeat. 'Okay, you win.' I bit my lip, cringing at the words that came out of my mouth. 'Thank you...' ---- 11:08 AM. August 16th, 2005. A Juarez neighborhood. My eyes traveled from building to building, they were painted in various colors, some of the houses looked like a rainbow had thrown up on them. Splotches of pink, pale blue and lime green didn't serve for the best appearance on houses with broken shutters and cracked chunks of Spanish tile falling off of the roof. As run down and disgusting as this neighborhood was it wasn't anything out of the norm, for Juarez. Broken down cars rested in lawns of dirt and rocks with a few weeds in between serving as the only form of vegetation. Trash blew down the streets, blowing from yard to yard. Overflowing trashbags rested on the sidewalk waiting to be picked up and disposed of and from the looks of things it would be quite a while before that duty was fulfilled. It was slow as the clock ticked down towards Siesta time, few figures could be seen in the surrounding area. The only human in sight was an elderly woman, washing her clothes in a metal tub, water spilling out of the sides into her yard. She was obviously poor, perhaps too poor to afford soap, because the water she washed with was dark from the filthiness of her clothes, no soap suds were to be seen. The only other form of life was a mangy dog curled up near her, brown and dingy, missing hair in places, his matted coat hanging loosely over his thin frame. As Juan's 1996 Chevy Tahoe rumbled down the street my eyes searched through the broken fences, and tired lawns looking for any sign of the four year old boy I had traveled so far to see. 'This is it.' Juan exclaimed, pulling to a stop in front of a ran down house. I turned my eyes to the house, brown with pink shutters and chipped Spanish tile on the roof. The lawn had no grass but it did at least bare flowers and was devoid of any weeds, showing that it had garnered at least some attention from it's owner. In spite of that, I remained unimpressed. 'This?' I raised my eyebrows. 'My son is living in this rat resort?' 'It doesn't look like much, but he's with good people.' Juan stated, turning off the engine. 'Javier always told me great things about his parents, I just hope that they live up to the hype.' I sighed, opening the wide door of the Tahoe and stepping out onto the cracked sidewalk, weeds growing up through it. 'Whether or not you like their home, it's filled with love.' Said Juan as he and Casey exited the Tahoe. I shrugged. 'Lead the way.' Juan did so and did so quickly as he marched to the front door and rapped on the splintered wood rapidly. 'Are you going to be okay?' Casey whispered into my ear as she squeezed my hand. 'I think so.' I answered, forcing a smile. Juan knocked again, this time the door opened while he was in mid-knock. The woman at the door was elderly, her hair white and short, her dark skin wrinkled, her brown eyes tired and weary. 'Juan!' She exclaimed, her weary eyes lighting up at the sight of him. 'Sra. Padilla, nosotros necesitamos hablar con usted. “D’nde est” su marido?' Juan responded in his native tongue, his voice flowing so smoothly it was hard for me to keep up with him, I did manage to make out that he had informed her we needed to speak with both her and her husband as well. '¿Hay algo malo?' As an elderly woman she spoke a little slower and I understood her; she had asked what was wrong. 'Por favor Sra Padilla, obtiene apenas a su marido, esto es muy importante.' Juan responded, as I struggled to make out anything beyond 'very important' and a request for her husband. She nodded. 'Entre, por favor, yo lo despertar?.' She said that her husband was sleeping, turning away she invited Juan, Casey and I to enter the house as she went into another room to wake him. The three of us took a seat in their humble living room, decorated in numerous pictures of Javier and his students, as well as pictures of Rosa and Espen, several of the family together, smiling and appearing to be ever so happy until all of that had changed on one dark night. As Casey and I lounged on a couch, Juan sat across from us in a wooden chair. We waited briefly, before Senor Hector Padilla entered the room. He was a small man, shorter than Javier had been and thinner as well. Standing at a mere five foot seven, weighing a generous one-fifty Senor Padilla was hardly an intimidating figure. His white hair was short, his face wrinkled and covered with a beard. “Juan mi hijo!, Senor Padilla exclaimed with a smile, he had just called Juan his son, Juan stood to his feet in response, Casey and I followed suit. 'Sr. Padilla, he tra’do a alguien verle, para ver m’s importantemente Espen.' Juan answered, telling him that I wished to see Espen. '?Ese cerdo! “Por qu” debe yo d’jelo ver Espen? ??l puede romper tan su coraz’n tambi’n? ??l ha lastimado a bastante gente ahora?' Hector roared, his voice loud as he cast the insult of 'pig' at me indirectly, proclaiming the harm I had done to his family. I spoke up, my Spanish far from perfect. 'Sr. Padilla, estoy verdad apesadumbrado. Era todo el un error y estoy aqu? fijar cosas. “Por favor, apenas permita que vea a mi hijo?' My words were choppy, perhaps even mispronounced but with great apology and sorrow I asked to see my son. Hector's eyes were full of flames as he spoke. '?Por qu?” “Usted puede romper tan su coraz’n tambi’n” Espen merece mejor que un bastardo del gringo para un padre.' I struggled to understand it all but I managed to make out that Espen deserved more than a gringo bastard for a father. At that point I literally fell to my knees, begging to see my son. 'Apenas deseo por favor ver a mi hijo' Hector's voice snapped violently. 'Mi esposa ahora ha llamado a polic’a, licencia antes de que “l le tire y arrastre su carcasse de la descomposici’n lejos que devoured por los gusanos.' His wife had called the police, we had to leave. Juan slowly turned to me to make sure I understood. 'His wife called...' 'I know.' I sighed, cutting him off as I stood to my feet. 'Josh, I'm sorry but we have to leave, there is nothing we can do...' Juan shook his head with pity. 'Maybe we can fight it Juan?' I asked, but deep down I knew the truth. 'Josh, he's living with his two loving grandparents, the courts would never hand him over to someone that makes his living kicking in another man's skull.' Juan said with brutal honesty. 'Juan, I came all of this way to see my son...' I shook my head sadly. 'Josh, I feel for you, but there is nothing we can do.' Juan said, with a grim look across his features. 'Faithless is he who turns back when the road becomes dark.' I whispered softly, quoting Tolkien. 'What?' Juan asked, unsure of what to respond with. 'ESPEN!' I screamed at the top of my lungs, much to the surprise of everyone in the room. Hector roared back with a command to leave. '?Licencia! “Aqu” ahora salga de usted del bastardo del gringo!' 'ESPEN, ESPEN!' I screamed, ignoring Hector. 'Josh, stop!' Juan screamed at me. 'No, I came here to see my son and that is what I'm going to do, NO ONE will stop me Juan!' I snapped, screaming out my son's name again. ESPEN!' 'Josh...' Casey whispered, reaching for my arm. I shook her hand off, screaming again. 'ESPEN!' Hector stormed past me and threw the door open, his voice at an all time high with another demand that I leave. '?Bastantes! “Usted ha hecho bastantes, se va antes de que usted lastimara a nuestra familia m’s!' 'Espen...' I cried out weakly, giving up all hope of the child appearing. 'Espen...' I fell back onto my knees, tears streaming down my face as my closed eyes burnt with the bittersweet moisture. Suddenly the pitter-patter of small feet snapped my eyes back open as a small boy stepped around the corner. He was four years old, almost five and a handsome little kid at that. He looked like his mother, his hair curly and bushy, a smile covering his dark face. One could hardly tell that he had any Caucasian blood until you looked into his eyes... They were blue... No... Gray” That wasn't it either... They were a strange...bluish gray. He had my eyes. 'Espen...' I said with a weak smile. Juan threw both arms around me and pulled me back to my feet as he attempted to drag me out of the door. 'Espen!' I screamed again, much to the little boys surprise. I could hardly fight Juan off in my weakened condition and with the help of Hector Padilla I was quickly forced to the doorway, using all of my strength I latched onto the doorjam with one hand, craning my neck back in to speak to Espen. 'Espen, Recuerde, su padre le ama.' I said, weakly telling him that his father loved him. Espen stared at me, confusion in his bluish gray eyes. 'Pero mi padre es muerto.' The words were slow enough that I could understand them perfectly, and as I fell to the ground outside of the door the words echoed through my head... 'Pero mi padre es muerto...' Echo. 'Pero mi padre es muerto...' Echo. 'Pero mi padre es muerto...' Echo. 'Pero mi padre es muerto...' Echo. 'Pero mi padre es muerto...' Echo. The translation? 'My father is dead.'
