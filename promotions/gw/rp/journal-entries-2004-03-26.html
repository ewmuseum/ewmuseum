26 March 2004

Date: March 26 He was sitting there, writing in the small journal. He did not often keep a journal seriously. He thought it was too sloppy, too unprofessional and most of all to sissy. The small notebook was not so much a journal as it was a release from the real world. A way to let go of all the pent up feelings inside, a way to tell the story of his past. A way to release. Anger, stress, pain, fear, hurt, joy, sorrow, happiness, excitement, pleasure, it could all be released. It was a place to keep his memories. It was many years ago. The night was dark and cold. The evening seemed to bring forth a feeling of loneliness inside. The two of us sat there, Ichiro was 16 at the time and me, I was 13. We were camped out in the mountain brush of Mt. Kidake. We were protected from the soft rain that fell from the heavens, by the thick foliage of the trees over head. We sat around, staring into the small blaze in front of us, the fire crackling as we watched. The flames flickered and the sparks of burning wood crackled and flew wildly from the fire. The light illuminating from the fire, gave us just enough light to see the loneliness on one another’s trembling faces. The air was cold, and it nipped at our faces and bare skin. We were alone out there, alone in the lonely bush. The small shelter we had made stood behind us, softly contrasting with the darkness of the bushes behind it. For the time being he had to stop. The memories in his head ran through like nightmares. Jushin put the pen down and buried his head in his arms. This night had been one of the most frightening of his life, the horror of the visions still haunted him to this day. He hoped that this would help him to put his mind at ease. With that he picked up the pen once again. The light trickle of the rain on the foliage hanging over head kept us awake. Its constant pitter patter enough to drive someone insane, although it was not strong enough to drip through and cause any physical discomfort. Perhaps it was not the rain, but merely the atmosphere of the bush that was causing our inability to sleep, perhaps we were just placing blame. There was a rustling in the bushes. Something out there stalked the two of us. Something hidden by the thick camouflage of the night and of the mountain forest. My fear grew stronger with every passing second. The fire would keep the animals at bay, but how long would the fire wood last was all that kept running through my mind. The writing became too much, the fear grew inside of his mind and once again Jushin stopped writing. It seemed stupid now to look back and to think how scared he was, and he was sure that to any one reading this journal, they would surely think of him as a wuss. But, as he dreamt about it, and as he thought about it more and more, his manhood strength and reason seemed to disappear only to be replaced with by the humble and confused mind of his thirteen year old self. Ichiro sat on a small wooden log, it was well shaped and comfortable enough to sit on for long periods of time. I, myself, sat on a similar log. They were comfortable enough, enough for sitting in the middle of the bush on a rainy night at least. I stood for a second, I could no longer take the cold bite of the wind, I walked to the shelter, and returned to my place near the fire, this time with a blanket. Ichiro sat and merely stared at the fire. Poking at the fire, with his damp poking stick my big brother seemed almost possessed by the fire, as if he was talking to it. As I sat, the uneasiness in my stomach grew stronger, as so did the sounds from the bush around us. This was one of their many tasks of initiation. Tests to prove that they were indeed the rightful heirs to the title of The Souls of the Black Lotus. One of the many tasks to prove that they were the Lotus? chosen. They had passed many tests previously. Some harder, some easier, some seemingly irrelevant and some of the most importance, that was the way it was for them, always being tested. But this one, this one had been a true test of all of their skills. Their fighting skills. Their survival skills. And most of all their teamwork and compatibility skills. The night had fallen on the second night of their week long journey of initiation. They would spend six nights and seven days alone in the mountain wilderness, six nights in the forests of Mt. Kidake. Their goal was to navigate their way all the way to a small outpost in the middle of the bush and retrieve a medallion from it. After that, they would turn back and make their way back to the Shao Lin Temple, atop the mountain. All this was to be done using only a small makeshift map and the light of the sun and moon as their guides. During their first two days they had made great progress. Ichiro was a great navigator and by the brothers? calculations they had about half of a day’s hike left to their destination. They would be on their way back home, towards the temple by the end of the next day at the latest. They would have to make it through this night first however. Jushin felt an ice cold shiver run through the length of his spine as he picked up the pen, and put its ball point against his paper. Whatever it was that was out there in the bush was getting closer. It was getting braver and braver by the second. Needless to say I was scared out of my wits at this point. I was nearly frozen solid, unable to move, or to even make a sound. As I looked at my brother, he still sat there. Stone faced, glaring into the flickering flame of the fire. “Aren’t you going to get some rest?” I asked him. “I’m not tired, if you need sleep, go now, sleep. I will be fine for the rest of the night,” came my big brothers reply. It was strange, because as unrealistic as it sounded it was true. Ichiro did not look like he had missed a drop of sleep, nor did he look like he needed it. He sat there, simply gazing into the fire, even when he spoke his gaze did not leave the fire. But I can still remember the look in his eyes from that night to this day, there was a strange intensity there. Something I had never seen in his eyes before, strangely it was comforting. It was a look of sheer concentration, and somehow with that gaze watching over me, I felt as if I was as safe as being in my own bed. There has always been something about my brother that comforts me, the sense that no matter what he will always watch over me. He is like my living, breathing guardian angel, and this night was just one of the many nights in which he proved it. The rules of the trip were simple, Jushin remembered as he dropped the pen and began to stretch slightly. They were not allowed any provisions except for a few blankets, a canteen for water each and the map that they were given. Then they were sent off into the forest. No food, no weapons to hunt food, nor to keep away any predators. And as the visions began to haunt Jushin once again, he remembered that night. He remembered that predators had been his biggest problem that night. I woke up about two hours later as far as I could tell from the light of the moon. I had fallen asleep not long after Ichiro had told me to rest up, I didn’t take much convincing. I stood and crept out of the shelter slowly and groggily. I wiped my eyes, trying to wake myself up as well as to make my eyes focused. I still remember the sight as if it was yesterday. The small fire, sitting still crackling but dwindling second by second. As well, the stack of firewood was gone, but the most frightening part of it all, was that Ichiro was nowhere to be seen. He was not near the fire, he was not visible anywhere. I stumbled out away from the shelter blindly, my mind not quite awake yet. I wandered closer to the fire, but walked past it to the clear opening. That’s when it happened, the scariest sight of my life. As I stared out into the bush looking for any sign of my older brother, I saw it. A large dog looking creature, to this day I’m not quite sure what it was, but to my best guess it was a massive wolf. The muscles seemed to ripple through the beast’s thick coat of fur. The fangs were exposed, and his mouth was covered in a foamy saliva. His claws dug into the ground, and made a terrible dragging noise as he walked. The scariest part however was the eyes. The cold, angry stare of the beast was enough to paralyze me. I stood like a dear in headlights. Jushin had to pause momentarily. He put the pen down, and shook the cobwebs from his head, it was a scary thought, but it needed to be documented. It would help him rest easier, he assured himself. And with that he picked the pen up once again, this time to finish the entry he had started. The beast began to slowly inch towards my paralyzed body. I was too scared to move, too scared to run, too scared to even scream for help. The beast was snarling and I thought for certain that I had met my match, and that I would not live to see the light of day again. That’s when he came to my rescue. Ichiro, dove down out of a tree. In his hand the wooden stick that he had been poking the fire with. However, attached to the end of it, tied on with some sort of weed or grass, was a small metal spear tip. That must have been why he was keeping it in the fire for so long, trying to shape it. I still could not do anything but fall to the ground and cower as I saw the beast lunge forward. It had not done so however on it’s own free will, but it was the urge of my brother’s spear which had sent it sprawling. Now in front of me, it laid dead and bleeding, Ichiro did not speak, he did not yell, he did not look at me in disgust. He merely walked past with his spear, and went back to his seat by the fire. He threw a couple more logs onto the fire and then stuck the spear back into the fire to wash the blood off the end of it. I slowly made my way back to the shelter, my head hung in shame, but my brother was not disappointed in me. He did not even glance as I walked back to the shelter. I felt bad, for endangering him, and was truly sorry. I planned to tell him the next day. But soon enough, I fell asleep to the same vision I had fallen asleep with before. My big brother watching over me and guarding me, keeping me safe. Finally satisfied Jushin put down the pen and stood from his seat. He grabbed his workout bag and made his way towards the door, leaving the pen atop the paper, still wet with ink. He walked out the door, with a sense of security over him. They would not fail this week, they couldn’t. He would not allow it. Ichiro had always been there for him, and now it was his turn to return the favor. With a renewed sense of confidence, Jushin left his apartment and made his way to his training.
