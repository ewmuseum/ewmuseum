Moving Day!

2 November 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/2/03 Most of the time, when it comes time for children to leave their home it is a sad occasion. So many broken hearted tears falling from the eyes of both the parent and child. Too many memories being left behind. Too much pain to rip from home, fly from the nest and start one of their own. However, that’s not always the case. Especially today for Johnie Ringo. He finally has the momentum (essentially the money) to move out of his mother’s house and go someplace new. But like always, everything comes with a price tag. “Why do you have to go now?” his mother sputters out as Johnie brushes by her as he goes back through the doorway into his room. He goes directly to one of the stacked boxes near the bed. He can already feel the sadness building in her. Why so soon? She knows the reason. Or at least she should. “Its obvious, isn’t it?” He can taste the contempt on his tongue, but it doesn’t bother him. He’s out. What does it matter? “I want to move out, I’ve got a place to go, I’m gonna go there. So in simple terms, I’m leaving. Why is it so hard for you to understand that I’m going now?” “Because it doesn’t make any sense why its happening so suddenly. I mean...ugh.” The words aren’t coming to her yet. Maybe it’ll be clearer to her later. She’s in the room now, standing near the door. She crosses her arms across her chest as she overlooks his packing job. “Look, I just need a place of my own where I can actually have some breathing room.” Johnie lifts the second stacked box just a little less than his arm’s width and starts heading towards the door. “Do you mean to say that I’ve been smothering you?” That stops him. He turns to her as she keeps grilling him. “Is that it” Have I been too hard on you or something?” “Don’t give me that shit, Mom. I don’t need any of this.” He heads out through the door and heads down the steps. Being followed of course, as he hears the clunking of the heels meeting the hardwood from behind him. Tilting himself back a bit, he readjusts the box to get his hands underneath it. Maybe it’ll get him some better stability. “I just need to get out of here. And you just need to accept that. Ok?” “Maybe I don’t want to accept it. Maybe I want to understand it a bit. To know where I went wrong.” She latches her talons on a bit more, still trying to hang onto whatever he can before he’s gone. “Well at least try to deal with it. Either way, I’m gone.” He squishes onto the soft carpet and heads towards the front door, being careful not to bump any tables along the way. Although, maybe he should. It would distract her for a second or two as she’d dive to save some valuable vase or a priceless heirloom of some kind. Still he heads towards the door. As he gets to the door, he tries to reach the sleek handle with his left hand but can’t quite get a hold of it. It keeps sliding out of his grasp over and over again. He switches over and tries with his right. Nothing. Dammit! Just get me outta here! His mother tries to dance around him to help, but Johnie keeps nudging the box into her face. She’s not getting by. Not as long as he can help it. “Let me hel...” “NO!” he shoots back at her with a glare of flame. “I’ll get it.” She shrugs to herself and looks down at the floor of the next room sighing regretfully. Eventually he gets the door cracked and pulls back enough to where he can get a finger around to the other side and swing it open. He steps down off the front step and heads to the driveway over the clopping of the walkway. This time he’s not being followed. Johnie heads over to tailgate of a late-90’s Dodge Ram truck with blue and silver trim spiraling around the sides. He shoves the box into the back and pushes it more towards the front of the bed. Leaving the tailgate down, he turns back towards the house. Of course he has a stunned roadblock in his way. “What?” he shrugs. “Where did you get that?” he asks as she continues to stare at the monstrosity in her driveway. “Does it matter?” He tries to step up to see if she’d move to the side. No such luck. “It matters.” Now she turns her head to him. This time with the “judging mom” look. “Where did you get it?” “I picked it up a few days ago. Big deal.” Forget waiting for her to move. He goes up and nudges her out of the way. She gives chase as he goes towards the stairs. “It is a big deal.” Her hands are on her hips walking now. She means something now. “Pfft.” First foot on the step, left hand on the banister. Here it comes, I bet. “Did you steal it.” Bingo. Johnie stops on that first step and twists around slowly, still with his hand on the railing. His eyes are ablaze with impatience. He’s about had it. He turns and heads towards his mother, stomping with thunderous steps. He plunges his nose into her distressed face. Still, his voice is calm. “No...I did not steal the truck. I do not care to steal a truck. And furthermore...” until he loses it. “how dare you accuse me of stealing the goddamn thing! I paid for the fucking thing! I got it legit. You understand” I’m not a fucking criminal!! My friends are not fucking crooks!! I am not what you think I am! I am not what you think I’m going to be!” Johnie turns and heads back upstairs. A little more stomp to his step. He shoves his door wide open as it wobbles against the wall. Instantly he heads to the last two boxes remaining. Probably would be wiser to take them both one at a time, but he’s done here. He’s had enough. His knees crackling as he crouches down to pick them up and turns to head out. Trouncing down the steps, he heads past the end tables, knick-knacks, vases, heirlooms, everything. Including what he wished was a pillar of salt instead of a mother. The door still swung open and inviting him to head out. Johnie tosses the boxes in with a cluttered clatter. Like he cared. He just wanted out. But he stops for a second. Panting angrily and loudly, mostly angrily, he leans himself against the open tailgate looking down. His arms extended and flexed. He’s had enough strain for one day. He shoves himself off and slams the tailgate shut as he gazes back at the house for a last look. He sees his mother standing in the doorway, still wearing the faded look of grief across her face. Johnie shakes his head leaning towards the ground and laughs for a second. Goddamnit... “I got a job, Mom.” He looks right at her this time. Not holding back. Not smarting off. Not being an asshole. Just looks. “Alright?” She says nothing. She doesn’t even move. Just stands there with the same look on her face. Not sure if that’s a good sign or not. Still, he scratches his head and continues anyway. “It pays good. Real good. I even set my own wages. I work when I want to. I just...*sigh* I just wanted you to know that.” Not a sign at all. Oh well. Johnie walks towards the driver side of the truck and creaks the door open. Guess that’s just one of the things to work on. He lifts his leg to get in, but he’s stopped. “Is there...” Oh here we go. “Is there something I did” Something I said?” Those are tears in her voice. Not sure how that’s possible, but there are. Johnie glances over his shoulder at her still in the doorway. “Yes.... Everything.” He hops up and sticks the key in the ignition. The truck rumbles and roars with power and horses. Like an untamed beast begging to be played with. Shifting the stick, Johnie shoves it into reverse and quickly backs out into the street. He quickly hits the stick again and throws her in forward and heads off around the corner out down the avenue. Doesn’t tilt his mirrors. Doesn’t move his view. Just heads out. Glad to be out. Hope I don’t come back.
