Date: 30th of October, 2005. The Way We Were: February 2003 The warm sea water of the Pacific Ocean floated up onto the shores of the port city of Fukuoka. The clearest of blue waters floated on the sandy beaches, this beach a distance from the busy port town of Shima, the gateway to the large city of Fukuoka, where the Fukuoka Dome lay in the distance. The docks of Shima were often crowded places, outdoor restaurants and caf??s as far as the eye could see. Today was no different, with the tourists flocking from Korea, China and the colder, north of Japan to enjoy a day in the sun. And it was here that twenty three year old Akoshi Kunamara, Radiant, the unwilling heir to a multi-billion-dollar Yakuza clan, hidden away from his troubles, and his family, who are so desperately trying to return to their island, and accept the title of boss. He walked with brown sandals with navy blue cargo shorts, and a white muscle shirt. He hadn’t cut his hair in months, and it was getting dangerously longer than it used to be. In his hands, Akoshi carried a tray of sliced of fish and various other seaside delights, two of every meal. One was for him, and the other was for his fianc?-should-be-wife Minori Kumako. She sat, with her three-quarter beige pin-strip pants crossed over one leg, and her arms folded over her white top. Minori was only two and a bit months older than Akoshi, and two met in the dingy areas of South Korea, on that faithful weekend Akoshi began this entire saga of hiding in the shadows, constantly looking over his shoulder for one of the men he knew like the back of his hand. The pressures of her family to become a Geisha became too much for Minori at age seventeen, and she ran away, like her fianc?, for a new life. “Here you go,” Akoshi said, sliding off a china plate of seafood to Minori, and sliding the other to himself. Minori didn’t say much, or look at the food, either. Her dark brown eyes looked over the waters of the Sea of Japan, peering toward Korea. Akoshi ate, feverishly, the last couple of days he hadn’t eaten. “Ah, dats betta,” Akoshi broke with the silence with a mouth full of food, completely unusual for a Japanese person. Minori still hadn’t said a word, and her fianc? frowned at her silence. “What’s the matter?” “I would have thought you’d know by now,” Minori said, poking at the plate of uneaten seafood in front of her, not really interested or hungry. Unlike Akoshi, she ate over the last couple of days, and wasn’t afraid to walk to a Supermarket and buy some food. “I mean, how long has this been going on?” “Um,” he said, thinking out-loud. “About six months,” he finished, following a frown from Minori, she hated this, absolutely hated it. Minori hated having to live in cheap motels where there were no hot spring baths, and the food was horrible. But, she loved him, and she told herself she’d live through this, so one day they could get married and raise a normal family. “See what I mean?” she whined, throwing in a few hand motions to prove her point. “Six months, six months! Yeah, the yakuza isn’t the most appealing life, but this is ridiculous!” Akoshi dropped his chopsticks onto the plate, half of his meal eaten, and doubtful the other half would. He sighed. She rolled her eyes. They were always arguing about this, and it happened at least twice a week. “Whatever,” he mumbled, and then quickly slide his chair out, and headed to the wooden beams separating the dock from the Sea of Japan, and eventually, Korea. He leant forward, thinking about his troubles with his brown eyes gazing on the ripples of green and mixed blue sea water slapping up against the wooden docks of Fukuoka. * * * The Ginza district of Tokyo was known as the nightlife part of the nation’s capital. Sewn down each side of the streets are neon billboards, flashing different advertisements every fifteen seconds. The streets are lined with rows and rows of nightclubs, fast food restaurants, and even Mini-Golf centres. But down a side-street, in a Video Game store, were Montaro Kunamara and his group of teenage friends, looking through an assortment of video games they could buy. Montaro was only eighteen, and probably was never going to be initiated into the Kunamara crime business. He was a nephew of Masanori Kunamara, born in the summer of 1985. Montaro knew what his family did; it wasn’t hard for him to find out when he spends so much time in the Ginza district, a natural haven of the Yakuza families, many of whom owned the nightclubs just around the corner from his favourite store. While Montaro and his friends gawked over the latest games on sale, and at the price, they hadn’t noticed two men, with purple pinstripe suits on entering the store, their eyes hidden behind sunglasses, and in the case of the shorter one, a hat. The two men whispered to each other, the taller of the two, by a good three inches, pointed at the kid in the centre of the group of video game fanatics. “That’s him,” he whispered, pointing his index finger at Montaro. The owner of the store, a migrant Korean man in his forties, noticed that these two were missing the top part of the pinkie finger on their left hand. They were Yakuza, he instantly realized. “Jikkyu, do you remember what Yukio told us to do?” “Of course I fucking well do, Saisuke,” he whispered angrily back to his partner, lowering his sunglasses so he could get a good look at Montaro. “We’ll wait for them to leave here, and grab him outside. Okay?” “Got it,” Saisuke replied, ducking his head on the way out of the store. Stupid Koreans, he thought to himself as they leant on the brick wall, lighting up a cigarette or two. They silently smoked their cigarettes, until Saisuke wanted to break the conversation. Plus, the kids inside couldn’t hear them. “So, how’s Nori?” “Fine,” Jikkyu said quietly. Noriko Hiriyama was his girlfriend, though they’d severely drifted apart over the last few months, and he had no doubt she was seeing, and sleeping with someone else. That uneasy silence befell once again, the ashes from the cigarettes plummeted to the concrete, mashed into nothing by the steel bottomed boots, and the butts fell shortly after as the purple nicotine smoke wafted into the air. Inside, they could hear Montaro saying goodbyes to his friends and the owner, Saisuke rolled his eyes in disgust. The skinny, young Montaro opened the door from the game shop and stepped outside. Within an instance, the two hidden Yakuza members jumped out, the bigger and stronger Jikkyu wrapping the skinny boy up in a full nelson, his screams were covered up by a snake-like quick bandage placed over his mouth. Through muffled shrieks, he mouthed to them, “Who are you” What do you want of me!”” Tears had formed in his terrified eyes, even though he knew they were Yakuza. “Relax,” Saisuke said to the scared Montaro, mockingly patting the kid on the head, flattening his mop like hair. “We’re not going to kill you. We work for your Uncle. Now, I’m going to take the tape off your mouth, and if you scream ?” he began to say, removing his knife from its sheath, “I’ll cut your hand off.” Saisuke watched the young man’s eyes widen as he leaned forward, and slowly removed the tape from his mouth, taking whatever facial hair Montaro had with him. Montaro rubbed his upper-lip, massaging the pain out of the raw skin, Jikkyu had let go of the full nelson. “There,” Saisuke said calmly, staring a hole through the young Kunamara. “What do you want of me!”” Montaro said through heavy breathing, his words broken and full of fright. “I-is it money!” I have money!” He leant back against the wall, an escape route was blocked. He was trapped. “We don’t want your fucking money,” said Jikkyu, angered that a person with Kunamara blood in them would dare associate with Korean’s. “We want information.” “Information” Like what?” he anxiously said, a river of sweat on his brow. “The train stop is over there! That’s information! He-he-he!” “We’re not amused,” Saisuke said, somewhat robotically. He lunged forward, grabbing Montaro by the lips and lifting him up, Montaro panicked ? he fought back from wetting his pants. “How well do you know your cousin, Akoshi?” No answer came from Montaro. Saisuke’s eyes blazed anger; he tightened his already vice grip on the boys lips, tears splashing down on his hand like a small child in a little wading pool. “It’s in your best interest to answer me sooner, rather than later.” Montaro whimpered, more tears ran down his face, he began to move his lips. “Yeah ? I know him,” Montaro said, sniffing loudly, and obnoxiously. “We used to talk a little when we were kids, but my uncle never really liked to socialise us with his kids ?” “Where is he?” Jikkyu barked at the frightened young Kunamara. A long pause came from Montaro. This only caused Saisuke to further tighten his grip on his lips; the lack of blood had turned them purple. “Okay!” Montaro whimpered again, breathing slowly. “He ? he’s in Fukuoka.” Saisuke let him go, Montaro slammed onto the ground, tail-bone first. He reached into his jacket and pulled out a small mobile phone, on the speed dial he pressed 090, and the little text below it read Fukimi-san. Only a second passed before he spoke into the phone, “Fukuoka”. * * * Minori Kumako screamed as the group of balaclava covered men stormed into their room, Akoshi was unconscious on the floor with a soaked cloth over his face. The thugs stood in front of Minori’s bed, she hugged the sheets to her chest. Minori watched the men, counting them in her head. One, two, three, four ? five, six, seven ? eight. I’m going to be raped, I just know it. “W-what have you done to Akoshi?” she stuttered out, nervously looking at the eight men in front of her, blocking out the images of what she knew was going to happen. They ignored her frightened question, but they began to spread out when footsteps from outside grew closer. The door slid open. Walking inside was a short, petite woman with jet-black hair tied back in a bun with a plain black kimono, black stockings, and black, wooden, clogs. All the men that were standing over Minori before were spread out; not blinking as the woman slowly stalked her way toward the terrified Minori. “W-who a-are y-y-you?” Minori stuttered, clinging to the sheet even tighter. Minori skidded back against the bed as the unknown female intruder climbed onto her bed, and started too slowly, and methodically, crawl forward. “I, am Fukimi Kunamara,” she said, whispering it to the sweat drenched Minori. “That piece of trash on the floor there,” she pointed to Akoshi’s lifeless body, Minori went to scowl, but thought better of it, “that piece of trash ? is my brother, Akoshi. And you are?” “I-I am his f-fianc” ? Minori,” she said, timidly back to Fukimi. Minori noted Fukimi’s somewhat shocked, yet interested reaction. “W-we were supposed to be m-m-married in March ? b-but ?” “I see,” Fukimi nonchalantly replied, whatever interest she had had quickly vanished. Fukimi leaned forward and took a light grip of the sheet Minori clung to. Slowly, sexily and seductively Fukimi pulled it down, leaving it to rest on Minori’s thighs. “You see, Fianc”,” she began to say, her hand had travelled up Minori’s top and through the top of it, and pulled the straps down. Fukimi began to massage Minori’s breast, the trapped woman just froze, not even daring to move. “What we’re going to do is ? we’re going to kill Akoshi.” Before Minori could even respond, Fukimi removed her hidden knife faster than a Cheetah chasing after a doomed Gazelle, and sliced Minori’s breast, making her scream a blood curdling scream. She leant forward and clutched her chest in agony as blood stained the white sheets ? all the while, Fukimi and her henchmen silently left the room, leaving Minori to cry herself to sleep.
