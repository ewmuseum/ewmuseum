Trusting Across America [Evo]

11 July 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 11, 2004 trust (tr st) n. 1. Firm reliance on the integrity, ability, or character of a person or thing. 2. assured reliance on the character, ability, strength, or truth of someone or something b : one in which confidence is placed trust (tr st) v. 1. to place confidence 2. to commit or place in one's care or keeping : ENTRUST b : to permit to stay or go or to do something without fear or misgiving [A church, Detroit, Michigan. White lace, fancy dresses, and black tuxedos. From the very back of a church, a wedding is being witness. Down the long church isle are the bride and groom, standing there in front of the minister. They’re facing each other, looking deep into each other’s eyes. Fully in love on their perfect day. With family and friends filling up every space of the church, half a dozen brides? maids completely decked out in baby blue gowns, and another half a dozen grooms men spiffed out in black tuxes.] MINISTER: “If there is anyone, anyone, who can find any reason why these two should not be joined in holy matrimony, speak now, or forever hold your peace.” [There is a silence throughout the church. There are small whispers through the crowd as the anxious couple looks on. Nothing. With that the minister looks back down to his Holy Book, but before he can even utter another syllable, there is an objection, literally.] MAC: “Whoa, whoa. Objection.” [From out of nowhere, Mackenzie’s slender figure is walking up the isle, between pews full of shocked family and friends. She’s all decked out in a slightly more street-wise version of the bride’s maid dresses. Baby blue, a little less than full length, fishnet arms, a plunging V-neck line, and big, tall chunky black boots.] Trust MAC: “Can I just get a quick question in here.” [She waits for no reply as she wedges herself right in between the bride and groom. With a smile she turns to the bride.] MAC: “You know, I’m thinking it has to take a lot of commitment and trust to take the plunge into marriage. Do you trust this guy?” [The bride, bowled over, just nods a little.] MAC: “Well, that’s not very convincing.” [Mac smacks the groom in the shoulder.] MAC: “How about you” Trust this woman with the rest of your life?” GROOM: “Yes.” [Mac nods, turning back to the bride.] MAC: “At least your hun-bun can manage an answer. I see that you are wearing white. Virgin” How about the honeymoon, do you trust him to be gentle?” [Silence. Mac looks at both of them, and then shrugs.] MAC: “I would hope you could trust him. Unless, of course, you’re not really a virgin. And with a body like that, girl, how could you be?” [Mac laughs to herself as everyone else looks on, still silent and stunned.] MAC: “Are you sure these people aren’t all sleeping?” [She nudges the bride, who is just as stunned at the rest. Sensing the tension, Mac decides to blow out of there before anything violent can go down. So, with a smile to the bride, groom, and minister, she quickly makes her way down the isle from wince she came. Then she disappears from the church, leaving silence, shock, and a wedding to finish.] No answers. [Outside the church Mac is climbing into a full sized van, the outside is decorated with grunge looking spray paint, mixtures of colors all over the place. Like a box of crayons exploded on the surface and melted together in the sunlight. Across the back of the van it has “Global Wrestling” spray painted in white, and across the hood “Mackenzie” is painted in a white with a red outline. Starting up the van with a rumble, Mac drives off into the distance, heading for her next stop, in her quest for Trust.] * * * [Clear, open interstate road, just outside Denver, Colorado. Pulled off on the shoulder of the interstate is Mackenzie’s van. The four way flashers are blinking steadily, as the van is left deserted. Just down the road from the van is Mac, walking down the shoulder of the road. Her clothing has changed; now she’s got some black high-heeled boots, fishnet stockings, a black skirt, and a red cami top. Slowly walking up the gravel of the shoulder, Mac is looking behind her shoulder, and then she stops, and holds out her thumb. Waiting, she watches for the next car to come by.] MAC: “Come on..” [Taking a step towards the side of the road, Mac rests her hand on her hip. Finally a large red truck stops at the edge of the rode, only a few feet in front of Mac. She smiles sweetly and walks up to the window of the truck. In the driver’s seat of the red Dodge, there is a young slender woman, dark brown hair. She smiles back at Mac.] WOMAN: “Need a ride?” MAC: “Yes. Much appreciated.” [With a nod from the young woman, Mac climbs into the oversized cab of the truck.] WOMAN: “So, got a name” I’m Amber.” MAC: “Mackenzie.” [Then the truck is in motion, and they’re driving away from Mac’s van.] AMBER: “Where can I take “ya?” MAC: “Anywhere with a payphone, really.” AMBER: “Van broke down?” MAC: “You could say that. You took a chance picking me up. Hitch hiking is a dangerous business. How do you trust random hitch hikers?” AMBER: “Well, generally I don’t pick up hitch hikers, but you didn’t look like much of a threat.” MAC: “Just my average every day Joe disguise. Really, I’m Spiderman.” AMBER: “Uh huh.” [There is a silence between them as they’re cruising down the interstate. Amber takes an exit into Denver.] MAC: “What is trust anyway” Ya know? Who do you trust? I could have been some whack job. Why trust me?” AMBER: “You still could be. But I’ll take my chances.” MAC: “So, no theories on this thing called trust?” [There is a pause before Amber responds.] AMBER: “Self-trust is the first secret of success. Trust thyself: every heart vibrates to that iron string.” MAC: “Wow. Deep.” AMBER: “Ralph Waldo Emerson.” MAC: “Nice.” AMBER: “I always thought so. Emerson was big on the self-trust. Locked himself away in a cabin for years, away from all things technological and human. Just himself and..well, himself.” [With that Amber turns into a gas station, parking in front of a pay phone.] AMBER: “Well, good luck with fixing your van.” MAC: “Thanks for the ride. And the quotationness.” [Mac jumps out of the truck and takes steps over to the pay phone. As soon as Amber drives off, Mac pulls out her cell phone and dials up a ride back to her van.] Answers, still not enough. * * * [The Golden Gate Bridge, San Francisco, California. About dusk, with the bridge lanes still buzzing with traffic. On one side of the bridge, away from the lanes of oncoming traffic, there is a group of people huddled together near midway of the bridge. Bungee jumping, yes. Parking the massive grunge van in a parking lot near the bridge, Mac makes her way up onto the walkway of the bridge, walking over to the jumble of people. As she arrives, they’ve already set up, and someone is on the edge of the bridge, three seconds from jumping. Her clothing as changed now, to a black tank top with “Global” written across in a blood red, and a pair of faded jeans. Near the back of the group, another person is getting suited up to jump.] MAC: “Now, do you really trust that bungee cord to save your life?” [She’s now standing beside the young woman who is getting suited up.] MAC: “How about this guy latching on the harness” Trust him with your life?” [Mac cocks her head towards the man helping the young woman.] MAN: “Can we help you lady?” MAC: “Yes, as a matter of fact, you can. Tell me, what is trust” Who do you trust?” [The man looks rather perplexed. The woman, rather annoyed.] WOMAN: “I’ve bungeed like twenty times. I trust my cords, and this is my boyfriend. I trust him to hook me up right. Answer your question?” [Mac nods, slightly, yet she still doesn’t look satisfied.] MAC: “What if he’s having an affair, and killing you would make it so he wouldn’t have to tell you” What if he’s only after your money? What if he’s screwing your mother?!” [The woman looks at Mac completely horrified.] MAN: “Hey! I’m standing right here, you know.” MAC: “Pulling the old kill your unsuspecting girlfriend with the unsafe bungee jumping harshness. Pssh. Get a new gimmick!” MAN: “HEY!” WOMAN: “HEY!” MAC: “Geez. Everyone around here is so uptight. West Coast people suck. I need to go back to the East Coast.” [Mac shakes her head with a sigh as she begins to walk away. In another moment, she turns back to them.] MAC: “Don’t you even dare think about running to me when I was completely right about him and you die on this jump. Because I’ll just tell you “I told you so!” and leave your ghost ass alone, crying in a corner!” [She turns and shoves her hands into her pockets, grumbling to herself as she walks along the bridge pathway, back to her van.] * * * [Black screen. Mackenzie voice over.] Trust. It’s a word we use everyday. A simple truth that dominates our lives. Do we really know what it is? Do we understand? Yes. On a basic, animal level we have to, we must understand, because we do it everyday. Trust. We trust our family, our mates, our friends, and most of all we place trust in ourselves. If we don’t put trust in ourselves, we’ll become lost in this world. My trust has always been in me. I can handle myself, and take care of myself. Yet, simply, I know when I need to trust others. Put myself in other’s hands. Can I trust my tag partner, Rich Sherwin? For one match, I’ll find a way. But in the end, all I have is me. All I trust is me. From experience, I know, me is enough. I can handle anything. [Silence.]
