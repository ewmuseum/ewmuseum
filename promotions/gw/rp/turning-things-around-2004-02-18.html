Turning Things Around

18 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 18th, 2004 Jim Dawson didn't have a lot of joys in his line of work. Corrections, after all, was not known for being the most rewarding of professions. Sure, it was a secure, steady job -- not a lot of people chomping at the bit to be jail guards -- and the benefits were good, the pension plan was excellent? but as for job satisfaction, it didn't offer much. The highlight of his day But as the door to the cellblock swung open, the day began to look up. The figure that stepped between the iron bars? exquisite was the word Jim would have used to describe her, if he'd been able to think of it. Legs that went from the floor to forever, sky blue eyes that sparkled like gems, lips that looked like they? well like they could do things she'd probably never do to him. All of that wrapped in a snow-white suit of impeccable quality, a suit that subtly hinted at what was underneath it. A suit that matched the length of pure white hair that cascaded down her back. Jim had never had a thing for Asian girls -- but he was pretty sure he was developing one right now. Realizing that the woman was heading directly towards him, he desperately tried to stop staring. 'Excuse me,' she said, 'Officer?' Jim gulped as she leaned in towards him, trying to read his badge. She squinted, and finally pulled a pair of wire-rimmed glasses with blue lenses from her pocket, and slipped them on. 'Ah, Officer Dawson,' she said, looking up from his badge and flashing him a heart-stopping smile, 'The officer at the front desk said you could help me.' With anything and everything, sweetheart thought Dawson. He was suddenly acutely aware that he stood several inches taller than this beauty. In fact, from his vantage point, he could almost see straight down her? 'Uh, yes, of course ma'am,' said Jim, forcing himself to look her in the eye, 'how can I help you?' 'I'm Yoshiko -- Kakita Yoshiko,' she said, handing Jim a sheet of paper, 'I'm here to take custody of my client, Akodo Rokku.' Jim looked over the paper, and sure enough, that's what it was. Orders to release Akodo Rokku into the custody of Ms. Kakita Yoshiko, attorney at law. Jim sighed inwardly. Shoulda figured, he thought, pretty lady, fine clothes? if she's in this place she's either a hooker? or a lawyer. 'Right this way, ma'am,' said Jim, 'he's just a few cells down.' 'Lead the way,' said Yoshiko, bowing slightly. As Officer Dawson lead the pretty lawyer through the cellblock, he cursed his luck. If there was one thing he'd learned in his years on this job it was this: Never get involved with a lawyer. *** 'Lion, Lion, Lion? what am I ever going to do with you?' Rokku sat straight up on his cot -- and immediately wished he hadn't, wincing in pain and reaching for the icepack he'd been given, which he quickly reapplied to his aching head. 'Yoshiko,' he said, dragging himself off the cot and taking in the vision of her, 'you are a sight for literally sore eyes.' Yoshiko smirked. 'I'd imagine. Officer Dawson, if you would, please release my client.' 'Of course, ma'am,' said the slightly forlorn-looking guard. He promptly unlocked the cell and opened the door. A few minutes and autograph signings later, Rokku was trying to adapt to natural light again as the couple flew down the highway in Yoshiko's BMW. 'Thanks for coming down and bailing me out, Yoshiko-chan,' said Rokku, still holding the icepack to his head. 'I didn't bail you out, sweetheart,' said Yoshiko, 'I cut you a deal.' Rokku blinked in astonishment. 'How'd you manage that?' he asked. Yoshiko glanced sideways at him. 'I'm a lawyer, sweetie, remember? Zero must have hit you harder than I thought.' 'I know you're a lawyer,' said Rokku, 'in Japan.' 'And Texas,' said Yoshiko, 'I passed the Texas BAR a few weeks ago.' Rokku blinked again. 'And you didn't mention this to me because?' 'I didn't think it was a big deal,' said Yoshiko, 'it's not like it was hard, after all. I only had to study for a day or two. Criminal law here isn't too terribly different from criminal law in Japan, you know.' Rokku laughed -- and then cringed at the pain that caused his aching head. 'You still manage to amaze me, Crane.' said Rokku. 'I'd be disappointed if I didn't,' said Yoshiko, winking. 'So how'd you do it?' 'It wasn't that hard,' explained Yoshiko, 'Holmes and Zero were sloppy as hell setting you up -- they were probably more interested in getting you hurt than getting you arrested, actually.' 'Yeah,' said Rokku, 'next time they want to get me in a car wreck though, they'd better use a car that's in good shape -- the cops told me they found me unconscious and stalled out in the middle of an intersection.' 'That was the first piece of evidence I brought to the DA's attention earlier this morning, actually,' said Yoshiko, 'The truck wasn't registered to you or me, there were no GTA reports that match its description, and it had no plates? so where did it come from? Second, there was the brick on the gas pedal -- I mean, who puts a brick on their own gas pedal? Third, your prints weren't on the wheel, which means at no time were you steering the vehicle. Then there was the story you told the police when you came to. After you called me and explained, I asked the police to do a forensic study of the glass shards in the truck's front seat, as well as your vodka-soaked shirt. Unsurprisingly, the vodka in your shirt matched residue on the glass. And uh? there was the matter of the recording.' Rokku raised an eyebrow. 'Recording? Somebody got the attack on tape?' 'Well,' said Yoshiko, 'not so much 'someone' as 'me'.' Rokku looked straight at his girlfriend and narrowed his eyes. 'What?' she said, trying to look innocent, 'Okay, so I kind of bugged you. Just a little. I thought it might come in handy, with this whole war thing we started.' 'Okay, fair enough,' said Rokku, 'where did you plant it? And when?' 'Not really questions you want answered, sweetie,' said Yoshiko, chuckling. Rokku just groaned. 'Okay,' he asked, 'so if you have an audio recording of the whole thing happening, why did you need to cut a deal, exactly?' 'Because I recorded it,' explained Yoshiko, 'as a biased party, I could have rigged it. It's not 100% reliable as evidence in court. It was enough to get you out of jail, though, provided I get the DA the last bit of evidence I promised him. Which is why we're on the way to your doctor's appointment. We need a certified physician to examine you and run some tests. The head trauma should be easy enough to verify -- the hard part was finding a toxicologist who was willing to run the blood tests on short notice.' 'Why blood tests?' asked Rokku. 'Chloroform,' replied Yoshiko, her lips curling into a smile, 'stays in your system a hell of a long time. It should be obvious that you were exposed to a heavy dose very recently, and that combined with everything else will pretty much lock the fact that you were not driving under the influence. I provide all this evidence to the DA, and you'll never even have to go to court. The DWI charge will vanish, and all you'll have on your record is Obstructing the Flow of Traffic. Which is a pocket-change fine, or a little community service.' Rokku leaned back in his seat and smiled. 'I'll do the community service,' he said, 'I'm sure we can set me up with a visit to a children's hospital or something. It'll be good PR for us and the fed, and we'll make some kids' days.' 'Exactly what I was thinking,' said Yoshiko, 'You have been paying attention!' 'Sometimes,' said Rokku, 'It helps that I'm learning from the best.' 'Flatterer,' purred Yoshiko, 'keep that up, and we might be a little late for your appointment.' 'Well,' said Rokku, 'I did just get out of 'da joint'?' Yoshiko smirked, and took one hand off the wheel, placing it on Rokku's thigh. '?but no,' he finished, 'business before pleasure.' 'Spoil sport,' said Yoshiko, mock-pouting. 'Sorry honey,' said the Steel Lion, 'If you're good, I promise I'll do you but good tonight, okay?' Yoshiko couldn't help but laugh. 'You better,' she said. 'Oh, don't worry, said Rokku, 'now where's your cell?' 'Glove compartment,' said Yoshiko, 'Calling the boys?' 'Yeah,' replied Rokku, bringing up the first number on speed dial, 'I think we've waited long enough, so I want to meet with them as soon as we get home. It's time for us to move.' 'It's time to activate The Legion.'
