the man stuck 2 the wall.

17 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: june 17th, 2003 They say that a picture is worth a thousand words. You see, the man on the poster was once in a situation somewhat similar to mine, only he had run into much worse problems. He fell into a world of drugs and unfortunately was not able to pull himself out of it in time. He was caught with a dangerous import drug, Asian Mist, while holding the world title in a federation called CWA. He had to relinquish his title and give up on CWA, and look for a new place to start. The drugs were his release, and he disappeared from the world thanks to them. Later, he reappeared, completely isolated from everything. He had no acquaintances, no women, no friends, and no knowledge of what had happened to him. Rock bottom is where he was, unable to connect with anyone else around him. He found himself a nice girl that followed his wrestling career, and he had also found love. He went on from here to climb back up the ladder, and defy odds. He wrestled in three companies at once, and ran another promotion. He won gold and became a legend. Right now, I’m where he was. No girls, no money, nowhere to go. Right now, the man on the poster is the only thing that proves me right. “I can’t believe this poster is all that keeps me going.” I look at the poster, examine him once more. He’s six feet tall. He has died black hair, brushed back to show the blood running from his forehead. I can see in the picture that he’s been through a grueling fight, perhaps for years. He holds his title high, proud of it in a way that only someone worthy of it could be. And there across his face is a smug grin, one that has been there ever since I bought him two weeks ago. “What are you smiling at?” I was getting sick of it, maybe putting him up so high on my wall had given him a feeling of superiority. Now he sits there, staring at me with a smirk across his face at all times of the day, my mind can’t escape him even when I leave to my bed. He doesn’t say anything back. He continues to stand there with a world title held high above his head. The man that stares at me with his smile, he has his story. Back in New Era Wrestling, that is where I first met him. This man beat me for the first promotional title I ever held, and did it with ease. This man took the record I held the world title for, and literally shattered it into pieces. This man was my best friend in the industry for four years; no one knew each other better than him and I. This is the man that brought me into the hall of fame, without him I wouldn’t have been a legend in NEW. With him I was just a shadow of his greatness, without him I was nothing. “Well, tell me. What is so funny?” His mouth stayed open, but not a word came out. I fumbled into my pocket and pulled out my pack of cigarettes and Zippo. “Hmm, only two left,” I motioned the pack towards my proud friend “well, would you like one?” Nothing. I grabbed one for myself, and left the other sitting in there, since my proud friend wasn’t interested in it. Bringing it to my lips, I torched the other end and swallowed into my lungs a tunnel of smoke. The problem with these cigarettes is that they are lights, and the filters are too long. That means you have to smoke more than usual to get the norm feel, which isn’t good for this room. The problem with this room is that there is no ventilation system, and the smoke hovers unless I keep my door open. The problem with the smoke hovering around is that you are more likely to die from second hand smoke. The problem with that is I haven’t opened my door in a day, and the apartment is stuffed completely with smoke. The problem with me is that I complain too much about my surroundings. But the only problem that I have right now is with that man on the poster, who is too good to talk to me. I sigh. “You know, you don’t have to be here, I can always just toss you in the trash.” “But you wouldn’t want that,” a voice explains. It was him, the man on the poster, he stopped smiling to let out that verse. I ash my cigarette into the trash can and ask him, “why is that?” He lets out an annoying, chatter laugh, “because I am what keeps you going.” Ha ha ha. El oh el. “I don’t need you to keep my heart pumping, you’re nothing but a large, shiny piece of paper.” “Not to you Trey, I am a lot more than that. I am the man that you want to be, I am who you have always modeled yourself after.” It’s not true, “liar.” “You are in denial Trey,” he explains. “I carried you in NEW, I carried you in EGWA, I carried you in NEGWA, CWA, TWF and NEW again. And then again in ? ? “Shut up! You never carried me, not ever. Not in NEW or NEGWA or CWA or anywhere!” But my words meant nothing to him as he just continued his rant. “And then again in EGWA, and then NEW after that. Now we’re here again, this time in Global Wrestling. I’m not around to save your ass, but you look to make up for my absence by placing my poster here” It’s sad Trey; I am too busy working championship matches to deal with your little problems again. You retired already, so just get out of wrestling and make it easy on the rest of the world - just kill yourself already.” I bit my lip. “You’re not helping anyone by sticking around, clinging to futile attempts of a comeback. You’re nothing like me Trey, you never have been and you never will be.” That was words from the man on the poster. He couldn’t be right. “You’re wrong, you’re just...wrong.” “Tell me, if I am so wrong...then why did you say yourself that I was the only thing that keeps you going?” Dagger. Through. Heart. He was right, here I was trying to take advantage of a 4 feet by 2-and-a-half feet poster. “Okay,” I said, looking for some kind of logical response. “Well...” I looked down at my cigarette; there was a long stem of ash dripping on the tip of it. My eyes widened, and before I could do anything about it, the hot gray cinders fell into my lap. I quickly brushed it off of me, and pulled out the cigarette, that was the last of my smoke. And all the meanwhile, my friend on the wall enjoyed himself in hysterical laughter. “I hate you,” came from the pit of my emotions. “I know Trey, I know,” is what I can make out between his laughter. “Look, as long as I’m here, I’ll take that cigarette.” I looked into the pack of lights, one left. He’s nice enough to insult me, and then ask for me last cigarette. I reach into my back pocket and pull out the twenty-dollar bill, then crisp it between my palms. I look back up to the man on my wall, he is still smiling at me. I smile back. “Yeah, sure, why not.” I walked up to the wall and ripped the poster out from under the duck tape. “What’s going on Trey” What, what are you doing?” I continue to smile as his grin drops, “something I should have done a long time ago, friend.” I crumple up my talkative buddy, and toss him into the metal trash can, then light up the last cigarette. “No Trey, you can’t do this,” he pleads. “You need me, you’ll never survive in this federation without me!” I drop the cigarette into the can, and set my good pal ablaze. He screams and curses me, “I wish you were dead.” “I know you do Jade,” I say slipping the twenty back into my pocket, “but you are the one that is going to die right now.” With one last look at the crumpled, charcoaled companion, I open the door and take a step out. I should probably leave it open, let the room air out a bit. For now, I’ve got some rummaging to do.
