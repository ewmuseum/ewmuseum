Changing

1 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 1st, 2004 '?so now he's dead?' 'As a doornail,' said Rokku, pausing to shovel a forkful of fried rice into his mouth, 'a doornail that moves, anyway.' Yoshiko leaned back in her chair and rolled her eyes. 'Rock-chan, you attract the strangest people,' she said, pausing to blow her bangs out of her face, 'first a vampire, then a psychopath, and now some kind of? thing. What exactly is Jamester anyway?' 'Don't know,' said Rokku, barely pausing as he grabbed an egg roll and bit fully half of it off, 'don't especially care. I've fought the dead before. I won. Doubt this'll be much different.' 'You're awfully nonchalant about this,' said Yoshiko, 'what if he's got some kind of weird? I don't know? undead mojo?' Rokku swallowed a mouthful of shrimp lo mein, then looked at his better half incredulously. 'Undead mojo, huh?' Yoshiko rolled her eyes. 'You know what I mean! Powers. Abilities. Some kind of supernatural hoohah. If the man's really been to the grave and come back, who knows what his capabilities are now. I guess if he's a vampire, you have some idea, but?' 'He's not a vampire,' said Rokku, before popping a fried dumpling into his mouth. 'Oh,' said Yoshiko. 'Wait,' she continued, looking at Rokku curiously, 'how do you know?' 'I just know,' he replied, 'okay? He didn't feel right, but he didn't feel like a vampire.' Yoshiko quirked an eyebrow. 'What do you mean,' she asked, 'he didn't feel right?' 'In here,' replied Rokku, tapping his temple. 'And in here,' he added, tapping his chest. 'It's just a? feeling,' Rokku continued, putting down his fork and leaning back from the table, 'A sense of wrongness. Like he doesn't belong. He's definitely not normal. But it's not the same feeling I get when I see Soma walking the halls of the arena. It's something very different. Whatever he is, a vampire isn't it.' 'Rokku,' Yoshiko said, 'you've never talked like this before. Talking about feelings, and senses—when did all this start happening?' 'I hadn't really thought about it,' said Rokku, scratching the side of his neck, 'I've been too busy just? well, fighting, training, running the Den? all that. But I guess? I guess it was after Tokyo. Things have been? different ever since.' 'And you've not mentioned this to your girlfriend why?' asked Yoshiko, frowning. 'Well? uh? heh,' Rokku chuckled sheepishly, 'I guess, um. Well how was I gonna explain it exactly, honey? Tell you I'm getting ESP? That when I close my eyes, and drown out all sound, I can feel? everything? That wind feels alive, that the grass sings? That I can feel a drink of water as it disperses throughout my body? How do you explain something like that?' 'Well,' replied Yoshiko, her lips curling ever-so-slightly into a smirk, 'kind of like that.' For a moment, Rokku glared at her. Then he slumped down in his chair, and sighed. 'Okay, okay. I guess I just thought, 'Guess what honey, I'm getting ESP!' might not go over so well. Training with the Thunders opened up my mind. I mean, I've dealt with the supernatural before. I watched my own father turn to dust. But it's never been this close before. It's never been part of me. So I guess? I didn't know what to do.' Yoshiko rose from her chair and walked around the table to Rokku, wrapping her arms around him and resting her head on his shoulder. 'I think what you did just now was okay,' she said, kissing him on the cheek. Rokku smiled. 'So you're not mad?' he asked. 'Not especially,' replied Yoshiko. 'You were confused and unprepared to discuss the topic. I can understand. After all, I learned about chakra control and all that from Sakura too. And because of that, I'm rapidly healing from injuries that doctors told me I might never recover from. It's wonderful, but also? overwhelming. So yeah, I understand the feeling, having never realized what we had inside us before. But now that this is all out in the open, what do we do with it?' 'Well,' said Rokku, picking up his fork again and digging into the General Tso's Chicken, 'I don't know about you dear, but I'm going to eat heartily.' Yoshiko couldn't help but laugh. 'How's eating like a pig going to help you understand the mysteries of the unknown, love?' she asked. 'Oh, it won't,' said Rokku, swallowing a mouthful of chicken, 'but it'll give me energy. Seikua's arriving tomorrow.' Yoshiko's eyes widened. 'Seikua! Isn't that?' 'He's the monk who first started training me in martial arts, yeah,' said Rokku. 'I used to think he was a crazy old man, with his talk of the elements, spirits in everything and mystical 'inner strength'. But now, well, I'm much more inclined to think he knew what he was talking about all along. I called his temple a few days ago, and arranged for him to fly out here and resume training me. If there's anyone in this world who can help me master my chakra, understand this new sense, and defeat whatever Jamester's become, it's him. But the old coot's the most demanding teacher I've ever had -- the kind of hardass that makes hardasses blanch. So yeah, I'm gorging myself. But I'm going to need all this later. Hell, he'll probably wrangle you into training with him too, sweetie -- whether you want to or not. He has that kind of way about him. So if I were you, I'd eat up too. Trust me, this is one time you can eat all you want and NOT worry about it affecting your girlish figure.' Seikua. Yoshiko furrowed her brow in thought. Rokku had told her stories about him before, but she'd never thought she'd actually meet the old monk -- Rokku wasn't the kind of person to swallow his pride and go back to someone he'd told off for help. At least, the Rokku she knew when he'd told her those stories wasn't. She looked at him, watching as he stuffed himself, and had to admit it: he'd changed quite a lot since then. Yoshiko looked down at her empty plate, then at the opened chinese food containers spread all over the table. Smiling, she grabbed a fork and dug into the lemon chicken. Her Lion was changing faster than she'd realized -- and if she wanted to keep up, she'd need her energy.
