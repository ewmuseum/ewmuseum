Who am I?

23 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/23/04 Have you ever thought about what it’s like to be dead? Have you? You have, haven’t you? Then you know basically how it works. You die. Your soul leaves your body, and your put into a hole in the ground for the rest of eternity. Alone. That state of aloneness that no living human being could possibly hope to achieve, because you’re not just dead, you’re alone, FOREVER. There is no consciousness; there is no emotion, there is nothing. There isn’t even darkness, or an eternal light, there is no color, there is NOTHING. You are a nobody, a nothing, you are DEAD, you no longer EXIST. Now let me ask you: have you ever been, a nobody? Has it ever occurred to you, that when it comes down to it, you aren’t even a tiny equation, a single factor, a tiny stain, a microscopic dent, in this big shit hole we call the Universe? Have you ever felt so insignificant, so unimportant, that you might as well be invisible, because your impact on the world around has meant absolutely nothing? Do you ever feel like that? Have you ever been so alone, you felt as though you didn’t exist? I do. For so long I’ve had time to think about these things. Because you see, I am a nobody. Even now, I don’t exist to you. Why? Because you’ve never heard of me. In your mind, I am nothing. I am a nobody. I am dead. And for 26 years that I have breathed this air, this suffocating, nauseous air, I have been a nobody. For ten or so years in this “business” I have known what it’s like to not exist. Do you have any idea what that is LIKE? To feel your chest heave in and out, to feel your heartbeat accelerate, to hear the thoughts in your head turn over and around, and contemplate your own existence, to think, feel, hear, taste, touch, cry, bleed, sweat, to feel sadness and pain and disgust and HATRED. All of that, all of what YOU thought made you human’all of that, means nothing. Because you ARE nothing. And all this time, I thought that I had something to live for. For 26 years, I was under the illusion that I had a reason to keep going, to believe in something. It used to be my mother. But she’s nothing now. It used to be my father. He is nothing. It used to be for Lance, and for Phoenix. Now they are nothing. It used to be for honor, for respect, for the love of the game. But those are just words. Honor. Respect. Love. They MEAN nothing. And do you know why? Because all along, I have been a nobody, a nothing. You can’t find meaning in something that doesn’t exist. Who am I? I have to ask you the same thing. Who am I? I’m just some guy who happened to join this new stable, this group of ruffians bent on world domination. Right? I’m just some nobody that happened to be in the right place at the right time, and now I’m here. That’s it, isn’t it? That’s the answer you want to hear. You want to believe that the truth is as clear as the lens you view the world through, that when it comes down to it, all that matters is what you think. Or, more importantly, what everybody else thinks. Because if everybody agrees on one thing, it must be true. Isn’t that right? You want to know who I am? I am Andrew “Ace” Harding, and I have spent my entire life fighting for something that was never there. I am a losing man in the battle for my own existence, for my own identity. And what am I doing here? Why have I come here? Do you really want to know? Because I am tired of being nothing’I am tired of being nobody’I am tired of lying in my grave, and rotting in my own coffin that society built for me. I am tired of not existing, of not living. All my career I’ve fought for respect, when I never even realized what it really was. I thought that character, that righteousness, that what you believed was the right way to live, was the answer. I thought that respect was something earned, something that other people gave to you because you worked your ASS off to get it. That if you took enough bumps, and pushed enough people to their limits, if you were good enough in that ring, that respect would take you in like a loved one’s embrace. But I thought wrong, didn’t I? Respect isn’t earned. It’s taken. And there really is no such thing as respect. It’s a word that doesn’t even really exist. After all, one word can mean so many different things. You know what the word respect really means? What that word is really trying to say? Do you know the word that is synonymous with respect? Fear. Fear is what it is all about. Fear is what drives men to their knees. Fear is in everything. It dwells in the darkest depths of that cavernous hole that we call the human mind. Everybody fears something. I don’t give a damn if you’re undefeated in battle, if you’re immortal, if you’re a stoic, if you are God himself. Fear is in each and every one of us. It crawls under your skin and speaks in your ear when you don’t want it to. But do you know what’s the worst part about it? You keep it inside, where people can’t see it’s ugly face. Because you’re afraid that what it’s telling you is the truth. And it is. For so long I was afraid I wouldn’t be liked, or that I would be hated, or that I would fail to make those fans content with the matches I’d put on. I was worried that I’d make some mistake and miss a move, or fail to impress and put on a 4 star match instead of a 5 star match. But it never occurred to me that all those times I lost, most of them cleanly and some of them submission, that I wouldn’t be gaining respect for my ability, but losing it because of my reputation. I have lost more matches than I have won through my career, and not once has a title belt sat on my shoulder or around my waist. Do you have any idea what that means for somebody who has been doing this for 10 fucking years? Do you have even the slightest comprehension of what that says about me? That I am a fucking loser, that I am a nobody, a nothing. But when Chaos drove that chair into my face and severed the last remaining strand that bound me to my old creeds and ways of thinking, I knew the truth. It slapped me in the face as hard as that chair bashed my skull in. I was not going to gain respect. All those years I spent were for nothing. I had wasted 25 years of my life, for nothing. But that was when I decided to find a reason. I restricted myself for all those years that I wrestled. Inside my head, I had a map of the human anatomy inscribed and memorized, I knew every nerve hold, every joint, every bone, every muscle, and all the inner workings of the human mind, and more than a thousand different ways to sever, splinter, tear and break each one of them. But I lived by honor, and I decided NOT to use what I knew for my betterment. Instead, I handcuffed myself so as not to hurt anybody, so as to ensure I didn’t unleash the dangerous, wild creature that sat in the chambers of my heart. But when I experienced my epiphany, my heart was no longer a factor. Within a few weeks, I stripped myself of everything I once thought was me. I rid myself of any obstacles or disadvantages I once had. I went out there, and I broke a man’s neck in front of thousands of people, just as easily as you break a twig off of a tree. That man is now retired, thanks to me. I went on to destroy a young rookie, greener than vomit, until he was so badly beaten the ring was red with his blood. And then, I didn’t even give him the dignity of a clean defeat. I walked out on him, because he wasn’t worth any more of my time. He was a nobody. A nothing. I gained more “respect” by doing what I believed was wrong, what I knew at one time in my heart was not the answer. But do you know what’s the scariest part about it? When I locked that man in the Article 92, pulled back on his head, and felt those joints pop out of place, and heard and felt his spinal cord snap and crack in my own hands’do you know what I felt? I felt’nothing. Nothing. Not anger. Not regret. Not hatred. Not even a sense of accomplishment. I took a man’s livelihood, I took the reason he went on living, and I CRUSHED it between my fingers like it was nothing. But inside’I felt nothing. I didn’t understand. I should have felt SOMETHING. ANYTHING! But I couldn’t find it. All I found was what had always been there. A grave. I thought about that for awhile, and again questioned my place in this business. Even though I had removed my handcuffs, I was still chained. I had gone nowhere. I gained nothing from my newfound rage, my newly acquired anger, it all meant nothing in the end. I was still nothing. Until the GW. And the Elite. What I’ve finally realized is that to make your mark in this business, you can’t simply scrape at it’s foundation while apologizing anytime you offend someone or something doesn’t go your way. You have to blow a hole right through the pillars that hold everything together, and bring the whole goddamn thing crumbling to the ground. Some people have called us a cancer. A plague. A disease that will spread, and needs to be stopped now. Cancer is what killed my mother. Cancer is that beast, that monster that took away the only thing that meant something to me, while I sat along in a dingy military academy, as life slowly left my mother’s body, without giving me the decency to say goodbye. Do you know what that’s like? To be stripped of the only privilege you’ll ever have of living peacefully? To know that the only thing you had lived for until then was taken away from you without even getting a chance to fight and lose for it? DO YOU?! But that is something I will NEVER forget. Cancer has left an aching scar in my soul, in my heart, in my mind, that will NEVER go away. It has imbedded itself into my memory, and all it started as was a skin condition. A tiny itch, a little flaking, and it grew into the worst experience of my LIFE. But if that’s what it takes to finally make myself known, to finally gain some respect, to finally be FEARED’then so be it. I am a cancer, I am a member of a group that will be remembered FOREVER in the history of this federation. Because after all these years I finally understand. You have to be a nobody, a nothing, a tiny itch, before you can spread into the most horrifying disaster the human condition has ever known, and FOREVER cement your place into the minds of people. The Elite is my way of doing that. This is my chance to finally show you people who I am, and what I can do. And you won’t like it. Not for one second. And I wouldn’t have it any other way.
