Hey Baby Part III: Clues and Charms

6 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/6/2006 And I can't breathe When you cry But I'll be there to hold you tight And I would kill I would fight To keep you close? ---- Monday, August 15, 2005 0333 hours, local time somewhere in New Mexico ---- The car seemed to be a part of the night itself, nearly invisible as it sped through the darkness. At every turn, Bruce McLeod’s life flashed before his eyes. His hands clutched the edge of the soft bucket seat, biting into the leather. His face was a ghastly, sickly shade of green. He’d never felt so sick, or so sure of his imminent demise before. The night blasted past the windows, in a dizzying blur, the colors muted by the heavy tint of the windows. McLeod felt slightly out of sorts, almost as though he was intoxicated. He turned his head, tearing his eyes away from the road to look at the determined visage of his employer. Jackson’s eyes were limned with an eerie glow. His jaw clenched tight, a muscle flexing as he ground his teeth absently. Dark circles carved hollows beneath his baleful gaze, making his eyes appear sunken. A few days worth of stubble covered his face, and his hair was unkempt and tangled like the hair of a madman. He grunted, and slowly swiveled his head, that jack-o’lantern glare meeting McLeod’s icy stare. For a moment, McLeod stared, transfixed, for a moment, he could have sworn that he saw a death’s skull outlined on Brad’s pale visage. He opened his mouth to say something, and then he blinked, and the image vanished. Perhaps it was merely fancy. Brad glowered at McLeod for a moment, and then turned his attention back to his reckless driving. His voice was full of acid when he spoke. “Must you stare at me like that, Bruce” Find something else to do? I don’t think you’ve quite chewed your fingers off yet.” Jackson swerved into the oncoming traffic lane, narrowly avoiding a collision with an 18-wheeler before swinging back into the right lane. McLeod grabbed the dashboard, cursing under his breath as he glared at Jackson. “You bloody idiot! Are you trying to kill us before we get” there? Wherever in the hell there is? Aren’t you even going to tell me where we’re going?” Jackson groaned, his tone exasperated. “Can’t you shut your goddamn trap” for more than three seconds?! If I had’ve known you’d be so goddamn chatty? I would have brought Larry Gowan, even if he is in a coma? at least he? can SHUT THE FUCK UP! Where we are going should be apparent soon enough, and if you had brains enough you would have figured it out. An accident that caused the damage to that tree would have alerted authorities. Hopefully, if Fate has intervened, Gabriel is dead. One thing I do know, Kitty was injured. Ergo, they would have taken her.” McLeod’s handsome face scrunched into an unflattering frown as he folded his arms, glaring out the window. Up ahead, an enormous building lit up the night, and McLeod figures that this must be their destination. It made logical sense? after all? with the burnt rubber on the road, and the marks on the tree? it seemed that perhaps they were on their way to pay a visit to the victim of that car accident. McLeod muttered to himself. “So what, we’re ambulance chasers now?” “Thought I told you to shut up?” Jackson snapped, his tone filled with irritation. McLeod shook his head, suppressing the urge to throttle the driver, thinking that would be counterproductive and would assure that he would reach the hospital regardless- in a body bag. His gaze returned to the road, and he watched as a black funeral parlor hearse turned out of the hospital parking lot. He glanced at his watch? 3:33am. He suppressed a shudder, fingering the charm on the chain around his neck. Trinity. The power of three. Something was about to happen, likely something terrible. He crossed himself absently, offering up a silent prayer. The car squealed to a stop before the main doors, and Jackson slid out of the driver’s seat, slamming the door without looking back. McLeod sneered and then flipped off Brad’s retreating back, swearing loudly. “Yeah, fuck you too, Jackass.” ---- Sunday, August 14, 2005 2345 hours, local time Albuquerque Regional Medical Center, Albuquerque, New Mexico ---- She awoke with a start, disoriented and in pain, surrounded by white light. It was a dark and stormy night, the perfect clich?, with just a hint of foreshadowing. Rain slashed against the window, lightning painting its flashbulb, paparazzi glow across the girl's face as the nurse checked her vitals. Sadness reigned in this room, etching its stain on her furrowed brow. The nurse frowned, brushing an errant strand of greasy hair back from Kitty’s parched brow. She was still burning up; her body wracked with fever? some unknown poison eating her up inside. The heart monitor beeped, slow and steady. Nothing more than background noise as the nurse sat down in the chair beside the bed. The energy in the room was reflected in the turbulent sky outside as though something was about to happen. The girl reached up with a trembling hand, grasping the nurse’s arm. Her chapped lips worked, but her voice was barely above a whisper, lost in the noise of the storm beyond the windows. “Help me,” she whispered. The nurse stood, grabbing a glass from the table beside the bed, quickly filling it with water, placing a straw between the girl’s lips, letting her take a few gulps of the cold liquid. When she began to sputter, the nurse removed the straw, smoothing back her hair. The girl looked at her with wild, frightened eyes, her chin trembling as tears filled those lovely green eyes. “Please,” she managed, “you have to help me. He’s going to” my baby.” “Honey,” the nurse crooned as she patted Kitty’s head reassuringly, “we’re already helping you. You could have died.” The girl shook her head, blanching from the pain in her shattered ribcage, but somehow managing to retain consciousness. “No, you don’t understand. He’s going to kill my baby. He told me he was. He said that he was going to sacrifice my baby to the darkness in exchange for power. He lied to me, told me the baby was dead, but” the priest told me that wasn’t true? and then he said he was going to have his way with me because Brad doesn’t want me back. I’I don’t want him to touch me?” She squeezed her eyes shut, but the tears flowed down her cheeks just the same. The nurse clucked her tongue, and checked the monitors; the girl was still burning up with fever, an unfortunate complication to the infection caused by her untreated wounds. “You must be delirious. There’s nobody here that will hurt you. We only want what’s best for you. My dear, I don’t know anything about a baby, you came in alone.” Somehow this was all familiar to her, as though this drama had played out countless times since she’d last been free. Perhaps it had. “No!” She insisted, her voice breaking painfully, “you have to believe me. I need to get out of here now, before he finds me!” The nurse nodded, turning to check the intravenous drip that was feeding penicillin and nutrients into her bloodstream. “I’m afraid that’s not possible. Your ribs are broken in three places, honey, which would make moving difficult. In addition to that, we had to stitch you up,” her eyes traveled down the sheet, drawing her attention there, to where an insistent pain throbbed like an infected tooth, “down there.” She concluded, pouring a cup of apple juice from a pitcher on the table, and screwing on the sippy lid before handing it to her as though she was a child. She dutifully took a gulp of the cool, sweet liquid, playing the invalid. But then, this woman didn’t understand her one bit. Broken ribs were nothing. She’d felt that before. Along with a separated shoulder and a sprained knee. That was just in one wrestling match. She waited for the nurse to bend in close again, this time brushing back her hair in a tender gesture, and then she sprang into action. She pulled hard on the IV line, making the rack fall, getting all the slack she needed on the tubing. “What-? the nurse began, but that was as far as she got before Kitty wrapped the tubing around her neck, drawing it tight. The nurse fell against her, driving the air from her lungs in a rush as she came down on the taped up ribs. Her eyes bulged as her breathing came in gasps as Kitty pulled it tighter still, cutting off her air supply. After a moment, the nurse passed out, and Kitty released her, letting her bulk carry the woman to the floor. She felt a pang of regret as she threw aside the sheets. She tore the IV needle out of the back of her hand, ignoring the blood that trickled sluggishly from the hole in her hand. She swung her legs to the floor and stood slowly, surprised that she was able to stand. The pain really wasn’t that bad. She crouched beside the fallen nurse, knowing she had little time to strip the woman naked and take her clothes. She clipped the heart monitor to the woman’s neck, buying herself some more time. Thoughts of Brad filled her mind as she relieved the woman of her uniform, the thoughts bringing emotions of anger and longing. She hated him then, for reducing her to this, and for leaving her with Gabriel for so long. ---- Monday, August 15, 2005 0337 hours, local time Albuquerque Regional Medical Center, Albuquerque, New Mexico ---- Brad Jackson approached the information desk with an air of nervous distraction. The hospital smell had always bothered him; it reminded him of his mother’s last days. She’d died in a place much like this one. He shook his head, pushing the thought aside. She was dead and gone, nothing now but moldering bones. He had no family? and that’s how it should be. He leaned over the counter, startling the woman bent over her paperwork. She gasped, and then smiled as she looked into Brad’s eyes. The dog tags around his neck jingled, tinkling against the counter as he rested his arms on the cool surface. A smile flickered across Brad’s lips, showing a flash of white teeth and dimples and then he spoke, his voice low and rhythmic. “I do believe that you have a patient here” perhaps listed as Jane Doe? she would be comatose, and possibly pregnant? give me her room number.” “I really can’t give that information without knowing?” she hesitated, groping for words, flustered as she looked into Jackson’s deep blue eyes. “who you are. I mean, are you her family?” Jackson shrugged, just a hint of movement in his shoulders, but it was enough to draw her hungry gaze. “I can’t be sure until I see her. I’m looking for my wife. She’s missing. Her name is Kaitlynn. Kaitlynn McIntyre-Jackson.” “Oh my God, you’re Jackson uh” something! From Global! My little brother has a ton of your posters on his walls. Wow! He’d freak out if he knew I was talking to you right now! Do you think you could sign an autograph?” The nurse’s fingers flew over her keyboard, her gaze flicking from his face, to the monitor, and back. Jackson smiled, pouring on the charm. “Sure. But only if you tell me the room number.” He accepted the scrap of paper she handed him, and scrawled his name on it, still grinning like an idiot, giving her that megawatt smile. After a moment, she spoke again, giving him what he wanted. “S-1025. Critical care unit.” The smile on Brad’s face became ghastly as he touched the woman’s face. “Thanks, you’re a peach. Here,” he reached into the inside pocket of his jacket, withdrawing two tickets for the next WCWF Sunday Night Impact from his wallet. “Give these to your brother. Tell him I said hello.” And with that, he walked away, his boots echoing in the silence that ensued. ---- Monday, August 15, 2005 0345 hours, local time Albuquerque Regional Medical Center, Albuquerque, New Mexico ---- The door was open, the room in darkness when he approached. Something smelled spoiled in here, rotten and sickeningly sweet. He hesitated on the threshold, holding his breath, suddenly afraid of what he might find in the bed. Squaring his shoulders, he stepped inside the room, his eyes adjusting almost immediately. Equipment hummed, machinery beeping and booping, nothing of consequence. A curtain was drawn around the bed, hiding her from his view, but he could see a shadowy lump beyond. A triumphant smile curved his lips, exposing his teeth, which gleamed in the light. With a sound of glee, he whisked the curtain aside only to find the bed empty. The smell was apparently coming from the spilled carafe on the table, the sickening smell of apple juice. A metal clipboard rested atop the rumpled linens, and with a trembling hand, he snatched it up, dreading what it would contain. He flipped it open, expecting to find her file with the word DECEASED stamped across it. What he found instead almost stopped his heart. A folded scrap of paper fluttered down on the bed. Her tiny handwriting crowded the page, making his head ache in time with his heart. “Brad, If you see this, know that I am alive, although I think Gabriel might be too. They say he’s not in the hospital here, and I didn’t see his name when I used the nurse’s card to access the administration system. I have to leave, as I’m sure you understand. Go to the place of the rose- I’ll wait for you there. Please, hurry. K.” He threw his head back and roared, a sound of pure rage, echoing an earlier sentiment, directed at the same man. “MOTHERFUCKER!” If Gabriel was still out there, then Kitty was still in danger. “I should have killed him when I had the chance. If you can hear me Gabriel, know this. If you hurt a hair on her head, I will hunt you to the ends of the earth, and when I find you, you will die. I will make it slow, and painful, so you can understand my pain. I will take everything away from you, leaving you with nothing, leaving you broken, like you left me!” Jackson grabbed the edge of the bed, and flipped it over, still shouting obscenities, drawing the attention of the nursing staff, and hospital security?.
