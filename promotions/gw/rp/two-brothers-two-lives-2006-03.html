Two Brothers Two Lives

March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 2006 Training Dallas, Texas Training began each day as homage to what had been destroyed. Modern men often ignored core strength. There were plenty of exercises labeled “old school” that helped build overall body strength. You needed to focus on moving your own body weight through space. Pitt had always been a huge body-builder, but he maintained his flexibility and core power through the use of calisthenics. The belief in old-fashioned calisthenics had led to the development of the Dozens by Pitt, John and Jake. Pitt had even begun taking up yoga for the flexibility training. He was rough, but benefits were showing in his flexibility. Now he began the morning routine of two hundred Dozens. The Dozens were a series of exercises done in groups of hundreds. So one hundred Dozens equated to twelve hundred repetitions. Pitt had spent hours doing the Dozens everyday back on the ranch. They had reestablished his strength and conditioning along with the running. The Dozens were: Punches-Jab, hook, uppercut and cross from each arm. Kicks-Snap, roundhouse, hook, and axe from each leg. Push-Ups Sit-Ups Lunges Squats Leg Thrusts Pull-Ups Dips Jumping Jacks Vertical Push-Ups Reverse Sit-Ups So Pitt began the routine starting with the punches. He kept count in his head. The workout room was set-up in a very specific way. Each exercise found him looking at one huge poster on one place on the wall or another. There he had various pictures of the legends of wrestling all around him. His friends, enemies and idols all stared down at him, challenging him to work even harder. Pitt Hix went through the workout of two thousand four hundred exercises, and then moved over to the square boxing ring. He had what he liked to call “sacks of humanity” ready o train with him. We would throw them around, run the ropes, come off the turnbuckles and go through everything for a straight hour. At the end of the training session, he was exhausted. He would soon be training in even worse conditions. South Texas was often a barren wasteland with no humanity around for miles and miles. In the center of this Hell, he would be venturing with his tag team partner and brother to find the spark they once had. Being prepared wasn’t just a Boy Scout’s motto, it was a core belief in their family’s success. Now they would begin paying the dues once again, become ready to be champions once more. Of course, before all that happened, Pitt had a special night out. He felt he deserved a reward before he disappeared into Hell. Going Out Dallas, Texas After the steamy shower he stepped out and wrapped one of the thick towels around his waist. He was cleaned once again, and he needed to get ready for the night out. He had so few moments of joy anymore, he wanted to drink it all in while he could. A quick application of the shaving cream over his face and head got it ready. He took the pearl handled straight razor in his hand and began sharpening the razor using the leather straps. The growth of black hair was thick and mean, and so he needed to take it off to impress the girls. The lines of hair came off his head as he pulled the razor back over his head. Each swath of hair was rinsed off under the hot water. Once finished, he ran his fingertips over the shaved head to be sure he hadn’t missed any spots. Smooth as possible now, he wiped off the excess water. Pitt sharpened the straight razor on the leather strap once again. He then expertly removed the slight shadow of hair growing on his face until he was baby smooth and clean. Aftershave lotion followed, smoothing out his skin and moisturizing the face that harsh Texas sun had assaulted all day. He finished drying off and then rubbed lotion over his body. He finished dressing and left his hotel room. Syn Bar was the destination of the moment, and he had no clue what kind of bar it was at all. He was meeting a friend there and with him some women. Leaving For Family Business Highland Park, Texas The duffel bag was open, and he piled the socks and underwear in first. He pulled open the drawer and looked through the tee shirts folded neatly in stacks to find some that were plain or non-descript. He had a whole closet full of tee shirts from work, but being easily identifiable wasn’t part of his plans. He chose several black and gray tee shirts without any logos and threw them over his personal items. As he reached for the small toiletry bag on the bed the voice coming softly from the doorway cut through him like a knife. “So you weren’t even going to tell me?” He game fully tried to play it off, knowing he had been caught but refusing to admit it to himself or especially to her. “I told you I was heading out early for some training. I need to get into top shape for the match.” “Oh, is it your arm giving you trouble again?” She asked as she came walking into the room. The accident had left her with a small limp, slowing her gait gently, although nothing could slow the fiery anger building within. She reached out to his bare back, running her red fingernails along his spine before crossing over to where a small scar showed across his bicep. “I saw your workouts this week, and you looked just fine.” He couldn’t turn around. Facing her would betray him, her shining light of truth exposing his deceptions. He hadn’t been able to lie to her face, not blatantly. There was no hiding what he was about to do to her. “Well, I’ve learned to deal with the pain. I’ve always been good at dealing with the pain my love, but now I have to be sure I’m ready. Big match.” “Don’t lie to me Xavier!” The nails came away, leaving light scratches on his arm that disappeared as quickly as they had appeared. The hurt in her voice sent a shockwave through his chest, cracking his heart. He grabbed the black Jack Daniels tee shirt and pulled it on over his chest, covering the two scars across his pectoral muscles. “I’m not lying. I am going to train with Pitt before we go to High Octane.” Xavier reached into his pocket and pulled a black band out. He tied his long hair back into a ponytail before turning around to look in to Bella’s hurt eyes. He didn’t flinch despite the instinct, “We’re doing a lot of road training down south, in the heat of the desert sun.” “You’re going to Houston first though!” She pulled away, the bitter acid of her tongue burning him as she did. “You’re going to pick something up for your brother! Haven’t we sacrificed enough for him?” “Bella?” Xavier walked over, embracing her from behind as his arms wrapped around her chest. He leaned in, laying a small kiss against her neck, “I’m going to Houston because Pitt and I made the arrangements in Japan. The man delivering the package expects to see us, and with everything we went through over there he isn’t ready to trust anyone but us.” “Make Jake pick up his own damn package!” Bella pulled herself free from him. “He’s the indestructible one, isn’t he” He could just walk in there and walk out no problem!” “We can do that too!” He pleaded. “No one but Pitt and I know about these arrangements. Nothing is risked here, and all we do is take the book with us to the Pay-Per-View. After that, there’s nothing left to do but wait for Robert to translate the text. Everything is almost over, and then we can go back to living free.” Bella turned, blue eyes flashing black rage. She could feel the long stress of pain along her leg, and knew she would always be feeling that pain for the rest of her life. The doctors said it would subside, but still haunt her from time to time because of the severity of the injury. “Do you really think this almost over?” “I do. We’ve come so far, and we’ve endured everything they’ve thrown at us. Nothing is going to stop us, and no one is going to hurt us anymore.” Xavier’s eyes were filled with the hope of his optimism. “You weren’t ever that na’ve before.” Xavier took a step back, rocked by the edge of coldness that had crept into her voice, “You were never that cruel before, never so bleak. I’m going to be back in two weeks, and then you’ll see everything is alright.” Xavier grabbed some jeans from his closet and threw them in over everything. He grabbed his keys, wallet and cell phone as he walked out of the room. He stopped, and thought about the tension between them. “I love you Bella.” Xavier turned to look at her, but stopped himself. “I still want it to be us on the beach house in France one day my love.” Bella said nothing at all. He waited, hoping for a response, but it never came so he left into the dark house. There was business to attend to in Houston. On The Road Dallas, Texas to Houston, Texas The pounding on the door finally woke him from his sleep. Pitt looked across the room, over the round curve of a woman to the door. He turned, seeing another outline of smooth, inviting skin beside him. Pitt sat up, and crawled down between the two forms. He stepped out of bed and walked over to the door. Unashamed in his nakedness, he threw the door open to see his brother, “What?” “We’re supposed to be leaving for Houston.” Xavier saw no reaction from his brother. “Now Pitt, we’re supposed to be leaving now.” Pitt slowly came to life, “Okay, do me a favor. Go get me a caramel macchiato with a couple of extra shots, and when you get back I’ll be all ready to go. I promise.” “Are you fucking kidding me?” Xavier roared. Pitt looked over his shoulder at the two women in his bed, “Come on, I need to say goodbye to my friends.” “Fine.” Xavier looked down at his watch, “I built some spare time into the trip, but you only have fifteen minutes before I kick your ass all the way to Houston.” Pitt held his hands up, “Fine, fine.” Xavier took off, knowing this was not the right omen for their trip. . . . When Xavier pulled into the drive, his brother was kissing each of the women on the cheek as he helped them into the black sedan. He had arranged the for the car service to come out while Xavier was away. Xavier parked behind, and Pitt picked up his duffel bag and suitcase. Black unlocked the rear of the Excursion with the push of a button. Pitt pulled it open and threw the luggage inside. He walked around to the passenger side and slipped inside. Xavier knew there would be bloodshot, tired eyes behind the dark sunglasses. He handed the grande cup over and took off. “Okay, what’s wrong with you?” Pitt asked after a deep sip of his morning salvation. Xavier gripped the wheel tighter as he made his way toward Interstate 45. He was trying to ignore the anger and emotions roiling inside him. “Are you mad because we’re late?” “Yes.” “No you’re not.” Xavier looked sideways at his brother, “Don’t tell me how I feel! This is a big trip! We have the fucking book and the damn training! You acting this way just tells me we’re screwing up already.” “Why are your panties in a wad?” Pitt took another deep sip. Xavier lashed out with his right hand, slapping Pitt across the chest. His brother almost spilled his coffee, but he managed to keep it from spilling. “Hey, this is hot coffee!” “I’m the one who bought it asshole.” Xavier grumbled through clenched teeth. “Remember, while you were running late and getting those two sluts out of OUR house.” “Hey, I seem to remember you running out with plenty of so called sluts before you decided to settle down with the French tart.” Pitt regretted the last comment as soon as he said it. Xavier turned the wheel, screeching the tires as he swerved to the side of the road almost hitting another car. He threw the car in park, and threw the door open. Xavier walked around the front. Realizing what was happening, Pitt slipped out and held up his free hand, “Wait a minute, wait a minute.” “Oh Hell no!” Xavier yelled, “I’m not putting up with that shit!” Xavier was running around after him, but Pitt ran down the side of the Excursion to the other side. Obscenities and profanities were exchanged as Xavier chased after Pitt, and they realized how comical they must have appeared after a few moments. Xavier turned around and got back in the car. Pitt waited a few moments to see if he was being abandoned. The window came down when he hadn’t returned. “GET IN THE FUCKING CAR!” Pitt looked at his coffee. He turned it upside down and downed it all quickly. He crunched the cup and dropped it on the grass, “No hot coffee on this man’s lap. No way, no how, not today.” Pitt opened the door and got back in the car. Xavier slammed the gas pedal down, and he took off down the street in pursuit of Interstate 45 once again. They didn’t talk for a full hour, until they were well on the way to Houston. “So what was it” Your wick not getting wet? Ya’ll fighting or something?” Pitt asked to break the silence. “Shut-up.” “Seriously, I just need to know.” Pitt looked at his brother, but he was focused on the road. “Look, back in the day I would have gotten a high five for two chicks. Now, I get crap about a late start. Then you chase me around the car.” “You called Bella a French tart.” He said testily. Pitt acted as though he were straightening a tie around his neck, “And you called those two young, fine, church-going women sluts. I let it go, but I say French tart and you call a damn Chinese fire drill.” “It’s completely different!” Xavier yelled. They drove in silence for another few moments. “No wet wick.” Xavier rolled his eyes. He spoke in a laugh, “Shut-up.” “No, I understand. Backs up and a man goes crazy.” Xavier playfully slapped him on the shoulder, “She doesn’t want me to go to Houston to get the book.” “Why?” “She thinks we’ll get attacked again.” The mood had become suddenly serious. “Yeah, I’m worried about that too.” Pitt admitted, “But we do what we have to do because that’s who we are. I can’t let my family down, and she knows you can’t either. This is just what we have to do in this moment.” “Yeah, but she saw me get shot’again. She has reason to be afraid.” “We could slip on the floor of the bathroom and break our necks. Life is a risk, but if we lived it any other way we wouldn’t be the men we are today.” Pitt said with more conviction than he realized he had. “I can’t be anything except this, and I don’t have anyone because I’m too scared of what it would do to them to live with the guilt. Jake doesn’t have anyone either, and I bet it’s for the same reason. You were braver than both of us, and she loved you enough to run across the world with you. She’ll be there. In the end, she’ll be there and you’re going to be luckier than we ever were.” Sea Port Houston, Texas Just outside the sea port, they waited in the small caf? that catered to the dock workers. They had taken a seat at a table near the window. They were eating burgers and drinking water as they waited for the meeting. Each man was on edge, wondering if this was another link in the long line of fiascoes they had been involved in lately. The door opened up, the small jingle from the bell drilled into the steel frame. The man was carrying a worn leather pouch, and he looked around the room. They recognized him, although the three months at sea had robbed him of some weight and color in his skin. The man walked over to the table, and sat down beside them. He looked around nervously, just as on edge as Pitt and Xavier were in the unfamiliar surroundings. He set the pouch down and slid it toward them with his foot, “Everything went fine on the trip. Any problems on this end?” Pitt reached down and checked the inside of the pouch. The book was there, easily recognizable. He pushed the bag further back under his chair, “Nothing wrong here. You look like the trip took a lot out of you.” “Three months, all over the fucking Pacific and it’s finally over.” He watched as the small gym bag was slid his way. “Of course, this time I’m flying home first class. You sure no one followed you?” Xavier leaned down, “As sure as we could be. We drove down on a long trip, stopping to make sure no one was following. We sat here for three hours watching the sun go down to make sure. We weren’t followed. You’re safe on this, and we appreciate you escorting the book to us.” “We all live to serve.” The waitress came over, “Hey, can I get an orange juice, a coke, a burger and some fries.” “Sure hon.” She replied. “Anything else for you boys?” “No, we’re headed out.” “okay, thanks for stopping by.” Xavier stood up. He pulled out some cash and dropped four twenty dollar bills down to cover their food and a nice tip for being there for so long. Pitt stood, the old leather pouch now in hand. “You stay safe.” Pitt said as he walked away. They walked outside into the humid night air. Each of them were on the lookout for anyone or anything suspicious. Neither saw anything as they climbed into the car and left. Across the street, concealed in the darkest part of the alley he watched them leave. They had sat for eight hours to leave with a different bag from some little Japanese man. There was something of vast importance in the bag. He had to know what it was. It could hold the secret I need?
