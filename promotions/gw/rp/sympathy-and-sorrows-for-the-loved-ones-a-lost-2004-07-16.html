Sympathy And Sorrows For The Loved Ones A Lost

16 July 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/16/04 Fountains of everlasting dreams. Nests of never-ending hopes. These are your desires. These are the things you long for. The things you’ve always longed for. Mountains of pain. Rivers of sorrow. These are your fears. These are the things you are afraid of. The things you’ve always been afraid of. So hide no more. Rest your burdens at my feet. Drop your tragedies off at my doorstep. “let me be the one to harvest your tears. Sometimes, you’ve always questioned yourself, but now you are slowly beginning to realize that the world is never fair. Whenever something seems to be going right, reality checks you hard into the jaw, shattering every bone in your face against the windshield. Or you open your eyes, hoping to see something remarkable and exciting, but instead, you see a bullet from a revolver coming straight at your face. Or even when you think your life is the closest thing to perfection, your daughter is taken from you within a blink of an eye. And it hurts more then a shard of glass to your eyeball. Actually, you wish that you can replace the tragedy with that. But what you want to replace, can’t happen. You already know this. There are things beyond human control. Even beyond the control of my magic, which we can not do. If we could, it would make life so much easier. Suddenly, you go from loving the prospect of life to attempting to escape reality every chance you get. Trust me, I’ve been there before? “My regards go out to you and your family.” When these tragedies strike, you wish you can talk to God and ask him, “why?”. You wish you can call him collect, or send him a text message. You wish you can e-mail him. “Why did this have to happen?” “This always happens to me.” “the truth is, it’s happening all around you. It is not just happening to you. You are just too stubborn to open your eyes and realize it. You watch the news, which I’m sure you do, and countless families” problems and tragedies are aired over a local or worldwide audience. But none of this matters to you, because it doesn’t involve you. None of this will ever affect your life, whatsoever. Don’t take me wrong, I make this mistake personally. I’ve asked myself these questions time and time again. And none of them seemed to get answered. “May God be with you during this time of need.” As I sit here, I remember all the things that people have told me whenever something happened. I remember the time my father passed away when I was a boy. I remember people caring, for the first time, because something actually happened. I remember people willing to give their right arm, just so that I won’t have an ounce of heartache. When something like that happens, you see the world reunite. Every problem they have in their life, is paused for a moment, just to comfort you. The best medicine for anguish and torture is sympathy. “May his healing hand rest upon your side.” Of course you can, shed many tears. Free yourself from the burdens you carry. Throw your guilt away. Leave any unnecessary pain on my doorstep. Let Me Be The One To Harvester Your Tears How many times have you wished your tragedies on another person? How many times have you said, “I wish it happened to somebody else?”. Well, maybe you are getting a taste of your own medicine. Maybe the Russian Roulette of life finally landed on you instead of Jason Meadows of Tampa, Florida. Instead of Albert Ross of Charlotte, North Carolina. Instead of Mary Lee Francis of the little town of Sweet Valley, Pennsylvania. So small, you never even knew existed. What about them? Do you mourn the death of Albert’s Uncle Ronnie? Would you let Jason cry on your shoulder after he claimed bankruptcy? Do you care about Mary Lee’s child that she just miscarried? “of course you don’t. You wouldn’t even give them a sympathy card. “I apologize from the bottom of my heart for this tragedy.” While scribbling words, hoping to comfort somebody who needs comfort, I think about the time I wished death upon somebody. I know you’ve done it before, the truth is, you can’t even remember exactly how many times that is. Isn’t that pathetic? What about if somebody wished death upon your cousin Frank? Say if he passed away the very next day, which would leave you in tears. You rest your head on your pillow late at night and shed a tear of sorrow. “He was too young to die.” “Why him?” People say that everyone dies for a reason. It’s true, I’ve even heard the man above say it himself. Does God have a list, a notebook, or a Word Text Document filled with a record of the people who are going to die next? Or the people who are going to experience a tragedy. Do you actually have more problems then Vicente Sanchez from Bradenton, Florida, who just had his son die from the terrible hurricane that hit his home last week. Hell, you probably didn’t even know it happened. “I wish you a full and speedy recovery.” You can blame everybody in the world, because it’s a world of blame. Or, you can accept the fact that things happen for a reason, and stop wishing such things upon other people. As I reread what I’ve written, I take my glasses off and I wipe a tear from my cheek as I remember the time Jessica passed away in that car accident. I remember my father dying at an early age because there was no amazing technology where we were from like other places. I remember passing away myself. The only thing I can tell you, is to take each day as they come. Some may hurt, some may feel great. Just don’t fear each day that you wake up in the morning. Time heals all wounds, but it seems a lot longer when the wounds are six inches deep, or never seem to heal properly. But eventually, everything will be alright. This, I promise you. “If there is anything you need, let me know.” I read what I’ve written aloud? “Dearest Jesse and family, My regards go out to you and your family. May God be with you during this time of need and may his healing hand lie upon your side. I apologize from the bottom of my heart for this tragedy. I wish you a full and speedy recovery. If there is anything you need, let me know. God Bless, Sage Sabbot I hope this helps him relieve some of the stress and sorrow. I feel bad, I honestly do for the poor man. He doesn’t deserve it, nobody does. But it is just another task to overcome. It is just another chapter to be written into the book of the life of Jesse Jamester. He can put it right after the chapter about winning the Global Title. Right after the chapter about qualifying for Master of the Mat and advancing into the second round. People say that for everything good thing in life, a bad thing will happen. And for everything bad thing that happens, a good thing will happen’evening out the sorrows of life. Sometimes, it doesn’t seem like this. Because we take every good thing for granted. If you actually sat down and thought about this, the great things in life outnumber the bad. Perhaps this is God’s card of sympathy towards us. For years upon years, he has watched us cry and mourn over such things. Maybe he is just trying to help us out. But we take so many things for granted. We think a warm meal should be automatically warped to our dining room table. We think that a clean shower is always in the upstairs bathroom. We think that the places we sleep in are there because of we earned them. I wish for once, we stop taking these things for granted and look at life as a whole. We look at the blue sky above, our loved ones around us, and the beautiful flowers below as something that has always been there. As something that we don’t care about. Perhaps for once, you should start noticing them and stop taking these little things to you, but bigger things to people who don’t have them, for granted. Sometimes in life, there are worse things then dying. Myself, I wish to see myself die’again, way before Cherish ever does. If she dies, then I don’t know how I’m going to cope with that. I can’t picture myself waking up each and every morning without her by my side. I can’t imagine sleeping each night without watching her for hours upon hours as she sleeps. And I believe Jesse felt the same way about his daughter. I picture him coming back from a tour we’ve had and seeing his daughter for the first time in months and holding her in his arms. I picture Jesse tucking her in at night. I picture Jesse, telling his daughter, reminding his daughter the he loves her more then every fish in the sea. Then every raindrop in a storm. Then every star in a midnight sky. And I sit back, wishing there was something I could do. Not just send a sympathy card, or call him over the phone and wish him that everything goes back to normal’or as close to it as possible. But I wish there was something I can do to help him. Something I can do to help? Something I can do to help? Something I can? The proverbial light flashes in my head. I reach in my pocket and pull out my wallet. I throw it on the table in front of me and flip it open. I pull out a $100 bill from the back of a stack of cash and fold it in half. I kiss it, giving the Jamester family my sincerest apologies and place it inside the card. I maneuver the card into the envelope and lick the sticky side of the envelope. I firmly press it down, closing it. I turn it around and write Jesse Jamester on the front in big bold letters. I write God Bless on the upper left hand corner? “This should help pay for the hospital bill after Evolution.” I sign my name underneath that?
