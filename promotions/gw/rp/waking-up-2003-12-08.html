Waking Up

8 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: December 8th, 2003 Light. Was that light? My eyes slowly pried themselves open, lids scraping over the orbs like sandpaper. Whiteness filled my eyes like fire, and I winced, slamming them shut again. Cautiously this time, I cracked them open just a bit, letting the light trickle in, until the burning subsided into a dull ache. Everything was white. Why was it so white? Taking a deep breath, I tried to concentrate on an imaginary point, to focus on something. Slowly but surely, the world came into focus. I realized I was looking at a ceiling, plain and white. I could make out the light fixture now, and even some of the cracks. It hurt to look at. Laboriously, I dragged my eyes away from the ceiling, and over the room. It was small and plain, the walls painted a soft blue that complemented the white. Thee was a single window in the west wall that overlooked a park, and a TV mounted on a stand in the corner opposite me. I tried to turn my head to look over the room more thoroughly -- and found that I couldn't. Confused, I reached up with my hand to touch the thing restraining me -- and saw that my hand an forearm were sealed in a thick cast. I lifted my other arm. An IV tube was plugged into my vein. I? I was in a hospital? Slowly, it all came back to me. The fall. The sickening crash at the end. But there was more than that. There was him. His hands, clutching the tools that brought me pain. His face, cold and impassive, as he inflicted every pain he could think of. Those cold blue eyes, unflinching through all the tortures inflicted on me, body and mind?. I snapped my eyes shut again, trying to think of something, anything else. Something to drown out those thoughts, those memories. Something to push away the pain. Tears began to creep out from the corners of my eyes as I struggled with those thoughts and feelings. I fought them, fought them as hard as I could? but they came just the same. I was too weak to fight them, too beaten down to pull up my famous stone-faced resolve. So I cried. I wept and sobbed, releasing the torment that brewed inside me. And then? then he was there. I wasn't sure if he'd been there all along, or had just arrived. All I knew was that I felt strong arms wrapping gently around me, felt soft hair brush against my shoulder. And when I finally let my blue eyes open, I was gazing into his eyes of emerald green. 'Hey,' said the Lion, brushing away one of my tears, 'I'm here now, Yoshiko-chan. I'm here, and you're safe.' Fiercely, I grabbed him with my free hand, wrapping my arms around him and clinging close. I could feel his warmth, his strength. I focused on that and only that, letting it become the center of my world. Slowly, I regained my center. 'I?' I stalled, unsure of what to say, what to ask. I released him, and laid back against the hospital bed again. 'Rokku-chan,' I finally asked, 'how long have I been out?' 'Two weeks,' he replied, clasping my hand in his. 'The doctors weren't optimistic you'd be waking up any time soon. But of course, here you are, stubborn as ever. I knew you couldn't stay down for long.' I smiled weakly. He always had such faith in me? 'Rokku?' I asked, 'what's happened? I mean? the last thing I remember is Star pushing me off the stage, and now it's two weeks later?' 'Um, well?' Rokku struggled with the words, like he knew I wouldn't like what he had to say. Finally, he swallowed hard, and began. 'I got thrown off the stage right behind you,' he said, 'and we were both rushed here by paramedics. Both of us landed pretty bad in those falls, but you were obviously much worse off from me, after what Ice? anyway. I spent a day in a hospital bed myself, with a concussion. As soon as I could, I got out of that bed and found where you were being treated. And I've um? I've pretty much been here ever since. More than once the doctors tried to make me go home and get some rest, but there was no way in hell I was leaving you alone. Not with Ice and Star still out there, not in the condition you're in...' His eyes shifted away from me as he said those last few words. I could tell that he didn't want to have to tell me something, that something pained him too much to say. I had a good idea what it was, but I had to know? 'Rokku-chan? please. Tell me how bad it is. I can take it.' He smiled ruefully. 'Always the stoic, Yoshiko-chan. Some of us have trouble with this kind of pain, y'know?' I squeezed his hand tightly. 'Rokku-chan? I have to know.' 'It's? it's pretty bad, 'shiko. Not as bad as it could have been, but? okay. You've got several broken bones -- in your arm, your legs, and other places -- but that's not the worst of it. When you fell, your head and neck absorbed most of the impact. That's why you're wearing that harness, to keep you from aggravating the injury while you were unconscious. Your neck? it didn't break. But it came awfully damn close. For a bit, the doctors weren't sure if you'd be able to walk again, but like always, you pulled through. Beyond that, though? they aren't sure you'll ever have the same range of motion you had before, between the head trauma, the neck trauma, the tissue and bone damage? it doesn't look good at all. You should be able to go back to a relatively normal life, but they aren't sure if you'll ever really be able to fight again.' I wanted to take a moment, and let everything he said sink in. I had been steeling myself while he spoke, readying my heart to receive the blow I knew was coming. But when it finally came, I didn't feel the thick, cold weight I was expecting. I felt something else entirely. I felt heat. A fire, a passion, rising up from inside me and spreading through me like a shock of lightning. I wasn't sad. I was angry. Who the hell did that sick bastard think he was? This was my life, damn it. My LIFE! What gave Ice the right to try and take it away from me? There was no way I could let him get away with it. Nuh-uh. I wasn't going to let him take one damn thing away from us -- not ever. I realized something, as this anger spread through me, this red hot emotion coursing through my veins. It wasn't alone. It had brought something else with it, something very important. It had brought hope. 'Rokku,' I said, my expression growing stern, 'those doctors are wrong. Ice was out to hurt you through me, out to try and break you down by breaking me down -- and god damn it, I'm not going to let him. Together, we're going to show Ice that he can't ever beat us down. When's Showdown in Tokyo?' 'Six days away,' said Rokku, 'but there's no way in hell I'm going back to Japan while you're in the hospital, Yoshiko-chan. Ice can keep the damn world title, for all I care. You're what's important.' 'Well then, I'm getting out of the hospital. Those doctors can try to keep me here all they want, but I will drag myself out of here with my TONGUE before I let anything get between you and beating that bastard in the middle of a GW ring. Call a doctor, Rock-chan. It's time for me to negotiate my freedom.' Rokku looked down at me, and his brow furrowed, his lips twisting into a frown. For a moment, I thought he was going to try and talk me out of it. Then his expression melted away, and was replaced with a knowing smile. 'Damn, Yoshiko. I love you beyond the telling of it, but do you know what it's like to have to give up on your argument before you even start it because you know you haven't got a chance in hell?' 'Not a clue,' I replied, smiling saucily, 'can't remember a time I lost an argument, myself. Now go get that doctor!' 'Yes ma'am,' said my Lion, as he hustled out of the room. The door swished shut, and I looked up at the ceiling again. I imagined the cracks in its structure as breaks lacing a glacier, weak points ready to be exploited to bring the massive force of nature down. It didn't seem so bad to look at, any more. In fact, it didn't hurt at all.
