From Out of Hell...A Hero!

30 September 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9.30.03 Seagate Prison. A maximum security prison from which no one has ever escaped. Seagate. Sometimes called 'Little Alcatraz' by its inmates... more often called Hell. Our story is the story of an inmate within these stone walls known as Carl Lucas. His stay at Seagate has been like the stay “Maybe now, you’ll learn some manners, boy,” Quirt sneers. All the while, Lucas stews internally. Out in the prison yard, Lucas is approached by another inmate -- a savvy prison politico by the name of 'Shades' -- seeking to enlist his aid some nebulous sort of mass 'demonstration,' in order 'to set this new warden straight.' 'You been awful quick to speak for the brothers ever since you came here, Shades,' Lucas observes. 'Sounds like now you gonna be quick gettin' their heads broken, too. If mine goes under the club, it's gonna be 'cause I put it there, not you!' Just then, a guard walks up to Lucas and Shades. “Lucas! Rackham wants to see you!” 'Rackham' is the outgoing warden and yet another of Lucas? tormentors. 'Man like you -- with no love for them smart-talkin' punks -- could do himself some good,” says Rackham. “Just have to help me learn what that bunch is up to, an' when they plan to make their move. You scratch my back, boy... an' I scratch yours.' By now, Lucas is disgusted. True, he has no particular love for Shades and his bunch, but he preferred them to a racist pig like Rackham. “You must have your head up your ass, Rackham!” This retort gets Lucas more time One day, Lucas meets Noah Bernstein, the newly appointed prison doctor. 'I grew up on the streets in Harlem. Back then, my best friend was a dude named Willis Stryker. We used to run in gangs together when we were kids, stealing purses, robbing liquor stores, stuff like that. Then, times changed and I wanted out. I worked a few odd jobs here and there. Willis stayed in the gangs, though, lookin? to make a name for himself. But we were still friends. Then came Reva Connors. We both loved her and for a while, she was with Willis. Then, he got hurt and we just started getting? closer. That didn’t sit too well with Willis, so he planted heroin in his apartment and tipping off police, which o? course, lands me here. It was bad enough, all the brawls and the beatings from Rackham. Then I heard that Reva Now I'm livin' for one thing, and one thing only -- to get out! Get out an' get Willis!' And in Dr. Bernstein, Lucas finds something he never expected to find in Seagate: a sympathetic ear. “Lucas, I believe I may have the key to your freedom. You see, I’m conducting an experiment in the electro-biochemical stimulation of human cell regeneration. If you agree to volunteer for this experiment and it’s successful, then I may be able to arrange for you to receive an early release.' Right away, Lucas volunteers. The experiment involves a tank filled with chemicals. Lucas steps into the tank and the experiment begins. All goes well when suddenly, Rackham sneaks in and knocks the unsuspecting Bernstein unconscious. Hoping to kill Lucas, Rackham turns the knobs up to their maximum settings ? and energy floods the prisoner's body, beefing him up to maximum strength and rendering him nearly invulnerable. An agonized and enraged Lucas batters his way out and strikes a frantic Rackham right across the jaw. 'He's out like you used a hammer,' a disbelieving Bernstein informs Lucas. 'This is serious.' Lucas is now furious with himself. His chances at winning parole were already marginal. This would certainly jeopardize them. 'Did I live through that brimstone bath just to get set up for this” If that worthless scum dies --!' In anger, Lucas slams one massive fist against the nearest stone wall and makes a fist-sized hole, in so doing. Presumed killed in the escape attempt, Lucas manages to make his way back to New York City. However, with no identification and knowing he'll be returned the moment he’s recognized, he’s unable to make a living ? until he spies a lone gunman attempting to rob a restaurant. Using his new abilities, he foils the robbery. The grateful owner gives Lucas a cash reward. An idea starts to brew in Lucas? mind. He can now use his abilities to earn a living. From then on, Lucas becomes Luke Cage, Hero for Hire!!! And somewhere, Brick wakes up. “I gotta get that part somehow.”
