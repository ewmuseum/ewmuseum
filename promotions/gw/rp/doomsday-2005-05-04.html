Doomsday

4 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05.04.05 The sun shone bright upon the architectural landscape of Metropolis, echoing with ferocity off of the great glass structures below. Somewhere, deep inside this great meca, stood an epicenter that stood as a hub of communication for millions upon millions of curious Americans. A smaller building, when standing among the rest, but a giant in the journalistic universe that so many had come to rely upon. Situated with a large globe atop it, stood the Daily Planet, and inside, all hell was literally breaking loose. 'This is the biggest story of the year and I can't get one decent photograph,' yelled the Planet's editor, Perry White. A stockier man with a head filled with salt and pepper hair, he was a very intimidating presence once he was felt. 'You've got to be kidding me, Olsen.' 'I'm sorry, Chief. The camera, it was... it was broken,' lied a wet-eyes and distraught Jimmy Olsen, the head photographer for The Daily Planet. Jimmy was there when the Man of Steel fell protecting the great city of Metropolis. 'Great Caesar's Ghost, Jimmy,' rang White again. 'I can't believe you were so clumsy.' 'I'm s-sorry, Sir,' gritted Olsen through a lump in his chest. The Last Son of Krypton was more than Earth's greatest hero to him, but a close personal friend. A man that had given Jimmy trust when he never had to. Jimmy would never forget him for that reason, if nothing else. Moreover, it was that reason that he would never disrespect his best-friend in all of the world by exploiting his death. A blue and red streak shook the sky as it plowed into what looked to be an eight-foot tall mound of rock in the shape of a small mountain, pushing both back a quarter of a mile through the interstate. As the debris settled, Superman and Doomsday were both just getting to their feet, rattled from the effects of the earthquake they had just caused. They battle that they were embedded in was one that had stretched throughout the globe during a span of multiple days. A war of which had never been witnessed with a ferocity of which could never be registered. Jor-El was not fighting for his life, but for the lives of the inhabitants of the planet Earth. He made his way to his feet and took another swing at the ferocious alien beast towering before him. As his fist connected with the solid granite of it's face, a true pain shot through his arm and into his neck. For the first time in his life, the Man of Tomorrow had felt true pain. 'What are you,' he asked, only for the response to come in the form of a loud roar. The sonic boom of the vocal emission actually shook Supes long enough for Doosday to connect with a brutal uppercut, launching Superman into the heavens before bringing him back down in front of the Daily Planet. He reached to his lip and pulled his hand away to discover a wetness of which he had never seen. His own blood, the crimson of his soul, was draining from him. He picked himself back up as he saw the beast approaching and readed himself with clinching fists together. The alien shot a giant blast into the right temlple of Metropolis' favorite son as he brought his fists up to connect with chin of the beast, breaking off the stalagtite-type formation hanging from it. All was silent after the wave of the blow allowed the dust to settle. Doomsday had fallen, he was defeated. But, laying next to him was a sight that world never thought it would see... it's fallen hero... lifeless. Lois Lane rushed to the now limp body of her love and held him in her arms, her eyes filled with tears and her face with the sorrow of a million tragedies. Jimmy's camera clicked. He had captured the most emotional moment that the world had ever had. 'Not even one photo,' yelled White again with a bit of Plea in his voice. 'No, Sir,' replied Olsen. 'Not even one.' 'Cut,' shouted the film's director Kevin Smith. 'That was great guys. What say we call it a day and pick it back up tomorrow.' With those words, a new type of rustle took place upon the extravagant set. Not one of a battle, but one of a crew desperate to get home to their families. One such member passed by a smiling Ken Partridge Jr, dressed in a battle worn and bloody blue, red, and yellow uniform witha torn cape. 'That fight sequence is going to be incredible once we aplice it together with the rest of the stuff you two are gonna' do,' chimed Smith. 'Thanks,' responded KPJ. 'It's really intense. I like how it's being pulled off. How every action has emotion.' 'That's all you, my friend. Computers can't make that type of thing.' Just as Ken was about to thank Kevin again, a beautiful young actress by the name of Mia Kirshner approached the two. She was the one playing the role of Lois, and was trailed by Jake Gyllenhaal and Albert Finney, playing the roles of Jimmy Olsen and Perry White respectively. 'We still having a cast meeting in about half an hour,' questioned Mia. 'Absolutly,' responded Smith. 'It wouldn't hurt to go ahead and prep for tomorrow. 'Yeah,' added Ken, 'it could be full of surprises.'
