Loose Ends Unraveling: Part IV: Trust No One

12 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/12/2004 Silence. I’d never really learned to appreciate it. Now, I despised it? it had become nothing more than my enemy. A co-conspirator in the mockery my life had become. I had an empty existence? a job I wasn’t very good at? an empty compound in the middle of nowhere that echoed the silence back to my ears, with the lonesome accompaniment of the coyotes. But tonight, I wasn’t at home, if home was what I was supposed to call that place. Instead I was sitting in my car, freezing my ass off, in the middle of south-western Ontario? ---- Monday, November 8, 2004 0131 hours, local time Fat Moe’s Bar & Grill, Sarnia, Ontario “Remind me again why I’m doing this?” he muttered to his companion, cranking up the heat to clear the windows. It had to be at least freezing out, and the night would have shouted of impending snowfall, if not for the crystal clear expanse of sky overhead. Indigo blue, the same color as his eyes, no less cold, or forbidding. Aleksander Lyashenko shook his head, chuckling with good humor that might have had much to do with the little white lines that had gone up his nose prior to this meeting. His icy blue eyes met Jackson’s across the car, and the sparkle in their depths was less than friendly. “You scratch my back, comrade, I scratch yours” is that not the saying, no?” Jackson’s sigh was quiet, but it still carried across the wintry silence in the car. “Yes, that is the saying. I would, at the very least, like to see something up front, before you blow the rest of it up your nose, Aleks.” “Comrade Jackson, you need to be more trusting.” The Russian shook his head, his fingers already drumming restlessly on the armrest. Jackson’s voice was terse, and slightly more gruff than usual when he replied. “Cut the bullshit, already. You know, as well as I do, that people I trust have a funny way of trying to take me out. You planning to kill me too, Aleks?” He turned in the seat, pinning the Russian with a malevolent glare. His lips curved in a smile that was less than appealing, one that revealed his fang-like incisors, reminding Lyashenko of a wolf he had seen once. “Er,” he scrambled for words, his accent growing heavier in his distress. “Nyet. No, Comrade Jackson, I mean no disrespect. I have nothing but good intentions in mind, you can believe me truly?” Jackson’s fist cut off the rest of the verbal diarrhea as he slammed it against Lyashenko’s chest, pressing him back against the seat. “Sit back and shut the fuck up, I don’t want to hear your well wishes and your ass kissing. You have a job to do, so do I. Now where the fuck is your contact, huh” He’s late.” ---- The motorcycle sped through the dark, and nearly deserted streets of Sarnia, weaving around the occasional car. He was pushing the engine as hard as he could, trying to make up for lost time, cursing the circumstances that led him to this meeting. Drugs. It all boiled down to the drugs. He’d often quipped that he’d said no to them, but they hadn’t listened. It wasn’t far from the painful truth. Bruce McLeod was a slave, and heroin was the master. That, however, didn’t change the fact that he was still one of the best at what he did. He was a bounty hunter, a few years retired, but for the right price, and with a jerk of the short leash from the right source, he could still be had. Born in Killarney, his family had moved to Canada when he was a boy? looking for a life better than being dirt-poor potato farmers. Or so he liked to believe, in his romantic moments, when his mind wasn’t occupied with other diversions. He’d travelled throughout most of the world, at one time or another, but he always seemed to end up back here, in this shitty little border town. Perhaps it was something in the water. He turned the corner at breakneck speed, leaning low, almost skimming his knee on the pavement, only his skill at riding kept him upright in the chill wind. He couldn’t even feel his body, and his breath steamed from his mouth in a white cloud, distracting him for a split second. “Late,” he muttered, as he skidded the bike to a stop on the road before a brightly lit building. He turned into the parking lot, easily finding a place for his Sportster next to the other Harleys. His element here, and everyone here knew his name, but even still, he felt a chill of foreboding creep down his back as he unstrapped his helmet, clamping it down on the seat. He stretched slowly, eying the lot, finding the black car easily amid all the others. It had Michigan plates, not abnormal round these parts, but something about the car seemed to ooze evil. He resisted the urge to make the evil eye sign, and instead turned his eyes up to the sky, seeing something odd. Stripes and striations? greenish. “The hell?” he muttered, and a man standing next to him on the patio spoke up, talking around the cigarette he was puffing on. “Northern lights, ain’t that a sight” We don’t usually see em this far south?” Bruce cast one last look at the sky, and nodded solemnly, not trusting himself to speak around the lump that was forming in his throat. He shouldered past the smokers clustered on the patio in front of the door, and pushed his way inside the warmth of Fat Moe’s Bar & Grill. ---- “Da. There he is, finally at last.” Lyashenko turned to lay a hand on Jackson’s arm as he opened the door. “What?” He glared at the Russian, his disdain evident. “You are not” as you say, carrying a piece, are you? Comrade McLeod is very particular? and as crazy as a communist rat, we do not want to have a bloodbath on our hands.” Jackson frowned, a single line marring his forehead, drawing attention to the scar that slashed up from the corner of his left eye. “Does he get to have one?” “Da. It is his way. It is how we do business, on his terms.” “Right,” Jackson withdrew the Walther P99 from the shoulder holster, laying it on the floor in the backseat. He didn’t, however, remove the second pistol that was strapped inside a custom-made one inside the breast pocket of his leather jacket. “Then I will play by the rules, for this one time. I don’t like it. I don’t like going in there unarmed, Aleks, especially not if that nutbar is carrying.” “Indeed. But as you have told me on many an occasion, you do not fear death.” Jackson chuckled, the sound as cold as the wind that slipped inside the car as he opened the door. “No, but death does fear me.” ---- Bruce sat alone at a table beside the pool table, twirling a glass of beer on the tabletop, listening to the sound it made as the glass made contact with the table. Tonight there was no karaoke, or a band, just loud chatter over the sound of the piped in radio. The place had had a jukebox, but they’d taken it out to make more room for the karaoke stage. Not that it mattered much; he had no coins to drop in it anyhow. He ran a hand over his close-cropped hair, reaching for the long strands that weren’t there anymore. He’d worn it long for so many years; he still felt that phantom tickle at the back of his neck. His arms had begun to tremble, and it took great effort to suppress the first signs of withdrawal. The gun felt secure against his chest, beneath the leather vest he wore buttoned beneath his worn jacket, and he knew it was more than a security blanket, as surely as he knew that dealing with Lyashenko was a bad idea. But then, desperate times called for desperate measures, and all things aside, the Russian still had the best crank around. The little bell above the door jingled, and somehow, without looking, he knew that it was Lyashenko, and whomever he was bringing with him this time. Some asshole, who wanted something done, no doubt. He looked up, and found himself looking at one of the tallest men he’d seen around these parts. The man in black had to be at least six-seven, and his face seemed to be carved from granite, hard and immovable. The duo approached Bruce’s table, and hesitated there. He made no effort to get up; instead watching the foamy head die down in his glass of beer. “Comrade McLeod,” Lyashenko’s voice was less than thrilled, although it was soft enough not to carry to other tables. “May we join you on this lovely winter’s eve?” “Aye,” Bruce looked up then, his eyes filled with equal measures of wariness and weariness. “I suppose you may.” Lyashenko sank gratefully into one of the chairs, and Jackson took the other, turning it around, sitting down stiffly with his massive arms braced on the back. Bruce looked back and forth between the two before letting his eyes settle on Jackson. He made no effort to hide his scrutiny, nor did he make great pains to shield himself from the power he could feel welling from the dark man. Jackson grunted, one side of his mouth quirking in a ghost of a smile. “Take a picture, it lasts longer.” To McLeod’s ears, he sounded American, but his accent was tinged with something European. Sounded like Dutch, or perhaps German descent. “Comrade McLeod, this is the man I spoke of on the phone. A man I respect dearly, and a man I owe a great many favors to, including my life, Comrade Jackson.” Lyashenko picked up the pitcher of beer from the table, and filled a mug, taking a generous swig, letting his restless gaze flick to and fro between the two men. “Aleks, drop the “comrade” crap, it makes you sound like a badly done movie villain.” Jackson’s voice oozed with sarcasm, but his face remained bland, carefully blank. “So, this is the infamous Highlander, pleased to meet you McLeod, your reputation precedes you, although, I think it may have been rather misleading.” “Meaning?” Bruce stiffened at the comment, feeling his anger creeping up from the black hole he’d stuffed it down into. He unsnapped the vest, one button at a time, letting Jackson see the strap of his shoulder holster. “I expected you to be bigger” and well? just a little bit scarier.” His chuckle slid off Bruce’s skin, feeling greasy. “And just what is it that you do, Mr”. Jackson, was it?” Bruce’s gaze was hard, cold and flinty, but he could feel a quiver of fear growing in the pit of his stomach, churning the beer into bitter acid. Jackson sighed, adjusting his position slightly, his movement making his jacket settle strangely against his chest. Bruce’s eye caught the movement, and he saw the heaviness in the pocket. Perhaps it was paranoia, but he didn’t think this man was the type to carry a metal cigar case. That heaviness was a gun, and that meant the rules had just changed. A flicker of something akin to terror flashed through his eyes, and he forced it away, forcing a smile instead, trying like hell to make it hit his slate gray eyes. Jackson’s movement had been as close to fidgeting that Bruce had seen since they entered. This one was cold, a professional? something. Jackson didn’t seem nervous in dealing with someone who killed? and raised the dead, he didn’t seem? anything. He could have been eating tea and crumpets, or reading a novel alone for all the reaction he was showing right now. “I do this and that, but enough about me, McLeod. I need you to do something for me. What I do for a living has no relevance on this case.” “I beg to differ,” he set down the beer mug with a resounding thud, wiping the back of his hand across his lips while Lyashenko snickered quietly, thoroughly enjoying this particular pissing contest. “It has everything to do with whether I take your case” or not.” “I think not,” Jackson leaned forward, his indigo eyes boring into Bruce’s stormy gray ones. “You see, McLeod, Aleks has already taken my money, and I have already repaid him in turn. That means you owe me now, and if you want more of that poison that’s slowly killing you, you have no choice but to comply.” “I don’t think so, gentlemen.” McLeod let his hands fall from the table, pulling his jacket aside, making it clear that he intended to go for his gun. “You don’t call the shots here, I do. This is my bar” in my fucking town.” If at all possible, Jackson seemed to grow calmer yet, every movement seemed to stop so that he didn’t even seem to be breathing. He simply stared across the table, naked hostility brimming from his strange eyes. He looked, for lack of a better word, dead. Like nothing more than a wax dummy, and that, in Bruce’s mind, made him downright scary. “Don’t make me have to kill you, Bruce” I just had this jacket cleaned and I would be really put out if I had to do it again. Nothing stains quite like fresh blood.” He let his hands fall away from the back of the chair, mimicking McLeod’s prior movements, getting his hands ready to drawn down, if necessary. Bruce’s eyes flicked to Lyashenko, and back to Jackson, filled with an entreaty. He was begging without saying a word, for what it wasn’t quite clear. “You changed the rules. That’s not right. I don’t have to help you now” you changed the rules.” Jackson smirked, and reached inside his jacket, pulling out a needle. He cupped it in his hand, letting McLeod see it, but still keeping it hidden from the other patrons. It was like showing a starving dog a bone. “Ah, yes” I have changed the rules, but does that mean you don’t want to play, Bruce?” His eyes were fixed on that little glass cylinder, already he could feel the sensation he knew it could bring. He found himself nodding, feeling the sudden, almost seductive pull of Jackson’s low voice. “Good. Good. Then listen to me, there is a man I need you to find” a man called Gabriel Delacroix. He used to be CID? now he is nothing more than a nuisance. The reason I need you, is because by now he may, or may not be dead. I thought I had killed him, now I’m not so sure.” Bruce blinked, looking up into Jackson’s face. “CID” No, man. No,” he shook his head, and then froze, looking at Jackson’s cold features. “Fuck, you’re CID too, aren’t you” This isn’t good, aye, I’m bloody well fucked, aren’t I?” “Relax,” Jackson let his features soften slightly, letting Bruce see something of his former humanity. His smile was almost sincere, almost? nice. “Yes, I am CID. But I’m not here for you, McLeod.” Bruce sat there for a long moment, his face flushed, his hands trembling as he looked back and forth from the syringe still in Jackson’s massive hand, to the man’s chiselled features. Finally, his reserve broke, the monkey on his back screaming for another fix. “Fine. I’ll do it. Please, please” just give it to me.” Jackson nodded, and rose, making his way to the tiny restroom at the back of the crowded bar, leaving Bruce no choice but to follow?
