Shattered Dreams

31 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/31/2005 Ramirez Home; Cochella, CA August 31, 2005; 1442 hours The shower water felt a little too cold for comfort, but it was still pleasant enough after a mile’s run out in the brunt of the summer heat. It helped clear Teresa’s mind... for now. Things weren’t going well for the masked Luchador; the loss to Unite having aggravated the wound that had been opened. It was the first time since their debut in GW back in January that Herencia had taken a loss by submission. That Teresa was the one who tapped out, after telling her husband that she wasn’t sure the legacy they’d swore to rebuild-- and finally felt they’d completed when they captured the tag-team championships-- was worth the emotional hardships they were going through. Juan was changing somehow... Teresa could see it; why couldn’t he? ?First things first,” thought Teresa. Juan was having a dentist appointment and wouldn’t be back for another two hours, while Maggie LaPortia would be bringing Melina home in about thirty minutes. Maggie was a skilled social worker, and Teresa hoped on the two of them being able to persuade Juan to enter joint marital counseling with Teresa. Stepping out of the shower and slipping into a thick terry robe, Teresa suddenly heard a slight sound from the living room. Her ears pricked up as she turned toward it. “Hello?” she called. “It’s just I, Teresa,” came Juan’s voice. “I thought you were going to the dentist,” said Teresa. Juan chuckled slightly. “They cancelled at the last minute,” he answered. “His son got into a fight at school and they wanted him to drop everything he was doing and come running over there for an emergency meeting with the principal.” Teresa grumbled slightly under her breath. It was an old argument that she and Juan had about modern family values here in their new country. “Maggie will be bringing Melina back in about twenty minutes,” she said. “Also, I’m expecting a call from Jos”--? Riiiiiiiing! Teresa’s head turned. “The doorbell” Is Maggie here already?” Juan shrugged. “Only one way to find out,” he said. Teresa went to the door and looked through the peephole. She frowned bewilderedly at the face that smiled back at her. “What is she--?” Teresa opened the door and put a hand on her hip. “Well...” she said. “What brings you here?” The dark-haired woman standing before the doorway grinned. “What, no warm welcome?” she asked quizzically. “It’s been almost a year,” said Teresa. “Maybe if we saw you more often, the welcome would be warmer.” A soft, apologetic frown creased the woman’s features. “I know how you feel, Teresa, but...” “I’m sorry too, but you know that I feel there are no buts,” said Teresa. “Even with all the stresses of your job, family makes time for family. Please believe me when I say I know and understand that you feel that coming to see us puts us at risk... but it’s a risk we’re willing to take.” The dark-haired woman lowered her head and sighed softly. “I wish you didn’t want to take that risk, Teresa,” she said, “but you’re the eldest... you always took care of me. I’d like to return the favor, but old habits die hard.” She grinned cockily and opened her arms. “So, maybe a hug for your little sister?” Teresa giggled lightly and waved her sister in, where they gave each other a big hug. The sister looked over Teresa’s shoulder and smiled at Juan. “Hey, Juan,” she said, wiggling her fingers in greeting. “Hey yourself,” said Juan with a slight smile. “Good to see you again. Melina will be coming home in about thirty minutes, and she’d love to say hi to you.” “And Jos” will be calling any time soon to...” Teresa’s words were cut off as her sister shook her head. “No... Jos” won’t be calling, Teresa,” said her sister. “Get angry at me if you wish, but that’s the reason I came... normally we don’t discuss this even with family... but you’re too closely tied into all of it. You have to know.” Teresa frowned. She knew that look in her sister’s eyes. “You’re scaring me,” she said suddenly. “What’s going on?” “I just brought Jos” in,” said Teresa’s sister. “He’s been arrested.” Teresa blinked at her sister. “ARRESTED” Why?” Teresa’s sister motioned to the couch. “Sit,” she said. “Please.” The three of them all sat down-- Juan and Teresa on the couch and Teresa’s sister on the loveseat next to it. “Jos” was one of the leaders of a Mexican drug ring that’s been operating through the Lucha Libre circuits,” said Teresa’s sister. “They acted as go-betweens laundering money and taking advantage of Mexico’s looser custom systems to help funnel the money and drugs between the United States and Colombia. The department has been conducting a major, elaborate sting operation over the past twenty years trying to shut it down and put it out of operation. It’s only now that we’ve been able to bust Jos” as one of the ring leaders and learn some of the other names involved.” Juan swallowed a lump in his throat. “My God,” he said. “And to think we brought Melina to the shows where they might have operated.” Teresa felt herself trembling. She was frightened as all heck at what she thought her sister was trying to tell them. “Who are some of these people?” she asked. “Anyone we knew?” “A few,” said her sister. “None that I can reveal to you... except one.” Teresa’s heart leaped into her throat and she was suddenly perspiring freely. “You don’t mean... it can’t be...” Tears welled in her sister’s eyes. “Yes, it was,” she said softly. “Our father was involved as well.” Juan quickly pulled Teresa into a hug, and she settled against his side, crying openly. “No... not Papa... he couldn’t... he couldn’t have...” Juan suddenly looked at his sister-in-law. “Is there any proof you’re able to let us know about?” Teresa’s sister looked like she might spit in pure disgust. The wound that all of this was opening was one of the most raw, painful wounds that could be re-opened. But the proof was there. She’d seen it. “We finally got our hands on some old microfilm from the 1980’s,” she said. “Data on informants, co-operating authorities.” She grit her teeth. “I recognized four of the faces... faces I knew very well, and you would too.” Teresa blinked through her tears. “What are you talking about?” “That gringo that made a pass at me back in 1988... that led to Father’s disgrace. And the three friends with him that beat Father up in that cantina fight.” “What about them?” asked Juan. “Federales,” said Teresa’s sister flatly. The two masked Luchadores stared at her wide-eyed. They were clearly in shock. “You mean...”” Teresa looked bleakly at her sister. But her sister nodded. “That was part of the sting operation,” she said. “They picked the fight with Father on purpose... they were going to arrest him on suspicion of being involved in the ring. They had beaten him in the cantina fight, and they were going to arrest him on charges of drug-dealing and resisting arrest.” Teresa lowered her head in horror. “But then...” she just shook her head in disbelief. “That’s right,” said Teresa’s sister. “You and I fought them, and defeated them. We sent them running out of the cantina. They were going to return with more agents to arrest us for obstruction of justice... but we’d left to bring Father to the doctor; and of course... that reporter threw the real monkey wrench into their plans.” “The newspaper article that got Pap” fired,” Teresa realized. Her sister nodded again. “Because of his getting fired, the federales could no longer tie him into the ring. The employment forms were going to be subpoenaed, but they were destroyed as a result of the termination. It set them back years in the sting operation. And of course, once Father was imprisoned, and then killed himself in our apartment... it was a dead-end file. A cold case, that wasn’t re-opened until three years ago... but the details of that are confidential. I can’t share them, even with you.” Teresa snuggled into her husband’s embrace... more closely than they’d done in a long, long time. That Mateo Ramirez... the very symbol they’d been fighting for... restoring his name, his legacy... and now to find out just what that legacy meant. Juan and Teresa Ramirez felt dirty down to their core. They’d spent so many years... trying to bring back and restore honor and integrity-- to a lie. Of course, that brought about the question-- “Where do we go from here?” asked Juan. “What do we do now?” “I don’t know,” admitted Teresa. “But I know what we don’t do. We don’t wrestle anymore... La Herencia de Herederos is finished. If this is the inheritance we’ve been trying to claim for so long... I don’t want it anymore.” Much to Teresa’s profound relief, Juan nodded in firm agreement. “Yes... to champion a cause that was built and centered around something like drug dealing... I could never look Melina in her eyes again.” The doorbell rang suddenly. Teresa snapped her head up. “That must be Maggie and Melina now,” she said. “What are you going to tell her?” her sister asked. She knew from email conversations with Juan and Teresa, who Maggie was and what her connection to the family was. “The truth,” said Juan. Teresa answered the doorbell to let Melina and Maggie in. Melina’s eyes opened wide at the sight of her aunt, and she ran straight to her, arms open. ?“T’a Alicia! “T’a Alicia!? she called. “Oh,” said Maggie politely. “Please excuse me, I didn’t know you had company.” “It’s all right,” said Teresa. “Maggie, please meet my sister...” Her sister decided to complete the introduction herself. “Alicia Ramirez. Glad to meet you.” She held one arm at an upward angle to shake hands, as she had kneeled down to give Melina a hug and a kiss. “But I try to make a habit of not revealing my name to too many people.” Maggie looked curious, but then grinned a little impishly. “A woman 007?” she asked. Alicia chuckled lightly. “DEA,” she said. “I need to do a lot of undercover. The fewer the people that know my true identity, the better.” Maggie caught the serious look in Alicia’s eyes and nodded soberly. “I have some news for you, Juan and Teresa,” she said then. “You’re slated to face Unite again in a rematch at Whiplash PPV... this time with the titles on the line.” Juan and Teresa looked at each other grimly. Their heads shook slightly but in a very decisive negative. Maggie looked puzzled. “Juan... Teresa... what is it?” she asked. “Sit down and we’ll tell you,” said Juan. ---- 30 minutes later... Maggie let out a slow breath. “What about the tag-team titles?” she asked. “Let Josh and Gray have them,” said Juan flatly. “What about the contract you signed?” asked Maggie seriously. She didn’t want to see Juan and Teresa sued and ending up in the same kind of serious situation they’d been in before Mateo was imprisoned. Juan and Teresa looked at each other grimly for several long minutes. They seemed at an impasse on what to do. Finally Alicia spoke up. “Face them at Whiplash,” she said. “Do your best, try to beat them. If you win, then after the match, surrender the titles and walk out. If you lose, then they keep the titles and you walk out. Don’t leave the league and the fans all hanging. However we feel about Father now... that’s not something you would do.” Juan and Teresa sighed in unison but then nodded in acquiescence. “All right,” said Teresa. “One more match... one more battle for what we built for ourselves. But win or lose, it’s our last.” Juan nodded in agreement, and Alicia nodded as well. Maggie took a good long look at the two Luchadores before trying her best to smile. “So what will you do afterward?” she asked. “We don’t know,” said Juan, “but we’ll find new careers. I can return to what I studied after high school and still know very well... cooking. There are some very nice Mexican restaurants here in Cochella, and I can apply for a job with some of them.” “I have a degree in political science... perhaps I can work as a legal aide for a local councilman,” said Teresa. “Tedious and thankless job... but it’s a living.” Maggie sighed wistfully. “So now you have a grudge against both your parents,” she said to the two Ramirez sisters. “Mother suspected,” said Alicia suddenly. Teresa did a double-take at her sister. “What?”” “She suspected what Father was doing,” said Alicia. “A few years ago, she sent the department some old photographs she’d been able to keep, from when we were growing up. Some people that had been over at the house, whom Mother had read about being arrested. It was those photographs that helped re-open the case... and lead to Jos??s arrest.” Teresa gasped, looking in shock at Juan, and then Maggie. “Maybe that’s why she divorced Mateo?” Maggie suggested. “It-it-it’s... it’s incredible,” Teresa stammered. “Alicia... should we...”” “I called her just recently,” said Alicia. “She’d like to see us again. She found out she’s a grandmother... she’s dying to see her granddaughter.” Teresa glanced toward Melina’s room; profoundly glad that she had sent her daughter to do her homework and watch some television while the adults talked. She looked at Alicia. “Perhaps Melina would like that,” she admitted. “Stuart and I will always be your friends,” said Maggie. “We’ll always be here for you.” She smiled suddenly at Teresa. “Maybe your knowledge would come in handy in social work. We could use the help of someone who knows how to lobby local council bodies and county officials...” Teresa laughed. “From a wrestler to a politician,” she said with a smirk. “Who’d have thought it?” All four of them laughed. “Stranger things have happened,” said Alicia. “Time to build your own legacy now... the way I built mine.” Juan and Teresa looked at each other and nodded. “I like the sound of that,” said Juan. “Our OWN legacy... something that’s just us.”
