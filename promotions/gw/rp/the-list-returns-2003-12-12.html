The list returns

12 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/12/03 Once upon a time, Brick used to keep a list of names. These were usually Global Wrestling superstars who had deigned to anger Brick, either with a sneak attack or costing him a match. Past GW stars who have earned the honor of making Brick’s list include members of the Nevermore, Brian Simon, Kamikaze Jones and Mr. UK. He even went so far as to put Gunner Addison on his list during a time when, as Brick might put it, “weren’t exactly close”. And if someone made this list, “honored” was the last feeling they should experience. In fact, “fearful for one’s life” should have Brick did not suffer insults and assaults gladly. He could be considered a personification of the old saying “Fuck with the bull, get the horns”. But the list Brick found himself putting together wasn’t of stars who had angered him recently. At least, not all of them were. Rumor had circulated that a former Global Wrestling superstar was going to make a return to GW and that said person had their sights set on Brick. Brick took a pen and began to check names off. The first name mentioned in the rumor section was that of Derek Draven. Extremely bloody and bitter was the feud between these two men. It started after an International title match a week after the 2002 High Octane PPV. Draven had won the match and the title by delivering his finishing move, the Dravenizer, to Brick onto the title belt. After the match, he did it again. About two weeks later, Brick and Gunner Addison were to face Draven and Lisha in an intergender tag match. The match ended in a no-contest with Draven hanging Brick from the rafters. This led to Clash of the Titans 2002 when Brick faced Draven in a no-holds-barred match. He exacted revenge by taking Draven to the top of the Globaltron and throwing him down into a dumpster filled with light bulbs and then dragging his bloodied and broken body to the ring for the three-count. Months later, Draven returned to enact his own revenge by kidnapping, torturing and then nearly killing Brick’s childhood friend Aaron Davis. Brick paid him back at High Noon. There was a short lived return from Draven, but the two had yet to truly settle the score. If it was Deacon Holmes who was after him, it wouldn’t surprise him. Holmes antagonized Brick from day one, often insulting him in public, goading him into clashes, and launching sneak attacks on him. Brick often retaliated in the same manner. After one instance where Holmes had pulled a particularly vicious prank on him, Brick evened the score by screwing him out of the two titles he held at the time. They never truly settled their differences with one another. Dane Rennier was another possible candidate. Brick and Rennier have definitely had heat with. Near the end of 2000 and in the beginning of 2001, Brick and his partner James Primus feuded with Renner and his partner Maverick Reed over the now-defunct Extreme Tag titles. Their first match climaxed with Rennier slamming Brick through some ice and leaving him in an icy river, either to drown or freeze to death. Their next match, Brick took the psychological advantage by kidnapping Rennier’s then-girlfriend Christina McCoy and handcuffing her naked to a shower head in order to get under Rennier’s skin. Brick won their final match. Top Dawg was one of Brick’s former partners and, while not a founding member of Ruckus Inc., he was an integral part of it. When James Primus had to leave, Dawg replaced him as co-Extreme Tag champion. They had been more than partners. They were like brothers. Of course, if it was Dawg, it wouldn’t be the first time someone turned their back on brick. Brick remembered none too fondly his days in the indy circuit when he was part of a stable known as the Rough Ryders. Brick often remembered the bloody feud he had in Terminator in the summer of 2001. The seeds were actually planted in the fall of 2000, when after a match the two had, Terminator powerbombed him through a table. The feud officially started in June of 2001 when Brick stopped Terminator from powerbombing his then-estranged and pregnant wife. Since then, the two engaged in a war of sneak attacks. They had also faced each other in some of the most brutal matches in GW history. Jonathan Davis alias the Scarecrow was one of the sickest, most depraved individuals to grace a GW ring. In January of 2001, he had left Brick for dead after bludgeoning him from behind with a crowbar. Brick was in a coma for days. When he awoke, he was determined to get even, which he eventually did. His animosity with Davis led to animosity with Salaok and Quartz. His animosity with Salaok was perhaps the greatest. It was Salaok who originally ordered the kidnapping and torture of Aaron Davis. When Salaok assumed co-ownership of GW, one of the superstars he toyed with the most was Brick. He instituted a clause where, if Brick were to lose a match, he would be indefinitely suspended until such time as Salaok saw fit to reinstate him. For weeks afterwards, they continued to attack one another. Their bloody rivalry culminated in a Hell in a Cell match at Wrestlecade 3. So many people, Brick thought to himself. But which one is it? One thing was certain. Whoever had Brick in his crosshairs was sure to be in Brick’s.
