The Long Road of Championism (Ch.2)

29 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-29-04 Prologue. As depressed as one could get over a lifetime, Jesse had become that and more in less than a day. He’d not slept, nor eaten, or even moved since he heard the comments made from the four boys outside the gym he’d been signing autographs at. Sure Jesse knew that people would say things behind his back, some of these people who’d never even talked to him before, but these were kids who’d just given Jesse praise for his talent and claimed he would win the match. Lies like those go deep, but how deep? Could they get him to think deep enough to get him wondering of his career choice, or maybe his childhood? It wasn’t a positive thing on neither an athlete nor a normal human being to have their emotions played with like a yo-yo. People who did such put others at risk for it was those type of games that caused teenagers of today’s youth to go into their schools and shoot their fellow peers and teachers. However this was a grown man, and it’d be awkward to think he couldn’t deal with some punk kids words after all he’s been through. Just look at the history of Jesse Jamester, he’d survived some of the worst matches in the world. Some of those being Japanese death matches, Taipei death matches, scaffold matches, cage matches, and matches that were so sickening that they were rated R for content in the United States. Though the question still remained, what had dug so deep into Jesse that it caused him to go paralyzed in the intellect? Story Begins. In Jesse’s past many things were seen as disturbing or unusual compared to what he now had as a life. Now that past would be confronted in the worst of ways and Jesse was reliving them in his mind. Long ago, way back when Jesse was thirteen he had been living with his foster parents. Those being his stepmother and stepfather who he’d been with near four years since his parents untimely deaths or disappearances, neither subject he having any knowledge of. One night a world of anguish would suppress Jesse into dust, and that would be the explanation we looked for in this mood of recession. Reliving the Past It was another normal night, their sat Jesse’s stepfather in the living room watching the Sunday night fight. He was all set up, his six pack to the right, an ashtray on his left and his cigarettes in his shirt pocket. Puffing away his life with each cigarette he didn’t have a care in the world as he argued with the Television over the participants fighting style. It wasn’t anything special of a match, but the old man had wanted to watch it. Sitting in his chair he was having a good time, and beside him on the couch was his son Jesse. Without a doubt he was interested in the match as his eyes concentrated on the screen for the brutality was too entertaining to give up just to go to bed. Yet his mother came out and she was livid, it was near ten o’clock at night and he was still awake. In a tone of fatigue and irritation she told Jesse to get to bed. He obeyed, knowing it was late and he had school the next day. As he closed his bedroom door he laid down and tried to sleep but the quarrelling from the living room was too loud and so he sat up and leaned against his bed post listening to it. ’You have work tomorrow honey, why don’t you get some sleep?’ asked his mother politely. ’I’ll get to it when this match is over,’ he says not really paying attention to her and just using his normal phrase for a response. As she goes through the kitchen to go back to bed her eyes stumble upon the sink full of dirty dishes. Already tired and upset over her husband letting Jesse stay up late she curses under her breathe as she rinses off the dirty dishes. Slamming the cupboards she does with a slap of the wrist just to annoy her lazy husband who did nothing all night. ’HEY! Stop that, I’m watching the match!’ he barked from his beloved chair. Her eyes flared towards the echo of his voice. She couldn’t see him but knew he was there for his hoarse voice and stench of what he called breath was unbearable to not notice. She didn’t obey though; she kept on slamming the cupboards one by one until he finally rose from his feet to see what she was doing. ’I thought I said STOP THAT WOMAN!’ shouted Jesse’s father as he trotted into the kitchen with feet of concrete, ’I told you to stop it, I paid thirty dollars for that boxing match and I’m damn well going to see it without you out here slamming my cupboards!’ She stared at him before uttering words through the slit she had open between her upper and bottom lips, ’YOU’RE CUPBOARDS!? I clean this house! I cook! I do everything! Yet you’re lazy no good for nothing self can sit here and tell me this is your house?’ ’Yeah because I pay the god damn mortgage and make sure you have money to spend when you buy groceries! Who the hell pays one hundred and fifty dollars on groceries, huh?’ muttered the father as he stepped up to her with his beer in his right hand and his other hand tensed up because of the arguing as it gripped the cupboard she was about to slam. ’I don’t see you arguing over what I cook you! It isn’t like you don’t eat it! If you didn’t I sure wouldn’t buy it for you, because you don’t do anything for me to deserve a meal. All you do is drink, eat, sleep, and repeat it all over again! Ha, father my ass!’ she retorted gritting her teeth before slamming the cupboard shut crushing his right hand and causing him to spill his beer. As she smiles in comfort knowing he deserved it he holds his hand close to his chest shaking it to ease the pain before back handing her with the hand that held the beer. ’What are you smiling about now huh?!’ he yelled in her face as tears began to swell at the corner slits of her eyes. He knew he’d hurt her and he loved the power it brought him. Bringing his hand back again he did it once more but this time she moved and he hit her under the jaw creating a red mark. Backing away she went down the hallway and then ran into her bedroom. Jesse’s father followed with thuds for steps, each one louder as it came closer to his door and then once it passed it began to fade a bit, but still was heard. ’Get the hell out of there! That’s MY room! This is MY house! I want you out!’ he grunted as he hit the door with his fist as if wanting to break it down but knowing that’d cause damage he didn’t want to fix. Inside she wimpered and moaned in pain as she looked into the mirror, her nose had begun to bleed, and her lip was fat from the first backhand he delivered. Wiping the tears away with a hankie she had in her top drawer she went to the phone to call the police. Hearing the phone dial Jesse’s father became enraged, but was also worried as he knew she would call. ’Don’t do this! You don’t want to bring them into this now do you? I would have you out of this house so quick you wouldn’t know what hit you, you hear me!’ he bellowed in worry but with a tone that scared her stiff, forcing her to put the phone down. Crying even more she made her way to the door and opened it, only to be hurled down onto the bed, ’YOU EVER, EVER, at any time call those police, so help me god I will do something I’ve never done in my life. You hear me?’ She couldn’t answer, for the fear in her eyes didn’t allow her to move. She was a one legged man without a crutch, no matter where she got up she’d end up falling back down. Nodding her head that she understood, he left her alone and walked out of the room. ’I’m going out…’ was all he said before slamming the front door shut and leaving the house. The truck revved up minutes later and he roared out of the driveway in a frenzy, drunk, but not drunk enough to not know how to drive. Rolling off her bed, Jesse’s mother was terrified still, even after he’d left she was frightened of what happened. Jesse’s door opened and he came tip-toeing out to see if it was all clear. Pacing as he walked to his mothers room he took in the images of the hallway. The door being a bit beat up where his father had hit it with his fist, and the floor littered by the mud his father had on his shoes, it looked quite horrendous. Finally reaching the room Jesse stared at his mother on the floor in a ball, her arms wrapped around her knees as she rocked herself back and forth while crying. Jesse walked past her and grabbed the phone, picking it up and dialing 9-1-1 but she stopped him two-thirds the way through. ’Don’t… you… do… that… I don’t… want… hi-m… to have rea-son… to hit me more,’ she murmured, hiccupping and losing breathe in between some of the words. Jesse sat down next to her on the floor cradling her with a hug as she was too fragile to get up and move without falling down at the time. Seizing the hankie he wiped her tears away, ’don’t worry mom, he’s gone now… don’t worry.’ That alone was a cure for her, knowing her abusive husband had left the house, but still her body was weak and she was tired. It was only eleven o’clock at night and the house was a wreck. ’Jesse… dear… don-‘t ever… (sniffle) tell any-one… about what- happened… here tonight, okay?’ she managed to peep through her sniffles and weeps. Jesse nodded, he knew it was wrong, he knew how it’d all been so very sick to hear, and though he wanted to he cared for his mothers wishes. The worst thing he could do was tell the truth he thought, but the truth was what was right, and so he was bewildered by it all. AlThe only thing that made sense was that he had to keep quite, and so he promised to do so. Holding her he sat their all night until she fell asleep. Back to Reality Deep in the basement of the house where no lights but the sunlight that creaked through the panel boards above appeared, sat the mythical man known as Jesse Jamester. His depression was now uncovered, his step-father was an abusive man, his mother was beaten a lot, and he’d been the ventriloquist puppet his father used to get away with it all when in trouble. The guilt in his past had lead him down many roads and when his heart had been chipped away a bit too many times, it began to deteriorate at a rapid pace as it did the day before when he heard the four kids talk about him behind his back. Lying had been a way he had to accept when young, it kept things in order and was what Jesse had to do if he wanted to live in a house with two parents. As much as he despised it he did what his mother told him, but once he left his parents house he went away from that life. Lying was now an objective in Jesse’s mind that would never take place again. He wanted nothing to do with that path, and so buried it with his memories. Except, those memories now were coming back to haunt him. The kids were just a piece of it, but now he was starting to see everything dreadful in his life come back through memories he recently took part in. Sitting in his rocking chair, he rocked back and forth gazing at the wall, not sure of what to do, nor caring to try and doing anything. He just sat their with a puzzled look on his face that gave no clue what he was thinking about. Would the demons Jesse had stored away for so long begin to escape his body and let him get on with his wrestling? Or would they eat away at his insides until he no longer seemed human on either the inside or outside? A test of his darkest weakness was about to be brought out in him and he had nothing to answer it with besides fear… The end may be nearer than many think… … …
