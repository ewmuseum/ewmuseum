Aftermath, Afterward and Afterthought

1 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/1/05 and 9/2/05 Aftermath Friday, September 1st Quick first aid allowed them to patch up the deep scratches Jake had inflicted on himself. A few quick phone calls downstairs, and the seamstress hired in case of any problems with clothes was upstairs sewing the buttons back onto his shirt. While she worked in the other room, they faced the problem at hand. “What happened?” Veston began. “Xavier died, for a moment he was dead.” Jake was still breathing heavily. Even though the wounds were tended to, he felt as though something inside him had tried to escape through the physical tears in his flesh. Robert stepped over to Jake, peering deeply into his eyes. He wanted to see what was going on in there as he inquired about what happened, “How do you know?” “I’I could feel it.” Jake tried to put words around what he had experienced. “Somehow, we’re connected. Me, him and Sid are all connected at some level. When he died, it was as if part of me was dying too.” “This isn’t good.” Robert turned away from them. He walked around the table to the windows. He stared out the window to the lights of the city beyond. He never liked the city at night. “Sid knows, and he’ll make his move now.” “Do you know where your family is Jake?” Veston knew what was at stake just as much as the rest of them. Jake nodded as he tried to slow his breathing, “Louisiana. I know that much.” “You can bet Sid knows exactly where they are. His hunting instincts for his quest are finely honed. He will send men to take Xavier, or he may even make an appearance himself.” Robert didn’t think they were ready for this. They needed the final manuscript to unravel the secret of defeating Sid. “No, he’ll stay out of this. Ever since what happened in New Orleans last time, he’s afraid to face me alone. He expected it to be a cakewalk, but he felt the power to destroy him running through my body. He’ll send that little prick who hangs on him like a damned groupie.” Jake stood up, feeling woozy still he sat back down quickly. The door to the conference room opened, and Stephen re-joined them. He had a hard set to his face, “I contacted Hawk to let him know. He’s meeting me tonight after the event.” “We need to decide what to do.” Robert turned back to the men in the room. Veston threw his hands up, “It’s not that hard, we go get them.” “We know where they are?” Stephen was lost. “Louisiana.” Veston replied. Seeing the confusion still on Stephen’s face he continued. “Jake knows they’re in Louisiana. He doesn’t know where, but he knows that’s where they were when Xavier died.” “Makes sense. With everything that’s hit the state they would be forced out into the open, on the run with hundreds of thousands of refugees.” Stephen sat down in the plush, leather chair. Robert kept moving his eyes back and forth as though he were reviewing documents in his head. He seemed the most oft-put by the situation, even more so than Jake was at the moment. “Saving your family might not be the smartest move.” “What?” Jake’s eyes snapped up. He locked them onto Robert’s, his fury evident. “Look, he will send everything he has. If he kills Xavier he becomes more powerful, but I think he still can’t win if we find the last text. Whatever the final key is to banishing him forever is in the book. While he’s got his attention on Louisiana we should press forward to find the book.” Robert rationalized. His tone said it all; he actually believed what he was saying. This was no rushed idea; this was his revelation pure and simple. “What in the Hell is wrong with you?” The voice was not from Jake, who looked crushed by the suggestion. Stephen had locked his own powerful gaze on the good doctor. “That family will not be abandoned. If we do that, then we’re no better than Sid is. And for me, I can’t betray Pitt like that. He’s my friend, and I’ll go to Hell and back for my friends.” “Are you sure?” Robert was agitated, “Because we’re talking about going to Hell if this doesn’t work!” Veston tried to be the calm voice of reason, “Robert, we have to save them.” “No, we choose what to do. We can get the book without any problems from his little helpers.” Robert and Veston began moving closer to each other. “I was with Pitt when he almost killed me for wanting to abandon his brother Robert. I learned my lesson. We need to get these people back into protection.” Veston was hoping his old friend would realize the truth. Robert gave a half smile followed by a single laugh, like a huffing laugh of scorn. “We can’t let your fear of another ass kicking drive the decision Veston. We’re supposed to be here to protect the world, not your pride.” “You’re half right Robert.” Jake pushed himself out of his chair slowly. He walked over, intervening himself between the two men. Jake stared down at Robert with brutal eyes. “This isn’t about pride. This is about doing the right thing, and do you think we would have any backing from anywhere if we abandoned the people we loved” No greater love has a man than this, he would lay down his life for his friend. Will you abandon that because of your pride? Your fear?” Jake walked past Robert to the windows. He looked out at the night sky, at the city he loved and called home. He wanted to retire to the city one day, not to the country like so many others did. “No. We won’t do that Robert. We need to find them. I need to find Xavier, and Pitt, and Bella, and Alexandria. I won’t lose my soul by abandoning them to Sid.” “I’ll find them.” They all turned to the end of the table. Sensational Stephen Reed, through steely eyes peering out of his emaciated face looked at the men. They could feel the power of his resolve like a sudden force pulsing out into the room, “Jake, you can’t go find them. If you and Xavier are in the same place in the open we risk too much. Hawk and I will go to Louisiana to find them. We’ll take some relief supplies and blend into the relief effort. He’s running the business now, and I have my businesses. We’ll take supplies down and just start looking.” “Do you just expect me to sit on the sidelines?” Jake was incredulous. “Yes.” Stephen leaned back in the chair, “After that speech you should know better. We can’t lose you. You’re worried about them, and we’re worried about you. We’ll bring them back here, back to you.” There was a knock on the conference room door. Jake saw the slight seamstress standing there nervously. He motioned her in. She came in holding his shirt, “I’ve got all your buttons back on Mr. Hix.” “Thank you Debra.” Jake smiled weakly as he took the shirt. Debra looked around the room nervously. There was a lot of tension, and she didn’t like the feeling of being surrounded by these men who were arguing over something. “If you need anything else let me know.” “I will.” Jake nodded at her as he slipped his arms into his shirt. “Have a nice evening Debra.” She took her leave’quickly. “Jake, we need to keep you safe.” Veston agreed with Stephen. “Fine, then you and Robert go to find this last book. Hawk and Stephen will find my family, and I’ll wait for the moment.” Jake was buttoning up the shirt and tucking it in. “For now, I’ll do that. If this goes south, I won’t stay on the sidelines anymore.” “Fair enough.” Stephen stood up, “I think we’ve tarried here too long. People will begin to wonder.” Jake finished getting dressed again. He grabbed his jacket, “How true. Gentlemen, keep me updated on what’s happening. We’ll see each other soon.” Stephen and Jake walked out of the office and down the hall. Veston remained with his friend, who looked annoyed as he gnashed his teeth. “What is it?” Robert let go of his tension long enough to speak, “We’re making a mistake.” “You’re the only one who thinks so.” Veston pointed out, trying to make Robert see he was becoming a little fatalistic. Robert walked away, “That’s what worries me.” Afterwards Friday, September 2nd The night had been a smashing success. The flash of cameras, the bidding out of control and in the end they would be sending plenty of money to the victims. Everyone had a wonderful time, but the pain inside of his chest wall was continual reminder of what was at stake. He had dropped Veronica off at her palatial estate before returning to his own downtown home. The Big Dawg had noticed several sets of eyes keeping close watch on him as he entered his house. He had a couch on the bottom floor, and he fell forward onto it exhausted by the spiritual battle he had been through. Jake’s eyes opened and closed slowly as he slowly slipped off into sleep. The cloaking darkness of the night was still out as he pushed himself up off the couch. He smacked his mouth open and closed, tasting something foul as he cleared the film from his tongue. Jake stood up slowly, feeling some lingering effects in his chest and back from the evening’s events. He found himself parched, and so he stumbled over to the stairs. Jake slowly climbed up to the third floor where his kitchen and bar were. He decided some food might help as well, so he ducked into his kitchen. Two slices of whole grain wheat toast went into the toaster as he pulled a bottle of water out from the refrigerator. He took a long drink of the cool water, washing away the vile taste. He heard the toaster pop up, and he grabbed both pieces from the slots. Something sent a chill up his spine. He turned around, but there was no one there. He kept looking around, trying to find what he had sensed. He finally shook the feeling away as he set his food and drink down on the counter. He unbuttoned his shirt down to his naval and pulled his tuxedo jacket off. He tossed the jacket aside, and got his snack as he moved into the bar. Jake took a bite of the toast, but he almost wretched. The toast was completely dried out, and didn’t even have the taste of toast. He dropped the toast as he spit the remains out of his mouth and ran his finger across his tongue to remove the flavor. When it didn’t go away he took another drink of water. The liquid was thick and salty, with a familiar tinny taste touching every taste bug in his mouth. He spit the liquid out and heard a voice from behind him speak. “And the food will be as ash in your mouth, and you will drink the blood of the land.” The familiar voice said. Jake dropped the water on the floor. In the moonlight streaming in from the open windows, he saw the dark scarlet liquid pour out around his feet. He turned slowly as his stomach twisted with the knowledge that he recognized the voice speaking to him. It couldn’t be him though’even with everything he had seen it could not be him.. As he turned, his hands were shaking as he gazed up at the monster in the shadows. Few men truly towered over him, but this monster was bigger and taller. He could still hear the dripping water, but then he noticed there was something falling down between the beast’s legs and hitting his wooden floor. The dark figure took slow steps forward, dragging it’s body like a burden as the moonlight began climbing up to reveal him. Cracked and decrepit skin looked like it had been left to ruin in the elements. Deep chasms gave view down to sinew; atrophied muscles and bones already bleached white. Leaking from each wound was the same black blood that kept dripping, over and over. Climbing higher, he could still see where the chest had been ruptured open to expose the man’s insides like a sadistic dissection. The face revealed the damage more than anything else. The face was permanently set in a scream of pain and suffering looking even worse as the side of his face was missing down to the jawbone. Black were set on him as his arm raised slowly. More black blood poured out of the hole in his wrist where a spike had been driven through into a crossbeam. Pointing at Jake, he spoke with a mournful voice that seemed far, far away. “You failed us Jake’you failed us all?” Jake began backing up. He slipped in the blood and fell back onto the floor. He could feel the blood being soaked up by his tuxedo pants as John Hix towered over him. The meaty hand reached down, grabbing him around the throat with power he had never felt. John lifted him off the floor and pushed his body straight into the air. Frantically, he tried to pull the huge hand away, but the monster was too strong. He was thrown across the room, slamming into the floor hard. He rolled over and over until he slammed into the cinderblock wall. “Poor Jake, you never learn do you.” The voice was smooth and calm. “All this time, and you still somehow think you can overcome the ravages of fate. The thread has already been cut, and this time you can’t win.” Jake looked over at his couch. Out of the range of the moonlight there was another hulking figure. He couldn’t make out whom he was, but he knew the voice. “You come to my house Sid?” Jake slowly got himself back up. “You’re dumb enough to come at me here?” “Why wouldn’t I?” The man took a sip of something, and then extended his hand into the moonlight, “You always have the best scotch after all. I was thirsty.” Jake rolled his shoulder a little, feeling like it was almost out of socket. He pulled his arm across his chest by the elbow as he eyes the dark corner, “Then that’s your last drink Sid. I won’t let this continue.” “Oh, I forgot the naivety of youth. You and your little friends have been running around under the assumption there’s actually something you can do to stop me. You forgot that what I am, what I will do, was foretold long ago. The fate of your family is sealed along with the fate of all God’s children. I have come to devour the world.” The man swirled his scotch around in his tumbler. “There’s nothing you can do to stop that. The sad part is you still don’t understand what’s happening. You’re already defeated, and you don’t even know it.” Jake took a step forward, but the monster that had once been his brother John steeped forward blocking his path. Jake eyed the beast wearily as he clenched his hands into fists. “Do you like him?” The man in the corner laughed, a strange laugh that seemed wholly inappropriate to the situation. “I created him to serve my purposes after his painful end. He’s my puppet, and he does exactly what I tell him to. No more soul left for anyone to claim, he’s just mine.” “I think I’m going to do this to the rest of the family too. I really need to have a few servants lying around here and there forever, and you know how hard it is to find good help. You’ll all die screaming, begging me to end it all and let you kneel down before me Jake. Isn’t that wonderful?” Jake was finished. He charged forward at the monster before him. He threw a punch straight ahead, connecting right in the beast’s face. The monster fell back, and Jake began stomping his foot down onto his face until there was nothing left except a black, broken puddle of flesh. The laughter continued from the corner. Jake ran over to where the man still had his arm outstretched into the moonlight, as though offering him a drink. Jake pulled him up by the arm, ready to slam his fist into him when everything stopped. He was looking into his own face, the eyes so familiar yet so cold. He fell stumbled back, strange incomprehensible mutterings escaping his lips as he couldn’t understand what was happening here. Jake tripped again, and fell down. He rolled over, and pushed up off the bodies. He had hit the monster’but the bodies on the floor were his family. He was looking at their twisted, violated bodies arranged in some macabre spectacle to ruin his mind. Jake heard his own voice, “Yes, you can protect them. Look how good a job you’ve done.” The monster that was his brother John spoke to him from the floor, “You failed us Jake, you failed us all.” Jake ripped open his shirt again as the burning inside his body returned. He clawed into his own chest, digging out strips of flesh with his nails as he screamed into the night. He fell off the couch as he woke up grabbing at his chest. There wasn’t any blood, or anything else damaged except the bandages from the night before. He stood up and looked around the entryway, making sure as the early morning sun streamed into the room from the high windows. “Just’just a dream.” He said aloud to himself. The dream would play over and over in his mind for days on end. Afterthought Friday, September 2nd Inside the house all the lights were turned off now. The only light came from the harsh sunlight streaming through the open windows. The large home built right in the downtown warehouse district long converted to an arts district was empty except for the one man. Two bottle of premium scotch later the tantrum had ended. The wrestling dummy, long used for practice, was now in pieces on the ground with the interior sand and stuffing strewn about carelessly. Bookcases that had contained an impressive collection of wrestling and mixed martial arts matches were tossed in pieces after a violent attack by a man drunk with liquor, sorrow and rage. The DVDs were all over the floor, some broken but many more simply tossed in all directions during the blind rage. Trophies, collected over the course of a lifetime had been shattered against the cinderblock walls before littering the floor like gold confetti. Left in ruins all around him, the perpetrator of the attack was laid out on the cold concrete floor with the bottle still in his hand as his snores echoed through the training room. The attack was driven when Jake couldn’t tell the difference between his fears and the real world. He kept running away from the dream, but when he couldn’t hide anymore he tried to drown his memories in the scotch. He hadn’t bothered with the Macallan then, instead emptying bottle of Chivas Regal 18 year old scotch for consumption. In the end, he had passed into the sweet bliss of unconsciousness after destroying his training room. The piercing ring of the phone stirred him enough to stop the snoring, and the continued ringing finally got him to open his eyes. Jake raised his hand to rub his eyes, instead hitting himself with the glass bottle. He threw it aside, the breaking glass bottle adding more mess to be cleaned. Jake rose up to a sitting position, a splitting headache overcoming him as he almost fell back down. He pulled himself up, and slowly stumbled through the debris to the wall. He moved down, able to catch the phone before the ringing stopped. “WHAT!”!” Jake was in no mood to hear from anyone at the moment. He listened to the other end of the conversation. His face twisted into confusion, “What the Hell is that?” He listened impatiently again, “You think I should?” Jake rubbed his free hand down his face trying to revive himself and find a bit of clarity. “I get to fight against people, right” Good, then book it.” Jake hung the phone up and staggered away. He needed a shower’badly’and he was wondering what the Infinite Gauntlet was as he carried himself to the showers.
