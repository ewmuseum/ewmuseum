On the Radio...

19 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/19/2005 Someone found a letter you wrote me, on the radio And they told the world just how you felt It must have fallen out of a hole in your old brown overcoat They never said your name But I knew just who they meant. ESPN 103.3 FM Radio Studio February 19, 2005 1100 Hours 'His name was Mateo Ramirez,' said Teresa, smiling softly as she reminisced. 'Oh, Pap?... I miss him.' Dallas radio host Dan Patrick nodded slowly, taking in everything and waiting for the right time to ask the next question. La Herencia de Herederos had gained some attention in the wrestling world with their win over the Connection. Juan and Teresa were the 'new kids on the block,' after all; and a win over seasoned vets Joshua Curtis and Zack Johnson was no small feat, even if it was the opening bout of the Evolution Card. 'Tell us about your Dad, Teresa... I can see you were very close to him.' 'Very close,' said Teresa, nodding for emphasis. 'I have a lot of fond memories from when I was growing up in Mexico with my parents and my sister... the bicycle rides in the parks, going to the corridas...' Dan's brow furrowed. 'Corridas... bullfights?' 'We understand the sentiment towards bullfighting here in America,' said Juan quickly. 'With all due respect towards animal-rights movements such as PETA, in our home country of Mexico, bullfighting is a great tradition with a long history. Our country is quite different from yours. American culture is in constant flux, changing very rapidly and sometimes drastically in short periods of time. In what you call the 'Third World,' cultural traditions are far more long-lasting. We don't think of it as backward... we see it as where we are, where we should be.' Of course, getting into a debate over cultural differences, and whether the Third World was lagging behind in anything, was not was going to be much good for the ratings... the fans wanted to know about the hot new tag-team on the scene, and what made them tick. 'So, your father was a Lucha Libre champion,' said Dan, getting back to the prima facie subject by probing into the history of Teresa's father. Teresa had talked about it briefly on the team's debut, and it was as good a place as any for the fans to be able to bond with the husband-and-wife Luchadores. 'His name was El Zorro Azul... which means Blue Fox in English,' said Teresa. 'Pap? was a very handsome man, and even though Luchadores are never seen without their masks, as per Lucha Libre tradition, Pap? was very charismatic even with much of his face covered. His smile could melt any woman's heart, even though his own heart belonged to only one woman...' Teresa's voice took on a bit of a darker tone here suddenly, which wasn't lost on Dan. 'Your mother,' Dan presumed. 'You don't sound very fond of her.' Teresa clasped Juan's hand and held it tightly before continuing. 'I was, once,' she said carefully. 'I have many fond memories of family outings growing up. Unfortunately, the situation with four drunken gringos from America changed all that.' 'You mentioned your father was disgraced,' said Dan. 'He was disgraced in your mother's eyes too?' Teresa pursed her lips carefully. She and her sister never thought anything good about Lupe's divorcing Mateo and marrying the rich elderly snob they were forced to call stepfather until moving out of his mansion... but it was always hard for them to condemn why. To Teresa and her sister, the reasons were noble, but the path she took to reach her goal was unforgivable. 'After the cantina fight, Pap? was fired from Lucha Libre,' Teresa began. 'The whole family became social pariahs... he was unfairly arrested and put in prison for a year. The day he was released and I took him into my home, he committed suicide.' She left out what her mother had done; too bitter over it to speak about it now, and knowing that the way she had worded the story thus far would draw attention away from that part. Dan took the bait... deliberately. He suspected there was more to the story than Juan and Teresa were telling; but again, right now, ratings for the show were all-important. If the fans tuned out because they were tired of listening to this Mexican couple... the proverbial censored stuff would fall from the fan onto HIS head... and that, he wasn't going to let happen. Radio show hosts were a dime a dozen and audiences were fickle; especially sports audiences... you could host a thousand shows and be thought of as a failure if ONE show-- the last one you did-- was objectionable. Now, now Don't it kinda strike you sad when you hear our song Things are not the same since we broke up last June The only thing that I wanna hear is that you love me still And that you think you'll be coming home real soon Whoa, oh, yeah, yeah And it made me feel proud when I heard you say You couldn't find the words to say it yourself 'So from a popular, beloved wrestling champion... your father died nearly broke, hated by all the fans who once loved him... and an ex-convict to boot,' said Dan.' Teresa and Juan gnashed their teeth thinking about the past. 'He died with regrets, and loneliness, and an empty heart,' said Juan in slow, almost hissed tones of bitterness at how thorough Mateo's disgrace had been... that there were only three people attending his funeral (until Lupe showed up uninvited)... and of course, that fateful Noticias article ten years ago that led to their pledge and mission. 'Worse, he died nearly forgotten. After he was sent to prison, he became nearly forgotten by Lucha Libre... everyone he'd had to deal with since he'd been fired. His daughters were finally able to obtain employment... and visit the cantina where the brawl happened. I met Teresa there; at the cantina... we were married shortly after Mateo's death. After our daughter was born, we were listening to a news report... and the sports anchor insulted Mateo's name and the family name again. That was all we could handle. Teresa and I vowed to restore Mateo's good name, and the legacy of his surname... Ramirez.' 'Interesting coincidence that your surname is also Ramirez,' said Dan. 'Maybe it's a common name in Mexico, but the fact you married someone with that same last name, sounds like a twist of fate or fortune. Did it give you a personal stake in your wife's mission?' Juan seemed to consider the question before nodding. 'Perhaps it did,' he admitted. He tapped a medium-sized chin covered with his ever-present 5-o'clock shadow that ran up both sides of his jawline and cheeks. 'But more than that, my ability to see Teresa as an every-day person, and not the family of a former media celebrity turned outcast, made me realize... if it could happen to Mateo, it can happen to everyone. Mateo was in that cantina not as El Zorro Azul, but as a loving father taking his daughters out to dinner after a successful show, where he'd just recaptured the Mexican All-Star Light-Heavyweight Championship. One moment, Teresa was calling her mother to have her meet them at the cantina... the next she was returning to the table to find her father encircled by four drunkards taking turns distracting him while another would sneak up from behind and punch him in the kidneys or bust a chair over his head and back... because one of them insulted, and made a lewd pass at, Teresa's sister. And THEY were the ones disgraced over it... Mateo and his family.' And now in my heart I know I can say what I really feel 'Cause they said it really loud They said it on the air On the radio Whoa, oh, oh On the radio Whoa, oh, oh On the radio Whoa, oh, oh On the radio Tears formed in Teresa's eyes as she struggled for the words. 'So many people who claimed to love him... and turn their backs on him,' she said bitterly. 'He was my Pap?... I loved him for who he was; the family man he'd been, taking care of me, my sister, my mother... the loving husband and father that meant nothing to a people that turned their backs on him. Family still reigns supreme in Mexico, Se’or Patrick. My father's family has not forgotten what so many people CLAIMING to be an extended second family did to him. We will restore his honor, and the family's honor... no matter how long it takes.' 'Well, you're off to a good start,' said Dan. 'Your win over the connection has turned a few heads. How long did you and Juan train to start this crusade?' 'A long time,' said Teresa. 'Thank goodness for our trainer and mentor, Jos? Sanchez. He took Juan and I under his wing and taught us all he knew about wrestling and Lucha Libre.' 'Tell us more about the team name,' said Dan. 'La Herencia de Herederos. You said it means Heir's Inheritance. Tell us about that.' Teresa lifted her chin up. 'Pap?'s family... his HEIRS, in the wake of his suicide... are setting out to claim their rightful INHERITANCE... the honor and love that Pap? had before his fall from grace. It's not just for Pap?... but for us as well. His legacy as El Zorro Azul, and his legacy as a loving family man... his future as both... taken away from not only him, but his family as well. We will reclaim what was his... what was OURS... the Heir's rightful Inheritance.' 'You said there's no English equivalent of the title you use in the ring,' said Dan. 'But from what I understand, Do’a is a kind of Spanish title... almost like minor nobility? Do you see yourself as a noblewoman because of your crusade?' Teresa smiled and shook her head. 'Not nobility, Se’or; you're mistaken. That's why I said there's no English word for it. The closest term in English is how a person might address an audience as, 'Ladies and Gentlemen.' It was a title, but not for nobles. I consider myself very much a lady, even though I was treated like dirt for some time in Mexico City.' Now, If you think that love isn't found on the radio Then tune right in you may find the love you lost 'Cause now I'm sittin here with the man I sent away long ago 'What about your latest match?' asked Dan, hoping to get some feedback on their next opponents.' 'We're still the newcomers in GW,' said Juan, 'so we're not that familiar with everyone currently on the roster. We understand that Rayne and Lei are also pretty new in the fed, looking to make their name and carve their niche. We can relate to this because that's exactly what Teresa and I are trying to do. Of course, there can only be one winner... one of us has to advance at the expense of the other. We respect Ethan Rayne and Choi Yen Lei... but since this is a question of who continues their climb up the ladder, pushing forward onto their goals; they or us... I vote for us.' 'As do I,' said Teresa, touching the side of a closed fist to the side of Juan's closed fist. 'We'll see whether or not the fans agree with us when we meet Rayne and Lei in the ring this coming Monday.' Dan nodded. 'I wish you the best of luck, then... how do you find your stay in America so far?' 'It's big; very big,' said Teresa. 'People here live large, and fast. But it's exciting. Dallas has been a hospitable host city.' 'Then thank you both for coming,' said Dan, shaking hands with both Ramirez spouses. 'We'll all be watching when you meet Rayne and Lei in the ring. Fans, you've been listening to the Dan Patrick show on station ESPN, 103.3 FM. Remember that GW Evolution is Monday night at the GW arena right here in Dallas.'
