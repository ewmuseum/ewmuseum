More Luke Cage news

2 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/2/04 It’s a lazy Sunday afternoon. Sometimes, Brick spends these types of afternoons surfing the Internet. Lately, he has been following on leads to one of his latest passions. He is looking to branch out into acting. His current goal is to play the role of Marvel Comics character Luke Cage: Hero for Hire. Brick logs on to his e-mail server and checks his mail. One of the messages in his Inbox is from Aaron. The title reads: “Some word on the Luke Cage movie” When he clicks the link, he scrolls down to see a message that reads: “Brick, check these links out:” http://www.comicbookresources.com [detail withheld] “You’ll thank me. Aaron” Right away, Brick clicks on the first link. He then scrolls down the page and reads the following: “According to Sneak Peek, Marvel Studios' Kevin Feige says that 'Poetic Justice' and 'Rosewood' director John Singleton will helm Marvel's 'Luke Cage,' slated for a 2004 release.” “Yes!” Brick exclaims. This is the news he’s been waiting for. A director means that casting is underway. That means he can start the auditioning process. Then he starts to read further. “In 2001, Singleton reported aspirations for the project with Ving Rhames as the title character. Neal Moritz for Original Films and Marvel Studios Avi Arad are attached as producers.” “Damn. He wants Ving for this?” Brick is worried. Granted, he has taken a few acting classes and his teacher had told him he had promise, but he wasn’t near the caliber of someone like Rhames. Not to mention, there exists a certain stigma about wrestlers attempting to break into acting. Many fans that are against wrestlers acting cite Hulk Hogan as an example. They also view the Rock’s acting skills as questionable. Then he remembers that it says “in 2001”. Much can change in three years. Singleton may be willing to take a risk, casting someone else in the role, other than Rhames. Even if that other person has acting skills Once upon a time, he took a risk on Ice Cube when he cast him as Doughboy in “Boyz in the Hood”. Brick then clicks on the second link, even though it seems rather redundant, since it says pretty much the same. “John Singleton will write/direct Sony's upcoming comic book film of Marvel's ‘LUKE CAGE’. Marvel Studios' Kevin Feige says the live-action ‘LUKE CAGE’ movie, based on the popular 'Power Man' comic book character, is in development for a targeted 2005 release. Director John 'Shaft' Singleton told SNEAK PEEK in 2001 that he would direct ‘LUKE CAGE’, starring Ving ‘Pulp Fiction’ Rhames as the title character. 'Singleton is a big fan of 'Luke Cage' and we're a big fan of his,' Feige said at the time. Producers of ‘LUKE CAGE’ are Neal Moritz for Original Films and Marvel Studios Avi Arad. ‘LUKE CAGE’ was Marvel's first African-American character to headline his own title, debuting June 1972. Premise focused on a streetwise gang member, sent to prison for a crime he didn't commit. Behind bars, he volunteers for a medical procedure that transforms him into a powerhouse with ultra-dense body mass. Using his new powers, Cage breaks out of jail and uses his abilities for his own self interests and profit, establishing the bodyguard/mercenary business ‘Hero for Hire’.” Brick reads the part of the article that stresses how Singleton is a big fan of Luke Cage. Brick, himself, is a big fan. So, he knows that if he were directing this movie, any actor who auditioned for him would have to do one hell of a job convincing Brick that he was right for the part. Not that Brick himself isn’t qualified for the role. If anyone could be considered a live action version of Luke Cage, it is Brick. While he may not have charged people money to help them out, he always believed in doing the right thing. Also, he has earned the title “Toughest Man in GW” on more than one occasion. He has walked away from injuries that would have crippled or even killed any other man. Although, it has yet to be determined if Brick is bulletproof. Brick instantly goes to the closet in his bedroom and pulls out the cardboard box with all of his back issues of “LUKE CAGE: HERO FOR HIRE”, “LUKE CAGE: POWER MAN” and “POWER MAN AND IRON FIST”. He’s doing some boning up for one of the most important ventures of his life.
