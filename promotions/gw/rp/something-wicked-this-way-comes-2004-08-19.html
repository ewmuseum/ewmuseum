Something Wicked This Way Comes

19 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/19/04 I sit here, alone, forgotten, feeling the rough stone against my back, feeling the cold leaching into my bones. But at least, it’s something. Something, as opposed to the nothing I was feeling prior to this. It’s almost like everything is a shadow of what it had been, insubstantial; like someone turned down the colors, and muted the sounds. I seem to remember what it was to feel? but now the concept is abstract, foreign even. So, I sit here, looking down on them. From this vantage point, I felt like an observer, an outsider looking in. Perched here, on the edge of this roof, amid the stone gargoyles, I almost felt like an angel? or perhaps even a devil. Either one, neither one, take your pick? neither word held much meaning for me now. I was both, and yet, somehow? neither one. I had transcended mere words and becoming something more, much more. It was, sadly, even beyond my understanding. Even as I sat here, I could still feel the weight of the last vestiges of humanity dragging me down. Fear. Doubt. One would think, that in becoming immortal, all those pitiful mortal concerns would have fallen by the wayside. Call it trite, but going to hell and back had a funny way of shifting your perspective on things. Black had shifted to white? and vice versa? and now, my eyes were open to the true nature of things. There was no true definition, I could see now; it was all a thousand shades of gray. Dolphin, gunmetal, thunderhead? all alike, yet so vastly different. I could see the filth that polluted this world; the corruption? the dark taint that even colored the bricks that adorned the edifice of the building I perched upon. A church, a gothic Cathedral of saintly Catholic proportions? should have been a bastion of pure, shining white, right? Should have been, but wasn’t. Like I said, there was no true black or white? just like there was no pure evil or pure good. It was like a coin, with good being one side, and evil being the other? your heads and tails. Can’t have one without the other, the eternal balance? yin and yang. They were inextricably mixed, thus the gray. And where was I, in all this mess? Black? White? Good? Evil? It was hard, really to say. With everything else, my sense of right and wrong had faded away. I still bore the tattoo on my flesh, the faded Celtic knot work. A symbol of my brotherhood? a strict moral code; reduced now to nothing more than a symbol. So, here I sat, watching the cattle moving about down below? the daily hustle and bustle of humanity. The rat race, like lambs led to the slaughter, they were. I had fallen back into old routines, ever the vigilant watcher. And now, I was above it all. Namenlos. Nameless. I let my mind wander, letting it drift as I sought peace. The smells of humanity sickened me, even this high; I could still smell their stink. The rotten reek of their stunted minds, and inadequate perceptions. And still, I thought of her. Yet another of my human foibles. They say, at least in legends that one is only allowed to come back if for a noble purpose- love, revenge. Perhaps both. I no longer entertained romantic fancies, but somehow, I was unable to shake her from my mind’s eye. As surely as I was sitting here, she had killed me. I knew that to be true, prior to waking just this morning that had been my last memory. Her face, that one tear of regret trailing down her cheek. The smoking gun? the snowflakes, I remembered it all. Down to every last detail. The smells? the sounds. All of it. Etched in my mind forever, with perfect clarity. Perhaps, if I were still human, I would demand my revenge? but right now, I was content to just sit here and brood. The bell beneath me began to chime, each gong reverberating through my marrow like my own death knoll. I held out my wrist, rubbing absently at the faded tattoo, as though I hoped I could rub it away. Watchers, we’d called ourselves, mockingly. Scholars. Chroniclers. Spies. Like the fictional Talamasca in those Anne Rice novels that were so popular. It was easy for society to dismiss us as fiction; we were nothing more than ghosts, footnotes in history. SchattenJaegers. Shadow Hunters. Paranormal Police. We had been called all of that, and more. The CID was what we were? Center for Interior Defense? such an innocuous sounding name? really, more suited for a collection of suits around a conference table, than a motley band of mensa scholars, spies and mages. It was our job to chronicle and observe, never interfere? and certainly never get involved with our assignments. I would have been in a lot of hot water, had I not have died so conveniently. Jade, as she had called herself then, had been my charge. She was thief, and a damned good one at that? what had caught our attention was just what she had stolen. Relics. Sacred relics. It was my job to follow her, to find out who had hired her? and to recover the items. Risky, yes. But for me, it was supposed to be a cakewalk. I had been the best. Young. Arrogant. And colossally stupid. I could see it now, in the perfect clarity of hindsight, just how well I had been played. I had nearly thrown it all away, for her. Given the chance again, even knowing what I knew now, I would still have done it. Movement below drew my attention, as a long line of cars pulled up, disgorging their passengers like dark clots of vomit on the pavement. They were all dressed alike. Dark suits. Curiosity got the best of me, I wanted a better look? it was after all, my funeral? ---- The church was packed with mourners; a veritable sea of dark suits? drab colors, in startling contrast to the bright swatches of color provided by the wreaths of flowers at the front. The empty casket was draped with an American flag, as was tradition, and atop that was a photograph of a smiling, much younger man, recognizable only because we had seen him only moments ago, atop the very same roof of this building. Who he was, though, was impossible to say, as he had never revealed his identity. Dante Lee, however, knew him well? they had been as close as brothers. He cleared his throat, taking another sip of the glass of water that had been pushed into his hand sometime ago, trying to resist the urge to break down sobbing. It just wasn’t the same without Jackson’s acid wit? and his cynical comments. He would have said something dry, and utterly hilarious about this whole situation. He steeled himself, and stepped up to the podium, gripping the wood with his trembling hands. “Brothers and sisters,” the microphone squealed in protest, and everyone visibly winced as the feedback ripped through the packed sanctuary. He continued after a moment, lowering his voice slightly, “it is with heavy hearts that we meet here today to pay our last respects to?” his voice caught on the last word, and he had to pause again, taking a shuddering breath before he continued. “Bradley Jackson.” ---- Ah, and there it was, the name. What’s in a name, really? I mean, if I were to call myself something else, would it make me any less a person? I wondered, if any of them could sense my presence here? what with all the magic in this room alone, surely someone could feel me up here? like a bat in the belfry. I suppressed a chuckle, and settled down to listen to Dante’s speech, all the while growing increasingly? angry. ---- Dante took another sip of water, gathering his thoughts as his nervousness grew. Something just didn’t feel right, and the fact that every eye was on him was making matters worse. He let his gaze skip around the room, settling on various familiar faces? people he’d known for years, letting his mind wander. It worked to calm his nerves, and after what had only been a matter of seconds, he was ready to continue, his voice stronger now, less quaver? more bass. “Jackson was my best friend. My brother in the truest sense of the word, although blood did not tie us together, it might as well have. I envied him, I looked up to him” hell, and I even emulated him. Looking around this room, I think I can speak for a good many of us in saying that today we have lost more than a friend. Jackson was a legend? a hero? and more than that, he was an integral part of most of our lives. I for one can say with utter verisimilitude, that I will miss him. Jackson, wherever you may be, I wish you good luck and Godspeed on the next leg of your journey.” He paused, only to wipe away the single tear that traced a track down his clean-shaven, boyishly handsome cheek. “I can’t believe he’s gone” I mean?” It was then, that Dante broke down weeping, clutching the edges of the podium for support as he let the maelstrom of emotions overcome him. He swooned, feeling dangerously close to passing out, and that’s when the room went utterly silent, the whispers and sidebar conversations of the assembled watchers fading away to an abrupt silence. Dante looked up, his grief forgotten, and there, in the aisle, stood Jackson. “No,” he shook his head, his hand going to his mouth in shock, attempting to stifle the scream that welled in his throat. “It cannot be” you’re? dead.” Jackson simply smiled, that old familiar lopsided grin, and spread his hands to the sides. His voice came from between his parted lips, but it seemed to echo in Dante’s head, pounding in his brain. “Dante. Do I look dead to you?” “I” well? but I saw your body? I helped them prepare the coffin?” Dante looked wildly at the coffin that stood between him and the man who purported to be the dead Jackson. Jackson closed the gap between himself and the coffin, and then roughly whisked the cloth away, letting the picture clatter to the floor. Glass shards flew up, glimmering in the light like precious stones, and then the lid of the coffin flew open, leaving Dante gasping. It was empty, upon the velvet lining was nothing but the fancy suit they had dressed him in. “No” nuh-no. It’s? imp-im-possible.” Dante stuttered, backing away, and then tripping over the microphone cord. He fell in an ungraceful tangle of arms and legs, helpless in his terror as he looked at Jackson with tears in his eyes. Jackson snickered, the corners of his mouth turning up in a sadistic grin. “You’ve read the bible, Dante” you’re the scholar among us? Jesus? and the tomb. “He is not here for he is risen.” And here I stand. Yet, still you doubt me. That hurts, Dante? really, it does. I expected more from you. And quite honestly, this funeral sucks. I envisioned more? more melodrama? weeping and tearing of hair? I mean, honestly, what do I have to do to get respect around here? Kill someone?” His lunatic gaze settled on Dante, and the smile grew colder. He nodded once, and then leapt, landing atop Dante, pinning him down even as he struggled to rise. Jackson’s breath was foul and fetid as he spoke in Dante’s face? it was like smelling the attar of the grave. “Look at me, Dante. Look into my eyes, and know what I know?” He took hold of Dante’s chin, and forced him to look. Almost immediately, Dante’s face went ashen, his mouth growing slack? and all the color bled out of his dark hair, leaving it stark white. Sheer terror. Jackson released his grip, and stumbled back, his expression battling between satisfaction and horror. Without saying another word, he turned and ran, vanishing into the shadows just as time resumed its normal course. The assembled congregation gasped collectively, and then flew into a frenzy of activity as they noticed Dante laying there, barely conscious at the edge of the stage. Somehow, the coffin had been righted, leaving no evidence as to the perpetrator of this heinous crime? but Dante knew. Even as his life faded, he knew, that something evil was walking this earth, wearing the skin of his oldest friend. “by the pricking of my thumbs, something wicked this way comes”
