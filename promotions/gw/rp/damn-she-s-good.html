Damn she's good...

undated

Date: Sunday “Victory has a hundred fathers but defeat is an orphan.” -Galeazzo Ciano Deacon Holmes sits alone in the solemn silence of his dimly lit bedroom. Several layers of white athletic tape adhere a soft cotton cast mat cast to his torso as a result of the injury he suffered at High Noon. Two bruised ribs on one side, and a minor crack on the other. Nothing new, and nowhere near the worst of his injuries. No amount of physical torment, pain, or punishment could even begin to surmount the anguish imposed on the young man’s ego. He’d had broken bones before. Cuts, bruises, all of these things were of no consequence at all in Deacon’s mind so long as they served their purpose... victory. No amount of misery was maligned for long in the bright, shimmering, warm, soothing light of triumph. Winning invigorated so much so that even the threat of losing life and limb was without gravity. It was like a drug. But now that feeling was gone. And it had been gone for quite some time now. So like any addict, Deacon was going through withdrawals. Bad dreams kept him up at night, tossing and turning in bed wrestling against himself within the sheets as nightmares of continued failure haunted him. Often causing him to awaken abruptly in a cold sweat. During his waking hours, his normally boisterous and jubilant disposition was replaced with one of malcontent and soul-numbing depression. Once considered overly talkative, Deacon was now borderline antisocial. Only speaking to others when need be, and even then only in curt three to four word bursts. Sometimes when you’re winning too much you start to feel like you should never lose again. Every week Deacon’s walked down that ramp he’s done so expecting to win, but he wasn’t necessarily fighting not to lose. Fame, acclaim, and multiple title reigns had thrust his ego skyward. He was the cruiserweight champion. He was the platinum champion. Everything was going so well that it was beginning to seem he was untouchable. All that soon changed. Deacon had made minimal effort to prepare himself for an upcoming match with the last remaining contender for his cruiserweight title. Max Roswell. Deacon had beaten him before, in fact, several times before. He’d faced him in normal matches, tournaments, contendership matches, and even faced him with titles on the line before. Each meeting yielded the same result, a swift victory for Deacon and another loss in Roswell’s column. Seeing Max Roswell on his schedule for Deacon was the equivalent of a champion hurdler seeing a popsicle stick laying on the track and being dared to jump over it. Too easy. In Deacon’s mind, Roswell stood no chance. There was no threat posed to his title. If anything, the match was considered more of a personal appearance and a chance for fans to catch a glimpse of the nineteen year old phenomenon as he quickly seized victory for himself. But on that night this was not to be the case. As a result of his showboating, Max Roswell was able to steal a win with a quick roll up pin while Deacon’s back was turned. Roswell had seen an opening and turned the one percent chance he had of winning into the spark to ignite an engulfing blaze. But it was just that, chance. Chance can allow you to accomplish a seemingly unattainable goal every once in a great while, but consistent achievement happens only if you love what you are doing. And on any given night in that arena after a win, it was more than evident Deacon loved what he was doing. It was all he knew how to do. Win. His achievement was nothing if not consistent, he was perpetually on the rise. Deacon Holmes was blowing up like spontaneous human combustion on a weekly basis in front of thousands of fans. He even began to refer to himself as “The new face of GW”. A few weeks ago it was a handsome face smiling mischievously with a middle finger held high overhead in everyone’s general direction. Now it was melancholy, unshaven, and staring at a middle finger every time it passed by a mirror. Deacon had chalked up his loss to Roswell as chance, but after losing the platinum title to Train in the following match in the cruelest loss he’d ever been a part of, then following it up with another to Juri Hyobanshi, Deacon full on hated himself. Roswell stole a win because he knew he had no chance, but Train’s victory over Deacon really irked him. Holmes hadn’t lost that title. It wasn’t he who’d been pinned. But having to watch as his beloved title slipped through the cracks and into the walking cesspool that is Train due to injury, that hurt more than any “Hollowpoint”. Having notched three losses in a row and with each one eating at him, self loathing beset Deacon like a pack of hounds on a bloody fox. With each pass by a mirror Deacon couldn’t help but scowl and sneer at the reflection he saw. A loser. He’d grown to hate himself so much in such a short time that just looking into his own countenance was too much for him. With each glance he could taste his own bile welling up on the back of his tongue. This was the taste of defeat. You can learn a line from a win, and an entire book from defeat. Yet all Deacon had seemingly learned to do was hate himself for it. He was usually described as unbearable in victory and unbeatable in defeat because he always came back even more ferocious than before, but now it would seem that his will had been beaten out of him. He hadn’t taken any calls or accepted any visitors in a number of days, opting to sit in the frigid silence of his room. His phones rang off their hooks and knocks at the door went without acknowledgment. Deacon had become a sovereign state, completely cut off from the outside world and it’s rules and values. Even those “rules and values” that went by the name Rhaiza. After several days of sullen sulking, a very melancholy Deacon Holmes heard a rattling noise coming from the other side of the door to his room. The same door which had been locked for days. At first he turned a suspicious eye toward where the noise was coming from, but chose to remain seated. Deeming himself utterly worthless had taken away his will to fight for himself, or his possessions. He’d decided instead to put himself at the mercy of whoever was to pass through that door. After a few more rough jerks, all became silent once again as whatever was on the other side of the door seemed to have stopped. Slowly, the cold steel door knob began to turn, making only the slightest of noises as the bolt slips from it’s cylinder. With the door ajar, the intruder surreptitiously ducks into the chilly room and with purposeful steps makes it’s way toward Deacon’s location. As the unknown visitor’s footsteps grow closer, Deacon finally decides to inspect as he wraps his boxer clad body in a thin sheet and stands to his feet. Just as he prepares to investigate, suddenly the lights turn on, almost blinding him in the process as he’d been in the dark for days now. When he finally regains his sight he finds himself standing across the room from none other than Rhaiza, who appears to be a bit fed up. Placing a hand on her hip, she taps her foot repeatedly on the floor with an expectant scowl directed at her dejected boyfriend. He hadn’t accepted or returned any of her calls in days, and she was sure he’d been home on a few of the occasions she’d attempted to stop by. It would seem she’d finally had enough and decided to take matters into her own hands. With a shrug, Deacon turned and shuffled back towards the bed, this time gingerly maneuvering underneath it’s sheets as if to shield himself from anything from the outside. “How you get in here?” comes from underneath the covers in a weak and slightly muffled voice. This causes Rhaiza to weave her neck a bit while cocking an eyebrow at Deacon, even though he can’t see it. “How did I get in here?” Rhaiza repeats as she walks toward his bed, angling herself toward the side he’s turned toward. “You’re not the only one who knows how to pick a lock! Good thing too, cause I’m sick of you acting like this!” She exclaims, her tone growing more exasperated by the second as she rips the comforter away from Deacon while continuing “Look at you! You look like who done it and why, this isn’t you...” Rhaiza’s words fall on deaf ears as Deacon just glances at her with glazed eyes before turning to the other side. “Get up!” Rhaiza pleads with him, then thinking quickly, she decides to change her strategy. Taking a seat on the edge of the bed she leans back a bit and rests her elbow on Deacon as she continues. “I know what it is, you’re afraid.” This finally manages to elicit some sort of response from Deacon as he rolls over to face her and looks up into her eyes with wild eyed bewilderment. A knowing smile creeps across her face as she stands back up to her feet and continues. “That’s right, you heard me... S-C-A-R-E-D, SCARED! You know you can’t beat Roswell so you just wanna wallow in bed all day and hope the day for your match with him comes and goes while you’re asleep. Well things aren’t always that easy, sometimes you actually have to try! Now get up!” Deacon glared at her a bit in disbelief as she stared at him waiting for him to rise from the bed. Slowly but surely, Deacon climbed out of bed and onto his feet. Standing before her in nothing more than a pair of boxers while exhibiting the poorest of posture, Deacon Holmes was truly a sight to see, and a far cry from the him that was usually seen on television. After sizing him up, Rhaiza began to rattle off instructions. “Now first, we’re gonna get you shaved and in the shower. Then you’re going to the gym and having a nice long work out while I go to my spinning class.” Deacon’s shoulders slumped even more at the idea of such physical exertion, over the course of the week he’d grown quite accustomed to sloth. Rhaiza took note of his ever deteriorating posture and quickly nipped it right in the bud after flipping her fiery red hair over her shoulder. “And stand up straight! Ya know, the worst thing you can do when you’re feeling bad is straighten up and hold your head high. Wanna know why?” Deacon lackadaisically shrugged at her inquisition before replying “Why?” in a tone that conveyed just how little he actually cared. Rhaiza quickly shot back “Because then you might actually start to feel better, now go get cleaned up!” With a scoff, Deacon collapsed back against the bed mattress and covered himself once more with sheets and blankets. Both of Rhaiza’s eyes grew wide as her boyfriend continued to sulk. Then, reaching into her bag for it’s last remaining trick, she finally struck gold. “Fine, you wanna play hardball” We’ll play hardball! Until you win a match... YOU’RE CUT OFF!” These words sparked a fire inside of Holmes who immediately burst from underneath the covers and sprang to his feet before they even had a chance to hit the ground. Staring at his girlfriend with bulging eyes, Deacon leapt from the bed and raced immediately into the bathroom. Seconds later water could be heard spraying from the shower head as Rhaiza smiled to herself while taking a seat on the edge of the bed to revel in her own genius. “Damn I’m good.”
