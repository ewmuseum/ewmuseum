mind over madness: asking why

7 March 2007

mind over madness: asking why Reginald Atkins Global Wrestling Promo #3032 Date: 3/7/07 Location: Fusion RP mind over matter: asking why Let me guess. You want to know why. It’s gotten to the point where everyone is asking me why. Nathan, Jetters, and now even Lyell are all questioning my motives, wondering why I do these things. So is this police investigator, apparently. He smells of rotten vegetables, and he’s breathing my air. I’m not a big fan of people breathing my air. “Why would someone send his friend to rape a blind girl?” he’s asking, cutting straight to the chase, being tough. Thinking he’s the first cop to ever stare me down. And breathing my fucking air. I’m not answering, which is of course his cue to take off, continuing his righteous rant about helpless women and pricks like me who are now in jail. I’m also not a big fan of the word prick. It seems too small. It’s demeaning, it makes me sound like less of a monster and more of a nuisance. But still I’m silent. “You know the rulebooks, pal. I’m sure you’ve watched enough crime shows in Merry Old England to realize if you send someone to do your dirty work, your hands get dirty too.” That’s always the right way to go, piggy: yeah, let’s bring some British stereotypes in the mix and see if it ruffles his fur. Like I said, I’ve done this before, I know the score, and damned if I crack for this fucking whore (heart of a poet, I know). My fur remains decidedly unruffled, which kills him. I know I’m safe, know I’m going home, so I smile. His countenance polarizes at that. Grabbing the ends of the table. Clutching at his almost-evaporating hair. Wishing he had the right or the balls to give me the old one-two. But all he does is shake his head and ask: “Why?” And, like I said, I don’t know why. This guy hasn’t got shit on me, so I wouldn’t tip my hat with any clues even if I did know. But to the other inquiring minds…I might actually have a theory. Read any Shakespeare? Then you know Othello. Or at least you know the Americanized “O,” with Mekhi Phifer and that squinty little berk trying to steal Harrison Ford’s gimmick. It’s sad that Americans have to water down great works like Othello because your average middle-aged minimum-waged bugger from Nebraska doesn’t know what the word “wherefore” means. That’s a tragedy in itself. Othello is pretty straightforward at first glance. You got your powerful black man and your crafty jealous white man, determined to break up the bond between the black man and his perfect porcelain wife. Othello is a tall drink of ebony perfection, although not really because the original Othello was a Moor, a Spaniard, maybe even an Arab if you twist the dialects. But for the sake of keeping modern minds into it, we’ll peg it as a racist play. Beneath all that, though, you’ve got Iago. This clever bloke who comes up with all these devices to trick everyone, this guy whose only weapons are his words, but they’re fully loaded and very effective. Iago tries to justify his actions throughout the entire play. He gives so many motives that the audience isn’t sure which one is correct. He hates Othello for promoting someone else, he has lust for Othello’s wife, he is a racist…the motives just don’t end. There are so many, in fact, that they pretty much cancel each other out and leave you with one possible conclusion. Iago’s just plain evil. Now…ask me why again. ---- We’re still on the Shakespeare kick, so let’s just describe the end of that police interrogation as full of sound and fury, signifying bugger-all (Modernized for you, of course). Now I’m with my friend, my confidant, my betrayer. Lyell is standing there, doing his best impression of a seven foot tall German sheep. He won’t say anything, he won’t look at me. He knows he did a cock-up. “See, I don’t mind you not finishing the task, Lyell. The scare was perfect, it’s all that we needed. The best part is the blind bint didn’t have a clue it was you. But when the police came, did you really need to look down and point at me, muttering ‘Keine Englische’?” I half-know what he’s going to say but I’m hoping for his sake he doesn’t say it. “Aber…aber ich spreche keine Englische. Nur Deutsch.” Oh no you fucking don’t. I usually don’t suggest slapping huge German men for other people, but really, it’s my only option. Saying he doesn’t speak English. “Don’t give me that bollocks, you fucking Kraut. I taught you English. You can speak it; you can understand me when I talk, what further proof do I need?” I can tell he’s not understanding that the English isn’t the important issue here. “Listen, Lyell. When you point at me and say you don’t speak English, you are doing two things. One: you’re essentially admitting you have done whatever ghastly act they are investigating. And two: you make me muddled in it as well. Luckily, I know how this game works and nothing came of it. But if we are going to finish what we set out to accomplish, we can’t keep being so obvious. Subtlety is the name of the game.” Nodding like a little puppy. I don’t know what happened to my friend. In fact, I don’t know what happened to any of my friends. It’s like there’s been a serious case of the morals going around, and both of them have been bed-ridden with it. First it was Nathan. Now you don’t understand, Nathan was amazing. I used to have some clips of what he would do to people he faced, managers of people he faced, family members of people he faced…he was a monster. And we had so much fun. But then he fell in love, how fucking sweet is that. And for some reason now Lyell is jumping on this morals train that seems to be winding endlessly around the tracks, headed straight for unhappiness. Because the more you question yourself and what you’ve done, the unhappier you’ve become. Let me throw out a wrestling example for you, in case you forgot about my occupation with all of the drama I go through. Claudius Augustus defeated me in the middle of the ring at the last episode of Fusion. Well, not exactly in the middle of the ring. More like the edge of the ring, with his feet placed firmly on the ropes. Good for him, another W for the column. Thing is, I tried it, and if it would have worked for me, big deal. The twit got a cheap victory? Stop the presses. That’s just what he does. But our friend used to pride himself on being a good guy. Still does, I believe, though I really can’t concentrate because every time he’s on TV I get the urge to pass out. He’s even a member of some blasted righteous group called ‘True Saints.’ Now, does a true saint cheat to win? Not by any means. His entire core of values has been compromised just because he couldn’t beat the up and comer cleanly. See what I mean? Being a goody-goody and caring about honest victories and being against raping and poisoning blind girls gives you nothing but trouble. ---- Fending off the cop, telling off the kraut, those were the simple things. Now I’m down to the challenge of it all, the real beef of the matter. “Tell me straight, Reggie…did you do what they’re accusing you of?” Nathan’s a frightening character, even in his softer, minge-like stage. He’s being calm, asking me nicely, and not showing any sign that a wrong answer will cause him to tear me apart. That’s because it won’t. Not anymore. But he doesn’t know I know that. He still has this notion I’m afraid of him because I knew what he once was. “Nathan. Come on.” The best part of a lie has nothing to do with the words. We all know that I can put gunpowder in my words just like Othello, but when dealing with Nate it simply isn’t necessary. All I have to do is look at him, and he breaks. “Yeah, I know, but they took you in for questioning. What do they know that I don’t?” There’s an easy answer. “I’m willing to bet not a fucking fig, my friend. I didn’t touch your girl, nor did I send Lyell to do so. He’s just shaken up because he happens to like your little tart, though for the life of me I can’t figure out why.” He’s already breaking. Now here’s the interesting thing about this situation. Nathan doesn’t really believe me. In his heart, he knows I did it. But he’s going to believe that I didn’t because right now he needs me. As much as I say I need to see the Nathan Boswell that once sent some Welsh bastard into a coma, the timid Nathan Boswell needs me that much and more. Because he wants to have that competitive spirit live in him once again, except now he’s trying to convince himself he can separate it from the monster that it ends up spawning. “Listen, Reggie. I am getting afraid of leaving her alone. I don’t want to keep putting her at risk. I can’t come to the next show.” “Nate, you’re coming to the show. I’ve got a big surprise planned, it’ll get you exposure you’ve never dreamed of. You need to be there. Just leave Arianna home with Lyell. He’ll take great care of her.” He’s taking some time to think about it, but I know he’ll agree. He misses it. And I miss him. I miss him the way he used to be. And soon I’ll be getting that Nathan Boswell. Because no matter how much he tries to fight it, you can’t separate the competitiveness from the monster. It just doesn’t work out. Something’s got to give, and it ends up being your morals. I'm proof. Maybe that's why I do these things. Maybe that's why I'm sitting here now, thinking about how I'm going to take advantage of Lyell and Arianna alone together. Maybe I'm just having trouble filtering my competitive nature. Or maybe I'm just like Iago.
