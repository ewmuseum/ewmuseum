Tight Schedule

28 March 2005

Date: 3/28/05 Tap, Tap, Tap.... Train’s knuckles gently collided with the beige door in rapid succession. The black gift box with the red bow under his arm slips a little so he reaches down to cinch it back into place. His knocks ring and echo up and down the narrow, white hallway of the apartment building. He stood, head down, hair swaying gently waiting for a response to his call. It was hard to believe he was here. It had been such a long time... a life time really. He’d never felt comfortable here. Most likely because he always knew he was never welcomed here. Besides, it wasn’t like he’d ever considered this some great oasis anyway. Not like the woman who called it home. Not that this wasn’t paradise compared to the place he’d come up. Hell downtown Beirut might have been considered the suburbs compared to the Cabrini Green housing projects. However, despite it being a world away from there this place hadn’t ever seen far enough. There was always the feeling that they were too good for this place. The building wasn’t the projects or anything, but it was hardly what he could have afforded to provide. When he was young Train had thought that just because the part of the city that he saw everyday was a shit hole that the whole place was exactly the same. It was a good a good little trick. The folks across town... the ones with all the money... They’d perpetrated a hell of a hoax. In order to keep all the “undesirables” from getting a piece of what they had they’d hide the wealth behind the decayed, concrete facade of the south side of the city. Too bad for them Train had grown up and realized it. Had refused to stay in the place they had deemed worth of him and his lot to stay. He’d stretched out his wings and decided to fly to wherever the fuck he wanted to. He’d always felt guilty that he, for the most part, hadn’t brought his family with him. But those twinges never lasted too long. It was never his fault they hadn’t benefitted. He’d have given just about all he had. Too bad his mother would never have accepted. As far she’d ever been concerned it was all covered in blood. He could have made his money selling ice cream... rescuing kittens from trees... being a champion.... It was all the same. He was a monster No chance of redemption. He’d never change. Were those assumptions or a truths? Eh.... 50/50.... The bottom line of it was the only reason Train was here was because she wasn’t. The lay over in Chicago wasn’t going to be long and he had no intentions of spending it arguing or being brow beaten. Time was too short for all that. The schedule was tight and he couldn’t miss anything. Clash of the Titans should probably have been his main priority. Actually there was no probably about it. There was a shot at the world title on the line. Scratch that. A shot at the world title in the main event of the biggest show of the year... Wrestlecade. He’d busted his ass for that shot. Ate and drank it. Sweat and bled for it. It’d be a hell of a stupid thing to miss because he missed his plane. And Rome wasn’t exactly a hop, skip or a jump away. There was no red eye that would get him there in time if he missed any of his flights. Most would call it bad scheduling as if it were just some oversight. A little mistake. Most would be wrong. It wasn’t any kind of silly oversight. It was well laid plan scheduled around perfect timing. He had a couple of stops to make before he left the country and this was one of them. A few months ago nobody would have been able to tell him that there could have been any date more important than Clash. But there was. If he’d taken the time to be honest with himself he’d have known that’s really where he was on his way to. Rome was just a secondary destination. He hadn’t been to his mother’s home in years. Hadn’t taken spoken to her in just as long, but that was ok. There was no steadier person in the world than her. Aside from one ugly period in her life that had consequently set off the events that lead to the rift between her and her son, she’d done the same thing most of her life. She got up, went to work and provided for her family. Today would be no exception. She’d worked the same shift at that damn food packaging plant for years. Didn’t matter that Train could have provided a life that meant she’d never have to lift a finger to work again. She got up and she went. He didn’t have to call and ask to see if she’d done the same this morning. And she wouldn’t be back for another 3 hours. His flight out of Ohaire doesnt? leave for another two and a half. The flight would land in New York late tonight. That gave him time for a good nights sleep and a couple of hours to go to the party before he caught the flight to Rome. It would land with enough time to get a good nap, warm up and kick some ass. It was a tight schedule, but with perfect timing. Train couldn’t decide it was lucky coincidence or fate. No matter. He only stood in the hallway, letting history flood his mind for few a moments before the light footsteps approaching from the other side of the door brought him back into the present. “Who is it?” inquired a young woman on the other side of the steel framed door separating her from the rest of the world. “Yo” momma!” Train replies with an intended bluntness. “Wha....” the young woman starts before apparently recognizing the brash tone. “Boy!” she yells before the sound of locks and dead bolts popping open fills the hallway. The door creaks as the rubbery sound of the insulating seal being pulled open, allowing the air to rush inside the apartment, an audible “whoosh” resulting from the force that the door is pulled with. He doesn’t even have a chance to move before a petite, caramel skinned young woman comes bounding through the door way as if she’s on a pogo stick. Her tiny arms latch on to him and hold fast. Train was stunned for a moment. Normally she couldn’t twist the top of a new bottle of mayo, now she caught him in a bear hug that threatened to cut off a little air flow. But it was no big deal. That just meant it was good to be home. He straightened himself up, pulling her off his feet as he returns the hug with a few pats on the back. Joanne was light as a feather, which made it ironic how she’d always held him in place like an anchor. But hey, that’s what little sisters were for. She was five years his junior and when he looked back on the time they were growing up he’d always felt a little guilty since, in her early years in the family, treated her pretty badly. Train had been the youngest until she showed up. It’s rough having to give up your spot in the family, but he’d made up for losing the baby title by taking full advantage of the new big brother moniker. Many a Barbie doll met an untimely end at his hands, either beheaded or meeting a horrible end being burned to death. He’d never appreciated her. At least not until Leon died. He was the real big brother of the family. A full 8 years a head of Joanne and 5 years in front of before Anthony he’d lost his “baby” spot twice. However he’d done a better job than Anthony had. Where as Tony had always hassled Joanne Leon had always looked out for everyone. Tony hadn’t felt the need to do that until the streets of this city opened up and swallowed him. That’s when Train knew it was on him to carry the load for the family. When Leon died it didn’t take too long for Tony to transform into Train. In the tempest that swirled around him, being young, stupid and motivated, despite being the younger one, she had always been the voice of reason. She was no gangster, but there wasn’t she didn’t know about what went down with him in the streets back in the day. He could think of a least 2 or 3 times her voice calling him back from the edge likely kept him from becoming a statistic instead of a boss. It had been forever since they’d talked. Not chat.. Say “hi” and “bye”... Talked. He knew he had to change that. He needed that voice to tell him what he should be doing. Because something had to change... he had to do something about... about... damn. He couldn’t even bring himself to say it. But he’d have to if he wanted to know what he should do. Train was trying to evaluate which of the 10 or 20 segways he’d run over in his mind on the plane ride up here could use to move from the friendly visit to the serious heart to heart when he felt a little ache in the same. Joanne quickly hopped back and launched a punch into Train’s chest pushing him back a step. “Owww, girl!” Train yelled at his sister as he’d likely done a million times before. “What the hell wrong with you”!” “What the hell’s wrong with me”!” she exclaimed back at him. “What the hell’s wrong with you”! All the sudden you can’t call nobody back? I been called you like a billion times trying to say congratulations on winning your title and you can’t holla back? What you too big a star to remember your family all the sudden?” Joanne could only shake her head and turn on her heels, much like she’d just turned on a dime, to head back into the apartment. “It ain’t even like that girl.” Train said calmly, not at all taken off guard by his sisters barrage coming so close after her greeting as he’d known her long enough to have seen that a million times before. He strolls casually into the apartment after her, closing the door behind him. “I just been busy. I mean if you know I won the title you know what kinda headaches I’m going through too. I gotta watch out for my own damn tag team partner. Gotta worry about whether that damn redneck McCoy is gonna pulls some more bullshit... It’s just been hectic. My bad for not calling you back.” “You damn right it is.” Joanne replies in an irritated tone even as she moves over to the fridge, pulling out a 2 liter of Cherry Coke, her big brother’s favorite, and retrieving two glasses before pouring. “C’mon now... Don’t even be like that.” Train says remembering his secret weapon, the black box he’d struggled to hold on to as his sister assaulted him both times. “I come bearing gifts.” “Ooooooo, what’d you bring me”!” Joanne says bounding back into the room with their sodas, reminding him of the little girl he’d known not so long ago. She played tough pretty good, but he knew full well she was a push over. Train drops the box on the dining room table, just outside the entrance to the kitchen and pulls off the lid. His sister puts the drinks down across the table so her hands will be free to clap frantically as she bounces on her tip toes. like every birthday and Christmas morning Train had ever witnessed. “Yeah, yeah.... Mmmhmmm... Yeah, nothing like free stuff to get back on the good side, huh?” “Shut up and give me my present boy!” Train just shook his head with a smirk and dug into the peace offering box. It didn’t take long to come back out holding a baby blue, velour, GW warm up suit just his sisters size. Joanne giggled with excitement and she quickly took them, calming just short of snatching them away. “Knew you’d like that.” Train said, shaking his head with a chuckle. “Oh, and here we go too...” Train reaches in again and quickly pulls out 4 front row tickets to the next GW show in Chicago. “Thank you Tony.” Joanne says giving him a peck on the cheek. “You damn right.” “Boy, you just got yourself out of trouble. Don’t go getting yourself back into it.” “Uhuh....” “I love the suit and I got some friends who’ll love these tickets. Who knows, maybe I can even give one to momma.” “Please.... Let’s get real.” “She’s coming around Tony. More than she’s letting on, she’s coming around. She’s not silly, she can see you’re changing your life.” “Yeah and you ain’t silly so don’t fool yourself into thinking that’s good enough.” “She sees Tony. Sooner or later she’ll realize it. I mean as long as you don’t go doing anything stupid like... Getting into some big beef with some stupid rapper who opens his mouth about you.” “Maaaaaaaaan.......” Train begins, grabbing his glass of Cherry Coke, taking a big sip. “Damn Lil” Flip. That little trick opened his mouth first and if he does it again I’m gonna close it for him as in his punk ass is gonna need to get that shit wired shut to let it heel. His punk ass mouth off to me one more time he’ll be singing through the wire like Kanye West or somebody.” “Tony.....” “Hell naw Joanne. His ass started this so don’t even start...” “That’s funny, I was just about to say the same thing to you.” “Pshhhhh....” Train says before working on his drink a bit more. It’s silent for a moment as Joanne knows that the subject is basically closed. No one could talk to him when he got like that. Or almost no one. “Where’s my present?” a high pitched, little voice inquired. Train turns, a quick pass of the room showing it still empty until he bothered to look down. Standing just under his radar was a curly haired little girl, her locks running wild as she stands looking up at him in her pink pj’s wiping the sleep from her eyes. “He munchkin!” Train bellows as he scoops his niece into his arms greeting her with a big hug. “Monique, baby...” Joanne begins, a bit surprised to see her. “What are you doing up?” “I heard you and uncle Tony.” she states plainly, sleep still hovering over her. “Sorry baby,” Joanne responds. “We didn’t mean to wake you up.” “Wake you up” I didn’t even know you were even here. Why aren’t you at school little girl?” “Cuz my tummy hurts.” “Ohhhhhh... Well that’s no fun.” uncle Tony. “I bet I have something that can make you feel better though.” “What?” his niece responds, resting her tired head on his shoulder that lays underneath a layer of dread locks. Train digs into the box again and comes back with one of the GW Teddy Bear line... a cuddly Train replica complete with dread locks and a title The viscious bear suddenly jumps at her and begins vicious round of tickling, bring out a laugh from the sick little girl before she clutches and holds him. “Thank you!” the little girl says. “No problem munchkin.” The next hour passed quickly. Most of it was spent sitting on the couch, the dusty family albums being opened for the first time in who knows how long. The gone by years, the long passed faces all seemed alive and well, in full living color. In those moments there were no dead brother or father. No family rift splitting the family in half. Time passes quickly and the next time he thinks to glance at his niece, she’s fast asleep in his arms. Joanne notices at the same time he does. “So,” she begins, breaking away from reminiscing. “You want to tell me what you wanna talk about?” “What you talking about?” Train responds, not used to having the momentum stolen from him. “Tony.... you should be on your way to Italy right now, but instead you’re hear with us. Now it ain’t like we don’t love having you... but I know this isn’t some quick visit.” Train freezes for a moment. Having a sister never buys your crap is annoying yet helpful. No segway needed. He slowly looks up from his niece and into his sisters eyes. Silence... the question is obvious, but it’s still hard to form the words. “Do you think............” “Do I think what?” “Do you think...... Do you think I’m the type of cat that could be a good dad?” ***** ***** ***** Spring had come early to New York. Gabrielle was glad the weather reports had been right. Sometimes you just never knew with those guys on the tv. But, for at least once, they lived up to their word. It was exactly what she’d been looking for. The park was beautiful today. The sun sparkled in the sky and glimmered off everything in sight. A refreshing breeze rustled the tree tops, made the balloons tied to the picnic tables dance in the air and did the same for her long red locks as they flowed over her shoulder. However, just because it was beautiful out didn’t mean it was going to be an easy day. Gabrielle dodged little bodies as they darted about, running every which way around the playground all as she balanced the large square cake in her hands. “Happy Birthday Brianne” stood out bouldly in purple letters, surrounded on all sides by butterflies and flowers. As much a mine field as the park had become with all of her daughter’s 4, 5 and 6 year old guests running around like bats out of a place none of them were allowed to say, Gabrielle could get rid of her grin. The huge “6' candle standing like a monolith at the center of the cake reminded here that her little girl wasn’t so little any more. She was turning into a big girl now. Gabby caught herself in mid thought, knowing that this train of thought could only lead to tears so she had to check it right now. All the other parents in the park doing their best to chaperone were armed with video cameras. Last thing she wanted to do was be captured on tape looking like a racoon at her daughter’s birthday party. Besides, here little girl growing up had some benefits built in. At least in theory. Perhaps as she grew up she’d feel the need to be a little more ladylike. Maybe the idea of a little dress and barrettes wouldn’t be quite so repulsive to her as she grew. Maybe as she transformed from a big girl into a young lady she’s let her voice do more talking to her fists. Gabrielle almost shook with anticipation at the thought of not being called down to have a conference with the principal ever month or two. Gabrielle actually had the never to think to herself that she didn’t know where the little girl got that hostile streak from. She stopped herself again, knowing full well that wasn’t true. She knew exaclty where it came from and it wasn’t from her. But that was ok. She’d get that under control come hell or high water. She wouldn’t allow him to ruin her life, especially he wasn’t a part of it. She finally reached picnic table unscathed and puts the cake down in the center of the red and white checkerboard pattern. She licks a small speck of purple icing from her thumb as she scans the playground, looking for the woman of the hour. It only takes a second to spot her. She’s in her favorite place. Brianne’s stands behind home plate. The red highlights she’s inherited from her mother glistens in the light brown, wild and curly mane she’s inherited from her father, the wind rustling it like a flag. He hair of course, as is per usual, means nothing to her as she stands crouched and ready in her purple t shirt and baggy overalls. Finally it’s time. The red rubber ball glides quickly and smoothly across the green grass of the park... and as quickly as it comes to her it’s airborne, sailing through the blue sky. She doesn’t even bother to run, just standing and smiling at the shot she’s let loose. “Told you to back up!” Brianne yells to the outfielders to try and fail to catch the ball. It lands well behind them and rolls into a thick set of bushes at the back of the open field.. “No killing it we said!” Yells the little boy occupying the would be kick ball mound. “That means you have to go get it!” Brianne suddenly remembers that little kink in the plan, her face curling up to much for her to tell anyone she didn’t know. She slowly trudges out through the middle of the field breaking into the worlds slowest jog. Gabrielle looks on at the drama, impressed that her big girl has decided to play nice without a fight. That is until the pitcher decides to stick his tough out at her as she passes...paying for it with a stiff punch to the shoulder, that he’s to busy clutching at to respond to. Gabrielle’s green eyes roll as she tries not to laugh. ***** ***** ***** The smoke from the “Black & Mild” cigar is carried briskly away as it’s blown free from Train’s mouth, his smoke rings breaking up almost instantly. He felt almost a little funny about smoking around the kids, but hell, it’s not like they could see him behind the bushes. He had no intentions of being part of the party... just near it... near them... near her.... She was still as beautiful as she was the first time she’d seen her. It had been way back around GTT4 when he’d found out about her. Life was a hell of a funny thing. He hadn’t seen hide nor hair of Gabrielle for years until he just happened to be walking down the streets of New York. They’d been more than a one night encounter when they met all those years ago, but it had been when they met again. What would have happened if she hadn’t dropped that picture from her purse in her haste to leave what she likely thought was a mistake? “It’d be a shit load less complicated.” Train chuckled to himself. The flight from Chicago to NY had offered plenty of time to think. His sisters words hung in his ear and the resonated the same way they always did. When he asked her wheter the’d be a good father her response wasn’t a nifty pat on the back and an “Of course you would” as he’d really been hoping the hear. Joanne was a single mother so she had an inkling of where Gabrielle would be coming from. “Tony,” Joanne had said to him being as gentle as he knew she knew how to be. “A good dad is somebody that can be there for their child. If you’re ready to have somebody in your life who’s needs are more important than what you think, feel or want. Then you can be a dad. But if you do this, there is no halfway. I know you’re scared, but don’t you dare walk into that child’s life and walk again cuz you’re scared. If you care about her then you’re in or you’re out... but it needs to be one or the other.” Train stood in the brush near the field of the park watching the party, knowing he had to be near it, but not sure he was ready to be a part of it. He’d been concerned enough to have some of his boys keep a few tabs on them... enough to know that it was her birthday coming up and that this party had been in the works for a while. He’d planned on walking up and being a part of the party, but the little talk back at home base had put it in prospective. Hell... he was protecting her feelings by not just dropping on her. He could care more about somebody else than himself. That’s why he was here.... in the bushes... hiding... it was for her. Train tries not to pull a muscle patting himself on the back. He’s trying so hard he barely even notices the red rubber ball as it rustles through the brush and comes to a rest by his feet. It takes him a moment to fully realize it’s there, but he finally bends stoops down to pick it up. Another round of quick rustling startles him. He shoots his head up, his dreads whipping back over his shoulders ready for whatever. Or almost. He freezes. A small pair of light brown place lock him in place. The look quizzically at him, seemingly taking his measure in a few short moments that seemed more like an eternity to Train. “Hello” Brianne says innocently. “Ahhhhhhhhh.... Hi.” Train responds barely able to form even that simple greeting. “Why are you in the bushes?” “What” Oh... ahh... Wait... Why am I? Why are you? Didn’t yo? momma tell you not to talk to strangers?” “You’re not a stranger.” “Yeah I am.” “No you’re not.” “Excuse me, I think I know if I’m a stranger or not.” “Obviously not.” “Excuse me?” “You can’t be a stranger I’ve seen you before.” “You have?” “Outside mine and mommy’s house. You were fighting with my uncle Chris.” Damn... She was right. Uncle Chris. An old nemesis from an old, long defuct fed Train had, had the displeasure of dealing with. The guy hadn’t liked Train from the second he arrived. It probably didnt? help that he’d slept with Gabrielle, his girlfriend at the time. He the big bastard just happened the have shown up the day he came to talk to Gabrielle. Train chuckled to himself. The kid had a mind like a steel trap. Fitting as that’s what he felt like right at the moment.... trapped. But still he could help but gaze at her. He couldn’t help wanted to look into her pretty little eyes. He couldn’t help but smile and she couldn’t help, but return the gesture. “Can I have my ball back?” “Huh” Ohhhh... Yeah. Here you go.” he said placing the rubber ball into her arms. “You should come have some cake.” Brianne said with a grin. No, no, no, no... he couldn’t do that. She was out there waiting. And actually... exacly how long would she wait. “Brianne”!” a woman’s voice, carried on the wind right into the brush where father and unknowing daughter locked eyes. “Ummmmmm..... Actually... I got a plane to catch. But.... Hap........... Happy Birthday.” “Thank you.” she said with another heart melting smile. He wanted to scoop her up and hug her. He wanted to run like hell. ***** ***** ***** Gabrielle didn’t run, hit a brisk walk as she strode across the open field towards the brush where her daughter had been for what felt like a little too long. As she approached the brush line Brianne burst out into the open running straight towards her mother. “Where were you?” Gabrielle asked the little girl. “I was talking to my new friend.” she responded gleefully. “New friend” Little girl haven’t I told you not to talk to strangers?!” “It wasn’t a stranger it was the stringy haired man.” “Stringy haired man?” “Yeah, you remember him. The one who doesn’t like Uncle Chris.” “Stringy haired...doesnt” like Uncle Chris? Baby who are you.........” It suddenly hit her. She slowly straightened, scanning the area. Nothing. “C...c...c’mon baby. Let’s go eat some cake.” ***** ***** ***** Train looked out the back window of the cab as the park disappeared behind him. He wanted to stay. Wished he could. Problem was... he had a schedule to keep.
