Karma — recent history (August 2007)

30 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.

Recent History

With an elegant gesture, Karma flicked his wrist, lighting a match. It burned out after only a few seconds, and he let it fall to the ground, its sulfurous fumes reaching his nose a moment later, burning his nostrils with its acridity. He shook his head; it was an unpleasant smell.

But again, he pulled a match from the packet and snicked it on his thumbnail. It burst into flames and burned almost down to his fingertips before her let it fall uselessly to the ground along with its twin.

It caused him, for a moment, to reflect on just how impenetrable many things were. To the ancients, fire coming from the tip of a stick would have been magic, to modern man only science, but how much could either reason really explain the physical processes that made the fire sprout from the little white match-head clutched in his fingertips?

He shook his head, answering the question inside his own head. People often settled for answers that were less than satisfactory, he reflected, based on reasons that were entirely their own, but were most often related to not wanting to make trouble, or buck the norm.

The thought occupied him perhaps because that was precisely what he was about to do. He stood outside the doors of the Global Wrestling headquarters, knitting his fingers together nervously into a web as he tried to make up his mind whether or not he should go in. But there was really no reason not to go in, not as far as he plumbed the depths of his brain.

They had called him back after he put in an application for employment. They had called him back presumably because they were interested in him and what he had to offer. He had looked over the resume that he'd sent them ahead of time. Briefly, he wished he could have conscienced embellishing it just a little bit. It wasn't that his credentials were unimpressive, merely that the promotions he had spent so much time toiling in for the past few years had been, all, so short-lived and pointless that he had never gotten anywhere of not in any of them.

He hoped that didn't reflect badly on him, but he worried that it would.

There was no sense in so much worry, though. And so he turned from the trash can that he stood by, walking towards the large glass revolving door that would lead him inside the building to his fate. Whether good or bad, he just had to trust that whatever happened would be for the best.

He paused, one hand on the revolving door, and turned back to the trash can. A sudden guilt overcame him, and he stepped back over to it, picking up the matches that he had carelessly discarded on the pavement. He closed his eyes in a silent apology, and put them in the wastebin, where they belonged.

As he pushed through the revolving door, cold air hit him in the face. At least the air conditioner worked, he mused. Almost immediately, he looked down at his hands, trying to avoid eye contact with the people that were milling about in the lobby. He generally hated being around crowds of people, but sometimes it was unavoidable.

His eyes widened as he looked down at his hands—underneath the fingernails was a good smudging of dirt. He couldn't have that, not going to meet his potential employer. It was enough to try to find something nice enough to wear; as he stood in the middle of the lobby, wearing his nicest pair of khaki cargo pants and a green sweater that brought out the olive hues in his skin, he wondered again if he'd made a mistake.

In a near panic, he walked up to the large circular desk.

“May I help you?” asked a bored-looking blonde woman.

“Um, yes... could you please direct me to the nearest bathroom with a hand-sink?”

The formality of his tone seemed to throw her for a moment. She blinked, but pointed down the hallway to the left with a long, manicured red nail.

“Down that way. Second set of doors on your left, can't miss it.”

“Thank you.”

She had already gone back to her typing and didn't seem to hear him. But he couldn't waste much time on worrying about that. After all, being late to his appointment would have been inexcusable as well. He turned and hurried down the hallway and into the bathroom.

Once there, the cold water across his hands seemed to revitalize him a little bit, to put things into a cool, clear perspective. He scrubbed his hands gently with the gel soap, and with the dirt swirling down into the sink, he took several deep, long breaths. Everything was going to be alright, either way.

Nerves assuaged, he walked back out into the lobby, and promptly back to the desk. The woman didn't look up.

“Um... excuse me?”

An aggravated sigh made him wince.

“Yes?”

“Could you please tell me where Bubba McCoy's office is?” he asked, in the same clipped and polite tone as before.

The woman looked at him with a withering impatience.

“Do you have an appointment?”

He nodded.

“Yes, I do.”

The woman yanked out an immense appointment book which thudded noisily to the white wooden surface of the desk.

“Name.”

She hadn't even looked at him. Karma felt wounded for a moment, but he quickly thought of how hard the woman's day had probably been already. He didn't imagine that working for a company as busy as this was fun, especially when you had to deal with every Tom, Dick and Harry that walked into the lobby.

“Karma...”

The woman looked at him skeptically, and his heart jumped a beat. Oh no... what name had he given them? Oy... Oystein? No. No, that wasn't it...

“Oyvind Oddmund, I'm sorry,” he said apologetically, “My ring name is Karma.”

She eyed him for a moment, but thumbed through the book, and nodded.

“There you are. Your appointment's in five minutes, so you better hurry. Mr. McCoy doesn't like to be kept waiting. Go to the elevator, and take it to the top. When it asks for your code, punch in 324-Star, and you should have no problems.”

“Three-two-four-star?” he questioned.

“Yep,” Again, she was back at work before he had ever stepped away from the desk.

Excitement took over, and he started quickly towards the elevator, before remembering his manners and turning back.

“Oh, ah... thank you.”

No answer.

He slid into the elevator, blessedly alone with his thoughts for a few moments as the craft shot up to the top of the towers. Not long enough, unfortunately, as he was barely calm again by the time there was a dinging to signify his arrival at his destination.

Slowly, to avoid error, he punched in the code. A moment passed. Then, a green light blinked, and the door slowly slid open on the top floor of the Global Wrestling complex. He could see outside another reception desk, where he would presumably present his credentials and go in to seal his fate... one way or another.

He took a deep breath, and stepped out onto the thickly carpeted floor. Fate would have to take care of him from here.
