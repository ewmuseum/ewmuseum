Former NFW U.S. Champion out of Retirement
Back in February of this year, former Newfound Wrestling superstar Xander Battleheart, better known to the wrestling world as Darcsyde, announced his retirement. Darcsyde retired from Professional Wrestling after 15 years, and was the last United States Champion to retire.

It didn't take Darcsyde very long to lace up his boots one more time. Earlier last week, Darcsyde was seen wrestling at Buffalo Wrestling Alliance, an Independent promotion in Buffalo, Colorado, and captured the BWA Heavyweight Championship for the third time, defeating his longtime friend and rival, Lynx.

Global-Online took notice of Darcsyde's retirement, and wanted to interview the new BWA Heavyweight Champion.

I sat down with the 7'0", 370-pound behemoth. He had the BWA Heavyweight title draped across his waist hours after winning it, and he looked better than ever. I asked Darcsyde why he wanted to come out of retirement. His rebuttal - a smile and a throaty chuckle.

"I couldn't stay away from wrestling," Darcsyde replied, the smile remaining on his face. "My body told me that I was too beat up to continue, and after 15, I had to agreed. So I went back home to Tombstone, spent five months with my wife and daugther, and I get a phone call from Johnathon Masters who runs BWA and wanted me to be his World Champion. I got to thinking about his proposal, felt that I was in pretty good shape, and shocked the world by becoming the champion for the third time. So my retirement wasn't set in stone; all I needed was a five-month sabbatical to rest all of my nagging injuries."

I asked him if he has a position in either Newfound Wrestling or Global.

"Joshua McClain called me after hearing that I won the BWA Heavyweight Championship, and he wanted me back in NFW," Darcsyde chuckled, reliving the phone call. "I said, 'Sure. I'll be in NFW to train the new talent once you get it back up and running. And I'll even do you one better - I'll wrestle in Global whenever you need me.' I've been wrestling independently since 2002, so travelling all over the place doesn't bother me."

I asked him what it was like to be on the road again, leaving his wife and daughter behind.

"They support me one hundred percent," Darcsyde said. "They were the ones who suggested I should go back into wrestling, as if they wanted to get rid of me or something." He laughed out loud, shaking his head. "I didn't think I was that big of a burden in the Battleheart household."

This is Noi Tsunami, signing off.
