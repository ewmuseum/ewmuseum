The Monster Turns 27!
Today marks the 27th birthday for Simon Anthony Kreed, known to the world of Professional Wrestling and in the sport of Mixed Martial Arts, "The Monster" Psymon. Happy birthday, Psymon.

Speaking of "The Monster," many are speculating that Psymon's career in Global is going down the toilet, and it all began when he lost the International Championship in a Three-Way International Championship Match at Fusion on June 15. He lost his title to Durian David Orthane, whom now is the former International Champion and sports an 11-1 record in Global, losing his title - and brandishing a blemish on his once-spotless record - to the most enigmatic superstar in Global today, Karma, at Whiplash. Speaking of Whiplash losses, Psymon lost to "The Joshua Kosidlo in an MMA Cage Fight, and lost his opportunity to enter the first round of the Master of the Mat Tournament.

I managed to sit down with Global's resident monster in an engaging interview. He is sporting a black eye after Sunday's battle with Kosidlo in the MMA Cage Fight. This marks Psymon's 4th loss in the sport of Mixed Martial Arts, and he said he was honored to have lost to Kosidlo. I found Psymon's remark rather odd. He chuckled at me when I asked him to elaborate why he was honored to lose to his most heated rival.

"Over the last three months, Joshua Kosidlo and I were at each other's throats. Kosidlo's obsession over Dr. Kasidy Drake and the World Championship was directed over to me. I was the International Champion and he wanted my title, so that started his quest to best me.

"I had two title matches with him, retaining and wrestling to a draw respectively, and after all of that, he had the audacity to pilfer my title. Even though he never tasted International title gold, he was successful in my never tasting International title gold again, and this enraged me. I'd never thought of a man of his notriety to do something so childish. I challenged him to something I knew he would never win: an MMA Cage Fight.

"I was 27-3; he had no MMA experience, but he bested me. After all this time, he has received a newfound respect from me, and I'm honored to lose to such a great fighter like Kosidlo. When he retires from Professional Wrestling, he will have a definite career in the world of Mixed Martial Arts, and hopefully, I'll get another crack at him."

I mentioned to Psymon that he did something he had never done in his career - whether it be in wrestling or MMA - he tapped out. I asked him how it felt to tap out for the first time in his career.

"Well, I want to point out that technical wrestling and fighting are beautiful and underrated styles. Nowadays, people want to see people beat the hell out of each other, want to see blood and want to see hardcore action. Whereas I'm a purist in my time, and I like to enflict pain in the art of submission wrestling or submission fighting. But Joshua Kosidlo gave me a taste of my own medicine. I'd never tapped out to anything in my life. I've had people place me in armbars, joint locks and chokeholds, and I would never submit. These holds would be applied for several minutes, and I wouldn't give in. But with Kosdilo, his technique and power overwhelmed me when he applied that Anaconda hold. I congratulate him for doing such a great feat."

I talked about the Second Chance Battle Royal and Psymon's role in the match.

"Well, I'll do my best to enter the Battle Royal and enter the first round of the Master of the Mat Tournament. If I don't make it, well, I'll find other ways to move up the Global ladder, and have my dream realized as a World Champion."

This is Noi Tsunami, signing off.
