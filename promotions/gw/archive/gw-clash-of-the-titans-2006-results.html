Clash Of The Titans By: Chuck Reno Who said I was only a Television Reporter? I know how to type, and besides, we need some additional presence here for all of our Internet fans. Now, I think we need to take a hard, long look at exactly what happened at Clash Of The Titans and what it could mean for Global for the rest of the year. Tag TeamChampionship The Enforcers (c) vs The Texas Wrecking Crew Now, this seemed rather poor of a match considering recent events. We had two great teams who we knew would put on a great match, but knowing The Enforcers were leaving made this a little anti-climatic didn’t it? The Enforcers had the infamous “Two Week Syndrome” before the match, so it was not surprising when they came in a little under prepared. Lots of back and forth, with both teams showing why they are top tag teams. Everyone knows I am no fan of The Syndicate, but I don’t like The Elite either. So I threw it up as a toss-up for the match as far as I was concerned, but someone had to win. In the end, the Texas Death penalty was final for The Enforcers as Ace Harding was put down for the three count. Now, The Elite wasn’t finished. We see Bill try to make the save and fail, but the possible final appearance of Ken Partridge, Jr. sent The Elite out on a high note when KPJ cleaned Pitt’s clock with Sweet Ken Music through the steel chair. Now with the Texas Wrecking Crew as new Tag Team Champions, who will they face next? The returning Hammer Brothers? Killer Pirates of the Outer Caribbean? We’ll keep a close eye on this one. WorldChampionship HardcoreMatch Brick (c) vs "Big Dawg" Jake Hix Once again, it goes without saying I am not a fan of Jake Hix. I am however beginning to like Brick. Not because he is talented, but because he was able to beat Jake Hix. There’s plenty of bad blood between these two men, and they were looking to settle everything in this match. Jake Hix was looking to regain his World Title, and Brick wanted payback for what Hix did to Top Dawg. The battle went all over the arena, and saw two men who seemed more interested in hurting each other than winning the match. Jake Hix went for several pin attempts, but Brick kept fighting back. Of course, when Brick looked in trouble we saw the reemergence of Top Dawg. Following Top Dawg we saw the newly crowned Tag Team Champions the Texas Wrecking Crew come in response. Sadly, in the end we saw Jake Hix capture the World Title for the second time. He also left Brick laid out in need of medical attention. Who will be the next challenge for our new World Champion? Let’s look to the Rumble. GlobalRumble Winner Advances to WrestleCade Main Event Talk about an event!?! This was absolutely crazy! I mean, anyone who could have predicted 10 Ninjas would come running into the Rumble had more insight into the event than I did. Kyo Kushinaga (Mike Biddle) In my experience, I always look at either one of the first two competitors or the second half of the draw to find a winner. Fazz wasn’t going to be a factor, but then we see HOF Jay Robson come out. Jay is presented with the opportunity to shake the foundations of the league with a run through the entire field, but alas, he did not make much of an impact. Decent showing, but not fantastic. Now, my favorites on learning entry order were easily Josh, Gorn Fire Hammer, Eryc Morgan, Darth von Doom or Mike Biddle. Gorn Fire Hammer had the size to win, but he let his own hatred of the Killer Pirates of the Outer Caribbean cloud his judgment. He could have done much more in this match if he had been focused. Josh was perhaps my biggest disappointment. He’s cocky, he’s talented and as long as he doesn’t mention that one streak he remains one of my favorites. Then he came in and accomplished…nothing. Darth von Doom looked like someone ready to make an impact of the evil variety, but ended up falling to lesser numbers after his ninjas were tossed out. Eryc Morgan fought hard, but it didn’t seem to be his night. Congratulations to him for overcoming the dark side the way he did. And at number 20 we saw Mike Biddle, and brains overcame brawn once again. Mike Biddle showed how great strategy was more important than outlasting anyone. In a move not seen since “The Brain” he was able to slip out and overcome all to take his title shot. More on that later…. Rumble MVP’s Who were the MVP’s of this event? Well, there were three in my mind and let’s count them down. 3. “Captain” Jack Morgan threw three competitors out of the ring and put on a spectacular performance. He may be getting even more attention now from the championship committees after that performance. 4. Kyo Kushinaga had what may have been the most impressive performance of the Rumble. He eliminated 6 competitors and 10 ninjas, and showed he is a dominant player. Despite not winning, he may have garnered the most attention from management and the fans. 5. “It’s The Win Stupid” should be what everyone writes on a poster to remind them of why Biddle is the MVP. You can toss out 18 people, but if you’re number 19 getting tossed then you’re a loser. Mike Biddle takes the win and the MVP award for the Rumble. Thoughts and Reflections Well, as stated, The Syndicate is not my favorite team. However, can there be any question as to them being the dominant team in the league right now? They walked out of COTT holding the World Title, the World Tag Team Titles and the next shot at the World Title. Is that the problem though? The seeds of destruction seem to have been planted by Mike Biddle being booked to face Jake Hix at Wrestlecade. Can Biddle trust the team where the majority of the members are all part of the same family? He can’t forget that if the situation where the build up to their match becomes heated Xavier and Pitt will no doubt side with their brother. So perhaps the greatest gift to come from COTT is that we have set The Syndicate on a course to rip itself apart. The intriguing aspects of the PPV involve Kyo Kushinaga’s great effort, Eryc Morgan and Josh not showing their best and the changing tides of Global. Kyo showed himself to be the top rising star in my opinion. He has the talent and drive to be the next major player on the scene. He is the man to watch. As for Josh/Morgan, both came in with high hopes that were smashed on the rocks. How many times has Josh made a splash only to retreat into the shadows? Does anyone consider this man a major player anymore when he keeps pulling the great disappearing act? Eryc Morgan seemed to be on the rise as we moved forward, but now we have The Elite abandoning their newest member, and we have him failing to live up to his own potential. A double whammy for Eryc this PPV. Finally, COTT shows us what is coming. We have new stars rising, old stars fading into retirement or the shadows, but more than anything else we have the great void. Global is filled with one thing more than any other, opportunity. With the departure of some superstars and the hunger of many young stars we may see a brand new day dawn.
