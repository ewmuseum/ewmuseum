THE RATINGS BOX. If you want a reason why people really did stick around through some of these early shows, this is a perfect example. Warren Hayes' little interview segment, and boy can Warren lay it on thick when he wants to. I've never seen Warren actually gush over someone (well, nobody other than Warren Hayes that is), like he does when he introduces and I quote... ============= He has been actually, one of the greatest men in the WWO and is one of the most recognised names in this sport. Synonymous with dedication, tenacity and intelligence, my guest tonight is not only a marquee name, but a name which imbues respect from others. He has, time and time again, proven his greatness by overcoming all the odds and walking out with his head held high. And he has done it again recently, despite everyone saying that a "certain gypsy" would get the best of him, he shocked the world and showed him that the is still THE MAN. ============= Usually when you see Warren making comments like this, it's usually about himself, but this time it's Randy Bryson. But you have to honestly see this interview in it's entirety to know just how good these two heels were... but anyway, Randy runs down the fact that he gets to name the stipulation match that he and Wuzho are going to have to compete in. And even Warren seems to be taken by surprise (although you can never tell with that actor) when Randy says it's not a penalty box match, because it'd be "too easy". (Too easy against Wuzho?) So Randy announces that the match that would make WWO history would be a Marathon match, a best out of nine match. Yep, a best out of nine. Okay, so five pinfalls wins, right? Nope.. not only is it a best of nine, but the decisions had to come in a certain order. Falls Count Anywhere, then a submission in the ring, then a no-DQ anything goes match, then a fourth "basic rules" match with no shortcuts allowed, and then a Texas Death match. Basically, nine different matches in one contest. And with Wuzho not being a submission wrestler, it was pretty much pre-ordained that there was no way Wuzho could get past the second fall. The interview itself was pretty lackluster considering, but then again, when you're setting up history, you can forgive some dryness. -- Then we get back to the ring, as Nekrataal and "Buzzsaw" Scott Sanders got underway. Scott Sanders was one of those rookies who came into the WWO who got underestimated by a lot of the fans, and ran off an impressive winning streak to start things off, being rumoured to be a hot contender for rookie of the year, but the wheels literally fell off going on a devastating losing streak. This match pretty much started the same way, as Sanders got beat down early in this contest, but managed to show some signs of recovery as he turned an armbar pickup into a small package for a near pinfall. But again, Sanders "downstreak" kept him from gaining any momentum, as an eye rake from Nekrataal regained the advantage. Fortunately, Nekrataal hadn't proven himself with the ring smarts, and after a little showboating after a couple of near pinfalls, walked right into a DDT and a piledriver. However, the frustration of the losing streak was obvious, as Sanders refused to go for the pinfall, and immediately began to threaten the referee for no reason. This gave Nekrataal the chance to low blow Sanders, but a blocked suplex led into another DDT by Sanders, followed by a face slam, into the chicken wing submission, the "Buzzsaw". Match: ** Not a great match, and the booking was definitely off, as it was obvious that a heel turn by Sanders was being hinted at, but with the series of moves followed by a pinfall victory, in effect killed off any effectiveness that turn would have made, and sometime shortly after this match, Sanders contract wasn't renewed. -- Mickey and Sam do a little more debate on the "best of nine" for Anarchy, as we go into Tracy Hudson and Night Stalker in a Television Title match. At the time, Tracy Hudson was one of the other hot "rookie of the year" contenders, and Night Stalker had won the TV title when Wuzho failed to show for an event (being arrested in Mexico by police, thanks to the influence (dollero) of Randy Bryson). On paper, this match was as Mickey put it, between two of the most underrated wrestlers in the federation. Things started off with Stalker complaining about oil or some other substance on Hudson's body, (later, we'd all be complaining about what might be IN his body), before catching Hudson with a cheap kick to the midsection. Stalker then went with the obligatory head to the turnbuckle action, and in the old school fashion, the first attempt was immediately blocked and reversed by Hudson, before taking him around the ring, and putting the champion face first in all four turnbuckles, and then hitting a running lariat that got a near fall in less than a minute in the contest. Hudson then went to the vertical suplex and again nearly getting a pinfall there (something that would be part of the reason Tracy used the vertical suplex as a later finisher in his career), before going airborne. But a moonsault from the top rope would miss, allowing Stalker to get a few taunts in the face of Hudson, before putting the rising superstar back down with a chop, and then a tilt-a-whirl powerbomb. And for the second time, the match was nearly decided in another two move combination. Stalker then turned to the leg of Hudson, working it over with a couple of knee drops and nerve attacks on the limb of the challenger, obviously wanting to set Hudson up early for the sharpshooter. A number more knees driven into the back of Hudson's leg would be followed up as Stalker pulled Hudson to the edge of the ring and drove Hudson's leg over the edge of the ring apron, again adding to the "old school" antics. After a warning from the referee, Stalker would get back in the ring, only to be small packaged by Hudson for a two count. Not appreciating the surprise pin attempt, Stalker laid into Hudson with the boots, and showed his vicious side, with a huge slap right to the face of Hudson that sent the challenger back to the ropes.. a fisherman's suplex (Stalker was renowned for having a great technical arsenal, just that he would never use it) by the champion would follow, but a foot on the ropes caused a break at two. Stalker then for some reason, went away from his assault on the leg of Hudson, and went with technical wrestling, getting a two and a half count from a leaping snapmare by the champion. Stalker showing his frustration with the official fired the challenger into the corner, before charging in with a corner splash, only to find Hudson sliding out of the way. Again, in a change of what we saw from Hudson at the time, Tracy firing off with a series of lefts and rights to the head of the champion, sending Stalker reeling back into the center of the ring, allowing Tracy to hit one of the sweetest standing dropkicks I'd seen in my journalistic career at the time. Stalker tried to leave the ring, but Tracy was right out on the floor after him, and put the champion head first into the ring post a couple of times, showing that he too had a viscious streak in him. A scoop slam on the guardrail brought a huge pop from the crowd. Hudson brought himself back in the ring to break the count, and then in an incredible moment that would make most daredevils cringe, Hudson went to the top rope as Stalker fell from the guardrail to the floor, and came off the top rope with a senton bomb to the floor. And somehow Tracy managed to pop up as if nothing had happened to him earlier in this contest. It's amazing to see how much Tracy has lost from his career thanks to the drugs and alcohol and such. Tracy put the champ back in the ring with Stalker, and a tombstone piledriver, a move that would later become one of Tracy's finishers, only got two (and probably the closest three count in WWO history at the time) from the official. However, Tracy left himself open for a swinging neckbreaker that put both men down on the canvas. At this point, Billy Club and Blade came down to ringside, for reasons' unknown at the time. With both men getting up to their feet at the same time, it was Stalker who managed to get the first shot in, hitting a kneelift right on the head of the challenger, before setting him up on the top rope and delivering with a superplex. And in one of Hudson's most resilient efforts, not only did he get out of the Superplex, but also out of front layout suplex that literally bounced the challenger three feet off the canvas after it hit, both times only getting 2 and 7/8ths. A missed clothesline by Stalker left the champ open for a spinning heel kick, and as Stalker got back up from that one, Tracy nailed the champ with a running bulldog, and you could see a new champion in the making, with each big impact shot. However, instead of going for the cover right then and there, Hudson went to the ropes, where Billy Club used the opportunity to shake the ropes and cause Hudson to straddle himself on the turnbuckle, at which point Night Stalker went to the top rope himself and hit the flying elbow drop, but again Hudson managed to kick himself out at two. You had to think that somewhere in here, a new champion was set to be crowned, when the booking from Hell set in, as Rusty Bowman and Wuzho both came out to the ring to go after the Lethal Weapons, as Night Stalker went to the floor to attack Bowman (who was rumoured to be the next TV challenger, although both he and Wuzho were obviously above the TV title level.) Hudson managed to get out to the floor to join the fight, in what ended as a double countout. Stars: ***1/2 A majorly disappointing ending to a fantastic match, especially with the realization of all the technical talent Stalker had and the aeriel abilities of Hudson as well. By the end of 1998, everyone in this feud with the exception of the Lethal Weapons were on the verge of becoming some of the next superstars of the World Wrestling Organization, but all had their runs stopped for various reasons. Hudson and Bowman would win the WWO US title, Wuzho would be the number one face in the company and on the verge of winning the World Title, and Night Stalker would become one half of Almost Better, who were in line to claim the WWO World title. -- Then a strange little ad for "Showstopper" Sperm. Even non-WWO members seemed to get their merchandise advertised on WWO shows. (ED. NOTE: No, this did _NOT_ refer to Zac Schneider. Thank God.) -- Then another "Night's" match as Night's Harbinger and Johnny Detson went at it in competition. Detson had been one of those young rookies who came into the WWO with Jude St. Claire, and had caught the eye of Warren Hayes, joining up with Simply the Best about the same time. It still surprises me to see Sam making any "anti-STB" at any time, but I suppose it was part of the fact that Sam was so opposed to the large number of heel stables that had dominated the World Wrestling Organization at this time, and of course, it was just one comment, that he probably got a group such as the Madhouse and Simply the Best crossed for one night. And then when Night's Harbinger came out to the ring, he brought with him the CWC crew of Al Perez, Scotty Williams, and Jose Garcez, well, Sam was even more verbal about the Madhouse. The start of the match had Detson elbowing his way out of a waistlock, before taking Harbinger down with a shoulder block, but Harbinger was right back up and daring him to do it again. However, Detson had other ideas, as he went under with a baseball slide, and catching Harbinger with a snap powerbomb, but only managing to get a one count out of the attempt. A savate style kick from Detson forced Harbinger to go for a rake of the eyes (seemed to be a favorite on this show), and reversed a wristlock before firing off a low blow on Detson, before backing the young rookie into the corner of the ring. This showed Detson's inexperience at the time in the WWO way of things, as he simply tried to walk out of the corner, and Harbinger simply arm wrenched Detson right back into it, before snapping him over to the canvas. A double knee to the arm would follow from Harbinger, before following it up with a T-Bone Suplex, but only a count of two would result. A dropkick and some more brawling from Harbinger would put Detson back in the corner of the ring. An irish whip across the ring ended up getting Detson elevated via a backbody drop, and Harbinger finally began to show concern about the STB members at ringside. However, Harbinger continued to assualt Detson, using the ring rope to choke on the young wrestler, before snapping him back into the ring. Using a distraction by Rick Wright, Hayes used the opportunity to trip up Harbinger, and pulled him out of the ring as the referee was checking on Detson. The CWC members all distracted by the antics of Wright, left an opening as Hayes put Harbinger face first into the floor, as the refere tried to separate Garces and Wright, as Simply the Best pushed Harbinger back into the ring where Detson was waiting, and delivered a fist drop to the head of Harbinger. However, the distraction of Wright and Garces kept the referee from getting back in time to register the full three count, only getting to two. Simply the Best, sans Warren Hayes, began to drive the Madhouse back up the aisle in a "mini-brawl", while in the ring, Detson was back in control, catching Harbinger with a crossbody off the ropes, but again, only getting a long two count. A swinging neckbreaker from Detson would follow again, but again, only a two count would result, and Detson would then using Harbinger's tactic of using the rope to choke out the other wrestler. After breaking the hold at the official's assistance, Warren used the distraction to nail Harbinger with a steel chair, but incredibly, all it seemed to do was wake up Harbinger, who sent Warren off the ring apron with a vicious punch. As Detson moved back in, Harbinger caught the "Blockbuster" with a discus punch putting the young wrestler down to the mat in one shot. A cover attempt only got one, but Harbinger had apparently had something snap in him, as he delivered headbutt after headbutt, holding Detson up in the ring before taking him over in a belly to back suplex, but again, only able to get a long two count of it. So Harbinger went to the ropes and came off the top with a somersault splash for what would have probably been the game winner, however, Warren, obviously smarting from the inability to affect Harbinger with the chair the first time, went back in the ring to attack Harbinger again, and drawing the disqualification, at which point, all hell broke loose on the floor between the Madhouse and Simply the Best. Rating: **. This was a match that simply didn't showcase Detson at his best, which would be late 1999, when it would take getting out from under the shadow of Warren Hayes for Detson and St. Claire to truly show what they were capable of doing. -- And then we cut away to a "satellite" location, in one of the strangest moves I can think of, Ric Studd and Michael Fulton, two gentlemen battled in an empty arena somewhere in Oakland. The word battle being the operative word, as this was nothing but a total squash. It got so ugly that .. well, it's not really deserving of anything to say except that this wasn't the worst part of the night! That was yet to come. -- Two bikers from "Los Angeles", the Centurions, came to the ring on a pair of Harley Hogs, and they announced that they were taking keen interest in an upcoming match between Thunderstorm and True European Excellence, and left a contract at ringside to take on the team of Thunderstorm. As we all would learn, the Centurions were actually the duo of Search and Destroy, who were looking to prove their critics wrong that they were washed up. In effect, S&D extended their WWO career through a fairly well-worked ploy. -- And Thunderstorm and True European Excellence II were set to meet in the ring next in a tag team encounter. Thunderstorm, well aware of the comments by Thunderstorm, verbally confirmed their accpetance of the match with The Centurions, but first they had to deal with Cerventes and Magnusson, two cohorts of Robert d'Artois. Marcus Thorn and Joaquim Cerventes would start things up with an overhead wristlock, which Cerventes would change to a side headlock thanks to the stomping on the foot of Marcus Thorn, before snapping Thorn over to the mat in a side headlock. However, Thorn would quickly use his size and power to pull Cerventes over for a one count, forcing Cerventes to rethink his position, and back up to his feet with Thorn in tow. Using his strenght, Thorn fired Cerventes into the ropes, and caught him coming off with a huge clothesline, and then the tag team expertise of Thunderstorm began to show through early here, as a scoop slam top rope elbow and a double leg drop from Thunderstorm got a couple more short two counts, as Thorn tagged out and back into the ring. Cerventes however, managed a blind tag to Magnusson as Thorn mistimed a backdrop, and as Cerventes ducked under a followup clothesline, Reiner Ver Magnusson caught the Thunderstorm member with an enzuilariat, before body slamming his own tag team partner on top of Thorn, and then catching David Holmes coming in the ring, and lifting him over head in a gorilla press throw. Preparing to lift up Holmes for a second time, Magnusson got clipped from behind by Thorn, allowing Holmes to go for a cover, but only scoring a two count out of it, as Cerventes made the save. Another tag between Thunderstorm members brought Thorn back into the ring. And again, as the official gets Cerventes out of the ring, Thunderstorm used the double team efforts, connecting with a double clothesline on Magnusson. However, again the pinfall would only get a two count, forcing Holmes to go down to wear down techniques, and into a rear chinlock. However, Holmes smaller size made it easy for Magnusson to get to his feet, and a few elbow laters managed to break the hold. With Magnusson running the ropes, Holmes tried to leapfrog over the big man, only to get caught in a vicious powerslam, nearly costing Thunderstorm their match. After a tag to Cervetnes, Cerventes caught Holmes in a hurricanrana and followed it up with a dropkick, but again, not able to keep the three count. Another tag between the Europeans allowed for a double shot to the midsection followed by a double axehandle, and again a tag was made between the Europeans, when the Centurions began to make their way down to the ring, using their double team effort to connect with a rocket launcher. Again, however, the count was broken up by Thorn, catching Cerventes with a back elbow. A double tag was made, and the two big men went fist to fist in the center of the ring, with the advantage going to Thorn, but the Irish whip was reversed by Magnusson, but the European missed with a clothesline, however, he wouldn't miss the second time, as the German caught Thorn with a sleeper, but Thorn was quick to counter with a jawbreaker, sending both men down and brining in both Holmes and Cerventes once again. Holmes was caught by Cerventes coming in, and quickly fired him out of the ring, but Holmes, landing on his feet, quickly went to the top rope, as the referee got the Spaniard out of the ring, and as Magnusson had a piledriver reversed into a backdrop by Thorn, Holmes came off the top rope with a flying elbow and quickly rolled out of the way as Thorn covered for the three count and the victory. Rating: **1/2. While the match didn't have heat, it was a sign that the tag teams were being forced to put some emphasis back in their tag team wrestling -- And then after a quick ad for Anarchy... and then.. well, there's only one way to put what I'm about to explain... A TOTAL DISASTER! A world title match was scheduled between Victor Braun and the WWO World Champion, Fury. Fury, had been built up as an unstoppable monster in the World Wrestling Organization, and had a lineup that pretty much kept the story line going. Victor Braun meanwhile had apparently sold his title shot to Jose Garces, the leader of the Madhouse stable, and had bought his way into a title shot (again, despite not being the playboy character of the federation. So now, instead of Victor Braun against Fury, you now had Garces competing against Fury for the World Title. However, it seems that "Ravashing" Ric Studd, who had come to the conclusion that he was losing favor with the WWO front offices, decided that he wanted to prove that he was still the man in the World Wrestling Organization, and came out to waylay Jose Garces and immediately began to call out the World Champion. And all hell broke loose literally backstage when Fury accepted the challenge. Fury came out to the ring, and proceeded to get manhandled by Ric Studd. (Now, let's not forget that just two matches earlier, Ric Studd was in some abandoned arena somewhere in Oakland.) The unstoppable monster was to get thrown around by Ric Studd, and in the rumoured "unedited" tapes of this show, it is claimed that Ric Studd simply laughed off any attacks by Fury, and after Studd put Fury in an ankle lock on the champion, Fury was claimed to have been heard to say "I quit, Mr. Studd, you win, you win". Unfortunately (or fortunately, as is more likely), this tape is rumoured to have been (permanently) loss by the World Wrestling Organization, however, the presented tape still showed Ric Studd literally manhandling the world champion. Rating: NO Match. A card that was decently solid in wrestling activity was literally destroyed thanks to one man's ego trip. In effect, Studd defaced Fury, the WWO title, and the World Wrestling Organization all in a three minute fiasco. After this show, the front offices pretty much put an ultimatum down to Warren Hayes and the Simply the Best crew, Ric Studd's career was officially being ended by President Stone, and in the worst possible way, and if they didn't dump Studd, they were going to be suffer a very similar fate. In effect, this show pretty much suicided the career of Ravashing Ric Studd, and for that point alone, will probably remain one of the most famous cards in WWO history. ------------------------------------ CLOSING BLURB by [name withheld] That's it for this week.
