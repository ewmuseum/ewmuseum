Welcome back. I guess that's the simplest way of putting it. This here is the WWO's wrestling magazine, and I'm your new editor, [name withheld], handler of longtime WWO jabroni Ultimate Thrasher, Ralph Nader voter, poutine lover and diehard fan of progressive Urdu dance music. And I'm also happy that it's 2001 and everybody can finally be satisfied that the new millenium has started. Now can we finally get New Year's back to being just about getting shitfaced? During the WWO's previous highly successful four-year run, this magazine was known as the Rye Report, Halfweek, and The Source. Since the Source was the last name used and I'm too stupid to think of anything different, the name will stick. I hope to keep up the quality of the previous versions of the magazine, but I also hope it lasts longer under my stewardship than the others, which were aborted due to real-life problems of the editors or, in the final instance, the closing of the fed. Most of my writing in this magazine will be done through my journalist character, Gary Hammond (who some of you might remember) who views the WWO through the cynical eyes of a "smark". I'll only drop the pseudonym for brief comments at the start of every issue, or when I feel like ranting about something out of character...like now. I mean, after all, it's only the return of the greatest damn e-wrestling federation in the history of the universe. What does the WWO mean to me? No, maybe the WWO wasn't as famous as some of the other e-feds out there. Maybe the cards didn't always come out on time, maybe some people got pushes at times that they didn't really deserve. BFD. To me, the WWO means reading someone's RP and laughing so hard that you wind up crying. Or reading a card and becoming so wrapped up in the action that hours can go by and you won't even realize it. Or making inside jokes on IRC at 3 in the morning that still get laughs years later. Most of all, though, the WWO means one thing to me, one simple word: Fun. And that's the only thing that matters in this hobby, right? So welcome back to Picky, Josh, Errol, Tommy, Eric, [name withheld], Dale, Jer and everyone else who I already know, and of course welcome to those of you new to the WWO...I'm sure we'll get to know each other in due time. And a big thank you to [name withheld], the greatest fed head ever, for getting things restarted....we promise not to give you ulcers this time around. :) If you want to send in articles for this thing, hate mail, or just want to gab for no apparent reason, the address is [detail withheld] ----------------------------------------------------------- #WWO by [name withheld] ( [detail withheld] ) Just a quick note that #wwo, on IRC server starchat, is a popular meeting place for many of our handlers, VPs and President Stone himself. Consider yourselves warned. :) THE HISTORY OF THE WWO (CLIFF'S NOTES VERSION) by Gary Hammond ( [detail withheld] ) Well, well, well. Look who's back. Just when the wrestling industry looked deader than Charles M. Schultz, the beloved double-dubya-oh is back from the grave. Now, I know some of you reading this aren't longtime fans of the fed, so as a public service I present to you this (somewhat) abridged version of the federation's history, just so you know who's who around these parts. Being the WWO's "official" unofficial historian, you will also occasionally see me write Retro Reviews of important PPVs in the federation's history, as well as other historical features, including This Week In WWO History. And off we go... Effectively, the history of the WWO can be divided into four eras, each of which produced its own generation of superstars: First Generation: 1996-early 1997 Major Stars: Ravashing Ric Studd, Tony Tobis, Master And Ruler Of The World, Vader X, Chris Stonebreaker, Hounds of Hell, Business Men, King $$$ Perfect, Ha-Ha Bandit, Derrick Michaels Notes: The WWO was founded early in 1996 by [name withheld]. I did not begin following the federation until the summer of 1997, so my knowledge of this era is sparse, but I pretty much know all I need and want to about it. The WWO's roster was HUGE, with over 100 wrestlers and nearly as many title belts, including the wretched Extreme title. Not all of these guys sucked, but some, like Vader X, REALLY sucked. I don't think there was really much at this point distinguishing the WWO from the millions of other federations out there, aside from an on-again, off-again feud with a midwestern federation, the WIF, that continued for over two years. In early 1997, Mark Stone took over as president of the federation and trimmed the roster by about 70 percent, eliminated the Extreme and Six-Man titles, and phased all of these guys out in favor of the younger, better Second Generation stars. By the fall of 1997, all the major First Generation stars were off the roster except for Ric Studd, who remained on the roster (despite leaving the fed at least twice) almost up until the closure of the WWO in 2000, although many believed that he actually died in 1998 but the announcement was just withheld for tax purposes. Second Generation: Spring 1997-Summer 1998 Major Stars: Randy Bryson, Andros, Rusty Bowman, Tyrant, Bellamy Albaneese, Iron, Underground Kingpin, Lord Robert d'Artois, Thug Life Organization, Lord Keeblor Notes: By spring 1997, it was apparent that Mark Stone was emphasizing quality over quantity...and these guys definitely had quality. Complex, interesting storylines and drawn-out feuds became the norm during this era. Not surprisingly, the WWO's popularity surged during the summer of 1997, as it became one of the world's most popular wrestling federations. The Anarchy and Season's Beatings '97 pay-per-views are both considered all-time classics. Unfortunately, the Second Generation eventually collapsed under the weight of its collective egos. Late in 1997, the WWO absorbed the WIF, which was led by [name withheld]. Although some great wrestling resulted from this, the WWO became effectively divided into two cliques and much behind-the-scenes sniping occurred. Iron's disastrous world title run caused irrepairable damage to the careers of Andros, Bryson, and Bowman, three of the federation's all-time greats. Iron and the rest of the WIF clique suddenly quit the federation in January 1998, leaving the WWO with three vacant titles and a largely alienated and decimated roster, leading to nearly complete creative bankruptcy and the near-death of the tag team division until the new Third Generation stars were able to pump renewed life into the federation over the second half of the year. Although a handful of Second Generation stars such as Bryson, Bowman and Kingpin remained forces to be reckoned with well into 1999, none remained a factor at the time of the federation's closing, and the only one from this group on the new WWO's roster is Lord Keeblor. Third Generation Summer 1998-Spring 2000 (overlapping with Fourth Generation) Major Stars: "Mr. Ratings" Warren Hayes, Wuzho, Fury, Ultimate Thrasher, Roundhouse, Uncivil Disobedience, Thunderstorm, Night's Harbinger, Al Perez, Jude St. Claire, Johnny Detson Notes: While the WWO floundered in the spring of 1998, with the extended reign of the horrid World Leaders as tag team champions, another aborted "invasion" angle (this time by the IWF/WOW), and taking television to new lows with "Attitude", two young stars who had cut their teeth in the mid-card while the Second Generation imploded provided hope for the future and won the adulation of the fans: Wuzho and Ultimate Thrasher, whose respective wars with Randy Bryson and Tyrant proved that a passing of the torch was taking place. Summer 1998 saw the meteoric rise of Fury, whose victory over Robert d'Artois for the World title showed that the Third Generation now reigned supreme, and the slower but no less spectacular emergence of "Mr. Ratings" Warren Hayes and Simply The Best, the most revered stable in WWO history. The additions of CWC imports Harbinger, Perez, Detson and St. Claire (among others) and the return of former WIF'ers Roundhouse, Search & Destroy gave the WWO its arguably strongest roster talent-wise ever. The tag team division underwent a total renaissance with Uncivil Disobedience leading the pack but vying for the titles with as many as half a dozen other teams at a time. Feuds became more complex and vicious than ever, as the federation's popularity skyrocketed. Unlike the first two generations, the Third Generation was never truly driven from power, but was diminished in early 1999 by the departures of Wuzho, Fury, and several others. However, Ultimate Thrasher, Roundhouse and "Mr. Ratings" Warren Hayes continued to reign as the federation's standard bearers up until the closure of the WWO. The only ones from this era listed on the current WWO roster are Thrasher, Roundhouse, Uncivil Disobedience and Johnny Detson, but I know for a fact that most of the others are still lurking somewhere out there and I wouldn't be surprised if they were seen again. Fourth Generation spring 1999-spring 2000 Major stars: Tyrone "Purple" Hayes, "Money Driven" Mike Sebastian, Apex, Latex Lex, "Prodigy" Tracy Hudson, Armed Forces, The Outlaws Notes: With this group, it's not so much what they accomplished as what might have been. Most of these stars emerged at a time when the federation was at its strongest, and were for the most part highly regarded upon their enterance to the federation (with the exception of Hudson, who plodded along for almost a year in mid-card hell before a heel turn revitalized him). These guys blended in quite nicely with the Third Generation in spite of the odd backstage political problem, and Mike Sebastian or Tyrone Hayes was seemingly poised to become the first World Champion from this group when the WWO closed up shop. However, Hayes, perhaps the WWO's best old-school heel ever, Lex, undoubtedly the sickest, and Apex (who with Hudson and Lex provided the two most painful-to-watch, in a good way I mean, matches in WWO history) are both around for the WWO's rebirth and I think one of those two will definitely wind up capturing the World title...which provides me with a convinient transition to my next article. ------------------------------------------------ WHO WILL BE THE NEW CHAMP? by Gary Hammond ( [detail withheld] ) Being the World champion of the WWO has, obviously, always been a very special distinction. So, one of the main questions on the minds of WWO marks everywhere is, who will emerge from The Bash as the WWO's top dog? Our last champion, "Mr. Ratings" Warren Hayes, is gone, but another man who previously held the title, Ultimate Thrasher, is very much on the roster, as are two others who were right in the World title mix before the fed closed, Tyrone "Purple" Hayes and Apex. Lord Keeblor has also wrestled unsuccessfully for the World title in the past. Otherwise, the field looks to be wide open. Nonetheless, it is my duty as an expert to give you skillfull prognostications on who will win, so observe, noting that these are my picks and do not in any way, shape or form reflect the opinions of the front office and should not be used as the basis for any wager (as tempting as that might be): TYRONE "PURPLE" HAYES Odds: 3-1 PRO: He's got the power, the skill, the knowhow, and the charisma. The World title only barely eluded him a year ago, and now it's his turn to reign supreme. CON: The World title only barely eluded him a year ago because he's a choker who's only won one out of a bazillion title matches in his career in the WWO, UEW and NLWA. APEX Odds: 4-1 PRO: He's literally willing to have himself killed in order to become champion, though it's more likely his opponents will end up with even more damage than he (just ask Latex Lex, that is if he's out of traction yet). He was just about as close to the title as Tyrone a year ago. CON: He's not devious, sneaky or ruthless, as most WWO champions have been. Besides, it'd be more fun to see him chasing after the title anyway. ULTIMATE THRASHER Odds: 6-1 PRO: He's held the belt twice before. If anyone knows what it takes to win the belt, it's him...and if anyone is willing to do whatever it takes to win the belt, it's him. CON: He's held the belt twice before, and it's time for the WWO to go in a new direction. ANDREW "FLASH" TUCKER Odds: 8-1 PRO: He's won titles everywhere he's gone. Success follows this guy like flies follow shit. CON: He's a cruiserweight. Cruiserweights have almost never won the WWO World title. LATEX LEX Odds: 10-1 PRO: Hey, it'd just be fun to see what he'd do with the title belt. CON: I don't see him upstaging Tyrone. "BLOCKBUSTER" JOHNNY DETSON Odds: 10-1 PRO: He's fought for titles in the WWO before, and actually has quite a good record in championship matches, witness his IP title victory. CON: Is he really any good without Jude St. Claire or Al Perez around? "THE SHOWSTOPPA" STEVE STARR Odds: 10-1 PRO: His ACWA record shows that he's a proven winner and can carry a federation. CON: This isn't the ACWA, and having a champion called "The Showstoppa" would be flat-out embarassing anyway. DARK SOUL Odds: 12-1 PRO: He was slowly but surely climbing the ladder of success when the WWO closed. I see no reason why that shouldn't continue now. CON: He lost a title to Miguel Quesada, for Christ's sake. Maybe he'll be ready in a year. "THE PROBLEM CHILD" CHRIS STRINGER Odds: 12-1 PRO: Like most previous WWO champions, he has an entourage to do his dirty work. Unlike most previous WWO champions, this entourage is obviously lackeys of his, not other wrestlers who could grow jealous and backstab him at any time. CON: He's undersized and a rookie. "THE MILLIONAIRE MARVEL" ERIC STRYKER Odds: 15-1 PRO: Hell, if all else fails he can just buy everyone off. CON: We've seen this act before, and better. LORD KEEBLOR Odds: 15-1 PRO: He has experience...and experience...and experience....and.... CON: Didn't he first join the WWO when Parts Unknown was still a colony of Portugal? "SMOOTH AS SILK" MIGUEL QUESADA Odds: 20-1 PRO: He's won a title before, and his rivalry (if that's what it really is) with brother Apex and Dark Soul could motivate him to greater heights this time around. CON: Does anyone actually take this guy seriously? BEHEMOTH Odds: 25-1 PRO: You can't beat genetics. He's the biggest and strongest on the roster. CON: No ring experience. "THE FUTURE OF PRO WRESTLING" CHRIS JOHNSON Odds: 25-1 PRO: He's shown before that he can be among the very best in the WWO when properly motivated. CON: He's shown before that he can really suck when not properly motivated, and he also has a record of choking in title matches. UNION JACK Odds: 30-1 PRO: The last two WWO champions were from the British Commonwealth, so why not one from England itself? CON: The highlight of his career was getting humiliated by the Society of Notoriety. STEVEN ERICKSON Odds: 40-1 PRO: He's a good cheater. CON: Almost everyone in the WWO is a good cheater, and Erickson seems to also be the latest in a long line of goth-type figures of ridicule in the WWO. MARKY MARVELOUS Odds: 100-1 PRO: It'd be nothing short of hilarious to see him win the title. CON: There's nothing wrong with this guy that I can see, except the minor detail that he can't wrestle. Like I said, it's a tight field. It might not be much in terms of quantity, but in quality I'd say we're off to a good start. And other people may have joined the roster by the time you read this, which could complicate things further. A major factor, of course, will be what format Mark Stone picks for determining a title. A battle royal will tilt things in the favor of big men like Behemoth, while a tournament will eliminate the handicap for men like Andrew Tucker. Either way, there's no doubt it'll be fun to watch. Next issue, I'll try and rate the tag team division....there were only four teams in the division at press time, so it's kind of tough to gauge at this point. ------------------------------------- A LOOK AHEAD by Gary Hammond ( [detail withheld] ) Here's the first of what will be a weekly look ahead at upcoming WWO action, with my thoughts on what should happen in each match: The Return of the WWO... THIS WEEK IN WWO HISTORY by Gary Hammond ( [detail withheld] ) January 7, 1998-Monday Night Extravaganza in Bismarck, ND saw Search & Destroy defeat the Dynamite Cripplers, Bellamy Albaneese defeat Lord Keeblor, Kelly Hart and Underground Kingpin battle to a no-contest, Robert d'Artois defeat Chris Johnson, Wuzho defeat "Half Rack" Clancy Simpson, Turbine defeat Black Caesar, the Assassin's Blood Clan defeat the Black Rock Clan in a six-man tag match, The Prophet defeat Terrible Travis Lee, and World Champion Iron defeat IP Champion Spawn. The show closed with a shoot by Stryke announcing his departure from the WWO, and would also be the last card before the departure of the WIF clique from the federation. January 12, 1998-Monday Night Extravaganza in Des Moines, IA took place under the shadow of the departure of the WIF clique from the federation, which vacated the World, TV and IP tag titles. Bruiser Bob Hacker defeated Chris "Assassin" Philips by disqualification, "Big" Al Marvelous defeated Masked Bulldog by countout, Angel of Death defeated Roundhouse by forfeit, the Dynamite Cripplers defeated the Excellence Squad, American Eagle defeated Andros, "Burning" Blaze Collins defeated "Chess Master" Miquel Khan, Bellamy Albaneese defeated Dr. Doom, Turbine defeated Wolverine, Kelly Hart defeated Underground Kingpin, and Ultimate Thrasher defeated the Bionic Team in a handicap match. January 14, 1999-Superstar Showcase in Manchester, UK saw Mark Stone declare that Wuzho and a partner of his choice must defeat Fury and Night's Harbinger in a tag team match in order for him to earn the #1 World title contender slot after illegally entering the New Year's Bash rumble with forged medical documents, Apex defeat Steve Dumars in the WWO debut of both men, Ultimate Thrasher, Roundhouse, Search & Destroy and Andros officially christen their stable the "Hall of Fame", Night's Harbinger and Fury form the Shadows of Malice, Colossus defeat Spawn by countout, Tyrant make his official return to the WWO, Thunderstorm defeat Dragonfire, "Money Driven" Mike Sebastian defeat Night Stalker to win the TV title, Ravashing Ric Studd defeat Tyr, Fire and Ice battle Johnny Detson and Jude St. Claire to a no-contest, Ultimate Thrasher defeat "Prodigy" Tracy Hudson, and Roundhouse defeat the debuting Techno Nic B (substituting for an "injured" Warren Hayes) in the main event, after which Hayes officially disbanded the Simply The Best stable and named his new alliance with Randy Bryson and Mike Sebastian "Even Better". January 8, 2000-Superstar Showcase in Sacramento, CA saw Randy Bryson beg Mark Stone for reinstatement on the roster, "Smooth as Silk" Miguel Quesada (wrestling for the first time under that name) and Union Jack battle to a double-countout, the Riche Brothers defeat Precious Moments, Mike Sebastian successfully defend his IP title against "White Hot" Darren Morgan, Vyolynce and Jack Lantern battle to a no-contest, Tyrone Hayes announce the "death" of Latex Lex, Lord Keeblor and Tracy Hudson battle to a time-limit draw, and Armed Forces defeat The Good, The Bad and The Ugly. A World title match between champion Roundhouse and Andros was scheduled, but was called off when Warren Hayes attacked Andros, leading to an eight-man brawl and culminating with Mark Stone scheduling a six-pack match for the title at Season's Beatings. ------------------------------------------- THE SOURCE OF RELIEF by [name withheld] ( [detail withheld] ) Well, that's it for this week, folks.
