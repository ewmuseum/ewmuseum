I m really, really flattered that my previous article on the RSPWF 200 made such an impact and actually got people to actually write articles to the Source. You owe me, Olvera. Thing is that, just as I suspected, most people considered it to be a bitchfest on why the WWO didn't make it on the list. To quote the article from one F. E. San Miguel about what I has to say about the RSPWF 200 "My comment to your writers incensed by the RSPWF 200 [...] is simply this: get over it and move on ". He demonstrates clearly he didn't really read the article, skimmed over it, and merely dismissed it as a another RSPWF crybaby who has to be put in his place . Okay, so I admit to getting carried away a little in my overall frustration to the WWO being ignored again. It just seems to me the WWO had a good year in its belt and once again failed to garner attention to it. Let's be honest here and state that since UEW and EMWC have become emblems in the field of our sport, many people don't care about what's going on with other federations and turn to them as the final authority on what's going on. You're blind and stupid to think otherwise. But as I said, my article didn't focus specifically on that. First of all, the essence of my article stood around the fact that I was disgusted that Darren Morgan hit the list, while other much more deserving WWO-ites didn t. Not that I was kept off the list. Jesus-Christ, I m not even a wrestler. Why am I complaining about it? Because I love the WWO, I love the standards it set for itself, and it just made my stomach cramp to see that Darren Morgan was thought to be a standard-bearer for the WWO. That was the main point of my article. That, my friend, isn't complaining, it's called commentary. That's what I m paid to do. Second of all, if there's one thing which is just as annoying as the people whining that their favourite wrestlers didn't make it, or make it high enough, it's those who get on their high horses, get all condescending-like and spew the Get over it, it's a popularity contest, it doesn't mean anything garbage. If the RSPWF 200 really didn't mean anything, then why is it still being done year-in and year-out? Why do people who are placed on the list make sure to point it out on their resumes, cut promos about it, and remind people that they were there? Why is it one of the most anticipated events of the year? Why are other top 200 attempts considered to lack credibility? Why do people discuss the choices that were made, wondering if the #1 spot is indeed representative of the past year in wrestling? Why do people comment on, for example, Valtharius The Mad's loss of positioning this year compared to last year? Why is all of this taking place if the list means nothing and we should all dismiss it? Why should we even vote? Basically, the whole "it's not important" argument holds about as much water as Swiss cheese can. Fact is that the Top 200 list is a benchmark, albeit a convoluted one, because it was presented and accepted as one. It shows itself as the margin for the top wrestlers in the business, while in fact it is, and should be called as such, a People's Choice list, as Mr. San Miguel so justly put it. Maybe it's all in the presentation of the list; yes, image is everything, and maybe if it continued to simply show itself as The Top 200 People's Favourite Wrestlers list, rather than trying to pass itself off as a legitimate example of who's best and who ain t, then maybe discussions like this would no longer be needed. As long as attempts at presenting some as better than others continue, no matter how fun it may be, elitism will continue and a self-eroding star-system will continue to gnaw at the heart of our sport. I also think that Mr. San Miguel needs to understand that there is a fine line between commentary and plain whining, and should also understand that I am not paid to solve problems, I m here to stick them in everyone's face so they can have something to discuss over dinner. If he saw my article as nothing but whining, then I sincerely believe that he has to lose the reflex of automatically interpreting anyone saying something negative about the Top 200 as whining . I m mad as hell that the WWO didn't have more people on the list, and from this commentator s perspective, from his seat in WWO headquarters, in his dingy little office on his old Pentium 133, the Top 200 does not represent the WWO well. If anything, my article was more of a wake-up call to the WWO than anything else. Besides, it's not as if anyone in the WWO really listens to what I have to say anyway. It's not like I was the final authority on everything going in and out of this place. In any case, I would like to thank Mr. San Miguel for taking the time to write to the WWO. Fan feedback is what we all live and thrive on. I hope he will continue to enjoy the WWO as much as you seem to. Reporting for your intelligence, I m Nathan Nesbit. ======================================== From the Office of the President. "Speak up. Silence gets you nothing." by [name withheld] comments: [detail withheld] ======================================== After reading something in last issue of the source, I definitely have to wonder, who's telling everything they're claiming. And no, this is not calling anyone dishonest, as I understand people's opinions change. However, the following is an excerpt from Editor Dave's closing commentary from the last issue of the Source. **** Is a fully written card worth the whole federation? No. Most of the handlers I have spoken to would be more than happy to accept a small recap or just the results, so we can get on with the fed. **** Four weeks ago, when I first announced that there would probably be a delay in this showcase, I went to a number of individuals asking them, if I should convert this Showcase into a recap/results card. The response that I got from the handlers to whom I talked to, was "No. The full format is one of the best that I have ever read or seen. If the card is delayed by a little, so be it.". Yet, I honestly believe that a few individuals did go to Dave, and did inform him that they would be happy with a results/recap show. If everyone would have been happy wtih a shortened show, with the standard in-rings and 'short' results, why was I not told when the original suggestion was made. In fact, it was only _this_ past week that anyone brought the suggestion to me. And if it weren't for the fact that part one was nearly completed, and a number of the volunteers for the week hadn't already taken match assignments, then I would have been more than happy to try to get something out sooner in the above format. Even after a couple of letters to the list, informing them of my situation here in reality, no one brought this suggestion to me. As such, I was assuming, and apparently wrongly, that everyone was okay waiting for the full results. My point to this is, if people have suggestions that they think will keep everyone happy and keep the federation flowing, then please, pass them on. I don't bite people's heads off because they think they have an idea that will improve the organization. Granted, not everything that's mentioned is going to be used. But if people don't say anything, then I can't read minds about what everyone wants. Speak up and you will be heard. Keep silent, and things end up status quo. ======================================== Tag Team Top 60 Courtesy of: Jay Ralston Comments/information: [detail withheld] ======================================== 60. Kentucky Fried Outlaws (Marcus Apollo & Johnny Conquest) [LWC] (31) - The Kentucky Fried Outlaws, one e-wrestling's most bizarre tagteams, stepped up their game in 1999, becoming a standout in the always-tough LWC Tag Team Division. Rich & Infamous (Vince Valor & Hayvin Coules) [EMWC] (17) - The Rich and Infamous proved this year that you CAN teach an old dog new tricks. The former MLWO Tag Champs rocked the EMWC's world earlier this year, owning the tag titles for a good time. Apparently, The Rich and Infamous were too good for their own good, as they ended up fueding with the only people as good as they were- themselves. Alas, even though the Rich and Infamous may be gone, their memory remains OH SO SUGARY SWEET-AH!!!! 21. The Benjamins (Bobby & Joe Benjamin) [EMWC] (6) - For the early part of 1999, the EMWC's Tag Division was all about the Benjams, baby. The brotherly team of Joe and Bobby dominated EMWC's tag division, and the tag titles, for a good while, before eventually heading off into retirement. Even with a shorter year, the Benjamins made a large enough impact to warrant a stellar ranking. 20. American Dragons (Joe Scalerico & Bob Ivery) [UWF] (139) - The American Dragons burst onto the scene earlier this year, and their stock has been rising ever since. Amid a slew of other rookie tag teams, the Dragons fought their way to the top of UWF's tag roster, no small feat, to say the least. A fued with Too Damn Sexy, one of UWF's top teams, and a big United States Tag Title win have assured the American Dragons a spot atop the UWF's Tag Rankings well into next year. 19. Armed Forces (Navcom & Nefcon) [WWO] (UR) - It's been a tumultuous year for Navcom and Defcon, WWO's very own Armed Forces. Whether upholding the laws of the WWO, fighting off the Fun Loving Criminals, or trying to be a good example for the people of America, their job is just never easy. But Navcom and Defcon do it very, very well. The current top contenders to the WWO World Tag crowns aren't afraid to make a stand, and next year they may very well stand even higher on this list yet. 18. Nine Inch Males (Rob James & Glen Hudson) [LWC] (UR) - Wugline may not have made a lot of friends this year, but they sure made a lot of bitches. Glenn and Rob, long-time LWC Tag Champions, continued their winning ways this year, holding on to the titles until LWC's closure earlier this year. As if holding the titles in a fed like LWC wasn't impressive enough, the men too big for Hollywood managed to make it through the entire year without suffering a loss. And, hell... Chicks dig 'em. 17. The Crazed Animals (Gorilla & The Psychotic Doctor) [AWMC, UEW] (45) - Veterans of the tag team ranks, the Crazed Animals hit stride in 1999. Winners of quite a few titles in the past, The Gorilla and The Psychotic Doctor began the year with a bang, gaining the AWMC Tag Team Titles in January. And in October, they added to the trophy case by nabbing the UEW Ultimate Tag Team Titles as well, which they hold to this day. The combination of wily veteran Gorilla and the... well... psychotic Psychotic Doctor is time-tested, tried, and proven. And they aren't likely to slow down in the year 2000. 16. The Fabulous Ones (Sho Satsuma & Agito Nakajima) [UWF] (3) - The Fabulous Ones, perpetual UWF Tag Champions/Contenders, kept up their tradition of excellence this year, winning titles left and right. Early in the year, the Japanese Superstars managed to capture the EMWC Tag Team Titles, which they held on to a for a good while. In addition, a UWF United States Tag Team Title reign and a heated fued with The Machines that ended in them being banned from UWF for a year solidified not only their top spot, but their "legend" status. 15. The Wildcards (Jack Baldwin & Judd Morley) [UEW, WOW, WWO] (79) - It's usually Wild when Black Jack Baldwin and "The Gambler" Judd Marley are around. And 1999 was no exception. Baldwin, Marley, and their manager "Godfather" Vito Scapelli know how to make things interesting. The multi-year veterans are finally getting their due, as one of the upper class of tag teams in the sport. 14. The Down Boys (Dan Oliver & Adam Peterson) [EMWC] (18) - The Down Boys, easily one of the most recognized and praised tag teams in e-Wrestling, continued to show the world just why they're considered legends this year. Following a stellar fued with the Epitome of Cool early in the year, the Boys went on hiatus through most of the summer. Unfortunately for then-champions Strictly Business, the hiatus was short lived, as the Down Boys came back and won the EMWC Tag Team Titles immediately. As of press time, the Down Boys have yet to relinquish the belts, and I'd hate to be the team that tries to take them. 13. Bad Ass Muthafuckaz (Lance Jordan & Michael Spears) [AWMC] (11) - 12. Partners in Crime (Benny McGregor & Jimmy Randall) [UEW] (35) - When you're good, they remember you. Jimmy "The Dealer" Randell and Benny "The Knife" McGregor, collectively known as the Partners In Crime... along with "trainer" Joe The Chair and his wife Paulina The Chair, of course... competed for half of the 1999 year. But in that time, their work was unforgettable, and their UEW Television Tag and Ultimate Tag Team title reigns were proof of their standing. The Partners have moved on, and rumors persist that Joe and Paulina have little stools to take care of, but it's still a good idea to "lock your doors, and pray they ain't in your hood." 11. The Explosion (Fire & Nitro) [UEW, IWE] (4) - Fire and Nitro wrestled for about two-thirds of 1999. But it took only about that long to stamp their names in history books. The two men racked up a winning streak that most teams only dream of, defeating teams such as the Suicidal Stallions and the Perfectly Perfect Duo, on their way to TWO UEW Ultimate Tag Team title reigns this year. Threats of a return loom on the horizon... if true, it would be an explosive situation, wherever they surfaced. 10. The Machines (Simon Oneal & Paul Wong) [UWF] (41) - If any tag team this year made a stellar impact within their fed, it was The Machines. Wong and O'Neal tore through UWF this year, leaving a lot of hurt wrestlers, a lot of bruised egos, and even more pissed off fans in their wake. Their huge year culminated with an underhanded victory over UWF's "Franchise" team, The Fabulous Ones, in a Loser-Leaves-UWF-For-A-Year Match. If that isn't Top 10 Material, I don't know what is. 9. Zokugun Sangui (Kashan Akuma & TORA Wanizame) [EMWC, G-Pro] (UR) - Talk about a Cinderella story. Two unlikely Japanese anti-heroes come into the biggest fed in America, and kick some gaijin ass. ZS showed their resilience, beating everyone EMWC had to offer, despite a crippling burn wound to TORA's ketsu. While their reign as champions was brief, as the team left the fed soon after their win, it was nonetheless remarkable. 8. Too Damn Sexy (Craig Miller & Johnny Caparti) [UWF] (80) - Too Damn Sexy, two of UWF's most talented, and stylish, wrestlers, had a career year this year. Between a near- 4 month UWF Eurasian Tag Title Reign, a big win over The Machines for the UWF United States Tag Titles, and fued with the American Dragons in which they kidnapped their own manager and teased a breakup, only to face the Dragons in full force, Too Damn Sexy was just too damn good. 7. Token White Boys (Billy Kane & Corey Atari) [GLOW] (UR) - TOKEN WHITE BOYZ IN DA HIZZZZZOUSE~! If there's one team on this list that can really, without a doubt, be called the most enjoyable team to read, it has to be these two cool cats. Corey Atari and Billy Kane blindly work their way up the ladder of each and every fed they make an appearance in. Whether it be playing old school 80's video games or trading Pokemon cards at Burger King on Tuesdays, with this combination of craziness there's one thing that's constant - They succeed. Whether they realize it or not, these two guys will be one of the most watched tag teams of the new millennium. 6. The Harlequins (Tragedy, Chaos, & Terror) [UWF] (85) - The Harlequins, former IIWF Tag Team Division Mainstay, brought their unique blend of practical jokes and unbridled violence to the UWF this year. Their success was helped along when their manager, Comedy, became interim President of UWF. Capturing the prestigious UWF United States Tag Titles, the Harlequins put an end to naysayers and proved that, hehehehehe, they still got it. 5. The Outlaws (Brent Maverick & Doc Holliday) [UEW, WWO] (1) - Brent Maverick and Doc Holliday have had an active year, in singles AND tags. The two Arizonans spent the early part of the year solo, and besides a couple of matches in the soon-to-close SJPW, they competed in several interfederational cards, where they excelled. Upon their return to full-time tag teaming, this time in the WWO, they continued a roll which has continued to this day. As of date, The Outlaws have not lost a match all year, and they seem ready to make another run for gold in 2000. 4. The Bounty Hunters (Brody Thunder & J.W. Hardin) [EMWC] (84) - Two former Singles #1's, Hardin and Thunder proved to the world that they still had it, coming into the stacked EMWC Tag Division and brutalizing opponents left and right. The two big Syndicate members made waves throughout their brief stay in EMWC, having a brief but vicious fued with then-champs Strictly Business. Sadly, all good things must come to an end, but it appears that not even "The Outlaw's" retirement can put a damper on this party. 3. Fraternity Boys (Gus Zampitella & Chris O'Brien) [MBC] (7) - EMWC icons and partyboys extraordinaire The Fraternity Boys spent 1999 where they spend every other year... on top. When they weren't kegging around, the Boys were busy capturing the EMWC Tag Titles and engaging in a fued with Zokugun Sangai. The term "legend" was coined for guys like these. 2. Epitome of Cool (Andrew Sterling & Dan Thomas) [EMWC, GLCW] (103) - Andrew Sterling and Dan Thomas are regarded in most circles as the greatest EMWC Tag Team of all time. They hold the record for the longest reign as EMWC World Tag Team Champions and there's good reason for it. Running roughshot through just about every team they came in contact to, these two made a huge impact in 1999, and a huge leap from 1998. Jumping 101 spots from #103 to #2 this year, they "retired" back in August after dropping the EMWC Tag Titles to Strictly Business, yet have just recently showed up in the GLCW, where they are sure to pick up where they left off and continue their winning ways. 1. Strictly Business (Andrew Tucker & Mike Sebastian) [EMWC] (UR) - Strictly Business was easily the most dominant team of 1999. Immediately upon their entrance to EMWC, "Money Driven" Mike Sebastian and Andrew "Flash" Tucker, two "EMWC Rejects" rocketed straight to the top, and at the expense of top teams such as Zokugun Sangai and The Epitome of Cool. Definitely the best tag team in what is arguably the strongest tag division in e-Wrestling, Flash and Cash earned their spot... and then some. Congratulations, guys, you truly are the best. ======================================== Contact Information inquistion by: David Olvera comments/information: [detail withheld] ======================================== Do you want to work with a certain handler, or just talk to them on one of the many popular chat clients?
