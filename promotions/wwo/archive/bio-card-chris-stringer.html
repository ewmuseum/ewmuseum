WWO bio card — "The Problem Child" Chris Stringer

29 May 2001

"The Problem Child" Chris Stringer Real Name: Christopher Szczesnewski Birthdate: 08/24/80 Wrestling Place of Origin: Portland, Oregon Real Place of Birth: Libertyville, Illinois Height: 6'2" Weight: 231 Alignment: Diabolic Entry Music: "The Real Slim Shady" by Eminem Manager: Glen Peeps Managerial Profile Manager: Luke Morningstar Managerial Profile Valet: Cherry Bomb Managerial Profile Physical Appearance Chris Stringer is white, generally considered to be very attractive by the opposite sex, and has a medium build. Fairly defined body, but not overly cut. He has dirty blond hair, which is cropped close to his head and spiked up, and blue eyes. He has no tattoos or other distinguishing features, outside of his trademark "cat-that-ate-the-canary" smirk. To the ring, he wears black tights and a black robe with "Got a problem?" emblazoned in red letters on the back. He also usually wears wraparound sunglasses to the ring. Psychological Makeup Stringer is both Machiavellian and utilitarian in his approach to life, as well as wrestling. There is very little he will stick at, unless the reprecussions far outweigh the potential benefit of his underhanded actions. He sees other people as pawns to be used to satisfy his needs and ambitions. However, he understands that blatant control of other people could turn them against him, so he is far subtler in his manipulations. He possesses a great understanding of human nature and, much like Shakespeare's Iago from the play "Othello," knows how to take the best parts of another person and bring it together with their darkest side to achieve Stringer's desired results. Stringer possesses near-genius intelligence. Strengths Intelligence. Not only in creating his schemes, but in understanding when to try high-risk moves and when to leave them alone. He will not make many "rookie" mistakes. Also, he may notice the crowd reaction if a face is about to attack him, and react quick enough to avoid it. Complete lack of ethics. Not afraid to use and abuse other people if it will get him what he wants. He probably has never really felt guilty in his life. Great natural athleticism. Agile enough and strong enough to wrestle in a variety of styles. Weaknesses Overconfidence. Make no mistake; his Plan A's are brilliant. However, he's so sure Plan A is going to work he never bothers to come up with a Plan B, often leaving Glen, Cherry and Luke to improvise something on their own. Lack of experience. WWO is Stringer's first fed. He may _think_ he knows everything about professional wrestling, but a more experienced wrestler may frustrate him in the ring. Inability to fight against extremes. Stringer is a solid wrestler, but he does not really excel at any one style. So, when confronted with an exceptional high-flyer, or a very strong wrestler, or a technical wizard, he will struggle to adapt, and have to rely on his cheating to get him the win. About the only guy he would really match up well against is a devious heel, because he will most likely be able to think up of a better plan than his opponent. Biographical Information Christopher Szczesnewski (pronounce shez-NEV-ski) was born to an affluent pair of lawyers in the Chicago suburb of Libertyville, Illinois. He always was near the top of his class growing up, but he also was a wild child, and his parents were either unwilling or unable to discipline him. He was caught shoplifting more than a few times in middle school, but his parents were always able to use their connections to keep his record clean. After his father told him in no uncertain terms that his parents wouldn't help keep him out of trouble anymore, Chris just got his friends to steal stuff for him. By high school, Chris was no longer at the head of his class, having decided that schoolwork was "too boring" of a test for his intellect. However, he still got great grades by getting along on brain power alone. He played football in high school, and remained highly popular as the team's star wide receiver. He also starred in basketball and baseball. Everything came easy to Chris, playing sports, making the grades, getting chicks, everything. He got a 1430 on his SAT's and went to Rutgers University in New Jersey. Between the end of his senior year of high school (in 1997), and his freshman year of college, Chris finally figured out what he wanted. Money. Popularity. Fame. Fast cars, faster women. And he didn't want to work hard at it. The popularity and fast women came easy. Chris was naturally good looking, and his understanding of how to manipulate people made it easy for him to come off as a great guy to hang out with. The money, fame, and fast cars did not come so easy. His parents were only giving him $1,000 a month in allowance, and how the hell is a guy supposed to by a Lambourghini with that? So Chris first got involved in a point-shaving scheme with the basketball team. The scheme was exposed, and Chris' record was kept clean in exchange for fingering other bettors. He then ran a one-man bookie service, which brought in some money, but not enough for his tastes. Finally, he tried to set up an internet Ponzi (pyramid) scheme with fellow computer engineering major Shanna McDonough, which resulted in him finally getting booted off campus in late 1999. (There were also unsubstantiated rumors that Chris performed sexual favors for a female professor in exchange for favorable grades.) Without a college degree, Chris' paths to money and fame became severely limited, as his family swore him off as a black sheep and his father gave him $100,000 and told him to never come back. Chris had to fall back on his athleticism, but he refused to play any minor league pro sports, considering it to be beneath him. And bouncing would have been slumming, and wouldn't have brought him fame, anyway. So that left one option: Professional wrestling. He had seen some ACWA cards while he was in New Jersey, and was always impressed with former ACWA Champion "Showstoppa" Steve Starr. After the ACWA closed down, Starr went back to Seattle, and needing income, opened a wrestling school. Chris, having since changed his name to Chris Stringer, basically showed up on Starr's doorstep and said, "I think you're the best in the biz Here's 25 grand. Now teach me how to wrestle." Starr, impressed with both the amount of cash and Stringer's brashness, took him up on the offer. Starr had only one other student at the time: the athletically challenged Glen Peeps. Starr quickly realized Stringer had the athletic ability and the necessary dubious ethics to go far in the business, so Starr concentrated on developing Stringer. Chris, realizing that Peeps could be a useful lackey, began buttering him up, pretending to take Peeps under his wing, and promising to take him along for the ride if Stringer ever made it. By mid-2000, Starr had taught Stringer everything he knew, or rather everything he wanted to teach him, and said, "You need to get out of here and get into a real fed. And if you find a place, let me know, because I need some real action, too." Stringer called up his old college friend Shanna and invited her to join his "posse" under her nickname Cherry Bomb, which she readily agreed to. Peeps tagged along for the ride, and Luke Morningstar was picked up in a South Dakota biker bar when the trio realized they needed some hired muscle. This is the group that Stringer brings with him to the WWO, always following the golden rule -- do unto others before they do to you. Wrestling Information Wrestling Styles/Grades Daredevil Fair High-Flier Good Power Fair Technical Good Submission Fair Cheating Excellent Brawling/Hardcore None Escapability Fair Stamina Good Ring Awareness Very Good Teamwork Poor Top Wrestling Moves Lights Out -- An inverted tiger-clutch faceslam (Christian's Impaler or Little Guido's Kiss of Death.) Screwdriver -- a vertical suplex into a piledriver. (Only used on smaller opponents) Flying elbowdrop Northern lights suplex Short clothesline Low blow (preferably when the ref is distracted by Cherry Bomb or Glen Peeps) Rollup while pulling tights for leverage. Inside cradle, then place feet on ropes for leverage. Sleeperhold Salt throw to eyes. Finishing Move: The Problem Solver -- Stringer hooks his opponent in a front-facelock, then runs and leaps on the second turnbuckle, leaping off and spinning back into the ring to drive his opponent's head into the mat with a DDT. Basically, it's a 360-degree Tornado DDT. This is exactly the same move as the Superstar Spike, the finishing move of Stringer mentor, Steve Starr, but Stringer doesn't even have the respect to use his mentor's name for the move.
