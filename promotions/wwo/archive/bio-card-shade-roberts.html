WWO bio card — Shade Roberts

26 May 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Shade Roberts Real Name: Shade Roberts Height: 6'2 Weight: 243 Date of Birth: Alignment: Scrupulous Hometown: San Diego, CA Entrance Music: "Back in Black" by AC/DC Manager: None Psychological Makeup Strengths Strength #1: Experience: Shade has been in the game a while. He knows how to use every inch of the ring to his advantage, and he has faced every type of wrestler you can think of. As such, he is often prepared for even the worst of situations. Strength #2: Technical Wrestling: The man is the best technician you've never heard of. He knows 1,000 ways to take an opponent down, and even more ways to make them tap out. He has a vast knowledge of moves, especially submission, and counter-wrestling is almost second-hand to him. In addition, he frequently uses chain wrestling, comboing moves together to help hold the advantage (see moves for examples). Strength #3: Style: Shade's style is unlike most you've seen. His entire style is defense-orientated, as he rarely, if ever, makes the first move. Instead, he'll sit back, allowing the opponent to make the first mistake, at which point he will capitalize heavily. And once Shade has the advantage, it's difficult to get it back without tagging out. Weaknesses Weakness #1: Trust: Roberts has always been a "lone wolf". He trusts one person in the WWO, and one person alone: Don Cameron. And even that trust is not complete, as Shade is always watching his back, just in case. Weakness #2: Hardcore: The guy doesn't like hardcore wrestling. In fact, he despises it. He considers it a "bastardized form of wrestling" and takes it personally that hardcore wrestlers would "disgrace the ring". He tends to pick vendettas against hardcore wrestlers, or even wrestlers who have a few too many hardcore spots, which generally gets him a chair to the head because of his refusal to use weapons. Weakness #3: Speed: Shade has great mobility and lateral quickness, as any good Technician must, but his actual overall speed is below average. If you get close to Shade, trust me, he WILL take you down. But it is possible to hit and run with success on Shade, and as such he will occasionally have problems with wrestlers with great speed or wrestlers who have an primarily aerial arsenal. Style Notes: Biographical Information At the tender age of 14, Shade's parents were killed before his eyes by a street thug attempting to rob them. Fortunately, Shade was not hurt. Unfortunately, he had no awareness of any remaining family, and as such took to life on the streets. However, he was deeply scarred by the incident. As a method of retribution, he became a bit of a street vigilante, hiding in the shadows in alleys and such and stepping in to prevent other crimes. This is where he became known as "The Shadow", and eventually he adopted the name Shade. Surprisingly, Shade was very effective. He maintained this role as a twisted vigilante peacemaker for a solid two years, before the life caught up with him. During a routine burglary, Shade was stabbed multiple times. The incident nearly took his life, left him in as a "John Doe" in a hospital bed for nearly a month, and led him to pursue a change of lifestyle before he lost his own life. As a child growing up in San Diego, Shade had always been a fan of pro wrestling. Not just a fan, but an extremely devoted fan whose childhood dream was to be a wrestler. Though he had been out of tune with it since his parent's death, hearing a couple of children talking about wrestling refreshed his memory, and growing up in an era of the classic face vs. heel confrontations (and remembering he was young and a mark the last time he watched wrestling), he thought wrestling would provide the perfect way to battle evil without risking his life. Having already developed exceptional mobility and quickness, as well as some strength, during his street endeavors, Shade had the physical ability to succeed. And his love of classic wrestling gave him the knowledge of moves and such. So, when he joined the Erick Cunningham Gym in his hometown, he picked it up surprisingly quickly. Driven by the motivation to succeed, knowing that it was what his parents would have wanted, anything he didn't pick up he kept at until he picked it up. This drive and desire was immediately noticed by the owner of the school, e-wrestling tag team pioneer Erick Cunningham, who took Shade under his wing and taught him everything he knew. Cunningham, a brilliant technician in his prime, refined Shade's raw potential and helped turn him from a raw kid with a lot of heart into a talented technician. Roberts' e-wrestling career began in a small Vancouver-based regional fed, the BCWF. He struggled at first, and just as he started to find his groove, the federation closed. Then he joined the IWF, where he found what he was looking for: another classic good guy vs. bad guy confrontation. The IWF's Cruiserweight Champion was a vicious heel, and Shade knew he had found his niche in wrestling. If he couldn't fight evil in real life, at least he could bring the classic heel vs. face confrontations back. A few months later, he became the IWF Cruiserweight Champion. However, he knew the happy-go-lucky face he played in the IWF wasn't him. When Erick Cunningham called one day to tell Shade that he was opening a fed, the AWA, Shade decided to attempt to be more himself in the ring. Outside the ring, he kept to himself and was a bit of a recluse, due to his lack of interaction with society during his street years. When the AWA opening, Shade had developed a very dark persona that he carried into the ring. However, to the surprise of many, behind that dark persona Shade maintained his good guy status. Apparently, the idea worked. On the first card, Shade became the first ever AWA Internet Champion, defeating a monster by the name of "Demonlord" Tony Foster in the finals. Foster would go on to be the inaugural AWA World Champion and formed his own stable, calling it Fear. Foster, still angry at Shade's victory over him, began to send members of Fear after Shade. After losing his Internet Title in a screwjob to a member of Fear, Shade set his sights directly on Foster himself. Recruiting the only men he knew he could trust, Fire and Ice and Scott Stevens, to form the California Crew, Shade Roberts and Tony Foster staged a legendary feud that continued for over 2 years in 3 different federations and involved 9 title changes over one another, including one by Foster which retired the AWA World Title at the AWA's closing PPV, a title that the two men had shared for 15 months of the 21 months of the AWA's existence. When Foster finally retired in a memorable shoot interview, Shade would go on to win other titles in other federations, continuing to play the unlikely dark face, but he never found another olid heel to feud with. He does have some WWO experience, about 3 years ago he and his AWA teammates and long-time friends Fire and Ice won the WWO 6-Man Tag Team Titles, but his stay was otherwise short and uneventful. After that performance, Shade decided to hang up the tights in favor of being an instructor at Cunningham's school. He was starting to age (he was 33 when the WWO closed) and wanted to pass on his knowledge to others. He also secretly hoped to find a protege, but that never came to fruition. One day, when Cunningham was staging a wrestling event at his school, Don Cameron came to the event at the request of his long-time friends Fire and Ice (thus the connection :)). Cameron and Shade were introduced, and instantly became close. The two had a lot in common, as both were retired (Cameron is the Co-Owner and President of another federation) but still yearned for the ring. As such, the two ring vets have decided to join forces in an attempt, perhaps, to prove to the world that they can still wrestle with the best of them. Wrestling Information Wrestling Styles/Grades Daredevil None High-Flier Poor Martial Arts None Power Fair Technical Excellent Submission Excellent Cheating None Brawling/Hardcore None Escapability Excellent Stamina Very Good Ring Awareness Excellent Teamwork Very Good Top Wrestling Moves "Shadowleaf" Shade's trademark move (read: use EVERY MATCH, at least once ). A Texas Cloverleaf variation. "Inner Darkness" Shade's backup finisher. A camel clutch/Cobra clutch mix. Basically, a Cobra Clutch (Million Dollar Dream, etc) on a face-down opponent, pulling back on the move like a camel clutch. "The Freeze" Shade's first finisher, and still one of his most effective submissions. It's a kata hajime, a.k.a. Tazmission. figure-four leglock armdrag into an armbar hiptoss into a cross armbreaker snapmare into a dragon sleeper rolling single leg takedown into an STF DDT into a sitting front chancery spinebuster into a Boston Crab Finishing Maneuver "The Shadow" Shade picks up the opponent as for a bodyslam, but in one fluid motion hooks the head, shifts their body and falls back in a DDT. The power of the move comes from it's speed and the shock factor, as it's roughly 1.5 seconds from standing until the pin. Tag Team Information Tag Team Name: Roberts and Cameron Tag Team Partner: "Dominator" Don Cameron Tag Team Moves "Blind Man's Holiday": Shade sends the opponent into the ropes, Don lines up behind him, and Shade back body drops the opponent right into Cameron's arms for a piledriver. "Pray for Night": Shade locks in the Shadowleaf (Texas Cloverleaf) on an opponent, as Cameron comes off the top rope with a legdrop. "O.D.D.": As Cameron lifts the opponent for a power bomb, Shade grabs the opponent's head and spikes it with a neckbreaker. Shade locks the opponent in a Boston Crab, while Cameron puts the opponent's head in a camel clutch. The opponent's entire body is lifted off the ground. Double knife-edge chops in the corner (hey, they're old school ;)) Tag Team Finishing Maneuver "Shadow Factor" Roberts picks up the opponent in a Hotshot, pulling the opponent down right into a Domination Factor by Cameron. Can be setup by sending opponent into ropes for added momentum and impact, but it's not necessary.
