Scottie Bolan - Inside the Minds

16 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.

Posted Thursday, January 31, 2002 by Knight

Well, another question, another bunch of responses. Needless to say, I'm very pleased with the fact that there has been so much cooperation with these questions. Makes me feel like I've actually done something with my life. Anyways, thanks to everyone who contributed, the new question will be at the bottom of the article. Here are the responses..

LAST QUESTION: Why does your theme song fit you?

Dark Soul: "'Breakdown' fits me if only for one line. 'You think that you're insane, won't you spare me?' Working in a business with people as ruthless as Tracy Hudson, Johnny Detson, and Tyrone Hayes, I grew very tired of their act. Sadly enough, I wasn't able to do enough about it."

Sean Collins: "Yo, Rehab iz da shiznit an' der song 'It Don't Matter' iz ma way of payin' homage to da band."

Tre Jordan: "I picked the pop hit 'Tubthumping' because it's a true testament to perseverance. You get knocked down...but then you get up again. I mean, shouldn't we all deal in such simplicity? We get knocked down, but that's not the end. No, sir, that's the beginning. Because then you get up again. I mean...whoa...so deep...yet so simple. And plus, 'tubthumping' just sounds like a cool sexual position."

Amazing Romanis: Why does "Because We Can" fit the Romanis? Because it can. Seriously, though, the song is filled with the same frantic, high-energy performance most people come to expect from the Romanis (especially Niklos). That, coupled with the fact that the reason for most of our actions is "because we can", made it an ideal choice, even if it was written by a _gaje_.

Chris Stringer: Absolutely. My song is meant to annoy and offend, but once it gets into your head, you can't get it out. That's me.

Latex Lex: Cuz its the only way to be for me, shall i demonstrate sexy?

Dr. Mal Practice MD: Ahhh, my theme, no doubt the greatest and most original theme music in the annals of wrestling history, "Humanitarian". The title says it all, but I prefer to elaborate. It is a stirring, gripping, first-class story about a man, no, a visionary, no, a genius visionary... and his quest to heal the peoples of the world, despite the resistance of THOSE VILE JACKALS IN WASHINGTON ATTEMPTING TO PROFANE MY NAME! THEY'VE SENT LAWYERS, ASSASSINS AND JEHOVAH'S WITNESSES IN AN ATTEMPT TO DESTROY ME! BUT I'LL GET THEM, YES, I'LL GET THEM ALL! AHAHAHAHa... uh, ha ha? Anyway, my music is great. You should buy the CD.

Johnny Detson: Why does my theme music fit me? That answer is quite simple. You see I accomplish everything with the greatest of ease. Everything in my life is easy. I'm not like some bum going off to his blue collar job working hard! I do things, and I don't even break a sweat. The other reason is because that since I am the best wrestler in the WWO I deserve the best entrance. You look at all the other jack-o's and there entrance doesn't compare to mine! That's why it fits me!

Dr. Ohno Ow: My theme is, name "MERCY". For that is what, I am all about as, anaTHEsiolo-gist. To end all suffer-ING. FOR-ever. If you in GREAT, pain call me AND, I get MACHETE and come over to, END you pain. Now that MERCY.

Xavier Feyr: My theme fits me because it is about embracing ones rage and anger. In harnessing ones own anger, one becomes capable of things one never would have otherwise never been capable of. It is through my rage at this pathetic world, and those who have opposed me that I fuel my strength and drive to destroy all who would stand in my path. Others call this madness, but I say it is the greatest wisdom of all, for I refuse to deny what I am... someday you will find that you and I are not so different after all.

Lion Tamer - "YtseJam" by Dream Theater is all about Lion Tamer. To sum YtseJam up in one word - "Majesty." That's me. To sum it up in forty-two words, it is a beautiful instrumental piece for a beautiful man. It lets the music do the talking, just like I let my fists of fury do my talking. Me and my song are made of pure godlike talent, filled with raw emotion. Now if you'll excuse me, I must go bathe my Plunger.

Brick - What? Why did we choose "The Meaning of Life" for ourentrance music? I happened to find that the lyrics really representingwhat we're about. Trying to take a new path, living life to the fullest, and doing the things that you know is right, no matter what other think...

Pak - Yeah, that and the fact that I love that song...

Apex: I'm not really sure why my theme music fits me. It's odd, I originally selected it, because I really didn't want a theme song with lyrics, and Everclear's instrumental was kind of catchy. As my career and personality progressed, the subtle intensity in the song started to remind me of myself, or, at least, of what I'm made out to be. The song is constantly intense and full of off the wall, unpredictable noises, and at times, I apparently am somewhat like this. Now a days, I'm more controlled, so, the song fits now because I'm comfortable with it. I mean, it would be pretty embarressing to select a new theme song, then have it play, and forget that it's mine... I could accidently forget to come to the ring.

Magnus Colby: I chose My Sacrifice by Creed, because the song makes me think of everything I've given to this game over the years. I've worked my ass off to be the best I can be. I've put in all of the years of hardwork and training. I've worked to put others over. And twice, I've put my career on hold to work behind the scenes. I've done all of this to make a name for myself in this game. Everything I've done over the years, and now I find myself back at the bottom trying to work my way up to the top. My sacrifice is giving up everything I've ever done to try to make it up to the top one more time.

"San Malo" Michael Donovan: Well, first off, let me say that the only reason that I consented to having a piece of American pop trash such as a band called "Gorillaz" was because this particular song featured the lyrics of Ibrahim Ferrer, an artist worth of my respect. If you haven't heard of him, I'm not even going to bother. While at first I thought having any American work would never do, just because it goes against everything that I'm about, but the lyrics are so very appropriate. They lament all that I despise about this great nation of America, and they do it through the one ideal medium, that beautiful language that is Espanol. It's a wonderful little piece. One the other hand, if you ask my partner why it's appropriate, he would probably say "because it has a good beat." Typical.

"Too Cool" Chris Hopper: I chose a combination of AC/DC's "TNT" and Kid Rock's "Devil without a Cause" for many reasons. "Devil....." is basically used because many people always thought I sucked and that I should drop off the face of the planet, but I am not only still here, but coming after my opponents to get the "W" As far as "TNT", I first tried turning heel back in 1998 and chose this theme music because it described all that I wanted the character to be...."a powerload, dynomite, etc" These days I keep the music around simply because people have written things telling me it is a bad choice......so I keep it partly to thumb my nose at them.

Alex Extreme: My music serves to understand who I am. It also allows me to get psyched up and right to fight. Currently, I'm using Eminem's "The Way I Am". It defines who and what I am far better than some color commentator or fan in the stands. Go pick it up, listen to the words, and understand what Alex Extreme is truly all about.

Fydor Samsonov: firstly, fydor does not no much of the tipeing. secratari wench who did stik shoe in fydors eye has helpd russian bear to right this emale. fydor is nott sure y but secratari wench seams to be finding somthing funni abowt this. qwestion was askt if for wat reezons russian bear does enjoy flight of bumble bee. when you does only hav won rekord to play groing from little russian bear of papa samsonovs collektion you does lurn to like it. it just tuk fydor too hours to tipe that. must go get noody girl books. dah

Tyrone "Purple" Hayes: One song cannot fit a person like me, since there are so many facets of my personality, each one crying out for attention. That is why songs like "Artierripp", "Big Bang Baby", "Laser Sheep Dip Funk", and others have been used by me for my enterance. I'm just too damned cool to be stuck with just one.

Todd "The Rod" Johnstone: It is named "Shut Up", you do the math numbnuts.

Matt Cole: I picked "Got no Shame " because it had that harrmonica part at the begining that could wake the dead. It kinda unsettles the fella facing me. There is also a line in the song that says "Rats and snakes always try to get the best of me..but they wont." To me that means.. To completely beat me ..you have to damn near kill me.

"Trixxster" Marvin Patch: Not that a theme song should have any barring on the wrestler in the ring, since this is *WRESTLING* that we are talking about... but I have been coming out to "Brain Stew" by Green Day for my entire career. When I started in the biz five years ago I was a huge Green Day fan, and the words in the song fit that pitiful jester persona I used to have rather well.

"Showtime" Rick Marley: Well...that's a tough one to answer. When I was starting out in this business, I needed to find a song that exemplified the way that I did things. Something fast paced. Something that spoke to the way that I performed in and out of the ring, so I chose "Ride the Wind" by Poison. I know that a lot of you guys have never heard those lyrics...or never really listened to them (after all, what could an 80's hair band have to say that could reach a pro wrestling crowd, right?). So take a look:

Ride the Wind:

#Hearts of fire #Streets of stone #Modern warriors #Saddle iron horses of chrome

#Taste the wild #Lick the wind #Like something they never saw before #Their jaws dropping to the floor #Steel made of soul and sin

#Rebels born without a care #And the day he listens #Only to fly where eagles dare #And the night she whispers

Chorus: #Ride the wind #Never coming back until I touch the midnight sun #Ride the wind #Never coming back again #Ride the wind #Never coming back until I touch the midnight sun

#Painted flesh #Loyalty #Humbled pride #Just as far as the eye can see

#Stories told #Two old friends #Of battle scars and lonely bars #And nights the rain wouldn't end

#Here's to withered eyes wearing gypsy smiles #And the day he listens #Here's to lovely ladies and a million miles #And the night she whispers

Chorus

#Ride the wind #I'm still the bravest soul in sin, #Burning till the night is done.

Solo

#Of all the truths and lies #And stories of riders in the sky #They say only the bravest try #Where eagles and angels dare to fly

Chorus

You see...the song was originally written about BMX racing...but the imagery...the scenes that the lyrics conjure...no other song addresses life as a high flying wrestler quite as well as this one. It addresses the crazed mindset that you need to fight a man that literally weighs twice as much as you...can bench press a main battle tank, and has body odor that's considered to be in violation of the Clean Air Act that can only keep one coherent thought in their head: Kill Liddle Man! To combat that all you have is your speed...and no regard at all for your physical well being. You do one death-defying move after another, leaving the audience asking: How did he do that? And: What will he do next?

So I guess the real answer to what does my entrance song mean to me is pretty simple after all. It means everything.

Marc Denis: "What our theme music means to us... to make it a quick story, all you have to do is ask Alain. He's the Hip freak."

Alain MacDonald: "When trying to pick out a theme song to make your entrance to... it needs to be special... it needs to have the right beginning... and it needs to get you pumped up. You NEED that right frame of mind when you make your way to the ring. Our music helps."

Marc Denis: "The Tragically Hip.. Gord Downie... sings with a lot of emotion. When he sings a song called 'Last American Exit', he's talking about a long trip back home to Canada. Whenever we're driving back to Canada, it's either that song cranking in the CD player, or it's Simon and Garfunkel's 'Homeward Bound.' You crank either song and try not to get inspired."

Alain MacDonald: "But with 'Last American Exit', we're also showing our pride in our home country. It's the same kind of pride that we take to the ring with us. And when it's played in Canada, our fans... well... the fans that we have LEFT... heh... can feel the same thing that we are. Especially that part near the end where he's building up to the chorus... it sends a shiver down my spine."

Papa Legba: Both of these songs fit mebecause dey do! Forgive the circular logic, mon but it's self-explanatory. I mean, when Papa come f'play, he Don't Play Nice! I-an-I come to Bring the Pain, hence the song...and with regards to 'Judgement Day', well I keep telling you that Dark Times are comin'...it's no joke - soonah ah laytah, you gwon hafta be ready for Judgement Day, and Papa Legba be right there to bring it to you! Period, point blank, end of discussion
