WWO bio card — Tyrone "Purple" Hayes

29 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Tyrone "Purple" Hayes Other Nicknames: "The Man, Mirth, and Litigation", "Verbal Dynamite", "Purple Aussie Beater" Real Name: Tyrone Hanes Age: 30 Height: 6'7" Weight: 287 Hometown: Tuscaloosa, Alabama Alignment: Taoist Entrance Theme: "Artieripp" by Saint Etienne Manager: Todd "The Rod" Johnstone Managerial Profile Physical Appearance A tall, light brown black man with round facial features topped off with a rather European looking nose. Hayes has rather straight black hair and a toothy smile. Usually wearing clothes from any time period except the one he's in, he seems to be an anacronism. Hayes usually is wearing some derrogatory shirt, some sort of purple shorts or pants, and whatever footwear he fancies at the moment. He's worn everything from a faux 18th century velvet suit to a sheep costume. Tyrone is someone who enjoys grievous crimes to fashion. Tyrone is a fickle man, and as a result he is always changing his ring entrance and opening rant. He sometimes wears a robe, other times he'll leave it at home. Sometimes there will be big pryotecnic shows, other times it'll be things coming down from the rafters. His manager Todd "The Rod" Johnstone will always be there though, and usually will do Tyrone's ring announcement, along with getting on the bad side of the crowd and Ty's opponent. Hayes loves to interact with the fans on the way to the ring, and generally tries his hardest to make every minute of his entrance an enjoyable, albeit annoying, experience for the fans. Psychological Makeup If there is one word to describe Tyrone, flamboyant would have to be it. Tyrone always wants the crowd to have a good time, regardless of their reaction. Tyrone loves the fans to react to his antics, it feeds him and keeps him going. Of course, the fact that Hayes is rather tactless and disrespectful help him out in the crowd pleasing department. Just don't let the fun loving attitude of Tyrone fool you, he also doesn't know when to quit, either in the entertaining department or the wrestling ring. He's been known to pull himself up after being beaten senseless, because giving less than 100% in a performance is not an option in Hayes' mind. Biographical Information "Offical Biography": One day a young black man was looking at a rather nice car parked outside a local AM/PM. The man in question was tall, dark, and a bit off kilter. He really wanted the car, and decided to see if he could "borrow" it from the owner, for a little while at least. While trying to "borrow" the car, the kid did not notice that the car's owner had already come out of the AM/PM with an .84 ounce Coca-Cola and a brown Sears belt. The two just happened to bump into each other. After "pleasentries" were exchanged, they came to an agreement: the owner doesn't prosecute, and the perpetraitor becomes a professional wrestler. Why? Because the car's owner was Todd "The Rod" Johnstone, and he needed a new wrestler to bilk out of money. After a period of training, Tyrone Hanes made his wrestling debut... and stunk. That didn't stop him though. Determined to make life better for himself and his immediate family, Hanes kept at it. Of course Todd was there every step of the way, and the two became great friends, due to the fact they shared many personality quirks and enjoyed insulting most everyone else on the planet. It was obvious that Tyrone Hanes wasn't exactly a catchy name, so the name Tyrone "Purple" Hayes was brought into action, and it's been a rollercoaster ride ever since. Tyrone finally managed to help get his mother and sister out of Tuscaloosa and to a suburb somewhere, but they disowned the oddball Tyrone after he insulted the Reverand Al Sharpton. Hayes and Johnstone didn't care much though, they were having too much fun being jerks. After a short retirement in 98, Hayes came back and brought Johnstone with him, looking for a place in the "big leagues". Tyrone has had several run ins with International groups on his treatment towards foreigners, particularly Australians and the French. This occasionally comes up in his unbaited bashing of the two nationalities/ethinicities, and has caused headaches for his employers. "Actual Biography": Born to a student couple at the University of Alabama on April 13th 1971, Tyrone Montgomery Hanes Jr. was thrust into a family with showbusiness in its blood. His mother, Clarisse Masterson, was a promising ballerina before she injured her left ankle, while his father Tyrone Montgomery Hanes Sr. was a drama major, in addition to his being a semi-talented painter and his mother an up and coming violinist. The first 7 years of Tyrone's life were good. His father and mother, although not married, did try to bring him up in a traditional mother-father paradigm, as well as instill an appreciation for the fine arts in the young lad. Young little Ty began attending ballet lessons from age 3 and playing the violin at age 5. From the get go, it looked as if the young couple had given birth to a future superstar entertainer. Of course, as with all stories dealing with a budding child prodigy, there is hardship. The senior Hanes, to the shock of all his friends and loved ones, finally realised something: he wasn't in love with Clarisse. Deciding to take flight, Monte Hanes fled to an unknown place in the continental United States, leaving his 7 year old son and now expectant wife in Tuscaloosa. Heartbroken, but undaunted, Clarisse left school, moved back in with her parents, and set out to raise Tyrone and his new little sister Abigail in the best possible manner. Tyrone continued his work, in both dance and music, and found himself some commerical work with local television stations. The young Hanes boy began to help bring in some modest income to his family, but it never seemed enough to make ends meet. In fact, at age 11, Tyrone sold off his violin, one of the few personal posessions he had, in order to help pay for his sister's tonsilectomy. Fortune did smile upon the boy though, as he was seen in a school play by a parent in the audience who just happened to be a friend of a dean of a dance school in New York, New York. Tyrone was offered a spot in the prestigous school, with a full scholarship. Hanes, who was 12 at the time, was frightened, but his mother leapt at the opportunity, and young Tyrone went to train in New York. Fate, fortune's more devious cousin, elected to bide its time until Tyrone reached age 16, when Tyrone grew from a mere 5'6" to a full on 6'2" in 6 months. Ty's new found height, and added weight, threw a monkey wrench into his fledgling dance career, but Tyrone kept on, adding voice lessons to his repitoire in order to try and salvage some chance at a career on Broadway. The boy's natural gifts just would not let him quit, and Tyrone managed to stay on at the school with a music scholarship. With graduation just around the corner, Tyrone threw himself into his music and occasional dance with full force. At this time, Tyrone had grown into his 6'7" frame, and was an imposing sight on the stage, but his presence and natural charisma allowed him a wide birth. The young Hanes graduated with honors, and a full ride to a famous overseas university to study. It was here Tyrone would face his biggest challenge, and it is here that he also would meet his long suffering on and off girlfriend, Antonia Silvestri, known to most as Toni Sliver, and actor pal Winston Allen Saed. Being an 18 year old in a foreign country is an amazing experience, and Tyrone soaked it up. Constantly in contact with his mother and sister Abby, Tyrone made sure that he sent any extra money he made waiting tables or doing odd jobs to them in order to help Abby through her own private school education. Tyrone was in heaven, doing well and frankly, in love with the girl of his dreams, a classy gal of English-Italian descent, and working his way up the theatre ladder... until his mother was in a life threatening automobile accident. One semester away from graduation, and circumstance elects to piss on Tyrone yet again. Tyrone never hesitated to do what he felt was right. Immediately dropping out of school and flying back to Tuscaloosa, Tyrone began to work a menial factory job in the day, and the lousy club circuit at night. The story would be over right here, if it wasn't for a portly gentleman who just happened to be going on a bender that very night. Todd "The Rod" Johnstone used to be a respected manager, but most of his proteges either retired, or decided they didn't need his services. Working, and partly financing, a fly-by-night federation based out of Arkansas that just happened to have done a show at a local high school in Tuscaloosa, it was just a coincidence that Johnstone had decided to stop in for a few rounds at a small nightclub. Todd managed to be just sober enough to see something in the tall kid doing a performance that was lightyears beyond what the yokels deserved. Approaching the boy, Todd offered to train him in wrestling and get him better pay. Tyrone refused, but took a business card from the man anyhow. Six months passed, and a 23 year old Tyrone feels an incredible urge do something bigger in his life... to dazzle crowds. The entertainment bug will not quit, nor would it let him be satisfied with playing to paltry crowds of, at most, 20. Calling Johnstone, Tyrone tentatively agrees to sign on. This is the beginning of almost 2 and a half years of intense training. Tyrone comes out of this ordeal stronger and raring to dive headlong into the world of professional wrestling. Thus begins the career of Tyrone Hanes... and it nearly ended with that because of a difficulty in grasping the concepts that were so painstakingly laid out for him. Initially Hanes was a face, a good guy trying to make a living for his immediate family, and of course, was not exactly liked by anyone at all, mainly for being "boring". Tyrone Hanes is a flop, a poor draw who's goody-goody, but the determined young man devises his own stage persona: Tyrone "Purple" Hayes. The wisecracking, showstealing, entertainment dynamo that relies on most every aspect of the arts that Tyrone ever remotely learned. Johnstone is skeptical, but gives Tyrone one more chance. The change is almost overnight. Tyrone goes from working small shows in the south to larger ones in the northeast and pacific coast. Finally, in early 1998, Tyrone is given the chance to strut his stuff on television, in the World Internet Federation. Todd and Tyrone milk this for all its worth, but alas the WIF closes because of inept accounting. The experience, and the animosty between Tyrone and his soon-to-be archnemesis Roundhouse, is priceless, however. Especially considering what's to come. Through the WIF, Tyrone learns of the NLWA, and is embraced as one of the top wrestlers of the federation. Taking his fued with Roundhouse right where it left off in the WIF, the two finally engage in brutal combat, leading to Tyrone's first victory over him in the summer of 98. With the overwhelming success of Tyrone, Todd, and Todd's new protege Latex Lex, the trio formed Carnage-a-Trois and were an enormous success. The future was looking good, not good enough to stave off fate. As with all federation closures, inept managment is what caused the NLWA to fold. This time, Tyrone was feeling homesick and considering leaving wrestling. To further add to this predicament, Todd's wife of 30 years is diagnosed with terminal breast cancer, and Todd decides to put away the "Rod" once and for all. Shortly after hearing word from Johnstone about this, Tyrone gave up wrestling, seemingly forever. His "retirement" lasted all of 4 months. Late in December of 1999 Tyrone recieves phone calls from David "Roundhouse" Grief and Ultimate Thrasher, asking him if he would be interested in a contract from the World Wrestling Organisation. At first a bit put off, Tyrone confers with Todd, and tentatively agrees to a year long contract. Johnstone, after dealing with the loss of his wife, is eager to get back into the sport he loves, and Tryone just wants to mess around. With the departures of Warren Hayes, Fury, and other key players looming on the horizen, some of the established talent felt the need to shore up the roster, and Tyrone was the one they turned to, and shore it up he certainly did. Tyrone and Roundhouse fought a fued worthy of an RSPWF year end award, in addition to snagging Tyrone numerous singles accolades. Hayes is not satisfied however, and manages to get in contact with his old partner in crime: Latex Lex, and proceeds to convince Lex to once again strap on the boots and join up for a Carnage a Trois reunion. CaT was not complete, however, as "The Prodigy" Tracy Hudson was deemed worthy to be a member of this little cadre of wrestlers. So the despotic duo and their manager of misfits added the rising property of Hudson, and a new chapter of the CaT began. After a while "Dios Loco" Rob James joins for a short stint, but is bogged down with other commitments to really be a force in the WWO. Time goes on, CaT breaks up after a series of botches by both management and talent, leaving Tyrone on his own. Ty and Mike Sebastian continue a fued that seemingly is forced on Hayes by management, in part because neither Hayes nor Sebastian get along backstage. Tyrone continues to be a top man in the WWO, but is bypassed for major title shots in favor of more "fan friendly" wrestlers. This continues until The Bash 2000, where Tyrone is so upset at the way the event is handled, that he is on the verge of quitting. The matter becomes a moot point when the WWO folds. Tyrone had a short 6 month stint in Ultimate E-Wrestling, but didn't seem to have his heart in it, depsite winning their television title. Now back in his old stomping grounds, will The Man, The Mirth, The Litigation be able to regain his former top tier form, or will he just fade away? Accolades: RSPWF Year End Awards (1999): Fued of the Year vs. Roundhouse (WWO) WWO Simply the Best Awards (1999): Rookie of the Year WWO Simply the Best Awards (1999): Heel of the Year WWO Simply the Best Awards (1999): Best Stable: Carnage-a-Trois Wrestling Information Hayes wrestles a hybred style relying mainly on technical brawling. Tyrone will pick on smaller men and try to out wrestle bigger men. Hayes isn't extremely quick, but more agile than most men his size. Hayes can pull off the occasional "big move", but tends to rely on psychology and out right annoyance to trick his opponent making mistakes. Todd Johnstone will interfere as much as he likes, helping Hayes in his quest to force his opposition out of their game plan and into what Ty wants them to do. Tyrone frequently tries to keep the pace of the match to his liking and will resort to leaving the ring and cheating to keep it that way. Noted for his stamina and resilence, Hayes has also been known to rope-a-dope his opposition. Hayes' wrestling arsenal is also has a distinct southern style wrestling flavor, with moves such as the flapjack, swinging neckbreaker, hotshot, slingshot suplex, and brainbuster finisher. Wrestling Styles/Grades Daredevil None High-Flier None Power Good Technical Fair Submission Poor Martial Arts Cheating Very Good Brawling/Hardcore Fair Escapability Very Good Stamina Excellent Ring Awareness Excellent Teamwork Poor Top Wrestling Moves Short Arm Clothesline Flapjack Hangman's Neckbreaker Savate Kick "Slipping a Mickey": Hotshot "Bounced Check": A slingshot suplex "Sidesplitter": A held press drop stomach breaker "Do I Stutter?": This is a spot basically stolen from another wrestler, Darren Morgan to be precise. Instead of an actual move, Tyrone does a series of 4 of the same move, such as 4 clotheslines, 4 punches, ect. After each of these moves, Tyrone screams one part of this 4 word sentence: "CAN YOU TOP THIS?". If this chain is broken, it cannot be resumed. Basically this can be used as a hulk up or something of that ilk. "The Sure Thing": A "BIG" leg drop. Tyrone places his opponent in the middle of the ring, and then plays to the crowd for a good 10 seconds. Hayes cups his ear, pleads the crowd for more "cheers", then bounces off nearest set of ropes, slows down, and jumps in the air to do an exaggerated leg drop. A pin attempt _ALWAYS_ comes after this... and _NEVER_ succeeds. Tyrone then yells at the referee and ignores his opponent for a little while longer while he argues the "horribly slow and erroneous 2 count." "Bum Rush": Inverted Atomic Drop coupled with a quick Stiff Clothesline Finishing Moves "Tuscaloosa Tumble": Tyrone's secondary finisher, a guillotine face driver, sometimes called a rockerdropper. "The Purple Poison": A standing vertical suplex into a DDT, otherwise known as a brainbuster.
