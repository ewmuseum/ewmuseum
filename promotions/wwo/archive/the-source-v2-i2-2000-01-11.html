I'm talking about the interaction of the handlers who are discussing important elements of the game in a fashion that's pretty rare in e-wrestling. A lot of eloquence and some rare insight into what it is that makes this particular roleplaying game an enjoyable one. For myself, I'd like to begin by concurring with Frank that complaining about a popularity poll is about as useful as trying to herd a bunch of cats. The RSPWF polls are fun but not exactly the most meaningful measure of how "good" a particular character is. Remember that all of this is *EXTREMELY* subjective and personal opinions can be debated until the cows come home and still nothing would be accomplished. I particularly liked Frank's comments on the evolution of Usenet and noted that my own participation in RSPWF has dwindled from being fairly active three years ago to not even bothering with it anymore these days. I don't think my experience is all that uncommon as a lot of people have said that 99% of what's posted in RSPWF is unreadable garbage. I can't predict what the greater emphasis on the web will do for e-wrestling in general but I will point to Geeksoft's completely web-based collection of feds as one sign of what the future may hold. The one thing I would like to add to all this is my distaste for people bashing other feds or characters simply because of their standing in a popularity poll. You may like or dislike the character of "White Hot" Darren Morgan or you may enjoy or despise a fed like UEW. But in the end, we're all players of a game. And it sounds too much like "legit heat" being generated when people take cheap potshots that have no bearing on whether the game is enjoyable or not. No offense to Duane but it's ironic that he would "hate reading UEW stuff because I have never liked a single one of their characters except Vitrone." I'm not about to argue in favor of UEW over any other fed, that's just personal opinion once again. What I will point out is that the irony stems from the large number of characters and handlers who have joined WWO after being in UEW. Will this mean Duane will dislike WWO characters now? I also think that's a particularly moot point since very few characters these days restrict themselves to just one fed. I suspect UEW is very similar to the WWO in that while there are a couple of "homegrown" characters, the vast majority of the roster started in other feds. Vitrone is a perfect example as he did not evolve in UEW but came there after first playing in other groups. Arguments as to which fed is better are meaningless. The level of interaction between feds is minimal at best so to bash one fed simply because it's ranked higher in a popularity poll doesn't contribute to the overall enjoyment of the game. I know wrestling has always been about blowing your own horn and slagging your opponents. But since feds don't "oppose" one another, I fail to see the point of making too many comparisons. The bottom line is... I like WWO. I'm not alone in liking the WWO. It has some great characters and some compelling storylines going on. Getting all wrapped around the axles about polls doesn't contribute to the enjoyment of this particular game. It simply expends energy pointlessly that could otherwise be used on more worthwhile pursuits. Like mounting a campaign to have The Backstreet Boys powerbombed through a stack of flaming, barbed-wire tables! Now *THAT* is a worthwhile pursuit!!! Look to the skies, folks. That next plummeting pumpkin just might have your name written on it. :-) Patrick Pumpkinhead =================================================== WWO Scouting Report, Part I by Gary Hammond comments: [detail withheld] =================================================== In this series yours truly will take a look at the entire WWO roster, in alphabetical order, and give you my take on their 1999 and what to expect from them in 2000. Let's get started... ANDROS What he did in '99: The former World and US champion completely tarnished his legend. Started the year in retirement after losing to Warren Hayes at Anarchy '98, then finagled his way back onto the WWO roster at New Year's Bash using a contract loophole. Was a founding member of the Hall of Fame, but was quietly fired by Roundhouse and Thrasher only weeks later after a humiliating loss to Lord Alexander Bennington. Faded into obscurity after Valentine's Day Massacre, but unfortunately resurfaced in late summer. Was rumored to be reuniting with Roundhouse or Thrasher, but plans apparently fell through and he suffered a disgraceful loss to Tigress. What to expect in 2000: Hopefully after his humiliation on the last Showcase he'll go back to the Business Mansion for good, but somehow I have a horrible feeling we haven't heard the last of him. He'll probably attempt 4 more comebacks and disgrace himself even more...if that's possible. APEX: What he did in '99: Came out of nowhere in January, and almost instantly became one of the WWO's most popular wrestlers due to his suicidal ring style and hilarious promos. Showed off his skill in two "rookie" matches, then entered into a feud with Tracy Hudson that helped both guys move up the WWO ladder and culminated in an awesome 4 chairs match at BOTT 6. Lost in that encounter, but proved his place among the WWO's elite shortly afterwards by beating Mike Sebastian for the TV belt. Fended off the challenge of Bobby Rojo with little problem, but then lost the title, along with his beloved "Mr. TV" to Latex Lex. He paid Lexy back though..causing Lex to lose the TV belt to Dark Soul one week later, and then defeated Lex in a bloody grudge match at HeatWave. Hasn't gotten involved in another big feud since then, but has still looked very impressive, beating Fury and Al Perez and losing controversially to Ultimate Thrasher. Is rumored to be becoming involved in another big feud sometime very soon, possibly with an important title at stake. What to expect in 2000: Believe in Apex. This guy is the real deal, and is one of the top five wrestlers in the WWO. Look for him to win either the IP or US belt sometime very soon, and don't be surprised if he's World champion within 8 months. ARMED FORCES What they did in '99: NavCom and DefCon exploded on the tag team scene in May, and almost instantly became so over that Search & Destroy pretty much tried to personally hand them the keys to the tag team throne room at War of the Ages. Unfortunately, though, the Fun Loving Criminals snatched the keys away, and when all was said and done it was Fire and Ice who wound up seated on the throne. After battling Uncivil Disobedience throughout the summer, they were then favored to win the tag team gauntlet at HeatWave, but failed to come through in the battle royals and wound up helping Thunderstorm win the tag team belts from Fire and Ice. With the departure of their adversaries, UCD, they seem to have possibly lost their direction, as shown in a recent loss to the Outlaws. What to expect in 2000: 6 months ago, they were the can't-miss team, the next big thing in the WWO tag team division. But with the arrival of teams like the Outlaws and Les Canadians, the window of opportunity may have been slammed shut. What they do in the next month or so will determine if they can one day be champions, or if they will just fade into lower-mid-card obscurity. BEST OF THE BEST: What they did in '99: Entered the WWO late in the fall, and have looked impressive thus far with a victory over The Good, The Bad, and The Ugly and a close loss to the Outlaws. Their high-flying style has been a pleasant contrast to the power wrestlers and brawlers currently dominating the tag division, but it could be their downfall. What to expect in 2000: It's a bit early to really judge this team, but I think they have the potential to become impact players in the tag team division in the year to come... though not as much potential as the Outlaws. RANDY BRYSON What he did in '99: The former champion and archenemy of Wuzho began the year as one-third of Even Better, the stable poised to become the great new heel organization in the WWO...but EB collapsed with the departure of Warren Hayes in early February. Undaunted, Bryson tried to replace Warren as the no.1 bad guy in the WWO, as he and Ultimate Thrasher took turns costing each other US title matches against Underground Kingpin . Became a bigger and bigger thorn in Thrasher's side as winter turned into spring, and spoiled Thrasher's World title win at Battle of the Titans by fast-counting the pin (he was the special referee) and stealing the title belt. After evading Thrasher and Stone's attempts to retrieve the belt (which was apparently leading to a Thrasher-Bryson showdown at War of the Ages), Bryson inexplicably departed the WWO in late May with little explanation and gave the title belt to Iron, leading to the "NO" fiasco. Many thought this was the final curtain on Bryson's WWO career, but he suddenly returned to the federation in November, assisting Chris Stonebreaker as a referee and is apparently on the verge of returning to full-time action and turning face. What to expect in 2000: Bryson supporting the forces of good? I'll believe it when I see it for an extended period of time. I expect that soon Bryson will find a new enemy and will either begin a bloody, protracted feud or inexplicably vanish again. Even another World title run, 2 1/2 years after he last held the belt, isn't entirely out of the question, but I don't think Randy posesses the killer instinct he once did. DARK SOUL What he did in '99: He debuted quietly in midsummer, and many WWO observers scoffed at his name, his gimmick and his "laters" catchphrase, saying he'd be quickly gone from the federation. But he soon was able to draw the attention of people like Silkk, and inched his way up the lower mid-card ranks until being in the right place at the right time to upset Latex Lex for the TV title in August with the help of Apex. However, his reign heavily damaged what prestige that title was beginning to get back, and was ended by Silkk on Thanksgiving. What to expect in 2000: Not much, unless he's able to be in the right place at the right time again. He'll likely remain a consistent On-Campus regular... I think that might be all he's capable of. "BLOCKBUSTER" JOHNNY DETSON What he did in '99: It was an up-and-down year at the box office for the "Blockbuster". At the beginning of the year, it looked like he was on his way out of the WWO, having been unceremoniously axed from Simply The Best. But he rebounded, joining with fellow jilted ex-STBer Jude St. Claire and the Ultimates to form the Showmen, the group that stole the show at Valentine's Day Massacre. After that, people began talking of him as a possible World title contender, especially after he apparently bought out Fury's contract, but Ultimate Thrasher ended those hopes at Battle of the Titans 6. With the other Showmen departing the federation, Detson then once again seemed primed to drop out of sight, and indeed did so for a few weeks, but then stunned the world by beating Al Perez for the IP title at War of the Ages..and completely flabbergasted everyone who stuck him with the "transitional champion" label by retaining the belt in a four corners match with Perez, Lord Keeblor and Tyrone Hayes two weeks later. During this match, Detson and St. Claire (who had returned to the WWO) joined forces with longtime foe Perez to form the Gentleman's Club stable, in response to the antics of Hayes' stable, Carnage-a-Trois. Unfortunately, the trio wasn't enough by themselves to fight CAT, and enlisted the aid of Mike Sebastian-which would be their downfall when Sebastian turned against them at HeatWave and then added injury to insult by beating Detson for the IP belt on Thanksgiving. The infighting over the decision to get help from Sebastian effectively destroyed the GC stable before it really even got off the ground, and Detson and St. Claire, formerly the closest of allies, are now on the verge of war. What to expect in 2000: Like we've said throughout much of 1999, Detson could either shoot up to the top of the fed or drop off the face of the earth. Whoever emerges as the winner in his brewing feud with St. Claire could be headed for a World title push...but I don't think 2000 will be as good for Detson as '99 was. STEVE DUMARS What he did in '99: Entered the federation at the same time as Apex and Tyrone Hayes, and for a brief period everyone's favorite fascist janitor looked just as promising as his fellow rookies. Showed promise and power in his "rookie" matches, and became a dominating mid-carder before finding a natural enemy in fellow Michigander "The True Living Legend" Brian J. Blottie in the summer. Their feud escalated after each man cost the other a match in the US title mini-tournament at War of the Ages, and many said that this feud would be a springboard for one of those men to wear that belt...but instead, it effectively destroyed both of their WWO careers. After an uninspired win over Blottie in a "Detroit street fight" in August, Dumars dropped off the radar, aside from a token appearance in the HeatWave tag team gauntlet. But he's still supposedly on the roster. What to expect in 2000: If Dumars comes back, he could again be possible US title material, given the current heatless state of that belt. If not, he'll just go down as another guy who had a good run for awhile and had the potential, but couldn't sustain it. FANG What he did in '99: Just entered the federation in November, but has already made an impact via his alliance with "Money Driven" Mike Sebastian. Looked very impressive in beating Eric Travers and Mikkael C. Kole, and in teaming with Sebastian to beat Detson and Perez this past Showcase. What to expect in 2000: He looks promising, but so did Sebastian's last ally, "Blackjack" Bobby Rojo, who is currently collecting trash in New Jersey. However, Fang has more credibility in other federations than Rojo did, so if Sebastian turns on him, MDMS could have a fight on his hands. If Fang keeps it up, he could be US title material before long...but he could also crash and burn a la Rob James. FIRE AND ICE What they did in '99: A year ago at this time, it looked like this veteran team's careers were on a freefall. They had been soundly whipped in their big feud with Uncivil Disobedience, and the emergence of new forces in the tag team division like Search & Destroy made them look old and irrelevant. They themselves seemed to agree with this, as they temporarily split up and Michael Ice pursued a singles career that flopped miserably. Quickly, they figured out that they work best together, and reunited, only to spend several months slagging along in the mid-card. They barely squeezed into the four-corners match for the tag team belts at War of the Ages as heavy underdogs...and then pulled perhaps the shock of the year in the tag team division by turning heel and joining forces with Uncivil Disobedience, which led to them winning the tag team belts. As the Fun Loving Criminals, the two teams proceeded to make life miserable for everyone else in the tag team division, especially Armed Forces, for the rest of the summer. By HeatWave, though, the bottom fell out, and they lost the belts to Thunderstorm. Since then, they've again took a bit of a slide down the ladder with the departure of their allies, UCD, but they remain a dangerous, experienced team. What to expect in 2000: They've been around forever, and it seems like they've already accomplished all that they can and can only go downhill from here...but of course, we said the same thing about them a year ago, and they came back and won the belts. I don't think they'll be champions again, but who knows.. "ROCKET MAN" JAMES GIRARD What he did in '99: The "hero for millions" made his debut late in the year, and quickly set about irking fans and faces by making clear his thoughts about the state of the WWO in general and Tigress in particular. In his lone match so far, he handily defeated "Southern Gentleman" Jimmy Lemon, and later also cost Tigress a match against Dark Soul. What to expect in 2000: It's obviously a bit early to call, but I think the Rocket Man might be the next great heel in the WWO. Look for him to win the TV title soon and possibly the US belt later in the year. SPENCER GREEN What he did in '99: The WWO's newest communist made his debut in November, and was able to beat Andros on his On-Campus debut. But who HASN'T beaten Andros lately? He got manhandled by Dark Soul on a Showcase shortly afterward, and from what I've seen so far that performance will be more indicative of what to expect from him in 2000. What to expect from him in 2000: Not much. NEAL HALESZ What he did in '99: Um...er...beat Mastermind unimpressively on an On-Campus in December, then got embarassed by Sparkling Dan a few weeks later. What to expect in 2000: A quick exit from the WWO. TYRONE "PURPLE" HAYES What he did in '99: Along with Todd "The Rod" Johnstone and a cast of thousands, The Man, The Mirth, and The Litigation made his WWO debut in January and immediately went about doing what he does perhaps better than anyone in the business: pissing people off and making enemies. Emerged as the victor in a 6-way rookie match on March 11 that included, among others, Apex and Steve Dumars, and then quickly came into conflict with Roundhouse, his old archenemy in two federations, and the rest of the Hall of Fame stable. Was fingered in the heinous attack that left Rusty Bowman with career-ending injuries, which caused his feud with Roundhouse to escalate even more after he decisively beat Lord Keeblor at Battle of the Titans 6. Latex Lex, an old crony from numerous federations, joined soon afterward and helped Tyrone get the better of his Aussie nemesis in their first encounter, on May 27. It was Roundhouse who would be victorious in their rematch on July 11, but Tyrone would get the last laugh, prevailing in their final match at War of the Ages. That show would also see the formation of the Carnage-a-Trois stable with Lex and "Prodigy" Tracy Hudson. They found an immediate enemy in Mike Sebastian, who Tyrone defeated in an IP title contenders' match on August 15, and were slated to be the next great force in the WWO, but were doomed nearly from the start with the addition of the badly overrated "Dios Loco" Rob James. Tyrone was favored to emerge as the winner in a subsequent 4-corners IP title match, but ultimately lost due to Johnny Detson's secret alliance with Al Perez. Angered at being cheated out of a win, Tyrone then declared himself the top contender for the WORLD title, which led to him being placed in a contenders' match at HeatWave with Warren Hayes and Fury (who he also had an issue with) but again came out on the short end of the stick when Warren Hayes prevailed with assistance from Almost Better. HeatWave also saw the disintegration of Carnage-a-Trois, as Tyrone turned on Tracy Hudson and Rob James (who he claims was "forced" on the group) and Latex Lex departed the WWO. Since then, he has scored a win over Randy Bryson in vicious fashion and has formed an apparent alliance with Ultimate Thrasher, while claiming that things between him and Sebastian are unfinished. What to expect from him in 2000: Tyrone has established himself as one of the most dangerous and talented men in the WWO. But how many enemies can one guy have and still survive? Roundhouse, Sebastian, Hudson, Warren Hayes, Bryson..the list of people who Tyrone has rankled reads like a Who's Who Of The WWO. What happens to Tyrone depends on what he wants... if he wants to make a run at the World title, he's capable of doing so. If he'd rather just torment the good guys of the federation, he'll do that. Whatever he does, you can bet he'll excel at it. "MR. RATINGS" WARREN HAYES What he did in '99: Began the year as the fuhrer of Simply The Best, the best stable in WWO history....but STB quickly collapsed around him. Undaunted, he assembled a leaner, meaner fighting force known as Even Better with Mike Sebastian and Randy Bryson, while carefully avoiding an inevitable showdown with nemesis Roundhouse...and suddenly took a hike for a television career which lasted all of six months, before he made a surprise and much welcomed return to the WWO at War of the Ages, crashing Ultimate Thrasher and Iron's main event party and winning the adulation of the fans in the process. The next week, he surprisingly assisted old enemy Roundhouse in beating Thrasher for the World title and drove Iron out of the WWO in the process, but got a rude surprise the week afterwards when Fury returned to the WWO and began assisting Thrasher in attacks on him and Roundhouse. His former lackeys, Almost Better, also resurfaced, along with the hysterical Midget Mason. His shaky "alliance" with Roundhouse was doomed from the get-go, and when Roundhouse lost the title back to Thrasher, Warren declared himself the no.1 contender for the belt...but was not recognized by President Stone as such, and had to win a triangle match at HeatWave which included Fury and Tyrone Hayes to earn the right to fight Roundhouse for the no.1 contendership slot....and lost, thanks to interference from Fury. His feud with Fury would culminate at Thanksgiving, when he defeated Fury in a scintillating career match. Since then, he's made no bones about the fact that he wants to be the official no.1 man in the federation, and has been placed in the six-pack match for the title at Season's Beatings as a result. What to expect from him in 2000: Barring another foray into the entertainment industry, Warren is going to win the World title very soon and probably hold it for a long, long time. Everyone's known what he's capable of since the first time he set foot in the WWO, and now he wants to truly be "the man" in the federation. "THE PRODIGY" TRACY HUDSON What he did in '99: What a difference a year makes. At the outset of '99, it was hard to mention his name in WWO circles without getting snickers in response. Having been humiliated in his TV title feud with Night Stalker, he was now jobbing left and right and seemed ready for the unemployment line...until insulting comments by Ultimate Thrasher on an episode of Re-Wind seemed to ignite a new fire in his belly. Hudson went after Thrasher, and was defeated, but made an excellent showing in his match. In his promos, he began emphasizing the perverted drug addict side of himself, which caught the attention of many fans and censors. Soon afterwards, he joined up with Uncivil Disobedience, "Loaf", and CJ Gesner in the new UCD stable, which functioned as a solid unit for a month or so (during which he lost in a close match to Roundhouse at Valentine's Day Massacre) but eventually fell apart when Loaf and Gesner left the WWO. Hudson then began antagonizing Apex, a feud which culminated in a Hudson victory in a scintillating four-chairs match at Battle of the Titans.This catapulted him into the four-man US title tournament at War of the Ages, and after beating Steve Dumars in the semifinals, he defeated Mike Sebastian for the belt with the help of two new allies, Tyrone Hayes and Latex Lex. Known as Carnage-a-Trois, the stable appeared ready to overrun the WWO with their sick pranks, but quickly made a fatal mistake by adding "Dios Loco" Rob James to the fold to help in their escalating battle against Sebastian and the Gentlemen's Club. After CAT fell apart at HeatWave, Tracy's career seems to have stalled a bit, aside from a convincing title defense against Al Perez on Thanksgiving. But he continues to be a dangerous and controversial figure on WWO television, along with sidekick Danny Janssen. What to Expect in 2000: I don't know if he'll ever take things seriously enough to be World title material, but if he doesn't die from an overdose, he'll remain a strong upper-mid-carder for a long time to come. I don't see his US title reign ending anytime soon.
