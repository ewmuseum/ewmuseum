He's been quite pleased of late. The WWO enters the back-to-school season on a bit of a rut. The Title of America's scene is on fire, but due to the fact the WWO has had a major facelift (plenty of newbies), the challengers to the top titles are quite limited. The Upper Crust seems vulnerable, Dark Soul and Craven are having trouble clicking, the tag scene is top heavy, and more than once, the name "Seabass" has been heard around backstage. Things are in peril, people. But hey, it's August. Nothing ever works in August. My answers for the rut is quite simple. First, use the World Title smartly. I know Dark Soul was trying to do something good when giving Lion Tamer a title shot, but he didn't deserve it and Dark Soul should have been wrestling someone different anyway. The first two opponents Tyrone Hayes faced after he won the title were Dark Soul and Steve Dumars, two mid-to-upper-card faces who were given the opportunity to shine. How about giving title shots to Papa Legba, Nate Knowitall, Eric Stryker, or even Matt Cole. Hell, have a preview of the inevitable. Johnny Detson vs. Dark Soul non-title. Will someone explain to me why P.A.I.N. doesn't look to be in a major feud? Are they just taking this cycle off or something? For that matter, are the Blood Brothers and them blasted Canadians ever going to feud? Oh, give Tyrone Hayes a full-time commentating job. Track down Ultimate Thrasher. More Frost (both of them) exposure. Less Archie exposure. And more than anything, more excitement is needed. More well thought-out matches. Maybe that will rise with the WWO getting rid of a lot of rift-raft. People have seemed dead around WWO HQ of late. Maybe the dog days of summer are upon us. Knight tells me similar things took place last year. Either way, the WWO is desperately in need of people to take the fed on its back and lead the WWO back in the right direction. The only trouble is...do we have those type of individuals left? Since last year, we've lost Hayes, Thrasher, and Apex. Three of the most dependable, sure-fire figures the WWO has ever had. The WWO maybe a more balanced act with no one man head-and-shoulders above the next (well, maybe Craven), but is the parity that good of a thing? Personally, I don't think so. But then again, I'm just a tired editor. --------------------------------------------------------------------------- Rumors by Rumor Will contact: [detail withheld] - Alex Perkins was away from the WWO for Hollywood. Of course, room and board is about all of the WWO's dollar I'm seeing, but I make enough money elsewhere to get by. Although they're certainly not paying me enough to sit through The Amazing Romanis' entrance again (which I fortunately did not have to do this week). But I did get a free ticket to another exciting WWO show, but I have to pay the price by writing about it at the same time. Let us just home that there's more actual wrestling, which of course what I'm in this business to see. If I'd wanted a job featuring lots of poorly formed insults and mindless brawling, I would have taken that job at the Jerry Springer show.. We open with an "earlier today" segment, as we see Xavier Feyr using his escort Lilith as distraction in order to ambush Alex Extreme outside the stadium. While you'll have to forgive me of being ignorant of these men's backgrounds prior to their WWO careers, I must say if what we've seen from them recently is any indication, this could get veeery messy before it's over. Randy Bryson vs. "Deathdealer" Tommy Ganz I've already explained my ignorance of the "legendary" Bryson, and Ganz is only wrestling his second match, so I don't know exactly what to expect out of this match. The fans around me are clearly more aware of Bryson, and he gets damn near booed out of the building, and cheer Ganz, trying to encourage him to upset the veteran. Ganz takes the early advantage with a whip and monkey flip. Bryson stops Ganz from getting any momentum going by leaving the ring several times, and then uses a good old test of strength ambush, but Ganz again takes control of the match. It takes some eye-poking and hair-pulling to regain control, and the fans aren't liking Randy's tactics one little bit. Bryson takes the padding off the turnbuckle, but Ganz regains control and starts working the arm with submissions. Bryson fights back with a low blow, gets some near falls, and then attempts to use that exposed turnbuckle, only to have Ganz counter into another submission. Bryson escapes, and attempts to walk out on the match, but Ganz chases him around the ring, only to get met with a cheap shot back in the ring, and Bryson pins him using the tights after a double-arm DDT. MATCH RATING: Three Stars. Solid match, good technical wrestling that reasserted Bryson as a crafty heel and put the newcomer over as well. The ending seemed a little sudden; otherwise it might have gotten a higher rating. Jack Frost shows up after the match, and while milking the crowd for laughs, says that he wants Bryson to punch him in the stomach, claiming that after the recent beatings, they will suddenly be even. Bryson doesn't buy it, and hits him with a hockey stick instead. It doesn't do any good, as Frost is wearing a metal plate under his shirt. Frost then challenges Bryson by, um, throwing down his red mittens. I think I like this Frost kids, he's, well, unique. And it's good to see Bryson not immediately demanding he be put back in the world title hunt. Backstage, Lion Tamer and Tiger Lily make fun of the new guy Joe Gonzo, saying he smells and looks like a muppet. Okay, is this why Tamer gets a shot at Dark Soul, or am I missing something? Rex Americana vs. Adam Wood So, now we have the jingoist Americana against some new kid no one's heard of. Americana dominates with a variety of power moves and even some hair pulling. The crowd boos Americana, but a lot aren't really into this match. Woods fights back, but not for long, and Rex hits a northern lights bomb, but then won't finish the cover, drawing more boos. He hits a couple more cheap moves, and then finishes it with the Final Solution, his finisher. MATCH RATING: Dud. And obvious squash, and Americana didn't do anything to make it worth my time. Americana won't leave the ring, and tries to call out the Canadian Rick Hanson, for no other reason then that he's Canadian. Hanson doesn't show, so Americana heads backstage. Backstage, security is looking for someone, apparently on the order of Archibald the Uninteresting. Wait, he wants to be called just The Uninteresting now, doesn't he? Well, we'll see what happens, he has a title shot tonight, which should be a make or break match for "The Magnificent," at least in my eyes. Dark Soul and the loverly Ms. Malone show up, and Dark Soul says that he doesn't want to waste his time with a non-title match, as since he his champion, he might as well defend his title. So now it looks like the Showcase match against Lion Tamer will be for the title. Whoo. "Moosejaw Madman" Rick Hanson vs. Victor Frost Frost clearly has the momentum in this match, coming off a big win against Glen Peeps. He should.. haaaaahahahaha, I didn't knew I couldn't keep a straight face after saying that. But seriously, Hanson should have his hands full here tonight, especially if he's more interested in the confetti from his entrance than Frost. Frost takes an early advantage by using, of all things, the Canadian National Anthem to distract Hanson. Hanson fights back with some athletic counters, but one wonders how he does it through all that makeup. A missed rolling thunder allows Frost to regain control, and Victor goes for a pin, only to pull himself up, drawing boos from the crowd. Hanson again shows his ability by landing on his feet coming off a backdrop, and drills Frost in the back of the head. After a pin attempt, a hurricanrana proves ill-advised, as it is countered into a powerbomb, and Frost nearly picks up the three count. Another near fall follows, but Hanson fights back, only to be caught in a Violator Mark II moments later, and Frost gets the duke. MATCH RATING: Two and a half stars. Hanson showed his stuff, but the pacing seemed a little off at times. The Magnificent's life is about to get more interesting, as Papa Legba jumps Francis Blake backstage, evidently not liking Archie's recent behavior. Michael Ice/Lew Spectre vs. Canadian Legacy We're treated to a highlight video of the participants past troubles, and suffice it to say, we should be seeing a bloodbath here today. Eventually, Ice and MacDonald get things started. Ice and Spectre take control early, using effective double-teaming, and Spectre even showing some high-flying skill. MacDonald fights back with a chokeslam out of NO WHERE and gets a two count. Denis tags in, and brawls with Spectre in the corner. Ice and MacDonald brawl outside the ring, while suddenly Aramis Mercedes, manager of Dark Haven, shows up on the entrance ramp. The whole match moves to the outside, and the ref is paying attention to Ice and MacDonald, rather than the legal men, and he misses a chair shot by Denis on Spectre. The match calms down eventually, but only temporarily, as Cameron distracts the ref and allows MacDonald to hit Spectre with his cane. A powerbomb later, and Canadian Legacy picks up the win, MATCH RATING: Two stars. There was some fun brawling, but this match was just too chaotic to make sense. After the match, Canadian Legacy beats down Lew Spectre, until Dark Haven appears, and attempts to soak MacDonald with brandy and set him on fire, only to get jumped by our Canadian Masked Man. Who soundly kills everyone, then reveals himself to be Alex Perkins, or Fire and Ice! In my experience, all Fire and Ice has done is lose, but Perkins looks to be back with a vengance, plus a whole lot of scars. Ice enters the ring, and we get a big group hug, so Fire and Ice seem to be back, and teaming with Canadian Legacy, so I'm glad I'm not Dark Haven right now. Backstage we see Rick Hanson get beat to hell by Rex Americana, who challenges him to a match. We then see The Magnificent and Company, sans Vincent Delacroix, preparing for their match. The Magnificent vs. Title of the Americas Champion Alex Extreme Xavier Feyr shows up with ringside seats a couple rows down from mine (trust the WWO to get better seats for a performer than someone trying to evaluate their product), with his lady Lilith. TM shows his strength early on, but Extreme outwits him, and TM bails to the outside. Feyr uses the ensuing calm to yell out some insults, drawing laughs from the fans sitting nearby. The distraction is enough for Magnificent to take advantage of and jump Extreme. TM pounds away, and when he loses the momentum he rolls out of the ring for a breather, only to take a baseball slide. TM regains control, and starts working over Extreme's knee, using some innovative offense, while Feyr continues to scream insults. Alex fights back, but is clearly hurting in the leg. He manages to drop TM with a neckbreaker and hit an Asai Moonsault, which picks up a near fall. Soon after he tries for the Leap of Faith, but misses it. Five Germans and a powerbomb very nearly put Alex away, but another powerbomb gets turned into a hurricanrana. The two exchange near falls, Extreme even improbably kicking out of The Requiem, a jumping piledriver. TM seems ready to put the match away, when Victor Frost suddenly comes out, dragging a bloodied Vincent Delacroix. Then Papa Legba shows up and jumps Frances Blake, and Rick Marley shows up and gets in a brawl with Feyr, and Feyr drops a set of brass knuckles in the ring. In all the chaos, Extreme manages to nail TM, and picks up the three-count. MATCH RATING: Three Stars. Solid match, with The Magnificent really stepping up and showing he can put on a decent match. He gave Extreme all he could handle, but it was too bad we had to get such a confusing ending, but it looks like everyone wants a shot at Extreme, which should make for interesting TV in the near future. Magnus Colby joins the fray, and the whole mess turns into a brawl beyond describing as the show fades out. SHOW RATING: Thumbs Up. I'm giving the show the benefit of the doubt because I'm seeing some of the new guys step up in a big way.
