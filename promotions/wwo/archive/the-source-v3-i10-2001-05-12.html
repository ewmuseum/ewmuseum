Now then.. -Chris Stringer is backstage, and he buys off Vegas Connection to take out the Quesadas later on. -We're live from Trump Plaza in Atlantic City, NJ. Your hosts are Mickey Ralph and Sam Bradley. Obligatory card shilling follows, and then.. -PAIN comes out, and act shocked about the accusations that they would have the audacity to injure someone for money via an attack, and instead they challenge Detson and "whoever he can drag up" to a regular tag match. Out comes Thrasher, who accepts the challenge "on behalf of the Great Detson" over the objections of Melissa. Sam is apoplectic. -World title tournament match: "The Old" Lord Keeblor vs. Icon-X. Man, the crowd heat for this one is out of the.....infield, barely. Lockup to start, Ike ducks a clothesline attempt and hits a flying elbow. Suplex, cover gets two. Guillotine legdrop gets 2 1/2. And then we get the obligatory Keeblor blown spot, as Icon telegraphs a backdrop attempt, Keeblor just stops, Icon stands up and Keeblor hits a high knee to the face about three seconds later. No, I don't see why he's being de-pushed at all. Keeblor with KICKS TO THE KNEE~!, chops unworthy of a "whoo", and a released belly-to-belly for two. Keebs with an elbow to the "injured" knee, cover gets 2 1/2. Keeblor goes for the Keeblor Bomb, but Icon does a sweet mid-air reversal into Fade to Black for the win. That was thankfully short. Rating: 1/2* -Apex and Dark Soul try to sell Joshua Lachance for $19.95, with a free Super Soul Seeker 3000 thrown in...hmm, maybe I'll pay $19.95 Canadian, otherwise forget it. Lachance himself tries to interrupt, but gets his ass kicked for his trouble. Ahh, how I love humiliation. -Out comes Mark Stone, who chokeslams the entire roster simultaneously and declares himself holder of all titles. No, actually he just bans OSE from ringside for the Stringer-Tucker match, lambastes Hudson for the sorry state of the tag division, and caps it off by booking Hudson & a partner against Cameron & Roberts for later tonight, with a special message to follow. Gee, the handwriting for Hudson getting turfed out hasn't been on the wall for two months or anything, has it? -World tag team title tournament match: The Griefs vs. Dark Soul & Miguel Quesada (w/Cristine, John Collins and Candy Malone): You know, there's nothing like some sweet Candy to un-nauseate yourself after seeing the "Patsys". And take note, front office....MIGUEL AND DARK GOT A MUCH LOUDER FACE POP THAN THE GRIEFS, SO DON'T HAVE MIGUEL PUT OVER ANOTHER DAMNED HAS-BEEN. Thank you. Dan with an armdrag, and slaps on Hold #789,015 (arm-BAR~!) before tagging in TAFKA Roundhouse. Kick, punch, stomp, clothesline and "soupbone right" from Dave (for the millionth time, exactly what in the hell is a "soupbone right" and why does Mick only use it referring to Dave Grief?), but a LEGDROP OF DOOM~! misses, and Dark stomps on the leg a couple of times, hits a headbutt to the midsection, drops an elbow and covers for one. Russian legsweep from Darky, tag to Miguel, double suplex from the Quesadas. -Kneedrop from Miguel, cover gets two. Hangman's neckbreaker gets two, but Dave decides "OY! No wait, he knew one more, a released belly-to-back suplex. Clothesline from Dave, a piledriver, tag to Dan. They go for a double-clothesline, but Miguel makes a blind tag to Dark Soul, ducks the clothesline and slides out of the ring...and both Griefs turn around into DS' flying spinning heel kick. Dan goes down, Dave staggers, trips over Dark Soul and falls right on Dan's right leg...you know, the one he just had surgery on? Cover from Dark gets 2 1/2. Kata hajime, but Dan finally fights his way to the ropes. Bulldog from DS, diving headbutt, cover gets 2 1/2. Short powerbomb gets 2 4/5. Tag to Miguel, but Dan ducks a double clothesline attempt and takes both guys out with a flying bodypress...and there's the hot tag. Dave is a House O'Fire, right hands for both guys, clothesline for Miguel, Dark gets tossed over the top. Belly-to-belly on Miguel, avalanche in the corner. Miguel gets press-slammed, cover gets two before Dark makes the save. Side suplex from Dave, tag to Dan, and another double-team attempt fails as Dark dropkicks Miguel down atop both Griefs. Pier-six ensues, and the Quesadas get whipped into one another. Dark gets hit with a spiked piledriver, but he's not the legal man, and Dan eats a clothesline from Miguel, then the Ali punches follow. Brainbuster, tag to Dark Soul, Asai moonsault gets 2 and 5/6. Dan leapfrogs a backdrop attempt, but his leg "gives out" and Dark slaps on the Soul Lock for the win....bravo. VC attacks after the match and gets in a few good shots as OSE watches in the dressing room, but Dave runs them off. Miguel leaves the ring seperately from Dark Soul after the match...ooo, dissension in the ranks. Solid match, considering 3/4 of the participants aren't really tag team wrestlers. L.T. doesn't like it very much. -World title tournament match: Major Damage (w/General Mayhem) vs. Latex Lex: Everyone decides that we've had enough of heel-heel matchups for the time being, and Lexy no-shows, instead preferring the "gentle, soothing touch" of Glen Peeps. Trust me, that was even funnier when I watched it drunk. Nate Knowitall (oops, I mean "KnowItAll") comes out and attacks, er, tries to attack Damage. Yeah, that's smart. Nate flips him off with both hands, and pays the mere price of having his middle finger broken, being put in the Dead Zone, and taking a spinebuster off the top rope. -Out comes Apex, who gets a big-ass pop of course. He warns Stringer that he's coming for him once Tucker is through with him tonight. -World title tournament match: "The Millionaire Mid-Carder" Eric Stryker vs. "Screwball" Danny Janssen: And Janssen apparently is going to be Hudson's partner tonight as well, unless "Dios Loco" Rob James is coming back for a Carnage-a-Trois reunion or something. Janssen gets attacked coming into the ring and ducks a clothesline attempt, but misses a flying bodypress. Facbuster from Stryker, but he pulls Janssen up at two just to be a dick. Stryker does a Behemoth-worthy no-sell job on a low blow, but Janssen lands on his feet after a hiptoss attempt and hits a double boot to the face of Stryker. Sunset flip (and why has that move become so rare of late, anyway?) gets only one, though, and Stryker just CLOBBERS him across the neck with what can only technically be called a clothesline. Geez, did Danny steal some gold bars from him to buy the first issue of X-Men with or something? Russian legsweep, cover gets two. Stryker hits another stiff right hand, Janssen lands on his feet after a backdrop, and hits a Ghetto Blaster. Missile dropkick gets two, but Stryker then catches him coming off the ropes and powerslams him for two. Janssen hits a flying headscissors for one, but Stryker throws him out of the ring and enter Hudson, with a chain. He tries to hand Danny a chain, but Danny won't take it for some reason. Stryker grabs Hudson, and Janssen rolls him up for two. Hudson tries to hit Stryker with the chain, but Stryker ducks, takes the chain away and drills Hudson with it, then hits Danny with a spinebuster and the Power Stryke for the win. Janssen storms away, throwing the chain down atop Hudson. Surprisingly adequate squash. I look forward to this feud already. -World tag team title tournament match: Vegas Connection (w/Rimshot and Louie the Lip) vs. Uncivil Disobedience: All right, here the fuck we go...the match that will forever prove if VC is in the upper echelon or just mid-carders. VC outdoes themselves once again with a hilarious enterance, and it sounds to me like they're drawing even more heel heat than UCD. Firm and Larry start off all scientifically and stuff, and Larry works on the legs before tagging to Lester, who hits a slingshot legdrop, then a Uranage for two. Tag back to Larry who hits a sidekick but misses a hobby horse....yowch. Tag to Rax, double axehandle gets one. Tag back to Firm, double backdrop, double forearm smash for both VC guys. Crowd is clearly behind UCD in this one. Leary trips Firm up coming off the ropes, and Lowbrow hits a running lariat sending Firm out to the floor. Firm gets pounded on the floor while 'Saki is busy keeping Rax and Larry separated. Back in the ring, tag to Leary....SMIYHTB, then a shoulderblock. Firm catches him with a gorilla press, but Larry saves with a low blow, leaving both guys down. -Hot tag to Rax, pier-six erupts which UCD wins, and they try to whip VC into one another.....so Firm and Rax dosey-do into a clothesline from Firm and a back elbow from Rax. Spiked piledriver on Larry follows, and Firm goes for a pin, but Leary tries to make the save with an Alabama Jam...only for Firm to move. Firm hits a bridging German suplex on Leary, but Hirohito is distracted by Louie, who gets knocked off the apron by Rax. Rax then hits a slingshot legdrop on Leary, but 'Saki is busy getting Firm out of the ring, Rimshot tosses the MIC OF DOOM~! in to Lowbrow, kaboom. Firm takes a shot with the mic as well, as Hirosaki is finally un-distracted, Lowbrow covers Rax...and yes, folks, Vegas Connection is for real. Dark Soul and Miguel attack VC after the match in retaliation for the earlier attack, setting up what should be a hell of a semifinal. This may very well go down in history as one of those "passing-the-torch" moments. Match wasn't quite as good as I expected due to time constraints, but then every card calls for at least one unscheduled UTD wank-fest. Oh, Christ and all the saints, please don't flush THIS match down the toilet so there's sufficient time for Mark Stone to put himself over again. -Tracy Hudson & Danny Janssen vs. Shade Roberts & Don Cameron: So which Hudson shows up for his farewell match, the bland face that jobbed to Night Stalker a million times in 1998, the badass heel of 1999, or the lazy drug addict who jobbed the US title to Keeblor in 2000? Janssen lands on his feet after a backdrop, then hits an Asai moonsault, but Shade counters a hurricanrana with a power bomb. Shade with a CHINLOCK OF DEATH~! as Sam wonders who besides him is left from the [name withheld] era. Mick claims Thrasher is, though he actually didn't start until just after Stone took over. Shade hiptosses Janssen into a cross armbreaker, then tags to Cameron, but Danny ducks a double clothesline before hitting a jumping clothesline on both men. -Hudson still refuses to tag in...Roberts and Cameron yell at him, which lets Janssen hit a missile dropkick on Cameron for two. Overhead belly-to-belly suplex from Cameron, tag to Roberts. Hudson tries to walk out of the match, but his path gets blocked by Stone and Olvera, who is confirmed as Hudson's replacement as tag team VP (gee, that's only been common knowledge since February) Cameron clotheslines Hudson from behind, drags him back to the ring, and Janssen finally tags him in to a HUGE pop. Shade locks in the Shadowleaf, but Hudson is too close to the ropes. Tag to Cameron, double chops in the corner (whoo). Stone gets on the mic again and officially fires Hudson after the match. So long Tracy, nice comeback though. Well...hey, I've seen worse comebacks. Does Andros' second one ring a bell? Honestly, a lot of people are asking what he's still doing in the sport, and after a lackluster match up with Icon-X, this writer has to agree with him. Part I: The Setup You know, I promised that I'd use this column to give you some insight into how things are going backstage in the World Wrestling Organization. However, for my own purposes, I decided that I'd hold off on that this week. But I thought I'd keep the same basic premise with the subject of this article. Let me ask you a question. How many of you have heard of the wrestler known as Jack Vars? If not, let me give you a little history lesson about Mr. Vars. Back before professional wrestling was the "in thing" among professional sports, Jack Vars competed in a ton of organizations in professional wrestling. In fact, he was in so many, one had to wonder how he was able to keep his schedule straight, or even more so, how did he ever get any time off to sleep? Just on logistics alone, one has to question how many people went around claiming to be "Mr. Vars". One day he could be in Philadelphia, the next day in Los Angeles, a third day in Tokyo, and perhaps even on another day some small town like Nantucket. Did Dr. Stykes learn the miracle of cloning before even joining the World Wrestling Organization and put it to use? Let me ask you this. How many jobs can one man (or woman) put themselves through before they have a legitimate nervous breakdown? The human body can only handle so much travel, jet lag, and not to mention the physical punishment we put ourselves through in this sport. How many federations are too many? And is there a way to choose the "right" federations to work with for your wrestler? Let's be honest. If I have a WWO superstar set to wrestle in Baltimore, MD, I wouldn't expect him to be in Tokyo, Japan the previous night, and be able to come in and give a full effort in this federation. Not only is he not giving the fans 100%, but he's not giving the federation 100%... and that means he's earning a paycheck for doing a half-ass job in the World Wrestling Organization. So when a WWO superstar comes out and talks about his NEO matches. Fortunately, for the fans of the WWO the two federations have not had dates cross one another up, and therefore have not had the resigning of Dumars cause any logistic problems. But what happens when a wrestler such as Andrew Tucker, who also wrestles in a number of "national" to "global" federations has his character cross time lines? I've always said exposure in as many organizations is possible. But at some point, you've got to slow down and ask when have you reached enough? Let's be honest. The human body CAN only take so much punishment. And the human mind can only follow so much as well. So what's the solution? Is there a solution? I think there is a solution, if people are willing to work at it. And if there was some sort of coordinated "effort" by the federations themselves. And it comes out of the tried and true, "umbrella" organizations, only in our case, we're going to refer to it as a "Circle of Federations". A typical umbrella organization works as follows: BANNER FEDERATION | |-----------|-------------|------------|-------| Reg1 REG2 REG3 REG4 REG5 As you can see, regional federation #1 is on the same level as regional federation numbers 2-5. And in most Umbrella federations, a wrestler in Regional Federation cannot wrestle in Regional number 5. So why can't the same premise work on a grander scale? Thus the "circle of federations". First there is no "top federation" that is used to tie them altogether. And all circles, while recognized from one another, work independently of one another. The key to a "circle" is that all levels of federations must be represented at some point in a circle. And the levels of a federation are as follows 1) GLOBAL: Have cards all over the world on a regular or semi-regular basis. 2) INTERNATIONAL: Cards will happen in select countries with the rare exception. For example, a US-Canada federation with the occasional card in Japan would fall under "International". 3) NATIONAL: Cards are limited to one nation or country, with only a very RARE international appearance. 4) REGIONAL: Specifically limited to a smaller region, county, or city of wrestling. 5) INDEPENDENT FEDERATIONS: "Backyard" federations, or unaffiliated federations. A few "rules" of the Circle of Federations. A single circle may have one and ONLY one Global federation. A federation such as the WWO would qualify itself as a Global federation, and would therefore have to remain separate from a Global federation such as the EMWC or UWF. International federations can have multiple instances, but it would be recommended that any more than two international federations that cover the same areas (two US-Japan federations) would necessitate any further federations that fit that definition to be placed in a separate circle. Where as a circle with a US-Japan federation and a Japan-Australia and a Australia-US federation without causing serious overlap. However, a circle federation which is willing to forgo a global federation could find itself including a few extra international federations, as needed to cover that global organization. National federations should represent no more than 2 federations per country. A circle should have no more than 2 US-national only federations, two Canadian-national only federations, 2 Japanese federations, and so on. Regional federations again work on the similar principle to that of national federations, although more leeway would be given as regions can be broken down in a number of varieties. Listed below are a couple of examples. And while some of these federation names are easily recognizable as existing federations, while others are simply "figments of my imaginaton". It must be made clear that this is no way is a "exact replica", simply a couple of samples of possible circles. There are a couple of notes involved in arranging these circles. First off, your federations must have a measure of trust and respect within the circle. That's not saying that they have to agree with everything that is done. But they must be willing to recognize one another as an operating franchise. Inner circle dependency requires that you admit that you're not the only federation within the circle. Now, before the questions are bombarding, about "isn't this the same as an umbrella" organization, let me clear up a few points. First, circles can obviously grow extremely large, and regional federations can easily overlap one another, unlike an umbrella federation, where you are limited to "non-active" regions for any newly added organization. Secondly, federations are not required to accept wrestlers in another federation on to their roster. Nor do federations have to recognize titles of other federations as the "official belt" for any given region, although that is recommended, for the unity of the circle. Third, federations may gladly recognize federations in other circles as being active, and even worthy federations. In umbrella organizations, the regional members are the "only" recognized organizations. Outside of the top organization, other federations typically are given much less weight, not even considered "equal" organizations. In a circle, one federation of circle one has the same recognition as one federation of circle four. Of course, the hardest part of the whole setup is for federations to recognize that they are in separate circles. Which for the most part, will be a difficult, if not downright impossible task for such a sport. But one could only hope that if the NFL could accept the AFL, IPLF, and even yes, the now-defunct XFL as viable leagues, then perhaps there would be a hope that in professional wrestling we could do the same? But the even more difficult part will be the responsibilities of the wrestlers in various federations. Yes, the responsibility of the wrestlers. It is they who have the job of "select and choice" which we'll take a look at next issue of the source, and how they must do their part to make the whole "Circle Theory" work.
