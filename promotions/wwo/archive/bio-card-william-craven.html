WWO bio card — William Craven

31 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
William Craven Wrestler Name: William Craven Date of Birth: 11/15/66 Height: 6'5 Weight: 320 Alignment: Diabolic Hometown: Detroit, Michigan Entrance Music: "Prison Sex" by Tool Manager: Doctor Victor Schwartz Managerial Profile Physical Appearance Standard Appearance Coming to the ring, Craven is every inch the nightmare. Hugely built up, and incredibly well-defined muscle bulges out from every part of his body. Deep striations in the muscle and traumatic scars add to his intensely monstrous appearance. In fact, it's hard to find a patch of skin anywhere that doesn't bear a scar of some sort on Craven's body. Cuts, tears, surgical scars, bones that were broken but never set, everything just seems *BLEEPED* up and out of place on this man. Perhaps the most intimidating parts of Craven's appearance are Craven's eyes. Frequently bulging and squinting, red veins nearly popping out of the white surface in which they're set. The intense cold blue eyes of Craven have an almost supernatural quality to them. Frequently they will be dilated, one pupil bigger than the other, his vision sometimes being offset by his medication or just his emotions. The color actually shifts sometimes from a rich sky blue to an almost white crystal blue depending on his mental state. Regardless, they seem to have the capability of intimidating others by relaying Craven's innermost self (if you buy into that new-age stuff) to onlookers and opponents. This, his baldness (shaved from head to toe), his scars, the whole package, make Craven look most definitely like a monster straight out of some horror novel... Covering his back is a tattoo portraying a pair of entwined serpents. Their tails meet at his tailbone, and their heads cross over at the base of his neck. Fangs bared, they're pretty nasty to look at, to be sure. Ring Appearance Wrestling in the ring, Craven wears a pair of long, shiny, vinyl, fatigue patterned neo-military looking pants, and a white T-Shirt, cut off at the shoulder, sometimes with hand-drawn epithets scrawled across them in red paint ... unless that really is blood. Thick combat boots cover his feet, which is a big change for a man who's spent most of his career wrestling in bare feet. When coming to the ring, Craven wears a flat black mask, without any holes for the eyes or nose. It would be easy for the casual observer to think him blinded by this headgear, but those who have wrestled him when he's worn the mask as Major Damage know better... When Craven enters the ring, the mask is removed, and he seems panicked by it's disappearance. Schwartz uses the mask as a Pavlovian mechanism, rewarding Craven's actions with the ability for Craven to hide his face. Psychological Makeup William Craven's ringwork centers entirely around his ability to both withstand and inflict tremendous amounts of punishment. In his mind he is weak, in his body he is strong, and this shows through in virtually every aspect of his public persona. Relying upon his manager (therapist?), Victor Schwartz for guidance, Craven seems more a tool than a man. The flat black mask he wears is now a big, big, big piece of the Craven puzzle. Without it, he's in murderer mode. With it, he feels in control. Without it, he feels that everybody hates him, and he must punish them for it. With it ... he's not William Craven. He has neither Craven's warped morals, nor his intense need to cause chaos. It's a mixed bag, to be sure. Strengths Physical power Possessed of an intense upperbody strength Craven is quite capable of benchpressing something in the neighborhood of 550-600 pounds. This is often used to strike as Craven brings his martial arts knowledge to bear on an opponent, to lift an opponent high overhead, or simply to grind him to powder... Stamina Craven has an incredible cardiovascular system and is capable of sustaining long periods of intense physical activity without tiring. Toughness Beyond having a Marathon runner's stamina, Craven's body is toughened by extreme training. He is very difficult to injure, weardown, etc. This combined with a complete and utter inability to feel pain makes submission impossible on Craven, a knockout difficult, etc. Additionally, Craven recovers from physical harm at a surprising rate. This allows him to go longer and wrestle multiple matches in a night with little weardown. Weaknesses No regard for personal safety Craven will do anything to win a match. This includes anything from psychotic-level suicidal actions in the ring (with little ability to execute such maneuvers) to purposeful self-mutilation with intent to frighten an opponent. When your opponent slices his palm open, then offers you his hand for a test of strength, are you going to grab that hand and play "mercy", or are you going to back away from the psychopath? It's this sort of thing that Craven counts on when wrestling. It can also cost him, as the loss of blood from a self-inflicted injury can weaken him, and his suicidal tactics can backfire, his huge frame hitting steel and giving his opponent the advantage. Easily distracted Just what it says. Craven can be pulled away from a match while he attempts to attack someone else. This sometimes leads to disqualifications and countouts. With a manager at ringside (Schwartz) to keep him in line, this effect is lessened, but it is still there, and without Mayhem in place, he will be subject to this limitation. Ring Savvy Some of what Craven does in the ring, maneuvers, using the ropes, foreign objects, etc, indicates that he has been in the business for some time now, however, he still hasn't learned to use the ring to his advantage defensively. Escaping a maneuver by grabbing the ropes is not something he would think to do. Additionally, he still does make rookie mistakes, such as going to the top rope when an opponent isn't weak enough to stay down, or stand still, to be struck by his attack. In short, there are a lot of holes in Craven's average gameplan. Biographical Information Born November 15, 1966 in downtown Detroit, Michigan, William Henry Craven II has been different from the beginning. It's not like he came out physically deformed or scarred the way he is now, but from the very start his mind was simply not right. While still a baby Craven was found to have a nerve disorder that made it impossible for him to feel pain. He would cut himself and laugh. Break bones and chuckle. Never caring a bit about the damage done, or his mother's worry. This is only one piece of the genetic puzzle he's inherited from his father, William Senior. While still young a traumatic event would occur that would bring his other disorders to the surface. His father, a Baptist priest, in a sudden outburst of violence, struck him with a baseball bat. It was little Billy Craven's ninth birthday, and all he wanted was a Louisville Slugger baseball bat. When he complained his father swung the bat, a no-name product, striking his youngest son once in the neck. It was his ninth birthday, and his tenth would pass before little Billy would walk again... After a brief coma brought about by this assault Billy developed Bi-Polar disorder and Schizophrenia while laying in a hospital bed, paralyzed. From then on he would be medicated, reliant on his pills to keep him from simply snapping and recreating the madness that was his father. It seems from this point on that Craven has been a combatant. Competing in kickboxing leagues, amateur wrestling circuits, and even the deathmatches and pitfights in Asia. His training in martial arts began when he was only six. He trained in karate and judo as a boy, and graduated to the high-impact kickboxing style of Muay-Thai when he turned fifteen. As an amateur wrestler he began in high school, captaining the wrestling team, and continuing to participate in it competitively until the end of his college days. In his early twenties, William graduated to Pancratium, a deadly form of Greco-Roman wrestling, and the only form intended, classically, to kill. Craven is a man who lives for fighting and the spotlight it brings. After many a trauma, and at the age of 31 Craven came to New Jersey with a friend. The promise of a clean slate, and jobs as bouncers drew them from the Motor City. His friend remained a bouncer for over a year, but William found a better job... Wrestling Background> ACWA: For the first solid year of the ACWA's existence, Craven was _the_ dominant force. The most feared man in the promotion. He held the ACWA heavyweight championship belt from April 9 to July 7 1999, and he won it again a year after his pro debut, only to quit the ACWA on the spot, walking out and causing a second title match to take place. WET: Bridging the gap between the ACWA and the EMWC, WET was a small British promotion that folded soon after it opened. During it's two month stint, however, Craven was undefeated, destroying every man that stood in his way. Only four matches, but still, it helped cement his reputation as a devastating competitor. EMWC: Entering the EMWC as a murderous nightmare creature, William wreaked havoc for most of his stint, his drive to succeed in the company failing after about a year. Looking around, William found himself without friends, without even any enemies, and without any reason to even try anymore. To the fans, William was just another psycho among psychos. To the guys in the back, he was a has-been that never was. Unable to even make himself compete, he gave a lackluster effort in a feud against Mike Justice, but stepped to the side long before he was officially released from his contract, not even wrestling in several matches where "William Craven" supposedly appeared. GLCW: Perhaps the hardest time of William's career. Lydia, the love of his life, and he were married for the second time shortly before they exited the EMWC. Lydia pushed and pressured Bill until he agreed to leave the worldwide promotion, and the pair returned to Michigan. In Detroit, they lived an oddly normal life, and Lydia reshaped Bill into someone she could be proud of. The "Hometown Hero" Billy Craven. A recreation of his college days at MSU. The gimmick was short-lived, however, as Craven simply couldn't keep up with the competition. That creature, "Billy", no longer existed, and the real Craven, under the surface, was unable to fight. Gradually, his older in-ring personality began to reassert itself, and Lydia, unwilling to put up with her authority being questioned outside the ring, left Bill, angrily crying abuse, and divorcing Bill, taking him for over $5,000,000, and leaving him homeless. At this point, Bill left the GLCW, left Michigan, and fled back to the Atlantic Coast. Back to Dr. Schwartz, his therapist. The good doctor gladly took him back, letting Bill sleep on his couch, and, more importantly, started making phonecalls... Having tasted fame in the ACWA, Schwartz was unwilling to let his second chance at stardom slip away. So, he contacted some people, who contacted other people, who contacted ... Chris Stringer. Stringer talked to his people, who talked to [name withheld], and William Craven was signed to a contract under the pseudonym (and pun) of "Major Damage". Schwartz playing the part of manager as "General Mayhem", of course. WWO: In the early going, "Major Damage" was an unstoppable force. A wrecking ball used by Schwartz and Stringer to hurt many a wrestler. Damage' finisher, the "Dead Zone", was devastating, being applied with such brute strength. But the plan, from the beginning, was to "release the beast". And, in the end, that's what they did. Stringer gave the order, the premise of "Operation: Scorched Earth" being a military force was dropped, and Schwartz pulled the mask away, revealing William Craven... Supercards: Participating in several, Craven won KNI III against Nyx Dunne, Devil Elvis and the Warlock. He was a finalist in the second R-Cup tournament, and returned in the third to defeat Alex Martinez and Rick Styles in a superbout alongside Luke Kinsey. He was a finalist in the Master of the Ring IV tournament, losing out to Chris Courtade. These haven't had much bearing on his career, but warrant a mention nonetheless. Wrestling Information Wrestling Styles/Grades Daredevil Fair High-Flier None Martial Arts Good Power Excellent Technical Excellent Submission Very Good Cheating Poor Brawling/Hardcore Excellent Escapability None Stamina Excellent Ring Awareness None Teamwork None Top Wrestling Moves Brawling/stiff martial arts blows (forearms, kneeshots, shin kicks, legsweep). Mafia kick. T-Bone suplex. Also known as an Exploder Suplex. Wheelbarrow suplex. Applied to an opponent who is prone on the mat, face down, Damage grabs both legs and heaves the man back with a german suplex. Throatslam Suplex. Also known as a "Uranage". Dragonscrew Legwhip. Crucifix Kneebar. Cross armbreaker\headscissors combination submission. A Stepover toehold facelock (STF). Suicide dive. Finishing Maneuver The Dead Zone - An elevated Crossface Chickenwing submission. Craven first applies a chickenwing, then reaches around, clasping his hands together solidly behind the victim's neck, then LIFTS the man up into the air by that grip. The result is a nigh-inescapable, crippling submission hold that can knock a man out, break his arm or neck, and, in general, finish a match very very quickly.
