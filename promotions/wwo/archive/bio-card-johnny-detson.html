WWO bio card — The "Blockbuster" Johnny Detson

31 May 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The "Blockbuster" Johnny Detson Real Name: Jonathan Thomas Detson Born: May 7, 1973 Height: 6'3 Weight: 248 Place of Birth: Billings, Montana Residence: Hollywood, California Alignment: Selfish Physical Appearance Detson is a typical actor type. White, well built, blonde hair that's always in place, blue eyes, and very good looking. Detson knows how good he looks and it shows with his appearance. To the ring Detson will wear a pair of stunning long golden trunks with a pair of black boots, and he will always have on a pair of shades. He will also usually wear a customized tee shirt that he made that either taunts his opponent or the crowd. Out of the ring Detson will either wear one of his many Italian suits or he will go casual. Whatever Detson wears he will always appear at a public appearance in style! Psychological Makeup Detson believes that his main purpose in life is to put on a show. Each and every night he's goes out to do just that. Everything in Detson's world revolves around Detson, so he believes that's the way it is for everybody else. He goes out to humiliate everybody who faces him, using fantastic moves and verbal taunts. Getting the "EW"'s and "AH"'s from the crowd is Detson's high. Detson is out to prove he is the best, to the wrestlers and to the crowd, and he does it by any means necessary. Biographical Information Born Jonathan Thomas Detson on May 7, 1973 in Billings, Montana. His father was a very wealthy man making his fortune in the oil business. Was an average kid growing up back then, but always had a passion of putting on a show. His mother picked up on that passion, and by the age of eight he was starring in local TV commercials. By the time he was a junior in high school he auditioned for a part on the soap "All My Children" and won the role. He used his nickname JT at first for a stage name, but later changed it to Johnny. He emancipated from his parents at sixteen and moved to New York City. After high school Detson attended New York University while still appearing on the show. After starring on the show for a little over three years he was approached by the people at HBO to appear in one of their movies, "Last Chance". After that he was finished filming that, he was cast for a major motion picture in a sidekick type roll in, "Blood Red". Simply put a star had been born. He had shared a mild deal of success in the soaps. Women often identified him as a hunk, but after "Blood Red" his popularity skyrocketed. At the age of nineteen he was a teen idol. Girls hung posters of him, and everywhere he went there was an instant mob flowing towards him. Two months after "Blood Red" was released his ABC contract was up, and he did not renew. Instead he dropped out of college, and moved to the West Coast, Los Angeles, California. He mostly took work in Action based type movies, but did appear in some comedies although it didn't matter. Whatever movies had his name in the credits was an automatic "blockbuster" hit! The money, fame, and power that come with success came to Detson, and he loved every bit of it! Just after his twentieth birthday he married the girl he fell in love with at college. At the same time he was being declared one of the sexiest men in all of Hollywood. Movie roles kept flowing in. The studios and Detson himself were getting richer and richer. However like most Hollywood success stories this one hit a snag. Detson was enjoying his life as a Hollywood star perhaps a little too much. At twenty Johnny Detson was known as a premiere actor, and he was also known on the inside as one of the biggest partiers in Hollywood. Every single week it seemed that Detson would either have a bash at his house, or at one of the nightclubs in Los Angeles. It now started to appear that this was taking its toll on the man. He was constantly arriving late for readings; in fact he was fired for one set because of his drunken behavior. It all came to a crashing halt on his twenty-first birthday when he knocked out a photographer at a nightclub, and then later that same night was arrested for drunken driving. It was then that Detson realized that he needed to get his life in order, but by then it was too late. The photographer was awarded two million dollars in damages. His marriage was ending after a little over one year. And now the movies producers didn't want to take a chance on the man many were calling a loose cannon. With the loss of money, jobs, and his wife Detson sank into a deep depression. Detson didn't get a major role for the next nine months. He starred in several independent and cable made movies, but his heart just wasn't into it. He had cleaned up his act, the parties and booze were gone from his life, but still no one would take a chance on him. Discouraged he moved back to Montana, and considered giving up acting all together. Until he got one phone call that started it all up again. The people at NBC saw Detson downfall, and realized they could capitalize on it. The approached Detson about doing TV again. At first Detson didn't buy it, he was firm on the idea that it was movies or nothing at all. But then he read the concept of the show and was immediately hooked. The script for the show gave Detson the chance to do something he hadn't done for some time, DRAMA. Detson poured his heart and soul into the show, and the pilot was released in the Summer. While Detson got great reviews for the show, the audience just wasn't behind it. The show was cancelled after six episodes, and Detson was left out of work again, but this time it wasn't for long. The big wigs in Hollywood saw the show and were immediately hooked on the idea of Detson doing drama. Detson was cast for some small roles at first where he did well, and then he was presented with the role of his life. The folks at Universal Studios approached Detson with a script in the fall of '94. The basis of the movie would be the story of a mentally retarded child who copes with life around him after the death of his parents. Detson's role in the movies would be the older brother who gives up his dream of becoming a major league baseball player to return home and raise his challenged brother. The name of the movie was "A Struggle At Hand". Detson loved the script and his role and immediately accepted to play the part. People loved the movie also, and it was one of the biggest hits of the season. The Detson Comeback was almost complete, but during the Academy Awards it was given its final seal of approval. Johnny Detson won the Oscar for Best Supporting Actor in a motion picture, and he was back in the spotlight. The media picked him up as one of the greatest comeback stories of all time. The movie roles were coming in fast and furious again; it seemed like Detson was back on top. However this comeback was to be short lived. While the movie roles were coming back in, the salary being offered was minimal at best. The studios were treating him as if he were still a caution. They weren't sure how long this comeback would be for, and how long the people would embrace him. Even the studio that first backed him, Universal, wasn't giving him the kind of money he thought he deserved. Detson, insulted by these offers, rejected every last one of them until he got the money he thought he had learned. The media first on his side, now labeled him selfish. Misguided reports of him wanting up to thirty million dollars per picture came in, and Detson was on the down side again. Detson was just trying to earn want he thought he was worth. Thanks to the studios, however, and the help they got from the media Detson was portrayed as the bad guy. Greedy up until the last cent. Detson, at the age of twenty-three, had the last straw when he walked of the set of "Titanic" after a heated argument with director James Cameron causing a one year delay in the filming. Detson vowed never to return to acting again until he was paid the kind of money he was worth. Once again in Hollywood's bad graces Detson returned home to Montana. He still had the desire to be in the spotlight. He just had no desire left to deal with the corruption of Hollywood. He had the slightest idea what to do. He had received offers to appear on Broadway, and even received several more offers for television pilots. It was at this time, when he was contemplating his next move, that he received the most intriguing offer of them all. When his uncle Vincent Longlo first approached him on the idea of professional wrestling, Detson scoffed. His uncle was a star in the mid fifties wrestling as the Italian Assassin in a post WWII era, and was certain Detson could pull it off. Detson however wasn't sold on the idea at all. But after thinking about the idea for a while he realized that it wasn't the worst idea out there. He had the body for it; his days as an action star ensured him of that. He even had an amateur background from his days in high school wrestling. And he was certain that entering the field of professional wrestling would direct the biggest buzz around him. Detson then decided that this was the choice for him. He was tired of the acting field all together, and decided to go on a new path. He went with his uncle to his training facility and for six months learned the ins and outs of wrestling. He added on about twenty-five pounds of bulk, and was then ready to enter the world of wrestling. If Detson hoped to create a buzz around him, he certainly achieved his goal! The media picked up on it as soon as they learned of Detson's plans. They laughed and make jokes about Detson's career choice, but Detson was more determined then ever to prove that he was a star. He moved back to California to make it his permanent home, and to soak in all of the hype. Detson decided that he would start from the bottom and then work his way to the top just like everybody else. With his uncle's influence he got his first job in the very small independent fed, IRW. What the wrestlers thought of as a joke and a publicity stunt, soon found out to think otherwise. Johnny Detson the wrestler was for real, and he was in it for the long haul. Soon Detson was out performing most of the competition and it was realized that he was ready for the big time. After a year of wrestling in several Indy circuits, Detson was picked up by an up and coming company known as the CWC. Detson finally thought that he had made it. All his hard work in the smaller federations got him pushed right to the top when he finally made it big. He received top billings and World Title matches, and people were now sold on the fact that Johnny Detson could wrestle. Unfortunately the CWC did not reach the level they were trying to achieve and when the ratings fell the company was left no choice then to file for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. It was at this point that Detson thought that he needed a change of pace. For two years now he was struggling hard with the fact that he was being promoted as a bad guy. The wrestling world had used all that press before about how Detson demanded Hollywood to pay him bug bucks and used it against him. The attitude that everybody loved him for in Hollywood made for the premiere "bad guy" in the wrestling world. People called him an egomaniac, arrogant, and said he was "full of himself". Detson was so used to the people cheering him all over the world, that had to get use to the fact that in the wrestling world he was the ideal bad guy with his current attitude. When he went to his next job opportunity, the WWO, Detson pleaded with the front office to drop the Hollywood part of Detson's resume. He solely wanted to be referred to as just a wrestler from now on. The front office granted Detson's request, and the new Detson was born. He dropped the Hollywood style from his name. His moniker the "Blockbuster" was dropped from his name. He wanted nothing to do with Hollywood anymore because he thought it had ruined his career once again. He moved to San Diego trying to get away from the Hollywood image. He wanted the people to like him once again, and cheer his name when they saw him. To be blunt, the new and improved Johnny Detson, well SUCKED. Without the arrogance and attitude that made Detson famous, he was just an ordinary wrestler. Nobody at all cheered him; they booed him for wasting their time. With nothing catching the audience's attention he sunk lower and lower in the rankings of the WWO. Even with his best friend Jude St. Claire by his side, Detson wasn't able to pull himself out of this funk he was in. Detson however refused to return to the Hollywood imagine that made him famous. The front office pleaded with him, but to no avail. The company was just about ready to release him when something made him change his mind. Detson got a call from an independent film group interested in acquiring Detson's services. They didn't want him to act, but to try something different. They hired Detson to direct the film they were making. At first Detson was skeptical, but after trying it, he fell in love. The spark of Hollywood returned to him and so did the arrogance and the attitude. His promos for wrestling were instantly better, and his interviews for the company had the same flair as when he first started. Before he even realized it the old Detson had returned and so did his success. With Detson doing two things he loved, he now considered himself on top of the world again. And with his newfound success again he realized that his ego was justified, and that he was the very best. Johnny Detson realizes now that he was destined for greatness, and now has no trouble promoting that fact. Whether they boo him or cheer him, whether they admire him or are jealous of him, whether they love or hate him Johnny Detson is going to go out each and every night and perform for the crowd. To him, he is the reason people pay money to see the show. He is out to prove one single point, that every thing he does he can be the best at. And sooner or later people will have to recognize that very fact.
