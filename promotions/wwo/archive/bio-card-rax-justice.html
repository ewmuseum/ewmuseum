WWO bio card — Rax Justice

26 May 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Rax Justice Real Name: Ernest Gibbons Height: 6'3 Weight: 200 Alignment: Unprincipled Hometown: Downtown Canada Entrance Music: (single) Lit-"My Own Worst Enemy" (tag) Daft Punk-"Da Funk" Manager: Big S-Ron Managerial Profile Physical Appearance Psychological Makeup Strengths Lack of Morals -- Rax will not balk at ANYTHING to gain a psychological advantage. He'll cheat, lie, steal, hit you backstage, kill your valet, kidnap your pet dog, anything. Risk-taking -- Rax is willing to try all sorts of self-destructive aerial moves and double-team combos with Firm. Cunning -- Rax (and, to a lesser degree, Firm) is able to devastate his opponents by thinking one step ahead, both in matches and in the backstage area. He's so sneaky, his name should be Sneaky McSneaking Sneak. Weaknesses Strength -- Rax isn't a particularly strong guy, and he can be overwhelmed by a bigger, stronger wrestler with the skill and stamina to counter his high-flying moves. Brawling -- While Rax always uses foreign objects, he's much better using them occasionally than in, say a Hardcore match, where they'd be the focus of the match. Showmanship -- Rax will showboat at the expense of his own offense sometimes. Biographical Information There's not a whole heck of a lot we know about Rax's background, except what we hear from the man himself (and that information's not always too trustworthy). Some believe that he grew up raised by existential homeless communist carnies on the north side of Toledo, others insist that up until 1990 or so, he was renowned movie star Judge Reinhold, but was forced to change his name due to excess disinterest. Regardless, let's get down the the place where the bear shits in the buckwheat, and talk about what we know of good old X-Rax. Rax's wrestling career began shortly after a minor run-in with the downtown Canadian authorities. After being brought into court for the usual piddling offenses associated with D & G (driftin' & griftin'), Rax was defended by a stout young lad named Hoover D. Flatt, who would later come to be known as The Firm. Flatt was unable to defend Rax against the charges made, so Rax, in a fit of frustration never before seen in the courtroom leapt at the jurors in a rough approximation of a tope suicida. While Firm was impressed, the judge was not--Rax was promptly shipped off. While in the slammer, Rax learned how to knit. His favorite stitch was the...oh, WRESTLING biography, sorry. Rax survived his prison sentence by befriending a crowbar he referred to as "August", and using August to defend his maidenhead. When not being chased about, he took time to watch professional wrestling, the sport of kings. He and August spent hours beside the television, watching men in their underwear hit men in their underwear with heavy objects. One day, two words streaked across Rax's brain like nightfire, and he knew that he must pursue wrestling. Those two words: I'M HAAAAAAARRRRRRDDDDDDDMOTHERF'K'NDDDDCCCCGODDAMNITOOOOOOORRRRRRRRREEEEE! After leaving the joint, he hooked up with young Mr. Flatt, and Uncivil Disobedience was born. Uncivil Disobedience has become a staple in the WWO tag team division, generally playing the foil to more established teams such as Search and Destroy, Fire and Ice, or the Outlaws, but always holding their own and impressing their cult following. In 2001, UCD will look to do what they do best--irritate people, preferably with blunt objects--and surely, their future history will write itself.
