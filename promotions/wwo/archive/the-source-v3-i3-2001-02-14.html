Johnson rolls back out and hits Behemoth with a chairshot from behind, then three more to the forehead, but all of these get no-sold because YOU CAN'T BEAT GENETICS!!! Sam thinks this chair would make good championship material, which is more than I can say for Johnson at this point. Edwards and Wright attack Stykes again to distract Behemoth, who rolls out of the ring and proceeds to no-sell various punches and chairshots before double-chokeslamming Edwards and Wright, before Johnson hits a cool slingshot plancha....too bad it's on Stykes. Beating up managers is HAAAAARRRRRRRDCOOOORE. Behemoth tosses Johnson back into the ring, but doesn't know what to do with Stykes out cold, and gets counted out. Um, if you're going to build the guy up as an indestructible monster like this, at least have him squash people like Johnson. Yeesh, Behemoth's selling skills make Dave Grief look like Chris Stringer. Johnson still has decent workrate, now all he needs is a refresher course in mic skills and a halfway decent angle, for starters. So, before I get to the list, I'll remind you to send your votes in to [detail withheld] . As before, you can vote for anything. 1st place votes will count for 5 points, 5 place votes will count for 1, and you can figure out the rest. Events, like matches, may also be voted for. In that case, all participants in the match will be given half of the normal points for that voting slot. For example, should someone happen to have voted for Behemoth Vs Johnson for their 4th place vote, both Behemoth and Johnson would be given one point each, instead of the standard 2 points for a 4th place vote. I apologize if that wasn't a clear option in the Hot 5's introductory article, but, I hope that cleans things up. Yeah, I know, we won the match. But Apex and Miguel messed with my _plan_, and that's the important thing. Miguel, I had a master scheme that could have worked for all of our benefit, but your stupid pride had to mess it up. Pride. That's an ugly emotion. It has no place in wrestling, in my opinion. Pride makes you avoid alliances that could be beneficial, or stick with an alliance long past its usefulness. It causes you to underestimate opponents you shouldn't look over. It causes you to turn down help when it may be needed. I'll give Johnny Detson his props. He understands this. Now, in a perfect world, Johnny would probably rather drink Heineken until his pubic hairs turn red than team up with the Ultimate Thrasher. But he understands that his pride is worth less than the help Thrasher could give him. Therefore, he swallows his pride and accepts an uneasy alliance, one that could take him to the top of the WWO. Miguel does not understand this. Rather than do myself and Steve a favor, his pride wouldn't allow him to take orders from anyone. Teams designated as the next big thing usually wind up falling flat on their faces, while those which are regarded lightly initially usually end up winning titles. The path taken by the division throughout its history has certainly made for excellent viewing, but also makes it a bit hard to gauge who the very best teams have been. Nonetheless, I'll give it a shot. Honorable mention: Almost Better, Lethal Weapons, Hounds of Hell, Moshpit, LA Wrecking Crew 10) THE OUTLAWS Accolades: None Their time in the WWO was all too short, but while in the federation Doc Holliday and Brent Maverick along with manager Tex James were able to make life living hell for the rest of the tag team roster, especially Uncivil Disobedience. One of the truly regrettable things about the WWO closing in April 2000 was not being able to see the Outlaws continue wreaking havoc on the federation along with the rest of the Society of Notoriety stable. 9) WORLD LEADERS Accolades: WWO World tag team title (3 reigns) Although the name of this team is most commonly associated with the worst period in the history of the WWO's tag team roster, Hussein and Napoleon still must be given credit for holding the titles a record-setting 3 times, as well as ending the reign of the WWO's first dominant team, the Business Men. 8) BIONIC TEAM Accolades: WWO IP tag team title WWO World tag team title Stunning Stu Stevenson and Pinman may not have been the world's greatest mic workers, but in the ring they were almost second to none. While the World Leaders made a joke of the World titles, the Bionic Team battled for the secondary IP tag belts against much tougher competition such as the Bruise Crew and Search & Destroy. Their triumph over the World Leaders for the tag team titles in April 1998 was a long time coming, but unfortunately was shortly followed by their departure from the federation. 7) BUSINESS MEN Accolades: WWO World tag team title 1997 tag team of the year Though they may mainly have been lackeys of Andros, Grant and Lariot were still the first WWO tag team to bring actual scientific and aerial wrestling skills to the federation, rather than mere mindless brawling. Their feud with the old-guard Hounds of Hell is considered one of the WWO's first "modern" feuds, and they reigned absolutely supreme in the tag team division over the first half of 1997, until suffering an upset loss to the World Leaders and ignominously fading away, despite a couple of aborted comeback attempts. 6) THE ULTIMATES Accolades: WWO World tag team title It is agreed by many that the WWO tag team division hit its peak in late 1998-early 1999, with half of the teams on this list being on the roster simultaneously. And the team which held the World tag team titles for a good chunk of that time was not Uncivil Disobedience, not Search & Destroy and not Thunderstorm, but rather this duo, comprised of Marshall Duncan and Mark Rock, which also helped make the Showmen stable into a dominant force at the same time. Unfortunately, by summer 1999 both they and the Showmen stable were a distant memory. 5) DON'T YOU WISH YOU WERE US Accolades: World tag team title 1998 tag team of the year "Mr. Ratings" Warren Hayes and Ravashing Ric Studd came to resuscitate the WWO's tag team division in spring 1998, a time when the Bionic Team was stagnating, the World Leaders were sucking, and the rest of the division was a revolving door of mediocrity. And while this team's impact on the revival of the fortunes of the tag team division (perhaps even the entire federation along with their Simply The Best stablemates) cannot be denied, this is not the greatest tag team in WWO history. They only gained victories over the likes of the Moshpit, and when real competition such as Uncivil Disobedience emerged, they lost the titles quickly and just as quickly split up. 4) THUNDERSTORM Accolades: WWO World tag team title 1999 tag team of the year Who were David Holmes and Marcus Thorn? Big, strong black guys who were alright in the ring, but had no personality. They were also the most persistent SOB's in the history of WWO tag team wrestling. For over a year, they bided their time in the mid-card, always giving excellent efforts against the likes of Search & Destroy and the Ultimates, but always coming up short of the ultimate glory. Finally, though, at Heatwave '99 they were able to come through and grab the titles....and well, if you didn't mark out for that, there's something wrong with you. I miss them. 3) SEARCH & DESTROY Accolades: World tag team title Okay, so maybe they were just an Australian version of an 80's tag team. I don't care. In their first stint, they were ready to bring new life to the tag team division before getting caught up in the politics of the WIF fiasco, but returned to the federation in late '98 with a vengeance, masking themselves as the Centurions and running roughshod over the Ultimates to win the tag team titles. They held the belts with panache for most of the spring and summer of '99, defending against every team that was thrown at them before finally succumbing to Fire & Ice. Knowing that their time was up following a back injury Destroy suffered, they bowed out with class, losing one final match to Thunderstorm at the Bash 2000. 2) FIRE & ICE Accolades: World tag team title Michael Ice and Alex Perkins may have been around before the Business Men (I don't know for sure) but they definitely lasted a hell of a lot longer. Their first '96-'97 stint was pretty unremarkable to my knowledge, but they came back to the fed a year and a half later and proceeded to show that even First Generationers could still be relevant, battling Uncivil Disobedience in one of the greatest blood feuds ever seen in the WWO. After coming out on the short end of the stick, barely, they again faded from the limelight for several months, only to then stun the world by winning the titles in a heel turn at War of the Ages 3. They and UCD, collectively known as the Fun Loving Criminals, proceeded to throw the tag team division into anarchy before losing the titles to Thunderstorm. See, unlike most teams and wrestlers in WWO history, this team's repeated comebacks actually had a POINT. 1) UNCIVIL DISOBEDIENCE Accolades: WWO World tag team title Their tag team title reign may not have been the longest.....but what makes this team the best is that regardless of who was holding the actual belts at the time, Firm and Rax Justice were ALWAYS able to make themselves the center of attention in the division and make the titleholders look like nothing but paper champions (especially Search & Destroy) .
