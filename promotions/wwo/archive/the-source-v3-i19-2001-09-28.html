Longtime JTTS hardcore numbskull The Genius had long thrown out challenges to Bryson and many other of the WWO's higher-ups, such as "Mr. Ratings" Warren Hayes. While these challenges were generally scoffed at both by the characters who recieved them and about 99.5% of the fan base, but to kill time on one Showcase in early August, a cage match was signed between him and Hayes, who was just then coming into his own as ringleader of the Simply The Best stable. Genius wound up outwrestled by Hayes and humiliated by the STB stable, and at the end of the match, Bryson, who Genius had been challenging off and on and calling a coward for nearly two hours, arrived to help finish off Genius. Bryson then informed Genius that he was sick and tired of his challenges, and would finally shut him up once and for all if he was willing to take Wuzho's place in the Heatwave match. Since Wuzho was not exactly unable or unwilling to wrestle, he immediately raced down to the cage and sent Bryson fleeing for his life, but a new twist to the feud had been added. "Half Rack" Simpson also made his welcomed return to the WWO on this card. And on the next card, Genius would retaliate, attacking Bryson before his scheduled match with Victor Braun, which Bryson would eventually lose by countout when Wuzho wound up chasing him away. Furiously, Wuzho demanded an explanation on the status of his match with Bryson from President Stone...and was informed that, in order to finally get a matchup with Bryson, he would have to first defeat Genius at HeatWave...in a "marathon pin" match where Wuzho would have to score three consecutive pinfalls without being pinned in order to win, thanks to a mysterious contract clause included by "someone or other". Wuzho was visibly close to physically lashing out at Stone, and all the while Bryson was smiling and taking it easy. However, Wuzho's sister, Mikaella, entered the picture at this point to become a calming influence upon her brother, and also hinted at a past relationship with Bryson, again shifting the balance in this feud. At Heatwave '98, Bryson would actually enter the tag team gauntlet (battle royals to determine #1 contenders for the World tag team titles) with Braun as his partner, and put on a nondescript performance before being eliminated in short order. He attempted to influence the outcome of the Wuzho-Genius match by actually clobbering Mikaella with a chair (which still ranks as one of the modern WWO's most egregious acts of violence against women), but ultimately to no avail as Wuzho won, and then lashed out at Bryson in a savage attack following the match. Bryson was "injured" as a result of Wuzho's "Knife" full nelson face slam, and again disappeared from active wrestling for about a month afterward, but continued cutting promos of course, and fans salivated at the looming matchup for the Anarchy '98 PPV. The gypsy, though, could not wait much longer, and finally challenged Bryson to a match on October 6, with each man being allowed to pick a mystery tag team partner and the winner being allowed to select the stipulation for their Anarchy matchup. And to open that card, Wuzho ambushed Simpson and left him lying, making it look as though Bryson would be left without a partner to take on Wuzho and his partner, who turned out to be none other than the returning Rusty Bowman. Bryson, however, had another ace up his sleeve, bringing in another old-timer, Prime Time Phill Massacre, as his partner. Despite Massacre's limitations, the two teams would go on to have a very good match, but Bryson and Massacre would get the win when TV champion Night Stalker ran in and whacked Bowman with his TV belt, to begin a feud that never really went anywhere. Bryson was able to pick the stipulations for the match....first man to get five pinfalls/submissions, each with its own special stipulation, would be the victor. A grueling marathon unlike any in WWO history was promised for the Anarchy '98 PPV, on November 2 in Tijuana, Mexico, the WWO still not having reigned in its globetrotting tendencies. Bryson and Wuzho would go on to live up to the heat for the match...and then some. The first fall was a wild no-DQ brawl, which had Wuzho DDTing Bryson for what looked like a pinfall after biting his hand on a neckbreaker attempt, but Bryson was able to get his shoulder up at the last second, and while Wuzho argued the count with poor, hapless referee Cordon, Bryson hit a low blow and small-packaged the gypsy to win the first fall. Bryson then trapped Wuzho in a Boston crab and very nearly got a submission for a 2-0 lead, but got salt tossed in his eyes by Mikaella, allowing Wuzho to DDT him for the pin to tie the score. Bryson was able to catch him in a crab again to take the lead, and seemed to have the match in control when he hit the "Fall From Grace" to make the score 3-1. Rusty Bowman, though, arrived at ringside to lend a hand to his friend, and after several more near-falls for Bryson Wuzho made his big comeback, catching Bryson in a flying hammerlock to get a submission and then clobbering him with a two-by-four to even the match at three falls apiece. The momentum, though, would again turn in Bryson's favor, as he took a 4-3 lead when Wuzho's back and leg gave out on a suplex attempt, but Wuzho tied it again shortly afterward, pinning Bryson with a crucifix to set up the dramatic ninth fall, in which one man had to be knocked out for a ten-count....and finally, Wuzho would hit a superplex, followed by The Knife, to get the victory and ascend to the throne as the WWO's greatest superstar. Bryson had classily done the job and passed the torch in what is still considered the greatest WWO match of all time by just about anyone who saw it, and was the crown jewel, upstaging even the World title match, in what still ranks as one of the WWO's top PPVs ever. By now, the WWO, largely on the strength of the Wuzho-Bryson angle, had far surpassed its previous pre-[name withheld] high point. Although Bryson had finally passed the torch and was officially no longer the WWO's top superstar, he remained very much a draw, and was still relatively young and in good shape. With the Wuzho feud finally settled, Bryson was paired back up with old friend/foe Bowman thanks to the latter's support of Wuzho. However, the front office was not keen on having an extended feud between two "old farts", and Bowman's long- term status was (rightly) questionable due to several nagging injuries. So, instead, this feud would prove to be short-lived and serve as a mere springboard to another massive angle. A Bryson attack on Bowman would lead to "Solid Rock" challenging Bryson to a Penalty Box rematch at Season's Beatings '98, another excellent PPV held on December 16 in Huntington, WV. This, of course, would be another vicious brawl between the two men, but Bryson would wind up prevailing...thanks to a ref bump and some timely assistance from "Mr. Ratings" Warren Hayes and "Money Driven" Mike Sebastian, each of whom was dressed along with Bryson in a hockey jersey reading "Even Better". A 3-on-1 beatdown resulted, until faces Ultimate Thrasher and Roundhouse ran in to make the save. What, or who, was "Even Better"? The answer would become apparent soon: Hayes had been the leader of the great Simply The Best stable, but that stable had begun to lose its heat due to it having degenerated from a fighting force featuring three ex-World Champions and one future one into essentially just Warren and his lackeys. It was decided that to help get Hayes more over than ever, he should be put in a leaner, meaner stable with two guys quite capable of pulling their weight...and Bryson and Sebastian were those two guys. The trio christened themselves "Even Better", and were soon in a full- fledged war with three other stables consisting of either faces already fighting with Warren or jilted ex-STBers: the Hall of Fame, the Showmen, and Uncivil Disobedience. Of course, many one-on-one or two-on-two battles were also being fought, and Bryson found himself in a war of words with the Hall of Fame's Ultimate Thrasher, another star who he had encountered in the ring before. Thrasher actually considered Bryson a mentor of sorts off- screen, due to his own Midwesterness, love of hockey and heavy metal music, and of course fondness for alcohol and drugs. But in- character, the two had been taking the occasional potshot at one another for close to two years now, and they seemed finally set to explode when Thrasher and Bryson took turns costing each other US title matches against Underground Kingpin in the weeks leading up to the Valentine's Day Massacre '99 PPV in Scotland. The plan seemed to be to either involve Kingpin in the feud to get some much-needed heat for the US belt, or to just transition the belt onto either Thrasher or Bryson later on. But after defeating Thrasher on the last Showcase before the PPV, Kingpin suddenly weirded out and left the WWO, and Stone put the title in mothballs, where it would wind up staying for nearly six months due to unforseen circumstances. The main event of VDM was scheduled to be a gigantic War Games match between the four warring stables. The fans would have preferred the Hall of Fame to get the win, but didn't really care as long as someone, anyone, beat the living crap out of Even Better, who had added "insurance policy" Tyrant to their ranks for this match. But what had started as a bad month for the WWO with the loss of UK became only worse when Warren Hayes left the federation almost literally on the eve of the PPV to pursue an acting career. Tyrant was also on his way out due to a contract dispute, and an attempt to bring in "GQ" Elan Desire to replace Hayes failed for reasons never explained. As a result, Bryson came to the cage for the scheduled match, and officially declared Even Better dead (though his split with Sebastian was an amiable one), which essentially left the Showmen to fill the void as the lead heel stable. They won the match, and their leader Johnny Detson replaced Bryson in the feud with Thrasher, in an attempt to create a "new Warren Hayes". Many felt Detson was a lazy, overrated performer who did not deserve to be one of the WWO's top heels...and Bryson, as well as Thrasher, was foremost on this list, proving as such when he delivered a promo not long after VDM labelling Detson "a one work wonder" and inviting Stone, along with a couple of dozen other wrestlers to kiss his nether-regions. Those who saw it immediately labeled this a "shoot", but Bryson in his heyday was a master at this sort of thing. Bryson was perhaps scheduled to be moved into a feud with another beer- guzzling power wrestler face, Australian behemoth Roundhouse, but this was scrapped when Roundhouse wanted to continue a long-running, multi- federational program with WWO newcomer Tyrone "Purple" Hayes. Detson, meanwhile, was not handling the pressure well, and things only got worse when World champion Fury suddenly departed the federation, leaving Detson as the actual top heel in the WWO, and, with a spate of other departures in the spring of 1999, leaving very few men capable of main-eventing at that point. Bryson was asked to reconsider his objection to being a part of the World title picture, and he went along. An angle was begun where Bryson actually performed a coup d'etat on Mark Stone while the federation was in Europe, taking over Stone's Boston office and declaring himself the new president of the WWO. He sent inebriated stooge Simpson to Europe on a seek-and- destroy mission against the Hall of Fame, but Clancy mistook one stable for another and wound up attacking the Showmen's Jude St. Claire by mistake, and soon wound up trapped by St. Claire and hulking bodyguard Romulus before Ultimate Thrasher ran out to make the save. Thrasher let Bryson know "via satellite" that he was owed something in return, and Bryson reluctantly signed a match pitting Thrasher against Detson, which was made a top contenders match for the World title. This also got the Showmen angered at Bryson, but the week before the Battle of the Titans VI pay-per-view, Mark Stone let Bryson know in no uncertain terms who the president of the WWO really was, and also that he was ordered to fly to Spain to be the special guest referee for the main event of the show....a triangle match for the vacant World title featuring Thrasher, Detson, and "White Hot" Darren Morgan (who was added to the mix for reasons known only to Stone). This seemed to tilt the scales heavily against Ultimate Thrasher for the match on May 10 in Madrid, but Detson and Morgan were completely not percieved as title threats by the public, so this twist needed to be added. As referee, Bryson would change the match rules on the spot to a tornado match, seeming to stack the deck even more against Thrasher, but when Thrasher caught Morgan with his Power Chord Slam, Bryson shocked everyone watching by actually counting a clean pinfall. However, with Detson and Thrasher left alone in the ring, Bryson seemed to be blatantly favoring Detson...until when Detson went to finish off Thrasher with his "Reality Check" sharpshooter, Thrasher rolled Detson over for a small package, and just as Sam Bradley commentated on how Thrasher was being an idiot for thinking Bryson would count the pin, Bryson did just that to give Thrasher his first World title. Detson went after Bryson in anger, but was quickly laid out with a Diamond Cutter, and Bryson prepared to present Thrasher the World title...before hitting another Diamond Cutter and counting his own pinfall, then escaping with the World title. The stage was set for a Bryson-Thrasher showdown, and a result of Bryson finally officially winning back the belt he never lost or Thrasher defeating Bryson to establish himself as one of the WWO's true immortals looked equally likely and acceptable. With Thrasher fuming and Bryson smirking, the showdown between the two men was signed for War of the Ages 3 in Atlanta, and sold out the mammoth Georgia Dome in record time. Naturally, the WWO being the WWO, something went wrong. Bryson's drug and alcohol habits had never been a secret to anyone, but the WWO front office was generally willing to turn a blind eye towards them as long as Bryson avoided run-ins with the law. In late June, 1999, though, Bryson was arrested by border police near Vancouver, BC, for possession of drugs. Not wanting to take a public relations black eye, the front office ordered Bryson to leave the federation for a few months and get his various monkeys off of his back...certainly the decent thing to do, but it left no one to fight against Thrasher. A horrible decision was made, and Bryson's final appearance on WWO TV for several months would show him handing over the stolen World title to his "proxy", Thrasher's new opponent for War of the Ages....Iron. To the shock of no one, the show was a near-complete bust, and the return of [name withheld] and his unjustified push to the main event recieved a severe backlash both from the fan base and inside the locker room, and the entire federation began unravelling at the seams while Bryson dried out. As the WWO got more and more desperate to stop the tide, Bryson was brought back in November, appearing as a special enforcer referee for a tag team match on Showcase, and even hinting at a face turn when he decked both members of Fire & Ice. But this wouldn't prove to be anything but a final nail in the coffin of the character...nobody wanted to see Bryson suddenly turning face, and many of the younger stars now inhabiting the locker room resentfully viewed Bryson as an "overrated old fart" and lumped him in with Andros and Iron as the ultimate representatives of the WWO's bad old days. After exiting the 2000 Bash royal rumble in short order, Bryson's large contract was up, and he and the WWO agreed to part ways. Not long afterward, the federation closed. Though he went out with a whimper rather than a bang, as is common, it will be a crime if Randy Bryson does not soon end up in the Hall of Fame. Without a doubt, he was "The Master Of Mind Games" who set the standard for what a WWO superstar really should be, and paved the way for many others to follow in his footsteps. ----------------------------------------------------------------------- CLOSING BLURB by [name withheld] Well, that's it for this time around.
