Simply The Best: My Ballot

by Donny McClamrock

Okay, folks, STB ballots are out, and I thought, instead of keeping my ballots secret, it'd be a cool idea to hang my votes and reasoning behind them out there for all to see. So let's get to it, shall we?

Mr. Executive Award -- Manager of the Year

1. Rimshot 2. Candy Malone 3. Todd Johnstone 4. Melissa 5. Cherry Bomb

Of the five choices, Rimshot was most instrumental to the success of his charges. Candy Malone shot Dark Soul to the top, but he's proven he can now survive without her. Johnstone is still the best cheap-heat getter in the WWO. Melissa didn't really do all that much as a manager, but she was the center of the hottest angle of the Anarchy cycle. Cherry Bomb, after Inquisition, was more or less there.

"Line in the Sand" Award -- Match of the Year

1. Tyrone Hayes vs. Apex -- Inquisition 2. Dark Soul vs. William Craven -- Inquisition 3. Tyrone Hayes vs. Ultimate Thrasher -- Inquisition 4. Apex vs. Tyrone Hayes vs. Holy Roller -- Anarchy 5. William Craven vs. Ultimate Thrasher -- Superstar Showcase, Towel Match

This was the toughest category for me. Very little separated these matches. Tyrone vs. Apex at Inquisition was for the WWO Title, on the first PPV since the WWO reopened, both men worked their asses off, and the f'n announce table broke. Dark Soul vs. William Craven was the most brutal match of Inquisition, and provided the impetus to slingshot both men up the WWO ladder. Tyrone vs. Thrasher was a match years in the making, and was worth the wait, but it was only a WWO Title tournament semifinal match. The three-way at Anarchy is most notable for the formation of the Upper Crust, and the Towel Match was the critical point so far of the Craven-Thrasher feud.

"War of 2001" Award -- Feud of the Year

1. OSE vs. The Quesadas and Dark Soul 2. William Craven vs. Ultimate Thrasher 3. Apex vs. Tyrone Hayes 4. Dark Soul vs. Tracy Hudson 5. Papa Legba vs. Eric Stryker

For the first six months after the WWO reopened, OSE vs. The Quesadas and Dark Soul was the best reason to watch WWO programming, and truth be told, the feud probably isn't totally dead yet. Craven vs. Thrasher was a classic triangle love story, only much more disturbing and infinitely more violent, but it really hasn't been blown off yet. Apex vs. Tyrone Hayes was fairly hot, but overshadowed in its own cycle by Craven vs. Thrasher. Dark Soul vs. Hudson is still the hottest feud right now, but I have a feeling that one isn't over yet, either. Legba vs. Stryker was a great feud to establish the worth of the TOTA belt, but ultimately falls a little short of the other four, mainly because it never resulted in a main event matchup.

"Golden Microphone" Award -- Best Announcer/Personality

1. Sam Bradley 2. Mickey Ralph 3. Kim Campbell 4. Mackey Jay 5. Chris Stringer

Bradley is still the funniest thing about WWO programming, and can get the wrestlers over while entertaining you. Ralph's wrestling knowledge is encyclopedic, but at times this year he's been a little "off." Campbell debuted this year, and really came into her own as Apex's mouthpiece, then as an independent reporter. Mackey Jay is still the quintessential backstage reporter, he just lacks the personality to get more camera time. Stringer is entertaining, but I cannot in good conscience rank him ahead of the full-time announcers and reporters.

"Tour de Force" Award -- Stable of the Year

1. OSE 2. Dark Soul and the Quesadas 3. Upper Crust 4. Greatness Personified 5. Team UEW

This was a bad, bad year for stables. OSE and Dark Soul and the Quesadas were really de facto stables involved in a hotter angle than any real stable was actively involved in this year. Upper Crust may turn out to be the best of the lot, but they formed too late in the year to get the top spot. Greatness Personified never really got off the ground, and Team UEW had massive turnover in the middle of its angle, which was lukewarm at best.

"The Drive of the Year" Award -- Comeback of the Year

1. Tracy Hudson 2. Ultimate Thrasher 3. Dark Soul 4. Tyrone Hayes 5. Miguel Quesada 6. Amazing Romanis

It's amazing to think that in the Inquisition cycle, Hudson was turfed out hardcore in a handicap match. So he sucked as a booker. He came back and proved he has very few equals in the ring or on the mic. Thrasher floundered in the UTD angle, but re-established himself with his feud with Craven. Dark Soul took the next step to main event level. Tyrone Hayes finally won the World Title, but he was at that level long ago. Quesada gets points for making two comebacks, one from his Silkk stage, and one from his failed heel turn. I don't quite know what the Romanis came back from, they were pretty much at the same excellent level all year.

Show of the Year

1. Inquisition 2. Anarchy 3. Superstar Showcase, 31 Aug, 2001 - Ottawa, Canada 4. Superstar Showcase, 20 Sept, 2001 - Detroit, Michigan 5. Superstar Showcase, 15 July, 2001 - Cincinnati, Ohio 6. Superstar Showcase, 18 Aug, 2001 - Minneapolis, Minnesota 7. Superstar Showcase, 2 June, 2001 - Baltimore, Maryland 8. Superstar Showcase, 3 July 2001 - Indianapolis, Indiana

Inquisition, in the words of Gary Hammond, was the best WWO PPV ever. Four 5-star matches, and another one or two that were close. Plus, it was the first PPV since the reopening. Anarchy was also very good, but because only one title was at stake, just a little less important. The Showcases from 3-7 on the list could have gone in pretty much any order. As far as number 8, let me say this to those that nominated it. Watch the show again. Then watch the show from Milwaukee. Now please ask yourself why the fuck you nominated the Indianapolis show instead of the Milwaukee show. Because if the Milwaukee show is on the list, it's my # 3, hands down. The Indianapolis show pretty much sucked a fatty outside of one match, a match where the only thing accomplished was Miguel's failed heel turn.

"Young Warrior" Award -- Rookie of the Year

1. Chris Stringer 2. Papa Legba 3. William Craven 4. Amazing Romanis 5. Nate Knowitall 6. Chris Hopper

Because I would have voted Ichiro for A.L. Rookie of the Year if I would have had a vote, Hopper and Craven get no penalty for already being big names in the wrestling world. Stringer has held the WWO's second-highest belt since it was brought out of the mothballs. Legba has held the fed's third-highest belt since it was created. You could flip-flop them, and you'd still be right. Craven probably has more talent than the both put together, but hasn't been as consistent. The Romanis are the best in-ring team in the tag division, and their antics are entertaining in their own right. Nate Knowitall suffered early, doing an extended stretch of jobbing to Lord Keeblor, but rebounded nicely. Hopper still has yet to really hit his stride, but turned out to be the leader of Team WWO in the Invasion angle.

"Heart of Glory" -- Face of the Year.

1. Apex 2. Dark Soul 3. Ultimate Thrasher 4. Matt Cole 5. Chris Hopper 6. Totally 80s

Apex over Dark Soul, but just by a whisker, and only because Apex got hotter quicker at the start. Thrasher got most of his face heat back during his feud with Craven after an aborted heel turn as a result of the UTD fiasco. Cole's got some long-term potential as the WWO's bad-ass take-no-shit face. Hopper, as mentioned before, led the fight against Team UEW, which merits mentioning. The fact that Totally 80s showed up on this list after their brief stay shows just how weak the face ranks have been. But where were Papa Legba and Steve Dumars on the nominations list?

"Heart of Granite" -- Heel of the Year

1. William Craven 2. Tyrone Hayes 3. Chris Stringer 4. Tracy Hudson 5. Todd Johnstone

Craven, both in his Major Damage guise and his current persona, defines the monster heel. Even when he makes a joke, you're scared of him. Tyrone Hayes, after some curious face leanings at Inquisition, really turned it on during the Anarchy cycle. Stringer would have won this if the voting was at half-year, but the feud with Cole, though hot in its own right, ended up getting overshadowed. Hudson came on with a bullet at year's end, but too late to snag a higher spot. Johnstone is a great cheap heat getter, but I can't rate him above the workers.

"Surprise of the Year"

1. Return of Tracy Hudson 2. Return of Johnny Detson 3. The appearance of Team UEW 4. Sweet Melissa turning on Thrasher 5. The unmasking of William Craven

Hudson's return, especially after leaving in disgrace earlier in the year, was totally unexpected. Ditto the return of Detson, but I think Hudson was an even longer shot to return than Detson was. The appearance of Team UEW shocked almost everyone, even if not in the best way at times. Melissa's turn on Thrasher was fairly heavily foreshadowed, and Craven's unmasking was overshadowed somewhat by the fact it happened on Inquisition, where it was just one important event among many.

[name withheld] -- Worker of the Year

1. Tyrone Hayes 2. Apex 3. Dark Soul 4. Chris Stringer 5. Miguel Quesada

Before you even start arguing about this at all, look at the match of the year nominations. Tyrone worked two Match of the Year candidates IN THE SAME NIGHT. No, he's not a technical genius. No, he's not a high-flyer. But no one can get a crowd to care more about a match by telling the story of it in the ring. Apex is a human bump machine, and his quirky personality allows for some creative ring psychology. Dark Soul and Stringer are about on the same level technically, but I think DS bumps better. Quesada, when he's on, is the equal to all of the above guys. When he's off, he's equal to Roundhouse.

"Simply The Best Tag Team" Award -- Tag Team of the Year.

1. Amazing Romanis 2. PAIN 3. Vegas Connection 4. Dark Soul & Miguel Quesada 5. Uncivil Disobedience 6. Canadian Legacy 7. Totally 80s

This is kind of a personal style vote, I guess. PAIN and the Vegas Connection are both "sports entertainment" tag teams with fairly limited gimmicks. Now, that's not a bad thing, but the Romanis have gotten the hang of the sports entertainment stuff _and_ provide the best ring work in the division. PAIN, face or heel, is usually the most entertaining thing in the division on any given week, however, with VC just a step behind them. Dark Soul and Miguel, if they decided to stay together, probably would be higher on this list. UCD was awfully inconsistent, but had fleeting moments when they were the equals of anyone in the division. Canadian Legacy gets the six spot by default because Totally 80s' stay was all too brief.

"Simply The Best" Award -- Wrestler of the Year

1. Tyrone Hayes 2. Apex 3. William Craven 4. Chris Stringer 5. Ultimate Thrasher

When a guy is the only guy to hold the WWO Title this year, cuts the best promos in the WWO, and is the best in-ring worker in the WWO, then he's gotta be the Wrestler of the Year. And KTFH is that. Even if Apex would have won one of his shots at the World Title, he still wouldn't take the top spot. Did anyone in the WWO get hotter, quicker than Craven? Stringer has been at or near the top of the card since the fed reopen, as has Thrasher. Stringer gets the tiebreaker on in-ring work.

"Streak Award" -- RP of the Year

1. Craven's Tool Video 2. Tracy Hudson's Peanut Butter Flash 3. UCD's Kai-Lin Interior Decorating Flash

Craven's video flash put him over and defined his character, without Craven even having to say a word until the very end of it. Hudson's RP let everyone know, if there was any doubt, that he was back and better than ever. UCD's flash was a glimpse of greatness that those just joining the WWO this year, like myself, were lucky to see, and left me falling down laughing every time I read it.

On-card segment of the Year

1. The Assault on President Stone 2. PAIN parody of Vegas Connection at Anarchy 3. Johnstone's suspension rant

The assault on Stone was not only significant for taking the WWO President and putting him in the hospital, but it set the stage for the formation of the Upper Crust and the Dark Soul-Tracy Hudson feud. The PAIN parody of the Vegas Connection was the most hilarious moment of WWO programming this year. And all of Johnstone's rants were like textbooks on how to get cheap heat, but that one stood out.

"Disappointment of the Year"

1. The collapse of UTD alliance, and the complete disappearance of the bounty on Johnny Detson 2. Andrew Tucker 3. The return and disappearance of Wuzho and the replacement by Magnus Colby at Anarchy 4. UEW-WWO angle 5. Greatness Personified 6. Fire and Ice 7. The collapse of the Cruiserweight Division 8. VC title reign

Andrew Tucker would have been # 1, but he was never really in a main event feud. UTD was supposed to be the main event angle that got the WWO off and running, but it sputtered and died. Tucker's foibles are well-documented, there's no need to beat a dead horse there. # 3 and # 4 can be lumped together, but the Invasion angle had so much negative heat to begin with it would have been tough for it to succeed. Greatness Personified became an afterthought shortly after it was formed. Fire and Ice have been disappointing, but, unlike much else on the list, they can be saved. The cruiserweight division never had more than three able workers at any one time, and I don't know how much really could have been expected of it. The Vegas Connection title reign is a mild disappointment, at worst.

Hall of Fame

Dave "Roundhouse" Grief "Mr. Ratings" Warren Hayes
