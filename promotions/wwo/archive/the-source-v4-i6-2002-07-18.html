You have to go around, meeting new people, and first impressions are quite the bitch. People aren't sure what to expect of you. You have many a run-in with security questioning whether or not you're a WWO employee. And then you're finally set...and some rookie editor chooses to talk about you in his column. Yes, welcome to the WWO for a few of our newbies. More specifically, Deep South Connection, Adam Woods, Rex Americana, Rick Hudson, and Joe Gonzo, you're now working for the World Wrestling Organization, a place that has been known to treat newbies as if they're less than Glen Peeps. I'm Remy and I'm here to help you fit right in. -It's very important to dismiss the belief that the WWO may be a clique in the sense that if you're not part of the group behind the scenes with the power, you're screwed. It's a misconception shared by many. Now, that's not to say WWOers are not different from others because many are. And for most, it's hard to come right in and make a big splash. But it really differs from wrestler-to-wrestler. Like take Chris Stringer for example. He showed up when the WWO was re-opening and was a star from day one, even though he was considered "green." Or William Craven, who under the Major Damage persona was a monster and "over" quite quickly. Now, maybe they are the exceptions, not the rule, but even if you don't have everybody's attention right away, it doesn't mean you're wasting your time trying to be anything in the WWO. Just look at the World Champion and flash back to 1999 when he began. Try to find one mark for him then. Other than John Collins, that is. -Make sacrifices. In the WWO, most new wrestlers must earn their way up. It's not that hard. Show the people behind the scenes you're willing to do whatever to takes to not only make yourself look good, but maybe even more importantly, make others look good. If you come in and in your afterglow about your past accomplishments, think you deserve the penthouse right away, you might as well pack your bags and join Sean Eden as former WWO wrestlers. -Make friends. It is very important to make friends. If you want to get in with the brain trust, find them. It's not that hard. Even if you can't speak to them in person, there is a loyal group of WWOers who frequent AIM chat to get in touch with. So find someone with competent typing skills and get into a discussion with the old-timers or those with power. Or just anybody in the WWO. The worst thing you can do is come off as someone who doesn't want to get involved with the WWOers currently in place. Many people have been handed their walking papers because of it. -With the exception of feds with direct relation to the WWO, do not mention past feds on TV. It's another thing to mention it in the Source, but when done on TV is outrageously in bad taste. Ask Chris Hopper who was known as the guy with other feds in his entrance before finally changing. You have to remember that this is the WWO...and we like to believe that we are different from other feds. Even places with direct relation to the WWO get tired after awhile. Don't even mention the UEW around James Knight. -Finally, "understand that some days you're the statue and some days you're the pigeon (unknown)." You will have some very good days in the WWO if you work hard. You will even have some bad days if you work hard. The key is that you continue to give it your all, understand the nature of the game, and be willing to do whatever is best for the company. While the WWO may not be a team, the mentality is still ever-present. So for all of you newbies, take my advice to heart. Because I've asked around for this column to try to help you and when I asked William Craven...he nearly killed me for complimenting his Jim Morrisonish ways. Yes, apparently they were short one show reviewer, and thus they turned to me, Phillip Byram, long time wrestling reporter, who happened to be stuck reporting on some of the Island Feds here in Hawaii. Now, as much as I love reporting from Paradise, I'm not about to turn down an audition with the WWO, which I have been following ever since its reopening. Let's just hope someone upstairs is impressed, and I can land a full time gig. And now, onto the review of Spotlight, which I attended LIVE at Blaisdell Center, Honolulu: We open with a segment of the much-acclaimed Randy Bryson attempting to get into the building, despite not having one of those elusive entrance passes. Now, let me say that I've heard plenty about Bryson, but I've never seen him in action, as I've never covered a fed while he was involved. Anyway, Stonebreaker approves Bryson to enter, and apparently he has some friends on the inside, as we here some cryptic conversations, as well as a last question that seems to indicate Bryson is signing a contract. Oh goodie. With that, we cut to the opening of the show, which starts as hot as any show I've attended on the Islands. The hot crowd reacts well as "Too Cool" Chris Hopper appears, and begins apologizing for losing his match to Eric Stryker. Not satisfied with the loss, he passionately calls out Stryker, who obliges. He comes to the ring, and after some insults to Chris and the crowd, he claims he wants to bury the hatched, obviously a set up, as Nate KnowItAll emerges and assaults Chris from behind. The rest of what's left of The Upper Crust soon follows, and continues the assault, as the fans boo relentlessly. Detson pronounces that despite recent losses, UC lives, with the addition of Stryker to its ranks. Miguel Quesada then comes to the rescue of Hopper, and drives off UC with a chair. We cut to Mark Stone's, where the owner is none too pleased with the UC's actions, and heads off to do something about it. The Magnificent vs. Jack Frost The first match of the night starts with the newly renamed "The Magnificent" taking on Jack Frost in his debut match with the WWO. The Magnificent (let's call him TM for short) starts out strong, dominating the rookie, but Frost uses some unconventional offense by tossing TM from the ring by the ponytail, followed by the crossbody to the outside to take out TM and part of his entourage. Interference by Blake, TM's bodyguard, allows him to regain control, until he attempts the superplex, which is countered. However, Frost's high-risk strategy fails to pay off, as Archibald continues to dominate, using his managers to help. TM argues with the ref after breaking a submission, and Frost takes advantage, using a series of moves that include dragging TM around by the ear. He gains an almost-three-count, but then another distraction allows TM to take control with a low-blow. However, the superplex again proves costly, as it is countered into "The Head Cold," one of Frost's finishing moves. This gains another near fall, and Frost looks to finish things with a big splash, but TM rolls out of the way. After that, TM hits his own finisher, the Grand Finale, and after that the pin is elementary. MATCH RATING: Two and a half stars. Frost's style is interesting, but this match was too one-sided to show his talent. He could have some potential. The Magnificent managed to dominate, but failed to do anything very interesting while doing so. I think the big man needs more then a name change to hold the audience's interest. Afterwards, Alex Extreme shows up to offer TM that TOTA shot he's been gunning for. Tune in next week to see if TM can step up and wrestling an interesting match. Next, A masked man comes out and performs a vicious beat down on Jack Frost, perfectly choreographed to Soundgarden's "Jesus Christ Pose." The mask comes off, and it's Randy Bryson, revealing that he does in fact have a contract. So we'll see if he can do anything other than beat up the new guy. We then cut to a completely different Frost, Victor Frost, who is prepared for his scheduled match against Chris Hopper. He hits on Kimberly Campbell, and then it is announced that he has a match against a member of the Upper Crust, probably Stryker or Detson. Victor Frost vs. ??? Frost comes out to a decent response, but is soon jumped by The Magnificent and company, who get booed pretty loudly. UC looks on approvingly from the looker room, and then emerge after some lead pipe shots and a Grand Finale have left Frost pretty lifeless in the ring. Detson teases turning this "match" into a IP Title contest, but then brings us down to earth, turning this into: Victor Frost vs. Glen Peeps Despite the beating he has already received, Frost still kicks out of Peeps early pin attempts. Detson strolls over to commentate, while somehow Frost comes back with some offense against the poor incompetent Peeps. Frost then takes the time to go say something to the people over at the announce table, and then goes to finish off Peeps, but Detson provides the distraction, and Peeps nearly gets the win with Chris Stringer's old finisher. But Peeps simply can't catch a break, and a DVD finishes him off. MATCH RATING: One star. Mostly just a laugh, especially since anyone else in the arena could have beat Frost in the condition he was in. Hopper shows up for some revenge, and Power Loads Peeps, but is soon overwhelmed by the rest of The Upper Crust. But again, Quesada to the rescue, plastering KnowItAll and Stryker, and Detson tries to escape, only to be brutalized by an even more vicious shot from Latex Lex, followed by the most humiliating submission move in the WWO, Oral Lex, on the most often humiliated man in the WWO, Glen Peeps. Solid Gold vs. The Blood Brothers After a clip of P.A.I.N eating some animated M&Ms, none other then Former WWO Champion, Tyrone Hayes appears, and addresses the crowd, saying he's just here to sell products. He then goes to the announce table to commentate, while those of us who paid money to see this show are left wondering why exactly he is here. The teams emerge, Solid Gold to little reaction from the crowd, and The Blood Brothers to remarkably little heat, considering the reactions they earned just a few weeks ago. The teams tie up with Michael Thunderwolf taking control early with some technical wrestling. However, Sam Bradley showed that his interest in the match mirrored that of half the crowd, by getting up and heading backstage, possibly to take a bathroom break. Despite a brief comeback, Thunderwolf continues to control the match, tying up with the other half of Solid Gold after a tag. Jake Thomson then tags in, dominates, and then tags out. Thunderwolf soon hits a nice belly to back to brainbuster combo (where have I seen that before?) and picks up the win. MATCH RATING: DUD. Maybe the appearance by Tyrone made this interesting for those at home, but to us in the audience, this was a waste of time. Hopefully I win can get the strangely silent Blood Brothers back on track. Bradley returns, and Rich Manning climbs into the ring with a mic, and introduces the new WWO Tag Team Champions, The Amazing Romanis. The gypsy duo makes their entrance, and, well, if they plan on making this their normal entrance, I'm going to give up this review job right now, because I never want to go through that again. Once I regained my senses, I heard the Romanis talking amongst the boos the entrance had earned them. I must have missed something, because I didn't really follow what they were talking about, but what it seemed to come down to was that most of us "gaje" lack sight, except for Papa Legba, who then came down to the ring. As the talk continues, the general gist of it seemed to be that the three were forming a group called "Arcana," as well as hinting that this new group was somehow related to Sabbat Justice, and, well, if you don't know what Sabbat Justice is, then I'm in no place to try to explain it to you. Matt Cole emerges, and expresses dislike for the new group, and he's followed by Legba's opponent for the night, Nate KnowItAll, backed up by Stryker and Shelly. Before long, a brawl breaks out, but somehow we get to: Papa Legba vs. Nate KnowItAll The match starts off in Legba's favor, as the brawling in and out of the ring continues. Legba shows some wrestling moves, even, applying a Sharpshooter, and then attempts a superplex, only get shoved off and hit with a dropkick (are we noticing a common result when it comes to superplexes here?). A sleeper followed by a DDT take the match in Nate's favor, followed by some two counts. He then raises the Romani's ire by stealing one of their moves, only to have Papa Legba counter a submission and regain control. However, Shelly interferes, and the match quickly goes to the outside, with Nate taking out Legba with some brass knuckles. The Romanis attempt to help, but Stryker and Legba take control, until once again Hopper and Quesada show up. This is chaos, and the bell rings for a double count out. MATCH RATING: DUD. Ya know, this could have been a good match, and it was entertaining while it lasted, but the ending was a huge disappointment. The brawl somehow comes to a climax when Hopper gets the knucks, and accidentally blasts Shelly with them, drawing a huge reaction from the crowd. Legba chokeslams Hopper, while Nate retreats with the stunned Shelly, looking pleased that Hopper got what was coming to him. Canadian Legacy vs. Team Nex A big Arcana vs. Upper Crust vs. Hopper/Quesada/??? match is announced for next weeks Showcase main event. Now that should be chaos. Canadian Legacy emerges and complains about Dark Haven and shoddy refereeing. The match then gets underway with CL dominating the smaller man, Pak. They manage to keep Brick out of the match, and after Pak's single moment of offense, a big chokeslam puts him down, and only a series of saves from Brick stop this from being a very short match. Pak rallies, and the fight goes to the outside, where a cane shot from Cameron and a Van Daminator put Pak out pretty good. Brick comes in and looks to pull off a comeback, but a ref bump stops him from making a fall. A missed cane shot nearly costs CL, but then out comes the masked man, and he smacks Team Nex around with their own boom box, and then a Ten Point Landing on Brick finishes things. The beating continues as the show ends. MATCH RATING: Two and a half stars. Very one sided at times, but some great spots redeem it to a degree. Still, yet another squash came off as sour in my mouth. Final Word: Thumbs Down. Some storylines are already taking off right after the big PPV, but some of us do come to wrestling shows for the in-ring action, not the talk and intrigue. Good effort from most of the talent involved, but I think the booking doomed this one to a poor conclusion. --------------------------------------------------------------------------- Superstar Showcase Review by Donny McClamrock contact: [detail withheld] -- Yes, yes, yes, I've returned! I believe Bryson can be something special once again, but until he proves he can handle a decent feud, he shouldn't even be considered for the considered lesser of the current titles, the Title of Americas. At all costs, avoid controversy with Bryson. Once he develops a new fan base both in the audience and in the locker room, that's different. But how would it make Eric Styker feel to be busting his ass for 18 months and watch as Bryson feuded with Extreme while Stryker was feuding with Rick Hanson? Feud him with the younger guys first. See if he objects to putting the young lions of the WWO over in matches. If so, there will be problems. While he is an icon, a feud can only go so well if one guy is made to look like the dominating one. And finally, we've establish he's a past legend. Now focus on the future. It's not the same as mentioning the UEW on WWO programming, but the old WWO bears minimal bearing on the current product.
