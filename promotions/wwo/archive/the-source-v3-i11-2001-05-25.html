Fear not, Brother Practice! We shall fight on! And it's a shame that the good doctor no longer is allowed to write for The Source, because this week's Stringing 'Em Up would be right up his alley. You see, today we are dealing with phobias. Specifically, homophobia. Homosexuality, and by extension, homophobia, have been getting a lot of attention in the sports and sports entertainment press recently due to the revelation by the editor of a national gay magazine that they are dating a major-league ballplayer who is "in the closet." One of the reasons the player doesn't "out" himself is fear of the reaction from homophobic teammates. It got me to thinking about whether that type of homophobia exists in the WWO. My conclusion: Oh yeah, big time. It seems that on every Showcase, one man casually bumps into another man, usually nothing that couldn't be dismissed with a simple "oh, excuse me," and it immediately starts a feud of epic proportions, involving not only the wrestlers, but significant others, announcers, allegations, recriminations, and the bane of all mankind, lawyers. What causes these wrestlers to go off at the slightest touch from another male member of the species? In short, sexual insecurity and deep-rooted, barely repressed homophobia. First off, your average wrestler has the mental capacity of a bag of rotting coconuts," says Dr. Victor Schwartz, world-renown psychiatrist, "So tolerance of other lifestyles, a trait most often found among more intelligent people, is practically non-existent. Also, most of these wrestlers put an extreme amount of value on exuding "machismo," a kind of stereotypical male sexual ideal in which homosexuality is considered to be a severe negative, perhaps even an evil. And yet, they spend their hours on the job rolling around on a mat with another man, which threatens their sexuality and their self-concept. All of this results in basically a walking powder-keg of conflicting emotions, confusion on sexual identity, and so forth, and a seemingly harmless bump or brush from another male can trigger an indignant rage." So what can be done about this problem? Can grapplers' attitudes change enough to make a kindler, gentler WWO, one where heterosexual men can shake hands without starting World War III? Sadly, the answer appears to be a resounding no. Something that a lot of people that I have talked to since that article agreed with, and actually commended me on. However, they all made one comment that had me "concerned". That comment was. "It will never work". That was it. It's a great idea, but it will never work? Why not? And then I came to the realization of why. If all the federation presidents you talk to would work with the idea of having "spheres of influence", then why should it not work? It's not because of the federations, it's because of the wrestlers. That's right. I blame the wrestlers. You know, I'm going to get hit every way possible for writing this article the way I am, but I'm a journalist. That's what I have to expect. You see, federation presidents and staff members understand, they have a responsibility to put out the greatest product possible. But when it comes to wrestlers? They don't have that responsibility, or that duty to insure that this sport is not laughed at as being a mockery of what it could be. As long as they get to compete in whatever federations they want, when they want, they don't give a damn about how things look. If they get to claim gold in three million federations, screw the sport overall. Now, I'm not accusing all wrestlers of this fact. Hell, some of the greatest this sport has had, understood the fact that you can't physically keep up wrestling in 8 federations over 7 days. Or that you weren't going to fly to a city on the California coast to do a video clip, just so you could fly back to New York the same night to wrestle a match. So where am I going with this? It's time for professional wrestlers to stand up! It's time to take accountability for your careers, and what federations you're going to fight in any given time in your career. It's simple. Remember those spheres of wrestling federations? It's time for you to pick one! That's right just one! You want to wrestle for the UWF for instance? Well, forget about IWF/WOW, since they're in the sphere with the World Wrestling Organization. You want to spend your time in the EMWC? Forget about E-Pro. Different spheres. For those of you who are professional wrestlers, you have to take some steps to insure that you're wrestling "responsibly". Step #1: Select a sphere you wish to compete in. It doesn't matter which one. But it's the only one your career is worried about at this moment. Don't worry, you can change spheres later if you wish. Step#2: Select your federation(s) Now, do you remember those federation labels we gave you? Global, International, National, Regional, and Independent? Well, this is going to take a little math, so let's give you a little help. You're allocated what I call "available federation points". And no, this doesn't involve rankings or such, so don't worry about your status. A Regional Federation has a value of 1 point. National Federations have a value of 2 points. International are valued at 3 points. And Global Federations top out at 4 points. Now for each professional wrestler you control, you are allocated 5 points for each specific wrestler. Those 5 points are what you are allowed to use in your application procedures. Let's assume that you join the WWO Sphere of Influence. According to our earlier example, that example sphere included the following federations. Global Federation: World Wrestling Organization International Federation: US-Canada: IWF/WOW Japan-Australia: E-Pro Eurasia: East Hemisphere Wrestling National Federation: US: USEW, NAAW Canada: IcoA England: UKW Japan; JPW Australia: South Seas Regional Federation: GLCW, KVW, AVW, Alberta, Tokyo Now limiting yourself to these organizations is a start. Let's say for instance now that you apply to and are accepted to E-Pro wrestling, an International Federation. International Federations are "3 point" federations. Now you've reduced yourself to two remaining points. This means that you have a choice of applying to a single national federation, or two regional federations. If you apply to a federation such as the WWO and are not accepted, then the value of that federation is not taken away from you. If you run or operate a tag team, then each character is allocated 5 points, and points are NON-TRANSFERRABLE between wrestlers. This prevents a tag team handler from spending 3 points on one tag team wrestler and passing the remaining two points to his partner giving him 7 points. Now granted, using this overall theory, one can still apply to five regional federations if you so wished. However, there is one more limitation to the "Point system". Although you could apply to five "one point" organizations, a limit must be enforced. Therefore your five points must be limited to no more than _Three_ organizations combined. This means that you may compete in three regional federations, you may compete in one national and two regional federations, or even two national and one regional federations. But your point total must not exceed five points, and your federation totals must not exceed three. STEP 3: EXCESS POINTS Yes, there is a purpose for keeping track of your points. Simply put, you must have a point left for participation in "independent supercard" events. Picture a point as time you have available in your wrestling career. If you have one point available, you have allotted yourself enough time to join in one supercard event. If you have two points left, you have allocated enough time to join in two supercards. No points.. sorry, you've booked yourself solid, and despite your status, you just cannot make the time to join in supercard events. Does it limit you? Absolutely, but you're the one who wanted to go for the big global federation. And to make it globally, you've got to focus yourself on that big federation. And if you want to participate in a supercard, you've got to focus there as well. But don't worry, you're not totally locked out. After a supercard has completed, you are reallocated your point back. So, for instance, using Wrestlebowl III, if you are signed for that event, you remove "one point" from your available time. After Wrestlebowl III, your schedule has been cleared, and you are given that one point back. A point that can go to another supercard, another federationm or whatever you have points allocated for. This point deduction, however, does not include federation-organized supercards. Joint federation pay per views are specifically permitted when you signed with a given federation, and therefore cost you no points or time. STEP 4: RECLAIMING POINTS As you have seen in step 3, points will automatically rebuild after the completion of a supercard event. However, you can reclaim points by leaving a federation you are currently in. If you are competing in 1 national federations (IWF/WOW) and 2 regional federations (AVW and Tokyo), you have thereby spent 4 "Time Points", as well as having reached your three federation limit. That leaves you with one available point, which would only allow you to join another regional federation at this point. However, if you were in the "sphere" listed above, you could pull out of one of your regional federations, thus giving you 1 point back, which would be added to your one available point, thus giving you two points, which would allow you the opportunity to sign with another national federation. BEFORE: IWF/WOW(N), AVW(R), TOKYO(R) (1 Point available) AFTER: IWF/WOW(N), UKW(N), AVW(R) (0 Points available) Or if you wish, you could pull out of one federation, and not sign with another organization at this time, allowing you the opportunity to sign with 2 supercards, before rejoining an organization. Again, it is a complicated system, but it allows for the sport to again show a touch of "realism" in determining where and when you can participate with a single wrestler or tag team. STEP 5: CHANGING SPHERES As we all know, not everyone is going to be happy in every federation that is out there. However, and this is where the "responsibility" factor plays in even more than in any of the above steps. You have joined a specific sphere. Let's say that you are in the WWO Sphere as listed above, and joined the WWO and Alberta Wrestling. However, you have decided that the WWO is not what you are looking for, and would like to try your luck in the UWF. Well, you still have ties to the WWO sphere, which must be completely severed. Simply put, while you are active in any single federation in your sphere of influence, you may not join any organization in a sphere outside of your current one. That means that before you can join the UWF, you must end your time in both the WWO and Alberta Wrestling. Alberta Wrestling and the UWF are in two different spheres, and therefore, a wrestler cannot participate in both organizations at the same time. Should you end your current time in Alberta wrestling, as well as the WWO, then and only then can you join the UWF or some other sphere's federation. The same limitation applies if you are interested in simply joining a regional federation. You must detach yourself completely from your current sphere of wrestling if you wish to move to another sphere to compete. As long as you have activity in one sphere, you are limited specifically to that sphere until your time has completed in its entirety. There you are... granted, it's not a perfect system, and I know a lot of you are already saying "it won't work". I've already heard it. And I'll guarantee you that most reasons can be lumped under a couple of complaints. But we're going to save that for part three of this article. But I want to know why you think it wouldn't "work"...
