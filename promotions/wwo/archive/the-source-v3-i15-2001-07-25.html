Andros at one time was perhaps the WWO's ultimate franchise player, but since late 1997 had changed from face to heel to face to heel, confusing the fans to a point where they nearly stopped popping entirely for him, aside from those who still hated him for his fence-straddling in the WIF war. Meanwhile, STB suddenly became the hottest thing going, with everyone from Arakida Yoshio to Night Stalker to the Genius being a target for mass beatdowns, pranks and spraypaintings as ratings for Superstar Showcase soared to new heights. Despite the presence of longtime superstars Andros and Studd, Warren became quickly viewed as the leader of the group and was overshadowing his stablemates. War of the Ages 2 went off on June 16, 1998 in the Bahamas. It was an event of monstrous proportions that featured eight hours of action (most of it poor) from SIX federations and concluded with a giant battle royal to answer the all-important question: Who was best out of the WWO, IWF/WOW and four now-defunct regional federations?? Since WWO fans were obviously tired of having other federations' stars pushed down their throats after the WIF disaster and the smaller, aborted IWF/WOW invasion, this was not exactly the smartest way of celebrating the federation's emergence from Armageddon, and there was a significant backlash from the fanbase, leading to the WWO losing a pretty penny on this show. Nonetheless, STB would still reign supreme on this hot summer night. The lumberjacks for the title match were five career jobbers, the washed-up Thug Life Organization stable, the beat-up Rusty Bowman, the still-green Tracy Hudson, and Chris Johnson, probably the most over heel in the promotion at the time besides Warren. After Warren carried the other three wrestlers to a decent match, DYWYWU used help from Johnson to get the victory and the titles, burying the Moshpit once and for all and marking Johnson's enterance into Simply The Best. Later on in the card, STB members Andros and Gesner would also interfere (to no avail) on behalf of World champion Robert d'Artois and partner L'Ordonnateur against Fury and Arakida Yoshio, and in the main event battle royal, STB members Johnson, Andros, Gesner, Studd, as well as d'Artois worked together independent of Team WWO, even eliminating "teammate" Randy Bryson, and were made to look largely responsible for the loss of Team WWO to the CWC. Although the show was a disaster, Mark Stone still gave the entire federation a month long vacation to celebrate the return to solvency. When Superstar Showcase returned to the airwaves July 21, Chris Johnson and Robert d'Artois were officially added to the stable. It was thought that Johnson's enterance into STB would provide the final push for him to gain true superstardom, and that d'Artois' World title reign, which had mostly limped heatlessly along since May, could be saved through the trashing of his "Revolution" stable and his assimilation into STB. STB had reached a level of total dominance that has never been seen before or since by any stable in the WWO. And it was all downhill from there. The enterances of Johnson and d'Artois totally backfired, as they too were seen as doing nothing more than hanging on to Warren's coattails along with most of the rest of the stable. Hayes and Studd (ESPECIALLY the latter) were unable or unwilling to give up their singles careers and concentrate solely on the tag belts....Warren became involved in feuds with the Genius and nearly half a dozen rookies (everyone forgetting that Hayes himself was a rookie), while Studd kept one eye on career nemesis Underground Kingpin and another eye on his ultimate aim, getting the World title back, despite that everyone but Ric himself realized that that was impossible barring the return of [name withheld]. By the time the next pay-per-view, HeatWave '98 rolled around, d'Artois had jobbed the World title to Fury and left the federation, never to return. Johnson had lost a ladder match for the TV title (albeit a great one) to Night Stalker, permanently cementing his reputation as a choker and exiling him to the lower mid-card forever. Gesner had quickly jobbed the US title back to Underground Kingpin and did nothing to build that belt's heat in the process. Andros rarely even wrestled anymore. Only Warren and his team remained unsullied, as Warren routinely humiliated the Genius and drummed rookies Steven Mercury and Eddie Parrish right out of the federation in between DYWYWU's rare title defenses. But it was clear that the stable had gotten too big for its britches, and something had to be done. That something would come at Heatwave '98, on August 26 in Mexico City. In typical WWO fashion, though, something went wrong...literally days before the card, it was discovered that a back injury suffered by team member Lariot at the 1997 version of Heatwave had not sufficiently healed to let him wrestle again. Since nothing had been done to elevate any other teams and the WWO couldn't put the World Leaders or Moshpit in the main event of its tag team showcase PPV, instead Andros and Gesner were stuck under latex Business Men masks (yes, seriously), and won the tag team gauntlet before facing off with DYWYWU in a tag team title match. Midway through the match, the real Business Men made their way down to the ring, and Andros and Gesner unmasked themselves to, well, the shock of not many in the crowd. The split of STB, though, allowed the WWO to milk a few more months out of the angle. Andros and Gesner were quickly replaced by CWC imports Jude St. Claire and "Blockbuster" Johnny Detson, great talents who unfortunately wound up buried along with Studd and Johnson in Warren's shadow. Warren remained his usual cocky self while Andros wrestled and interviewed like he had not done in a long time, but still failed to get over to any great degree as a face since the fans never forgave him for his part in the WIF fiasco. New tag teams like Uncivil Disobedience and the Ultimates finally began to emerge as legitimate threats to DYWYWU's apathetic supremacy in the tag division, but Hayes and Studd jobbing the belts still seemed to be a while off yet. STB, and the WWO as a whole, were plodding along and not really doing anything special in the early fall of 1998. But the spark that would ignite the greatest boom period in WWO history would come on an early October edition of Showcase. A brief in-ring brawl between Fury and STB was supposed to take place to set up a token World title defense for Fury against Studd for the next week (Studd routinely incessantly demanded such shots from Stone, who certainly never minded jobbing Studd). During the brawl, though, Studd pulled the most egregious shoot in WWO history, forcing ref Pierre Cordon to start an impromptu match between him and Fury, and proceeded to laugh at Fury's punches before hitting a couple of piledrivers and chairshots which caused the World champion to legitimately bleed all over the ring, before locking in an anklelock submission hold and forcing Fury to yell "I quit Mr. Studd, I quit, you win". As a flabbergasted audience of millions watched on live TV, Studd left the ring with the World title in tow. To say that Mark Stone hit the roof at Studd's blatant display of unprofessionalism would be putting it mildly...Stone reportedly ripped the title belt right out of Studd's hands as soon as he reached the locker room. After profusely apologizing to Fury and begging him not to quit the federation, and finding that there was no legal way to fire Studd at this time with a year and a half left on his contract, Stone put his foot down and declared that Studd would be buried in the low mid-card for the rest of his WWO stay as punishment...after being squashed by Fury on the next episode of Showcase. With a hot feud developing between Uncivil Disobedience and Fire & Ice, DYWYWU was no longer needed to carry the tag team division, and on October 23, 1998, Hayes and Studd jobbed the titles to UCD. Andros had worked hard in the fall of 1998, trying desperately to re-establish himself as the main-event face he once was and even winning a match over old rival Randy Bryson, but things just weren't clicking for him. Finally, it was decided he would put his friend Warren over in a career match at Anarchy '98 before becoming either a Chris Stonebreaker- type goodwill ambassador/front office man or a manager for a new anti-STB stable. In fact, the latter appeared imminent as when DYWYWU lost the titles to UCD, Andros showed up to taunt Warren...with none other than Hayes' old WIF enemy Roundhouse in tow. Warren and Andros put on an absolutely stunning performance on November 3 in Tijuana, part of what is perhaps the greatest WWO pay-per-view of all time. It may have been the greatest pure technical wrestling match ever seen in the WWO, and was overshadowed only by the pulsating Wuzho-Bryson blowoff on the same card. Andros would come out of retirement twice, but for all intents and purposes his career ended with this match. With the tag belts on UCD, Studd and Johnson's stars falling fast and St. Claire and Detson mostly mucking around in the lower card in residual CWC feuds, in the aftermath of Anarchy '98 the time had finally come to end STB once and for all. With Roundhouse allied with Andros, as well as Gesner and Ultimate Thrasher in his "Ikonoclast" guise, and none of them wanting more than to get their hands on Warren, the WWO had a perfect way of doing so: send the lesser STBers out to stop them and fail, leaving Warren irate. Studd was manhandled by Roundhouse, and Detson failed to defeat 的konoclast" during the month of November. Since Warren didn't even enter the rumble...well, there you go. Gesner broke away from Andros and joined with Uncivil Disobedience and Tracy Hudson to form yet another new stable, and the biggest "stable war" in WWO history was on. Ric Studd and Chris Johnson were left to run out their contracts in the lower mid-card. Warren, meanwhile, continued to infuriate everybody. Even Better, despite having TV champion Sebastian as their only titleholder, took arrogance to heights even STB had not known, and added "insurance policy" Tyrant and "pledge members" Techno Nic B and Vanilla Stalka (former TV champion Night Stalker recast in a newer, hipper gimmick) to their ranks in preperation for the huge steel cage Massacre match between the four stables at Valentine's Day Massacre. And, only a week before Valentine's Day Massacre, he suddenly packed his bags and left the WWO, probably without any intention of ever coming back. Flags flew at half-mast, but the WWO roster certainly looked strong enough at that point to withstand even the defection of Warren. Hell, he wasn't even in the World title picture, right? Well, Warren's departure wasn't the only one the WWO felt between February and June of 1999. He was joined by Wuzho, Night's Harbinger, Fury, Underground Kingpin, Rusty Bowman, and Randy Bryson, important players all. Although stalwarts like Thrasher and Roundhouse remained and hot rookies like Sebastian, Tyrone Hayes and Apex also emerged, the men the front office tried desperately to build into the next Warren Hayes, those being Johnny Detson and Darren Morgan, failed so badly in the lead heel role that by summertime they had no choice but to go back to Randy Bryson as lead heel despite the fact that Bryson's personal problems were obviously distracting from his in-ring performance and that he was obviously on his last legs as a draw anyway, and run an angle where Bryson stole the World title belt in a match where Ultimate Thrasher won the actual title, and book him in a showdown with Thrasher at War of the Ages 3 in the Georgia Dome...only to have Bryson be forced to leave the WWO due to drug problems. Scrambling, somebody came up with the brilliant idea of bringing back none other than [name withheld] as Bryson's "proxy" and putting him up against Thrasher in what was billed as a showdown of best friends (ignoring the fact that Thrasher and Iron had been allied for about a total of two weeks in the WWO), with Thrasher's ally Roundhouse as special referee. Iron had no chance of winning the title, so the obvious outcome of the main event would see him and Thrasher teaming to turn on and pummel Roundhouse in what would be a total letdown for the huge crowd on hand in the dome. Fortunately for the WWO, all this happened at the exact time that Warren discovered that life in the entertainment industry wasn't all it's cracked up to be, and decided to leave the field of acting once and for all and return to the world of wrestling, much to the delight of the front office. And he showed up to do guest color commentary for the Thrasher-Iron "match" (no, the quotation marks are not a typo) at WOTA, and when Thrasher and Iron began pummeling Roundhouse, Warren suddenly cast aside his headset, entered the ring, and made the save for his former enemy as the crowd went berserk. Literally, he saved the show, and turned into a mega-face in the process. He topped that off by making Thrasher and Iron look like total fools in the main event, as he and their supposed ally Mason helped a mockery of Roundhouse suddenly turn into a serious title match where Roundhouse defeated Thrasher for the World title. Seeing the writing on the wall, [name withheld] left the WWO immediately following this card, thus depriving us of seeing the privelige of him finally getting paid back for his years of anti-WWO activity. Thrasher came out for the match alone and wrestled a handicap match for the first several minutes, and with Roundhouse and Warren unable to put him away friction between the two old enemies clearly began showing before the arena lights went out, and suddenly out walked former WWO champion Fury, who had left the federation without actually losing the title. He proceeded to lay a hurt on both Warren and Roundhouse before Thrasher got the pinfall on Roundhouse. Warren and Roundhouse, of course, both blamed each other for the loss, and Thrasher and Fury were obviously nothing more than a brief alliance of convinience, a four-man angle over the World title could easily be set up with tons and tons of heat for all involved. With Fury's contract nearly expired and his worth as a monster heel to the federation diminishing in the era of increasingly sarcastic, cynical characters, it was decided that the blowoff to his feud with Warren would see Warren going over in another career match, this time at the regular-TV Thanksgiving Special which replaced the Anarchy PPV in 1999, partly to thank the fans for their patience and partly because the WWO had no time to build any more storylines up for an important PPV. The fans in attendance in Salt Lake City and watching at him witnessed another splendid encounter that saw Warren go over cleanly after hitting the "Ratings Plunge" angel dive, and served to give him the final rub he needed to carry the WWO on his back...after all, he beat the unbeatable monster. But rather than simply setting up a Warren-Thrasher feud for the title, the front office instead had Roundhouse win the title back from Thrasher on this same show and then put the title up for grabs at the Season's Beatings '99 PPV in Honolulu in a six-pack match involving Roundhouse, Thrasher, Warren, Tyrone Hayes, Mike Sebastian and Apex. Why? Partially to finally pacify behind-the-scenes sniping that had occurred ever since the Iron fiasco in the summer, with many people complaining (somewhat validly) that the Clique was holding them down from main eventing, but partially also because Warren wanted to turn heel and go back to running a stable, in spite of the fact that he was on the verge of seemingly becoming the top money-drawing face in the history of the WWO (not that the competition for that is very tough). Although the six-pack match featured some great action, the finish really did nothing to elevate anybody, and the WWO wound up with egg on its face when even more production problems delayed "New Year's" Bash well into February. The delay left the WWO with no time to book more than two opening matches for the card, and as a result left the royal rumble itself with a ghastly amount of airtime to fill, resulting in the match having a slow, turgid pace with almost as many as two dozen wrestlers usually in the ring and very few eliminations. Not even Warren could really wake up the Japanese crowd, but eventually he would come through, finally dumping Tyrone Hayes and Mike Sebastian to win the rumble and become the first man ever to win both of the federation's World titles. Captain Hood unmasked as former UEW superstar Nevermind, the "Luchadores" unmasked as hot new tag team the Outlaws, and their "witch doctor" unmasked as Ultimate Thrasher (whose desire for the World title was apparently forgotten). Tyrone Hayes, who could have filled the role, was thoroughly fed up with being passed over for World title shots and was reportedly on the verge of leaving the WWO entirely at this point. Apex? Was forced to take time off to rehabilitate injuries suffered due to his insane style. Roundhouse? The fanbase was visibly sick of him by this point, and he was being depushed as fast as possible. End result? The SoN basically dominated every card with the same sort of pointless beatdowns on JTTS and mid-carders that had become a wrestling cliche by 2000, Mike Sebastian was not bought by the fans as a threat to Warren's title, showing once again how the WWO's fanbase hates being told who to cheer for, and ratings plummeted just at a time when leftover financial problems dating back from the [name withheld] Era began to rear their ugly head in a big way. Finally, by April 2000, Mark Stone could find no other way to stop the bleeding, and closed the federation. The final match on the final episode of Superstar Showcase was an appropriate one, as it showed Warren easily defending his World title against "Southern Gentleman" Jimmy Lemon, and the SoN humiliating Lemon in a postmatch beatdown where they literally made lemonade right in the ring. Warren was one of the five WWO superstars whose contracts were immediately sold to the UEW, and he was really the only one who was able to rise above various political problems and misuse to make an impact in that federation, winning its TV title and being seemingly destined for greater things.
