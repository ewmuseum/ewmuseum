James Knight --------------------------------------------------------------------------- Take It From Me by [name withheld] contact: [detail withheld] So, here goes another attempt at keeping the Source alive. After many people decided having a reviewless Source was better than anything, I went with that. The Source now changes to a more magazine style in its regular format. Now, that's not to say there won't be a review. It'll just be a very tiny review. Couple of paragraphs highlighting the big moments and on to the next match. Next comes help. I wanted to put out a general Source to give the feel of what I want. I would like it for [name withheld] to get Kimberly Campbell to continue with the Hot Five, but that depends on the WWO roster. For newbies, the Hot Five is an article voted on by the WWO fans and handlers with the five people you consider to have the most heat. You can, and some do, add in a little line as well. Josh R.'s Inside the Minds was also an interesting article but not as well received as it should have been. Obviously up to Hootie if he wants to continue it. The Roundtable is something I'd like to continue, but would like your, being the fans and handlers, opinion first. For those who don't know, the Roundtable was a four or five person moderated discussion on a particular issue or set of issues. They usually lasted 30 minutes or slightly more and were done in the #wwosource room on IRC. E-mail me at [detail withheld] or AIM me at [detail withheld] if you would like to see the Roundtable continue. If no one tells me, I'll leave it to special occurrences or never . If anyone wants to take on the abbreviated Reviews, that would be nice. I'm not going to ask for a lot. Just a paragraph or two hitting the high points. I also would like to get some Preview people for the Source. These are small little articles wrote in-character giving a small blurb on the match. For those new to the WWO and to the Source, I would recommend going to groups.google.com and doing a search for "WWO Source" and specifically those with James Knight in command to get a feel as to what the Source was doing before the recent troubles. I always felt the Reviews were a staple of the Source, something unique to it...at least, somewhat. But that's a lot to ask and it takes a specific kind of person to write them up. Mail Call will continue on, hopefully. I think this column can really kick off if we get some feedback on it. I like the idea of a "Letters to the Editor" type column and I'll be using both Lewis and Knight to give feedback on the each letter. Stupid nonsense letters and actual questions are both appreciated. You can use fake names and locations. I'm also likely to bring back the PowerPoll, but maybe next time. There will be several new features brought into the Source hopefully. Spotlights on wrestlers (called Reflections) will be one feature I think could take off. This would be especially helpful for newbies in getting their wrestler a little more time to be known. I chose to do one on my little known jobber named Tre Jordan and I did it as a more bio with interview mixed in, but it's up to you how you do it. I ask that if you do use a Q&A session, you use a WWO roving reporter like Mackey Jay. Another new feature is a new editor. Remy Lewis is brought on board to give the Source a new voice and new direction. Lewis will differ from my other character and former WWO Source editor James Knight in a variety of ways, most of which will be apparent when I get to his lead-in column, My Time. Basically, Lewis is the youngest male reporter the WWO has and is a liberal voice, more likely to cheer the faces than the heels and more likely to not be as cynical as Knight. He's also more likely to get on WWO TV because well...nobody actually wants to see Knight. Anywho, I would like to thank Dale for chipping in an article dated May 3, 2002 so understand the time for it. Please help me out cause the Source is harder to put together when you have no help. As more people volunteer, I'll add to the Contacts list. Oh, and by the way, Dale and I have a site if you don't know. [detail withheld] Contacts: James Knight, Remy Lewis, and myself: [detail withheld] --------------------------------------------------------------------------- My Time by Remy Lewis contact: [detail withheld] We all need a start. Most journalists begin with dreams of a Pulitzer and international fame. I'm really no different. However, most don't start with a wrestling 'zine, but that's just fine. We all need a start. Anywhere is fine. Introductions are in order, of course. Being the new kid on the block, most of the WWO security still cast looks of surprise when I show them my identification. Yes, even a 25 year-old from Cali. can get a job in Biloxi, Miss., working as the editor of the WWO's Source. My resume is none-too-thrilling. My work includes the high school paper, the college newspaper, and even a short seven month job working obituaries for the Roanoke Times in nowhere, Virginia. I basically tried to find new challenges and new homes to start fresh because every job before ended in the typical sleeping with the boss's daughter. Stone doesn't have a daughter, does he? Not to complain, though. That last job landed me my shot here. I was hanging out with a couple of my fellow reporters who had far better jobs when one of them mentioned how he was going to do an interview of Dark Soul before his big World Title match with Tyrone Hayes. Being a Dark fan, I accompanied my buddy to go meet Dark and so forth. Well, eventually, after the interview was over, I talked to Dark about my vision and so forth and he seemed rather impressed. He told me that the WWO was looking to demote their current editor and bring in an entirely new voice who of course would cost less than a vet. Well, who doesn't jump at that chance? And so, here I am and I am praying Dark Soul never loses the World Title because something tells me it was not a coincidence I got the job offer officially no more than 48 hours after Dark Soul won the belt. But that's really of no concern. What is of concern is where the Source is heading and to me, I would like the Source to continue what made it the best 'zine in the business but improving little elements of it. We'll focus more on what can happen...and what didn't happened...rather than what actually did happen. The Reviews will be short, but the information on wrestlers will hopefully be increased. James Knight stays on to be one of the top reporters for the Source, of which I'm thankful. Though he hates me right now, it's nice to have some experience on the staff. Right now, my staff is him and me because I wanted to start anew. Hopefully, I'll be able to bring back some of the usual faces. And hey, I'm one up on Knight already. Kimberly Campbell actually did return one of my phonecalls. I plan on using this column to speak out on bigger issues than stupid introductions, but until then, I hope you enjoy the Source. --------------------------------------------------------------------------- Mail Call by Remy Lewis and James Knight contact: [detail withheld] >From Hootie H. in Miami, FL: HOOTIE HOO! Or something like that. So, this is my way of putting some closure (at least temporary) on my "career" as Apex's handler. There are two parts to this article. The first is a letter from Apex, it'll be done in-character and will probably be sappy and stupid. The second part will be from me. It's going to be out of character (obviously). So, here goes: ----------------------------------------------------------- "A Letter from Apex" ----------------------------------------------------------- To the WWO and its fans, As I'm sure you've heard by now, my career has suddenly fallen into the toilet. The injuries I suffered in my neck at the hands of Tracy Hudson are pretty extensive and are going to require surgery. Apparently, surgery is unavoidable. As of right now, I'm stuck in a wheelchair, with minimal use of my legs. That is, they move and twitch and what not, and the muscles still look like they're there, but nothing responds. The surgery has its share of risks, but the doctors aren't overly worried about killing me on the table. Well, if they are worried about killing me, they aren't letting me know about it. There's a funny story that goes along with that. I was trying to push to get Dr. Practice and Dr. Ow as my surgeons, but Stone didn't seem to think that was such a good idea. Stone didn't think it was a good idea for a precision chainsaw to be used in process of exposing my spine. Oh well, I can't complain, he (and the WWO) have been good enough to keep paying me, despite the fact that I'm down. They're even going to pay for the surgery and recovery. Consequently, I've decided to expand on my surgery and have opted for adamantium to be laced to my bones and additional arms to placed under the pre-existing ones... I'm going to kick some serious ass when I get back. That brings me to the next thing I wanted to discuss: my future. I love wrestling. I eat wrestling, I sleep it, and damn it, I am wrestling. And I'm going to do everything in my power to make a full recovery and get back into the ring. The doctors and experts aren't so optimistic. Some have said that I have a 50/50 chance of coming back, others have said that I will never come back. That's pretty much the state of all things me. Now, since I won't be seeing anyone, I'd like to toss some words out to some folks. I won't say anything to those in my camp (Charlie & Zombie Corners), because they've been with me since the injury, and they know how I feel. But, here's to all of you other important people. The fans: Yeah, even in cripplehood I'm a sappy good guy. I love my fans. Without them, none of us would be here. I hope I was a good example and a good performer. I only wish I could've done more to make each and everyone one of you proud. Dark Soul: Chris, out of everyone in the WWO, including my brother, you're the one I feel the closest to. We've been on the road hitting the arenas together for a long time. At times we were enemies, and as far as that goes, you were one of the best competitors I've ever been in the ring with. More often, however, we were together as allies. And I've never had a better ally. Every time I had the spotlight and the chance to do something great, you were there to give me support. I will be in touch with you man, you know it. And I'll be watching you at Renaissance, I know you'll do great. Mark Stone: You silly man. Here I was, another typical daredevil wrestler with a bad attitude, and you took a chance on me. You hired me... you're either brilliant to crazy, maybe both. I don't know what you saw in me, but I hope I lived up to your expectations. I really appreciate everything you've done for me and everything you're doing for me now. You're a good man and you run a good company. Miguel Quesada: Little bro', I never expected to see you here. I didn't want you to be here. There were times when I considered personally kicking your ass off my turf. I'm glad I didn't. You've had your up's and down's, but you're making mom proud. If mom's happy, I'm happy. And I'm man enough to admit, you're a better technical wrestler than I am. If you get head clear and focus on the tasks ahead of you, nothing can hold you down. Kimberly Campbell: Ok, I admit, there was a bit of sexual tension between us. Well, at least I thought so. Damn that Papa Legba and his insights. Anyhow, you've been here as long as I have, and you've always given me more than my fair share of interview time and personal support. You're a good reporter and interviewer. I hope the WWO uses you more to your potential in the future. There are probably a dozen other people I should thank and recognize. The obvious people that I'm missing are those that in Dark Soul's camp, especially Candy (she did flash me, after all), Source editor James Knight, and all of those zany WWO VP's. But, really, those five entities I listed above top the list. To everyone else, thanks for your support. I'll miss you guys. Dale --------------------------------------------------------------------------- Seasonal Awards by James Knight contact: [detail withheld] Taking a page (and an article name) out of former Editor Gary Hammond's book, I bring you the unofficial awards for the WWO from after-Anarchy to Renaissance. If you want to bitch about them, then do. In fact, I would applaud said bitching. I try to remain neutral, but who knows if I actually will? WRESTLER OF THE SEASON Dark Soul I hate going with the simple World Champion pick, but one can't deny he went through a lot following Anarchy. From losing to Tracy Hudson in a Loser-Leaves-WWO match to beating Tyrone "Purple" Hayes at Renaissance, Mr. Soul came back from completely bottoming out to the top spot in the WWO. How long he remains there is certainly a question, but right now, he's the new King. Honorable Mention: Tracy Hudson, Johnny Detson, Tyrone Hayes TAG TEAM OF THE SEASON Dark Haven This was a hard pick considering P.A.I.N.'s always stellar work, Blood Brothers' rise, Canadian Legacy's rebirth, and more than anything, the Amazing Romanis becoming champs, but Dark Haven's streak remains intact and that meant they went through a tournament and faced top-flight competition to keep it. Romanis lose out only because of the anticlimactic nature of their PPV match. Honorable Mention: Amazing Romanis, P.A.I.N., Blood Brothers, Canadian Legacy FACE OF THE SEASON Dark Soul Lack of competition for the top face spot withstanding, before this last cycle, Dark Soul was arguably the most popular face in the WWO. Now, it's a no-brainer. With the return of Candy Malone, his stock only rises in the eyes of the fan and it's hard not to cheer his blue-collar work ethic. Honorable Mention: Apex, Miguel Quesada, Matt Cole, P.A.I.N. HEEL OF THE SEASON Tracy Hudson Despite a disappearing act at times, Tracy Hudson was by far the most hated wrestler in the WWO the past few months. His feud with Dark Soul made him that, but that's not to forget that this man ruined Apex's first title reign since Latex Lex ruined his TV Title reign in 1999. Not only that, but there are some serious concerns whether or not Apex will ever wrestle again. Sadly, it looks as if Hudson may be on the outs with the WWO community, but no one can take away what he did the past several months. Honorable Mention: Tyrone Hayes, Nate Knowitall, Johnny Detson, Amazing Romanis ROOKIE OF THE SEASON Xavier Feyr To be honest, there wasn't much competition for this slot, but Feyr has came in fists firing and looking for an all-out war. His in-ring talent isn't under question and taking the simple method of having the argumentative chick, could get over rather quick. Honorable Mention: The Magnificent FEUD OF THE SEASON Dark Soul vs. Tracy Hudson These two attempted to completely kill each other. They hated each other with passion and when the chance came, tried to tear the other apart. This feud technically began long before the cycle actually did (remember, it was back in early September when Hudson attacked Candy Malone), but was at its hottest following Anarchy when both could focus completely on each other. They shared three great matches with Dark Soul taking the first and final match. It propelled Dark Soul to the main event. What more could you ask for? If there was a title involved, this feud probably becomes the biggest feud of the year with months still remaining. Honorable Mention: Legba/Extreme, Stringer/Detson/Apex, Quesada/Knowitall, Romanis/PAIN MATCH OF THE SEASON Dark Soul vs. Apex: IP Title To a fan, this was a dream match. But, with that said, I might as well put it as a tie with my other three favorite matches of the last season. However, what makes this match a step over was all the intangibles added to it. The history between the wrestlers, Tracy Hudson's influence on Dark Soul and being at ring side, and add into the return of Candy Malone on the same card. Not to mention they wrestled matches of their career. Of course, Apex was attacked afterwards and it ended in a screwjob way, but not a typical screwjob and the heat was at a major high point for both. Honorable Mention: Tracy Hudson vs. Dark Soul: Loser Leaves WWO, Tyrone Hayes vs. Latex Lex: Non-Title (second match of three), William Craven vs. Chris Hopper: Freezer Match PLEASANT SURPRISE OF THE SEASON The Tag Team Division There always seems to be one part of the fed the WWO leans on through a cycle. Through the first cycle, it was the feud between Operation: Scorched Earth and the Quesadas and Dark Soul. Through the second cycle, it was the feud between Ultimate Thrasher and William Craven. In the third cycle, it was the tag team division. The WWO has a rare blessing of talented tag teams. Not just a few, but several. When the singles ranks at times seemed to not gel right, the tag team pulled off some great matches. With the Romanis and Dark Haven in the driver seats and Blood Brothers, the Canadians, P.A.I.N., and some new teams added to the mix, I doubt very much the tag team division will fall. Honorable Mention: The Return of Miguel Quesada's Face Heat, Lesbian Kiss Between Stacy and Shelly, Return of Candy Malone, Return of Mark Stone BIGGEST DISAPPOINTMENT OF THE SEASON (TIE) The Losses of Chris Stringer and Apex Chris Stringer had grown into the main competition of Tyrone Hayes for heel heat. He finally got a chance to hold a title for the first time since getting cheated out of the TV Title in 1999. Fire and Ice 3. Blood Brothers --------------------------------------------------------------------------- Reflection: Tre Jordan by Remy Lewis Tre Jordan wasn't always considered a weak wrestler. In fact, Jordan was at a time considered one of the greatest independent CruiserWeights in all the land, but Jordan contends he is not bitter by his placing in the WWO. "I'm here," he says. "I get a shot. Most people in the indi's don't even get a shot in the big time." Born Trey Jordane, Jordan grew up with the typical rich boy life. A product of two highly successful lawyers in the Toronto area, Jordan acted out many times as a youth in response to his neglecting parents. Because of their work schedules, Jordan was usually left to the watch of a plethora of butlers and maids, which he most likely hated. However, Jordan can not remember most of these times. Part of that can be attributed to suppression of the memories, but also notable was his drug and alcohol use from his early teens to his early 20's. Sent to private schooling in Western Canada, Jordan excelled at sports, lettering in baseball, football, and wrestling. However, tragedy struck at 17 as he was involved in an automobile wreck. He would be okay, but the wreck claimed the life of young man in another car. Blood tests showed incredible amounts of alcohol in Jordan's system. His parents did their best to make the situation go away, paying off the victim's family, but they had enough of Jordan and kicked him out after his senior year was finished. However, to keep Jordan quiet, they gave him a good allowance and the rich kid was left to remain rich. "I don't remember much," he recalls. "All I do remember is the drugs and how much they were running my life." >From there, he moved to the states, going from town-to-town looking for enjoyment and purpose. In 1998, he found himself in a Hampton Roads, Virginia rehab clinic, cleaning up his act, or at least attempting to. "All I knew were that drugs weren't making me happy," Jordan notes. After getting out of rehab, he worked as security at the Norfolk Scope when on a fluke, he met Dark Soul. Jordan's physical skills needed to be fine-tuned, but after rehab, his allowance was stopped and he was hoping to make some money. He joined the DIWL, an independent scene in eastern Virginia which would spawn over around the United States. After a short crash course, he was brought into the DIWL under the name Tre Jordan and joined up with Dark Soul's stable, New Blood. Jordan's personality breed immediate conflicts and within a few months, he was kicked out of New Blood. He disappeared and many though his career was over. Jordan himself hit rock bottom and fell off the wagon. He found himself broke and in an insane asylum once he came out of his stupor. Asked to come back to the DIWL and compete in the rapidly growing CruiserWeight division, Tre Jordan returned as a man not very in touch with reality, which in all honesty, he wasn't. Though born an only child, he claimed himself the brother of DIWL owner [name withheld]. He also feuded with New Blood, especially Dark Soul and a 7'4" giant named Lava. He even found time to feud with Apex for a short while. But in the ring, no one could doubt Tre Jordan. After a slow start, he began to carve a niche for himself, winning the DIWL CruiserWeight Title three times, and climbing the charts of CruiserWeight wrestlers in the independents. He joined forces with future WWO wrestler Midnight Assassin for a period of time before Assassin turned on him. In March of 1999, Tre Jordan left the DIWL and set off to be a rock star. He spent close to year and a half attempting to take his small knowledge of the bass guitar and turn it into something special. After several bands failed, he came back to wrestling, searching up Dark Soul's name and suddenly arriving on the scene on November 29, 2001, attempting to get past security on Superstar Showcase and failing. He would meet Billy McWilliams, a blind former butler who he brought in for help. "Mack's da man," Jordan believes. "He keeps all my whites white. I don't know how he does it, but he does it just the same." He would then bring in the services of Sean "Da Superiah Talent" Collins as the two began to team up on a semi-regular basis. Jordan believes the two are just a win away from stardom. "Sean and me have been training. We just need our shot to beat up on somebody. No more handicap matches." What does the future hold for Jordan? Making the transition of a lesser-used wrestler, used for entertainment purposes only, to the big house is very hard and only a few in history have ever accomplished the feat. But Jordan believes he has the talent and whether it be as a tag wrestler or in singles action, the three-time CruiserWeight Champ thinks one shot is all he needs. Not just comes the hard part. Earn that shot. --------------------------------------------------------------------------- And in conclusion... by James Knight contact: [detail withheld] One would think I'm bitter, but really, I'm not. Okay, I'm kinda pissed off. I'm far more worthy of the Editor's job than that kid and I hate Dark Soul now with a passion. But, I'm going to complain. In fact, my subject of this article will be the faces, or good guys, of the WWO. It's sad when Dark Soul is the main guy, but even sadder by those following him up. Dark Soul has heat, plenty of it. His last five matches have been against Tracy Hudson (twice), Operation: Scorched Earth, Apex, and Tyrone Hayes. It's hard not to have heat with that type of competition. Following him, though, are a pathetic group of faces who either have little or no heat or are too new to have the heat needed. Let's start with Matt Cole. Roughish, all right. Tough, sure. But a man who the fans can grow attached to? My left pinkie has more charisma than this guy. Yes, that's his style. Hard-worker, blue-collar, works his ass off. But that's also Lew Spectre's shtick and I'm not much for the no entertainment, all wrestler scene. Cole needs something quick to grow an attachment to the fans. It took a woman for Dark Soul and it took insanity for Apex. I'm just not sure what Cole needs. Miguel Quesada is always popular. He's a WWOer from way back certain to have his own marks. He's been back-and-forth from heel-to-face-to-heel-to-face and is coming off what can be considered his biggest win of his career over Nate Knowitall. He can't step back, though. This is the same guy that watched as his brother and his former tag partner all improved their place in the WWO while he stalled. The heel turn worked as well as my sixth marriage. He's finally getting over it and I credit hard work for that. But now, can Naza and his quest for to be respected actually end up in a positive manner? He's a wait-and-see type guy, but how much can we count on? Latex Lex!?!?!?! Who friggin' knew Latex Lex would be considered one of the top faces in the WWO? Lex is coming off beatings from Tyrone Hayes and now wants to wrestle despite the injuries. If Lex continues to fight the Upper Crust, the heat will follow most likely. But can he really match up through his disappearing acts? The Papa Legba/Alex Extreme came to a close and I'm not sure which was is the face and which one is the heel. Whichever one, if either, are faces have a good shot to climb the WWO ladder quickly. Both have great talent and know how to play a feud. However, they seem tweeners leaning heel. The face division needs some serious repair and quickly. Sure, the World Champion is set, but what about the titles? Who faces Detson? Cole? He didn't earn his first IP Title feud.
