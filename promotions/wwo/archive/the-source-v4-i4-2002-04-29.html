Your question should be, "do you have a life?" --------------------------------------------------------------------------- Spotlight Review by Jack Malarky With apologies comes a shortened review of Spotlight. Reporting is my second job, and my first one has been nasty lately. Damn Romulans... We open to see this mysterious Aramis Merceds fellow who's been trying to get Micheal Donovan motivated for the past few weeks, speaking with Sigma. Gist of it is, there's been no improvement, and Sigma's on his own tonight against Micheal Thunderwolf. "San Malo" is there, but in a wheelchair with an expression vaguely reminiscent of your typical William Craven opponent. Credits, and WE ARE LIVE from... Candy Malone! It's been about six months, and absence makes the heart grow fonder, and is likely making other organs grow if Bradley's tone of voice is any indication. She seems oddly happy, considering that her charge is now in the camp of the man who injured her in the first place. She tells us that she offered to help Dark Soul in the loser leaves town match, and he declined her help. She proceeds to complain about Dark Soul's joining Hudson when we are joined by Hudson himself, who looks like he dressed in the dark. Moongirl accompanies him. Hudson compares WWO to whack-a-mole, and Candy to the mole. Candy says she wants to speak to Dark SOul, and Hudson obliges. Dark Soul emerges (to "Loser" by Beck, no, not THAT Beck), and tries to tell Candy to leave, while she tries to get him to stand up to Hudson. Tracy eventually intervenes and orders Dark Soul to attack Malone. He refuses, and Tracy decides to do it himself. A shot of Moongirl looking in disbelief is thrown in... there's an eventual split being teased here. Before Hudson can hit Candy, Apex comes running out wielding an ATC (Apex Titanium Chair), and Hudson decides it might not be good to have his head smashed today. He orders Dark to come with him, and informs Apex that he'll be wrestling Dark Soul instead of him in the IP Title Match later on. Apex pleads with Dark Soul to come back, going as far to say that he'd retire, and they could "go work someplace else" (and if I were Mark Stone, and that happened, and I were alive, I'd fire Hudson). Dark Soul leaves with Hudson, and Apex throws a fit. Great segment; moved the plot while being entertaining, a hard combination to master. The announcers run down the card. Any announcer who buries the talent by insinuating that the matches will be boring, thus encouraging people to change the channel, should be fired. -Armed Forces vs. Canadian Legacy: Great reaction for the Forces, and due to production problems, we get to hear it twice. You'd think they'd have corrected the feed timing to get the five seconds necessary for TV Editing BEFORE Hudson got on the mic. Canadian Legacy comes down second, which is odd as traditionally that's the face spot. I'm not going to do play-by-play here, due to time constraints. Legacy starts fast, uses some illicit tactics, but the Forces come back strong. Early back-and-forth action goes in the Forces' favor. McDonald manages to get Defcon trapped in the Legacy's corner, and cheating ensues. Defcon turns the tide and the Forces regain control, using sound wrestling. Several near falls culminate in Don Cameron getting involved to help escape a pinfall, and a low blow again swings the tide toward the Legacy. The dichotomy of styles is working well... Armed Forces fighting fair while the Canadian Legacy cheats at all corners. Navcom moves out of the way of a Denis missile dropkick after a Legacy offensive to turn the tide again. Defcon cleans house until Cameron whacks him with his cane. Defcon isn't fazed, but this distracts him for a knee clip, and the Legacy goes after Defcon's leg. Cameron gives leverage to a Denis figure four... nice to know there are still heel managers that know what to do at ringside. Cordon makes him break the hold, and Defcon reverses it, at which point Alain McDonald decides that he really doesn't need the winners' paycheck, and he pastes Defcon's head to the canvas with a BRUTAL chairshot, drawing a DQ. Match Rating - ***, and could have gone higher if not for the abrupt finish... I suppose they're saving that one for PPV. Postmatch, the Legacy takes Tracy Hudson's whack-a-mole analogy and applies it to the Armed Forces, who deserve a Purple Heart for taking those shots. Cordon gets a chairshot too, and you could hear a few cheers with that one, but otherwise, the crowd is rather incensed. They 'bury' the Forces with the American Flag and bodybags. Cameron cuts a promo on America, and announces that he'd like to do permanent dental damage with some dogtags, but the Blood Brothers run in and beat up the Legacy, driving them away. So, which feud does the Legacy pursue? And don't the Blood Brothers have something going with Dark Haven? We'll see... I like the level of activity in the tag division. Archibald The Magnificent blathers a lot, and tells us the Midnight Assassin doesn't deserve a title match. Wow, there's a news flash. -"Trixxster" Marvin Patch vs. "Bullitt" Dwayne Rogers: Rogers is good, but Patch is one of the best in the world. Despite Manning's comment to the contrary, nobody in the arena believes that this is going to be a fight. Announcers misidentify this as Dwayne's second WWO match; it's his third (he's fought Fydor and Colby). We are served a heaping helping of squash as Patch wrecks Rogers, and wins it with a DDT-Dragon Sleeper combination for the submission. It's too bad; Rogers could do good things in WWO if he puts the effort in. Patch, however, is going to go as far in WWO as he wants to... he's that good. Match: .5* Postmatch, Patch decides to reapply the hold, and make Dwayne suffer. As he leaves, the camera gets a close-up, and then an extreme close-up as it slams into the back of his head. Xavier Feyr probably isn't a licensed camera operator, but he plays one on TV. Feyr beats up Patch, slams him onto the ringsteps, then attempts to break Patch's leg with the camera. We can only assume that officials break it up, because we unceremoniously cut to... Christopher Stonebreaker's office. Alex Extreme rushes in, cuts an angry promo on Stonebreaker, and demands to face Legba in a match at Renaissance "in the match you keep insisting you don't want to see on WWO TV". If it's Ultimate Death Match, I firmly agree with Stonebreaker. Or even a Skyscraper match, cool as they were. Or even Six Degrees Of Devastation, mostly because that was a lame match concept to begin with. Or a TEMB Says Match, which should never happen ever again anywhere on Earth. Speaking of which, Bradley decides to beat a dead horse with anti-UEW comments, and thank you for pissing on the hard work of a lot of people, now learn to call a match. -WWO Title Of The Americas championship match: Papa Legba (c) vs. Midnight Assassin: I can only assume that there's a point to this. Assassin is much better than most give him credit for, but Legba is a beast. However, Archibald The Magnificent decides to come out (as Vincent Delacroix distracts everyone by cutting a promo at the top of the aisle) and squash Assassin before the match, hitting a Grand Finale on Assassin after attacking from behind. The gist of this is that Archibald is making a 'declaration' that he will win a title as soon as he gets into contention. If Assassin is a contender, then I'm pretty sure Archibald is too. Legba enters, and another squash ensues. Assassin moves out of the way of a moonsault(!) and gets a few weak shots in, thus getting in more offense than Dwayne Rogers, but is still summarily defeated with the Roots-Voodoo Spell From Hell (Modified Remix). Match: .5* Postmatch, Alex Extreme attacks, but lest we let them build up the feud, the Romanis run down to help out, and Extreme exits. Of course, that establishes the Romani-Legba alliance further. Lion Tamer, Tiger Lily, and a doctor enter the ring. No, that isn't the opening line of a bad joke, but I'm sure I could make one up if I wanted to. Tamer gives a farewell that culminates with the classic line "There is a plunger inside each and every one of you!" Rick Marley enters for a rebuttal (and it's about time he got some mic time on WWO TV), and Tamer cuts him off by introducing his doctor as Doctor Bob. Bob backs up Tamer's story, except states that there's a chance that he might live. Marley refuses to back out of the match, and Lion Tamer dies. No, really. A masked man attacks Marley with a chair as Tamer is wheeled out in an ambulance, laying him out with five chairshots. -Micheal Thunderwolf vs. Sigma: Thunderwolf enters with his tag partner, as does Sigma. Sigma also brings Aramis Mercedes (who is wheeling San Malo in his wheelchair) and the infamous pane of glass. Thuderwolf counter-wrestles Sigma early, but is caught playing to the crowd and has his knee clipped. Sigma goes to work on the knee, and then showboats for the crowd as he tries to get a reaction from San Malo. This gives Thunderwolf time to recover, and lay out Sigma with a clothesline. Dueling chops is stopped by a Sigma kick to the knee. Sigma commences to beat the hell out of Thunderwolf for a couple of minutes, to the point where Rich Manning apologizes to all the WWO fans for his behavior... now that's old school. Thunderwolf makes a sudden comeback, fights Sigma down (without ever using a closed fist), and goes to work on Sigma's arm. Thunderwolf combines some of his holds with clawholds, but is eventually countered with an eye rake. Clawhold regains control for Thunderwolf, and a War Dance and a nasty double wristlock suplex (called a Raw Deal Indian-Handshake Suplex by Manning) follow. After some more offense, Thunderwolf attempts an airplane spin, but his injured knee can't sustain that pressure (PSYCHOLOGY~!) and he drops. Sigma throws Thunderwolf over the top rope (and this MUST be an old school wrestling match because the referee was distracted for that), and superkicks him when he tries to skin the cat, sending Thunderwolf crashing into Donovan's wheelchair. Sigma chucks Thunderwolf back in, ignoring his crippled tag team partner, drawing an angry look from Mercedes. Mercedes decides to blame Jake Thomas, and Thomas chases Mercedes around the ring while Thunderwolf executes a spinning Uranage to regain control. Suddenly, Thomas is blasted out of nowhere by a wheelchair... Micheal Donovan lives! Donovan whacks Thunderwolf with a flourescent lightbulb, drawing yet another intentional disqualification, and who said WWO wasn't HHHHHAAAAAAAAAAAAARRRRRRDDDDDDDDDDDCCCCCCCOOOOOOOOOORRRRRRRRRRREEEEE~!? Match: ***, and definitely would have been higher if not for the ending. Postmatch, Donovan applies a Kata Hajime to Thunderwolf after the match. Sigma gets the glass, but security stops Dark Haven before they can use the items. If this match was any indication, I think Dark Haven versus the Blood Brothers would be a match I'd rather like. Johnny Detson breaks into what I can only assume is Chris Stringer's locker room, and has another discussion with Cherry Bomb. You'd think that Stringer would actually stay around if he didn't want these sort of things to happen. Detson thanks Cherry for last week's accidental assist and gives her a 1961 Corvette. All he asks for is Cherry's backup during his match with Chris Hopper. Rather a pittance, when you consider that a '61 Vette probably would eat up over two-thirds of Detson's yearly salary. Unless, of course, he recieved gratuities from killing Mark Stone. That, I can see. -"Blockbuster" Johnny Detson vs. "Too Cool" Chris Hopper: Detson's entrance clocks in at well over three minutes. Let me get this straight. We've cut short almost every match and pullapart that we've had all night, and Johnny Detson gets a 3:38 entrance, enough time to go through almost his entire theme song? Maybe it was the production staff that gave him the gratuities. Then again, Cherry Bomb is with him, so it's a watchable entrance. Detson gets the mic and demands that the crowd shows Cherry some respect, obviously attempting to impress her. Now, if Stringer's in the building, and not near a monitor, wouldn't he at least be able to hear that? Chris Hopper's entrance is now so long, he needs TWO theme songs... that, or he felt upstaged by Detson. Seriously, we could have had another match already. Hopper beats up Detson early, but is caught with a low blow that was so sneaky, they have to call a replay just to catch it. Detson works the crowd, Hopper works the crowd, there's some stalling and eye gouging, and finally Detson goes to work, blowing kisses to Cherry and playing to her with every move. Eric Stryker enters the scene, and takes third headset. Detson capitalizes with illegal tactics, as Hopper is now completely distracted. However, Detson is also distracted by Cherry, and the match slows down until Hopper regains control off an irish-whip reversal. Hopper hits some of his usual offense, Detson tries to steal the match, but can't stop Hopper's momentum. Detson manages to duck a lariat and send Hopper over the top rope. Detson attempts a senton off the apron, but Hopper's up, and both men crash into the railing. Detson breaks the count, sends Hopper into the rail, breaks the count while playing to Cherry, and hits a nice slingshot legdrop after rolling Hopper in. Detson works the ropes off an abdominal stretch, but Hopper counters with a Hip Toss after the referee sees his tactics. Hopper applies an STF from a standing takedown, but Stryker goads him into breaking, giving Detson a chance to counter. A very good series of countermoves ensues, ending with both men falling over the top rope. Detson manages to control his fall, and as Stryker distracts Hopper by goading him, Cherry distracts the referee while Detson gets a foriegn object. FINALLY Chris Stringer notices all of this and runs to the ring, assaulting Detson. The referee manages to see this and calls for the bell as Stringer plants Detson with Solve This. Match results in a No Decision. Match Rating: **, it was slow and disjointed, but mainly because of the angles that needed to be furthered, so it did it's job. Postmatch, Stryker gets completely in Hopper's face, trying to get Hopper to hit him. Stryker reaches into the ring, snatches the foriegn object that Detson dropped, which turns out to be a bag of powder, and blinds Hopper with it, following up with a PowerStryke on the floor. Stringer berates Cherry in the ring, and we go to commercial. Ilya and the penguin as Vdare X and "King" Horton H. H. Perfect in a fatty Bouyz Meat Snax commercial. Rating: ***** Main Event: WWO International People's Championship title match - Apex (c) vs. Dark Soul: Thomas J. Smith enters for commentary, and adds nothing, as usual. Hudson and Moongirl accompany Dark Soul. It takes about fourty seconds for Apex and Dark SOul to make contact, which is a handshake. Hudson distacts Dark Soul, and Apex attacks him very aggressively, going for a pin early. Dark Soul counters a top rope attempt with a slam, and equals the aggression shown by Apex. Aerial offensive by Dark Soul leads to a SOul Crusher attempt, which is countered by Apex into an inverted DDT. Apex goes for a high-flying move, and the two men engage in a slugfest on the top rope. Apex ends up with the advantage, and a front face release superplex sets up the flying spinning heel kick, taken from Dark Soul's playbook. Dark Soul then counters a power bomb attempt into the Soul Lock. The pace here is unbelieveable. Soul Lock slows it down, but Apex probably doesn't appreciate that greatly. After a struggle, he reaches the ropes, only to have Tracy Hudson distracting the referee so as not to cause a break. Dark Soul releases the hold as soon as he sees Hudson, and starts arguing with him. Apex recovers, and knocks Dark Soul into Hudson, sending Hudson crashing to the floor in a very nasty bump. Apex ducks an attack by Dark to send him over the top, and leaps with a Somersault Legdrop Suicida... that's insane. Apex recovers, goes for the DDT on the floor, and is countered by a low blow. Hammerlock inverted DDT on the floor by Dark Soul, and an attempted spinning heel kick off the apron by Dark Soul is countered by Apex and recountered into a flying headscissors by Dark Soul. The match goes back inside. DDT, and a near fall. Double knockout spot, and Charlie gives Apex his drugs. Now THAT is a wonderful message for all the kids. I'm amazed noone's sued yet. Apex goes with the unstoppable no-sell offense, and caps it off with a Zombie Splash and a near fall. Apex is frustrated by now, and uses the chops in the corner to little effect. Series of kicks by Dark Soul leads to a Death Valley Driver(!), and Apex kicks out. Apex reverses an Irish-whip, twisting Dark Soul to send him hurtling through the ropes into the ring post. Apex uses the ringpost to work over Dark, and drapes him neck-first on the railing, to kill him dead with an elbowdrop off the top rope. Apex crashes into the crowd, but recovers again and rolls Dark Soul into the ring. Apex goes for a Tornado DDT on Dark, who should be dead, but is countered into a Soul Crusher. Apex kicks out, and if the finisher won't do it, there's gonna have to be a screwjob. Candy Malone heads to the ring, and everyone is now thinking screwjob, but perhaps not in the same way. She tries to talk Dark Soul into stopping. Dark doesn't, but a Soul Seeker is countered into the Apex DDT (he really needs to name that thing to set it apart from everyone else's DDT), and it finally ends, and cleanly. Now that's the pleasant surprise of the night... the only clean (heck, the only pinfall) finish that wasn't a squash. Match rating: ****.25, Incredible pace, but lacked the in-match psychology and consistency needed to hit *****. For a TV match, it doesn't get much better. Postmatch, Hudson throws Dark SOul from the ring, lays into Apex with chairshots, and legdrops Apex's throat onto the edge of the chair. That'd have been especially nasty if Dark Soul hadn't withstood an even worse version of that from Apex in the match. Candy jumps on Hudson to stop him, and Hudson goes to attack, when Dark Soul finally has enough and attacks Hudson, driving him out of the ring. Credits. OVERALL: Squashes and non-finishes marred the show. But again, the main event stood up and carried the day. An overall improvement from last week. **.5 --------------------------------------------------------------------------- Hot Five by Kimberly Campbell contact: [detail withheld] The WWO Hot Five is compiled for each issue of the Source by Kimberly Campbell. First off, I'm considering another change to the point system. I'm considering adding in a factor that takes into account the number of ballots a particular personality appears on. I know, I know, this is kind of silly. The simple points allocated for each vote should suffice, but, I'm afraid they don't. At the moment, a wrestler could get a number one vote from a particular voter each week. And maybe a #4 or #5 spot from a couple of other voters each week. As it stands right now, getting 5 points for a number 1 spot is allowing this wrestler to appear higher on the overall scale than I believe the majority of the Source readers would believe him/her to be. On the other hand, there are several other personalities that receive several small point value votes from numerous voters, but their totals rarely equal that of the above hypothetical wrestler. My proposed change is simple. In addition to getting the standard number of points for a vote (ie. #1 vote = 5 points, #5 vote = 1 point), each wrestler/personality/team that is mentioned frequently gets a bonus. In sticking with this article's mathematical simplicity, the bonus would apply to the top 5 wrestlers, according to # of votes, that's votes, not points. And, sticking with Hot Five Tradition, the personality with most votes would get a 5 point bonus, while the person with the 5th most votes would get a 1 point bonus. I will take the time to discuss this with some of my peers and get their thoughts on the matter.
