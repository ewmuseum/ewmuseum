WWO bio card — Michael Thunderwolf

26 May 2003

Michael Thunderwolf Wrestler Name: Michael Thunderwolf Date of Birth: Height: 6' Weight: 255 Alignment: Principled Hometown: Wounded Knee, North Dakota Entrance Music: Justice in the Barrel by Jon Bon Jovi Manager: None Physical Appearance Standard Appearance Michael Thunderwolf is of average height at 6' and has a little bulk at 255 to keep him competitive. He has a nice build with a little defination that can be seen in his arms and chest, similair to Ricky Steamboat. He has long dark brown hair that comes down to his shoulder blades and dark brown eyes. Ring Appearance Michael Thunderwolf wears a mock half size headress with bull horns coming out the sides and a wolf's skin with the face covering his, and the back going down his back, with dark feathers down along the sides. When he reaches the ring, this comes off and it is revealed that Michael Thunderwolf has black and white paint covering his whole face almost like a skull, his war mask. He wrestles barechested, with black tights and brown moccaskin boots. Psychological Makeup Strengths Wrestling knowledge. Michael is part of a large family of Native American wrestlers and his knowledge is handed down to him from the many that wrestled in his family before him from his Grandfather (Amatuer wrestling holds and counters), Father(Suplexes and slams), and many, many Uncles (The old school high-flying). Thinking wrestler. Rather than just hit highspots, Michael is a chain wrestler with the knoweldge of his family behind him. Instead of the high risk, high flying luche libre spots... he mat wrestlers (sometimes pulling out a move noone has seen in ages) and tosses in some old school high flying for a well planned attack - instead of the hit and miss strategy of the daredevils. Every move he uses has a purpose and only leads as to the next move, and to then - to its ultimate conclusion. Stamina. He can be a 60 minute man and take an hour if he has to, to wear you down the way he wants, until he is still standing and his opponent is laying on the mat sucking so much wind he causes a black hole... or he can be a Ricky Morton and take an incredible amount of punishment and still somehow - manage to survive for a surprise move, or get the energy enough to make that hot tag tag. Weaknesses Refuses to go Hardcore. Michael was taught the ways of the old and has firm beliefs. The strongest of which he will never close his hand into a fist and punch... the other - it all stays in the ring. He will never take a fight outside the ring... nor pick up a chair, or any other object to carry the fight on. If he is thrown outside the ring, he does whatever it takes to get back in the ring. Refuses to wrestle in any gimmick matches (For the weak minded) except for an Indian Strap Match (Only as last resort, or ultimate conclusion for any injustice.) Doesn't adapt well - one track mind. Michael Thunderwolf sticks to his style, and doesn't try to brawl, or highfly/highrisk against those he wrestles. He sticks to what he knows, and thats it. He trys to beat them with what he knows, and constantly tries to change the pace and style back to the way he likes, instead of adapting. As well planned out as he is, as the old saying goes well laid plans... He sometimes thinks ahead of himself, and while on a clear course for a certain action, some opponent sometimes throws a wrench in his plans, forcing him to slap on armlock... and rethink or restart his plan of attack. Pressure to succed. Coming from a long line of wrestlers and from a small, yet proud minority, Michael has the weight of the world on his shoulders to succed and do well. He feels that when he loses, he not only lets himself down, and his tag partner down, but his family and his race. He tries to be an overachiever for the recognition for himself, his family, and his race and sometimes inserts himself into battles he should not be a part of. Biographical Information Michael Thunderwolf was born into a large Native American wrestling family. His father was a wrestler, his grandfather was a wrestler and he has numerous uncles who were wrestlers. Being the only son, (He does have many sisters) he had no choice but to be a wrestler too. Quickly became the best wrestler on the reservation and soon ventured out with his family on many tours through America, Latin America, and the Carribbean to learn more. Decided he wanted to venture out, and chose another name... so as not to bring shame upon his family if he lost, also deciding to wear a facepainted warmask for the same reason, vowing never to fully reveal himself until he felt he was worthy enough for his proper name. As he toured the midwest, Michael Thunderwolf came across Texas and soon ran into a pack of wrestlers calling themselves the James Gang. Taking a strong hatred of him because he was a Native American, they soon made themselves a thorn in his side at first just taunting him from ringside, he soon found them injecting themselves into his matches and finally... one day they attacked him after a match and ultimately insulted him by whipping him with a belt and trying to hang him with a rope. Michael later challenged the James Gang, and they chose Jake to teach him a lesson. After defeating Jake Thomas rather easily he quickly left the ring to avoid any further confrontation. After being curious as to why they did not follow their harrasment, Michael looked back to the ring moments later, only to find that they were beating Jake Thomas to abloody pulp. His good nature, sense of justice and morals sent him back to the ring to fend of Jakes attackers, and saving him from permanent injury. They became friends and soon after tag team partners. Michael took Jake under his wing and began teaching him what he could, and vowing with him, revenge against the James Gang. Jake was a quick study, and feeling conident enough, Michael and Jack then went on to beat the James Gang in various tag matches, and finally took the Texas Tag titles from the James Gang to complete their vengeance. Seeing the potential in the tag team, Michael convinced Jake to take to the road with him, and find larger prizes than the Texas Tag titles. Jake agreed, wanting to see the world and sticking with his friend, Michael in hopes of learning more from him and Michael found someone he could confide in and help pass on the wrestling knowledge that he had. Wrestling Information Wrestling Styles/Grades Daredevil Poor High-Flier Very Good Martial Arts Good Power Poor Technical Good Submission Very Good Cheating None Brawling/Hardcore None Escapability Fair Stamina Excellent Ring Awareness Fair Teamwork Excellent Top Wrestling Moves Chops. And lots of 'em. Rather than punch, Michael Thunderwolf chops. Someone starts slugging him, he fights back with the chops. If he is caught in a corner, Michael tries to chop his way out of it. Sometimes he'll whip his opponent to the ropes and lay out a big chop across the chest to take him off his feet... sometimes Michael just goes off on some poor guy with chops like he's a log, and cuts him down to size with a series of chops starting criss cross across the chest, criss cross across the head... then he lays him out with a wardance and a big chop, right across the top of the head. Armdrags galore. A person can really rack up the frequent flier miles when he's wrestling Michael Thunderwolf. Thunderwolf loves to keep an opponent off his feet and confused by armdrags. His favorite are the deep and flying ones, Steamboat style. He mixes it up quite often though. After hitting two of the previous armdrags, he'll usually follow it up with a Japanese armdrag, and finally a Mexcican Tabletop armdrag, which he then keeps a hold of the arm of, usually right into a Fujiwara armbar, then proceeds to abuse the hell out of it. Dropkicks. Michael Thunderwolf liberally peppers his matches with some dropkicks, to keep the opponent on his back and loosen a few teeth. When he really wants to kick it up a notch (BAM!), he does a backflip dropkick to level the opponent and wow the crowd. When finding it difficult to get someone down on the mat, he does a sliding dropkick to the knee. Flying, rolling side headlock takeover. Michael Thunderwolf loves to slap on the headlock, and run up the corner, bounce off the ropes and flip the opponent over with a headlock to keep his opponents head spinning. 99% of the time, they try to get back up to their feet and gain their sense, to which he obliges them with another flying, rolling side headlock. Sometimes he mixes it up with a flying, rolling head scissiors. Michael Thunderwolf sometimes gets the opponents around the neck by his feet and sends them flipping with the flying, rolling headscissors. Now and then, as a change of pace he'll do the victory roll, or grab hold of the corner ropes behind him, and swing his body down, and while still having the opponent's head locked with his feet., slam the head into the corner. Elbowdrops and legdrop combo. Nothing fancy about how he does it, its just the way he does it. Michael Thunderwolf will get an opponent flat on his back and will just lay out 3 of the lightning quickest elbowdrops you ever saw, then does a standing, leaping legdrop that gets some major height to really knock the opponent for a loop. Leglocks. Michael Thunderwolf likes to use the Indian deathlock, or the Bow and Arrow leglock/chinlock to weaken up the opponent and bend them all out of shape. Sometimes he'll do the STF too when he really feels like folding someone in half. Armlocks/bars. He loves to take the arm, chopping the living hell out of the shoulder, twisting it like crazy, yanks on it like a pitbull, chopping it some more, then jumping up on your arm, and forcing you down with his full body weight, and pulling at that arm while it's in a Fujiwara armbar or hammerlock like a junkyard dog jaw-locked on his favorite bone. When he's had his fun, sometimes he'll tease the opponent by letting up for just a second, letting him get to his feet, and he flips them over with a monkeyflip, still holding the arm, and going back to the hold he had on before. Cross body block. As offense, when he tosses someone else to the ropes or defense, when he gets tossed to the ropes the cross body block is an old standby. Claw hold - Often, just to add more injury - Michael Thunderwolf slaps on a claw hold. Now and then, just out of nowhere, he does the Indian claw to someones face, but more than likely its when he has an armlock - he slaps the claw onto the shoulder (almost nervehold like, but not quite). When he has someone in abdominal stretch, he does the claw to the side of the ribs. When it's some leg lock or when he has an STF on, he'll use a claw, or knuckle noogie. Rolling Moon Suplexes. Michael Thunderwolf takes someone in a waistlock belly to back suplex, hits 'em with it, rolls back up to his feet, the opponent still in the waistlock and nails the move 2 more times, then after the 3rd suplex - when he pulls them back up, he spins them around - and hits a belly to belly suplex. Finishing Maneuver Thundering Tomahawk Flying Legdrop off the top rope Tag Team Information Tag Team Name: Blood Brothers Tag Team Partner: "Thunderwolf" Jake Thomas Tag Team Moves Michael Thunderwolf tags Jake Thomas in and they double whip an opponent to the ropes, as he comes off, they both launch him in the air, as he comes down, they both grab an arm, and do a double side shoulder breaker/flapjack. Jake Thomas hooks the leg for a pin and Michael Thunderwolf guards the save with a dropkick. Michael Thunderwolf tags Jake Thomas in, they both grab seperate legs of their opponents and left him up for a double face first powerbomb, then pull him back up, to double wheelbarrel suplex him backwards. Jake Thomas hooks the leg for the pin, and Michael Thunderwolf guards the save with a dropkick. Jake Thomas tags Michael Thunderwolf in and backslides his opponent as Michael Thunderwolf comes in with a top rope legdrop/kneedrop. Michael Thunderwolf hooks the leg for the pin, and Jake Thomas guards the save with an elbowsmash. Jake Thomas tags Michael Thunderwolf in, whips him to the ropes and press slams him onto the opponent. Michael Thunderwolf tags in Jake Thomas and whips the opponent into the ropes for a Samoan Drop, Jake follows up with a flying fistdrop off the 2nd rope. Tag Team Finisher Thundering Stampede It's a combo of both their own finishing moves. Jake Thomas tags in Michael Thunderwolf and does his facefirst powerslam into the corner, than the middle of the ring and Michael Thunderwolf comes off the top rope with a flying legdrop across the back of his opponent's head. Michael Thunderwolf hooks the leg for the pin and Jake Thomas guards for the save with an elbowsmash.
