Defined as 1) Lack of emotion or feeling. 2) Indifference. Does anyone realize what this word means? Sometimes I wonder. Either that or I'm totally ignored as a journalist and an idea man. Let me explain what I mean. This sport is filled with "apathy" in my opinion. You see for the past couple of weeks, I've tried to bring to the forefront that professional wrestling has had some serious credibility issues. And in the process, I've come to the conclusion that people simply don't care. As long as their world is precisely "their" world, do what you want to the rest of it, but leave our portion of this sport alone. Let me make it clear... folks, we are a sport. Or at least that's what we claim to be. But you'd never know it seeing all the stuff that goes on. We see guys wrestling 7 days a week, twice a night in organizations half a world apart, and nobody complains. Why? Because nobody cares. Hell, I mean, why should they? They get to be the "big man" in their respective organizations, despite the impossibility of it all. Why kill their golden-egg laying goose? Simply put, they don't want it repaired. Because if it's repaired, they're going to be forced to come to the realization that if they concentrate their efforts, they're not going to be able to do it. Like I said.. Apathy. They don't think it's broke, and they don't want it fixed. I put my idea to a few very knowledgable people, including wrestlers, federation presidents, and professional wrestling fans, as well as those of you who are regularly readers of the Source. And you want to know what I was told? "It's a great idea, but it'll never work." I'm truly grateful Edison, Franklin, Benz, and other great inventors never listened to those kind of comments. Because if they did, we'd all be sitting around in the dark, wishing we could turn something on, or wishing we could actually travel hundreds, if not thousands of miles away. But 'it'll never work'. And you know why? Oh, I got a few reasons that were given to me. In fact, let's take a look at the top three given to me. 1) You'd need a huge bureacracy to make it work. Recently I came across a boxing league that is "enormous" in scope. ( http://www.vivi.com ). Yes, first it's a single league that consists of smaller regional units, but it puts limits on what their boxers can do. Why? Because they know they need some sort of controls to insure their league has a real-life flavor to it. Yes, it varies from our sport, because there is only one "top man" at any given time, world wide. But everyone knows that they're one small part of the whole. Something that professional wrestlers refuse to admit. "There's us, and nobody else, and nobody can make me think otherwise". Guess what folks, it's not 'nobody else', despite what you think. Some federations even recognize other federations in this sport. But hell, that doesn't matter. When it comes to the wrestlers, it's _always_ us and us only. Wake up boys... it's not that way, and you can't pretend that it is. Hell, I hated watching the political wars between the UWF and EMWC drag one another down, but they at least admitted to the above fact. Hell, if it was just "you and nobody else", you _can't_ have great supercards during the summer, Master of the Ring, Wrestlebowl, Weekend to Remember, Battle of Britain, and many of the other great shows we see put out. Would it take some bureaucracy to make the whole system work? Absolutley. But people see that "b" word and worry about total control, instead of working with the system outside of umbrella organizations. They're afraid that someone's going to try to step on their personal control of their careers. All because of a "bureaucracy". Gentlemen, right now there is no control, there is no "career". It's a free-for-all. It's not professional wrestling. It's a world of "I can do what I want and you can't stop me". And that only hurts this sport as a whole. 2) "But nobody considers professional wrestling real in the first place." Guess what. There's a reason for that. We don't make it real. We let people fight six times a night, when it's physically impossible for the human body to do it. In other sports leagues you see boxers only fight about once a week. The "non-NFL affiliated" football leagues don't have the same players in multiple leagues. In "non-MLB affiliated" baseball leagues, we don't see the same players hitting homeruns in two different parks in two entirely different cities. Only in professional wrestling. Others don't consider it a sport, because we _don't_ treat it as one ourselves, and we're in it! 3) Timing Federations don't put shows out on time. So how can you enforce such a rule or rules when things are non-static? You know, this is the closest that could come to a valid argument... when you don't know what days various shows are going to occur on due to various production delays. But unlike others, some of us are willing to accept the fact that some shows are tape-delayed. Just because an announcer says that a show is coming to you "live" from any given arena, doesn't mean it has to be. Hell, anyone wanna know when that Thursday night show is recorded? One hint, it's not Thursday, but the announcers aren't going to say that, now are they? But because we "have" to go live with our broadcast, at least according to many believers, timing alone makes these things an impossibility. Which is a shame, because we're unwilling to accept "production" problems, this sport will never be accepted as a "sport". In other words, the whole idea "is a great idea, but it'll never happen"... Simply because nobody seems to want it to happen. "It's not broke... " Yet, despite that fact, we ask why doesn't our sport get credibility? Guess what ladies and gentlemen, it's not credible? It's broken.. and it needs to be fixed, and some simple fixes would be a nice start. Who knows, perhaps that guy up in Connecticut was right, when he said it was never about being a "Sport". It's about the "sports entertainment", and screw the reality of it all.
