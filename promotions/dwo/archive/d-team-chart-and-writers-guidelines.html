Writers Guidelines 1. Description: This is a very important element in writing, you have to make it feel like the person is right there in the action. Describe things that no one else would really think of, the expressions, the emotions, the clothing, the feeling of the entire action taking place. Think of what it looks like when a WWE wrestler is inside of the ring or fighting someone outside of the ring. Take what you would expect to see from a real match and add it in to your writing to make things more realistic for the readers. 2. Audience: In every show there will be an audience, and audiences never just sit around and do nothing. They are alive, jeering, cheering, making rude comments. Whatever it is that an audience would do during something happening that involves their opinions or views should indeed involve them. If a heel is making an entrance then make sure he/she gets the right kind of audience heat. If a face is coming down and people like him then make sure he/she gets the right reactions. Think of if someone you hated on wrestling was coming out, how would you and your friends react? The same with someone you liked. 3. Referees: These officials do exist in the match, and no not just to count. They are there to regulate the match and they add to the match overall. Nothing is more exciting then when a referee gets involved in the match up, it adds color to the match. Don’t just make them pop up when it is time to count, add them in to the match during the whole thing. 4. Entrances: We have a lot of problems with this usually.. You can’t just have a wrestler or wrestlers be in the ring automatically. You have to announce them and do their intro, with music and so on. If anything at all at least have their theme song, and the announcer announcing them. 5. Commentators: Mike and Jay, yes these two crazy characters play a huge role in matches. Instead of huge blocks of action text have them come in with comments and so on, instead of showing the move full out have Mike or Jay freak out and be like “Oh My God! Neckbreaker!! Ahh!” Something to entertain the reader while they are going over the match. These two are somewhat bisexual, even suggestive with one another in a hilarious sort of way. They are always arguing, and often switch from heel to face depending on the matches. They rarely agree on anything and almost all the time beat on one another. Add them in, make some comedy! 6. Estimating time: At the end of each match, and this is new of course, I would like it if you would estimate how long it took for the match to be completed. This is from beginning, bell sounding, to end, bell sounding. Think about if you were really watching it inside of the ring at home on your television, then estimate a time. Be realistic, most low card matches don’t go over three minutes, middle matches range from four minutes to six minutes and high cards rarely exceed 12 minutes, unless it is something like a pay-per-view main event. 7. Stating Winners: Before you place the time estimation at the end of your match I would like if you would clarify who won the match. Then after that put the estimation of time, this way if someone is in a rush and can’t read the entire match they can see at least who won and in what time frame. This helps out a lot. 8. Appropriate move patterns: Don’t have Brawlers flying off turnbuckles, aerialists big booting people, or anything else of that odd nature. Moves are to be placed in a believable order, you can’t have a wrestler do a clothesline and then immediately powerbomb the same person. Create a pattern, have them clothesline the person, stand them up, or pick them up some way and then powerbomb them. This all goes with the description ideals in rule #1. Just make sure you can indeed to the moves in the order that you put them in, if you can’t then it looks pretty ridiculous in the end. 9. Spell check: There is nothing more than a person hates than having to go back and rewrite paragraph after paragraph because wrestlers names are spelt wrong, or there are too many grammar and spelling mistakes. You should be old enough to understand how to punctuate, spell, and put words together. Remember - All matches and events are in present tense, meaning that moves are to be like this ‘He scoops so and so up and lands a hard powerbomb in the center of the ring.’ All present tense. Just make sure to take your work in to Word or something like that and spell check it, grammar run it, and so on. Allow all mistakes to be fixed and then post it as done. 10. Posting places and times: Each match you are assigned is to be completed and turned in one day before the event is to take place. This gives me the time to put the entire show together and place any in-carders, segments, or interviews that are turned in to me in to the proper places. If we can do this timing and turn ins properly we will have an on time Travesty and Pay-Per-View each week/month. Match assignments are to be turned in underneath the main post I will make each time the card goes up. Label your matches so I know which is which and don’t have to search through everything. -That is all of the writers guidelines for now, I feel these pretty much cover everything that could be considered. If you have anything you would like to add to this list then please get to me about it and it shall be added. I expect these guidelines to the followed because they will overall help our shows greatly. Below is a list of the official names and the main DWO workers you should keep in mind while writing. Thank you.
