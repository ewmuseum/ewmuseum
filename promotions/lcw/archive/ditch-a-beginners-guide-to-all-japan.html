A Beginner's Guide to All Japan

2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A Beginner's Guide to All Japan: All Japan is a company with no angles, no gimmicks, no real gimmick matches, and matches which are only rarely less than ten minutes in length. So, for those of you who only watch wrestling for the interviews, TURN BACK NOW. All Japan is known for having great heavyweight matches which have a similar feel to boxing, featuring tons of nearfalls, high-angle 'head drop' suplexes, and incredibly stiff blows to the head. Also, the matches are chock full of counters, reversals, and fighting over moves. The pace is slower than what we get on Raw, but once you get used to it it tends not to be a problem. For those of you who consider Chris Benoit their favorite wrestler, this is a goldmine! And, for those of you who are still a little leery, it's at least worth checking out to see something different than the norm. Ten Names to Remember: Misawa- The 'Hogan' of All Japan, so to speak. While he does do stuff like selling, bumping, and realistic offense, he hardly ever loses... especially to those who deserve it most. Still, he's one of the greatest workers in wrestling history, and has all kinds of great matches over the course of the 90's. Kawada- He's in a league of his own. Misawa's arch-nemesis, Kawada's primary offense is kicking people really hard. If you thought Tajiri was stiff, you haven't seen a thing. Kawada is very unselfish in terms of putting others over, and is putting on better average matches today than anyone on this list, despite having been wrestling for over 20 years! Kobashi- A great wrestler, who rounds out the 'big 3' of All Japan along with Kawada and Misawa. He's got more charisma than the others, has a very deep moveset, and has altered his style over the years such that he remains fresher than most. Sadly, years of moonsaults have ruined his knees, and he last wrestled at the end of 2000. More on that later. Akiyama- Much like X-Pac, he plays the "young punk" even after years in the business. Unlike X-Pac, he's fun to watch. While he hasn't had the epic battles of the 'big 3', he's still got all the important characteristics of a quality All Japan wrestler. He really came into his own around '98, and is now a main-eventer. Taue- His main accomplishment is being Kawada's tag partner in a series of stellar matches against Misawa and Kobashi. At 6'5", he's huge for Japan. However, he's also very awkward, and isn't that strong. His main weapon is a chokeslam, but not a very good one. "Doctor Death" Steve Williams- Resident badass, Steve isn't exactly Mr. Workrate. He's a very solid wrestler, and his finisher in Japan is among the nastiest in wrestling history. Vader- Though he didn't even wrestle in All Japan for 2 years, the time he spent there was remarkable. He came in after being a WWF jobber, and made an immediate impact. His matches defy all kinds of physical laws, as not only does he deliver some of the biggest suplexes you'll ever see, but he's able to take some big moves himself! Kikuchi- He doesn't have many matches, but they're all fun to watch, as he takes absolutely hellacious beatings, generally at the hands of Fuchi. Johnny Ace- Brother of Road Warrior Animal, and a current WWF booker. He's faster than his chubbiness would have you believe, and he invented the move that has since evolved into the Stunner, Diamond Cutter, Twist of Fate, and 3-D. Stan "The Lariat" Hansen- One of the relics floating around the company in the 90's, Stan is fat, slow, and much too old-school for anyone born after the Ford administration. The Time Periods: 1990-1992- The 'big three' slowly come into the picture, and replace the old guard led by Jumo Tsurura. 1993-1995- Misawa becomes the undisputed top dog in the promotion. He and Kawada wage an awesome war with each other in singles and tag matches. '93 sees all kinds of great action throughout the year, including some fun stuff with a very young RVD. 1996-1998- Hansen and Williams are largely phased out of the main event picture. Taue has a run in '96, but fortunately after that he stops getting much of a push. Kobashi really kicks it up a notch in '97, and Akiyama does the same in '98. 1999-2000- Vader comes in, and everything changes. Matches actually get faster, with main events dropping from well over thirty minutes and loaded with restholds to a more Americanized twenty minutes of action. This is probably more palatable to those of you only used to Raw matches. In 1999, Giant Baba, the owner and founder of the company, died of liver failure. Misawa took over as the booker, while Baba's wife assumed ownership. In 2000, Misawa and Mrs. Baba clashed over the direction of the company. Misawa left, and took pretty much everyone with him except for Kawada and Williams, and formed NOAH. Johnny Ace left for WCW, where he helped book matches right up to the end. Neither All Japan nor NOAH has ever managed to regain the great spark that All Japan had from 1993 to 2000. Tournament Time: Every year, All Japan holds two round-robin tournaments (meaning, everyone in the tournament fights everyone else, and each match is worth points). One in spring is a singles' tournament called the Champions Carnival, and one in winter is with tag teams and is called the Tag League. While they rarely provide all-time great matches, they also allow for matchups that might not normally be seen. If I use the words "CC" or "TL", they are abbreviations for matches occuring in the respective tournaments. Ten Matchups To Look For: Misawa vs Kawada- The most heated feud from All Japan. Average match sees Misawa get the holy hell beaten out of him in the form of head drops and kicks to the face, only to make a comeback and win. This pattern was altered in '98 thankfully. Highlights include their 6/3/94 match, which many consider the best singles' match in Japanese history; their CC '95 match, where Misawa has his left orbital bone (that's in the face) broken, then he keeps wrestling; their 1/22/99 match, where Kawada breaks his arm, but keeps wrestling and delivers the awesome Gansobomb spot. Kawada vs Kobashi- Not flawless from a technical standpoint, Kawada and Kobashi use stiff blow after stiff blow to batter each other. A give-and-take series, they reach two 60-minute draws. Highlight is their 6/12/98 match. Misawa vs Kobashi- The pinnacle of All Japan singles' matches, in my opinion. They trade the biggest head drops that they can muster, and often have very long finishes trading nearfalls between them. Highlights include the 1/20/97, 10/21/97, and 6/11/99 matches, which build on one another in terms of what is needed to end the match. Also, two of their tag matches feature Kobashi doing the rare Burning Hammer move, which is among the best head drop moves ever created. Misawa/Kobashi vs Kawada/Taue- The greatest tag rivalry ever. One important match in '93, one in '94, and then several in '95. Main highlight is the 6/9/95 match, which is considered the greatest tag match ever by many, including me. Misawa/Akiyama vs Kawada/Taue- An extension of the above. The 12/6/96 match is spectacular, in the same vein as the 6/9/95 match from above. Kobashi vs Akiyama- These two bring out the best in each other. Starting with their CC matches '97, they built with matches in CC '98 and '99, one on 7/24/98, and climaxing at the NOAH 'Great Voyage' show at the end of 2000, which is Kobashi's last match to this point. Fuchi vs Kikuchi- I don't have many of their matches, but what ones I do have are killer. Not necessarily all-time great, they always feature Kikuchi taking all sorts of insane stretch holds and drops on the noggin, whether it be one-on-one or in tags. Vader vs Misawa- Vader beats the crap out of Misawa, but Misawa pulls off good offense of his own. Fun, and packed into an under 20 minute timeframe. Shinzaki and Hayabusa vs All Japan- While these two hail from other federations, their time spent in All Japan was memorable. The main highlights are from the TL '97, in which they fight valiantly against the best All Japan can muster. Williams vs Kobashi- Kobashi has taken several of Williams' all-time best "Homicidal Backdrop Drivers", the raw brutality of which requires the full screen of a television and a good slow-mo button to appreciate. These occur in their 8/31/93 and 9/3/94 matches.
