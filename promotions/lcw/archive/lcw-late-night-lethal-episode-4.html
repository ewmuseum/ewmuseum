Late Night Lethal Late Night Lethal Episode 4 Written for the Screen (But not Really) By: [name withheld] Interview Responses Provided By: Dave Ditch Interviews: Harbinger and TR Saxton PRE-OPENING-SEGMENT: We are treated at the opening to this new episode of Late Night Lethal with a black screen for a few moments, and the faint sound of tape hiss in the background. Just as people are about to start wondering exactly what the blue hell is going on and why there's a black screen instead of something worth looking at, some words begin to scroll up from the bottom. Plain white text, nothing fancy, and while they're scrolling and hopefully being read by the audience, they are also being announced by a very serious-sounding Sacapuntas Jones (our announcer for those of you who have forgotten. You spiteful bastards.) Sacapuntas (speaking the text): Every once in a while, a television event comes along that gives one pause. Collectively, we avert our eyes from other, less worthy programming. The unforgivable mess of a sitcom that Jenna Elfman stars in; the program depicting an upsetting Australian shouting things like "awesome!" and "but be careful, they BITE!" with reckless Aussie abandon…these things no longer hold our attention in favor of these purer, intrinsically better moments in television history. A brief pause in both voice and text, and then the words and the disembodied voice (which isn't really disembodied, if you think about it; we're just not SEEING the body it is attached to) continues. Sacapuntas: It is these moments that define American culture. When we all put down our Hungry Man Dinners, our Sun Chips, or our small, furry animals that we found on the side of the road, and watch together, as one large family. Tonight is one such occasion. Here and now, history is being made. Let us come together for this; to forget, if even for a moment, that these are trying times for the entertainment industry; times where Keanu Reeves is still free to make films. Times when a film which depicts a greasy, throaty gypsy bastardizing "Roxanne" by the Police can be nominated to receive "Best Picture" at the Oscars. Times when nobody bats an eye at lyrical deficiencies such as "I'm not a girl/not yet a woman" or "My breasts are small and humble/so you won't confuse them with mountains," or, perhaps worst of all, "Hey baby/hey baby/hey/(boys say, boys say)." Another brief pause, while the audience wonders how long the writers planned to allow them to stare at a blank screen. One might be reminded of the opening segments to the Star Wars movies, and how even they got dreary and long-winded after awhile. But worry not; we're winding down. You might want to take a moment to think about whether or not you recognize any of the above lyrics, and what a sad human being it makes you if you actually like the songs from which they've emerged like demons from the mouth of blackest hell. (Note: The opinions expressed by Late Night Lethal do not necessarily reflect those of the performers, writers, or anybody else, fictional or otherwise. You sad excuse for a living creature.) Sacapuntas: Ladies and gentlemen, we are on the brink of a cultural moment which will echo in the hearts of mankind for generations. We at Guy With A Hose Studios are livid with proudness, alive with pleasure in presenting: Late Night Lethal… Large, dramatic pause. EPISODE FOUR. OPENING SEGMENT: Camera fades in to the New York Cityscape in the early evening, with a jazzy, brassy opening tune in the background; the camera pans across it as the announcer speaks. When he mentions the show's name, the show's logo appears superimposed over the city. Sacapuntas: Live from Guy With A Hose Studios in New York City, it's Lethal Championship Wrestling's Late Night Lethal with Steve Lieberman! Tonight's guests: A picture of one Harbinger meanders up onto the screen. Sacapuntas: Harbinger! Harbinger is replaced with TR Saxton. Sacapuntas: One good turn deserves…TR SAXTON! This picture replaced by one of five disheveled-looking British guys. Sacapuntas: Musical guest: Radiohead! This picture replaced by a picture of six huge guys holding various musical instruments. Sacapuntas: Featuring: The Swesley B. Sweet band! This replaced by a photo of Ashley Mendel, the co-host. Sacapuntas: Ashley Mendel! Camera cuts to the inside of the studio; a large audience is seen applauding. Some of them are holding up signs, which contain such interesting phrases as: "I can't wait until the spiders eat you!", "Captain Homeless Drunk RULES!" and "Your weapons are useless against me!" Upon the stage is the traditional late-night fare: a big desk with a nice comfy chair in back of it, with the ever-necessary mug and small pile of papers set upon it. Next to that, the guest/co-host couch, where Mendel is already seated. In back is the giant glass window with a fake skyline in back; cardboard apartment buildings with little lights in the little windows, and the like. To the right of this (from the camera's perspective), we see the Swesley B. Sweet Band playing the aforementioned jazzy opening tune. Sacapuntas: And I'm your friendly, harmless, only-arrested-once-for-indecent-exposure announcer, Sacapuntas Jones! And now, your host, the man who just wants to hold you, make you feel safe, treat you right, and then FREAK YOU WILD! Ladies and gentlemen, THIRD…NIPPLEMAN! Steve, a moderately tall man with medium build and a relative tower of black hair, comes running out from backstage, and stands to the left of the main stage, in a large vacant area. The lights dim for the rest of the stage, obscuring Mendel and the Swes band in relative darkness. A spotlight comes up to hit Steve where he stands. The Swes B. drummer brings the opening song to a close with some flashy rolls and cymbal crashes, culminating in the typical final crash. The applause dies down and Steve begins to speak. Steve: Oh my God, WE'RE BACK! The audience launches into applause and cheers at this. One might find it odd, and wonder where all these people so invested in a talk show devoted solely to professional wrestling came from. One might also do well to keep his mouth shut, if he knows what's good for him. Steve extends his arms, first soaking in the applause, and then trying to cut off the audience by making those "cut" gestures with his arms. Eventually this works. Steve: I know, I know, I'm glad to see you too. Random Audience Member: I LOVE YOU STEVE! Giggles from the audience in response while Steve raises an eyebrow in mild surprise. Steve: Later…later… Steve looks off into the distance, seemingly enraptured at least for the moment, at the promise of audience-member-love to come. Then he shakes it off and looks back to the camera. Steve: But we must not neglect the present moment. Now, I know a lot of you are wondering just where the hell we've been. Well let me tell you, it hasn't been easy. 'course, you all know that we've been gone more or less since the big Evo-Mercury split that happened way back last year. In fact our last show was all the way back in July, if you can believe that. Steve shrugs his shoulders, a half-assed apology to the viewers. Steve: I know, we're bastards. But let me tell you, I worked like mad trying to get this show back together. Mendel in particular was difficult to find. I checked every single Arby's in the western hemisphere. Which is a LOT, in case you were wondering. I finally found him perched on top of one of the signs, leaning over the edge of the big neon hat, vomiting into his own shoes. The audience, as are the rest of us, is clearly disgusted by the mental imagery. Steve: Well, enough of that then. Suffice to say, we're back, and we're STAYING, my friends! For God's sake, LOVE ME! More cheers. For some reason the audience is just one big mark for Lieberman. Steve: And we've got one hell of a great show for you, too. We've got Harbinger! We've got TR Saxton! And sweet lordy mama but we've got RADIOHEAD! As he's saying these names, the audience cheers. As well as it should. Especially for Radiohead. God they rule. I mean, I know I'm just stage direction, there to create more of a visual image to accompany the dialogue, but even I, as an intangible, non-sentient being, can't get over how much Radiohead rules. Steve: But folks, there's somebody else here that I haven't told you about. And no, it's not the old man from the first couple episodes. Disappointment from the audience. Steve: I know, I know. We're all sad he's not here right now. But he'll be back, I assure you. In any case, why not follow me on over to my desk, we'll say hi to Mendel, we'll say hi to Swesley B., and we'll break out our surprise guest right away! Steve wanders over to his desk, where a light goes up, revealing Mendel already seated at his chair next to it. The band plays Steve over to the desk, and the audience pops respectably for Mendel as the two men shake hands. Mendel: …vomiting into my own shoes? Steve: Don't deny it. Mendel: I was returning a canoe paddle. Steve: To Arby's? Mendel: …I was drunk. But never mind that. Who've you got for us today, Steve? You didn't even tell me. Steve: Did you have a receipt? Mendel: Just get on with it, ok? The camera cuts to the Swes band, where Swesley B. is looking just as angry as ever. Swes: Quit holdin' out on us, you slice o' s**t. Who the f**k you bring? For a moment, Steve looks very solemn, his eyes lowered to his desk. He pauses like that for just a moment, and then a small smile begins to creep out onto his face. He raises his eyes back to the camera, his smile becomes a huge grin, and he shouts: Steve: Ladies and gentlemen, the one, the ONLY…PRINCE OF SPACE!!!! SURPRISE INTERVIEW: "THE RETURN OF PRINCE OF SPACE": Before we have time to wonder how that guy with the sign in the opening sequence knew about it, Prince of Space is out on stage and the audience is going absolutely crazy. He is in full PoS outfit: white thermal underwear with matching gloves, a cape that drops to the small of his back; no more, no less. He has a (also white) helmet, the sides of which have two wooden blocks attached where his ears might be, each with a nail hammered squarely into the center. His face is partially obscured by a white cardboard mask that goes down and over his nose. Eyeholes have been cut out. A belt made of some sort of black felt material is wrapped around his waist and covered with large costume jewelry. Completing the ensemble are a black sash across his chest with what appears to be a bar of soap mounted at the center, and white, admittedly comfortable looking boots that reach the shin. He also looks monumentally confused, and at the sight of the audience he removes his trusty grill lighter from inside a fold in his tiny cape and begins struggling with it, pointing it at various people in the audience. Getting panicky, he begins skipping (that's really the only way one could describe it) back and forth across the stage, his arms flailing frantically while the grill lighter is still tightly clutched in his right hand. His mike has not been turned on yet, but those of us with lip reading skills can clearly make out the words "QUIET DOWN!" being repeated over and over again. Seeing that PoS is disoriented by the crowd and perhaps the hot and bright lights of the studio, Steve goes over to help him. At first PoS reacts as though to hit Steve, but rather quickly concedes to be led to the chair that Mendel had, until recently, been occupying (because obviously they're not gonna SHARE it. Mendel moved down to the next one. As well he should.) A close-up reveals that PoS has been crying a little. We have to wait a fair amount of time for the applause to die down. PoS: I had a very good reason for not killing you now! Steve: I know you did, my friend. And believe me, I'm more than grateful that you didn't. And let me just say what an honor it is to meet you. PoS: You don't know who I am! It is enough that I know you. You are Phantom of Krankor. You want to destroy the whole world. I won't let you go through with it! Steve: I DO know who you are. You're the Prince of Space, and you're pretty well known around here, too. I mean, who could forget that memorable two-part series you did with David Chambers and Aaron Antioch? PoS looks confused. PoS: …Anti….ock? Steve: Yeah, you know, Antioch. Lost? The Asylum? Any of that ring a bell? PoS: Uh… Just then, a man suddenly rushes out from backstage. Perhaps dressed even more unusually than PoS himself. THIS guy is dressed in a bright yellow bodysuit that has a hood that comes up around his face, pointed on top like a dunce cap. On top of it is a small antenna. He is also wearing a dark cape and boots. One thing he definitely is NOT wearing: underpants. In fact, those unfortunate enough to be in the audience are subjected to a VERY clearly outlined batch. Those watching at home will thank their lucky stars for that little blurry spot. The oddest thing about this man, though, is his face. A large mustache crosses it; in fact extends beyond the hood that covers all but his face. He is possessed of a very, very large nose, but upon closer inspection we can rather clearly see that it is made of wax and therefore very, very fake. He walks towards Prince of Space, who has risen out of his chair and is also walking towards this new arrival. The crowd is confused. So is Lieberman. Mendel looks…well, vaguely hungry. Strange Man: Who are you? Tell me! What's your name?! PoS: I AM PRINCE OF SPACE! And you are Phantom of Krankor! Phantom: …oh, yeah. Phantom shakes his head briefly, as if to wake himself up. Phantom: Prince of Space! You're still alive, are you! Show yourself! Otherwise, we're gonna kill some diffrin! Steve and Mendel just look at each other. Steve: Kill some diffrin? What the…? Mendel: I think he meant to say "children." Steve: What makes you think that? Mendel: I don't know. I hear a little voice. Steve: Does yours tell you to kill things? Mendel: No. PoS: QUIET DOWN! The audience, silent up until this point, cheers wildly as PoS uses the phrase that won over the hearts of…at least a good half dozen. PoS: I hear you! I'm not running away! No, I wanna stay because I want to prevent you from getting Dr. Makken's rocket fuel! Steve: I wonder who this Makken guy is. If he even exists. Mendel: Didn't he play football? Steve: No, that's Madden. And he wasn't a doctor. Mendel: You say that NOW. Steve looks at his co-host. Mendel: I mean…you did. You did say that now. Steve: Are you sure you're not drunk NOW? Mendel: I don't remember implying I wasn't. PoS: QUIET DOWN! Phantom: That's right! You're no match for my Thorian bombs! PoS laughs. PoS: Your weapons are useless against me! Suddenly, both Steve and Mendel, as well as every member of the Swesley B. Sweet band, pull out what initially look like very realistic guns and point them at Prince of Space. PoS: YOUR WEAPONS ARE USELESS AGAINST ME! Dejected, they all drop their weapons to the ground (which are, in fact, squirt guns. Mendel's even cracks open as it hits the floor, forming a puddle on the stage. Steve: Where'd you get that water? It smells funny. Mendel smiles proudly. Mendel: Made it myself. Steve: …oh, sweet Jesus. Phantom: Be silent. Each of you will enter a space capsule. Steve puts a hand to his ear. Steve: What? Phantom: Each of you will enter a space capsule. Mendel walks up to an audience member in the front row and puts his hand over the man's ear. Mendel: What? Phantom: EACH OF YOU WILL ENTER A SPACE CAPSULE! The camera cuts to a small room, where seated on a stool in the center is the old man from previous episodes. Old Man: What? Cut back to the stage, where Phantom is obviously quite angry. Phantom: Never mind! You scum! Steve and Mendel look at each other. Steve: Where'd that come from? Steve shrugs. Camera cuts back to the old man. Old Man: The ancient name of the dark planet… Cut back to the stage. Mendel: What the hell is going on here?! I think…I think…I THINK I'M FREAKING OUT! The camera slowly fades while we hear PoS and Phantom bickering in the background, to a small room with a large bed. We start with a close-up of Mendel tossing and turning, mumbling something unintelligible. Then he sits up with a start and yells. The camera pulls back a little bit and we see Lieberman next to him in the bed, looking to him with concern. Steve: What's wrong, Ashley? Mendel: Oh…Steve…it was horrible. The Prince of Space was on the show. And then the Phantom of Krankor showed up. And then there were lots of random cuts to that old guy in a little room. And it was just this terrible contrived rehash of something that wasn't that funny to begin with, the kind of thing a guy would only write into the show if he was really desperate to fill space…and we had to sit there and watch these two guys in weird suits bicker with each other and not really accomplish anything and…and… Mendel breaks down into sobs for a moment. Steve lays a hand on his shoulder to comfort him. Abruptly the sobbing stops. Mendel: What are you doing here? Steve: I got lonely. Mendel: You're not…naked under there, are you? Steve: No. Mendel: Oh, thank God. The sobbing begins anew and Steve continues comforting him. Steve: Don't worry, Ashley. It was all a dream. Just a dream. Suddenly, Steve's right hand moves into the frame. In it is clutched…a grill lighter. Steve: OR WAS IT?! Mendel screams, and the camera begins to fade again, while we hear Steve saying over and over in the background: Steve: Quiet DOWN! Quiet DOWN! Quiet DOWN! HA HA HA HA HA HAAAAAA!!!! Cut to the stage, with Steve shaking a sleeping (and muttering) Mendel awake. Steve: Geez, Mendel, wake up! We're on tv for crying out loud! Mendel stirs. Mendel: Oh, Steve, I'm sorry. I just dozed off while you were…babbling…incessantly…just had a bad dream, I guess. Steve: Oh, did you? Steve looks at the camera with an evil grin, and the lights dim. Steve: We'll be right back. And then…each of you…will enter…A SPACE CAPSULE! Mendel: NOOOOOOO! The camera fades to a commercial. FIRST INTERVIEW: HARBINGER: The camera fades in with a pan over the audience, the Swes Band rocking as only the Swes Band can, and we cut to Steve and Mendel at the desk. Steve: Man, how about that Prince of Space, huh? Mendel: Truly the greatest American hero. Steve: I disagree. Mendel: I'm pregnant. Steve: What? Mendel: I said I'm thirsty. Steve: Oh. Mendel: I don't think it's mine. Steve: What? Mendel: I said I never liked mimes. Steve: Me neither. In any case, I'd say it's high time we bring out our first EXPECTED guest, don't you? Ladies and gentlemen, that guy from Mercury with a penchant for chicken wings, the man with a righteous gland, HARBINGER! The Swes Band actually does its job this evening and plays Harby's theme music as he saunters out from backstage. The audience likes Herb and gives him a nice big pop while he walks over to the chairs. Mendel moves down again, shaking Herb's hand in the process (it's really impressive how one can do that, I think) and Herb takes a seat, but not before shaking Steve's hand as well, who was actually nice enough to get out of his chair for the occasion. Herb: Wow. You know, it's always been a dream of mine to be on LNL. And if I'm lucky, the dream ends after ten seconds. Steve rolls his eyes good-naturedly. Steve: Come on, Herb, you're glad to see us. Admit that you love us, we won't mind. Besides, it can't be as bad as this dream I used to have when I was in the movie theater naked and Yeltsin made me dance for him. Herb chuckles. Herb: Sure, you guys do good work... about twice a year. Seriously, if I only had to work twice a year, and got to do a shoddy job every time out, I'd be MADE! Steve: You're gonna make me cry, Herb. And nobody wants that. So rather than spit your horrible, painful venom at me, why not tell me something I can USE? Like, how do you actually feel about the split? Herb: It was a mistake. Kinda like signing yo- oh, wait, I'm supposed to lay off. But to be serious, I really do think it was a mistake. When you look at the talent on the Evo roster, and how little they've been used, that's the only conclusion I can come to. That, and I miss all the ring rats Lex attracted. Seriously, the guy's the Wilt Chamberlain of wrestling, only with five fewer zeroes at the end of his number. Steve: What do you think of the other guys in Mercury? Like, say, who's your favorite? Or who would you like most to have a match with? Herb: I think it's clear that I love working with Keith... Pariah. He's willing to take all my moves, and he didn't even seem that upset when I cost him a chomper. As for who I have yet to face, well, I would've said Memosarks before he left. I think he and I could make sweet love on the mat. The audience gives the Memacious One quite a large amount of applause, the funniest and oddest man in wrestling gone his Mem-tastic way. Steve just stares at Herb, horrified beyond belief. Herb: Well, in the sense of having a good match. Steve looks skeptical but nods. Steve: Ah. Herb: But he does have a nice ass, too. Lieberman taps his cards on the desk a few times, then flips to the next without changing facial expressions. Steve: While I'm on the subject, is there anybody you MISS working with? Any folks down Evo way…that you enjoyed…platonically? Herb: Well, Lex and Rook for obvious reasons. I'm still waiting to get back my investment, if you wheel. I've also wanted to see what Dean Wallace can do, he's always seemed like he has his head screwed on the right way. Steve: Okay, now, Herb. You're sitting here in front of dozens of rabid fans, one strange old man who never seems to go away, and at least a baker's dozen of viewers at home. Give us a little insight to YOU. Are you similar to Harbinger, personality wise? What's the REAL Herb Ingles like? Herb: Just like anyone, a lot of what you see on the screen is real. I was trained extensively in Japan, but I also had some tours in Mexico and the US before coming to LCW. While in Mexico, I discovered Ignacio... Apex. Davey Boy gave me the task of teaching him how to work. I don't have to tell you how arduous a process THAT was. Um... I'm single, I'm big on coming up with new moves for the boys, I'm close to the owner, I like chicken wings... that good? Steve: Good enough, though I think there's something you're missing. Herb: That being? Steve: Well, Harbinger had been somewhat attached to Sickness... Herb just grins. Herb: A gentleman never tells. Lieberman stares at him with an 'oh, really' expression. Herb sighs. Herb: No, nothing going on between us. Not that I haven't tried. Steve: Speaking of Harbinger's current status, what's in store for Harbinger in the coming weeks, months, and years? What can we expect? What shouldn't we expect? What do YOU expect? Feel free to stop me any time here, Herb. Herb: No, keep going, I think some of the audience is still awake. Lieberman rolls his eyes again, and this time rears back his hand with the cards in it, as though he means to slap Herb with them. Herb laughs and fake-cowers. Herb: Anyway, Herb right now is involved in what we call the 'Reluctant Heel' storyline. It was pitched by Olmstead of all people, though Keith helped plant seeds for a heel turn. Herb at the moment is conflicted between what he views are his duties to Beaumont and the company, and between his responsibilities as a champion and a wrestler. Eventually, something will cause him to lash out, and evolve into the 'Deadly Herb' storyline. That's gonna be real fun. As for what not to expect, don't look for me to take a break from wrestling any time soon. It's my addiction, and matches are my mainlining. Steve: Wow, and you said I was putting 'em to sleep. Now it's Herb's turn to mock-hit. Herb: Whyioughta… Steve: How about that, we're just about out of time. I was wondering one last thing though... do you think these pants make me look fat? Herb: Nothing could make you look fat. Steve: Oh, you. Steve puts a hand to his cheek, and if he could control such a thing, he'd probably blush. Steve: …do you think these pants make MENDEL look fat? Herb sizes up Mendel, who sucks in his stomach. Herb: Those pants make Mendel look like Al Roker in a cheesecake shop with a half-off coupon. Steve: …clever…and yet…somehow…not! Intriguing in its contradiction! Ok kids, I give you Harbinger! And we're taking a break; I'll see you in a bit with one TR Saxton! Steve flings his cards at the camera as the audience applauds (and you just KNOW some poor intern will have to collect them and give them back, too, the ungrateful bastard!), and we fade to a commercial. SECOND INTERVIEW: TR SAXTON: Camera fades back much in the same way it did the FIRST time (you can't expect originality everywhere, people), and rather than make this longer than it already is we'll just leave it that the camera cuts to Steve. Steve: So, how about YOU? Are YOU glad to see us? Because we're happy to see YOU, AREN'T WE FOLKS? The audience gives a Pavlovian cheer. TR seems to shrink into his chair. TR: Well, I was happy, but now I'm mostly scared. Steve: We all get scared a little from time to time. If you want to…you can hold me. TR: No. Steve: You were a little quick to respond. You don't want to think about it for a minute? TR: No. Steve: Dammit. Anyway…Is your name really Theodore Roosevelt Saxton? I mean, what's THAT all about? TR: Well, my real name is Jason Downer, but that didn't sound too good to the guy behind the curtain, so he asked me to pick a president. I'm a big mark for Teddy, with the 'big stick' thing and all. Steve: What made you go with Mercury during the split? While I'm at it, why did anyone go anywhere? Nobody tells me these things; they just kept me in this little room for almost a year and then took me out when it was showtime again. TR: I started in Mercury because they needed veterans. You had the guys with the most history together going to Evo, and the guys with the breakout potential in Mercury. Now that those guys have 'broken out', I'm back in Evo to kick some righteous ass! Steve: So you're feuding with Johnny Righteous? He's back, you know… TR: ...hm. I hadn't thought of that. Steve: Well then who do you most want to work with in Evo? TR: Well, Used comes to mind. It was a lot of fun when we did our thing last summer. Lots and lots of bloody, scar-leaving painful fun. Oh, and Olivia. Steve raises an eyebrow. Steve: They'd let you wrestle her? TR: Well, I said I'd like to. She gots boobies and all. Mendel: Nobody wrestled me when I had boobies. Steve: I thought about it. Mendel: That's a lie and you know it! And I paid good money for that surgery! Steve: Nobody put a gun to your head. Mendel: YOU did! Steve: But it wasn't a REAL gun. In fact, it's the same one I pointed at Prince of Space before; it had no effect on HIM either! Mendel: Wait…you mean that really happened?! Steve looks down at his desk and coughs. Steve: Just a dream, Ashley. It was only a dream. Steve suddenly realizes he had all but forgotten his interview in this exchange, composes himself and continues. Steve: ...anyway, who do you wish you'd tangled with in Mercury when you had the chance? TR: Geoffrey Slate. That guy is money in the bank, and he's 2 and 0 against me! I can't let that happen. Steve: Ok, well since we've got you BOTH here, what do you think of HERB? TR: I dunno. He's a great guy and all, but have a drink once in a while, you know? We get a shot of Herb's dressing room, where he's drinking a bottle of V8. Cut back to the stage. Mendel: Hey! That was MINE! Steve: I said he could have it. Mendel: You rotten bastard! TR just watches this exchange and then shrugs. TR: But you see what I mean? And here he could have any drink he wants at the bar. Hell, you've even got some 'party favors', am I right? Suddenly Steve looks more than a little nervous. He rustles his cards around on the desk with a panicked expression on his face. Steve: I have ABSOLUTELY NO IDEA WHAT YOU'RE TALKING ABOUT TR SAXTON! TR: Oh...kay... Steve: In fact the director is giving me the sign that says it's time for me to stop talking and for you to go far far away and never speak of this again! Everybody say goodnight to TR Saxton; we'll take a break and we'll be right back with Radiohead! The camera pulls back as if to say "we had nothing to do with this mess of a show and we're sorry," and we fade to another commercial. MUSICAL GUEST: RADIOHEAD: Camera fades in on a darkened studio. Steve stands in the same place he did when he did his opening monologue, a spotlight shining down on him. Steve: Friends and viewers, I have a bit of an apology to make. You see, the show has run a bit longer than it normally does; our interview with Prince of Space took up a lot more space than we originally thought it would, and therefore…Radiohead has left. Disappointment from the audience. Steve: I know, I know. But you can't expect such incredibly cool and detached British guys like Radiohead to just hang around while we do our little wrestling talk show. But we have something that we think will tide you over given the absence of Radiohead. And that thing is…Radiohead. Ladies and gentlemen…Radiohead. We will ignore the fact that the word "radiohead" was spoken several times in quick and irritating succession in the previous paragraph. It was an obvious and stupid mistake but it is also a fact that the writer is a lazy, lazy man and decided to leave it in. What we WILL focus on is the fact that Radiohead is behind Lieberman, the lights have come on, and they are playing "I Might Be Wrong" off their latest album. Thom Yorke, lead singer and songwriter, writhes about madly, as he is wont to do while performing live. To the point, in fact, that the lyrics are all but unintelligible given the (further) wavy quality is voice has taken on from all the writhing. But all told it is quite a good performance and the audience is pleased, and they make this point clear by offering up thunderous applause. Upon completion of the song, Steve appears on stage next to Thom Yorke, shakes the man's hand (an easier feat now that he has stopped shaking around than it was beforehand, as one might guess), and waves to the camera. Steve: You people have been flop-doodle-tastic! Tune in next time, and there WILL be a next time! We've got Frank Heimdal! And…maybe other people too! Thanks, and goodnight! Lots and lots of applause while the Swes Band starts up and Steve seems to be having a conversation with Thom Yorke. Wondering what they're talking about, and what the hell Radiohead is doing at Late Night Lethal in the first place, the camera fades to black and we escape another episode of LNL, but just barely. ------ And that'll do it for Episode 4 of Late Night Lethal. I hope you enjoyed it. It's good to be back. -TV's Adam
