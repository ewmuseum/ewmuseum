Late Night Lethal, 21 May 2001

21 May 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
LCW Central:: Where It All Comes Together Lethal Late Night Show 05.21.01 OPENING SEGMENT: Camera fades in to the New York Cityscape in the early evening, with a jazzy, brassy opening tune in the background; the camera pans across it as the announcer speaks. When he mentions the show's name, the show's logo appears superimposed over the city. Announcer: Live from Guy With A Hose Studios in New York City, it's Lethal Championship Wrestling's Late Night Lethal with Steve Lieberman! Tonight's guests: A picture of everyone's favorite Bear-centered professional wrestling stable appears superimposed over the ever-panning cityscape. Announcer: The Asylum: Lost, The Doctor, and Bear the Bear! That picture is replaced in favor of a certain skinny, wispy-sideburned musician. Announcer: Musical guest: BECK! This picture replaced by a picture of six huge guys holding various musical instruments. Announcer: Featuring: The Swesley B. Sweet band! This replaced by a photo of Ashley Mendel, the co-host. Announcer: Ashley Mendel! Camera cuts to the inside of the studio; a large audience is seen applauding. Some of them are holding up signs, which contain such interesting phrases as: "Lieberman for President!", "All hail Kick Asserman!", and, of course, "All your base!" Upon the stage is the traditional late-night fare: a big desk with a nice comfy chair in back of it, with the ever-necessary mug and small pile of papers set upon it. Next to that, the guest/co-host couch, where Mendel is already seated. In back is the giant glass window with a fake skyline in back; cardboard apartment buildings with little lights in the little windows, and the like. To the right of this (from the camera's perspective), we see the Swesley B. Sweet Band playing the aforementioned jazzy opening tune. Announcer: And I'm your announcer, Sacapuntas Jones! And now, your host, the man who puts the "potentially dangerous" in "potentially dangerous growth," it's Kick....ASSERMAN! Steve, a moderately tall man with medium build and a relative tower of black hair, comes running out from backstage, and stands to the left of the main stage, in a large vacant area. The lights dim for the rest of the stage, obscuring Mendel and the Swes band in relative darkness. A spotlight comes up to hit Steve where he stands. The Swes B. drummer brings the opening song to a close with some flashy rolls and cymbal crashes, culminating in the typical final crash. The applause dies down and Steve begins to speak. Steve: Hey, great, thanks a lot. I'm Steve Leiberman. Good to have everybody out here; glad to see all you guys could make it out to our first-ever Late Night Lethal show. Big pop. Steve: So yeah...well, this is the time in the show where the host would normally go off on a little monologue, a little "riff," if you will, usually focusing on the current news of the world. But you know what, here in LCW-land, we do things a little different. Steve: See...I know what all you guys are thinking. Every single one of you, in fact. You're all thinking things like: "Did I leave the iron on?" or "My, but Circle Pines, Minnesota would be a nice place to raise a family!" or maybe even "If I could only get my hands on that hot-as-hell Steve Lieberman, I would shake the paint off his walls, let me tell YOU." Pause. Steve: ...is...anybody thinking that? Maybe you, up there sir? Steve points, and the camera cuts to an old, balding man wearing glasses, seated in the audience. He and the rest of the audience laugh, but he shakes his head "no" emphatically. Steve: Damn. ...well, that's not the point. The point is, what MOST of you are probably thinking is: "Who is this odd, hefty Jewish guy standing here before me? And can I hurt him?" Well, no, you can't. But what we'd like to do right now is offer a little...biography, if you will. A little peek into the life of your host, so you can understand me better; learn about me, learn from me...and perhaps maybe learn to love me. Especially YOU. Camera cuts back to the old man, who chuckles good-naturedly as the audience laughs. Steve: ...I do so like him. Chuckle from the audience. Steve: Right, well. Without further ado, here is a brief history of the man, the myth, the mystery: Steve Lieberman. Camera fades out. SKETCH: LIEBERMAN BIOGRAPHY Fade in, with harmonica music starting up in the background. The camera shows a montage of grainy, black and white photos over the narrator's voice, in accordance with the events he is describing. Narrator: Steven Lieberman was born the son of a Norwegian nobleman in what has been estimated to be roughly 75 A.D, during a tumultuous time in the Roman Empire. However, records of this period are somewhat sketchy, due to outbreaks of syphilis and other sexually-transmitted diseases near, and occasionally in Steven's home. His mother described him as a "puling, drooling freak of a child," but she loved him dearly, along with his 16 brothers and sisters. In fact, when we asked, one of his brothers recalled the love of his mother as "brutish...like a Viking." Abruptly following this statement, he slipped into a coma from which he has yet to wake. Narrator: Steven's father was eventually arrested and executed under Roman law, due to the then very serious offense of being Norwegian. Steven, his mother, and his siblings were also executed, only to be promptly revived by the good graces of a passing holy man. Narrator: As Steven progressed through his childhood, he began to show an interest in such varying activities as: running, jumping, crouching, shaving, cruelty to small animals, pottery, and perhaps above all, wrestling. Yes, early on Lieberman expressed a love of sweaty men in tight pants tossing each other around an enclosed space. He expressed a desire to form what would have been the first official professional wrestling federation, with himself as a performer. He went by the name "Lightning Liebs," and reportedly had a costume consisting solely of a thundercloud tied to a string, wrapped around his pelvic area. The montage stops on the picture of Steve in his wrestling costume, and then begins to zoom in on the grainy portrayal of his cloud-laden batch. Narrator: However, Steven's dreams were to be dashed. A local farmhand one day saw Steven parading around in his costume, as he was often wont to do, and unleashed a taunting upon the young aspiring wrestler which was so fierce that it scarred him for life, and forever ruined his dreams of professional wrestling. Camera cuts to Steve in your typical interview-for-documentary room. Steve: "Fatty." That's what he called me. "Fatty." Interviewer (off camera): Is that all he said? Steve: No...it was variations of it. "Hey, Fatty." "Look at that Fatty." "Looks like a coastal storm warning over your batch there, Fatty." It...it hurt. I couldn't ever put the costume on again. Interviewer: Couldn't you have just put on a different costume? Steve pauses. Steve: Hey, look, f**k you, buddy, all right? v Interviewer: I was just suggesting that - Steve: No. Just...just shut the f**k up, ok? G*d DAMN. Camera goes back to the montage of photographs, and the narrator's voice. Narrator: In 1985, Steve was 23 years old, and ready for the world. When questioned about how he only aged 23 years in a span of nearly two thousand, he simply replied that he does not "believe in linear time," and would offer no further comment. It was during this turbulent period of history that he discovered two things: his love of television, and his extraordinary case of herpes. Cut back to the room. Steve: I had herpes. He moves to get up from his chair. Interviewer: Why don't...why don't you just stay over there for now. Back to the montage. Narrator: After receiving the proper medication, Steve enrolled in and graduated from the Pratiwadibyankaram School of Broadcasting after only 16 years of studying there. Shortly after his graduation, he met Dave Beaumont in a parking garage, and after a brief conversation, Steven was given the position of host on the fledgling Late Night Lethal program. Back to the room. Steve: Dave was a great guy. He offered me the position for practically nothing. All he asked was that I try my best, and he get a couple quick shots to my kidneys. It hurt...but it was a good kind of hurt, you know? Interviewer: No. I don't. Steve: Oh. There is a relatively awkward pause. Interviewer: So...what will you be doing now? Steve: ...living. Working. Trying to be the best host I can be. I've come a long way, after all. I've paid my dues, I've taken my lumps. But now, by God, it's STEVE'S TURN! STEVE'S TURN TO SHINE!!! Steve leaps up out of his chair, flailing his arms, and promptly overturns it. He charges the camera, and it tips over, crashing to the floor. The camera and music fade out from that position, a shot of Steve's overturned chair, the sound of punches, and the wails of the attacked. END SKETCH. Camera fades back in to an applauding crowd. It pans back to where Steve was standing, revealing him looking up to the camera, his face moist with tears. Steve: ::chokes back a sob:: Right. We've...we've got a great show for you tonight. The Asylum is here... Big pop. Steve: And Beck's going to be here too... Huge pop...for some reason. Steve: So stick with us. We'll...oh God...we'll...have a good time. Applause again, and a cut to commercial for the upcoming Tempest. BACK FROM COMMERCIAL: After commercial, we fade back in to the studio, panning over the applauding audience, as the Swes band plays yet another jazzy little riff. Camera comes to rest on a shot of the desk and couch, where Steve and Mendel are now seated, respectively. Mendel's done up business-casual style; khakis and a rather lovely dress shirt. He's a medium-sized chap with bleached blond hair and a goatee. Steve has the patented "late night talk show host little index cards" in his hands, and he slaps them on the desk Letterman-style as the audience applauds. Steve: Hey kids, welcome back to the show; before I bring our first guest out, I'd like to take this opportunity to introduce you to the regulars. Seated on the couch here, giving me a look at his beautiful thighs, is Ashley "The Dream" Mendel. How you doing there, Ash? Audience gives co-host Mendel a respectable pop. Mendel: I'm doin' good Steve. I was...I was watchin' your biography just a few minutes ago. I had no idea that you were Lightning Leibs. I loved that guy. Steve: Oh, you knew about him, huh? Mendel: Oh yeah, absolutely. Every day when I was, you know, a young kid growing up in downtown Oregon... Steve: Did you grow up in Oregon? Mendel: ...no. ::pause:: Anyway, every day when I was a young kid growing up in downtown Oregon, I'd wake up in the morning, slide down the banister in my little feety pajamas, and run over to the TV to watch Lightning Leibs kick some serious ass on the USA Network. Mendel goads the audience into giving this a pop. Steve: I was never televised, Ashley. Mendel looks confused. Mendel: No? But what about... Steve: No, man, I'm sorry. What are you even talking about? Mendel looks down, contemplating, as the audience chuckles. Mendel: But I remember your thundercloud-covered area so vividly.... Steve: That's because I gave you those pictures. Remember? Mendel raises his eyebrows and nods in recognition. Steve: Anyway, that's Ashley Mendel. And over here on my left, we have Wesley Underwood and the Swesley B. Sweet Band! Camera cuts to Wes at the head of the band, bass guitar in hand, headset on his...head. Audience applauds. Wes is an enormous black man dressed in black pants, with a black leather jacket. Steve: How are you doin' over there, Swes? You enjoying the show so far? Wes: Shut the f*** up, you stupid white bastard. Camera cuts back to Steve, looking surprised as the audience laughs. Wes: Man, what the f*** are you people laughing at? Don't make me come up there! Damn. Steve: Swesley...why must you always hurt me? Where's the love? Anyway...I guess it's about time we bring out our first guest. FIRST INTERVIEW: THE DOCTOR Steve: Fresh from The Asylum, this guy's relatively new to the LCW world, but then again, so are we. He debuted at the Suicide PPV several weeks back, and since then he's managed to grab himself the Universal Title, form an odd little organization centered around a stuffed animal, and win the hearts of all the crazy-wrestler fans out there in LCW-land. Mendel: Yeah, I like this guy. He's good. Steve: That's exactly what I thought. Well, let's bring him out, the man known to LCW only as The Doctor! The Swes band does an interesting little "Kick A Picnic" cover (which is tough to do, I'm sure) as Doc walks on out. He's dressed perfectly normal for a change, but pretty casual too. He's got blue jeans on, and a solid dark blue t-shirt, with sneakers. He's holding the Bear, and upon entering the stage from the back, he raises the Bear up, and the crowd gives him a pretty big pop. He walks on over to the couch, shakes Mendel's hand (who shoves over to accommodate the new guest), shakes Steve's hand, and sits down, setting Bear on Steve's desk, facing him. Doctor: Man, I tell you, this is GREAT. YEAH! Steve: What, the show? Yeah, I know. Doctor: No, I mean, talking without having to put on the Bear voice! I was beginning to forget what my actual voice sounded like! Steve: Sure, I can see how that'd happen. Ok, well, I know what the first question I want to ask you is. Doctor: And what would that be, friend Steven? Steve: ...saaaay...um, right. What I want to know is, what's the DEAL with The Doctor? And the stuffed bear? I mean, it's such an odd path to take a professional wrestler down. Mendel: Oh I don't know, what about that guy with the head? Steve: Ash... Mendel: You know, that guy. "What does everybody want?" Steve: Ash... Mendel: And then everybody goes, "HEAD!" Steve: Mendel! Mendel: Yeah? Steve: We don't talk about them here. Shh! Mendel: Oh, right. Sorry. Steve: Yeah, really. Jackass. Anyway, Doc, what's the deal there? Doc leans back in his chair as he begins his story. You can insert your own "yeah" and "uh-huh" noises on behalf of the host. Doctor: Well, see, the way it basically worked was that I got scouted out by some execs at LCW. I was playing around in some local feds where I came from; out in Jersey, actually. Wasn't doing The Doctor bit at the time, though, just a regular guy with a made-up name and a smart-ass attitude, you know? Doctor: But they found me one day...I actually remember, I had just finished off a match with some weird chubby Italian guy who called himself Talran Korvassian. Kicked his ass good, actually. He had it coming, too. I mean, what the hell kind of name is that? Steve: Talran Korvassian, huh? Sounds like something out of a Renaissance festival. "Hither yon, there goeth Talran Korvassian and his noble band of knights...to...I don't know, go bury some plague victims, or contract syphilis from the town whore or something..." Doctor: You know, I couldn't help but notice while I was watching backstage- Steve: Oh, you were watching backstage? Doctor: Yeah, they make you. They actually hired Rook Black to stand back there and beat the crap out of anyone who tries to leave the green room. The audience, easily-led sheep that they are, give up the necessary pop at the mention of Rook. Inexplicably, the cameraman cuts back to the old man from the opening segment, who smiles and waves to the camera. Doctor can be heard laughing, and they cut back to the desk. Doctor: Who is that guy, your dad or something? Why do they keep going to him? Did he invest money in the show? Steve: No, we just have a soft spot for the elderly. Doctor: As do we all. Anyway, I couldn't help but notice backstage that you have some sort of inclination towards sexually-transmitted diseases. Steve: ...hey! Doctor: No, I didn't mean it like THAT. But anyway, yeah, so these LCW execs pick me out and they go "hey, buddy, come on over and do this pro wrestling bit...LCW...yeah..."...well, in so many words, you know. Steve interrupts him. Steve (laughing): Dude, you can't just tell me I have an inclination towards STDs and then go on with your little STORY! My MOM watches this show! Doc shrugs. Doctor: Well, she should be well-informed as to your health. I'm sure she'd be worried about something like that. Steve laughs again. Steve: OK, fine, tell your damn story. I think I like you better when you talk through the BEAR. Doctor: We'll get to that. Anyway, so they bring me into the fed, but they tell me I should come up with some neat gimmick, so I can stand out from the other wrestlers. There were some huge amount of new guys about to come in; there was me, Wild Bill, Sonny Calzone, Ebay, and Carlos... Carlos' mention gets a respectable pop from the audience (one female voice in particular gives a rather loud "WOOO!" sound), and Ebay gets a small one, but not much for the other two, here and gone too quick as they were. Doctor: So to keep me separate from the rest of these guys, I decided to go down the crazy path. And then I go: "Hey, you know what'd be neat? If I didn't talk directly; if I had some sort of inanimate object to talk for me." Steve: And you settled on the Bear? Doctor: No, not right away. I was actually quite prepared to go with a sock puppet. Steve: A sock puppet, really! Doctor: Yeah. They're easy to make...they don't eat much, you know. But I ended up with Bear, because I was cleaning out my basement one day, and I stumbled upon him. I knew right then that he'd be the perfect companion for The Doctor. Steve: Where did he come from? What's...you know, the history of Bear? Doctor: Very boring, really. Bear's a childhood toy, simple as that. I had him when I was a kid, and I guess we just kept him. He took a beating or two, it seems. I must've been a violent kid or something, cuz the way he is right now is how I found him. Steve: Huh. Fancy that. Well, we've gotta cut to a commercial real soon, but a quick question: can you give us Doc's...or yours...for that matter...real name? I mean, he goes under, like, a different one every match. Doctor: Not just yet, no. There's a reason I asked to be introduced on the show as just "The Doctor", and it's because we've been working on a very big project recently, that's going to reveal a lot about The Doctor's past history. And then everyone'll find out his real name, along with lots of other stuff. Steve: OK, great, well, we're looking forward to that. Thanks a lot for coming out tonight; good luck with your upcoming stuff. Steve turns to the camera. Steve: We'll be right back after this with Aaron Antioch and Beck! Swes Band starts up, and Doc leans over to have a discussion with Mendel and Steve as the mikes get cut, the camera zooms out and we cut to another commercial. BACK FROM COMMERCIAL 2 Camera fades in onto Steve, Mendel, and now Doc, sitting before the desk, as the Swes Band continues their routine of jazzy riffs at returns and departures related to commercials. Everybody's favorite beaten up stuffed animal is still placed and resting (silently, for a change) on Steve's desk. The audience is applauding and quiets down after a few moments. Steve: Hey, and we're back. My next guest has had a much longer career in LCW than the good Doctor here, under a different name and partnered with his cousin as one-half of the former tag team, Divine Intervention. But recently, after a defeat at the hands of Apocalypse, the Apostle Aaron Antioch changed his name and tune and joined up with Doctor and Bear, forming one of the...well, one of the oddest stables I've seen for some time, The Asylum. Doctor chuckles at the brief critique of his stable, and the camera fades. It fades in on the footage of Doctor and then-Apostle in the hospital together, when they first decided to form the Asylum. When the clip is done, they fade back to the desk. Doctor is looking shamefacedly down at his hands, rested in his lap. Doctor: I know, it's ridiculous. I don't know what we were thinking when we wrote it...we...we were drunk, basically, and I... The audience, Steve, and Mendel get a decent laugh out of that. Mendel: I actually thought it was one of the finer moments in hospital-and-stuffed-animal related wrestling history. Doctor: That says a lot, Ashley, and I thank you. Steve: Well never mind all that, let's just bring him out. You know him now as Lost; the former Apostle, Aaron Antioch! INTERVIEW 2: AARON ANTIOCH Aaron comes on out from backstage to a nice big pop and an interesting rendition of Nothingface's "I, Diablo" by the Swes Band. (Isn't it nice how they take the trouble to learn them like that?) He shakes Mendel's hand, and Steve's, and both Ashley and Doc sidle on over so Aaron can sit in by Steve to...you know...be interviewed. Aaron: Hey, Steve, glad to be one of the first on the show. Steve: Well we're damn glad to have you, Aaron. We're big Asylum fans around here, after all. Actually, before you arrived we were just talking with the audience, and there was this one guy who was all about the Asylum. I think he's over there. Steve points out to the audience, and the camera cuts to that old man again, who laughs and waves yet again. Aaron: Oh, him. Well, that's my dad, so he's naturally interested. Steve: Really, that's your dad? Aaron: No. Steve: Oh. Well, anyway, I guess the first thing I'd like to touch on is the big changeover. Aaron: Yeah, the Apostle-to-Lost switch. Steve: Exactly. What brought this about? Aaron: Well, the management and I thought that the religious angle had run its course, especially with Angel needing some time away to marry Danielle, who you all know as Destiny. Angel's marriage to Danielle gets a nice round of congratulatory applause. Aaron: So it was really just a matter of finding something new to involve the character in. Angel wasn't going to be around anyway; Divine Intervention would've had to take a bit of a break anyway. Steve: But you ended up joining up with Doc. How'd that come about, exactly? Doctor, currently off camera, speaks up and is cut to. Doctor: Oh, that. I found Aaron in a bassinet on my doorstep one frigid autumn morning. It was quite interesting. Very large bassinet. Aaron laughs and shakes his head. Aaron: No, that was just the post-hypnotic suggestion we left you with after we... Aaron coughs. Aaron: Yes. Steve and Doc laugh. Doctor: Bastards. I feel so...dirty. Aaron: Oh, if you only knew. Steve laughs again. Steve: I believe that's enough highly-disturbing innuendo for today, kids. Aaron: I believe you're right. What ACTUALLY happened was that when Doc arrived in LCW, we thought that he might need someone experienced here in LCW to help him get a foothold and get comfortable. So there we had point A and point B, it's just the road in between to settle on. Steve: You needed a way to explain the switch in character. Aaron: Exactly. So we did a thing where my past caught up with me, and the house of cards came tumbling down, and I couldn't be happier with how it turned out, it's been great, the fans are really loving it. Doc and I work well together; it's been a helluva lot of fun. Steve: What about Angel...or I guess it'd be Lunacy now? How are things between you, him and Destiny? Aaron: We're still very close. Mike and I are cousins, as everyone knows, and he's been a great influence on my career and my life, and I consider him more a brother. He's a very positive person, but at the same time, when it comes to his ring work, he's a real stickler and pays close attention to detail. That can make him depressed if he has a bad match, so then Destiny comes in and picks him up. She's a great addition to the family, and she's just a terrific person. The funny thing was, you might remember, when we did the angle with Destiny being kidnapped by Legion, I gave her my Phillippians 4:13 dogtags, which eventually was going to lead to an angle where we were in love. But the management saw the real-life relationship and went with that instead. I'd have actually liked for Mike to be here today, but he couldn't make it out. Steve: Oh, that's too bad, we would've loved to have him. So, what're the plans for your future, Aaron? What do you see happening? Aaron: Well, I'm one of the more veteran guys on the staff right now, but I still have a lot of good years in front of me, and have managed to stay pretty healthy. I've already been a champion... This gets a decent pop from the audience. Ah, memories of champions past! Aaron: ...and I don't think that I'm really the sort of person who will go after any high level titles. So I suppose my goal is to just keep working, keep entertaining, and help out some of the new breed in LCW. Steve: That was a very respectable...beautiful answer. Sir...I applaud you. There's a long pause as Steve just looks at Aaron fondly for several seconds. Eventually, the camera cuts back to the old man again, and then back. Mendel: That's gonna get SO old, SO fast. Steve: I know, it's like four times now; what the hell are they thinking back there? Doctor: I LIKE him. Aaron: Yes, but in MODERATION. Only so much elderly man per show. Steve: Ha HA, you say that NOW. ...hm. OK then, well how about the Asylum? What's going to be with that? The whole Dave kidnapping bit? Aaron: Well, as you'd assume, the kidnapping of Beaumont puts Asylum in a very convenient position. Doc, Bear, Lost, and now Lunacy as well...they have control over the owner. Expect some interesting matches in the future where Asylum is concerned. We had a meeting on that today and a lot of productive things came of it; the following months are going to be very interesting for us. Steve: So can you guys let us in on anything? Aaron: If I told you any more, I might have to kill you. Aaron laughs big and loud and maniacal, and then sort of tapers off, coughs, and continues. Audience gets a kick out of that. Aaron: In terms of the company, I think we serve a good role, we're in a great angle with Apocalypse, a lot of talented workers... Nice pop for Apocalypse. Aaron: ...I think that's the sort of thing, while not always at the forefront; it's one of the more subtle feuds that can make LCW even greater and more intriguing to the fans. Doc laughs. Doctor: Subtle? Aaron: Well, I think it's subtle. Doc shrugs. Steve: Well, thank you for that. You've told us nothing. And I hate you. But that's not important! Ok, we've gotta go to another commercial now, but you guys have been great; couldn't have asked for a better pair of jackasses to interview on my first show. Steve turns to face the camera and it zooms in on him. Steve: OK, so we'll be right back with BECK! Swes Band starts up again, camera pulls back and pans over the audience, and then there's a commercial. AFTER COMMERCIAL Camera fades back in to Steve at his desk. Mendel's the only one left, Aaron and Doc are gone, but Bear is still sitting on Steve's desk. Steve: I'm keeping this damn bear. Anyway...my musical guest this evening has been best known for such ridiculous songs as "Devil's Haircut," and "Sexlaws!" He's an odd-faced lanky guy with wispy, pubescent sideburns who showed us all what it would be like to have sex with a drum machine and embarrassed Tori Amos on national television at the same time! Mendel: What the hell are you talking about? Steve: Shh! Right! Without any further ado, here he is...BECK! BECK Much applause from the audience. Camera cuts to a lanky man who very much IS Beck, standing in a small room with bare walls. He raises one hand in greeting. BECK!: Hi. I'm Beck. Camera immediately cuts back to Steve and Mendel at the desk. Steve: GOODNIGHT, everybody! Mendel: Wait...that's it? Steve: Yeah. Mendel: But he never played anything. Steve: Never said he was going to. Just said he was going to be here. Mendel: ...what? Steve: ...it's funny. Mendel: ...no it's not. It's ridiculous. You built it up all this time like you were gonna have Beck perform. I expected to see him out here doing all that ridiculous stuff he does, singing his odd, irrelevant lyrics. And you passed all that up for the sake of a cheap, idiotic gag?! Steve: ...it's...funny. Mendel: ...oh, g*d dammit. It's not even remotely funny! It's lowbrow, and stupid. I'm ashamed I had anything to do with this entire show. This is bulls***. I'm out of here. Mendel gets up and heads backstage into obscurity. Steve looks perplexed. Steve: ...I thought it was funny. The lights go out. A voice is heard over a PA system, apparently a crew member. Crew Guy: Actually, Steve, we thought it was pretty stupid. You're on your own, buddy. Steve: Don't you people appreciate HUMOR? Crew Guy: Danny, cut his mic, ok? Steve's mic is cut as he protests. The audience applauds, the camera fades, and the credits roll. At the end of the credits, a voice is heard. Announcer: Late Night Lethal was sponsored in part by Dee's Nuts. Dee's Nuts: Grab A Handful today.
