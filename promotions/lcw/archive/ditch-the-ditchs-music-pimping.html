The Ditch's Music Pimping DITCH'S MUSIC PIMPING WARNING! The views expressed on this page belong to the Ditch. They reflect the opinion of this website as a whole, 'cause I own it. Comments of any sort are encouraged. Thank You! The Ditch, editor. Contact the Ditch at [detail withheld] Music Pimping, May 6, 2002: Heroin. I'm sick of this. Artist after artist has their life taken away because of an addiction to drugs. Layne might have just died, but he was dead to the world when heroin became the center of his life. I'm not going to write a weepy column about him. It sucks, it was his choice. Instead, I'm going to say this. Nobody WANTS to take heroin at first. Everyone knows how bad the stuff is. Everyone knows about the lives it's taken. Heroin is the end of the road of addiction, a road which typically starts in one of three places: cigarettes, alcohol, marijuana. The weaker drugs (it's hard for any of them to cause serious damage in a short amount of time) open a person up to the prospect of using drugs for the purpose of having a good time. From there, SOME people decide to experiment with other things; I don't need to go over the list. These drugs are typically more potent, and thus, give the user more of what they got from the original drug. The second-level drugs lead to more, and more, eventually leading the user to heroin. It might sound nutty, it might sound unrealistic, but it's what I believe: if you don't begin to use drugs for recreational purposes, then there's one less thing with which your life can be ruined. It's as simple as that. Given that there are so many ways to have a good time, why tie yourself to something which can cause so much damage? If you or anyone you know is considering using heroin, I recommend watching the movie Requiem for a Dream. It's intense, it's slightly extreme, but it's effective. I promise that the next column will actually deal with music. Music Pimping, April 17, 2002: Devin Townsend I'd be stunned if more than ten of you had ever heard that name before. This Canadian had most of his commercial success as the driving force behind the band Strapping Young Lad, and has since done a series of unique, high-end metal albums. Yay metal! The albums really defy categorizing other than 'metal'; they're a lot less grating than the Strapping Young Lad stuff, on average. MP3.com is a good place to sample his songs. Biomech: Sometimes referred to as having come from the band "Ocean Machine", this is really all Devin's doing. 13 songs, and I overtly enjoy 9 of them. Physicist: I only like "Kingdom" and "Irish Maiden" off this. Least recommended of the four. Infinity: 6 out of 10 good songs, and the first four are awesome. "Bad Devil" is way fun, and "War" rocks hard. This gets the strongest recommendation. Terria: 6 out of 11. More Devin goodness. Final thoughts: Devin Townsend is a great musician, and a fantastically bizarre looking human being. Music Pimping, March 30, 2002: Alice In Chains A lot of good stuff came out of the Seattle scene in the '90s, but none of it quite measures up to Alice In Chains. Their stuff, whether "hard-as-it-gets-before-metal" rock or acoustic melody, is always HEAVY and meaningful. Facelift: Nothing fancy here, just lots of rocking. "Man in the Box" is the big hit, but "Bleed the Freak", "Sunshine", "It Ain't Like That" and "I Know Somethin'" are all quality stuff as well. Sap: Features my favorite AIC song of all, "Got Me Wrong". Dirt: This album best shows AIC's raw musical ability. "Rooster" and "Would?" are the hits, but it's really all about "Rain When I Die", "Them Bones", "Down in a Hole" and "Hate to Feel." This is the album I'd say would be liked by the highest percentage of people. Jar of Flies: They delve more into acoustic, but it's still good. "I Stay Away" and "No Excuses" are the hits, while "Rotten Apple" and "Nutshell" are both incredibly powerful. Final Thoughts: It's a shame that drugs both inspired and destroyed the band (not that it's the first time that happened). At least they left several great albums behind for us to enjoy. Music Pimping, March 8, 2002: The Gathering. The Gathering. What's to say. THEY RULE. At least, as of their third album. Blending great instrumentation with the stirring, beautiful vocals of Anneke Van Giersbergen, they have a sound that blows almost every other band I've ever heard away. Even now, as they've moved towards a slightly less metal sound, it's still great music. Mandylion: Anneke debuts with a bang. "In Motion" parts 1 and 2 rule. "Strange Machines" rocks on so many levels. The rest of the album is well above average as well. Nighttime Birds: GET THIS ALBUM RIGHT NOW! Every single song is at least an 8 out of 10 in my opinion. "On Most Surfaces" and "Third Chance" are so awesome. This is one of the most enjoyable albums I've ever encountered in my life. WHAT ARE YOU WAITING FOR? BUYYYYY IIIIIIIT! How To Measure A Planet: Not 'great' but still 'very good.' Fans of the above albums will like it, since the vocals are still stellar. "Travel", "Liberty Bell" and "Red is a Slow Colour" are the highights for me. If Then Else: Same as 'Measure'. Highlights: "Saturnine", "Colorado Incident", "Analog Park" and "Rollercoaster." Final Thoughts: The band is so very distinct from anything else you'll hear. Good guitar riffs, great female vocals, generally uplifting melodies. Give me this over the workings of 'artists' like Fred Durst and Mark McGrath any day. Music Pimping, February 24, 2002: The Crystal Method. I like electronica. Not "acid" or "drum & bass" or "house", but electronica. It's the difference between indie rock and metal; there's a level of style and professionalism that allows for the best expression of an artist's talent. Sorta like how RVD is a better wrestler in 2001 than he was in 2000. Crystal Method, to me, is the pinnacle of electronica. Only two albums, but both are great. Vegas: This is THE electronica album, for all time. The stuff is so high-end and intense, and none of the songs sink into mediocrity. "Busy Child" and "Keep Hope Alive" are the singles, but I prefer the three songs between them, "Cherry Twist", "High Roller" and "Comin' Back." Tweekend: It took four years to release the second album, but they didn't hit the groove like they did on the first. Still, the raw talent of Jordan and Kirkland is enough to make over half of the 11 songs good stuff. "Name of the Game" is the big single, while I prefer "PHD", "Wild Sweet & Cool" and "Over The Line." Final Thoughts: Damn. This was way faster to write than I expected. "Vegas" is must-have for fans of electronic music, and should be looked at if you don't have much exposure to the genre. "Tweekend" should then appeal to those who like "Vegas." Music Pimping, 2/16/2002 Well, there's a reason why I'm doing this column. I know a lot, and I mean a LOT of bands that simply aren't played on the radio, MTV, VH1, and so on. I hope that I can expose you to new bands and new sounds, broadening your musical horizon. I like music that is "powerful" in some sense of the word. I tend not to like bands that are "pop", "mainstream" or "overpushed". I don't like country, don't like many rappers, don't like R&B, and more than anything, don't like Fred Durst. I do like hard rock, metal, electronica, and bands that find ways to make it better by using the simple axiom of "add a violin to it". I'll cover one band per column, album by album (of the albums I enjoy). I urge the short-term use of MP3s as sampling devices to find what you like, followed by the purchace of said albums to reward the artist. What better way to start than with my very favorite band... Metallica Some love them for their great 80's heavy metal songs. Some hate them for de-metalizing, and helping to bring down Napster. I love the music, every single album from the top to the bottom. And I'll tell you why. Kill 'em All: The roaring debut. They're very much thrash metal, and the vocals are only 'decent'. At the same time, this album kicks more ass than can be classified my science. The riffs on this album, as a whole, are better than any other album I've ever heard. "No Remorse" is the textbook example of a riff making a song. "Hit the Lights" and "Jump in the Fire" are great. "Pulling Teeth" is painful, but then you can just skip it. Ride the Lightning: Not the mindblowingly great riff collection, but a lot more professional-grade heavy metal. "For Whom the Bell Tolls" is good on so many levels it's scary. "Call of the Ktulu" is a nice instrumental. "Escape" is a hidden gem. Master of Puppets: This is a fairly hit-or-miss album. "Thing that should not be" and "Sanitarium" are very mediocre. "Battery" and "Master of Puppets" both flat-out rock. The solo work on "Disposable Heroes" is out of this world, and can NOT be missed for fans of the guitar. "Orion" is another nice instrumental. And Justice For All: I happen to not be a huge fan of "One", but it's still plenty good. Everything else is great. "Frayed Ends of Sanity" has a two and a half minute solo that once again can NOT be missed. Metallica: Something for everyone. There are the easy to enjoy hits like "Unforgiven" and "Enter Sandman", but also really hard-rocking songs like "Sad but True" and "Struggle Within". "Through the Never" is another hidden gem that nobody talks about. Load: The band transitions from 'metal' to 'hard rock'. If you can't handle that, then ignore them. If you can then by all means give this a listen. It's very diverse, as there are great rock tunes like "Ain't My Bitch" and "King Nothing", slow but heavy masterpieces like "Bleeding Me", "Thorn Within" and "Outlaw Torn". I also enjoy "Poor Twisted Me" and "Ronnie" quite a lot. None of these will be your all-time favorite song, but there's plenty to enjoy. Re-Load: See above. "Fuel", "Better than You" and "Attitude" rock without restraint. "Carpe Diem Baby", "Bad Seed" and "Fixxxer" are all unique. This is all awesome driving music. Garage, Inc: If you're a Metallica fan, you MUST BUY THIS ALBUM. There's everything. They bring the METAL~ with "Mercyful Fate", "Helpless", "Am I Evil?", "The Prince", "Breadfan" and more. "Sabbra Cadabra", "Astronomy", "The Wait", "Stone Cold Crazy" and "Damage Case" also rule. There's just great song after great song. Those of you who think that they aren't any good with short hair, listen to the Mercyful Fate medley and shut the hell up. S & M: Being the fan of metal/orchestra collaborations, I'm sure you can understand that I absolutely adore this. Not all the selections make sense, especially "Thing that should not be" while ignoring 'Kill 'em All' entirely. The second disc is much more potent than the first, especially "For Whom the Bell Tolls" and "Outlaw Torn". Final Thoughts: Not everyone will like every Metallica album. However, if you like a couple albums, it's at least worth listening to all of them.
