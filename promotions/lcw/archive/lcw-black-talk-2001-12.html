Black Talk

December 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
LCW Central:: Where It All Comes Together Black Talk ACDC's "Back in Black" plays and we slowly fade in on the Blacktalk set. Joe Black sits at the desk wearing a blue silk shirt with a bolo tie, with black leather vest. Joe holds up his coffee mug and takes a sip. It says "TEQUILA!" in large letters and "coffee" in very small letters. Joe: Welcome everyone, and first the bad news. It's another edition of Blacktalk. And the other badness, I'm on the show again. Joe: I tried to say I needed an indeterminate vacation from the show, and tried to pin the show on Rook and make him do it. He has the same last name and all that crap so it'd go with the title. I felt Rook owed me one after the Tijuana incident. But before I could even sabotage his schedule I got confronted, cornered, with a lot of demands. So that's why I'm here. Drinking my coffee in front of the camera in a suitable professional wardrobe. Joe: And I'm hard pressed to decide what sucks worse, me having to do this show, or Angel Teves. Joe: Angel's relationship with LCW is terminated. It was a problemic relationship to start. A lot of promises. No substance. Angel, we'll forget you as soon as we can. Joe: In other news, Apex's arm is pretty badly damaged. That particular move from Rook is called the Gauntlet. And to be completely forward, I'd never seen that before from him at all. And I'm not so sure it's acceptable in LCW to go to such extremes to deliberately cripple one's opponent. So if a certain cowboy gets the hint, we're not going to see the Gauntlet again. Joe: Did I mention that Rook Black tapped out in the match where he crippled the world champion? And I don't want you people to think that Apex no longer sucks. He still sucks. But Rook Black, argued by some people to be the greatest wrestler in LCW, which does not include myself, has tapped. Swallow some pride, Prodigal, you're a human just like everyone else. Joe: And before we break for commercial, T.R. Saxton is FIRED! (Commercial Time) We see a commercial with Jason and Clyde Bird beating each other up while fighting over a bag of Doritos which is eaten by a small child at the fight's climax. Awwwww. Joe: Oh my God, you both suck more than TR Saxton, who coincidentally, is the subject of a heinous innacuracy, and is not fired at all. We check our sources on Blacktalk, and since the source is me, I'll say whatever I damn well want. Joe: In the past week, the office reports having exceptionally cooperative behavior from Dante Anderson. So they are considering lightening up a little bit on their original decision to curtail his television time and activity. Though not on the Tempest Card, Dante will most likely be on television, and may be involved in some kind of match up to be announced. While the office's take on things is unsubstantiated, after all, those guys make no sense to me what so ever, the locker room tension seems to have eased up a little bit, so their may be some truth there. Joe: From a recruiting standpoint, Frank Heimdal and Dean Wallace have greatly exceded my personal expectations, as they both suck far more than any new comer I've seen in a while. Joe: We're looking forward to Dexter Riot and Inferno Kid to lower the expectations of LCW as they stink up the ring with each other. Something that I'm not all that pleased about at all. But hey, what can you do? Joe: And now we're introducing a new feature of the show, a special question segment. Joe: In many shows of these kinds, a team of operators would painstakingly screen calls to let some smhuck on the air to ask a stupid question. Well, I decided there's no way in hell I'm going to tollerate that crap. But as an alternative, some shows take questions from the mail, or even the internet. And there is no way in hell I'm going to do that. So I've decided to simulate the question and answer type feel to those shows, I'm going to actually ask questions myself to the LCW superstars. Joe: So here's our first question. Joe pulls out a few index cards. Joe: Question one is for Duel. What the hell is wrong with you? Joe raises an eyebrow at the camera. Joe: Question two is for the Gol: What the hell kind of name is "the Gol"? Joe touches his chin thoughtfully. Joe: Question three is for Apex: How's your arm doing? Joe laughs it up. Joe: I kill me. Joe: Question four is for Pariah: What will happen to you if you decide to partake in illicit substances of a criminal nature on my show, Little P? What will happen? I bet you can guess. Joe snorts, not amused. Joe: Question five is for Rook Black: What begins with a T and rhymes with "rap"? Joe: Question six is our last question and it's for Used: How's your neighbor? Joe: And that's the end of Blacktalk. I wish it was final, but it's just for a week. I'll unhappily see you all next time as I struggle to find a way to make LCW's roster into something that doesn't suck nearly as much as it used to. Joe tosses back the entire contents of his coffee, stands and tips his desk over, revealing the Joe is wearing a pair of Boxer shorts, blue with white stars on them, sans pants, sans shoes etc. He casually walks out of camera shot, credits roll, and Joe Black has left the building.
