A federation that reopened in 2000 and again in 2001 around its own Master of the Ring tournament, an invitational drawing talent from many other federations; the tournament's 2003 seventh edition, fully structured, crowned a champion from a sixteen-man bracket, and its 2005 eighth survives only as a proposed card. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Master of the Ring began in the spring of 1999 as a four-night structure: an opening card settling unfinished business from an earlier, same-name promotion, two nights of outside qualifying matches, and a closing night of quarterfinals through the final, open to an invited roster drawn from more than a dozen other federations across its life.

Two standing attractions ran alongside the bracket itself: SuperBout, an annual grudge match fought outside the tournament proper, and Pentagon Pain, an annual multi-man specialty match.

Its seventh tournament, “The Supreme” (2003), opened with promoter Nick Demola ordering an on-the-spot rematch to settle a year-old dispute over the sixth title — only for both combatants to turn on him afterward, throwing him over the guardrail and stretchering him out. Demola reappeared later by hospital video to hand off the rest of the broadcast.

“Violent” Victor Frost went through Ace of Hearts, Jason Keening, and a hobbled Terry Boston to become the seventh Master of the Ring, winning the final by holding the top rope with his feet for leverage where the referee couldn't see it. Elsewhere on the card, a first-round bout between Vince Viper and Vandal Gomez spilled into a built tanning-salon set as a Tanning Bed Deathmatch, ending when Gomez hit Viper with a fireball and chained him inside a running tanning bed.

“Bad Eye” McBaine became a two-time SuperBout champion by beating Eddie Jacobs in a brawl the two men opened by crashing a van through the arena entrance, and November won a six-man Pentagon Pain over five others.

An eighth edition survives only as a proposed lineup and a “we're back” announcement; no card, roster, or result confirms it was ever run, and no closure notice survives.
