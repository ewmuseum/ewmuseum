Me and My Goals My name is [name withheld] and I've been either handling, running a fed, matchwriting, critiquing, or running supercards for e-wrestling since July of 1996. Before that, and somewhat during, I wrote columns for newsboards and newsletters about professional wrestling. Above all of that, I'm a mark.
My goals with Master of the Ring have always been the same, though one might be emphasized more over the others on any given card. They are:
1) To create a fun, non-political environment for which to roleplay, develop a character, and tell a great story. 2) To give the little guy a chance by putting him in there with the best e-wrestling can offer and having him stand on his own merits. 3) To top the last MotR. Those are what I want to do. MotR's I and III focused heavily on Goal #2. The top priority of MotR's IV and V was Goal #1. MotR VII was not successful in Goal #3... and that's what I'm aiming for with this show.
II. Tips on Characters (aka - How To Get In) First and foremost, use a character you have a history with. In the past
I've allowed great handlers to dump a secondary character into the mix...
and each and every time the character is forgotten by the handler. First rule of thumb: If you don't have a history with the guy, you won't care, and therefore will limit the impact you have on the card and the fun you have with it. Use a guy you know.
Second, fill out the entire application. Don't think that I don't notice half-ass efforts. I ask a lot of myself when running this show, and all I ask of you is your cooperation and willingness to write as well as you can.
III. Master of the Ring Touranament A. What it comprises The Master of the Ring Tournament has used many different formats in
Qualifying Matches in the past. But the goal is the same... to make it to the Standing Six, one match away from the Master's Cell.
MotR I saw a bunch of insane qualifying matches and a Battle Royal to see who qualified for the Standing Six. MotR II was straight-forward, one-on-one matches to qualify. MotR III comprised six threeway dances to see who qualified. MotR IV used six fourway matches. MotR V went back to the one-on-one scenario. MotR VI had a single elimination, sixteen man tournament, leading to a one-on-one final for the gold. MotR VII went back to the formula that worked for MotR II and V. 12 men, six first round matches to get to the Standing Six.
MotR also holds a SuperBout pitting two of the top men in e-wrestling against each other at every card.
B. How to win Roleplay the best. Okay, that's too vague. Work hard, first and foremost. That doesn't mean "write 20k". That means, really hone your writing. Really look at what it consists of. Get to it, man, and do it well.
That's still vague, but more is covered in the Roleplaying section below. C. Master's Cell Match The Master's Cell Match is the traditional final match of every MotR
Tournament. The three men who survive qualifying, and then survive their match in the Standing Six make it into this final match.
The Master's Cell is an enclosed cell that extends five feet out from every side of the ring to the arena floor. On each side of the ring a table is stacked up to be used or not used at the wrestlers' discretions. The rules are simple... win at all costs. No bodyguards, managers, or valets allowed at ringside. Just the men themselves. Elimination rules apply here.
IV. SuperBout Matches A. What they comprise SuperBout matches pit two top men in e-wrestling against each other in a
"dream match" of sorts. The wrestlers choose the stipulation of the match and then wrestle in it. Every SuperBout has to have a special stipulation.
In MotR I LOCO defeated Brody Thunder in a Lumberjack Match. In MotR II
Setzer Van Strife defeated James Pillow in a Ladder Match. In MotR III
Jason Keening defeated 2000 RSPWF #1 man Alex Martinez in a Demola Rules
Match. In MotR IV it was the culmination of a six year feud, when Bad Eye
McBaine beat Alexander Lawson. And at MotR V it was Alex Martinez defeating
Dan Kauffman in a Badlands Ironman Match, known as one of the greatest matches in e-wrestling history. MotR VI had no match, and MotR VII had Bad
Eye McBaine fight Eddie Jacobs in a crazy, no disqualification brawl that lasted the entire show.
V. Outside Fed Matches This year we will invite outside feds to put on matches that are written by the fedhead. We want to use MotR to promote some of the great e-wrestling going on nowadays. If you have a feud or characters you want to promote, write me and I'll see about putting it on.
VI. Roleplaying A. What to do in a roleplay A long time ago I wrote a "Roleplay Bible" that is actually still around on some websites. It outlined 23 things to do and not do in a roleplay to make it effective. I won't reprint that here, thank God. And I'll keep my comments short.
First, get to the point. I don't want to sift through 10k of introductions and meaningless bantor. Get to the stuff of the matter.
Second, keep people interested. Don't ramble on for 3k before getting to the good stuff. Start off intriguing and build from there.
Third, have a well defined character. Know what your man would do in any situation and have him act it out in the roleplay.
Fourth, have fun. B. How often/Talk is Cheap Show times Talk is Cheap are the roleplay shows that go on before the actual MotR card. There will be three Talk is Cheap shows (see the Schedule for times). VII. Strategy Writing There are many different opinions on how to strat. Here's mine. First, keep it short. Again, don't blab on endlessly about why your man does what he does, or about the history of a move. Anything describing the character should be in your app anyways, so I don't need to read it again.
Second, don't map out move for move. With the exception of Iron Man matches, I find this tactic revolting. And yes, I used to do it myself.
You will not get your way. Map out a couple of spots, maybe a couple of ways to combat the other man's big moves... but do that at the MOST.
Third, write it as if someone is trying to write your match in five minutes and just needs to get the good stuff in order to do your strat justice.
VIII. Injury Continuation If you are injured on one part of the card, the injury continues on for the rest of the card. Nuff sed.
IX. Interviews (on card) Do whatever you want for interviews. Backstage interviews are preferred and don't do an in-ring unless you get permission first.
X. My Pledge My pledge is to make this one of the best fantasy wrestling experiences you'll ever have. I will make each card better than the last, keep in contact with everyone, and have fun.
