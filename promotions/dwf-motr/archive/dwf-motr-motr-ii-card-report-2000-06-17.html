MASTER OF THE RING II – FADE TO BLACK RECAP OF MATCHES MATCH 1 “Black Knight” Alex Martinez vs. “Screaming Drillbit” Jason Keening 2 of 3 falls. 1st fall, Technical, strict DQ’s. 2nd fall, No
DQ, Hardcore. 3rd fall, DWF Woodsman Rules.
The entire wrestling world was on the edge of their seats for this titanic clash between two worst enemies from the UEW.
Jason Keening, the winner of Master of the Ring I, would’ve been a favorite to take MotR II’s title as well… if it weren’t for his first round opponent, Alex Martinez. The “smarks” were split 50/50 as to who was going to advance out of this matchup… and for good reason. These two men are so well matched that they decided to have a two of three falls match to settle the outcome and make sure the best man won. Keening’s strength is straight-up wrestling, so special guest ref Nick Demola enforced strict disqualifications and only technical wrestling holds were allowed in that fall. Fall number two consisted of a falls count anywhere brawl, which plays to Martinez’s strengths. Fall number three is a concoction called a “DWF Woodsman” Match where lumberjacks (legit lumberjacks) surround the ring to keep the action inside while the competitors are connected to a strap by the wrists. The way to win is to keep your opponent down for a five-count after either: A) scoring a pinfall, or B) touching all four corners of the ring. Any foreign object or outside interference gets a DQ win for the other man.
Martinez took to the ring first, to an overwhelming chorus of boos. He was followed by the MotR I champ Keening who was given an ovation from the fans… after the brutal match he won to take
MotR I, he deserved nothing less.
“Black Knight” was immediately assaulted by a quick barrage of takedowns and near falls from the technical master,
Keening. “Screaming Drillbit” got the upper hand with many great and innovative moves, but Martinez hung in there with great smarts and technical wrestling of his own, shocking the less-informed fans in the audience who didn’t think he had such skill in the ring. After a failed “Drilldown”, Martinez rolled
Keening into a brilliant German Suplex into leg grapevine bridge pinning combo that gave him the first fall in what some would call a shocker.
The second fall had a much more nasty element to it. Immediately Martinez ran to the outside to set up a table, but when he was done setting it up Keening came off the apron and put Martinez’s face through it with a DEVASTATING bulldog!
Keening then took to the offensive, showing a mean streak that again shocked many of the less-informed fans in the crowd. He beat on Martinez’s face with a series of chairshots, then dumped the helpless big man into the crowd to proceed with the beating. After a desperate beer-in-the-face move by “Black
Knight”, he once again got the best of the MotR I winner and was setting up for a powerbomb off the balcony… which was reversed into a backdrop onto concrete steps. Martinez’s ankle seemed to catch on the corner of a concrete step, which would play a role later in the night. Keening then executed his patented “Facebreaker”, driving Martinez’s already bloodied face into his knee and taking the fall, making it one-to-one.
At this time the huge lumberjacks came out and beat on both men! They then carried the two hurt, tired combatants to the ring and dumped them inside. They tied them together with a leather strap and the bell rang… both men staggering to their feet. After a wild series of missed clotheslines, punches, and counters, Martinez took over with his reach advantage and laid
Keening down with a nasty belly-to-belly suplex. He then created a series of welts and gashes over Keening’s torso and back with the leather strap and attempted to touch all four corners, thinking Keening was done. He got all four corners, but Keening didn’t stay down for the five count as Demola Rules mandate he must to end the match. “Screaming Drillbit” instead pulled a shocked Martinez back and whipped him with the belt himself. Keening then executed The “Drilldown”, a one-man spike piledriver, and got a pinfall on Martinez… but Martinez got to his feet at about the four and ¾ count of guest ref Nick
Demola. The match became a series of counters and brawling again, with some strap spots intertwined. At one point, Keening had Martinez hung over the top rope while the lumberjacks beat mercilessly on Martinez’s ribs. At another point, Keening was put in a Boston Crab by Martinez, and when he reached the ropes for a break the lumberjacks nailed him in the face repeatedly.
The end of this match came when Keening hit Martinez with a european uppercut and attempted a “Black and Decker Drop”, an inverted Rocker Dropper, onto a steel chair that was thrown in the ring by a lumberjack, but Nick Demola stopped him… saying any use of illegal weapons would get him disqualified. Keening was upset at this, since it was a street fight. A limping
Martinez, still staggering from the affects of the ankle drop on the concrete step, took advantage of this momentary distraction to charge at Keening, who was holding the chair up. Keening turned and both men got BLASTED with the chair. Nick Demola counted a double pin, with both men’s arms on each other, and then counted to five. Both men were declared out of the match after a TREMENDOUS show of toughness and talent!
WINNER – DRAW in 31:18. **** MATCH 2 Menelaus “The Black Rose” vs. Max “The Butcher” Bradley No special stipulations accompanied this match… just the storyline of the shocker of MotR I, Menelaus, getting in the ring with the man who many are saying will shock us in this tournament, previously unknown NorthWest Wrestling Alliance
Heavyweight Champion, Max “The Butcher” Bradley.
Max Bradley came out with a fury as Menelaus came out limping
VERY noticeably from his injury in an altercation just previous to this match with Nick Demola, a hated rival from MotR I.
Bradley came out to a mixed reaction and meat cleaver in hand. Menelaus began the match on the defensive. Perhaps the most shrewd defensive wrestler since a young Steve Austin made his way through the USWA, Menelaus found a counter to every power move “The Butcher” attempted. For every powerbomb, there was a faceslam. For every powerslam, there was a pushoff into a clothesline. Bradley became frustrated and took a quick exit to the outside, thinking he could get some time to plot… think again…
Menelaus ran to the apron on the side of the ring where Bradley stood and leapt off with a DEVASTATING leaping clothesline that smacked Bradley’s head right into the guardrail! Menelaus then threw Bradley into the ring and began a very simple barrage of moves to the back to set up for his finisher, “The Shadow
Driver”. Backbreakers and powerslams were the name of the game… until Bradley pushed Menelaus off on an attempt for his finisher and out of the ring.
Bradley then went to work with a chair on the outside, smashing it into Menelaus’s already-injured ankle repeatedly! He then followed that up with a PAINFUL T-Bone Suplex through the table that left the “Black Rose” unconscious. Bradley then hooked
Menelaus’s ankle up in the chair and climbed to the top rope as
Menelaus laid motionless on the broken table. Bradley leapt off the top rope and “Pillmanized” Martinez’s ankle, sending a sickening “CRACK” sound through the arena, along with Menelaus’s wailing. Bradley rolled Martinez into the ring and attempted to hook on his ankle-lock/crossface submission move called the “Rose City Ringer”… but Menelaus grabbed his head as he bent over and hooked the unsuspecting Bradley in an incredible small package out of nowhere! Bradley’s shoulders stayed on the mat for a three count and Menelaus moved on!
WINNER – Menelaus “The Black Rose” in 12:22. ***1/2 MATCH 3 Ricky Deathwish vs. Andrew “Flash” Tucker The war of words between these two top names in wrestling (one in the indies, one in the mainstream) came to a head in this matchup. The joke backstage was that you’d need four intros… two for the men and two more for their egos.
Deathwish came out to a ROUSING chorus of boos. Tucker actually came out to cheers, which seemed to perturb him a bit.
Deathwish immediately began with the mind games and the taunting, attempting to throw the short-tempered, short-minded
Tucker off from the beginning. He picks off every one of
Tucker’s quick moves and smacks him behind the head after each one, then proceeding to do a Steve Austin imitation double middle finger and a cheap imitation of beer drinking. Tucker then becomes enraged and charges at the quick thinking
Deathwish, who side steps the smaller man and tosses him over the top rope and to the outside, where the savage wrestling great gives Tucker a few chops along the guardrail and a cradle piledriver on the floor for good measure before rolling him back in and getting a two count. Meanwhile, old Deathwish enemy Nick
Demola makes his way down to ringside to take in the match.
Deathwish hits Tucker with a devastating suplex into a vertical neckbreaker for two again, but Tucker won’t quit. Instead, he counters a Samoan Drop into a crucifix and gets a two.
Deathwish tries to lay him out with a clothesline, but Tucker uses his quickness to duck, spring off the ropes, and hit an
Asai Moonsault for another two. This is when things get interesting. Nick Demola all of a sudden leaps up onto the ring apron with a steel chair in hand. Tucker whips Deathwish into
Nick, who swings at him. But Deathwish ducks under the shot and slides under the ring and stands on the ring apron next to
Nick. He kicks Nick in the stomach, sending him to the floor below. Tucker comes in and Deathwish swings at him, but Tucker ducks it. Tucker goes for a spinning heel kick into the chair, but Deathwish ducks that. Deathwish throws the chair at
Tucker’s head, leaps over the ropes, and catches a dazed Tucker with a sunset flip. The referee counts to three and Deathwish advances!
WINNER – Ricky Deathwish in 8:31. *** MATCH 4 “The Iceman” Jeff Westfall vs. “Iron Man” Eddie Jacobs Westfall, one of the foremost tacticians in the sport today, got set for his match against the former INWI World Champion, Eddie
Jacobs. Again, this was a battle of indy vs. big time, where Westfall was picked by some in the indy leagues to take it all the way, and Jacobs was more of the “household name”.
Westfall, forever the heel, came out to an absolutely DEAFENING chorus of boos. Jacobs followed and got some of the same treatment. This was definitely NOT going to be your grandfather’s “Golden Age of Wrestling” match, that’s for certain.
Following a collar-and-elbow tieup, Jacobs got the cheap shotting started with a knee to the groin. He continued his offense by whipping the UUW and IWA World Champion into the turnbuckle and charging at him with a high knee to the ribs. He continued to work on the ribs, and then summarily tossed
Westfall outside the ring so he could stand in the ring alone and thrive in the boos that rained down upon him from the capacity crowd.
But the cock-sure Jacobs didn’t take into account the tenacity of “The Iceman”. Westfall slid back in the ring almost immediately and turned the gloating Jacobs around, delivering a barrage of punches. Jacobs traded punches with him for a time, and got the upper hand with another low blow.
Jacobs then went to the top rope for a flying bodypress, but was caught in a devastating powerslam. He then whipped “Iron Man” into the ropes and gave him an absolutely stellar Tiger Driver variation called “Ice Pole ‘91” that sealed the deal rather quickly and led to Westfall advancing to the second round to meet up with his longtime friend and stablemate Ricky Deathwish.
WINNER – Jeff Westfall in 4:29. *1/2 MATCH 5 “Bad Eye” McBaine vs. “Britain’s Nightmare” Alexander Lawson This would be the final match for the indy great Lawson. He had a three-year long feud with McBaine that had bad blood still boiling to this day, and they decided to settle it right on MotR II.
Lawson came out first to a chorus of boos. McBaine didn’t even wait for entrance music. He just tore down the rampway and into the ring to start the match.
It didn’t go much longer than that. McBaine charged at Lawson and leveled him with two consecutive clotheslines. He lifted “Britain’s Nightmare” on his shoulders and gloated a bit, then delivered a NASTY Death Valley Driver that caught Lawson right on his head. “Bad Eye” then hooked on his patented “Blinders” finisher, a camel clutch/sleeper combination submission move, and Lawson took about five seconds to tap out.
Lawson left the ring to some cheers for his past accomplishments as he went off into retirement. McBaine moved into the second round.
WINNER – Bad Eye McBaine in :39. *3/4 MATCH 6 Sabbath vs. Jeff “Madfox” Matthews The MotR I vet, Sabbath, drew yet another tough matchup for a first rounder in the ring vet, Jeff Matthews. On the first show he fought eventual winner Jason Keening. Now he was taking on the man who many said was the consensus pick to take it all the way in wrestling great, “Madfox” Matthews.
Sabbath, the New Wave Wrestling Association’s Television Champion at the time, actually came out the face in this one, with much respect from the MotR faithful in Madison Square
Garden. Matthews was absolutely booed and hissed, since Sabbath was such an overwhelming fan favorite.
Matthews began the match by showing a great technical display, which proved his experience difference was indeed going to be a factor in this one. Sabbath attempted to brawl his way out of it, but to no avail. Matthews continuously pounded on the young guy’s leg, making him hobble just two minutes into the match.
Matthews began to get a little cocky, though, and that was the opening Sabbath needed to make everything go his way. On a
Figure Four Leglock attempt, Sabbath tossed Matthews outside the ring by his hair, which bought the younger competitor time to shake off the cobwebs and come up with a better battleplan. He slid under the ropes and came outside to brawl a bit, smashing
Matthews’ skull into the ringpost repeatedly, then tossing him into the guardrail and giving him a barrage of kicks for his troubles. Sabbath rolled Matthews into the ring and had it all under control.
But Matthews showed his mettle by delivering a devastating
Russian Legsweep to Sabbath when he got back in the ring. He then climbed to the second rope and hit an elbowdrop, which got a two count. He then attempted a La Majistral Cradle, but only got a two out of that one as well. Thinking the match was well in hand, he sent Sabbath to the ropes to give him a Samoan Drop… but Sabbath timed the move right and hit his finisher, a variation of the Rocker Dropper called “The Coffin Breaker”. He then got the three count and the right (sentence?) of fighting
Bad Eye McBaine for the right to go to the finals.
WINNER – Sabbath in 7:19. **1/2 MATCH 7 Menelaus vs. BYE Menelaus “The Black Rose” moved into the Master’s Cell final match via a bye from the earlier Keening/Martinez draw.
WINNER – Menelaus “The Black Rose” in 0:00 (BYE) MATCH 8 Ricky Deathwish vs. “The Iceman” Jeff Westfall These two cornerstone members of Generation Genesis, the premier stable in all of independent wrestling, have been the cornerstones for some of the greatest feuds of all time, dating as far back as 1994. Now they would get in the ring on opposite sides for only the third time in their illustrious careers.
Deathwish entered the ring with some cheers, and looked a bit disturbed that the fans would cheer for him. Westfall was his usual heel self and got engulfed in boos.
They began the match by shaking hands, then simultaneously trying to low blow each other… which got laughter going from both them and the crowd. Deathwish extended his hand again, but
Westfall wasn’t having it. After a minute of posturing, they got in a collar-and-elbow tieup and Westfall inched Deathwish into the corner. He then hit “The Mangler” with a series of european uppercuts that sent Deathwish straggling out of the corner. He hooked Deathwish’s leg and arm and took him over with a terrifying T-Bone Suplex… that Deathwish landed on his feet from! Amazing show of agility! He then leapfrogged over the charging Westfall just enough to get his legs wrapped around
Westfall’s neck, slide down his back, and hook the legs for an innovative pinning combo that got a two-count. Westfall then made his way to his feet and Deathwish took him right back down with a legsweep faceslam. Again a quick cover, again a two count.
No breathing room was given in this match. Both men traded counter after counter, pinning combo after pinning combo… and each man getting his share of “ALMOST A WIN!” calls from the announcers while they were at it. Westfall finally gained the momentum by hitting Deathwish with a running inverted bulldog that sent “The Mangler’s” head into the ground with a very audible thud. Westfall then set his partner up for his finisher, the variation of the Tiger Driver… but Deathwish wasn’t having that either and countered it by lifting Westfall up for a painful backdrop driver!
Deathwish then hooked a full nelson on Westfall and ran towards the ropes. He bounced off the turnbuckles, leapt in the air, and DROVE Westfall’s face into the mat! He kept the full nelson on and was hoping for a submission… but Westfall is a fighter.
Exhausted and now busted open, he reached the ropes after about
45 seconds in the painful hold. Deathwish was in shock! As soon as Westfall reached a knee, Deathwish went for a clothesline, but Westfall ducked and hit a Rock Bottom out of nowhere! He got a VERY CLOSE two count and thought he had the win! As soon as he got up, though, he was met up with the Belly to Belly into a Spinebuster move called “The Barbie Doll” and was all of a sudden laid out for a three count. Deathwish became the second man to advance to the MotR II finals!
WINNER – Ricky Deathwish in 17:18. ****3/4 MATCH 9 “Bad Eye” McBaine vs. Sabbath The battle between two “enigmas” was an intriguing one. With the innovative Deathwish and the gory Bradley already in the final matchup… these two men who thrive on pain would only add to the potential bloodfest that was sure to arise in the cage in the three way final!
McBaine came out the heel in a big time way. He taunted the crowd on the way to the ring, with chants of “Show us your
OneEye” dominating the crowd. Sabbath got a rousing face pop to start and seemed energized by his “upset” win over wrestling great Jeff Matthews earlier in the night.
Much like earlier, McBaine wasted no time getting to his opponent. A series of clotheslines was followed by lifting
Sabbath on his shoulders for a Death Valley Driver. But nothin’ doin’ this time, as Sabbath had scouted that pattern. He slipped off McBaine’s shoulders and gave him a barrage of rights and lefts to the jaw that had the big man reeling. He leveled
McBaine with a clothesline that took the “one-eyed one” over the top rope and right into the guardrail, where he actually hit one knee!
Sabbath then crawled under the bottom rope to follow. He knew it was going to take everything he had to take this monster down, so he grabbed a steel chair and started to go to work. He clobbered McBaine on the head with the chair not once… not twice… not three times… but FOUR TIMES until the big man finally busted open and fell down! Sabbath then grabbed McBaine and tossed him violently into the steel ringsteps and choked him with a cable from a television camera, then rolled him in the ring.
Sabbath saw that he had the momentum, and knew he should finish the match off quickly. He saw the opening for a flying clothesline and leapt onto the top rope as McBaine struggled to his feet. But before the youngster could fly off the top rope, the grizzled ring vet McBaine rattled the top rope and crotched
Sabbath on the top turnbuckle. McBaine then pressed Sabbath off the top rope and climbed up himself. He came off the top with a leap and SMASHED Sabbath’s face with a kneedrop that he calls “Vision Revision”. McBaine got the cover for three and he moved on to the final match against Deathwish and the surprising
Menelaus.
WINNER – Bad Eye McBaine in 8:00. *** Got questions? Get answers over the phone at Keen.com. Up to 100 minutes free! http://www.keen.com
