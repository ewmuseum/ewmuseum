DWF/MotR — Rules

DWF FAQ/Application Version 4.1

22 August 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Oh hell yes. After months of deliberation and planning, and a few choice words of prodding, the DWF is coming back. I've run MotR IV and MotR V since the last time this fed opened, and it's going to be a blast. Cards come out every two weeks. PPVs every three months, with semi-PPVs on halfway through each cycle. All roleplays are put on the shows. The DWF will be centered in Tucson, Arizona and focus on the southwest U.S. And, most controversial of all, handlers will be allowed to write their own matches with their opponent's approval. If they put themselves way over in the match... the "Lord Raphio Rule" applies. See the FAQ below for info on that. All the favorite parts of the DWF will return. The Atlantic Storm title. The Hardcore Bag of Goodies. The squash matches and descriptive jobbers. The loose-to-DQ referees. I've got matchwriters aboard, so read the FAQ, fill out the app near the bottom of it, and let's have fun. I can't promise everyone who applies will get in, since I'm keeping the roster small, but you can go ahead and fill out an app and I promise a reply. Thanks, Nick DWF FAQ/Application Version 4.1 1. Basic DWF Information A) – What the acronym “DWF” stands for In the past, it stood for Demola Wrestling Federation, DownSouth Wrestling Federation, and Darkside Wrestling Federation. Now it stands for nothing. It is simply the DWF. B) - Mission Statement The Darkside Wrestling Federation is the alternative to the high-politic, low-output federations that rule the roost in e-wrestling today. I will come right out and say it a million times… the handlers make this federation. They are the lifeblood of the federation. They will come up with the material and ideas, and I will come up with entertaining cards and shows as well as chipping in my own ideas to create one great final product. In-character, the founder and the Commissioner of the DWF is Nick Demola ( [detail withheld] ), one of the most experienced wrestlers out there. His goal, his only goal, is to have the best brand of wrestling and the best talent to participate in that brand of wrestling. He sold his shares in MotR, Inc. to buy BattleGround Arena outright and move it to Tucson, Arizona. He will not appear on television much, if at all. In real life terms, the fedhead is [name withheld], with an e-fed career spanning about 150 feds and five years (on June 7 it’s five years). You ask it, he knows it pretty much. He is the head booker and the main matchwriter. The DWF Attitude is simply that the federation is the best in the world… even if they stay out of the spotlight. They have a legion of fans that follow them everywhere, to every gym or arena the cards may play out in, and to the shows that are usually held in BattleGround Arena in Tucson, Arizona. the cards play out in. They have a national TV contract with SportsChannel America to air at midnight every two weeks. If you have great talent, the desire, and the personal character that the DWF strives for, then you will have a blast here. If you look to want to dictate your terms, and want to lay back on a reputation, you will be in for a rude awakening here. B) - History The DWF actually got its start as a backyard wrestling federation in 1990 in Sacramento, California. It finally hit the internet in late 1997 and crowned its first net World Champion in early 1998 in Brian “The Terminator” Perry. Many legends have walked through the ropes of the DWF. Morgan de Sade, Chris Courtade, Jeremy Locke, Dave Pietka, Tommy Grayson, Nytro, Mark Marleau, Havoc Hate, Lord Raphio, and many more have all been a part of this great federation. Combine these “names” with the tremendous talent of men like Mike Waldrop, Robert Mula, Bob Muretic, Damien Orenthal Anderson, Desolation Row, and others and you have a hotbed for tremendous e-wrestling action… which is exactly what this place has always been. The DWF closed for a two year period, then re-opened with maybe its most talented roster ever in 2000. After a great opening supercard, the Master of the Ring III pay per view really put the federation on the map… with DWF World Champion Chris Courtade beating superstars such as Rick Styles, Bad Eye McBaine, and William Craven to end up the Ultimate Master of that event. MotR III parlayed into two of the greatest cards of all time, in MotR IV and MotR V. From Brody Thunder to Dan Kauffman to Alex Martinez to Chris Courtade... almost every wrestler worth a damn has walked through an MotR curtain. But now a new chapter is being written off of the profits of MotR. Now the DWF re-opens for the last time, with a plan for the long haul and a vision for the future that cannot be matched. It will run in the seldom-used Arizona/New Mexico/Colorado/Texas/Oklahoma territory. 2. Cards, Shows, and Schedule A) - DWF BattleGround BattleGround aims to be a four to five match show that runs about 150k per show. Short enough to read in one sitting, and long enough to allow for angle developments and for characters to get themselves over. It comes out every two weeks. All roleplays will be a part of BattleGround cards. B) - Supercards There is one Supercard every three months. The four supercards are listed below… Mid/Late January – The Big Chill (all titles defended) Late April – On The Cutting Edge (DoomDraw Tournament for to-be-determined title) Late July – Master of the Ring (12 man invitational tournament and other matches) Late October – Dance at the Slaughterhouse (THE Supercard. Feuds settled, things move on, etc.) D) - Schedule Cycles run in the following manner. Two BattleGround cards (two weeks apart for each card, remember), a mini-supercard entitled “High Noon Showdown”, then two more cards, an extra week off to write (with a huge Talk is Cheap right in the middle) and then the Supercard. That makes a 13 week cycle. Sample Cycle: Sun August 5th – Roleplay/strat deadline Sun August 12th - BattleGround Sun August 19th – Roleplay/strat deadline Sun August 26th - BattleGround Sun September 2nd – Roleplay/strat deadline Sun September 9th – High Noon Showdown mini-supercard Sun September 16th – Roleplay/strat deadline Sun September 23rd – BattleGround Sun September 30th – Roleplay/strat deadline Sun October 7th – BattleGround Sun October 14th – Roleplay/strat deadline Thu October 18th – HUGE Talk is Cheap PPV Press Conference show Sun October 28st – Dance at the Slaughterhouse Sun November 4th – Roleplay/strat deadline Sun November 11th - BattleGround Etc... 3. Title Information A) - DWF World Heavyweight Title Everyone in the federation can win this title. This is the top singles title. B) - DWF Atlantic Storm Title The secondary singles title. Every Atlantic Storm Title match has to have a special stipulation, whether it be a tag match, a ladder match, a cage match... whatever you want. It must be defended every card. C) - DWF Hardcore Bag of Goodies The Hardcore Bag of Goodies is defended in falls count anywhere, no DQ/no countout matches. The DWF Hardcore Champion (and holder of the Hardcore Bag of Goodies) chooses the contents of the knapsack sized bag. Whatever you want, strat for it and it’ll be in the bag. D) - DWF World Tag Team Titles The top and only Tag Title. Any two men can win it. 4. Handler Stuff A) - Roleplaying Roleplays air on every BattleGround show, which occur every two weeks. Roleplays are due on the off week between BG shows. There’s no “YOU MUST ROLEPLAY!!!!!!!” rule, but if you’re inactive you will be removed. B) - Strats Don’t do move-for-move, tedious strats. Point out what you want your character to do to get him over. Sneak attack plans go here. Match plans go here. C) - Angle Creation Read this carefully. I won’t place you in an angle. If you want an angle, go get it. I’ll give you any addies you want, just go get it. I’ll throw in my two cents after you show me the angle you have, but it’s your baby and yours alone. D) – Matchwriting This will be the most controversial part of the new DWF. I am allowing handlers to write their own matches for the week. The matches must be about 10-15k and should put over all men in the match and follow all strats. Mostly, I’ll let people involved in angles write their own matches... since they know how they need to put everyone over to make the feud run well. If you fuck around and get smart and put yourself over too much in a match, you get to face off with... E) – The “Lord Raphio Rule” The rule is simple. If you put yourself over too much in a match, the card immediately following will see your character scheduled against Lord Raphio, who is Satan reincarnate. You will be jobbed and sacrificed to the demon lord and fired on that card. No bullshitting here. So if you’re going to write your own match, please make sure you do it fairly. I’ll give everyone the benefit of the doubt, but if you write a match where the opponent gets zero offense and the crowd chants your character’s name following a headlock... Lord Raphio will be waiting the following week. F) – Squash Matches If you aren’t booked for a regular match, I invite all handlers to write their own “squash matches” for the card. They should be about 5-8k and should feature one of the Jobbers listed in the section below against your wrestler. Write me at [detail withheld] to book a jobber for a squash match so they don’t get double-booked and take too much of a beating on one night. 5.
