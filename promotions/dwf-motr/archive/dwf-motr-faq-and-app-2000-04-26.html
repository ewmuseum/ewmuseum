DWF/MotR War of Attrition - Master of the Ring III F.A.Q. 1) Mission Statement 2) History 3) Rules 4) Card and Roleplay Shows a) War of Attrition b) Talk is Cheap 5) Participation a) Roleplays (amount, shows) b) In-rings c) Strats 6) How do we operate? 7) Titles 8) Fictional Faces 9) In closing Mission Statement This Supercard is designed for a couple things. To give some of the best handlers and wrestlers in the world overdue exposure, and to have a blast doing what we all love to do. No corruption. No politics. No preconceived notions of winners. This is about fun, not winning.
Though winning with this field is nice… The founder/CEO of the DWF and the co-Commissioner of Master of the Ring is Nick Demola ( [detail withheld] ), one of the most experienced wrestlers out there. His goal, his only goal, is to have the best brand of wrestling and the best talent to participate in that brand of wrestling.
The other co-Commissioner of Master of the Ring is Big Dick McGee. He was named to this post for MotR I and II and has remained in power, despite having a difference of opinion regarding the direction of the tournament. He prefers the theatrics of it all and suggests rather bizarre stipulations for matches.
In real life terms, the fedhead is [name withheld], with an e-fed career spanning about 100 feds and four years (on July 7 it’ll be four years). You ask it, he knows it pretty much.
He is the head booker and the matchwriter. That’s right, saving special circumstances he is the ONLY matchwriter. Tough task, but much better for all involved.
History Master of the Ring is a Supercard that has been held once before, and planned twice before. The first Master of the Ring had a final match of Ethan de Sade against Menelaus against the eventual winner, “Screaming Drillbit” Jason Keening. They competed in a
Master’s Cell final match, establishing a precedent that still goes on to this day. The SuperBout on that card was an epic Lumberjack Match pitting LOCO and “Lone Wolf” Brody Thunder on opposite sides of the ring.
MotR II never quite got off the ground, but had a stellar cast of characters. All unresolved issues from MotR II will be settled at MotR
III… with Bad Eye McBaine, Ricky Deathwish, and returning finalist Menelaus battling for the top honor. We’re still in negotiations to bring in Setzer Van Strife to fight James Pillow in a SuperBout rematch.
The DWF was based in the South region of the United States (Virginia south and Mississippi River west, saving occasional jaunts into Texas)
and does not come out of that region. Rules DWF/MotR will allow whatever characters you want, but be aware that no matter how "impossible" it is to beat them, if you don't roleplay your guy is dead. You may have an 8 foot monster, but be aware that I'm a prick about powerful guys and I love to knock them down.
You can only submit one singles wrestler or one tag team in the card. Only the best characters and apps with the best quality will be chosen. DON'T use another player's wrestler, unless there has been an OK by the other party and by the President. Furthermore, don't ask for your wrestler to hosptialize another wrestler unless there has been consent from the other party and the President.
DON'T attack announcers or any of the DWF staff, unless there has been express permission by the President. You can attack as many no name jobbers as you want.
Cards ALL matches are written by Nick Demola and the card will take place from July 10-13. It’s a four day event with about 30 matches in total. It will feature the final DWF pay per view mixed in with Master of the Ring Tournament matches from MotR II and III.
- War of Attrition The biggest pay per view of the DWF’s year will be its last. Combine the greatest roster in wrestling with legends and champions of other federations and you get an EXPLOSIVE combination.
Talk is Cheap Every Friday night for four weeks, from May 26th to June 16th will be the clip show, Talk is Cheap. This is where people will interview, be put in scenarios, and show their stuff.
Participation Roleplays, In-rings, Strats, and Angles fall under the Participation category. Roleplays will be the deciding factor as to who wins... but the other things will definately tally points for you.
- Roleplays There is one DWF/MotR roleplay show, called "Talk is Cheap". It occurs on Friday nights at 11:59pm sharp. If you want to not job horribly, roleplay every week. For any shot to win, roleplay WELL every week.
There is no limit to how many you can do per week, though they will be graded on quality, not quantity. Use only your own characters or DWF announcers... no using other characters.
- In-rings Strongly encouraged. Don't have to be long, just have to add to the card. Will help in your chances of winning.
-Strats Again, strongly encouraged. They will be used to their fullest extent and will help your chances of winning. Need help with what should be in strats? Write the fedhead. All strats are due by June 9th… giving me time to write the 30 some odd matches we’re going to have.
How does the DWF/MotR operate? DWF/MotR is a ''booked'' supercard. Meaning the match results are predetermined by the DWF/MotR council (basically, Nick Demola and Big
Dick McGee). Roleplaying is the key to sucess in this supercard. Angles play a big part too, but if you roleplay better, you win. Period. Strats will have a minor effect and are used more as an outline for how a handler wants the match to run and how a handler wants his wrestler to get put over by the writer.
DWF Titles DWF Southern Title - The main title of the federation. The holder of this title is the top man in the DWF.
DWF Atlantic Storm Title - The secondary title of the federation. Relaxed rules and loose DQ's are the stipulations for any matches for this championship. Also, there is always a special circumstance to every Atlantic Storm title match (such as a ladder match, cage match, etc.).
DWF Television Title - Defended every week, regardless of what match the champion wrestles in.
DWF Southern Tag Team Titles - The only tag team title. Fictional Faces and Places DWF Founder/President: Nick Demola - veteran of feds, GOD of Wrestling, etc. MotR Commissioner: Big Dick McGee - has somewhat of a jealous streak against Nick Demola… DWF Backstage Interviewer: "Scheme" Gene Dunderhead - absolute dork who just likes the spotlight once in awhile
DWF Ringside Announcers: Stuart Jennings - Face/Jim Ross-type Juan Vasquez - Heel/Jerry Lawler-type DWF Ring Announcer: Mitchell Harris Butler - also the historian of the fed. DWF Referees: Paul Pierce (main ref) - Refs all main events and big matches. A veteran ref who always calls it clean and tries to stay out of the way.
Slow to DQ... but who in the DWF isn't? Brian Johnson (secondary ref) - A total wimp. Pencilnecked geek if there ever was one. Jason Allen (rookie ref) - Takes NO shit. WILL knock out someone at the drop of a hat. Do _NOT FUCK_ with this guy.
Brian Hunter (rookie ref) - one half of one of the great tag teams of all time, Desolation Row. Will take shit from nobody and will be quick to hit with martial arts if anyone starts shit up. Will only DQ in extreme circumstances.
In closing (out of character): I'm putting a lot of pressure on myself to make sure that this supercard is the best damn supercard on the planet. And I can damn well guarantee that you're going to have one of the best experiences of your careers here. If you're in the fed, always know that I will answer any mail, any question you have at any time.
Thanks.
