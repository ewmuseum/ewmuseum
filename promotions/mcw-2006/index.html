A forum-hosted federation on the FW Central hub whose own mission statement promised a stage for wrestlers overshadowed by sibling feds' recycled main-eventers, and whose title lineages ran through an on-air defection, a battle royal, and a rename before its own operator declared it dormant. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Major Championship Wrestling opened with a mission statement posted 13 March 2006, its own operator pitching a fed deliberately built around wrestlers passed over elsewhere: anyone who currently or formerly held a world title in the FW Central hub's other promotions was barred from applying with that character, on the stated principle that recycling the same main-eventers across every fed “cheapens the game.”

Its own first World Heavyweight Champion was crowned at “Zero Hour,” a supercard from the Staples Center in Los Angeles, after the title's prior holder, Bryan Storms, was stripped for an on-air defection to a rival federation rather than losing it in the ring; Chandler Maxwell won the resulting battle royal for the vacant belt. The promotion changed hands that same spring, with a new pair of operators taking over day-to-day running in April 2006.

On 30 January 2007, the promotion rebranded from Major Championship Wrestling to Midwest Championship Wrestling under the same MCW initials, relocating its own headquarters to Springfield, Missouri and pivoting toward independent, on-the-road cards over its earlier national television ambitions. Under the new banner it crowned a North American Champion through an eight-man tournament and awarded a Legend belt to two wrestlers at once after their sixty-minute Ironman match ended in a draw.

The North American Championship was declared retired by the promotion's own title history document on 28 January 2007, with no reason given. On 29 May 2007, its own operator announced the fed dormant, citing more than a year without the time to keep running it.
