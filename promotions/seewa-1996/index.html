A February 1996 regional promotion built around a weekly 'Showdown' broadcast, launched by the producer of the prior year's Blue Heaven Open supercard as a smaller-scale, southeastern-focused successor. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Southeastern Electronic Wrestling Association introduced itself on 20 February 1996 by trading on its producer's own résumé: “From the mind that brought you.. Blue Heaven Open '95 (Thought by many as the BEST e-wrestling ppv EVER)... From the former Head Booker of NCW.. Golden Armlock Award Winner (Best E-Wrestling Fed of 1995)... Bringing you TOP NOTCH regional e-wrestling! Starting next week!” It billed itself as an NWA federation and promised a smaller, more focused successor to that prior supercard.

Its first Showdown broadcast, five days later, opened before a claimed 18,000 fans at the Lawrence Joel Memorial Coliseum in Winston-Salem, North Carolina, called by a three-man announce team of Preston Payne, Carlos Lyons and Mr. Clayton Barnes. On camera, the promotion's own booking figure, Mad Money Murphy, announced two singles titles (the SeEWA Southern States Championship and the SeEWA Television Championship) and one tag title (the SeEWA Southern Tag Team Championship), deciding the first contenders by a hat draw rather than a tournament — “A tournament can be long, drawn-out, and boring. This way, one match, you have your champion” — with purses of $100,000 for each Southern States finalist and $50,000 for the Television finalist.

That first card ran six matches: IC Kool defeated Slick Matt Richards; the Twin Peaks (Olympus and Everest) put away the Dynamic Duo (Daniel Arrogance and Paul Pompous) with a top-rope spiked piledriver; Hashimoto Tengen and Dragon-San, managed by Billy Fan, beat Search & Destroy by submission after a kendo-stick strike from ringside; Chuck Disco defeated Rano with a hold the broadcast named Saturday Night Fever; the Stancil Brothers' match against the Psycho Twins ended in disqualification when the Twin Peaks ran in and brawled with both teams; and Big Body Brown closed the show over Petor “Stalin” Andresku on an assist from the Stancils, who held Andresku's legs down through the pin. The broadcast named Brown, the Stancils and their allies as part of a stable calling itself the Black Power Movement. A drawing held during the broadcast set the promotion's first three title matches — Southern States, Southern Tag Team and Television — for its next Showdown, from the Baltimore Arena.

How it ended isn't in the surviving record. Its surviving documents run only to that first Showdown broadcast.
