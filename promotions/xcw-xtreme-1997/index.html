A real-roster fantasy-booking federation staging the working 1997 WWF, ECW and WCW rosters under Jim Ross and Joey Styles' own announce team, running at least fourteen months by its own later broadcasts. Four title lineages and sixteen shows survive. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

X-Treme Championship Wrestling introduced itself on 7 September 1997 with a boast, not a pitch: “Are you sick and tired of joining feds that close after three weeks or even three months?… X-Treme is a fed that can back up it's words… We're the best Fed in all of Fantasy Wrestling.” It promised two weekly cards (Monday Night RAGE and Thursday X-Treme) and a pay-per-view every other month, “to make the angles and story lines HOT, HOT, HOT for the PPV's we do have.”

Its surviving 1997 broadcasts ran under a different weekly name, Monday Night MALICE, called by Jim Ross and Joey Styles over an entirely real-wrestler roster. On 17 November, World Champion Sting retained by disqualification over Davey Boy Smith after Bret Hart, newly arrived and already resentful of his reception in the promotion, ran in against his old rival. Three weeks later a debuting Taz confronted Sting with a signed contract for a title match, calling Dean Malenko's United States Championship the only belt on the roster “worth a damn” — on-air business the fed's own “Prez,” Paul Roma, presided over in character between matches.

By its next surviving broadcast, 6 February 1998, the promotion crowned a first XCW Television Champion: Chris Jericho over Shane Douglas, the finish turning on a mid-match heel betrayal by Shawn Michaels.

Its own September 1999 handbook describes a relaunch: its president wrote that he had shut down his previous real-wrestler promotion after burning out its own players, took a break, and returned “with the intro to the WOW chance, and the web page, and the fire inside of me.” The relaunched XCW kept the same four titles — World, United States, Television, and World Tag Team — and the same two-card week, Monday Night Malice (Jim Ross and Paul Roma announcing) and Thursday Night X-Treme (Jerry Lawler and Magnum T.A.).

Its own record shows the same flagship show still running years later, into 2003 and 2004, crowning champions across pay-per-views named Superclash, Wrestlecade, Night Of Destruction, and Clash Of The Titans; a World Tag Team Championship, introduced by tournament at Superclash I in July 2003, belonged to Al Snow and Bob Holly by the end of the surviving record, and the Television Championship passed through Goldberg, Taz, and Jeff Jarrett before Randy Orton took it from Orlando Jordan on 11 October 2004. A recruiting post that May had its own president dating the promotion's run to 1996, a year earlier than its own attested September 1997 founding.

No closing statement survives anywhere in the record; its last surviving document, dated 13 October 2004, is an ordinary card, not a farewell.
