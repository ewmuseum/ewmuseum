The All Star Wrestling Association is a fantasy based wrestling league. The ASWA is based out of San Francisco, CA and most cards will originate from there. However, if the ASWA kicks off right, we will travel throughout North
America and make special appearances in Canada and Mexico as well. 2. Who runs this place? a) Fictional President The fictional President of the ASWA is Jake Albright. Albright is the man who envisioned the ASWA and put all his money and time into creating the league and scouting talent. He's the man that signs they pay checks and books the matches. If you piss him off, you're screwed.
b) Real President The Real President of the ASWA is [name withheld]. [name withheld] has been around this game for nearly 4 years now and knows the ropes rather well. This time around, it'll be him and him alone in running the federation. He plans on having small cards, with big implications.
3. How a handler succeeds in the ASWA? A handler doesn't succeed by talking to the President week in and week out. Kissing ass won't help, either. However, if you do the work and participate, that's a good 50% chance of winning your matches. Sending in a flash per week is a good start. Sending in a strat is even better. Giving angle ideas for your character is also a good way to succeed in this league. But remember one rule, "You can't win if you don't roleplay!"
4. What style does the ASWA incorporate? The ASWA uses the ABS System. ABS stands for Action Bracket System. Basically, ABS is a format where the action is written inside brackets and the commentators comment on the action going on. For example.
[Wrestler 1 goes into the ropes and off of the rebound, wrestler 2 hits wrestler 1 with a vicious clothesline that sends wrestler 1 to the mat hard.]
SR: Good lord! Vicious clothesline by Wrestler 2! What strength! CO: Where's that damned hot dog vendor? Oh, good move by Wrestler 2. And that's your basic idea of ABS. If I need match writers, all are expected to follow that type of format.
5. Where do we "travel?" All Star Wrestling Association will wrestle its first few months in the Bay Area. After those first few months, the league will be re-evaluated and if it has been successful in that time, ASWA will start to go out of the Bay
Area and into other states. We'll start off small, and go big as time goes on. But in order to do that, we'll need help from each and every handler.
6. How are matches decided? ASWA matches will be booked by a human. We won't use simulators to decide the winners, all matches are decided by yours truly. The way you win your matches are in the following order.
1) Flashes 2) Strats 3) Angles 4) Participation Flashes are the most important part of the ASWA. This is what the entire roster will see from you. If you have an awesome flash, you are more than likely going to win your match. That's not guaranteed, but it's a good indicator. If there is an angle going on at the time, even though you might be better with your flash, you may lose. That's just the way it works.
Strats will be used to help the match writers out. This is a big help to the writers [myself] and helps me figure out what you plan on doing with your character. These are also good tie breakers if the flashes were pretty close. It's a very good idea to get strats in. The ideal strat sheet for the
ASWA would look something like this. Wrestler's Name: Finisher Name & Description: Entrance: [Include Richard Jones' ring introduction, commentary, appearance, etc.] Match Plan: [Give me an overall strategy of what you want to do in the match.] Winning or Losing situation: [What your character will do in each] Angles are good to bring up to the fed head. It makes things much more interesting. If you ever have angle idea, feel free to bring it up to me and we'll work on an angle. Plus, it takes the burden off of my shoulders trying to put you into an angle.
Participation is also a big factor. Just sending in a flash is 50% of winning. But discussing angles and everything else discussed above will help you out as well. Participation is a key factor in making this league fun. If you have questions or comments, feel free to email me at anytime. Or even send me an instant message on AOL, ICQ or IRC. I'm always available to talk and discuss things.
7. ASWA Shows a) Tuesday Timebomb The timebomb is ticking and the ASWA is ready to let it explode. This is the show where the ASWA showcases all of its major talent. Title matches, grudge matches, debuts, you name it, it happens here. This is where the stuff is shown. You'll usually see around 4-5 matches on this show per week. Your hosts are Steve Russel and Chris Owens.
b) The Hot Seat This is where the superstars of the ASWA will speak their minds. This show is usually on Saturday's or Sunday's and will include commentary by Steve
Russel and Chris Owens, the two commentators of Tuesday Timebomb. c) Supercards These are where the ASWA lets it all hang out. All the titles are on the line. Your feuds come to an end. This is the stuff that the ASWA is made of.
You want the best, an ASWA Supercard is where you'll get it. Supercards happen whenever I see fit, which usually happens once every 2-3 months.
8. ASWA Personalities a) Steve Russel - Russel is a retired sports announcer and journalist based in the San Francisco area. The ASWA got on the phone and gave Russel a ring and he couldn't pass up the opportunity at this. So he took the job as lead play by play man. He typically will cheer the faces of the league and likes the "good" in wrestling.
b) Chris Owens - Chris Owens is a younger white man. Heavy set, and constantly makes references to women and food and not about actual wrestling. Basically, he's the comic relief during matches. He'll do crazy acts to get attention. He doesn't care about the fans and cheers for all the
"bad" in wrestling. c) Richard Jones - Jones is the ring announcer in the ASWA. He has a really deep, bellowing voice that echos throughout the arena. Many ladies find it sexy, the guys like his style of introductions. He does it right and is typically laid back when he does his introductions.
d) Dan Stevenson - Stevenson is the ASWA's head interview guy. He's around 6 '2 or so, average build. He asks the questions that the viewers want answers to. You don't really mess with Stevenson, unless you're pretty ballsey.
e) Thomas Winters - Head Referee of ASWA. He's rather large in stature and isn't pushed around by wrestlers. If he's pushed, he'll push back.harder! He doesn't take too kindly to cocky wrestlers either. He'll call the matches the way he seems them and right down the middle. Totally unbiased, very strict.
f) Jim Black - Black is a smaller official. He gets pushed around a lot and cannot really enforce the rules. He is basically forced to let anything go because if he calls for a DQ or countout, he usually gets beaten up.
g) Frank Rose - Rose is a somewhat larger man, around 6'3 or so and is very thick in his build. He is more so of a "Bad" referee and allows things to go once in awhile when the man breaking the rule should obviously be DQ'd. He'
ll favor the bad guys, and totally shaft the good. 9. ASWA Championships a) ASWA Heavyweight Championship If you hold this title, you're DA MAN! [And no, not Bill Goldberg] You will defend this title at least every 30 days AND at all Supercards.
b) ASWA Western Championship This is the ASWA secondary title. This is the number two guy in the company. This title is defended every week and at all ASWA Supercards. c) ASWA Lightweight Championship This is the belt for the lightweight guys. 220 and under qualifies. This belt is defended once every 30 days and at all Supercards.
d) ASWA Tag Team Titles These belts signify that you're the best team in the ASWA. They will be defended every 30 days and at all ASWA Supercards.
10. What are the basic rules of the ASWA? The number one rule of the ASWA is.ROLEPLAY! If you don't roleplay, you won' t win. I LOVE to write matches where one guy gets the living piss kicked out of him, so you better roleplay.
Another rule.YOU WILL LOSE! Don't come into the ASWA thinking you'll win every single match. Because you won't. If you have an ego, leave it at the door. I don't want egos to run amuck around here.
Flashes are due on Friday Nights. Doesn't matter when, just on Friday Night. The Flash show, Hot Seat, is on Saturday. But strats and in-rings are due on Friday as well so I can get matches ready and piece the card together over the weekend. When writing roleplays, always address them as so, "[ASWA] Mike Waldrop Flash" or whoever your character is and if it's a strat or in-ring. Keeps me organized. That should do it. Don't bitch or whine, or I'll drop you so quick, you won' t know what hit you. And that's it. 11.
