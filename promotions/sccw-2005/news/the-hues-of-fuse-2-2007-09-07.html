SCCW — Column

The Hues of FUSE #2

7 September 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Welcome to The Hues of FUSE! My name is Flux, and I’ll be your host along this journey. This edition will feature final thoughts on War In Washington (might I say wow), of course a few general thoughts and comments, and lest I forget, an interview with none other than Jonathan Rhine. Quite a nice fellow, might I add. Anyway, let’s get started. War In Washington was a great show! The Livewire Title once again changed hands. Is there no one in this company who can have a solid run with that belt? Step it up Cruise! With plenty of challengers we’ll see what you’ve got. Rest assured that El Diablo will be looking to reclaim his title, not to mention the match on Killzone 50 which guarantees someone a title shot. Something tells me that El Diablo and Jason Cruise have only just begun. I expect things to jump start coming out of Washington. I have to say, I’m disappointed that Aimz and Darcy Crisis weren’t able to pull off a victory against The Affliction members Lane Stevens and Jeb Stewart. It’s not that I was surprised, The Affliction has seemed to have free reign of FUSE as of late. It just would have been nice. Congratulations to the Cowboys From Hell on their successful Fatal Faction defense. I hear that there is talk of ending the Fatal Faction division, but can only hope that it survives. We have had some great tag team matches here in FUSE, and I think it would be a shame to deny not only the wrestlers in FUSE, but the fans, this great opportunity. Quite frankly, if it hadn’t been for the tower I think that Timo Bolamba and Jonathan Rhine would have stolen the show. Can you say ladder match? Now I know Rhine is tired of the stipulations tied to the Custom Chaos Championship, but you have to admit that was a great show these two put on. I can’t help but hope that Rhine can get through to “The Dastardly Bastard”. Can we expect a change on the horizon? Like him or hate him, Clinton Sage is the most deserving Universal Champion FUSE has ever seen. No matter the opponent, no matter the show, Sage has yet to back down from an opponent, or even feel his title reign threatened. Is there anyone who can stop this monster? Which brings us in to the final match of the night, The Tower. I’d like to start off by congratulating everyone involved, both rookies and veterans. This was an outstanding matchup. A few people disappointed me, Kid Cool and Czar. While a few people pleasantly surprised me, Sean Sterling, Mayson Colby, Ethan Frost, and Prince Pride. This was a HUGE night for Jonathan Rhine, with him not only retaining his Custom Chaos belt, but taking the big prize of the tower victory. I must say, I can’t wait for him to have a shot at the Universal Title. The introduction of The Highwaymen makes me feel all giddy inside. Finally someone is standing up to The Affliction! I can’t wait to see how this develops over the next few weeks. I’m sure we’ll see a few more wrestlers going to each side before this one is over. Feuds To Mark Out For Jason Cruise vs El Diablo- It’s personal. It’s now spread across stables. A Livewire rules match does not do these two men justice. I expect good things from both these men, and am looking forward to a future match with less constraining regulations. Sean Sterling vs Mayson Colby- I keep hearing the same feeling backstage…have your damn match already! The suspense is killing us. At this point, I can’t imagine this feud ending shy of one man killing the other. Clinton Sage vs Jonathan Rhine- Exactly the thing this company needs. Both of these men have brought a newfound respect to the Universal Title and Custom Chaos Championship, respectively. With the introduction of The Highwaymen, this becomes stable head vs stable head, which I absolutely love. Now I know you all have been waiting to hear what Jonathan Rhine has to say, so without further stalling, here it is. Flux: This is Flux, author of the Hues of FUSE and I was just wondering if you would be willing to take a few moments out of your time to grant me an interview, as we come out of the Pay Per View, War In Washington. Jonathan Rhine: No problem, Flex. Flux: It's Flux, Mr. Rhine. Flux. Anyway, let's get started. Jonathan Rhine: Sorry, Flux. I didn't mean to disrespect. Flux: Thank you, Mr. Rhine. Getting down to business, what were your feelings going in to War In Washington? Not only did you have your Custom Chaos Championship on the line, but you were also a competitor in possibly one of the most brutal matches in FUSE history. Jonathan Rhine: First off, thank you for this opportunity. It really all boils down to the reaction of the fans, and I like to know that, completely independent of the results, I have a group of people that cheer me on. Flux: Your fans certainly had something to cheer about, as you not only retained the CC title, but won it all in the tower match. What are your thoughts and feelings after WIW? Jonathan Rhine: Now, regarding the results, I am mixed with a sense of pleasant surprise and a sense of omniscience here. I'm pleased to have won, and very glad that it has opened up so many doors for me. At the same time, I know that I have the skills that it takes to survive in this business. So I can't tell you with complete honesty that I didn't expect to win. I know what I'm capable of. Flux: We appreciate your honesty. Jonathan Rhine: Still, that match was monstrous. There was so much talent in it, and the match itself was an innovative and brutal match. So I'm glad not only to have survived that match, but to have come out on top. Flux: Here's another shot to toot your own horn. Clinton Sage. We all know you are currently undefeated, but Sage has seemed unstoppable as of late. What are you going to pull out to win this one? Jonathan Rhine: Now, you ask me what I think of Clinton Sage. And again I'm torn as far as professionalism and personal feelings. As a professional, I am nothing if not in awe at Clinton Sage's current Universal Title reign. You can say what you want about his character, but he has sextupled the number of defenses that our longest reigning champ had. He is a defending champion, no doubt about that. Flux: Good luck to you in that match, by the way. No disrespect, but I do believe you may need it. Jonathan Rhine: The only thing is, Clinton Sage doesn't fight an honorable fight. He's a cheater. And honestly, that sort of practice is something I can't get behind. The Affliction, in short, has led to corruption in this business. And that's part of what I, and the rest of the Highwaymen, are out to fight against. We want to show that it is talent and drive, not illegitimate means and cunning, that truly reigns supreme in matches. Flux: Exactly what brings me to my next point. Everyone wants to hear about The Highwaymen. They took a stand against The Affliction at the end of WIW, and I have to say it's nice to see someone standing up to them. What can we expect in the future? Jonathan Rhine: You can expect change. You can expect more than having the Affliction logo open up every show. Than having every match an Affliction member is in end in hollow victories. Amy, Jason, Cyrus and I are going to keep working to make sure that the end of War In Washington wasn't a fluke, and every time The Affliction tries to pull any of its tricks, we'll be there. And hopefully the rest of the roster takes our cue. Flux: That’s very promising. Care to give us some insight in to the mind of Jonathan Rhine? How crazy do you have to be to be in this business? Jonathan Rhine: The world of FUSE is certainly a crazy one. I was brought up on a certain group of ideals, and it's a bit of a culture shock to see a world where there are so many different views. It's hard to keep afloat, but all I can do is be me, do the best I can, and hope everything works out for the best. Luckily, it seems that it has done so. Flux: We can only hope it continues to do so. My final question, with so many new comers to FUSE, what advice would you like to hand down to them? Jonathan Rhine: Before bidding you adieu, I do want to acknowledge the newcomers in FUSE and tell them that there is something being done about the state of wrestling. For everyone before who viewed the Affliction with scorn, you can know that I and my friends are coming up with plans to stop them. We are no longer in an era where the evil can run amok over what is good and right with wrestling. Don't worry about image, rookies. Don't worry about perception, about gimmicks, about any of that. Just come in, do what you are best at, and follow your heart. That's the best advice I can give. Flux: Thank you for your time Mr. Rhine. It's been a pleasure. Jonathan Rhine: Thank you for granting me this time, Flux. I hope I have answered your questions sufficiently. I'm always willing to talk with the company that's responsible for my ascent. There you have it, straight out of Jonathan Rhine’s mouth. That concludes this edition of The Hues Of FUSE. I hope you enjoyed it as much as I did. Until next time, I’ll be watching FUSE shine through. -Flux
