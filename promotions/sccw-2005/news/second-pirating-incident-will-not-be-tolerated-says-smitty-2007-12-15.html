Second Pirating Incident "Will Not Be Tolerated" Says Smitty

15 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
NEW ORLEANS, LA (4 days ago) It was a night of heroes and a night of villains; it was a night of winners and a night of losers. For Smitty T. Duluth's FUSE Wrestling, the DEFIANCE pay-per-view was also a night that saw repeated attempts to subvert the broadcast's signal. It was the second consecutive event to feature such an interruption, an event that has proven troubling to the many of the federation's backers. "Absolutely it concerns us," said a representative of one of FUSE's sister federations, speaking to this reporter only on the condition of anonymity. "If Smitty can't control the signal his trucks are broadcasting, there are questions that raises." When asked about the company's investigation into the broadcast signal intrusion incidents, FUSE Wrestling spokesperson Harold Francis was reticent to speak of any progress. "I can't speak about an ongoing internal investigation," Francis said, a far cry from his vow to the International Press on November 26th that he "had no doubt" the culprit would be caught. However, rumors swirl around the FUSE locker room about possible internal assistance given to the signal pirate, and the DEFIANCE event saw the appearance of an alleged on-site conspirator, a hooded woman calling herself "Trouble". In reaction to these events, it is widely believed Duluth may take expansive and wide-ranging action in the ensuing weeks to curtail the perpetrator of these videos. The next scheduled FUSE Wrestling event is FUSE Uproar: The Young Lions Tournament, scheduled to air the week before Christmas, again on the federation's home network of Showtime. "I cannot comment on anything that Mr. Duluth may have planned," Francis said at the end of the post-DEFIANCE press conference. "But if this 'Monet' character believes we will continue to just sit back and allow this to happen, he has not been watching FUSE programming. This kind of thing will not be tolerated." DEFIANCE was a night of heroes and a night of villains. It was a night where Jonathan Rhine decided he had finally had enough of Clinton Sage. It appears to this reporter he was not the only one tired of being muscled around.
