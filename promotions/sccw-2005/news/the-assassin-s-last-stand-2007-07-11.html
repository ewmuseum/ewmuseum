The Assassin's Last Stand?

11 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(redirected from The Shooter's Press website. July 10, 2007) Going in to what would be his third title reign, Damien Cruz seems somewhat distracted. And for those closest to the FUSE Superstar, he has good reason. According to his management company, Verona Enterprises, The Latin Assassin has canceled several meetings with FUSE talent coordinators in the past several weeks. Despite a strong resurgance in popularity following a high profile match against Tsuyoshi Tanaka, Damien Cruz has remained out of the spotlight. "It is, as my experience has shown, not a promising sign when a talent like Damien begins to draw away from his role in front of the camera. I have no official word from either side, but I doubt FUSE and Damien are on the same page." Sources close to Damien offered up other insights. A friend and former partner of Cruz, Che Reyes, remained protective of his friend's privacy, but did disclose that Damien had been in contact with him, and that Cruz was "not exactly happy with the way his career had been going as of late." Fernando Adorno, wrestling columnist and blogger, argues that this may be something that was obvious for a long time. "Cruz is quickly becoming a has-been, and it is not his doing,' he states. 'He is still very young, still very talented, and probably at the peak of his abilities. But the FUSE management structure is an ill fit for guys like him. Greg Junstin counters, "Damien is part of the flush of talent that came into FUSE right before they took off. But unlike the rest, he has stuck around. It's a different time, and a different place. Cruz, I hate to say, may be past his prime. Sometimes, a guy like him has to know when to gracefully step aside." Amid all of the speculation and heresay, one voice remains noticably absent. That of Damien Cruz. That is, until he agreed to a telephone interview with Shooter's Press, hours before his match with Clinton Sage. SP: Mr. Cruz, there have been a lot of rumors...and I think you know what I'm referring to. So I'll ask flat-out, are you leaving the ring anytime soon? DC: You know, I don't know. I still feel competitive. I still feel like I have a lot to give to the sport. So there is no easy way to make a decision like that. Based on how I feel physically, I'd do this for the next fifty years. But emotionally...well, I'm tired. I'm tired of the the road. I'm tired of the aches and pains. The politics, the spotlight. It's hard to be Mr. Superstar 24/7/365. I have a wife who'd love to see me home. I have friends who have walked away and are constantly telling me that they don't regret it for a second. So I that's what I had to digest for a while. But I think I know where I stand. SP: And where is that? DC: Officially, and on the record, I'm walking away. Win, lose, or draw, this match against Clinton Sage will be my last. SP: So that was your official announcement? DC: That's it. I'm going to go out there in a few hours, and I'm going to put on the show of my career. I'm going to give the fans 110% of myself. And if I am carried out in a body bag, so be it. But after tonight, I'm walking away. SP: For real? Clinton Sage will be your last opponent? DC: Yeah, and I hope Sage understands what that means. I hope that he's ready for what I'm bringing to that ring. But like I said, after the bell rings, I'm going home. I intend to take the belt with me. And if I don't, it won't change a thing. I'm calling it a career. SP: It may be argued that you are still among the elite on the FUSE roster. Statistically, you have won three to one. Why walk away if, as you said, you still have so much to give to the sport? DC: Because there is nothing sadder than being the most talented guy in the lower card. For whatever reason, I've allowed myself to become complacent. Put a guy in front of me, and I'll probably outshine him. But at the end of the day, I no longer feel the fire. I decided a long time ago that I'd quit the day it was no longer fun. And I have to say, it's no longer fun. I've accomplished everything I set ot to do. I have the credentials, I have the decorations. And even if the collective memory of the FUSE audience fails to remember it; I was out there blazing the trails and breaking the records that the new guys are casually crossing everyday. I know what I am capable of, and I know where on the spectrum I sit. I don't need the validation anymore. I know who I am. SP: So what's Next for The Latin Assassin? DC: I have a Universal Title to win. And after I do that...I dunno. Maybe I'll earn my degree and teach HS biology. SP: Thank you for the interview, Mr. Cruz. And on behalf of a lot of your friends and our readership, thank you for everything you have brought to wrestling as a sport. DC: I tried. No matter what the outcome, I always tried. The career of Damien Cruz was a long and arduous one. History may debate if he left too soon, or long after his time, but what cannot be debated was the passion and distinction that he brought to every match. Damien Cruz, The Latin Assassin, will be missed.
