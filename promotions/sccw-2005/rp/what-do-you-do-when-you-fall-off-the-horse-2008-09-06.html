What do you do when you fall off the horse?

6 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 06 Optimism ran wildly through Dualta Sheridan’s veins as he sat parched upon a wooden stool in the Foggy Dew, and Irish Bar in lower Woodside, Queens, NY. Whilst true happiness and security still eluded him, the path with which he followed let him see light at the end of the tunnel. Perhaps true happiness is not found with the possession of your goals but with the pursuit of said goals, those weeks and months that you work to achieve what you have set for yourself, knowing that you are on your way. The Foggy Dew had been a popular bar with the Irish population of Queen’s for over thirty years. It had been owned by Dualta’s uncle Dermot for the majority of that tenure. Dermot sold up early to a close friend as he anticipated the demise of the Sheridan Empire and the ‘hostile takeover’ administered by the Russian Odessa Mafia. Dermot was a smart businessman, perhaps more so than any other member of the family, Dualta respected him. The new owner was Malachy O’Rourke. An Irish-born man in his mid-sixties, a good man. He supported the Sheridan’s over the years, rain or shine. The Sheridan’s had plenty of ‘fair weather’ friends, but few who had stuck by them in the current crisis, Malachy was a rare breed. A Friday night meant a busy bar. Men still in their work clothes, fresh of the construction site drank in their usual corner of the bar, talking shop, talking how much plaster board they carried today. Then you had the posers, the young Irish kids who were just loading up on some cheap drink before they hit the gleaming lights of East Village in Manhattan. Dualta sat with a slightly older contingent, with the exception of his cousin, Sean, who sat hunchbacked, staring into the bottom of the pint glass in front of him. The transition to life in the gutter was hard on all the family and any optimism Dualta possessed could soon be extinguished with one glance in the direction of Sean. He was two years older than Dualta, but to look at him, you would swear he was ten on top of that. The last year had not been good to him physically. His stubble was slowly becoming a thick untended beard, his hair was scraggy and unmanaged and his eyes cried out with weary desperation. A far cry from the work hard play hard demeanour he had sported for twenty-four years. "I just never would have though it ya know..." Sean’s stare never broke from the quarter full Guinness in front of him as he spoke "... I knew you could always look after yourself, we all did. But you going off, without a word to anyone, and becomin’ a goddamn professional wrestler!?" Dualta simply smiled at Sean’s summary, he said nothing. Sean went on, "We always had it so good ya know? And suddenly we’re in the gutter. And who is the one who does somethin’ about it? Dualta, the fuckin’ youngest outta all of us. I... I feel so fuckin’ helpless." Cracks in his voice interrupted every second word he spoke; Sean’s emotions were beginning to overpower him. Dualta’s eyes narrowed as he now looked down at his own drink, replicating Sean’s form, not really knowing what to say. Dualta had struggled to get Sean out tonight; few of the Sheridan’s pursued a social life as of late. They all worked long hours on building sites, and in some cases less permissible endeavours. Just to try and make the monthly payments to the Russians. Dualta himself had given up most social activities, but he felt he deserved this, and more so he felt Sean needed this. Sean was not only Dualta’s cousin but his best friend. They had lived next door their whole lives and been through the good, the bad and the very bad together. Now they live in opposite rooms of the dilapidated, abandoned apartment building that all Sheridan’s call home, begrudgingly. "We were brought up on the philosophy of combat Sean. We were fighting since we were six years old. That underground shit was too much punishment for too little money, the mathematics didn’t add up," for some reason, Dualta felt he had to justify his actions, if for no other reason than to give Sean a little self-esteem back, "I decided I was a good enough fighter as plenty of these boys on national television, so I took a shot, and it worked. And what’s more it adds up, it’s good money and it doesn’t cost me as many teeth." "You’re fuckin’ right it worked, you’re on a roll kid." Sean lips widened into what nearly became a smile, the closest thing Dualta had seen to one in months anyway. Dualta thought he could hear a sense of satisfaction in Sean’s voice, like he was proud of his little cousin. "I mean look at you, you’re six foot four and you don’t even way two hundred pounds, and you’re goin’ up against guys twice that weight and winning. It’s crazy!" Now Sean was smiling, which in turned caused Dualta to gleam. "Yeah well they weren’t born tough like us Sean." "A fuckin’ roll Dualta, and don’t you let no fuckin’ overgrown piece of shit get in your way." Roll, that word again. The SCCW were selling the Dualta Sheridan brand on that very word. The thing about a roll is, it ends, and it ends instantaneously. Then how would they sell Dualta? As the son of a mobster? Dualta prayed that information stayed out of their reach, but he knew in the long run that was not possible. All he did know is if something’s not broke don’t fix it and right now his roll was working for him. Dualta’s eyes shifted to the demoralised frame of his cousin. Inebriation was beginning to get the better of Sean, he did not even realise that Dualta was looking straight at him, he simply stared at his beer, engrossed in his own thoughts, not positive ones Dualta alleged. "I’ve got enough money together for this month’s payment," Dualta did not want to bring up these sorts of issues tonight but felt this was a necessity, "So whatever the rest of you have gathered, put it towards some new stuff for the building, and maybe some toys for the kids." "Thanks Dualta" Sean spoke lazily and without expression in his voice, he felt so dependent. This particular subject brought any contentment Sean felt to a bitter end. Dualta swore his cousin’s eyes were beginning to water, Sean may well have been the toughest man he knew, he never would have believed he would see him like this. The last month had been defined by particular junctures, small instances in Dualta’s everyday life that motivated him. Moments that made him take his ambition, his drive to the next level. This moment was perhaps the pinnacle of them all. He watched half the man he had once known beside him, sucked of all self worth and devoid of any impetus. It was then he realised, paying of the Russians wasn’t enough. "Come on Sean, let’s go home I gotta get up for training in the morning." Dualta was direct. Sean rose without question, head slumped, and walked to the door, familiar faces bided him farewell upon his exit, they were given a barely noticeable nod in return. Dualta hung back a bit. "I’m gonna call you sometime next week Malachy, I may need a favour." Dualta spoke to Malachy O’Rourke who stood behind the bar. "Sure Dualta, whatever you need." Malachy had a certain sincerity in his voice that Dualta knew he could trust, the man was one of the few men he would trust his life with. Dualta had a plan, it had formulated in his head within seconds and in true Sheridan fashion he began to execute with immediate efficiency. He had seen his cousin become no more than a zombie, going through life with nothing but fear and depression. He had seen it happen to his entire family, so he had made up his mind. The Sheridan’s will rise again. There was only one stumbling block, finance. Being on the wrong end of the SCCW card every week would not change that, so it’s time to spice things up. He would do whatever it takes, but the big bucks had to starting flowing in or his plan was dead, and his family were as good as.
