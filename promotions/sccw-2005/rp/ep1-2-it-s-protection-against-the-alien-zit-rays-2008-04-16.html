Ep1.2: 'It's Protection Against the Alien Zit Rays.'

16 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/16/08 (AND SOMETIME IN THE PAST) Hard-soled boots crunch against a gravel pathway up to an old house that sags so much it seems to frown, pressed up into the side of the middle of nowhere. The young girl in the green slicker ducks her head against a lash of rain carried on the wind. Her hood hangs low over her face as she pauses again, pulling a small stack of papers, folded lengthwise and stapled once in the corner, from within the jacket. The girl flicks a glance over her shoulder towards the cul-de-sac that the street sits on, as if to be assured that her rental car (a 1996 Dodge Intrepid, silver-gray) still sat at the curbside. It does. Thunder echoes overhead, and the girl hunkers down lower, presenting a significantly smaller target to the deluge as she races forward. The sky opens up angrily, rain splattering along the rocky walkway. She hurries her steps, ducking onto the rotted-out wooden patio. Two rusted metal chains hang from the ceiling of the deck, likely for a swing long since gone. The metal of the screen door is nearly brown from age, broken and bent away from the frame. The girl knocks softly on the gray wood of the door. No response comes. She looks away again, thunder shaking the rickety overhang that's protecting her from the thunderstorm. Within, the oaken door opens, and...there are certain things the girl expects from this sort of house. Something out of Deliverance, perhaps. The soft, biting voice of the man that opens the door is clearly not what the woman swaddled in the rain slicker is expecting, however. 'Ah, Ms. Pendleton,' the man says, and she has to squint to even make out his slight silhouette in the darkened recesses of the house. 'You found the place. I'm glad.' 'You people need to know that I don't do porn or anything freaky,' Ms. Pendleton replies, frowning slightly. Perhaps the slightest bit of white teeth emerges in the darkness behind the threshold. 'No one's going to ask you to do anything you don't want to, Kirsten. Now come on in out of the rain and hear me out. If you don't like what we're offering, you can leave, no strings attached.' Kirsten Pendleton steps into the deep, dark house in South Florida, even though she knows that every other time she's heard the phrase 'no strings attached,' it was a hook that had many, many strings attached. REC 4/16/2008 1:06 PM PDT Two thousand, five hundred, twenty-four miles away (and sixty-two days later), a brand-new Sony 1080i high-definition handheld digital camcorder opens with a shot of a neon sign reading, 'BEAT' in purple letters. Beside the glowing sign is a haphazard poster (and by 'haphazard'...we mean 'probably made today, from bright white posterboard with a Polaroid of a Pixie Scotch-taped to it'). The sign reads: 'Meet Sin City Champ. Wrestling Superstar Charlotte Ramone 1-3PM.' The door to the station wagon slams, as a tall, willowy blonde crosses her arms atop the beaten burgundy vehicle. 'So...let me get this straight,' she says. 'Us leading the crowd into singing Dropkick Murphys...badly, I might add...has made us into a highly sought-after commodity in, like, a week?' Who is this wise girl and where can we buy her Kool-Aid? 'Krissy Jamison,' the script under her picture reads thanks to the miracle of post-production. 'She's Adorable.' It's our camerachick who answers in a low, throaty voice like Kathleen Turner after a night of binge drinking and chain smoking (so, like, after last Thursday). 'I told you it would,' Charlie Ramone says. And how do we know it's Charlie Ramone? Because there is flickering, hot pink, graffiti-style writing around the edge of the shot, reading, 'Words By: Charliffic Ramone'. There are even arrows. 'And this place just called up out of the blue?' Krissy asks. 'All, 'hi, can you come sign autographs?'' 'Well...it's not as simple as that,' Charlie says as they turn away. The 1080i presents a lot more of a stable platform for filming than whatever camera they were using last week, so there's a little less wiggle, a lot less shake, and a ton less nausea-inducing shimmy as the pair crosses the asphalt parking lot towards the Beat. A bell chimes softly, but thumping bass guitars and an ululating howl drowns out the electronic ching-a-ding designed to alert the employees as to new customers. It's some heavy metal band...we're not told which (royalties are a bitch, you know). So just use your imagination and figure there's a bunch of wordless screaming. 'Ah, the music of my people,' Charlie says and the camera shakes for the first time (headbanging and steady shots are not compatible). 'What did you mean, 'it's not as simple as that', Charlie?' Krissy looks suddenly concerned. 'It's always that simple with you. If I looked up 'simple' on wikipedia...well, pretty sure I uploaded your picture last week.' 'Just what are you saying?' Krissy sighs. It's a sigh that says, 'I sigh a lot because of you, you know.' One hand, nails painted forest green, blurs through the shot, a staying gesture. 'Don't even go there with me. Edie has a friend that's friends with the manager...' 'You must be Charlotte,' the bored monotone of a man's voice intones from the counter nearby. The camera swings that way, and it's one of those guys who's manning the cash register. You know what we're talking about; the ones with the mop-top of brown hair, the Avenged Sevenfold t-shirt, the multiple facial piercings, and the chunky goddamn wallet chain. He's what you call a 'poseur,' and we don't mean the kind that can make cool, human-like pictures. 'Must I be?' frowns our narrator/cameraperson/starlet. 'I wanted to be Princess Mononoke today.' 'Chuck!' scolds her conscience-slash-uhhh...gonna go with 'friend'. Krissy, by far the more affable of the two, folds her arms across the countertop, favoring our friend the poseur (his glow-in-the-dark name tag says, 'Kevvyn') with a bright smile. 'Heya, Kev,' she says. 'My name's Krissy...how are you?' 'Good, I guess. Swear kids come in here all the time with new shit they steal from the Wherehouse down the street and sell it for drug money.' 'Really?' Krissy does 'sympathetic' pretty well...living with Charlotte Ramone will teach that sort of instinct. 'You're part of a drug ring?' 'I wouldn't call it a drug ring. Does that stuff even happen in the real world?' Charlie chuckles. 'Rumor has it that, like, six or seven of my colleagues are real live murderers,' she says. It starts off solemn, but gets a little more nervous with each word. 'True story. It's just a 'rumor', though. Not like I'm worried about being killed in my sleep or anything. Really. I'm not. Completely comfortable in a locker room with people that the grapevine say have killed other human beings. Thanks for getting me into this madhouse, by the way.' 'Charlie, I told you.' Krissy's tone is scolding. 'They're just stupid, macho-man rumors. Probably started by the guys themselves. 'I'm so big and bad I eat puppies!'' She makes a face that's probably supposed to be scary: her lips are curled to show her teeth, her eyes are wide...it's kind of like the stereotypical 'vampire that's gonna bite off a face' expression. Except on Krissy Jamison, it kind of looks ridiculous (blame those cherubic cheeks), and it generates a throaty, chuckly sound from Charlotte, who is helpless to resist the cuteness, as if Krissy were an adorable kitten. There is the grabbing of a blonde woman's head in a headlock. There would be noogies, too, if they weren't out in public. Kevvyn, for his part, stares at the two of them like they're aliens, nibbling at the flesh surrounding his lip ring. 'So...I imagined you would've been taller.' 'I imagined you would've been...' There's the briefest flash of a silver claddagh ring flying through the air, as the (left) hand it is attached to slaps over Charlie Ramone's mouth. 'Sorry,' Krissy apologizes through an exasperated smile. 'Someone decided to have the super-size can of Jolt today.' Charlie's response is muffled by Krissy's fingers...probably thankfully. 'Do you have, like, a table for us?' Kevvyn points towards the back of the room. 'Great! Make sure to let people know we're here, will you, sweetheart? Thanks.' Not releasing her grip on her...gonna go with 'friend's' mouth, Krissy leads Charlie back to the rear of the store. It is, strangely, not the weirdest thing the people who flip aimlessly through records at the Beat have ever seen. REC 4/16/2008 2:15PM PDT Sure, the first few minutes of this footage may have been more exciting or whatever, but that's not what they're about. It was mostly Charlie bugging random patrons until Kevvyn's supervisor told them they'd best be quiet or he was going to have to throw them out. After an hour, though, the camera is sitting on the lonely, lonely table, marked only by a red posterboard reading 'SCW' in black letters (Krissy tried to add the other 'C' in, with middling success). It's pointed towards our heroes; Charlie has her head down on the table, Krissy's propped herself up by the cheek. Neither one looks terribly interested in the goings-on around them. 'Maybe it was too soon for an autograph signing,' Krissy says. 'I'm starting to lose interest in giving witty responses as to why I wear the face shield,' Charlotte responds glumly. 'Yeah...though I've gotta admit that 'my boss is afraid I might punch myself out rather than look at you' was clever. In a mean sort of way.' 'I'm partial to 'It's protection against alien zit rays. Pew-pew.'' 'That one guy jumped! Tell me,' Krissy says with a sudden spark of interest. 'When you decided you were coming to SCCW, did you think it'd involve you sitting here in a record store in Sacramento for...geez, how long has it been since someone came over here?' 'Like, I don't know, a hundred gazillion hours?' Charlie asks, talking mostly to the balsa tabletop. 'No, I wasn't expecting this. I was kind of hoping for a line around the block.' 'You'd have to be a rock star or...' Krissy Jamison trails off, sitting up suddenly. 'I'll be right right back.' She bustles away, and Charlie picks up the camera, looking into the lens. 'This can't end well.' REC 4/16/2008 2:27PM PDT We're back outside the Beat now, though the shot is much less stable than previous shots. It starts with a shot of the same posterboard sign we looked at to begin this week's episode. Only 'Charlotte Ramone' has been crossed out. Underneath it, the sign has been changed. The new addition is in a flowing, flowery script. 'LAST MINUTE CHANGE! SMOKING HOT SCCW SUPERSTAR AIMZ AT 'THE BEAT' FOR PHOTOS AND AUTOGRAPHS!' The camera spins into the grin of Krissy Jamison. With a bullhorn. 'This is going to rule,' she promises as she bends to set the camera on the sidewalk, pointed up towards her. She flicks the bullhorn on. 'H-hello?' Her voice echoes through the parking lot and up the street. 'Ladies and gentlemen! Come to The Beat! Meet multiple-time FUSE Fatal Faction and former Gateway Champion Amy Campbell! The sizzling Red Raver is here to sign your autographs for just forty-five minutes! Come see the Nubile Nova Scotian in an up-close and personal forum... only at The Beat.' The ching-a-ding of the electronic bell announces the presence of Charlotte Ramone outside. The Punk Rock Pixie is frowning. 'Kris, what are you doing?!' 'Drumming up some business. Now go back inside...' She turns her face to the street, raising the bullhorn again. '...AIMZ so all these great fans can see you -- only at The Beat.' Krissy bends over, flipping off the camera with a wink and a smile. REC 4/16/2008 2:40PM PDT 'I don't remember Amy Campbell having a face shield,' the snooty voice says as the shot begins again inside; same table, same chairs, only a few more people. One of these people is a slightly rotund wrestling fan with thick, coke-bottle glasses and a black 'Seventh Sin' t-shirt, so you know he's old school. 'I, uhhh, hurt myself at a house show.' Charlie Ramone is quick-witted and fleet of tongue. Except when she's put on the spot. 'Doctors say that I have to wear this. Sorry, dude. Gotta keep my face all together so I can be all, 'Where are Jon Rhine's testicles? I need my magnifying glass.'' 'You guys don't do house shows.' 'What do you mean we don't do house shows? Are you with us all the time? Do follow the trucks they carry the ring on, just to make sure they don't set that bitch up anywhere but where the next show's supposed to be?' 'I read on the Internet...' 'Oh, he read on the Internet! Hey... random SCCW staffer girl?' Charlie bade Krissy come closer. 'Give this asshat a t-shirt and get him out of my sight, for I am the Red Raver and I don't like people, y'all.' 'Yes, Miss Campbell.' A t-shirt is handed over (extra-large, as is required by that type of fan), and there is peace. Charlie glances at Krissy. 'I don't think this is working, babe,' Charlie says. 'Why not?' asks Krissy. 'I mean, I know Ames said I was, like, a mini-her or whatever, but I can't do 'raging cuntbag' like she can.' 'But you're a girl wrestler and you're witty and you like rock music.' 'And she's got the market cornered on that?' 'Apparently.' Krissy shrugs. 'Just roll with it for a few, few more minutes.' Charlie just sighs, because another fan, tall and narrow like a telephone poll, steps up to her table. 'Aimz doesn't have a face mask...what kind of bullshit are you playing?' 'Look, I got in a terrible accident involving my ego and a steel cage, ok? I'd rather not talk about it. Aren't you going to ask me a question or tell me how much I suck because Jon Rhine is adorable and blahblahblah?' 'Actually, I was hoping to see Charlotte Ramone.' Charlie brightens noticeably...well, as noticeable as it can be under that mask. 'That's me!' 'What? You just said you were Aimz.' 'Yeah, that was a total lie. No one was coming to get my autograph because I'm too new.' 'I don't believe you.' Krissy leans down on the table, smiling ruefully. 'It's true, kid. My fault...I'm the one who changed the sign.' The kid grinned. 'Heh. Okay, maybe I believe you a little.' 'Hooray!' Krissy exults. 'I'm glad, otherwise I would've been frowny with Krissy until the show. You don't want that to happen, believe me.' 'You may have saved our relationship...' Krissy looks down at the notebook dropped down before Charlie. 'Pete.' 'And he did it...' Charlie begins, but Krissy cuts her off. 'Do not make a 'for Pete's sake' joke here, Chuck. You're above that.' 'Whom do I make this out to?' Charlie asks. 'Pete C. If you could add something about the Sex Pistols being the greatest, that would be awesome.' 'Could I? It's true. Them and Midnight Oil are my faves.' 'Midnight Oil?' Pete asks. He sounds surprised.' 'Beds are Burning is fantastic.' One autograph is scrawled; the notebook is passed back across the table. 'Thank you so much,' Pete gushes. 'No problem, kid. Remember to root for me at the ARCO Arena when I take on Scary Spice and the Multiple Man.' 'And Nex,' Krissy chimes in. 'And Next,' Charlie says with a nod. Pete wanders back towards the stacks and stacks of tracks that The Beat has to offer, but Charlie calls after him. 'Hey kid?' Pete turns. Charlie puts on her best bitchface. 'Maybe I am the Red Raver and I did fool you!' Pete's laugh echoes back, but it's masked by Charlie's own actual honest-to-goodness snort. After a moment, she gathers herself, glancing towards her 'friend'. 'You realize,' Charlie says. 'That we'll have to cut all the Campbell stuff before this airs?' 'What? Why?' Krissy frowns...it was her idea after all. 'Because she'll sue the pants off us.' Cut. Print. That's a wrap, folks. Epilogue Inside a lonely, dark house in Fort Lauderdale, Florida, Kirsten Pendleton sat down on a moldy couch badly in need of new upholstery. A pair of small black boxes sat in front of her. One was a speakerphone; it was about to ring. The other is a soft, pillowed velvet box featuring a silver ring in it. A telltale design of a pair of hands caught the light. Kirsten had fondled this ring many times while she waited for the phone beside it to ring. It would, eventually. What was said on that call would change her life forever. That is, however, a story for another time.
