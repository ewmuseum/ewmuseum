Kamen ('Mask')

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: A week before Temptation at the Fairgrounds For a while, now, I wanted a crack at Sin City Championship Wrestling. There's just so many talents that I'd love to tangle with some day, from Aimz to Lance Marshall. I won't be modest here when I say that I think I'm pretty good at my craft, and that there's probably a few guys out there who'd agree with that. Either way, I'll be the first to admit that I got pretty excited when I got a call to come visit. The last couple of years haven't been terribly kind to me, and a lot of it might be my fault. I'm usually the first to admit to going with the flow of things, which has led me into partnerships that didn't exactly go my way when it came right down to it. And while I have a lot of freedom to do whatever I want to do in the independents, the money hasn't exactly been rolling in any more. The idea of wrestling in a promotion with the visibility that SCCW could provide was like a dream come true. Then I was shown the mask. I've met a lot of masked wrestlers, who pretty much live and die by the mask. The guy who wrestles as Cosmo Kid, for example, is so devoted to luchador traditions that I don't think he's ever been seen without his mask. I remember that he even showed up to Malone's training camp wearing the mask. He's kind of a hardcore guy, but he treats the mask as his identity. I, on the other hand, already had an identity before this. I figured there had to be somebody out there in Sin City who knew who the hell I was. But, of course, it's because the good doctor who was left in charge of Sin City Championship Wrestling didn't know who I was that I got stuck with this... thing. It's hard to really call it a mask in the sense of what luchador masks would normally look like. The mask has an opening for the mouth, nose, and eyes, but other than that... well, it's shaped like a blackberry. It stays on perfectly fine, it's just that it's aparticularly absurd mask to be wearing. Every time I put it on, a part of me dies on the inside. I think it might be my pride. I'm not totally sure, some people like to claim that I don't have much of that. In any event, I keep having this feeling that I'm going to get thrown into a pie with a bunch of other blackberries, and get baked to death. That's the sense of dread I get when I even look at this mask that I'm having to wear. Maybe it's cursed. Maybe there's some sort of ancient Aztec curse associated with this stupid blackberry mask, and any man who puts it on will be doomed from then on. You know, with locusts and blood and some of the other Egyptian plagues. Or maybe I've been around strange people for so long that I start to get weird ideas in my head. Or maybe I've watched too much science fiction. Either way, when I first saw the mask, I felt deflated. My visions of working with the aforementioned people were reduced to visions of being the resident job boy to the stars. I didn't exactly enjoy my prospects, being what would essentially be a comedy role alongside Jared Sykes of King Blueberry fame. I feared just how this would affect me, because I don't want to be seen as a joke. Then some thoughts hit me that make me rethink this whole wearing a mask thing with a bit more detail outside of my initial disappointment. Was I really going to put my own personal pride ahead of the enjoyment of the fans? And was I really going to deprive myself and, more specifically, my fiancee, of a better paycheck just because I didn't want to wear a stupid mask? So, yeah, after all that thought, I agreed to be a guy in a blackberry mask, running around like an idiot with Sykes and his posse. I took the mask from Dr. Kensington, then I went to the hotel room where I was staying for the night. It was late in the day, so I didn't have a lot to do but ponder what laid before me. I set the mask on the top of the television set, but spent more time staring at it than I did watching any television. Besides, the channel selection at the hotel really bit the big one, and most of them had to do with the weather anyway. It's not exactly what you'd call compelling television. I thought about my role as this character for a while. And God forbid, I started thinking about what he would be like. Kensington didn't exactly give me a heads up on what he, or Sykes, wanted me to do as a masked blackberry sort of guy. If I acted too much like myself, I feared people would figure out who I was, and I sure didn't want to think about theconsequences of that. Then I started to think about what I could do to make sure nobody thought my name when I acted like this guy. That's where 'Baron von Blackberry' came from. He is me, but he isn't me. He will talk about taking over the world, build bizarre inventions that may or may not help him in this endeavor, and will always threaten to destroy those that get in his way. He's smart, but he's alsothoroughly stupid. He's not the type of guy you want to meet in a dark alley, but that's mostly because he'll just laugh maniacally into your ear. He wears a lab coat and he smells of science. I thought the idea was brilliant in some bizarre way, and the first thing I do when I come across a really good idea that's stuck in my head is to let my fiancee know. She laughed at me. Actually, to put it better, it was more like a giggle. I'm about to marry an especially girly girl, after all. She told me that she liked the idea, though whether or not I should believe her is anyone's guess. I did, however, beg her not to tell anyone else of the idea, especially not some certain people who would just use the knowledge torelentlessly make fun of me. This would be between me, Dr. Nigel Kensington, Jared Sykes, and my fiancee. The thought of having to get Jared Sykes to keep this to himself, though... ...that's gonna keep me awake at night.
