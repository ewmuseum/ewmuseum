A Second Chance

1 January 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1-1-07 The stench of sweat hung in the air like a pair of shoes on a telephone line, filling his nostrils with the familiar scent of home. Jadian Bridden quickly scanned the gym to see if he could find Benny working with any of the guys when a heavy set guy working the heavy bag noticed him. "Hey, Jay," he called out as he grabbed the bag with both hands to steady it. The sound of his name attracted a few of the other guy's to turn their attention toward the door as well, and it wasn't long before a group of guys had made their way into a circle around the young man. Pats on the back were accompanied by various forms of congratulatory praise for his efforts in FUSE's Young Lion's Cup tournament. "Th- thanks, guys," his words were sincere, but the nervous smile on his face showed how uncomfortable it made him feel to be the center of attention, while the stumbling of words made it even more noticable. "Alright, alright, break it up," Benny Minor walked up behind the group and took control of the situation. "You guys got some work you should be doing, don't you?" The cruel taskmaster had shown his ugly face, at least in their own opinions as they headed back to whatever training they had been doing before Jadian had walked in. "Thanks." Benny shook his head as he wrapped his arm around Jadian's shoulders. "Don't worry about, son. Considering how great you looked in your match, I shouldn't have said a thing. You're going to need to get used to stuff like that with all the fans you're going to have." "Fans," Jay said with a scoff as they headed toward the back, where Benny's office was. "I lost my match. I couldn't even get out of the first round." "Don't be so hard on yourself," Benny tried to console his student. "Everyone watching saw that Drake fellow steal that win from you. You were the star of the show until then, and I already got a call from Brad Pholic about getting you signed to a contract." "Brad who?" Benny twisted the knob before he pushed open his office door, glancing back over his shoulder as he walked in. "Brad Pholic, he's part of FUSE's talent relations department. They were real impressed with what they saw." Jadian closed the door behind him, as Benny pulled his chair away from the desk and took a seat in it. "You're kidding, right?" Jadian could hardly believe what he heard, considering the end result of his match did not turn out as he had expected. The sense of failure he had felt as he walked to the back after that match weighed down on him like a ton of bricks, and he thought the last thing any one would do was commend him on that failure. "Hell no," Benny replied, a smile as wide as the Mississippi painted on his face. "All they're waiting for is a phone call from me saying you're interested, and they'll fax over copies of the contract for you to sign." "This isn't happening," Jadian tilted his head back and stared up at the ceiling, each breath deep and steady as he allowed the situation to play in his head like a scratched record. "I'm getting punked or something, right?" "Why can't you just accept the fact that you're better than you think, Jay?" Benny stood up, the chair slamming against the back wall as it slid out behind him. "You're always so damn hard on yourself. You don't have to be perfect, Jay; that Drake fellow being the poster boy for that statement. You're good enough to compete with quite a few guys on that roster, and I've done everything short of slamming a brick against your head trying to get you to realize that." "I don't know what to say," he looked across at the man that has been like a father to him for most of his life, the smile of a proud parent plastered permanently on a normally stoic expression. "Just say yes, Jay," Benny said softly. He leaned forward a bit, his hands pressed down on the weathered old desk in front of him, and nodded slightly. "This is the opportunity you need to fulfill your dreams." "Alright," the sheepish smile caused a few wrinkles to form in the corners of his mouth, but that was the only evidence that he was happy about this chance. It had been a long time since he had been truly happy. "I'll do it." December 31, 2004 Austin, Texas "Hey Sandra, did you wash that shirt like I asked you to?" Jay heard a sigh from his mother before she replied, "It's hanging up in the closet, dear." He looked up to find her shaking her head, a smile on her face as she dried her hands on the floral patterned apron tied around her waist. "I swear, your father would lose his head if it wasn't stuck to his neck." Jay laughed along with his mother when his father walked into the room. "What's so funny?" he asked as he fastened the top two buttons of his uniform. "Nothing, honey," his mom responded before she leaned in and gave him a kiss on the cheek. "Do you have to go to work tonight, dad?" Jay frowned a bit, hoping he would get a different reply than usual. "Crime doesn't take a day off, son." He didn't. "Besides," his father said as he adjusted the gun belt around his waist. "I thought you had plans with that Jenny girl?" "We were supposed to go to her grandparent's house, they have this ranch near San Marcos, but her grandmother got real sick so they decided to cancel." "Well good," his dad said as he ran his fingers through his hair. "Now I don't have to worry about your mother being here all alone." Anthony Bridden had been a part of the Austin police department for the past twenty years, protecting and serving the party city long enough to have a trophy case full of medals and awards for his service. With only a few months left before he transferred to the robbery/homicide division, more of a desk job than anything else, it was no surprise that he was having trouble getting used to the fact that he'd be off the streets. But it would make his wife happier, and that's all that really mattered to him. "Just be careful out there," Jay pleaded with his father. "You know how sixth street gets." "Yeah, I'd rather not get into that conversation with you right now," Anthony said with a laugh, "I'll be late for work." Jay watched his father give his mother a kiss before walking out the door, a wave of his hand in Jay's direction as he pulled it closed. "So I guess it's just you and me now, huh?" The flashing red and blue lights and knock at the door at four in the morning were an unwelcome awakening to how true those words actually were. "Hey Jay," Benny said in a chipper tone as he lifted his head, the creak of his office door drawing his attention toward it. He wasn't expecting to find a set of tanned legs pouring into a pair of black high heels. "Juli?" The red and black skirt suit hugged her curves nicely, though it was doubtful her father was paying much attention to that when he sprung from his seat and rushed over to her. "I can't believe you're here, mija." With her hair pulled up into a tight bun, and gold framed glasses low enough on the bridge of her nose so she can look over. With a briefcase in her hand, Julissa Minorez didn't look like she was there for a social visit. The lack of emotion when her father pulled her close in his arms, relayed that message to him. "I highly doubt you'll feel the same in a few minutes," her voice was devoid of compassion, but practically overflowing with spite. "How could I ever not be happy to see my daughter?" Benny asked as he motioned her toward the seat across from his desk. "Is there something I can get for you? A soda? Some water, maybe?" "How about a father." it was more of a statement than a question, and Benny found it hard to find it amusing. "You don't mean that," he told her as he sat down behind the desk. "I know you're angry with-" "Angry?" Julissa said with a grin. "Clearly you have me mistaken with someone who gives a damn about you." "Don't you talk to me like that, I'm your fa-" "Don't you dare," Julissa cuts him off. "You lost the privilege to call me your daughter the day you decided to put others before family." "I was trying to protect you, mija," Benny argued. "I didn't want the business to chew you up and spit you out like a piece of meat. I don't want that life for you!" "It wasn't your choice to make, and pulling strings to have me fired from FUSE when you found out what I was doing makes me sick to my stomach." Benny shook his head, a few tears leaving streaks down his cheeks as they drip from his chin. "Why couldn't you just accept me for who I was, instead of who you wanted me to be?" she questioned, her lips curled into a sneer. "You took something away from me that night, Benito." The sound of her calling him by his first name made him snap his attention to where she was seated. "You took away everything that I ever wanted, that I ever dreamed about, and now I'm going to do the same to you." She pulled a folder from the briefcase and placed it on the desk. "I expect you to sign those papers and have them ready for me before five tomorrow afternoon." She closed the briefcase, then proceeded to stand up and make her way toward the door as Benny opened up the folder. "But this is the deed to the gym?" Benny slammed his palms onto the desk as he pushed himself away so he could stand up, but there was no reply from his daughter. "Juli!" His screams fell on deaf ears as she pulled open the door and disappeared into the night. "Son of a bitch!" "Benny," Jadian ran into the office with a towel wrapped around his waist. A puddle formed beneath him as he stared at his friend. "Benny, what's the matter?" Benny just stood there in silence, his eyes fixated on the door his daughter just left through. The door his life just exited.
