Size Matters

February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 2009 'What are you even going to do with a big screen, high def TV anyway? You don't even have a home, you're on the Motel Six Across America Tour. Where will you put a big screen?' Rosa Dawes pulled her coat tighter around herself, dodging puddles of slush in a Baltimore Best Buy parking lot under the slate grey sky of late winter. Walking next to her was Austin King, the only person in professional wrestling she knew that hadn't gone crazy in the year since she had returned to the sport. 'Roight! Figger I'll take it with me. Yannow, upgrade the hotel to two beds, so the telly has a place to relax.' Sometimes it seemed like the only reason he hadn't gone insane was that he had already started out that way. Austin stopped under the raised eyebrow stare from the large woman, something he had taken to calling 'The Vulcan Eyefuck,' but only when he was outside the reach of her punch. It wasn't that she was violent or abusive, but there was a certain physicality to the way she expressed herself that when coupled with her six foot plus frame, tended to leave Austin wincing and rubbing his arm more often than not. 'Hey, I don't plan on being in this business forever. I am not going to be some fifty year old jabbing needles in my arse and chewing painkillers like Life Savers just so I can make it through the next show in a high school gym for 67 people. I've got maybe a dozen years if I'm lucky. What I really want to is get into acting, make my money, retire early, buy a house, white picket fence, chihuahua named Spike, the works.' Her smile was subtle, a small upturn in the corners of her mouth while she listened to him talk. She found the way his outlandish Australian accent tended to drop when he was being serious about something endearing, although the one time she had mentioned that he 'dropped character' he spent the next two weeks pouring it on so thick, he was practically unintelligible. 'Sides, it's me birthday, an' if I wanna buy a telly big enoughta replace tha SINScreen, I will!' 'What part of half life don't you-' 'It's still brighter than-' '-caught on FIRE and took out the whole house!' Rosa sighed as the army of employees in their blue shirts and white shirts that had gathered around Austin as soon as one of them recognized him as a professional wrestler continued their attempt to be helpful, which had mostly devolved into a shouting match between the ones who supported plasma technology and those who supported LCD. It looked like it was about to escalate into a full scale geek war, until a slight, bearded blue shirt climbed onto a counter and raised his arms high. 'Gentlemen, gentlemen! It is clear that we will not be able to decide this merely by the volume of our voices, so it falls to our time honored Best Buy traditions to settle this... We must enter into hand to hand combat! We must have... A STREET FIGHTER IV TOURNAMENT!!!' Hearing that was enough to send her deeper into the store, seeking solace from the alpha geeks beating their chests. She wandered aimlessly through the store for several minutes before a voice sounded behind her. 'Excuse me, do you need any help findi....oooogh.' It was a Best Buy employee, one of the ones in a white shirt, tall and gangly. As Rosa turned and he saw her, he stopped mid-sentence, his hand flying to his forehead. He staggered backwards, eyes rolling into his head, to be caught by a muscular man in his blue Best Buy shirt. 'Are you okay there, sport?' the man ask him before offering an explanation to Rosa. 'He's got a...condition. Fainting spells.' Rose took a step forward to assist, 'Is he-' The man practically growled at her as he drug the woozy man away. 'I said he's fine!' Rosa watched as the odd pair disappeared through a doorway marked EMPLOYEES ONLY, then turned her attention to the cheering throng where the tourney was still going on, and decided that unless she wanted to spend the rest of her day trapped in this strange retail Hell, she would have to take matters into her own hands. 'Damn, mate, that was just sick! Hey, Rosa, you want in on this?' Austin held the game controller out towards her, then shrugged when she didn't take it. 'Looks like ol' Wombat is betta at Street Fightin' than he is at wresslin, huh? Mebbe I should-' 'Are you any closer to buying a TV? No? I'm ready to go, Austin.' Austin sheepishly handed the controller to his fellow Street Fighters and ignored their protests as he hustled to keep up with Rosa. He followed her out the door, skidding to a stop when he saw a pair of employees loading an enormous cardboard box into the rental car they had came in. 'Happy birthday, dork. I know a thing or two about TVs. Who do you think bought Vincent his?' Austin climbed into the car's passenger seat, mouth agape as he stared at the back seat. 'So... Whoa! Thank you thank you thank you! So this is the same as the the one in Casa Mayhem?' Rosa shook her head, fastening her seat belt and starting the car. 'No, this one is bigger.' The rented car drove off, splashing through the slush in the parking lot, heading back for the hotel. 'So, ya think ya could tell everybody 'Austin's is bigger than Mayhem's' for me? OWW! I was kidding!'
