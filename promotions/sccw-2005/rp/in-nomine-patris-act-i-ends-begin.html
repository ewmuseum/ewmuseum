Date: Today OUR STORY SO FAR The woman called Desade is better known as Alexandra Pierce; she is Sin City Championship Wrestling's Spider in the Web, and, at one time, she was one of the most hated figures in this profession. She was vile, cruel, and heartless, but, then, so were many of her contemporaries. Her crowning achievement – if one could call it that – came in early 2004, when she conspired with Wyatt Connors to close the National Wrestling Council, closing down four federations and leaving almost two thousand people suddenly looking for work. In the spring of 2008, Pierce returned to the sport after what she would term a 'sabbatical' but others would call a lengthy commitment. She came at the head of the devastating Dead Man's Hand stable, and they ran roughshod over SCCW for well over a year. Her successes are noteworthy; her triumphs – again, if one could call them that – almost legendary. There were no lengths the Hand would not stoop to, no deceptions she considered too convoluted. Though Pierce has never ascended to the top of Sin City's food chain, she simply lurked outside it. Few wrestlers have been as feared. And that was only Pierce's day job. Between shows, away from the wrestling matches and the lies she spun on the Temptation program, the Spider answered to another master, a group called the Order of St. Julian. The Julian Order purported to enforce some arcane balance that, conveniently, only they saw or understood. As in the ring, outside of it, Alexandra and her fellows were determined, unkind, and ruthless, and their occupation easily explained travel across the United States and around the globe. Her colleagues considered the wrestling business little more than a cover; some enjoyed it more than others, but it was still less important than the mission, and they thought nothing from SCCW could cross over into their world of secrets, rumors, bargains, and lies. They never counted on Amy Campbell. Campbell's meteoric rise to the top of SCCW ran on a parallel with the Hand's. Though Aimz was never a card-carrying member of the sect, Pierce's plans often coincided with Amy's matches, and they shared enemies by the bucketload. If those foes could not be felled by the three-time Universal Champion's athletic acumen, they would often falter due to Pierce's planning. What no one – not their fellow wrestlers, not Pierce's superiors in the Order, and not even Campbell's own husband knew – was that Alex and Amy started seeing each other in secret over the fall of 2008. What neither woman knew was that that would change everything. It was Amy who unearthed Quinn Gregory, Alexandra's hidden teenaged daughter in a private school in Dallas. More importantly, it was Amy who showed Alex there was a better way, and opened the icy Desade's heart to honest emotion. Their relationship splintered the Dead Man's Hand, costing Pierce her best friend, her former lover, and eventually her membership in the Order itself. With Alexandra's newfound appreciation for doing the right thing came her own rise as a professional grappler, culminating in Pierce's win in the seventh iteration of the GTT tournament, an accolade no other woman has ever come close to. It should have been the happiest moment of her life, should have finally shed her image as the villain who had closed the NWC and then returned to destroy lives. However, that victory came the weekend Amy Campbell discovered she'd been manipulated, pushed, and twisted by Desade, just like everyone else. Campbell left her then, left Alex alone with her guilt and her self-recrimination, with only her daughter willing to remain at her side. Alone, lonely, and out in the cold, Pierce has tried to help others where she could, tried to undo the damage people like the Julians caused. But some sins can never be forgiven. Some lies can never be untold. The Order of St. Julian is not what it was when she was a part of it – not even what it was two weeks ago. History is about to rear its ugly head once more, and this time its sights are squarely set on Pierce's child. PROLOGUE: Heavy Hangs The Head Concord, New Hampshire Three Days Ago It may not have seemed like it, but Kieran Whitechurch was running for his life. Trained operatives avoid foot chases whenever possible; when you're actually fleeing armed attackers, chances are the situation was already well out of hand. Kieran fit easily into the world of lawmaker and lobbyist, though, thanks to his lantern jaw and his smart, thousand-dollar suit. To the untrained eye, he was just another visitor to the Capitol in search of a bite to eat before returning to the business of government. Whitechurch was called the Paladin, and he certainly looked the part – he'd lost count of the number of people who expected him to speak with a British accent. Kieran turned away at sound of blaring sirens approaching from the south, peering over the wire frames of windowpane eyeglasses to study the menu displayed outside a nearby restaurant. The rescue personnel whipped down the road, headed for the burned-out husk of what used to be a quaint little brownstone. It was the last safehouse he had found, and he said a silent prayer for the kindly old couple who'd given him a cool glass of lemonade and some pleasant conversation just the night before. Movement in the reflection disturbed his reverie, but Kieran's vivid blue eyes were a touch slow in following and the shadow disappeared, another wolf hidden amongst the flock. Whitechurch kept his expression neutral, tugging open the diner's door to duck out of the record-setting heat. A three-piece suit might aid him in blending in, but it also made him overdressed and eager for someplace with air conditioning. The restaurant was owned by a chain and filled with dark wood and retro memorabilia. The girl behind the podium tugged on a sunny smile, but it was all rote and it never reached her eyes. 'Hi,' the woman (her nametag read 'Maria') trilled, pulling a laminated menu from the stack on the table beside her. 'Just one this afternoon?' Kieran flicked his gaze through the sparse lunchtime crowd, smiling weakly. 'Afraid so,' he said, his voice a low rumble. He didn't come any closer; his world lived by old adages of books and covers and he felt justified in his paranoia. If Maria noticed, she didn't let on, leading Whitechurch on a somewhat circuitous path through the tables to a secluded table along the western wall. 'Here we are...' She fake-smiled again, setting the menu down. 'Victoria will be your server, and she'll be right with you.' 'Great, thanks.' Kieran didn't sit, plucking the Bluetooth from his ear with a disdainful sigh. 'You don't by chance have a payphone in here, do you? Seems my cell's given up the ghost.' It was as close as he could come to the truth; telling her it was in pieces in a burning building seemed out of bounds. 'Oh, that's terrible.' Maria's frown was as convincing as her smile. 'The payphones are in the back by the restrooms.' She pointed the way. 'I'll tell Victoria you'll be a moment.' 'Thanks.' Whitechurch took a long gulp of the water left on the table for him, waiting just long enough for the hostess to wander away. The heavy soles of his Italian loafers banged out a muffled rhythm on the carpeted floor, his pace as fast as he dared around the corner. He slowed when he reached the alcove, helpfully labeled 'Restrooms & Telephone'. One of the lights inside flickered strangely, casting the room in an odd kind of shadow. It was only a moment of hesitation, but it wrenched a small, aggravated sneer along his lips as he stalked inside, though he wasn't sure if he was angrier with himself or the people chasing him. These past few days had been especially hard, ever since the coordinated attacks that brought the Order of St. Julian to its knees. He didn't know who his friends were, but that was a regular thing for a Julian. This was worse – he didn't know who his enemies were. When they'd come, it was a faceless, nameless horde of men in black suits and mirrored sunglasses and military-issue hardware. His senior staff, friends all, was cut down in an eye blink, dead before anyone knew what hit them. Men and women he worked with, whose wives and children he'd seen grow up, and they were just gone. Kieran closed his eyes briefly, trying to shake away the rage and sorrow and guilt and more that threatened to consume him. There was no one left for him to call on for help – he wasn't sure if there was an Order of St. Julian anymore. Common sense said he should go to ground. His training told him to disappear until the manhunts stopped, keep his head down, and find out the extent of the damage that'd been done. Whitechurch was many years removed from active fieldwork, however, and he was up against an unknown force without support, without assistance, without anyone. He was only delaying the inevitable if he ran. The people who'd died to buy his escape deserved better than just the inevitable, however, and desperation made for strange choices. That didn't speed his fingers on the phone's keypad, though. The number was just a message line, an answering machine in a small room in the U.S. Virgin Islands, and he hadn't had cause to use it for some time. It wasn't that the number was long and he was dialing from memory that slowed his hand. He just wasn't sure if she'd respond. The machine picked up on the first ring – no message, no recorded voice, just a single sharp beep and then silence. Whitechurch knew better than to dawdle. He began without preamble. 'It's the Paladin.' She'd been the first to call him that, and no other introduction would be necessary. 'Don't know if this line's still being checked or is tapped or... what. I need help. It's bad out here. Chapterhouse has been compromised.' He paused, wiping the back of his hand against his mouth. His chuckle was bitter. ''Compromised'.' The word made him want to spit. 'It's... it's like that all over the place. I know you probably don't care. I know we've been...' His sigh was a sharp sound, like a knife drawn through silk, his eyes dancing past the phone and into the restaurant. No one paid him any mind. 'I hope we've been friends for too long to let what's happened get in the way. We've all done bad things. You don't get a reprieve just because you recognized it and couldn't make it work. You know how to find me. Just travel the way the gulls fly to the place where no sunlight shines.' Kieran slammed the receiver onto the metal hook with more force than was strictly necessary, hard enough that the phone shook. Leaning forward, he pinched thumb and forefinger to the bridge of his nose, suddenly acutely aware of how tired he really was. Protocol said he should wait for a response; if she were monitoring the line, she had twelve minutes to get in touch with him. Whitechurch doubted any answer was coming, but he could use some food in his stomach, and he forced himself to turn away from the phone. Three steps into the dining room and Whitechurch froze in place. Something was wrong. He couldn't place it, though – nothing seemed any different, at least on the face of it. There were no new guests, no overt attention from the waitstaff or unfamiliar cars lurking on the curb. Yet something felt off, like there was a buzzing in his head. Kieran touched the back of his hand to his brow; he was still perspiring even after fifteen minutes in the air conditioning. His palms were damp, his fingers shaking. His stomach rumbled, gorge rising, and he made a mad dash for the men's room. Kieran shoved open the pea green door to the first stall, dropping to his knees over the commode. In ten minutes, the main line to the restaurant would ring. Maria the Hostess would smile her empty-lipped grin, quirk her head to the side, and cast her green-eyed gaze across the restaurant. Her expression would twist in confusion as she glanced to the empty table where she was certain she seated the man. She'd apologize, and she'd tell the woman on the other line that the gentleman she's looking for isn't around just now, but that she'd be happy to take a message. She won't write it down, but it won't matter, because by then, it will be too late. ONE: There's No Place Dallas-Fort Worth International Airport Terminal C, Gate C-17 There was something about a used paperback Alexandra Pierce simply adored. The way the spine was already cracked, the indefinable stains on the paper, the folded back pages. She didn't shop used because it was cheaper, but because it was alive. She'd run her fingers across the book, feeling the creases, wondering what the person who read the book last was like, what they were doing, what they thought of it. It was almost as intriguing as the story itself, another world for her to get lost in. She was lost in just such a book (A Gentleman's Game by Greg Rucka) when the dark-haired girl at her side poked her in the shoulder. 'I still can't believe I'm back,' Quinn Gregory said. 'I thought you hated this city.' At sixteen, she was taller than her mother, dressed in skinny jeans and a black t-shirt; she had quite the extensive collection of them, each with a different slogan. Today's read, 'My Life Is A Very Complicated Drinking Game'. Her mother looked up from the book; she wouldn't leave a mark of her presence by creasing the page herself, so she used her boarding pass to mark her place. Alexandra Pierce was an icy cold marble statue in the wrestling ring. She was inviolate, unflinching – the kind of foe no one wanted. She was also a mother, and no one else in Sin City could earn that kind of smile. 'I do hate it,' Alex said quietly. Her voice was always the kind of whisper that made a listener lean in just a little to hear it. 'It's hot, it's humid, and it's full of all kinds of nasty memories.' 'And some good ones, right?' the girl asked, her eyebrows climbing behind the dark frames of her cat's-eye glasses. 'I mean, you didn't leave me in a city you absolutely loathe, right?' A soft chime sounded, announcing the fasten seatbelts sign had gone out, and the plane suddenly became a cacophony of conversations, a hubbub the two women soon became lost in. 'I don't completely loathe it.' Pierce never sat in the window seat of an airplane – too many people to get in between her and the exit. She'd bought an extra seat between her and her daughter to assure they'd have some semblance of privacy. 'It's... it's complicated.' She didn't have to look to see her daughter mouth the words along with her. It was true: Quinn had spent almost nine years in Dallas under the care of a foster father, away from the chaotic life her mother lived. It was not a situation Pierce liked to revisit, and it had been very many years since Alexandra stayed in the city for longer than a night. 'It's always complicated, Mom.' Like many foster children, Quinn learned to travel lightly, just a patchwork, cross-stitched satchel slung over her shoulder. 'Just once I'd like you to be like, 'Yeah, that's pretty much what happened.'' 'My life doesn't get much easier than this.' Pierce's own bag was just as light, a wheeled leather suitcase she pulled from the overhead compartment. 'If we hate the place, why are we back?' They snuck and skirted through the line of commuters getting their luggage, slipping past with a small smile here and a softly spoken apology there. People in a rush in an airport were not uncommon, and it's quite amazing what a pretty girl is forgiven if she smiles. 'You have a match,' Alexandra finally explained. Once they were out in the concourse and she was able to stretch her legs, Pierce's speed quickly outstripped her daughter, who had to scurry to keep up. 'So do you,' Quinn said breathlessly. 'Clyde Fox, the Elite Tournament? The, uh... the Dirty Dozen, they're calling it.' 'I'll be ready for Clyde.' Pierce's words were clipped, as hurried as her steps. 'I knew a man like him once.' 'Okay, so we're here to... to train?' The girl had become roped into her mother's affairs over these last few months, and was herself scheduled to compete in just a few weeks against one of Pierce's former associates in the Dead Man's Hand. It was a match both of them dreaded, and one Alexandra had gone to great lengths to prevent, thus far fruitlessly. 'You have a place in Oakland for that.' The Dallas heat was legendary – upper nineties on the thermostat, the sixty to seventy percent humidity making the air thick like an electric blanket – and it assaulted the two women the moment the automatic doors parted. 'I do.' Pierce nodded slightly, sliding her sunglasses over her signature stony gray gaze. 'But you don't.' Quinn stopped in place as her mother charged ahead. 'Of my own? Uh, no? I was just gonna use yours. You know... until I decide whether this is for me.' 'They're making me cover insurance for your match personally.' Alex Pierce had a way of smiling so that you believed she was sincere, even when there was no earthly way she could be. Even Quinn had a difficult time telling when that smile was real. 'Amy is going to throttle me simply for agreeing to do that much. For my sake, you'd better not decide this is your career until you get out of high school.' Pierce turned, one hand lifted to shield her eyes from the sun as she waited for a tram to the Avis office. Quinn's lips twisted into something of a frown. Her mother was never the most approachable woman, never truly comfortable in her own skin. But her yearlong romance Aimz brought out a new side to the fearsome Spider – a lighter, almost... almost human spark that now threatened to go out whenever Campbell's name was mentioned. The breakup hurt Alexandra much worse than anything she'd ever receive in the ring. Quinn fell in at her mother's side. It was these bouts of depression that led her to accompany Alex on the SCCW tour, a decision that had hampered her schoolwork and led to the girl's upcoming, unwelcome match. 'Hey...' Gregory touched her hand to her mother's arm. 'Earth to Desade, come in please?' 'I hate that name.' Pierce's eyes dropped down to the street. 'I know.' Ordinarily, Quinn was louder than her mother; she was a teenaged girl, after all, and these things sort of came with the territory. But as the minibus purred to a halt at the curb, the teen's voice was quieter even than the Spider's death rattle. 'You were saying?' 'Ah, yes.' Alex wet her lips with the tip of her tongue, her smile for the driver more noticeably fake than Quinn had seen it in some time. 'I've arranged somewhere for us both to prepare.' 'Where?' 'You'll see.' If there were two words that Quinn Gregory hated to hear more than 'It's complicated,' it was those. * * * It may have been surprising to an onlooker how well-behaved Alexandra Pierce's only child really was. She looked like she'd be trouble, with her counterculture t-shirt, her dyed black hair, and her slouching shoulders. Even the nickname she'd proudly taken – the Devil's Daughter – implied some rebelliousness. In truth, the girl was almost stunningly obedient. She was attentive, mild-mannered, eloquent, and (her mother would argue) brighter than Alex herself at the same age. Which made her outburst all the more noteworthy as they pulled up to a mid-level ranch on the outskirts of the city. 'No way,' Quinn said. 'This is... you can't ask me to do this!' Alex rented a Chrysler 300M, raven grey with an engine like a racehorse. Her singular allowance had always been for fast, powerful vehicles, the one luxury she'd always been denied as a young girl. Pierce's father died when she was quite young, and her brothers were wrestling royalty, but she'd never shared in the fruits of the family orange juice fortune. On her sixteenth birthday, the Brothers Pierce conspired to send her into exile – men who plotted against her were forever the story of her life. She ended up in England, alone, cold, and penniless, robbing from churchgoers. She remembered one day, shivering on cobblestone sidewalks with a bit of cardboard for an umbrella, and she promised herself that when she had money, she would not settle. It was one of the few things that got her through those hard times. Of course, a few months later, Alexandra met Margaret Winters, and then she learned the true meaning of hard times, but she still never settled, and she always drove the best. Pierce enjoyed the custom leather interior, the premium handling, and the soundproofed interior, which kept her daughter's tirade contained inside. 'It's just for a few days,' her mother said calmly. 'I don't see the problem.' 'You don't see what—' Quinn began, so flabbergasted she could hardly speak. Her mother slid from the sedan, and the girl wrestled irritably with her seatbelt, stumbling out to follow. 'You don't see what the problem is? How about that the last time we were here, I was seven and you left me behind!' Pierce used the button on the key fob to open the trunk, retrieving both of their bags. 'Well, I'm not going anywhere this time,' she said, tossing the crocheted bag to her daughter underhand. 'I thought that much was obvious.' Gregory caught the satchel with numb fingers. 'So we're just going to knock on the door and ask my foster dad if we can, what, hang out for a couple of days while I train to wrestle a guy? David was pretty great, but I'm pretty sure that's pushing it.' 'Don't be silly.' Alex tromped up towards the house, the sharp snap of short heels on concrete a distinct counterpoint to the rumble of her suitcase's wheels. 'I happen to know he's not at home.' 'Uhm...' Quinn put a skip in her step, circling along the manicured lawn to walk backwards in front of her mother. 'And how do you happen to know this?' Pierce looked back to the car, shifting the handle she tugged the case with from her left hand to her right. 'Because he's won a Hawaiian cruise,' she murmured, digging again for the remote, this time to lock the car. She was making a show of their arrival, Quinn knew. She just hadn't figured out why. 'Some kind of sweepstakes or something.' Thin brows climbed Gregory's forehead. 'David didn't play—' Her mother flicked her gaze back to the door, hard gray eyes looming overtop of her sunglasses. The look was brief and droll, but enough to transmute the rest of that sentence to a muted 'Oh.' Alex unbound her ponytail, rolling the well-worn hair band that held it in place onto her wrist. 'Do you know where he keeps the spare key?' she asked, styling her wild locks as best as she could with just her fingers. 'What?' Quinn blinked in confusing, her mother's question taking a moment to process. 'Oh, yeah – it should be under that plant.' She knew enough not to point. 'Tie your shoe,' Pierce instructed, using her reflection in the dining room window to touch up her lipstick. The girl nodded, lifting her foot onto the stoop to tighten the laces of her sneakers. She was certain she'd be spotted as her slender fingers crept under the reddish brown pot, but no one raised an alarm as she stood, the key pressed into her palm by her thumb. 'So what's this?' Gregory asked. 'Little Girl List? Puppy Dog Eyes?' Quinn had been surprised to learn there actually was a manual for her mother's other line of work, and moreso that Alex still had hers, a dog-eared, spiral-bound thing that looked like it could've been printed at a Kinko's. She'd committed the handbook to memory; it was a piece of the lifestyle that'd so fascinated Gregory, a no-nonsense description of just how easy it was to pull the wool over the eyes of the common man. She knew firsthand that it worked, though she'd never admit just how well to her mother. Pierce touched her finger to the doorbell, summoning a smile that actually seemed fake for a change. 'Avon calling!' Alex beamed, a bit of twang burrowing into the words. The teen sighed in embarrassment and annoyance, half-turning to peer back longingly at the car. Her hand touched her mother's just long enough for the key to pass into Pierce's cupped fingers. They'd worked out mother-daughter versions of many of the old standards, though most amounted to her acting like a sullen kid. 'Just stand still,' Alex hissed stridently, louder than she would have normally. She maneuvered to keep any nosy neighbors from getting a good look at a girl they might recognize. Pierce waited a beat before she glanced up, tugging on what Quinn could only call her salesman's smile. 'Hi!' She trilled the word, tugging it out like a piece of taffy, sweet and stretched out. 'I'm Mary Lou Roper, and this darling girl is my daughter Lola...' Gregory felt kind of silly waving to a door, but she played her part. The motion allowed her mother to slip the key into the bolt with practiced aplomb, cracking the door open. 'Well, then...' Alex continued, launching into her nonexistent spiel. 'I'm with Avon, and I was wondering if we could get out of this accursed heat and I could show you some of the lovely new colors we have for the summer...' Pierce pushed the door open gently, and Quinn took that as her cue to dart inside. Alex followed, again struggling with her case. The girl didn't say a word until the door was safely shut and bolted, but once it was, she wheeled on her mother. 'What the fuck is going on?' she demanded. 'As far as the people out there are concerned, I'm David's daughter, and they wouldn't have blinked twice if I just wandered up and opened the door, you know.' 'They would have if I were with you.' The redhead spun away from the door, yanking her suitcase onto the sofa. 'Tell me again why we're here?' Quinn hadn't moved from the foyer, still feeling uncomfortably like an invader. 'I told you.' Alex pulled a plastic wrapped bundle from the bag. 'If you're really serious about doing this, you need to have something to ground you, some reason to keep going, and that can't be me.' She glanced up, binding her hair back into its typical spray of a tail. Once she'd shed the plastic smile of Mary Lou Roper, Pierce's expression dipped back into inscrutability, but at least she looked earnest. Gregory met her mother's expression with a cold, flat stare; it was in these moments that she most resembled the Spider. 'Now how about the truth?' 'Quinn, that is the truth. No matter how we both wish otherwise, this is the first home you had, and I'm hoping—' 'It's not all of the truth.' The girl folded spindly arms. 'If it were, you wouldn't have sent David away, we would've stayed at a hotel, and we damn sure wouldn't have broken into his fucking house. So I want to know what's going on.' 'It's... it's—' 'So help me, if you say, 'it's complicated,' I'm going to open this door and go dance on the front lawn.' 'Dammit, Quinn, it is compli—' 'I'll do it naked.' Gregory's brows lifted in a silent dare. 'That's bound to attract some attention, don't you think?' 'It's just a feeling, all right?' Pierce's own brow flickered worriedly. 'These past couple of weeks have been too... too quiet. It wasn't like Kathryn not to show up with Lane's belt up for grabs. All of my best sources have disappeared – Malcolm's, too. I don't even think I was being watched last week.' 'It's been like eight months since they passed that stupid judgment. I'd hope the fucking Order had better things to do by now.' 'I can't explain it, you know?' Alex perched at the edge of the couch cushion. 'It would be like waking up and being able to see only through one eye, or suddenly discovering the wind made no noise. It's there all the time, digging right under my skin. Something is going on, and I don't feel comfortable at home.' 'Why not just tell me? Why hide it?' The girl crept closer. 'Why are we sneaking around here?' 'I didn't tell you because you would have worried.' 'Fair enough.' Quinn's response was clipped, her arms unfolding to drop to her sides, fists still balled. 'And the other thing?' Pierce's eyes dropped, and she busied her hands with the bag. 'I think someone might be watching the house.' 'What?' Concern crumpled the girl to one knee. She tucked a lock of pitch-black hair behind one ear. 'Someone's watching David? But he... he's just a guy. He's not part of this, he didn't do anything!' Quinn looked away, blinking the tears out of her eyes. 'I always wanted my mom. For seven years, I always wanted my mom, and I never...' She swallowed hard. 'He was there for me, Mom.' 'I know.' It was all Alex could think of to say. 'How can you be sure that someone's watching the house?' 'I can't.' Alex turned the plastic bundle in her hands. 'But it's what I would do.' The girl's sea-green eyes snapped back up to her mother's, her lips jerked into a bow. 'Sometimes I don't know what to say to you,' Quinn said as she slid back to her feet, silent a moment. Alex did not say anything, always terrified she'd shatter the peace between them. Quinn finally sighed. 'So we're here because something is happening but you don't know what, and whatever it is made you nervous, but not so nervous that we go to, like, Sri Lanka, but instead to a place that you think is being watched.' 'Pretty much.' Pierce's smile was small. 'Sounds pretty crazy when you say it like that.' 'The shirt's not an accident, Mom.' Quinn turned, adopting a light crouch, left foot behind the right, one fist up. 'That's it, right? No other secrets?' She mimed a few punches. 'Just you and me, holed up in someone else's home, practicing wrasslin'?' 'Well...' Alex squinted one eye closed, glancing up. 'There is one other thing.' Gregory's hands fell again, palms slapping against her hips. 'Something else?' 'Just... I have tickets to the Rangers game tomorrow.' 'Baseball?' Quinn made a face. 'Snoozeville. Skip it.' 'I happen to like baseball. And they're expensive seats.' She favored her daughter with that smile again, and Quinn's jaw clenched. 'You don't have to come,' Pierce qualified. 'Yeah, I should... I should find out if the guys ever found someone to sing for our band.' 'You had a band?' 'We were...' Quinn wiped her hands on her pants. 'We were spectacularly awful.' She trotted back to the foyer, picking up her satchel. 'So it's okay if I hang with them tomorrow while you're doing the whole baseball thing? I promise I'll keep an eye out for anything suspicious, but these people... I think they'll protect me if it comes down to it.' 'It's fine,' Alex said. She'd hardly finished the sentence before the girl's stomping footsteps carried her back to the room she'd used for years. Pierce flicked her gaze out to the street. Not for the first time, she wished she had someone she could tell the whole truth to, but those kinds of luxuries came few and far between. TWO: To The Ballgame Rangers Ballpark at Arlington The next evening Randall preferred his popcorn in a box, something he could only ever get at the game. He'd say it had something to do with texture – it felt fresher, tasted the way popcorn was supposed to taste. The truth was that it was old-fashioned, and the same could be said about the man himself. He was tall and lanky, with beanstalk arms and longer legs, as if he'd never grown past that gangly stage of youth, though from looking at him, he could have been anywhere from thirty-five to sixty-five. His face was cut from sun-burnished leather, and it only showed his wrinkles when he frowned – something he'd been doing more than his fair share of recently. 'You're sure?' he asked the air, knobby fingers digging back into the box. His voice, like his eyes, held the promise of thunder, laced with the hint of an accent most would call Texan but the more refined ears hereabouts would place in western Arkansas. ''Pretty sure' is not the same as 'take it to the bank,' an' I'm disinclined to act on half-assed assumptions.' He was on the promenade level, not far from the concessions stand he'd twice visited already. On the field below, the Rangers were losing again to the goddamned Orioles, for crying out loud – a fact that did not improve his mood one iota. 'Now, Miss Baptiste. Not later, not tomorrow, not 'in time for the seventh inning stretch,' but now. You tell Calloway they've been looking for near unto nine goddamn years, and he can give me a half-hour of pictures. 'Thirty-two, head of the Order of St. Julian cell based out of New England. His men call him Paladin. He's smart and he's clever, and, as of a couple of days ago, he's on the run.' Pierce walked around the outside of the table, her hands still behind her back. She'd changed out of the garb of the baseball fan, and her kitten-heeled pumps made a hollow clatter on the floor, unintentionally setting a rhythm for her explanation. 'I'm sure you have received reports of my former associates' activities quieting down these last few weeks,' she continued. 'I am here today to tell you that this is because they have been compromised on every level. Thanks to some information I've received from a very reliable source...' She tipped her head to Devonshire. 'I can say with nearly one hundred percent certainty that the group you've been tracking, the organization I was a member of, has had its head chopped off and something else is growing there in its place.' 'What's growin' there?' Milo was the first of them to speak. 'We don't know the answer to that yet,' Randall answered. Milo nodded, squinting up at the plasma screen. 'But you think this Whitechurch guy does?' 'I think...' Alex said carefully. 'I think that if anyone is going to have a chance to, it's Kieran. In my experience, he's stuffy, arrogant, and has a stick up his ass a mile long, but he got to where he is – was – because he is smart and observant.' 'And he's a friend of yours,' Nick muttered. 'That's what this is really all about, right? You'll give up information about what's left of this stupid 'Order' if your buddy is snatched up off the street before the mysterious guys with guns fill him full of holes? You two sit here while we're your errand boys?' 'That's enough.' Randall put some extra bass in his voice. 'I tolerate your mouth because you're good at what you do and because your daddy's support goes a long way to keeping the lights on, but—' 'I can handle this.' Alexandra stepped around the table, leaning down on the armrest of the man's chair. 'I assure you, Mr. Lee, I have no intention of staying here in this...' She cast a disapproving glance around the room. 'This... place while you carry the heavy load. I have known Kieran Whitechurch for almost seventeen years now, and he called me to fix this. Despite that, I am going to conspire to have him arrested by a group that I didn't even know existed until just a few days ago. I know this is for his own good, so I will be doing this. But I am going to be out there, in the field, with my hands just as dirty as yours.' The whisper was a growl, the exasperation finally slipping into her voice. 'If this fails, it will fail because of me, not because of anyone else. If we fuck up, you will all come back in here and read fantastic things that you think can't possibly be true. But if we fail, my friend will be dead, no matter how ridiculous you think the people that killed him are. So forgive me if I don't have as much patience for your rebellious antics as Randall here does. Do we understand each other?' 'Y-yes.' Nick nodded furiously, glancing to Randall for support but finding none. 'I just wasn't... wasn't sure.' 'Well, get sure, Mr. Lee.' She straightened, tugging at the hem of her shirt again. 'Otherwise, you can stay behind, no matter how good you're supposed to be.' 'All right, all right,' the Southern man finally lifted his hand, his cigarette tracing a path of smoke in the air. 'Knock it off, the both of you.' Randall ground the cigarette out in his ashtray. 'Ms. Pierce has a lead that might help us find Whitechurch before the bad guys do. I'll be going over operational parameters with her. Rest of you, be ready for wheels up in an hour. Dismissed.' The rest of them stood up, Milo turning his smirk to Baptiste. 'When was the last time you saw anybody make Captain America look like he wanted his mother?' Milo asked. 'I don't know.' Baptiste cast a furtive glance to Pierce, who was immediately engaged in huddled communication with Randall. 'I'm not even sure what we just saw.' Drusilla stood with them, stacking a pile of (utterly useless) papers. 'You just saw Desade,' she said, beaming. If she noticed the strange looks she got as she trailed the others out of the room, Devonshire did not let it on. * * * Ten minutes later, Randall leaned back in his chair, kicking expensive boots onto the table. 'Can't be done,' he said. Alex perched beside him, leaning back on her hands. 'It can. I've done it.' 'With a team that had your back.' He turned over his shoulder towards the narrowed hallway. 'Half of those people think you and your ex are sociopaths.' 'They'd be at least half right,' Pierce said. 'They can think whatever they want as long as they do what you hired them to. But if you're serious, then I need to see what they can do in the real world. Not in a small, controlled environment with me in a pregnant suit, but out there in the muck.' For the longest time, Randall didn't say anything. He didn't even move, save for the his lips on the filter of his Marlboro. She didn't continue the argument, or press him for an answer, but let him work it through. 'You ain't gonna fuck this up just 'cause you think you'll get drummed out back to your real life?' he asked finally. 'With respect, I'm not sure I have a real life anymore,' she answered. 'Not after this. I'm just as much at risk as Kieran and Drusilla.' A third cigarette butt joined the first two in the turquoise tray. 'You've got a green light,' he said. Pierce slid off the table, her smile more eager than she could remember it. 'Thank you. You won't regret it.' 'See that I don't.' He leaned back, head tilted to the side. 'And you keep an eye on your girl. I don't trust her.' 'Neither do I.' Alex lingered briefly in the doorway. 'Not sure if I ever did.' 'Not exactly comforting, you know.' She did not answer, her heels distressingly noiseless on the hard concrete floor. Randall was left alone in the conference room, his faraway storm-blue eyes focused on nothing, deep in thought. FOUR: Like Home The next morning Santa Monica, California 'The place where no sunlight shines?' Alexandra Pierce had long since gotten used to a voice in her head that wasn't her own, but every so often, it still sent shudders down her spine, especially when that voice belonged to someone like Drusilla Devonshire. 'There's an awful lot of sunlight here is all I'm saying,' Dru continued. Even at ten o'clock on a Monday morning, the Santa Monica Pier was quite crowded, the high heat drawing families out to play in the ocean. By mid-afternoon, it was supposed to be close to a hundred, and by then the place would be packed. Alexandra Pierce stood at the edge of the pier, resting her forearms on the railing. She knew the others were buried out there, but the only one she could find was Drusilla, hovering nearby on a stool in front of a pad. She was drawing caricatures for tourists, a light brown wig held down by a tie-dyed headband. It was an over-the-top hippie disguise, and it was surprisingly fitting in this place. Alex's own garb was much more muted, a lavender sarong tied around her waist, with a matching, floral print bikini top. Her hair was red, but still not hers – it was longer, curling down to the middle of her back, and she had a pink flower through the side. She squinted up at the sun, fat and yellow in the crystal blue sky. 'It's the right place,' she said. 'How do you know?' Since Alex took him to task, Nicholas Lee had been quieter and much more respectful. His position in the water marked the end of their dragnet, and he floated listlessly out in the ocean. 'He could have been talking about anywhere.' 'He wasn't.' Pierce turned away from the rail, buying a frozen lemonade from the concessions stand. She sucked delicately on the end of a bendy straw, circling down from the pier to the beach. 'He'll be here if he can be.' 'And you don't think he can.' Baptiste had said the fewest words to her of any of them, but Alex could see the alert, observant girl that Randall took under his wing. 'You think he's gone already, don't you?' The redhead's path seemed haphazard, not carefully planned, but it was designed to bring her the most attention – it would be up to Kieran to find her. 'No, I don't.' Her voice was quiet, distant. 'He'll be here.' 'It's been four days, Alex. He was half a world away.' 'He'll be here,' she repeated, harder the second time. 'Now knock off the chitchat unless you see something.' 'I've got a black SUV, how 'bout that?' Milo was among them in a floppy canvas bucket hat, a cooler at his side. 'We're twenty minutes out of L.A.,' Baptiste pointed out. 'I grew up a half-hour from here. There's probably more black SUVs per capita here than anywhere else in the world.' Milo made a grumpy noise, half a grunt and half a growl. 'Hundred degrees outside and two guys stay in their car at the damn beach? That seem right to you? I think we found us the bad guys.' 'I think we found our boy, too.' It was Drusilla, currently drawing expansive pigtails on a painting of a seven-year-old girl whose father would later term the picture 'creepy.' 'Guy in the Miami Vice leisure suit, seven o'clock.' Alex found the man, his hands in his pockets as he walked towards the pier. She hurried her steps as much as she dared. 'Confirmed. Keep an eye out.' She didn't look up as she approached, but they met once he stepped into the shadows. 'Been a long time,' she murmured. 'I didn't think you'd get the message. This is the second morning I've been here.' They were old souls, and older hats at this. Neither turned to face the other, not fully. Kieran dropped to a knee, adjusting the straps of his sandals. Alexandra stood six or seven feet away, her iPhone touched to her ear. 'I had some help with that.' She tucked a lock of hair behind her ear. 'I wasn't certain you'd be alive.' 'Always told you you'd have to kill me yourself to get rid of me.' His smile took years off his face. 'You look good.' 'What's going on, Kieran?' She finally looked down to him, her brow creased. 'I've heard some crazy stories.' 'The truth is a good deal worse.' Whitechurch rose, shielding his eyes with one hand. 'It usually is.' 'Look, I'm sorry,' he began. 'We've got incoming...' It was Milo in her ear, voice laced with worry. 'I count four – no, six – bogeys.' She held up a hand, shaking her head gently. 'Not now. We need to get you out of here.' ''We'?' 'There's no time to explain,' Alex said briskly, stepping to his side. Her voice softened considerably. 'There's a group of men here for you. I'm with the Center for Espionage—' 'The Crowd?' Kieran hissed. 'You set me up to be taken in by the goddamn Men in Black?' 'Not 'set up'.' She tugged on his elbow to impel him into motion, but their foots were slow on the sand. 'I just started working there today, and I think they can help. Besides...' She peered down the pier as a woman on a bike slowed one set of black-clad gunmen – that would have been Erica stammering apologies. 'It's not like you have much of a choice.' 'Jesus Christ!' Nick cried. Somewhere he was staggering out of the water, his board under his arm. 'Five now!' Drusilla sounded almost giddy; Alex imagined her skipping. Kieran sighed sharply, but he was smiling. 'I hate you.' 'The feeling is mutual, I assure you.' Pierce's smile was tight against her skull. She'd hate to admit it, but she'd missed this. 'Now move.' She gave him a hard shove up the soft incline, and quickly, the sand gave way to concrete and then asphalt. No bullets came, no ricochets or warning shots to deter them, and Alex turned over her shoulder once they'd gotten into the parking lot. 'This is wrong,' she said. 'What's wrong? You said people were coming, so shouldn't we be going?' 'If they're as good as Drusilla—' 'You brought Drusilla into this?' Kieran's eyes widened. 'She's a psychopath!' 'I told you I had some help with that message.' She pressed her fingers into her ear. 'Dammit, where are you people?' 'Two minutes.' It was Nick, and Alex couldn't hold in the groan. 'It's the best I can do, dammit.' 'Fucking hell.' Alex raced through the lot, her eyes focused on the buttons of the car doors, looking for one that was unlocked. 'We need two minutes.' 'I've got a back door,' Kieran said. 'We can get into the front from here.' An SUV tore into the parking lot, tires squealing. 'We don't have time,' she said. 'Just get in!' Kieran slid into the backseat of a boxy, early '90s sedan. Alex followed, unbinding the knot at the nape of her neck. 'What are you doing?' Whitechurch asked. 'Alexandra—' Pierce pulled the flower out of her hair, pulling it down to cover her face. 'Shut up, Kieran,' she growled. The gunmen's vehicle crept through the lot, both men giving hard stares to each vehicle in passing. When they reached the sedan, Alex arched upwards, tossing her longer-than-it-needed-to-be hair back, her fingers splayed but her hands only partially covering herself. She knew they could see, but could only hope they were focused on finding their target. It was a long, long moment before she heard the crunch of tires on asphalt, and Alex rolled off Kieran, seated in the footwell as she retied her swimsuit. He moved beside her, sitting up partway. 'That was...' 'That was business, Kieran.' Alex Pierce had the coldest voice many people would ever hear. 'Don't get your hopes up.' 'They're not, I just...' He sighed. 'Here, let me. Much easier when someone else does it.' Quick movements repaired the knot, but Alex leaned forward, away from his touch. She'd lost the earpiece in the uproar – thing didn't fit right in her ear, not the way the old ones used to – and she jammed it back in. 'Where the fuck are—' Their government-issue van jerked to a halt outside the sedan. 'We're here.' It was Drusilla; her worry was disconcerting. Whitechurch clambered out of the car first, taking Pierce by the hand and pulling her to her feet. 'Where did they go?' he asked, approaching the car. Nicholas emerged behind him, still bare-chested from his surfing expedition. He shoved Kieran hard into the side of the car. 'Kieran Whitechurch, by order of the United States of America, you are under arrest and bound by law.' 'We don't have to do this,' Alex said quickly. 'He's going to come willingly.' 'We gotta.' Milo shrugged slightly; he was behind the wheel. 'Protocol.' Lee continued without a word, binding Whitechurch's wrists in front of him. Nick's shove into the van's interior was no gentler than the one into the side of the vehicle. 'We'll get you out of this,' Alex said. 'I promise you.' Kieran pulled himself up onto the bench with as much dignity as he could muster. 'Everything go okay?' Devonshire asked Pierce as the Spider climbed in after. The other redhead spared them all from her glare, focusing it out the window. 'It went fine.' She sighed sharply. 'Where did they go?' 'Your girl there disabled one of them.' Baptiste sat in the front beside Milo, and her jaw was set as she stared ahead. From her expression, Pierce guessed the 'disabling' had been somewhat violent. 'Once that happened, the rest scattered. We have clearance to take off from Burbank in forty minutes. Kieran will be debriefed once he's secure.' Baptiste went on from there, laying it all out in excruciating detail, but Pierce wasn't really listening anymore, staring out the window. A lot of things were bothering her about this, but one more than the rest. She didn't feel any guilt. It felt natural, freeing – it came back to her like riding a bike. She felt like she'd come home, and she wasn't sure if that was a good thing or not. 'You're sure everything was okay?' Dru murmured. 'Amy lives nearby, doesn't she? Do you want to go say hi?' Pierce didn't answer any of the questions, just nodding. She didn't say anything until well after they'd taken off. She didn't feel Devonshire's eyes on the back of her neck, but if she had, she'd know why. Kieran had tied a different knot. EPILOGUE: That Wears The Crown Nineteen Hours Later The Sandpit The place hadn't been built with a brig in mind. At its core, the Sandpit was a research installation, and they'd been forced to hold Kieran in an office in the Sandia Industries complex itself. The room had been set up for interviews, and a one-way mirror separated it from the next room. Alex sat in the next room for a long time. She hadn't ever been a heavy sleeper, and the night they'd returned was full of debriefing sessions, a lot of rehashing, and the study of satellite photos. This was followed immediately with a phone interview to hype the SCCW show scheduled five days later in Richmond, where she'd endured a morning radio shock-jock who only seemed to want to talk about Amy Campbell's 'landing strip'. Then came the reunion with Quinn, whose muscles were sore, and her eyes bloodshot, but her smile terribly grateful. Pierce negotiated a half-hour reprieve from Randall to spend with the girl, and she'd spent most of it trying to steer the conversation away from what happened in Santa Monica and towards Quinn's training here. Even after that – after Alex had pleaded exhaustion and begged her daughter for the chance to sleep – she'd found herself up here, alone, watching Kieran, who slept on the chair they'd left him in, his face on the table. No one had questioned him yet; the guards said they were to wait for Randall himself. When the knock came at the door, she expected it to be another one of them. Only Milo hadn't come to see her since they landed. The others wanted to express their thanks for the help at the pier, to tell her that her reputation hadn't been a lie – in Drusilla's case, to make sure Alex was all right. Really, they were sizing up the competition. 'Go away,' Alex muttered into the mouth of her teacup. 'It's me, Mom.' Quinn slipped in without being given permission. 'I'm worried.' 'So am I.' 'I heard Dru killed a guy.' Pierce chuckled softly, taking a delicate sip from her tea. 'Typical. It wasn't fun, Quinn. It's not fun.' 'But it is what you do.' 'Yeah.' Her whisper was little more than a breath. 'Seems that way.' The silence that fell between them was awkward, like Alex put up a wall between them, one Quinn wasn't quite sure yet how to climb. 'What was so important about him?' She gestured into the room. 'It's not about what happened to the Order or what he can bring or even that you guys used to be friends, is it?' 'No.' Alex shook her head just a little. 'It's not.' 'Then what?' Alexandra Pierce turned slowly to the side, setting the cup back down into its saucer. The clatter of china on china was the loudest thing in the room for quite some time. 'He's your father,' Pierce said finally, very, very quietly. * * * Six Days Earlier Concord, New Hampshire Kieran Whitechurch staggered out the back door, his head down and his stomach lurching. He'd been poisoned, he was sure. He didn't know when or how – it could have been the water there at the diner or even the lemonade the night before, for all he knew. His fever was high, his palms sweaty, and the vertigo nearly sent him to his knees on three separate occasions. Whitechurch didn't see the Rolls Royce until he'd already staggered into the hood, the rearview mirror poking him in the ribs. 'Hey, watch it!' he slurred. 'I'm walking here.' The driver put the car into park, tugging his gloves tight as he clambered out of the car. Kieran looked up, seeing two of the man – and he was already twice the size of a normal man. 'I don't want any trouble,' Whitechurch said, and he tried to run. The state he was in, just the thought of it sent his stomach to lurching again. The chauffeur grabbed him by the back of his collar, spinning Whitechurch around into a short jab to the ribcage. That took the fight out of him, and the big man in the dark suit was able to drag him back to the car, tossing Kieran into the back. There were two people in the backseat already, but first thing he remembered was the shoes – deep red Manolo Blahnik Hukas, the left crossed over the right. Unwillingly, his eyes followed the bare leg up, up, impossibly high, and he groaned. Kathryn Shaw was Alexandra Pierce's right hand for years, a devastating brunette with a smile worth dying for – a smile she shared with him then. 'Hello, Kieran,' she purred. He'd always liked the way she said his name, and he suspected many men shared that same feeling. 'I have someone here who'd love to meet you.' She glanced to the side, and Kieran followed her dancing hazel eyes. He pushed himself upright, vision swimming, but he wasn't sure how he'd missed the man in the turquoise suit and eight hundred dollar loafers. The man had strange hair, the same color as a trumpet, yanked back into a tight tail. A wisp of a goatee struggled to make him look less feminine, and it did not quite succeed. The only name he ever gave was Mr. Bronze, and it was by his command that the Order of St. Julian had been brought to his knees. And what Mr. Bronze said was: 'I have a proposition for you.' It was followed immediately by: 'Please don't puke on my shoes.'
