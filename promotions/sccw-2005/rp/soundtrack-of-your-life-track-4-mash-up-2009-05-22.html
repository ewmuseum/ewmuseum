Soundtrack of your life: Track 4 (Mash Up)

22 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 22, 2009 Liam Lynch, "My United States of Whatever" (Fake Songs, 2003) vs. Barenaked Ladies, "Falling for the First Time" (Maroon, 2000) May 22, 2009 Michael It was a competition of sounds that saw the end of this competition. It was an anticipated sound that followed a sickening thud. The silence of us in attendance only amplified the sharpness with which the referee slapped his hand against that canvas. One. Two. And another hand slapped the mat with what was a photo finish to the ref’s. It was all over. There was no clear winner, but it was clear that our horse had lost this race. ----- Mitchell That sound was quick on my heels, and stuck in my head. With each passing layer of concrete and plaster I put between us, my ears honed in on that faint sound even more. It was nothing descriptive, yet a primal sound that struck at the hearts of men, or this man at least. It plucked the heart strings to form a harmony of the external and internal. It was interrupted by my own out of tune grunting as we went. "We are just about there Mitchell," the man under my arm encouraged me, "just a little further." "Stevie," I responded following another grunt, "I told ya man, I am alright. I can do this on my own. I’ve been through more than a little swat to the steel before and walked it off. I can’t see how this would be any different." The man in the zebra shirt slowed our pace and pulled my arm from behind his head, to get a good look at my face. I must imagine he saw the grin curled up on my lips that added to the confidence of my brows, and was subtracted by the sweat pouring from my forehead over the discoloured skin. Or maybe he just saw a damn stubborn fool. Either way he turned from my head and just shook his own. It was obvious that his concern for my well being far outweighed my own. Begrudgingly, he took my weight off his shoulders and took a step back from me. As I put the full weight of two hundred and forty pounds on that leg I felt the rush of the blood flowing past the area of swelling, and it felt anything but swell. I could feel his eyes still on me, looking at my walking, just waiting for me to break down and ask for his help again. I don’t blame him; he really hasn’t had the time to get to know me. He couldn’t know how fiercely independent I liked to think myself. After about two dozen more careful, yet still awkward steps, we had reached the centre of the labyrinth that the EnergySolutions Arena had easily become to a weary competitor. There before us were two fresh faced kids in red golf shirts, who looked way too happy to be seeing me hobbling their way (When I say ‘kids’, I mean they were probably only a few years older than me) . There was a bounce in the step, and a quickness with which the attendants whisked me to a chair they had set up. With NASCAR pitcrew-like efficiency they had striped away boot, sock and knee pad from my left leg. The female attendant was busily feeling up and down the leg. Job done, Steven Timmons began back to his post in the guerrilla position, to wait for his next assigned match. That was until I bellowed to him, "Stevie." He stopped in mid-stride and turned toward me. "Steve, thanks." He gave a knowing nod of his head, and was all the receipt I needed. "Hey man, I am sorry too. Looks like the tear down crew will be short one tonight." And he was gone around the corner, back into that maze. "Mind not poking that swollen part?" was my simple request to the overeager emergency attendant. Not so simple apparently, as with a quizzical looks about her, she dove back to the swollen area of my leg, halfway down the shin, about the original break site. So I shift my glance from the young girl’s face, which I ashamedly felt like introducing to my right fist, up a little ways to my knee and the lines of red beneath the course of hair. Raised marks that tell a story, like a treasure map to a memory. There was that long gash over the knee cap where they went in; twenty marks along side it to correspond to the ten staples they used to shut it up. Two spider marks to the side of the leg, just below the knee, matching to the two top anchor screws. The swelling was such that I could not see my ankle and the two more spider marks that I know were there. After another half minute of inspection her head swung up with the helpful advice of, "there is some serious bruising here, and substantial swelling. I cannot tell whether or not there is any fracturing. You should get an X ray." My first thought was that she was probably getting paid better than me, and her best diagnosis was the same as mine, but I decided not to voice that displeasure. She was probably enough of a sadist that just such a comment would have her back to the swollen leg, and the power trip of making a guy my size cringe. No, instead I opted for what I think she misinterpreted as a joke. "What? Go to a hospital, here in the States? And pay for health care. HA!" I don’t know if it was the Canadian pride in Tommy Douglas’ work, or just the stinginess with which I went about departing with the few bills in my wallet, but I resolved there that this night would not be passed away in a waiting room in Salt Lake. No, I would enjoy my time as a fan, with a free ticket and backstage access to a pay per view production. And so with one boot on my foot, and the other in my arms I walked back to the locker room to slip out of the spandex shorts. I didn’t say a word to the boys in the back, and they returned the silence. Not one question of health, and that’s just about the only way I would want it. ----- Michael As the night wore on my voice was wearing out. If I could count so high after such stimulating beverages, I would count over four dozen moments that drew out my much heated and passionate shouting. But I ordered up another round to wet the whistle. The undercard was done with and the feature presentation was on the precipice. Nothing says that you are a fan more than paying $9.50 for cheap American draft. ----- Mitchell The view from the upper bowl of the EnergySolutions Arena might not have had the best sight of the ring, but it was great to see all these people packed into the building to watch this entertainment. These children were fuelling their dreams with thoughts of wrestling and performance, if only a piece to the vast potential that the children possessed within them. I remember when I was them just short years ago, and now with this profession perhaps my last link to them, to say their acceptance of me was anything less than thrilling would be a horrid understatement. It was this sound of acceptance, the primal and childish cheering and jeering that filled this arena once more in anticipation to the main event of our card. The cage was being lowered and secured around the ring when I felt a vibration in my pants. "He- Hello?" I answered. The other end tried to say something, but I had no luck picking that sound out of the decibel levels of the acoustics about me. "Hold on," I instructed whoever was on the other end while I made my way into the corridors, and a place not much quieter, but enough so that I might carry this exchange. "Hello?" I tried again. "Hey Chief, that was some show you put on there tonight." That voice belonged to a dear friend, Tommy Kowolski. It was a welcomed one, and maybe the only one that could stir up a conversation, get me out of my head, and shift the focus away from the throbbing five feet from my head. "Yo, Tommy! You actually splurged to watch a SCCW pay per view?" "And miss your PPV debut? Not a chance. You had it too man…" "I know." "And then that angel dude showed up. How is that leg doing?" His voice sounded sincere, but without pause enough for me to respond, he was back to talking about the match. "I mean man, without him you could be chilling with a title shot in your back pocket right now." "Yeah man, I know, it was close. But the leg is doing alright," I lied. I don’t know why I would lie to a friend, but I guess I have always been down playing my injuries. Lie to a promoter just to keep working, but the dishonest reporting of my health had come commonplace. Besides, the commandment was against baring false witness against a neighbour, not whether or not you think you will be able to walk in the morning. "How is all that going though man? I mean, what’s it like working with those guys? Have you met Aimz yet?" Tom had plenty of questions he wanted to ask, I could tell. I started by explaining the questions in the order that I remembered them being asked, but must confess I might have disregarded a few. "It is really going well man. It’s a lot of travel, getting the see the world and all. I think I might be just as happy to be confined to Ninth Ave for the rest of my life though. Everyone in the back seems to have formed their own cliques. It is more than a bit like a high school atmosphere back here. And kind of, I have passed Aimz in the hall. Never really talked with her though…" "How does she look?" he interjected quickly. His interest was the same interest that we share about this woman, or rather girl back then, when we were teenagers and the diminutive redhead illuminated the television screens on Tuesday nights. And I must have told you before that Tommy’s interests in this profession were heavily hinged on its ‘chick getting’ ability. "Heh," I chuckled to myself as he reverted back to a sixteen year old," she isn’t looking too bad. I mean, taking in compensation for that fact that she has, for a living, for the past eight plus years, been stuck in close quarters with juiced up muscle bound berserkers looking to kill her in the face. And the drug addiction. Other than that, she ain’t looking that bad. A solid two out of three." "I guess TV does add a half a point. Oh, yeah, they started ripping up Ninth today." Getting home this time would be just that much harder. In the background noise of the phone, I could hear the needs of my favourite little girl being voiced, and foresaw the conversation coming to an end. Confirming my beliefs, Tom started, "Ah, hey man, the little one is just waking up from a nightmare. Daddy stuff I got to attend to, you know?" "Yeah, I know." "Alright man, you take care of yourself." "Take it easy Tommy. I will be seeing you shortly. Night." "Night," was the final word. I slipped the folded cellular back into my pocket and myself back into the stands. I had missed a good chuck of the match already, but with a quick glance saw that the action could not be contained by a cage. This would be a night, a match, a moment that will live on in the fleeting thoughts as these children grow into adults. ----- Michael The final bell went, and the people that would have been animalistic in their grunting and screaming through the duration of the night now relished the opportunity to prove that the comparison extended to actions and manners. It was nothing short of a mosh pit trying to find our way from the floor seats. I think I saw Dave double over one man with a well placed, yet not well intended, elbow to the gut. I am sure they must have piped some message to the ones watching from the comfort of their own living rooms that these men, and women, were trained individuals, and to not try this at home. Me thinks the same sentiment might have needed to be spread to those in attendance. Did these people absorb the testosterone that poured into that cloth stretched over plywood? ----- Mitchell Soon it was all over, and the title still remained around the tiny waist that wore it in. I was able to slip my way out before the Utah crowd started the stampede to the traffic jam that was awaiting them getting out of the parking lot. A visit to the locker room to grab my stuff and I was all too willing to bid adieu to Salt Lake and bury tonight’s disappointment in the sands here. The talents’ entrance has been a densely populated area after the shows, I was quick to recognize, and normally that would have been nothing to me. However, this night was not normal, I just wanted to get in a cab, get to the hotel and get some sleep. That must be why it took an empty water bottle to the side of my head to get my attention. Before it even hit the ground, I had my hand cocked and was turning to meet my victim. To my surprise the faces I saw were already in the memory banks. A thousand miles away from anywhere I thought I would see these fellows. Before me stood Andrew -hidden behind his glasses, David - hidden behind hit gut, and Michael - hidden behind his discouraging lack of self-esteem. ----- Michael "Gentlemen!" It was nice to know that with the short fame he hadn’t forgotten us. "How’d they let you across that border?" "It wasn’t easy," David started into his joke, "Michael kept bugging those guards about them robber gloves, and suggesting he was smuggling in drugs. Oh yeah, he wanted his bum checked." "Fuck you," I was quick to respond, but still kept my eyes glued to my shoelaces. The focus at this point was about keeping that street meat down. "Did you think that we would miss our protégé’s first pay per view? We would have been in South Dakota, but Michael’s bucket of bolts decided she wouldn’t go that day." Andrew was wearing a confident grin when he asserted that the big fellow was the protégé of us three. "Protégé? You boys got me to this dance, I will give you that. But I don’t really see myself as your protégé." "Of course not, my protégé would have won his match." Dave’s humour was based on sneaking in insults and shaking his own belly with enough laughter that you never seek retribution. I once ribbed him back, he did not laugh. "Hardy-Har-Har. So what, are we just going to stand here all night slinging jokes at me, or are we going to find some pub where you boys can get drunk and make me laugh?" "Ah, yeah. I’m parked over in the Itchy lot," I said, finally and slowly poking my head up to point in the direct of which lay my spot of pride. "Good, saves me paying for a cab," he said, and took me in a headlock. Just a quick mess of the hair and he quickly let go. You can’t do much when a man that size decides he wants to give you a noogie, especially when you are trying to refrain from having to see your dinner on the ground. I shifted my weight, and started into a slow pace, leading the way. It was a long way. ----- Mitchell That walk was one I could have done without with the condition that I found myself, but hell if that pain wasn’t enough to remind me that I was alive. Is feeling alive supposed to remind you of losing? Of that feeling that the brass ring was something tangible, but like Charlie Brown, the field goal is never going to be made? I was afforded such thoughts in the company of those who had driven such a distance by the constantly vocal David. Most of the words the he spoke were jokes, so I found that if I ever lost track of the conversation if I just laughed or chuckled when he stopped talking, I would be safe half of the time. This was one of those times. We finally got there and although the idea of being crammed into the back seat of that wagon was not necessarily a favourite one, it was the idea of being off of my feet for the time it would take these three to find and then agree upon a bar that had me smiling. That and of course the wide grin on Michael’s face when he saw that no one stole his prize possession. He was really quite paranoid some times, and that does not make for easy living, trust me. He fished the keys out of his pocket as Andrew and Dave went into a shoving match for shotgun. Andrew had little chance pushing the mass of Dave away from that handle, let alone the light slaps to the back of the head he did not hesitate in using for such spats with friends. Michael and I were sitting inside for a good while whilst those two continued on the idiotic, alcohol fuelled tussle for a better view and slightly more leg space. David won. Michael twisted the key to a loud noise, but not the sound of life being breathed into the old ride. A second chance proved more successful. ----- Michael By the time we reached that tavern the effects of tonight’s previously downed drinks had faded. For some silly reason the only thing I could think about as I followed the boys in was to wonder what the consequences for getting caught driving drunk. Certainly being a foreigner would not have helped. I just thought us lucky that Salt Lake’s finest hadn’t set up a ride program outside the arena. Mitchell was the first in, and when he cracked that door the sweet sounds of Skynyrd were there to greet us. It wasn’t Hess Village but this place had a hint of the Hamilton charm. The place was a mix of the local population. Kids who bought their beer with a fake I.D. were drinking with the workers near retirement, here to wash away the weeks stress. I was home for the night. ----- Mitchell I didn’t have much on me, but the group refused to think that I could be broke after being called up to the big leagues. Wouldn’t it be grand if I was really getting paid what these guys thought I should? I think I am still a zero or two away from the salary them at the top are raking in. Whatever. A man of merger needs just requires this measly paycheque. That said, first round was on me. I sipped my Pepsi while they tipped the Coors Light, Canadian and Blue to their lips. They were content to carry the conversation on with topic of the quality of girls in this Salt Lake establishment taking dominance. Things like the antiquated and highly subjective 10 point system, guessing the age and why whenever anyone thought a girl cute, how they had no shot. Andrew spotted her first, but Michael couldn’t take his eyes off her afterward. This cute little thing, probably 19 years old, shoulder length blonde hair and the over protective boyfriend that I think he didn’t see. "Yeah, I bet I could bag ‘er," David bragged to Michael. "These chicks dig an experienced man." "I bet you couldn’t even pay a pro in this place Davie. She is something though, eh Mikey? Hey Andrew? Andrew? What are you looking – ah no man! Can’t you see that adam’s apple?" The place was a tad on the darker side, but still is no excuse for any man to be looking at a transvestite like that. "You boys think you could keep yourselves out of trouble for a minute while a guy hits the head?" Nature called. ----- Michael Dave’s making an idiot of himself gave me the perfect opportunity to go ahead and talk with this vixen. As cool and confident as I knew how, I walked up and introduced myself. "Hi, uh, I am really sorry about my friend there. He is an idiot and has probably reached that cut off limit for tonight. My name is Mike." "Oh, hello, the name is Barbara. It is alright, believe me it is not the first time that a drunken, middle aged dude tried to hit on me, and sadly I doubt it will be the last." "Yeah, I guess that is just one of those things you have to deal with when you look so stunning." "Ah... thanks. You are . . . sweet . . . to say that." Her body language was telling the whole story; I was being shut out before I even took the opening faceoff. The girl was a game killer. I couldn’t really tell what to make of her eyes getting bigger though. Well, I did get the idea when it was her boyfriend tapping on my shoulder. Just a few taps, followed by a heavy hand on it, then that heavy hand spun me around. This is bad, very bad. Hell if those two jackasses could bother to stop their laughing long enough to provide the much needed backup. And with the big guy in the washroom . . . ----- Mitchell I couldn’t even dry my hands before the entire bar was in an uproar. Whatever was out there had best be better than the Garfield comic that I didn’t finish reading. Sure enough it was the traditional circle forming. Alas sweet bar fights, how your awkward pressures always have seemed to raise those hairs I forget I have. I managed to push my way to the front of said circle trying to get a better understanding of what exactly is about to go down. When I did get there I wish I could say I was surprise to see it was one of my boys. But it was out of the ordinary to see it was Michael there, panicked look about him, in front of a larger size kid, mad as hell. Dave and Andy were doing nothing. What are friends for? ----- Michael What are friends for anyhow? I was about to be turned into a mush of my former self, and all they cared about was finishing their brews. And the big fighting machine that you helped get some major exposure? Where was that big lug? This could not possibly end well, I thought. My only chance was to get that first shot in, and make it count. ----- Mitchell I tried to stop it from ever getting started. I wish I could say that I was quick enough, but the leg just wouldn’t permit any lightning fast shifts. Mike, why’d you have to throw that punch so quickly? Mikey, why couldn’t you get lucky with that first punch? Barbie, why did you try to get between them? The dazzling blond made the mistake of walking between two men, one that was ready to kill, another fighting to survive. He hit her. Mikey stopped immediately and was caught with a right cross. He had to be stopped, so I stopped him. This is the way the night was going to end. We would soon be ushered out of the bar, and mercifully without the presence of the boys in blue. There were just some issues to attend to. First there was the well being of the girl. I didn’t know much about her, but her bad taste in men was obvious. The prick knocks her down and out, and then promptly sulks away from the bar and me. In a perfect world, in my world, I would have him inside a ring, and be able to inflict as much justice as I saw fit without repercussion. This is not a perfect world, and what slight restraint I had that night kept me from doing something that I would regret. But back to the girl. I spent some time looking around, looking for any friends that she might have been there with. No one would claim association to the sleeping beauty, or at least no one believable. I carried her out into the parking lot were we spent some time waiting for her to wake. When she finally did come through she was not in a talkative mood, which was understandable. I called her a cab and sent her on her way to whatever place she calls home. I could only imagine how she will remember this night. Secondly there was the matter of that wicked right that caught Michael. It sent him for a loop, but he was a trooper. At least he wanted to present himself as one. I gave him the dignity that he sought. It might be best if he did not remember this night. Lastly, there was the chastisement of the two who did nothing to stick up for their friend. That is business left undone. Who am I to preach about teamwork? Antisocial sentinel as I am, I thought that I had no place to teach. I hoped that they remember the expense of their selfish ignorance from this night. As for my night, what can I say? It was disappointing, embarrassing, and regretful. Just another good time will the fellows.
