A Dose Of Reality - Episode 1

23 August 2009

Date: August 23rd 2009 August 23rd 2009 A lot had happened in the 3 years since Anna Kingsley had fallen pregnant. Obviously, she had carried on with her deception, letting Matt believe that he was the father of her child, when in fact it belonged to another man. For his part, he never told her that he knew she was lying, that he’d met the real father and paid him off because of his love for her. At least, he hadn’t said anything until a month ago. It had been a big fight, even by their standards. The issue that set off was banal, so banal that she couldn’t even remember what it was, but the argument that followed was anything but trivial. Truth be told, it had been coming for a while, their petty tiff merely acting as the catalyst for all the other issues to rise to the surface. They’d been married for almost 2 years, the ceremony taking place a few months after the birth of baby Luca. Since then they’d had their ups and downs like every couple, but by and large things had been good. That all changed when Luca started developing his own personality. When that baby, that cheerful, gurgling, happy baby had been born, Matt had ignored that it wasn’t his flesh and blood, swore to himself that he would treat it like he would his own son, never worrying about the harsh, brutal reality. As Luca grew, Matt grew to love him and it seemed that things would work out for him and Anna. They’d even talked about trying for another baby. The niggle was still there though, eating away at Matt each and every day. Luca had his first birthday and Matt felt like a fraud for the whole day, playing the part of the doting father. That Luca was growing to look more and more like his real father each and every day made it all so much harder. Anna noticed it too, although she’d never say anything, always reassuring Matt that ‘he has your nose’, even when this was quite clearly false. This constant reminder kept building, causing him and Anna to argue, often over the most frivoulous things. Then Luca started talking. The first time the child looked up at Matt and said ‘Dada’, he could have strangled it. For he wasn’t its father, merely some chump who’d decided to stand by his girlfriend, hoping against hope that she’d just made a mistake, had a brief bout of insanity with a mysterious stranger. That wasn’t the truth though. Matt knew it and Anna knew it. More than once he’d caught her looking at the child wistfully, remembering some past experience and wishing for a different future. No matter what he did, he’d never be what she wanted, would never make her happy. So he did something he thought he’d never do. He cheated. At first it’d been a one night stand, some random woman he met in a bar when Anna was off visiting her family in San Francisco. Then another and another and another, until it got to the stage where he was having to hide the existence of the one night stands from each other, never mind from Anna. It helped his relationship with his wife and his pseudo-son, gave him something that was actually his, a secret ‘Up yours’ to Anna and the life he now had. Then she found out. Like any woman scorned in such a way, she hit the roof, calling him every name under the sun and then some, every bit the heartbroken wife. Questioning how he could do something like that when she was at home caring for their son. Matt almost cracked then, almost let slip what he knew. Somehow he managed to hold on to his tongue, apologise, and force himself to believe that her pain was real, that she was actually as devastated as she seemed by his infidelity. So they carried on, but Anna wouldn’t let it go. Sly digs each and every day, disapproving glares in public, the total cessation of any physical relationship. Eventually he could take it no longer and in the midst of yet another disagreement over a minor issue, he let it slip. He told her that he knew Luca wasn’t his and that he was leaving them. Told her of how he’d always known, described how he’d paid Josh off to leave her and how he’d had stayed with her in the hope that they could be happy, that he’d strayed as a way to regain some of his own self-respect. Anna had no comeback, she knew she was bang-to-rights and there wasn’t a thing she could do to change Matt’s mind. The thing was though – she didn’t want to. She’d been in love with Josh from the first moment they’d met, their time together had been magical and had resulted in her beautiful son. Whether Matt’s version of events was true or not, deep down she had never wanted to be with anyone else. Now she was free to try and get him back, to make a family with him without any worries or guilt. What could go wrong? *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ September 2nd 2009 It had been a hell of a couple of weeks for Josh Marquez. He’d made his return to the wrestling business at Just Wrestling’s event in Berkeley where he’d defeated the ever- entertaining Skylar Montgomery. It had been tough, a definite shock to the system despite all his preparation, but good at the same time. He’d felt like he could beat anyone. Then he’d been swiftly dragged back to reality when he made his SCCW debut a few days later. He wasn’t embarrassed, far from it, but his defeat to Darius Drannen had proven to him that no matter how good he once had been, he’d have to work his ass off to keep up with these guys, at least for the first few months back. He was a few years older now and although they may say that youth and talent is no match for age and treachery, in the wrestling business this wasn’t strictly true. He’d been just a little off-form and he’d paid for it, losing in what was supposed to be his big televised comeback. Not exactly the way the script had been written. Luckily, however, he’d done enough to get himself on the card this week again, this time taking on Jay Draven, a name he recognised in a vague sense. Josh wasn’t counting any chickens but he felt that he’d learned from the last match and would push Draven right to the limit, hopefully picking up a win and earning himself a place on the pay-per-view the following week. Josh was a businessman and he knew that if you weren’t on that card, you weren’t important and if you weren’t important, you weren’t getting paid. And so much for all that. Outside the ring, things had been getting hectic too. He’d moved into a new apartment, leaving his old place at Sunset Tower behind. The new joint was an upgrade for sure, paid for with the handsome profits he was seeing from his chain of nightclubs. The car had been changed too, the lure of a Lamborghini proving stronger than the desire to buy something with slightly better fuel figures. All-in-all, it felt like a bit of a fresh start, one that he needed after a few aimless years. He had a focus now, to be the best pro- wrestler he could be, to reach the heights he’d always aspired to before it was too late. The businesses were solid, they could take care of themselves and as for women, well, the possibilities were endless. Life was peachy, but as the old saying goes, the best laid plans oft go awry.
