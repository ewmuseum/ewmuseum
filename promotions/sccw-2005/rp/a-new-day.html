A New Day

Date: Today -Moving In- The apartment was sparsely decorated. There was a TV on a white stand in front of an old brown leather couch and coffee table. The dining room table was small and had only a couple of chairs. The bedroom had a full size bed that was covered in "Heroes of the NFL" sheets. The door to the apartment opens and Paul Searle walks in. He sets the box that he was carrying onto the table, tossing the keys that were in his hand just a second ago next to it. Paul then carries the bag that was slung over his shoulder into the bedroom. He returns after taking his shirt off and is startled to see a six foot blonde standing in the dining room. "You must be Paul. I’m Melanie; Mike told me that you were going to be staying here for a while." Paul looks at her with a quizzical look. ‘Great, another blonde.’ he thinks to himself, remembering a lot of what happened with Kirsten Pendleton. "Yes, ma’am." Melanie gets a chuckle. "Listen, I know that Mike is looking after you because you were Jason’s protégé, but if you want to continue to live here, you will learn one quick rule. Don’t ever call me ma’am. You call me ma’am, and I think that my mom is standing behind me." "Sorry, Ma’…….lanie." Paul caught himself before he really got in trouble. "Very good, just wanted to let you know that dinner was at seven." "I was thinking of just…" Paul points at the box. "eating something that I just picked up at the store." "Hush. Come down and have some dinner with us." Melanie takes a box out of the box. "It’s better than O’Day Mac and Cheese." "I survived on that stuff at Mr. Ryan’s place." "I’m sure if Jason found that out, he’d probably kill you. With all of the health food garbage that he had at the warehouse. He’d kill you if he knew you ate this." "You talking about that tofu crap?" "Yep, you tried it." "I’d rather eat Spam than eat that stuff again." "I don’t think that there’s much of a difference between the two." "Well, it was either this or eat at the Coney. By the way, where is the nearest Coney at, anyway." "No, no, no. That’s the other thing. No Coney Island. Ever." "Ever?" "Listen, I don’t know how Jason ever enjoyed eating at those places, but that garbage is going to take years from you." "Well, they are the ones at the end, aren’t they?" Melanie stops to try and hold back the laughter. "Yep, you’re definitely not the norm." "Uhhh, thanks." "Well, like I said, dinner is at seven." "OK." -The Kid- Paul was washing the Jeep. He knew that one of these days; Jason was going to return and that if it wasn’t clean, he’d be in serious trouble. When Jason gave him the keys and told him that he could use it, he was told that he didn’t care what Paul did with the Jeep, as long as it had a full tank of gas and that it was clean enough to eat off of. He knew that Jason would need to drive it now that his bike was gone. "What are you doing?" a little voice asked. "I’m washing Mr. Ryan’s Jeep." "Why?" "Because I told him I would keep it clean." "Why?" "So he would let me drive it." "Why?" "So I didn’t have to take a cab everywhere." "Why?" "Because I couldn’t afford it." "Why?" At this point, this kid was no longer being cute, but rather annoying. "Because I’m a rookie pro wrestler that doesn’t get paid all that much, I have to pay for my own transportation, my own hotel, my own wrestling gear, my dues." "Why?" "I don’t know." "Why?" Before Paul can answer, Melanie comes out of the house. "Kieran, you should let Paul finish what he was doing." "But…" "No, buts young man." "Fine. Can I have a cookie?" "Not until after lunch. But if you don’t let Paul be, you won’t get one, OK." "OK, mommy." Kieran runs off and goes back into the house. "I hope Kieran wasn’t bothering you too much, you know how four year olds are." "Uh, not really. I don’t have any younger brothers or sisters." "Trust me, you were just like that when you were his age. You probably drove your mom and dad nuts." "Not my dad." "Really?" Paul places his hand on his chest followed by tapping the side of his forehead with his thumb. He then moves it over his shoulder, and finally points at his ear. "What?" "My father was deaf. I didn’t learn to sign until I was 8." "That had to be hard." "Not really, we learned to deal with it. My mom didn’t care that he couldn’t hear, she loved him anyway." "You keep using the past tense." "He passed away a year ago. Cancer." "I’m sorry." "Don’t be, he’s in a better place now. I imagine where he’s at now, he can hear symphonies." "How did your mom meet him?" "She told me that they met when he started teaching her sign language. My mom wanted to teach kids that weren’t able to hear, she always wants to help people that are less fortunate than she. It didn’t take long for them to fall in love, even though they had a language barrier that was very hard to get around. "They were happy, she misses him. It’s hard on her sometimes." "When was the last time you saw her?" "On the way back from the last show. She was happy to see me." "What does she think of you doing this?" "She doesn’t like it, but she understands that this is what God wanted me to do at this time. This is the path that was put forth for me. Just like her falling in love with my dad was the path that God intended for her." Melanie nods her head at this. "You don’t believe in God, do you?" "Paul, there are some things that I do believe in. I believe that I love my husband, because I do. I believe that I do what I need to do to be a good mom, because I try. "I want to believe in God, because that might make life easier, but I don’t know if God does exist." "Melanie, trust me when I say that there are only two certainties that I found in life. One day, I will die and see my father again in Heaven. The other is that God does exist." "You’re still young. Eventually, this business is either going to turn you bitter or rich, sometimes both. Mike got out before the bitterness set in." "What about Mr. Ryan, he doesn’t seem bitter." "Jason isn’t happy. This business, it’s an addiction to him. Every time he gets in that ring, that’s his fix. He’s lost three marriages because of it. I hate to see what he’s going to be like in a couple years when his knees are so bad that he can no longer walk. When calcium starts to deposit on his spine, making it unbearable for him to move. "He’s bitter, he just handles it better than the rest of us." -Understanding- The dinner wasn’t what you would expect from a family that lives in Detroit’s Northern Suburbs. Baked chicken, mashed potatoes and gravy, all the fixings. The food was almost completely gone. Mike was sitting in his den, working on paper work when a rapping was heard on the door. "You can come in, Paul." Paul entered the den, carrying a can of Cherry Coke. "Where do you think Mr. Ryan went to?" Mike didn’t raise his eyes from the papers that sat on the desk in front of him. "I thought that he might have gone to Nevada. When we were first getting our start as a tag team, the two of us would ride our bikes into the desert and camp. "For us, it’s how we bonded. We would go out there, drink beer, hunt small animals and roast them on an open fire. When the sun rose, we would hop back on our bikes and ride back into civilization." "Sounds like the two of you had a good relationship." "We’ve been like brothers since the day we first met. Didn’t hurt that he fell in love with my sister. Jay and I were thick as thieves. He got his foot in the door in many areas, and made sure it stayed open long enough for me to walk through." "Kinda like what he’s done for me." "Maybe he sees a little bit of me in you. Or maybe he sees a little bit of himself when he was your age. He didn’t go to California to be a wrestler. It just…happened. Just like you didn’t go to Cleveland to be a wrestler, it just happened for you." "I’m worried." "If there is anything that I knew about Jay, it’s that you shouldn’t worry about him. He survives. Somehow, he survives. He’s survived his mother killing his step-father, he’s survived his father sending his younger brother to live with his older sister. "He’s survived drug addiction. He’s survived the death of his sister. He’s survived the dissolution of the NWC." "I don’t think I can survive this business without him being here to guide me." "Sure you can. All it takes is a little bit of self confidence and some luck. You don’t need Jay for that. You need to look in your heart and find out whether this business is right for you or not. Maybe you will find that it’s not for you quickly enough that you won’t have to go through the pain of not being able to walk away. "Or worse, be forced to walk away because even though the life is right for you, your body just gives up. Just hope that you never have that happen to you. It’s painful to know that you want to continue, but that your body just isn’t willing anymore." "Then I should quit and go home?" "You need to follow your heart. That’s probably what Jay is doing right now."
