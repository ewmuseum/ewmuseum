Part Twenty-Three: Turning Points.

22 September 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/22/09 The worst had been the hour immediately after the close of the show. Lance Marshall didn’t know specifically what had happened that night in Oakland and wouldn’t know until three nights later when he viewed a tape of the show. What he remembered was having Lane Stevens’ throat in his hands, wanting to squeeze the life out of the bastard who had made the lives of him and his family so miserable. There had the impact of steel upon skull, again and again and then…nothing. Blissful, peaceful nothing. Then the paramedics had roused him, rushing the ring to check on his condition, to take him to the hospital, get him the care they were convinced he needed. He had pushed them away, tried to convince them that he was okay, that they were overreacting. Felt his mouth betray him as his speech came out slurred and halting. Felt his legs turn to jelly as he tried to get up. They had compromised, helping him onto a gurney while insisting he was most certainly not able to walk on his own right now. They had bundled him into the ambulance as quickly as possible, securing him before speeding their way towards the hospital. The ride over was a quiet one, the motion and rhythm of the ambulance lulling him to sleep. With this sleep came not dreams but memories. The day that Doctor Rasmussen had told him and Alanna that Alanna’s cancer had gone, that she was fine, that everything would be all right. The joy they had felt at the wonderful news. The anger and pain when Rasmussen announced in the arena that it had all been a lie, that Alanna’s disease was still within her, that these precious weeks without treatment may have done her irreparable harm. Stevens, smirking and smiling, pleased at the destruction he had caused. Smashing Rasmussen into the ring floor over and over. Despising the man’s weakness. Wanting nothing more than to punish the man for forsaking his oath, for allowing himself to be intimidated, for letting Stevens and Pierce (for Stevens did nothing without Desade’s approval) once again try to ruin his life. Realizing that he could kill Doctor Rasmussen right then and there. Not really caring if he did. The emergency room doctors had been quick and thorough, conducting a detailed examination as they assessed the state of Lance’s health. There had been a wealth of medical jargon, most of which Lance hadn’t even tried to remember. "Concussion" had stuck, more by luck than anything else. He had just nodded and gone "uh huh" at the appropriate intervals, making it look as though he was paying attention. Once they could no longer hold him (whether they were convinced was another question altogether), they had sent him up to a room on the fourth floor to rest for the night. Now, as he lay on a bed with crisp white sheets and stared at yet another marathon of Law and Order on cable, he found himself mentally counting down the minutes until he could his wife’s familiar tread making its way down the hospital corridor. SCCW would have called the minute the ambulance left, if not sooner. Alanna would have quickly called in Mrs. Estevez from next door to babysit, would have been in the car and punching the accelerator within minutes. With the drive from LA to Oakland, traffic and the time it had taken him to make it through the ER, he reckoned he had about ten minutes before she caught up with him. It actually took her seven. As the door to his hospital room opened, Lance found himself grimacing. It wasn’t from pain. That would come later, he was sure, but for now the pain killers were doing a very good job of keeping the edge off. But even feeling as he was, he had absolutely no desire to have the discussion that he knew was coming. He had no desire to relive what he could remember of the night’s events. Not that he had much choice in the matter. Alanna zoomed over to her husband’s side, taking in his bruised and battered face with wide eyes. "My G-d, baby, are you okay?! What happened?!" "I’m not sure," Lance replied, "I know I got chair shot a few times. " "What do you mean, a few times?" "Not sure, I lost count after a while. I was focusing on trying to choke the life out of Lane Stevens." "Not that I don’t understand the urge," Alanna replied, "but what made tonight so special?" Fuck, Lance thought. This was gonna be the hard part. "He lied." "What, Stevens? Babe, lying is what Stevens does. It’s a job requirement when you work for Desade." "No," answered Lance, shaking his head as much as he could, "not Stevens. Rasmussen." "My oncologist? How does he factor into this?" "He lied when he told you the cancer had gone. He announced in front of the entire arena in Oakland that what he told us had been wrong and that you were still sick." Alanna stopped, stunned, her expression resembling someone caught in the path of an oncoming train. "What?! Why would he do that?" "Because they got to him. Because Stevens and that bitch Pierce…I dunno…they bribed him, they threatened him, they did something to him and convinced him to lie. To get to me. To get to us." "No," Alanna said, not wanting to believe. "I don’t like Alex anymore than you do but she wouldn’t stoop this low…" "Stevens was the one who paraded Rasmussen down there, he was the one who made Rasmussen announce what had happened. And you know Stevens doesn’t do anything without Alex pulling the strings." Tears started to form in Alanna’s eyes, her bottom lip quivering. "No…oh my G-d, No…" Lance got up gingerly from bed and made his way slowly to her, wrapping her up in a tight embrace. They stood there for what seemed like an eternity, Lance holding tightly to his wife as she let out her anger and fear and sadness until she finally had nothing left. Once she was as calm as he imagined she would get, Lance slowly walked his wife over to the bed and settled her in next to where he had been laying down. He got in gingerly beside her, letting her cling tightly to him as she slowly drifted off to sleep. And when the nurse came in an hour later, he very calmly informed her that his wife would be staying with him for the evening as she had just had a major shock and was in no condition to drive, thank you very much. So the morning sun would see the sleeping form of Alanna Marshall in a hospital bed in Oakland, nustled against the form of her husband. She would awake sore and stiff but otherwise unharmed. Sleep would prove considerably harder to come by for Lance. After a final going over by the doctors, Lance had been formally discharged and Alanna had been allowed to drive Lance home. They had arrived home just in time to see Zach off to school, whereupon sleep had finally overtaken Lance and he managed to grab a good solid four hours. Lunch had been consumed automatically. The rest of the day had been comprised of staying at home, alternating between working out and watching television as Alanna had insisted that he was not to be doing any driving today in his condition, no sir. Lance didn’t have the strength to argue…and knew that he’d lose if he tried. The day passed by in a blue of reruns and movies and trips to the bathroom until Zach’s return from school; an occasion Lance cherished as much for a chance to see his son again as for an opportunity to escape from the nagging thoughts and doubts that had been plaguing him throughout the day. Whether he was skating too close to the edge. Whether things had gotten as bad as they were going to get or did Stevens have yet another depth he could sink to. How desperately he wanted to snap Stevens’ neck. He could hear the words of August Monday in his head, reminding him that sometimes, even when we do everything that we can, we can’t protect the ones we love. It made sense. The part of Lance that was still listening to logic right now knew that August had a point, that he couldn’t reasonably be expected to see off everything that might threaten Alanna or Zach. Despite what people might believe him to be, he was only a man. There was only so much he could do. It still didn’t make him feel any better. There had been lessons to go over and games to play with Zach, things which allowed him to forget for a while. The two men Marshall were currently seated together watching cartoons, a smile coming from Alanna Marshall as she looked on from the kitchen where she was making dinner. She still didn’t know what, exactly, had happened in Oakland (like her husband, it would be a few days before she knew all the specifics) but, from what she managed to figure out, the evening had obviously been fairly traumatic for him. The peace couldn’t last, it never did. But for this moment, for right now…it was here. And she’d enjoy it while it lasted. She could feel him sneak in behind her as she stood over the grill, the smell of meat drifting over the outside air. He moved in against her, pressing his warmth against hers. She stood silent for a moment, enjoying the feel of their bodies together. "Where’s Zach?" "Still glued to Phineas and Ferb," Lance answered. "I’m not sure he even knows I left." Alanna laughed lightly at this. "Gee, I wonder where he gets it from." "Hey, be nice. I’m not a well man, you know." Alanna giggled. For a man feared by so much of the SCCW roster, her husband could be such a child sometimes. Lance sighed. This next part wouldn’t be easy. "I’ve been thinking and…I think I’m gonna need to leave for Vegas." "What, already?" "Yeah," Lance replied, "I need to try and get myself in the right headspace for this match and, as much as I love you babe…I’m not gonna be able to do it here. I need a little time by myself, time to try and improve my focus." Alanna turned to look at her husband. He could look so damn sincere sometimes. She knew that his explanation wasn’t the whole story, that there was something he was holding back from her. But pressing, she knew, would only make him shut, retreat into himself even more. "You promise you’ll tell me everything after the pay-per-view?" Lance nodded. "I swear, babe, on my life." Alanna nodded in return. That would have to do for now. "When do you want to leave?" "I figured tomorrow morning after Zach leaves for school. I’ll take the SUV and drive to Vegas, doctor didn’t want me flying just yet. " "Promise me you won’t do anything stupid?" Lance held his hand up in a perfect Boy Scout stance. "I do solemnly swear." "I’m serious, Lance. Promise me that you won’t do anything reckless or foolhardy, please?" Lance looked his wife dead in the eye. "Nothing dangerous, nothing reckless, nothing that can get me any more hurt. I’ll call every day and make sure to say goodnight to Zach every night. Okay?" Alanna nodded. Again, for now, it would have to do. "At least I made your favorite tonight." Lance dropped Zach off at school the next day with a hug and a smile, telling his little soldier that he had to go away for a little bit but he’d call home every night and make sure things were okay. Plus, when he got back, they’d do something special, whatever Zach wanted, promise. Zach hadn’t exactly been pleased but he’d nodded and said he’d understood. Lance stood in the school driveway as he watched Zach head inside, marveling at how truly amazing his son could be sometimes. Lance’s next stop had been the record shop, to pick up some new tunes for the way (including the last copy in the store of the new Beatles stereo remasters set). The rest of the trip to Vegas had passed in a blur of gas stations and cheap restaurants and discount motels. Long stretches of barren landscape punctuated only by the sound of Lance signing along loudly to all the songs he knew. They still came, those pesky thoughts and fears and doubts that the music and the distractions were meant to block. Maybe not as often, maybe not as strongly…but they came. The Bellagio wasn’t exactly what one would call an inconspicuous destination in Vegas. It was big, flashy and not a little gaudy…but, thanks to a friend the Marshalls had in management, not overly expensive whenever the Marshalls wanted to stay. Lance smiled at the check in clerk as she found him a room, a nice little King room on the tenth floor. Nothing terribly fancy, she apologized, but it was a very nice room nonetheless. Lance assured her that would be just fine. A quick elevator ride later and he’d dumped his meager luggage inside his room, desperate to cure his hunger. To his mild shock, Prime Steakhouse had a table open and he elected to take advantage. It was more money than he’d intended to spend and he’d certainly have to pay for it the next morning…but he felt he deserved to indulge himself slightly. An hour and a half and one very fine piece of meat later found him back at his room, just managing to make it inside the door as sleep began to overtake him. Quickly setting his cell phone alarm for 5:45 a.m., he quickly got ready for bed. He had just made into bed when blessed unconsciousness overtook him and he fell into a deep, solid sleep. The harsh buzz of his cell phone alarm jolted Lance out of a dream that, if the way his was feeling was any indication, he probably didn’t want to remember. The last time he’d felt like this had been the last time he’d had a drink…and he knew he hadn’t been that stupid last night. The usual morning rituals had taken even longer due to the unfamiliar surroundings and, as he gathered up his stuff to head to the gym, he found himself running behind his usual schedule. He cursed as he made his way to the parking lot, realizing that he hadn’t called home last night as he’d promised. He made a mental note to do so as soon as Alanna and Zach would be up. The Las Vegas day had dawned clear and bright and Lance found himself taking a few moments to drink in his surroundings in silence as he drove to the gym. Everyone thinks of the Strip as loud and bright and sensory overload, he thought. If only they could see this… The sound of Muse’s "Uprising" broke through his reverie as his cell phone began to ring. Hurriedly pulling over, he grabbed his gym bag and yanked open the side pocket where he’d put the cell phone in that morning’s haste. This couldn’t be good. It was way too early for Lani to be calling. Not unless it was bad news. Oh, shit, if anything had happened… He glanced quickly at the number on the phone, not recognizing it and feeling none the better for that. Frantically, he accepted the call. "Hello?" The voice on the other end of the line was tight and strained, sounding somewhat nervous and definitely the worse for wear. "Hey, um…Lance?" But it definitely wasn’t Alanna. "This is Thad."
