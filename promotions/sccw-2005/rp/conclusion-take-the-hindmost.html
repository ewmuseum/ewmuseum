Conclusion: Take The Hindmost.

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Today Who is Wyatt Connors? It is a very complicated question, and not easily answered. Perhaps we can gain the most insight from those who knew him best. His victims. # # # Two days after SCCW: Homecoming. Location unknown. Beep. The harsh sun burned his eyes, before he even opened them. He put his hands in front of his face and tried to sit up. His head swam, and he lied back down again. He tried to speak, but his throat was dry, and his mouth was covered in dust. All he could manage was a feeble, 'Rrgh.' Beep. It took a few minutes, but eventually his eyes adjusted to the bright light, allowing him to examine his surroundings. The ground was a cracked hardpan, scorched by the blazing sun overhead. There were mountains off in the distance, although he couldn't quite tell which direction they were. Everything else looked the same...no roads, no rivers, no signs of civilization. Just miles and miles of wasteland, as far as the eye could see. Beep. Once more, he attempted to achieve an upright position, stabilizing himself with his hands. It made him feel dizzy, but not so much that he would fall over. He tried to shake loose the fog in his brain and try to remember something--anything--that would explain his situation. What the hell am I doing here... Beep. ...and what the hell is that noise? He made a brief search of the ground next to him, but found nothing. Instinctively, he checked his pockets...and there, he came up with a black cellphone, the kind that are waterproof and amazingly durable. Abby's cellphone. Recent memories washed over his mind like a tidal wave. He'd gotten so close--he and Abby had made it out of the Decency Compound. They'd run through the fields for at least half an hour before arriving on the nearest road. He still had the metal case, the one he'd stolen and hid over a year ago. He had it all--the goods, the girl--and all he needed was for one car to stop and pick them up. Just not that car. Not the beaten old black Lincoln that belonged to Harley Roebuck. Or Buck Watkins, or whatever the fuck his real name was. Harley held them both at the point of a shotgun, demanding they surrender the money and themselves. He refused to just let Abby go--in fact, Harley pointed the gun at her most of the time. She tried to make a move, but Harley knocked her out with the butt of the weapon. Things went black not long after that. Must have done the same to me. Then...not much. A lot of muffled voices, and the occasional knockout blow. And now, this. Left out in the middle of nowhere, with nothing but his clothes and... Beep. The noise snapped him out of his reverie. He looked down at the phone, and saw that a small piece of paper had been affixed to it. The message Password: 2148 was written on it, in astoundingly neat penmanship. He flipped the phone open, and the screen informed him of new voicemail. His shaky fingers pressed the star button, followed by an 8 and a 6. 'Please enter password,' the automated voice told him. He quickly input the numbers on the paper: 2-1-4-8. 'You have four new messages. First new message.' The voice was so thick with smugness, the speaker could have choked on it. 'Hello, Mr. Bowdry. I'm sure you know who this is.' Alan Bowdry--known to some as Brother Seth, to others as Jeremiah--growled. He knew exactly who it was. Wyatt Goddamn Connors. 'Well, Alan--can I call you Alan?--it seems that I've left you in quite a pickle. Stranded out in the desert, with no real idea of where you are, let alone how to extricate yourself.' 'It's not fun, is it?' 'You son of a bitch,' Alan barked, temporarily forgetting that the voice was a recording. 'When I get my hands on you, I'll--' 'I have to hand it to you, Alan,' the message continued. 'You had me in a tight spot for a long time. Not many people have managed to do that, and I want you to know that I respect your performance. As a token of that respect, I have allowed you to keep your pants. Of course, if you'd kept your pants on before, you might not be in this situation now.' 'End of message,' the automated voice interjected. 'To reply to this message, press five. To delete, press seven. To--' Alan pushed the 'seven' button, sneering angrily. 'Message deleted. Second new message.' Connors' voice picked up again. 'You'd been doing remarkably well until the woman showed up. Do you have any idea how close you came to completely fucking up everything? I could have lost it all...and worse than that, I could have lost it all to her--just because you couldn't keep your dick under control. It's a damn good thing my associate, Mr. Roebuck, showed up when he did. As you have no doubt realized, I have managed to escape from the good Reverend Seck's control.' 'Still, the question remains: what to do with you? Had this just remained a game with the two of us, I might have simply snuck off in the middle of the night with everything you owned. At the very worst, I would have--' The message cut off again, mid-sentence. Alan Bowdry growled and deleted the message as soon as the other voice began to speak. He thought about trying to call back, but somehow he felt that there was something important in these messages. Besides, there was no guarantee that the number Wyatt called from was even valid anymore. 'Message deleted. Third new message.' 'As I was saying. At the very worst, I would had Harley inflict minor injuries upon you. Instead, your colossal error in judgment has necessitated a more severe form of punishment. This is why you now find yourself in the middle of nowhere.' 'It's not all bad news, Mr. Bowdry. As I said earlier, I do respect the way you were able to confound me for months. I still think of you as a fool, considering you didn't run to the hills with my belongings as soon as you found out that they were of value, but I do not think you are irredeemable. I have given you a possible route of escape, and I eagerly await the day when you show up out of the blue and try to kill me.' 'End of mess--Message deleted. Fourth new message.' 'The answer lies in your friend's cellphone. I put it to good use beforehand, of course, but there's a little bit of juice left. By my estimation, just enough to let you call for help--maybe they won't be able to find you straight away, but at least they'll know to start looking.' 'Unfortunately, you've wasted all that power listening to these messages. So if you're still listening, you've got about...oh, eight seconds until the phone dies completely. Good luck!' 'End of message. To reply to this message, please press--' 'SHIT!' he cried, and immediately pushed the 'end' button. His shaky fingers tried to dial another number. The phone rang once before someone on the other end answered. 'Nine-One-One Emergency Resp--' 'Hello? Hello?' Alan shouted. 'Hello? I got thrown out into the desert, and I need help!' From the other end of the line, only silence. 'No! NO!' Alan feverishly pushed buttons, but nothing came up on the black screen. In a fit of rage, he threw the phone as hard as he could. Alan Bowdry's mind reeled. He was besieged by questions that had no answers. How long had Connors been free of Seck's influence? Why hadn't he run earlier? Why did they regard Abby as a threat? What was her role in all this? In the end, Alan stared up into the empty desert sky, and tried to fill it with curses. He cursed God, Reverend Seck, Harley Roebuck, Abigail Proctor, and the desert itself. But most of all, he cursed the man who left him here. Perhaps you will meet him someday, if he can find his way back to civilization. Obsessed to the point of insanity, consumed with rancor. Every word on his lips, breath from his lungs, beat of his heart, will carry the burning desire for revenge. When you meet him, you may ask: 'Who did this to you? Who twisted your mind so badly that you can barely function? Who tore out your heart and made you fill the hole with anger?' And he will answer: 'The Motherfucker.' # # # One week after Homecoming. Location: The Decency Compound, rural Oklahoma. 'God grant us serenity,' they had prayed, but that was one prayer that had gone unanswered. Everyone in the Compound seemed slightly on edge ever since Reverend Connors and his faithful soldiers had gone off into the wilderness to combat the forces of evil. They had performed this task many times, and had always come home victorious, so the Decent People had never worried about it. Of course, they'd never been gone this long before. After a few days, the rumor mill began to churn. Had there been an accident? Had they fallen to Satan's dark power? Were they ambushed by the heathens that populate the Salt Lake area? They might have completely lost their heads, were it not for the calming influence of Mavis Jessup. She was the pillar of strength for the Decent People in those dark days, her faith acting as a pillar of strength that kept the community going. 'Believe in Him,' she said, 'and He will never fail you.' The others would relax slightly, but were not completely at ease. Mavis knew not to worry, though. There had been harder times, and He had led them through. She knew all too well about the hard times, of course. She had been one of the original congregation for the great Reverend Ibrahim Seck, all those years ago. When He had gone out into the world to preach His message, her faith was the keepsake that would always remind Him of where He had come from. When He actually did forget, her belief was the shining beacon that would light His way home. No matter what evils befell the great Reverend, He would always prevail. Her faith ensured it. Let the others worry, then. She knew that the Reverend would be coming home soon. Of course, she felt that every day...but today would be different. He would come home today...or at the very least, send word. In the bottom of her heart, she knew this to be true. In the meantime, she set about her daily duties. Cooking, leading prayer services, and generally being a pillar of the community in the Decency Compound. Shortly after lunch, there had been a commotion by the front gate. 'Somebody's coming!' came several shouts. Mavis immediately stood up from her knitting and headed in that direction. She could see the trail of dust from a way off, and her steps came a little quicker. She arrived at the main gate, eager to see the faces of the Crusade, and her beloved Lord as well. Instead, what she saw was the county sheriff. She began to fear for the worst, but once more she was buttressed by her faith. She stood proudly and waited for the county official to exit his car and approach. The sheriff--a burly man with a thick, graying mustache--strode toward the gate, holding a document in his hands. 'Need to see whoever's in charge,' he said gruffly. His voice had an odd staccato to it, and he sometimes used words as if he had a limited amount. Mavis nodded at him and replied, 'Reverend Seck and Reverend Connors are unavailable right now. Do you have business with them?' 'Business with all of you,' he answered, and handed her the document. 'Tell everyone here about that.' Mavis slowly unfolded the document. The red letters on the front page drained all the color from her face. NOTICE OF EVICTION. 'Wh...what?' 'Land's been sold. New owner wants you out. You got a week.' 'But...but that's impossible!' she cried. 'We own this land! It's been in our family for years!' 'Courthouse says different. Used to belong to the Church of...whazzit. The Church of Decency. Sold it six days ago.' 'What? But that doesn't make any sense! How could they sell it?' Mavis' concern grew into outright distress. Another member of the Compound, who--at the moment--had a clearer head asked, 'Who bought it?' 'Hrm.' The sheriff thought for a moment, then reached into his pocket. He pulled out an envelope and a folded sheet of paper. He quickly scanned the paper, and soon found what he was looking for. 'Someone named...Connors. You know any Connors?' At that moment, Mavis' world began to collapse. She could only stare forward, almost blankly. The sheriff continued, 'He seems to know you. Wanted this to go to a Mavis Jessup. That you, ma'am?' She nodded, more in response to the sound of her name than out of comprehension. He stuck the envelope in her hand. 'Like I said, you got a week to clear out. Be seeing you.' He tipped the front of his hat toward the gathering crowd, and walked back toward his car. Mavis stood stock-still, completely dumbfounded by the events. Some of the nearby congregation whispered about the envelope, and some of them asked her to read it...but she couldn't hear them. In the end, a young man, one who'd only been at the Compound for a few months, took it from her hand and read it aloud. Dear Mrs. Jessup, Your natural instinct will be to blame me for this. I will not accept that blame. While you may be outraged at my own dishonesty, you must admit that you have been tricking yourself, long before I showed up. You are the one who willingly followed Ibrahim Seck. The man was an obvious lunatic, and a dangerous one at that. Yet you were all too eager to put your trust in him, even before he administered mind-controlling drugs to everyone. The crime here is yours, not mine. It is a crime of willful ignorance. That you are also the victim is an unfortunate circumstance. As you have been reluctant to recognize the truth, it is my duty to point it out. Ibrahim Seck is never coming back. Your false Messiah has abandoned you. He can no longer control you, which also means that you no longer have the luxury of being controlled. I am casting you and the others out of this fool's paradise. Quite honestly, I think I'm doing you all a favor. Tell them to go back home, if they still have one. And if you take nothing else away from this experience, I hope you heed this advice... Maybe if you'd learned to think for yourself, this wouldn't have happened. Sincerely, Wyatt Connors. The man who read the message let it slip from his hands. Mavis fell with it. The others, too caught up in their own shock, did not console her. In the end, for all her belief in others...she was completely alone. Over the coming days, people began to filter out of the Compound, taking along whatever of their original possessions they could find. A community that was over one hundred strong dwindled to eighty, then fifty, then nineteen. A week later, the only one who remained was Mavis Jessup. She would often be seen standing outside the Compound wall, a forlorn look on her face. Sometimes her husband would try to talk to her, but she could not hear or see him. She would remain there, silent and despondent, buried underneath the ruins of her once indestructible faith. The rest of the Decent People had scattered to the four winds by then, returned to what little remained of their previous lives.. Perhaps, in your travels, you will meet these people. You will know them by the burdens they carry. Their guilt and shame are impressive, but even those are infinitesimal next to their sadness. They mourn their lost Paradise, even as they acknowledge that none of it had been real. When you meet them, you may ask: 'Who did this to you? Who filled your hearts with empty promises? Who offered water, and delivered sand? Who lifted your spirits and your hopes, higher than they had ever been...and then laughed as he watched them burn?' And they will answer: 'The Rainmaker.' # # # One hour after the end of SCCW: Homecoming. Location: the parking lot of the EnergySolutions Arena in Salt Lake City. It had been a very successful night for the Dead Man's Hand. Their most vocal opposition, Dusk, had been defeated by their WMD, Lane Stevens. Desade had faced Lance Marshall in no-holds-barred combat, and won. Most importantly, Aimz had managed to retain her Universal Championship against former Hand member Philip Kennedy. The Hand had plenty of reasons to celebrate, but little time. As soon as the medical staff had cleared her, Desade and her band of henchmen headed toward their car in the parking area reserved for the talent. The Director led the way, followed closely by Roderick Ashe. The enigmatic Cozen was a few steps behind, occasionally taking interest in some random thing. Mr. Hawke brought up the rear, moving as quickly as his cane would allow. It was Desade who first noticed something was wrong, and stopped short about twenty yards from the car. The others gathered alongside her, and awaited her signal. With a pair of simple nods, he sent Ashe and Hawke to either side of the car. Hawke kneeled down to get a better look at the trunk, wincing in pain as he did so. Ashe stood back, ready to act at a moment's notice. 'Well?' Desade asked flatly. 'Someone has tampered with the lock,' remarked Hawke. 'Quite obviously, in fact.' 'Hmm.' The Director retrieved the keys from her pocket, and handed them to Cozen. She approached the vehicle with an odd little spring in her step, while Hawke labored back to a standing position, and readied his cane for use as a weapon. 'Ready confetti?' Cozen whispered, drawing a quizzical look from the big New Zealander. Hawke, on the other hand, nodded curtly. Cozen inserted the key, turned it, and slowly opened the trunk. As soon as she did, she heard a muffled voice. Immediately, Hawke's cane swung downward...and stopped mere inches from its intended target. That target was a young lady with shoulder-length, dirty-blonde curls, dressed in a blue shirt, cut-off shorts, and hiking sandals. Her legs were bound together, as were her arms, and ring of duct tape was wrapped around her head at mouth-level. She was completely unknown to most of the SCCW roster, but there were a select few who had a name for her. That name was Abigail Proctor. Alexandra Pierce knew who she was. Apparently, so did someone else. The girl in the trunk struggled against her bonds, and tried to speak, but to no avail--the ropes were too tight, and the tape muffled her voice. If the others had any inclination of freeing her, they weren't showing it. Tucked in between her arms was a white sheet of typing paper, folded in half. Cozen snatched it away, and quickly handed it to Desade. The Spider unfolded it, proving it to be a brief message. The note consisted of three simple words, in perfect script. Be Not Afraid. While Ashe and Cozen kept their eyes on the captive girl, Hawke cast a brief glance back at the Director. In that moment, he saw the briefest twitch of her right eye. Most people might never have noticed it at all, even given Desade's normally stringent countenance. But Hawke had known and worked with her for years. He knew that such an expression, miniscule though it was, spoke volumes. 'What should we do wiffer?' Ashe mumbled. Desade responded without hesitation. 'We will debrief her later. Not here. Cozen?' Cozen nodded, and then smiled toward Abigail. Proctor tried to make muted protests, but they did not stop Cozen from closing the trunk on her. While her underlings opened the doors of the car, Desade looked down at the sheet of paper in her hands. Her lips curled into a sneer as she spoke the name of the man responsible for this insult. Perhaps you will meet her one day, although you may not wish to. If she wishes to discuss things, the topics will be of her choosing. One topic that will definitely not come up is one of the few people who have crossed her, and is somehow still alive. When you meet her, you will ask: 'Who did this to you? Who has proven as dangerous in defeat as in victory? Who stung you in the heel as you tried to crush him underfoot?' And she will answer: 'The Scorpion.' # # # Midnight, the night of Homecoming. Location: a few miles outside of Salt Lake City. On a bridge that overlooked a county road, two men stood and smoked cigars. They had just been a part of a very successful evening, and were taking the time to celebrate. Not too much time, though, and not too close to the city...because neither of them believed for a second that they were completely safe. 'Can't believe you managed to find a good smoke in this state, Harley,' the smaller man said. He puffed at his cigar and exhaled slowly, savoring the taste. 'Easiest part of this job, Connors,' Harley replied. 'Getting this thing...that was a bitch.' He hefted up a large metal case and rested it on the rail. Wyatt scolded, 'Careful with that. Imagine how stupid we'd look if we lost it now.' 'Keep yer pants on. Just figured you'd want to take a look.' 'That I do, my friend. That I do.' Connors sidled over to the case and began fiddling with the combination. Hesitantly, he lifted the lid...and smiled broadly at the contents. Stacks upon stacks of U.S. currency, plus account information for half a dozen banks. The work of half a lifetime--and that's not even counting the stocks. 'How much is in there?' 'Two million in cash. Another eight in the accounts.' Connors lowered the lid of the case and snapped it shut. 'Not even sure what the stocks are at right now. Probably not as much as I'd hoped, but certainly more than I'd technically need.' He pulled the case off the rail, and nearly dropped it to the ground--its weight had come close to jerking his arm out of the socket. 'Ahh!' Harley snickered through his teeth, which were clamped around his own cigar. 'Heh. Sissy.' 'Fuuuuuck you.' Harley laughed so hard that his cigar fell out of his mouth, and over the bridge. 'Shit.' 'Don't worry about it. We needed to move along anyway.' 'Right. So, what now? Back to the plan?' 'Slight change. You take this...I think you know what to do with it.' Connors passed the heavy case into Harley Roebuck's hands. Harley looked at Wyatt, a look of mild shock spreading over his face. 'Hang on. You're not coming?' 'I can't. I've still got a few loose ends to tie up.' 'Y'mean Seck?' Connors chuckled bitterly at the mention of his former 'savior.' 'Oh, he's definitely on the list. But he's not the only one. There's all manner of unfinished business.' Harley shook his head. It was that kind of thinking that caused all kinds of messes in the past. Wyatt had no problems about cutting loose from small cons...but he just couldn't seem to let go of the big one. 'What kind of unfinished business?' 'I've got a title belt to defend, for one thing. Besides, I feel the need to properly re-establish myself in the company.' 'I thought you didn't give a shit about any of that.' 'Normally, I don't. But the last year and a half did a lot of damage, Harley. I can't leave until I...fix things.' 'What about Pierce? You gotta know she'll be gunning for you...especially after that little present you left her.' 'Heh. You think she'll like it?' A slight smirk played across Connors' lips, but only for a moment. 'You let me worry about Pierce. The important thing is that the money goes where it's supposed to. After that, it won't matter what she does...she's never getting it back.' Harley nodded. 'So, you want me to go now?' 'Not just yet. I'll still need your help dealing with Bowdry and Seck. You got the girl's phone, right?' 'Yeah.' 'Perfect. This should be fun.' Wyatt took one last puff from his cigar, and ground out the stub on the railing. He turned and headed for the old black Lincoln that was parked a few feet away. 'No sense standing around all night. Much to do!' Harley Roebuck had not been expecting the sudden move, and had to move quickly to catch up. He flicked his cigar over the side of the bridge, picked up the case, and followed. 'And what about Seck?' he asked. Connors' face spread out with a wide, sinister grin. 'Oh, you're going to love this...' In the coming months, Wyatt Connors will begin a new crusade...one that has nothing to do with Decency. He will make them forget the dogma-spouting buffoon they had seen for the past year. He will inflict himself upon the lives of hundreds of people, and not a single one of them will be better for it. Perhaps you will meet some of them, as they desperately try to reassemble the pieces of their lives. You will know them by their bitterness. Their curses. Their paranoia. When you meet them, you will ask: 'Who did this to you? Who made you believe a man who cannot be trusted? Who drew you into a game that you already knew was rigged? Who has, time and time again, made you play the fool?' And they will answer: 'The Wise Guy.' # # # Time: unknown. Location: the dark. Ibrahim Seck had long since given up screaming. No one had yet answered his cries...and every time he opened his mouth, something fell in. Whatever it was, it did not taste very good. He had no idea how long he'd been in here. Could have been hours. There had been periods of movement, but most of these last few hours (days?) had been spent in a sort of sensory depravation. No one to talk to, to beg, to threaten. He was all alone, with only his thoughts to keep him company. Thoughts of betrayal. Seck let out a low hiss as he thought of the man who had brought ruin down upon him. His big brother had Judas Iscariot...and Ibrahim Seck had Wyatt Connors. How did they escape my influence? he thought. The question had bothered him for days, before he finally worked out the answer. When Brother Harley--then claiming to be Wyatt's brother--had asked to refresh Wyatt after his confession...that's when it happened. He was supposed to use the blessed Kool-Aid, but they deceived their Master. Soon, Wyatt's heart was once more filled with sin...at which time, he must have done the same to the others. Pablo, Jorge, Macarthur...even Brother Colt, the greatest achievement of the Decency Crusade. They all turned against him in the end. All led astray by Connors. He was amazed by the sheer unfairness of it all. How dare they treat God's Youngest Son this way? Had he not already suffered enough for his faith? Six years, locked away in a dungeon, prisoner of the beast known as BloodAngel. He had lost contact with his loyal followers, and sometimes days would go by where he did not even feel the presence of his heavenly Father. One blessed day, he managed to escape, and tried to rebuild his holy empire. A year of hard work had begun to bear fruit, and the prize Seck desired most--the heart and the mind of the Beast that had once tormented him--was in his grasp. Then, at the exact moment of triumph, it was all taken away. He was brutally attacked by BloodAngel, and then Connors and the former Decency Crusade had dragged him offstage, and then... Then, blackness. He still had no idea where he was. Was he dead? No, he could hear himself breathing. Still, they might have buried him. Thrown into the ground, covered with dirt, and forgotten...what an ignominious end to the Savior of All Humanity. Never! A spark of defiance lit up in his mind. Did they believe he could be defeated so easily? Ha! He was Ibrahim Seck! The Second Son of God! If his big brother could escape from being buried, so could Ibrahim--and he hadn't even been killed first, so this ought to be a snap! He wiggled his arms, trying to make enough space to move them properly. It was minor, but it was still progress. After a few minutes of work, he managed to make contact with a flat, hard surface. Possibly wood. 'A coffin?' he said, not caring that nobody could hear him. 'Barely even an obstacle!' He tried scratching at it with his fingers, but could not find purchase. He started to position himself closer, so that he might try to strike at it. Seck heard a muffled voice. He couldn't make it out, but he knew that it came from somewhere...beyond. Proof that he was on the verge of a breakthrough--and possibly, not just a physical one. Yes! This could be a spiritual transcendence! This could be the moment where he, in the words of the great prophet, breaks on through to the other side! He heard a loud creaking sound, like metal being pulled from wood. Surely this means he was breaking down the walls of this reality, and was about to discover something new! Now was his chance to leave them all behind--the traitors, the heathens, and the sinners of all kinds! Another creak. A brilliant beam of light. And then, the entire world opened up before him. 'BEHOLD!' he screamed, his madness reaching a fever pitch. 'I HAVE BIRTHED THE UNIVERSE! I HAVE BECOME THE LI--IIIIAAAAAAAAH!' His bold claim of godhood was interrupted by a sudden removal of support from his left side. Ibrahim fell downward, awash in a sea of packing peanuts. He landed hard on what appeared to be a wood panel, something akin to one side of a shipping crate. Seck looked up, and recoiled from the brightness of the sun. From what he could see, this brave new world he had created looked a lot like the old one. He struggled to a seating position, so that he could get a better view of his surroundings. As he did so, he heard an all too familiar voice. 'Well, son of a bitch.' The Reverend looked up, knowing full well that he did not wish to see what was there. Sure enough, Ibrahim found himself face-to-face with the vicious grin of his immortal enemy, Edward Lambert. BloodAngel. Ibrahim Seck tried to rejoice at the tasting of fresh air, but he found himself unable in the presence of his enemy. Instead, he slowly arose, sending a cascade of polystyrene down onto Lambert's driveway. His anger flared, but curiously, it was not directed at the man in front of him. Instead, the focus of his outrage was at Wyatt Connors, the man who violated his trust and delivered him back into captivity. Does his treachery know no bounds? But of course, he should have already known the answer to that. 'What the hell's that?' Edward muttered and shot out a hand toward Ibrahim. Seck tried to jump back, but his reflexes had never been that good, and he fell down again. He was able to offer no resistance to Lambert, who reached down toward Seck again, grabbing for his chest. Or, more accurately, something affixed to Seck's chest. It was a simple sheet of notebook paper, with a message on it--obviously by someone with impeccable handwriting. Happy Father's Day, Edward. -WC Edward laughed as he crumpled up the paper and tossed it aside. Then, the man known as BloodAngel looked down upon the great and mighty Reverend Seck, showing a crooked smile that chilled Ibrahim to the core. 'Good to see you again, Seck. Got your room all ready for you.' Ibrahim's face fell, and he scarcely had time to protest before Lambert grabbed him by the neck and dragged him toward the house. 'Oh, Lord,' he wailed. 'Not again...' Perhaps you will meet him someday, although it is not at all likely. His already unstable mind will have fractured many times, rendering him little more than a caged beast. A man who had fought fiercely for his freedom and power...only to see it cruelly snatched away. When you meet him, you will ask: 'Who did this to you? Who led you into temptation with his honey-sweet praise? Who raised you to the heavens, all the while plotting your fall? Who tricked you into the very same hell from which you had once escaped?' And he will answer... 'The Devil.'
