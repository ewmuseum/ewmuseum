Onnanoko ('Girl')

Date: After Equilibrium 'Hey, hey... don't you think these look cute?' This was Annie. Annie had some issues. At least, that's what I started thinking when I turned my head and saw these toy cat ears planted firmly on her twin-tailed head. She must have picked them up when I wasn't looking, and decided to show me just to try and get a funny reaction out of me or something. Instead, I tried not to laugh. She gave me a sour look, anyway, because she could tell how badly I was failing at holding in the laughter. It wasn't that she didn't look cute wearing the ears, it was that for a girl that's about to turn 24 next month, she still had this child-like innocence. Given the people I often surrounded myself with, Annie was the one shining beacon of purity I had remaining. 'Annie' was my pet name for her, and it's short for Annabelle. I met her about five years ago in some Greek restaurant in Cleveland. I actually went there on the recommendation of a housemate, and though I became a frequent customer back then, I assure you that it wasn't because of the food. It was because she worked there playing a live violin. I was... moved by her, as it were. She had a lot of talent as a musician, yet what she played there spoke volumes of how lonely she was feeling at the time. Not long after I started going to the place, I came up to her and spoke to her. I don't even remember anything of what I said, but I do know that she liked me immediately. I became close friends with her, though I didn't exactly start right out in a relationship with her. Actually, at the time, I was dating this blond-haired bimbo named Patricia Grayson (or 'Trish'), who served as one of the referees at that Texas promotion a few years back. Trish and I never really clicked, though whether that was my fault or not, I couldn't say. I guess I can say for the record that she wasn't especially bright, and she used to get mad at me for even talking to Annie. I heard once that Trish came up to Annie and demanded that she stop 'stalking' me. Even though most of the times Trish and Annie encountered one another were pretty much because of me, anyway. Most people think I'm pretty bad with women and that I lucked out with Annie; maybe they're right. Hell, some people even think I'm gay, which was just ridiculous. Though I can understand why they'd think that. Long hair, often jokes about what matches my eyes, professional wrestler, wears tight spandex, friends with both Marcus Dox AND Jared Sykes... geez, if I didn't know myself, I'd swear that I really was gay. Anyway, Trish and I simply fell apart one week, so I decided to start dating Annie afterwards. At least a small part of me, at the time, felt like I was treating her as the rebound girl, but thankfully, it didn't become anything like that. Sometimes, you just meet someone and you think to yourself 'this is going to be the girl I'll spend the rest of my life with'. That's Annie for me. I won't lie and say that everything's been perfect, there's always complications and it took me five years to summon the guts and intestinal fortitude to take the big plunge and propose to her. All and all, though, Annie has easily been the best thing to happen in my life. With or without cat ears. Annie's about eight inches shorter than I am and had a fairly slight build. Her black hair was long enough to reach her waist, and she habitually tied her hair in long pigtails on the sides of her head. She had this wide-eyed, innocent-looking nature that made her stand out, especially amongst the women I've met in Sin City so far. She was childish, playful, a bit of a crybaby, and also a bit stubborn. She also sometimes still judged people based on her first impressions of them, and still didn't fully understand the wrestling business that I work in to this day. I'm actually amazed she's still like this even after five years of dating her. Despite her flaws, she was a gentle person and the music she liked to perform was equally gentle. She understood what my job meant to me, and willingly supported me wherever I went. Especially if it meant my frequent jaunts to Japan, as she happened to love that country. After all, she'd spent so much time in her childhood living there. Besides, I honestly needed her there, because my knowledge of Japanese was intermediate on a good day, and I still couldn't read the damn symbols to save my life. Right now, I was home after my disappointing performance at Equilibrium, which saw one of my opponents getting injured and me not even involved in the decision that cost me the championship. Annie and I lived in an apartment that was just big enough for the two of us. Annie recently took a job as a composer for a local gaming company's current project, which I'm sure was something that thrilled her. Of course, the work on the music didn't exactly set well with the neighbors. I often promised her that if my new wrestling gig paid enough, we could move into a house. I'm still waiting for my last paycheck. Sigh. 'They're fine.' I finally managed to choke out, now barely being able to contain my laughter, 'Although I think if you said 'meow' or something, I won't be able to hold in my laughter any more.' Annie looked sort of annoyed, and her cheeks were sort of puffed up out of frustration. Somehow, this just made her all the cuter. We sat together on a couch in my apartment, both of us half-heartedly watching that Spirited Away animated movie. You know, the one where that little girl was taken into a magical world beyond human understanding. Come to think of it, that was me with Sin City and this whole Baron von Blackberry thing. Half the time I'm working there, I never understood what's going on. From what I can gather, all of the women who work here were clearly insane, and at least half the men who work here don't really give a crap about anyone but themselves. It was truly a cutthroat wrestling promotion, even when simply viewing it from the outside. That's why I asked Annie to stay at home when I first started working here... if anything were to happen to her, I wouldn't really know what to do with myself. Anyway, I'll admit that I was paying more attention to the girl sitting next to me than the animated girl on my low-budget television set. I don't even have cable, which Jared once swore to me was an entire KFC bucket of tragedy. His words, not mine. 'I'm not saying 'meow'.' Annie said, ironically saying exactly what she didn't want to say. I laughed. I guess it couldn't be held in any more, after all. Annie realized what she said as soon as I started laughing, and she went into her puffy-cheeked frustrated expression again. Sometimes I wondered just what my life would be like if I hadn't met Annie. No doubt, I'd have tried my luck with some of the very questionable women I've met through the years. My first girlfriend, at least my first since I got into this profession, dumped me for a particularly ugly-looking idiot who practically spoke an entire different language of internet lingo. Then I had several particularly bad dates before I met Trish, and then I met Annie. Most of the women I dated were pretty strong-willed and independent, and I guess they just saw me as a harmless toy until they could find something better than me. I imagine I'd have been used and abused like that until the end of time. Thinking about stuff like that made me appreciate what I have now just a little bit more. I should probably change the subject, 'How's the music progressing?' 'For the game?' she asked. After I nodded, Annie looked up at the ceiling and thought about it before coming up with an answer, 'Alright, I suppose. They were happy that I was a traditional musician and knew how to compose. Most people they'd worked with in the past just knew how to mess around with a computer. The deadline's a bit rough, though. They want a lot of music in such a short amount of time. I'm trying to branch out my music a bit since this is a space shooter and all that. I was thinking about taking some inspiration from Battlestar Galactica... you know, the epic, sweeping, orchestral music? Something like that. I've never really conducted before, and I'd much rather play an instrument, but... um... Is something wrong?' Apparently, she noticed my smile. 'No, nothing, I was just thinking that I'm marrying the right girl.' I said. Okay, so I said this to the girl who was turning 24, sometimes acted 14, and was wearing cat ears made for someone who was 8. Cut a guy some slack. 'Please, continue.' I said. 'Ahh, there's really not much more to say,' Annie said, who was trying to hide her blushing from me and failing adeptly at it, 'I have the idea and all, it's just a matter of figuring out how to accomplish it. I've a feeling I'll be playing most of the instruments myself in a studio and then putting it together on the computer. So troublesome.' 'You can't hire a real orchestra?' I asked. 'The budget's too small for that. I've thought about hiring one of the local bands, though... maybe combine my traditional instruments with hard rock. It wouldn't be quite as good in my mind, though, but it could still work if they cooperated with me.' Annie responded, still looking up as she thought about it. The thought of her working with some of the local long-haired grunge bands that I've seen around here filled me with equal parts amusement and dread. I doubt that's who she'd drift towards, though, but you can't blame a guy for worrying about his girl. I guess being a music director was harder than it sounded, though. Annie looked at me with those eyes of hers. A lesser man could be melted by her eyes, 'So, how goes the wrestling? I'm a bit busy to tag along, now, but...' 'No, I wouldn't want you to.' I said, interrupting her almost as quickly as she started talking. Up until now, I was fine with Annie joining me on my journey to perform as a professional wrestler. This place, though... I don't know what it was about it, but I considered it dangerous. I thought about what Lane Stevens had done to the Marshalls. I thought about the sheer effort it took to push Jonathan Rhine out of the promotion last year. I even thought about what happened to Xander Searle in Vegas. I thought about all of that and realized that this wasn't a happy and friendly wrestling promotion, where I would happily spend my days performing my art amongst my peers. This was the proverbial jungle. Only, instead of the lion being the king of the jungle, it was the spider and the rat. I kind of sulked. I'm not sure how I felt, but I knew I felt pretty down just thinking about it. Losing a title (and the paycheck that would've come with it) will do that to a guy, 'Annie, there's something about Sin City that just rubs me the wrong way. It's not even the part where I don't get my paychecks in a timely manner. I'm thinking that maybe it's truly filled with sinners, and that it's not simply part of the promotion's name.' My thoughts lingered in the air for a moment. Annie thought about it for a moment, before she giggled at me and looked away. I felt like my opinion wasn't being appreciated by a girl who was wearing cat ears, and for some weird reason that eluded me, this was actually something that bothered me. Annie turned back towards me, holding a hand to her mouth to stifle her giggle. 'Always so concerned about me. It's what I like about you. Well, that and you're handsome. That helps a lot, you know.' 'So handsome I am that I get to hide my face, huh?' I said with a laugh. Annie smiled and leaned up against me, looking up at me with those eyes of hers, 'Sure. You're all to myself.' Screw it. Today, I am a lesser man. I sort of melted in my seat, letting Annie lean against me even more. I looked at her with what I thought was a sly grin, but it was probably goofier than I thought it was, 'Yeah, okay. I don't have a thing for cat ears or anything, but... dammit, girl, you're going to make me develop one.' 'Just as planned.' she said with a smile, leaning in closer for a kiss. Annie might have been like a child at times, but that just made me want to protect her more. She was passionate about her music, and passionate about me. To my credit, I didn't scare her off in less than a year like some other women I've dated. She accepted me for who I was, and I accepted her for who she was. I may have had my concerns about what was in store for me for Sin City, but as long as my lungs still held breath, I would protect the person I loved the most. At least, that's what I thought when I returned her kiss. Hm. Perhaps I think too much.
