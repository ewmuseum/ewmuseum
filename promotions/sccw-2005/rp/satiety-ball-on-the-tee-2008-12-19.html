Date: 12/19/08 December 19, 2008 Somewhere on the Eastern Seaboard of the United States The hood was goddamn tight, breath dampening the cloth so it was uncomfortable around the mouth and nose. The figure sat in the back of a van, wrists crossed and cuffed, head down. Sneaker-clad feet were manacled -- it hadn't started that way, but a solid kick to one captor's kneecap necessitated a response. It had taken two of them to bind those ankles, and the man with the short blond crewcut who sat in the opposite bench would be nursing a bruise to his shoulder for a good while. Who knew someone would try to bite him, though. The figure tried to think of what to do in this situation -- what she would do. Trying to calm down, thin lips moved under the hood, counting the seconds the trip had taken. Ears strained for any background noise; that's what you do when you've been kidnapped, right? The counting lasted for just under a minute before the stream of vitriol replaced the numbers. The small group of captors merely turned up the radio in response. The hooded figure was just starting to clamber into a standing position when the van jerked to a halt, ending with an uncomfortable stumble into the bench. Even through the cloth of the full-face mask, the outside light was visible, but it was the marked drop in temperature that sent nerves to blazing. ‘‘We're here,’‘ a man's voice (the one who'd been bitten? The figure couldn't tell) growled. ‘‘Can you walk?’‘ For the first time in the trip, the kidnapped victim said nothing. ‘‘I said,’‘ he ripped the mask off the woman's head. ‘‘Can you walk?’‘ Amy Campbell answered the man with a headbutt and a sneer. ''I dunno,'' She growled, ''Maybe you should try uncuffing me, see what I can do.'' ‘‘That's not going to happen, Campbell,’‘ another voice said, outside the van -- one that snapped Aimz' glare to the open door. Kathryn Shaw's soft-heeled boots clicked softly as she stepped into view, long nails glittering in the early morning sun. ‘‘I come in peace,’‘ she grinned. ‘‘I'm here to make sure nothing bad happens to you. You agreed to stand witness, and -- Lexi's thoughts on the matter aside -- you don't get to avoid the panel.'' I have an interview lined up with that ridiculously ghetto idiot, not a kidnapping. Now tell these fuckheads to let me go, or I'll choke them with the handcuffs. I'm not in a mood for your cloak and dagger bullshit. And you're gonna want to do it quickly if you enjoy things like air or--'' A quick blow to the cheek dazed Campbell, but only long enough for two of the men to pin her and re-cuff her hands behind her back. ''Fuck!'' came the initial squeal as Amy squirmed, ''That hurt!'' ''Try chokin' someone with 'em behind your back.'' One of the cronies chuckled, triumphant as he pulled her up by the collar of her sweater. ''This can't possibly be for real. Are you that dumb, Shaw?'' Katsidy's shrug was a gentle, flowing thing. ‘‘You missed the appointment. Lexi has refused to schedule one. You have to understand that they're a liiiiittle pissed that she's avoiding the inquiry.’‘ Her smile was quick and full. ‘‘You know you didn't do anything wrong, so why don't you just go quietly?’‘ ''Maybe because I think you're a fucking moron, this whole thing is an absolute crock...'' It was hard to say, partially because of the time, lack of a morning drug buzz and the glaring light of the early sun. You know I'm here to look after Lexi's interests, and, besides, she'll be here soon.’‘ The first time she lunged at Kathryn was just inside the front entryway, and it got her yanked back by the big men surrounding them. The second time, she was pushed into a wall and scolded about the necessity of cooperation in these matters. The third time, well... they were far enough indoors for the yelp of being thrown onto the cold tiles of a plain, dank room to be inaudible to any outsiders. At the front was yet another reason she should have never gone back to cocaine -- it was her biggest tell, her gargantuan soft spot and a weakness only people like Kathryn Shaw would so gladly exploit. That was actually the only standing addiction her husband knew anything about, and it was yet another weakness that the organization behind Desade would happily take advantage of.
