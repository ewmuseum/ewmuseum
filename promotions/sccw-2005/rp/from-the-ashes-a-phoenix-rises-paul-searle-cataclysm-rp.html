From The Ashes, a Phoenix Rises (Paul Searle Cataclysm RP)

Date: Today ---Prologue--- Paul Searle didn’t fly into Ottawa like many of the other wrestlers that were members of the SCCW staff. Instead, he drove into the city in the Jeep that he had borrowed from Jason Ryan’s garage. His duffle bag was fairly light, only a couple pairs of jeans and a few t-shirts. Before the event, he managed to get Jonathan Rhine to sign a program from the show. Now, Rhine was no longer on the roster, having been betrayed by Charlotte Ramone and Krissy Jamison. "Mr. Ryan isn’t going to like this." Paul didn’t know where Jay Draven, the man that pulled him from the faceless crowd in Cleveland and started him on this journey, was. All he knows is that he was alone in a world where being alone could either bring you the greatest amount of protection…or the worst repercussions. He got to the border crossing and saw smoke from over the Detroit River somewhere near Delray. He didn’t think anything of it. Even after he crossed the Ambassador Bridge and the stench of burning metal filled the air. "Just another day in Detroit." Paul thinks to himself as he pulls out from the American side of the Ambassador Bridge and into traffic. His stomach starts to growl. He knows he’s not too far from a Coney Island. Maybe he’d stop to get a bite to eat before heading to the Warehouse and relaxing. As he drives, he’s tempted to turn on the radio, only to realize that all the sports radio stations are going to talk about the Detroit Lions and how they already are having a bad season, the Detroit Tigers and how their bad season is about to end, and how happy everyone one is that Red Wings season is almost upon them. For a young man from Ohio, it’s almost enough to want to hang himself from the Belle Isle Bridge. Paul eventually turns into the parking lot of the Coney Island. He’s been there a couple of times; they are getting to know the kid. "Lemme guess, sweetie, you want a burger, Greek Salad and a Coke." the waitress says to Paul as soon as he enters the restaurant. He points at a seat and goes to sit down. The young man’s jaw is still a deep shade of purple after being hit in the face by fighting sticks by the woman once known as Krissy Jamison. He picks up a napkin dispenser and takes a look at his reflection at it. "Great, mom is gonna kill me." Paul said to himself as he sets the dispenser back down. The waitress comes around with Paul’s Coke. "Here you go, sweet……Jesus, what happened to your face?" Her eyes got wide as she saw the bruise that covered Paul’s jaw. "Don’t wanna talk about it." "Well, maybe you should talk to the police; have the person that did that to you arrested." "It’s not going to help anything trying to get the police involved. Anyway, it didn’t happen here." "It wasn’t that other guy that I’ve seen you come in here with, that man I can tell is no good." "No, it wasn’t him. He wasn’t anywhere around when it happened." "Well, if you want talk about it, hon, just let me know." "Thanks, but I don’t think I will." The waitress heads back towards the counter of the Coney, as Paul pulled out his cell phone. He began thinking whether or not he should call his mom, just to let her know that he’s doing ok. After a moment, he decides that he will call her later, she’s probably at the church doing something for the reverend. After a few minutes, the waitress comes back to the table with Paul’s salad and burger. Interesting combination, especially, for a young kid that was just getting started in the wrestling business. He decided that it tasted better than the wheat germ that he was forced to drink in high school to prepare for basketball season. At least this way, he would know that he was still getting his protein, and it didn’t taste like shit. Paul relaxes a few moments while he eats the salad, and then scarfs down the burger. With his hunger gone, he heads to the counter to pay for his meal. He gives the waitress 11 bucks, which was enough to cover tax and tip as well as his lunch. Paul then heads out of the restaurant and starts to head back to the Warehouse. ---Entering the Inferno--- At this point, the fire had completely engulfed the steel structure. You could see that the walls have already started to collapse from the heat. The firefighters are fighting through thick black smoke. That knew that thankfully no one was in the building. The frame of a Harley Davidson could be seen through the flames. As could the steel skeleton that once was a part of a wrestling ring. The Warehouse was being destroyed. Because of the motorcycle that was in the fire, the Detroit Fire Department pulled back. They knew that the tank, as soon as it was hot enough, would probably blow. They were at the back of the building trying to extinguish whatever it was that they could save, but at this point, they knew that all they were doing was keeping the fire from spreading. The rest of the warehouses were vacant, but they brave men and women fighting this inferno were working tirelessly to make sure that they remained standing. Paul pulls up, and stands as he sees the place that was starting to become home for him slowly becoming just another burned out building in an already blighted city. "Kid, you can’t be here right now." the Fire Marshall said as Paul looks on in disbelief. "That’s where I’ve been staying." Paul barely was able to find those words, as he’s watching what had become his world being destroyed by the most unforgiving of Earth’s elements. "Well, I don’t know what to tell you, but it’s gone." The Fire Marshall tried to say this as nicely as he could, but Paul didn’t hear those words. "My…my…no." Paul stammered. "Kid, you have to go. I’m not going to tell you again." The Fire Marshall was more forceful in his tone, hoping to get through to the kid. Paul didn’t move though, as the words just passed through him without registering. "Kid, you see that motorcycle in there, in only a few moments, it is going to explode. We don’t want you…" the Fire Marshall’s words are interrupted as the concussive blast from the exploding fuel tank on Jason Ryan’s motorcycle knocks a couple of fire fighters to the ground. The Fire Marshall gets to his feet and brushes himself off. He gets on the radio, "OK, boys. Hit this thing with everything that you’ve got." More water started to hit the flames. But it was more than apparent that the only thing that it was doing was keeping the fire from spreading to the surrounding buildings. Paul slumps down in the seat of the jeep, unable to get his eyes off of the fire as it eats the steel that made up the Warehouse. "Oh, god." Paul lets slip as the fire continues to burn. "What am I gonna do?" The Fire Marshall watches as Paul gets out of the Jeep and walks towards the blaze. His eyes affixed to the reds and yellows. Another fire fighter comes up and takes Paul by the arm, trying to keep him from getting too close to the flame. A tear rolls down Paul’s eye. This wasn’t just his world that was being destroyed, but the world of his mentor as well. "Kid, come on, you shouldn’t be here." the fire fighter said in a calm, composed and caring voice. But Paul doesn’t want to listen. It takes a much larger, gloved hand taking his shoulder to get his attention. Paul turns to the man who is now standing behind him to see a man that easily looked Paul dead in the eyes. At one time, the man was known as Legion. Now, he went by his real name of Michael Durant. "Kid, you better do what the man says." ---The Child In Time--- The Doubletree in Romulus was used to Pro Wrestlers staying there when there was an event in town, so they knew how to treat them properly. The restaurant that was on property was a known hang out for the stars when they came to town, especially now that the Denny’s on Middlebelt was shuddered. Inside, at one of the tables, sat two rather tall gentlemen. The waitstaff originally thought that they were NBA players that came to Detroit to possibly try out with the Pistons. They were wrong. One, in his mid-thirties bore a visible scar down his right eye, a momento of a past life. The other looked to be barely old enough to do anything legally. They just sat there waiting on the waitress. "I’ll tell you something, Paul. I’ve known Jay for a long time. He has his reason for doing what he did. You might not know what it is now, but eventually, you will." "But what do I do until he shows back up? I can’t go out there and wrestle. I don’t know what I’m doing." "You do what he taught you. You do what you pick up from other wrestlers. But most importantly, you do what you need to do in order to survive." Durant scornfully told the youngster. "What if I can’t?" Paul asks as Durant takes a sip from his coffee. "Listen, Paul, I don’t care about what you think you can or you can’t do. You now have an obligation to SCCW. That obligation came when you signed your name on that dotted line and accepted a standard booking contract with the Kent Fusiliers." "I’m not like you or Mr. Ryan. I don’t think that I can get in there and wrestle without someone being there." "Kid, you can do it. You just need to believe in yourself. When Jay first started, he’s was barely a buck-eighty. He was told plenty of times that he wasn’t going to make it in the business. Even by our trainer. You know what he did?" "Took steroids and superkicked him to oblivion?" The response breaks Durant’s concentration for only a moment, before he just lets off a little chuckle. Before he’s able to respond though, the waitress at the restaurant walks up. "Are you two gentlemen ready?" the waitress asks as she’s ready to take the order down. "I’m fine with the coffee, ma’am." "Same here." "Kid, order something." "My check from SCCW hasn’t arrived yet." "Don’t worry about it, I’ll cover it." Paul then turns to the waitress, "OK, I guess I’ll get two eggs, over easy. An order of bacon, and do you have white toast?" "Sorry, sir, we ran out of white bread. But we do have a light wheat that you might enjoy." "That’s fine. Don’t put any butter on it or slice it in half. Just the whole slices would be fine." "Will that be all for you gentlemen?" "Can you put the order on the Damage Inc. account?" Durant asks. "I’ll have to check with the manager, but I believe I can." "Thank you." The waitress walks away. "Damage Inc?" Paul asks. "The company that my sister and I run. We have an account with all the local hotels. We bring in a lot of businessmen from around the country. That’s also who’s paying for your hotel room until the apartment is ready." "Apartment?" "Yes. I don’t think you know this, but when Jay took you under his wing, he asked me to take care of you in the event that something happens. The fact that the Warehouse is destroyed and Jay is nowhere to be found would warrant that I move you into an apartment that I have above my garage." "Uh…." "Don’t worry, you are going to get it rent free for a while, it’s separate from the main house, and you will have way more privacy than you did when you were staying at the Warehouse. The only thing that I ask in return is that when you leave for a show, that you lock the door." "Uh….thanks, I guess." "Listen, I don’t see it myself, but Jay sees something in you. He has a feeling that you could do something in this business. Otherwise, he wouldn’t have pulled you from the crowd in Cleveland. "Oh, and as I was saying earlier, when Jay was just getting started, he went out, he found the biggest cat that was training with us, and he took him down. Jay marked his territory. He didn’t give it up until he was damn well good and ready." "You think I should do that? Take down the biggest…cat?" "No, you have got to be true to yourself. That was Jay showing that he was true to himself. Jay was never the kind of person to take that he wasn’t good enough to heart. He had to prove himself to everyone else." "You’re saying that I don’t?" "Kid, look at you. You are 6’8". You gain 50 pounds, which is real easy in this business, and you are going to be looked at as an impressive specimen. Unless you feel you have to, you don’t have to prove anything to anybody." Paul just sits there in quiet reflection over what Michael Durant had to say as the man that was going to take him into his home continues to drink his coffee. After a few minutes of silence, the waitress brings Paul his food. "Here you go, sir. Was there anything else that you needed at this time." Michael looked at the waitress. "Did you find out if the order can be put on that account?" "It can sir." "Well, then I guess I’m all set then." Durant stands up and takes a look at the young Paul Searle. "I’m taking care of your room, if you need anything, just charge it to the room." Paul stands up as well and holds out his hand. "Thank you, Mr. Durant." Durant shakes Paul’s hand and then starts to walk towards the door. As the man formerly known as Legion leaves the restaurant, a rather striking blonde, that, up until recently, Paul would have considered a friend, walks in and heads towards where Paul was sitting. The blonde is wearing dark shades, darker than they needed to be this early, her curly, honeyed hair wrapped up in a tie. Her smile for the hostess is small but considerate, and the other woman nods to her as she creeps up to Jason Ryan's protégé. Before he could react or stand himself, the blonde drops into a chair opposite Searle, setting her bag to the floor. 'Morning, Paul,' the woman formerly known as Krissy Jamison says quietly, a light smile curling her lips. The barest of British accents slips through Kirsten's words, caressing more than engulfing them. She leans across the table slightly, voice lowering further. 'Don't make a scene, love.' 'What are you doing here?' Paul replies as he avoids looking at the woman that caused the considerable bruise on his jaw in the eye. 'I was... in the neighborhood, you might say.' A waitress steps up to the table with pad in hand and a smile on her face. 'Can I get you anything, ma'am?' When Kirsten turns to the woman, the smile was bright and practiced in its innocence, and the undercurrent of accent in her voice dissipates as if it was never there. 'Gosh, I'm starving, but I'm trying to watch my weight. You know how it is. Just a glass of grapefruit juice, I think. I'll snag some of Paulie's food if I can't control myself.' The waitress dutifully jots down the request, and Kirsten watches after, her cornflower-blue eyes light. It isn't until the waitress was well and gone that she turns back. 'What are you doing here, Paul?' 'Just waiting for one of Mr. Ryan's friends to let me know that the room I'll be staying in is ready.' Paul takes the wheat toast that was on the plate in front of him and places 4 slices of bacon inbetween the slices of bread. He goes to put the egg that was also on the plate on the toast as well until Kirsten stops him. 'I wasn't lyin' when I said I was hungry, bear.' She snagged a fork, scooping a bit of egg up. 'Still haven't heard from Jason, have you?' Her tone stayed light and friendly -- were it not for the fact that this woman assaulted him moments before Jonathan Rhine was screwed, they could still be friends. 'Must be difficult for you.' 'I guess I'll survive. I'm sure Mr. Ryan knows what he's doing.' Paul replies as he places the other slice of the wheat toast on the bacon and takes a bite from the sandwich. 'Are you? Dear, he pulled you from a crowd, said he'd train you, said you'd become tag-team champions together. Then he abandons you, because Jason always comes first. Doesn't strike you as a wee bit unfair? And you're here in a hotel because his warehouse was torched? Tsk-tsk, love, seems like he might've been erasing any trace of his presence.' She sat back in the seat, pulling up her shakes into her hair. 'But I didn't come to see you about that or to have a lovely breakfast with a close friend who I let look at me nearly naked for a couple weeks, did I?' 'Krissy...Kirsten...whatever your name is, how did you find me anyway?' Paul asks as the waitress returns to the table is Kirsten's grapefruit juice. Kirsten adopted Krissy's naïve smile again as she accepted the cool beverage from waitress. 'Thank you so much.' Again, she waited for the waitress to step away to another customer's table before continuing. 'I could say that I have powerful friends, but, while I shan't expect you to believe me, I didn't need Ms. P's resources.' She took a long sip of the drink, glass in both hands, eyes to him. 'When I called around the area looking for 'Paul Searle,' this was the only hotel I found. Truly, dear, you'll need to learn to use different names. Heaven knows it works for me, but if I had a big match this week, I certainly would be wary of fans.' She set the glass down on the table. 'I hear some of them might be crazy.' 'Well, Mr. Durant is taking care of that stuff for me. He told me that he's going make sure I had a good psuedo-name to check in under. He said something about Kelly Tripuka.' Her chuckle was soft and subtle. 'That's adorable. Tell me...' She leans across the table again, her smile light. 'I don't usually intentionally tease men -- I'm not Miss Shaw or anything. Did I do a decent job?' It seemed like an honest question, but it amounted to Austin Powers' 'Do I Make You Horny, Babe?' Her eyes were wide, smile curling. Normally, when an attractive blonde says that, the answer would always be yes, especially when you're a teenager that doesn't have a girlfriend. But, when that same blonde used your head as a tee-ball, it makes Paul Searle want to vomit. 'You can finish it, I'm not hungry anymore.' Paul says as he pushes his plate towards Kirsten. 'Oh, come on, don't be like that, Paul.' She reaches out, laying her hand on his, and he reacts like he'd been bit by a snake. 'In a perfect world,' she began. 'In a perfect world, a bloke like you would be perfect for a girl like me. We could be happy, and I'm terribly monogamous when it comes to these things. In a perfect world, I could tell Miss Shaw and Miss P to get bent and we could sod off somewhere and ensconce ourselves in a hotel like this for weeks. I have been pretending to like women for months, and in a perfect world, we could forget the nastiness on Monday and you could make me forget about the touch of a woman.' 'In a perfect world, I wouldn't have lost all my things in that fire.' 'It's not a perfect world, though, is it, Paul?' The question hung in the air between them for a moment, before she leans back. 'I hope you know that what I did to you on Monday, it wasn't personal, love. I did intentionally attract your roving eye, but I didn't do that or strike you out of malice or anything. It was merely business, the completion of a job. I didn't intend to bring you harm.' 'But you did, and it hurt.' She nods softly. 'I know,' she said. 'I could tell you I regretted it, but you'd disbelieve me, and perhaps think I was tricking you again.' 'Did you know what you were doing was wrong?' 'I knew what I was doing would pay my bills. I... ignored thoughts about its morality.' 'Why?' 'You would be surprised what kinds of debts a lady such as myself can wrack up, especially moving so far away from home.' She shifted uncomfortably, flicking her eyes away to find the waitress -- the sunny grin and aw-shucks American accent don't fit as well this time. 'Can we get a box? Thanks!' 'You know, you are a bad person. I wish that I never met you or Ms. Raymond.' She lowers her head, eyes to the tabletop. 'I know you do, Paul. And I can only say that I regret that deeply and hope -- hope -- you can forgive me someday.' The waitress brings back Paul's breakfast in a Styrofoam box, which Kirsten seizes as she stands. 'I'll be going now. Don't try to follow me. There's a little something for you in the bag I'm accidentally leaving behind. Nothing harmful -- not a bomb or anything -- but something that I was supposed to bring back home.' Kirsten stood, slipping out of the chair. She slides her sunglasses back on, favoring him with the same smile he'd seen for weeks while they lived together in Jason Ryan's warehouse. The same smile he almost certainly knew to be a lie. Paul looks in the bag and sees that most of his possessions are in there, things that were still at the Warehouse when it burned down. He looked up as Kirsten walks through the door of the restaurant. He looks back down into the bag and starts to show a dejected look on his face. 'It's not here.' As she pushed through the doorway of the restaurant, Kirsten Pendleton favors Paul Searle with that bright smile again and a cheery wave. ---Epilogue--- On the bed rests a beat up duffle bag, a suitcase with the letters "KP" embroidered on them, and the young man that seemingly still lacks purpose. The words from Michael Durant gave Paul Searle strength and hope. But the words of Kirsten Pendleton seemed to dash them once more. Paul never thought the day that he and a couple of high school buddies would drive from Republic to Cleveland would bring him into this world. Maybe he should have stayed home that night. After all, he was getting ready to head to Community College when Jason Ryan pulled him from the crowd. He lies there, rubbing the bruise that was still on his jaw. He didn’t know where he would go if he left this world. It was too late for him to start the fall semester. He doesn’t even know if his parents would let him back in. For Paul Searle, all he knows now is abandonment. Abandoning his parents’ dreams for him. Being abandoned by the man that pulled him into this world. He takes his phone from his pocket and sits up. He opens the flip and hits a button to find a number. As he finds it, he hold his phone up to his ear. "Hi, Mom. I just wanted to let you know that I was alright. I love you, and I miss you."
