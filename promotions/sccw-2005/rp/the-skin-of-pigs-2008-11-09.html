The Skin of Pigs

9 November 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/9/08 "GAH! Bullshit! Bullshit!" A fairly appropriate interjection, especially since the individual yelling it was almost hung in front of a live audience just one week ago. Yes, if the nylon strap of a pink-and-green fanny pack were tied around your neck and the opposite end were held by people hell-bent on keeping it taut, you’d become a fireball of emotional rage, too. Charley Crisp, above all things, is a relatively easygoing individual – almost to the point of lackadaisical. Certain things, however, send him into a fit that would make a Mayhem steroid rage seem like a box full of kittens. "I cannot BELIEVE this." Oh, to be in the size-four (not really) Velcro sneakers of Jaidan Bridden. To know that after her transgressions, Ms. Drusilla Devonshire shouldn’t dare think about sleeping without one eye open. If there were a camera filming this display, rest assured Roderick Ashe would be squeezing a boulder-sized loaf in his heart-covered undergarments. If he were watching it, that is. "I’m about to fly down there…turn the wreck shit knob…and butts are gonna be FOOTED with the fury of a Bruce Lee roundhouse." Thankfully, this uncontrollable anger is in no way directed toward said offending party. "This is what happens when you start a white boy at halfback." It is a sunny Sunday afternoon in downtown Boise, and Busters Sports Bar is running thin on weekend warriors. Normally, it is a sea of blue and off-orange, and the number 6 on the backs of fans is so abundant that you’d think Satan himself would pop in wearing an Eagles jersey at any second. Jay Cutlerism is the only religion in this joint, however, and with the exception of a handful of people, Busters was desolate due to the Thursday night game for Week 10. Charley is one of these in the handful, but that isn’t stopping him from acting like the atypical sports fan. His black Raiders hoodie was not a badge of pride this season, nor had it been for a while…but it did not stop him from wishing famine and strife on every member of the Carolina roster. An equally angry young woman sitting across from Cookie added plenty of fuel to the fire. "I swear, this keeps up and I’m gonna be a ‘Niner." The snug-fitting white tee featuring Taz spinning a tornado on a football field while clad in black and silver said otherwise. Anyone who had a Looney Tunes character representing their team emblazoned on a shirt meant they were in it for the long haul. "Or, you could get with the program and hop on the Bronco," her boyfriend smiled, tossing a jab on behalf of the local favorites. The brunette in the glasses sneered, sticking her finger in her mouth to induce vomiting. "It rather eat Bronco shit than cheer for Jay Fuckler." "I LOVE THIS GIRL," Charley yelped, doubling over in laughter. He was joined by the others at the table…the boyfriend, the quick-witted Raider girl, and his old friend. Leyla sat next to the man who once paid her to spank him with a studded paddle, enjoying the company of the Fro and their two new pals. Occasionally she would sneak glances at the flat screen in the near corner while the Pats laid one out on the Bills. Admittedly, her heart wasn’t into it a whole lot…as she fell into the 75% of women football fans category that wanted to run a slant route and receive a pass from (EXHUBERENT SIGH) Tom Brady. "That’s probably why I stood you up when we first met," Cookie joked when he saw her slip on the jersey that morning. Stacey and Jack, the couple across the table, were turning out to be a great find for the day’s festivities. Both seemed intrigued that they weren’t the only odd couple with rivaling sports allegiances, and meeting a fellow Raiders fan in Idaho was about as likely as a potato shortage in Boise. They ran a mom-and-pop coffee shop just down the street that Leyla had visited a couple of times. "Could’ve gone pro, though, if I hadn’t torn my ACL in high school," Jack remarked. It sounded casual enough, but Charley could tell that he enjoyed reliving his glory days through conversation with strangers. Cookie smiled and nodded politely while he rambled on about his five-touchdown game when his passing attack walloped their regional archrivals. He figured it appropriate not to mention his PAC-10 scholarship offers that he turned down to make spunk-inducing films for a living. But, who needs to lead the NFL in interceptions when you get paid to be powerbombed from thirty feet and hang out with a guy who dresses like a penis on cable television? Besides, there can be only one flamboyant dancing king in football…Crisp happily settled on being the Ocho Cinco of professional wrestling. "Three picks and you still can’t put the damn ball in the end zone," Charley gruffed, taking the last remaining swill of Magic Hat in his pint glass. "Who’s Denver playing next week?" He glanced up to Jack, shooting him a smile. * * * * * * * * "It’s nice, you know?" Leyla and Charley held hands as they walked the five blocks back to her loft at the edge of downtown. With her free hand, she pulled the hood of Charley’s sweatshirt over her head, betraying her team for the chill of the air. "What is?" Charley responded, focusing on the walk back and wishing he had opted for a long-sleeved shirt this morning. "To tell people I’m a student and it actually be the truth, instead of having to lie about how I make a living," she replied. Charley nodded. Two weeks ago, she broke the news to him that she was officially out of the business. While he certainly understood her reasons, he had experienced first-hand her "artistry" and felt that her potential was now flushed down the drain. With her Academy-award winning wickedness, he was sure he could probably get her a position in the Dead Man’s Hand – if she were really a cold-hearted snake with no soul or conscience, that is. That, and they both agreed that she should stay far away from the wrestling business as humanly possible…she’d kill a bitch before it was all said and done. "So I guess this means I won’t be tongue-bathing you tonight for a hundred?" Leyla laughed, punching Cookie on the arm. "If you really want to undergo slavery, my bathroom sure could use a good scrub-down." "I’d rather blow Tom Brady than clean your bathroom," Charley remarked. "Meeeeeee toooooo," she cooed, adding the obligatory exuberant sigh for love struck effect. They had made it back to her place without any more fellatio banter. The night found itself relaxed as they pounded can after can of malty alcoholic beverages, running through half of the second season of The Office. Lounging in pajamas decorated with teddy bears and a pink Baskin Robbins T-shirt, Charley didn’t seem to be too uncomfortable by the fact that Leyla was stripped to her panties and a sports bra. The television was eventually muted as drunken conversation escalated with each gulp of beer, growing more serious every minute. "I can’t STAND Siamese cats," Leyla meowed, a glare of disgust spreading across her face. "Tell me about it," Charley agreed. "My mom and sisters had three of ‘em in the house. Ugly and fuckin’ moany." He opted not to mention the tortures his sisters used to subject him to when he was young…something the animals played a large role in. In an attempt to avoid such a tale, he sighed with the intention of changing the subject. "How are the sand-shitters nowadays?" Leyla wondered. "Dead, thank Christ," Charley mulled. "I meant your sisters." An otherwise great one-liner garnished no chuckle from Cookie, not even a smile cracked. "Alive, unfortunately. I guess. Haven’t heard much from that crew since the trial." The trial. The other activity that took place just after Charley’s hiatus from the ring. A call from Cookie’s former brother-in-law had pushed the ball forward into reconnection with his family. As far as his role in the custody battle, John’s lawyer had assured him that he’d done well…although not well enough to convince such a conservative judge that a homosexual man who’d had an affair ought to be granted the right to see his daughter. Charley felt the saddest for the little girl who’s picture he still carried in his wallet, knowing that she – just like him – would grow up never knowing who her father was. As far as his brother-in-law was concerned, Charley made it a mental note to drop him an e-mail once every few months, maybe a Christmas card if he could remember. He certainly felt sorry for him and the bad taste that a Crisp had left in his mouth. Cookie often joked to the locker room that some of the shit he’d pulled on women probably turned them lesbian. Honestly, he didn’t know if that gene was inherited, because a woman like his sister could have easily been the prime motivator in John’s coming out. Amanda had approached him in the courthouse lobby after the judge called recess. The usually-frigid expression on her face was replaced by a sincere enough smile. Charley should have been smarter about accepting her invitation to walk outside, because a civil reunion could have just as easily taken place indoors. Unwavering, he gave her a hug and the siblings strolled through the glass doors to the marble stairs leading down to the yard. "Regardless of what your intent is, I’m sorry you were dragged into this," Amanda said, ruffled sleeves of her plum-colored dress slightly flapping from the light wind. Charley shrugged, taking a seat next to her on the stone bench. "Sorry I stuck my nose in where it probably doesn’t belong. But I hope you know where I’m coming from." "Actually," Amanda started, "I’m confused. Is this some sort of revenge therapy? I mean, we haven’t heard from you in seven years, and now suddenly I see you in a Wyoming courtroom on behalf of my ex-husband." Charley shot her a defensive glance. "You’ve got a phone, Manda. I know we aren’t exactly breaking bread on Thanksgiving anymore, but it would have been nice to know that I had a niece in the world." "Two nieces. And a nephew," his sister corrected, shedding a little more light on the mysteries of relatives he never knew existed. "Not surprising I didn’t know that either." His tone turned biting, an angry sarcasm as the disdain he held for his sisters slowly started to ignite again. Amanda took note of it…secretly enjoying every moment of pain she knew she was inflicting. Striking back, she dug her veins into another artery. "Did you think we believed you cared? After Mom’s funeral, were you around to pick up the pieces of our family? No. Deep down inside, Charley," she hissed, "you were glad Mom passed." Charley’s response was muted. His brow arched, and his mouth stood agape. This bitch hit harder than any accidental stiff shot from his comrades… "It was the opportunity you always wanted. With Mom gone, you had no other excuse to stick around. So in a way, it was a blessing to you." "You…you take that back," he stammered, barely able to catch his breath from the knockout punch. Amanda stood up. "I will not. Face it, Charley…the reason you aren’t part of my family, part of Harriet’s, part of Sarah’s, part of Marielle’s family…was because you were NEVER a part of ours." Charley shifted his gaze groundward. For almost twenty seconds, the silence lay heavy like the weight of a grand piano on their backs – both refusing to carry their share of the burden. "And that’s just the way you want it," Charley mumbled dryly, rising to his feet. He turned opposite, loosening the knot on his tie in order to acquire the oxygen needed to walk away from the gates of hell that stood beside him. "Give my everything to her. Send my love to the others. That includes you and the rest of our sisters." It was the last lie he’d told. * * * * * * * * * * He had been beaten, whipped, tread upon, and undergone the most immense brands of torture for the sake of both pleasure and pain. One thing was a testament to his will, though: Charley Crisp shed no tears. As much as the waterworks begged to leak from his eyes, he would never give in. He bottled it, corked the top, and threw it out to sea – even in the most intimate of settings such as this. Leyla and Charley embraced on the couch and this defeated boy had poured out his heart through the course of the evening without so much as a sob. He didn’t need to. She was crying enough for the both of them.
