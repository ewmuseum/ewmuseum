Success

11 January 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/1/2008 McKail stood by his motel room window, took a drag of his cigarette and smiled. The ferocity of the storm that had embroiled the whole area for the past few days or so was too beautiful for him to do otherwise. The motel room car park was flooded and the rain beat down so hard and with such vigour it reminded him of the time he and his late wife Mary visited Niagara Falls on their fifth wedding anniversary. He remembered the day clearly, hell, since she died he remembered every day he’d spent with her in perfect clarity. They’d stood there for hours and watched the natural beauty continue it’s time honoured routine; uncontrollable, unforgiving, ferocious. Now, each rain drop danced as it splashed down upon the ground, happily joining a collective of is peers and defying anything and everything else around them. Out of the diner across the way from the motel, McKail took another drag of his cigarette and watched on as the patrons of the establishment exited the building and made dashes to their transport, holding coats, magazines and newspaper up to cover themselves as if the water was corroding their skin. They cursed under their breaths, both their day and their mood ruined. McKail’s smiled widened. "So," she started, "are we going to talk or are you just going to stand there and grin like a simpleton for the rest of the day?" It was Caroline; McKail’s potential love interest and his late wife’s sister. Their relationship over the past few months had been rocky at best and through no fault of Caroline’s. She wanted commitment and McKail just couldn’t give to it her. At least not yet, not as long as he clung on to the memory of his late wife with every last breath in his body. It’d been over a year since she passed away and yet, McKail still hadn’t mourned her loss. He drank away his problems and pursued a perhaps foolhardy promise he’d made to her on her deathbed; the promise to fulfil his potential and dominate the wrestling industry. Perhaps the notion was entirely far-fetched, but he owed to her at least to try and much to Caroline’s chagrin, he found himself with little passion for anything else. "The latter," he responded, stubbing his finished cigarette into the motel ashtray resting upon the windowsill before him. He didn’t bother to turn to face her. And why should he? She’d stormed into his motel room and back into his life this morning, gung-ho had just about ready to cut his balls off without warning nor explanation. She’d calmed down some in the meantime, but not much. "Damn it, Jacob!" She cursed. "This is serious!" McKail shook his head. "What’s serious?" "Us." She told him. "Our relationship." McKail raised a speculative eyebrow. "It’s a serious relationship?" Caroline shook her head, frustrated. "That’s not what I mean…" "What do you mean?" He asked her. "That’s not what I meant," she informed. He nodded. "I know. But that’s what you said." "I meant this conversation about us is serious." Caroline brushed away her whisper of a fringe and rested her forehead in the palm of her hand. "Why didn’t you tell me?" McKail scrunched up his face, confused and finally wrenched himself away from the delightful image of pissed off people running for cover. Caroline was sat on his bed, her eyes close to welling with tears. "Why didn’t I tell you what?" "About that," she said, pointing toward the belt resting carefully upon the counter by the TV. It was the FUSE Gateway Title; his FUSE Gateway title. "Why didn’t you tell me about winning a championship?" He glared at it and then at her, but still he could make no connection between the two. "What?" "Oh let me guess," she began, standing from the bed, "because you haven’t seen me for a month. I invited you to be with me and my family over the holidays, Jacob and you didn’t even have the common courtesy to call me and tell me that you couldn’t make it!" At that, McKail remained silent and cast his gaze downward at his feet. Caroline might judge this to be guilt or shame, but it was far from it. This was McKail remembering something that he really wanted to forget, something he didn’t much want to think about, much less talk about. Just before Christmas, McKail found himself entangle within a drinking binge to end all drinking binges and by the time he couldn’t feel his face anymore, it was already too late. A fan approached him; young, female and utterly obsessed -- manically obsessed. She opened herself up to him, told McKail that she loved him and that they’d be happy together. What the hell was he supposed to do? He was drunk and all sense of tact had dispersed hours previously. He let her down and let her down hard. She didn’t react well. She committed suicide and it was all his fault. How the hell could he spend time with Caroline and her family after that? How was he supposed to maintain stability after that? He practically killed her. McKail grabbed a bottle of bourbon from the bedside table and took a mouthful. Caroline shot him a look of contempt, but at that point he didn’t much care. "I suppose that doesn’t matter to you, does it?" Caroline questioned, perhaps rhetorically? McKail didn’t much care. The alcohol was taking affect and right about now, that’s all that really mattered. "But this does." She grabbed a hold of the championship belt and struggled to hold it up. "You’ve told me it does. This is what you wanted, this is what you needed to begin to mourn Mary. This is what you needed to move on with your life." She threw it at him the best she could, but it fell short. "Success. That’s the one thing you’ve been gearing yourself toward since her death and why? Because you promised her it. Well now you have it and--" "No," he interrupted, "I don’t." "But--" "This isn’t success." McKail bent over and picked the belt up off the floor. He stared at it for the longest time, before resting it beside him on the windowsill, just by the ashtray. "This is just a title. There’s a difference." Caroline shook her head and made for the door. "I can’t believe you!" McKail nodded. "Neither can I." She left and McKail didn’t follow her; he was busy trying to forget.
