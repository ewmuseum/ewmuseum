What If We Could Have Nice Things

Date: Various, Alternate Timeline Massachusetts General Hospital Boston, Massachusetts November 28, 2008 He'd made up his mind; it was time to get out. It was not the first time he'd questioned whether or not to leave the business, nor was it the first time he had the thought sitting in a hospital, but in the early November morning he'd decided that this time it was just too big to ignore. Hospitals made Jared uncomfortable, dating back to his childhood. The echo of footsteps on tiled floors, the white walls with pale seafoam trim, the nutty stench of antiseptic spray, the tangled webs of tubes and wires; it made the experience surreal, like walking from one dream into another. This is where people go to die, he thought, his gaze drifting listlessly from person to person, each wearing the same nauseating green seen on the walls. "Complications," the doctors had told him, and what followed was a dumbed-down version of the diagnosis, explained in terms they'd hoped a layman like Jared could understand. He'd silently nodded along, trying to take it all in, to wrap his mind around the words, but found it hard to move beyond the thought of something gone horribly wrong. This was the problem with an overactive imagination like his: with the right seed, it would spiral out of control, conjuring unbelievable scenarios both magnificent and terrible. His best friend, the person he was closest to in the world, was lying in a bed on this floor and he wasn't allowed in, not yet. Jared checked his watch, a hybrid Nike sweatband, and the clock read 4:17am. He leaned his head against the wall, slouching deeper into his seat. It wasn't comfortable, but the constant fidgeting and self-adjustment had helped keep him awake. The music had helped, too, at first, though after ten hours of listening to the same looped play list the sounds ran together, and tracks became indiscernible. He looked nothing short of a train wreck, wearing the same pair of jeans and World of Warcraft tee shirt that he'd worn the last two days. His unkempt hair was held in check by a gray and black knit hat, which he'd simply been too lazy to remove. He glanced at his watch again. 4:17am. For a moment he entertained the idea that time itself was taunting him. Footsteps in the corridor outside the waiting room pulled at his attention, and he turned his head in the hopes of seeing something, anything, to break up the monotony of sitting in solitude. "Wow, I'm honestly surprised that you're still awake." Mervin Humperdink stood at the entrance to the waiting area, a large Dunkin Donuts cup in each hand. Jared's laugh was weak, the result of extreme fatigue. He pulled the orange buds from his ears, turned off his iPod, and pulled himself up in his chair. "You hear anything yet?" Mervin continued, offering one of the cups to his friend. "Nothin' new," Jared responded, taking the beverage. "What do I owe ya?" "Please, it was, like, two bucks. Don't worry about it." "Thanks." The Black Sheep yawned, putting his coffee down for a moment and pressing his eyes with both palms, trying to wipe the sleep away. "This is fucking killing me. I didn't expect things to drag on so long. And it's like, there's nothing I can do, you know? Fucking kills me." There was no strength left in his voice, having reached the point of emotional exhaustion hours ago. "Her parents still here? I didn't see 'em when I came in." "Nah, her mom was pretty wiped-out. They went back to the house. I told 'em I'd call as soon as I heard something, or if they'd let us in to visit." "Darren gave me the same responsibility. I'm supposed to let him know once it's okay to visit. Also wants me to say that if there's anything you need that he's around to help." "Cool." For a moment there was silence, until Mervin broke it. "It’ll be okay, dude. She’s going to be okay." Jared rolled the tungsten ring between the side of his little finger and thumb, turning it slowly, feeling the cool weight against his skin. It had taken him a while to get used to the heft of the metal, but now, little more than a year after he first put it on, it felt natural. His line of work necessitated that he remove it from time to time, and in those instances he noted his hand became lighter, though at the cost of a part of himself. Sometimes he'd take it off just to sneak a glance at the inscription on the inside: Jared + Justine 10-27-07. The inscription had been a gift from her, a surprise revealed on their wedding day. He was unashamed to admit that he'd cried openly upon seeing it for the first time. If he was any less tired he might have cried now. "They’re both going to be okay, Jared." *-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-* The attending physician had stopped by a few times during the night to check in, but it was close to seven hours later by the time Jared got the news he had been waiting for. His wife, Justine, was awake and in recovery. The "complications" had been corrected by means that Jared would never be able to recount, and he was allowed to see her if he wanted. Mervin, who had fallen asleep not long after arriving with coffee, had been sent home to get some real sleep, and to pass along the new to Darren. Jared briefly stepped outside, so as not break the standard "no cell phones" rules most hospitals employed, and passed along the news to Mr. and Mrs. Calvin. Stepping into the room was like seeing her for the first time, and a swarm of butterflies took flight in his stomach. "Hey," he said, sliding his thumbs into his pockets and stepping over to the bed. "Hey, yourself," she said, her voice soft and sedate. It was all he could do not to throw himself at her, to take her in his arms and promise not to let go. Self-control was never his strongest suit, but he employed it now, if only because he had no alternative. She was still too frail, the stress of childbirth and the resulting problems weighing heavy on her emotionally and physically. He cautiously stepped to the side of the bed, and tenderly kissed her forehead. "You look like shit," she whispered, and for the first time in days his laugh was genuine. "Oh, yeah, you’re the friggin’ portrait of hot right now," tired as he was, the part of his brain responsible for playful sarcasm was still engaged. "Mm, we both know I’m still the pretty one." Amidst the mess of blankets and tubes his hand found hers, and for a moment everything was all right again. "How’s Charlie?" she asked, tightening her grip on him. "He’s okay. He’s a tough little kid." 47 hours ago Charles Walter Sykes had come into this world, a tiny little thing of five pounds and eleven ounces. He was 5 weeks premature, the result of an infection in his mother. She closed her eyes and smiled. "He must get that from me. God knows his dad’s a pussy." Conversations like this were not uncommon in the Sykes house. As quick as he was on his feet, she could match him. He rarely admitted it, but Jared actually thought her the quicker. "They’ve got him in one of those giant egg-warmer things." "Incubator, love. The word is ‘incubator’." "I prefer egg-warmer thing. Sounds less medicine-y." She shook her head, gently patting his. "He’s beautiful," he continued, removing his hat and running his free hand through his hair, "I think because he looks like you." "When can I see him?" she asked, blinking away a tear. "I want to see my baby." "Soon enough, I think. He’s still so little, and you still need to recover a bit, but I promise you that as soon as he can leave his incu-egg-thing I’ll bring him to you myself." Justine simply nodded. "I called your parents, so they should be here soon. I sent them home at two this morning. Your mom was half-asleep in the waiting room." "What about you?" "What about me?" "When was the last time you got any sleep?" "About, oh," he glanced at his watch, "day and a half ago." "Jared," she scolded, "that’s not healthy." "I had to make sure you were okay, Cal’. Wasn’t going to nap until they let me see you. Even if I wanted to, I couldn’t." "You should go home, you should rest." "Maybe when your parents get here. Until then I’m not going anywhere." *-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-*-* Trenton, New Jersey March 15, 1999 "You can do this. All you have to say is ‘Hello’, and the rest gets easier." He wasn’t sure why he was taking the advice of Gabriel Simon, a man who until 20 minutes ago had been making Jared’s life miserable just by living, but it didn’t matter. Jared already had the phone’s received in his right hand, his left suspended in space over the keys. "You sure about this?" "Totally. What’s the worst that could happen?" "I look like a complete asshole." "And that’s different from any other day how?" Jared glared across the table at his traveling companion, trying to wish Gabriel into spontaneous combustion. Gabriel took the hint. "Alright, alright, I’m sorry." Jared sighed. He did his best to steady his nerves, and started dialing. With the receiver at his ear he did his best to keep himself calm while he waited for her to pick up. "Anything?" came the whisper from across the table. "It’s ringing," Jared whispered back. "I don’t know if she going to… oh!" From the sudden change in expression on Jared’s face, Gabriel could tell that someone had picked up on the other end of the phone. With Jared in shock, Gabriel immediately jumped into the role of coach, a Cyrano to Jared’s Christian. "Say ‘Hello’," he mouthed, trying to stifle a laugh. "Hi, C-Cal’? It’s, uh, it’s Jared. Did I wake you?" A quick thumbs up from The Black Sheep gave the affirmative that she’d picked up. "Yeah, no it was pretty rough," he continued into the phone, "but not nearly as bad as it must’ve looked on television. Sorry I wasn’t around to help you move. Everything go okay?" A pause. "Yeah, that’s cool. Hey, um, I know It’s kinda late, but do you have a minute?" Another pause, and Jared shot a quick nod at Gabriel. It was time for the show to begin. "So, there’s something I wanted to talk to you about…"
