Date: 8/25/08 July 4th, 1974 William Marshall sat stiff-backed in the uncomfortable chair, his eyes making another quick scan across the hospital waiting room. A cup of coffee sat on the table to his side, its contents long since cooled. Will doubted he’d be able to drink it. Hell, the way his stomach was churning right now, he doubted he’d be able to keep anything down. Boot camp, combat, having a gun pointed at his face…all of that he’d handled with a calm demeanor and a steely expression. But have his wife just a few hundred feet away, giving birth to their latest child and suddenly it was like the night that he had proposed all over again. Hell, the way he felt right now, it was close to the night he’d first asked Anna out…all butterflies and stomach flips. Will’s oldest, Steven, sat in a chair a few seats down, idly flicking through an issue of NEWSWEEK that had to be at least six months out of date. Will had been clear that none of the children had needed to accompany him to the hospital (indeed, Jim was at home looking after Adam and Charles) but Steven had insisted, making a point of coming home from Princeton a week early just so he could be there for his parents. Besides, he’d added when they’d last spoken on the phone, there was no way he could have his new baby brother or sister being born without him being there, now, could he? It had been at least three hours since Anna had been rushed into the delivery room, hurried declarations of love and support making way to doctors insisting that Will had to stay outside while his wife was giving birth. Since then, there had been a grand total of two updates, both from harried nurses who had hurriedly informed him that "Mrs. Marshall was doing just fine" before hurrying off to attend to their other duties. Will didn’t blame them. Hospitals were incredibly busy places, even more so on nights like tonight. But part of him desperately wished that, just for a minute, someone would come in and explain exactly what was going on. In the distance, Will could hear the sound of fireworks exploding into the sky. Bright flashes of color briefly shot across the hospital windows before disappearing again. "Looks like we’re missing the show this year, huh, Pop?" said Steven, glancing over at his father. William looked over at his son, repressing the urge to laugh. There were enough fireworks going on inside this hospital to suit him just fine. "We don’t know how much longer we’re going to be waiting, son. You can go on outside and take a look at the fireworks if you’d like." Steven just smiled, that grin full of charm and mischief that had gotten him into many a scrape over the years…and gotten him out of just as many. "C’mon, pop, I told you. I was gonna stay here with you until this was all over. Besides, I gotta see if we continue the Marshall family tradition. It’ll be cool to have another little brother to pass on all my wisdom to." This time, Will allowed himself a chuckle. "G-d forbid," he said with a smile. As if on cue, a set of double doors at the end of the hall banged open and a doctor made his way briskly down the hall. He stood a few inches shorter than Will, his face lined and his eyes heavy lidded. He made his way down to the waiting room, peering at the crowd assembled inside and asking "Mr. Marshall?" Will stood up, instinct and years of training rocketing him upwards until he stood ramrod straight. "Yes, that’s me." The doctor made his way over, offering his hand which Will shook firmly but briefly. "I’m Doctor Montgomery. I’m your wife’s obstetrician." Will nodded. He’d heard Doctor Montgomery’s name mentioned in passing but had never actually met the man before…the last doctor he remembered was Doctor Wallace who, he remembered Anna telling him, had passed on due to a heart attack two years ago. "How’s my wife, Doctor?" "Both mother and baby are doing just fine, Mister Marshall. I do hope you’ve done up the nursery in blue, though." Will felt an enormous wave of relief crashing over him. Anna was fine. She was well and fine and everything had gone okay. And it was a boy. Another boy. Will felt Steven clap him on the shoulder, could hear the laugh behind his words. "What did I tell you, pop? The tradition continues!" Will said nothing in return, instead greeting his son with an embrace which Steven joyfully returned. After a few moments, Doctor Montgomery broke the moment by loudly clearing his throat. "Would you like to see your wife, sir?" "Yes, please." Doctor Montgomery led Will and Steven down the corridor leading to the delivery room, holding the door open for them as they made their way inside. At the center of the room, still covered by a blanket, lay Anna, looking as beautiful as Will could ever remember having seen her. Cradled in her arms, swaddled in a small blue blanket, lay the now youngest member of the Marshall family. The child looked up at Will and smiled, reaching his hand out to him as though he instinctively knew exactly who Will was. Will let the child take hold of one of his fingers, marveling at the strength in the child’s grip. "Hello, little one. Welcome to the world." Steven peered over his mother’s shoulder, taking in the sight of his new born baby brother. "Heya, runt." "The family tradition," Anna sighed. Will simply nodded. "I’m your big brother," Steve continued as though his father hadn’t said anything. "You and me, we’re gonna be the best of buds." Doctor Montgomery piped up with "Have the two of you decided on a name?" Will and Anna looked at each other and nodded. Will then turned back to face Doctor Montgomery. "We had decided that if it was another boy…we would name him Lance." September 16th, 1988 "I don’t need your help, runt!" Class had been in session at Bryant High for several weeks now, football practice nearly as long. Coach Baldwin had already moved the team to two-a-days, testing each of the players out, assessing their strength level and seeing where each of them shined. This was the day he’d promised to announce the starting lineup. Most everyone on the team assumed that, once again, the Marshall boys would be making their mark on the Bryant High football team with Charlie named as starting quarterback and his younger brother Lance (now looking bigger than some of the seniors on the squad) managing the miracle of making starting offensive line in his freshman year. Obvious to everyone, that was, except Jason Moretti. Moretti was new to Bryant High, having transferred in from rival school Lombardi High. At Lombardi, Jason had been the undisputed star of the football team, helping Lombardi to smash everyone in their path…except for Bryant, Lombardi’s one loss that entire season. Jason’s father, no longer content with having his son be a big fish in a small pond, had decided that his son needed to be where the winners were and Jason, convinced of his own greatness, had been equally convinced that the Bryant High team would welcome him with open arms, beg him to lead them to victory. That hadn’t happened. But, in spite of everything, Jason had remained convinced that he would snag the starting quarterback spot…until this very moment when, in what came as a surprise to virtually no one, Charles Marshall was named as the starting quarterback of the Bryant High football team for 1988. And that had gone over…well, about as "well" as could have been expected. "What!" Jason exploded, now that Coach Baldwin was safely out of earshot. "This is bullshit! What the hell does Marshall have that I don’t?" Perhaps unwisely, Charlie Marshall snapped back "Talent, Moretti." In what would prove to be one of his defining characteristics, Jason waited until Charles’s back was turned before launching himself at Charles, tackling him down to the ground before raining wild, uncoordinated punches onto his head. "You motherfucker! You don’t fucking talk to me like that…" The rest of the team just stood, stunned, not knowing what to do. Everyone, that is, except for Charles’s younger brother. "Charlie!" Lance pounded across the room, yanking Jason off of his brother with one fluid move. If the punches he was throwing at Charles hadn’t clued him, the wild haymaker that Jason threw at Lance was proof that Jason was running purely on adrenaline and anger. Lance ducked the punch and snapped back with one of his own, his right fist crashing into the side of Jason’s face just as his coach had taught him. The effect was immediate. Jason crumpled to the floor, his eyes rolling backwards in his head as he collapsed unconscious. The team cheered as Moretti hit the floor, several of them slapping Lance on the back in congratulations. Everyone seemed thrilled that Moretti had finally gotten what was coming to him. "Charlie, you okay?" Everyone except for Charles Marshall. "I don’t need your help, runt!" Lance stopped, frozen to the spot. The look on Charlie’s face…he was mad at him. He’d only been trying to help him out and he was mad at him?! And calling him that name…in front of everybody… Lance’s mind raced. He could feel the anger boiling up inside him, feel the bile start to rise in the back of his throat. He tried to remember what his father had taught him, about the importance of keeping your anger under control. But right now, none of that seemed to matter. "Fuck you, Charlie! To hell with you!" And with that, Lance turned his back on his brother (ignorant of the shocked expression on his brother’s face), hurriedly changed out of his uniform and stomped his way out of the locker room. He was vaguely aware of people trying to catch his attention, calling to him, waving at him. He ignored them, his gaze set determinedly on the road to home. It didn’t matter that home was about eight miles away or that he would normally be relying on Charlie for a ride home. Right now, as far as Lance was concerned, Charlie could just go fuck himself. It took Lance over an hour to reach his house during which he ran the incident over and over in his mind, convinced that Charlie (who was home by now, for sure) was telling his parents his side of the story, painting himself as the victim. Lance could feel his stomach churning, sure that Charlie was telling pop how humiliated he was about his little brother coming to his rescue… Dinner had passed in silence, neither Charlie nor Lance speaking to each other and their parents exchanging frequent knowing glances. It was only an hour after dinner, when Lance had gone down to the basement to take out his frustrations on the heavy bag there, that his father came down after him. "One punch, huh?" asked Will, watching as his son pummeled the heavy bag. Lance stopped, his breathing slightly heavy, his body with a sheen of sweat. He simply nodded at his father. "Your old coach would have been proud." Lance looked at his father in surprise. "Aren’t you going to tell me off?" "What for? Your brother already told me what happened." "Great. Probably tried to make me out to be the villain…" Lance began pummeling the heavy bag again. He had barely started when his father interjected. "He apologized to me for being such a jackass to you. I told him I wasn’t the one he needed to apologize to." Lance stopped again, hanging on to the bag for support. "Really?" "And he will apologize," answered his father, "when he’s ready to. But he knows you were just trying to help him…and he knows not to let his pride get in the way anymore." Lance walked over to his father, enveloping him in a hug which his father warmly returned. "Thanks, Dad." July 15th, 1992 The sun felt good against Lance’s skin as he lay on the deck chair, the waves lapping against the shore in the distance. The trip to Hawaii had been pop’s idea, one last grand family adventure before Charlie graduated and Lance went off to the Point. Lance still couldn’t believe it when he thought about it. Almost nobody got into the Point…and he’d made it. Pop had been thrilled and Mom had been proud, like she always had. Mom, Pop and Charlie were off on a sightseeing tour, driving off to some volcano Lance couldn’t quite remember the name of. He’d expected more of an argument when he asked to just stay behind and take in some sun but, surprisingly, his father had been very cool about it. After all, he’d argued, Lance was looking at nothing but hard work for the next few years so he was entitled to take it easy if he wanted. Which is why this particular bright, sunny day in Honolulu saw Lance taking in the sun and taking in the girls in equal measure. Hawaii seemed to have more than its fair share of pretty women, most of whom were dressed in swimsuits which left nothing to the imagination. One pair in particular had caught his eye. The blond was cute, sure, but her friend…she was wearing a skimpy little bikini which hung to her body like a second skin, showing off her body to its full advantage. Unlike many of the girls he’d seen on the beach, the blonde’s friend wasn’t trying to look like she had a cup size greater than nature intended or was skinny to the point of eating disorder…she simply prowled across the sand gracefully, her toned body glistening in the heat of the sun. Lance was more than familiar with what lust was but the way he felt when looking at this girl…this was different. This was more than just the desire to get in her pants (although he certainly wouldn’t mind that)…simple fascination or desire wouldn’t account for how she continued to hold his attention, why he continued to take every opportunity he could to memorize every curve of her skin. Wait, had she seen him? Roxy looked over at Lani, slowly applying suntan lotion to her legs. "Girl, that haole back there has got it bad for you. He’s been scoping you out over since we got here. Trying to be subtle but…hell, no." Alanna looked over at her friend in confusion. She hadn’t been aware of anyone checking her out. "Where?" "Deck chair, ‘bout 50 feet back, wearing blue trunks." Alanna glanced casually over her shoulder, catching just the barest glimpse of the boy Roxy had pointed out. What she could see looked promising but… "So?" "Cute, built like a brick shithouse and that okole…damn, girl, we’re talking grade-A." Alanna turned her head forward, a smile creeping across her face. She’d usually been the one to initiate the first move and this time, she thought, it would be no different. Besides, it might be fun to have a little fling. "Hand me the suntan lotion." Roxy looked over at Alanna, one eyebrow raised. "You are not…?" Alanna smiled. "Yup." "Aznuts, girl." It probably was crazy, thought Alanna, as she walked over to the chair Roxy had pointed at. But what fun was life if you couldn’t get a little crazy sometimes? Lance shuddered as he felt the cool liquid drizzle against his back, quickly replaced by the warm heat of someone expertly rubbing the lotion into the muscles of his back. "You’re a big boy," purred a voice from behind him. "In every way that counts," Lance replied. He quickly bit his lip to keep from moaning at the stranger’s expert touch. Alanna smiled. Confidence, huh? She liked that. "So tell me, what brings a big boy like you down to the islands?" "I’m here on vacation. And it was my birthday a few weeks ago." The voice now leaned in closer, purring right in Lance’s left ear. "A birthday boy, eh? Well, it’s time I introduced you to a local tradition. See, down here, you turn a year, you have to get your birthday dirty lickin’s." Lance had no idea what this mystery woman was saying. But the idea to come to Hawaii was looking better and better by the second.
