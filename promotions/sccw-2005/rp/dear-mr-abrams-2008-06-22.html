Dear Mr. Abrams

22 June 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/22/08 '...well, to be quite honest, I’m not surprised that J.J. Abrams isn’t returning your phone calls.' Lawrence Spiderman, perhaps the world’s most forlorn personal agent, sat at his desk, the telephone receiver held in one hand, his head cradled in the other. This particular call had been going on for close to twenty minutes, which by comparison was much shorter than the others just like it that he had been fielding all week. 'No, no, I’m sure it’s not because he hates the idea.' To most people the lie would probably be unconvincing, but King Blueberry was a special breed of eccentric. So far, he seemed to be buying into it. 'It’s just, you know, with Star Trek in production and more seasons of Lost to worry about.' He paused. 'I know that there’s a sequel in the works, but I don’t think that giant-sized Dean Malenko is really the approach that they’re going for, and technically speaking the Statue of Liberty doesn’t have legs. You can’t leglock a monument.' He paused again, the frustration becoming more evident on his face. 'Okay, so how would you ret-con that?' Spiderman could do nothing but shake his head and sigh. Such are the woes of trying to reason with a psychopath. 'A cease-and-desist? Not yet, why?' The answer surprised him. In one motion he violently threw himself back in his chair and facepalmed. 'No, I don’t think he’s going to send the black smoke after you.' Spiderman stood from his desk. With his free hand now rested on his hip he began pacing by the window, perhaps wondering if the three-story drop to the ground below would be enough to end the pain. 'So... wait... no, hold on a minute... Abraham Lincoln with nunchuks?' Pacing the room, Spiderman now moved closer to his desk, once again taking a seat in his chair. 'No, I do not have the number for WETA Workshop!' A few hundred miles removed from the frustrated Mr. Spiderman, King Blueberry sat hunched down over a kitchen table, scribbling furiously into a notebook. Even in what was apparently his own home he felt compelled to wear his mask. 'Hey, Mer’... umm... Dragon?' he called to the other room, 'would Cybernetic Abraham Lincoln be able to tell a lie?' The masked face of Blueberry Dragon peeked through the doorway. 'So are we not using real names anymore?' he replied, 'Don’t you think we’re taking this a little too far?' 'Nope. This joke may only be funny to about 5 or 6 people, but that sure as shit’s not gonna stop me from running it into the ground, killing it, and then juggling flaming kittens on its grave. Anyway, that’s not the point. I need to figure out whether Cybernetic Abraham Lincoln would be able to tell a lie.' Blueberry Dragon stepped into the kitchen fully, removed a bottle of Moutain Dew from the refrigerator, and took a seat at the table across from the King. 'What kind of lying would a robotic...' 'Cybernetic.' '...whatever... version of Abraham Lincoln need to do?' 'Well, assume he’s surrounded by a group of Communist Ninjas, or, even better, Instant Ninjas, and the leader of the Instant Black Hand of Death approaches him all stealth-like and asks outright, ‘Do you have any nunchuks?’. Would a Cybernetic Abraham Lincoln be able to say no?' Dragon unscrewed the cap from his bottle and took a slow sip. He’d known the King for well over 15 years, and they had been working together for at least the last 10. There was no denying that, while King was anything but dumb, it was Dragon who was the most logical and level-headed. Still he found himself dragged into conversations like this from time to time. Not getting an answer quick enough, the King went right back to his point. 'Because, like, regular Abraham Lincoln was all honest and shit, so you would expect that a cyber-Lincoln would also have that trait, but because he’s got computer parts I suppose you could program him to do whatever you wanted, right?' 'You know, it’s days like this when I am truly convinced that we should have committed you years ago.' 'I’m serious here.' 'That’s the scary part.' 'If we programmed Abe Lincoln’s techno-brain to be able to lie, but the human part of his brain wanted to prevent him from telling a lie, then his circuits might overload and make him go kaboom. I don’t want my presi-bot to explode when it’s getting ready to whip up on four score and seven asses. If we’re going to build this thing...' 'Wait.' Dragon put his hands up and leaned back in his seat, clearly unprepared for what he just heard. 'We’re going to do what?'
