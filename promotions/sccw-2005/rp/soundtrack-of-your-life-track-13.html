Soundtrack of your life: Track 13

Date: Today Flatliners "Eulogy" Sweat stings my eyes and I can feel the bruises forming as I make my way through that curtain. The elegantly combined gold and leather weigh heavy on my shoulder, but you still weigh heavy on my mind. The screaming of thousands serves only to shock me for the briefest of moments out of this depression. Just a few feet past the gorilla position and it is fleeting fast. Readjusting my eyes to the fainter light I can squint to see a few friendly faces eager to pat my back. They offer words of congratulations and good riddance to the title reign of the Dead Man Hand’s Ace. I hardly picked up the panic in James’ grin, but he made sure I knew. "Hey good job getting the one title," he softened me up, "but why the hell’d you leave without even trying to take the Gateway?" It was a bluntness reserved for allies. I was not quick to respond because, to tell you the truth, I didn’t even know it was for the taking. When I did part my lips to reason away my actions with this exhaustion he had lost patience in getting the response he would be happy with. "Know what, it doesn’t matter. You got the one, and that’s what counts." He comes to wrap an arm over my shoulder and help me back to whatever little slice of the arena we have called a locker room this week. It was with weary feet that we made our way, only to be halted by the last face I cared to see. Maybe if I got a little more rest before speaking with Dr. Kensington the venom I spat would be a touch less toxic. "Here to tell me that it was all a gag again? Send me back to the unemployment line!?" "Mitch…" Jimmy says softly, a plea to keep my temper under wraps. "Not at all, Mister Quinlan. I was here to offer congratulations on a hard fought victory and wish you luck in the defence of the title. Miss St. Germaine wished me to inform you that your first chance to do so will be in Tokyo." "I’m not sure if you what it is you are going to regret more Doc: Letting me go the first time, or given me my rightful shot." I cleared my throat and spat to my side. "’Cause I am never letting go of this." Satisfied that he had run his errand, the shadow of a man that once proudly ran this organization skirted away, back to the lap of the monetary savour of Sin City. I think you would hate her too. When we did walk into the room I had given up. I crashed to the floor and rolled to my side. This ground was good enough. -=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=- And the darkness parted with my eyes. A vision of another random ceiling with cigarette stains from a time when the smokers where not so marginalized. In this business where travel is a prerequisite you never do quite remember where it is that you are waking up. Being dumped on your head a few times each show sure doesn’t help either. But slowly everything comes back to you. My first real hint of any timeline was propping myself up to see the early morning sunlight shimmering off the LiveWire title. The cheap flowery wallpaper is not exclusive to Hawaii, but indeed I was still on the Pacific Isles. Another day relaxing poolside might be just the thing to heal this broken body, but it is Tuesday isn’t it? My flight was scheduled to set sail in the mid-afternoon so seeing the sun set over the waves was out of the cards. But then it all comes back to me; the nightmare that this life has been ever since you have left my life. Memories of blood and the smell of gunpowder scare me. Just hold on. Why couldn’t you just hold on? Into the washroom to splash water over my face. I don’t look very much like I did. Is innocence found in the face? Certainly no long in mine. The depression grips my heart and head and tells me that breakfast isn’t what I really need. Just a few more hours of sleep, lest I be forced into conversation with some young woman that reminds me of you. -=-=--=-=-=-=-=--=-=- A copper key jiggled itself into the lock and I turned to open the door. I stood on the other side of the threshold for a good moment and gathered a few more breaths. The staleness of a house not lived in in a few weeks wafted through these nostrils. With enough courage I entered my home for the last time. I laid down the suitcase, leaning it against the wall and take a walk into the kitchen to draw a glass of water. I stood over the sink bringing that cool, refreshing liquid over my lips and looked out the kitchen window. It was that very window that not so long ago I looked out as if my entire world existed only in its gaze. My world was so small, and I was content with it. The current view offered only to peer at cold houses whose residence I no longer knew. On the table still lay the photo album. It was my baby album, full of embarrassing and nostalgic snapshots. A very rare sight you got to see. I understand the giggle now. I opened it to the one of the newborn in my mother’s arms. Did that little person ask for life? My travel bag was in the basement, a sorely missed set of items this past week, made a little hard to get when the entire place was marked a crime scene. So I descended the creaky steps and turned into the rec room. There it was sitting on that casino carpet, ugly as sin. Now what I wanted to be thinking about was how it was on that carpet that I walked my first steps to my long passed grandfather, but all I could remember was standing here realising a beast was still in the house with me. In all I spent maybe five minutes in that house. I doubt I could take much more. In the trunk of my Malibu was all I needed to survive on the road, and really all I wanted to see right now. I was just about into the driver side when my cell phone buzzed. "Hello?" "Mitchell, where have you been? I have been trying to get a hold of you for a week now," the words of a concerned big brother. "Hey Tim, I was out of the country, remember? Crappy little thing doesn’t give me service in Hawaii," if only that was all. "Right, right. Are you sure nothing else has happened?" Had our incident hit the press and he was just too curt to cut to it? I am not embarrassed by you, but I still was in now state of mind to begin to talk about it. "Actually Tim, I got to ask you to do something for me. In the house, anything you want… take it. I am moving out and know that you still got some memories in that place too. Just… don’t ask why." And what followed was my thumb pressing end and another buzzing phone that I decided did not need to be answered. I was done with this place and the child that grew up in it. One more thing was nagging at me; a burden I sought to unload. -=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=- You were in Windsor, and that’s were I headed. As I pulled into the Becket Glaves Funeral Home my skin crawled in the present of Death. I was never good with dealing with this sort of thing. I hope you appreciate how much of an effort it was put into this. An elderly man pulled open the door for me and lead me in. The walls were covered with a hopeless attempt to add cheer to this place of misery and condolences. Down the hall and to the right, and I really wanted to bail. By all apparent signs you were a very popular girl. The line extended at least 40 people and it wasn’t long before I was sandwiched in front of more people come to say goodbye. That line was a long time to think about exactly was I was doing. I knew that it might not be the right person to deliver the message. Everything told me that this could wait, except for some voice telling me to stick with it. Was it you? Five minutes and two mints later I was in the on deck position and my hands got clammy with sweat. Two grieving parents stood before me. Closest to me was your mother, and a spitting image of you. "I… I just have to tell you. The last thing she told me was that she loved you," I struggled to get out of my throat. I fidgeted with the suit tie waiting for some response. "I am so sorry," I offered. The only answer I got was a fresh stream of tears running makeup down an already sad face. This was not what I wanted. "I am so sorry," I repeated. "You have some nerve showing up here," your father, a thick man greeted me. "I just," I started. "You just, what? Had to come here to see the family you have devastated? You are lucky I don’t punch your teeth down your damn throat right now," he snorted out each word. I could tell that was no hyperbole when I saw the red face behind a grey moustache. "Just get out of here." "I… I just wanted to offer a few prayers … if I could." "I said get out of here," he said with each word a little less quiet and refined. I didn’t even get to see the casket, but I suppose it was for the best. How was it that with all these people that love you, you had to seek out affection from someone you didn’t even know? You were loved. You are missed. -=-=-=-=-=-=-=- The sun was setting and the people had long since scattered away. Under a violet sky and with six feet of dirt piled overtop of your corporal remains. I strolled along the column of stones and monuments until I happened upon it. With one knee on the ground I ran my hands over the smooth stone and bevelled text. My God child, you were only 19. "Our angel" it read. I wonder if it was me on the other side of this would you come to visit? A single white rose was placed on top of the headstone and I was back to my feet. The only thing left to say is the one thing I refused to say when you were alive. "Goodbye." [End of disc]
