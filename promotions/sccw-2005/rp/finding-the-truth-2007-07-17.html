Finding The Truth

17 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/17/2007 A realisation had swept over El Diablo, and he was going to find out the truth. For his memory showed him something that he hadn’t even thought of for years, his time in the Happy Hills Mental Institution. It wasn’t a recollection of his usual routine of a day there, but of being strapped down to a bed and repeatedly injected with something. Simply put, El Diablo had to know what happened to him. SUNDAY 15TH JULY Packing a rucksack, El Diablo was fretting as he placed random t-shirts, boxer shorts and a pair of jeans in there. His wife Angela was sat on a black leather chair, adjacent to where Diablo was packing, watching his every move, noticing every little nuance which worried him. "Where are you going?" Angela gently asked him. Angela stood up, her long red hair flowing slightly behind her, and walked across the cream coloured carpet towards her husband. "Honey, where are you going?" she asked him once more. Dropping the black rucksack down to floor, he turned around to face her, looking like a man with the weight of the world on his shoulders. "I have to go somewhere tomorrow and I don’t know when I’ll be back," he replied. Diablo pushed the brown wooden drawer of the cabinet shut with the sole of his right foot. "Why? What is it?" she asked him. Looking at his beautiful wife, standing in front of him, wearing black denim jeans and a plain white t-shirt, El Diablo knew he couldn’t tell her the truth about where he was going, because she would try to stop him. "I’m finding an old friend I lost contact with," Diablo responded. Touching his left arm, Angela tried to reassure him that everything would be okay. "I know you’re frustrated about losing again, but just leaving isn’t going to help," Angela told him. Gently pushing her hand off of his arm, Diablo shook his head. "I’ll be back for my match with Fred Fusion. But it has nothing to do with losing, I need to find this friend of mine," he lied to her. Wearing a black t-shirt and black sweatpants, El Diablo was determined not to let her know the truth. Giving her a tell, he looked away from her and stared intently at the white wallpaper around the silver television. "It’s just very important I find him," he finished. Knowing he was lying, Angela conjured up a little plan. "Okay, so when are we going?" she plotted. Diablo’s eyes darted away from the wallpaper and directly onto his wife as he tried to think of ways to let her know she can’t go with him. "I haven’t seen him in years, he doesn’t even know I’m married. It’d be best if I went by myself," he continued to lie to her. Realising she wasn’t going to get her away, Angela gave up the pretence of wanting to go and walked back to the leather chair to take a seat again. "Okay dear, have fun," she told him. Relieved at escaping with his lie, Diablo picked up the fully packed rucksack. He looked lovingly at his wife as he had a crisis of conscience about lying to her, but soon overcame it as he realised it’d be better with her not knowing what was going on with him. "I don’t leave until tomorrow afternoon, so we can spend plenty of time together until then," Diablo tried to reassure her. The Mexican Devil walked across the room, passing the large black leather couch and glass table in the centre of the room as he went to place his rucksack in the hallway of their Chicago home. "That’s great news," Angela feigned excitement. Knowing that he was lying to her, Angela was worried about what lay ahead for her husband. MONDAY 16TH JULY The seat was shaking softly with the movement of the train as El Diablo sat down in a near empty carriage with his black rucksack placed on the floor. The pattern of the carriage was two large windows which stretched down either side and two rows of two seats, meaning around fifty people could be in a carriage at one time. "Only a few hours before I arrive," Diablo thought to himself. Turning sideways in his seat, Diablo placed his feet up on the dull green coloured seat next to him and leant back against the glass window. "It seems the internet does more than just porn and wrestling rumour sites," he continued thinking. With nothing to do for the duration of the journey, Diablo switched himself off from his surroundings and engrossed himself in his own thoughts. "Eight long years have passed, but now I have located my tormentor. But what do I say to him? Will he even remember me? What if he lies to me?" Diablo began to doubt himself. As the train swayed, El Diablo sunk deeper and deeper into his thought process on how to handle the situation. "I need a plan. I need to have my own questions in order to interrogate him. I need to know what happened there," he plotted. Running his hands through his greasy black hair, Diablo began to wonder about the realities of Happy Hills Mental Institution or if anything that happened there was true. "I can’t even recall most of my time there, surely that isn’t right?" he questioned himself. Straightening out his blue short sleeved shirt, Diablo sat up and placed his left hand into his army camouflage coloured khaki pants to pull out a little notepad and pencil. "What to put?, what to put?, what to put?" he pondered. The Creator of Controversy wanted to formulate his plan carefully and plot it to the most precise detail in order to get his answers from Dr Murray, the answers he felt he deserved to know. "What did you do to me? That’s a good question to start with," he declared. Writing it down in the notepad, Diablo was flooded with ideas for other questions to ask Dr Murray, and promptly began to put them down in the pad as he continued with his journey. TUESDAY 17TH JULY The sky was a fantastic blue colour with no clouds in sight. The sun was shining down on this little neighbourhood, where one house stood out amongst the rest. The house was much larger than the others, with a beautiful green garden out front, it probably looked even prettier as right next to it was a red Porsche on a neatly paved driveway. The owner of this house wasn’t lacking in money. "This has to be the place," said El Diablo. Emerging just outside the house was The Mexican Devil, who had just located Dr Murray’s home. He could tell it was Murray’s, not because of how lavish the house looked, but by the simple wooden mailbox reading "The Murray’s" In an instant the sunshine stopped and clouds filled the sky, almost as if it was indicating a change of the mood thanks to El Diablo’s arrival. The heavens opened as Diablo walked down the driveway, heading towards the front door as thoughts of his plan were running wildly throughout his mind. KNOCK KNOCK KNOCK Using the wooden knocker on the brown varnished door, Diablo turned his back to it so that Dr Murray wouldn’t see him if he looked through the peephole. "I’m coming, I’m coming," came the voice from inside the house. El Diablo was a mixture of nervous and vengeful as he heard the footsteps approaching the door. The door opened slowly as The Master of Manipulation turned around to face whomever was home that day. "YOU!" shrieked the man. The man was 71 year old Dr Harold Murray. He looked much the same as El Diablo remembered him, only now he was bald and had a grey goatee beard. At 5’6 and 168lbs, Murray was not a physical threat to The Mexican Devil. "You remember," Diablo calmly stated. Attempting to slam the front door shut, Dr Murray was clearly startled, however Diablo easily overpowered him as he forced his way inside the mans home. "I believe you owe me some answers," said the bitter Mexican. Slamming the door shut, Diablo felt in control of the situation he had created. "I can’t," murmured the old man. Crawling along the floral pattern carpet, was Murray, trying to get away from El Diablo. "Where do you think you’re going?" uttered Diablo. El Diablo latched the door to ensure nobody could interrupt him, threw his rucksack to the ground, then calmly walked toward Murray, pulled him up to his feet and pushed him down into his comfy lilac chair. "ARE YOU GOING TO ANSWER ME!" yelled Diablo as he finally lost his cool. Looking around Dr Murray’s living room, Diablo could see that he was doing well for himself. There was a three piece suite, beautiful wooden cabinets with miniature statuettes inside, a huge 30inch television, a large fireplace and mantelpiece and on the white painted walls were copies of all Dr Murray’s credentials. "Yes, I remember you," said Harold. Having being recognised by his tormentor, El Diablo was ready for the answers of his ordeal. "What did you do to me?" Diablo asked him. Looking nervous, Dr Murray couldn’t speak a single word. This infuriated El Diablo, who walked briskly towards the mantelpiece and picked up a photograph to examine it carefully. "If you don’t want to answer my questions, then maybe they will want to know what I have to say," stated The Mexican Devil. The photograph he had in his hands was of Dr Murray and his daughters Terri and Paula and his four young grandchildren. Dr Murray was very scared and began to perspire profusely as he could see by Diablo’s eyes that he wasn’t messing around. "Okay, I’ll talk," he caved. Relieved to be getting some answers, Diablo placed the photo back on the mantelpiece and stood over Dr Murray to intimidate him. "I injected you twice a day with what was supposed to be a breakthrough medicine in helping the mentally sick, but it didn’t work. It left the patients unconscious and had side effects like hallucinations," Murray told him. Hearing the word "hallucinations" suddenly brought some clarity to the situation for El Diablo, as he now knew that his routine inside there was a façade. "Carry on," commented Diablo. Gulping to regain his composure, Murray continued. "The tests didn’t work, but they kept telling us to carry on with them. By the time the injections worked on you enough to let you leave, you were a threat to lose your sanity again," Smirking, El Diablo had found a reason for his paranoia. He began to pace the room rapidly as Dr Murray, wearing a white vest and purple sweatpants, sat and watched him, scared out of his wits. "Why me?" queried Diablo. Shaking his head, Murray clearly didn’t know why El Diablo was chosen for the trials. "What was real?" asked The Creator of Controversy. Stroking his goatee, Dr Murray was preparing an answer. "The reality is that the building existed, but everyone in there only ever saw their room and the entrance and exit, everything else was all a figment of their imagination due to the injections," Harold replied. Hearing the truth was both relieving and infuriating for The Master of Manipulation, as he finally knew what happened to him in the ten months he spent there but was very angry at what happened and wanted vengeance. "Why shouldn’t I take revenge on you for my suffering?" Diablo questioned him. Surprisingly Dr Murray didn’t look scared at that threat, he seemed very calm and relaxed about the situation. "Because I was only following my orders. They recruited me and told me what to do every single time. I shouldn’t even be talking to you," responded Murray. Diablo sat down on the adjacent sofa and pondered about who "they" could be. "Who? Who is behind this?" El Diablo demanded to know. Murray looked weary, not really wanting to tell him who was behind it, but knowing he would have to if he wanted to get out of this situation unscathed. "They’re called the IPC, The International Pharmaceutical Corporation. They bought Happy Hills and used it as a testing area for their new medicines," Murray informed him. This was a big revelation, as there was a conspiracy out to get El Diablo and he wasn’t just imagining it. El Diablo’s paranoia was finally justified. "What did you have to do with it?" quizzed Diablo. Looking ashamed, Dr Murray wiped the sweat from his brow. "I was out of work and desperate for the money. I wish I never took that job, I’d rather be dead than having people like you seeking me out," he mumbled. Standing up, The Mexican Devil began to pace up and down the room again, like a police interrogator might do when seeking answers. "Did the medicines that were injected into me, change me at all?" Diablo demanded to know. Dr Murray nodded slowly and wearily. "HOW?" screamed The Mexican Devil. Murray’s expression changed from that of calm to of a real concern for his own safety as he knew his next answer would be devastating to El Diablo. "It still causes occasional hallucinations and as the years pass heightened paranoia, and…." Dr Murray didn’t want to say the next issue. With El Diablo staring a hole straight through him, Harold Murray knew he would have to tell him the truth. "….it made you sterile," he muttered. Shocked and furious, Diablo didn’t know what to do. Angela wanted children and here he was, an empty vessel. "WHY WOULD THEY DO THIS TO ME?" shrieked The Master of Manipulation. Confronted by a former patient, Dr Murray was ashamed of his work at the institution. "They didn’t know. You were their guinea pig," responded Murray. El Diablo stopped pacing and wondered what to do next. He had got the answers to all the questions he pencilled down on his notepad and had no reason to interrogate Dr Murray any longer. "You better hope we don’t cross paths again Dr Murray," Diablo threatened. Slowly heading towards the front door and picking up his rucksack, Diablo unlatched the locked door and made his exit into the pouring rain outside. Devastated by Dr Murray’s final revelation, El Diablo was now hell-bent on exacting a brutal revenge upon his tormentors.
