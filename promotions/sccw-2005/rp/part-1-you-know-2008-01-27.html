Part 1: You Know

27 January 2008

Date: 1/27/08 This is a story about the truth, and about lies. It is time that you had a proper introduction to the True Prophet. # # # January 27, 2008. 6:00 p.m. Oklahoma, just south of the Kansas border. A light blue 1999 Buick Century rolled along a county highway, five miles over the speed limit. The driver was in a hurry, but he wasn't about to take unnecessary chances. He was sticking to the back roads. The interstate was faster, true, but also more likely to be watched. It wasn't likely that people would be looking for him specifically, but he wasn't about to get sloppy now. Not when he was so close. He'd gone almost non-stop since he left the Big Apple. He gassed up when he had to, and usually grabbed something to eat in whatever convenience store he stopped at. No hotels on the trip; when he needed to sleep, he would pull off to the side of the road for an hour or two. Most hotels required a credit card or driver's license anyway, and the last thing he wanted was to leave a trail. He felt weary--the long hours of the journey weighed heavy on him, as did the long years of his life that he was now leaving behind. A quick shot of coffee shook the cobwebs loose, allowing him to focus. There'd be plenty of time for sleep when this was all over. He grabbed his cellphone from the passenger seat and dialed a number. He'd been doing this the last two days--driving and calling. He had a limited amount of time to conduct his business, and he was going to make use of all of it. 'Mr. Brown, please.' 'Ah. Hello, Jeremy.' 'You know who this is.' 'There should be a new deposit in the account; I put it there yesterday morning. Divide it up and send out the payments.' 'Good. That's the last of it, Jeremy. Dissolve the network.' 'Yes. Thank you for your service. All of you.' He flipped the phone shut. Then, he held it out of the window and threw it downward. The phone shattered into a thousand pieces, leaving it in a state beyond recognition or repair. Sure, it was just a TracFone with two minutes left on it. But he couldn't risk throwing it in the ditch, where it might remain intact...where someone could find it. Anyway, that was the last call he had to make--all his remaining business needed to be in person, and he could take care of that tomorrow. He drove on in silence, not even bothering to turn on the car stereo. He found some humor in that--throughout most of his life, the man was unsettled by total quiet. He needed some kind of noise, whether it was music, the clacking of a keyboard, or some manner of conversation. He even developed the habit of talking to himself as a result. If anyone asked, he would always tell them it was because nobody else was worth listening to. In truth, it was because he was afraid of it. After all, silence invites introspection, and he had no interest in looking into his own soul. As they say, stare too long into the abyss... Now, at the end, he found the silence comforting. And like all good things, it must come to an end. It ends with his own voice, prompted by an obstruction ahead. 'Oh, what the hell is this?' He slowed the car to a crawl, so that it would not smash through the barrier that had been placed on the road. 'ROAD CLOSED AHEAD: TAKE DETOUR,' it read, and featured an orange sign with a black arrow, helpfully pointing the way to said detour. The side road was in bad shape, as if nobody had bothered repairing it in the last, oh, thirty years. The asphalt was uneven and pockmarked, and grass grew through cracks in the center. It's the kind of road you normally see leading into towns with a double-digit population. 'I can't believe this shit. See if I ever listen to Mapquest again.' The driver briefly considered taking the county road anyway--it doesn't look too bad; maybe they've just put up the signs so far, and haven't started tearing it up yet. Still, he didn't want to run the risk of finding the road impassable and being forced to backtrack--time was short enough already. Besides, driving on roads that are supposed to be closed attracts attention, and that's the last thing he wanted. As much as he hated the idea, he turned onto the side road, hoping that he could get back to his main route before long. Time...it was strange that he obsessed so much over the time now. He'd been in the game for years and years; what did a few minutes matter? A few hours? After all he had seen and done to get to this point, why get upset over a minor delay such as this? He knew the answer to his own question, of course. He always did. In this case, he knew that his impatience was merely the final burst of adrenaline, leading up to the finish. The moment when he can quit hiding, and actually disappear. He took a look down at the passenger seat, which was the resting place of his only possession: a large metal case. The culmination of his life's work was inside that case, and only he knew the combination that would open it. He had it specially build, with a six-number combination instead of three. Anyone wishing to steal the contents would be at it for quite some time. Unless, of course, they didn't care whether or not they got the contents intact...but some things couldn't be helped. Rather strange, really, that a man would travel across the country with a locked metal case as his only baggage. The man had other possessions, once upon a time, but he had declared them all to be unnecessary burdens. His clothes were given away to Goodwill or the Salvation Army, or occasionally to bums on the street. His vast collection of information had been erased, destroyed, or returned to whoever he'd...borrowed it from. Anything else was simply left behind--anyone who found it was welcome to it, since it would offer no clues as to what became of the previous owner. If someone looked for a trail there, they would only find red herrings and dead ends. Some might call him paranoid. He had good reason to be, though. His life was a trail of stabbed backs and broken promises. It hadn't even been two full days since he walked out on the last two people in the world who would have anything to do with him--probably ruined their lives in the process. He didn't even think about it, though. He'd have time for regrets later--now, all that mattered was the end. As the saying goes, 'take the money and run.' Not 'take the money, feel really bad about anyone who got hurt in the process, go through counseling to deal with your guilt, and THEN run.' The driver scowled as the road he was on came to a T-intersection. He looked to the left, and saw another barrier blocking his path back to the main road. To the right, he saw the asphalt run out, replaced by gravel. He growled in annoyance--perhaps he should have stuck to the interstate after all. He turned the wheel to the right, hoping that by going farther from his intended path, he could find his way back. Incidentally, there is a rather famous book that begins in a similar manner. # # # An hour later, and the driver was well and truly lost. It seemed that every time the road divided, it would lead him farther from where he wanted to go. But he knew that as long as he kept heading west, he was getting closer to his eventual destination. Besides, he had to stumble upon another paved road eventually, right? He started to become frustrated, but quickly beat back those feelings. This was just a temporary setback. This was not part of some sort of vast conspiracy perpetrated by roadwork crews or any other of his enemies. Being mad at the world no longer served a purpose. 'Haha!' Off in the distance, he spied pavement, always an early warning sign of civilization. He hit the gas, knowing that the sooner he gained his bearings, the sooner he could leave his old life behind. Sadly, it was not to be. For, no matter how you try and escape your past, it has a way of catching up with you. PLAM! The right front tire exploded, shredding itself to pieces as the Buick careened down the gravel road. He'd been going too fast, and was having difficulty keeping control. 'Shit! Shit! Shit!' SKKRRRRRRRRRRRRRCHHHHHHHHHHHH-- The car skidded sideways before leaving the road entirely. The driver tried to bring the car back under control, but the wheel seemed to turn on its own, no matter how hard he tried to steady it. The came perilously close to tipping over at one point, before coming back down. It was cold comfort, considering that the car was now headed straight for a tree. Go figure, he thought to himself. One tree in the whole fucking state, and I'm about to run into it. Making one last effort, he wrenched the wheel to the left in an effort to avoid the tree. The good news was that he managed to change the orientation of the vehicle. The bad news was that its direction stayed the same--the car now skidded sideways, headed inexorably toward its destination. In the split second before impact, the driver found that he suddenly had a free moment. He used it to curse himself for being so stupid, to allow such a thing to happen. All his plans and schemes, years of hard work, all for nothing--because of dumb luck and one lapse of judgment, he was going to die out here, in the middle of nowhere, Alone, unknown, and with his final goal so tantalizingly close. He also tried to curse the universe, but ran out of time. The car slid sideways into the tree, crumpling the passenger doors. The driver's body jerked violently to the right, then back to the left. As it did, his head collided with the window. The glass shattered, causing further injury to the driver in the process. His head listed to the side, and he tried to unbuckle his safety belt, but his arms were less than cooperative. There would be no escape. He stared into the side mirror and watched the blood pour trickle down his face, before losing consciousness. And nobody was there to see. # # # There was someone who heard it, however. And he took off running. # # # Thirty minutes later, three people arrived in a clapped-out old pickup. The two in the cab were a couple of late middle age, bordering on actually being 'old.' The one in the back was their farmhand, a very large, long-haired man. 'Here he is!' he shouted, hoping that the people in front could hear. They did, and brought the pickup to a stop. All three of them piled out and approached the scene of the accident--someone had gone and ran their Buick into a tree. There was a man, presumably the driver, lying on the ground a few feet away. 'I heard the crash and came running,' the farmhand said. 'Pulled him out of the car, made sure he was still breathin'. Then I ran back and told you, sir.' 'Ya done good, Seth,' replied the old man as he got out of the truck. 'Is he all right?' 'He's still breathing, but he looks like hell,' Seth answered. 'Watch yer language, Seth,' the woman snapped. The old man looked down on the accident victim and frowned. 'He looks like a guv'mint man. Lookit that suit.' The woman--his wife--rolled her eyes. 'Oh, hush, Pa. What would one o' them be doin' way out here? Anyway, he's hurt. You ain't about to turn away an injured man, are ya?' 'No, Ma, reckon I ain't. Seth, gimme a hand loading him up in the truck.' 'Yessir.' Seth went to the head of the victim and picked him up by the shoulders, while the old farmer grabbed his feet. Together, they lifted him into the bed of the truck as gently as they could. Seth climbed up with him, to keep him stable for the trip back home. 'Any idea who he is?' the wife asked. The very large individual called Seth reached into his pocket, and pulled out a scrap of paper. It was a proof of insurance slip. 'Found this in the car. Says here his name is...' But you're not ready for that particular truth just yet.
