Date: Today i. HUNTER TO HUNTED It’d happened so quickly, the kid’s shock didn’t even have time to register. The "bum", sitting motionless against the building’s crumbling walls, flung open his worn duster and pulled out two handguns. The kid was unable to conclude the make, he simply saw the silver barrels flash out from as the bum centered on his target. The wolf, who had every intention to slaughter Roger Dodd, had suddenly become the hunted. Even after being alerted by the request of a "light", the mafia hitman was much too slow. He whirled toward the bum, but found himself beat by mere seconds. A few second lead was plenty for the seated gunslinger. As the first and only shot resonated off the building’s archaic walls, the hitman, an extra hole in his head, fell backward and landed on the black asphalt. The bum, his black fedora pulled low, stood and glanced briefly at the recently deceased. On the street, he heard a vehicle fire to life followed shortly by the squealing of spinning tires on dry blacktop. The bum tensed up, but, after he caught the black Mercedes heading for the hills, his tension eased away. It was now that his gaze fell upon the door, the number nine hung loosely from the pressed wood. With one mighty kick, his black construction boot leading the way, the bum had gained access to the room. It coincided well with the rest of the New Orleans slums. The tattered room was rank with stench, but not enough to ward off the recent entrant. Instead, his striking emerald eyes stared down at the man curled into the fetal position on the floor. The man was dirty; the bum imagined the tears flowing from their ducts were the closest the man had been to showering in some time. "Yer name?" the bum asked. His answer came in sobs, quiet at first, but growing uncontrollably. In a flash, the bum had snatched the balding man by his remaining hair. The man whimpered as he found himself being pulled into a seated position. "I asked yer name, fella." The balding man, fearful of what he might see, slowly looked at him. What he saw was nothing – a shadowed face, blonde hair, and those emerald green eyes peering through the darkness at him. "Roger Dodd," he mumbled. "Roger Dodd," Shadow repeated. "Why-oh-ever was dar’a man outside, with every intention a’killin’ you?" His words were much less out of concern, but more out of a humored curiosity. Dodd did not answer as his attention was focused on the gun in the bum’s hand. The bum, realizing the balding man’s worry, slipped the gun back inside his duster. "Go ‘head an’ answer, Mr. Dodd." "I … I don’t know," Dodd answered, starting with a stumble and finishing frantically. "That’s some coincidence," the bum declared with a grin and slow nod. "Now how ‘boutcha tell me tha truth," he deadpanned. Roger hesitated before answering, "I owe some people money … bad people." With the gun away, Dodd seemed to be relaxing a bit. His eyes shifted from the unknown man next to him to the expired hitman on the pavement outside. "Looks … looks like I’m safe now." He seemed to grow more and more human by the second, although his pitiful surroundings and poor hygiene said otherwise. "Who are you?" Dodd, turning back to "Shadow". Shadow walked toward the busted in door, which remained on its hinges, and closed it. For a moment, he simply stood facing the door. "You’a family man, Roger? Wife ‘n little ones?" Shadow lowered his hand closer to the floor as if to measure a child’s height. "Y-yeah … wife and my son, Nick. He’s in high school, yet," Dodd answered, his mind drifting somewhere far away from the rancid room. "Ya’know, Roger, dat when they find out da fella they sent is dead, dey ain’t goin’ to be happy. Trut’ is, long as ya’ah alive, ya family is in harm’s way." Shadow turned toward Roger, whose face had returned to the grave state it was previously. Roger’s mind was clearly racing, trying to come up with an answer. There was no answer. Only the gun. Shadow pulled one of the silver barreled guns from his duster. The very gun that had saved Roger Dodd’s life. Dodd, eyes darting around the room, began to quiver and the crotch of his pants became wet. "God no," he spoke in a hush. The gun was already leveled, his fate decided. "This has nothin’ to do with God … only Saint, this is my judgment." The shot rang out and closed Roger Dodd’s eyes.
