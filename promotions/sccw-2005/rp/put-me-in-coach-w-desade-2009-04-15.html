Put me in coach w/Desade

15 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/15/09 An excerpt from Lane Steven’s journal – April 14th, 2009 Katsidy tricked me. And that’s the funny part, when I started this, when I agreed to come back, that’s the one thing I thought would never happen. I was a wrestling fan back in the dying days of the National Wrestling Council, and I had watched her do this to so many men. All of whom entered a situation thinking that they, unlike all of those who came before them, were not going to fall victim to her games. Although with me it wasn’t sexual. Sure, I would put my penis inside of her vagina. Thrust, repeat, etc. In fact I would be enthusiastic to do so. Everyone always cuts the STD jokes with her, but she’s smart enough to know that she has to be careful, or her assets are gone. She can’t go around manipulating men as some kind of balding AIDS patient. Wait; is it cancer where you lose hair? I dunno. She knew how eager I was to impress Alex, to prove my worth. And she twisted that. A second after I hit Ambrose with that belt, I could tell already I had done something wrong. The look on Alexandra’s face told me all I needed to know. I played it off all cocky and cool, cause that’s my schtick. But I felt like a dog who had shit the rug. For whatever reason, she wasn’t ready to let Amy know I was back. She was protecting Amy Campbell. When I met Alex, I didn’t get the impression that she would ever spare anyone’s feelings. * * * Three months earlier. "Would anyone else like to share?" "I think I can handle that." Middleton, Iowa was a town of about ten thousand people. It’s proximity to Dubuque meant that many of the residents were people that had moved here to commute to the larger city, but didn’t want their kids to go to a densely populated school. They liked living out here, but as soon as they found a better job elsewhere, they would be gone. While another portion of the population had been born and raised here, and would most likely never leave. Their parents had been the same way. And their parents before them. In the basement of the Middleton community center, both sides of this coin were well represented. And there was another man who didn’t fit at all. "My name is Lane, and I am an alcoholic." The room came alive with clapping, and congratulations. The River Rat scanned his audience, and was not surprised at all by his audience. Except for the woman in the corner, who through the fog of his vicodin buzz looked vaguely familiar. He was dressed in a gray hoodie and jeans. His reddish hair had become shaggy, and his stubble had almost manifested into a beard. "And really, that’s never gonna change." From the audience someone yelled something like, "I hear ya brother." Lane shook his head. "No, I don’t mean that like…once you’re an alcoholic you’re always one, or any of that cheeseball crap. I mean I’m never going to stop drinking. I think it’s pretty fucking awesome actually." Silence. Lane scratched his head. "I have no wife, no kids, I don’t talk to my parents. I have no brothers or sisters. I don’t really have a friend in the world, only enemies that never really understood me. I’m not very good with women. The only job I was good at I lost. I used to use things like this as an excuse to drink…but the truth is I don’t need one. I just like it. I like the way my head can feel heavy, and the sudden urgency I get to say sappy things to strange women. There is a quiet dignity to waking up with a terrible hangover, the way it can feel like the very sunlight that others depend on is peeling off your face." A chuckle from the former livewire and gateway champion. No one else laughs with him. "Sometimes I feel like this cannot possibly be my home planet." It should be said at this point that elsewhere in this room are signs for potlucks and youth football. It has not been privy to talk like this. Stevens’ eyes found a man who had called himself Terrance, a balding man in an Iowa Hawkeyes shirt. He had told such a dramatic story of falling off the wagon repeatedly, and his wife leaving him, and so on. Terrance could not even look at the speaker. "Because all I’m concerned with…is maximum fun. Cheers!" Lane holds up a fictional glass, smiled broadly, and sat back down in his seat. Once the attention of the room was off of him, the smile quickly faded. "Thank you…Lane," the organizer said, obviously flustered. It was a thin woman in a red sweatshirt that The River Rat imagined would gouge out someone’s eyes for a drink. Lane tried to pay attention to the other stories, but, really, who gives a fuck, right? Instead, he found his eyes drifting among the gathered. They were all losers, and the fact that he shared so many of their traits was an irony not altogether lost on him. All of them except for the redhead hiding in the back of the room, face nearly concealed in a black hood. He narrowed his eyes; sure he'd seen a gleam of metal on her face. No, this one hadn't been to any of the meetings, not in this building. After Susan told the latest version of her story, this time involving her daughter slapping her, the head of the meeting called for a break, and Lane drifted to the snack table, where the girl in the hoodie (that was some skull on the back) picked over the same stale brownies Stevens would swear had been there for a half-dozen missions. 'They're teasin' me with brownies, right?' Her voice was scratchy, a little bit of an accent clinging to the words, and she spoke without turning, like she'd known he was there. 'Like they wanna flaunt that we used to bake weird shit into them.' The River Rat’s brow furrowed slightly, his eyes floated over her figure. "Still do, actually. I just come here for the comedy. Same reason I watch the news, or leave the house really. I stand up every week…tell them I’m never going to quit; they think I’m calling for help, and we just keep dancing around in circles. Some of these assholes send me Christmas cards. I’m in their hearts, and shit." The woman turned slowly over her shoulder, striking gray eyes locking onto him. 'So you're fucking with them? Huh.' She leaned towards him, lowering her voice slightly. 'What do you think they would do if I stood up and told them that Lane Stevens was fucking with them?' Maybe it was just him (with the shit he was on, he thought that was very likely), but Lane was sure she emphasized his last name – the same name he'd never given. After a few moments of mental processing time he recoiled at this, as if struck. "Excuse me?" Lane offered, and got no response. "Who the fuck are…what did you say?" His mind was racing now. It had been a while since it had worked at full capacity. Maybe this was nothing, but it didn’t feel like nothing. The redhead in the hoodie turned to face him, putting her hand on his chest. 'Do not get the wrong idea, Mr. Stevens.' Her voice was whisper-soft, but it wasn't the pleasing kind of whisper women used around him. It was something harder, something that made a lie of the smile that accompanied it. 'I have a business proposition for you – nothing more. But the rest of the people here... they do not need to know that.' It suddenly dawned on him that he was sweating. "Oh…kay," he finally stammered, and suddenly his legs were taking him out into the hallway. He could feel her presence behind him. There was an authority to it, a confidence that he needed. They entered a smaller room, some kind of storage closet, and she closed the door behind him. "I don’t have anymore vicodin," Stevens stated, the words sliding out awkwardly. He knew that wasn’t what this was about. It was only once the door closed that she let the hood fall. The woman's eyebrow was pierced, but something about it didn't look right, especially as she stopped slouching and her back snapped straight. 'Good evening, Lane.' More of that hard voice eked out without her need to push it down into the persona of a mumbling addict. 'My name is Alexandra Pierce. I doubt you have heard of me.' There was none of that bullshit false modesty women used so often; it was a statement of fact. 'I represent an entity in Sin City Championship Wrestling, the federation you used to call FUSE Wrestling.' The River Rat studied her for a few moments before a sharp laugh escaped his lips, and echoed throughout the closet. "Well Christ on a cracker, if it isn’t Desade!" She couldn’t help but look slightly surprised for a moment. "Yeah I used to watch the NWC a ton when I was coming up. Black Ops vs. Malignance was some sick shit. And the whole crusade to end the council…very cool, very cool indeed. Wow, what the fuck. Small world." 'Be quiet, or I will leave you in this hole with your only entertainment being these walls, those losers, and your right hand.' Pierce snapped off the words., and Lane found himself backing away from her wrath, until there wasn't any room left in the closet. 'Perhaps I have made a mistake here. I am looking for Lane Stevens, the man who tried to put Amy Campbell out of professional wrestling forever. Not another yokel reminding me of something I did years ago. Have I found the wrong man?' Lane swallowed these words, and digested them. "Well, you found a different man…but I wouldn’t go as far as to call him the wrong one. And let’s just go easy on the threats, shall we? There are no cameras in here. If you want something, you can just ask for it." Stevens smiled then, in an attempt to look confident. But he knew he had no cards to play here. He was not in shape. If she wanted to rip him apart, she likely would. 'I rarely posture for the cameras, Mr. Stevens. I have an... asset, let's say. Someone you have an appreciable knowledge of and a skillset that should be able to properly manage the situation, should this asset become uncontrollable. I am looking to bring you back to the sport you enjoyed so much, to do the horrible things I know you love, and, if the situation becomes untenable, to avenge yourself upon the woman who ended your last stint.' Lane’s smile was no longer fake. It grew, the way a virus might. "Well in that case put me in coach, I’m ready to play." 'It is not as simple as that.' Lane quickly learned one thing – when it came to dealing with the Spider in the Web, it rarely ever was. * * * But things have certainly changed since then. You know, I watched several back episodes of SCCW television. The way Alex touches Amy’s back, just seems to me they might be fucking. Just my read. But hey, I’ve called a river bet with ace high before and they’ve shown me quads. So who knows. But all of this feels bigger than me. I feel like I’m surrounded by characters in a Shakespeare play, all slowly being pulled towards an unhappy ending they cannot control. But I’m not paid for giving advice or anything like that. So I suppose I’ll just keep my mouth shut, and watch the show. But I feel like in a year from now, if anyone were to just go back in time, and ask ol’ Lane Stevens what to do, I might have really had some solid pointers on how to avoid this whole mess. Or maybe I would have just said I’m busy. * * * At the Pine Cone truck stop, just five miles north of Iowa City, Walter Halvorson was eating scrambled eggs. Walter, in case you haven’t been following, was a low level grunt of Alexandra Pierce’s. His obsessive nature has long been a tool of Desade’s, going back to her previously mentioned NWC tenure, where he uncovered several legal loopholes that made her goal more easily attainable. But he was nearing retirement now. His last task before said retirement? Babysitting the Hand’s WMD on off days, and making sure he was content and out of trouble. He was to watch over their problem child, their River Rat. It was not the kind of job that he specialized in, but no one else was exactly really leaping at it so it fell on his lap. Looking around at the truck stop, Walter wondered if things could have been different. If maybe he too could have been an aging regular at a place like this, socializing with fat waitresses who overused the term "honey". His train of thought was interrupted by the woman who slid into the booth across from him. Savant’s t-shirt advertised the band "And You Will Know us by the Trail of Dead". All she said was: "He will enjoy this". Lauren Fox took a drink from the water that was set in front of her, and slid a manila folder across the table to Walter. A few moments later, she was gone. Left alone with the folder, Walter Halvorson put down his fork and dug through its contents.. The first few documents he came across were only text, very detailed documents by the look of them. The fifth page in was a picture of a man. SCCW fans would know him by the name of Dusk. I’m busy hurting people Walter studied the picture for a few moments, wondering what was to come. He imagined it was unpleasant.
