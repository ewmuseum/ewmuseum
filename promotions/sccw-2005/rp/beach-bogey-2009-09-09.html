Beach Bogey

Date: 9/9/09 There was nothing sexy about a seventeen-year-old jumping out of your grown-up bed in a pair of Snoopy boxer shorts. With disheveled hair spiking out only on the left side of his head like his scalp had been a victim of a stroke, Thad wasn't a firestarter when it came to wine-drinkers like Mrs. Alexander. His hand covering the quarter-sized birthmark two inches above his waistline, he cocked his gaze behind his shoulder and smirked -- not even a trace of stubble on his jawline. He could have passed for fourteen, which was another twig in the tepee of the disturbing affair that had continued every day after school for three weeks now. She had to admit that the sincerity she saw in those blue eyes right now was quite a turn on, though. Especially considering they were usually tinted pink from the lingering high he tried to maintain from the minute Physics let out at three. When he bent over to pick up the Alvarez from out of his hard shell case...well, that wasn't too bad, either. She doubted very seriously that she'd be doing this if he weren't as defined or as sweet-faced as he was, otherwise, she could've just waited on her husband to get home. The stocky, hairy-chested grunt's photograph currently faced outside the window as a symbolic blue star. His gaze currently away from the adultery taking place inside his own home, as he stood proudly in front of Old Glory in his fatigues. Thad was not brave, not disciplined. His appeal was more of the kind of a cute, rebellious charm; like Teddy Ruxpin in a cutoff denim vest, complete with motorcycle rocker patch. He wasn't sexy. His intimacy skills were novice at best, and that's being generous. He couldn't properly make love if it were a microwaveable Hot Pocket. When he kissed, he had a real issue with opening his mouth wide enough -- like making out with a ventriloquist dummy. His hand placement was fairly awful, too...the clockwise rubs on the small of her back felt like an amateur disc jockey attempting to scratch a record. Thankfully, endurance wasn't an issue for him, which served as a saving grace...while he may have been the equivalent of a NASCAR jobber in the sack, it at least gave her time to daydream. Naturally, he thought he was awesome, and there was NO WAY she could be faking it. Truth be told, though, he could have been hung like an infant and had the IQ of Lloyd Christmas, but once he stood facing her with that guitar strapped around his bare shoulders, he instantly transformed from Clark Kent into Superman. The best part for her was the allure of his potential. There was no doubt that he was good...real good...at crooning and strumming. Kathleen was turned on by the idea that she'd be the future subject to some intense B-side that he'd proclaim to be his favorite in Rolling Stone interviews. God dammit, she thought, I was a nice girl. Until she realized how amazing the thrill of being bad was for the first time in her 36-year life. She didn't even have to ask him to serenade her after the first week. Like clockwork, he'd roll his bony little ass off the mattress without even bothering with the post-fuck kiss. Just pulled the violet sheets back over her, trotted to the corner, and did a by-ear tuning of ye old lyre. Dessert was then served. Thad unnecessarily cleared out his throat, and strummed downward with a flick of his four right fingers. 'I want to try a cover,' he beamed, bashfully looking down at the oval rosette on his guitar. Kathleen nodded in response, slumping down and adjusting herself within the folds of the down comforter. 'What one?' It was a typical response for a Southerner, rather than the grammatically correct 'which one?,' and something Mrs. Alexander tried to cover up in a public setting. 'I reckon you gon' see!' Thad teased, laying the accent on thick in mocking fashion. Beginning his performance, he began to alternate strokes in a progression of D-A-B-F, swaying slightly to count the rhythm silently to himself. For Kathleen, it would take more than the music to trigger any sort of familiarity -- but it honestly didn't matter to her if he were doing a Charles Manson cover. She just watched his lips move and fingers slide over the fretboard, both as natural as breathing to the boy wearing the Peanuts undies. 'I may not always love you,' While a hundred rehearsals in front of the mirror in his own bedroom made him look quite confident, the gears in his head rotated like a Spacely Sprocket. That first line, 'I may not always love you,' was the source of much contemplation in the song choice. He was, at least by his own observation, a teenager madly in love with a girl. Albeit, a mature woman, but probably with those girl tendencies: like his classmates who he'd overhear bitching about how 'Justin totally wouldn't even look me in the eye at lunch when I asked him if he thought this skirt was pretty,' or 'Jake was totally trying to feel up my ass, but won't even tell me he loves me in front of his friends.' Thad didn't want her to read into the line, but he feared that would be the case. '...but as long as there are stars above you,' The elated smile on her face was all the indicator he needed. It still bothered him, though, that this amazing ballad still had that line stuck right in the beginning. Not the most proficient way to communicate the language of the heart, he mused. Though it was probably a lot better than what he'd considered replacing it with for a laugh: 'I really wanna fuck you.' '...you'll never need to doubt it,' Kathleen didn't think twice about the message, didn't try to analyze the way the vocals complimented the instrument, and didn't care that this was one of the greatest love songs of all time. All she did was run her tongue across her fangs, let her fingers slither down past her belly button, and slide underneath the waistband of her boy shorts. To achieve swan song for the day's rendezvous, Kathleen euphorically slowed the tempo, layering over Thad's twinkling with harmonizing glissandos of her own. '...I'll make you so sure about it.' He felt himself go into vertigo, as the room spread out upwards and outwards. Everything around him appeared to be growing larger with the exception of his guitar. While he panicked within himself, his feet stayed glued, and his song did not skip a beat. 'God only knows what I'd be without you.' The sheets were pulled back and a gentle shaking knocked Thad backwards on his ass as Kathleen stood over him. A tower of a woman, she made a flat peace sign with her fingers and lifted him up, causing the tiny guitar to fall and crash into a thousand pieces on the floor. God only knows what I'd be without you.' He had missed the second verse...although he KNEW the bridge was only one line, he had lost all control of his will. His internal record needle remain stuck on the same track line, the phrase echoing until it became merely whistles of a higher pitch. The sound of the cage door opening caused his head to turn almost 180 degrees as she slid an index finger underneath his feet. He gripped it with ease, until routinely latching onto the wooden swing placed in the center of his home. Staring at the mirror in his bedroom, he knew all that practicing had paid off. Thad poked out his chest with pride, examining the colorful markings on his face...until the sheet fell over the metal bars. She was pleased with her songbird.
