Date: Today 'Oh, I can think of several reasons why I might wake up to find myself strapped to a bed. Only one of them is good...and judging by the fact that none of you are a high-class Mexican hooker, I believe we can rule that one out.' But of course, we're getting ahead of ourselves. # # # Recently, the world's population of Decent People had been increasing, thanks to Wyatt Connors' Kool-Aid Distribution Service. It was only up and running for one show, but that was enough time to 'save' almost thirty more souls, who then followed the False Prophet all the way from San Antonio to the Compound just outside of Ponca City, Oklahoma. There was also one other pilgrim who made the journey, but he wasn't there for salvation. He was there for Wyatt Connors himself...and when Harley Roebuck had a job to do, come hell or high water, it would get done. That was how he found himself in the inner sanctum of the Crusade, a simple farmhouse several hundred yards from the Decency Compound. There, he met some of the Crusaders that he saw on TV, as well as one that he hadn't seen on TV in several years. There, he looked into the face of pure madness, known to the rest of the world as Reverend Ibrahim Seck. 'I trust that we have handled your...friend...in a satisfactory manner?' the Reverend asked, the words dripping with false consideration. 'Should be,' Harley answered, and stuck an unlit Lucky Strike into his mouth. 'He ain't gonna be happy when he wakes up, and we can't have him runnin' off, can we?' He quickly lit the cigarette, then tucked his engraved Zippo back into his pocket, keeping an eye on the Reverend as he did so. Seck had watched very intently, and almost certainly noticed the engraved initials on the lighter. T.H.S. Roebuck took a drag before asking, 'How much longer d'you think it'll take?' 'Satan should have restored his claim on Brother Wyatt's soul by tomorrow...at the latest, the day after. Are you sure you wouldn't care for something to drink?' Harley almost agreed, without fully realizing it. There was something hypnotic in Seck's voice...like it had the same effect as the Kool-Aid, if it caught you off guard. He'd have to be careful. 'Nope. I'm good.' 'Suit yourself. Now that your satisfactions have been met, let us discuss mine. I believe we had matters to discuss involving my dear friend, Brother Edward.' Harley nodded. 'Right. You don't want him screwing up your operation anymore, right? Not a problem. I can just wait for him out in the parking lot and--' 'I'm afraid you don't understand,' Seck interrupted. 'I don't to destroy him. I want to save him.' Roebuck arched an eyebrow. 'Save him?' 'Of course, Brother Harley. For all of his display, I know that Brother Edward is merely a slave to his own desires, crying out desperately to be delivered into the bosom of the Lord. I have offered help, but he stubbornly refuses to accept it, even though he must know that there is no way to the Lord but through Me.' Harley thought for a moment. He had a good grasp of both sides now...Seck wanted to get Lambert into heaven, and Lambert wanted to send the Reverend in hell. Now it was up to him to get both sides together. 'You'll need to have something he wants.' 'Oh, I do. Once they are introduced properly, Brother Colt will be of great interest.' 'How do you--' Harley began, when realization dawned. The height was about the same, and the build was similar, and it would have been a dead giveaway if the kid had some bulk. But the eyes...the ice blue eyes told the whole story. 'Does Lambert know?' 'Not yet. However, once he does know, he will stop at nothing to claim the boy. That's what I'm counting on.' 'I see. You back him into a corner...and then what?' 'I doubt that Brother Edward will come to the fold willingly. However, he does have some semblance of honor. If he were to agree to certain...stipulations, I believe we can claim his soul for the forces of good.' 'Uh-huh. Sounds like you've got most of this figured out already. What do you need out of me, then?' 'You have contact with Brother Edward. He trusts you. Perhaps he might be willing to share his battle plans?' 'I doubt it, but it's worth a shot. And if that doesn't work?' Ibrahim grinned again. 'Then we can always count on divine intervention.' Roebuck took another puff, then put on a smile of his own. 'Don't know much about divine...but I've had some experience with intervention.' 'Marvelous. You are a gift from above, Brother Harley, for surely you will assure that My plan for Brother Edward comes to fruition.' Seck's praise made Harley's skin crawl. 'So, I gotta ask. How do you know he's going to go for this?' 'Believe Me, My son. Once he knows, there's nothing he wouldn't do for the boy. Brother Edward is usually selfish to a fault, but the most important thing to him has always been his family. Do you understand?' 'Yeah, I guess I know something about that.' Ibrahim gave a curt nod. 'I'm afraid I must retire for the evening. We shall have an even more trying day tomorrow, and we shall both need our strength for it. Good night, Brother Harley.' ''Night, Rev.'' He stepped out of the room, leaving Seck to his prayers and preparations. As he stood in the hallway, he weighed the option of seeing Connors, but quickly decided against it. It had been bad enough already, and tomorrow was likely to be the worst of all. Harley muttered, 'Back to work,' and crushed the half-smoked cigarette against the wall. He couldn't stand the taste of them; he'd always been more of a cigar man. But he smoked them here for two reasons. One, because the sense of smell is linked closely to the memory. Two, because Lucky Strikes had always been Edward Lambert's brand of choice. # # # Drying out Wyatt Connors had been a slow process. They had to knock him out just to start the process, due to his refusal to 'deny the blood of Christ.' After that, they strapped him to the bed that they used for 'forcible conversions,' to keep his thrashing to the minimum. And there had been plenty of thrashing. After the thrashing came the withdrawal symptoms--headaches, nausea, and the like. After that, the night terrors. Then, the convulsions, the howling--oh God, the howling--and a little bit of foaming at the mouth. Then, one night, the False Prophet closed his eyes. The next morning, for the first time in almost nine months, the Wise Guy opened his. As his eyelids cracked open, they were instantly filled with bright light. He screwed them shut again, but it wasn't quite enough to keep the light out. 'Blkkahhkkhh,' he said, trying to speak through a severe case of cottonmouth. 'Please, Brother Seth. If you could refresh Brother Wyatt's palate?' Something cool pressed against Wyatt's lips, and his eyes flew wide open. The first thing he saw was Seth, pouring something into his mouth. Wyatt gurgled and sputtered for a moment, then spit it out into Seth's face. 'Screw you! You're not getting me with that shit again!' he screamed, and tried to leap out of bed. He made it about a fraction of an inch, restrained as he was by the sturdy leather straps. 'Please relax, Brother Wyatt,' the Reverend Seck calmly said. 'We want to help you in every way possible, so that you may return to the Lord's embrace. But first, you will have to control yourself.' 'Get fucked, you lizard! When I get out of this fucking thing, I'm going to make you eat it!' He struggled against his bonds, but still made no progress. 'Jesus,' Seth muttered. 'He wasn't this rabid when he was actually foaming.' Wyatt snapped his head toward Seth, ready to go after him next--again, had he not been completely pinned down. Eventually he saw the pointlessness of the struggle, and he darted his eyes around the room in an effort to find out what was going on. He was in a room with dull, gray walls. Seth stood immediately to his right, holding a glass pitcher full of a clear liquid. Probably water--which would explain a few things--but Wyatt wasn't about to trust it. Down by the foot of the bed was one of SCCW's jobbers, Mac Deesul, looking like he was one math question away from shutting down. Reverend Seck's chair was placed immediately to Wyatt's left, with Ibrahim staring very intently at the restrained man. Connors could feel the hot stink of Seck's breath against his cheek. Wyatt couldn't see anyone else, but he knew there was one more person in the room. 'Good. Perhaps now we can talk like civilized adults. I suppose you're wondering why you have been...secured...in such manner.' 'Oh, I can think of several reasons why I might wake up to find myself strapped to a bed. Only one of them is good...and judging by the fact that none of you are a high-class Mexican hooker, I believe we can rule that one out.' If memory serves, that's where we came in. 'A man has come to me asking for help, Brother Wyatt. And it is help that only you are able to give.' 'Then your man can choke on his own balls for all I care,' Wyatt snapped. 'I wouldn't piss on your head if your hair was on fire, and that goes double to any of those slack-jawed assholes who follow you around.' 'Are you sure? Wouldn't you like to meet him first?' The corner of Seck's mouth twitched upward, forming a smirk. 'I don't give a shit who your mystery guest is. If you don't let me up, and let me out, then there is going to be hell to pay.' Another voice provided the response. 'Interesting choice of words.' Connors tried to crane his neck in order to see the speaker, but he couldn't quite move enough. Fortunately, the other man came closer, giving Wyatt the first piece of good news he'd had in a long time. 'Harley! Harley Goddamn Roebuck! Thank God! Harley, you've got to get me out of here! Call in the rest of the boys and sweep these bastards clean, would you?' If seeing the man was a pleasant surprise, then seeing the look on his face was a crushing disappointment. 'There aren't any boys, Wyatt. It's just me...and I'm the one asking the Reverend for help.' Wyatt let out a short, bitter chuckle, making it clear that he did not find this amusing at all. 'Is this a joke? This had better be a joke, Harley. Any second now, the rest of the gang will come in, guns blazing, and we'll burn down the whole damn place as we leave. Right? Right?' ''Fraid not, pal. No daring rescues today...and really, I don't know where you're getting that 'guns blazing' shit from. Hell, I haven't even so much as held a gun since I left the Marines.' Frustration put an even harder edge on Wyatt's voice. 'Don't give me that shit, Harley! I've worked with you for years! Hell, I'm the one who got the Squad half their business for a couple of years!' 'The Squad? You don't think--' Connors' face went red as he interrupted Harley with a fit of screaming. 'Yes, the Squad! The Tijuana Fucking Hit Squad! Hired thugs? Murderers? Bad-Asses-To-End-All-Bad-Asses? Doesn't this ring any bells?' 'Sure it does, Wyatt. You know it does.' Wyatt felt the anger subside, and a small amount of relief washed over him...but that was all too short-lived, and his brow knotted as Harley continued his response. 'That was the gimmick you gave us when we started out.' A smile played around Ibrahim's lips like a snake around a tree branch. 'So the fabled Tijuana Hit Squad wasn't real?' 'Oh, they were real. Nasty sons of bitches, too...right up to the point where they all got killed in an FBI shootout, back in '97. Wyatt read about it in the paper, thought that hitmen would make a great gimmick, and gave us the call.' The last statement sent Connors into a near apoplectic fit. 'You're trying to make my brain explode, aren't you? Why are you lying to me?!' 'Ah, shit,' Harley muttered, as he buried his face into his hand. 'I was afraid of this.' 'Afraid of what?' Seck asked, as if he were amused by the entire spectacle. 'He doesn't even remember what's real and what's a lie anymore. He's been playing the part so long that he can't tell the difference.' 'Fascinating.' Connors, however, did not find it fascinating. 'You're the one putting on an act, Harley. What the fuck did I ever do to you, that you're going to stab me in the back like this?!' Harley just looked down at him, half-shocked. 'You don't remember, do you? You don't remember a thing.' 'Remember what?!!' Harley Roebuck reached into his back pocket and pulled out a ratty brown leather wallet. He opened it, and removed a small photograph, which he then held in front of Wyatt's face. There were three people in the photograph; two men flanking one woman. The man on the left was tall and burly, with a shaved head. He wore the dress uniform of a United States Marine, and had the face to match. The woman in between them was pretty, but a little plain. Shoulder-length brown hair framed bright eyes and a kindly smile, and a green dress covered her body rather well. The man on the right was short, almost as short as the woman, with light brown hair that refused to stay down. He wore a short-sleeve white T-shirt, blue jeans, and an ugly knot of an even uglier tie. He also flashed a big, cheesy grin on his face, and had his arm wrapped around the woman's waist. In fact, if nobody had told you, you'd never guess it was... 'Oh, Lord. Is that...is that me?' 'We took that the day I came home from the Marines. Had a buddy of mine hold the camera.' Wyatt's eyes nearly bugged out of his head. 'This...it just doesn't make sense...' Nothing felt real. Was the room spinning? Or was that just a side effect from the drugs? 'Remember? We had that shot developed a couple times, because we all wanted one. You used to have yours stuck to the fridge with a magnet that looked like a bunch of grapes.' Harley put the picture into Wyatt's right hand, and then undid the buckle that secured that arm to the bed, allowing Connors to get a closer look. 'I...don't remember...' He took the photograph in his hands, turning it over once or twice. It was an old picture...fifteen years at the very least. He then took to staring at the people again. Could that really be him? The guy in the uniform looked a little like Harley, sure, but he wasn't a very descript person to begin with. Yet, no matter how long he looked, his eyes always went back to her. 'You remember her, though. What was her name?' Wyatt racked his brain. It all looked familiar, but nothing was clear... 'It was...Shir...Shirley? Cheryl?' 'Cheryl, that's right.' Harley smiled; it looked like he was finally getting through. 'Now...can you tell me how you knew that?' Wyatt didn't answer at first, and he seemed to lose focus. He muttered something under his breath, too quiet for anyone else to hear. All the while, looking at the picture. Then, all at once, his eyes sharpened. He looked at--no, straight through Harley Roebuck. 'Because it's written on the back of the photograph, dipshit. Next time, try pulling the other one. It's got bells on it.' He tossed the picture away, watching it flutter through the air for a moment before pointing an accusatory finger at the man he once thought was his friend. 'I just got done sleeping off a heavy dose of bullshit from these other assholes, I don't need you feeding me anymore. What the hell are you up to, Roebuck--and don't give me any of this 'forgotten life' garbage.' 'It ain't garbage. We took that shot just outside the airport in San Diego. Afterwards we went to a Padres game, and then you put us up in your hotel room for the night, before we all headed back home the next day.' 'Really. So who are you then, that I'd be doing all this for you?' Harley shook his head, sadly. 'Shit. And they said you were smart.' For a moment, their eyes locked...and this time, Wyatt turned away. Perhaps he was afraid of making any kind of connection...or maybe one had been made, and he couldn't face it. 'I'm your brother.' Again, Harley looked to Connors for a response. Again, it was less than optimal. 'That's the most ridiculous thing I've ever heard.' 'Oh? Then answer me this. Why is it that I know all these things that you've never told anyone else? Huh?' 'Because you're lying...and quite poorly, I might add.' 'Okay, then. How come I'm the one you always call when you're in trouble? And every time you've called on me, I have never let you down, not once. Not in Phoenix, not in New York, not that time you called me on the phone screaming about how Pierce was going to skin you alive and you needed to disappear--even this time, right now. You begged me for help, and I have always come through for you. Explain that.' Wyatt was less than convinced. 'Because I paid you extremely well,' he scoffed. Harley scoffed right back. 'Trust me. There's no amount of money that would make it worthwhile to put up with all of your bullshit. Some stupid hired muscle wouldn't go to the wall for you like I have, time and time again.' The man on the bed merely smirked, completely confident in his assessment of Roebuck's story. 'Look, I appreciate the effort you're putting into this. No, really. Few people enjoy a good lie more than me. But I really think it's time for you to give it up.' The bigger man shook his head, and looked down at the man tied to the bed. 'God damn you. You're going to make me do this, aren't you?' Nonplussed, Connors replied with a scornful, 'Do what?' 'You made me swear on our mother's grave that I would never do this, not until it was all over. And I've held to that. I've never spoken a word of the truth until now, and I've never even thought of doing this. But now...now, I don't have a choice. You need to remember.' Slowly, he leaned into Wyatt Connors' ear and whispered something. It was so quiet that even Wyatt could barely hear it...but whatever it was, the effect was dramatic. Connors' eyes glazed over, then rolled back into his head, which promptly fell back onto the pillow. His right arm draped over the edge, lifeless but for a few spasms here and there. Then, the eyes opened again...and when they did, it was as if it were a completely different person. Not much had changed in his body language, restrained as he was. But the eyes made all the difference in the world. Eyes that were once cold and analytical were now soft...and tired. Very, very tired. It was Harley Roebuck who broke the silence. 'Gentlemen,' he said. 'I'd like you to meet Frank Watkins.' The man strapped into the bed felt a tear roll down his cheek. It was the first time he had cried in almost twenty years.
