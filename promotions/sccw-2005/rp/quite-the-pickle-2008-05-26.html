Date: 05/26/08 YESTERDAY 'Basil...basil...ah, basil.' Let it be known that if you should ever want to tailor your physique to that of a professional athlete, following Hulk's rules will only get you so far. Train, say your prayers, take your vitamins--all sound advice, but the blond lawsuit magnet left out the importance of having a significant other with the ability to cook the hell out of some vittles. 'Dash of margarine...bloop!' Unfortunately, when your Myspace profile hasn't read anything but 'Single' in the past five years, and the extent of any relationship you've been in consists of a customary 'gift transaction' at the end of the night, you start to take an active interest in the Food Network. 'Yousa pretty bird!' A poorly-imitated female voice responds. 'Why thank you, Charley! I suck yo dick for that!' 'Nah, baby, I'm happy to help out a damsel in distress.' 'I've always depended on the kindness of strangers,' the bird responds. 'HEY! Wait! What are you DOING?' A chuckle, as Crisp lifts the cookware containing the bird of prey and carefully slides it in the oven of his suite kitchenette. 'I trusted youuuuuuuuuuuuuuu!' 'Bake, bitch!' However, the acquired talent of culinary art over a dating relationship gives you plenty of opportunity to develop dialogue with inanimate objects. A fetishist, a delusionary, a professional fucking wrestler...Charley Crisp was batting 1.000 in the psychological nutbar department. 'Sauté...flambé...and sashay!' Cookie drops a clear casserole dish cover on top of a boiling container filled with baby carrots, simultaneously flipping the stove knob to the 'SIMMER' setting. Taking a running start, Cookie slides to the end of the slick kitchen floor, squeaking his argyle socks along the tile and pointing to the television with his oven-mitted hands. Nothing terribly unappealing about this Risky Business-esque lifestyle, you know? Surely any nosy neighbor donning a pair of binoculars could appreciate a guy doing the cabbage patch in Power Rangers underwear, wiener posting through the left leg of the elastic grip. Unlucky you, as you thought maybe you'd get through at least one scenario involving Crispy Cook that didn't feature a dick--whether his or someone else's salami impersonator. In regards to the reason Vincent Matthews got tagged with the Mayhem persona, Charley no longer believed in 'it's a good size' anymore. The night of Temptation, after a couple of aspirins, a phone call was made to an industry friend to check the cost of an enlargement apparatus approved by the Swedish government. $159.99, and a money-back guarantee: six centimeters in six days, or two embarrassing transaction lines on your credit card statement. It was a small price to pay to ensure that he'd be the number one contender for Mayhem's 'Wrap It Around And Use That As a Belt' Championship. Then, he could always hold out for an unfortunate chainsaw accident. Charley leaped over the arm of the sofa and landed with a gentle thud on the cushions. Retrieving the remote from behind his ass, he flipped the buttons to 67, hoping to catch a glimpse of his own handsome mug on television. 'Being called a (muffled edit)cocksucker(end edit) isn't personal?' 'No. It's two nouns combined to elicit a prescribed response.' 'Oh...fucking RIGHT ON!' No, it was not one of his circulating spots on SPIKE, but the only other thing on television that could bring a smile to his face: the network's tri-weekly showing of the movie Road House. Granted, this plot had more holes in it than a UWF storyline*, and any woman who claims she enjoys the flick allegedly had a penis at one point in his/her life, but there was just something about Patrick Swayze kicking everyone's ass within boot radius and proclaiming that pain does not in fact hurt that gave Cookie a half-stack. Not literally, you fucking perverts. Maybe if we were talking Dirty Dancing, but still... Engrossed in the flick that he could probably have recalled word-for-word, Charley probably wouldn't have given a second glance at 'Yackety Sax' omitting next to him on the lamp stand. However, the first glance revealed an unknown number, and the lingering suspicion that it might just be a certain wife who shot him a certain glance that could certainly spell a crippling if he were to certainly try some sneaky antics led him to answer immediately. 'This Charley...talk to me.' 'Charley Crisp?' A puzzled male voice was the receiving end of the greeting. Oh, shit. 'Uh-huh. Who's this?' 'Oh, hello. Listen...I'm sure you don't know me, but the name's John...John Taylors.' Charley scratched his head. The name certainly did not ring a bell. 'Nah, don't know you.' 'Oh...well, I figured Amanda never told you about me.' Cookie's attention slowly came to order, as he quickly stood up from the couch upon the mention of his sister's name. 'I haven't spoken to her since I left. How do you know her?' The man known as John Taylors gave an uncomfortable laugh. 'I made the mistake of marrying her two years ago, so I guess you can say we're acquainted in that aspect,' he responded. 'Shit...that must have been one hell of a hangover.' John, laughing larger this time around, trailed out in his speech. 'You aren't kidding. Listen...first off, I don't want to creep you out or anything, but I had to jump through hoops to get your number...and I'm positive you aren't happy about it, but I really need your help.' 'Nah bro...it's no problem. Must be serious if you went to all this trouble. What can I do for you?' John's good nature quickly took a turn downhill, as a sigh revealed the desperation in his voice. 'Well, the mistake I made came with a blessing...it's all a hell of a lot to get into over the phone...so I was wondering if we could maybe have lunch. I assume you're holed up near Chicago right now--not a fan of the whole wrestling thing, but I couldn't help but see a few commercials...' 'How I love the celebrity life,' Charley mused with a laugh. 'Must be nice. Anyhow, I'm gonna take a couple of days off work and book an overnight out there--that is, if you're willing to hear me out.' Charley's response was immediate, as he picked up his former brother-in-law's plight like a bloodhound at a murder scene. That, and he felt a connection to relate to someone who obviously had been put through hell by one of his siblings, which was a subject he should've been given an honorary doctorate in. 'Absolutely, John. Sounds serious, and my schedule is pretty lacking until showtime.' A deep exhale of relief. 'You don't know how much this means to me, Mr. Crisp.' 'Please...'Charley'...we're past the business pleasantries.' 'Right...Charley. I'll give you a ring when I get settled tomorrow. Thanks so much, friend.' 'Have a good flight.' --------------------------------------------------- TODAY 'Club sandwich, no mayo...and, my love?' The waitress, a portly number in a sky-blue uniform with dirty blond hair piled on top of her head in a bun, blushed at the charm of Cookie's address. 'Yes, dear?' 'Could I get an extra pickle? I loooooooooooove pickles.' Most pickles in these little road car diners don't serve Mayhem-sized dills, don't you know? 'I'll get you an extra pickle, no problem!' Flo gingerly collected the menus off the table. 'You're a slice of heaven,' Charley smiled, giving her a wink. The waitress giggled as she walked on air back around towards the counter. It was a staple of the Cookie mantra...there was no woman on Earth he wouldn't sweet-talk to get an extra spear of flavorful goodness. John was tickled pink at this display of bullshit, smiling warmly at his once-brethren. 'Damn, you're a regular Terrence Mann.' 'Who?' 'Terrence Mann...big name on stage...played Rum Tum Tuggler in Cats...Inspector Javier in Les Misérables,' John explained, a wealth of theatrical information pouring forth like a fountain. 'Sweet...I guess. I would've accepted Swayze or Cruise, but I'm sure...Torrence Munn...is cool, too.' John chuckled a bit out of politeness, obvious to him that he and his ex-relative had very little in common--and that Charley was the polar opposite of his former wife. He was definitely a lot better looking, for starters...examining the ripples that shaped his exposed arms and upper chest around the neckline of his wife beater, exposed via an unbuttoned plaid flannel. And those big brown eyes, calm as a desert scape, certainly couldn't have swam in the Crisp gene pool. John caught himself staring, but immediately snapped out and collected himself, remembering the reasoning behind this meeting in the first place. 'Once again, thanks for meeting me. I know it seems a little far-fetched, but I think you're the key in helping me get what I need.' Charley smiled, close-mouthed, and reached across the table to pat John on the shoulder. 'You facing quite a divorce, I presume?' 'No...thankfully, that part is over,' John stated emotionlessly, reaching in his back pocket to retrieve his wallet. Pulling it out, he flipped the fold upright and handed it to Cookie. 'That right there is what's important now.' A solemn grin...half-shock, half-delight...formed on Charley's grille. 'Oh man...she is...adorable...' He pulled the photograph closer to examine the cutest little blond-haired girl he had ever seen, decked out in a pink dress with a stitched yellow duck on the front. Her toothless smile radiated through the gloss and seemed to omit a warmth from the photograph. 'Her name's Leyla...your niece,' John stammered. 'I just hope she gets the opportunity to meet her Uncle Charley one day.' Charley took a glance at John, slightly blinking his eyes shut for a moment. He knew that the man staring back at him was not the reason that this beautiful child had no idea a nappy-headed bro of an uncle existed in her life...never once had she laid her beautiful blue eyes on him. 'I hate to ask you this...but can I have it?' Charley asked, hope lingering in his voice. 'Of course. I have plenty more photos at home.' 'Thanks,' Charley responded, carefully taking the portrait out of the plastic sleeve and setting it next to the napkin holder on the table. 'She's threatening a custody battle.' 'How'd you guess?' John humored, rhetorical hint not taken by Crisp. 'You seem like a good guy...I highly doubt you were a bad father or an abusive husband...' 'You'd be correct, sir.' Charley nodded. 'So why the divorce? I'll admit this, Leyla sure as hell didn't get her looks from her mom, as you're a good lookin' fella...an affair, I take it?' John sighed and shook his head in response. 'Kind of. I mean, I didn't technically cheat...but you're fucking asking for trouble in a marriage if you don't clear your Internet history on a nightly basis.' 'So you flogged it to a high bit rate...big deal? I mean, Amanda is a total bitch, but I don't see how that constitutes a split when you've got a child to raise, right?' 'It does...' John looked out through the plate-glass, observing the traffic creep past in slow motion, touting the hustle and bustle of the Chicago afternoon. '...if what's on your history is a palate full of files featuring two grown men.' Charley joined his gaze, no response illicited. For a brief second, he contemplated a knowing nod, but felt it best to put the issue at ease. 'So you see my case isn't all that strong. It's a Wyoming court circuit, and I don't know if you've ever set foot in that hole, but it's not exactly 'rainbow-friendly,' if you know what I mean. Not to mention her bank account could afford Johnnie Cochran being reanimated to argue her case.' John paused, as his voice began to strain. 'I can't BEAR the thought of never seeing my daughter...it would absolutely DESTROY me. I'm at wit's end, Charley.' 'Sounds like it,' Charley agreed, a sympathetic tone lacing his throat. 'Where do I come in?' 'I don't know if it'll help, but I've heard stories about both of your childhood upbringings. About her teenage years...she and I shared drunken laughs at your expense.' 'Glad I could be the butt of your enjoyment,' Charley sneered. 'I'm really sorry,' John stammered quickly. 'I thought it was funny at the time...before I realized what a...what a...' 'Cunt,' Charley interjected. 'Yeah,' John laughed. 'What a...you know...she could be.' Charley smiled, examining the picture once again, tracing a finger along the outline of Leyla's bright complexion. 'You've got a case on the fact that her father was virtually nowhere to be found throughout her life too...and how that ended up molding her awful personality.' 'Never even thought of that,' John replied. 'But you've got a point.' A sighing exhale officially became overplayed in that seedy little restaurant that day, this time from the pipes of our little brown mess of an antihero. 'I just don't see how a testimonial from someone she hasn't had contact with in ages is going to help you, John. That, and I'm not exactly a halo-toting seraphim when it comes to good moral character. You think a court that can't accept a gay man's plight for raising children is going to take me seriously...a straight man's plight for having a paddle hit my ass by a leather-clad woman?' 'Who knows? All I'm asking is for you to help. It's my little girl, man. It's your little niece. Your little niece that you'll NEVER have a chance in hell of seeing...except in that photograph right there...unless I gain part custody.' 'I'd like to think that you wouldn't just use me to get what you need, then forget that her uncle exists...but I'm not exactly versed in the world of trust,' Charley mumbled, looking down at the table and fiddling with the rolled-up napkin containing the greasy silverware of the Chi-Shack Cafe. 'I can promise you that won't happen.' A plate consisting of a mediocre sandwich complete with THREE pickles abruptly halts the conversation. 'Enjoy your lunch, fellas,' the waitress beamed, smiling down at Charley.
