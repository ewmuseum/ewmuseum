Birth of a Warlord

11 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/11/2009 "War is a game that is played with a smile. And if you can’t smile, grin. And if you can’t grin, keep out of the way until you can." – Winston Churchill * * * Eldora, Iowa. Beep. Beep. Beep. "Hmmph." Beep. Beep. Beep. "Ugh." Beep. Beep. Beep. Light crept into the second story apartment, illuminating the yellow ceiling, a half dozen half full coffee cups, a single roach as it scurried across the floor, a fourteen inch TV, and a twin size bed in the center of the room. Beep. Beep. Beep. A hand reached out from under a fortress of covers, and turned the alarm off. A few moments later the rest of the covers were peeled off, revealing the face of a thirty something year old man. He had blonde hair, almost down to his shoulders, and a soul patch on his chin. He had recently suffered a black eye, by the looks of it. The man’s physique was impressive, if not imtimdating. Tribal tattoos that he had regretted for years were present on both muscular arms. Meet Miles Cavanaugh. He sat up in bed, and looked to his nightstand. Besides the alarm clock, there were two items present on said nightstand. 1. A picture frame, sitting face down. 2. A pistol. His hand lingered on the former momentarily, before gliding past it, and finding its way to the the latter. He held the beretta in his hand for a moment, feeling the weight of it. The gun found its way into his mouth, as if often did. Miles held the position for a moment, imagining what his brains would look like splattered against the wall behind him. "Nah", Miles said aloud, his voice muffled by the gun. He put it back on the nightstand, and began to get ready for work. * * * Ugh, Miles thought. "Where the hell were you?" "Took a long shower." "Jesus Christ, can’t I even get a worthwhile excuse? Like a traffic jam?" "In this town?" "Well shithead, ya got a point there." Miles and his boss, a short stocky man named Tim (who appeared to be years younger), stood outside the rear entrance of Gordie’s bar and grill. Cavanaugh was dressed in a mustard stained Gordie’s t-shirt and jeans, while Tim wore a much cleaner version of the same shirt. "And are you to tell me the place is jumping already? There’s only two cars in the lot," Miles said, shielding the sun from his eyes with his hand. "That’s not the point," Tim replied sharply, "if I can’t rely on you for the slow times, how do you think you’re ever going to get scheduled on a Saturday night?" Miles did not have a good counter for this logic, so he simply stared off into the distance. "Get in there," Tim barked, "the old guy is sucking down Gin like it’s going out of style. Apparently no one’s told him that it has." "Yes sir," Miles replied with as much enthusiasm as he could gather at ten in the morning. He brushed past the day manager, and opened the back door. Upon entering the establishment the three patrons at the bar were nothing but silhouettes, thanks to the sun lighting them from behind. He could barely make out anything at all. But as he got closer, details became more apparent. The walls were decorated with beer signs, and sports memorabilia. This was not the kind of place where people went to debate the meaning of life. The man sitting to the far left was Charles. He was a drunk, recently laid off from the plant. Miles couldn’t even remember what kind of plant it was. Charles wore a plain white t-shirt, had strands of gray hair going in several directions, and had what were rumored to be the yellowist teeth in the county. "Hey pretty boy," Charles bellowed, upon Miles’ entrance. "Your boss is squirting his pussy juice in the drinks again, I didn’t come here to drink Coca Cola." "I’ll fix ya up something real good Charles, just be patient for me." Miles assumed his place behind the bar, and eyed up the other two gentlemen. One of them was a nervous looking balding man in a flannel shirt that seemed way too warm for this time of year. He was avoiding eye contact with Miles, mumbling quietly to himself. There was no drink in front of this one. The other wore a brown trench coat over a black suit. He took down the last of his drink, and slid it towards Mile, along with a hundred dollar bill. "Gin on the rocks, the best you got… and whatever Charles wants," the man said. "Beam and coke!" Charles chimed in, almost immediantly. "Not sure if I even got that kind of change in the till this early," Miles said, grabbing a 1.75 liter of Bombay Sapphire. "Keep it," the man replied, and lit a cigarette. "You look familiar, what’s your name?" "Miles." "I’m Mr. Hayden, it’s a pleasure." "You got a first name?" Miles asked, as he grabbed the hundred off the bar. He wasn’t all that impressed by rich people throwing money around, but he wasn’t going to turn it down either. "Mister," Hayden offered, his voice free of sarcasm or humor. Miles nodded at that. "Well alright mister, enjoy." He handed Mr. Hayden his gin on the rocks, and Charles his Jim Beam and Coke. "This is Spencer," Mr. Hayden gestured at the awkward man sitting between he and Charles, "he doesn’t say much, but he’s glad to make your aquaintence as well." "Hi," Spencer offered, adjusting his glasses. "Hi," Miles said. "Hi," Spencer repeated, and then suddenly looked embarassed. Miles had an odd feeling about these two. There was something beneath the surface that he couldn’t quite put his finger on. He silently wondered if they were more dangerous than he was. He silently decided they were. "You look like you got all kinds of stories," Charles blurted out, seemingly oblivious of any other conversations going on, "you should tell me a good story." Charles took greedy gulps of his beverage, and stared directly at Mr. Hayden, who in turn took down most of his gin in one swig. He returned Charles’ stare, and it was enough to make the drunk sit farther back on his stool. "I know a good one about a secret order created to maintain balance in the world, employing men and women of all ethnic and moral backgrounds." Miles almost laughed. Mr. Hayden did not. "There’s all kinds of interesting characters, like this old man who was kicked out for philosophical differences," Hayden started, taking another drag of his cigarette. Miles noticed how stressed out this line of conversation was making Spencer. "This old man, he never stopped though. Doesn’t know what else to do with himself." "Hah!" Charles snorted, "sounds some kind of dork ass movie!" Mr. Hayden nodded, a cloud of smoke lingering around his face. "You know what kind of characters end up winning in those stories, Mr. Cavanaugh?" Miles glanced up from drying a pint glass, a look of confusion registered on his face. "Never told you my last na…" "The ones that see an opportunity coming, and take it. They make the big move," Mr. Hayden interrupted, and glanced at his watch. The sound of a vehicle pulling into the parking lot was heard by all. "Your big move is now. Mine is later. Maybe I’ll seeya down the road, kid." "Sounds fun," Miles said, not knowing how else to reply. "No, it won’t be." Mr. Hayden took one last drag from his cigarette before putting it out in the ash tray. Spencer hurried to the back door, and held it open for Mr. Hayden. This duo managed to exit the bar at almost the same exact time that another duo came through the front door. To Miles, this didn’t feel like a coincedence. In fact, things had never felt more connected in his whole life. The two newest par patrons stood in the doorway. Miles’ mouth opened slightly in surprise. "Holy…" * * * Blue Ridge, Alabama. "…shit." Jeb Stewart, former Affliction bodyguard and FUSE wrestler, looked up at the sky. It had begun to sprinkle. He was at the Blue Ridge Memorial cemetery, standing at a plot near and dear to his heart. Directly in front of him was a modest stone. The name was "Renee Stewart". Below the name it said: 1956 – 2009. Jeb, standing near seven feet tall, looked larger than life. A black XXL FUSE t-shirt and blue jeans covered his frame. His gaze shifted from the sky, down to his mother’s grave. He had been here everyday for months, paying his respects to the only person he had ever respected, the only person he had loved. He wondered if most people ever understood what it was like to really feel loss, and promptly decided he was being melodramatic. People lost spouses and kids all the time. Surely that had to be worse than this, right? He had only kissed two girls in his life, and had a hard time imagining these things. The rain intensified. He allowed so many things to build up these last few months. Mostly, hatred. Amy Campbell, that miserable bitch. If she wouldn’t have broken his bosses’ neck maybe the doctors would have kept getting paid. Maybe the experimental treatments would have continued. Maybe he wouldn’t be staring at this grave stone. He heard a vehicle approaching, and didn’t bother to turn around immediately. Sometimes he would fantasize about one of the doctors coming home from work, putting their coat away. They would walk into the kitchen, thinking that their wife has dinner ready, and everything is okay in the world. But there was no dinner. There was only Jeb, wrapping his gigantic hands around the neck of one of those puny little bastards. And he’d just squeeze, and squeeze, and squeeze. Was he really capable of these things? He wasn’t sure. But on certain days, he wanted to be. Jeb could hear the car stop, and a door open. He finally turned around. "Well I’ll be," Jeb remarked, and trailed off. * * * One day earlier. The office was exactly as Spartan as Lane imagined it would be. Desk in the center of the rear wall, short bookcase beside it. The Big Slick bracelet – Desade's reward for six months wearing a mask and pretending to be some goodie-goodie – rested on the palm of one of those ceramic hand sculptures. All seemed a little too creepy, but he didn't ask any questions. He never asked any questions. When he watched on television and saw Desade in the pantsuit, in the trenches like a general, pulling schemes on the fly, Lane would sometimes wonder what she did in her off-hours. Did she wear the suit all the time? (The answer to that was 'No,' though the blazer was hanging from a hook by the door.) Weirdest part of the room was the picture on the desk; a keepsake for a life Stevens doubted existed. It was an older couple that bore some resemblance to the Spider, the man accepting some reward. 'Thank you, Mr. Pierotti,' the banner above read. He didn't know who 'Pierotti' was, but Lane didn't ask questions. 'Have a seat, Lane.' Desade spun a well-used leather desk chair away from a pair of computers off to the side. 'Thank you for seeing me.' The River Rat sat down very carefully, as if his chair might be explosive. He prided himself on not being taken off his game, on not being intimidated, but this was quite the spectacle. There was a reason he was on the side he was, and this was it. "Of course," he said, his head still on a swivel, "what’s the good word?" 'The good word is Monterrey, but that is not truly your concern, Mr. Stevens.' She wheeled the chair back behind the main desk, all of it precisely organized. She didn't meet his gaze, not immediately. 'I am finding the climate of Sin City is not... it isn't what it used to be.' Slippage into more common parlance was one of the few tells the Spider had that was obvious. She wanted something from him. 'I mean to fix that, and I am going to need your help to do that.' Lane suppressed a smile. As indifferent as he acted from time to time, for as smarmy as he could be, he liked being needed. "Children don’t fear the ruler anymore, I’m afraid," Stevens remarked. Pierce's sudden glower caught him slightly off-guard. 'You stop throwing people off cages, and that is what happens, I suppose.' Finally, her hard gray eyes met his murky blue ones. 'I doubt it would surprise you that professional wrestling is a lark for me. A game I rather enjoy, but a game, nevertheless.' "I’m aware of some of your other…pursuits," her WMD started, "but this one seems to be one of your favorites. What is it you would have me do?" He surprised himself at how serious he was taking this. 'I need to split focus.' Her fingers ran across a stack of papers for no other reason than to even it. 'I need some breathing room, and your reputation as a loose cannon is something I cannot afford to overlook. Put together a team. Handle it. I will point you, but I will stand out of the way.' Lane’s eyes had a way of making him look younger, despite the beating that Dusk and Aimz had laid upon his face. They had a way of dancing with ideas, and mischief. "It would be my pleasure, Director." 'Make no mistake.' Her words were sharp. 'If I call you off a target because I need them, you will stop whatever you are doing to fit my team in. If you make it more difficult for me, there will be consequences.' He sat back, as if absorbing the impact of that statement. "Wouldn’t dream of it," The River Rat replied, and winked. * * * Miles Cavanaugh saw the man in the doorway, he saw him clear as day. But he could barely believe it. "You son of a cunt," Miles said, his emotions bouncing back and forth between enraged and elated. Laughter filled the room. "How’s it hanging barkeep?" Lane Stevens replied, a smirk forming at the edge of his mouth. Standing behind him was Roderick Ashe, who was sweating profusely - and as hungover as anyone in the entire state. "Fuck you," Miles seethed. * * * A man holding an umbrella approached Jeb Stewart from behind. After the initial shock of seeing his former tag-team partner walking towards him, he turned back towards his mother’s grave, preparing his next words carefully. "Heavy rain at a cemetery. It’s so fucking cinematic. Now all we need is that shot looking straight down on you…as you scream NOOOOOOO!!!!!!!!" Jeb shook his head, and actually had to stifle a smile. "Hi Lane." "Hi Jeb. How’s things?" "Been better." "Well as a matter of fact that’s why I’m here." * * * "I should kill you," Miles practically hissed, throwing aside the rag he was drying the glass with. He stepped out from behind the bar. Charles was taken back by all the excitement. "What the fuck?" he tittered. Mr. Ashe started to step forward, but Lane put a hand on his chest. Which is just fine by Roderick, as any more sudden movement might make him throw up. "Eh, that’s one way of looking at it. I doubt it’s the most profitable way, however." There was decades of history here. It was threatening to spill out all over this bar. "Could I get another Beam and coke?" Charles said, and nudged his bartender. "Not now old man. Not now." Even in Charles’ highly inebriated state, he understood the look on Miles’ face. It was a look he had only seen on a human being once in his entire life, right before his father had killed the family dog for biting his baby brother. His Dad had lifted a shovel high in the air, and a grimace registered on his face for a moment before he brought it down, caving in the animal’s skull. So the drunk turned around and minded his own business. "I am in a position to make things right," Lane said, backing up slightly. He noticed the look too. "I’ve just been this…this fucking guy for years. Working in this shithole. Every morning, waking up, just…dude, fuck. FUCK!" Miles had forgotten what it was like to be angry. He had put a gun in his mouth every morning for years; it was how he got ready to go out into the world. It was not done for sympathy, for there were no eyes watching. It was something he considered, and gave thought to, every single morning. But now, in the face of his former friend, he was angry indeed. "You come here to rub your success in my face, Lane? It’s not enough that I see you on TV, riding in limos and all this shit. You have to bring the show here? What, you going through some kind of storyline where you’re human now or something? You fucking twat!" The River Rat laughed. "No, this is the storyline where I offer you…" * * * "…a job." Jeb’s brow furrowed. "Didn’t know you were in the hirin’ position. Pretty much looks like you’re doin’ Desade’s laundry from here." "Well yeah, I do that sometimes," The River Rat started, and Jeb wasn’t sure if he was serious. "But things have changed slightly." "How so?" For a minute or so, rain was the only sound. Lane was trying his best to answer in a way that would still appeal to the big man. "My way of doing things is…preferred, for handing certain targets. I have been given permission to recruit my own team, so here I am." "But you’re sided with…with her." Lane figured Amy Campbell would come up. They both stared at Renee’s gravestone then. Lane’s hand had a way of finding his neck, his formerly broken neck, when Amy came up. "I’ve become quite fond of her, actually…but I think our alliance is temporary. You might have the chance to crush her sooner than you’d think." "How do you figure that?" Jeb shot back. What Lane wanted to say to Jeb just then was that Alex and Amy had fallen into some kind of love. And that this business doesn’t support love. It rips it out of you, twists it, and crushes it. But he hated to say such cynical things to his former tag-team partner. "Just a hunch," Lane offered. * * * "Are you shitting me?" "No," Lane shook his head, "I’m not. You may not trust me, and I don’t blame you. We’ve been through a lot, you and me. But one thing you can trust is that my employer will not accept failure. I would not be here unless I needed you, and the fact that I need you means that I’m not going to fuck you." "For now," Miles growled. Lane considered this, and nodded. "Yes, for now. But now can last a long time. I live with the idea that my employer will one day toss me away into the garbage heap, but I can’t let that get in the way of…now. And surely what I’m offering you has to be more intriguing than the life you have built for yourself." Lane leaned closer, taking the volume in his voice down, "You were on the top of a very short list. I don’t need mopey Miles. I need the fucking monster." He punctuated that sentence by jabbing his index finger into Miles’ chest. The bartender stared down at the finger that poked his chest. Just then the day manager, Tim, emerged from the back. "For Christ sake get back behind the bar and do your jo…" He never got a chance to finish his sentence. Miles Cavanaugh pivoted suddenly, throwing the pint glass he had been drying across the room, striking Tim directly in the side of the head. Glass shattered on the ground, but Tim was not conscious to hear it. Charles gasped, and Mr. Ashe looked taken aback. The River Rat, however, looked completely unaffected. Miles promptly marched over to his fallen manager, and gave him one violent stomp to the face, letting out an almost primal grunt as he did it. He walked over to Lane, with the same kind of intensity, the same kind of purpose. Cavanaugh stopped for a moment, right in front of his former friend, and Mr. Ashe was ready to pounce if necessary. But instead of violence, Miles hugged the River Rat so hard Lane could barely breathe. "You guys got lawyers, right?" the former bartender asked. * * * Stevens and Stewart were walking towards the limousine. The former talked, the latter tried his best not to cry. It was hard for him to come here, even months later. "I used to think I was going to be a movie star or an astronaut when I was a little kid. I thought I could be something great. Everyone thought I was so smart. And I just kind of coasted on that potential for years, until eventually it dawned on me that I just didn’t have it in me, ya know? Maybe I was just too lazy to be great, to really challenge myself. I just wasn’t that guy that was going to have a happy ending. So I’ve been just trying to have as much fun as possible, until something absolutely horrible happens to me. And it will, trust me. If you play the odds, I don’t see how I’ll make it another five years. I just don’t see the point in all this shit. I see no great purpose, no great truth. Your mother did, and I envy her for such a thing. She was a good woman, and I mean that." Jeb nodded at his former partner’s statement. Though he was still obviously choked up, he still replied. "Thanks Lane. Where we goin’?" "Arizona." "I ain’t never been to Arizona." The River Rat opened the limousine door for Jeb, who paused for a moment, looking back at his mother’s stone. "I hope there is a heaven, but I hope she’s got plenty of stuff to do other than watch me." Jeb said, "Cause I don’t really feel like behavin’ no more." The big man stepped into the vehicle. "If there is a heaven, I think they’ve been having some signal issues the last couple years. I haven’t been struck by lightning yet or anything like that," Lane replied with a bright smile, and followed him into the car. A few moments later, the limo sped off. * * * An hour later, a text from Walter Halvorson to Alexandra Pierce will say the following: Your Warlord has found his Warriors
