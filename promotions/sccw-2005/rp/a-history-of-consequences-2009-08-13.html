Date: 08/13/2009 Present Day "Danny, you gonna drink this?" She held up the soda can as though it was the greatest treasure in the world, but for somebody who had never been able to sort out their priorities this wasn’t even the slightest bit strange. Danny knew this, and so he didn’t bat an eyelid as he turned around to see her looking at the can with glee and sheer delight. "No, apparently you are." She smiled the way a little girl smiles when she gets her own way, and took a victorious sip. For an onlooker, it would seem as if she had just achieved everything her life had built up to, and the grimace on Danny Vista’s face told of nothing but animosity for the disgusting display- at least, that’s what he considered it- before him. "Isn’t this great, Danny? Me and you free together." She spoke with pride, the way an alcoholic speaks once he has beaten the addiction. "What do you think?" Her eyes were those of a puppy dog desperate for affection. "I really wish you hadn’t followed me to the SCCW. You’re meant to be starting your life afresh." Danny didn’t relent, and it was a burst of honesty that had escaped him against his will. She looked at him, greatly confused. "Baby, I thought you meant together. Me and you against the world!" "We’re not an item; I don’t even like you! Nobody likes you. It’s me and the world against you." He had hoped this comment alone would set her straight, but this was merely wishful thinking. "I didn’t think you were gonna come with me, I just thought we were both heading north..." His final sentence of the day to her seemed to go in one ear and out of the other. The vacant smile stood firm on her face, and the delight in her eyes stayed as wistful as ever. Selective hearing, he thought it was called. But then, her selective hearing and other bad habits had gotten them in trouble too many times before. But from now on, Danny thought to himself, he’d never be getting in trouble again. ***** 15 Months Ago The office looked like it came straight out of a movie. Even ignoring the stereotypical spiral staircase which Vista had walked only moments ago, accompanied by two guards he felt entirely unnecessary, there was something unnerving about the perfectly furnished office he now stood in. Two of the walls were lined with shelves packed with dusty, unread books, and lavish rugs were laid underneath a leather couch and a brown, luxurious desk facing it. Surrounding the desk was what could have been an indoor primrose garden, and at the far end of the room hung a perfect replica of the Mona Lisa painting. It was all very grandiose, and suddenly Danny Vista felt out of place. He’d been to big houses and he’d seen rich folk before but, as far as offices went, this was the top of the spectrum. This was the crème de la crème. He looked around for any sign of the man he had intended to meet, but the office was empty; it could have been a showroom for all he knew. The two guards that accompanied him to this point also seemed confused at the emptiness of the room. They took a few steps towards the other end of the office, looking for him in places he simply couldn’t be; under the desk, in between books on the shelves and, at one point, underneath the large rug. It seemed the search was futile, and so the two guards stopped. One of them directed an unfriendly glare at Vista, until his attention was taken away by the other. "What are you doing?" "I’m sitting down, Michael." The man being lambasted by Michael had taken it upon himself to plant himself into the leather chair that was previously tucked into the bronze desk. At the precise moment he had done this, a look of a mixture between shock and fear crept on to the Michael’s face. "If Charlie sees you doing that he’ll have your head. What’s the matter with you?" The comment was said with an attempt at indignation, but the high pitched tone it was squealed could do nothing to hide the fear. Vista was ready to turn around and do just as he had said- these guys were not to be messed or disagreed with- until he heard a loud banging noise and felt a stiff vibration on the floor directly underneath his feet. He shamefully panicked and jumped somewhat, causing laughter to erupt from the other two in the room. However, this laughter died down when all three men instantaneously caught a glimpse of a man emerging into the room on the other side. A tall, well-built man in an Armani suit looked to be dusting off this suit, before brushing his shoes. The broad shoulders and the height would have got anyone thinking he was a perfect image, but his face told a different story. Though it may have once been handsome, it was now covered in small scars. The nose was crooked, and the hair was thinning, but his eyes were still as strong as ever. Vista suddenly realised how the man had apparently appeared out of thin air; beneath the spot he was standing, Vista noticed a small hatch with a handle. There was a secret room underneath the office, although what had caused the vibration and noise Vista could not figure out. "Hey, boss." The guard named Michael suddenly seemed to reveal himself as more than a guard. The man in the suit, no doubt the most important man in the room, embraced him in a quick, masculine hug, before his attention was diverted to the man sitting in what Vista realised was his chair. "Lucas, you appear to be sitting in my chair." These were the first words Danny had ever heard the man speak, but these alone were enough to convince him of the power this man held. He reminded Danny of an old, dominant lion; wilting in appearance, but powerful in the eyes and throaty in his voice. The simple way in which he spoke, eloquently but imperatively, was truly terrifying. "I’m sorry, Mr. Castle, I didn’t realise you were around." Lucas’ voice was shaking, his nerves coming through. "I can hear everything you say up here from down there," Castle spoke sternly. "And is that the way to act around your superiors? Is that your philosophy? Undermine me, as long as you don’t get caught?" He spoke the last word especially ferociously. "No, I...I wasn’t thinking straight." Lucas was panicking; he knew what was coming. "The only thing I demand from everybody who works for me is respect." He glanced at Danny while talking, as though alerting him that this was a fact Danny would need to understand. "Without respect," he added, "there is no order. I would have thought you had learnt that by now." Lucas gulped. There was no way he was getting out of this now. He tried to start speaking, tried to defend or explain himself, but nothing came out as Castle began talking again. "Lucas Romeo, I want you out of this office. I want you out of my business. You should have known your place. Get out." He didn’t need to raise his voice; his growly voice was authority enough for Lucas to do exactly what he said. Lucas slowly walked out of the office, his face telling only of regret. He knew there was no way back, and that he had ruined his chance. Castle then ushered Vista to sit down on the leather sofa that opposed his desk. As he beckoned Vista to look around the sizeable room with his arms out wide, Vista thought he resembled a mighty Lord, blushing with pride as he overlooked the kingdom of heaven. He had obviously spent years working hard to earn this fortune and respect. This was why he had been so harsh on Lucas Romeo. "You can spend years earning the respect of the people below you, Danny. All it takes to lose it is one display of disrespect like young Lucas’ just now, and word would spread. You lose respect the instance you stop demanding it." He smiled surprisingly warmly. "I know." Vista couldn’t bring himself to say anything else; his overwhelming concern that he would say the wrong thing stopped him from doing so. He needed this job, and needed the money. An elongated moment of silence passed between the three men, before formal introductions became the next item of discussion. It seemed that Castle was avoiding discussion of the job for now. "My name is Charlie Castle, although I imagine you’d guessed that by now. This here," he motioned to the man standing to his left with his arms in front of him. "Is Michael Raine, my right-hand man... so to speak." Vista and Michael Raine shared a look between them that did not speak of respect. Though Castle was friendly enough, for the head of an underground crime group, Raine was eyeing Vista suspiciously. He did not trust Vista, of that there was no doubt. "It’s a...pleasure to finally meet you both." Vista spoke cautiously. "I was really hoping to start work for you straight away, Mr. Castle. To be frank, money is tight ever since I left the WWA and I really need this job." Charlie Castle continued to smile down upon Vista, although it had now transformed into a more patronising smirk. Castle and Michael Raine glanced at each other and Raine failed to suppress a grin; the way Vista’s hands were clutched in his lap made Michael think he was going to say ‘please sir, can I have some more?’ any moment now. Nonetheless, Vista seemed sincere, and so Castle took it upon himself to ask further questions. He knew he was more than likely going to hire him anyway, but it didn’t hurt to explore his mentality a little bit further. "So Danny, you only in this for the money?" Castle looked closely at him. Vista wasn’t sure what answer Castle wanted to hear. He could say he was in it because he respected Castle and respected the business, but something told Danny he would not tolerate lying. "Well," Danny started nervously. "Basically, yes. I wouldn’t take this job if I didn’t have to." Vista paused and looked up at a momentarily frowning Charlie Castle. "But if I take on a job I put every-fucking-thing I have into it. I’ll refuse to quit until I’ve got the job done, and I’ll give you the respect needed." Vista looked up, trying to see how his answer was perceived. He felt as though this interview was almost literally life or death. Castle’s frown suddenly turned into a grin, almost looking amused at his answer. "I like your honesty, Danny." Vista smiled impatiently, still not knowing what the verdict was. "Sharply dressed, too," he glanced at Vista’s shirt-and-trousers attire as he said this. "I like that, as long as you don’t mind getting your hands dirty. And with honesty in mind, I’ll be up front with you. We deal in a lot of things that are frowned upon by the city; loan sharking, forced protection, theft and drug dealing, that kind of thing. You okay with that?" Vista didn’t hesitate; he needed the money no matter how dirty the job. "I’m in," The two stood up and shook hands warmly while Michael Raine watched from afar. "I’d like to stay away from dealing drugs if I can though." "Ok, Danny. We’ll see what we can do." Vista walked towards the door, ready to leave now that he had got what he came from. As he walked, though, he felt a weight still pressing upon his chest. He turned around, and looked curiously at Castle and Raine. There was something he just had to say. "I’ve just been...forced into this, you know. I’m not a bad person." He spoke as if he believed this more than he had believed anything in his life. Charlie smiled knowingly. "No man who walks through that door ever is." ***** One Month Ago "I don’t wanna be taking risks like that again, Charlie." Vista and Castle sat in the corner of a strip club, discussing the latest job. Though the scene appeared innocent enough to onlookers, what they were discussing had recently been headline news. A bank robbery had taken place in the National Bank of New York, arguably one of the highest security banks in the country. "Maybe you’re right...I’m just starting to wonder if the money is worth it all." "Most people would kill to be in your position, Danny." Castle smiled; there was something ironic about that statement, he thought. Pondering his next move, Charlie ignored the obvious tension between the two of them and threw a booklet of documents down on the table, before pushing the subject forward. "Here’s your next job." Danny looked down at the documents and was almost certain a little part of him was dying inside with each job. Looking around, he was surprised that this highly suspicious-looking meeting hadn’t caught anybody’s eye; he supposed they were distracted by the naked pole girls, the dimmed lights, the naked pole girls, the heavy flow of alcohol and the naked pole girls. He was tiring of it all. He was tired of the risk he put himself in every time he took on a job, tired of having to see Layla every damn job and tired of answering to Charlie Castle. He scanned over the specified job on the documents. "Yeah, I can do that...as long as I don’t have to take Layla." Vista brought his eyes up to meet Castle’s; he wanted this to be a deal breaker. "She can do a good job, Danny, and you know it," Castle said indignantly. "Just because you don’t enjoy her...flamboyance, doesn’t mean she isn’t good at what she does." "She’s an idiot!" Castle’s jaw clenched, mostly because he now wasn’t appreciating Vista’s tone. "Remember, I’m still your boss Danny...Layla might make mistakes from time to time, but without her a lot of our jobs wouldn’t work. She’s an asset...but if she ever knew that, she’d demand too much, so that little comment stays between us two." He unclenched his jaw and even winked at Danny as if the argument had never happened; he’d said his piece. A good five minutes of silence passed between the pair, and Castle took advantage of this to start talking to one of the ‘dancers’ passing by. Vista, meanwhile, sat and stared into open space. Where was Michael Raine right now? Why hadn’t he attended this meeting? Raine would get Vista in a lot of trouble one day, of that Danny had no doubt since a long time ago. Maybe it was just the paranoia Vista had built from being involved in this world for over a year, or maybe Vista was onto something. Until something actually happened, however, he could do nothing, so he turned his attention back to Charlie Castle. "So where’s Michael tonight?" Vista tried to look nothing more than curious, although he suspected Charlie knew what he was getting at. "Michael’s out on other business," Castle shot him a knowing glance. "Don’t worry; I know what he’s doing. Layla will be here in a few minutes Danny-" "-a few minutes?! I haven’t even had time to prepare, Charlie! I’ve got to get weapons, get the right people, make sure none of the wrong people are there, make sure that-" Vista spoke with an exasperated tone. How did Charlie expect him to do it so quickly? "-Layla has taken care of everything. She’s got everything in the trunk of her car, and tonight is the night." Charlie’s reassuring words failed in their bid to reassure Danny, who stood up and wandered towards the door. Charlie didn’t chase after him. He didn’t need to; he knew Danny was going to get the job done. As Castle sat back and relaxed with the dancer he had been talking to, Danny wandered outside, and saw Layla’s old Camaro already parked at the side of the road. He walked towards it, and as he got closer he could see her grinning to herself with a cigarette hanging lazily out of the corner of her mouth, listening to something on the radio. He opened the passenger door and climbed in haphazardly, before reaching over and pinching the cigarette out of her mouth. He threw it out of the window with an almighty smirk on his face, squinting his eyes ironically to gauge her reaction. Nothing gave him more pleasure currently than getting on Layla’s last nerve. She punched him on the arm and cursed under her breath. "Whatcha do that for?" She said indignantly. "Not while I’m in the car, Layla." "It’s my car," she replied defensively. "I’ll do what I want." Sighing and swearing to herself once more, Layla drove off at speeds permanently faster than the limits, and this was just one more thing about Layla that Vista disliked and distrusted when on the job; it was little mistakes like this that could cost them the money they were going for. Vista continued to stare straight ahead, but after a while Layla began to giggle and look over at Danny playfully. He did not respond, but she didn’t seem to care. "We make good partners Danny. "You telling me you don’t think we’d be great together honey?" She asked genuinely, and perhaps hopelessly. "That’s exactly what I’m telling you. Just keep driving, and shut up." Both had always looked at the other with an air of suspicion and distrust, but the similarities between the two men could not be ignored; both were tall and relatively well-built, both were sharply dressed and both were somewhat handsome. Although, while Raine had a rugged handsomeness, Vista was more of a pretty boy; people always said he had a likeable face. "So Danny," Castle began to speak in an unusually jubilant manner. "Michael tells me that you’ve been doing everything asked of you without fail." At this point Raine turned his head away with a sneer; it was clear that he didn’t want anything to do with Vista being congratulated, and that Vista’s successes came as a great disappointment to him. Nevertheless, Castle continued to smile. "Are you happy?" The blank expression on Vista’s face didn’t change despite the verbal pat on the back Castle gave him. Vista liked Castle for the money his jobs had given him, but as a person Vista was suspicious of him. It was the same with anyone in this business, Vista thought to himself; he would never fully trust, or respect, any of them. But he needed the money, and getting out would be nearly impossible. Thus, he lied. "I’m good, boss." But Vista couldn’t help himself. "It’s just, I-" Castle interrupted Vista before he could say something stupid. "I know what you’re thinking, Danny." "You’ve been pairing me up with that Layla chick every time," Vista said as though he was confronting a truly magnificent problem. "I don’t wanna work with her anymore." He shifted his eyes to the ground, hoping Castle would see his side of things. To Vista it seemed as though several moments of silence were passing through, blinding him with regret for even demanding anything from Charlie Castle. However, it seemed his luck was in. "Okay, Danny," Castle announced loudly. "As long as you say so, you don’t have to work with Layla." It seemed too good to be true, and so Vista stood up quickly and satisfactorily; he almost wanted to run out of the room before Castle could change his mind. Castle followed suit and stood to his feet, shaking hands firmly with Vista before watching him walk out of the room. As he closed the door, Castle turned to his protégé Michael Raine, who appeared to be biting his lip. "Well, Michael, say what you want to." Castle spoke this as an imperative, not a suggestion. "I don’t trust him," Raine burst out surreptitiously. "I think he’s only looking out for himself. You don’t have his loyalty." Castle considered his next move carefully, before continuing. "Danny Vista has never disrespected me, Michael." He looked at the younger man as though teaching his child a basic fact of the world. "As long as he is loyal, he will come under no threat." ***** 4 Months Ago "Come on, Danny, I need this."She spoke with desperation. The words were a worthy substitute for nails on a chalkboard for Vista. They seemed to pierce his ears with a frustrating simplicity, and what made it all the worse for him was that this veritable malaise of his senses was being accomplished on his own doorstep. Only moments ago he had been relaxing on his sofa, nursing a glass of wine and listening to what he considered to be the greatest piece of music of all time, the Clair De Lune, until a loud and abrupt knock on his door interrupted his rare moment of peace. Opening the door, he couldn’t imagine anybody worse to have been behind it. There stood Layla, lately the bane of his existence, with the most pathetic pout he had ever seen on her face- and that was saying something. "Need what?" He spoke vociferously; her sheer presence was enough to ruin his night. The truth was he knew what she was talking about. She was referring to the past couple of months, which Danny had considered some of the greatest of his life. He was rising to the top of the business, and only 2 months prior Charlie Castle had finally allowed him to stop working with the girl standing in front of him now. Lately, however, she had been trying her hardest to get back in with him; he was rising to the top and she was being left behind. She’d visited his house unannounced like this three times already in the past fortnight, and each time Danny had simply slammed the door in her face. Jobs were easier without her, and days were unbelievably more pleasant. If he was going to be involved in this world, he didn’t want to have to put up with somebody like Layla in the interim. His hand began to move forward of its own accord, ready to slam the door once again, but for some reason Vista stopped it and allowed her in. He was feeling a strange moment of compassion. She strode through the hallway with a purpose, and turned around to face Vista. She would have smiled, but the ever-present cigarette would have fallen out of her mouth; with her priorities the way they were, this would have been a fate worse than death. "Danny, I-" She began to plead, but Vista interrupted her impatiently before she could really start. Holding his hand up, his face still resembled one of disgust. "There’s no smoking in my house." His facial expression didn’t change even minutely. "Wow, you gotta thing about that don’tcha?" She grinned, pulling out of her mouth with her index finger and thumb. "Where you want it?" He didn’t flinch; when she was around, his sense of humour was non-existent. "Not in my house." He brushed past her into the living room, and after a few seconds of tapping his foot, she waltzed into the room abruptly cigarette-less and looked at him coyly. With her infamous mischievous smirk, she stepped slowly towards him as he stood motionless, simply looking at her. "Look baby, we both want something," she started slowly. "I want to work with you again, and I’m willing to bet there’s something I can do for you if you let me..." Vista burrowed his eyebrows and looked at her perplexed. Was she...? "I know you must be lonely, Danny..." she continued, more excitedly the further she got. "You’re a dangerous man, and you don’t like to put yourself around anyone else. Her driving had never been smooth and relaxing, and that was why Vista had prepared himself for the borderline-crash by clinging on to the seat and the handle of the glove compartment. The slight grimace that had become almost permanent to his face since getting into the car with Layla refused to relax, even as Layla turned to him and smiled apologetically. Vista wondered why she did this, considering she lacked the basic emotion of regret; perhaps she had learnt when to display it, as a domestic dog learns new tricks. And that brought into his mind a curiosity as to how that was possible, considering the phrase about old dogs and new tricks, but he digressed. He surveyed the scene around him, stepping out of the car and taking in a lungful of countryside air that he rarely had access to. This was short-lived, however; it was only moments before this was interrupted by the dirty smoke spilling from the engine of Layla’s aging car. She herself stepped out of the car as soon as this violation of Vista’s lungs had begun. Oblivious as ever, she walked round towards Vista and ignored his spluttering and coughing, instead choosing to try impressing him by keeping to the task in hand. He had always said she was distracted easily, and part of her wanted to prove him wrong as she motioned towards a row of trees in the near distance. "They’re in there," she spoke softly, as if someone might overhear them within the miles and miles of vacated countryside they stood in. Vista popped the trunk of Layla’s vehicle, looking down at the weapons and notes inside. The job they had been given was one Vista had become all too familiar with; they were to oversee a large drug deal between two groups of people they had no affiliation with. This was a more common job than any other, and Vista was nearly always the man to do it. Today, as scrawled on the notes by somebody with impressively scrappy writing, a deal was taking place to exchange around two kilograms of cocaine for $100,000. Naturally, both sides were suspicious of the other, and so Vista was brought in to ensure a smooth transition for a reasonable price. They began walking towards the trees, where the deal would take place. "Danny?" She could have continued to her point, but she always liked to know Vista was listening. "What?" He sighed, knowing the next sentence or question would almost certainly not be about the job they were about to face. "If the dealers try to stiff the buyers, what’re we meant to do?" This had never been the outcome in the deals Vista had advised over, but the fact Layla was still thinking about the job brought a moment of sympathy; perhaps he was occasionally harsh to her. Nonetheless, the answer was a simple one. "Shoot them." He spoke with a deadly serious look on his face, and motioned his hand to the gun in his back pocket he had taken from the trunk of the car. However, it was highly unlikely that this would be the outcome. After all, both sides had agreed to Vista and Layla ensuring a fair deal took place. They walked within a few yards of the trees and looked around for any sign of the groups they were looking over. Both deadly silent, they stepped forward, allowing the only sound to be the crunching of leaves and the flattening of grass beneath their lightly treading feet. There was something eerie about this place, Vista thought. Something wasn’t quite right. Nevertheless, they continued to walk forward and, just as Vista was about to suggest they turn back, they heard several muffled voices about a hundred metres away. Using pure instinct, they both started to run towards the voices instead of away. They reached the last tree group and walked past it, as they saw two groups of people surrounding two separate cars. This was the deal. Yet, the closer they approached, the more they realised something wasn’t right. Vista casted a sideways worried glance at Layla, who reciprocated it with a worried widening of the eyes. Slowly but eventually, they came within easy sight of the situation, and to their confusion saw both sets of people with their arms raised in the air. Sure enough, moments later they heard a voice directed at them, and it wasn’t a welcoming one. "This is the police; we’ve got the area surrounded! Put your weapons on the floor and your hands in the air!" The commanding voice came from somewhere in the trees surrounding them, but Vista had no idea which direction. They had no choice but to begin to put their weapons to the floor, but this action was short-lived as a shot was fired just to Vista’s left and both instinctively dived to the floor. "Oh shit. Oh shit. Oh shit. Oh..." The panicking came from Layla, and instantly Vista felt repulsed. Had she been the one to snitch to the police? He didn’t know of any motive she would have, but somehow he felt it would be a ‘Layla’ thing to do. Shots began to fire from all directions around them, and it soon became a panic of sprinting feet around them and approaching policemen with shields. Staying on the floor, Vista dragged the nearly-weeping Layla behind the nearest car as a few of the dealers began to fall shot. Now policemen were running around the dealers and buyers, and all were armed. The bulletproof shields being carried by the law made for less officer deaths, but Vista was certain he saw at least one fall wounded. All of a sudden, in a whirl of panic and confusion, Layla pulled herself away from Vista and began to run in the wrong direction, screaming as she went. Vista saw as a dealer only inches away from her was shot, and saw how the same policeman who had fired this shot tackled her to the ground. The sympathy Vista had felt only minutes earlier seemed to have an effect; he didn’t want to see her arrested, so he ran out to where she was and knocked the officer out cold with his gun. He didn’t want to shoot when he didn’t have to. Grabbing Layla, who by now was on the floor with her hands on her head, Vista started running back towards the car, unable to do anything about the gunshots and bullets that Vista was certain were flying around and between their heads. Vista considered it a miracle that the pair of them had reached the car without injury, but he supposed the police had been too caught up with the dealers and buyers who were actively trying to shoot them to bother with those running away. "What the fuck?" Layla seemed just as genuinely shocked and pissed off as Vista, and so he didn’t feel the need to ask if she knew anything about it. "Somebody snitched." With that fact firmly registering in their minds, alongside a certain curiosity as to who did it, Vista and Layla jumped into their car and drove away, simply relieved that they hadn’t been caught. Or worse. ---------------------------- Twenty Minutes Later ---------------------------- It was extremely rare that Vista allowed any kind of situation to overcome his cool and calm exterior, but the saying ‘desperate times call for desperate measures’ rang true at this moment in time. Sprinting up the spiral staircase, Vista and Layla ran into Charlie Castle’s office with nothing but anger to show for the night’s work, verbally showering the room in a delightful range of expletives. Vista kicked the infamous bronze desk in frustration. Several questions were running through his mind. Who grassed the deal to the law? Was it Castle? Was it Raine? Did Castle know he was sending Vista into such a dangerous situation? Vista wanted answers to every single one of them. "What the fuck is Castle playing at?" Vista sent spits of saliva in all directions as he struggled to keep the decibels to a minimum. "Danny, sit d-" Her pleading was futile. "Shut the fuck up, you cannot possibly be calm about this fucked up situation!" He was seething, struggling to hold anything back. "Well how do you know it was anything to do with Castle?" She asked genuinely, thinking she had a valid point. "It could have been somebody from the dealers, or the buyers or something..." Valid as her point was, trying to reason with Danny Vista now was impossible. Any anger or disappointment he had built up over the last fifteen months were coming out in one expletive-fuelled rant, and the more he continued the more scared Layla became. He finally seemed to notice, and gave her something of a proposition. "Layla, we gotta get out of this." He tilted his head, trying to second guess her response. "Get out of what?" Layla had no idea what Vista was suggesting. He should have realised, Vista thought, that Layla was still the same Layla who took three or four explanations to get anything. "The business. We gotta stop working like we are, you know. Start our lives fresh." Layla knew what he was trying to say, but she thought it was ludicrous. Vista didn’t even know why he was trying to convince Layla to get out too; perhaps the slightest ounce of sympathy was still there. "But what about Castle-" She was interrupted by an exploding voice. "Charlie Castle is a fucking idiot! He’s ignorant and oblivious to what we do, Layla, I swear. He wants us all to be fucking martyrs!" He was breathing heavily now, feeling more emotion than he had felt in his entire life. "But what if he-" "We could go further up north. Hell, maybe I’ll start wrestling again!" He probably wouldn’t, but Layla knew how hard wrestling again would be for Danny, and Vista wanted her to know he was fully serious about this. "He’ll find us." "So fucking what? As far as he knows we’re still ‘Castle’s men’ through and through, right? The old bastard lives off respect, and as far as he’s concerned we still respect him." Vista was more certain about this than he had ever been about anything. "How do you know Castle won’t-" "Fuck Castle! Fuck him." Layla stood and looked uncertainly at the heavily-breathing Danny Vista. Was he seriously considering running away from it? Was she seriously considering running away? She needed the money more than anything, but she could see how desperate Danny was. She slowly thought about it, and then finally replied. "...We could write a note?" She eventually said. "What?" "A note telling him we’re leaving. He thinks we adore him; if we write a note saying we’re out and ask him not to find us, that could be it." Vista looked at her for a moment. "Yeah, okay. You do that now, and I’ll get us a car." She grinned at him and to herself as he turned away and made a call on his cell phone. Writing their note of freedom, she couldn’t help but feel things were going to turn out for the best. ***** Present Day "So who’s this Darius Drannen guy then?" Vista had always hated people who asked too many questions- it was why he promised himself he would never have children- and this hatred was multiplied further when the questions were being asked by somebody he already felt a deep contempt for. Too many times in his life Vista had had to put up with being around those he would happily see fall of the face of the planet, and so his grudge towards Layla was, in his eyes, entirely justified. "I don’t know," Vista replied quickly and bluntly. "Some guy who joined SCCW when I did." His answers to her questions were never more than a few words. Why should he have to talk enthusiastically to people he didn’t even like? That’s how he saw it, anyway. The truth was, matters from a month ago were still fresh in his mind, and every time he glanced at Layla he couldn’t help but wonder if she had snitched the deal to the law for some reason. This continuous thought had apparently vanquished any possible sympathy Vista had started to feel for Layla. Sure, he wanted to make the very best impression on his SCCW debut against Drannen, but his mind was balanced between thoughts of this and thoughts of the snitch. He couldn’t shake it. Nevertheless, he’d been training hard, working hard and relaxing hard. Those three things were the three vitals of success, he thought. "Layla," Vista started asking beyond his control. "I’m just gonna ask you this once..." "What’s that, baby?" She said flirtatiously. Incidentally, her pet names almost made Vista gag; he barely liked her at all, let alone in that way. "You didn’t snitch that deal to the cops, did you?" Curiosity had certainly been getting the better of him lately. "No!" she exclaimed innocently, however her face quickly changed to one of typical mischief. "I don’t have sinister motives, Danny. I just wanna be around you..." She winked at him and walked past him, but by now Danny had become invulnerable to her weird flirtatious attacks such that this didn’t register with him at all. "Good," he started. "Because he may be a fucking idiot, but anybody who gets on the wrong side of Charlie Castle has got a death wish." ***** One Month Ago Rarely does one walk into a room and see everything relevant that is within the walls. This had rung true many times for Vista and, as he walked out of the room behind Layla as she finished her letter and placed it on Charlie Castle’s desk, he couldn’t help but feel as though he had missed something. As Vista closed the door of the room he hoped never to see again behind him, a movement to overshadow the importance of all other movements at that moment happened back inside the room. The trapdoor that Charlie Castle had stepped out of at his first ever meeting with Vista cracked open once again, and out stepped Michael Raine into the office. He touched his feet onto the floor hiding the secretive room below and a shocked grin appeared suddenly on his face as he remembered the time Castle had appeared from the trap door at Vista’s hiring meeting, ready to send Lucas away for good. The surprised grin still etched as fully as ever on his face, he took his cell phone out of his pocket and pressed in a phone number. "Hey boss, it’s me," Raine spoke with delight and glee. "You are not going to believe what I have just heard the golden boy Vista saying in your office."
