Nick Cave & the Bad Seeds – Red Right Hand (Let Love In, 1994) 24th of April, 2009. Seth Renner rarely got any time to himself. These days, the homicide squad was his life and any time spent with his family was a bonus. Renner wished he could get the job off his mind and hoped these few days he, Irene and his daughters, Jenny and Kylie, were spending together in Portland might allow him to switch off and play the family man for a while. Just for a couple of days at least. The dinner they stopped in for lunch smelt of grease and cheap coffee. Ladies, old beyond their years, rattled around behind the counter with pens behind one ear and a cigarette behind the other. Each time they bustled past Seth could smell their last cigarette break, mixed with their corner-store bought perfume, and it got him to thinking he wouldn’t mind a smoke himself. He reached into his sports jacket pocket and plucked out a cigarette, holding it up for Irene to see. He sported his best Cheshire cat grin and began to rise from his seat, much to his wife’s disappointment. "Awww, Seth. We’re about to order. Can’t you wait ‘til you’ve ordered?" Irene threw a defeated hand into the air. "Just get me a coffee and one of them." Renner pointed at a man chewing on a sausage and fried egg roll. What looked like some sort of home made chutney dripped out of the end. He called to the man. "What’s that?" The man told him it was the house special. Seth thanked him and smiled at his wife. "Coffee and house special." Weaving through the bustling waitresses, Seth eventually got to outside. He hadn’t been to Portland, Oregon, since he was a kid. He loved it then and he figured that he could love it enough now. That maybe the girls would love it. Maybe the change in scenery could help him to switch off. He walked along the front of the diner, peering in to give Irene a wave. She replied with a shake of her head and a roll of her eyes, a slight smile was trying to burst through her façade. Seth didn’t mind. They’d grown accustomed to each other’s little nuances and if she wasn’t used to his nicotine fixation yet then she never would. Seth had contemplated giving up but he didn’t think he could be as good of a cop as a non-smoker as he was a smoker. Hell, he didn’t think he could be as good of a person if he smoked, police work aside. He put the Camel in his mouth and patted down his pockets for a light. The search was fruitless so he turned his eyes to two guys at the end of the diner already lit up. "Hey, fellers." He raised a hand and clicked his thumb up and down. "Got a light?" The pair both looked at each other and sighed. It was an unwritten law amongst smokers to carry your own cigarettes and your own lighter. Seth had broken one of those laws. Albeit, the more trivial. But the code of smokers said that one of them had to supply the light. One began patting down his pockets as the other watched expectantly. He pushed one hand into his inner jacket pocket and held out a light for Renner who replied with a smile. "Appreciate that." Renner took the light and lit up, taking a long pull on the cigarette before handing back the light. "Thanks. I needed that." Renner noticed the newspaper tucked under the other smoker’s arm. He noticed the headline. Stripper Lost Right Hand. He did a Hanna-Barbera style double-take. "Can I look at that?" The guy omitted yet another shake of his head and plucked the paper from beneath his arm, handing it folded up still, with the headline out, unimpressed by the stranger’s imposition. Renner almost snatched it, but didn’t fail to thank the guy. He stepped away from them to read the story, expectant of the outcome. He held his breath with the cigarette trapped between his teeth as he read. The story was about a stripper at a local gin joint, the Blood ‘n’ Bone Tavern. Famous around Portland for being owned by a wrestler’s father. August Monday. Renner couldn’t quite recall where he’d heard the name ‘Monday’ but was sure it wasn’t unfamiliar to him. He read on to find out that a stripper known as Candy Doll, real name Jaccy Onasis, not the heiress to the Kennedy throne, had been brutally mutilated by an unknown suspect after what appears to have been consensual sex. When Renner read that her right hand had been removed by what local CSI thought to be a cleaver, his eyes near popped right out of his head and he choked on the stagnant smoke in his lungs. "You okay?" One of the smokers graciously put his hand on Seth’s back and gave it a pat. He spoke with almost a redneck drawl and his breath stank of nicotine and bacon. Seth responded with a single hand up. He just needed a moment to gather his breath again. "I’m fine." He pounded his chest with a fist before handing the paper back. "That today’s paper?" The two smokers nodded. One unfolded it to see the story that Renner was reading. "You int’rested in that dead hooker?" "Stripper. I’m a cop from back in Albany. Homicide." Renner felt the urge to flash his badge, but he was so far out of his jurisdiction he figured better of it. "I investigated a case like this last October." "No shit," breathed the one reading. "Wow. That’s pretty weird, huh? Same MA?" Renner squinted, trying to figure out the abbreviation. "MA?" "You know. The same way they gone done it." The guy reading the paper seemed to be racking his brain as his friend punched him in the shoulder. "M-O, you idiot. You don’t watch Law & Order, do ya? Modus Operandi." His friend threw a thumb gesture his way that said, ‘can you believe this idiot?’ Renner couldn’t help but chuckle, despite the topic of conversation. "Right. Same MO. Very good. Exactly the same…" Renner trailed off into his mind, which ran at a million mile an hour, already jumping from conclusion to conclusion but it didn’t seem to make sense to him. The same MO in Albany, on the east coast, as in Portland on the west? Didn’t add up right. "You means you’re sayin’ that some feller up east gone an’ done zackly the same thang as here in Portland?" The man without the paper gave one an impressed whistles. "Don’t they reckon that’s serial killer kinda bullshit?" Renner’s head near spun clean off his shoulders as he snapped it to look at the redneck making all of his conclusions a reality within his own head. He didn’t answer. Just took another draw on the cigarette, looking through the man and back to October 23, 2008. He remembered the scene vividly. It wasn’t everyday you got called into a Motel 6 to find a woman with her right hand lopped off. He remembered the blood. The way it caked in the carpet. How black it was against the stark white. The shimmer of the moon on the cleaver. The chill in the air, like evil had come to stay the night. Most of all, he remembered Brock Shepherd in the middle of the room, clutching the victim in a hug to his body, wailing like a school girl had lost her pet bunny. He remembered seeing Shepherd distraught. How he heaved with each hyperventilation attack. How he gasped for air between the snot and the tears. But it didn’t seem like he were crying over the girl in his arms. Renner knew he was sad. But not for her. Not for Samantha Gideon. Episodio Dos Baby Steps: invigorating Present Day. "I’m excited, Augie." April bustled around the motel room like a moth in a lamp shop. Her heart was pumping at one hundred mile an hour. It’d been a long time since she took a part in a wrestling show and Sin City Championship Wrestling was like a new chapter in her life. A new page where she could change the plot of a story. Her and Adam relocating from New York to Portland, to be closer to her father, was the best move she’d made. Adam was really taking a liking to his grand-daddy, despite August cringing at the idea he was so old to be a grandfather. Fact was, she was a young mother and never expected things to happen like this. She was wise beyond her years thanks to the grounded grandparents who raised her. And the 21 years she’d spent on this earth she’d seen more than most people would ever see in 80. They’d amounted to three appearances now, in Sin City Championship Wrestling, and every moment for April was like a child at Disneyland. Her blood pumped and she got the butterflies. She loved those butterflies. Sure, she was sick before she ever did anything on screen… but she loved those fluttery little devils making her sick. She paced back and forth, her mind on the match at hand against the Kings of Popsicles. "Would you sit down? You’re makin’ ME nervous." August pulled open the mini-bar fridge and glared inside. "Corona? Why don’t they have any REAL beer? Fucken cerveza’s. I wanted to drink Mexican beer I’d go to fucken Mexico, right?" April dangled a mini-bottle of Jack Daniels between her thumb and index fingers she’d collected from a shelf. "They got little bottles." She was sing-song as she said it. "You love little bottles." August pulled a Coke can from the fridge and took the bottle out of April’s hand. He opened up the can and took a long pull before pouring the content of the mini-Jack bottle into the can. "There. A old chip off the block. Old man’d be proud of that one." "Ha! Ernie Monday WOULD be proud." April smiled at her father as he sat down on the end of the bed. These past few events were really growing their stature within the company and getting another win might put them in a title contendership. Lord knows, their debut win was the equivalent of three victories in the eyes of the fans, since April got the pin and gave the Las Vegas Lariat a win over some of his mortal enemies. Tonight was another opportunity to take a step in the right direction. She could tell her father was nervous, but he’d never show it. He was one of those guys that learnt if you expressed emotion you were weak. The most emotion August would show might be a tear if he snagged a nose hair when he was shaving. He liked people to think he was tough as nails. But truth beknown… he was a big, old kitten. Especially to his daughter. More so to his grandson. "Bitch at the counter said we got ESPN. I can only find the bloody porn channel." Monday cursed and held out the remote to his daughter. "Figure it out, would ya? I don’t have the patience for motel television." April took the remote off her father and after flipping through a few channels, she’d come to ESPN in the middle of a sports report. She handed the remote back to August and patted him on the shoulder. "Baby happy now?" She couldn’t help but grin cheekily at her father. He flushed red with embarrassment that she’d shown him up so quickly. "Wise ass. Don’t you gotta put your face on or somethin’?" "Yeah, yeah. I’m goin’ already." April snatched her make-up bag off of her single bed and headed into the bathroom, leaving August in the glory of a sports report. "We leave in an hour. Make sure you put on your angry face. We got an impression to make!" He leant back as he called to her, like it might help the words carry into the bathroom. He sighed heavily as he watched the NBA report, talking up how Orlando were the under dog coming up against the Lakers for the Championships and how they didn’t have what it took to relieve Kobe and company of the crown despite all the analysis during the series which told August that the Magic had everything it took to defeat the Lakers. "At least Michael fucken Jackson ain’t on God damn ESPN." Baby Steps: instigating 24th of April, 2009. It took some time for Detective Seth Renner to convince his wife that he needed to check in on something. She hoped that he’d left his badge back in Albany but when Seth had a bone in his mouth he wasn’t about to let go. She loved that about him and hated him for it all at once. Out on the sidewalk, Renner looked at the neon sign that looked right out of the seventies. It flickered like it were powered by bad electrical wiring. The stains on the sidewalk suggested that more often than not skirmishes had come outside and men had spilt one another’s blood. A patrol car drove by. Renner gave them a nod, to which none was returned. Renner stepped into the threshold of the Blood ‘n’ Bone Tavern only to be greeted by security. He opened his wallet and handed over the five dollar cover charge and stepped into the domain of Ernie Monday. Sleazy music boomed its bass sounds right into Seth’s chest. His shoes stuck momentarily to the sticky floor and he cringed at the thought of what that floor was covered in. The whole place smelt of a mixture of booze, cigarettes and cheap oils. The dingy scent of a dingy strip joint. On stage, a girl twirled around a pole, winding up upside down making a "Y" shape with her legs. Fat business men goo’ed at the sight of her shaven, pierced pussy. They waved greenbacks at her. Elegantly, she swizzled down the pole to be upright and offered her boot for the men to tuck the notes into. Turning his attention from the stage, Renner saw an older man at the end of the bar reading the New York Times. Renner found it odd that someone on the west coast might trivialise themselves with the Times. The east might as well have been a different country to them. He put two and two together and came up with the old man being Ernie Monday. Renner took a barstool nearby him and waved the bartender over. "Something cold." The bartender nodded and pulled open a fridge, putting a beer before Renner before prising the top off with his pocket size bottle opener. Renner reached inside his coat and plucked out a Camel cigarette, patting himself down for that elusive lighter once more. He cursed at the thought of another fruitless search when the bartender stretched across the bar with a lit Zippo to the rescue. Renner thanked him, took his beer from the bar and drank. The old man payed Renner no attention. Seth spun around, pressing his back to the bar, propping one elbow on it and taking another long pull of the beer. It was good beer. He sighed contentedly, a little louder than usual hoping to get the attention of the old man reading his paper. It worked. "Fuck a duck. Don’t you cops no nothin’ ‘bout tryin’ to act casual? You stuck out the minute you walked in here. Dog’s balls. That’s what you are." He folded the paper and put it on the bar, putting his elbows on top of it like it were a prized possession. "Whaddayou want?" "Got some questions." Renner took another swig. Monday looked him up and down. Noting the socks and sandals and chuckling to himself. "Ain’t you out of your jurisdiction, officer?" "Detective, actually." Renner pulled out his wallet and showed his badge. "Detective Seth Renner." "Well, Detective Seth Renner, this ain’t Albany. You’re tryin’ your luck ain’t ya?" The old man waggled a finger at the bartender. Thirty seconds later, the bartender put a cold beer before Ernie Monday. "What brings you here?" "You playin’ dumb, Ernie?" Renner said, never making eye contact but watching the girl gyrate her groin in time to the bass on the stage. "You know why I’m here." "Shit. I ain’t dumb. I just can’t figure out why Albany PD is worryin’ about somethin’ AAAALLLLLLL the way over here in Oregon, ‘tective. You look like you’re on holiday." Ernie pointed at the white socks. "Why’d you have go wearin’ socks and sandals? Is the cop holiday uniform or somethin’?" Renner laughed. "Funny guy. Pretty funny when your dancers get their hands cut off. Don’t you think, Ernie? Tell me somethin’… do you offer a program for amputee strippers to learn how to swizzle that stick again?" Monday got off his stool and up in Renner’s face. "You’re pushin’ your luck, asshole. I dare you to push it more." "Just sayin’, Ernie. Kinda strange, don’t you think?" Renner took another pull on the beer, he now had eye contact with Ernie Monday and didn’t give the old man an inch. "You got some tie to this. I know it for sure." "Oh really? You do, do ya? You some kinda psychic, boy? A regular fucken medium?" "I’m a realist, Ernie. I investigated a case exactly the same back home." Renner swirled his beer around in the bottle, between his thumb and index finger. "Ya see, funny thing is this. A wrestler was my main suspect." "No shit." Ernie looked off into the distance, like he was catching up with his own train of thought. "Who was it?" "Who was who?" Renner said, playing dumb. "Asshole. Who was the wrestler?" Monday sat back down on his barstool. The anger in his face had been removed and replaced with a curious urgency. "You prob’ly don’t know him." Renner continued to dangle the bait in front of Monday’s face. "Brock Shepherd?" There was a flash of awkwardness in Ernie’s face. Just a moment of acknowledgement. Quickly replaced by that anger again and then moving on to dismissive. "Never heard of him. I’m afraid I’m wastin’ your time." Renner shrugged, pulling the last of the beer and putting the empty on the table. That moment was all Detective Seth Renner needed. "That’s okay, Ernie. I appreciate you takin’ a moment to talk to me. Say… you wouldn’t mind if I spoke to a couple of the girls?" "Girls are here to dance, Detective. They talk with their pussies. I don’t pay ‘em to make conversation. Shit. Some of ‘em are dumb as aborted kittens. You don’t strike me as a man who likes anything further than intelligent conversation." Monday chuckled to himself. Renner cringed. "Classy analogy. Guess I’ll just enjoy the show." Monday nodded, clearly unimpressed by the stranger’s brashness. "Yeah. Sure." Baby Steps: inopportune Present Day. Shepherd ran through the night, never turning back to face where he’d been. The urban streets of Augusta, Maine, were like a maze to a stranger. Shepherd figured had he been here long enough to understand the place a little more he might’ve made better sense of them. Every town is planned logistically with a strategy in mind. The fact that Shepherd’s visit was a short one never gave him the opportunity to explore that any further. He was only here for one night. One night and he failed to do what he came here to do. Running at full pace, Shepherd pushed through the thick air, trying to ignore the humidity as it lapped his face. He could feel his shirt sticking to his body as he ran through the night. Dogs chimed in, as if on cue. Porch lights illuminated to the sound of the man running, huffing and puffing, through their quiet streets. Running without any real destination in mind, as long as the destination put him farther from where he was it didn’t matter. Tears streamed down his face as he ran, evaporating to a sticky residue in the Augusta summer. He couldn’t go through with it. Not when he saw the kid. When those big doe eyes came up and rested within his he knew that there was no way in Hell he could go through with it. What on Earth was he thinking anyways? He’d never tried to take the hand of another man before. Was this some sort of homoeroticism he was trying to live through the ritual? Was he some kind of faggot but he didn’t know it yet? Whatever it was, Shepherd couldn’t go through with it. He couldn’t bring himself to do it once he’d seen that kid look at their daddy. Once she looked down the hall and her eyes met her father’s. When she looked at the doorway to mom and dad’s room to find Alexander Dot in all his pyjama glory. One Max Danger. All-Star Championship Wrestling World Champion. So he ran into the night, leaving the door to their home wide open. He was sure Danger had slammed it shut by now. Replacing the deadbolt in the morning, Brock was sure. If there was anything Shepherd did know it was this. The Red Right Hand would not be impressed. He would have to perform the ritual sooner, now that he’d failed. Something impressive. Something more callous. Something more for the Red Right Hand. He would please him with the next ritual. He just needed to find a sacrifice. Someone that would make the Red Right Hand smile.
