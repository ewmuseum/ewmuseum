Calm before the Storm: The Video Tape

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Early January A wind rolls in from the west like an angry whisper, slowly gathering power and growing louder and angrier with every passing second. The wind rolls on, and in its path the weak objects are helpless to stop it, going about in the same path. The leaves flow in circular motions, dust on their tails like a small child after his mother. The image is nothing but glorified wind, knocking over anything in its way. This process is tried and ordinary. This, my friends, is the calm before the storm, and it comes in the form of a video tape. This tape has been in many hands, but has been seen by seldom, if any, eyes. It has circled through federation after federation, and the few who have seen it have sent it back and hoped they would forget such an ordeal. It’s really too much on one’s plate to even consider letting on such a project. One pair of eyes, though, has yet to see this video, and won’t shy away when they do. This pair belongs to Smitty Duluth, and it is he who is holding the video tape now. Without a word, he hands it to an assistant, one more set of hands to hold the tape before airing it to the CEO of FUSE. There is a flicker of static before the tape plays, and STD sees Wilhemmina Brown for the first time. **** "Esteemed owner or promoter of the federation who is viewing this, I want to apologize. I want to apologize for wasting your time, and I want to apologize for getting your hopes up. There is a man on this tape who wants desperately to wrestle for you. He wants to represent your colors as one of your best, to wave your banner as he does the one thing in life he loves to do: fight. "He is strong, he is agile, he is talented. He calls himself Bishop, and I am begging you not to accept him into your federation." The elderly dark-skinned woman staring at the camera pauses, adjusting her hands upon her lap. She is nearly slumped in her chair, the years and stress negatively affecting her posture. The simple lace button-up blouse moves rapidly as her breaths become heavier, although her face remains calm. "He can give you many things, I’m sure. He can give you ratings. He can give you entertainment value. He can give you a check mark in your quota for wrestlers of different races. There is no doubt he will do all of these things. "But he will do other things. Things you don’t want him to do. You see, Tavon – for that is his real name – Tavon is my son. He was always doing things I didn’t want him to do. I guess…I guess I was never really a good mother…" This is cut off for a second, as the tape skips. For only a second, it jumps back to the footage originally on the tape before Wilhemmina Brown recorded her warning over it. But in that one second, a face is visible. A snarling, angry face. Something else is visible as well. There is blood on his chest. But then Mrs. Brown comes back, and normalcy is once again retained. "I wasn’t hard enough on him. I just…he was the only thing I had left. His father was gone. The rest of my family had died, terribly. Tavon was my only thing in this world, and I couldn’t stand to see him unhappy. So I gave him everything I could. It wasn’t much…growing up in Mississippi isn’t easy for anyone. "But Tavon didn’t react to it like others did. He constantly questioned. Constantly wanted. What I couldn’t give him, he found on the streets." She shakes her head slowly as she looks towards her feet. "And that’s who raised him. He learned how to fight, how to steal, how to hurt. And I never could get him back on the right path." She looks up at the camera, a sad smile forming. Her eyes reach to her face to adjust her glasses, but if one were to look closer they’d swear to see the pinky on her right hand brush at her eyelash, as if to catch a tear that was forming. Her hands lower after a moment, and the smile looks stronger. "I’m boring you now, I’m sure. You’re in the business, of course. You’ve seen these things thousands of times. ‘Don’t hire him, he’s too dangerous.’ A tactic to generate attention, something of playing up the prospect to make him more appealing. "Please don’t mistake this as something of the sort. This is not an embellishment, this is not a joke, this is a warning: do not hire my son. Do not bring him into a controlled environment. Do not make the same mistake I did by giving him a chance." A tear falls down her left cheek; she is no longer trying to hide it as she continues her staring contest with the camera. "He’s my son. I love him. I love him so much. But I can’t be blind to it anymore. I can’t ignore the signs. The screams. "I have to tell someone. He wants a new game now. He’s stopped picking fights in bars, and now he wants a new challenge. Please don’t give it to him. Please reject this application and sign someone who is more docile. Someone whose bark is bigger than his bite. "Don’t hire my son, sir. Don’t give him the chance to do to you what he did to me." The video then cuts to a scene of a tall man standing shirtless in a dimly lit bar, his hand beating his smooth dark chest fiercely. He roars, and small spittles of crimson fly from his teeth. **** Smitty Duluth watches the rest of the video without reaction, then signals to an assistant. "Bring me the envelope this tape came in," Duluth tells his assistant, "and give me some stationery." This will be the shortest invitation letter he’s ever written. Dear Tavon, Fuck your mom! Want a job? Think about it, Smitty D
