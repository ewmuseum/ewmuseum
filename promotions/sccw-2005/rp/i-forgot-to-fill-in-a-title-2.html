We're far from obvious this time. -Evans Blue "Cold (But I'm Still Here) Prologue – Not Quite Right September 7, 2007 Rome, Italy Cardinal Keller could tell from the way his body was feeling that he was slowing down. Every ache and pain and little bit of stiffness told him that ever more clearly. Yet his hands were steady as he went about his duties, and his eyes glittered with excitement that he always felt when the Council was to meet. He adjusted his red robes once again, for what may have been the hundredth time since his arrival at the meeting place hours earlier. It was important, he reasoned, for all of them involved with this meeting to look their best. It set a good example for the rank and file members of the Church to follow. The meeting place may have looked from the outside like a tiny church, with its high steeple and small cemetary, but it was anything but. The graves were empty, only there for show. Inside, there were no pews. No altar, nothing at all to indicate that worship took place there. Instead, the only futniture was a long wooden table, its polished mahogany surface gleaming under the effects of polish and wax. It bore no adornments save a piece of paper around every one of the twelve chairs. The old man smiled, his mouth stretched over his few remaining natural teeth, knowing that everything was ready for the meeting. Now, all that was needed was for the other Cardinals and the Council's right hand to arrive. The thought of anybody not coming was, at this point, not thought of by Keller. He bustled around, making sure everything was perfect. It had to be, for these men that were coming were powerful, even more powerful than the Cardinal himself. The Pope himself took advice from these men. Really, the old priest considered himself lucky to be a part of it. "Eight o'clock... it will be soon." The other Cardinals would begin arriving, in groups of two, through various tunnels and secret entrances, any minute now. They had to, for the enemies of the Church were many and their safety was the most important thing. Soon, however, the Cardinal knew that they would use their right hand, the man who did their dirty work, and crush the Church and its enemies. They would be free. His thoughts were interrupted by the sound of a loud scraping noise, as though something heavy were being drawn along the cement floor of the basement. He knew that it would be Baggia and LaSalle. They always arrived first. Indeed, they shuffled slowly up through a doorway, having entered through a secret tunnel in the lower floors. "Welcome, my brothers." His voice was quiet, respectful as he held out his arms to accept the embrace of the two men. "As usual, you are the first here, but I do not expect we shall have a long wait." LaSalle returned his smile, his impeccable white teeth glimmering in the room's dim lighting. He was the youngest member of the Council, at only thirty eight years of age. But what he lacked in experience and age he more than made up for in terms of enthusiasm, intelligence, and sheer common sense. The younger man was a head taller than Cardinal Keller, and he would probably qualify to most women as a good looking man. Keller was quite sure a lot of them were disappointed to find out about his vow of celibacy. He had eyes that were deep blue, a perennial tan that he seemed to be able to maintain with little or no difficulty, and a deep voice that would have driven a great many women over the edge. It was said that LaSalle would have had a promising career in soccer, but he chose the ministry instead, for reasons known only to himself. Holding tightly on to the muscular arm of LaSalle was Baggia, a thin, dour faced old skeleton who was the mentor to the younger man as well as the head of the council. His face looked as though he were dead already, as if the encroaching hands of death had already cinched themselves around his throat and were holding on for dear life. The bones in his old face jutted out through sagging, translucent skin, and his eyes were a watery shade of gray. His walk was shuffling, halting, and he wavered back and forth on his spindly legs as he accepted the embrace from Keller. "It is good to see you again, old friend." The old man's voice was gravelly, as though his mouth were full of pebbles that were rubbing his throat dry. "I trust everything is ready?" "Indeed, Cardinal. Please, sit down and wait. The others will be here shortly." He nodded and shuffled over to one of the high backed, hardwood chairs at the head of the table, settling his old bones into it with a creak of both the ancient wood and his own ancient bones. LaSalle motioned Keller to come with him for a moment as the old man settled in, and he quickly pulled him into the next-door room, where the bodyguards of the old men waited. "He is not well, Keller. The cancer is eating him up inside. I think... I think he does not have long." Indeed, the old man appeared to be sliding off of his chair, unable to support his own body weight. "That is unfortunate. But he is a man of deep and impeccable faith. I believe that he will be healed, LaSalle. I believe that with all my heart." He could tell the younger man was troubled. Baggia had been his mentor and somewhat of a father figure to him for years, shaping and guiding his young life from an early age. Not unlike, the older Cardinal thought to himself with a smile, the way the life of the Council's right hand had been shaped and molded by the Council members. Two more old men, lesser members of the council, had arrived, and they were greeting the old specter at the table as their bodyguards checked out the room. So typical of men of their profession. They were big, hulking monsters, dressed in straight black, wearing sunglasses and heavy caliber pistols in shoulder holsters. None of the three, Baggia, Keller, or LaSalle ever used them. Bodyguards tended to be men out only for money, and the three knew that they needed only the power of the Almighty to protect them from harm. "He has been like a father to me. When he goes, I do not know what I will do." LaSalle's youthful face was twisted with grief barely suppressed. "It is difficult for me to see him wither away like this." "As it is for all of us. He has had a great life, been a man worthy of respect and admiration. And when he goes, he will go to a reward worthy of his life. Do not grieve for him, LaSalle. Do your best to emulate his life, and to remember what he has taught you." More men were drifting in now, a mix of the old guard and young blood. Some wore black robes, others blood red, some young and some old. But all were here with a similar purpose. To direct the only man who should have been there who was not. Keller frowned to himself as he pulled a small black cell phone out of his pocket and punched in a number he hadn't dialed in a long time... Chapter One – Not Over Yet The dream had returned to visit him for the third time in as many nights. And just like the two previous times, it had left him terrified and screaming in the darkness, his light blue eyes open wide. Those eyes were glittering with tears and his body was running with sweat that soaked not only his blue pajamas but the covers on top of him. Joaquin was afraid now, of what he did not truly know. It was, after all, only a dream. Wasn't it? 'Joaquin?' Marie... of course he had once again disturbed her sleep. Damn it, would this never end? 'Are you all right?' She was beautiful to him as she rubbed the sleep from her eyes, her chestnut colored hair cascading around her shoulders, the tiny streaks of gray in it reflecting the room's dim lighting. Her eyes were tired, as he knew she had suffered through this sleepless spell along with him, and that there would be no more sleep for either of them tonight. 'I am fine, Marie. Just.. the dream again.' It had been more vivid, more real, this time. A man named Harrison had stalked his dreams, a man who looked like a skeleton, a specter the like of which Joaquin had never seen. He had an evil soul, did the demon of his dreams, and he tormented Joaquin with words the first night. The second night, he told the sordid stories of his own life, of his murders and his torments and of his evil. And on this night, he had come for Joaquin, and had done so with a vengeance. He could still see in his mind the horrible deaths of his loved ones. He could still see Gabriel, hanging by his neck with a cruel strand of barbed wire... Joaquin shuddered, remembering that that particular death had occured in this very bedroom. Marcel, slumped against the stone walls of the famed Catacombs of Paris, his eyes staring vacantly off into space while rats feasted on his exposed internal organs. His flayed open chest had seemed like an obscene smile, gaping at him with the ribs sticking out like teeth. Little Joan, only a baby, lying with her skull crushed. Marie, shot in the head, her brain matter and blood washing over Joaquin, who held her in his arms as the light left her eyes... it was too horrible to think of. 'It was worse tonight.' She nodded, somehow knowing that her husband had never been as afraid of anything before in his life. She had always had a way of being able to read him, even when he tried to shut her out. 'You can't go on like this, Joaquin. Maybe you should see...' 'I do not need therapy, Marie. This is my mind showing me my deepest fears, my most horrible phobias.' And so it was. He was afraid of losing them, and he had not realized until just now how much he needed them. Perhaps it was the mental stress of his career choices of late, leaving Global and the success he had there and moving to FUSE.. but something had opened him up to the fear. And he had to figure out how to close down again. 'I...' He couldn't think, couldn't even begin to figure out just why this was happening to him. Slowly, feeling as though the muscles in his two hundred and five pound frame were coated with lead, Joaquin heaved himself up off of the bed and to a vertical base, shaking his head and whiping the tears that seemed determined to drip from his eyes. 'I need some time alone, Marie. Please, do not wait up for me.' She opened her mouth, and he knew that she was going to want to stay with him. That was the thing about his wife, she always wanted to help anybody in trouble, even if it meant her own physical comfort and well being was compromised. And he would not do that to her, not with the baby growing in her womb. 'No, my beauty. I'll be back.' As he left the room, he hoped she had understood the tone of seriousness in his voice, the tone that told her there was no use arguing with him. 'My God... what is happening to me?' It was then that the phone he had left sitting on the table in the kitchen began to vibrate, emitting a slight buzzing sound as it did. He picked it up and groaned when he saw the number. He had never heard from the man on the other end of the line when things didn't end badly. 'Hello?' He spoke softly into the phone, knowing that Keller would want him to once again raise arms in the name of the Church. He had told him the last time that he was finished. 'What do you want, Keller?' 'We need to talk, Joaquin. You know where, in two days at midnight.' With that, the old Cardinal hung up the phone, the dull click on the other end the only sound to answer Joaquin's curses.... Chapter Two - Old Friends? Joaquin approached the church with a sense of foreboding, knowing that nothing he could do would get him free of whatever it was that Keller wanted. He would have to do it. 'Why did I ever make that vow?' He asked himself quietly. 'Why did I ever agree to this?' His faith had once been powerful, a mighty, raging force that had driven him forward and made him want to be a mighty avatar for God and for His Church. He had been on fire. His original plans had been to join the clergy. He wanted to pray for the sick and spread the word, bring the Lord's love and salvation to untold lost souls. But the Church, and primarily Cardinal Keller had other ideas. They had seen the skills the young man possessed, not only in hand to hand combat, but also with the sword, and believed they had found the perfect man to destroy the enemies of the Church. And thus, the path of his life had been set. He entered into the church's big swinging double doors, his body making a bow toward the altar more for the sake of habit than any lingering faith or devotion. The candles that burned there, the expensive decorations and cloths, they made no difference to him. All he could think of was all the times he had been ordered to kill, to snuff out a human life in the name of God. He wanted no further part of the Church. Keller preferred to meet in places that people would take care not to listen in on the conversation. Hence why he had picked an out of the way country church's confession booth. Joaquin noted the shadow within it and he quickly stepped inside on the other side of the screen, sitting down with a bang. 'Forgive me, Father. For I have sinned.' 'Your cheek is not appreciated, my son. We both know that you have no wish to be absolved for your sins.' Fair enough. 'What do you want?' Keller sighed, wishing that this could be easier. This man had to be on board or all of the council's plans would fail. 'We have a job for you, Joaquin.' 'I though so. You should know that I'm not doing it.' 'You took a vow, a solemn vow to do what was asked of you.' He knew the man's forays into professional wrestling had been to spite the Church, to prove to them that he was still his own man. Free to do what he chose. And they had allowed it. 'You will do this job. Then, and only then, will you be free of your vow.' 'Free? What do you call free? I thought I was free for the last year.' Keller's mouth tightened into a thin line as he grimaced at the venom in Joaquin's voice. 'You have been. We have allowed you to pursue your goals, first in Global, and now, apparently in FUSE. But we allowed you to do this with the full intention of calling you back into service when you were needed. Now...' Quickly he outlined the target and the way the Church wanted him killed. There could be no better man for the job than the small man in the other side of the booth, and Keller knew that Joaquin would see it done... Chapter 3 - Only the Beginning The security surrounding his target had been a joke. The man was contained with only two bodyguards in the basement of a church in San Diego, a priest in fact. But he had a bad compulsion. It was the same thing that had the church in such a bad light in recent time. A priest with busy hands and a lax view of his vow of chastity. A disgusting freak who liked the altar boys a little too much. Joaquin would have gladly killed the man for free. Joaquin had easily gained entry into the church, carrying his sword hidden in a tube meant for carrying the tripod of a video camera. He wore a long, black trench coat that concealed a pair of Beretta Nine millimeter pistols, his usual firearm of choice. From their location on his body he could draw and fire them quickly and accurately if needed. He moved through the church, noting the late evening worshippers with a smile and a nod. There were not many, a few older ladies and a very nice elderly gentlemen who shook Joaquin's hand in his own calloused old mitt and then sat down to continue his prayers. He proceded through the sanctuary, ducking out of a side door, and found himself in a narrow hallway. Some steps, rough hewn and made of wood, creaked under his weight as he stepped down them, and he groaned. Anybody listening would know full well that he was coming, and they would have time to get ready. His right hand grasped the handle of one of his Beretta handguns and drew it out. He held it in front of him as he reached the bottom, finding himself in a small room that smelled of mildew and mould. A small table with a lamp on it sat in the middle, the lamp burning and illuminating the books on the table's surface. They appeared to be written in fresh ink, but Joaquin could not read the words, which appeared to be Latin. 'Looking for me?' The voice was deep, and had a note of malice to it. 'I suppose you are the one they sent after me?' Joaquin whirled, covering the massive man who stepped out of the shadows with his gun. The man mountain was at least three hundred and fifty pounds and probably close to seven feet. From the look of his massive arms and chest, Joaquin knew he did not want to get caught in a fistfight with this man. 'They sent you to kill me.' It was not a question. 'Well, I can't have that.' He lunged forward, unbelievably fast for a man so large, and Joaquin didn't even have time to squeeze off one of his parabellum rounds into him before the man's huge fist cracked on his jaw. Joaquin Pierre, a man who had fought some of the toughest strikers in the world and taken their best shots without flinching, was unconscious before he hit the floor....
