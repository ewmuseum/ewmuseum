Episode 4: When I Was Young

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Now and Then The door of the garage built into the Warehouse opens as Jason Ryan, the man that the fans have come to know as Jay Draven, enters with his luggage in hand. Coming into the garage behind him is the young man that Draven picked up in Cleveland to be his new tag team partner, Paul Searle. "Through that door, Paul, is going to be where you will live and work for the next week. Then we drive up to East Lansing, and we see what you’ve learned." Draven opens the door to see small changes, thanks to the young lady that has also taken up temporary residence at the warehouse, one Kirsten Pendleton, better known to the fans as Krissy Jamison. "Up there is the residence. There are three bedrooms. You will be sleeping on the couch temporarily." Draven says as he points up at the apartment. "Or you can sleep down here on that cot, but I don’t recommend it. You don’t want to kill your back before your first match." "Mr. Draven, why did you bring me here?" "Because, you’re not going to get by as a wrestler eating Cheetos and drinking beer. When I was your age, I was already wrestling around the country." Paul looks at Draven in disbelief. "Wow." Draven walks around the ring, and pulls on the ropes. "When I was your age, this was my playground. Only, it wasn’t in an air conditioned Warehouse in the middle of Detroit. No, my playground was a sweltering oven in San Jose. "What I’m going to teach you, I was learning at 18, 19 years old. Only, I didn’t have the luxury of being trained one on one by a man that had a vested interest that I get better." "You’re going to train me?" "Yes. When I look at you, I see over six and a half feet of nothing. Look at your arms, I bet you couldn’t even lift your own bodyweight without fear of injury." "Actually…" "When I was your age, I was lifting twice my bodyweight on a daily basis. When I was your age, I was leg pressing almost three times my body weight. All I ask is that by the time you get in the ring with me, that you’re able to hang on for dear life. "I’m going to give you the opportunities that I didn’t have." FLASHBACK: 1990 "Jason, get your ass moving. I didn’t bring you back here so that you looked good. I watched that match you had last week, it was an embarrassment to me and to this profession." "Jay, I tried my best." "Well, kid, your best wasn’t good enough, now was it? You see that barrel over there. You don’t get to leave until you carry it across the gym." "Jay, that barrel is full. That has to be almost four hundred pounds." "Well, I suggest then that you don’t worry about lunch or dinner then, if you don’t think you can carry it because it’s too heavy for you." Young Jason Ryan walked over to the barrel, and with all the might that he could muster, he couldn’t lift the barrel. No matter how hard he tried, it wouldn’t move. He was wrong about one thing. It wasn’t almost four hundred pounds…. It was closer to five hundred. As much as he tried, he couldn’t even get the barrel to tip over. He ended up sleeping in the ring that night. He woke up the next day, and stared intently at the barrel. He felt weak, he hadn’t eaten since the previous morning. He knew that the only way he’d be allowed to leave is if he either accepted defeat and quit, or if he moved the barrel. Again, he used all the strength that he had to try to budge the barrel. It still wasn’t moving. PRESENT DAY "Paul, I’m going to start you off with something simple. You see that barrel over there?" Paul looks in the direction that Draven was pointing. "Yes." "You don’t eat until you move it..." Draven then points at the opposite corner of the Warehouse. "…over there. Seems simple, right? It isn’t going to be. That barrel is five hundred pounds…" FLASHBACK: 1990 "You can’t do it, can you kid?" Jason Ryan just looked. The metal plates on the bottom of the barrel didn’t allow him to tip it, and even if he did, he wouldn’t be able to roll it. "Let me tell you something. In all of my years here, no one has moved that barrel from here to there. You know why?" "Because they were weak." "No, and if you truly think that, you will never move that barrel from here to there. Face it, kid, it’s impossible to move that barrel from here to there, just like it’s impossible for you to be a good wrestler." "I don’t believe that." "Then move it." "I…I can’t." "Then you have failed. I will pass along to as many promoters as I can that you aren’t ready to be a wrestler." "You can’t do that." "Sure I can. There is only one way to stop me. Move that barrel." "It’s too heavy." "That’s just an excuse. An obstacle. You don’t leave here tonight until you move it." "But I’m hungry." "Your hunger should be motivation." "I’m tired." "Why are you tired? You haven’t done anything." "I’m…I’m weak." "That is beside the point." PRESENT DAY "When I was your age, I had to move that very same barrel. You have an advantage over me though." "What’s that?" "When I had to move it, I hadn’t eaten in 6 hours. You ate while we waited on the cab." "I can’t lift five hundred pounds." "Neither could I. It took me three days, three days that I slept on a hard ring mat, three days where I didn’t have anything to eat to figure out how to move that barrel." "How did you do it?" "I’ll tell you how I did it. If you can’t figure it out by 8. Until then, see if you can figure it out." FLASHBACK: 1990 "Jason, have you given up." The man that would eventually become Draven was lying in a fetal position on the concrete in front of the barrel. His hunger had become unbearable. Every movement exacerbated his pain. "No. Just in a little bit of pain." "Jason, face it. You lost. You’re no wrestler." That was the last time he ever heard Jay Callion say those words to him. "I am a wrestler. I will prove it to you." "Then move that fucking barrel and stop wasting my time." ‘It’s 8, I haven’t figured it out yet.’ "I can’t." ‘The secret wasn’t in your muscles. I watched, all day, as you strained yourself trying to move it with your apparent lack of strength.’ "You say you’re a wrestler, and that you want to prove it to me, then move that FUCKING barrel!" ‘I’ve done everything I could think of to move it.' "It’s too hard." ‘Have you?’ "Wrestling is hard. This is easy." ‘You told me that you would tell me.’ "I…" a look came to Jason Ryan’s face of a realization. ‘It took me three days to figure out that the answer was just that simple.’ Jason Ryan rose to his feet, and walked over to an object that he had looked at every day as a trainee. "It’s not that simple." ‘The lesson that I learned that day was to know your surroundings. To outthink your problem.’ Jason Ryan grabbed the chain that was against the wall. Underneath it was the pallet jack that was used to move the pieces of the ring when it was broken down. ‘I lost the match that caused Jay Callion to call me back for more training because I didn’t realize that I was close enough to the ropes to break the pinfall.’ Jason Ryan pulled the jack to the barrel and slipped it into the spaces in the plate. He lifted the barrel and pulled it back to the spot that Callion wanted it placed. "You’ve finally figured it out. Maybe you will become a good wrestler." ‘I never forgot that lesson. The pallet jack is against that wall. When you’re done, come on up, I made some Galbi for the three of us.’ ‘Three?’ ‘Yeah, a friend comes and stays here from time to time. She’s the reason you’re on the couch tonight. If anyone, especially someone wearing a face shield asks, you’ve never even met Krissy.’
