A Starburst Heart

24 July 2009

Date: 7/24/09 So phosphorescent was the afternoon sun, it was like the Earth had an idea. With the Helios-shaped bulb sending hyperviolet sparkles that sponge-painted the gentle rolling hills, you would almost expect to hear light instrumentals accompany the pace of the boy and his dog as they ventured through the cemetery. Unfortunately, this was not a David Lynch film, and regardless of the ironic mixture of gloom and glow -- nothing paranormal was going to happen. This was not a dream sequence, nor would it be a monumental reconnection with a lost loved one. Thad did not feel as though he needed to make peace, and he knew that there would never be any such thing as closing the book on this particular story. He had a piece of her life that he would always carry with him, not to mention a constant reminder of her in the form of something very special to him. He could remember the way she smelled and the way she tasted: there was no need to pay a gravel plot, a wooden case, and six feet of dirt to separate the two a friendly visit. The tombstone couldn't talk back to him. There was not a cloud in the sky, and the atmosphere was the typical Georgia summer stickiness -- so there would be no gentle breeze to 'indicate she was there.' Chances were better that a frail, flesh-scabbed zombie hand would protrude through the rock to pull him down than any of that other cinematic feel-good bullshit. He wasn't going to suddenly look up to Heaven and see her reflection smiling back at him...with her auburn hair covered in Andromeda's sequins and shoulder blades fitted with a brand new pair of ivory wings. Thad ignored Quasar's curiosity with the wide open space as his nose brushed up against the floral remembrances resting on the glazed granite stones. Silently, he read the last names on the slabs as he walked past plot by plot. Hanson. Jones. Hart. Whitehead. Fortson. Certain that the same dark green tarp had been placed over the sites with those same folding chairs that were covered with the plush material you'd find in a tricked out El Camino. He supposed it was in the best interest of the funeral home to keep their patrons as comfortable as possible while their loved one was being lowered into the great big rectangle. Thad never got to sit in that chair. In fact, the site he was currently walking toward, he'd never even been less than a hundred yards away from it. He was only able to remember its general whereabouts when he saw that big green tent when driving past the service the afternoon of her burial. Smith. Kelly. Roberts. Lane. They'd finally made it. From the crushed velvet chair to the pine-box death vessel. A great canoe, that coffin made. Sailing the soul down the slimy waters of the Styx, and reaching the harbor of the afterlife. When Thad died, he wanted to board the S.S. Minnow version...or float his ship and pirate all the incoming boats. I must be getting close now, he thought. His nerves began to chirp like distress signals from seafaring sarcophaguses, with the crewmen in his eyes looking for signs of the lighthouse. With his bearings now fully focused on his target destination, his hands slowly tightened into fists. Unfortunately, the thorns from the two roses he had in his grasp did not sympathize with his anxiety, as they penetrated the soft spot just below the thumb bone. A pretty suiting punishment for bringing roses to a grave site, though -- something else that was just all-too fitting for a scene like this. Another cliche, that while he felt was completely unnecessary, he had bought into. As if she would be able to enjoy them via the next plane of existence. Quite the stiff show of appreciation for this woman's memory who had left a permanent dent in his life -- who he used as a standard of comparison to every other living soul he'd met -- a friendly visit with two of the most meaningless objects in history. But, he didn't flinch...didn't turn back...didn't question these things like he had on the drive over to this golden with gray landscape on the edge of the small town of Lexington. He just walked. Laurens. Davidson. Lansing. Another Smith. Where was she? -------------------------------------------------------------------------- There was nothing new to smell, but I pressed my nose to the ground anyway. Sometimes, I feel like these things are my real eyes. Guess as I get older, this ol' nose has started to become the predominant sense! Sure wish I were a pup again. I bet you do, too. 'Cause although I'm sure you think I'm cute now...I was a lot more lovable then. Especially considering my background, huh? I guess I could elaborate a little more on that whole sad story you heard a while ago, about those two yokels setting me down on somebody's yard and leaving little ol' me cold and afraid. I guess I was more confused than bitter at the time, left wondering how someone can give you the best darn scratching behind the ears you ever received just to treat you like you don't exist the very next day. No need to cry over it, though. I understand that Disney's conditioned you to sob anytime something happens to a cute ball of fluff such as myself, but life's turned out a nice shake interesting for this dog. I don't hold hard feelings towards Ellie Mae and Mr. Clampett, although you gotta wonder how somebody could just abandon their pet, right? I minded my manners and never meant any harm...well...I don't count the instances with that damn cat. Little shit was asking for it, always struttin' around with her tail in the air, showing her butt-hole for the whole world to see. Cats. So arrogant, they think everyone wants to look at their pink Cheerio where the poop comes out? And then, by dog, then they go to licking themselves wherever and whenever they please! I mean, don't get me wrong, though, I enjoy a good moment of affirmation as much as the next son of a bitch -- but I've got enough sense not to go to Tongue Town when the mayor's over for dinner. Know what I mean, Vern? Sorry 'bout taking you on the trail to nowhere on that one. Yeah, I tend to go off on tangents like that, even in this here soliloquy. Guess you're lucky I'm not talking to you for real, because you'd probably get aggravated unless you happened to be fluent in barks and arfs and yips and yaps. I've yet to find a human that was, then again the jury's still out on that Cesar Millan guy. Thaddy sure thinks he does, but then again we're talking about the same guy who pretends to be an alien and serenades beautiful women with songs they haven't listened to since 1995. He's all the time spillin' his guts to me, asking for advice and acting like I'm the one giving it to him when it's really just what he thinks he should do himself. 'Should I get an Oreo or a Heath crunch Blizzard? Oh, but wait...should I have Dairy Queen and break my diet? Yeah, you're right, Quasar...I'll just burn off the calories with an extra mile on the run.' Which he never does, by the way. But, I reckon I encourage it sometimes, though. I'll 'woof' in response, just to humor him a little bit. Not like the kid's got anyone smarter to talk to. I guess it'd be acceptable to just assume that I should keep along with the story we play on television, about how he picked me up from an animal shelter on Jupiter or what not. However, the truth of the matter is that we both came from the distant galaxy known as Northeast Georgia, or 'Georgie' as the locals call it. Ya'll ever see that cartoon show about the octopuses who live in the woods? They actually lived a couple trailers down from my first home. Let me tell you, both Thaddy and I spent many years getting rid of the accent -- but if you were to see some old VHS footage of him as a kid, you'd be laughing your ass off at the little mullet-wearing turd running around a yard exclaiming how 'purdy' his brother's girlfriend was. Yeah, he had a bad case of the Southern drawl despite growing up in the more civilized part of the county, which was comparable to the Ritz-Carlton if you were to ever see my old place. There were nice green lawns instead of the damned ol' red clay I was used to rolling around in. There wasn't even a dirt road in sight -- even the driveways were paved. No rickety old Fords sitting in the front yards...ah, except if you want to count that Mustang on cinder blocks at the Clemens' place. Heh, I got chased out of that yard after Mr. Clemens stepped in a surprise I left for him next to his prized azalea bushes one day. Old Man Clemens hates shit, and he didn't think too much of kids, either. Especially one who put a baseball through his truck windshield, or toilet-papered his trees on Halloween with his hoodlum friends. Yep, next door to Ted Clemens lived a young man you all know as Spacely -- but back then he was just plain old Thaddeus David Denver. The 'next Paul Simon' who's momma taught piano and led the Sunday choir, and who's daddy made his living running Notes-2-Chords, a thriving little music shop downtown. I'm sure he could adequately bore you enough with whiny details about how he was 'forced into music' and that his parents were too controlling or whatever, but they seem nice enough to me. Granted, they've somewhat shut him off after he announced his intention to flip around and run the risk of breaking his neck on a premium cable channel infamous for dirty movies -- but it wasn't the stunt he pulled during the Idol audition. Sure, they figured he was groomed to be an entertainer, but wrestling wasn't quite what they expected. I think if they just took a little time to see what he does and see the way he makes eyes light up, they might have a little change of heart...then again, I'm probably biased considering I'm now something of a quasi-celebrity myself. It beats catching frisbees or running down a damn obstacle course for Beggin' Strips, that's for sure. Even if I do have to wear that dumb thing over my eyes. Admittedly, I don't know a whole lot about Thaddy's kid life, other than what he reminisces about on the road with that Steve guy and what I've seen on that video cassette. The thing is, while I've been by his side for a while now, we never saw a whole lot of each other until about three years after I was adopted by Kathleen and Ryan, the nice couple who found me curled up on their front porch the morning after the infamous drop-off. I vaguely recall his hand coming down to give me a tussle behind the left ear and a pat on the butt on the occasions he'd walk past our yard on the way to Casey Langsler's house, but he wasn't a familiar sight until I'd come to recognize the blue pick-up truck he started driving on his eighteenth birthday. You thought a dog's long-term memory was pretty non-existent, huh? Really did hate to ruin it for you. I miss my mom. You have to understand that there's pet owners, and then there's parents. Pet owners are the people who take the time out to make arrangements for their animals to be fed when they're on vacation, build them a nice little house to keep them out of the rain, and buy the occasional chew toy or two for us dogs. Parents are the people who cuddle up next to you when they watch reruns of Perfect Strangers, take you along on vacations, doesn't yell at you for chewing up one of their favorite shoes, and have a comfy bed for you right next to theirs. Kathleen was my mom. Ryan was a pet owner, although thankfully I never got to see too much of him due to his military career or what not...but I liked him alright. Newspapers to the nose weren't exactly a real treat for me, but I can forgive and forget. Wait...what's that smell? ------------------------------------------------------------------ ALEXANDER KATHLEEN (1970 - 2007) Now twilight lets her curtain down and pins it with a star. Thad exhaled as he scanned the epitaph several times from the side of the pebble surface, taking caution to not stand directly on the mound. For whatever reason, he'd formulated the idea in his head that it was disrespectful to be directly on top of a tomb. (If she were to re-animate and try to pull him down with her, she'd surely know to reach a little to the left.) After he'd unintentionally memorized the verse engraved below his feet, he quietly dropped the two roses down on top of it. Adjusting his Braves cap over his platinum-covered dome, Thad continued to stare at the ground, mentally debating whether or not to have a conversation with the imaginary notion of Mrs. Alexander's spirit. Meanwhile, Quasar had taken interest in the floral gift that his owner had bestowed to the honor of his former lover. The wind did not blow, the dog did not bark, and the sun wasn't shining only for him. He once laughed at the ridiculousness of the notion that the dead were watching and listening to the sappy and melodramatic pouring-of-hearts of people who would come out here for a means of reconciliation. There was no way in hell that this was going to make him feel better. 'Hey,' Thad mumbled dryly, gaze fixated on the filthy rubber soles of his Vans slip-ons. 'Sorry I only brought two flowers. Money's a little tight, especially with the whole 'spur of the moment trip home to talk to your dead girlfriend' thing. Good to know you're still here.' What a pile of shit I am, Thad mused. Glad I can have such a sense of humor about this. 'Just joking,' he chuckled, retracting his steps. 'Glad at least one of us got the hell out of this city, right?' Thad forced a chuckle at his totally ironic attempt to start a dialogue. 'Not like bouncing from Monterrey to Cheyenne to Atlanta and back to Kalamazoo is necessarily ideal, but I'm not here -- and that's good enough for me.' 'Guess you could say I've really ran away from my past,' Thad continued, now staring down directly at the plot as he continued his conversation. 'Well, I'm not running from my past, Kay...I'm fucking jetting from it. Trying my best to get away from you, from mom and pop, from everybody -- not because I can't take the negative from the positive, but fuck! Thad dug a left heel into the ground and spun clockwise, facing east when the hilltop horizon line blocked anything that might be past it. 'You know what my life is like now, Kay? I drive from place to place, hanging out with the damn dog...the one thing that constantly reminds me of you every single fucking time I look at his goofy face. It's like you're haunting me, just weighing me down when I need to move on...find someone else...whatever it is that I'm supposed to do. But, no. I'm in love with a memory. In love with lips I'll never be able to kiss again. All because you felt like you had to move on. Too fucking afraid about what these SHITTY PEOPLE in this SHITTY FUCKING CITY might think about you.' 'Just for me to come out here,' Thad said, voice remaining passionate and angry, volume louder than when he'd first begun, 'and babble in a cometary, look like a complete fool for ever acting on the silliest crush a boy could have.' 'When you decided to make this decision,' Thad continued, his voice quivering ever so slightly, 'did you honestly think that I was that young and stupid that I'd just forget you ever existed? Guess that backfired, huh? Guess you didn't think that I'd take a notion to fly halfway across the country because I still love the hell out of you. I guess you would go back and change things if you could now, huh? But no...instead of us packing up and leaving this town together, now it's this. Why? Because it wasn't practical? Because I was an eighteen year old kid and you were a respected member of the community? Because your piece of shit husband would have beaten you? I guess all these things really matter now, huh? You fucking left me alone when all we needed was a tank full of gas and a signature of divorce. Instead, it's this. A much more practical escape. Now you're in this place forever...the one thing you and I both swore would never happen.' Thad, coming to grips with the fact that he was raving to the point of incoherence, sighed as he turned back to the grave site. Taking a deep breath, he kicked the lining hard with his foot and cocked his head up toward the sky. There were bullets popping inside his guts as an air raid led by the Red Baron himself poked and prodded the lining in his stomach. He wanted nothing more than to vomit out these tiny little supernovae that boomed in the form of both fond and painful memories. Chunks of his brain -- like phlegm from the lungs -- needed to be spewed out right here in the cemetery. He would gladly dig for hours without a shovel just to bury them amongst the other deceased recollections in the skulls of skeletal remains. He wanted to forget the way they locked eyes for the first time as they sat next to each other with the history book opened at her kitchen table. He wanted to purge the way he had tingled when her tongue slid into his mouth, and how he had instinctively cupped the sides of her face as she shifted onto his lap. How he didn't think about the taboo nature of the chain of events that were happening -- just that two people were sharing one another at such a religious amount of intensity, with limbs moving like serpents in the preacher's hands and raising the spirit of the all-powerful and infinite into them. He wanted to be free of their conversations afterward, where they would share the same pinky-swears and deviate plans to be together. 'You make my heart go starburst,' he said, guiding her palm onto his chest as she straddled him. Her phoenix-like hair lightly hit him in the face as she leaned in to kiss him passionately once more, just before juices blended with blaze and Northern lights lit up the Arctic evening. ---------------------------------------------- Glue was just not going to get the job done for this one. Although the yarn was tied tightly to the connector wire, Elmer's alone wasn't going to stick to the red rubber ball. This was a crucial piece to this masterful design, and it was something he was quite proud of -- too proud to just write off this last part. Shifting his eyes around the classroom whose walls were decorated with what was probably a forest's worth of construction paper, he searched for something with a little more adhesive power than the horse hoof concoction he'd been given. Scotch tape? No, not good enough. Maybe he could stick a clothespin on there? Dang. No. Nothing at all to use...except...wait a second...yes, that would work! Like the world had gone black-and-white so that the cherry-colored object shone over everything else in the room, the young Thaddy Denver saw the stapler right in the middle of his teacher's desk. A gleam in his eye reflected off the forbidden tool that had been stated in the rules he'd had to write over and over on the blackboard for the many transgressions he'd committed during his second-grade tenure. Mrs. Wimley had his number, and Thad had made his way on her list of troublemakers that she provided to her young substitute for that week. However, the woman's attention was currently diverted -- her back turned as she leaned over the cluster of desks at the far corner of the room to assist other students on their science projects. All he had to do was mosey on over there with mobile in hand, make a quick KA-CHINK through the Hi-Bounce, cover it with paste enough to hide the staple, and he'd be good to go. Like taking candy from an itty-bitty baby, he thought. Thad grabbed the bottom of his Mighty Morphin Power Rangers t-shirt and stretched it down to just above his knees before releasing it with a snap. Taking another look back to double-check that Miss Mayberry was still not looking, he slid the hanger off the desk and retrieved the ball, making his journey over to the holy grail of school supplies. Thad made sure to step softly so that the bottoms of his light-up British Knights wouldn't squeak against the smooth tile floor. As he closed in on the teacher's desk, his eyes widened as he reached out a star-stamped hand to grab the Swingline. Victory was soon to be his, and he just knew that his project would win the 8-year old's equivalent to the Nobel prize: an opportunity to pick out something from the goody bag on Friday! Aligning the thread onto the rounded surface of the ball, Thad grabbed the stapler and brought it close to his chest, opening the handle part in order to smash the lever down onto it. 'OOOOOHHHHHHHHHHHHHH! I'm tellllllllllllllllllllllllllllllllinnnnnnnnnnnnn'!' Dang! The airy, birdlike shrill of the pig-tailed princess standing behind him in pink overalls startled our blond-haired scientist, causing him to release his grip on the object and turn around quickly. 'I didn't do nuffin',' he cried out to Heather, a girl who'd been an enemy of his since Kindergarten. They'd had a cute history -- she planted a kiss on him once and proceeded to squeal to the teacher that 'that boy kissed me!', which had landed him a twenty-minute time out during recess. As they had gotten older, they would recall the incident whenever the two happened to run into each other in public. Once, it actually led to a drunken make-out session at a party when they were sophomores...but at this point, he hated her guts. 'Yes you did,' the brown-haired and lightly-freckled girl shot back. 'I saw you trying to use Mrs. Wimley's stapler!' 'Nuh-uh!' Thad responded sharply, in vain. He knew he'd been caught red-handed, but uselessly tried to muster up his defense in the hope she'd change her mind about tattling on him...but it was not going to happen. 'Did too, did too! And I'm gonna tell Miss Mayberry and you're gonna be in troub-bulllllllllllllllllllllllllllllllllllll!' This is when Thad started to panic. He knew that something like this would only earn him more Bart Simpson-like punishment after school if it were just old Mrs. Wimley -- but this was a substitute teacher. While she seemed nice enough, subs wielded the ultimate power of sending rule-breakers directly to the office, which was not a place he wanted to go. His father was a follower of the old-school Southern disciplinary techniques, and if the principal were to call his parents, things would not look promising for his bottom. At their house on the edge of the yard was a hickory bush, and Thad would have to select the branch that would be used to spank him. If he chose poorly, he'd get a double-dose of the whipping...so it was always a lose-lose situation. Thad cringed as Heather turned to get the attention of Miss Mayberry, her pigtails waving mockingly as she walked away in do-gooder stride. He was frozen with fear. While the thought of conjuring up some waterworks in hopes that the substitute teacher would go easy on him, crying was not something that came naturally to him. His eyes were usually as dry as two deserts -- not because he was cold per se, but even when he was overcome with emotion, he remained fairly stoic-looking. Almost autistic. 'He tried to use the stapler and Mrs. Wimley said we can't do that!' Miss Mayberry only smiled at Thad, dismissing the accusation with a couple of kind words and no doubt leaving the little brat standing next to her disappointed. He would see this woman again the first day of his U.S. History class when he was a junior in high school...both obviously a bit aged from their previous exchange and her with a fairly respectable rock on her finger. Today, on this summer afternoon in his old hometown, he'd stand at her final resting place...until his knees could no longer take the weight pressed down upon him by his conscience. 'I'm so sorry.' He hoped that his eyes would have drier days to come.
