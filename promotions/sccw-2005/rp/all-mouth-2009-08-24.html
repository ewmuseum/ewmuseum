Date: August 24th 2009 Today ‘You know what, screw this.’ Jeff Samuels was up out of his seat, his finger punctuating every utterance. ‘This is bullshit and you know it!’ This would make more sense if we rewind just a little bit… *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ 6 weeks earlier ‘I’m very sorry, we just can’t agree to such a big raise in the current climate. In 6 months we will be able to review the situation but right now it’s just not feasible.’ Jeff had hoped this wouldn’t be the case but had prepared for it nevertheless. In front of him was a file, full of facts and figures, graphs and statistics, all to show just why they should give him a raise, even when others were being laid off. ‘Well, I’ve got some reports here that I think might interest you.’ As he went to pass them over the desk, the CEO raised his hands and shook his head. ‘Sorry Jeff, it’s non negotiable.’ This, he hadn’t planned for. ‘But surely yo-‘ ‘No more. I’ve told you where we stand, it’s just not on the cards right now.’ Jeff was shocked and unfortunately, he reacted in the one way he shouldn’t have – he gave them an ultimatum. ‘I’ve given my blood, sweat and tears to this company for the last 4 years and you’re treating me like this?! Well I won’t have it. Either I get that raise or I’m walking and I’ll take my clients with me!’ ‘Jeff, come on, there’s no need for that. Let’s just calm down and think about this.’ ‘No, I’ve thought about it. Those are my terms. So what’s it going to be?’ The boss looked almost pained as he gave his answer, but he had been put on the spot and had to think of the company. ‘I’m sorry Jeff. If that’s how you feel, then we’ll have to let you go. I’ll give you until the end of the day to reconsider but if you don’t, I want your desk cleared by the end of the week.’ That was it. Jeff’s relationship with the company ended that quickly. On the drive home that night, he considered his options and came to the conclusion that he’d be fine. He was damn good at his job, had a laundry list of top clients and was still relatively young. He’d be making what he deserved in 2 weeks tops… *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ 4 weeks earlier He’d been unemployed for 2 weeks, had applied for 6 jobs and had received positive responses from none of them. The reasons were always the same and were beginning to grate on him. ‘I’m sorry, we’re looking for someone with more relevant experience.’ ‘We are really looking for someone for the long-haul and we aren’t sure how committed you are.’ ‘We can’t justify that level of wage right now.’ The pile of savings was diminishing, not to mention the decline in his mental state. One of the most depressing things was to go on a night out with his buddies and get chatting to a girl. Now, the conversation invariably turned to careers and as soon as he uttered the words ‘I’m between jobs at the moment’, the woman inevitably had to ‘go find my friends’ or ‘go to powder my nose’. Not cool. Still, he’d applied for another few roles this morning and was pretty hopeful about something happening soon. Things couldn’t carry on like this for too much longer. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ 3 weeks earlier Another week, another series of rejections. Times were getting tough. Jeff was spiraling deeper down into the depths. Still, sometimes salvation comes in unlikely packages. It happened purely by chance, a misdirected click of the mouse on one of the innumerable recruitment websites that he seemed to spend his life on. The job page opened up and initially, he didn’t see anything special. An average sort of sales job, the salary nothing to write home about, but when he saw the phrase ‘Uncapped commission’, his interest perked up. You see, Jeff was nothing if not confident in his abilities. So for him, the phrase ‘Uncapped commission’ meant an extra 60% on top of his basic wage. Giving him more than enough to live in the manner to which he had been accustomed. So off went the CV, leaving him to just sit back, relax and wait for the phone call. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ 2 weeks earlier This was more like it. Granted, he didn’t have his own office yet, but he’d picked up the swing of things pretty quickly and reckoned that in a couple of months, with some careful negotiating and maneuvering, he would be making a good percentage over his basic salary. He just had to get hold of those old clients and convince them that he was their best bet. So far, they had been less than receptive. Still, at least he was in gainful employment again, on his way back to making the big bucks and attaining a title he felt he deserved. The powers that be knew he was good, they’d be willing to give him a little leeway, a little more time than anticipated. Or so he thought. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ Today What a morning. He’d been in the office from 8:25, a little later than usual, and ever since he’d been on the phone, trying to put out the fire that was threatening to cost him one of his biggest customers. He thought he’d sewn it up yesterday, managed to tempt them away from his previous employers with the promise of better rates and a more personalized service. Then he comes in this morning and his answering phone message hits him like a sledgehammer in the gut. ‘This is Jonas from HT Miller. After our conversation yesterday I went through some figures and discussed it with our Directors and unfortunately we won’t be switching over to you. It’s just too big a risk to take and we’re going to be staying with Johnson.’ Jeff’s first thought was that he had misheard the message but upon listening to it again, realisation dawned on him. He was in trouble. You see, in his interview, Jeff had been very vocal about his ability to poach big-name, high-spending clients from his previous employers. In fact, he had guaranteed that he could bring at least 70% of them with him. This move would, if it went without a hitch, make him the biggest revenue earner in the company. Even if he fell short and got half of his target, he’d easily be making more than all except a couple of people. Turns out, Jeff had been too optimistic. You see, as good as he thought he was, he also had a hell of a lot of luck. Sure, he had gained a lot of new clients through his hard work, but he’d also inherited a lot when another guy had left the company. In fact, right now, someone at his old place was benefitting from this. Strange how things happen like that. This loss, however, was the straw that broke the camel’s back and was why he had been called into the Director’s office. ‘Jeff, when you started it was made clear this was a trial period. Any permanent contract offer would be dependent on your performance over the first few weeks.’ He nodded in agreement, yet a heavy sense of foreboding was engulfing him. ‘I’m sorry to say that your performance hasn’t been what we expected. In fact, it’s been very poor. You’ve brought in none of your old customers despite your continual reassurances that you have them ready to be signed up at a moment’s notice. Additionally, you’ve not been proactive in garnering any new business. If you were a trainee I’d be mildly disappointed but given that you’ve been in the business for a good period, I’m really shocked at your effort.’ He could see where this was going now, clear as day. He couldn’t let it happen. ‘Sir, I know my performance hasn’t been up to scratch, but I can get better. Just give me a couple more weeks and I promise you’ll see results.’ ‘Jeff, it’s too late. You had the chance and you didn’t take it. I wish you the best of luck in the future but for now, we can’t justify keeping you. You’ll be paid for the full month but can finish today.’ ‘No, wait, I can do th-‘ ‘Mr Samuels, it’s not up for discussion. I’m sorry.’ I think this is where we came in. ‘You know what, screw this.’ Jeff Samuels was up out of his seat, his finger punctuating every utterance. ‘This is bullshit and you know it!’ Jeff got up and walked out, slamming the door behind him in more ways than one. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ There is a valuable lesson to be learned from the experience of the unfortunate Mr Samuels, one that he will probably be too ignorant to learn. Josh Marquez, however, is well aware of the pitfalls. You see, like Jeff, Josh Marquez fancies himself as somewhat of a big shot, a high-flyer destined for great things. He knows he has all the tools he needs at his disposal and believes that given the right opportunity, he can reach the heights, no matter what has happened before. However, unlike Jeff, Marquez is a pragmatist. He knows that he can’t live off his name and past accomplishments, that he has to show from day one that he deserves to be there and that he can in fact back up what he says. This week on Temptation, it’s his first opportunity to really show the SCCW roster and fans what he can do. His task isn’t easy, for Darius Drannen is going to be striving to make an impression just as much as our man from Vegas. He has the same motivations as Josh, the desire and drive to be the best. The one thing he doesn’t have, however, is the game-changer, the one thing that is pushing Josh on so that anything other than a win will be like a kick in the teeth. Fear. Fear of not living up to his potential, fear of having his contract terminated, fear of failing. This factor means that come bell-time, Darius Drannen can throw the kitchen sink at him, but Josh will keep coming, and pushing until he gets the 1-2-3. Get ready for fireworks.
