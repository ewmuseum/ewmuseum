Date: Today "I don’t want to go amongst mad people," Alice said "Oh, you can’t help that. We’re all mad here," the cat said. -Lewis Carroll * * * Two weeks ago. A Pontiac Grand Prix sped through Northridge, a suburb of Los Angeles. For the last twenty minutes it had been going in a rectangular pattern, over and over. The passengers saw the same rows of houses repeatedly, and by this point could no longer discern them from each other. And despite the conversation, the world looked very simple. The remains of a cigar flew out one of the backseat windows. And in that very backseat, terrible things happened. "Los Angeles…it’s just New York lying down, really," The River Rat offered, watching the houses go by. "I don’t know what that means," the other man in the backseat said. "Eh, I think you do." Lane Stevens smiled then. It was a private smile, the darkness afforded no one else a glimpse of it. "A whole city, just given up, becoming a parody of itself. It kind of makes the rest of California seem authentic, don’t you think?" "I don’t…I don’t know," the other man in the backseat stammered. The River Rat turned towards him then, sizing up the man. He was dressed in a charcoal suit and slacks. The sweat on his nose was making his glasses sink down. This was David Rasmussen, Alanna Marshall’s oncologist. In the frontseat Miles Cavanaugh whistled along to the radio, and behind the wheel Jeb Stewart didn’t make a sound. "So tomorrow, just to recap, you’re going to do the worst thing you’ve ever done in your whole life. And in return, your wife doesn’t find out about the second worst thing you’ve ever done in your whole life." David’s head was buried in his hands. The lack of a response was basically a yes, and Desade’s Warlord knew it. Lane’s mind flickered over other evidence he had accumulated over the last few months, how easy it would be to send Darcy Markson a package just like the one he had to Mr. Rasmussen. But for whatever reason, that idea didn’t really amuse him. But this one, oh this was a real dandy. "But look on the bright side Doc," Lane started again, "you get to be in a pro wrestling storyline! You might even get to fuck Katsidy, I’m not sure." "When do I get to fuck Katsidy?" Miles asked from the front seat. "You need to give her a reason to seduce you, man. Like information or money. Or…you know, just don’t hold your breath in this one," Lane shot back. "Epic bummer," was Miles’ reply. "I could lose my job over this," David’s voice was muffled through his hands. "I will lose my job over this." "Its just a misdiagnosis. Maybe someone beneath you accidentally switched the charts, or the test results…I don’t know, you can figure it out. The Marshall’s aren’t going to sue you or the hospital, not their style." Suddenly Lane’s voice became very dramatic, like a movie trailer. "Cause they’re stand-up folks, who believe the best in people, and eat oatmeal every morning!" Jeb Stewart mouthed "oatmeal?" to Miles, who just shrugged. "Have you ever met Lance Marshall?" David asked, finally removing his face from his hands. "Yep," Stevens answered quickly. "You’re insane. He will kill you for this." "Maybe, maybe not," the River Rat said, and wrapped an arm around Doctor Rasmussen who twitched at Lane’s touch. "But if we’re living in a world where even the great Lance Marshall can lose his mind, well I’m just not sure I want to go on anymore. Who will I believe in? What about the kids!?!" Stevens giggled in David’s ear, causing him to recoil as if bitten. "I will pay for some of the things I’ve done, and so will you Doc. But not because of some kind of cosmic balance, or karma…" * * * Now. A woman applies too much eye-shadow. "…hell sometimes a butterfly flaps its wings in Europe, and we’re fucked." A woman applies too much lipstick. "And sometimes there’s so many wrong times, wrong places, that its not even a coincedence when it happens. It’s just the math." This is Tiffany Sands. If you are a customer of the Crazy Horse Gentlemen’s club in San Fransisco, then you might refer to her as "Daisy". She is in her early twenties. Some of her physical attributes: blonde, blue eyes, tan, large breasts. "Two minutes Daisy," a female voice says from the other side of a curtain. She is staring into a mirror. Surrounding her are women in various stages of undress. They make conversation about their lives, their customers, their boyfriends, the economy and so on. But Tiffany zones most of this out. What gets her through this night, just like any night of this job, is focusing on details. She’s given a lot of thought to her song choice, her outfit, and her make-up. Shte stands up, and looks at herself one final time. This evening she is wearing a cheerleader outfit. It is black and white, with the outline of a woman’s figure on the front. Tiffany puts her hair in a ponytail, and becomes Daisy. Her blank stare lights up into an innocent smile, something she used to wear a lot more often. "Next," a female voice says. And with that Daisy bursts through the curtain, and several hoots and hollers are heard from the crowd. The DJ says this: "LADIES AND GENTLEMEN! GIVE IT UP FOR DAISY!" * * * "Eight dollar cover tonight, gentlemen," the door man says. "Jeb." "Yup." Jeb Stewart reaches into his back pocket, and produces a twenty dollar bill for the door man. He is dressed in an Alabama Crimson Tide t-shirt, and jeans. His boss, or his CO as Lance Marshall would think of it, is dressed in a short sleeve white buttom up shirt, khaki shorts, and a tan bucket hat. The River Rat’s scarred face betrays his carefree look. "Have fun," the door man says lifelessly. "I always do," Lane chirps. They step into the Crazy Horse. Lane has been to plenty of strip clubs in his life, and he immediantly notices the difference in layout. There is a long runway, with rows of seating lined up on each side. He takes a moment to look over the scene, and observe the talent. "How come Miles didn’t come? He loves this stuff." "Dude gets all moody and dissillusional somedays, the fucker thinks he’s a poet or something," Stevens says. "But we’re going to have ourselves a super time though, maybe get you laid." "That’s okay," Jeb replies, "I’m kind of seeing someone." In the interest of getting Lane off his case, he is lying. "Huh, no shit. Is she huge and dumb too?" "No." Jeb smiles to himself, as for a moment he found himself getting defensive about a fictional girlfriend. "Just fuckin’ with ya, that’s cool man." After a motion from Lane, the two men walk over to the bar. The DJ says this: "LADIES AND GENTLEMEN! GIVE IT UP FOR DAISY!" * * * ‘Girls just wanna have fun’ is blaring over the audio system. What makes this moment even worse, is by the time she sees him, she is totally naked. Daisy had just slowly dropped her thong. The flashback becomes so much more real, so much more vivid. Daisy is gone, and Tiffany is frozen by him, causing a few disparaging comments from the crowd. "C’mon, I tipped you slut." "Get the next one out here! What the fuck!" And still, she doesn’t move. She stares at him, and he doesn’t even seem to notice her. He is talking to his big friend, and laughing about something. Lane Stevens is oblivious to her plight. Eventually he looks up, and whistles at her. But there is no recognition in his eyes. He takes a sip of his drink, it looks like a whiskey and coke from here, and tips the cup in her direction as if to say "cheers". Finally, she starts moving again, trying to pick up where she left off. She grabs the first man she sees, and rubs his face harshly into her chest. From there, the attitude of her performance seems to change. Her face does not display a smile, as much as a snarl. "I think she’s mean," Jeb says. "I think you’re right," Lane replies, and smiles faintly. Daisy walks over towards Lane and Jeb, her shame having twisted into something else entirely. She suddenly drops into the splits, and is eye level with The River Rat. "How’s it going tonight fellas?" Stevens leans closer. "Better now." * * * Like most strip clubs, the Crazy Horse Gentlemen’s Club has a private area for lap dances. Behind saloon style doors, a couch sits. And on that couch sits Lane Stevens, who waits patiently for his dancer. By now he is drinking gin and tonic, his sixth different drink of the evening. He hums along to a rap song he vaguely remembers from his childhood. After a few minutes of this finally the doors open. "Sorry to keep you waiting," Daisy starts, "just had to grab something." "I got something you can grab," Lane fires back and chuckles, the booze having made him slightly less clever. And in the darkness that a place like this affords, she smiles in a way that Lane might have recognized. He might have recognized it from the mirror, for it is a viscious thing. Suddenly the song changes. "Satisfaction" by the Rolling Stones begins to play on the audio system, and Daisy approaches. With each step she swings her hips dramatically, and Lane certainly notices. "One second, tired of walking in these things," Daisy laughs, and bends over. She removes her heels quickly, throwing one of them to the side. Stevens takes a gulp of his gin and tonic, his eyes glued to her chest. What he fails to notice is that she only discards one of the shoes. And there is something else in her other hand as well. "Hey you look kinda famil…" The River Rat does not finish his sentence. It should be noted that before this goes down, Tiffany (or Daisy) does not have anything clever to say. "AAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAH!" Lane Stevens just got maced in the face. His scream goes momentarily unheard, given the amount of noise in the establishment. He blindly swings in her general direction, but instead catches her heel right in the face. Shoe in hand, she swings again, and again. "JESUS FUCK BITCH! FUCK!" …and again. "FUCK!" His hands find his face, they find the blood. She swings again. And again, he screams. Finally, she begins to let it out. "DO YOU KNOW WHAT YOU DID TO ME! DO YOU KNOW!" It doesn’t even sound like her voice. "WHAT THE FUCK!" Lane yells. "YOU WILL NEVER KNOW!" And she connects again. He falls to the floor, blood splattering on the carpet. "YOU ARE ALONE!" she screams. It is ironic then, at this moment, that the bouncer comes through the door and grabs Tiffany from behind. Tears are streaming down her face. "Jesus," is about all the bouncer has to say, as Tiffany thrashes violently in her arms. The River Rat simply gets into the fetal position. "I CAN HURT YOU!" is the last thing Lane hears her say before losing consciousness. * * * In the back of a cab, the next morning. The ride from the hospital to the hotel seemed much longer than ten miles. Lane Stevens holds an ice pack to his head. Jeb Stewart sits next to him, and has said nothing for the entire ride so far. That is about to change. "So, she was mean." The Warlord laughs uproariously. Jeb actually joins in for a moment. "So what did you do to her, anyway?" Stevens stops laughing. "I don’t know." Jeb’s eyebrows elevate for a moment "What do you mea…" Jeb’s voice trails off. "I don’t remember," Lane says calmly, and stares out the window. "Hey you want pancakes?" * * * Two weeks ago. In the back of another car, The River Rat was slightly less scarred. "Don’t go far Doc, gonna need you in a few weeks. Big show in Oakland. Your big TV debut. Might want to lose a few pounds." The Grand Prix stopped in front of Dr. Rasmussen’s house. And without a word, the oncologist exited the vehicle, walking briskly towards his home. Lane watched another broken man walk away. "Drive," Lane uttered. And like that, David Rasmussen was in the rearview mirror.
