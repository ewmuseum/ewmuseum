The First Act

16 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-16-07 Lexington, Kentucky. Monday morning… In the dead light of the new dawn, we sit on the hood of my car, overlooking a city set on fire by the sunrise. Silence, just our cigarettes tracing ladders up to heaven. All the stars have all burnt out and now it’s just her, me, and our unborn child. One big, happy family. This time of day is the only time of day anyone can be truly be at peace. With that orange glow from the rising sun swallowing everything you know in warmth, it’s like God is writing a poem and you’re the main character. All you can do is sit and relax; the rest of the day can wait. "Do you have any more smokes, Sean? I’m out." Grim thoughts on a day like today. Too grim for the time of day. Thoughts like what to do next. Thoughts more like worry than real, actual, coherent introspections. Jump to conclusions; assume the worst, regret and dread. Fear and loathing. The human condition. "Sean? Hello-oh! Is anybody home?" I look over to her, droplets of sweat rushing across her skin, week old bruises covered up by a thin veil of makeup. Black eye blues, swollen lip nightmares, her black lungs craving to breathe smoke, but only breathing guilt toward me instead. She’s pathetic, in the worst way, but she tries her best to remain calm, strong, as beautiful as ever, even though she’s scared to death and can’t quite muster up the courage to ask me the question she’s been dying to ask me all night. "Sean! Sean Sterling! Hey! HEY! Wake up! Give me a cigarette already!" I look over at her, and try my best to give a grin that’s loving. I put on my best mask, try real hard to say something clever, something to add a little humor to the situation, "You’re not supposed to smoke when you’re pregnant, you know." "Who says?" She asks, raising an eyebrow and leaning in, grinning. This is the sweetest moment we’ve had in a long time, right here and now, and it’s also the fakest. A giant façade and we both know it. "The Surgeon General, of course. It works the same way as cocaine. You know, some half-assed whore snorts some coke with a baby stuck up her snatch, and the baby comes out all wide eyed and itchy, screaming bloody murder. You know why? That baby wants some goddamn crack. Same exact thing, except your kid’ll come out craving nicotine." "It’s your baby too, you know," Ouch. Pow. She hit me right in the kisser with that one. "And our kid will not come out screaming for a cigarette. If he does, we’ll have the Marlboro Man there to help with the delivery and Camel Joe can cut the umbilical cord. Deal? Now give me a goddamn cigarette." Deal. I give her a cigarette. It’s not like I wouldn’t have given her one anyway. Hell, I would force her to go through ten packs a day, if at all possible. Keep her stomach covered with nicotine patches too, enough to make her pass out if need be. Maybe the baby would come out dead and rotten, skin pale and veiny, organs turned to ash from a constant barrage of smoke. Anything to kill the seed growing in her belly. Her child. My mistake. Silence. Just our cigarettes tracing ladders up to heaven. She says, "You’re scared to death, aren’t you?" I can’t even bring myself to nod. I just look down, avoid eye contact. She says, "I’m just having a baby, Sean. It’s not like it’s the end of the world." Cringe. Sigh. Nod. It’s not the end of the world. *** Right after Killzone 45... A dirty light bulb in a dirty bathroom in some bar on Beale Street hung overhead, shaking and stuttering like the moon in the night. Outside, the Mississippi River flowed like the Styx itself, inviting me to leap in and get swept away from Beale Street to Oblivion. For a moment, the idea was tempting, and it took every half sober cell in my body to keep from doing it. With my hair matted to my forehead, sweat drenched and smelling like Memphis, I leaned over the sink, staring into the cracked mirror. The floor was riddled with ancient trash, piss stains, and half dead cockroaches. My reflection didn’t look much better off, my eyes turned bloodshot, my complexion snow white, puke still dribbling down from my lower lip like a rope. This is how I’ve learnt to deal with stress. This is how I’ve learnt to deal with those low moments you never see coming. Those earth shattering events that seem to appear out of thin air. All those times when it seems God has it out for you. Some people like to wrap themselves up in blankets, collapse on their beds, and be forgotten for a couple of days. Some people, they like to pretend a razorblade to the wrist does them some sort of good. Me, I make a beeline for the nearest bar and drown my sorrows. And every single time, when I’m in between that state of drunken stupor and that state of puking up my mortal remains in some shit clogged toilet, I feel born again. Warm. Immaculate. Perfect. The way church should make you feel. The way an orgasm should feel. For a moment, all my worries are pushed to the back of my mind. For a moment, every single problem I’ve ever had is forgotten. Afterwards, when it’s all over, when I’ve woken up in some anonymous bar’s bathroom, laying in a puddle of puke and filth, I stand up and I stumble back out, paying no mind to the barrage of stares from the rest of the patrons. I stumble out on the streets and I’m baptized in neon light from every bar on the street. So, I walk to the next bar and I stumble inside to repeat the process all over again. It was 2:58am. The bartender had just shouted out for the last call. The jukebox played some sad sappy country song by fuck knows who. I still hadn’t been back to the hotel. I couldn’t bring myself to go back to the hotel. Judith was there. Our unborn baby was there. I wanted to be faraway from both of them. Fuck responsibility. Fuck the ‘right thing to do’. Fuck my conscience. I stumbled back outside, onto the streets, and down the sidewalk. All the bars were shutting their doors. Every alley was filled with bastards who would like nothing better than to jump me and take my wallet, leaving me for dead in the gutter. Even the hookers were calling it a night. Fuck me for not having anywhere else to go. *** 30 minutes later… Imagine yourself as a teenager. You’ve been out past your curfew and now you just know you’re in deep shit. You walk up your driveway, real slow like, breathing into your hand as if to smell if there’s still weed or cigarettes on your breath. You walk up onto your porch and slide the key into the lock, turn and try to be as quiet as a mouse as you step inside. The living room is pitch black. A sigh of relief. You’re home free. You shut the door behind you and a light flickers on to reveal your father sitting in his recliner, staring at you with eyes piercing, a look on his face like the way you think of God. All at once, everything crashes and you prepare for one big silence. Then it comes out. Those four words that could make any man want to drop dead, "I’m disappointed in you." Same story. Different setting. As soon as I stepped foot inside of the hotel room, the lights flicked on and there she was, her mascara running trails down her cheeks. A frown like an upside down ‘U’ tacked onto her face, her voice coming out silky smooth, those four words, "Fuck you, you bastard." Crossing the carpet, I sneered out a reply, something like, "You’re fucking lucky I didn’t come back here first thing," I coughed, stepping into the kitchen where she’d laid bleeding only nights before. "And honestly, what the fuck did you expect? Am I supposed to be happy? Am I supposed to act like someone who wants to be a father? Do I look like a fucking family man to you?" "You know, I don’t know what the fuck I expected, Sean, but I at least expected you to show some sort of responsibility. This isn’t just my fault, you know. I figured you’d at least come back to the hotel and be with me. You think you’re the only one upset by this?" She stood up, yelling across the room as loud as she could. "Well, guess what? You’re fucking not! I’ve the last couple of weeks crying my eyes out and becoming a nervous wreck over telling you!" This is how most American families spend their evenings. Yelling. Screaming. Etc. The Leave it to Beaver families of old are long gone. This is the replacement. "What the fuck, Judith?! You’ve known for a couple of weeks now?! You fucking bitch!" "Oh, shut the fuck up, Sean! Do you realize how close I was to just packing my things and going back home?!" She walked into the kitchen, her eyes blazing red, her fists balled, a look of absolute anger on her face. She lifted her fist and slugged me across the face before I could even react. I stumbled backwards until I hit up against the kitchen counter. For a moment, my mouth hung wide open, my eyes wide with shock. Complete role reversal. Every bone in my body wanted to lift my fist and hit her back. Everything I knew told me to grab her by the throat and slam her head against the wall until she had more concussions than hell has sinners. I wanted to rip her hair out. I wanted to bloody her nose. Break her fingers. Punch her stomach until the she threw up the fucking baby. I wanted to slit her throat and leave her for dead in this hotel room. But I couldn’t. All I could find myself doing was staring her in wide eyed shock, mouth gaping, like some goddamn deer in the headlights. She must have busted my lip, because I could taste blood. I couldn’t find any words to say, so I spoke in vowels. I couldn’t move. I was frozen solid. And she spoke, with that sound of disappointment in her voice. Here comes that big long lecture. The guilt trip. The big letdown. "Sean, I expected better from you. Honestly. Even if you’re the worst thing that has ever happened to be, I expected you to at least compassion to be there for me. Do you realize how goddamn scared I am about this? Do you even begin to comprehend how I feel right now? Here’s a clue: Like complete and total shit. I’m horrified. I’m petrified. I don’t know what to do." She began pacing back and forth, fingers fumbling with a pack of cigarettes and then a lighter. Her mascara was running again. More trails were being blazed down her cheeks. She looked to me, her eyebrows curved up in a way that says ‘I’m hurt’. Her lips pouting. Her eyes wide, brilliant, beautiful, and broken. She said, "Have you not wondered why I haven’t left yet? You’re leaving tomorrow. Shouldn’t I have my things packed and a plane ticket prepared to go back home? Well, let me tell you, Sean. I don’t have a job. I can’t afford a place of my own. My parents refuse to harbor your whore. I’ve pretty much been thrown out of my own goddamn house. You are the only person I have left and I figured maybe, just maybe, I could depend on you. Am I making sense now, Sean? Do you understand now, Sean? I hope you’re fucking happy, you selfish prick." And then she turned to walk away. Left me standing in the kitchen, shocked. Slammed the door to the bedroom and I suppose she locked it. That was that. I slept on the couch. *** Lexington, Kentucky. Monday morning… We’d spent the night driving in silence, and the silence wasn’t broken until morning, when we found ourselves overlooking the city from the hood of my car. She asked for a cigarette and then we tried to make conversation. Now she stands, smoking my cigarettes and trying real hard to look a little better off than I do with this whole situation. Even though we’d both seen each others weakness. Even though we’d both confessed to being horrified. We’d fought, we’d bickered, we’d made each other feel like complete shit. Now, it was as if we’d both ran out of words to say. She has something on her mind. Something on the tip of her tongue that she wants to ask and can’t bring herself to. She’s scared of the answer. She’s not sure what I’ll say, so she’s been keeping it locked up. She wants to know how I’m going to treat her now. She wants to know if things will be different. She wants to know if she can stay with me or if she’s on her own. She wants to know if I still love her. "You know," I say, almost unable to speak the words I’m about to say. They seem foreign. New. "I’m sorry for some of the things I said." She looks over at me, the sun glowing behind her, making her shine bright like an angel. She nods, smiles a little. Tries to find her own words. Eventually, all she can say is, "So am I." She walks over and sits next to me. The city is starting to breathe. People are waking up, going to their jobs. Cars fill the streets and you can hear the honking horns from all the traffic. She takes a deep breath, tossing her cigarette butte to the ground. Looking over to me slow, she says, "What next?" I look back to her and smile. All I can think of to say is, "Who knows?" The future is a hell of a lot more frightening than I ever could have imagined.
