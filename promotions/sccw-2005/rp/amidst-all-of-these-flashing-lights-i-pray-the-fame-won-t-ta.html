Amidst all of these flashing lights, I pray the fame won't take my...

Date: Today I'm not writing a book, I just don't want my head to have to make up its own version of my history. I've never had a good memory, but it's been getting worse. Future self? You should consider skipping the next thousand words. There are things here I don't particularly want to remember, but it's only fair to give myself the choice. I'm already guilty about what happened. I'm sure he was lying about part of what he said, but I can't remember to know for sure - that part pisses me off more than anything. This is the week when I had one of the hardest talks with Darcy I'll ever have. This is when Jared saw the worst of me, and reminded me how Alex always dealt with them. This is the week that my tag partner stepped over the line. It's when that button got pushed, that partner ran his mouth and caught me on the wrong day. This is the week I end the career of Lane Stevens. ... Again. At two o'clock in the morning, there was nothing left in her head or her heart. It was a strange emptiness that provided no clarity and still somehow left no room. She'd played World of Warcraft for two hours, clicking her way through the motions, passionless. That sort of thing was usually a good way to clear her mind. Instead, the pointlessness frustrated her. She missed him - all of them, actually - and it hurt. It hurt more than not remembering how much it had hurt to ruin Darcy. It hurt so much that she just felt so fucking empty about it. The emotions were there; She knew what she was supposed to be feeling, but the mental disconnect killed the kind of pain that would have made her feel human about it. Instead, there was emptiness. She'd cry for an hour after that. As much as Amy had always wished her history would disappear - that all the bad could be erased - it wasn't supposed to happen this way. It wasn't supposed to be slipping away from her. There, she wished not to be forgetting her mistakes. She didn't want to be detached, wanted her memories back. Instead, emptiness. Her memory had always been bad - drugs and head injuries hadn't helped matters - so she'd occasionally find herself writing a journal of big events or little nothings. A few had been lost to bad harddrives or Mac upgrades, so she decided to take pen to paper if she was going to make a real effort to catalogue her memories moving forward. Maybe it was the crying, that she hadn't eaten or those few glasses of wine that made her hand that much less steady than it usually was. Her dominant hand had always tended to quake when she tried to focus with it, and it worsened after a broken wrist from a match against Lane Stevens years before, but it usually wasn't that bad. Perhaps her mood, more than anything, was what led her to abandon the idea of traditional writing - her handwriting was terrible, especially with her nerves shaking her, and the idea of being able to read it in five years was laughable. She probably wouldn't have even remembered why her writing was so uneven five years down the road, it'd just be like everything else - she'd know it occurred through some means of permanent reminder, the failed sheet of looseleaf in this case, but she'd have no recollection of why or what it felt like in that moment. So she picked up her laptop. Every day, she would do this. If it was inevitable that the drugs would catch up with her, she sure as hell wasn't letting them claim the memories that created her sense of self and who she was, no matter how bad a version of Amy Campbell she considered it to have been. Among the hundreds of lists of hundreds of things she would and wouldn't do, Amy would not rely on chemically-fried neurons and other people's perceptions to tell her who she was. When most people have bad days, they've had a long, rough day at work. Maybe their boss yelled at them, or they have to miss Timmy's soccer game to work an extra file. My bad days are bad fucking days. Today didn't start as one, but it got there fast enough. I still think it's weird that I kinda miss the blonde girl who used to give me my chilled tea on the mornings I'd walk to the little cafe near my... our, whatever... house in Oakland. In truth, she was the closest thing to a friend that I had near home, and I would've killed for a silly conversation with her this morning. Now I'm in a nice neighborhood where nobody knows me and I think a few of my neighbors are convinced I'm living with a sugar daddy. I'm getting ahead of myself, though - I'm supposed to be organizing this shit. Let's start with a few days ago, what little I can remember of Japan... 3AM Local Time Japan She'd hoped that pretending to sleep might actually lead to it - that her mind would drift off and become distracted enough not to tear her back to consciousness the moment rest broke down the barrier. She was wrong, and felt a little guilty because she was actively jealous of his ability to fall asleep, seemingly worry-free. It was annoying, she thought selfishly, because she had to deal with the weight of her vices before any peace could come, even though her body was worn down and exhausted from the withdrawal already. It was hard not to groan when she rolled, ever-so-slightly inching out from under the arm that Jared had draped over her. The back of her hand hit the nightstand lightly, and she slid it around until it scooped up her BlackBerry. Her other hand went to work to steady the slight shaking when she tried holding it still to check the time. ''Fuck.'' It needed to get easier to reason through; She couldn't panic in front of Jared. The poorly-paced breathing wasn't helping, and neither was the mountain of blankets that kept her body temperature at a boiling point. When she threw them aside, something moved near the window - the dull, red glow from the city lights through the blinds and curtain had been momentarily interrupted - and that froze her in place, eyes fixed on where the neon trickled in. Unconsciously, one hand clawed at the sheets near her new beau, causing scraped up knuckles to accidentally brush against his ribcage. She winced and hoped he hadn't felt it. Again, the lights outside dimmed for a beat. If not for the chill that creeped up her spine in spite of the sweat on her brow, she would've sworn she'd just blinked without realizing it. Everything tensed up, goosebumps rose across her stomach and Amy reached again for the covers she'd cast off. None of this helped her control breaths that came quickly enough, but refused to fill her lungs. She felt suddenly nauseus, then finally realized what that thing in the window was. ''Of course,'' She squeezed her eyes shut. ''God damnit.'' Being rational wouldn't work - she needed a helping hand, something to ground her. Someone to ground her... but he couldn't fill that role. He shouldn't have had to. She gave her head a violent shake, not realizing that one fist had still been balled in the sheets and thrashed along with her. A hissing intake of air followed; it was the kind of deep breath Amy had been hoping for, but it wasn't hers. A relaxed sigh came after, and it seemed like he was stirring mid-dream, but the slight roll over confirmed her fears. ''Go back to sleep.'' When she whispered, the little redhead tried to reach out and touch him. Her arm suddenly refused to move. ''Everything's cool.'' 'Yeah.' It was almost a groan, and barely audible due to the way his face rested on the pillow. A contented smile formed on his lips as his arm instinctively stretched across her. He'd draw her back to him, let the scent of her shampoo fill his nostrils, and dream of peach trees in a sun-drenched orchard. His hand came to rest on the flesh of her stomach, her tee-shirt having ridden slightly up. The skin was hot, feverish even, and soaked with sweat. 'Wusrong?' he asked. It took a great deal of effort to pry his head from the pillow even a few inches, and the one eye he opened took in the world as a pixellated blur. 'Burning up.' ''I'm fine.'' She gasped at the tickle of the light touch, somehow tensing more. ''Maybe jet-lagged. I'm fine.'' Jared's eyebrows curled up when he started seeing her clearly; Beads of sweat anywhere her skin was bare, damp hair clinging to her face, and the light coming in from the window drew shadows across every clenched muscle. It didn't take a genius to feel the concern take over him, and that's what moved Amy to speak again - still glaring at the window. ''I lied. Just don't call anyone, okay? Even if...'' She swallowed and drew another short breath. ''Even if I look really sick. Even if I act crazy.'' 'Wait, what?' He pushed himself up as best he could, still waiting for the rest of his motor functions to come back online. But his mind had booted-up quick, sent into a state of red alert over the unknown. There would be no peach trees tonight. 'Sick? Crazy? Amy, tell me what's wrong.' ''Nothing.'' She insisted, and ''Everything'' she eventually relinquished. ''It's... I can barely breathe, so please don't decide that I'm a maniac and you're leaving me. Give me a chance not to be too heavy for you.'' There was a split-second of complete darkness again. She'd caught it surely that time, and managed to point to the window. ''Fuck! Make sure that's... just don't leave, okay? But you can if you want to. Just... don't.'' She swallowed, sighed and clearly didn't realize that she'd just had a few moments of breathing perfectly. ''I sound crazy right now. I'm just... it's a panic attack and worse. It's my fault.'' Black again. That time, she was jolted by nerves. ''Tell me we're okay, okay?'' 'I... Yeah, we're... What are you looking at? What's over there?' He rolled off the bed and his feet found the floor, but on his way to the window he stumbled on a shoe and inadvertently kicked the coffee table. 'Ah, fuck,' he hissed, and hopped the rest of the way. Confused, Jared drew back the curtain, looking for any sign of... anything, but instead found only fabric and glass. 'What's over here? What's going on?' ''It was... I... uh. Just something weird. Blinking non-lights or whatever. Fucked up.'' She managed to flip herself over, half-burying her face in a pillow. ''Stay away from the window. I don't wanna worry about you. Come back.'' ''Stay away from the...?'' Confused, he looked to the window, and then back to her. He did obey her request, sliding along the carpet until he was bedside. There he knelt, lowering himself to eye level. ''Amy,'' he said softly. ''Please, just tell me what's wrong.'' ''I usually have some to get through the craving. Alex always used to keep a little for me.'' She said. ''I... I can't deal with none. Thought I could.'' ''You're not making any sense...'' ''I'm lying. I can deal with none. I just didn't expect... expect this. I'm gonna get sick tonight.'' Amy whimpered, ''Really sick, and it's gonna be hell on both of us. You should go. Or stay. Actually, stay. You don't have to, though. Fuck cocaine, that stupid fuckin' cocksucker.'' She squeezed her eyes shut before continuing, trying to keep reason on her side. ''I'll tell you what to do if you stay. If you don't, we'll both wake up tomorrow and I promise everything will be okay - you don't have to do this with me. Nobody's gonna think less of you, just... just don't call anyone in, even if I seem like I'm half alive. Career and all that.'' Jared moved to his knees, careful to not make any sudden movements which might startle her. He was even more careful when he leaned his elbows onto the bed, and gently rested a hand on her back. Terrified as he was, controlling it was tantamount. Don't run, he told himself, no matter how much you want to. ''Hey, it's okay,'' he said. ''Amy? Hey... It's okay. Whatever's going on... Whatever you need. I'll do the best I can, I promise.'' ''You used to live here. Do you know anyone who could get--no. No matter how I act, don't go out looking for a fucking drug dealer. I'm a goddamn idiot.'' If her arm could move again, she would have smacked herself. ''Just... I dunno. Empty out the ice bucket, and don't listen to anything I say overnight. Promise that.'' ''Yeah. Yeah, okay. I promise.'' He sprung to his feet and dashed in the direction of the minibar. He contemplated just dumping the ice on the floor. ''Do you need anything? Water maybe?'' Thirty seconds later, Jared needed a new t-shirt and Amy tried holding down pills to supress nausea. The night went on slowly, and Campbell's throat burned from bile and groans of pain by the end of it. At one point, she honestly thought she'd been dying for the hundredth time; Jared stayed through it. He fed her ice cubes and assured her things would be okay. When she finally passed out, things were okay. The apologies for actions and things said that night would start the moment she woke up, and continue for weeks to come. Present. For a year, he'd insisted that damn squeak wasn't there, and it still gave his approach away, but watching through the window was not an option. The second she heard the brakes grind, her legs got weak and her heart nervous shiver worsened. This must be how chihuahuas feel, she thought and tried to spare a chuckling for herself when taking the last swallow of tea. Reaper was already on his way to the door; It had been a long time since he'd heard that car, and it was a little depressing to see how quick the chubby cat was to prance about it. When she wasn't able to hear the car squeaking any longer, Amy made her way down the stairs. It was a slow process - the stairway in her new place was jointed, and her bad knee would never get used to the moment of rest on the landing before they continued. She quietly vowed to install a fireman's post when she hit the ground floor only a moment after the doorbell rang. ''It's not locked.'' She grumbled. Another ring. ''I said it's n--'' Campbell had growled when she pulled the door open, misjudging her step forward enough to bump straight into the chest of her visitor. He'd half-heartedly put an arm out to stop her fall, but the little redhead was on her backside by the time it extended. ''Damnit, Darcy!'' ''Thought single life might've improved your balance. Don't chicks with a reputation need to wear heels to snag a guy's attention these d--nevermind,'' He'd chuckled at first, but stopped himself and shook his head, kneeling down to help her up. ''That's a shitty thing to say. Here.'' As expected, Amy was quick to swat the helping hand away. She desperately tried to look strong - like she wasn't a blink or two away from tears at any given time - in keeping her distance and not giving her ex anything more to laugh at. Nothing needed to be said; She simply led the way, heading toward the small dining area off the kitchen while the tall, dark-haired man lagged behind, scooping up the stout, dark-haired cat that rubbed against his leg. ''Hey, buddy! I missed you!'' No matter how much she cared for Jared Sykes, her break from Darcy was still in a stage where the slightest gesture tugged at her heart. It was only made worse by the guilt that hung over her; The feeling that she should remind him she's sorry, hug and never let him go didn't fade nearly fast enough for her liking. The more recent split with Alexandra Pierce wasn't nearly so bad - Amy was the betrayed party there, and she resented Pierce for helping cause the things she felt so guilty and obliged to Darcy for. That wasn't to mention her history with Desade was short, worth a tenth of what she'd had with her husband. Again, Campbell shook her head - she wasn't in a mindframe to deal with these things. ''C'mon. You know where I live now, just gimmie whatever I need to sign.'' ''I don't need you to sign anything. I'm here to talk.'' ''You drove for six hours to talk? You could've just ca--'' ''I could have called, yeah, but I drove six hours to look you in the eye.'' ''And do what?'' ''To talk.'' He wasn't given an invitation to sit down, but Darcy did so anyway. The dining area was decorated pretty subtly, which was surprising. More surprising were the photos squared off on the wall - one of she and Jared Sykes sitting on a staircase somewhere, both laughing at something off-camera with wide, genuine, happy smiles that incensed Markson to see. ''About what happened to us, among other things.'' He continued, ''Like that I thought you'd be back in Canada by now and...'' Amy finally had that wine rack she'd always wanted. Most of the bottles in it looked empty, but she found one and poured herself a glass. There was no offer extended to Darcy - she even made a little show in the way she sat down, scratched the cat when he hoisted himself onto the tabletop and finally took a sip. ''... And?'' ''And that I'm happy you're not.'' When she swallowed that first bit of wine, the bitter squint wasn't because of where the sugars had settled. ''I... what?'' Her legs got weaker, because she'd imagined this speech before, but hoped for the sake of her willpower it wasn't what was coming. ''Because I'd be happy there and you don't want--'' ''That's not it at all,'' When Darcy answered, he rubbed a hand on the back of his neck. He wouldn't look her in the eye anymore, even when he asked a question. ''Why does it have to be like this, Ames? Just tell me that.'' If there was any sensation other than the pit in her stomach churning or the numb, shakiness to her limbs, she might have stood up to make a point. Instead, Amy mustered up her best matter-of-fact glare and shot it, even if he wasn't looking. She silently wondered if he could hear the shattering inside her. ''It's like this because you filed for a divorce, Darcy.'' The wine glass was pushed away, half-emptied. ''Because I fucked up royally.'' ''But you're sorry for it.'' ''I am, but--'' ''You begged me. You were miserable. You are miserable.'' ''I cheated on you, for fuck's sake.'' ''Why are you fighting me here?'' Amy shook her head. ''Because now I know where you're taking this and I know that we can't do it.'' ''Why?'' ''A hundred reasons! All the shit I pulled aside, I loved someone you aren't anymore.'' ''You were just clouded. There was work, there was... look,'' He said. ''I think you loved who I am, I just lost sight of who that was for a little while.'' ''Even if that's the case, you loved what I can't be.'' ''Who's to say that?'' ''Me!'' She squeaked. ''I was late for our ceremony, remember? Jeph found me drinking in the reception area, hiding behind the bar in that goddamned dress, and he had to convince me that I was worth marrying. I had to be talked out of walking away from you because I thought you were making the biggest mistake of your life. I knew I wasn't grown-up enough, I wasn't willing to sacrifice or compromise to be someone's wife. You shouldn't have to do that, right? But I didn't listen to that logic long enough. I cracked, let myself get talked down from the ledge. Think of how much easier it would've been for me to have run then. That's years you'd get back, years I might have grown, not felt obligated to force myself down... maybe we'd have been together again by now, ready to dive into it with no reservations.'' There were the beginnings of tears threatening to break past the little bit of eyeliner she hadn't washed off from the night before. She'd claimed to have been at an important meeting earlier in the day and just hadn't had time to get a shower. In reality, she'd been up from dusk 'till dawn on Skype with Jared, and only woke up a half an hour before her ex-husband arrived, but she pulled it all together. Despite her voice cracking, the front was still strong when her features softened. It was less about the silliness of having spent the night watching and discussing movies over webcam, and more about the fact that she'd cared enough to wear makeup to do it. ''I... what was I saying?'' ''Oh. Right. I'm... I'm ready to start making those compromises, those little adjustments. I'm trying to cut out the negative and put in that effort, just...'' She took a moment to blink, having drifted off course. Sykes would have hated to know this conversation was taking place, and that threw her off - there was no explaining why she'd been thinking about him the whole time, though. ''Just not with you.'' Not even Markson's eyes were dry. He blinked back the tears, though. ''I'm not asking you to come back, Amy. I'm telling you that I'm getting to a place where I'm becoming more willing to forgive, more willing to put in the work. Just because you say you're immatu--'' ''Saying I'm still immature isn't an excuse, and admitting it doesn't make it any better. Being self-aware only makes it dumber that I'm still this way, but I'm not working on it for you.'' She snapped. ''I'm trying to become a functioning human being, not the next 'Remembering...' mini-header on a wrestling blog. I know who you are, what you want, and that I can't be it.'' The conversation had made its way back into the entry during Amy's speech, because her husband had tried walking away from it. He'd always done that. She thought it would carry him right out the door, but he turned around before reaching the handle, and the little redhead was hardly ready to chase him out. ''You don't know who I really am, because I barely do.'' Markson shook his head. ''I don't think either of us gave one another a proper chance in the marriage to stand up and show ourselves. Right now, we've seen and done too much to go back to it right away, and I know that.'' He cringed and sighed, before slamming the door behind him. ''I drove six hours to tell you that none of any of it means we have to be this way forever, and that I'm happy to see you crack a smile on TV every so often.'' Then and only then did Amy open her mouth to say something, but it was too far gone. She croaked, and he waved a hand as he went down the walkway, knowing she wouldn't - couldn't - follow him out there. If the words had come, she would have told him she was happy with Jared; Content with life. ''Good luck this week, Ames.'' He muttered the last words as he neared the parking lot, so chances were that she hadn't heard them. ''I love you.'' Later... It was already the day from hell. No contact from Sykes, too much contact from Darcy... But as always, a sense of safety wafted after Malcolm Girard. You'd just never hear his daughter tell him that. It was rare enough for her to own up to being his daughter. She was working through a second teenaged rebellion at the age of twenty seven. That didn't stop her from unloading her problems when he knocked at her door, though - but unlike Darcy Markson, he didn't call before he came. He'd caught her wearing a t-shirt, sweatpants, the melting crust of leftover eyeliner and a whole lot of tear stains. That's when Malcolm knew his request would have to hold off. ''I believed him, because he doesn't want to hurt me - not all the time. He's blind, blames Alex more than he blames me, and he knows how to hurt her. He's not afraid of her, either - he thinks she's soft, pathetic. Y'know, like a lot of people do now... but he'll never do anything about it, because he's soft.'' ''Not for her, though.'' Malcolm noted. They'd been chatting for a few minutes after Amy went through a particularly trying conversation with her soon-to-be ex-husband. At least, he was fairly sure Markson would be her ex-husband; The little spitfire was moved by his words, but seemed unwilling to move for them. As for Girard... well, he'd let her finish her tangent, then ask him why he was there in the first place. His shields were at the ready for that occasion. ''No - but for Quinn. He thinks it'd be best for her to be far, far away from Alex - and he knows that'd kill Al - but he'd never make a move to get her back with her adoptive parents because Quinn would resent him. Hate him, even. You don't... I dunno, think I should consider what he said?'' ''I just want whatever you feel is best,'' was his mantra. ''And a favor, but I'll get to that when you're--'' ''A favor.'' Amy leaned back in her chair, arms folded. Only a little while ago, she'd been face-to-face with her ex in that same seat, so dealing with her father seemed easy in comparison. ''Of-fuckin'-course that's why you'd show up.'' ''It's nothing terrible, I was just---'' BRRRRRIIIINNNNNNG! When the doorbell interrupted her biological father, Amy's eyes rolled. She gestured for him to pause and headed to the door, half-expecting to find Alexandra Pierce looking for help, while praying it would be Jared Sykes flying across the country for a hug and a kiss, because that was the only thing she needed to unwind from the day it had been. Malcolm had followed after her shortly, because he didn't know Darcy well enough to know the man wouldn't come back feeling rejected and exact some stupid, macho vengeance. He watched from afar as Amy opened the door to the face of a single stranger. ''K. Amelia Campbell?'' She wasn't carrying an oversized novelty check. There were no cameras. There was no way this was a good visit. ''Yes... can I help you?'' ''Ma'am, you've been served.'' ''Is this a j-'' Once Amy grabbed the papers, the woman walked away. It wasn't a joke, though some parties may have considered it humorous to deliver flowers shortly after a process server's arrival. As anyone would have guessed, Lane Stevens was one of those people. Campbell narrowly avoided decking the boy who delivered her the 'Happy Lawsuit' bouquet. Malcolm wouldn't get to ask for the favor that day; He'd be too busy convincing his daughter that one wrestler suing another for 'undue force' used years before was a transparent case, a waste of time meant just to annoy her. He'd also help her burn the flowers and card Stevens had sent behind the process server. The singing telegram that came an hour later was even more difficult to get rid of. The next morning, he ended up being the one to drive her to the airport after Jared Sykes wasn't answering his phone overnight. She'd had enough of that day, and needed nothing more than his company -- she just hoped he felt the same way when she made it to Boston to surprise him. As much as Lane had done to her - including suing for the first time she broke his neck - there were things even Jared Sykes couldn't fix. Stevens still managed to hold a full hand at the ready, willing to throw whatever he could at her. There are a lot of things Amy Campbell's memory has let slip by, and even more that alcohol and drugs both caused and erased. Those lost memories had given The River Rat the worst of his ammunition, and Amy would never know or believe it.
