Date: October 4th 2009 The Spark The thud of the ever-present dance music, deadened by its passage through the walls of the bathroom is permeated by another sound, one not easily identifiable and with no visible source. The urinal stalls are empty and there is nobody is at the sinks. One of the cubicle doors, however, is closed, the red circle on the handle indicating that it is occupied. Inside the cubicle is Chris Roth, 24 years old, born and bred in Winter Park, Florida, a small-town boy come to the West Coast with visions of fame and fortune. With him is a credit card, a bottle of Corona and a line of the finest Colombian cocaine, half of which has already gone up his nose. Our unidentified sound – snorting. You see, like so many others, the dream didn’t quite live up to the reality. After signing up with all the agencies, going to all the castings, trying to get in with the right people, Chris got no work and precisely no offers for acting work, modeling work or indeed work of any sort. Apart from cleaning up sick at Islands Of Adventure that is. So he drifted along, got a small room in a cheap apartment building, spent his wage on clothes and entry to clubs, hoping to catch a lucky break, you know, running into a casting director, something like that. Tonight was that night. ‘How’s that goin’ down?’ Oh yeah, Holly. The other occupant of the cubicle. Chris’ ticket to the big-time. Okay, maybe not the big big-time, but the pretty big-time. Holly worked in modeling you see, said that Chris had the look to land the new campaign for an unpronounceable Italian clothing company. Good money, she said, but more importantly, a foot in the door. They’d party tonight and go see the agent in the morning together. ‘Whoo! That’s…that’s a rush!’ The laugh that came from her lips was coquettish, teasing. ‘I thought you’d like it! Finish the rest of it and I’ll see you on the dance floor cutie.’ She slides out of the cubicle, her breasts pressing against Chris as she leans in to speak in his ear. ‘Don’t be long,’ she whispers. Then she’s gone, leaving our Mr Roth to finish off the end of his line. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ When the paramedics were called to the club, they were able to make an instant diagnosis. It was an overdose, no doubt. Strange to see one here though, these high-class clubs were usually pretty good about keeping the dealers out and for those party-goers who did use, ODs just didn’t happen. They knew their limits and never exceeded them, doing it for a quick buzz rather than any deep-seated need for a hit. As they loaded Chris into the ambulance, the woman known to him as Holly watched from the middle of the crowd, a small, satisfied smile spreading across her face, before melting back into the shadows. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ ‘Find out who she is.’ Mr Marquez said this calmly and evenly, but there was no mistaking what he was feeling. His eyes were sharp and focused despite the late (or should that be early?) hour. ‘We’re trying, but all we have is a scan of her drivers license which turned out to be a fake. She could be anyone.’ The stare that Brian Jacobs received for this would have killed a lesser man. ‘Well she isn’t just anyone, is she? She’s a person who has deliberately come into my club, almost killed someone with some tainted coke and tried to have us shut down. If Julia hadn’t found that stash hidden in the cloak room, we’d have been in deep shit.’ ‘Okay, I’ll try to pull in a few favours from a contact downtown, see if we can pick her up on any of the traffic cams near the club.’ ‘Good, I want to know the second you have anything. Make sure we hav-‘ The phone stopped him short. Brian glanced first at it, then back up to his boss. ‘Should I leave?’ Josh pondered this for a second before responding. His answer was surprising. ‘No, it’s okay, stay.’ A quick move of the hand and the speakerphone came to life. The second the caller identified themselves, Josh knew it was more bad news. ‘Hello, it’s Frank.’ ‘What are you calling at this time for?’ ‘We’ve had a situation here tonight sir. Drugs. One of our customers has-‘ ‘Wait, let me guess – OD in the toilets? Coke found stashed on the premises?’ ‘Uh…yes about the OD but we haven’t found any coke.’ ‘It’s there, search the place from top to bottom, call me when you have it.’ ‘…Sure thing.’ The line went dead. Brian was unsure what to do now. Mr Marquez was still calm and collected…but he’d seen him go from that to a frenzy of anger in a split second before and didn’t want to be on the receiving end of it. ‘I’ll go see if anything else has happened at the club sir.’ Marquez didn’t respond and Brian eased himself out of the room without any further ado. Settling down into the large leather chair, Josh steepled his fingers and leant back, his exterior a mask while inside his mind was working overtime. Something was going on, he just didn’t know what. He didn’t like that. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ 24 hours later, the plot had thickened. All 6 clubs owned by our Mr Marquez had experienced an unexpected drama. From New York to Las Vegas, the pattern of events was remarkably similar. A customer, usually someone there on their own, not a regular, is found in the toilets having had an overdose. Elsewhere on the premises, large stashes of drugs are discovered. Nobody sees or knows anything, the suspects unidentifiable, their IDs fake. Fortunately, all the drugs stashed had been discovered by club staff before the authorities had thought to search the premises. As for those who had overdosed, it was a fine and a slap on the wrists for the respective managers, nothing more. Josh Marquez, however, wasn’t a man to let things go. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ The Search The search had been going on for 3 days before the breakthrough came. The voluptuous young woman who had led our Mr Roth astray had been caught on camera getting into a car just down the street from the club. Josh had had to pull a few strings and call in a lot of favours but eventually he’d managed to get a trace done on the car registration and this in turn gave him an address. An address to which two very large men were now en route… *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ The BMW X5 pulled up outside the house without fanfare. The driver turned to his companion and asked a simple question. ‘Front or back?’ ‘I’ll take front.’ ‘Okay. Give me 15 seconds when you get there.’ That was it, no more communication was needed. The two had worked together so many times before that their link was almost telepathic. As one co-worker was very fond of telling them, they were like a married couple. For two men of their size, they moved with surprising quietness and stealth. So much so that the man sitting in the front room of the house never heard them coming… *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ Thirty seconds later… ‘I swear, I don’t know who she was!’ For Eddie Falco, 37 years old and general layabout, this was not a good situation. Lucas and Rick, the two large gentlemen we met earlier, had burst into his home with out warning and were currently interrogating him. While Lucas held him up against the wall, Rick leafed through the letter rack, searching for anything of use. ‘Why are you lying Eddie? We know you picked up this woman, all we want to know is where you took her and who arranged the pick-up.’ To say he was scared would be an understatement. He’d had his share of run-ins with dodgy characters, but never with guys like these two. They weren’t angry or acting in rage, no. Instead they were calm and collected, methodical in the way they operated. Not your everyday hired muscle. ‘I told you, I don’t know!’ Rick turns round at this and makes his way over to them. ‘Eddie, this is your last chance. The next time my colleague here asks you what you know, you will tell him everything you know. For every time you refuse, we will hurt you, understand?’ Falco was almost trembling as he nodded his understanding. ‘Good, I’m glad we have an accord.’ With that, Rick made his way out of the sitting room and started to search the kitchen area. Lucas kept hold of Eddie and asked the questions. ‘Who was she?’ ‘I don’t know her name-‘ ‘Don’t mess around Eddie…’ The menace in Lucas’ voice was unmistakable. ‘Okay, okay! I don’t know her name, but I can tell you where I left her!’ The address which Eddie gave was that of a very nice apartment complex on the other side of town, only a few miles from the club. ‘Good Eddie, you’re doing well. Now tell me who set up the pick-up.’ ‘Some guy, down at McIlhenny’s! Came up to me, asked if I wanted to make some quick cash.’ ‘You know him?’ ‘No, never seen him before. Looked like he had money, not the regular kinda guy we get at the bar.’ ‘Anything else you think I should know?’ ‘The woman, she was on the phone in the car, said the name Greg or Gregory or something a lot.’ Lucas pondered this information for a second before he started lowering Eddie to the ground. When he let him go, Eddie didn’t move, not wanting to antagonize him for no good reason. ‘That’s all I know, I swear.’ ‘It better be Mr Falco, because if we find out you’ve been lying or keeping back anything, you’d better hope your health insurance is in order, understand?’ ‘Yeah, yeah, I get it.’ With this, Lucas turns on his heel and walks out of the room, calling Rick as he exits the front door. ‘Let’s go, we’ve got all we need.’ With that they are gone, the peace of the house restored, with the only sign of their having been there the sweat on Eddie’s brow. He didn’t care that he’d told them about the woman or the guy in the bar, even when they’d specifically told him to say nothing. Something told him that the men who had just visited him were not to be messed with, no matter what the consequences. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ ‘Boss, we have some news for you.’ As Lucas recounted the information they had garnered from the unfortunate Mr Falco, Rick drove at a steady pace towards the address they had been given. ‘We’re on our way to the apartment building now, what are your instructions?’ After listening for a moment, Lucas nodded his head. ‘Okay, understood.’ Hanging up, he turned to his partner, who spoke without taking his eyes off the road. ‘We on?’ ‘We’re on.’ *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ The Turnaround The apartment of Eva Tulane was, from the outside at least, elegantly understated. An assortment of potted plants lined the paved path to the brand new door, the whole exterior reeking of class. To the rear, a small grass area, the rest of the space filled with a swimming pool and Jacuzzi, double doors opening out onto it from the main house. The parking area is to the side, full of German cars. A black Audi TT, polished within an inch of its life and with rims so shiny they would blind God is this car that Eva is making her way to, walking surprisingly fast considering the height of her heels and the tightness of her dress. At 10PM most people are settled for the night, but her job requires her to be awake and active until 4 in the AM. Eva’s night is just beginning. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ The diversion was a nuisance but she reckoned she’d still be on time if she pushed the pace just a little. So down pressed the flat sole of her driving shoe and up went the needle as the car’s speed increased. The speed limit was 50, she was doing nearer to 80, but at this time she’d be fine. Or so she thought. For unbeknownst to her as she sat in her seat, bobbing her head to the classic sound of ‘My Sharona’, she was being followed. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ When the police car pulled alongside, flashing its lights, the female cop in the passenger seat waving to her to pull over, her first thought was simple. ‘Oh shit, this is just what I need.’ Despite this, she wasn’t overly worried. Traffic violations like this were a dime-a-dozen to cops, she would probably get a fine and a warning to be more careful in future and would be a little late for work. As she pulled in to the side of the road, the cruiser parked behind her, both cops getting out and making their way to the car, hands on holsters. This in itself was a little odd, but then she supposed these guys had to be careful, they never knew when they could be stopping a nutcase. The cop on her side motioned for her to lower the window and she willingly obliged. ‘What seems to be the problem officer?’ ‘Could you please step slowly out of the car ma’am, keeping your hands in view at all times.’ ‘Officer, I’m sorry I was speeding a little but this is ridic-‘ ‘Ma’am, please step out of the car.’ Confusion was etched across her face as she carried out his request, stepping slowly out of the vehicle and wondering what the hell was going on. ‘Turn round and put your hands on the roof of the car ma’am.’ ‘What is going on?’ ‘Ma’am we have reason to believe that you are in possession of controlled substances and an unlicensed firearm. As such we are going to search your vehicle. Do you have any objection to this?’ ‘What? That’s insane. Of course you can search my car, I have nothing to hide.’ ‘Very well ma’am. Officer Hodge, it’s all yours.’ *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ ‘Miss Tulane, you are under arrest for the possession of cocaine with the intention to supply and the possession of a concealed and unlicensed firearm. You do not have to say anything…’ As the cop Mirandised her, Eva’s head was spinning. How had that coke got there? She had been careful not to use her own car when doing the job and she’s never had a gun in her life. Yet there they were, hidden under her backseat. So here she was, being taken to the police station in the back of a cruiser, with no idea of how this all had happened or what she was going to do to clear her name. The only thing she knew was who she’d be calling with her one call. Gregor. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ While this scene was unfolding, a reasonable sized crowd had gathered. The common thinking was that something big was going on, as it wasn’t often that cops stopped and searched cars round here. The multiplex on the other side of the road had just emptied out and it seemed like the cinemagoers were keen for another show. The diner next door was now empty save for two customers. Two large men, who sat with their coffees and watched the events with a cool detachment. As they drained their mugs and stood up, leaving a healthy tip on the table, one pulled a phone from his pocket and selected a number from his contact list. Two rings later, the call was answered. ‘She’s toast, do you want us to continue?’ ‘Okay, it’ll be done tomorrow.’ Ending the call, he and his partner climbed into a BMW X5 and pulled smoothly out of the lot, leaving the unfortunate Eva Tulane in the custody of the authorities. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ The End When Gregor Lukevich received the call from a hysterical Eva Tulane, alarm bells started ringing. He’d known her for 6 years, she’d done things for him that he wouldn’t have trusted anyone else to do and if there was one thing he knew, it was that she was careful to a fault. For something like this to happen meant that someone else had interfered in his affairs. This didn’t sit well. ‘Yes, yes I understand. Calm down and we will fix it.’ Despite his years in the United States, his Ukrainian accent was still pronounced in his speech. Eva was fond of teasing him that he sounded like a Bond villain, to which he always responded that he wasn’t quite that diabolical. ‘I will send a lawyer to you, the best I know. Do not worry, it will be alright.’ He hung up with a frown on his face, pressing the intercom button on his phone. ‘Lucia, hold my calls please.’ ‘Yes Gregor.’ Relaxing back in his chair, he spun round to face the window, taking in the view over the city as he pondered his next move. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ An indeterminate amount of time had passed when the silence of Gregor’s reverie was shattered by the peal of his phone. Angrily, he reached back and picked it up. ‘Lucia, I said hold my calls!’ ‘I know Gregor…but this is urgent.’ ‘Urgent how?’ ‘I’ll let Daniel tell you that.’ Daniel? Why would Daniel be calling him at this time? In 3 years of working for Gregor, Daniel had never once called at this time, had never needed to reach him urgently. Something was seriously wrong. ‘Put him through.’ *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ Gregor was in shock. Of all the possibilities he had conjured in his mind about the reason for Daniel’s call, this hadn’t even been considered. That this had happened so soon after Eva’s arrest was a sure sign of a concerted campaign against him. He would find out who it was and he would make them sorry they’d ever crossed Gregor Valerie Ulyanov Lukevich. ‘Lucia, get my car ready. I’m going down there.’ ‘No problem Gregor.’ ‘Mr Lukevich. Today, I am Mr Lukevich.’ ‘Sorry, Mr Lukevich.’ *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ The fire marshal made his way through the barricades towards the owner of the club, who was deep in discussion with the club manager. ‘Mr Lukevich, Mr Stevens?’ ‘Yes.’ The response came from the owner, the club manager taking what appeared to be a secondary role, a step back and to the side of his boss. ‘I’m George Sharpe, the fire marshal.’ ‘Do you have any idea about what happened?’ ‘It’s too soon to say definitively Mr Lukevich but it looks like arson. We’ve found traces of accelerant in the storage room where the fire originated and judging by the rate that the fire spread, it was definitely set on purpose.’ Gregor took this surprisingly well, all things being considered. His manager piped up at this point. ‘I’ve talked to my staff and nobody noticed anything suspicious or out of the ordinary. Obviously we were slightly short-staffed with Eva not being here but even so, we would have noticed anything strange.’ ‘Well gents, I don’t know what else I can tell you. I do know that there is a detective over there who wants a word. I’ll let him know you are here.’ *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ ‘Any unusual incidents in the club tonight Mr Stevens? Anything that could have distracted your staff and allowed someone to gain access to the private areas of the building? ’ The questions had been coming thick and fast for the last 10 minutes and Daniel was losing patience. ‘I’ve told you, no! Nothing strange happened, nobody caused any trouble, none of my staff saw anything!’ ‘Sir, I understand you’re upset, but that tone of voice isn’t helping anything. Now just a few more things and you’ll be free to go. Did you have anything delivered today?’ There it was, the loophole. The way that the arsonists gained entry. Unfortunately for Daniel, Gregor and the police, the "deliverymen" were precise and clinical in their movements. No clear shots of them are visible on the CCTV, no fingerprints were left, their truck had fake plates on it and the company they were supposedly from had never received an order from the club that week. In short, they were untraceable. Exactly as Josh Marquez had planned. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ You see, when he’d been told of the woman’s phone calls to a "Greg" or "Gregory", Josh Marquez had known instantly who had been responsible for the drug incidents at his club. Gregor Lukevich was an old rival, who’d been continually trying to outdo Josh and his clubs since they opened 4 years ago. That he had had the audacity to try and ruin Josh’s enterprises was a provocation that Marquez could not, and would not, let go unnoticed. So he’d sent his two best guys, Lucas and Rick, to visit Eddie Falco. He’d instructed them to plant the coke and the gun in Eva Tulane’s car. He’d given them the green light to burn Lukevich’s flagship club to the ground. Everything had gone off perfectly and he could rest easy. Well, he would be able to after he took care of one final thing… *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ The mood in Gregor’s office was somber. To repair the club was unfeasible and to rebuild it, prohibitively expensive. So he was faced with the prospect of selling the site, cutting his losses and moving on. Not ideal. Life, however, went on and so did the menial tasks he had to attend to daily. The stack of mail in front of him was the usual junk, bills, invoices and statements. All except one. The letter in question was in an off-white envelope, with the address on the front printed in precise handwriting. Definitely not the kind of mail Gregor normally received. As he unfolded the sheet of paper inside, he was intrigued but as soon as he saw the flamboyant signature at the bottom of the page, his intrigue turned to anger. Dear Gregor, Terribly sorry to hear what happened. There seems to be a lot of bad luck around at the moment, let’s just hope you can move past this and carry on as normal with the rest of your businesses. Having read the papers I guess someone has a grudge against you and I guess if they were willing to that, it’s probably in your interests to make your peace with them now, before there is any more unpleasantness. Sincerely, Josh Marquez *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ That’s the thing about the man from Vegas, he’ll do what others won’t. Whether it’s in business or in the ring, he’s always willing to push the limits and do what most would shy away from. Sure, it’s high-risk but he’s always lived his life that way and it’s served him well. He sure as hell isn’t going to change now.
