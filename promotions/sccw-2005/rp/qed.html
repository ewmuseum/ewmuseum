Date: 10/20-10/21 Concord, NH Motel 6 10:00 AM To put it mildly, Phillip Kennedy was...bored. One might wonder how this was possible, really. After all, he was a member in good standing with a group of complete assholes who executed plans of a degree of evil that few could imagine. Moreover, he was a professional wrestler, one-half of the SCCW Strength In Numbers Champions with Reginald Victor Lovecraft. And if all of *that* wasn't enough, Kennedy's girlfriend was Kathryn Shaw, a woman in her early thirties with a libido akin to that of an eighteen year old man, a tongue like a hummingbird, and a distinct lack of the word 'no' in her vocabulary. So how could Kennedy possibly be bored? Well, for one, said hummingbird-tongued girlfriend was nowhere to be found. She had, it seemed, disappeared from the world, though he knew that was impossible. Reginald Lovecraft was gone as well, which made Kennedy wonder if there was some secret important Hand meeting he hadn't been deemed on a need-to-know basis for. But no, Reginald had called and told him that he had a presentation to make. Day job and all. The long and short of this all was that Kennedy was stuck in New Hampshire alone. New Hampshire had many wonderful amenities, such as skiing, nature walks, and a better chance of snow than any location Phil had ever been, but to a man conditioned by Sin City, it was...boring. Dreadfully so, which was why the Big Stack was propped up on a bunch of pillows on his hotel room's bed, cell phone pressed to his ear. 'Mr. Kennedy,' Phillip heard, in his ear. 'How are you? To what do I owe this call?' David Walter Smith always got down to business, after all. Kennedy smiled to himself. If nothing else, he appreciated the big man's openness and sincerity. 'Boredom,' Kennedy admitted. 'I'm stuck in New Hampshire untl Temptation this week and I've got nothing to do. It sucks.' Somewhere in the back of his mind, Kennedy wished Katsidy had at least booked him a hotel room in Worcester, Mass (the site of Temptation), rather than a state away. Come to think of it, she'd booked him the same room last week, too, when the company was in Burlington, Vermont. From his own hotel room in Burlington, Massachusetts (a small suburb of Boston), the once-Logic let his smile grow. 'If there is a way that you could perhaps get to Massachusetts, we could meet and discuss a variety of matters.' David sighed for a moment, and then made a slight addendum. 'While playing golf.' On his end of the phone, Kennedy's face contorted as if a particularly bad odor had permeated the room. 'Golf,' he asked, almost in disbelief. 'I suck at golf.' 'As do I, Mr. Kennedy,' Logic admitted. 'In fact, golf may be the only sport that I can think of that so many people who are extremely bad at continue to play. Still, I am not asking you to Pebble Beach. It is a small nine-hole municipal course that we play twice for eighteen holes. Beginner to intermediate. We will still fail, of course, but it will be relatively less frustrating.' 'Wait a minute,' Kennedy managed, a fact that he'd nearly forgotten jumping to the forefront of his mind. 'Aren't you in a wheelchair?' On his end of the phone, David smiled. 'I am in a wheelchair. At least, as far as Sin City Championship Wrestling is concerned. The fact is that my knee is well enough to walk, well enough to play golf. It is not well enough to compete, but still. You are a poker player. You should know quite well that advertising weakness when one is strong, or stronger than expected, can be highly profitable.' Kennedy nodded. 'You're right. Just a bit of a surprise, that's all.' Weighing the prospect of an afternoon of boredom against an afternoon of golf, the Big Stack came to the slightly sad conclusion that golf with his agent was better than a depressing afternoon of watching pay per view porn and wishing Kat was here. 'Okay, okay, I'll come down there. Gonna have to rent a car and clubs though.' 'That is fine,' David said. 'It should be a two hour ride, tops. Ask the concierge, he or she should be able to refer you to a rental car company in the area.' 'Alright then,' Kennedy agreed. 'I'll be there as soon as I can. Should I meet you at your hotel?' 'No, no, that won't be necessary,' the Thinking Man replied. 'I will call you once you get closer with the course's location.' 'Damn, man,' Phil murmured. 'What's with the secrecy? 'The course's location.' Feel like I'm in a spy movie or some shit.' Somehow, Kennedy could *feel* David smile through the phone. 'Something like that,' David stated, before hanging up. Hearing the tell-tale click, Kennedy closed the phone, and shoved it in his pocket. 'Shit's getting stranger and stranger by the day,' Stack observed. 'David's all high-strung, Kat's off more and more, and Lovecraft always seems more stressed out that a law student during finals. Something's going on...' One might have posited that Kennedy *should* have been more stressed, what with Sean Sterling about to very much be on the rampage, a SIN title match, and his membership in the Hand making him a target for half of SCCW. Stress, though, was a foreign concept nowadays for a man who once knew stress as having to win x amount of money at the poker table to make rent, or gamble just enough money to earn a comp to a buffet so that he could have a decent warm meal while not running out of bankroll. Sin City Championship Wrestling was nothing by comparison. 'Geez, I hope this place isn't one of those fancy-pants golf courses,' Kennedy said, letting a shudder escape him. 'Not in the mood to put on goofy pants.' Unzipping his suitcase, the Big Stack reached inside to pull out one of the several variations he owned on the Phillip Kennedy uniform: blue jeans and a t-shirt. He wore the uniform whether it was three degrees out, or one-hundred and three. It was part of who he was, along with the black Stetson that he wore both on Temptation and on the street. And with a gathering of his personal effects, he was gone, on his way down to the lobby to get the concierge to put him in touch with a car rental company. A long drive with some driving music might do him well. So too might a golf game. Because whether or not he wanted to admit it, Phil was not the happiest man on the planet right now. * * * In his hotel room in Burlington, MA, though, David Walter Smith's adrenaline was positively flowing through his veins. By a stroke of luck, he had decided to go visit Sin City's website about thirty minutes ago. Written as a headline on that site were the words that finally gave him an in. The words that finally gave him a reason to believe that behind the well manicured facade of the Dead Man's Hand lurked something far more evil than even the stylishly so exterior promised. 'The game is afoot,' David Walter Smith whispered to himself, opening a notebook on his computer desk, and beginning to write on a fresh, crisp, blank page. And like all games, there had to be an opening gambit. ---------------------------------------------------------------- ''And the lamp fell over by accident? Hon, I lie about these things for a living. You have no idea how close I thought we were to the thing with Daniel coming out. Poor dear is so nervous about it, I had to get him off over the phone to calm him -- '' -- Kathryn Shaw, October 15, 2008 October 14, 2008 Phillip Kennedy wasn't the smartest creature walking. Not by a long shot. But nor was he the dumbest. Which was, probably, why he was on the phone with his girlfriend after having watched an episode of Temptation in which Dan Mulholland, agent of Amy Campbell, was part of a behind closed doors meeting. That bit had been curious for several reasons. One, the Dead Man's Hand conducted a great deal of business on camera. A great deal off it, as well, but the show itself was as much a weapon for Desade and company as were their individual plots and plans. Temptation sent messages. Desade had deliberately taken the meeting off camera. Which, of course, prompted the question 'why?' Two, Amy Campbell was quite positive, seemingly, that Katsidy was fucking around with Mr. Mulholland, and if not Mulholland, her husband, Darcy Crisis. Either scenario was unpleasant for Kennedy to think about. Three, it was yet another example of something that he wasn't seemingly important enough to know about. All of this was enough to inspire his call. Not like it would ever amount to anything. Kathryn, after all, always had an explanation. Always. One day, Kennedy would decide once and for all whether or not that soothed him or angered him. Not that it mattered. By the end of the call, his hand would be in his pants thanks to a few well chosen words whispered into his ear. Sometimes, he would think, much later, it really sucked to have a penis. ---------------------------------------------------------------- Rest Stop #453 on the Massachusetts Turnpike 12:00 PM. 'Thank you, Phillip. I'll see you at 2:00 PM at the course. I trust you will be able to find it with my instructions.' Conversation over, David Walter Smith hung up the phone. It was not, however, his cell phone. His cell phone was turned off, tucked safely in his pocket. What he hung up, instead, was a pay phone. Pay phones were, for all intents and purposes, a relic of a bygone age. Back in the day, if you needed to make a call while you were out, they were the only option. Today, of course, cell phones are practically a part of most of us. The pay phone is a backup option for most people. Of course, there are some who still embrace the pay phone when the time is proper. Cell phones, after all, can be used to track their owner by one with the proper know-how to do so. One man who knows this is David Walter Smith. Why else would he have travelled to a rest stop on the Mass Pike? It certainly wasn't for the cuisine, though David *was* carrying a paper bag with the world famous Golden Arches on it. Inside was a McChicken Extra Value Meal that David would devour in a relatively short time. It was not the sort of food he preferred, to say the very least, but it would sate him until he could find something superior. Sighing, David sat at one of the laughably small McDonald's tables, his huge frame barely fitting in the chair. By most measures, he figured, he was a pretty crappy agent. He only had one client, after all. Most agents juggled a full load of athletes, spending most of their waking hours taking care of contracts and disputes and hold-outs. David probably could have signed more clients if he really wanted to. The problem was more that he'd been...side-tracked. He wasn't called Logic for nothing, after all. David was a card-carrying member of Mensa, a man who spent his spare time enjoying crosswords and sudoku and logic puzzles. Mental challenges made his world go round. And right now, his life's great mental challenge was the Dead Man's Hand. Kennedy, his client, was a member of the group. This in and of itself wouldn't have been bothersome...save for the fact that Kennedy was very close with both Kathryn Shaw and Lauren Fox, Desade's two right hand women. There was also Reginald Lovecraft. who seemingly did his level best along with the other two to keep David at arm's length whenever possible. They didn't want him involved. That fact meant but one thing to David; there was a REASON they didn't want him involved. And 'they' wasn't just Kathryn and Lauren. No, David was quite positive that NOTHING in the Hand happened without Alexandra Pierce's say-so. His curiosity had professional reasons, of course. If he found something that would jeopardize his client's health, safety, or job security, well, it was in his utmost interest to deal with such an issue swiftly and effectively. If he was honest with himself, though, Phillip was no longer the real concern. The real concern was his own need to know. The chance to pit himself against the closest thing the real world probably had to an evil supergenius. It was a real life logic puzzle, far more entertaining than paper and ink could ever be. Reaching into his sack of deep fried, unhealthy fast food, David resigned himself to a long, difficult road ahead. Long and difficult yes, but potentially extremely rewarding As he saw it, it was himself sitting across from the Director at a table, expressions blank, chess pieces set up for the start of the game. Whether she knew it or not, the existance of the Dead Man's Hand was an invitation to David. An invitation to out-Desade Desade. Invitation or not, though, one didn't play another person's game without understanding the rules completely, or researching one's opponent. Which was, of course, the point of today's golf game. ------------------------------- Burlington Municipal Golf Course 2:00 PM Burlington Muni was a local sort of place. You didn't get many outsiders coming in to play golf, there were better, more expensive courses for that. But for locals who wanted to play golf several times a week without breaking their piggy banks, the place was paradise. Many two and foursomes were headed out, even on this cold October day in New England. One of them, though, just happened to be a few out of towners. Phillip Kennedy and David Walter Smith held precious little in common. Their dress indicated that pretty clearly. Smith wore a black polo shirt and khakis while lounging in the driver's seat of his rented golf cart. His golf bag was placed neatly in the back of the cart. Kennedy wore a t-shirt and blue jeans. Luckily for him, this particular municipal course lacked a dress code. Unluckily for him, his golf bag was proving unwieldy as he dragged it from the clubhouse towards the cart. Even more unluckily for him, it was farking cold out and he didn't have a jacket. 'I hate this goddamned sport,' Kennedy seethed, finally hoisting the bag up and dropping it into the back alongside his agent's with a loud crashing noise. With his clubs subdued, Kennedy hopped into the passenger's seat of the cart, and Logic began to drive off towards the first hole at a speedy ten miles an hour. 'Something the matter,' David asked, looking at his comrade's glowering face. 'It's nothing,' Phil said, quickly. Far too quickly, in fact, and David and Phil shared a knowing look. Kennedy had been caught, and the Stack was man enough to admit it. 'Just sorta annoyed at a lot of things right now, Dave,' Phillip admitted, digging into his pocket to pull out a white golf ball and an orange tee. 'I'm a good soldier, but no one sees fit to put me in command of anything.' David allowed his eyebrow to arch, and forced down his smile. Phillip Kennedy was an open book. Getting him talking wasn't difficult in the slightest. And once he started, well, the man in the cowboy hat wasn't easy to stop. 'What do you mean?' David asked, watching as Kennedy dismounted the cart, and went for his driver. 'Bridden and Mayhem ain't done shit in weeks,' the Big Stack groused, planting his tee in the grass at the first tee box. 'Right now, the Dead Man's Hand in ring may as well be Phillip Kennedy and Desade, and she's not even supposed to wrestle all the time. We've got Amy too...but she's not even a member.' Smith nodded politely, watching Phillip affect something that might be considered to be a golf stance, addressing the ball with the clubhead of his ridiculously oversized driver. 'Just feel like she knows more than I do. That she's more important.' 'Well,' David replied, trying to both be sympathetic and twist the knife a bit more in a bid for information, 'she *is* the SCCW Universal Champion. Having her under the Hand's sway is, I would imagine, one of Ms. Pierce's higher priorities.' Weighing Kennedy's reaction, he continued. 'Besides which, Campbell is not an especially good soldier. She is erratic. Emotional. Highly prone to make non-logical decisions. Preventing her from doing something potentially dangerous is a job I would not wish upon most people.' Still glowering, Kennedy nods, and winds up, swinging his club downward with an angry, aggressive motion. Clubhead hits ball, and the golf ball tears off down the course, richocheting off a tree two hundred yards down and disappearing into the rough surrounding it. 'Fuck,' Kennedy grimaced. 'That's gonna be a bitch to find. I told you I sucked at golf.' Logic nodded, the faintest of smiles on his face as he calmly approached the teebox with a ball, tee, and seven iron. 'Again, I am no better at golf than you are,' David reminded his charge, planting his tee and ball. Winding up with a loose swing, Logic connected, driving the ball 100 yards, straight as an arrow, right down the center of the fairway to Phillip's consternation. 'I thought you sucked at golf,' Phillip said, shaking his head at the much larger man. 'I do,' Logic deadpanned. 'Which means that I don't take out clubs that I can't use. I use the nine iron, a sand wedge, and a putter. I am terrible at golf, I assure you. What I am good at is swinging a nine iron, a sand wedge, and a putter.' Phillip sighed, walking back to the golf cart. 'I suppose that there's a lesson for me to learn from that description,' he muttered, looking at the Immense Intellectual. 'I hadn't intended there to be,' Logic admitted, 'but if you give me a moment, I am sure that I can come up with one.' 'Please don't,' Phil asked, as his large playing partner took his seat behind the wheel and began the ride to his golf ball. Whether or not he wanted to admit it, there was something to be said for David's approach to golf. He would play consistently. Consistently over par, most likely, but still consistently. There would be no tremendously lucky birdies, nor any double or triple bogies caused by horribly misplayed shots. There would be bogies and pars, for the most part. 'You compete like I play golf,' was the comment that Phillip had known was coming. He hadn't known exactly what it would be, but he had known that David would say something. 'Humor me and tell me how,' Phillip requested, and David was all too happy to explain. 'You are consistent. You do what you are told. You have a record in SCCW that perpetually hovers around .500. You win as many matches as you lose. In short, you are....average.' 'Average,' Kennedy thundered, 'I beat the fucking BloodAngel!' 'And you lost to Jay Draven, for example,' Logic retorted. 'The flaw to my approach to golf is that I will never become great, or even good. I never take calculated risks. With my strength, I could drive the ball three-hundred plus yards, but there is a great chance I would not be accurate. I have played golf off and on for six years and never had a single birdie.' 'So, your point is...?' 'Amy Campbell is SCCW Universal Champion because she takes her own chances. She could just as easily be dead in a gutter somewhere, however. I do not recommend her approach. You would do well to look at your leader's approach instead.' 'Desade,' Phil asked, questioningly. 'Yes. Desade acts in a calculated way most of the time, but she is not afraid to take a risk when the reward is great. Her ruse as Charlotte Ramone indicates this. There were chances that she would be found out, injured using the high risk style Ramone used to great effect. However, in the end, her reward was winning the Big Stack bracelet and cementing the Hand's supremacy through the elimination of Jonathan Rhine.' 'So, what you're saying is that I need to take a risk if I ever want to rise in the Hand's ranks?' 'Indeed,' David smiled. 'Those who listen and take direction well are valuable in this world, but they will never be more than footsoldiers. Essential, but lowly. Officers must look at a situation, synthesize information, and make decisions. You have made but one decision in your entire time in SCCW, the choice to make Kathryn Shaw your partner, and it was overruled. You must make a decision that cannot be overruled. A decision that will spell success if made properly. One that will show you to be capable of making proper decisions under pressure.' Kennedy scratched at his chin, cocking his head to one side. 'You know, that's not a bad idea. Not a bad idea at all, Dave. I should sit down and come up with something, God knows I've got enough opportunities with everything on my plate. ' Mr. Smith said nothing, but nodded like a proud father. Clearly, Kennedy was useless for his greater purpose of figuring out just what the Hand and Desade were, but Kennedy was not entirely bereft of purpose. No, on the contrary, Kennedy was very important indeed. Phillip was the smokescreen. The justifiable reason for David to hang around people he otherwise had no reason to be around. To ask questions he otherwise had no reason to ask. 'I don't think I'm going to be able to find my ball,' Phillip said, suddenly. 'You mind if I take a drop next to yours?' Logic shook his head for a moment to clear his thoughts, then nodded, 'Certainly, Mr. Kennedy. Go right ahead.' The irony of the situation was not lost on David, even as he watched Kennedy drop another ball and take out what looked to be a three iron, lining himself up with the hole, which happened to lay across a small lake. Both Alexandra Pierce and David Smith used Phillip Kennedy as a pawn. Neither felt much remorse about doing so. The difference, Smith believed, was that Smith's usage was far less likely to get his client arrested, shot, stabbed, or fired than was the Director's. Once again, Kennedy wound up with his angry swing, and this time the ball jumped off the clubhead, sailing comparatively straightly over the lake, landing on the green, and bouncing once before rolling to within ten feet of the hole. Phillip turned, to find David, appropriately enough, golf-clapping. 'See what happens when you take a risk, Mr. Kennedy?' In spite of his willingness to try and further his career in SCCW, the Big Stack shook his head. 'Last time I took a risk, I bounced my golf ball off of a tree.' David shook his head in reply. 'CALCULATED risk, Mr. Kennedy. Calculated risk. You should be well aware that you can't use a driver, from the looks of things.' Kennedy's nod was rather sheepish. At the same time though, he realized it wasn't that bad, in fact. The Dead Man's Hand, after all, didn't like using drivers. They much preferred to tear up a course with precise irons, good chips, and surgical putting. Kennedy's mindset, as of six months ago, was driver. Driver all the time, even if it wasn't the best. But he was learning under the tutelage of both Kathryn Shaw and Lauren Fox, wielders of emotional scalpels par excellence. He'd used the driver on Sean Sterling on enough. Now was the time to pitch and putt. After all, the saying about golf is quite clear. Drive for show, putt for dough. ----------------------------- The sheet of notebook paper, ripped from the spiral notebook, remained on the hotel room's wooden desk. Written on it, in David's neat script, was an underlined heading: 'Intelligence.' Five names were written, one each on its own line. Atop the list was 'Phillip Kennedy,' a neat line drawn through it. Below that name was 'Lance Marshall.' Below Marshall, 'Wyatt Connors.' Below Connors, 'Amy Campbell.' A great deal of blank space sat between Campbell's name and the final name on the paper. Clearly, David had more questionings intended over the course of some indeterminate period of time. Once that space was crossed, the final name sat, underlined and circled. 'Alexandra Pierce.' And beside the name of the Spider in the Web lay Logic's greatest hope. A hope beyond Phillip Kennedy becoming SCCW Universal Champion and resigning for millions of dollars. The words were written out where they were usually abbreviated. They held great meaning for David Walter Smith, both when using them at the end of a long proof in his academic life, or as his exclamation point finisher, a back-to-belly Tombstone piledriver that had earned him victories over some of the sport's greats. Though in a wheelchair he sat nowadays, moreso out of ease on his injured leg than out of pure necessity, his competitive drive remained. And he *would* get to the bottom of everything. He would find out how far the Spider's web stretched, to what depths it reached, and if necessary, he would ruin the Spider's most prized creation like a child in a dusty attic. Quod erat demonstratum.
