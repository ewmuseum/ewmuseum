Date: Today Alexisonfire, "Sharks and Danger" (Watch Out, 2004) "I really wish that I could make this as evident to you as it is for me. It has been growing in potential as the years passed, and is near critical mass. This disposition to death and despair has haunted me for as long as my memory serves. My thoughts confined to my head, I have always struggled to communicate myself, my thoughts and my fears, and I fear it is becoming way too frustrating to bear. Always too afraid to tell the ones I love just that. Too damn proud to express my weaknesses and seek consoling. I am a little too nervous that I am starting to lose interest in playing my own psychiatrist. "It is this massive size and increasingly aggressive response to problems that foreshadow an eruption that will doubtlessly be deemed evil by even the most sympathetic of souls, having long since designated mine to Hell. I simply hope that these demons claim only this life. "To whoever reads this letter of prophecy heed it as a warning for yourself. You cannot help me, but do help the ones around you," I stopped, having read this exercise in catharsis for what must have been the fifth time. How in between the looping l’s and the ink that danced about the lines I still could not make clear to myself what it all really meant. I refused to believe this was nothing more than a case of homesickness set in at just the right time. It amounted to nothing more than another failure of a creative mind without any artistic talents. I took my sights away from such sorrowful scribbles and cast my gaze about me. Cathode Ray TV locked to the desk. Did anyone steal these antiques anymore? Sparse walls save the solemn picture of a boat crested on the white sands of paradise. That all too common dream hung in to remind the road wearied traveler that they should too. From that cheery ship wreck back to the bed that housed families, single men, drunks, lovers, sinners and saints, each only for the briefest of times before leaving no ‘visible’ evidence of their existence. I read this residence to be as conflicted as I. Anxiety sat on my chest. The flame started in the upper right corner. The shambles resembling the next great emo song was oxidized, and really much quicker than any shit I had seen in the movies. It was in the toilet before the smoke of the match had chance to drift into the ventilation. It reminded me of birthday cakes. ----- The engine ceased, letting the cool autumn wind talk ease it into idle. I pulled down the visor and flipped the mirror. The hair looked well enough, about as dishevelled as ever. I questioned the oxford, and the douche appeal that flowed. Just something that I thought might look akin to the adult style for the task at hand, far removed from play and toys and everything else about my life that I deemed childish. No time for second guessing. This was the time to be the confident man that my size allowed. Visor back snug to the ceiling, I swung wide the blue doors, locking them in one swift motion. Five steps away I pressed down on the remote, and again, for a total of no less than six times before the lights flashed and a brief hunk told me all was safe and secure. Who said a confident man could not be a little paranoid? It wasn’t too far to traverse the sporadically occupied parking lot, but I had lost count of how many cigarettes had met there end against that pavement. An elderly woman stood holding the door open for me before she went back about whatever the rest of this overcast day held in store. I felt like an ass. So I stood at the end of a lengthy line, and passed time thinking about what shark I was about to shake hands with. Some grizzled vet to this game, with his hair slicked back and a portly belly that distanced himself from the desk he sat behind. He would stick out that clammy hand and I would take it; two opponents set to do battle. Mathematics was on my side, experience his weapon. I would be disappointed in myself if this was anything less than epic. My realisation of my foe had worked well to speed my wait, as I stood now in front, being told that I was in fact in the wrong line. Now shifted to what the teller assured me was the correct position, I waited only seconds before the antagonist approached, introducing thyself. Amazingly he had lost the slicked back hair, about one hundred pounds and the penis from my test dummy. "Mr. Quinlan?" she asked walking behind an island. "That’d be I," said I. Carefully I studied her attempts to make me feel welcome, comfortable. The young, stunning thing behind a smart pair of glasses extended a supple, smooth hand, which I took. She shifted her weight, spinning on short heels and bid me, "just this way into my office, if you would." "Of course." For a moment I caught myself carried away by the swaying hips, and for only an instant I allowed myself to forget that I would not want to be fraternizing with said enemy. She led me into this cozy little corner room. It was simple and efficient. By no means was it an executive suite, but enough for me to stretch my arms. All and all, it would prove a sufficient site for the battle of Mitchell’s Mortgage Negotiation. I expected the shedding of greens, and would be offended if there was at least not once insult to my intentions. "Thank you for meeting me here today. It has been a little hectic around these parts, and I am sorry that I have had to reschedule this," painted lips parted. "Oh, it was no trouble at all." Far from crying ‘havoc’ but the dogs were off their leash. "So, when last we spoke, you were pre-approved for a two hundred thousand dollar mortgage. Were you looking for anything more than that?" While she spoke to me, she busily shifted screens on the desktop PC at her disposal. How convenient that a man was no longer flesh and blood, but an information dossier electronically available at any whim. "Nope, actually I will not even need that much to secure the home I want," I said, fidgeting with the collar of my shirt. That was enough to draw her away from the monitor and into my bruised face. "Well this is an excellent buyers’ market right now. I am glad that you have been able to take advantage." And so we exchanged pleasantries while we worked through what she explained as crucial steps to finalising our arrangement. An interest rate smaller than Jadian Bridden. A debate to the merits of fixed versus variable. And the payment structures discussed, and then changed, and discussed again. We settled with what I had wanted all along after her valiant effort to part me with my equity, preferring aggressive payments rather than prolonged interest to be paid for. And then there before me sat all of the things we talked about, committed to paper and binding pending an autograph. The thing was rather imposingly large, and contained legal jargon that threatened serious consequences I agreed was a path best left not travelled. It was the half pound of paper, not the gorgeous thirty something now hunched over my shoulder that had beads of sweat collecting in my brows. The pen was in my hand, and I swore its mass a hundred pounds with the weight it carried as I looped the l’s exercising the cursive I stopped improving on since the third grade. And it was finished. I was spent. She was smiling. She said some parting shot thanking me for my business, wishing me a pleasant day, but it was lost on me. I hadn’t yet over come the commitment I had just signed on for. Money I had never seen in my life. All this with an employer that is dragging its feet in presenting me with any future cash flows at all. Santa Maria. ---- It seemed to be the best way to see my new found worries away was to fill my ears with loud, melodic music. Nodding my head to the latest Alice in Chains was just me trying to shake my head clear. Somebody check my brain. The swift wheels that were to carry me home had veered from the initial target in favour of the relief and everything else close friends have the power to grant. Living little more than ten blocks away from my house, I was disappointed with how rare it had become that Tommy and I got together these days. A quick pop-in I thought might remedy such growing unfamiliarity. The ’06 Malibu slowed to a stop on the street in front of that unimposing house. Purchased as little more than a first home for a small family, it had grown comfortable and a little harder to move away from as Thomas Kowolski ever thought it would. I walked past the family car, and hopped stairs onto the stoop. Opting away from the buzzer I rhythmically knocked thrice. I gazed at the neighbourhood and the subtle signs of community most over look, and it made me smile. The door opened behind me while I observed. "Hi Mike," Mary greeted me, Tom’s wife. "Hello Mary," I returned, turning to meet the stumbling of my favourite five year toward the open door. "Look who’s here Emily." "Hi Uncle Mike," she said sporting that goofy grin that just made me melt. It was short order before I picked her up in my arms with a giant hug. "And how is Emily today?" "Hehe," she laughed as I tickled the tiny armpits, "I am good." "That’s what I like to hear. Been running around making a mess for mommy?" I asked, glance back to Mary with a joking grin. "Nope," the little one denied. The mock frustration on her mother’s face called a bluff. I let down the pint sized Kowolski and began to enquire as to the whereabouts of the man of the house. She scurried back to whatever game it was I had disturbed her from and Mary answered to the tune of away, with her brother. Explained the car still sitting ready to go wherever. She promised me that she would have him call me whenever the two returned from the excursion of theirs. Satisfied I bid her adieu and walked back to my ride with a dissolved anxiety and directed it back to my home, even if not everyone under that roof was aware of it yet. ---- The hum of a television in commercial from the living room was broken by the sound of the rear door swinging open. I was no longer alone, my parents returned from another round of house hunting. I walked out of the kitchen to meet them in the hallway where shouts of hi were transferred back and forth. "So, how did we make out today?" I asked the two as they unzipped light coats. A groan was my father’s response, while my mother more insightfully added, "It went alright. Nothing exactly what we are looking for, but plenty of places with potential." I doubled back to the kitchen to retrieve the drink I just poured and left behind. My father darted to the living room and his Lay-Z-Boy, while my mother joined me, seeking to quench her own thirst. "Getting tired of the hunt yet, Goose?" I asked my affectionately nicknamed mother. After downing a gulp of cold water, and amazingly not suffering from the ice cream headache she said, "No. It is a pleasant way to spend an afternoon, and it is making your father and I feel young again. Just that we are this close to leaving behind all the bullshit that we have dealt with is well worth all of the open houses." "What about this place?" I asked coyly. I might have even had a smile on my face. "Much interest swelling?" "Well," she started, taking the glass away from her mouth and the intended second sip, "actually, this place is pretty much already sold. Ten more than what we thought we would get for this place. We are really just waiting on all the paper work now." "Wow, that was quick. So, if you don’t mind me asking, how much you hauling in from our humble home?" I asked, in part just to reassure myself I was not outbid by some unknown. "$210, 000," she said, her lips now curling into a grin of her own. "You know we bought this place for $50,000 in 1980?" "Yep," said the one who had heard the tale many a time before, and the one who was a bit more relieved. "Haven’t even met the buyer yet," she stated the strangeness of the entire procedure. "They’ve just been dealing with the real estate agent." "Oh, I don’t know, I think you might have met the buyer before," I said damn near beaming at this point. There is a reason that I could never succeed in the poker scene. "What’s got you so happy? That wrestling of yours finally catching up," she said looking at the healing cut and random bruises that adorned my face, "in the form of punch drunkness?" A shaking head uttered a matching, "nope." "Well," great segue, "are you still insistent on getting your own place? You know that your father and I will still have a bed for you as long as you want one. And I mean it really doesn’t make too much sense that with how frequently you are several hundred miles away in some motel room that you would need anything extravagant." "Actually, just about finished with that myself. Focused my sights on this little place that seems perfect for me. A lighthouse calling me back home from my travels. Truth be told the thought of coming back here is my favourite amongst any I have on the road." "Really? Where?" "Close by. Don’t want to leave God’s Country." Eagle Place that is. "Well, what are you spending on this place of yours?" my mother asked, her expression a mix of curiosity and concern for me that I could have sworn she previously stored away to combat my combat sport career. "$210, 000," my response. The wheels began to click inside her head, and the vague way in which I had been speaking cleared. The Cheshire cat grin and my slow yet increasing bounce; I was just a touch surprised it took my mother to piece it all together. "No," her first reaction. "Yep," I confirmed. And there it was. I had my dearest Mama in a stunned silence. I waited patiently to be showered with love and a parent’s pride. I was expected something of a hug, words of congratulations. Explains why I was blindsided. "Wha... Why Mitch? Are you really so stubborn to grow up and move on?" "Whoa, whoa, I think this was a rather grown up decision on my behalf." She paced a bit back and forth – never a sign I wanted to see – before she continued tearing down whatever sense of accomplishment I thought I had. "Did you really have to hide behind agents? You couldn’t just talk to your parents?" "I think I was trying to avoid a blow up like this. I thought you would be happy for me, investing money into a place I really love. This is home to me. I don’t want to be forced to have to change just because it is something that ought to be done." "Mitch," she said in an almost belittling tone. I did not let whatever else had to be said be said. "Alright, I get it. Just another mistake to distance me from whatever standard of a good son you had in your head. Getting to be a healthy stock pile there. I’m going to take a nap, don’t bother waking me for supper," and stormed up the stairs to a bed I longed to crawl back into anytime I was away. ---- Waking at 10:42 pm I lay in silent laughter, never bothering to remove the sheets from on top of I. I flicked to life the 13" television in hopes of avoiding this, but it seems my intentions have failed with just about every act this day. In came flooding a review of recent events. I stopped laughing. I pondered what it would mean to be a better man, the man that they want me to be. Did that man suffer this same paranoia, depression and inadequacy? He had a loving wife, and dutiful children, but did I want that? So I was not perfect, but I would continue going about things with my aggressive solutions and worry about their consequences later. Then I resided to sleep the rest of this one away while I prayed that the next day to come would be if nothing else less eventful.
