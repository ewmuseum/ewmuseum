Zayed Ali Khan - The Lion (Inception)

3 June 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3rd June 2007 A block located in an eerie, empty location in the outskirts of East Rutherford, NJ. The only structure in the area is a small bar to which the scene faces. The bar’s name was on a rotten old signboard, “Jacks’ Bar & Snacks”. The wind blows south, taking with it the sand and dust, which whirled around. Crowing can be heard, coming from the massive neem tree erected by the side of the bar. The stomping of a boot at the face of the screen changes the screen. The screen changes to the inside of the bar; it’s a usual place; drinks, old men, hookers and the amateurs. It’s decorated with old lights, posters of the 80’s and the jukebox is playing music that dead people are singing. Just then, across the door, a hooker is being pushed and beaten by two unknown men in suits. As they open the door and shoot her out, a man (Zayed) walks in. Has his long jacket on, and the sand boots that he almost always uses. He’s wearing his head turban, beard unshaven. His bushy eyebrows help his eyes to stare even harder. He steps into the bar with a stern look on his face to the two men who had just let the girl out. They stare at him as if he’d replaced her, one problem replacing another. Zayed stares the two men down, then calmly continues his walk towards the bar. A few heads turn to see the new face, but no commotion is created. He walks to the bar and sits at the counter, looks to the bartender; roughly old man about fifty had a wrinkled hefty face and the usual beer belly and tattoos on his arms as well as creeping up to his neck. The turning heads returned to their routine drinking and politics and things seemed to look normal from afar, just as the world does. But if you look inside, you see the hatred. Zayed felt this hatred. He knew that the discrimination was yet to come. He noticed them stare at his turban and saw the reaction and change in expression as their faces turned. But he controlled. He kept it inside and thought to himself, “For a later time.” “Your not from around here are you?” asked the bartender. Zayed nodded his head and announced in his strong, heavy tone, “Salaam, I am Zayed Ali Khan, The Lion of Afghanistan”. He knew before saying this that it would cause some reactions and it right well did. Gasps and wheezes were heard around the room as Zayed introduced himself in his manly manner. This time, he was able to turn all the heads at the bar, rather than the few desperate ones who look to anything that enters the room. Zayed kept his eyes on the bartender but, at the same time, was well aware of the fact that his two eyes were the only ones that weren’t looking at him. He once again ignored it showing no sign of trial waiting on the bartender. The bartender, trying to hide his uneasiness, asked Zayed, “The only part of that sentence that I understood was ‘lion’. So Mr. Lion, what’s it going to be?” Forgiving the old man’s lack of knowledge, he spoke with confidence, “Well I am new to this country of yours and, although I did my research, I am unaware of many things. But since I am here in a new country, with new people and new accomplishments to attain, I guess I must try out something new. After all, to understand your opponent, understanding his world is imperative.” Muddled by Zayed’s words, the bartender realized that everything went through him and doing his job he stated, “Alright, I guess that means your have a regular.” And continued on to slosh his drink for him. Zayed shuffled his legs to sit on the bar stool to his satisfaction. He then reached his pocket, and pulled out a piece of paper. It was sort of a letter, with the heading ‘Fuse Wrestling in America’ on the top. The bartender couldn’t help but be nosy and took a peek at it. “Oh, you’re a wrestler huh? We get plenty of those in this area. Your just a kid, how do you expect to beat those giants in the ring? Fuse is known to be one of the greatest in the business, your lucky to have this as your start!” he added, “You better be a quick mover because that may be your only advantage. I’ve seen some of those men in real life and they look like titans to me!” Zayed moved his eyes from the letter to the man and with the first smirk that he had given ever since he arrived at the country appeared on his face. Bewildered by it, the bartender was unaware of a reaction and thus gave a prim smile back. “What you smiling about son? I am not lying to you, these men can ‘crush you with your fists!’” He spelt. “Yes, the man is right.” Said another man who had just walked up to the counter. He was an eerie man, seemed like the perfect kind of guy who one would find at this type of bar. Had a hat on, wore black all over, about a head shorter than Zayed and skinny as hell. But there was something about his eyes. His age was much younger than that of the bartender, but it were his eyes that threw in the distinction of his character. Just by looking at him one could easily tell that this man had seen enough in his life to make judgments. The man cleared his throat, approached Zayed and murmured, “This isn’t the type of place you would find easy money or easy wins. You’ve seen nothing yet; some of the boys die in these kinds of things. They aren’t going to take it easy on you. Just look around you, everyone looks at you different. I don’t mean any offence, but the fighters against you will notice this difference and take advantage of it! Be very careful my friend, this is America, anything can happen!” The man waited for a response from Zayed, but got nothing but a sip of his beer. Zayed made a hard face, as he tasted beer for the first time. Alcoholic beverages were strictly forbidden in Afghanistan. In fact, at times they would whip a ‘disobeyer’ two hundred times for consuming it. Few would barely survive this punishment, but it was a rule. The Bartender noticed his expression and said, “Ha, that’s just a sip lad. You got to do better than that if you are to get anywhere around here. Our youthful children can take more than that, you don’t want to be as bad as your kids, now do ya?” Zayed says nothing, he tries to control his agitation but is unable to and grabs the mug and gulps down the entire content. To his surprise, he is able to handle it this time. Some faces are watching this entire episode and a few ‘Oohs and Aah’s’ are heard. The bartender is taken a back, as is the other man sitting next to Zayed. “So who is your opponent?” asked the furtive man He put down the jug, harshly. Then he moves his eyes towards the man and stares him down for a bit. The man continues to stare at Zayed in confusion but waits for his response. “Opponents.” Says Zayed looking away and trying to learn more how the atmosphere works around here by seeing other people talk and move. He knew it was important to know how body language is here, not only in order to fit it but also to see how they might move in the ring. He had already been introduced to the way women are treated here. They were treated much different than how in his land. “Is this how you treat your women? You beat them and man handle them?” Zayed questioned. “Yeah mister. In our ‘world’ women are treated like the men are! You will find that out in the ring as well!” replied the aggressive bartender trying to provoke Zayed. “How do you treat your women in your world?” “With love, respect and compassion! Women in my country are so well respected that we lower our eyes in their presence! Women have rights over men due to their fragile nature and our lust for them. We learn to treat them as equals in their own rights. But we must never forget that, biologically, men and women are different and because of this they must be treated different!” Zayed commanded. Although preaching words as a missionary to feminism, inside Zayed understood more about the culture of these people. It occurred to him that if, in this country, women are treated like men, then he must treat them the same, or at least try to. “So who is your match against young man?” Asked the man, once again. “I am against two men. I know nothing of them. But that is not important, because this is my debut match, and as my debut I will win it!” Zayed answered. “Their names are Carys and Jamie Hauser if I am pronouncing it correct.” “How in the world can you be so sure that you will win it, son? Just ‘cause its your debut match doesn’t mean that you’ve got the right to win it!” the bartender accuses Zayed. The old man adds in his two cents as well “Yeah kid, the man is right. You have to be better than that. I watch Fuse Wrestling closely and if I’m not wrong then the two opponents your against are also fighting in their debut match up, so do they deserve the right to win?” Zayed smirks, and then adds on, “You fools. You don’t understand it do you? I am not just another wrestler who is fighting his debut match. My name, as I previously boasted, is Zayed Khan! And I am the champion of champions in my country. This match I am to wrestle is only a contest that I fight is only a warm up for me. I have bigger things to come ahead of me. You’ll see. It’s only a matter of time before you all begin to show me some great respect. I see the way you and the rest of these people around are looking and me and I do not like it. But I guess all that will change soon, actually I don’t need to guess it, I know it. When I became champion in my country, I got to high places. I met the president, had my very own gun collection that I used during the war and earned novelty from all Afghanis around me. These were the same Afghanis you people attacked, the same guns that I used against you people, and of course, the same president who you people pawned into our system. But you know what, sometimes these things are okay because they don’t need to matter that much. If I were in my true senses I would be beating the life out of you people for what you caused my people and my family. But I am a madman am I not? That is why I seek only to be a common friend to you, in my country we call them ‘brothers’.” The bartender reaches for his mug and refills it. “This one’s on me” he states. Maybe he was felt by Zayed’s words, or maybe just threatened and wanted to cool things down. Its certain for sure that a sense of ‘terror’ was present at the bar now. Although Zayed said these words only to the two men we was talking with, his body language and distinct accent caused many others to over hear, thus a slight silence was present. But nobody budged; surprisingly nobody decided to step up to the man. It could be due to his kindness of not ‘blowing himself up’ like most of his people are renown for doing. For another few moments there was silence, Zayed hadn’t touched the drink as yet but was still in his thoughts. He was trying to cool down. Cool down from what could have been much worse of a reaction. He was praying to himself and thanking his God for giving him the ability of patience. Just then the silence between the two men was broken, “She’s a girl.” “What?” Zayed asked. “She’s a girl! Hauser, she’s a girl. I wanted it to be a surprise for you but you should know that one of the opponents whom you are going to wrestle at Killzone is a female.” The old man revealed to Zayed. Zayed was confused; he didn’t know how to react to this. Never before had he wrestled a female, not even in bed. “They do that here as well? Men and women wrestle one another? This is insanity!” Zayed Proclaimed. The old man responded, “Why? Can’t you fight a girl? She’s only less powerful; you should be able to finish her quicker Zayed! This is America my friend, and as I said, here we are all equal! We got some great rights for people, which is why minorities have a great power along with feminists. “You imbecile! The fact of the matter is not how quick I will be able to finish her but it is that she might be offended about me touching her. It is great disrespect for a strange man to touch a woman he does not know. And I am sure she will not approve of it!” Zayed publicized. The two men began to laugh… maybe at Zayed for his naivety. After snickers the bartender continued at him. “Ha” he laughed and then sighed. “Son, if you don’t get her first she will rip you apart. You are darn lucky to have met us before your match so you would at least know who and what your fighting, ha. After hearing such puzzling information from the two men, Zayed began to think. From a very young age he was taught never to disrespect women. Being an adult today, he was unable not to abide by this rule. He then thought to himself; ‘maybe I just treat them like men, if I make belief they are not women.’ This seemed to be the only way that Zayed was to approach this deal. “Alright, not a problem. If I am to fight a female, I will fight her. Since there is no other way around it, a female will be easier to beat. Besides I have another character to look upon. I have already done my research on his female. I thought that ‘he’ was an ex-marine. But to my surprise, ‘she’ is an ex-marine. That does not change anything. I am sure that being a marine she must have lived under the conditions where I am from. But come to think of it, there is a difference in being a Krypton and living on earth and being a human and living on earth. There is the difference that will give me the edge over the female.” He’d done his research. In his country, only few were educated and due to this job enlargement was common. Besides being a full time wrestler, Zayed took over some administrative businesses in the government, mainly research and evaluation. Due to this he gained some sort of know-how for deductions about people and it greatly helped him in the ring. Ironically in all the research done on Carys and Hauser, he ‘missed’ out the part about one of them being a female. Zayed moves on from the topic, realizing that he has two opponents against him and will take both serious, equally. “This Carys. Who is he?” “Another new guy like you. But I’m sure that even this Carys kid has got some balls to talk to same way as you. I heard he isn’t a nice guy either” The man responded. He seemed to know quite a lot; maybe he was a hardcore Fuse fan. Zayed argued, “Well I guess I will have to deal with him too. I don’t have any other option but to take out my opponents and prove to this federation and this country what I am made of. Whether it is this Carys or this female, the opposition is the same, two wrestlers. It’s in my blood to be a big part of this and I strongly believe in it!” Zayed pushes the drink back to the bartender. He acts as if he only made his point. To prove that it wasn’t the fact that he couldn’t handle it, it was just that he didn’t like it, just as he didn’t like any person in that room, as they hated him back. Zayed pushed his stool and got off. The two other men were confused about his departure but didn’t question it. “Hope you didn’t change your mind about the wrestling, Lion.” The bartender threw in as Zayed was walking out. Zayed continued to walk and as he was about to step out he tied up by saying ““Salaam, I am Zayed Ali Khan, The Lion of Afghanistan fighting for Fuse Wrestling. This Killzone you will see the first middle-eastern wrestler to have stepped into a Fuse ring, and win. Just remember that my friend, you’ll see me soon.” With that statement he walked out the bar and the American life continued, heads turned back and life went on. Zayed had made his inception in the US world felt. Well, at least at a local bar. He seeks to make it more effective after his performance at Killzone!
