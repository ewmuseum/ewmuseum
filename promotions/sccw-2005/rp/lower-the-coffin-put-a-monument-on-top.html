Date: :) Manchester, Tennessee. When I step foot into a wrestling ring, boos aren’t just expected, they’re damn near guaranteed. For a moment, thousands upon thousands of people are supposed to hate me. This is my job. This is what I get paid to do. To be loathed by millions, all for the sake of their viewing entertainment. When I step foot into a funeral home, needless to say, I don’t expect the same reception. Suit and tie. Hair combed to perfection. Surrounded by former friends and people I’ve never met stabbing daggers into me with their eyes. They didn’t boo me, and they certainly didn’t wave signs above their head proclaiming that I sucked, but they definitely weren’t showing off their southern hospitality. Each face held an angry disposition. Every pair of eyes, whether they were young or old, either bore deep into me with more resentment than a Jew to a Nazi or only glanced before looking away in disgust. Rumors and gossip were exchanged via whispers. Old folks talked behind my back, as if I couldn’t hear them. They said things like, "That’s the sorry bastard who was dating Judith." They said things like, "Did you hear? They say he killed her." They said things like, "If I could, I’d be strangling him right now." Funny. If I could, I’d be strangling myself. It’s sad, but these sort of things always seem to bring everybody you used to know back together, even the ones you don’t want to see. I just so happened to be the lucky bastard that nobody wanted to see. Manchester is the type of town where a bad reputation lasts forever. It’s the type of town where everybody knows everybody else and a rumor will spread like a wildfire. Needless to say, most of the things that have been said about me, be it before I left this town or after, were mostly true. In a roundabout way, I’d become an urban legend in this town. The way Ireland has the Loch Ness monster, the American Northwest has Bigfoot, and New Jersey has The Jersey Devil? That’s the way Manchester has Sean Sterling. The only real difference was that I actually existed, which just managed to make me that much scarier. Everything I had ever done had been blown out of proportion, turned into folklore, like a modern day Tom Sawyer. Parents had become accustomed to telling their kids bedtime stories about me, warning them that I’d come back to town and take them away to God knows where if they weren’t good. While the rest of the world wanted Bin Laden’s head, Manchester still wanted mine. To Manchester, I was still public enemy number one. A childhood wrought with an abnormal amount of rebellion goes a long way, doesn’t it? The Sterling name was made out to be a curse in this town. My entire family were treated as outcasts. Every time they turned their backs, whispers and rumors could be heard in their wake. I was the worst thing that ever happened to this town, but at the same time, isn’t it safe to say that I’m the best thing to ever happen as well? After all, without me, who else would they have to hate? Who else would they have to talk about? What else would they have to base all their idiotic stories off of? Sean Sterling. Manchester’s golden fuck-up. --- I-81 S. From Washington DC to Manchester, TN. Three hours down. Seven hours to go. This was a long drive with no one in the passenger seat. The space where Judith once sat was now decorated with trash. Cigarettes burnt down the filter, empty soda cans, ancient newspapers. Every now and then, below the noise of the radio, I thought I could hear her quiet whisper. Something soft, something delicate… but, of course, when I looked, there was nobody there. Just trash and the few remaining memories. Sigh. Turn the radio up louder. Keep driving, and don’t’ fucking stop. An early morning sun rose up over the horizon, forcing everything metal to shine. Some sappy old country song rambled from the radio, filling my head with even worse thoughts. Every mile, at least once, I considered rearing the car off of the road or crashing into some unsuspecting vehicle. Get buried beneath a fifty car pile-up. Hell, if I survived, at least I’d be a vegetable. Leaves clung to their trees limbs. Birds sung from their boughs. A fucking rainbow stretched out above my head. Every car that passed had a smiling driver at the wheel. Everything seemed right in the world, except for me. Strange times. Strange days. The bad thoughts came in by the truckload. I’d received the invitation over a telephone with a nervous voice on the other end. They had shipped Judith’s body back to Manchester to be buried. Back to our old hometown we both tried so hard to escape. For some reason, they felt it necessary to invite me to the funeral. I figured it was most likely to get me alone in a crowded room full of people who hated my guts so that they could maim, torture, and humiliate me in the worst ways possible. Needless to say, I wasn’t handling this situation with any sort of grace. When Judith died, a part of me went with her. It’s hard to believe that I had every intention to kill her and then kill myself… As for why I wasn’t going ahead and slitting my own throat now, let’s just be honest here and say that nobody wants to die alone. My life had become filled with things that I didn’t understand and things that I didn’t want any part of. There’s nothing more horrifying than consciously losing your mind and having no way to stop it. Insanity is a slippery slope. Next thing you know, you’re playing Scrabble with a bunch of unbathed Neanderthals with Down’s Syndrome in some backwards-ass hospital built for retards and dumb fucks. Drugs. Chainsmoking. Alcohol binges. Hallucinations. Voices in my head. Some crazy motherfucker telling me he’s me from the future, come back to help me kill my own parents. You name it, I had it going on. Every problem a person can deal with, loaded up onto my own two shoulders, slowly crushing me with the weight of it all. So, I turned the radio up louder, to block out my thoughts. I rolled the windows down and let the wind whip my hair. I took in slow, deep breaths… …and I fucking prayed that Manchester wasn’t going to be as bad as I thought it would be. --- Manchester, Tennessee A preacher stood up on his podium, face red with fury, preaching hellfire and brimstone as his toupee waved and wiggled on his head. He slammed his fist down on the podium, sending his unintelligible screams over Judith’s urn and to the pews, filled to the brim with wrinkled old ladies crying and holding their hands up in the air as they screamed their own praises in reply. Meanwhile, I stood in the back corner with wide eyes, paranoia plucking at my brain as I considered scooting in behind a wreath of half-dead flowers, sent by someone who didn’t bother to make sure they would arrive alive. This was not the type of place that I should be. Not only did almost everyone in every pew hate me, but I couldn’t help but feel buried beneath a bunch of beliefs that didn’t jive too well with my own. You can practically see God laughing up in the heavens as I stood, awkward and shredded to ribbon in the very back of a funeral parlor. Judith’s ashes sat on a small desk in front of the preacher, with a black and white portrait sitting beside it as some sort of half-assed reminder of what she looked like. A memorial pamphlet was crumbled up in my fist as I finally decided it best to make a mad dash for the exit, and quick, before anyone noticed. I slipped out just as they began speaking in tongues. I almost immediately pulled a cigarette from my pocket as I gave a long sigh of relief, leaning back against the red brick wall, flicking the lighter against the tip. And then, of course, almost on cue, a voice: "Those things will kill you, you know…" As always, there was no one around me. Not a soul. Just the pitch black darkness of night. "So, why are you even here?" I took a long drag from my cigarette, the ashes falling down from the tip like snow. Down, down, down to the asphalt below. "You don’t belong here. They don’t want you here. Judith wouldn’t have wanted you here." Insanity is a slippery slope. "You’re slipping, Sean. You know you are. And when you finally fall? You’ll have no one but yourself to blame." One minute, you’re perfectly fine. "You won’t be able to point your finger at Judith, obviously." Next thing you know, you’re sliding down and there’s absolutely nothing you can do. "And you certainly won’t be able to point your finger at me." You can try to catch yourself. How many people do you think manage to safe themselves? Look at the world today. How many lunatics do you see running rabid in the streets? "You brought this all upon yourself." I threw the cigarette down to the pavement below and stomped it out, hanging my head low as I walked across the parking lot, to my car, full of trash and the few remaining memories of Judith. The engine roared as I turned on the ignition. And I drove. I drove as fast as I could, toward the beer lights glowing bright like lighthouses in the distance. Alcohol doesn’t heal all wounds, but y’know, it certainly doesn’t hurt. --- A whole bar full of lonely losers, with cigarettes cascading out of every ashtray, a thousand empty beer bottles laying on every table. Someone slides a quarter into the jukebox and next thing I know Bob Dylan is whispering some sad sappy song in my ear. Here, no one knows my name. Hell, no one cares what it is. There’s no one staring knives in my back and there’s no preacher telling me I’m going to hell if I don’t give my life up for the Lord. Here, there’s nothing but a bunch of likeminded failures congregated under the common cause of drowning away their sorrows. Here, everything is fine and nothing hurts. Some fiery redhead from across the bar is staring me down, sipping on her bottle of vermouth, looking innocent behind the hazy fog of cigarette smoke. Every now and then, I would look over my shoulder to catch her grinning at me before looking away quickly, playing coy even though we both knew what the other was thinking. They say the best way to fight loneliness is to have company. I stood up from my bar stool, taking my beer with me as I crossed through the maze of tables and old drunkards, my eyes now staring back at hers. "Atta boy, Sean. Go get her." After a relationship, while the dust still hasn’t cleared and your mind is still a whirlwind of confusion, the only thing you really want is to feel wanted again. You want to feel loved. You want to feel needed. If you’re a woman, it makes you feel like you can move on without the sorry bastard. If you’re a man, it makes you feel empowered. Strong. The alpha male. To know that you can still have your way with any woman you set your sights on. "Judith is dead and you’re alive. You can’t keep dwelling on the past, now can you? Move on, move on."
