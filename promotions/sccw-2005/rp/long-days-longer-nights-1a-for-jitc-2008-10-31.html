Long Days, Longer Nights (1a for JITC)

31 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/31/2008 March 13th 1996 Austin, Texas 2:25 am Sharron Byrd stared silently at the telephone on the wall. She glanced at the clock as the second hand rounded the nine on the way to the twelve. It was two-thirty in the morning, and she had been waiting on a phone call that was supposed to come at midnight for nearly two and a half hours now. Her frustrations mounted as a child came running out of one of the rooms in the small apartment. Jacob Byrd was three years old, and was the most important thing in Sharron's life. She loved her son, but right now wasn't the most opportune time the child had ever decided to have a nightmare. Jacob was in tears, and ran directly for his mother. She scooped the child up in her arms and began murmuring the classic parental line used in this situation. "Honey, honey, it's not real, it was just a dream. Take a deep breath honey." Jacob still was crying, the sobbing slowly coming down from it's climax. Sharron looked up at the clock and sighed, frustrated. She picked up her son and carried him into her and her husband's bedroom. The bed was made neatly, and Sharron quickly tucked Jacob in before laying down beside him, holding him. Sharron was still in her work clothes, and in the best motherly voice she could muster she said "Jakey, I'm gonna change for bed and then we'll talk about the bad dream and you can sleep in here with me tonight, alright?" Jacob nodded and Sharron got up grabbing a set of pajama pants and an over sized t-shirt out of her dresser. She walked into the bathroom and began to undress as she looked in the mirror. Her straight black hair contrasted perfectly with her cream colored skin as it laid on her shoulders, her angled facial features and her perfect lips were silent as she undressed. She stood there for a moment, analyzing herself. Her ice blue eyes ran up and down her own body, for a moment brief insecurity overtook her as she slid the t-shirt on and the pajama pants before she continued staring in the mirror. She was pretty, right? Clay hadn't found some Asian geisha to love him, right? A tear slowly trickled down her cheek as she stared in the mirror. She knew none of that was true, Clay was just too busy with his wrestling to care about her. He always assured her this is how they'd have all the nice things, but currently all Clay was providing were hours of loneliness. He wasn't here for her, or their son. He wasn't here to tell her how beautiful she was like he had done in college, she wasn't here to console their son about his nightmare. He.Wasn't.Here. Sharron quickly came back to reality as she heard the soft knock on the door and the small weak voice from behind it. "Mommy, I'm scared," Jacob said quietly as he stood behind the closed doorway. "One second baby, just go lay down and I'll be in." She knew Jacob wouldn't go lay down, he'd stand at the doorway waiting for her to come out. So she quickly hurried getting ready for bed before taking her son into her bedroom and laying down with him. She was silent as she heard the horrors that plague little boys, the monster under the bed, the bogeyman in the closet. All of these fictitious ideas warping into their fragile psyche and morphing them into paranoid superstitious creatures who become creatures of habit. Hiding under the covers at night for instance. March 13th 1996 Tokyo, Japan 5:25 pm Clay Byrd was twenty-seven years old, and was in the prime of his life. He looked over at the man to his right, the representative from the National Wrestling Alliance. Tonight the big gaijan Clay Byrd would be wrestling for the World Heavyweight Championship, and anytime a title shot happened some one had to be there to discuss it. To Clay's left sat his college roommate and best friend Bob Sullivan. Bob was an undersized special teamer for the Texas Longhorns who had a knack for all things involving money, which during the current wrestling boom seemed to flow from the industry better than the oil fields in Texas. Clay had been picked by the NWA to bring the title back state side, and had came to Japan for a tour of work. Recently he main evented the Budokan, and they were all ramping up for the big blow off at the Tokyo Dome. Apparently there was a problem though, by the look on the face of the angry Japanese people sitting across the table from him. Apparently Clay Byrd wasn't over enough for them to have their champion and huge draw take the fall in the Tokyo Dome. They wanted to do it the week after on the start to the new tour, Clay had felt this coming on and had made a call to his agent Bob Sullivan. Which is why he was here today, normally at this time of the year he'd be gearing up for the NFL draft. Today though, he had to be their for his best friend, and today no matter what was said he had to make sure all promises of the agreement were lived up to. "What's the problem fellers? Can't we try an' figure this out with out so much Japanese yellin'?" Clay spouted off, as the Japanese bookers began an entire new tirade about the big Texan. Finally, after another good ten or fifteen minutes the NWA representative nodded and stepped up from the table and motioned for Clay to come to the door. Clay and Bob both immediately got up and walked over and outside the door. "Well Clay, we have a bit of a problem." The man stated sheepishly, Clay Byrd was always an imposing figure telling him there was a problem wasn't exactly something you put on your list of things to do. "They said no way in hell are they having you go over tonight, they want you to stay in Japan for another two months to make the change credible." Clay exploded, slamming his fist into the door. "So what you're lil New York ass is tellin me, is that those slant eyed motherfuckers aren't gonna give you your belt back. So you're gonna let them shit all over me?" Clay yelled, making sure the Japanese men inside the door understood he was rather infuriated at this point. Bob realizing how out of hand this was getting stepped in between the two and motioned for Clay to take a step back. Bob Sullivan was no slouch on stature, while being undersized on the University of Texas football team, he was in the real world a rather intimidating fellow. "So they want my client to be away from his family for another two weeks?" Bob stated, trying to get the representative to give Clay a little sympathy. "Essentially yes, this tour would cap off at the Budokan and would involve Clay tag teaming with an imported American worker. He'd also be getting a pay raise from the twenty thousand dollars he was alloted before, to forty thousand for these two months." The rep said, he knew how to appeal to agents and wrestlers alike. With the almighty dollar. Clay and Bob both looked at each other for a second before Bob began again. "How much?" "Forty thousand for this tour, and an offer of Clay coming back to do future tours. Once a year, fifty thousand dollars per tour. This tour would make him immediately marketable in Japan." The rep continued on as Clay looked over at the concrete walls of the Tokyo Dome. Forty thousand dollars for another two months. Sixty thousand dollars in a year? Of course he was going to accept. The idea of coming back for another two or three months once a year for fifty thousand dollars? Of course he'd accept, the representative knew that. "So Clay, what do you think?" Bob asked him, and Clay nodded his head smiling. "Yeah, I reckon you go in there and tell them boys I'm sorry I dented their door," Clay said smiling. Forty thousand dollars? Sharron was going to love the news, or so Clay thought. October 31st 2008 Suburbs of New York City 3:00 pm "So Bob, whatcha think?" Clay said as he sat in the passenger seat of a black 2005 Lexus sedan. His black cowboy hat nearly touching the roof on each bump as the two cruised through a suburban New York neighborhood. "I think it sounds too good to be true, truthfully Clay. They want to pay a thirty-nine year old man that much to chase the big group of bad guys out on live television? There has to be a catch." Bob said, running his hand through his salt and pepper hair. "Yea, I 'magine there is a catch," Clay said as Bob nodded his head and responded back. "And what's the catch Clay?" Clay took a deep breath before finally explaining to Bob exactly what he had to do. "You evah hear of the dead man's hand?" Clay said as he handed over a manilla folder with what little information he had gathered on the wrestling group. "I ain't nevah had to run baddies out like this Bob, Imma need some help." Clay said as the car continued rolling through suburban New York. "I don't exactly put on tights Clay... Although these girls might get me to." Bob said, both of them sharing a bit of a chuckle before Clay started talking again. "Bob, I tink you know wha' I'm talkin 'bout, I need help on the home front. I need someone to help me figure out what all I'm up against." Clay said, letting the words hang for a moment before he continued. "I need someone to make sure I ain't made the biggest mistake of my life." Bob paused for a minute, pondering the situation. "Five percent?" He asked as Clay nodded his head. "Alright, I'm in." Clay smiled but then Bob held up his hand and stopped. "But you need to do something for me." Clay was stumped for a brief second until Bob pulled out a a small pamphlet, with the words "Jewel In The Crown" across it. "Your thing in SCCW might be important, but Clay. This is what I care about, winning, and winning big. This old friend is your chance." Clay took the pamphlet and flipped through it, "Aight, I'm in." The two sat in silence as the car rolled to a stop. Clay sat mystified, perplexed, stunned. It had been ten years since he had last looked upon Sharron Byrd, ten years of talking through their son, ten years of emotions, and all Clayton Byrd could do was sit dumbfounded at his new predicament. May 28th 1996 Dallas, Texas 7:17pm You work your entire life to care for your family, to give them the best possible life that you can possibly manage to give them. Clay Byrd was traveling the world just to make sure his family had all the things in the world. He made sure Sharron had a new car, he made sure his son had all the clothes and toys he would ever need. He'd done everything a father was supposed to do, or so he thought. He had provided for his family, and yet as Clay Byrd walked through the door to his apartment he could feel everything. A layer of dust was across the kitchen table where he placed his bag. The air was warm and stagnant, he looked around the living room noticing the blank spots on the wall. Where pictures of his family had once sat filling the home with the warmth that only a family could provide sat lonely nails. Clay immediately put his hand through the nearest spot where a picture had hung. He let out a sceam of frustration and sank down to the cold gray carpet. Before Clay knew it tears began flowing down his cheeks, his breaths became short and labored. He needed his family, they were the only thing of any importance to him. His son Jacob, his wife Sharron, he needed them. He needed their love, it fueled the fire inside of Clay Byrd. It made Clay who he was. And Clay sat alone in his apartment, tears streaming down his face. All because he wanted to give his family the best life possible. He slowly brought himself to his feet and walked back the short hallway to the bed rooms. He started to peak into his son's room but stopped himself and walked into his own. He looked at the blank spot on the ground where the bed used to be, he layed down on the floor, curled himself in the fetal position and resumed crying. October 31st 2008 Suburbs of New York City He had made his way out of the car as he reassured Bob that he'd be alright. He watched as the car pulled off down the street as he turned back to Sharron who still hadn't noticed the giant Texan with the black cowboy hat. Clay reached into his pocket and pulled out a pack of Marlboro's and quickly had one fired up. He inhaled the first hit deeply, hoping with the toxic smoke that it would bring him some form of courage. Slowly Clay walked towards her and finally she noticed him. She looked up from removing the groceries from her minivan and stared at Clay. The look of shock on her face was almost priceless. "Trick or treat, darlin" it sounded stupid but it was the only thing Clay could force out. His mind was moving a million miles a minute, every molecule firing different emotions into his head. "Uhh, hi Clay..." Sharron stumbled over her words, like Clay her mind immediately went into overdrive. Her thoughts looking for the exact thing to say, part of her wanted to call Clay an asshole, another part wanted to run into the house scared, and yet another wanted her to wrap her arms around him. Clay smiled before he took another hit of his cigarette, Sharron finally noticing it immediately questioned him. "When did you start killing yourself?" Sharron snapped at him, that sly smile of Clay knowing he had an advantage infuriated her. "I think ya could 'magine when abouts that was," Clay said finally putting the cigarette out on the pavement. "Jake inside?" Sharron nodded yes in response before opening her mouth. "I was just getting ready to take him to a friends house," Sharron responded as Clay took a deep breath. "Well I'm not in town for too long, Bob is runnin' down the block to meet with a client before stoppin back, mind of I go in and surprise him?" Clay asked looking at the bags Sharron was trying to pick up. Immediately his chivalrous male instinct made him try to carry as many of the bags as possible. "Yeah Clay, but you can't show up here like this. I need to know when you plan on seeing Jacob. I'm not going to keep you from him, but what if we had something planned as a [i]family[/i] and you just showed up," Sharron said as the two walked to the front door step. The word family stung Clay, it was a knife in the heart. "I reckon I'd be standin' in that road smokin a ciggeratte waitin' on Bob to come back and get me, but yeah next time I call I'll make sure you three aren't out havin' a blast," Clay said, Sharron recognizing she had got under big Texas' skin a little opened the door and the two walked in. As Sharron came through the door carrying the a bag or two immediately Chad Hollisting shot up off the couch. "You need a hand honey?" he asked, the last few words trailing off as he saw Clay walk in carrying the rest of the groceries. "No, Clay decided to stop by and surprise Jacob. Coincidentally I put him to work carrying things," Sharron said as Clay chuckled a bit before handing a few of the bags to Chad. He hated that little weaselly bastard but nothing Clay could possibly do to Chad could put his family back together. As the three walked into the kitchen his son Jacob looked up "Mom I nee... DAD!" Clay sat the bags down as Jacob gave him a big hug. Clay made sure he lifted the fourteen year old up in the air with the hug. He hadn't seen Jacob in a little over a year. He squeezed tighter holding onto his son, cherishing the moment. "Dad you're killing me," Clay let out a loud laugh and squeezed a little tighter before letting go. "I missed you Jake," Clay said as he messed up his sons recently gelled hair. Clay looked at the gel on his hand a bit quizzically before wiping it on Jake's face. "Hey!" Jake yelled as the two slowly but surely succeeded in ruining Jacob's recently done hair and Clay's shirt. The two fell to the ground laughing, before Jacob immediately jumped up and ran upstairs. "One second Dad, I got something to show you." Clay nodded and looked up at Sharron and Chad smiling. "Lil' dickhead ruined my shirt." Sharron laughed a bit as Chad just looked at the big hulking man sitting Indian style on his floor. "Ya know Clay, you don't have to sit down there like a savage," Chad said as he pulled out a chair. Clay slowly brought his body up to his feet. Stretching a bit, his back still hurt from Temptation a few weeks ago. Jacob came running down the stairs, and tossed a football across the room that Clay swiped out of the air. "That's the game ball I got from the last game of the year, my first one." Clay looked at the small ball and smiled "Good job son, how many sacks?" Jacob smiled a little being a bit modest he quietly said "Five." Clay laughed and tossed the ball back, "Just like your Dad. Minus the fact I'd of gotten six," he said with a wink. "Whatever," Jake said with a small smile. Right as Clay said that he felt his phone vibrate in his pocket, "I guess I gotta get goin' kid," Clay said as he stood up. "I'll give you a call later this week, oh and you know that PRIME place?" Clay asked as Jacob nodded, "Watch that tournament, your old Dad might have to show up and drop someone on their head. Jake laughed before giving Clay a hug, "I'll make sure I call this week, Dad" Clay nodded and hugged Sharron. "I should be home here in a few weeks, maybe dinner? On me?" The second question he directed at Chad who nodded his head, "sounds good, Clay." Chad responded before the big Texan walked out the front door of the house and into the black car awaiting him. "Fuck me man, that was brutal."
