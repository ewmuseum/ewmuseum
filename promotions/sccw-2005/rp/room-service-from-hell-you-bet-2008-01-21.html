Room Service from Hell? You Bet

21 January 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/21/08 [Time: 7:32 PM, days after his first FUSE match] [Where: A small gymnasium in California] [People: RYUJIN, Johnny Gold] -The scene opens up with the camera following RYUJIN and Johnny walking around some bleachers in a poorly lit gymnasium. As the two take a seat, the camera pans the area. There‘s a shabby wrestling ring set up in the middle of the gym. The bleachers aren‘t even close to full, with maybe 50 or 60 people in attendance at the event.- Gold: Kid, what da hell are we doin’ here? RYUJIN: Look, I didn’t ask you to come. You insisted on following me. Gold: Of course I did. Ya still haven’t signed the contract I gave ya! RYUJIN: Oh, that? I flushed that down the toilet before my match. Gold: Ya WHAT?! RYUJIN: Yep, right down the pipes. Gold: Well, that’s alright kid. -Johnny takes his always present briefcase and sets it on his lap. He opens it up and starts rummaging through some papers. He hands RYUJIN a packet.- RYUJIN: What’s this? Gold: Another contract. I have dozens of ‘em in here! Anyway, you still haven’t told me why we’re here. RYUJIN: Shut up. You’re about to see… -No sooner than when the last words leave RYUJIN‘s mouth, a rather overweight wrestler makes his way to the ring. He‘s soon followed by what appears to be a teenage girl. This girl is his opponent. The match gets underway, with RYUJIN watching intently. The fat wrestler immediately dominates after the bell. After 6 minutes of domination, the fat wrestler hits a splash on the female and gets a three count. RYUJIN didn‘t take his eyes of the ring the entire time.- Gold: Dear god, kid! That whole little display there was kinda messed up. Why the hell did we even watch that?! I think that poor girl’s gonna cough up her liver! RYUJIN: I’ll explain now. I’m here scouting. I’ve been following one of those wrestlers for a bit. Gold: What, the fat guy? He don’t seem that impressive. So he can beat a woman half his size, big deal. RYUJIN: It’s not the slob I have my eyes on. It’s the other kid. Gold: What, the girl?! RYUJIN: Wrong. He’s a guy. His name is Kei Takino. He’s 16, having moved to California from Japan last summer. He lives on his own, supporting himself with wrestling and other odd jobs around the city. Gold: Damn. Well why da hell ya interested in him? He just got his ass whipped! RYUJIN: I didn’t expect him to win. I’ve watched him wrestle 6 times, and all 6 times, he lost. Gold: Christ, and ya wanna back this loser? RYUJIN: Yes. I wrestled against his father a bit in Japan before tagging with him. Kei’s father taught me a lot. When he told me his son aspired to become a wrestler, I took an interest. Gold: But da kid’s built like a freakin’ teenage girl! He won’t amount to nuthin’! RYUJIN: Wrong. First of all, he already has spirit. He’s lost 6 times, but he’s giving his all every time. Second, he did gymnastics growing up. He has natural flexibility and agility. Third, he started younger than most wrestlers. This kid can be molded into a decent pro wrestler. I guarantee that. As a matter of fact, I told his father that when he asked if I thought he should let Kei move to America. I also told him I’d take the kid on as a protégé when I felt he had some experience in the ring. Gold: What, ya lookin’ for a successor already? RYUJIN: Maybe. You can never be sure of what might happen in the world of pro wrestling. My first match was proof of that. I thought I prepared well, had all my bases covered. But in the end, my hand wasn’t raised. Look, we’re done here, lets head out. -The camera fades out as RYUJIN and Johnny stand up and start to exit the bleachers.- [Time: 9:56 PM] [Place: Johnny‘s rental car] [People: RYUJIN and Johnny Gold] -The scene fades in as the camera looks on from the backseat. Johnny seems to be telling what he thinks is a hilarious story as RYUJIN stares out the passenger side window. The audio picks up the end of Johnny‘s story.- Gold: And then I says, ‘Boner? I just met ‘er!’. HEHAHEHAHEHA!!! RYUJIN: … And then she slapped you? Gold: Yeah, that’s about how it turned out. -RYUJIN just shakes his head, a look of annoyance on his face. The two drive forward in an awkward silence for a few minutes as they pass a sign saying ‘Welcome to Arizona’.- Gold: That reminds me. You read about this kid Aleister yet? RYUJIN: Of course I have. Gold: What do ya think? RYUJIN: Oh, I’m thinking a lot. Most of it is pretty negative. It seems the kid and I both have certain goals and that they revolve around changing certain worlds. But that’s about where the similarities end. This moron seems to think he’s some visionary, some kind of revolutionist that can change the ‘conformity’ of the world we know. This is no doubt one of the STUPIDEST things I’ve ever heard when it comes to pro wrestling. This numbnut has things a bit confused. Gold: Yeah, he does seem ta be off his rocker. RYUJIN: Oh, it’s not just this talk of a fake revolution that bothers me. I’ve stated before that I plan on cleansing pro wrestling of those fools who don’t have any damn business being in it. This kid easily falls into that group. He speaks of conformity as if every American walks down the street, wearing the same thing, doing the same thing. Because heaven forbid some people like the same brand of clothing! Heaven forbid people like to do the same thing! HEAVEN FUCKIN’ FORBID people go and buy iPods and other things of the sort. They have no right to buy nice things with the money they earned at a job! That reminds me, you see anything about this non-conformist having a job other than pro wrestling? Gold: Nah, I didn’t see nuthin’. RYUJIN: Yeah, didn’t think so. He’s so non-conforming that he’s a broke SOB! Yeah, that’s a great thing to aspire to be in life. Gold: You’re layin’ inta this kid kinda rough. RYUJIN: Yeah, but his whole deal has me annoyed and confused. Okay, so he wants to change the world. How the hell is he gonna do that by being a pro wrestler? I don’t see the connection here. Maybe he wants to try and use wrestling as a springboard to other things. If that’s the case, it at least makes sense. However, if that is the case, then I have a certain problem I plan of helping out the former ‘problem solver’ with. That problem being, of course, that his spine is currently fully functional and intact. I will not let someone treat the sport I love as some sort of stepping stone to another life. It’s one thing if certain pieces fall into place and circumstances lead you out of pro wrestling and into something else. It’s something else if you enter the sport with the aspiration of getting out. I’ll be more than happy to help him get out. I’ll just make his exit a lot quicker than he wanted. Gold: Well, what’s your strategy against this kid? RYUJIN: I’m bigger than him, to start off. Much bigger. He wrestles a high flying style with European mat wrestling thrown in. That doesn’t make a damn bit of sense to me. This kid aint big enough to wrestle on the mat the way the Europeans do. That leads to another problem. To wrestle me on the mat, he has to get me there first. I plan on dropping the poor bastard on his head too often to let him wrestle me on the mat. That’s my broad strategy in a nutshell. Drop him on his head until he’s ready to be pinned. I might break out a high flying move or two, but with this kind of size advantage, there’s no reason for me to play it risky. His bio doesn’t list much about his moveset. His finisher is a top rope flipping Stunner set up by a 619. I love opponents with top rope finishers. It’s so damn easy to plan for and avoid moves like that. First, he’s gonna want to try and hit me with the 619. I’ll make sure that’s enough of a challenge for him, let alone actually hitting his big finish. A move like that can’t have great range due to how complicated it is to hit it. I just have to watch myself near the corners. It’s not that complicated. Gold: Alright, nice strategy kid. Got any other concerns or opinions ya want known? RYUJIN: No, I’ve said all I want to say. Chris Aleister is in for one hell of a wake up call when it comes to pro wrestling. You can nickname me room service for this match if you want… Room service from Hell. -The scene fades to black as Johnny starts to fire into yet another ‘hilarious’ story.-
