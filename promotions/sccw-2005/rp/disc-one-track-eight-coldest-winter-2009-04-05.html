Beep. Beep. 'Come on, let's move it here people! Get him into OR1 right now! Move!' Beep. Beep. Beep. They didn't have much time to get ready; the phone call had come thirty minutes earlier. When the call came the air in the room instantly changed. Tensions rose to a new high and the eventual stress arrived much sooner than anticipated. Dr. Kelly Walters walked out of her office with an intense focus in her eyes as she strode down the hallway, ignoring everyone else around her in the process. Beep. Beep. Beep. 'James, get your ass in there. I'm going to need you too,' her confident voice was directed at an older white male walking in the opposite direction as she was heading. The man sort of froze mid-step as he looked at her, but she simply walked away. He opened his mouth, but found nothing coming out. James licked his lips and swallowed hard before he found the strength to respond. 'I don't think I sh--.' James didn't get a chance to finish his statement as she instantly cut him off. 'That would be why they pay me to do thinking.' Beep. Beep. Beep. Her left arm pushed open the door and inside of the room were a handful of nurses and technicians. Each one of them couldn't begin to explain what exactly was going on here. When her phone rang, Kelly didn't know what to do or think. That phone hadn't rung in years, maybe even a decade. Part of her wondered if it was a telemarketer or even someone accidentally dialing the wrong number. It had never happened in the past, but there's always a first time for everything. 'Open that door and make sure you don't overdose on the anesthesia. You,' she then pointed at young nurse, almost trembling in her stance. 'Stop shaking and turn on the damn faucet for me. The woman hurriedly turned on the faucet as the doctor immediately placed her pale hands underneath the sterling silver faucet and felt the warm water run over her wrists. Her hazel eyes were intently focused upon the man lying on her table and watched as his chest steadily rose less and less, his breaths becoming shallower and shallower with each passing second. Beep. Beep. Beep. Beep. As the heart monitor started to beep faster, Kelly knew time was of the essence. She began to scrub faster as her eyes never wavered from the patient. The phone call was short and to the point, much like it was designed. He’s hurt were the only words muttered to her from the frantic female voice on the other end of the line. If she hadn't laid eyes upon him then the Doctor wouldn't have believed it for a second. Yet, there he laid on the perilous edge of life and death. She would be the one to save him. Beep. Beep. Beep. Beep. Beep. Maybe. Beep. Beep. Beep. Beep. Beep. Beep. Beep. The heart monitor began to race faster with each passing second to the point that she shut the faucet off herself and ran into the operating room. Yet, as her foot crossed over the threshold, the monitor was racing so fast that she only had mere seconds before it became too late. 'He's crashing people, let's go!' she yelled out to the room as the Doctor raced towards the man's quickly dying body. Her eyes quickly examined the wound and shook her head. 'Come on, I need a scalpel, stat people!' In the blink of an eye, the long metal object was placed in between her fingers and she cut directly across his shoulder to see how extensive the damage was. From an initial look it was a superficial wound, but while taking a deeper look at it she could see the muscle torn and the blood from the ruptured artery. EEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEE! Her eyes instantly looked at the monitor as the line went completely flat. Kelly shook her head as the crash paddles came without her even having to open her mouth. As she was handed the paddles she looked into his eyes and could feel an instant bond come between them. 'Charge to 300!' she yelled at the technician and within seconds the cold steel of the paddles was against his bare chest. The electricity coursed through his entire body with not even a hint of life coming from the monitor. She cursed loudly under her breath as she waited the necessary seconds for the device to recharge before she let off another round of electricity that jolted the man's body. EEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEE! Still nothing. She tossed the paddles aside before she balled up her left fist and pounded against his chest. Nothing. She did it again as the moments after that fateful phone call was still fresh in her memory. If the first phone call wasn't a shock to her, the second one was, especially since it was for a completely different person. She slammed her closed fist against his chest again, but still nothing. Kelly looked around at the nurses and could see the coldness in their eyes. There was no hope, nothing there. EEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEE! Kelly ripped off one of the latex gloves and tossed it onto the man's lifeless body before she took a few steps back. Her eyes instantly shot up to clock and read it with heaviness. 'Time of Death... 3:47AM,' she said somberly before walking back towards the now dead body. Kelly took a long look at him and shook her head. Time was of the essence though and there was one more person she needed to help, someone she still might be able to save. 'I'm sorry, Craig.' It was all that she could offer. 'I didn't know what love was then,' she said in a small voice. 'I don't know if I do now.' He finally looked away, the discomfort of then both too much for either to bear. When he looked back, she was gone, just as she promised. And, as always, she left him with no answers, only questions.
