Soundtrack of the life you didn't lead

25 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 25, 2009 I Mother Earth, "No Coma" (Quicksilver Meat Dream, 2003) June 29, 2006 I spent that night in bed, but I did not sleep. It was that anxious energy that, for no matter how long I laid with my eye lids shut, simply refused to let me get any rest. It was that same feeling that a kid would get on Christmas Eve. No, scratch that, it was more akin to the time I spent waiting to get into the operating room. I knew that it was pain that lay ahead of me, but I needed this to be better. I needed to make that call just to feel something more than this damned numb. It was 7 am when I kicked off the covers and gave up on trying getting any sleep. I picked up a discarded shirt off the floor and slid it over my torso. Slowly, the heavy thuds of my two hundred and some pounds could be heard venturing down the creaking steps. Upon reaching the base, I waded myself, messy hair and all, through to the kitchen and a waiting bowl of cereal. Something sugary that went well with the early morning cartoons I flopped in front of. Eight o’clock rolled around and I began my intrepid dialling to the Squared Circle Gym. It took several attempts to complete those digits. What was I going to say? Was I really going to do this? Well, my confusion of cowardice came to a halt when the other end answered. The minutes that followed were a blur, but by the end of it I had a date to be there, and a debt of five hundred dollars. It was a thrilling thought. I was going to shed the hopelessness that seemed to present itself in everything I deemed adult. My heart was racing and my knees were weak. The next several minutes were spent zoning out of the Sponge Bob cartoon and recalling the dreams of youth. And there I was, standing in front of thousands, a man with a zebra stripe shirt lifting my hand in the air. The cheering fans were actually just my mother vacuuming. I got about halfway through my iPod in search of a theme song when I remembered another important phone call I had to make. I had to know if I was going to be making this journey alone. I had to dial an old friend and ask if he remembered the deal. He did. Tommy Kowolski remembered the pact that two little boys made. He laughed when I first brought it up. His chuckles were stifled when I mentioned the first call of the day. This brought upon it the serious tone of possibility to a childish vision. It was a decision that would require some time. I couldn’t blame him; I had spent the better part of the last two years trying to build the courage to take this up. I told him I would give him all the time that he wanted to take, and just asked him to give me a call back when he did decide. I got the call back fifteen minutes later. He was in. ---- It was quite the whirlwind experience. Two years had passed, and I had never really had the moment to pause, reflect, account. The first things that came to mind were the bruises, bumps and cuts. I don’t think that I spent more than a week without some physical reminder of why my parents didn’t want me in this business in the first place. It was always fun to walk into the office on Monday mornings and get that look from the ladies in the office. You know the one; it is the mixture of pity and are-you-really-that-stupid. I had to look past that, and dig for something that resembled progress more than a general ass kicking. What I did see related more to Tommy than myself. He was the one getting over with the crowd, where as I had no way to relate to the bar full of drunks. He was the one getting along better with the veterans – the sentinels to the sport that made damn sure that you paid your toll. The thud that would echo through out those crummy dives, that was me. Every point of success that I could see would shine in Tom, and not in me. Fans, he even began to gather a following of fans who would attend whatever Southern Ontario city that we found ourselves that weekend. In this tag team, it was clear in many minds who it was that was carrying the load. Had I even grown at all? This particular weekend we were doing a show in Hamilton. The ride up was spent in silence, heavy in thought on my behalf, and enjoying the radio on his. I finally started talking after we were finished changing for the show. "Listen Tommy, you remember the deal, right?" "Yeah Mitch, I remember the deal. That is why we are here, is it not?" "It is how we got here, yeah. But what did we say? We give this a year to see if we can’t make something of this." I cast my gaze past him as I tried to figure out how to put this. "What is it you are trying to say Mitch?" "Tommy, what I am saying is, things ain’t exactly going to where I thought they would. I wouldn’t call what I am doing, ‘working’." "Are you shitting me? We are killing this tag team thing." "Nah Tommy, you are killing it. I, hell, I am just dead weight; A fucking twenty three year old who needs to grow up." "You trying to tell me you are quitting on me?" "It’s been two years; I respected the deal we had. It is just time that I fade away, back into some nine to five job, and you shine. You got a real chance to be something special here." "Mitch," was all he could say. I knew I was letting him down, but he didn’t need me for this. Our last match as a tag team was like many before that. I hit a few spots, but Tommy did most of the work. The crowd really got behind him, and I spent most of the match as a cheerleader on the apron. When it was over, and to my surprise, Tommy announced to the crowd that it was my last match. In line with my expectations, however, was about as little reaction anyone could draw from a crowd of drunken, middle aged men with nothing better to do than watch an indy wrestling show. In what we called backstage for that night, which was nothing more than a storage room for empties, I saw Tommy talking to some suit. Turns out this guy was from one of the bigger players. I took solace in knowing that I was bowing out at just the right time. Tommy was going to the big leagues, and I would have been stuck otherwise to lacklustre matches with men past their prime, far past. ---- It is great seeing Tom enjoying his success. I just cannot help but think that he is messing things up. He has lost his fidelity, and the faith of his daughter’s mother. I love that little girl, and he is a damn fool to put her through anything like this. We have exchanged ideas on this more than once. He is in a place called SCCW. Sin City Championship Wrestling, I think. Only ever heard of Aimz though. ---- June 27, 2009 I have always held open the option to fail. I think it is important for people to recognize that they always have a choice. And I think it is important to anyone reading this that this is my choice. This entire process of growing up, and into an adult, was never appealing to me. Now I see it ever so much clearer that it is not for me. I do not think I was meant for any of this. I did not ask for this life, but I thought I would give it a chance. My patience has run up today, and I am very tired. I have found the courage to answer a voice, and lingering thought in my head, which has been present as far back as I can remember. Today I take the leap into the great unknown, certain that Hell and damnation await. To my mother Angela, and my father Jerry, I know this will be tough. I am sorry for any hardship that this might bring to you. If it helps at all, I can count several occasions where I should have died. Consider the time between then and now as a bonus. I thank you for everything that you have given me, and it is time I stop taking. Tim, my brother. My protector. My constant friend. Thank you for everything that you have done for me in my life. You made this place bearable. Please live your life to the fullest and I wish you all the happiness in the world. Tommy, I am sorry that I held you to that stupid pact. I hope you enjoy the success that you have now, for you have sacrificed far more important things. I will always love that little girl, and my deepest regret is that I will not get to see her grow into a woman. Get your head right man, and give them a show. I don’t think they have cable where I am heading, but I still want you to win that title. To anyone else who might read this, forget who I am. Forget who I was. Do not make significant an end to an insignificant life. Forever Yours, Mitchell Quinlan.
