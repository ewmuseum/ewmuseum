Cracks in the Surface

9 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/9/07 The Rabbit Hole 3 hours after his match with Tsuyoshi Tanaka at Killzone. St. Francis Hospital, Maryland The match was epic, yes, but it was not a new experience. Pain was nothing unfamiliar to me. Neither was the deep ache of the day after. The sight of my own blood, the feel of something anatomically out-out-whack, nor the momentary panic of a disobedient limb that stayed motionless for a beat or two after the brain told it to move. All-said, I am very accustomed to the aftermath of a hard-fought match. While I knew that I shouldn’t expect the people around me to share my insight, from the way Sylvia was looking at me, one would think this was her first time. She didn’t say much. She simply stared off into the distance of the ocean that the hospital overlooked. When I tried to start a conversation, she’d let it die with one-word answers and thin politeness. After a while, I stopped trying. I resigned himself to staring, like she had, until the awkwardness became palpable. She sighed, and then began to gather her things. "Well, Damien, I think I should get back to the hotel. I have an early flight in the morning." "I’ll be out real soon. Really, I’m just here for observation, you know. They just want to make sure that the bump on my head is just a bump. You know how that that is." She nodded, not looking at me, "I know." "I’ll be fine." I reached out to take her hand, but the IV line in my arm tugged him back. The fucking metaphor formed itself. "As soon as they discharge me, I’ll be on the first flight back home. I think they’re giving me a few weeks off to fully recover. Yeah, I may do an appearance or maybe an interview. But the rest of the time, I’m off the clock. Maybe you can get some time off from the hospital and we can go somewhere nice. Maybe visit your family in Florida." Sylvia gave me a chilly smile, and offered a non-committal agreement. I let it slide. The last thing I wanted was an argument in a far-away hospital that would simply leave us both exhausted and nowhere new. I am a wrestler, she wanted a husband. It was an age-old debate. To be fair, it was a debate that we had the first time she left; so I’m sure what the hell she expected to be different this time. I guess she’ll get used to the idea soon enough. Or maybe she’ll just wait for me to get bored of what she thinks is a stupid phase, and then retire. She’ll get what she wants, and it will be a clean victory. After all, it would be me walking away, not her pulling me away. As I watched her leave, realizing that she hadn’t even given me a kiss, I knew that this time might have been somehow different. And there was a part of me that didn’t care. Call it stubbornness, or call it rebellion, but if I went home to find my wedding ring weighing down divorce papers, I’d know that she wasn’t strong enough anyway. What’s the point in trying to get blood from a stone? If she wanted to run away again, then let her. I wasn’t going to chase her. I didn’t the first time she left, and I wouldn’t now. I stood up, and made my way to my room. And as I took that first step, the floor dropped away from my foot, and I stumbled forward. The room began to spin around me, and I could feel my hands gripping the chair so tight that my nails began to dig into the wood. I closed my eyes and called for the nurse. Then I felt very weak. I sat down hard, and waited for the nurse to help me to my room. When she arrived, I waved her off, and made arrangements to be signed out. There was nothing wrong with me. I didn’t need a doctor to tell me there was. I packed my belongings, thanked the medical staff that was trying to convince me to stay, and signed myself out against medical advice. In the taxi on my way to the hotel, I rehearsed what I would tell her. I couldn’t lie and say that I was discharged. She’d smell that lie a mile away. I couldn’t tell her that I had left against the doctor’s orders because that would add fuel to a fire that was already smoldering. With no good solution, I told the driver to keep going. To the airport, and to a place where I could clear my head. I’d check into a hotel where I landed, and have a few days to figure out what happened next. As I made up my mind, my vision swam, and I could feel the car spinning around me. I braced myself, and the sensation passed. I was tempted to go back to the hospital, but that might mean hearing that there was something… …no, I wasn’t going to do that. I closed my eyes and rode the rest of the way in darkness. When I landed, they’d know what to do. (OOC: ten minutes ago, I opened the document to add the finishing touches to realize that almost two day's worth of work was gone. I don't know what went wrong.
