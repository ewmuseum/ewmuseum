Part Eleven: Fragmentation

13 November 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/13/08 Boring is as boring does. Boring is as boring was. Boring is simplistic. Boring is reductive. Boring is a judgment word. Boring is overrated. Boring is…well, boring. So, maybe it’s time to not be boring. ---- Lance Marshall sighed as he trudged down the driveway towards the SUV parked there, his gym bag bouncing slightly against his side. Despite the impression that his rather massive size tended to give people (or rather, despite the many impressions that his massive size tended to give people), there were days that going to the gym was the absolute last thing he wanted to do. But in Lance’s line of work, working out on a level some people might kindly dub as “obsessive” was one of the hazards of the job. Some people have to deal with co-workers who gossip, play office politics or slag you off behind your back. Lance Marshall’s co-workers tended to want to put him in a wheelchair…or, in their really deep, unspoken fantasies, put him six feet under. Some life, huh? Today was starting to shape up to be one of those “can I just go back to sleep” kind of days. Sleep had come fitfully, when it had come at all. Most of the evening had been spent staring up in silence at the wall, taking comfort from the feel of Alanna’s warmth and heat against his body. He had pretended that everything was fine when she had woken up, of course. It wasn’t that evenings like this tended to happen often enough to actually be something to worry about. Not lately, anyway. Lance hauled himself into the SUV, mentally going over his list of tasks for the day as he did so. He started the usual checks on mental autopilot, checking his windows and mirrors as he pondered the errands he had to run, the training session he had to attack today, what he might want for dinner that evening. Five seconds later, he place the key into the ignition and turned. Thirty seconds later found Lance Marshall burning to death as his SUV exploded around him, several thousand tons of Italian engineering turning into a smoking, gutted ball of flame. That’s it. End of story. Game over. … Nah, not really. But I bet I had you going there for a moment, didn’t I? ---- I’m worried about Lance. I’m not exactly worried worried, mind you. It’s not like I’m afraid he’s going to do something stupid or rash, like beat someone up (well, in a non-work related capacity) or go all Falling Down on me. I know he likes to talk about having a temper (hell, he’s even made it part of his professional schtick) but, well, the whole “for better and for worse” things…it helps you learn what a person is really about, you know? My husband is not a monster. There are a hell of a lot of people that would like to pain him that way (primarily one bitch queen of a redhead…wait, these days, that’s not specific enough, is it?). But he does not beat me and he does not mistreat our child. He is not some monosyllabic idiot who can only speak in grunts, he is not some vapid piece of meat, he is nothing less than a total and equal partner in every sense of the word. Because anyone who claims to truly know me knows full well that I would not be having that shit. Who the hell is that skank bitch to be questioning my parenting skils, anyway…?! No, what worries me about Lance is…well, it’s a little hard to explain. See, coming from the family situation he came from, my man is a little…competitive. Hell, having met his family, I can safely say that all of the Marshall brothers are that way. Five boys, my guy being the smallest of that particular bunch? Competitiveness was necessary just to get a word in edgewise. So, when he first got into this business of ours…well, it was kinda easy to see why he was doing it. At first, he wanted to prove something. To himself, to his family, to the world. And he did. Then he wanted to be a success. And he was. Then, Alexandra Pierce came along and things kinda went to shit…some days, I think all that kept Lance going was knowing that just putting one foot in front of the other was giving a big old middle finger to Alex and the rest of the Hand. But lately? Lately, I’m not sure. I know he feels he’s got to provide for me and Zach, to take care of us, to support us even though we’ve done well enough for him to be able to leave the business. And I love that about him, I really do. But we both know it’s not necessary. And if you asked him to go deeper than that…I’m not sure that he could right now. If you were to ask my husband, point blank, why he keeps doing what he does for a living…I’m not sure he could tell you. And that kinda scares the hell out of me. ---- Lance Marshall forcibly exhaled as he lifted the weight back to its starting position, his breath coming out as more of a growl than a human noise. It hung strangely in his ears and he could feel rather than see the “what the fuck was that about look” that Mike shot his way. He ignored it and the two men continued on, pushing themselves on further. The remainder of the session was spent in good natured banter, the men trading friendly insults, jokes and stories. The conversation continued as the two men shared stories over lunch at the gym canteen, each filling the other in on the details of their lives. If you were to ask Lance Marshall later what he and Mike had talked about, he wouldn’t have been able to remember a word of it. It was on his way to the showers that Lance was intercepted. The blow to his midsection was fast, the instrument made of metal. Before Lance could get the breath back in his body, the gun barrel was placed against the back of his head, two shots let off in quick succession. The body was left lying in the shower, steam coming off the hot water that the killer had left running. One throwaway weapon disposed of in the garbage and the killer quickly made his way out of the gym, none of the patrons any the wiser. That’s it. End of story. Game over. … Wait…that just doesn’t really work, does it? ---- G-d damn, that feels good. I love the moments like this, when I can just feel the warmth of the water seep into my aching muscles, relieving them of their burden. Giving myself over to it, allowing myself to be carried away. A warrior may live for the fight but he cannot be expected to do nothing but fight. Moments of peace are hard to come by…they need to be treasured. Protected. It’s just all the other stuff tends to get in the way. Hmm, the shampoo smells different. Lani must have picked up something new. Halifax. Don’t think I’ve ever been to Halifax before. Been to Canada a lot for the job, generally enjoyed myself. Canadians are cool, nice folks, vocal fans. They make for a good crowd. Not really looking forward to the Halifax crowd, though. Because Halifax is Aimz’s home town. I’ve been on the right side of the hometown audience before. Hometown audiences are incredibly loyal and will support you through anything. They will stand by you even when it seems everyone else has written you off. I mean, the Princes of New England are complete scumbags, right? But put ‘em in front of a Beantown crowd and it is nothing but love. Hell, I can’t count how many times I’ve come back to New York and felt like a pilgrim coming to Mecca. It’s just felt right, like I was back where I belonged. So I understand that high, that buzz that comes from fighting in front of people that will love you no matter what. Which is why this week is going to suck. The stadium staff will be fine…stiff, overly formal and not wanting to be anywhere near me but at least they’ll be professional. The fans on the other hand… Fuck it. Can’t worry about it, can’t change it. Best to keep my focus on my match this week. Strange as hell as that’s going to be. Because I don’t know who the hell this meat sack is who’s going around calling himself Wyatt Connors…but he isn’t Connors. Not the real Connors, anyway. I’ve known Connors for years. Hated his guts for most of them. But if there was one thing I could say that I knew for sure about Wyatt Connors, one absolute certainty that I would stake my life on…it’s that he needed to be in control. Not liked to be, not wanted to be, not preferred to be…needed to be, a need as fundamental as food, water or oxygen. The thought of not being in control, of being at someone else’s mercy…it scared the shit out of him. It’s why Desade scares him so bad…because she won’t allow anyone else to be in control. But the “thing” calling itself Connors…I look into its eyes and I see nothing but the dull gaze and glazed expression of the indoctrinated. The mindless babble and programmed speeches of the fanatic. This “thing” has willingly taken leave of control and given it to someone else. And Connors…the “real” Connors…would rather die than allow that to happen. I will not deny, this “thing” is dangerous. What’s happened to Misfit has made that clear. His army may be useless but they do have numbers on their side. I discount them at my peril. But something or someone has taken Connors…sunk their hooks into him…corrupted him…made him wrong. That cannot be allowed to go unchallenged. ---- There’s really no such thing as a grace period in retail. And so it was that, smack dab on November 1st, the retail world went immediately from “Halloween mode” to “Christmas mode”. When it came to Starbucks, it tended to involve words like “pumpkin” and “peppermint” appearing more regularly on their menus. So it was that, on this November afternoon in Los Angeles, this particular Starbucks found one Mister Lance Marshall walking out of it clutching a Venti Peppermint Mocha Twist. It was an indulgence, he knew, one he shouldn’t have allowed himself. But he’d been good for the rest of the week and he could spare it. Besides, he was in desperate need of some caffeine…which was his primary thought as he took his first gulp of coffee. Flavored syrup can hide a multitude of sins. It was less than a minute before the first spasms began to rock Lance Marshall’s body and he found it difficult to breathe. The strength left his legs and he found himself stumbling to the ground, coffee spilling out around him from the now discarded cup. Concerned customers would hurriedly gabble into their cell phones, frantically begging emergency service personnel to get over here quickly as there was someone in trouble and oh my God, I think he’s dying…! It would take the ambulance five minutes to reach the spot where Lance Marshall now lay. Lance Marshall would be dead in three. That’s it. End of story. Game over. … Okay…now this is just getting stupid. ---- What was playing on the television doesn’t really matter. It was loud and colorful and bright, a piece of Japanese animation inexpertly dubbed into English, a glorified thirty minute commercial (actually twenty two minutes due to all the other commercials, ironically enough) for a line of toys. It would not have been Zach Marshall’s first choice for something to watch. But it would have entertained him…or at least diverted him for a few moments. Normally. ---- I liked Mommy and Daddy taking me to work last week. Most people were really nice to me and I had a ton of cool stories to tell everyone when I got back. Mommy and Daddy weren’t even all that mad at me when I went off on my own…which was kinda weird as they’re always telling me not to do that. I think it all started getting weird when I met Miss Amy. I know Mommy and Daddy don’t like her. They don’t know that I know but I do. It’s okay that they don’t like her. You can’t like everybody. Jerry Carsey was being really mean to some of the younger kids so I thumped him. Now he doesn’t like me and I don’t like him because he’s a jerk. So, I can understand if Mommy and Daddy don’t like Miss Amy. I’m sure they have good reasons. And I know that Miss Amy doesn’t like Mommy and Daddy. She didn’t say so but I could tell. Grownups get so weird around me. They start talking all strange and acting all funny, like they think I’m stupid or something. You can tell that they’re lying and I hate that…Daddy always says that it’s not right to lie. But I kinda like Miss Amy. She was nice to me and funny and cool… Can I like someone my Mommy and Daddy don’t like? I’m confused. ---- Lance Marshall made it through the rest of his day without incident: going to the hardware store, buying groceries, hitting the video store, picking his son up from school. Lance and Zach returned home to be greeted with hugs and kisses. Playtime with Zach soon gave way to dinner: salmon for the grownups, chicken nuggets (his favorite) for Zach. Zach would then go on to face two of the most difficult choices in his young life to date: chocolate or vanilla (chocolate, of course) and Kung Fu Panda or WALL-E before bed (well, duh!) Two hours later would find Zach tucked safely in bed, another hour would find his parents doing the same. But what does any of that matter, eh? ---- We now return you to your regularly scheduled lives, already in progress.
