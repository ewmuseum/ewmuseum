Date: Various August 22, 2008 'You know, we used to do some pretty dumb stuff when I started.' The last time King Blueberry was up on the roof of his apartment building he’d barricaded himself there to get some quality alone time. In the process he’d engaged in a full-on argument with his on conscience. Verdict is still out on who exactly won. 'We, or more aptly, you, still do some pretty dumb stuff. The mini muffins were a nice touch. I’m sure that they’ll just gloss right over that.' The second voice belonged to Blueberry Dragon, lounging comfortably in an Adirondack chair, his William Shatner Commemorative Towel draped over the side. 'No, I’m not talking about that stuff. I mean, have you taken a look at the bio sheets for anyone on roster lately, or even better in one of those books they sell in the souvenir stands? You know the ones, they have a section on everybody and what their bag is.' 'When I have to, yeah. I mean, I haven’t looked at one recently, but I doubt they change much.' 'So, Edward - BloodAngel, right? If you look at his bio in one of those books, it’s all ‘Drop you on your head’, ‘Drop you on your head again’, ‘No, fucking seriously, I drop you on your head’, ‘Spinebuster’.' 'Yeah? And?' 'Hell, even the stuff I’m doing now is different than what I used to do. Do you remember the ‘Angry Moose Charge’?' Behind his glasses (which were conveniently located outside his mask), Blueberry Dragon was rolling his eyes. 'No, I can’t say I remember that one. Does this mean you’re going to remind me?' 'Why, yes it does! So it started with a whip into the corner, right? And then I’d put my thumbs against my forehead and extend my fingers, kind of making the ‘neener-neener’ face. Then I’d get a running start, jump, and BAM! Right into the other guy. It ruled. Way different from what I’ve been doing since.' Blueberry Dragon could only stare in wild amazement. Unfortunately, King Blueberry wasn’t finished. 'It wasn’t as cool as the Angry Bear Dance, but then again nothing is.' Trent Shylax rules. 'Well, to be quite honest, you were kind of an ass back then. But that was almost 10 years ago, I’d kind of expect you to have grown since then.' He paused, thinking over what he just said. 'Or at least, I’d have hoped you’d have grown by then. I mean, seriously, when can we take the damn masks off?' 'Not yet, kiddo. Not yet.' 'That’s totally unfair! Lloyd took his off. It’s not like the return of ‘Violent Jesus’ isn’t a great big red flag. We might as well be wearing sandwich boards with our names on them!' August 18, 2008 OMG Violent Jesus in SCCW! Plz comment. ---------- He’d left Blueberry Dragon back in the locker room, only bringing Blueberry Mask 14 with him. The logic was that since Jensen had a ringside associate that he needed one, too, but if it came down to a brawl Dragon was useful only for hiding and maybe running away, and even the latter assumed that he wouldn’t trip on anything. Just as King Blueberry has predicted, things did in fact start to trend in that direction. Sameer, Jensen’s associate, had provided a constant distraction, trying to divert the attention of the referee so that Jensen could reach into the Big Bag O’ Heel and pull out a random cheat. Sameer had once again jumped up on the ring apron, however Blueberry Mask 14 was close enough to do something about it. He pulled Sameer off the ring apron, beginning a brief struggle between the two. During the struggle Sameer had managed to get a solid hold on the mask of Blueberry Mask, a mask whose chinstrap wasn’t able to properly close due to the presence of a bushy beard. 'This is unbelievable,' as always, David Gibson was calling the action, 'We have a battle going on in the ring, and now we have one right in front of us.' This is when it happened. Blueberry Mask 14 was now suddenly without mask, and the crowd went into what could only be described as frenzy. Cue Eugene Ware with the call. 'The mask is off! Violent Jesus is Blueberry Mask 14! My God, the Taliban is pissed, and with good reason, ‘cuz if there’s one thing that a terrorist hates it’s Jesus!' During the distraction Jensen had gone for the finish, but the King had ducked. Though shorter and smaller in stature than Jensen the King was able to get him off his feet. Jack LaLanne Driver. Ballgame. August 15, 2008 9:17am Once upon a time there was a man named Sebastian Parker. He was chubby, and a doctor of psychiatry, and rode a bus with 5 sociopaths to a wrestling event. It was awesome. Hooray! That was six years ago. Today he’s still chubby, still a doctor of psychiatry, and it’s been quite some time since he’s spoken to those particular men, or rode a bus. That particular bus actually had to be decommissioned and taken off the road. Things are good, but considerably less awesome. Boo. He had relocated his practice to a suburb of Raleigh, North Carolina. Every summer he would hire an intern or two to help out with keeping things in order, and give a few students the chance for some experience in the field. One of these interns, Melissa Burton, was in the process of sorting through some patient files when Dr. Parker came into the office. Never the most graceful man, he tripped over a network cable and stumbled into Melissa, causing a chain reaction that ended with some fumbling, an embarrassed doctor, and a single file on the floor. 'I’m so sorry about that, Melissa. Sometimes it’s like my feet don’t want to work.' Melissa smiled. In the two and a half months since she’d started the job she had experienced more than her fair share of 'Parkeresque' blunders. 'Not a problem, doctor Parker. I’ll get that file. I don’t think anything got out of order.' As Melissa brought the file from the floor back towards the stack she was carrying, something on the folder caught Sebastian’s eye. 'Can I see that file?' he asked, craning his neck to read the name on the label. Melissa handed over the file. 'Is there something wrong, doctor?' 'No, nothing at all.' He smiled as he thumbed through the pages, pausing to look at a photo that was included in the file. He said nothing else as he carried the file into his office, nodding to Melissa before closing the door behind him, leaving him to his file and her to her work. Original notes to appear in parentheses. (Begin video.) PATIENT: The little red light is blinking, does that mean we’re recording this? PARKER: Yes, (REMOVED). We’re recording this. I record all of my sessions. When I’m working with someone I like to have footage to go back to so that I don’t miss anything: facial expressions, body language, things like that. PATIENT: Are they going to make you show this to them? (Patient emphasis on 'they'. Apparent concern over perception of well being by others.) PARKER: No, (REMOVED), I’m not showing this to anyone. Even if I wanted to, there are laws that prevent me from discussing anything we talk about here with anyone. (Silence.) PATIENT: Okay then. PARKER: Great. So, I leave it up to you. Where would you like to begin? PATIENT: In the waiting room. PARKER: I’m not sure I follow you. PATIENT: It started in a waiting room. It always starts in a waiting room, except this one was different. PARKER: What was different about it, (REMOVED)? PATIENT: It was the first one. PARKER: Tell me a little about it. PATIENT: Well, it was about 3 years ago, maybe a few months more. I hadn’t been out of training for very long, but Darren (Stracker, 'The Prophet') and his dad said I was ready to go. PARKER: Was this...? PATIENT: Rich Stracker. He ran the school that I was training in. Darren was his son, about 10 years older than me. He was working for a promotion called Global Championship Wrestling - one of about 6 billion companies with that name, and certainly not one that you’d heard of. I think it had that name for about 4 minutes before they changed it to Cyber Championship Wrestling. It was a strange place. The guys in it had all defected from a company where things got weird. PARKER: Tell me a bit about that, about the weird. (Patient laughs.) PATIENT: Apparently they had some dude who would float around on a cloud and shoot lasers from his eyes. Thought he was God, or something. You should totally find that dude. I bet you’d get nothing but gold out of him. PARKER: That certainly is (pause) strange. PATIENT: Yeah. Kind of wish I’d thought of it, though. So yeah, so I’m sitting in the waiting room, about to talk to the vice president of this group, and two massive guys come walking out of his office: Cameron Smart, called himself 'The Devastator', and some Michael Clark Duncan-lookin’ guy named Roderick Frost. He was 'The Iceman'. PARKER: Then what happened? PATIENT: Smart walks over to me and he tells me that the boss, Matt Krawczyk - and that’s weird on it’s own. That’s my landlord’s name. Turns out that they’re related. Matt is like the nephew or the cousin or something of this old guy who gets my money every month; my landlord and my boss, both in the same family. I’m not in the company 10 minutes and already things are bizarre. 'My Life As Run By Polish People: The (REMOVED) Story.' (He laughs again.) PATIENT: As messed-up as it seems, it only gets more nuts from there. PARKER: I can’t wait to hear about it.
