The King of New York

25 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 25 August A summer’s day in the city is generally not the most comfortable surroundings. Humidity grabs your throat and chokes you, car fumes fly through your mouth and poison you and melting chewing gum sticks to your feet as you trudge through the streets taking advantage of any shade you get so the sun cannot squeeze that lasts drops of sweat out of you. But not today. Today was perfect. Dualta did not see the sun too often anymore. His swarthy skin was fading, being replaced by a dry withering complexion, slowly diminishing his looks. He usually fought by night and slept by day in a futile attempt to pay what needed to be paid. Perhaps now some life could be restored to this troubled face. He had a new sleeping pattern, a new reason for getting up in the morning, and he liked it. He had been walking for over an hour. All the way to Maspeth, the community in which he and his family had been vanquished from. It had been a while since he had shown his face around there, but he was feeling nostalgic. He walked through the park where he and his cousin met the first girls they had ever kissed. It was as if a dam had been knocked down in his head as memories flooded through it. Though one stood out more than others. He was fifteen years old. It was around nine o’clock in the evening as dark began to descend upon the same very park. Dualta lay with his back perched against the catch net of the baseball field. He was carefree, life was flawless. He had everything he wanted. Though one problem often existed, he was still a Sheridan, and that often equalled retribution. Dualta squinted his eyes as the empty park began to fill up with all too familiar faces. From every angle they seemed to come, walking towards him with their eyes unblinking. He knew what was coming. The Iti’s were not too fond of the Irish around these parts, and when crime families were added to the mixture, it as easy to assess the general outcome. They were not all Bianchi’s that strided towards him, but they were all Iti’s and all determined to exact any possible punishment on a Sheridan. Dualta had never been afraid to fight, but here he began to calculate the odds and he had determined that, given recent events of the time, he would probably not be alive five minutes from then. So he did the only thing he could, the only thing that would allow him to die with honour. Powerful thoughts for such a young mind. He got up and a he ran, not away from the twenty odd assembling Italians, but straight for them. And with every ounce of his being, as he stood but feet away from the first antagonist, he leaped into the air and threw his right fist into the unguarded face of the man who had not time to react. The next part could be tricky but he was equipped for it. When Dualta fought he was not like his father, he was a problem solver, he worked out exactly how to beat his opponents, and in this particular circumstance he concluded that as his main physical asset was his speed he was going to run straight for his aggressors and get as many fucking hits in as he possibly could because there was no way he was going to let any less be thought of a Sheridan. And he did just that, he punched and he kicked and he elbowed until he could do no more. He was wrestled to the ground and pinned there by three boys barely older than himself, he recognised them. It was strange how you could pass these people in school and nothing would be said, but because of who you are they would kill you in a heartbeat if told to. What a strange world he had grown up in. The rest of the men stood over Dualta, staring at him. A gap formed in the crowd as a man walked through straight over Dualta’s struggling body. It was Massimo Bianchi one of the most notorious criminals in Queen’s, his Uncle was head of the Bianchi crime family, and Massimo did his dirty work. He had killed many times before but never been convicted because of the powerful support he had. As soon as Dualta caught a glimpse of this man he knew what was about to happen. "I’m not gonna keep ya long here Dualta, just gotta little message for your father." said Massimo. His analogy was palpable, and Dualta was sure it was one he used on the majority of his victims. Expressionism would not be one of Massimo’s more creative qualities, due to his supreme lack of intelligence. Massimo extracted a knife from the inside pocket of his knee length coat. Dualta did not flinch nor did he say a word. He simply stared Massimo dead in the eye refusing to show any weakness, he could not give them the satisfaction. He was not at peace with his imminent death by any means, in fact he was petrified. He was fifteen years old, he did not deserve this, but he knew it was going to happen. But it didn’t. Dualta did not remember what had been said or heard to make all the Italians turn around. What he did remember was what they were turning round to. Dualta’s five uncles, six of his cousins, and his father, out in front. Donnacha Sheridan was not to be ignored when he stood before you; this was perhaps the most powerful man in the city, the king of New York. What happened next was oblivious to Dualta for the only words that came out of his father’s mouth were; "Go home Dualta." All he knew was that no fists were thrown except that of his fathers and Massimo Bianchi that night, and as he had done in every confrontation he had ever been involved in, Donnacha Sheridan took care of his business. Shortly after that the Bianchi family moved away from New York taking their criminal activities with them. Donnacha Sheridan takes great exception to anyone who uses his son as a message. That was eight years ago now, but it was an incident that had never left Dualta’s thoughts. To this day he did not know how his family knew to come for him, he never asked, but they were there, they were always there. That is why it pained him so much to see what had become of them, what had become of his father. He was no longer a leader but a recluse. He was embarrassed by his downfall and felt responsible for what had become of his family. Dualta swung back and forth gently propelling himself with his feet as he sat in the playground of the park that had played such a huge role in the ambience of his life. He had won his first SCCW match and suddenly he had all the confidence in the world again. As he remembered that night eight years ago he remembered what his family had done for him. Now he would be the leader. He would pull them all from the deprivation they had fallen upon. He would do that in the wrestling ring. And when the big cheques start coming in, that is when the New York underworld will re-crown its true king. It started with Austin King, ironic. The King is dead, long live the King.
