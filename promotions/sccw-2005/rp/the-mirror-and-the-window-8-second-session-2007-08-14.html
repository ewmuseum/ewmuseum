The Mirror and The Window 8: Second Session

14 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/14/07 8.1 the mirror: reverse add It’s not uncommon in the past few weeks for you to look around and suddenly wonder how you got where you are. You have never been the type of guy whose mind wanders from anything important to him. It’s kind of like reverse ADD. Once you were focused on something, you didn’t abandon it for whatever shiny thing came your way. This has done worlds for not only your wrestling career but also for your relationships. So it’s kind of strange to suddenly not be completely focused on your girlfriend, or on wrestling for that matter. It’s also strange that the object of your newly wandering focus is an older woman who apparently is interested in you. The strangest thing, though, is that you kind of enjoy it. 8.2 the window: hero "You saved someone who hates you. I’d have to say that if you weren’t sure about your heroic nature before, you probably should be now." Sarah Riley is dressed more appropriately than she was during the last session: she has on a black strapless dress, hanging below the knees. As she speaks, she is putting on earrings. Jonathan Rhine stares at her blankly. "Well, I don’t know. I mean, he wanted me to prove myself to him. He wanted me to show that I’m a worthy person." Sarah nods. "But did you only save him so he would face you at the pay-per-view?" Jonathan thinks about this for a second. "No." Only a second. "I helped him because The Affliction need to be stopped. They need to be taught that it’s not okay to run roughshod over everyone just because we work in a federation with a lax rule system. They need to know that eventually we’re going to stop rolling over for them." "I see." Sarah Riley smiles as she sits down across the room from him. "No, that doesn’t make you a hero at all," she says, mockingly rolling her eyes. Slightly uncomfortable at being called a hero and slightly more uncomfortable that his psychologist is being informal with him, he shifts in his seat. "Look, I don’t like being called a hero. I know that it looks like I do a lot of good things, and maybe to the average wrestling fan I represent something like a hero." He sighs. "But I’m not one. I have the same problems as everyone else. Maybe I look good in comparison to scumbags like Lane Stevens and Clinton Sage, but I’m not a good guy, really." Sarah Riley leans back, looking intrigued. "Why do you not think you’re a good guy, Jon?" Jonathan shrugs. "Well…I’m here with you. Instead of being with my girlfriend, I mean." Sarah nods; she knew this was coming. "We’re not doing anything bad. Hell, we could be and you won’t. You should be proud of yourself." "Yeah, I know," he sighs. 8.3 the mirror: proud But you’re not. It’s easy to cover this up with a simple excuse. It’s easy for you to say that you aren’t doing anything wrong because you haven’t kissed her, held her…barely touched her besides when you shook her hand upon your first meeting. Looking at it from that perspective, yes, you haven’t done anything wrong. But in your mind you have. Because you’ve thought about it. Tons of times. Every time you look at Sarah Riley, you think about taking her up on the offers she has been making since you got there. You think about her long brown hair being splayed out on her silk sheets. Her black dress tossed uncaringly on the floor. You’ve made a movie out of it in your head. You never do it, though. You always talk yourself out of it. It takes a lot of nerve, and maybe she’s right: maybe you should be proud of yourself. But you’re not. Because you realize the only reason you aren’t doing anything with this woman is because if you were caught, you wouldn’t know what to do next. It isn’t devotion to your girlfriend, or repulsion to cheating…it’s uncertainty. And that’s something you just can’t be proud of. 8.4 the window: different "I don’t know," Jonathan says, wiping his hand with his face to clear the sweat that’s formed. "You asked if I was a different person last time. And I wasn’t really sure of how to answer. Well, I’ve given it a lot of thought, and I think that the answer is yes." Sarah nods. "Tell me why." "Well, because of the way I feel about myself, pretty much. Man, when the lights go off and my music hits, and those fans start to cheer, I can’t help but feel good about myself. I mean, what other person in their job gets thousands of people on their feet simply by their appearance?" Jonathan smiles, losing himself in the daydream of it. "So, yeah, as a wrestler I feel like this good guy who gets a bunch of support." "And you don’t feel like that when you’re not wrestling." "No, not really," he sighs. "I mean, I’ve been warned that everyone was going to start changing when I got signed to FUSE, and so far everything has. My family has turned on me, my friends feel that I’ve neglected them, and now my girlfriend is feeling isolated because she thinks living a normal life with her won’t make me happy." Sarah stands up and begins checking herself in the mirror. "And will it?" "Huh?" Jonathan looks up at her, confused. She turns around to face him, her face looking somewhat worn down with fatigue. "Will living a normal life with her make you happy? Go there. Let’s talk about how you feel leaving one life for the other. It’s the day before an event, and you’re packing your in-ring gear. How do you feel?" Jonathan smiles. "Excited. Ready to take on the world. Confident." Sarah matches the smile and nods. "And when you’re coming back here to be with your girlfriend?" And that smile is erased. "Well, uh…" he looks down, ashamed. "Kind of disappointed." "Disappointed. Why?" "Because fighting is something I love to do. Being a normal guy who does normal household chores just…well…" Sarah finishes the sentence for him. "Isn’t." 8.5 the mirror: normal That was one of those things that you never realized until it came out of your mouth. You just really don’t want to be normal. It kind of scares you, because you’re afraid that admitting that somehow makes you egotistical, wanting to be better than everybody else. But that doesn’t change the fact that being a wrestler is all you want to be, and the status elevation it gives you is definitely part of the appeal. You like being recognized as a great wrestler, as a great man. Does that make you narcissistic? Possibly. But it also makes sense. You’ve never been the kind of guy who made waves or tried to make yourself above anyone else during your entire life. And while that is a good way to live, it’s left you feeling like you needed some attention. And going out there every week to tons of cheers gives it to you. But when you get something that big one night a week, the other six days are generally spent looking quite small in comparison. Maybe you’re not such a good person after all. 8.6 the window: boundaries "Don’t feel bad," Sarah immediately says, going to her closet and opening the door. "It’s completely natural to want that sort of validation. And when you get it in one place and not the other, it’s easy to feel negative about it. But you have to remember something." Slipping on a pair of heels, she looks in the mirror once more before turning around to smile at her new client. "The differences between professional you and personal you aren’t big enough differences for you to alienate someone. Those are your issues, not hers. The problems that you have with dealing with normality and mediocrity…that’s all on your side of it. I understand that they affect you." She walks over to Jonathan and leans down that she is staring into his eyes, her hands on his knees. "But she doesn’t deserve to be affected by them. She loves you, and cares about you, but she needs to learn that she can’t take on your problems as her own…and you need to learn not to give them to her. The problem with couples living together is that they each believe that they are the same person, and they don’t have any boundaries. You need to realize these boundaries and respect them," she concludes, her hands going further up Jonathan’s thigh. "Maybe you should heed your own advice," he says shyly, trying to back up. Sarah laughs and leads him to his feet, where he finds out the heels she is wearing have made her his height. Intimidated, he starts to back up. "That’s fine," she says, her smile somewhat faltering. "You’re really interesting, you know. You’ve been here for a few weeks, and I’m the one making passes. That’s not really the way it works around here." Jonathan smiles politely. "Sorry. I just think that I should be coming here to fix my relationship, not ruin it." As he scrambles for a recovery, he finally gives her a full look as she is completely dressed and blinks a few times in surprise. "You’re very…uh…wow. Though." She laughs again. "I’m very wow? Why thank you. That’s good a compliment as any." Looking at her watch, she claps her hands together. "Well, it’s about time for my date." "You never told me, who are you going on a date with?" Reaching for her purse, Sarah makes a face that Jonathan interprets as one less than happy. "Just some wrestler from Global, wants an armpiece for a dinner so people don’t think he’s gay." Jonathan nods for a second, then asks, "Does it ever get to you? You know, just being there for tough guys all the time? Bending to what we want, giving us counseling or whatever else we need?" Sarah shrugs. "Not really. This is my job, and I make good money from it." "But you’ve got to have your own feelings, right? You’ve got to have your own wants and needs that you aren’t getting." Jonathan notices she is walking towards the door, so he follows her and grabs his coat. As they get towards the door, he sees the smallest hint of a tear at Sarah’s eye, but it’s gone before he can notice. She shrugs again and simply says, "Why do you think I just made a move on you?" Before he can react, she opens the door. "See you next time, Jon." 8.7 the mirror: two girls It’s an interesting thing, caring for two girls at the same time. On the one hand, you have Katie Malick, a small town girl who is modest and simply good, a wonderful girl who you are lucky to live with. She might not support your profession, but she is your girlfriend, your friend, and your confidant. She’s easily the girl you want to be with forever. But on the other hand is Sarah. Which is ironically the hand you use when you think about her. She’s much more open than Katie, but still has a soft side to her that she’s not exposing. She supports your profession, and she makes you feel justified for feeling the way you do about your life. But she’s not your girlfriend. She’s not the girl you live with. And as much as you want her to be, she can’t be your lover. It’s interesting because at face value caring for a girl other than your girlfriend is wrong. However, seeing her feels right. And as long as you don’t act on the feelings you’re starting to get, you’re pretty sure that this is one balancing act that you can survive. You just hope that she never grabs your thigh again, though, because if she does then there are no guarantees you’ll stay on the rope.
