The Fifth Question

7 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/7 Outside a Perkins, just past the Las Vegas city limits. Taking a long, slow drag from the first cigarette that he'd smoked in ten years, Phillip Kennedy took a look at the world around him, or at least, the pathetic stretch of stretch of it that he currently inhabited. Huddled up under the overhanging roof of a 24-hour Perkins on a stretch of highway outside Las Vegas as the rain poured down, Kennedy exhaled, and watched the smoke curl out into the torrent. So often in his life as a professional, he wondered how things turned out the way they had. For once, though, and for perhaps the first time in his year plus tenure in SCCW, Phillip was absolutely sure how he'd ended up here, fresh off of throwing down some pancakes mere hours after assaulting a man who had done nothing but stand up for him. To understand that story, though, you have to understand this one. ------------------- The Wet Whistle Bar & Grill Las Vegas, NV 'OOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOHHHHH!!' *crash!* The two sounds in question came one after another in the cramped sports bar that just so happened to have one of its TVs tuned to Cinemax. Sound #1 was the general reaction of the bar to the fact that Amy Campbell and Alexandra Pierce, together the most dominant force in the promotion, were seemingly hooking up. Sound #2 was the result of a glass mug full of Sam Adams shattering into a thousand pieces on the bar's floor. It wasn't even audible over the catcalls of about forty-five horny, single men who now had the evening's spank material firmly uploaded into their databanks. Phillip Kennedy's right hand opened and closed a few times, before realizing that he'd lost his drink. The Las Vegas Lariat didn't especially care that Darcy Crisis was on his way to finding out that his wife was fucking perhaps the most evil woman on the planet. His initial reaction was astonishment. Open-mouthed, fish out of water, omigod shock. But then, the pieces started to fall into place. One piece in particular was hurtling towards him with all the finality of a meteor strike. It was the answer to a question. THE question. The one that he had asked Alexandra Pierce time and time again, and never had answered. 'Why Amy?' Now he knew, and it was more than he could take to find it out in such an outrageous way. The Big Stack rose to his feet, and began to move. His first instinct was to go get a replacement beer, but that instinct was washed away by the disgust and anger that coursed through his veins. No, there would be no more alcohol tonight. His car was parked outside. If he got to it, he could drive back home and try to do...something that he wasn't sure of, yet. Kennedy was two steps outside the sports bar when he made the snap decision that getting in his car would be a bad idea. No, not just a bad idea. A disastrous idea. 'Fuckin' Hell,' he snarled, whirling around and driving his forearm into the brick wall of the bar hard enough to make him growl in pain. Just drunk enough that he didn't think he'd be able to drive well, at least not coupled with his anger, and just sober enough to be able to know that fact. His hand was in his pocket to find his cell phone, the intention being to call a cab to get him home. What prevented this from happening, though, was the arrival of an inebriated man behind the Big Stack, who placed his hand on Phillip's shoulder. 'Hey, yer that Kennedy guy from TV!' the voice slurred. Sighing, Phil turned to face his new visitor. Sizing the other man up, Phillip figured he had a few inches and twenty pounds on the guy. Not that such always mattered, but... 'Yeah, I am,' Phil replied. 'What's it to you?' 'You get yer ass kicked by girls! Yer a disgrace to this town!' On most other nights, Kennedy would have walked away. Career as a pro wrestler aside, he wasn't much for fights. The real Phillip Kennedy was a lot less confrontational than the man who showed up on Cinemax every week. Except in cases when he was mad, tipsy, or some combination of both. 'Look, asshole,' Kennedy retorted, 'get the fuck off my case before you get hurt. I'm having a shitty enough night without you being a douchebag.' 'Big words froma guy who cain't even beat up a couple dumb redheaded bitches, ain't they?' That would be what we call 'pushing Phillip Kennedy's buttons.' The bar drunk was smart enough to figure out that a blow was coming, and he raised an arm to block. The only problem with this reasonable plan was that, in the process, he'd forgotten one thing that was about to be very important to him. Phillip Kennedy was a southpaw. The blow Kennedy threw was a looping left hook that caught his opponent on the chin, dropping the drunk to a heap. Phillip glared down at him, loading up another left, when a single word came echoing through the air that made him swear under his breath. 'POLICE!' He might have been tipsy, but Phillip Kennedy was no idiot. To the ground he went, arms spread wide. The next few minutes of Phillip Kennedy's life played out as if they were an out of body experience. Sure, he was a gambler. Sure, he'd done some shitty things in his time, but never had the cops ever gotten involved. His final thought as his handcuffed body was neatly bundled into the back of a squad car was the hope that no one was there to get pictures to send to TMZ or Perez Hilton. ---------------------------------- Clark County Detention Center Las Vegas, NV It was sort of ironic to the mind of Phillip Kennedy that, considering the line of work that his girlfriend was in, he was the first member of Kensidy to have metallic bracelets slapped on him and be thrown in the back of a cop car. Processing had gone about as he'd seen it on TV. He'd been fingerprinted for the first time since he was a Cub Scout and the officers at his local police station had shown the children how it was done. Even though he was in the capable hands of the officers from Clark County, Phillip knew it could have been a lot worse Considering a lot of the shit he'd gotten into, and the company he kept, it was sort of amazing that this was the first time he'd been taken to county jail. Now, as he was being led down some sort of corridor, the reality of his situation was starting to set in. 'You're a lucky fucker,' he heard one of the guards say, as the door to what seemed to be his home for the evening was opened. 'The guy at the bar's not pressing charges. We'll hold you overnight and then you'll be free to go in the morning. Probably some charges for disturbing the peace, but it could have been a Hell of a lot worse.' Kennedy stepped into the holding cell, sitting down on one of the built-in benches that lined the wall. Last he'd checked, it was probably about one in the morning. If he just closed his eyes and got some sleep, he'd be out before he knew it. Even so, the sound of the door closing shut behind him jarred his senses. 'Shit,' he groaned, shaking his head, 'this ain't even a real jail cell...' It made him wonder how Lauren, Kathryn, and Alex dealt with the possible threat of this--no, far worse than this. They were confessed murderers -- well, confessed to him, anyway. If any of them got caught, there were looking at life in prison, maybe the chair or the needle depending where they were tried. Judging by the fact that they had not only not been caught, but were pro wrestling celebrities, it seemed that they were all very good at evading capture and detection. With a sigh, Kennedy stood. No way in Hell he was getting to sleep now, not on a hard-ass plastic bench with his mind going five miles a minute. 'I still don't believe any of this,' he groaned, pacing from one wall to the other of his new surroundings. 'I said all that shit to Amy at that pay-per-view to piss her off, and it's actually true?' Amy and Alexandra were an item. 'Were' might have been operative, considering that he'd caught Darcy Crisis arriving on the scene out of the corner of his eye as he'd made his way out of that bar with bad intentions. He really wanted to turn his brain off for the night and sleep, but that wasn't looking likely or possible. Besides, along with being one of the toughest places in wrestling to compete in, night in and night out, SCCW required its athletes to be at the top of their mental game at all times to keep up with the constant twists and turns. It had been particularly difficult for Phil, considering he'd been out with a concussion. Watching the show on TV was helpful (and something that, thanks to Alexandra's machinations, was rapidly becoming required), but being in the arena allowed him to talk to techs and other employees to glean any information he could. 'Wish I had a pen and paper or something. Makes it much easier when I can write this shit down.' Sin City's complicated nature was perhaps most obvious when one considered that a once-professional poker player, who made his living off of reading people and juggling hundreds of bits of information, needed to write down the comings and goings in the company. Of course, that was largely because in poker, you had bluffs, semi-bluffs, and non-bluffs. Here, you had people bluffing that they were bluffing that they'd become allies with someone else who was bluffing about something entirely different. 'You want a pen and paper,' a voice chimed in, and Kennedy's eyes whirled to the doorway to see a guard standing there. Phillip simply nodded, wondering exactly where this was headed. The guard walked away for a moment, coming back with a spiral notebook and a pen. 'Sign an autograph for my kid and they're yours. It's not department policy, but you're no danger. If it wasn't for protocol I'd have let you out tonight.' The Big Stack smiled. 'Sure. Who should I make it out to?' 'Jeremy,' the officer replied, stepping over to the door, unlocking it, and handing the pen and paper inside. Kennedy took them, and flipped to the first page of the notebook. Scratching his chin for a moment, he then scribbled 'To Jeremy, Ante Up!', followed by his signature, with 'The Las Vegas Lariat' written beneath it. Carefully tearing the page out of the notebook, Kennedy handed it back to the officer. 'Will that do?' 'Absolutely. Thanks!' 'No,' Kennedy insisted, 'thank you. Now I won't go out of my mind.' Phillip returned to the plastic bench with his new bounty, but he noted as he sat that the guard was still standing there, door left open. 'Whatcha gonna write,' came the guard's question, just as Kennedy was about to put pen to paper. 'Your kid watches the show, right? I'm sure he'll tell you all about it when you get home.' 'I watch the show, too,' the officer corrected. 'At least, when I'm not stuck working graveyard shift. We don't even have cable here, let alone Cinemax. What happened tonight?' 'Pisses me off to even think about it, let alone say it,' Kennedy griped, 'but what the Hell? Found out tonight that Amy and Desade are shacked up.' 'No shit, seriously?' was the guard's reaction, and Kennedy nodded. 'That's how I ended up here. Sorta lost control for a little bit after finding out. All that time spent in the Hand being second-best and not knowing why...and I find out its because Amy's the star student screwing the teacher.' 'Damn. Sounds like I missed a Hell of an episode. Hope Jer remembered to tape it.' 'Even if he didn't,' Kennedy said, 'they replay it a couple times a week. We get better ratings than the softcore porn.' Whether that was a testament to SCCW's quality, or simply America's love of violence and odd public aversion to sex, Phillip wasn't quite sure. 'I'll keep that in mind then.' The officer was quiet for a few moments, before gesturing to the notebook. 'So, question still stands, whatcha writing?' 'Everything that this changes,' was Phillip's immediate reply. 'May take me until you let me out of here to figure it all out, but I'll do the best I can.' The sounds of screaming could be heard in the distance, prompting a long, heavy sigh from the guard. 'Looks like I'm gonna have to leave you to that, Phil,' he said. 'Sounds like we've got another one coming in for processing, and he sounds belligerant. Probably gonna need all hands on deck.' 'Best of luck with that,' Phillip said, and he meant it. It was impossible to think with the screaming in the background. 'Take care, and thanks again.' 'Don't mention it,' the guard shrugged, standing up to begin headed over to help out his comrades. 'Lot of the guys on the force really like the show.' Before leaving, the guard stepped forward, carefully closing and locking the door to the holding cell. 'Keep on keeping on. I'll be back for you in a few hours.' Cracking his knuckles, the guard left, disappearing behind a door, presumably on his way to go help out his fellow officers. Once again alone and locked in his cell, Phillip let the pen dangle from his fingertips. This whole thing reminded him of something that had happened a while back. Months ago, Kathryn Shaw had told him that, in her travels, she'd come to the conclusion that there were a surprising amount of police officers who were big pro wrestling fans. When asked why, she'd simply shrugged. At the time, he'd thought it was because, well, she'd probably slept with enough police officers to curry a good opinion of the sport through her. Now, though, he had a new theory. Being a police officer, Phillip imagined, was very frustrating. Officers were always taking in suspects who had often done horrific things, unable to do any more than detain them. Sometimes they were convicted. Other times they were not. In pro wrestling, punishment for crimes were meted out with fists and feet and suplexes. Well, and chairs, tables, ladders and the like. Police brutality was banned. Wrestler brutality was encouraged. His pen was just about to hit paper again when the low but still unmistakable sound of fist on face sounded, followed by a thud that Kennedy presumed was a body hitting the floor. Phillip couldn't help but chuckle. Legal or not, maybe policework was more like pro wrestling than he'd initially thought. And with that distraction out of the way, Kennedy began to write. By the time his friend the policeman returned at 8:00 AM, the first ten pages of the notebook were filled with scribbled thoughts. Once the door was opened and Kennedy was released, his first move was to regain his personal effects. When it came to making sense of things, there was only one person in his life that he knew could help with the task. That was why David Walter Smith had been known in the ring as Logic. --------------------------------- The Offices of David Walter Smith San Francisco, CA David Walter Smith considered himself a reasonably good observer of Phillip Kennedy by now. Even if he hadn't been, though, it was pretty clear that the man many had once pegged to be the next big thing in Sin City was pissed. 'So,' his charge concluded, quietly, 'that's why I ended up in a jail cell last night. Did a lot of thinking and writing in there, and I'd like for you to listen to what I've come up with and make sure that I'm not overreacting.' He plopped the spiral notebook down onto Logic's desk, and opened it to page one. Privately, Logic was impressed Kennedy had gotten that far. It was, after all, one of the reasons that neither he, nor Katsidy, nor Savant had ever told Phillip about the relationship to begin with; the fear that Kennedy would snap and try to take down as many people involved as possible. 'Very well, Mr. Kennedy. I am all ears.' Phil rose from the comfortable chair across the desk from his agent, and tapped his fingers on the notebook. 'Lauren and Kathryn knew. The entire time. They were Alex's right hand women before they broke off from her. There is no way in Hell that was Alex and Amy's first kiss.' Rare though it was for a 6'9' man to look sheepish, David nodded. 'The two of them did know, yes. Though, the fact that I'm telling you this admittedly reveals something else.' It was David's turn to rise from his chair. Phillip had just spent a night in jail due to being unable to control his emotions. The possibility for a fight was always there, bubbling beneath the surface, no matter how calm Kennedy was being at the moment. 'Yeah,' Kennedy snorted. 'I'll say it does. You're my agent, Dave. You've been a damned good one. So I'm going to give you til the count of five to explain why you never told me something that'd be a damn big impact on my career, and if it's a good explanation, I won't fire you. One...' 'There is no need to continue, Phillip.' Kennedy winced. God, he hated being called Phillip, probably because of the condescending tone of voice that Alexandra and sometimes David always said the name in. Phil's eyes were narrowed slits as he waited for Smith to go on. There were times when lying was necessary, the once Logic knew, but this was one time where he'd be going with the God's honest truth. The beans had been spilled. David was hoping that Phil would be mature enough to handle the entire truth. 'Had you known earlier, during the run-up to the finals of the No Limit Tournament, the three of us feared you would do something rash. That you would go after Amy, or worse, Alex. You were already set to do battle with Amy for professional reasons. Alexandra Pierce stayed her hand against you because of that fact. As she herself told you, contending with her on personal levels is how a great many people have wound up killed or crippled.' Kennedy's wince indicated that he understood. He didn't like it, not one little bit, but he understood. 'You know,' Phil spat, 'if I'd have known about this, I wouldn't have left the Hand when I did. I would have played this whole thing differently.' 'Really?' David was a bit astonished by that fact. For his part, Kennedy simply sighed at his agent. 'I'm not as smart as you or Alex, David, but I'm not an idiot, in spite of what a lot of people in Sin City seem to think. I wanted to know why the Hell Alexandra kept backing Amy in the power struggles when I was winning match after match on my own merits, and she needed the entire Hand to run in. If I'd have known this, it would have been obvious.' 'And you wouldn't have turned on them anyway out of spite,' Logic queried, sounding more than a little bit skeptical. 'Maybe I would have, maybe not. This scenario wouldn't have been an indictment of my abilities, it would have been Alex putting her fuck buddy first. That would have sucked to hear, but in a weird way, it would have made me feel better.' David titled his head a bit, but he sort of understood. Phillip was a proud man -- the Hand as a whole was made up of prideful, egotistical people. Dealing with a slight was easier when the blame for being second best was taken off of yourself and placed elsewhere. 'At any rate,' Phillip continued, 'your answer's good enough. I believe you had my best intent in mind. Katsidy and Savant, on the other hand...' 'I have hesitated to say as such because of your romantic involvement with Kathryn Shaw,' David said, 'but I firmly believe that the two step-sisters have their own agenda. What it is, I do not know, but I would be extremely cautious.' Phillip was about to open his mouth and concur, but something changed his mind. The word 'cautious.' Everything in his life for the past year had been 'cautious.' When he was in the hand, he'd been told not to go all out. When with Kathryn and Lauren, all they'd ever told him was to be careful. 'Fuck being cautious,' he growled, instead. 'I'm sick and fuckin' tired of being cautious all the time. What's it gotten me? A lost shot at the Universal Championship and a Grade-Two concussion. If that's what being cautious means, I'm done being cautious.' 'Phillip,' David interjected, trying to reason with his increasingly emotional client, 'these are dangerous peop--' 'You think Lance Marshall doesn't know that? You think Jonathan Rhine doesn't know that? Fuck no. They both know it and they still go balls to the wall 100% every Goddamned night for what they believe in. This place IS dangerous, Dave. So why the Hell am I only playing good hands against the best players in the world?' Smith sighed. One of the ways to tell when Phillip was truly annoyed was when he began to break out the poker analogies. 'If I played poker like I've handled Sin City,' Kennedy proclaimed, 'I'd have been broke and panhandling for change on the Strip three years ago. I'm done letting the Dead Man's Hand have the monopoly on being dangerous around here. There's going to be a cost for fucking with Phillip Kennedy from here on out, and I'm going to make sure it's one people don't want to pay.' David looked into his client's eyes. Having been a wrestler for a few years, Logic had learned to tell a lot from the expression on another man's face. He could tell whether an opponent was confident or not, injured or not, ready for battle or not. The look in Kennedy's eyes indicated more than anger. More than confidence. It was a mixture of emotions that he had never quite seen, a cocktail of shock, anger, arrogance, annoyance, and frustration. 'I've got a few phone calls to make,' Phillip said, already turning to leave. 'Thanks for the talk, David. I know it doesn't seem like it right now, but you've been a big help.' David simply watched Kennedy leave, the conviction of the Las Vegas Lariat's voice ringing in his ears. With no client to speak to, the agent simply began to riffle through the impressive stack of paperwork that sat on top of his desk. If you asked him, caution was a good thing when dealing with people like Alexandra Pierce and Lauren Fox. At the same time, though, he could understand Phillip's decision. Being 6'9', 300 lbs, agile, and a true student of the game had never really brought Logic the success in pro wrestling that he had always felt he deserved. Retirement, and the benefit of hindsight, left David wondering if his overly analytical approach to life had cost him a career as one of the best in the business. If nothing else, it had gained him a new career, living vicariously through his best-known client. ------------------------------------- SCCW Equilibrium Backstage following Desade vs. Jonathan Rhine Even after disappearing behind the curtain, Phillip Kennedy could still hear the boos of the SCCW faithful. It had been bad enough that Kathryn Shaw had turned on Jonathan Rhine, but Phillip Kennedy's betrayal followed immediately on its footsteps, a one-two punch for a man who deserved a Hell of a lot better than he'd gotten in his career. Arrogant as he'd sounded in front of the crowd, away from the prying eyes of the fans, Kennedy was now less sure of his decision. Even if you asked Kennedy if Rhine deserved what had just happened, he would be the first to give an unequivocal 'no.' But that, to Kennedy's mind, wasn't the question. What he'd done was wrong morally, but morals weren't worth a damn in the crucible of SCCW. Results were. Phillip didn't have any intentions to stay around and watch Amy Campbell face Wyatt Connors, as much as he was rooting for the False Prophet to take the Red Raver's championship. For the first time in his career, he had done something that he truly thought was wrong, and simply ignored that feeling. Even when in the Dead Man's Hand, he hadn't done anything that he felt truly bad about. Retiring Sean Sterling had been a personal war between two men who didn't like each other. Attacking PRIME had been about getting respect for himself, his group, and his company. Amy Campbell, as far as he was concerned, deserved far worse than he'd been able to dish out. He had promised on Temptation to answer the question 'why Jonathan Rhine?' At the end of the day, the answer to that question was the exact same as the answer to the question 'why leave the DMH?' In the Dead Man's Hand, he was second-best to Amy Campbell. In the minds of the SCCW fans, he was second best to Jonathan Rhine. The cheers of the crowd for Rhine supported that fact. Rhine had been unfairly exiled by the Hand, and now that he had returned, to take up HIS fight, Phillip Kennedy was...unnecessary. Everything the Hand had done to him was forgotten in the wake of the return of the former Universal Champion. Phillip Kennedy had been wronged, and he was setting about righting that wrong. The only way for him to so happened to be the assault and eventual destruction of the innocent man championed by those who had done the Stack wrong. And really, he was okay with that. Morality was a hindrance in SCCW, if not an out-and-out liability. As much as it might have made him feel better to believe that, lurking deep down, he had to admit that it wasn't purely professional. He didn't *like* that Rhine had been the one to save Katsidy from Lane Stevens, not him. He didn't LIKE that Rhine was the number one seed in GTT, a tournament that he wasn't entering due to advice from his doctors to not compete TOO much upon his return. In short, he didn't like that he was being demoted from the front lines. It didn't take too long for him to get out one of the back entrances, especially considering he was powerwalking about as fast as he ever had. His rental car was parked as close as humanly possible to the arena's exit, so he'd be able to get in his car and get on the highway as soon as possible. The one thing he hadn't taken into account with that plan was the fact that he'd have to hike through the whole parking lot, past various groups of tailgaters watching the show on TV with their portable satellite dishes. 'Aww, hell.' Predictably, the booing began again upon being sighted, as did a small shower of empty beer cans. Usually, when greeted with such abuse, he turned up the theater a bit, flipping birds left and right, but tonight was different. Tonight, he just wanted to get the Hell out of there. He was prepared to deal with the consequences of his actions, but if he could wait until tomorrow to start, that would be quite lovely. Kennedy disappeared behind a row of parked cars, skulking in shadowy areas not illuminated by the big street lights sprinkled throughout the parking lot. His keys were out of his pocket and in his hand in an instant, unlocking the driver's side door and slipping into his car as quickly as possible. Moments later, Kennedy was off, fleeing the scene of the crime. On his passenger's seat rested the only companion he needed for the time being, his spiral notebook. Over the days leading up to Equalibrium, more pages had been filled with notes. It was those pages that had guided his actions tonight. It would be the pages he was about to write that would guide his actions for the future. ------------------------------------- Outside the Perkins 'What a fuckin' couple of weeks.' Kennedy ground out his cigarette on the wet asphalt, discarding it in a nearby trash bin. If you'd asked him when he was home recovering from his concussion if any of this would have happened, he would have simply laughed. Before he could think too much more on it, 'Save A Horse (Ride A Cowboy)' began to play, and his hand went to his pocket out of habit to fish out his cell phone. A quick glance at the number confirmed who it was. 'Kathryn,' Kennedy whispered, the name invoking memories both good and bad. The song continued to play as he continued to debate answering it. The fact of the matter was that he really didn't want to talk to her, right now. Nor did he want to sleep with her. Nor did he, really, want to have anything to do with her. He was done letting people lead him around by a leash. No one, really, had done it better than Kathryn. All it had taken was a dollar bill on the first SIN on SPIKE. For over a year, he'd laughed it off. Now, though, with the present circumstances, it was hard for him to laugh off her transgressions. He had attacked Amy Campbell, and Alexandra Pierce had exacted her toll on his healt and well-being He'd laid out Jonathan Rhine, and now, Jared Sykes would come for him on Temptation. Philip Kennedy was a man without friends in perhaps the most dangerous environment in the history of professional wrestling. As he stepped out into the pouring rain, though, black Stetson protecting his head and face from the downpour, he didn't feel like he was in that much trouble. Instead, he felt like the cowboy he'd always claimed to be.
