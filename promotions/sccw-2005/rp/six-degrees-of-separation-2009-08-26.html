Six Degrees Of Separation

26 August 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/26/09 Darius Drannen stood in front of a mirror, glaring back at his own reflection. He looked as though he hadn't slept in days, and his cheeks were showing a couple days worth of stubble. 'I should've had him.' 'It's not like he owned you, or something. He just capitalized on one tiny mistake,' Jean Guy Marshall said in an attempt to calm him. 'He got lucky, that's all.' Drannen shot him an angry glare. 'That mistake cost me the damn match,' Drannen replied bitterly, unflinching in his self-recrimination. Jean didn't offer another comment on the matter. He knew better than to even try. When Drannen got in one of his moods, it was best to let him simmer for a few hours -- a couple of days, tops -- until he got it out of his system. Drannen rubbed his chin where he'd been struck, then shook his head in annoyance. 'I should've had him.' He turned, suddenly, and headed for the bedroom exit. 'Where you going?' Jean called after him. 'For a drive,' Drannen's answered, his voice muffled as he headed farther down the hallway, toward the front door of his apartment. 'To where?' Drannen's only response was the slamming of the front door behind him. ~ ~ ~ Sparrow was the name most knew her by. In fact, she'd been going by the name for so long that, sometimes, she'd have to think long and hard to even remember her given name. So now when she met someone new, she simply introduced herself as Sparrow. It was just easier that way. The last person she'd met, however, she hadn't even had a chance to introduce herself to. Things had been chaotic, events happening a lot faster than most people could process the information. It seemed that he and his friend had gotten in over their heads -- probably some boneheaded get-rich-quick scheme that had almost cost them their lives. Lucky for them, Sparrow had arrived in time to pull their asses from the proverbial fire. Normally, she'd have killed them, too. Leave no witnesses. It was much more tidy that way. But she decided to spare them, and ever since that chance encounter, she'd been trying to figure out why. The one guy was kind of cute, she thought. She quickly admonished herself for thinking such juvenile thoughts. Shaking her head, she decided there had to be a more rational explanation. She was too well trained to let a potential witness go, all because she happened to find him attractive. She just wasn't sure what that rational reason was yet. Her task had been to retrieve the briefcase and deal with anyone who got in her way of accomplishing that job. She hadn't been told what the contents of the briefcase were, nor who it had belonged to. That wasn't out of the ordinary. She'd learned long ago that it was best, for all parties involved, if she didn't ask any questions. If there was any pertinent information she'd need to complete the task, then those who hired her would surely have filled her in on the details. When she'd arrived, she'd heard the sound of gunfire. She came upon a scene right out of a cheap B-movie, with two big goons wasting ammunition by the truckload, seeming more interested in causing massive amounts of damage to the room's furniture than in actually hitting either of the two men they'd been sent to kill. Between the combination of the deafening sound of rounds being spent and the fact that the two of them were likely at the very bottom of their profession, she'd had no trouble sneaking up on them and dispatching them with lethal force before they'd even known she was behind them. After the shooting had come to an end, she had indicated for the two other individuals in the room to show themselves. A part of her regretted not finishing them off with a couple of bullets between the eyes the moment they came out from behind their cover. But another part of her, the one that apparently controlled her trigger finger, decided not to. In the end, she let them walk away. Of course, she didn't indicate any of this in her report to her superiors. In that version, she'd described how she came upon two individuals who were in the process of removing the briefcase from the hotel room, and had to use necessary force to ensure that they didn't make away with what she'd been sent to retrieve. She made no mention of the other two men, or the fact that she'd let them go. For all intents and purposes, none of her superiors even knew those two existed. And yet, here she was, sitting in her car, waiting patiently for them to emerge from their apartment building. For what reason? She hadn't a clue. Glancing down at her watch for the fifth time in the last half hour, she wondered not for the first time if she should just forget about this little obsession of hers, and return to her boss' office. Lord knows he'd have a lot of work waiting for her upon her return. But each time she'd reached for the key in the ignition, her hand had paused and she'd convinced herself to wait just a little while longer. Just as she was about to go through the motions of reaching for the ignition again, she spotted a man exiting through the front door to the apartment complex. Tall, slender, and to her eye, handsome, he seemed to be in a hurry as he made his way toward the parking lot at the side of the building, suitcase in hand. It was him. The cute one. And he was alone. Her mind raced, trying to decide whether there really was a legitimate reason for her to be following him, or whether she still suffered from bouts of immaturity, when her hormones overrode her common sense. Convincing herself it was the former, she turned the key in the ignition and waited for him to pull out of the parking lot. ~ ~ ~ Drannen just wanted to be left alone. To all outward appearances, Drannen's dour mood had everything to do with an unsuccessful debut in SCCW. His friend, Jean Guy Martell, had even spent a good part of the day trying to cheer him up, insisting that Drannen had been dominating the match, and that Danny Vista had simply gotten lucky. Maybe Jean was right. Maybe that's how it happened. But that didn't matter much to Drannen. It wasn't the loss to Vista that still plagued his every thought. It was Vista himself. But try as he might, he couldn't focus his mind, couldn't think straight. It was like a movie being played in constant fast-forward inside his head, and he didn't have the remote control. He needed to get away, clear his mind, so that he could concentrate and figure out once and for all why Danny Vista looked so damn familiar. Maybe it was a mistake to confront Vista before he had all the details. Was he someone Drannen needed to fear? An old acquaintance that he'd owed money to, perhaps? Doubtful. But in retrospect, Drannen couldn't shake the feeling that he'd erred in approaching Vista before he'd put the pieces of the puzzle together first. A persistent buzzing at his hip shook him from his thoughts. Glancing down in annoyance, he noticed that it was his cell phone that was buzzing. Another call. The third one since he'd left his apartment. The third one he'd ignored. He should have left his cell phone behind. Chances are, he'd continue to be bombarded with calls, from his friends, from his family, from his agent. He didn't want to speak to any of them right now. But old habits die hard. Grabbing his cell phone as he walked out the door was about as automatic as grabbing his keys and wallet. Who knows? Maybe the SCCW brass would be calling him to inform him he'd been booked in a title match on the next show. Drannen scoffed at the idea. They don't reward people for failure. That simply doesn't happen in this business. Until he proved otherwise, Drannen didn't deserve a shot at glory. And if he didn't get his mind off Vista and concentrate on preparing for his next match -- against who, he had no clue -- one loss would quickly turn into a losing streak. While he wasn't informed of who he might be facing at the next show, he knew that if he was going to walk away with a win, his mind would have to be clear of any distractions. In other words, he knew that the only way he'd be able to concentrate on his upcoming bout, was if he got to the bottom of this mystery regarding Vista. At the moment, he hadn't the first clue how he was going to go about doing that. But that's why he figured he needed to get away from the distractions around him, so that he could think clearly. And with a clear head, he could solve the mystery that had been occupying his every waking thought since he'd met Vista backstage. I know you from somewhere, Vista. I'm sure of it. I just have to figure out from where. ~ ~ ~ Sparrow scowled. She'd been following Drannen for what seemed an eternity, pacing his every turn, and still she had no idea where he was leading her to. It was as if he was driving aimlessly, with no clear destination in mind. She watched as he turned off the main streets, and veered down what looked like a back alley designed for delivery trucks to unload their cargo without having to block traffic. Waiting until he'd gained a bit of distance on her so as not to draw attention to the fact she was following him, she let him get a few car lengths ahead, before turning off the same back alley he'd turned into. It seemed like such an odd route for him to take. There weren't any homes down this road, only the rear entrances to the various shops and restaurants on the next street over. And unless her sense of direction was way off -- which it wasn't -- this alley way lead to a dead end. What are you thinking? Meeting someone? Her danger sense began to tingle, wondering if perhaps he'd spotted her tailing him, and was leading her on a wild goose chase. She doubted it. Drannen didn't strike her as the type who methodically planned things. But she decided to keep alert, just in case. As they closed in on where Sparrow remembered the road ending in a dead end, she saw Drannen's car parked in the loading bay of a Chinese food restaurant. The ignition was still running, but the car was empty. You idiot!, she chided herself. You let him give you the slip. Biting back her annoyance at herself, she pulled up next to him and, reaching into the passenger side glove compartment, retrieved her trusty side-arm. She didn't think she'd need it, but she liked to play it on the safe side. After all, she'd underestimated him once already. She didn't want to underestimate again. Getting out of her car, Sparrow surveyed the alley way, trying to determine which was the likeliest direction he would have gone. Only two viable options presented themselves to her; through the back door into the Chinese food restaurant, or up the steel steps to the second floor apartment overlooking the alley way. In the time he'd been out of her sight, those were the only two within reach of a person on foot. She decided to check the second floor apartment. ~ ~ ~ Drannen was crouched behind a pair of large barrels, sitting just outside the Chinese food restaurant's back door. He'd noticed that he was being followed about two minutes back, but at first dismissed it as an overactive imagination. But when she'd followed him and turned down the back alley, he knew his first instincts had been correct. At first, he had no clue who the person following him was. Then his thoughts returned to his confrontation with Danny Vista, immediately before their match. His fear that he'd showed his hand early returned, wondering if perhaps it was someone associated with Vista coming to deal with him. Perhaps there was something about Vista's past that Vista didn't want anyone finding out about, and Drannen's accusations were enough to rattle the man? But when he saw the person following him -- an attractive woman, her slender physique attired in a close-fitting black pants and shirt combination -- get out of their car, he instantly recognized her from the ill-fated attempt by Jean Guy Martell and himself to snatch up the briefcase of the recently deceased Luc Bellemore. What the hell does she want? The phrase 'tying up loose ends' came to him, wondering if perhaps she'd second guessed her decision to let him and Jean walk away. Perhaps whoever had hired her was displeased with her leaving witnesses, and now she'd come back to finish the job. But why follow him, then? She obviously knew where he lived, since he had no doubt she'd tracked him from there. So why not finish him off in his sleep, quietly and efficiently? At the moment, his instinct for self-preservation was in a battle with his curious side. And right now, his curious side was winning. He watched her make her way to the steel steps, leading to the apartment above the store next door. When he was confident that she was far enough away that he could peek his head around the corner, he got up from his hiding spot and made his way to the edge of the docking bay ramp, inching his way along the wall, and peered around the corner. His heart nearly jumped out of his chest when he heard a female voice with a thick English accent say, 'Don't move.' ~ ~ ~ Drannen did as he was told. He didn't need any extra incentive, although the cold muzzle pressed against his temple made his decision an easy one. Silently chiding himself, he raised his arms, bent at the elbows, above his head in a gesture of surrender. 'You can get up, too.' Suddenly feeling foolish, Drannen realized he no longer needed to keep crouching. He stood up straight, and slowly, turned to face the female who currently had a gun held to his head. 'Okay, take it easy,' Drannen said. 'You don't have to ...' 'Shut up,' she responded, her English accent giving her commands an authoritative sound. She then waved removed the gun slowly from his temple, and used it to gesture with a wave toward where their two vehicles were parked. 'Move it.' The sarcastic part of him was tempted to tell her to make up her mind. First she told him not to move, and now she's telling him to move. Using his better judgment, he bit back the remark. 'Alright, I'm going,' Drannen said as he began walking slowly toward his car. Making sure to keep his hands up in the air, he turned to glance over his shoulder at her and said, 'You gonna tell me what this is about?' She offered him a taunting smirk. 'Maybe. For now, just shut up and do what you're told.' For the first time in his life, Drannen did what he was instructed to do without response. When they reached their vehicles, she instructed Drannen to stand by the trunk of his car, hands planted on the trunk and legs spread apart. She then quickly ran a hand over his body, tracing up and down the outside, then inside, of each leg, patting down all his pockets. Again, he was tempted to make a flirtatious comment, but decided against it. When she had finally finished her search, she said, 'Okay, you can turn around now.' He did as instructed, unsure of what to do with his arms. He held them halfway between over his head, and elbows down by his side. Noticing his indecision, she snorted and shook her head. 'You can put your hands down. I'm not going to shoot you.' Drannen wasn't yet convinced of that, but he lowered his arms to his side anyway. When it appeared as though it was safe again for him to speak, he took the opportunity. 'Aren't you gonna introduce yourself?' Drannen asked. She didn't answer immediately. She eyed him up and down first, as if deciding how much information she wanted him to know. Finally, she said, 'Call me Sparrow.' 'Like the bird?' Drannen asked seriously, his eyebrow arched. Her eyes narrowed slightly. 'Something like that.' Realizing he was treading on dangerous ground, he decided to move on. 'So what now?' 'Now, we go for a little ride.' Before he could protest, she continued, 'I've got some questions for you, but this isn't the best place to ask them.' 'What kind of questions?' Drannen asked warily. 'You'll see,' Sparrow responded cryptically. When Drannen was about to climb in the driver's seat of his car, she made a shrill whistling sound to get his attention. 'We'll take my car,' she said, indicating with a wave of her gun toward her vehicle. He hesitated for only a moment, then made his way around his car to hers. 'You drive,' she indicated. 'That way I can keep my full attention on you,' she added with a sly wink. He did as he was told and got in the driver's side, closing his door behind him. She made her way around to the passenger's side, then handed him the keys after she entered. Keeping her gun trained on him, she reached behind her to close her door. Turning the key in the ignition, he turned to her for further instructions. 'Where to?' 'My place.' She ignored his expression of surprise, and said, 'Here's the directions ...'
