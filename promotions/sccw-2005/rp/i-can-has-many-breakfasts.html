I Can Has Many Breakfasts?

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Today Looking up, there's a zillion miles of black and a trillion swooshes of heather gray. Amidst it, a hundred million specks of crystalline -- like Heaven's parking lot with no noticeable wear and tear. Cleaned of the blood that's been spilled upon it, but the chalk outlines of Greek gods remain as a testament to their legacy -- for they had created the very surface they died upon. An occasional eye would twinkle, either seductive to a stargazer or in approval of a prayer seeking to be answered. A streak is a pebble kicked by a stampeding bull, or a stone cast by twin boys bored at lakeside. Asphalt could never catch fire, and the same was true of the metamorphic sky. His dreams of space were waterproofed, made of pavement. So they could never be put out. * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * 'UFO! UFO! Mom, Dad...a UFO!' With a cold, wet nose pressed against the backseat window, he stared upward to the sweet serenity of infinite. Hands perched on the arm rest, he positioned himself for a better view and craned his neck to follow the trajectory of the blinking craft overhead. His parents, seated up front, made no response to the child's outlandish mistake -- youth and their imaginations are best left to grow. The airplane on its red-eye course flew over the highway, prompting the boy to scamper over to the other side and subsequently peer out the other window. 'Mom! Dad! Look at the UFO! Look! Look! There it goes!' The child's voice pulsated innocence, dripped with curiosity. It was a voice you couldn't help but adore. Matched with always-excited eyes and strands of blond, it was unfathomable not to love him. Dad remained silent, scanning the desolate pine-lined shoulders to make sure no deer prepared to leap out in front of the blue Silverado. His blue flannel shirt went perfectly with his woodcutting demeanor -- a full red beard and mustache covered any sign of emotion. The sound of his lover in the seat next to him softly sobbing...almost rhythmically to his own pattern of swallowing saliva...sank the sailing ship buried within his chest. Slights bouts of anger over her inability to comprehend that this hurt him as much as it hurt her were quelled by the reality of the situation. It was mutual and civil, their agreement. They didn't think it would be this hard, however. Dad sighed, raising his non-driving arm behind Mom's head and pulled her left ear to his chest. She felt his heartbeat...a thump-thump reminiscent of a Poe work that Dad liked to keep buried underneath the sturdy floorboard of his left pec. The sobbing coming from her was almost enough to drown it out, however...oddly enough coming from the tiny little button-nose of the very petite woman. The sleeves of the Minnie Mouse sweatshirt were now soiled in tear stains. The boy poked his head over the back of their seat, peering at Mom's face. 'Mom! What's wrong, Mom? Why are you crying?' The boy kissed his mother on the cheek in an attempt to alleviate her sadness. This, of course, only made her tear up even more as she weakly raised her hand up to pat the boy's skull. No audible answer from here, though, as the traffic lines switched from dotted to double-solid beneath them. Attention diverted, the boy fell back into the seat and stretched out for a nap. While truck rides were fun during the daylight times, this cruise under the moon was making him sleepy. He knew that he would be home inside his bed in mere moments, and that when the sun woke up, he'd be ready for breakfast. And toys. And the back yard. And breakfast. And toys. And breakfast. Then a nap. Then maybe, if he was a good boy, Mom would take him to the park to play with his friends. Yeah...that would be really...zzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzz... * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * * Mom and Dad heard the light breathing above the pitter-patter of the road beneath the tires. 'We could've at least waited until the morning to do this, Jim. It just ain't right, not by God's eyes.' Jim sighed, bringing his arm out from around her shoulders and placing both hands on the wheel. 'I don't know what else you want me to do, Liza Mae. You know we cain't barely 'ford ta go into town no-ways, much less feed 'em.' 'We could if you'd give up drinkin',' Liza Mae shot back. 'God dammit,' Jim replied with a sharp cut, 'that ain't the reason we in trouble. And YEW know it!' Liza Mae nodded under a sob. 'I know, honey chile, I know. Just upset is all. I shoulda jus' stayed at the house. Knew this was gon' be too hard.' Jim released his right hand off the wheel again, reinstating the embrace of Liza Mae's small frame. 'Shoot, you the bravest soul I know. And don't you go thinkin' otherwise, ya hear me?' Liza Mae curled up closer to her husband. No doubt she was defeated after nine rounds of her heart boxing her conscience. This certainly wasn't an easy decision to make -- but all of the factors weighed in during the ride into the city limits. While it sure as hell wouldn't seem like a very ethical decision in the eyes of most people, she had convinced herself that it was the right thing to do. The boy would be alright - he had a natural instinct for adaptation. She was confident that one of the upper-class families in the wealthy suburbs would take him and raise him as one of their own. It was either this or the state taking him when they finally came around to repossessing their home. While the orphanage might have found the boy a suitable place to live, she had heard too many horror stories about what could happen to him if he were placed in there. It simply was not an option when Jim had originally suggested it. In the distance as the hill sloped downward into the valley, a barrage of street lights greeted the dusty travelers like a reset breaker in an otherwise black hallway. The flying saucer above recounted its mythology within patterns in the brighter bulbs forming the seven-headed Gangorolithos, the god of knowledge. The Chevrolet truck zoomed headlong like an asteroid into the constellation, periodically stair-stepping on the speedometer to come down to a quiet twenty-five miles per hour. 'Welcome to Cuesta Verde...' Jim read from the sign aloud. 'Home sweet home,' he mused. Jim shut the lights off and pulled into the first driveway on the right. Throwing the stick in reverse, he backed out onto the road...facing the direction in which they came. Liza Mae positioned her knees on the seat, reaching into the back and grabbing the still-sleeping boy in her arms. He miraculously did not stir as she began to cry again, lightly kissing him between the ears before handing him to Jim. Standing outside next to the curb, Dad took the boy and set him upon the grass in one of the many upscale housing lots that filled the like-new subdivision. '...dad? This isn't our house...' A yawn and a stretch followed the still woozy whisper as Jim turned back to the truck and quickly climbed inside. Slamming the door, his foot gassed the accelerator, causing the Silverado to squeal its back tires and dart off toward home...without the boy. 'MOM! DAD! MOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOMMMMMMMMMY! Wait for ME!' He gave chase, but the poor thing was far too small to run for very long, as his tired little legs gave out quickly. Defeated, he sat on the grass and stared in the direction of home. His bed. His breakfasts. His chew toys strategically buried in different parts of his backyard. A tiny tear rolled out of the corner of his eye as he whimpered softly and cried out one more time to his parents. 'Come back...please?' It was a cry unanswered. The instinctive wagging of his tail disproved the theory of the signs of a happy pup.
