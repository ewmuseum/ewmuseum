Cocaine.

18 August 2008

Date: 8/18/08 I had a very simple "to-do" list that contained three items and only seventy-two hours to complete it. One: Defend my world title. Jan. 11, 2008. 10:01 p.m. Tokyo, Japan. After a roaring elbow sent the challenger to my World title to the mat I decided that it was time to take things to another level. Namely the top rope. The pain in my back from an earlier superplex shot up my spine and into my neck and shoulders. I put my boot into the middle turnbuckle pad and grabbed the top rope, stepping up slowly. Before I could turn around I felt my opponent, Adam Joy’s fist square in the middle of my ribs slumping me into the turnbuckle. Joy hit me again, but I was quick to react firing a blind mule kick, which caught him square in the stomach and sent him staggering back. Leaping to the second rope I jumped back, twisting into a round kick as I rebounded. Adam Joy ducked and I kicked the referee in the chops sending him down to the canvas and rolling across the ring. As I tried to stand Adam Joy capitalized on the mishap, falling into a low dropkick, the heels of his boots stamping my forehead. My head slammed into the back of the mat as I lay prostrate in the ring. I saw Joy step over me, headed for the top rope. Big mistake. I played possum until he had almost reached the top. As soon as his feet were on the top rope, his back turned to the ring I leapt into action, rolling over to my stomach and standing to my feet. All it took was a simple push as I shoved Joy hard in the back sending him off of the top rope and sending him crashing face-first into the guardrail on the outside. Joy rose slowly to his feet with the help of the guardrail and turned, leaning against the steel. One springboard somersault later and I had the momentum back. Joy lay prone on the floor as I crawled toward the ring. Lifting the apron I peered into the darkness beneath the ring before spotting a Singapore cane. Extracting the cane I turned and stood to my feet, waiting for my shot. Joy managed to find a vertical base and I took my shot, doing a little batting practice with his face. The first shot caught him on the left cheek and turned him, he staggered to his right and I caught him with a cane to the ribs. He slumped over and I brought the cane down across the back of his head. He stumbled a bit and I laid another shot across the back of his head, which dropped him to a knee. I hit him again. His other knee hit the floor as he grasped for something to keep him upright his hand found the ring apron. Reaching up he grabbed the bottom rope, his back still turned. I hit him again, right in the cerebellum. As I did some Babe Ruth type work with the back of Joy’s head I noticed a trickle of blood running down his neck. Seeing red only encouraged me. I saw the spot on his skull where the trickle had started and I blasted him violently in that exact spot. One shot… two shots… three shots… four shots… five shots… six shots… I got a little carried away with the cane, breaking it over the back of his head. I beat him savagely with the splintered end left in my hand until the gaping wound on the back of his head resembled a vagina. Only when my face was splattered in his blood did I roll him back into the ring. It only took a soccer kick to the ribs of our fallen referee before he would return to a state of consciousness. Once he did, the rest was simple. Cover Adam Joy, hook the leg, stay there till the bell rings. One… two… three, there’s the bell. I stood quickly and rolled out of the ring, not even bothering to wait for the referee to hold my arm in the air. My steps were quick and deliberate. I walked to the timekeepers table and grabbed my World title. Turning toward the stage to leave… that’s when I felt it. Something small, hot and wet hit me in the neck. I turned my head and got hit again… it was spit from the fans. Fuck ‘em. I turned on my heel and exited the ringside area, heading back up the ramp. A plastic cup launched from the third row landed at my feet, the beer splashing my boots. Another plastic cup followed, this one missing me entirely and landing beyond me. In defiance I raised my Pure Japan International World title high into the air lauding it over the PJI fans. They roared their disapproval, inviting me to roar back, "I’m still the World Champion and there’s not a goddamned thing you can do about it!" A few more steps and a plastic cup hit my shoulder soaking me in beer, another cup hit my thigh, splashing sticky soda down my legs and into my kneepads. Fuck ‘em. Exit stage right. Stepping through the curtain changed everything. The adrenaline rush, the sharp pain in my left shoulder and neck, the mixed feelings of anger, despair and relief all hit me at once. Slumping against the cold steel of the framework that composed the entryway and held the superscreen aloft I slid down it and to a seated position, my knees at my chest, my head slumping to rest on my knees. "Jesus fucking Christ… that was insane…", I whispered to myself. My face, hands, arms and chest were all covered in blood. None of it was mine. Beating your opponent to a senseless bloody pulp, incapable of basic motor functions is an excellent way to win, however, it is not a particularly enjoyable way… unless you’re a sadist. I raised my head gazing into the black curtain, curious as to what may be happening on the other side. Slumping my head back against the steel structure I exhaled sharply. "Fuck." Then, without another word I shook the sweat out of my hair and stood up with the help of the steel structure. I don’t enjoy getting booed every night but if that’s the price I have to pay for being the best wrestler in Japan, then that’s the price I’ll pay. Popularity is a small price to pay when weighed against my pride. The only thing that mattered in the here and now wasn’t my popularity but the fact that I had defended and retained my World title. Two: Renegotiate PJI’s current television contract. Jan 12, 2008. 2:18 p.m. Tokyo, Japan. "You’re late", Donovan Knight snapped. "Fuckin’ chiilax, yo", I sneered back at him and his Armani suit. The scolding didn’t stop there. "And what are you wearing? This is a very important meeting!" The answer was ragged jeans and a white tee, stamped with the Nine Inch Nails logo. Instead of giving him the obvious answer I aimed to pacify. "Sweet zombie Jesus on a pogo stick, will you lighten up? "Lighten up? Lighten up? This is…" I interrupted. "Donovan look, I’m sorry I’m late… but drop the issue and the attitude or I’m going to slap you across the fucking room. You may be my friend, attorney and business partner but no one talks to me in that tone." His face turned a shade of red as he snapped his head away from me and turned toward the elevator, I followed him into the elevator and we ascended to the sixth floor. Upon reaching the sixth floor I followed him down a hallway and into a conference room where an administrative panel awaited us. Donovan and I both took seats on the opposite side of the table. Donovan kicked into attorney mode. "My apologies gentlemen, we realize that we’re late. We appreciate your patience." The lone gaijin on the panel nodded. He was a French investor by the name of Jacques Jules and owned a third of Fight TV, the company responsible for airing PJI programming. The two Japanese men seated next to him remained silent, less impressed by the late gaijin’s than the Frenchman was. "We were expecting the other shareholders to attend as well, were they unable to make it?" Asked one of the Japanese men whose name I hadn’t bothered to memorize. He had a thick gray beard and I stamped him with the cognitive label of graybeard in order to remember him. The other Japanese man at the table wore round classes and I noted him as four eyes. Easy enough to remember. "I guess you haven’t heard the good news. We’re the only shareholders." Donovan Knight said with a phony lawyer smile. "Wait, the two of you are the only shareholders?" Asked four eyes. Donovan nodded. "Yes, it’s a recent development." "What about Andrew Yoo…" Graybeard began before Donovan Knight cut him off. "Gentlemen, if it’s alright by you I’d prefer not to disclose the detail of our recent acquisition. As far as I’m concerned it’s a private matter. Plus, we’re not here to discuss stock, we’re here to discuss television." The current contract between PJI and Fight TV consisted of 3 pay-per-views a month, one every ten days. On these shows we were given carte blanche and Fight TV had yet to interfere with our programming. Our pay-per-views cost $9.95 per show in Japan and $14.95 per show in the United States, though only one US pay-per-view ran, and it was not live but rather a compilation of our previous 3 shows. The contract was acceptable, though there was room for improvement. That improvement of course being: more money. "Excellent." The Frenchmen smiled as he spoke. "Let’s get down to business." Donovan’s time to shine. "Absolutely. We’ve been looking over our contract; given the increased number of pay-per-view buys throughout the duration of our contract and the popularity of our brand of wrestling we’ve came to realize that… we should be getting a larger cut." Four eyes chimed in. "Pay-per-view buys are down." Donovan chose to rebuttal. "Sir, you must be mistaken, I think if you look you’ll see that…" Donovan stopped talking as graybeard slid a stack of papers across the table. Leaning over I peered at the papers, analyzing them. Japanese pay-per-view buys were down by thirty-two percent between January 1, 2007 and January 1, 2008. Pay-per-view buys in the United States had slipped even further, falling by fifty-four percent in the same timeframe. "As you can see PJI has lost roughly a third of it’s Japanese audience and over half of it’s audience in the United States.", said graybeard. "What? How is that possible?" I asked, shocked by the analysis on the pages in front of me. "I’m going to answer you honestly, and just a little roughly…, said the Frenchman. "It is our belief that the fans of your product want to see more diversity, more competition, rather than the show being focused primarily on you Josh." I’m not good at biting my tongue, but Donovan shot me a look that suggested that I do so and I did. "Look, all I do is provide commentary on the shows. As far as Josh - my client and joint shareholder is concerned, he has worked his way to the top of the card and remains there due to his outstanding ability. He was at the top of the talent pool in the United States and he’s at the top here in Japan. You can’t hate someone for being damn good at what he does." Graybeard didn’t want to let the issue go. "Actually, from what I saw on last nights show, it looks like the fans do hate him, and not for being so damn good, but for being an egomaniacal sadist who…" I had to respond."Whoa motherfucker, I suggest you close that hole in your face that you use as a dick receptor before I kick your teeth…" Donovan elbowed me in the ribs as he cut me off. "I think what Josh is trying to say is that you can’t dictate to us how to run the show. We book the cards, make sure the talent makes it to the ring and we let anarchy reign from that point forward. The best man wins, not the most popular." It was four eyes’ turn. "Well…that’s a problem. By my calculation Josh has been the reigning World Champion for ten months. You may be the most talented sir, but you’re far from being the most popular and the fans are tired of seeing a gaijin who practically owns the company, represent the organization in such a way." My temper was going to get the best of me eventually. "I was the first roster member hired by PJI when we opened up shop and goddamn it…" "Josh… let me handle this." Donovan cut me off again. "Look, we realize that he’s been the only World Champion in the time that PJI has been open but he was also the longest reigning World Champion in the history of Global Wrestling. Do you understand the kind of talent that came through Global Wrestling? The kind of talent that my client defeated to become the star he is today? Ice, Ken Partridge Jr., Maverick Reno, Deacon Holmes, Jay Robson, Sage Sabbott, Leinad Soma…the best that Global had to offer… and he beat every single one of them. Or how about the imported talent from PTC? Jesse Jamester, Seymour Almasy, the list of asses that my client has kicked goes on and on. " Jacques Jules broke his silence. "Josh, your career has indeed been one of great accomplishments, but when you competed in the states you were usually loved by the fans. Since coming to Japan you’ve changed. Every fan that attends a PJI show knows that you’re only in Japan because Global fired you for using drugs, namely heroin and cocaine." I winced slightly at the mention of heroin and cocaine but held my tongue. Donovan Knight however did not. "What is this? Some kind of witch hunt? I don’t believe for even a second that the Japanese fans are so ignorant that they would loathe my client based on rumors about why he is in Japan. I’ll agree that he’s been on the receiving end of a lot of boos, but you can’t hate someone for being damn talented." "Mr. Knight, its clear to me that you do an excellent job of representing your client, but how well do you know the world of wrestling?" Asked four eyes. "Better than you." I sneered, answering for Donovan. "I doubt that." Four eyes responded. "Because the most important thing on the business side of wrestling isn’t how many wins or losses one individual performer has but rather the number of asses in the seats. The problem here is that Josh’s grip on executive power has led to the decline of fan interest. For the sake of your own company Josh, I recommend that you either lose the title, or relinquish control of your executive powers." "What? You’re telling me to lose?", I gasped in disbelief. "Yes." Answered four eyes. "No. I’ll lose my World title when someone is man enough to take it from me, but I’m not going to lay down for someone who can’t beat me on their own individual merit." Jacques Jules spoke up. "Josh, it is of the utmost importance that you allow someone else to lead the company for a while. In fact… your television contract depends on it." "You’re not going to renew our contract if my client doesn’t pass his title on to another competitor?" Donovan asked. "That’s bullshit!" Before Donovan could open negotiations I decided to open a can of whoop ass. I stood to my feet, grabbing the bottom of the heavy wooden table and flipping it over onto the three men across from me. The contract and five full glasses of water slid onto them as the three men toppled in their chairs, succumbing to the weight of the table, pinned beneath it. "Fuck you, fuck you and especially… fuck you." I said to all three men, turning to walk out the door. Donovan rushed after me. "Josh, what the hell?" "Fuck them and their stipulations Don. I’m not going to lose my title or relinquish my executive privileges because some network stooges told me to. I’m not a conformist piece of shit willing to bend over backwards for these douche bags. Fuck ‘em, our contract expires in March, we’ll get another." And with that I walked to the elevator, a shocked and dismayed Donovan Knight following in my footsteps, his mind already racing. It isn’t easy being a lawyer. It’s even harder when you’re my lawyer. Three: Smuggle cocaine from Venezuela to Japan. Jan 14, 2008. 12:43 a.m. Caracas, Venezuela. Hotel Altamira could probably be described as a shithole, though that term may be deemed derogatory by pigs and other barnyard animals that live in a literal shithole. But regardless of how cold the shower was, how thin my pillow was, or how lumpy the mattress was, I was happy to finally be off of that goddamned airplane. I’ve seen my fair share of long flights in my day but nineteen hours is enough to wear me out. Especially when all nineteen hours are spent thinking about cocaine. The question isn’t "where do I find cocaine?" Cocaine is easy to find. Even in Japan, the hardest country to find nose candy in, I never run out. Being a celebrity doesn’t hurt but in Venezuela I’m practically unknown. Just another gringo who tips well and speaks mediocre Spanish. Thankfully the Yakuza had my back and promised to provide me with a contact in Venezuela. Upon landing in Venezuela I had been met by fellow Yakuza cohort Ricky Shaw who was bound for Bolivia. Shaw had arranged for transport from Simon Bolivar international airport to Hotel Altamira. Upon our separation Shaw handed me a cell phone, explaining that when the yakuza had arranged for their contact in Venezuela to meet with me he would call. Shaw also gave me a duffel bag filled with stacks of colorful Venezuelan currency, bound together with rubber bands. My mission, according to Shaw was "simple." Wait to be contacted, meet said contact, buy as much cocaine as possible and transport said cocaine to Japan by any means possible. When I asked him "how", Shaw just laughed… and told me to use my imagination. Jan 14, 2008. 9:56 p.m. Caracas, Venezuela. "Hello…" I answered the phone on the third ring. "I have what you need." Said an unfamiliar female voice on the other end of the line, her voice tinted with a latino accent. "Excellent. Where do you want to meet?" "Meet me in the lobby in ten minutes. From there we’ll go to your room for the exchange." "Got it." I confirmed. Without another word she hung up, and I headed for the lobby. Jan 14, 2008. 10:10 p.m. Caracas, Venezuela. Something about the way she looked told me that the girl walking into the hotel told me that she was the one. She was dark skinned with black hair and big brown eyes, her lips full and pouty, her figure protesting against the black booty shorts and maroon soccer tee she wore. Over her shoulders was a backpack. She walked directly up to me and extended her hand with confidence. "Are you the American?", she asked. I nodded. "Yeah, my name…" "Isn’t important.", she concluded, turning toward the elevator she glanced back over her shoulder at me. "Shall we?" I followed her into the elevator where we ascended to the eight floor in total silence. We didn’t speak again until I had closed and locked my door. She shrugged her backpack off of her shoulders and onto my bed, where she took a seat. "Do you have the money?", she asked. I nodded. "It’s in the bag." She reached across my bed but I gently caught her wrist. "Not so fast… is that mine?", I asked, gesturing toward the backpack. She glanced up at me, surprised that I had cut her off. "Relax. It’s yours." She unzipped her backpack and turned it upside down, dumping the contents onto the bed. What fell out were well wrapped bricks of cocaine. "Now this is mine.", she said with a smile, reaching again for the duffel bag. Again I stopped her. "Wait." "What?", she snapped, shooting a look of annoyance at me. "For all I know that’s sugar." "My… aren’t you the untrusting type.", she commented. I smirked. "You’re right. I don’t trust anyone." "Then why don’t you join me…", she smiled, unwrapping a block of cocaine. "… in doing a few lines?" I matched her smile. "If you insist." Reaching into the duffel bag I pulled a banded wad of cash out, extracting a single bill and rolled it into a tube. "Ladies first." She took the bill and put it in her nose, lowering her head to the block of cocaine she snorted long and hard, leaving a deep crevice in the coke. She handed me the bill and I followed her lead taking a long, hard snort of coke matching her. Her smile hadn’t faded as she reached out for the bill. I handed it to her and she snorted another line. She handed it back and I snorted another line. "I know you.", she said. "A lot of people know me… but not a lot of people around here know me." "I remember you from when you wrestled in Mexico. My cousin lives in Mexico, he’s your biggest fan. "Great. What do you want? An autograph?" "Actually…", she said, reaching for the bill again, "I was hoping we could mix business…"… she handed me the bill, her hand lingering on mine, "…and pleasure." To be continued…
