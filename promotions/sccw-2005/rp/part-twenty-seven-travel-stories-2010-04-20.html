Part Twenty-Seven: Travel Stories

20 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/20/10 When you’re in the line of work that I am, it can become really easy to hate the road. Travel’s been an integral part of my life since…shit, I can’t even remember. There always seemed to be somewhere I needed to be, somewhere else to arrive on the journey. Heading upstate to school at the Point. The trip to Hawaii where I meant Lani for the first time. Serving out my commission at Camp Zama in Tokyo. Traveling all over the US…hell, even the world…for wrestling. I always seem to be packing or on a plane or dealing with jetlag, always navigating strange restaurants or chugging down cups of coffee or saying goodnight to Zach over video phone. I’m not going to tell you that this is what makes my life hard. I’ve had my shit to deal with, like everybody else, and I’ve just tried to put my head down and get on with things the best I can. I know I’m in a privileged situation, I know I’m lucky. Sure, I ended up putting my body and health on the line but I get paid handsomely for it. Enough to make sure Lani is taken care of, that Zach gets everything that he needs, that the people that love me suffer as little as possible for my absences. But it’s so easy sometimes for things to dissolve into one eternal grey blur, an endless cycle of hotel-arena-taxicab-airport. Lather, rinse, repeat, ad infinitum. I do have my toys, my distractions: my laptop, my iPod, magazines, dog-eared and battered paperback books. But that’s all they are, really: distractions. Something to try and take my mind off the fact that the plane is late or the coffee I ordered tastes like mad or that there’s a volcano in Iceland spewing out ash and all flights have gone completely to hell… Stuff to make me forget where I am, to let me think I’m anywhere but the place I’m currently calling "here". Which is why I keep having to remind myself not to switch off completely some days, not to go travel numb. To make sure to pay the barista at Starbucks a compliment so that she’ll forget the guy who was an asshole to her a half hour ago. To pay for the order of the person in line behind me, just because. To find a quiet corner of an airport lounge that I can slip into, to thank God for all that he’s done for me and all that he’s blessed me with. Those moments don’t just come along. You have to take them, carve them out of your day, make sure to have them happen. Otherwise…well, I shudder to think. I’ve been called into the corner of Yokohama Arena that Nigel Kensington, our CO, is calling his office for this show. Nigel’s not really the kind of person you’d imagine running an organization like SCCW. He looks to be in his late forties, hair slightly thinning, and belly showing the slowly growing signs of middle age. The glasses he tends to favor and his British accent tend to make him seem like a college professor. I always get that strange feeling in my gut when I see him, the one I used to get when I got called into the Commandant’s office. That’s not to say he’s a bad guy. He’s always been polite with me, always been reasonable and available. He’s just not terribly chummy, not a guy to go out of his way to make friends with the rest of the roster. Which is cool, not every CO has to be best buds with his subordinates. This is a business to Nigel. I can understand that. Then there’s our new 2IC, Adrienne St. Germain…her, I can’t understand. "You wanted to see me, sir?" Nigel looks up from the stack of paperwork he’s been poring over, his glasses slipping slightly down the bridge of his nose as he does so. He pushes them back up with one finger, so automatically that I can tell it’s the kind of thing he’s done a million times before. "Mister Marshall. Thank you for coming so promptly. Have a seat, if you wish. This should only take a few moments." "Have I done something wrong, sir?" I don’t think I have but, then, I can’t think of any reason why Nigel would want to see me now. "Not at all. You are aware that our next show is scheduled for Camp Hammond in Iraq?" I nod. I’m sure not all of the roster are thrilled at that…hell, most of them probably wish we were going anywhere but Baghdad. I can appreciate that. But me? I’m looking forward to it a bit. It’s gonna be a bit like a homecoming for me. "Yes, sir." "Well, to my surprise, I received a message from Camp Hammond just a short while ago. From a Colonel Derek LaRue, who mentioned you specifically by name." I know I’m smiling even before I can feel it on my face. Me, Matt, Derek, Andy…we became fast friends as plebes and stayed that way all through the Point , through being firsties and graduation. We’d lost touch over the years because of time and work and all the other things that get in the way…but just hearing Derek’s name brings all those memories rushing back. The hell we raised. The things we got up to. Getting called into the Commandant’s office. "What did Derek have to say, sir?" "I must admit, I didn’t quite understand his message. It said, and I quote, "tell Betty that I still owe him an ass whupping from when we were cows…and that I’m looking forward to seeing him again."" I can’t help it. I’m laughing my ass off before I realize and I can tell Nigel’s more confused than he was before. It’s just…damn, I can’t believe he used that nickname. Hell, I can’t believe he even remembered it after all these years! "Is it a private joke, Mister Marshall?" "No, sir," I wheeze as I try to control my laughter. "It’s just…are you familiar at all with Archie comics, sir?" "Can’t say that I am. British comics were more about Dan Dare." "Myself, Derek, Matt Monroe and Andy Lake…we were best friends at West Point. We thought of ourselves as brothers in every sense of the word…but put for guys like us together and, well, we can get pretty competitive." Nigel smiled. "I imagine so." "Matt and I were the worst at it," I answered. "We got into it about everything. So much so that Andy started calling us Betty and Veronica, after the two main female characters in the Archie comics. And it stuck...not exactly thrilling Matt or myself. At heart, I didn’t mind because was the sweet and nice one that everybody liked." "And Mister Monroe?" "His biggest complaint was that his family were nowhere near as rich as Veronica’s family was in the comics." I saw Nigel laugh at that. Just a small one, mind you. "I see. But "cows"?" "It refers to our third year together, sir. It’s what third year cadets are informally called." Nigel nodded. "I gather you’re looking forward to seeing your friend again?" "Very much so, sir. I actually wasn’t aware he was stationed in Iraq." "I’m pleased to be the bearer of glad tidings, then. Now, shouldn’t you be on your way? I understand you’re throwing a party this evening for the roster?" I nodded. "I’m on my way there, now. Will you be coming, sir?" "I’m not certain I’ll be able to, there is quite a mountain of paperwork for me to get through." I grab a piece of paper from off of his desk, quickly scrawling down the address of the restaurant where the party will be. "If you can make it, sir, it would be much appreciated." "Understood, Mister Marshall. Now go. Just because I’m stuck here working doesn’t mean you have to be." "Yes, sir." Jurakusen is a small little place in Ono, just the right size for a roster party. Food was incredible, full range of sake on tap and just the right size for a roster party. Not that I was expecting everyone to show up. Everyone got an invitation, sure…but I’m well aware that there are plenty of people in SCCW who’d deliberately avoid this get together just because I was the one throwing it. I’d seen no sign of Xavier Kannon, thank God. And Wyatt Connors was doing his usual, sticking to the shadows. Lane had shown up, accompanied by Regan. I assume he thought it would bother me but, to be honest, his presence had barely registered to me. He’d been sequestered in a corner for most of the night, downing what he believed to be the good stuff (though I’d had Kinuichi keep the real premium stuff away from Lane) and generally made a drunken pig of himself. Others had come as well. Devlin…Shannon, I think…was perched on top of a table belting out a rousing chorus of something I couldn’t recognize, his partner Matt Elias looking like he just wanted to fall down a hole. Jared and Amy were seated together at a table. Jared had been nursing the same bottle of Sappporo all evening while Amy was wolfing down sushi and sake, explaining the finer points of sake bombs to Jared in between bites. Quinlan, The Mondays, Legion and his crew, Baron B…hell, even Thanatos and the rest of his bunch showed up, Thanatos making a point of raising his glass in my direction. "May I join you?" And then there’s this woman. Alexandra Pierce. Time was, just the thought of saying her name was greeted with a shudder of fear. She tended to evoke strong emotions in people: fear, loathing, hatred. Wasn’t something she minded at the time, not as far as I could see. After all, calling yourself "Desade" isn’t just a sign that you have a thing about pain…it’s that you enjoy inflicting it on others. But something about her has changed recently. She really does seem to be trying to change her ways, become someone better. Ask most of the roster, they’ll tell you this is a work, another of Alexandra’s elaborate cons. Me? Maybe I’m just being too trusting but I don’t think it is. Not this time. "You’re always welcome, Alex," I answer in Japanese, hoping to throw her for a little loop. It’s rare that you get one over on Alexandra Pierce. "But I would have thought you would have wanted to sit with Amy." "Things between us are a bit awkward after this evening’s events," she answers, her Japanese flawless. "Not to mention that she’s with Jared…and I am far from being his favorite person in the world." I nod, gesturing for her to take the seat opposite me. She slides in, as gracefully as she can given the war she and Amy had waged just a few hours previous. I smile, holding up my bottle of Evian. "I’m strictly doing the French stuff tonight. But let me get you something." I flag down the waiter as he passes, ordering a bottle of Kame no O for the table. Alex immediately starts to protest but the waiter is quickly gone before she can finish. "No," she says to me, switching back to English. "Absolutely not." "It’s the good stuff, Alex." "I’m well aware. It’s also five hundred dollars a bottle!" "Good thing I’m only ordering the one bottle, then?" I can feel Alex looking at me as I take a pull from my bottle of water. I don’t think she quite gets me, quite understand where I’m coming from or how I think. I know this used to be a source of irritation to her. Now…I’m not sure. "I don’t understand you sometimes, Lance. Of all the people on the roster who might have reason to doubt my intentions, you should be heading the list. I wouldn’t blame you if you never forgave me, given our history. And yet here you are, placing your trust in me, willing to put yourself at risk for me…and ordering me five hundred dollar sake. Why?" I nod at her. "One, I don’t believe anyone is irredeemable. And two…do you mind if I tell you a story?" "I don’t see why not." "People send me comic books all the time. Probably a result of the Superman nickname I seemed to have picked up. Anyways…in one of those big events they’re always doing in comics, there was a villain named Maxwell Lord. And his big secret weapon was the fact that he had a way of completely controlling Superman’s mind. The most powerful man on Earth completely at Lord’s mercy." Alexandra just nodded as the waiter placed the bottle of sake on the table. "I see." "Of course, someone has to step up and stop him. And Wonder Woman does. Oh, sure, she takes a heck of a pounding from Superman in the process…but she stops him and Lord. And Lord taunts her. Tells her flat out that the minute he gets out of prison, he’ll just put Superman under his control again. And he’ll keep doing it again and again until everyone who opposes him is dead. So, what do you think Wonder Woman does? Better yet, what would you do? If someone had a way of controlling my actions, of making me hurt people I loved and cared for…that he would make me hurt them unless you stopped him. What would you do?" "Lord would be dead before he crossed the room," Alex answered, taking a sip of sake. "His continued existence is too much of a risk and his death would serve the greater good. But I imagine the comics found some other way of handling the situation." "Hardly. Wonder Woman snapped Lord’s neck on the spot, killing him." It’s rare when you actually get to see surprise on the face of Alexandra Pierce. "Two. It isn’t what you do, it’s why you do it. If I ever run into my Maxwell Lord, I know that you would be my Diana." I tip my bottle in her direction. She responds by tapping her ochoko against it. "Thank you." We’re at Kadena Air Force Base in Okinawa, waiting on the transport plane that will take us to Camp Hammond. Part of me finds it somewhat perverse to be relying on flyboys to get us to an Army base but I say anything. Instead, I try to find a quiet corner somewhere in the hour or so dead time we have before the plane arrives. There’s a quiet corner in the mess that meets my requirements. I take out my laptop and boot it up. As it comes to life, I offer a silent prayer to whoever it was that invented Skype. Genius. I start Skype up, making sure the webcam is turned on. Once that’s done, I dial our number at home. Lani answers on the second ring. "Hey, baby, glad you called. Zach and I were about to sit down to dinner." And we talk. About work and things going on at home. About how my iPad has arrived, waiting for me in the bedroom. I try to hide how much I’m missing her, how much I wish we were talking face to face instead of over a video phone. I’m pretty sure I fail. After about ten minutes, she calls Zach over to the computer. I sit, silently, as Zach tells me all about his day at school, the new video games that he got to play with his friends, how the doctor said he was doing very well. I tell him to be good for Mommy, to eat all his dinner and make sure he does his homework. I tell him I love him. He says he loves me too. After Lani and Zach hang up, I have to take a moment. I have to remind myself that they’re who I do this for. That, in the face of everything I’ve had to deal with, they’re the ones who give me hope and strength. Thank you God. For everything.
