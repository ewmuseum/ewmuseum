Part Five: Bleed It Out

10 June 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/10/08 Dim lighting. The thick smell of sweat in the air. The constant sound of clanking metal. A fist, then its partner, pounding away at a heavy bag. The grunts and groans, closer to animal noise than any sound a human would make. The gym we find ourselves in today is quite some ways off the standard L.A. fitness trail. You will find no reality show starlets looking to tone up or actors getting in shape for a movie (and hoping to be photographed at the same time). No, the folks who tend to frequent this particular establishment are of the sort colloquially known as "muscleheads" (and some other, less printable names). They come here, to this gym which has the "atmosphere" of your average dive bar, for one simple purpose: to push their bodies further than they ever imagined possible. For some, it is a professional calling. For others, a way of life. But no matter what the reason may be, the men who come here (and it is overwhelmingly men who come here) share the same common goal. In one corner stands a small clutch of boxing equipment, looking as if it’s just about to enter the phrase known as "seen better days". A heavy bag, three speed bags, a few jump ropes. Only one man makes use of this corner today. Lance Marshall circles the heavy bag like an animal waiting to strike, looking for the first sign of weakness in his prey. Glove-clad fists lash out at canvas with a speed many would not imagine their owners to possess. With each strike, a sound pitched between a grunt and a growl escapes Lance Marshall’s lips. No one bothers Lance on this day. No one walks over to him or attempts to engage him in conversation. Lance finds himself grateful for this. He bears no ill will towards anyone here. But his head feels so clouded, his temper straining at its leash like a rabid dog…he knows that his grasp on control is tenuous at the moment. He can bear this but he has no right asking anyone else to do so as well. Fuck that! Let’s tear some fuckers heads off! Quiet, you. Giving you free reign is the last thing we need right now. Jab. Jab. One-two. What the fuck are you talking about? The Hand needs to pay for what they did. They need to hurt. They will. But things get all screwy when we let you take control. We get sloppy, lose focus. End up losing to men we have no business losing to. Jab. Jab. Right hook. I don’t care about winning or losing. I just wanna make the Hand bleed. You don’t think Pierce is expecting that?! We let you take control, we just play into every stereotype they paint of us. Big, dumb, roid-raging jock who can’t string together two coherent sentences. Do you really want to allow Pierce to point at us and go "and they call me evil"? She hit her with a lightbulb! That fucking whore took a fluorescent lightbulb and bashed it over Lani’s head! You can’t tell me I’m not right! Left hook. Jab. One-two. I’m not saying you’re wrong. What Katsidy did is infuriating. But we have to be smart about this. We cannot play into what Desade might expect. So how do we go about doing that? Trust me. I have an idea. The hospital room is cold and impersonal, as all such places tend to be. There is an unnatural sense of quiet in the air, punctuated only by the soft sound of voices coming in through the slightly open door. The smell of disinfectant and industrial grade cleaning products are everywhere. Lance Marshall’s gaze never breaks from his wife, his eyes taking in every inch of her as if she might disappear the moment he allowed himself to blink. His eyes are red and puffy, his face unnaturally pale. He looks at her as she lies there, her chest rising and falling with every breath, her face relaxed. She sleeps peacefully, her whole body at ease. Lance can’t help but to marvel at how beautiful she is. His reverie is interrupted by the sounds of Alanna returning to consciousness. "Wh…where am I?" Lance is at her side in an instant, taking her hand in his. "Hey, baby. We’re at Cook County General. We had to take you here after the show." "Right," Alanna replies, "I remember…I got hit with something." "A lightbulb. The bitch hit you with a goddamn fluorescent bulb." Alanna eyebrows raise in disbelief at this rather…unusual…answer. "You’re kidding." "About as much as I’m kidding when I tell you I’m gonna snap that bitch’s neck the next time I see her." "Honey, no. Don’t take this all out on Kat." Alanna’s hand slips from Lance’s grasp as he takes a step backwards. He struggles with his next words, struggling to keep his voice from rising into a yell. "Now who’s kidding?" Alanna’s voice is slow, measured. "It’s not like she had a choice, baby. Desade has a hell of a hold on her, I knew that going in. Being her friend, it has its share of risks…" When Lance thinks back to this moment, he will come to regret the tone in his voice, the volume. He will apologize to Alanna for how he spoke to her and she will accept his apology readily. What he will not apologize for, however, is what he actually said. "That fucking whore is not your friend! You have been nothing but nice to her…to a length which drives me crazy, might I add…and she has done nothing but stab you in the back and spit in your face. Kathryn Shaw does not give a damn about anyone in this world besides herself and Alexandra Pierce. She’s like all of Pierce’s little martinets, carefully chosen for their ability to follow orders." Alanna’s reply is a simple "That’s not fair." "Desade’s a lot like the army, hon. They don’t want you to think. They just want you to do." "Things between Kat and I…they’re a lot more complicated than that." Lance waves his hands in frustration. "You know what? I really don’t want to talk about this anymore. Because if I keep talking about the Hand, I’m just gonna want to punch somebody in the face." Alanna sighs in frustration. This is an argument she does not want to have right now…and one she knows she will not win. So, on to plan B. "Okay. Can we go home, then?" "No. The doctors want you to stay for the night, for observation. I was gonna head home and bring you back a change of clothes for tomorrow once you woke up." "This is stupid. I feel fine." "The doctor’s say there’s a slight chance you might be concussed, they don’t wanna chance it. They figure if you don’t show any symptoms at night, they can let you leave in the morning." "Fine, whatever." Lance smiles, full well aware of what a lousy patient his wife could be. Delivering this next bit of news, though, he knew would be harder to do. "They also want you to stay at home for a week." Alanna pulls herself to a sitting position, about to get to her feet and give the doctors a piece of her mind. "Oh, hell no! What the hell do they think they’re…" Lance meets her at the side of the bed, keeping her from getting to her feet. The look in his eyes is one of the deepest tenderness, the greatest love…and the deepest concern. "Baby, they smashed a light bulb over your head. Every time I think I know how low the Hand will sink, they crawl down even deeper. Who knows what they’ll do next time?" Lance’s voice lowers to a choked whisper. He can feel his eyes starting to water but he no longer cares. "Do you know what it would do to me if they did something to really hurt you? The thought of being without you…it makes me die inside. It would kill me to see you get hurt again so, please, just this once…please do what the doctors say." Alanna reaches a hand towards her husband’s face, tenderly caressing his cheek. "Hey, hey…don’t worry, baby. I’m not going anywhere and nothing’s going to happen to me." "Just…please do this. For me, okay?" Alanna smiles. "Just one week?" "Just one week." Alanna nods, smiling. "Okay." The relief on Lance’s face is evident. "Great. I’m just gonna run home and grab some stuff for you, okay? I’ll be right back." "See you soon." A quick kiss and Lance is on his way out the door and down the hall. It isn’t until he hits the parking garage that the feelings start rushing back…the worry, the fear. The way he’d felt as if he’d been stabbed in the heart as he he saw Alanna lying there on the arena floor… Stop it, he thinks. Things will be fine. I’ll make sure of it. Jab, Jab, Jab, one right after the other. So, that’s your plan? Yes, it is. Sounds a lot like my plan. Jab, Jab, Jab, Jab, Jab… Your plan involved hitting people until they stopped moving. My plan is a little more…focused. Screw that. Will it work? Wait and see. Lance had stayed until a half-hour past the end of visiting hours. Traffic had been light and he’d made it home quickly. Mike, to his credit, had volunteered to look after Zach and the two of them greeted Lance at the door, Zach running up to his father and hugging onto him tightly. Somehow, little man, Lance thought, you always know exactly what I need. Dinner had been…well, to be honest, it had simply been. He didn’t remember the food standing out in any way so he assumes it must have been at least edible. Stories were told, jokes exchanged, all the right noises made. Mike had brought over an advance tape of that week’s episode of "Gladiators", promising Lance that he would have a laugh at what the producers had gotten him to do that week. And normally, the sight of Mike covered head to toe in green body paint would have raised more than just one laugh. It would have been the source of weeks of endless teasing, of Lance reminding Mike that Lance was supposed to be the one with the Incredible Hulk fixation. Tonight, it was greeted by a smile. They said their goodbyes at the front door, "Unca Mike" promising Zach that he would come over again soon, assuring Lance that Alanna was tough and would get through all of this okay. As Lance watched Mike drive off, he thought to himself how much appreciated what Mike was doing, the message that Mike was trying to send. It didn’t help, though. After getting Zach ready for bed (and having to choose from three bedtime stories in the process), Lance Marshall now found himself sitting in front of the television, listlessly fast-forwarding through the programs stored in the DVR. He stopped as the familiar logo of Marvel Comics came into view, fading into a shot of Robert Downey Jr. as Tony Stark…for a movie that wasn’t Iron Man. Christ, I can’t believe they’re showing the Stark cameo in the previews… "Daddy?" Lance starts and hits pause on the DVR, snapped out of his thoughts by his son’s voice. Zach stands in the doorway, a battered brown teddy bear hanging loosely from his right hand. "Zach? What’s wrong, champ, couldn’t sleep?" Zach nods as he walks over to his father, his eyes heavy with sleep. "Uh huh. I miss Mommy." Lance picks up his son (eliciting a sleepy giggle from Zach) and places Zach next to him on the sofa. "I miss Mommy, too, champ. But she’ll be home tomorrow, okay?" Zach nods again. "Okay." He points at the TV screen. "Is that the Hulk?" "Uh huh." "He looks big. And strong." "Well, he is." Zach turns to look at his father, a look of admiration in his eyes (the sort of look that can only ever be managed by the very young). "Not stronger than you. No one’s stronger than you, Daddy." Lance tousles his son’s hair, the first real smile of the day lighting up his face. "Thanks, champ. Say, you wanna watch something until you go to sleep?" "Uh huh. Can we watch the movie with the mices and the funny name?" "Oh, Ratatouille? Sure." The movie was barely ten minutes in before Zach lay in his father’s lap, sleeping soundly. Pausing the Blu-Ray player, Lance carefully picked his son up and carried him back to his bedroom. He placed him in bed, bringing the covers to his neck and giving him a kiss goodnight. "G’night, champ." And as he left the room, Lance heard the sleep fogged of his son calling to him in response. "G’night, Daddy." Right hook. Andre Jensen. What a fucking moron. Right hook. No. He’s an idiot. He’s not stupid. Jab. There’s a difference? Jab. He’s an idiot in thinking that posing as a member of the Taliban and throwing around money somehow passes for humor. But he’s not stupid. He knows that people underestimate him and he uses that to his advantage. We can’t allow that to happen. One-two. So, what then? One-two. We use Andre. To send a message. To the Hand…and anyone else who might harbor some doubt. One-two. You think they’ll get it? Trust me. I think this message will be impossible to miss.
