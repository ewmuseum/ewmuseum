Part Nineteen: Moments

12 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/12/09 It is 6:30 a.m. and Lance Marshall finds himself in a chrome and glass strewn temple to the pursuit of physical perfection, the sickly glow of fluorescent lighting standing in stark contrast to the soft light of the new day dawning outside. When you make a living out of catering to those who make their living from their bodies, something you come to realize very quickly is that most of your clientele don’t exactly have what could be called "regular hours". Early in the morning, in the dead of night, smack in the middle of the day…availability as and when needed is the key. It is for this reason that Lance Marshall now finds himself in this particular gym, at an hour when most folks would be grumpily smacking the snooze buttons on their alarm clocks and desperately praying for another ten minutes of uninterrupted sleep. A pang of guilt arose from somewhere deep in the back of Lance’s mind as he thinks of his wife sound asleep in their bed, unaware of his absence. Alanna’s been sleeping more lately, he thinks, ever since…ever since they got the news. Which, to some degree, comforts him. Asleep, she will be warm and comfortable and protected…she will be safe. Lost within her dreams, able to seek refuge from the world. This was not, Lance thought, a luxury he had the benefit of being able to indulge in. Much as he felt it should, the world had not stopped simply because his wife had been diagnosed with a serious illness. Bills still had to be paid, Zach had to get to and from school…work had to get done. For his part, that required dragging himself to the gym when he would rather be in bed, arms wrapped around his wife, watching her as she breathed softly. But professional wrestling required too much of Lance Marshall. It required him to keep his body at peak physical condition at all times, to be able to withstand tortures which would surely have crippled a lesser man. It was a position that Lance understood, that he accepted…and that, right now, he needed more than anything. When he had first received the news of his wife’s illness, Lance’s reaction had been immediate and visceral. He had wanted to scream, to cry out, to rage at G-d at the unfairness of everything. But he knew he couldn’t. He knew that he could not allow himself to be weak, could not allow himself the selfish luxury of indulging his own sadness. Alanna, Lance thought, was everything right now. She would try and be strong, he knew. She would fight this disease with everything she had in her, he knew that as well. But the doctor had been clear. The course of treatment he had outlined for Alanna would be hard. The effect on her body would be powerful and profound. So, he thought, he would do this. He would not just be Lance Marshall, loving husband and father. He would be Lance Marshall, the man whose physical strength was spoken of in hushed whispers in the locker rooms of SCCW. He would be Lance Marshall, the man capable of treating even the biggest athletes in SCCW as though they were mere children. He would stand by his wife’s side as she fought. He would be there by her side, he would be there to catch her as she fell, to hold her up as she faltered. By not allowing himself to be weak, he reasoned, he would allow her the ability to draw upon his strength when she needed it. If you were to grant Lance Marshall the ability to step outside himself at this moment, to view himself as an impartial observer would, there would be two things that would become immediately apparent. The first is that Lance is moving amounts of weight that have some of his fellow gym patrons open mouthed in shock. The more seasoned gym rats just glance over and nod, knowing from painful experience that the big man will definitely feel the after effects of his efforts for some time. Lance understood and accepted this. Physical pain was nothing. He would bear the sore and aching muscles with pride, wield them as scars of battle. This type of pain was acceptable, this type of pain would replace the type that he could not, would not allow himself to express. The second thing is something that Lance himself was not even aware of…but it was something that had a few of Lance’s fellow gym goers wondering if they needed to call the police. For with every repetition, every exertion, every thrust of weight came a sound profoundly unlike anything one tends to hear at a gym at the early hours of the morning. This was not simply the grunts and groans of hard work, not even the exaggerated cries of those looking to show off with how hard they were working. No, this was something far more…primitive. Primal. Feral. Atavistic. Almost animal. Upon his departure, several of Lance’s fellow gym goers will believe they now know why Lance Marshall has the professional nickname of "The Lion". It is 9:30 a.m. and Alanna Marshall is awaiting the rest of her day with a mounting sense of dread. Today will mark the first day of her treatment, the first of a series of chemotherapy treatments designed to shrink the tumor in her ovaries to a size where it will be able to be removed via surgery. What faced her now, Alanna thought, was weeks, if not months, of pain and discomfort. Her stomach churned at the thought as she took a sip of her coffee. It took her a moment to realize the feeling that was slowly creeping over her. It had been a while since she had been afraid of anything. Now, facing a long and painful course of treatment… She was surprised at just how quickly the urge to cry washed over her. Lance had come back home around 8:30 a.m. She had woken up to the sound of the shower running, listening in silence for a few moments as the water ran. She then slowly made her way to the bathroom door, slipping off her nightgown as she did so. Quietly, she opened the door and snuck inside the bathroom, making her way into the shower before Lance had been aware of what was happening. When Lance became aware of her presence, he did not react with surprise. Instead, he looked at her with a smile and a look of such affection that Alanna could feel her heart melt right there on the spot. "Hello, gorgeous," Lance had drawled. They had then just stood there, arms wrapped around one another, enjoying the feel of the warm water washing over them as their bodies stood entwined together. Alanna could not remember the last time she had felt so comfortable and protected…so safe. Lance had then slowly and tenderly washed her hair before leaving her with a soft, deep kiss. Alanna had just stood there for a few moments before finishing up, her mind lingering in the heat from the shower and from her husband. When she had dried off and dressed, Lance had already gone. There had been a note on the kitchen table: wishing her a good morning, letting her know that he would be taking care of that morning’s errands, that they would be heading to the appointment together. That he would call later. That he loved her and was thinking of her. Alanna had wanted to cry right then and there. She didn’t know what she had done to deserve Lance, didn’t know how she had managed to find someone who loved her like he did. And right now, she realized, she didn’t care. As she slid onto the couch and flipped the channel to "The Today Show", she thought that knowing Lance loved her was enough. She wouldn’t have to go through this alone. For right now, that would be all the comfort that she needed. It is 2:00 p.m. and Alanna Marshall is trying very hard not to fall asleep. "It’s all right, Mrs. Marshall," the nurse is telling her in calm, even tones. His voice is soothing, almost hypnotic. "Falling asleep is a very common reaction to the treatment. It’s good that you have someone here with you, though," he adds, pointing to Lance sitting in a chair in the corner. "You’ll need someone to take you home." Alanna nods weakly, her head barely stirring on the pillow. All of her focus is on the IV slowly pumping the drugs into her bloodstream, the drugs that will go after the tumor. She feels her eyelids start to flutter as the nurse continues speaking. She is vaguely aware that his focus has shifted from her to her husband, that he is explaining to Lance what will need to be done after today’s session. She can hear Lance speaking, affirming the nurse’s statement, occasionally breaking with a question or request for clarification. She is not aware of when the nurse finally leaves. Lance is sitting by the bed, her left hand resting in his right. The TV is playing softly, something bright and colorful playing on one of the cable movie channels. "Hey, babe." She smiles, nodding with her head at the film. The two watch together, Alanna lasting only a further ten minutes before finally succumbing to sleep. It is 5:30 p.m and it has been a long day for Zach Marshall. Mrs. Gross had been the one to take him home from school today. Zach knew that was going to happen, Mommy and Daddy had told him so. They’d had to go to the hospital and were really sorry they couldn’t come but they’d make it up to him, promise. Zach hadn’t cared about that. He just wanted his Mommy to get better. So, if he had to go over to the Gross’s house and play with Jamie and Seth for a few hours, that was fine. Besides, Seth’s Mommy and Daddy had just gotten him some cool new games for his Playstation and Seth had promised Zach that they’d be able to play them when he came over. The ride to the Gross’s had been weird. Sure, Mrs. Gross had talked to him about school and stuff…but she sounded so scared the whole way. All the grownups he knew were behaving that way, Zach noticed. Like they thought he was going to break or something. He wasn’t a baby. He knew his Mommy was sick. Okay, so Mommy and Daddy hadn’t told him everything…but that was because, as Daddy had explained, there was some stuff that only grownups could know about. So he was okay with that. Daddy had come to pick him up after a few hours, thanking Mrs. Gross for looking after him. She had talked to Daddy like she had talked to him and Daddy just nodded. When they got home, Daddy said that he could see Mommy for a few minutes but he had to be really quiet because Mommy really needed her sleep. Zach went into his Mommy’s bedroom. He stood on his tippytoes next to the bed, placing a kiss on his Mommy’s cheek. "I love you, Mommy," he whispered. He then left the room as quietly as he had entered and joined his Daddy on the couch on the living room. They’d watched a movie, the one with the panda. They’d talked about school and the kids in class and what he’d gotten up to that day. And when Zach told his Daddy that he was worried for Mommy, Lance didn’t say anything. He just held his son close to him as Zach cried. It is 1:47 a.m. and more than anything else in the world, Alanna just wants the vomiting to stop. Dinner had been a minimal affair. Lance and Zach had eaten normally but she…she hadn’t had much appetite. Oh, she’d forced something down because she knew she’d be far worse off if she didn’t. She hadn’t particularly enjoyed it and her stomach seemed to be somewhat annoyed by the intrusion…but it hadn’t been nearly as bad as she’d been told. That had changed about twenty minutes ago. She had been in a restless mood, tossing and turning, trying desperately to get some sleep…when her stomach, which had been cranky all night, suddenly took a lurch so nasty that it had sent her sprinting for the bathroom. She had just made it to the toilet before she started throwing up what little she had eaten. That had taken a scant few moments and still her stomach had continued to seize up, spasming and clutching until she started to cry with the pain. She was barely aware of the soft padding of footsteps behind her as she sat on the marble floor, wasn’t aware of Lance’s presence until he pressed a cold washcloth to her face. First, he dabbed gently at her head and face before cleaning the area around her mouth. Alanna didn’t struggle, simply drawing in close to her husband, drawing comfort from the heat and warmth of his bare torso. She was vaguely aware of Lance flinging the washcloth away and it landing with a plop in the sink. As she snuggled into Lance, Alanna could feel herself start to fade away, her eyes closing and a heavy veil of darkness descending upon her. As his wife slipped into unconsciousness, Lance gathered her up into his arms. He carried her back into the bedroom, placing her down upon the bed with the utmost tenderness and care. He then quietly crept into bed alongside her, wrapping his arms gently around her sleeping form. Eventually, despite his best efforts, sleep overcame Lance Marshall as well.
