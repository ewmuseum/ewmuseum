Date: Today "It is a good rule in life never to apologize. The right sort of people do not want apologies, and the wrong sort take a mean advantage of them." ~P.G. Wodehouse * * * Right now there is an e-mail sitting in Alanna Marshall’s inbox. Inside of that e-mail is an attachment. The attachment is a video file. The filename is: "urstilldying.mpeg". When she opens it, this will happen. * * * Audio feed. "First off, you show Lance this…and I start making designs on that kid of yours. We clear?" Fade up. Lane Stevens wears a polite smile. "Good." Behind the River Rat the San Francisco skyline shifts. He is on a small boat. Lane wears a "Get Fucked" t-shirt (a pretty big seller on the old FUSE website) shades, and a sailor hat. He looks comfortable, but then again he always does. His conscience is clearer than that of Jonathan Rhine, or Lance Marshall himself. "We don’t really need him getting more worked up, ya know? Heard a rumor he was drinking backstage. Tragic." The Warlord shakes his head. "Now if I had done this to you fine people, and of course I’m only speaking hypothetically, but if I had…what’s the big problem? It was timed so that you didn’t miss any scheduled treatments, you got a few weeks of happiness, maybe the last few truly happy weeks you’ll ever have. Now, stop me if I’m wrong, but shouldn’t I be hypothetically thanked? Plus now you get all this renewed attention again, its like having a birthday every day. Except you’re going to die soon…so we’ll call them death days. But shit, who doesn’t like balloons?!?" From off camera, balloons enter the frame. Lane bats them about, smiling broadly. "See? Look at how much fun I’m having! Sure, you won’t have this kind of energy…but the basic principle is there." After a few moments he tips them out of the frame again. "But anyway, there’s this favor I’m going to need from you. So listen up, kay babe?" * * * A lot of casinos could be a lonely place at 4:30 in the morning, but the MGM Grand was not one of those. Various dinging noises ringed out, senior citizens robotically pulled slot machine levers, rich men cursed at the craps table, and drunks aimlessly wandered. If you were to spend enough time in a casino at this point in the morning, it would be easy to become convinced that the human race was doomed. This was not Nigel Kensington’s crowd, but he couldn’t sleep. In fact, he felt like it had been weeks since he got a good night’s sleep. The president of SCCW sipped on an orange juice, and played video poker. His suit made him blend in with the thousands of other run down people in this town, men who had come here on business and been swallowed whole by the city. There were dark rings around his eyes, and a blank expression on his face. In the last few days Nigel had blamed himself for what had happened to so many people, the Marshall’s included. But at the same time, he didn’t know what else he could have done. Sure, he could have suspended or fired Stevens after the attack on his person, but wouldn’t that have made things worse? Desade knew more about him than he was comfortable with. Certainly, her retaliation would have been swift. But good god, something could have been done. Something. If he had lost control, if he had really lost control once and for all, was he really the right man for this job? "How’s the machines treatin’ ya?" a voice snapped him out of his thoughts. He was not even playing the game anymore, only staring lifelessly ahead. Nigel quickly turned his head to the side, his eyebrows raising in shock. Jeb Stewart smiled sadly at the fear on Kensington’s face. Nigel had devloped post traumatic stress disorder after being mauled by Lane’s faction of the Dead Man’s Hand. "Sit down, sit down. I’m not gonna hurt ya," Stewart said quietly, taking a seat at the next machine over. Kensington froze for a moment, going over all his options. He seated himself a moment later. * * * "I’m assuming that won’t be a problem for ya. You can just shoot the pictures over to me at this e-mail address, I’m sure you have a scanner. Lance has made a lot of bank over the years." Lane Stevens is still talking. Still picking away at scabs. "And if you even get an idea about not complying…well, again. Let’s just hope it doesn’t come to that. Hasn’t the Marshall family suffered enough?" He smiles without malice. If you didn’t know any better, he wasn’t enjoying this. "I’m not going to apologize for all of this Alanna…because, well, I don’t apologize. I was hired to do horrible things to people, and Lance is here to stop me from doing horrible things to people. It’s just the nature of the game. I can assure you, Alexandra did not authorize anything that happened to you, and if she could go back in time and undo any of it…I can say with the utmost confidence that she would. But there is no rewind button, is there? Your husband may very well beat the living hell out of me, he may end my career. Because, and here’s the thing, he’s better at wrestling than me. And no matter what he has said to you…he last lost control, he wishes to actually kill me. But I will never be as afraid as you are right now, in your most private moments. I got over that a long time ago, laying in a hospital bed with a broken neck. I will not cower from The Lion. Your husband thinks that I am just another vulture picking away at an innocent man’s life, just a dumb kid who has kicked a hornet’s nest. That I don’t know what I’ve gotten myself into." The River Rat laughs uproariously. "Do you believe that, Alanna? Or do you know better?" Stevens smirks. "Yes, I think you do." Fade. "You might want to say goodbye to Lance. The man that comes back from Las Vegas will not be your husband." End. * * * "Are you serious?" Nigel Kensington asked. Jeb shrugged. "As far as I know." Kensington thought this over for a few moments. "I don’t have a problem with it, even though I feel like I should. I don’t feel like this is in anyone’s best interest, as much as I want to see it happen. Tell Lane it is now no DQ. The man might as well be signing his life away, though." "He done signed that away a long time ago," Jeb replied, getting up from the chair. As he walked away Nigel stopped him. "Jeb, surely you can’t…you can’t be onboard with all of this. Your mother she…" Kensington trailed off. Jeb stopped in his tracks, and took a deep breath. "Sometimes I pray that there isn’t a heaven, so she can’t see what it is I’ve done. But the other times…I feel nothing." Nothing.
