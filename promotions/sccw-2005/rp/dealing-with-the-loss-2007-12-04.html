Dealing with the Loss

4 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/4/07 15.1 the mirror: lost something special You’ve discovered that you have a grieving process. The funny thing is, there was no real reason for you to grieve. Katie has only been gone for about 13 days, but in that time you’ve gone through a lot of emotions, because she has been with you nonstop since April. It’s really weird being with someone every day for eight months and then suddenly having that person leave you. You spent the first few days sad, depressed, feeling like you had just broken up with someone. Any attempts to convince yourself the reality of the situation were futile. You couldn’t avoid feeling like you had lost something really special while you were all alone in your house. You decided that being alone was the problem and, as much as you didn’t want to, made the trip to New Orleans earlier than planned. Because you figured maybe some company would do you good and take your mind off of everything. You’ve really got to stop being wrong all the time. 15.2 the window: worth anything Jonathan Rhine lies on a pink comforter, his eyes closed, his mind relaxed and open. Jessica Rhine sits in a computer chair, twirling around in it lazily. "…so they’re going to rank me at 6th place instead of 5th because of the Driver’s Ed grade, and it wasn’t weighted, so now I can’t graduate on stage with the top five graduates, and mom is really upset with the school but they can’t do anything, and…" Jessica notices that Jonathan isn’t really listening, so she stops spinning and looks at him. "What’s wrong, Jonny?" "Hm? Oh, nothing." He looks up finally, looking at his sister and seeing that she is slowly becoming a woman now – worrying bout problems such as graduation when she will find much worse things as time goes on, he’s sure. "I’m sorry, I’m just really tired from the flight yesterday, and…" "That’s bullshit," she says, smiling. "You slept all of yesterday. Mom laughed and said it was like you hadn’t missed a day in your old bed." Jon laughs, but Jessica shakes her head. "So I know it’s not that. What’s really wrong?" Jonathan thinks of an excuse to tell her, and in the end decides it’s best to tell her the truth – or at least a version of it. "Just missing Katie, is all." Jessica’s face turns red as her grin grows wider. "Awwww," she says, beginning her spinning routine again. "You guys are so cute together. I really am glad that you two moved in together, even though the rest of the family hates that you moved to Indiana. To me, though, if you have a love like that, it’s worth it." She looks off to the ceiling, her smile growing into a dazed pensive stare. "Worth anything." Jonathan laughs. "You’re not even 18, what do you know about love?" "I know enough," she says simply. "We’re not talking about me, though. We’re talking about you and Katie. How did you two even meet?" "Well, we worked together at the grocery store for two years after my scholarship to Rice fell through. I was down on my luck and feeling sorry for myself; plus, I was the only person working there not at college. I felt like it was going to be my job for the rest of my life, and so I was bitter to everyone around me." "Even Katie?" Jessica’s smile has disappeared now; she is completely immersed in Jonathan’s story. "Yeah," Jon said, smiling at his sister’s enthusiasm. "We didn’t really get along. I liked her and thought she was beautiful, but made no move because I didn’t really talk to anyone. I didn’t have the confidence until I started wrestling." "So then you asked her out?" Jessica asks, still full of excitement. "Yeah," Jonathan says, but his voice trails off as he looks down, his face losing its previous luster. Jessica notices, and she puts her hand on his arm. "Jonathan, what’s wrong now?" 15.3 the mirror: nightmare What’s wrong is that you’ve reached the second stage of dealing with being alone for the week: contemplation. You’re thinking about everything about your relationship with Katie: the good times, the bad times, and above all else the beginning. The beginning is what matters, because it was when you first get impressions of the person you’re dating, when you’re the least open to their faults and intricacies. And for you, it was a first date that you’ve tried hard to forget about now, because it wasn’t something you want to think about too much. It was a nightmare. 15.4 the window: nice February 28, 2007 "Thanks," Katie says tentatively as Jonathan clumsily holds out a chair for her to sit down. He moves around the other end and sits, looking around nervously. "This is a nice place," she says, but her eyes don’t reflect her statement. "So, how have you been doing since you quit at Jubilee?" "I’ve done well," Jonathan says, smiling. "I’ve made a few bucks doing some house shows for Mr. Nackedy’s school, and everyone seems really impressed." Katie nods. "That’s…nice." Again her eyes betray her, and Jonathan notices. "Yeah…how’s school?" he asks. "It’s good. I probably only have a year left if things work out right. I might even graduate in December." "December? That would be great," he answers. The waiter comes by and takes their orders, and once he leaves Jonathan looks at Katie. "So what’s after that?" "I don’t really know…" she says, shrugging. "I actually would hate to leave the grocery store, because I love it there." "Really?" Jonathan laughs. "I hated it there." Katie looks down, uncomfortably. "Well, that’s probably because you didn’t spend enough time getting to know people. If you were friendlier…" Jonathan snorts. "Friendlier? I was excluded and treated like I was a moron because I wasn’t in college. They made jokes about me and always made me feel like shit. Was I supposed to suck up to them or something? Beg them to treat me nicely?" Katie’s face turns red as she sees a side of the quiet bag boy she had never seen before. "I just…nevermind. Let’s change the subject." Instead of changing the subject, neither of them speak for the rest of the meal. As they exit the restaurant, Jonathan sighs and tries to find something to say. "I’m sorry for what I said earlier. I didn’t mean to say…" "It’s okay," Katie murmurs, but once again her eyes show the truth. Jonathan sighs once more and lets her through the door first, looking back to make sure he got his jacket. By the time he turns around, a body flashes in front of him and Katie falls backwards into his arms. "Wha…" "My purse!" Katie shouts, pointing at the man running down the street, dressed in black. Without a word to Katie, Jonathan takes off running after him, gaining ground quickly. The man turns a corner, and Jonathan uses his hand to grab a light pole and swing himself forward. He reaches for the man in black, but he barely eludes him. The man with the purse sees a fence and goes for it, pausing a second so he can hop over. Jonathan does not need to pause, as he leaps straight over the fence and onto the guy, crushing him. The man lets out an exhale of air as Jonathan punches him in the head and grabs the purse from him. Lifting him up by the neck, Jonathan looks at him and says. "Get out of here, and don’t steal from anyone ever again." The man in black runs away, his hands going to his neck as he does so. After a moment, Jonathan returns to Katie with the purse in her hand. Her smile is wider than it has been all night, and this time her eyes match. "Thank you! That was so nice! No…that was…amazing." Slightly out of breath, Jonathan shrugs. "I wasn’t about to let him get away with that…" he starts, but isn’t able to finish as Katie kisses him hard on the mouth. He pulls her in closer and closes his eyes, thinking that the date turned out okay after all. 15.5 the mirror: is it enough But it really didn’t turn out okay. You can mark the end as a good thing, but in reality it was merely a successful ending to a terrible date. The two of you had no chemistry, nothing to talk about, and you didn’t understand anything about each other. But the kiss changed everything. Afterwards you called her the next day and asked her out again. Your second date was better; you basically talked about the end to your first date, and how proud she was of you, and how she told everyone that you were her hero…and it stuck. It built a foundation that your relationship has stood on ever since. But is it enough? You try to think about anything else in the early stages of your relationship that was a positive structure of bonding, but you are hard-pressed to find one. All you can think about now is your devotions to her in the wrestling ring. But was that only because you were on camera? You’re not entirely sure, and that scares you. You’ve always taken your love for Katie for granted, as if there was no doubt that it was there. But now, as you sit there and recall the beginning of something Jessica sees as "worth anything," you ruminate over it and wonder if it’s really worth anything at all. As you think about this, you have come to the third and final step of loss: the realization that you didn’t need that person at all, and you will be just fine without them. The only problem with that is Katie is coming back. 15.6 the window: happy couple "What’s wrong?" Jessica asks again. "Nothing," he sighs. "Let’s go back to the kitchen and see what mom’s doing for dinner." As Jessica and Jonathan leave her room and walk through the hallway, Jonathan thinks he hears a door open in the front of the house. When he gets to the kitchen, he sees the food cooking, but his mom isn’t there. As he turns to the hallway towards the front door, he already knows what’s coming. "Jonathan?" his mother calls from the front. "Can you come out here now?" He reluctantly follows her voice into the living room, where he sees Katie standing there, a big smile on her face. "Hey, baby. I got your message. Ready to win that thing?" She runs into his arms as he instinctively hugs her, looking over her shoulder to see his mother and Jessica smiling, so excited for the happy couple. The happy couple that Jonathan may no longer be happy with. 15.7 the mirror: really spending time She’s back in town, and everything you thought of to deal with her being gone can now be eliminated. Only you’re not so sure. Because you’ve thought yourself into a big hole, now. You spent all of that time questioning your relationship so you could cope with being alone, but even though she’s back, you’re still thinking about those questions. Whether your relationship was built on a shaky ground, whether you can truly be together without knowing anything about each other. And whether you can live without her. Amazingly, you haven’t thought much about Sarah this week, but now that you’ve thought about what you’re doing with Katie, Sarah becomes an even stronger and stronger prospect. There might be some scary things about dating your psychologist, but at least you guys have a connection. You don’t even know if you and Katie like each other. But she’s your girlfriend, and she’s been there for you. She’ll support you as you show everyone that you can beat the best, and that has to count for something. So you promise yourself you’re going to spend more time with her, really spending time. Knowing her, figuring out who she is, and seeing if you can establish a real connection. Before you throw away a nine month relationship, you want to see if there’s actually something there. The worst part of it is, a small part of you is praying there isn’t.
