The Brothers Apell, or, The Obligatory Introduction.

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: The day after yesterday. You may become confused over the next several months. While you will no doubt think that what you see is about the two men – my brothers -- wrestling in the ring, this story that’s going to unfold is about me and me alone. While they are able to entertain you through their unbelievable athletic prowess, I hope to show you that they would be nothing without my incredible mental ability. First, a word about my brothers, Dag and David: They are idiots, yet, they are my idiots and I love them beyond all scope and bound. If I were capable, there would be nothing I would do for them. Had I the strength in my arms, the breath in my lungs or the life in my veins, I would prove to them that I would stand and fight at their sides. I would surf waves with them, eat their precious fish tacos, and speak with them in my own voice. Unfortunately, I cannot. The only strength I have is in two fingers on my left hand, and occasionally, my thumb. My eyes move, my lips tremble on rare occasion, but, for all other intents and purposes, I am nothing more than a liability to these two men who’ve sworn to take care of me. I am trapped in my own brain, the only way to express myself is through the buttons on the keypad that I touch now, talking to you. There are no buttons for the primal scream I would emit if it were so. There are no ways to express emotion – just the cold, somewhat robotic voice of Nancy, the woman who speaks my words. Of the brothers, I am the oldest by six minutes. We’ve always been close, though the two are far closer, born at the same time, thanks to an emergency C-section my mother endured to save the brothers from certain death; Ironic that they’re such free spirits now, as their first moments were of life and death. Perhaps it’s why they’re so free spirited. They have nothing to fear, aside from the certainty they’ve faced once before. I lived my life alongside them, and we used to surf and play together. We entered the wrestling business together, and the day before my first match, I felt a tingle in my leg. Four hours later, I was told that my body would eventually deteriorate to nothingness. I would become a living rock, no more alive than a dead body, with the curse of a brain active within my head, a heart pumping blood to useless limbs, a nervous system that knew nothing about the pain it could feel. I would never wrestle, and soon after, I lost my legs to atrophy. If you’re familiar with Stephen Hawking, we’re similar, though I fear he’s slightly more intelligent than I am. "Dude, what are you doing?" Dag asked me, a moment ago. In fact, it was close to six minutes ago. I chose to ignore him, as I often do. He’s easily distracted, and the sound of a wave roaring to shore piqued his curiousity. David stood in the ocean outside of Playa Del Mar and smelled the air, Dag walking from where I sit on the beach joining him. At times, I think they’re taunting me, showing what they can do with their bodies, of which I cannot. Other times, I think they’re having enough fun for the three of us, and it may seem strange, but, I feel what they do, through some cosmic connection. The two began playing leapfrog in the water, leaping over each other into the ocean, laughing heartily. Two of their friends joined them, and soon, it turned to a splash party. I laughed, my throat actually making a sound, a tear faling from my eye a second later in shock and awe. Dag and David heard it and rushed over, smiling. "Dude! You said something! Awesome!" Dag cried out. "Dude! What’d he say, you think?" David wondered. "Maybe he was talking about the gulls." "Seems an awfully weird thing to say after two years, man. What was he saying before, dude?" "I think something about his knee. No, wait. Hey, I’m hungry." "Me too. Vegan breakfast burrito?" "Nah." "Vegan nachos?" "Nah." "Vegan breakfast burrito?" "Hell yes." I decided I'd tell them that I'd started their wrestling career again later. They don't talk over vegan breakfast burritos.
