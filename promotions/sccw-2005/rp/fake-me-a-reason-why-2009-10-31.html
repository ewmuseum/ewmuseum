Fake Me A Reason Why

31 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/31/09 It was the laziest excuse for a Green Lantern costume that he could come up with. Black sweat pants, a long sleeved black shirt, all complemented by a DC Comics licensed green tee shirt. Jared even had a sterling silver power ring replica, which at the very least lent some credibility to the outfit. His mask though, that was truly a sorry little number. It was fashioned from a dress sock (most people were surprised to know that he ever had formal attire to begin with) with two small holes cut for his eyes and tied in the back. On a child it might have been cute. On Jared it just looked sad. The evening had seen a steady stream of costume-clad visitors. Jared's ego wanted to believe this had to do with some celebrity status he might have earned. In truth he was fairly well known in the area, though the number of trick-or-treaters was better attributed to word getting out among the neighborhood that Jared was handing out full-sized candy bars. Wrestler or not, no kid in their right mind had it in them to pass up that opportunity, which is why as the hours grew later the candy reserves had grown precariously low. 'Ouch!' the girl in the princess costume cried out. 'Jacob,' the woman at the door scolded through clenched teeth, 'stop touching Alysson's butt!' Jacob, the small blond boy in the sun costume, did as his mother commanded, opting instead to hold out his plastic pumpkin in hopes of receiving a delicious treat. As the Black Sheep obliged his request he couldn't help but think the kid reminded him of someone. 'Thank you,' the woman said, gathering up the children and herding them off the front stairs. The last thing Jared heard before he closed the door was a chorus of 'Happy Halloween!' said in tiny voices. The last thing he saw was the young boy in the sun costume reaching out again for the girl in the princess outfit. "Jacob!" It was loud enough that Jared heard it through the door, chuckling to himself as he set the empty bowl on a nearby table. All that remained was to turn out the light over the front steps, the universal sign that the house would no longer be handing out candy. For a moment he was sad. In his childhood he’d looked forward to stopping at every house, and missing one only meant disappointment. Next year, he thought, I’ll stockpile. He slid his makeshift mask up onto his forehead and headed for the living room. Tradition dictated that he'd burn through a stack of DVDs, each movie carefully selected because of its low production value and potential for accidental comedy. Tradition also dictated that Mervin would bring the Chinese food, but he was currently out of state trying to market an entrepreneurial venture. The list of replacements was also sparse. Gabe and Jamie were out with Emma, who according to all reports was forcing her father to wear his Blueberry costume. Darren and Vanessa were no doubt out with Marty. Amy was four thousand miles away, plus there was always the chance Alex might intercept any calls or text messages. In truth he'd rather crawl naked through razorblades than risk that. He was about to hit 'play' on the first film of the evening when a ring of the doorbell diverted his attention. A quick glance at the light switch confirmed that he had indeed shut the outside lights off, though his first thought was still that he would be forced to deliver some bad news to a group of children on his front steps. With a sigh he pushed himself up out of the couch, pulled his mask back down over his eyes, and stepped over to the door. 'I'm sorry,' he said, pulling it open. 'I'm afraid I'm all out of...' It was a warm night, especially for fall in New England. The news had reported highs around seventy degrees for the day, and though the night had brought cooler air and the feeling of impending rain it didn't change the temperature to the point where a sweatshirt was necessary. Though, in fairness, the weather had little to do with the reason Justine Calvin wore that particular hoodie. His hoodie. 'Hey.' It was all he could get out, and given the circumstances even that was something of a feat. 'Hi,' she said back softly, eyeing his outfit. She grinned slightly as he rushed to pull the sock-turned-mask away from his face. He let his body fall against the threshold, bracing himself between it and the door for support. 'I called you.' 'I know.' She tucked her hands into the shirt's front pocket, rocking back and forth softly on the balls of her feet. He let his gaze fall to a small collection of leaves that had piled up near the railing; anything to avoid eye contact. 'So,' she said, raising her eyes to try and meet his. He didn't return the look. 'So.' Instinctively, he tried to slide his hands into the pockets of his pants, feeling a bit sheepish when he realized they didn't have any. He fumbled for a moment, trying to think of something to do with or somewhere to put his hands. He clasped them behind his back, then in front, and then finally settled on simply crossing his arms. It took the whole of Justine's will not to laugh in spite of her own nerves. 'So do you think maybe I could come in?' She tucked a loose strand of hair behind her ear. 'I guess.' He stepped away from the threshold and pushed the door open, still unable to meet her eyes. She stepped through without a word. 'I promise not to throw up in the hall this time.' For the first time since arriving she managed to earn a reaction beyond stoic half-silence. He chuckled once. It was weak, but it was there. 'Great.' Jared followed her through the hallway (pausing to remove the ring he still wore) and into the kitchen. She took a seat at the small table in the corner of the room. He simply leaned against the wall. "Haven’t seen you at Darren’s at all," she said, leaning forward onto the tabletop. "I go, just…" "Not when I’m there." "Something like that." She cocked her head, resting it on her right hand. She wasn’t getting much in the way of answers, but that didn’t change her outlook on the situation. All things considered she thought it was going remarkably well. If nothing else he was at least forming complete sentences. "I kind of expected you to just show up one day, demanding answers." "Not really my style." He pulled his gaze away from a particularly interesting piece of floor tile to look at her, and their eyes met for the first time since she’d arrived on his doorstep. "I figured that if a few weeks of calls weren’t going to get your attention then it wouldn’t do me any good to try and hash this out in person. I figured you made your opinion on the subject pretty clear." Jared pushed away from the wall and once again tried to slide his hands into pockets that didn’t exist. "I also hoped that we were past this." He stepped closer to the table but did not sit. Instead he let his hands find rest on the back of a nearby chair, giving him something tangible to hold on to. "Like, I guess I can kind of understand how it got weird the first time around. I mean, we were young, and you could probably argue that we didn’t know any better even though I think we’d both know it’s a lie, but this…" "I know," she said weakly. "Do you though?" His smile was nervous and short-lived. "I guess in a sense we’re doing better than before, since we’re nine years and one bleeding Jared ahead of schedule." She winced slightly at the reminder of their first meeting in a decade; a meeting where she’d unleashed her bottled-up frustrations and lashed out physically. The encounter left him with a black eye plus two bruised ribs that he never confessed two. "I kind of almost wish you’d just take a swing now, Cal’." He pulled the chair away from the table, taking a seat opposite her. "It would probably be a lot easier. I’m sorry. Like, I don’t mean to start ranting, but… this just really sucks." "No, you’re right." She shrugged. "I guess I just didn’t know how to handle it, you know? Didn’t know what I was supposed to do." The change in expression on Jared’s face, slight as it was, signaled that this was not the kind of response he was hoping for. "So you decided to handle it by not handling it at all?" "Come on, Jared. That’s not fair." "No, you’re right. It’s not fair. It’s not fair at all. Ten seconds of your life. That’s all I wanted. Just ten seconds even if it was to hear that you needed to figure this out, it’s certainly better than the alternative." "What the hell does that mean?" The remark had put her on the defensive, evidenced by her change in tone. "What alternative?" "This! The alternative to dealing with shit is this, where you show up at my house after a month of hiding from me and we argue about it. And this is stupid, because there’s something here, Cal’. There always has been. I carried a picture and you carried a grudge, so I know I can’t be the only one who sees it." "No, you’re not." "Then… Like… Is there something so wrong with me that you would rather run than give this a chance?" "No. Jared, it’s not like that." "Then this fucking sucks. So, what, we just sleep together when things get tense and we need to take the edge off? I don’t want that. Twice now things have gone from good, to momentarily amazing, to god-awful, and each time I get stuck wondering what happened. I can’t tell you what it’s like for me. I think I’d rather you just flat-out tell me that you hate the though of being with me." "But I don’t!" "Then what’s the problem? Why do I feel like I’m living the lyrics to ‘Purple Rain’? If there’s something here then why the hell are we fighting about it?" He pushed his chair away from the table, slouching back and letting his eyes wander to an empty corner of the room. "I know what I want. I just… I wish I knew what you want." "I don’t know what to tell you, Jared. I honestly don’t." She glanced quickly across the table, for the first time really making note of Jared’s outfit. In that moment, and even then only for a second, she questioned her sanity in arguing about relationships with a man dressed like a superhero. "Is there something here? Is there chemistry, or whatever you want to call it? Yeah, there is. But…" "And this is where it goes to hell," he whispered to himself. He believed he’d said it soft enough for only him to hear. The hard look he caught from across the table corrected that thought. "Yeah, maybe it is," she said, folding her arms defiantly. "I don’t know what I want, Jared. Maybe it sounds like a cop out, but it’s the truth. I’ve thought about it, and I don’t have an answer. I just… I just don’t." He sank lower in his chair. For a moment he began to regret answering the door. "So where does that leave us?" he said at last, steeling himself for an answer he dreaded hearing. "I don’t know that, either." * * * * * The first kiss broke through his defenses, but the second is what left them in ruin. It didn’t matter that he knew this was wrong, or that it had the potential to end just as badly as the first time, that particular voice in his brain had been temporarily silenced by the promise of something he hadn’t known in years. Sex was common; it was the intimacy that was rare. Still he had his conscience to contend with. "Are you sure you want to do this?" he gasped in between fevered kisses. "I thought I told you to shut up." Her lips grazed his skin as she whispered into his ear, sending a pulse through his nervous system and forcing the hairs on his neck to stand on end. The little voice in the back of his head was starting to grow louder. He would need greater reassurance before he could concede. "I just don’t want this to get weird." If she thought it might shut him up Justine would have hit him in the mouth then and there. Instead she again pressed her lips to his, parting them slightly and allowing her tongue to dance with his. Biology had taken hold of both of them, and she knew that it was only a matter of time before Jared gave in. If she wasn’t so damned impatient she might actually have waited. As it stood she felt no harm in taking charge and moving things along. "Jared," she cooed, cradling his face in her hands. "Don’t make me say it again." His shirt was the first casualty. He’d untucked it earlier in the evening when things had become more relaxed, which made it all the easier for her to contend with. The buttons were undone with such ferocity that at least two were torn free, landing on the hardwood floor with a tic-tac too soft for either of them to hear. His shirt soon followed, and with it a simple white undershirt. She leaned in again, this time pressing a hand against the bare skin of his chest. His heart raced fast enough that he feared it might punch through his sternum, joining his clothes in a pile on the floor. "Wow, we’ve really got you worked up." Her words were followed by a playful bite at his bottom lip, tugging it away gently and letting it snap back. "You have no idea." The voice he had heard, first quiet, then loud, was now muted. Its last words drowned out by the pounding of his heart echoing in his head. "You going to be okay?" She pulled the elastic from her hair, letting rich brown locks flow to her shoulders. His right hand found the zipper at the back of her dress, drawing it down between her shoulder blades to the small of her back. The dress itself soon joined his pair of shirts in a heap on the floor. "I’ll be fine." She gasped as he lifted her off her feet, smiling playfully as he carried her to the couch, the closest soft surface to where they’d stood. Within a few seconds a new pile of clothes had taken shape in what served as the living room of her studio apartment. He closed his eyes, pressed his lips to hers, and grinned to himself as she arched her back and took in a sharp breath of air. "Jared," she purred. He was never happier at the sound of his own name. He opened his eyes and glanced upon the redheaded beauty beneath him. With a gasp of his own he threw himself violently backwards, tumbling off of the couch and onto the floor. The shock of the dream had jarred him awake. "Motherfucker!" he cursed, rubbing his elbow where it had connected with the glass top of rather weighty coffee table. Panicked, his eyes scanned the room. He was home, he was alone, and he was still very much dressed like a superhero’s understudy. This particular dream was nothing new. He’d experienced it at least twice a week since their encounter mid-September, but this time there was something different; something startling enough to pull him out of the dream, and right before the good part no less. Unfortunately, that particular detail didn’t survive the trip back to a waking state.
