The Risk of Degradation

15 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15 August Risk is an interesting characteristic in a world made up of far too many characteristics. It is one of the few that generates interest and exhilaration amongst us. The general theme of a risk goes; the bigger the risk the bigger the reward and thus the bigger the cost. If it all pays off it is one of the most satisfying feelings one can experience. However, when it all blows up in your face, when it has completely ruined you in your own mind, then there can be nothing as deflating. Dualta had always considered risk to be measured within his own ability, and he had never doubted his ability. Now he has taken a risk and he failed. What ability? One of the few perks of what many considered one of the worst airports in the world was that it was in Queens, only fifteen minutes away from home. Dualta exited through the revolving doors of JFK airport with his tail between his legs. His weariness was visualised through the black bags under his eyes and his refusal to look in any direction except the two metres of concrete in front of him as he made his way to the taxi stand. Cabs were queuing anxiously, waiting to commit extortion on their next unassuming victim. Dualta went straight to the front of the queue, a large Nike sports bag in wrapped over his right shoulder. The small Indian taxi driver exited the driver’s seat of the cab and, with a rare enthusiasm, grabbed the bag from Dualta, placing it neatly in the open trunk before slamming it shut. The cabbie had a smile on his face as he ushered Dualta into the back of the cab. Dualta was somewhat bemused with this behaviour, although he found it refreshing. How could a taxi driver who does this day in and day out still do it with a skip in his step? It would almost give someone who was losing all hope a little faith in this so-called existence. "Where to?" Asked the cabbie as he turned round to Dualta from the front seat. "Lower Sunnyside, just off Queens Boulevard." Responded Dualta in a grateful yet monotonous tone. The cab took off. Dualta rested his head on the back of the seat looking up at the ceiling. His large skinny hands draped over his face before rubbing his stubble with vigour. No matter what he did, where he looked he could not get the last week of his life out of his mind. It perplexed him. He was a good fighter, a very good fighter. He knew that. So why, when he stepped onto a boat with five "wrestlers" was he embarrassed? He shifted aimlessly, he just could not settle. The cabbie glanced through his rear view mirror. His overgrown eyes seemed inquisitive. His eyes narrowed and his mouth formed an O shape. He was trying to decipher something as he peered at Dualta. Suddenly a realisation struck his face. "Hey, I know you!" the cabbie said with a victorious tenor. Dualta did not look at him; he simply gazed out the window as rain began to shower down upon the streets of New York with an ironic ferocity, summing up his journey in it entirety. Pathetic fallacy. "Oh yeah?" said Dualta, barely listening. "Yeah, you are a Sheridan! Man I thought you were all dead! I have seen you in the paper!" The cabbie was desperately trying to convene with American expressionism, unsuccessfully. Dualta did not reply. He had no intentions to be unmannerly; he just did not have the energy to absorb the attention of z-list cult local celebrity status. However, Dualta obvious reluctance to involve himself in conversation did not diminish the cabbie’s enthusiasm. "Where were you anyway? I’d think when you Sheridan’s get on a plane you wouldn’t be coming back in a hurry." Said the cabbie as if speaking to himself. Dualta smiled, he found the carefree straight talking stance of the cabbie droll. He was warming to him. "I was off making a fool out of myself, thinking I could change things. I guess I glorified my capabilities." In some way the cabbie seemed to understand. He went no further, Dualta’s evaluation was enough. It was his stop. He opened the door of the taxi and slowly exited. He was stiff, he had really pushed himself physically over the past week, that made the taste of defeat that much more astringent. He did not really have the money to give the cabbie a decent tip, but he did anyway. ______________________________________________________________________________________________________________________ He did not talk to anyone since entering the building. He met his cousin Seamus on the way to his room, not much was said. Dualta did not really know if anyone knew where he had been, he was sure they would find out if they had not already. SCCW was a massive organisation, and All In! was a worldwide Pay-Per-View. He was sure his father and uncles would be furious with him. He had just advertised himself on international television. Not really what the Russians had in mind when they were talking about all Sheridan related activities being done discreetly. What to do now then? He sat in a dark room of the building, the window looked out onto the well-lit streets of Sunnyside. Rain poured down in frightening fashion. It was a makeshift gym. Dumbbells, a bench, a punching bag, a treadmill, just about any piece of equipment members of the family had got to take with them when they were kicked out of their homes. They needed this stuff. Sheridan’s were not taught to fight with guns, they were taught with their fists, it was all they knew. Dualta assessed his options. He could cut his losses and quit the SCCW, maybe in forty years he could talk about his fifteen minutes... of humiliation that is. Or he could fight back. He did not know Josef Basturk, he did not care who he was. Too much was at stake, he had to win this time, or else he would spend his life in the gutter, watch his three-year-old niece grow up with nothing. That was not an option. All of a sudden Dualta rose. He hit the punch bag once, and he did not stop for the next forty minutes. This time he would be ready. Motivation is a funny thing.
