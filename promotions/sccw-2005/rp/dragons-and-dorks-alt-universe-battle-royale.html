It was an afternoon like any other in the proud realm of Bluetopia, and the lord of this realm, King Blueberry, looked out over his subjects from the sitting room window in his mighty tower. On this particular afternoon, and joined by his most trusted advisor, the king gazed down through a pair of binoculars at a lovely farm girl attending to her crop of watermelons. Okay, let’s not kid ourselves, they were checking out her rack. "Look, sire, a group of riders approaches!" Mervinius, the king’s advisor, a thin man whose appearance seemed better suited to the inside of a laboratory than a king’s court, pointed into the distance. Indeed a group of riders were approaching. Few in number and showing signs of recent battle, they rode past the outer gates, beyond the local Best Buy, through the French Quarter, and up to the castle where they were greeted by the king’s trusted gatekeeper, Lloyd the Butch, a tall man with a coarse beard and a fondness for descriptive sentences with excessive punctuation. "Well, there goes my afternoon," the king said, putting down his binoculars next to a stack of Playberry magazines on a nearby table. "Sucks. A whole day of melon-watchin’ down the toilet." For a man of his stature, the king’s language tended to be a bit "colorful". "I suppose we should go meet ‘em, huh?" "They will most likely wish to see the king, sire." "Fiddlesticks." A few moments later saw the king welcoming the group of travelers in his throne room, a large, cavernous space complete with fog machine and disco ball. A "Velvet Elvis" hung on the near wall next to some lava lamps and a pink flamingo. "Your majesty," the first visitor said, kneeling as he spoke, "we come bearing unfortunate news." The king convulsed in annoyance. Regardless of what he may thought upon seeing this motley gang from his tower window, he had secretly hoped they’d only been poorly dressed, and thus his afternoon of video games and napping would go on unabated. "So, uh, what is it?" "A dragon, your majesty." "COOL!" Clearly, this was not the response the weary travelers expected, but the leader continued to detail the events of their journey.
