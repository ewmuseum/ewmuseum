Everyone was happy at sometime...

15 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 15, 2007 16 Years Ago… It was a beautiful spring day. The birds were singing the song of Mother Nature, while the sun shone down brightly. Blooming wildflowers stretched upward, eager to take in the sun’s nurturing rays. The long blades of grass swayed as a slight breeze ruffled through the trees, adding to nature’s song. Butterflies swoop up and down in the currents of the wind, as though dancing for unseeing eyes. The earth is hilly, and trees, sparse. The bright beautiful colors of the wildflowers are present for as far as the eye can see. A small creek flowed, in between a few hills, its shallow water polishing the smooth white stones which lined it. "You can’t catch me!" She giggled as she ran, with her raven like curls trailing behind her. Her face shone brightly with the innocence only a child can possess. She stopped when she reached the top of one of the hills. Stretching out her arms, she spun around as if taking in the sights all around her. She lets out a squeal as I reach her. Grabbing a hold of her wrists I ran around in a circle, spinning her faster and faster, her feet just grazing the tops of the vibrant flowers. As I collapse from dizziness she laughs and skips away, joining the butterflies in their dance with nature. "Watch me Mayson!" I smile at her as she runs around the field. Turning away, I walk towards one of the few trees dotting the landscape. Underneath is our picnic basket, with lunch tucked safely away. Mother always did pack the best lunches for our little excursions in to the country side. Normally she would be right here beside us laughing and playing along, or nestled nearby with a book. However, today she says she’s not feeling well and to go on without her. I pretend not to hear the little footsteps as they shuffle up behind me through the grass. I grab the blanket from out of the basket and hold it up, letting the wind blow it out, before smoothing it out on the soft ground. Suddenly she jumps in front of me with an ear to ear grin. "Boo!" I pretend to be surprised and fall backwards. She giggles and lays a beautiful bouquet of flowers by my feet before grabbing my arm and trying to pull me up. She lets go as I sit up and begins to dance around the picnic basket. "Look what the butterflies taught me!" "That’s wonderful Heather. Why don’t you show Mother when we get home?" "Oh, Momma! I picked these flowers for her." She runs to pick up the flowers, thrusting them into my hand. Looking through them I find the 2 most beautiful ones, threading them through her hair. She reaches up and gently pokes at them, as though by feeling them she can see every detail. Her beaming smile fills up her face. "Do I look pretty?" "You look beautiful, brat. Time to eat now." And there we sat and ate, with nature’s soothing song always present. Most of the food was devoured, and the little that was left was packed away. I gathered the dishes, carefully stacking them and stood, heading towards the creek. "I’ll be back in a few minutes. Stay close to the tree. Mother will be expecting us home soon." Her little head bobbed a nod as I started down the hill. Soon I found myself humming to the tune of "Ragtime Lullaby", a song Mother used to sing to us. When I arrived at the creek I quickly washed off the dishes and turned around, ready to start back up the hill as something caught my eye. I set the dishes down carefully and reached in to the water, pulling out a shiny purple stone. I quickly shoved it in to my pocket, grabbed my load, and headed back up the hill. After reaching the picnic site and packing away the dishes, I looked around for Heather. She was nowhere to be seen. Frantically I climb to the top of the tree, standing perilously on the top branches as they sway with the wind. Off in the distance, a small unmoving shape flattens the grass. "Heather!" Still, no movement. I half climb, half jump out of the tree and run over to her. As I reach her she jumps up and the large black dog she had been lying on lumbers up and licks her face. She giggles and wraps her arms around its neck. I let out a relieved sigh and pat her on the top of her head. "His name is Mellow! Can we keep him?" "Mellow, huh? I don’t know, brat." "Pleeeease!" "Ha, you’ll have to wait and ask mother. He may already have a home you know, but for now it’s time for us to go. Hop on." As I squat down she jumps on my shoulders and grabs my hair as I stand up. As I trot back up the hill, ‘Mellow’ follows closely at my heels. Occasionally he runs ahead, barking, but never strays too far away from us. When we reach the hill, I bend down and Heather scoops up the picnic basket. I grab on to her legs and spin around in a quick circle, before heading back down the hill and towards home. Today… The footsteps echo off of the cold tile and plain walls as I hurry down the narrow hallway. Large black numbers are painted near the numerous doorways that line the white walls. 234…235…236…237…238…239 Standing there, I stare at those numbers trying to imagine what might be inside. Taking a deep breath, I quickly slip inside and close the door behind me. She’s lying on the bed, dark hair matted to her face from sweat. Her wrists and ankles are bound to the bed by leather restraints. She is wearing a white jumpsuit with the number 239 printed on the left breast. Her eyes are closed, and a line of drool has dried down her cheek. Occasionally her body will twitch or thrash against the restraints as though she’s dreaming. I slowly walk over and sit on the edge of the bed, pressing my hand against her forehead. Her eyes open and her body thrashes forward against the restraints. Her wild eyes scan the room before settling on my face and she sinks back in to the bed. "Hey, brat. It’s been awhile. How’re you holding up?" Her arm reaches towards me, but snaps back in to place against the bed. Her eyes seem to be piercing in to my thoughts, as though she can read them and is pleading with me to help. I silently nod to her as I undo one of her wrist straps. Her eyes double in size and she looks to her other wrist. I nod again, and undo the other wrist, leaving her to undo her feet. I wait patiently until she’s finished, and then I thrust my fist towards her. "I brought you something." She looks at me as though puzzled, but puts her hand underneath my fist. I open my hand and let the smooth, purple rock drop in to her palm. She falls back and leans against the wall, bringing the small rock up to her eyes. She rolls it over and over in her fingers, staring at her own reflection on its shiny surface. "Brought you something else, too." I pull a large blue collar out of my pocket and lay it on bed in front of her. She drops the rock and dives for the collar, clutching it in her hands. Clutching the collar close to her chest, she begins rocking back and forth mumbling to herself. I slowly rest my hand on her shoulder. Her head whips towards me, and her mouth turns in to a snarl. At the same time, her leg shoots out from under her and her foot catches me under the chin. I fall off the bed with a thud. "I WANT MELLOW!" "Heather, you know he’s gone." I immediately realize that was the wrong thing to say as she becomes hysterical and begins screaming inaudibly. She jumps off the bed and begins throwing herself at the wall. I walk towards her and try to grab her, but she slips out of my grasp and runs to the other side of the room. The door swings open and 3 large men rush in the room, quickly surrounding Heather. They carry her back to the bed, and replace the restraints on her. One of them herds me outside and begins lecturing me in a stern voice, but he’s drowned out with my own thoughts. As I turn away the man is throwing up his arms, and the sounds of Heather screaming drift down the hall. My head feels as though it’s in a vice. If we could only go back. Everyone was happy at some time…
