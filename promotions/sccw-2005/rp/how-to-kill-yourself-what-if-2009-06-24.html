How to Kill Yourself (What if...)

24 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/24/09 "There is no joy in life" – Flannery O’Connor * * * A voice. On this particular night, it stumbled over words. It was thick, awkward. It was drunk. "You ever play poker?" In response, another voice grunted. The sound was muffled. "Yeah, I know, I know. You’re mad. It’s cool." When we step out on the other end of the rabbit hole into one of many alternate universes you will be privy to this week, the setting is a damp basement. The floor was cement. Sporadic yellow stains dot the walls. This looks like the kind of place that has played host to several bad things. And as it turns out, something bad was happening right now. In the center of the room a woman was tied to a steel folding chair. There was a black hood over her head. Lane Stevens dragged another chair across the room with one hand, which made a most unbearable sound, and held a Corona in the other. "Mexicans. They can build roofs, and make beer." The River Rat laughed then. It was a lazy, intoxicated laugh. He stopped abruptly, feeling slightly embarrassed at the lack of humor in his statement. "I feel like roofs shouldn’t be a word though," he mused, "like maybe it should be rooves." He shrugged. "Anyway, I was talking about poker." Stevens placed the chair down backwards, and sat in it, facing the woman. He was dressed in a white button-up shirt, which seemed to have a few small blood stains, and khaki pants. It was not an outfit that seemed to mesh with his environment, except for the blood. He took a swig of the beer, and continued. "I was playing the 5/10 no-limit hold’em game at the Mandalay Bay poker room in Vegas. Pretty small room, a lot of drunk tourists. It’s the kind of place rounders go to rebuild their bankroll. My game is all fish, except one guy. When you look in his eyes, you forget he’s a human. Fuck I can’t even remember his face, just his eyes. Looked like a tiger. Anyway I’m up a couple grand for the night, I’m a little drunk and thinking of calling it a night when I get dealt two cowboys." Poker slang for pocket kings, in case you were wondering. The woman had stopped grunting beneath her hood, perhaps excepting her fate, perhaps thinking of a way out. "So this bad motherfucker, this Bengal tiger, he limps in for ten bucks. Couple folds to me, and I make it thirty five bucks to go. Folds back around to him, he calls the extra twenty five. The flop comes with two sevens and a four. He looks right at me, and moves in for over six big ones, for over six grand. It’s all of my chips. Now I assume some of this shit is familiar to you, cause of the company you keep. But it’s hard for me to put him on a huge hand here, cause you’d figure he’d try to trap me or something. But yet I don’t really feel comfortable calling off six thousand dollars with one pair, you know? That’s what the great ones do. They get you out of your comfort zone. This is true in poker as well as…well, all the shit you’re into." The River Rat chucked the empty beer, and it shatters on the wall. The woman did not flinch. He got up out of the chair and staggered over to a mini-fridge in the corner of the room, under a staircase. As you could have guessed, he retrieved another beer. "In the end, I think of all the shit I could do with that money. All kinds of pussy and video games and whatever, and I call. This fucker had pocket fours, flopped fours full of sevens. And he just moved in. MOVED IN!" He gestured dramatically, even though there was no one present that could see him. A moment later he sat back down in the chair. "It was the biggest pot I ever lost in my life at the time. And what I figured out, what this sick fucker, this tiger taught me…was that sometimes a play can be so fierce and direct, that it can’t help but work. See that’s the beauty of this play. Of all the people who have labored tirelessly over how to deal with you, all of the elaborate plans that have been cooked up my men and women twice as smart as me…I just, well…" Stevens leaned forward in the chair. "I just took you." He laughed, oh did he ever laugh. * * * A few months earlier. Iowa City, Iowa. Nigel Kensington walked down the hallway of the Holiday Inn Express he was staying at on that particular evening. Normally a man of his stature would be staying in a much finer establishment, but times were tough for the company, tougher than some people knew. He was setting an example to the rest of the higher-ups. These were not the only things that weighed on his mind. He was involved in a political war, one that required great subtlety and great force at the same time. Alexandra Pierce’s contract had many provisions, but he could not allow her to run roughshod, or at least as much as she had been. He needed warriors to enforce that on one level, while at the same time not provoking her into drastic action. Was this even possible? Or would he continue to play damage control for his entire tenure here before he eventually lost his goddamn mind just like many of Alexandra’s other opponents? Sometimes he wished he could just get that part over. A sense of dread pushed down on him as he inserted his keycard into its slot on the door. He pushed the door open, and stared into darkness for a moment. The first thing he noticed was a slight chill in the air. It felt as if a window was open. The second thing was the smell of cigarette smoke. Kensington remembered requesting a non smoking room. The sense of dread pushed down harder. As the door closed behind him, Nigel flipped the light on quickly, his brain likening it to pulling off a band-aid as fast as possible to get it over with. Once the room was fully illuminated, a couple things were confirmed. 1. The window was open. 2. Cigarette smoke wafted in the air. "Hello, Nigel." A man with a worn down face sat in the chair next to the twin bed. It wasn’t worn down in a violent way, there were no scars. But instead, it was worn the way a baseball mitt could be, or a comfy pair of shoes. The man was older, but not ancient, not ancient enough to look the way he did. He wore a brown trench coat over a black suit. After a few moments of awkward silence he lit up another cigarette. The ash tray on the table next to him seemed to support the idea that he either had been here for some time, or he smoked like a fiend. Nigel guessed that both of these things were true. "Your first thought or impulse is to run, I’d imagine." The man in the trench coat’s eyes finally found Nigel’s. He was no longer a peaceful grandfather, or an aging stockbroker. He was a murderer…he was worse. The President of Kent Fusiliers thought in just that moment that the stranger had walked straight out of another world. "Wouldn’t recommend it," Mr. Hayden offered, and took another drag from the cigarette. There was a bored confidence in his voice. "How can I help you?" Nigel asked, still standing near the door. "The real question is…how can I help you? And as it turns out, I have the answer as well." * * * Daisy Duke’s – Dubuque, Iowa. There is a phenomenon that brings young men from the borders of Iowa’s neighboring states, and that phenomenon is: BYOB strip clubs. Daisy Duke’s not only had this fact going for it, but often had celebrities from the porn world, and certain other shady happenings that drew a different kind of crowd. If you were looking for drugs, or perhaps a little more than a dance, you could talk to one of the bouncers. The bouncer’s name, as it were, was Lane Stevens. The bouncer casually leaned up against a wall by the bar. He wore a plain black t-shirt and jeans. "For an extra forty, you can finger her," The River Rat stated plain as day, as if he was talking about the weather. The customer was a guy in his early twenties, obviously hammered out of his mind. "Now why in the fuck would I want to do that?" the kid asked, wobbling back and forth. "Don’t you take any joy in pleasuring a woman?" "Fuck man, I don’t come to no titty bar to stick my finger in there man. Fuck that. I came here to get that pussy, know what I’m saying? You can finger bang ‘em and take ‘em out to breakfast man, if that’s your thing." The young man turned to look at one of his friends standing at the bar, who was laughing at this exchange. Stevens nodded to himself, smiling slightly. Just another white kid coming in here with his pants sagging, wearing a bandana, had too much to drink and watched too many 50 cent videos. "Fair enough," Lane started, "for eighty dollars I’ll fuck you right in your wigger ass." This got the young man’s attention. "The fuck you just say?" "Oh, I think you heard me fag-pac." A moment later the young man lunged at Stevens, and was surprised to find that he could not breathe. Before he was even upon the River Rat, he got jabbed right in the throat, and fell to the ground gasping for air. His friend at the bar backed up slightly, and Lane winked at him. A moment later Stevens had the kid by the hair, and was pulling him back up to his feet. The young man wheezed incessantly, his eyes seemingly attempting to escape from his head. "Got a rowdy one here," Stevens said to the other bouncer on duty, who nodded back at him. It took about ten seconds for the former livewire champion to get the kid to the door. He shoved it open and threw him out onto the pavement. "Have a good night chief," Lane said, and turned around to go back inside. But blocking the door was a short balding man in a plaid shirt. He wore glasses and looked quite nervous. Stevens briefly thought to himself that this man was the walking cliché of a pervert. But there was something that differentiated him from the rest of the customers he came across at this establishment. The shorter man, who went by the name Spencer as it were, slowly lifted up his shirt to reveal a pistol stashed in his navy dress pants. "Hi," Spencer offered, still not making eye contact. "Shit," Stevens replied. * * * "You have a problem, Nigel. A problem with Miss Alexandra Pierce. The problem is worse than you can imagine." Back in the hotel room, Nigel Kensington was now sitting down on the edge of his bed, listening to the stranger. "I think I understand the scope of the problem, dear sir." "No," Mr. Hayden re-iterated, "you don’t. But your current level of dislike and distrust of her is at an appropriate level for this exercise to be worthwhile for you anyway." "Why is it that you are so interested in assisting me, Mr…" "Mr. Hayden." Kensington coughed. Even with the window open, it was becoming quite smoky in here. "Miss Pierce and I," Hayden started, "we used to work for the same people. And let’s just say that my retirement benefits weren’t what I thought they would be." The image that flashed in Mr. Hayden’s head was this: bleeding on the cold floor of a warehouse. "I do not have time for revenge," Kensington said, feeling more courageous than he previously gave himself credit for, "my schedule is far too busy." Mr. Hayden considered this, as he killed the last of his current cigarette. "But do you have time to make the world a better place?" Mr. Hayden asked. "That is hardly your aim." "Doesn’t mean it can’t be yours, Mr. President." * * * In the front seat of a red Chevy Cavalier, Spencer and Lane passed the time. The River Rat drove, and Spencer sat in passenger seat, aiming a pistol at him. They talked too. Some of their dialogue went like this: "If you were to attempt to suddenly roll out of the car, my course of action would be to quickly assume the wheel, pull a one hundred and eighty degree turn, and either strike you with the vehicle in a non lethal way, or shoot you in the back of the leg on the way by. I would then get out of the car, and assist you back inside of it. My surgical background would allow me to stop the bleeding enough to make you comfortable." After a bit of silence Lane nodded thoughtfully and said, "Good to know." * * * Nigel paged through a stack of photos and documents, his mouth slightly ajar. Atrocities of various degrees, shapes, and sizes were laid out before him. The case against Miss Pierce, the case that she must pay. "How I figure we do this is we make it look like a wrestling storyline gone wrong. Someone loses their grasp on reality. I figure since this Dusk character has already betrayed you, and joined up with Miss Pierce, you need a new sheriff. Maybe you go a little unorthodox with your selection. I know just the fellow." Mr. Hayden put his last cigarette out. "My god," Nigel said aloud, still zoned in on the evidence before him. * * * "There is no joy in life…read that in a book once," The River Rat said. "Kind of a downer, ya know?" He lifted the black hood off of his guest. Alexandra Pierce did not make any sound, but the rage in her eyes was as effective as any sentence. Duct tape covered her mouth, and a bit of dried blood lingered underneath her nose. Her arms were still tied behind her back, her legs to the chair. "Most emotions we have, just chemical signals in the brain. So many concepts we have of love, and hate…are just that, concepts. It isn’t real. There is no purpose to any of this. No god. No right, no wrong." He smiled sadly at her. "There is no joy in life," he said again. Nigel Kensington’s WMD reached towards her face, ripping off the duct tape. She still said nothing. "I’ve thought a lot about offing myself lately, ever since Amy broke my neck. I’ve never told that to no one. And now after all the fucked up shit I’ve done, and what a miserable asshole I’ve become, not only do I find life to be hopeless…but there’s no one to even take pity on me. I mean, that’s one of the things that keeps most people going…sharing hopelessness. So now I just mostly do stuff for people with money. Never seen a job for this much money though, I’ll tell you that." He laughed then, and for the first time that anyone had seen in a good twenty years, he sounded as if he was laughing through tears. He was choked up. "I was supposed to kill you, but I’m not going to do that, no ma’am." Her eyebrows raised slightly. "You’re going to kill me. But hopefully not before I spend all the money. It’ll be like hide and seek." Lane stood up, and took a few steps towards the staircase before looking back at Desade. "Of course you’re going to have to get yourself out of here on your own. I can’t do everything my friend. And judging by our location…that will be a little while. Long enough for me to report mission accomplished, anyway." He wasn't even to the top before she'd tipped the chair over, the friction against the floor peeling the tape off her face (and taking a bit of flesh from her cheek). 'Stevens.' It was still muted, muffled by the remaining tape, but it's like they say, you can hear your name across a crowded party. Lane stopped, turning back over his shoulder, brows lifted. 'Determined one, ain't you?' Another, harder grind of her face against the floor left the tape dangling from one side of her lips, faster going lubricated by her own blood. 'Stevens.' It was a hoarse bark, lips chapped, throat raw. 'Live well, Lane,' she growled. 'Soon, I will find you. You'd be better... better off doing it yourself. Because when I find you, it will not be quick. It will not be painless. You'll go to your grave screaming, and I will bury you with every red cent he paid you for this.' 'You, uh, you got some schmutz on your cheek there.' He turned back around, tipping an imaginary cap. Despite her precarious position, on her side on the chair, he didn't doubt her. 'Be seeing you.' * * * A few months later. A bar, any bar. The TV said this: "It seems that yet another tragedy has hit the world of professional wrestling," the blonde female reporter did not seem too concerned with the story. "Former competitor Lane Stevens, known by his alias ‘The River Rat’, was found dead Sunday in the Jacuzzi of his hotel suite in Reno Nevada. Authorities are ruling it a suicide, because of the note found near the body. But the grizzly nature of the incident has many investigators wondering if there was not foul play." From a corner booth, Spencer and Mr. Hayden regarded this story with little surprise. "To Mr. Stevens," Mr. Hayden said, holding up his glass of scotch. "The most honest liar to ever walk the earth." Spencer held up his glass of water. Clink.
