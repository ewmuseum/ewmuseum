The Demon Vassago: Chapter One

1 March 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/01/2008 The Demon Vassago The Journal of Jasper James Chapter One: My name is Jasper James, and I was once a very vital, respectable man. Now-a-days I am a mere shell of the man I once was. My biggest accomplishment in life was a series of pieces I wrote entitled "The Demon Vassago". The pieces were believed to be made up stories of my own imagination. Those that believed that, believed wrong. It was the summer of 1997 and I was in a vulnerable part of my life. My family was gone, taken from me in the most heinous of ways, and in truth I think I was looking for death. Little did I know that night that death would find me. I was at the Double Down Saloon on Paradise Road, drinking away my life when a man approached me. He was tall and solid, without any visible hair. His complexion was fair and his eyes were icy blue. Many of the women in the establishment had noticed him, and even as he began talking to me I became aware of the stares he was receiving. He drew attention to him the way a celebrity would, an undying recognition. He introduced himself as Vassago, and from that point on I found my friend in depression. Vassago knew exactly who I was, but nothing of what had happened to me. For the first time since my family was lost, I never once brought it up in our hours of discussion. We discussed politics, religion and the fairer sex. He spoke with such eloquence and an impressive array of proper manners. Before the evening was over he asked me if I’d be interested in writing a piece on him. We scheduled an appointment for the following day and then went our separate ways. > The next morning I arrived at the address provided to me. At first I thought the address had to be a mistake, the building was an old brick warehouse no longer in use. I double-checked the napkin he had scribbled the address on and sure enough the address matched, so I went in. Upon entering the building I came to a set of stairs leading down into the basement. A soft amber glow flickered across the stone walls, like the light of a torch in a cave. The smell of mildew assaulted my nostrils as I descended the staircase into the darkened depths of the earth. The hairs rose on the back of my neck and my flesh goose pimpled for what I assumed was no reason. I shook off the gut reaction to turn and run and continued to the bottom of the staircase. When I reached the bottom the room was large but very dark with only a few splashes of light etching the walls with a fiery glow. I was drawn to a room in the far corner of the basement that was the source of the fiery glow. When I entered the room there was a dusty table with a large black pillar candle placed in the center. There were two chairs placed on each side of the table that looked to be plagued by dry rot There was a hole in the wall to my left that served as a window letting the moon light pour into the room. I turned around quickly because I felt as if someone was staring a hole into the back of my head. There he stood, his icy blue eyes staring at me intently. An eerie smile crept across his lips before he spoke. "I see you found your way without a problem." "I wouldn’t quite say without a problem, but I’m here." I chuckled, mainly out of nerves. Vassago gestured to the chair closest to the door and sat down in the one closest to the wall. Pulling out my writing tablet and my trusty tape recorder, I took my seat across from this peculiar man. As I placed the recorder on the table I noticed the way he was staring at me, frankly it made may hands clammy and my stomach grow queasy. Somehow this did not seem like the same man from the night prior, more so that the man I met the night prior was merely a mask of the true man that he was. "I’m glad you could join me Mr. James, I couldn’t think of a better man to tell my story then you." He smiled again, but not the same warm smile I had seen from him when we met. No, this was unsettling, almost… evil. I didn’t want to alarm the man, so I played things as cool as I could. "I’m glad to tell your story sir." I replied politely. "So where do you want to begin?" "I’d like to begin by asking you a question Mr. James, that is if you don’t mind?" Without hesitation, "Sure, what would you like to know?" "Do you believe in God Mr. James?" He asked, a slight accent to his voice. The question caught me off guard, not so much that he asked it but that I didn’t know anymore. So, I did what made the most sense to me, and I answered him honestly. "Honestly, I don’t know anymore." His eyebrows cocked at my answer, and I could sense his intrigue. Vassago leaned his massive frame back in the wooden chair, his weight causing the warped wood to creak under pressure. "Your answer, surprises me Mr. James." "Oh?" I asked, "how so?" "Well you see when I ask this question of someone they either answer yes or no. I’ve never been given the same answer you just gave me." It was then I began to wonder many a things about this man. I kept it all to myself of course, but my curiosity continued to grow. He continued, "why is it you don’t know Mr. James?" "I use to believe in God." I began without even considering what I was about to tell him. "But after my little girl was murdered I can’t believe any God would let that happen, so I don’t know if I can believe there is a God." My response left him silent for a moment. Finally he rose from his chair and turned his back to me. "Mr. James, you words sound as though you are seeking death." Before I realized it I was responding, "I guess I am." Until the words had left my mouth I never realized it, but the words were true. "Mr. James, I’m afraid today is not a good day for this interview, would you mind coming back tomorrow?" "Umm…" I began, his request was not what I was expecting, and before I could process things he began again. "Tomorrow night around nine would be better." "Okay…" I replied, still confused. I rose from my chair and made my way out of the room, as I crossed the threshold I felt his powerful hand on my shoulder. I turned to face him and sensed a certain disappointment in his eyes. "I can’t kill you Mr. James, you don’t fit my standards." At first I thought I heard him wrong, but deep down I knew I hadn’t. "Tomorrow I will begin telling you a tale that will revamp your career, you will become my chronicler." > When I left Vassago’s den I felt different then I had before. Though nothing would replace my wife and daughter, for the first time since they were taken from me, my life had a purpose, and though I didn’t know it yet this was going to be the biggest story of my life.
