Do we live but for survival? Or do we survive so we can live

2 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02 August Light crept up from the Manhattan skyline as night turned into day like the flick of the switch. It would be a beautiful sight if not hindered by the ruminants of whatever had been spilled, wiped or flung at the filthy subway tube window with which Dualta peered through. The weight of his head was nestled in the palm of his left hand which in turn rested against the slight sill beneath the window. His fingers lightly caressed the purple protuberance that had cultivated on the surface of his left cheekbone. His legs were spread out across several plastic spaces on a near empty tube. He battled furiously with his eye lids, desperate to stay awake. He was not in the mood to miss his stop; the night had been long enough. An obscure brand of vodka wrapped in a brown paper back was grasped in Dualta’s right hand, attached to his dangling arm. He was not one for pushing stereotypes, but it is hard to not observe the irony in a practically homeless man in a baggy, hooded jacket with week old stubble clutching a brown paper bag with an alcoholic spirit inside. The thought brought an infrequent smile to his face. In this circumstance he cared not for the habitual thoughts that would enter the mind of anyone who scrutinized him. This was about the only reward he got for his work, and on top of that it helped numb the pain, two birds with one stone. Usually he would be on the tube for a couple more stops, until it reached Woodside, from there he would make the walk up to Maspeth, to his home. But home was home no more. All assets had been relinquished by the Sheridan family. Although relinquish is perhaps too loose a term, it was more analogous to a hostile takeover. A shift in the decree of the New York underworld meant that any Sheridan, any associate of a Sheridan, now had nothing more than the shirt of their back and whatever they could stuff into their pockets. For it seems that in modern western society the venerable traits of respect and tradition came second to overwhelming brutality and the ever modish mind frame of ‘by any means necessary’. The Odessa Mafia are immigrants of the former Soviet Union. They are considered the dominant Russian organized crime group in the United States. This group established itself in the Brighton Beach area of New York City between 1975 and 1981. In the early 1980s the Odessa Mafia sent two sub-groups to San Francisco and Los Angeles with their leadership remaining in Brighton Beach. Not until now have they gained the number, the structure and the resources to purge their rivals. Dualta pulled himself together, shaking his head rapidly from side to side and squeezing his eyelids tight together before separating them as widely as possible in an attempt to diminish his fatigue and dry off his lingering inebriation. He stepped out of the cool, air-conditioned pipe into the musty humidity in the Queens Boulevard air. He ambles down the steps of the subway to the familiar streets of Queens, a place he once felt safe in, a place he once kept safe, now his head shifts from side to side, suspicious of anyone who casts a glance in his direction. He swiftly pulls the hood of his jacket over his head in a hope of concealing his identity to potential adversaries. That said, this small portion of Queens is one that the Irish can still feel relatively safe, Dualta however, is in no position to take chances. He turns right as he leaves the steps and then left down a tight, dimly lit alleyway. He climbs up a set of steel steps on his left, taking one last gulp of vodka before tossing to the ground below him. He shimmies open the door leading him and enters. The room is large, at least fifty square feet, its dark, lit only by a large fire in the middle of the room and several lanterns springing up in random locations. He walks over to the far right corner of the room, were a makeshift kitchen counter has been built, stacked on old plywood. Dualta reaches to his inside jacket pocket and pulls out an envelope, slamming it down on the table, he begins to walk away. "How much did you make tonight?" A voice asks from behind the counter. "About 500" Replies Dualta lazily. "And how did you do?" "I did too much again, lost the last fight; you know how fatigue sets in when you fight for three straight hours." Dualta’s mordant reply to his mother was his last action before leaving the room. He enters a corridor, he was not there long however as he immediately turns the handle of a door on his left. A dark clammy room, with nothing more than a small window, a bed and a drawer-cabinet. Dualta falls on the bed, his left cheek burned and his kidneys felt like they had just been through a blender. Underground fighting was his only way to make money; he did it nearly every night. He could not keep it up. His youthful body would soon turn into a deformed vegetable if he did. Five fights tonight. Too many. But he needed the money, every last bit of it. Time for a career change, its now or never. But does he want to enter a public spectrum for everyone to see him? The risk is evident, but the reward is extensive, and his family’s well-being is expensive. The SCCW was a top wrestling federation; he had already been accepted after a tough scouting trial down in Brooklyn is month ago. Now he was booked into his first match at the All In! Pay-Per-View. Would he crash and burn? Questions ran through his head like a steam train, this could well be the best or worst decision of his life. He had not told anyone, not sure if he was apprehensive about the humiliation of failure or the fear of openly exposing himself outside the boundaries of Queens Boulevard, the boundary which is all the Odessa’s gave the Sheridan’s. Stay in QB, pay your monthly rental to the Russians and they survive. Survival was not good enough for Dualta. He gets up and opens the top drawer of his cabinet. He pulls out a large envelope filled with papers, his contract, passport and other such documentation. Along with it is a ticket, to Kentucky, far away from the Odessa. He has only made money doing one thing, fighting. He may as well do it on a bigger stage. He gritted his teeth looking down at the ticket, a vivid intensity surging through his eyes. He had to take a chance, he cannot be trapped forever. So he packed.
