Age of Aquarius

15 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2010/04/15 Will O’Gallway fumbled around his pocket. He sighed and pulled out a keychain, attached with the Pokemon "Sudowoodo." Behind him were a steel elevator door and a darkly illuminated hallway. White stucco walls barely appearing below amber track lighting. Will opened up the double doors and dragged his small rolling bag. Inside, he tossed his keys and phone into a change bowl, and then thumbed through a pile of envelopes. Purely disinterested by his mail, he tosses it back down and leaves his entryway into his living room. He purchased the Aquarius Den last summer; at 21, he never had a definite home since training and felt it seemed "right." He spent 4 years on the road, living in dojos & hotel rooms. Focusing back in the states, he took advantage of Miami’s horrible housing situation and became the owner of this two-story condominium. Miami always seemed like a fun enough town, and it had a decent enough of an airport. It was time for him to be adult-like. Life was always supposed to be serious and adult-like. Home schooled by Irish Catholic ranchers in small town Texas, he was always destined to be in a serious lifestyle. His intelligence dictated it. Overbearing parents force-fed him Kierkegaard, Calculus and Latin upon puberty. He never really had a chance to "sow his wild oats" as his grandpa put it. Then he got his early diploma and was accepted to read for Philosophy at Columbia only at age 14. He could not take it any more. He got out of his parent’s destined life for him. The prodigy packed up his backpack and got onto a bus to Weatherford. To Brian Spaes’ Flying S Ranch, the wrestling commune. It worked out at first. Will was able to grasp wrestling pretty quickly, and the former "Impulse" Brian James took the teenager under his wing. Will walked into his sleek living room. Slick, smooth, reflective white tile amplified every step of his turquoise and red Nike SBs as he walked over to his orange loveseat. He hated the furniture here. Glossy and ultramodern, not necessarily sleek and techy like how he saw it in Japan. Rather, it was ostentatious and gauche. Somewhat useless. But, this was stuff that came from free in the "Aquarius Den Fire Sale." Until he made some of that Frank Silver money, he would be stuck with this interior design. He didn’t "hate" his home. Actually there was a whole lot about it he liked. A balcony that looked out onto the bizarre area known as Downtown Miami, Brickell and across to South Beach. A big enough bedroom to put all of his books and a nice, albeit, weird easy chair to read them in. Will still didn’t get the concept of a chair being shaped like a messed-up dial a number bed. But he found it right. Everything here was considered "right." Everything did work out well as first with Brian Spaes. Then Will unraveled the mystique of the Flying S Ranch. The wrestling utopia and ranch commune was only surface deep. He stumbled onto Spaes one night smoking meth out of his wrecked truck on the side of the highway. At 15 he had to bail his maestro out of county lock up and bring him home to the destroyed household he created. The ranch fell into shambles; no one wanted to train under Spaes and it was impossible to do ranchwork when a tweaker constantly sells his equipment and livestock to feed his habit. So Ryan got onto a bus and escaped to Mexico. There at age 16 he started his pro wrestling career. Instead of playing Texas high school football or going to homecoming dances, Will was wrestling all over the world. Lucha in Ciudad Juarez, Lancashire and Catch in Aachen and Leeds. Slowly but surely he created an international name as a wrestling prodigy. Will started to use his middle name and dropped the overly-Irish "O" from the beginning of his last name. By 17 and 18, he "graduated" from being a Young Lion and started to collect gold straps. With this, Will encountered an issue. His life started to diverge. It was easy, initially, to be both himself and his persona in the ring. Both individuals were unassuming, talented, and no-nonsense. He was himself, he was comfortable. Then he traveled to Japan and joined Tokyo Pro. They wanted him to be a brash, hateful, young gaijin. At first, this was awkward "How can I be something I am not?" Slowly he came to love this attitude and the reaction it garnered. Sponsors and man without fingers pushed drinks, money, clothes, and women upon the young adult. He even had an opportunity to attempt Mount Midoriama for Sasuke. William O’Gallway developed two separate identities: himself and the rudo superstar Ryan Gallway. And there lies the concern. William was comfortable with how his life was out of the ring. He got to read, write and generally kept to himself. Calling him antisocial would be a disservice, he considered himself happiest when left to his thoughts and his books. This is what initially drew his parents to push academia upon their youngest. Breaking cattle and patching fences wasn’t a skill that came natural to him, unlike his strapping beasts of brothers. Young Will would be best suited away from their ranch, in the libraries and classrooms of the world’s historic institutions. But being Ryan Gallway? He now craved it. The sound of boos through out Kouraken as he swiveled his hips into the face of a prone tecnico. The fact he could even invoke hatred in the typically reserved Japanese Crowds. The money and treasures gained from his sponsors. He did not mind being a shill for the Yamitori Corporation, and their splendid "OKAY!" cleansing socks or dental floss. And the Shibuya and Roppongi girls that followed his every step. Oh the girls that loved to prove "rebellious" by dating a scummy "gaijin." He knew they would never last. But that was part of the mystique. Part of being the World Warrior. And now the "Intergalactic Space Cowboy" laid prone on his couch. Feeling disturbed, he fumbled around in the pocket of his olive in tone manpris. Out of them came a shimmering gilded silver case. Inscribed his initials in a futuristic block fashion. WGR. With a satisfying ‘click’ he moved his thumb across the side latch. The top of the case flicked open and inside were small business card sized slips of paper. Repeated about 20 times on them were the iconic "smiley face." Will tore off two perforated tabs and placed them on his tongue. Sighing, he laid back and rolled his eyes shut. For two years, he faced these two characters. Ryan and himself. He should have known this was a losing battle to start. A cloistered boy with many weaknesses preyed upon by an industry full of "lost boys." This personality battle is not he can ever win. One side will always win out. That is why he takes his blotter tabs of— "Who are you?" What do you mean? "You’ve been talking to me since I came home. I don’t know if you are someone whom got pocket dialed or just—" I am no one. I am just an observer. "Then why do you know all of this? How can someone know about all my past?" Now that is a silly question Will. One that I know you know the answer. Where was I? Oh Yes. Talking about your inability to handle your two divergent and different lifestyles. How it is tearing you apart. "How do you know that? That is none of your business overhead voice! Why don’t you answer my questions!" You know I cannot answer these questions. Just like how you cannot answer questions about how to solve your crisis. William, you have heard of the saying "hurt people hurt people?" Well individuals with personality disorders and drug issues cannot really figure out why they hear what they hear. "Are you—" Yes I am saying exactly what you are thinking. That is part of my job. You are me and I am you. "I don’t think I like this. Get out!" Easier said than done Will. You can use me however you wish. But I am with you. I am sorry that I do not please you right now. Perhaps one day this will make sense and we will grow to enjoy each other’s company. William picked up the throw pillow, he spoke in a muffled tone as he tried to snuff out the voice. "I SAID STOP! GET OUT!" William knew his screams did not mean a thing, but still gave them a futile try. Slowly they quieted down into a whimper. The LSD took over his system and placed him into a different world. One where he would not have these battles. At least this time
