Date: Today Two weeks ago. Chicago, Illinois. Lilian Baker put on her best 'selling' face, even though she knew this client was a no-go. You learned to read people in this business, and he might as well have had the word 'No' painted on his face. Meticulously groomed hair, sharp creases in his black suit, rigid posture--the man was obviously a perfectionist, and even she would admit that this property was less than perfect. She'd done her best to point out the location's positive qualities, but they were all met with a disapproving frown. He never spoke out for or against anything, but she could tell that he wasn't pleased. It was the way he looked at things...like, he was judging them. No, not judging. Measuring. Bravely, Lilian continued her pitch. 'All in all, I think this could be a very good place for you, Mr. Chandler. It's a central location, and with a few minor changes, could be very eye-catching to your customers.' The man--R.W. Chandler, he called himself--had been facing the other direction, but he cast her a backward glance. 'It's a little small,' he said. 'I know it's not quite the dimensions you specified, but given the right decor, I think it could be a very nice atmosphere. I believe that this property is more than enough to meet the needs of your company.' She couldn't help but blanch slightly when he finally turned to meet her gaze. Even standing face to face with a man three inches shorter, Lilian Baker had never felt so small. She smiled nervously and wondered whether he knew about the coffee stain on her blouse...as if he could somehow see it through her tan jacket. 'Ms. Baker,' he said, in a very biting tone. 'You do not know the first thing about my company. If you did, you might not have bothered to show me this place. The electrical output is insufficient for a modern computer system, the carpets need to be replaced, the adjoining room is too small, there is noticeable damage to the walls in several places, and there are only two windows. If this is truly the best option you have, I am curious how you stay in business.' The woman looked slightly put out, thereby betraying all of her training as a real estate agent. He'd managed to find the problems she hid with double-speak, as well as find one that she didn't even know about. 'I don't see that there's anything wrong with the carpets, sir...but I can show you another location, if you like. It has a very nice view of Lake Michigan.' The man's mouth twitched into a smirk, which faded quickly. 'No,' he answered. 'I believe this one will be fine.' 'I'm sorry?' she replied. Her surprise would have been apparent to anyone who looked...and therefore, blatant to the man she addressed. 'I believe I can make this work. I'll take it...unless there is a problem on your end?' 'My en--no! No, of course not. I just thought you weren't interested. After all, you made it clear that it doesn't meet your expectations.' The man grinned, showing off a wolfish set of bright white teeth. 'Ms. Baker, I've often found that raising something to your expectations is often easier than waiting for something that meets them.' Lilian Baker smiled warmly, because that's what you did when you closed a deal. 'Excellent. I'll just go to the car and get the paperwork, Mr. Chandler, and we can take care of this right now.' 'That will be fine.' He gave her a small nod as she headed out the door, leaving him alone to study the property. There certainly was a great deal of potential here. It wasn't perfect, of course--it would never be perfect. But it might be better. R.W. Chandler--which was, of course, not his real name--smiled. 'Time to go to work,' he said. # # # Three days ago. Amarillo, Texas. A small envelope landed at a man's feet. Inside the envelope was a stack of money--a stack much shorter than it should have been. 'You wanna tell me what the fuck this is about, Henderson?' A thin, reedy man looked up from his notepad, every bit as calm as the other man was not. The individual who addressed him was large, sweaty and angry...but Ryan Henderson was used to that. Such was the life of a wrestling promoter. 'Light gate tonight, pal,' he said. 'Sorry.' 'Sorry? You promised me five hundred, Henderson. That's barely half.' The wrestler crossed his arms and glowered, but Henderson was completely nonplussed. 'So, what do you want me to do? Tell the other guys they're not getting paid?' The wrestler stuck a thick finger into Henderson's face. 'This is bullshit! You practically fuckin' begged me to be on your show, and now you're going to stiff me? 'Like I said, light gate. I guess you're just not the draw you used to be, buddy.' The sarcasm was so heavy, you could have anchored a boat with it. 'Fuck you, you little turd!' The big man reached down to grab the promoter by the front of his ratty Seven Mary Three t-shirt. As soon as he made contact, however, he found his arms pinned behind him by two other wrestlers. He struggled against them, but couldn't seem to break free. Soon enough, they pushed back their captive, forming a wall between him and the promoter. Ryan Henderson stood up from his chair, and slowly stooped down to pick up the envelope. As he rose, he offered it toward his would-be assailant--a man nearly twice his size. 'Don't want to forget this, chief. Last one you'll get from this company.' The bigger man glared back, almost forgetting about the two piles of muscle in front of him. I oughtta spit in his face, he thought. Tear up that stupid money and throw it back at him. Punch him in the face. Hell, I oughtta do something. In the end, he did do something. He snatched the money out of the promoter's hands, and left. As the man stormed off, Henderson shook his head. 'And he wonders why his career's tanked the last couple years.' Back in the locker room, the wrestler angrily stuffed his ring gear into a gym bag, muttering curses all the while. 'Shouldn't fucking have to put up with bullshit like this. Snot-nose punk thinks he can screw me...little goddamn bastard.' The rest of the boys in the back slowly moved away, having heard most of the tirade already. As the wrestler jammed his kneepads into the bag, he thought idlely of earlier times. Couple years ago, he wouldn't have even thought twice about plowing through those two 'roid-raging clowns and choking the life out of that punk kid Henderson. Hell, there was a time when he was on top of the goddamn wrestling world--world tag team champion and everything. Now? Now, it took him half an hour to get out of bed. A ten-minute match against a local hero--should have been five, if he'd been able to dodge that missile dropkick--took almost everything out of him. His body was starting to break down, he was approaching his late thirties, and all he had to show for a two-decade career was one glorious year-long run in the spotlight...and a whole lot of nothing apart from that. It had taken him so long to make it to the top...and the fall had been farther, faster, and sooner than he could have imagined. It just wasn't fair. Twenty years of paying his dues, and for what? Getting short-changed by some two-bit indy in fucking Texas. He sat down on the bench...and the longer he rested, the more tired he got. He hunched over, burying his head in his hands, digging his fingers into his short black hair. 'Fuck this shit,' he mumbled. 'Just...fuck it.' In the career of every professional wrestler, there will come a point where he decides it's just not worth it. The pain, the travel, the bullshit outweigh the money, the fame, and the love. He looks at his career, and decides it's good enough...or that he no longer cares it's not. Our subject had reached that point. He wasn't thinking about tonight's match, and he certainly wasn't looking forward to the next one. By then, he wasn't even all that angry at getting shorted anymore. He was just...tired. 'Yo, D!' He looked up, wearily, and saw the bright face of a kid named Bobby...something. Or Billy. Hell, he couldn't remember--he just knew that it was the kid he had a match against that night. Son of a bitch, he thought. I beat the piss outta that kid for ten minutes, and he's up and around faster than I am. 'What is it?' he asked. 'Someone out there, says he's got a package for ya! C'mon!' His knee joints groaned a little as he stood back up. He took his blue gym bag in his hand, and walked toward the kid, who led him down the hall. 'Great match tonight, man. I'm going to be stiff as fuck tomorrow.' The older man chuckled without humor. Talk to me in ten years, kid. 'You were pretty good, too. That dropkick of yours is somethin'.' 'Think so?' The kid beamed. Couldn't have been more than nineteen, but there was already a little bit of buzz surrounding him. 'Yep. One of the best I've ever taken.' 'Wow...' The younger wrestler's eyes almost glazed over, visions of stardom dancing before them. His counterpart wanted nothing more than to shake the hell out of him and tell him to get out while he had a chance. Get a respectable, higher-paying job. Like, substitute teacher. Soon, the two of them had made their way to the side entrance, where they met a delivery man. 'Which wunna you is--' he began, in a thick Texas drawl. The older grappler pulled out his wallet and showed his driver's license. That satisfied the delivery man, who handed him a small box. 'See y'all around,' he said before leaving. Billy, or Bobby (Barry?) looked expectantly at his erstwhile foe. 'Gonna open it?' 'Might as well,' he muttered, and tore the tape off the top flaps. He caught a glimpse of something lightweight and black. And his eyes went red. 'No...' Slowly, he pulled the item out of the box. It was a long, black robe. A judge's robe. Slowly, Barry (Terry?) backed away. He knew better than to stand too close--this guy had a reputation for going apeshit at the mere mention of 'the J-Word.' Some guy in Odessa said it to him once, right before a match, and ended up with a broken arm and severe internal bleeding. As the kid snuck away, the other man stood there, holding the robe in his hands. But to him, it wasn't a robe. It was a ball and chain. A noose around his neck. A symbol of the complete shambles that his career had been. The gimmicks. The...fucking...gimmicks. 'AAAAAAAAAAARRGGGGHHH!' The primal scream echoed through the halls, and he tore into the robe like a wild animal. Some people ran to see what had happened, while others decided to increase their distance...but he paid no notice, and kept on screaming and ripping. A minute later, he was surrounded by strips of black cloth. It wasn't until his rage had quelled that he looked into the box again. There, he saw a small white card, which he picked up. It read: I thought this would get your attention, Mr. Dale. There was a signature underneath, one he knew all too well. It signed his paychecks for a couple years. And at one point, it also signed his career's death certificate. He growled the name, barely loud enough for anyone to hear. 'Connors...' He turned over the box...and sure enough, there was a return address on it. A sick grin spread across his face, and as he stormed out of the building, all thoughts had turned from retirement to revenge. He couldn't quit now. Not when there was a job to do. # # # Four days ago. Chicago. The last of the movers were leaving, having placed his desk in its proper position--back in the corner, just out of view from the doorway. Wyatt Connors surveyed his new office, and was pleased with what he saw. For the most part, it was very simple. There were the standard office trappings--desk, chairs, lamp, filing cabinet, water cooler, coffee pot, fake potted plant. In the back was the adjoining room, but of course he always kept the door closed. Really, there were only two things strange about the place. First was the lack of computers, or anything generally related to computers. No printer, scanner, fax machine. There wasn't even a telephone. The other strange thing was the very large dry-erase board that more or less covered the west wall. There were already a few indeterminate scribbles in various colors; nothing with any meaning to the casual observer. Maybe it had meaning to Connors, but that was no sure bet either. The moment the door closed behind the movers, Wyatt Connors approached the board and started writing more things. Letters mostly, a few numbers, but no complete words. He scribbled a few more symbols here and there, and then stepped back to admire his handiwork. He made a satisfied nod, then reached into the pocket of his suit and pulled out a small tape recorder. He pressed the 'play' and 'record' buttons, and spoke. 'Notes. One.' 'The high-tech option is unavailable, so have been forced to go low. Must make sure to keep close eye on sensitive materials. Audio recording provides a certain comfort level; allows me to think out loud. Must be sure not to say anything compromising without editing or destroying the tape afterward. The board lacks permanence, which is both advantage and flaw.' He paused the tape, long enough to take a sip from a coffee cup. It was black, and bore the message, 'Hands Off. I Have Killed For Less.' Refreshed, he resumed recording. 'First goal: assistance.' 'I had hoped to delay this as long as possible, as there is a certain amount of challenge in being a man alone. Unfortunately, it is no longer possible in today's SCCW. Sykes has his fruity cohorts, Kennedy as Kathryn and Lauren to protect him--although I'm still not sure who is supposed to protect him from them. Stevens has his own goons, and yet is a goon himself. Naturally, that ties back to Alexandra Pierce, who seems to have one quarter to half the roster, depending on the day. Not a single one of them would leap to my defense, nor would I expect them to...but if events occur as I expect, some from of protection will be necessary for survival.' 'Have compiled a list of possibles. Not as long as I would hope, but I am not surprised. I have not exactly engendered goodwill over the course of my career. Honestly, it's a bit of a shock that the list is even this long.' He faced the board directly, focusing first on a pair of letters in the middle of what looked like the world's most disorganized word search puzzle. 'Subject one, K.T. Have had limited contact over the past five years. Returned to Japan shortly after the fall of Seventh Circle. Research indicates that he retired two years ago, following a botched knee surgery. Unacceptable.' He took a few steps back, then turned toward the desk. When he reached it, Wyatt set down his coffee mug, and grabbed an eraser. 'Subject two, D.M. My first choice under any circumstances. Unfortunately, he seems to have dropped off the radar. All attempts to contact have met with failure. Extremely annoying, and slightly worrisome. Marching back toward the board, he quickly erased both pairs of letters that he had mentioned, before turning toward a third. 'Subject three, H.R. Probably my best option, both for his own skill set and the company he keeps. However, may have used my last favor with him after events in Oklahoma. Best to keep distance, at least for now.' Another swipe of the eraser cleared off more space. 'Subject four, C.B. Completely useless; has been for the last few years. Even if he wasn't, I'd never get him on board without Number Two.' The Rainmaker paused the tape again, long enough to erase those letters and catch his breath. Even a legendary motormouth like Connors needed his oxygen. 'Subject five, L.R. Has talent, and there is a personal matter to consider. Still, his lack of discipline is matched only by my lack of tolerance. Still under consideration, but only as a last resort. He swapped out the eraser for a purple marker, uncapped it with the flip of his thumb, and underlined the letters 'LR,' and followed them with a question mark. 'Subject six, R.T. Short answer: no. Long answer: NO.' Rather emphatically, Wyatt crossed out those letters multiple times, before picking up the eraser and disposing of them properly. This only left two pairs of letters yet unchanged: 'D.D.' and 'T.R.' 'Subjects seven and eight. All along, probably the best and most likely options. I have worked with them before, with marked success. They might not be receptive to a reunion, unfortunately--our parting was under less than optimal circumstances. There is a very good chance that they will hold a grudge.' 'However. If I can press the right buttons, I do believe I can get one of them to come back...and with one, I'm certain I can reel the other back in as well.' Slowly, Wyatt circled the two pairs of letters. His lips twisted into a smirk. 'It's a good thing pushing buttons fits into my skill set.' 'Note One, End.' His speech completed, Wyatt Connors slipped the recorder back into his pocket, and immediately started clearing off the board. # # # The day before yesterday. Knoxville, Tennessee. 'Don't do...no, don't do that...' 'Oh no? Well what if I do...this?' 'This', whatever it was, caused a high-pitched squeal, followed by a whispered voice. 'Not now!' 'Right now!' came the answer, and two pairs of lips pressed firmly into each other. The woman tried to push away--although, admittedly, not very hard--and she tried to plead her case through a staccato of kisses. 'Mom and--dad wi--will be home--any--minute now!' 'Then we'd--better hurry--huh?' Vanessa Weaver squealed again, and wiggled away from the hands of her boyfriend. 'I'm serious! It's bad enough that my parents are staying in my apartment for the weekend. Can you imagine them walking in on us?' 'Tell you what. I promise not to look at them when they do.' 'You're not helping!' Many times, Vanessa wondered what she saw in her boyfriend--much like everyone else did. After all, he looked like some kind of hippy, had poor hygiene, and was none too bright besides. But, she was quick to remind herself, he was sweet...in his own way. Also, there was the dynamite sex. Before she could escape, he had grabbed her again, and started planting more kisses on her neck. His hand started to slide up the front of her sweater, but she swatted it away. She started kissing back, though, sweeping away his long, stringy hair so that she could nibble on his ear. 'See that?' he whispered. 'Now you're getting into it.' He dug his fingers into her blonde hair and pulled lightly, as he kissed his way down her neck and shoulders. She let out a slight moan, but managed a weak protest. 'But what if Mom and Dad...see us?' 'Meh,' he said in between pecks. 'Let 'em get their own girls.' He tossed Vanessa lightly onto the bed, her hair cascading in a halo around her. Slowly, and with a wide grin plastered on his face, he prowled towards her. 'Beep beep!' 'What was that?' she asked. 'I didn't hear a thing,' he answered slyly, and continued to stalk his prey. 'I'm sure I heard--might have been my cellphone.' She darted her hand to the night table, grabbing her Chocolate 3 moments before her man could swipe it away. According to the display screen, there was a new text message. 'It's from Dad,' she said. 'I have to take this.' 'Aw, c'mon. It'll keep for an hour. Or six.' Vanessa rolled her eyes--she loved her man, but he only ever had one thing on his mind--and stepped quietly out of her bedroom. She hated teasing him like that, but she didn't want to risk the goodwill she'd built up between her parents and her boyfriend. He really had changed a lot over the last two years. He finally left his old line of work--he hadn't even watched it on TV in six months--and got a job at the Home Depot. It wasn't glamorous, but it was steady, and it paid well enough. Once in a while, she could even talk him into wearing clean socks. Of course, you couldn't tell by the state of the bedroom--she had to tiptoe around some of his dirty laundry as she went into the living room to read the message. From: Dad Met freinds 4 drimks dont wa4t up Vanessa giggled, both at her father's inability to text, and at his probable drunkenness. Soon, her thoughts wandered back to her man. If her folks weren't coming home until later... She immediately started looking around the living room. 'Where is it...it's always here somewhere...ah!' She snaked a hand around the couch, and found a wadded-up piece of clothing. It was a gray t-shirt featuring George Thoroughgood and the Destroyers, which her boyfriend's dad bought for him when he was sixteen. Of course, it had been black back then. Now, it was faded, holey, dirty, and would probably disintegrate if you put it in the wash. None of that mattered, because it was his Very Favorite Shirt. She snuck quietly off toward the bathroom. As she closed the door, she caught the faint sound of his ringtone--'Cherry Pie' by Warrant--and smiled. That should keep him occupied, she thought. Quickly, Vanessa peeled off her light gray Volunteers sweater, revealing a white bra underneath. She undid the clasp and removed it, and then slipped the ratty old t-shirt over her slender body. She blushed slightly, noticing that one of the holes came awfully close to being indecent. She then undid the button on her pants and let them fall to the floor, careful not to let them make too much noise. Didn't want to spoil the surprise, after all. She stood there in front of the mirror, wearing only a well-worn t-shirt and white cotton panties. Slowly, as if savoring the moment, she hooked her thumbs into the sides of her underwear, and slid them down her legs. 'Oohh...he is going to love this,' she whispered. Vanessa tiptoed out of the bathroom, and back toward her boyfriend, her heart racing with anticipation. She saw that the door was only slightly ajar, so she softly pushed it open. 'I've got a surprise for you, baby,' she cooed. But when she entered the room, she found that he had a surprise of his own. His duffel bag lay open on the bed, and he was feverishly packing whatever of his own clothes he could find inside. 'Baby? What's going on?' 'Deke called,' he answered, his mind suddenly in another place. 'I have to go.' 'Go? Go where?' 'Chicago. We finally know where to find that son of a bitch.' 'Who?' 'Connors! He's had this coming a long time...' Vanessa felt her heart fall. Connors meant Wyatt Connors, his old boss. And that meant wrestling. 'Honey, I thought you were done with all that.' He finally looked up from his work, having just enough boyfriend-sense to know when eye contact is necessary. 'This is important, Ness. The way he--' It had taken that long for his brain to process all the things his eyes saw on their way to meet hers. Suddenly, he found it difficult to continue. 'I, uh...wow. My favorite shirt.' 'Do you really have to go?' she pouted. There was a brief debate between his penis and...some part of his brain that was not controlled by his penis. Over the course of history, it had been a fairly one-sided contest, but this was one of those rare occasions where the underdog won out. 'Sorry, babe. Plane leaves in two hours. And I...I gotta do this.' He stood up, bag in hand, and approached his love. He kissed her--not the usual warmup-before-getting-in-your-pants kind like normal, either. It was deep, slow, and tender...the kind that made her hear violins in the background. When it was finished, he whispered, 'You're the best, babe. I'll be back as soon as I can.' A minute later, she heard the roar of his Grand Am from the street. She told herself that this was the last straw. He was going back to wrestling...and she swore she'd leave him if he ever did that. Of course, such promises don't carry much weight when you're wearing his Very Favorite Shirt. # # # Now. Here. Wyatt Connors calmly looked around the office, making sure nothing was out of place. They'd be there soon, and he needed things to be perfect. 'Everything seems to be in order,' he said. 'Time to review the plan.' Once more, he retrieved the tape recorder from his pocket, and pressed play. 'Note Seven,' the recorded version of his voice stated. He listened intently for the next several minutes, before his guests arrived. BANG BANG BANG. 'It's open,' Wyatt said, calmly. The door flung open wide, and two men burst through. The first was six and a half feet tall, over two hundred eighty pounds, short black hair, two-day stubble, and bloodlust in his eyes. The man behind him was much smaller--under six feet, not quite two bills--with long, ratty, light brown hair, and a face that looks just as unwashed as his shirt...and as much malice in him as his friend. Deacon Dale, and T.J. Ratigan. 'Deacon will almost certainly try to punch me on sight. Dodging will make him feel impotent; a block or counter would make me his enemy. Best course of action is to take the punch.' 'Gentlemen!' Wyatt beamed. 'Let me just tell you what a pleasure it is to--' was all the farther he got, before Deacon brought a right hand crashing into his face. Connors spun around from the impact, and staggered backward. That gave Ratigan enough time to step up and elbow him hard in the gut. 'Oof!' 'Once they've got a couple shots in, beg off. They can't hear my proposal if I can't talk.' Connors skittered backward across the floor, doing his best to stay out of arms' reach. Eventually, he managed to put the desk in between him and his assailants. 'Gentlemen, please!' he cried. 'I did not invite you here for a fight!' 'I'll have to work fast on the diplomacy, as I don't expect it to work at first. Deacon will almost certainly bring up the fact that I abandoned them in the World Wrestling Alliance. Trevor...will likely say something disgusting or stupid, possibly both.' 'Fight's what you're going to get, ya little bastard,' Dale barked. 'Or maybe you want us to forget how you left us high and dry, right before a goddamn title defense?' 'Yeah!' T.J. shouted. 'So you keep talkin', and we'll just kick your ass so hard you'll have to shit in your hat.' 'I'm sure that's not necessary,' Connors retorted, speaking a little faster than he was used to. 'I merely invited you here for a business proposal!' 'I've had enough of your goddamn business proposals, Connors!' Dale shouted, as he and Ratigan tried to flank around the desk. 'Every time you had some kind of business deal, we were the ones who ended up getting the business!' 'There ain't nothin' you got that we want, Wise Ass.' T.J. made a dive for Connors' leg, but Wyatt managed to dive out of the way. Unfortunately for the Scorpion, that put him right into the grasp of the man known in wrestling as 'The Judge.' 'If things start to go sour, launch immediately into the proposal. The most important thing to remember is that it's about them, not me. The one thing I need to get across is how this will benefit them.' Deacon Dale hefted the much smaller Wise Guy up, and pinned him against the wall. He drew back his fist, readying a knockout blow. 'How about the revival of your wrestling careers?' he asked, and screwed his eyes shut. A moment later, he carefully opened one eye. The punch had never come. 'If they haven't murdered you by now, that means they're on the hook. Start giving them the hard sell. Mention the women; that'll get Trevor's attention.' 'Think about it!' Connors shouted. 'You two could be part of the SCCW roster! Home of Aimz! Lance Marshall! Alexandra Pierce! Katsidy!' A quick glance told him that Trevor's resolve had weakened slightly. 'And you two! Bigger crowds! Bigger events! Bigger paychecks! Media exposure that makes the WWA look like a county fair! Think of it, gentlemen! Deacon Dale and T.J. Ratigan, true household stars!' By now, Deacon's grip on Connors' shirt collar had loosened, allowing the Wise Guy to drop to the floor. Still, the smaller man was not exactly free just yet. 'I don't like it,' Deacon said. 'What's to keep you from leaving us holding the bag again, like last time?' 'Push the benefits harder. Try to slip something by them--but make sure it's obvious, so they catch it. The most important thing is for them to feel like they are in control.' 'It'll be different this time! Your contracts will be through SCCW, instead of through me. I can negotiate the whole thing, get you a sweet deal--for a nominal fee, of course--and then we--urkkkk!' Connors' pitch was interrupted by the sudden clamping of Deacon's hand around his throat. 'All right, all right,' he whispered hoarsely. 'I'm willing to waive the fee! Every dime of your contracts will be yours to keep!' 'At this point, Deacon may grow suspicious. Don't be noble; he'll never buy it. Flattery is the key.' Deacon released the choke hold, giving Connors a moment to catch his breath and straighten his tie. 'What do you get out of all this, Connors?' he asked. 'I know you don't give anything away for free.' 'All right,' Connors said, almost sheepishly. 'I've got some big plans brewing, but I can't do them alone. I'll need help...and I couldn't think of any better help than the two of you.' 'They might make some last-second demands. Acquiesce, within reason.' 'You're sure we'll have contracts with the company?' Dale asked. 'Absolutely.' 'No mind games on us? Like that stupid thing you did when you pretended to be injured?' 'Of course not. All the mind games will be directed at the opponents.' Ratigan piped up, 'No more of your Training Nazi bullshit?' 'None. The two of you are more than capable of handling your own training schedules.' T.J. Ratigan smiled--and although Connors pretended not to notice, it was all he could do not to smile back. 'One more thing,' Deacon said. 'Name it.' 'I never have to wear that stupid goddamn robe, ever again.' 'Agreed.' Deacon Dale took a step back, finally allowing Wyatt Connors some breathing room. 'All right, Connors,' he said. 'You got a deal. He extended his large hand, and Connors filled it with his small one. The Devil in the Details grinned widely. 'Excellent. It will be a pleasure doing business with you again.' 'Congratulations, Wyatt Connors. You've got yourself some lackeys.'
