Wild Discovery: Devil in The Desert

17 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/17/07 In the nightland wastes that encompass the south of America, long tall grass sways perfectly in a slight breeze. In a flat area, just beyond the grass, a small Kangaroo Rat (Dipodomys deserti) hops to a tuft of scrub grass and begins to nibble precociously. As the small rat ingests the sprigs, his attention is turned away from danger, and ever so slightly, a pair of glassy eyes blinks a set of impermeable but clear eyelids over slit pupils. A rustle in the weeds is enough to alert the rodent, but not nearly fast enough as fangs enter the rats system and inject a lethal dose of venom into it. Teeth that resemble hypodermic needles slowly retract into the mouth of the Pygmy Rattlesnake (Sistrurus militarius) and the rat jerks violently a few times before the final breath is drawn into it’s tiny lungs. Almost methodically, the rattler unhinges its jaw and begins to engulf the rodent, slowly working it down into its long gullet until nothing is left but the tip of a tail. Soon that is gone as well, and a tribal drumbeat begins stampeding. Scenes of wildlife flash by in an instant as the music rolls on. A tiger languishes in a pool, a pride of lions enjoys a feast of a wildebeest, and an elephant trumpets triumphantly before the words "Wild Discovery" flash across the screen in bold lettering. As a small logo for "Discovery Channel in HD" pops up in the corner of the screen, the scene fades to a lithe reptile zipping from brush to brush before the shot fades into darkness and the title frame emerges. Wild Discovery: Devil in The Desert" Deep is the cold night air in the desert. Life in the arid environment is put on a standstill during the scoring day for most. The wildlife preferring the cool breeze that carouses the sandy dunes and scrub brush under the moonlight. Traipsing across the hither and yon of the barren landscape is best left to the best of the best. Forget Bear Grilles. Don’t bother to mention Steve Irwin. Jeff Corwin need not apply. This task can only be applied to the most stalwart of wildlife hunters. A man amongst all men in the field. Doctor Mumbo Q. Jumbo (the Q is for Clancy) steps triumphantly onto a stone. His faithful crew that has followed him for years on his non stop journey of discovery steps lightly after him, careful not to disturb the tranquility of the night air. After such adventures with the good doctor like the discovery of the Immortal Stag in Ireland, the journey of the Cobra whilst within India, and the lone wolf in the Rocky Mountains, this band of rugged sons of bitches was ready for anything. Anything, except of course, Dr. Mumbo Jumbos legendary impetuousness. Dr. Mumbo Jumbo smiles as he faces his crew and then to his longtime friend and translator, Dr. Bill Stevens. As he rattles off his gibberish, Bill is right behind him, converting the dialect into English. "Tonight…we have found…traces of a…Pygmy Rattlesnake. This excites me…because I spent years…devoted to the study of…reptiles in my home." The small Dr. stands on his stone, looking down at a shrub. Hopping down nimbly he kneels and grabs for something within. His hand emerging and a smile growing on the pith helmeted man’s face, he turns to reveal a snake shed. The doctor turns to the camera and speaks in his rapid fire manner again, leaving the translator to inform the viewers of the doctors words. "A snake will shed…every so often to renew…it’s scales and color…the outer layer is dry…and comes off with time. This snake is weathered…probably very experienced…and could be trouble." Overhead, soaring in the night air, a large bird of prey swoops low, narrowly avoiding the camera crew and the good doctor as he stands on a rock. The Golden Eagle (Aquila chrysaetos) lands gracefully on a dead tree and scans the surrounding area. The rare spectacle of a golden eagle flying in the night to hunt is one that leaves many of the adventurers mouths agape. Dr. Jumbo himself turns his head to the stump and watches as the giant raptor peers back casually. He turns to Bill Stevens and points at the raptor. "What an oddity…a Golden Eagle…in the night? Why would…such a curious creature…appear now? Suddenly, drawing attention away from the eagle, one of the crewmen screams and the good Dr. runs to the sound quickly. Laying on his back, a pack carrier in a red shirt holds his calf and curses as he flails about. Belying his stature, the smallish Dr. Jumbo pounces, holding the man flat and calming him with soothing words and noises before inspecting the wound. A grave look crosses his face as the mans breathing soon becomes more labored and a cold sweat forms on his brow. Doctor Jumbo stands up and Bill walks to his side. They look down as the man begins to twitch and throe in spasms. Dr. Mumbo Jumbo spits and walks away muttering to himself. Soon, by a campfire, the crew catches up to Jumbo and Bill sits next to his old friend. The Dr. turns and shakes his head, offering some words of explanation. "Damn my foolishness…when I turned my attention…my crew was struck…by the serpent…I knew…there was a foul temperament…behind this snake." The night wears on for the crew, and they soon find themselves a pack carrier short. Dr. Jumbo silently walks to the head of the group and finds a perch to purchase before addressing his men. Dr. Stevens accompanies him at the forefront. "I have made a decision…and you may all choose now…to come with me…or to continue…without me…I will search…for this snake…The Devil in The Desert…and study him…You men are free…to do as you wish." Bold Dr. Jumbo strode from his perch, turning away from his crew, and without looking back, entered the night scrub. He did not know it, but above him, a curious eagle flew, always watching the intrepid journeyman as he set forth. A short distance ahead Dr. Jumbo stopped and surveyed the landscape. A few crags to his left provided adequate hiding places for the snake, and what appeared to be a small patch of grass ahead of him appealed to the snakes base instinct to ambush. A murmur from behind the good doctor caused him to smile ever slightly as his men strode up behind him. In fact, Dr. Stevens had never left his side, a fact that Jumbo did not let on to knowing. He leaned down and ran a hand across the ground, finding a snake trail in the sand. His head shot up when a sharp rattling noise sprung to life before him. There, in a small clearing, lay the culprit. He was coiled up and hissing, baring his teeth to the band of men. The eyes of the serpent followed Dr. Jumbo always. He studied the Doctor’s movements, careful, ever observant, one might even say controversially. The magnificent Dr. Jumbo took a step forward, into the clearing, and the small snake struck out, lashing violently. It was as if the snake meant to destroy Dr. Jumbo’s every facet in his life. Jumbo stepped back, the coiled pit viper spitting pure hatred towards him. "I have never witnessed…such malice in a creature…I do not understand…it’s hatred for me…I do not wish…to harm it…Yet I cannot see it free…for it’s attack." As suddenly as the doctor says these words and Bill translates them, a shadow blackens the moonlight and a dark form hurtles down, smothering the small serpent in vicious claws. Even as the snake is held to the ground by the large bird, it continues to try and strike its enemy with the lethal venom it has used to kill so many. Dr. Jumbo steps forward, pondering the new revelation before him and he smiles. "I understand…the eagle now…He hunts his prey…in a time unexpected…and though he does…not quite belong here…he has adapted…and is now stronger…This is essential…to evolution…itself." The small snake strikes out once more before the eagle digs its talons into the serpent and cries before beating its wings heavily and launching itself into the sky. The good doctor, who has seen many a most excellent adventure smiles and waves as the eagle fades from view. "This can be a note for all…to always do what is expected…can often lead…to failure…Yet for those…who choose to…the option of…the journey…to be different…can yield great results. He turns to his men and shakes the hand of Dr. Stevens before turning again into the wild darkness of the desert air and stepping forward on yet another adventure. In the night, deep within his own home, a man tosses a little as he sleeps. "The Samoan Silencer" Timo Bolamba dreams of a vast skyscape, and looks down to The Devil of The Desert in a pair of eagles claws, and to a group of hardy adventurers as they endeavor to explore. Later, as the crew sets up for a rest stop. A young cameraman voyages off to film an Arizona Bark Scorpion (Centruroides sculpturatus). As he kneels down and focuses on the small arachnid he hears a faint rustle and a noise not unlike a rattle shortly before feeling a burning sensation in his leg. The camera trembles and falls to the ground, followed by a hand that lands just in front of it. The last vision of this Wild Discovery is the hand, twitching ever so slightly before it grips into a fist one final time…and then falls limp. Do not trust a safe pass with serpent in the grass, for even man must fear the bite. But if you soar upon high with an eagle eye, you can hunt safely through the night.
