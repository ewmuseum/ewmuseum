The Second Act. (Bad Omens, Fear, and Loathing)

31 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-31-07 From the notes of Sean Sterling: I saw him again tonight, standing on the rain swept balcony, his eyes dyed black by the shadows enveloping his face from all sides, the only part visible his crooked grin, those pale lips, teeth jagged and cruel. Crowned with hair as black as the devil’s heart, messy and soaked from the downpour outside. His skin, from what I could tell, was snow white, an amazing contrast to the rest of him. He was like a 1950s television show put to reality. Black and white, no colors. He made no movement and said no word, just stood behind the pane of glass, staring in at me in my dumbfound shock, and with a cliché, blinding flash of lightning, he was gone. This marks the third time he’s visited me. I’m not sure who he is or what is. An omen. A ghost. A figment of my own overactive imagination. Something evil, for sure. Something foul and wrapped in death. Judith thinks I’m going insane. She thinks the constant workload, the constant traveling, the shock of my slowly approaching dive into fatherhood, as well as the sudden changes it has brought about on my way of life, and the fear that comes with this slight fame I’ve received is getting the best of me. She told me I need rest. Sleep. Relaxation. Some time off. Some time to get used to the changes going on around me. I told her she needs to fuck off and mind her own goddamn business. What I need is a head full of so many insidious alcohols and strange drugs that my mind burrows itself into some faraway Shangri-La. What I need is to keep moving, fast and vicious, violent and vulgar, with all those grim thoughts pushed to the back of my head. Though, I have to admit, for the first time in a long time, I’m frightened. Maybe I am going insane. Maybe I’ve already been insane for several years now and it’s finally catching up with me. Either way, if this scum sucking pigfucker who has been appearing before me, clad in black and looking like death’s incarnate, is in fact real, then goddamn. What else? Just goddamn. 7/29/07, S. Sterling *** Greenville, 7/30/07 Judith awoke to the sound of the ceiling fan spinning in the other room with wild abandon and me, her lover and the soon to be father of her firstborn, suspiciously missing, leaving nothing but a cold spot on my pillow. She sat tangled in her bed sheets, head slanted to the side, as light from the hotel living room peeked through a crack in the door, sending a sharp, glowing knife of illumination across the carpet, piercing the bedside table and beckoning her to come take a look. Faintly, buried beneath the loud commotion of the ceiling fan overhead, a pencil was busy etching into notebook paper. The floor was littered with wadded up pieces of paper, a whole garbage can overflowing with scribbled words and shitty doodles. Then there I was, leaning over the table, my only companions a tall bottle of vodka and the usual cigarette hanging half burnt, ashes snowing down onto the table from time to time. I wrote like a madman, a whole head full of terrible thoughts put down to paper for the sake of relieving whatever stress had made it’s home in my skull. Lately, things were simply not working out for the best. The past two weeks were filled to the brim with horrible horrors, deadly visions of men clad in black, coming to surely kill me. Judith, the girl I supposedly love, her belly starting to bulge from that train wreck nightmare of a child growing inside of her, was driving me mad. Fuck love and fuck her. As far as I was concerned, if this was the sort of torment that love brought around, it could be burned at the stake, for all I care. Deadly thoughts on this grim night. Bad vibrations and terrible ideas running rampant in the forefront of my brain. I could hear Judith slinking out of bed, all faux-stealthy, her tiptoes creak-creak-creaking the floor. She was trying real hard to be unnoticed, but I already knew she was coming. It was of absolutely no interest to me, though. I kept writing. Kept smoking. Drinking. Fuck her. Let her be curious. But why? Why be curious? Can a man not drift into solitude? Is being alone so unaccepted into today’s society? Fuck her. Keep writing. Keep smoking. Keep drinking. Her opinion is of no consequence whatsoever. When I’m king, she’ll be first against the wall. She peeked in through the crack in the door to see me taking a long, long swig out of my bottle of vodka; enough to make a goddamn Russian gasp in shock and awe. I slammed the bottle back down on the table with some sudden burst of wanton fury, sending a splash of the cruel elixir across the desk, flooding the latest words I’d written down and turning them into nothing more than smudges and inkblots. I couldn’t even fully see her, but I knew the look on her face must have been one of absolute horror. Why wouldn’t it be? She was peering into a hotel living room deep in the dead of night, watching the future father of her child act in a rather erratic and coarse manner not unlike that of your average drunkard or even your common drug abusing space cadet. But why was he acting in such a manner? Why was Sean Sterling, the so-called love of her pathetic live, burning the midnight oil all for the sake of sitting at a desk until dawn, writing God knows what? Had the man slept, for God’s fucking sake? To answer that question, I hadn’t slept in days. You don’t need to sleep enough drugs in your system to put an elephant out of his misery. *** Indianapolis, 7/25/07 Cruel lights and flashing nightmares on these dirty streets underneath the stillborn moon of this depraved Indianapolis night. I was alone, hands stuffed into my pockets, walking the cracked sidewalk of the apparent ‘bad side’ of town, leaving a trail of a thousand cigarette buttes in my wake. Judith was at the hotel, asleep, and I had snuck out in the dead of night with every intention to drowned every goddamn sorrow I‘ve ever had. Sorrow can’t swim against the waves of cheap liquor, so I intended to make my way to the nearest bar. Just another night. Nothing new. I do this at least three times a week. Take a few wrong turns and suddenly your intentions will shift as the city that surrounds you makes a slow metamorphosis from streets armed to the teeth with neon beer lights to a seedy underbelly, the freak kingdom, where whores roam like a wild pack of dogs and junkies shake in the shadows with imaginary bugs crawling underneath their skin. On this side of the tracks, in such a large town, you have an entire galaxy of drugs at your disposal. All you have to have is money and the communication skills to deal with the dealers. Suddenly, cheap liquor isn’t enough. Suddenly, just this one time, you want something harder to set your mind at ease. Suddenly, you find yourself in a lonely alley exchanging currency with a man who calls himself Esteban. Suddenly, you find your pockets overflowing with a goddamn smorgasbord of illegalities. Depressants, stimulants, hallucinogens, those goddamn narcotics, you just fucking name it. Suddenly, your late night cravings for alcohol have turned into something foul and diseased. A decadent and vile trip into the realm of debauchery. Suddenly, it’s too late to turn back, and now all that’s left to do is pick your favorite flavor and drift into that slow, strange delirium that of which we call ‘being high’. I made my way back to the hotel with glee, and there in the hotel’s cheaply furnished living room, I partook in a virtual feast of dissipation and degeneracy. This particular night, I ordered a large dose of LSD from the menu with an appetizer consisting mostly of marijuana and what I can only hope was hashish. By the time the idea of dessert came around, I was far too full and hallucinating wild and gruesome things. The television set swallowed the room whole, me with it, and I went into a mad scramble across the carpet to the balcony, where my every intention was to make some great leap of escape, but the idea passed and I settled down when I realized how beautiful above my head was. The stars all hung like ornaments from heaven, and in my state of mind, I took them to be angels slowly descending to take me away to some faraway place which held some sort of resemblance to my idea of heaven. Soon after this thought, I suppose I passed out in a heap on the balcony, because the next thing I saw when I opened my eyes was Judith standing over me in a bathrobe, her hair dirty and twisted in a train crash nightmare, a cigarette breathing smoke from one hand and a cup of coffee breathing steam from the other. And of course, on her face, a look of puzzlement, confusion, and slight disdain. *** Greenville, 7/31/07 I woke up surrounded by wadded up pieces of paper, my body still easing off the feelings of euphoria from the night before, but at least I was coherent. I can only imagine what I wrote and I can only imagine how I ended up on the floor, passed out amidst many aborted writings. I stood slow, using a nearby chair as a means of balance. My knees were wobbly and desperate for me to fall back down, but I wouldn’t have it. I looked around the room, and right about then was when I noticed the state that the room was in. It looked as if a goddamn tornado tore through while I was sleeping. The walls were covered in lipstick drawn graffiti. Things I didn’t understand. The stuff you would find etched onto cave walls by some overly artistic Neanderthal, only nonsensical and vulgar in origin. I don’t remember what a single thing meant, but I’ll be damned if I didn’t draw each and every one. The kitchen floor was covered with what I can only imagine was dried coffee, spilled with some wanton anger that I don’t recall. I directed my attention to the ceiling fan that had been spinning like helicopter blades the night before, but now was bent and beaten. It didn’t spin anymore. Goddamn, it barely even looked like a ceiling fan. There were pills and powders of every shape and size scattered on every surface, including the ground below my feet. My entire body was covered with sweat. The bottle of vodka I had previously known and loved was shattered on the ground, it’s contents soaking into the carpet. And then there, out of the corner of my eyes, I saw a part of the wall that differed from the rest of the graffiti. It was unmistakable, and I knew beyond a shadow of doubt what it meant. Blood was splattered on the wall. Not much of it, but enough to come to the conclusion. that there was some grim altercation. Suddenly, flashbacks. Shouting. Screaming. Punching. But what about Judith? Now I was the one sneaking. Slow movement across the carpet. Half of me wanted to collect my belongings and leave the hotel without a trace, the other half was curious as to what strange fate had descended upon Judith. I wasn’t bleeding, so it had to have been her, unless some foul miscreants found their way into my hotel room last night. I peeked through the crack in the door, slow and cautious, before pushing it open and revealing… absolutely nothing. Judith wasn’t there. In fact, the only indication that she was ever there to begin with was the unmade bed where the sheets now laid bunched up. I didn’t a see a damn thing that belonged to her. Her clothes were gone. Her suitcase was gone. Her makeup, her jewelry, everything was gone, as if everything just decided to disappear while I was out cold. And, of course, the car keys that had previously been on the bedside table were now suspiciously missing. I made a mad rush out to the parking lot, down several flights of stairs and through the lobby. I must have looked like an absolute wreck, because people stared, and they stared hard. I ran with a certain sort of urgency that a man can only feel when faced with the possibility that he is stranded at a hotel in Greenville without a car or any means of transportation. I ran with this prospect causing chaos in my head as I jumped to far too many conclusions for my own good. And when I arrived at the parking space where my car had been parked… I found it still there, the car keys sitting silent on the hood, and that’s when I knew Judith had got the last laugh. That’s right about when I noticed the punch line. Beside the keys, Judith had etched her goodbyes into the hood. She’d carved into the paint and left a paragraph in messy, key-writ, handwriting. And even though I should have been pissed, even though I should have been screaming bloody murder, all I could do was smile. And that’s when I saw the man I’d seen on my balcony on the Sunday before, across the road, clad in black, staring at me before disappearing behind the traffic whizzing by. Just another bad omen. The shit had just begun to hit the fan. *** From the hood of Sean Sterling’s car, written by Judith Kindle: Dear Sean, I’m sure, by now, you’ve noticed that I’m gone, and I apologize that I didn’t personally say goodbye, but I can’t handle this anymore. I can’t handle you. I can’t handle the way you treat me. Flat out and simple, I’m done. I’ve given up on you. I’m going back to Manchester, I’m going back home, I’m moving on. Bye, I love you, I love you, I love you. Love, Judith P.S. Sorry about doing this to your car. Notebook paper seemed to cliché.
