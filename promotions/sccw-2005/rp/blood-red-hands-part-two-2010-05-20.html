Blood Red Hands (part two)

20 May 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 20/05/2010 The door swung open easily, moving on greased hinges and floating nanometres above the plush carpet. ‘The Valkyrie’ Suzanne Larsson entered, dressed in jeans and a loose blue t-shirt that served to conceal her prodigious muscles. She dropped her gym bag on the floor and walked to the bed. Relaxing, she was about to drop, dead weight, onto the welcoming mattress until she spied a man sitting in the chair in the corner. He’d been so still that it was almost as if he had blended into the furniture. Suzanne did not need to even look at his face to know who it would be. It was the man who had brought her into Sin City Championship Wrestling and given her the spark to compete after her GTT loss. It was the man who had protected her from the Icelandic Council. It was the man who never seemed to be there when she needed him, who came and went as he pleased, always knowing more than she did, always with a smile born of mischief on his face. This time, she reflected, things would be different. "I almost didn’t see you there," she said. "I’m good at disappearing." The smile, never far from his lips, was present again. Suzanne was going to take great enjoyment in seeing it go away, as she started to erode whatever advantage he felt he held. "So I hear. In fact, your Icelandic friends thought that you were long gone." "They did? They should learn that you can only trust what you see with your own eyes... and even then you really should be careful." "Perhaps you should," said Larsson. "Still, I have a question for you." "Feel free to ask," said the man. "Okay," she replied, savouring the moment. "Why the fuck didn’t you tell me you were my father?" ***** One week previously, at SCCW Temptation "Suzanne!" In the blink of an eyelid, The Valkyrie knew that her evening was about to get significantly more complicated, for the voice belonged to Gunnar Arnisson, and from the certainty in his voice, he was probably not alone. Turning slowly, Suzanne marshalled her emotions. She’d last seen Gunnar in Iraq, and had managed to beat him half senseless before getting him taken away by a group of marines. She didn’t know the specifics of how he had talked himself out of that one, but she knew that with his Gift of Coercion, it wouldn’t have taxed him unduly. "Suzanne Magnusdottir," he said, the softness of his voice belying his appearance. "Don’t call me that. I left your country and its conventions behind me long ago." Gunnar inclined his head gently, as if to ask forgiveness. Suzanne was taken aback at how well he had healed. For a man of pensionable age, there was barely a blemish on his face from where Larsson had used her bare fists to split skin and rend flesh. "You are looking well," said Gunnar. "Not as well as you. Perhaps you can teach me that trick?" "We’d be more than happy to do that, Suzanne," said another voice, from behind her. This voice carried a weight of authority behind it, and The Valkyrie now knew for certain that Gunnar was not alone. "Nice party trick, Lars, but let’s cut out the bullshit. What do you want? I’ve warned you before and I’ll say it for the last time. If you keep on at me like this, keep on stalking me, I’ll have to take matters into my own hands, and next time it won’t be something your friend will be able to heal from so easily." Lars slowly walked around Suzanne, to stand by his colleague and old friend. Where Gunnar was a barrel-chested bear of a man, Lars was taller, thinner and older, but yet he still managed to exude a certain air of menace that Suzanne could not ignore. Not ignore, but she could choose very well to fight it. "What do we want, Suzanne? We want to help you." "By killing my husband?" "We did not do that, though I will admit that we shed no tears at his passing. He struck out at you, at a member of our family, and we do not normally take such things lightly." Something about the calmness of the older man’s voice was starting to shred Suzanne’s nerves. "Get to the point, Lars." "So impatient, Suzanne. When you’re as old as I am, you will hopefully have learned that certain things take time; time to accomplish, and time to understand. It has taken me fully half a year to understand what has happened to you, or perhaps I should say who has happened to you." "What do you mean?" "You have someone advising you. He is telling you about us, and about some of the things that we have done." "Are they true?" asked Suzanne. "Most of them, probably," said Lars, nodding gently. "Not everything that the Council gets involved in is strictly legal, or even moral, but deprived of context many worthy actions can begin to look like evil. At all times we operate in the best interests of our family, and of our country. Sometimes that brings us up against enemies. Sometimes those enemies are people that we thought were dead. Maybe even someone that you thought was dead." Larsson’s eyes narrowed as realisation began to creep up on her. "How much do you know about Magnus Larsson?" said Lars. "How much do you really know about your father?" ***** "Why the fuck didn’t you tell me you were my father?" Suzanne glared at Magnus Larsson, waiting for his smile to turn to anguish. It didn’t so much as flicker. "A number of reasons, Suzanne, some of which I might tell you and some of which I might not." "That? That’s what you’re giving me?" spluttered the exasperated Valkyrie. "I hope that in time you’ll understand why I did what I had to do. The longer I stayed aloof from you the longer the Council wouldn’t realise I was still alive." "You think that I’d have told them?" "They’d have worked it out. Trust me; Lars and Gunnar are no fools. Do not, under any circumstances, underestimate them." "So that’s it? You were just scared of them? Trying to protect yourself and screw the rest of your family?" "Suzanne," said Magnus, standing up and looking right into her blue eyes. "If I was solely interested in self-preservation do you think I would have come here at all? I had escaped. I was free to live the rest of my life, and live it well. You were living your life too, and so was Charlie. I wished that I could talk to you, or see you, hold you, be any part of your life, but it was too dangerous. The Council didn’t know where you were, but when Gifts start to manifest themselves, they tend to find out, somehow." Suzanne couldn’t look her father in the eye. "So, wait, you mean that you aren’t Gifted?" she asked. "On the contrary, my daughter. I just learned how to hide them, safe from the prying eyes of those who I wanted to avoid." "Lars told me that he and I had Foresight, and that Gunnar had the Gift of Coercion. How many different Gifts are there?" "Four." "And which one do you have?" "Suzanne, my dear," said Magnus, the familiar knowing smile back on his face. "I have all of them." ***** Gunnar Arnisson was not a man built for doubt, yet as he followed Lars Halldorsson down the hotel corridor, he couldn’t help but feel in his gut that something was wrong. He was used to being in control of not just himself, but those around him, and now there were just too many factors he couldn’t nail down. "Lars," he said, slowing to a stop. "Do you think we should have told her?" "Gunnar, do you doubt me?" responded the head of the Icelandic Council. "I do not doubt your strength or your wisdom, but this is a very different game to the one we have been playing." "I understand," said Lars. "I share your concerns, but this is something that has been playing out for decades. I regret that we were fooled by Magnus faking his death, but now we have all three of them, and I planned for that." "You did?" "Gunnar, my oldest friend. There are scenarios I plan for that you would consider so unlikely as to be laughable. It is my burden. I was granted the Gifts of Foresight, Coercion and Sense. With these, I am sure we can overcome any betrayal from Magnus, and set his children on the right path." "Have you seen this? Do you know how it ends?" "I have, Gunnar, but it is sketchy, as if obscured in some way. I know that this is a dangerous path, and that Suzanne is the key to everything. I can see her, kneeling down, her hands covered in blood. She’s just killed a man, and she’s had to make a choice, a decision, but she thinks it was the right one." "Do you know who the man is?" asked Gunnar, hesitantly. "I do not." "Are you saying that to protect me, Lars? I am willing to die for this, if I must." "Gunnar, I honestly do not know how this will end for the two of us. All that matters is that it ends well for Suzanne, and that is why we are where we are." "We have to involve Charlie." "He must be in the right place at the right time, or none of this will work. If he does not play his part, I fear those blood red hands will be coming for my throat, and there will be nothing I can do to stop them." ***** In her room, Suzanne Larsson sat in silence, trying to think of the words that could express her emotions, and coming up short each and every time. Her father sat opposite her, calmly observing with an air of detachment about him. Eventually she had to do something; inertia not being her natural state. "So, what do you want from this?" It seemed to take a couple of seconds for him to realise she had said a word. At first nothing, and then he came back to himself and his eyes focussed in tightly on her. "I wanted to get a chance to meet you, and to spend time with you. I missed those years that were stolen from us." "Forgive my scepticism," replied Suzanne. "I don’t need a Gift to know that you’re here for more than that." "Yes," said Magnus, and with that he laughed, and it seemed like genuine emotion for the first time. "I do want more than that. I want to stop being on the run. I want you to fulfil your potential. I want to be free of the damned Icelandic Council once and for all." "You think that they’re going to come after you again?" "I know they will. I know they’re doing it right now. Just across town in Charlie’s hotel they’re speaking to him, Coercing him to do things against his will. You can’t trust him anymore, Suzanne." "The fuckers!" she exclaimed, getting to her feet and looking for something to slam a fist down on to. "What right have they to use him like that?" "They don’t care, Suzanne. Just the way they never cared about your husband before they Coerced him to commit suicide. All they want is to protect their little monopoly and go back to the frozen wastes they came from." "So if you have all the Gifts, why can’t you just stop them yourself?" asked the Valkyrie, though she already knew the answer. "We are limited as to how we use our Gifts on others like us. I can’t, on my own, overcome them. That’s why I’m here with you. We can benefit from this mutually. I gain revenge over what they tried to do to me, and you can finally lay your husband to rest. And then, we can really get to know each other." Magnus smiled, but this time it was different to his superior mocking that she was used to. He was radiating warmth, as if it was the first time he had opened up to someone in years. "So, the specifics," she said. "What do you need me to do?" "You have to kill them. You have to kill them both."
