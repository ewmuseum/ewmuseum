Adapting

23 September 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 23rd 2009 So he got a ‘1’ in the win column. Trouble was, he hadn’t had to compete to get that W. For most people this wouldn’t be an issue, they’d just take it for what it was and move on. Josh Marquez isn’t most people. *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ ‘What the hell are they up to?’ The way Josh had been pacing up and down the room, it was a wonder the carpet didn’t have a furrow worn into it. Harry Reed, as ever, was acting a deflector, taking his friend’s rant in his stride, calming him down bit-by-bit, simply by being straight. ‘It was my second match and the guy no-shows. It’s not right, it’s just disrespectful.’ ‘Josh, you’re my boy, but you gotta remember, to them, you’re nobody right now. Sure you used to be a bit of a star, but not with them. You gotta expect them to throw the odd curveball early on. Just forget it.’ ‘It just really pisses me off. I mean, I bust my ass to get to that arena in time and for what?! So I could stand there like a chump while that jackass was off doing Christ knows what! It’s bullshit!’ ‘Okay then, if you’re going to be a little bitch about it…’ Marquez pauses and looks at his friend, eyes glowering for a second before he starts laughing. ‘I guess you’re right. Let’s move on. Pay-per-view this week, big match, all that, yeah?’ Now Harry is chuckling, a smile breaking across his face. ‘You’re damn right.’ *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ 2 days later ‘Is he always like this?!’ Josh says this with a grin as he struggles with a demanding adversary – his son, Luca, who at this stage is very adamant about not getting into his stroller. ‘He just likes the attention, like his dad.’ Anna Kingsley looks happy, seeing her son and his father interacting, the three of them looking like a secure and stable family unit. Usually when that’s the case, some disaster or other is just around the corner but for once, Anna wasn’t worried. With her two boys together and in her life, no problem was insurmountable. Well, except getting Luca into his stroller. ‘He’s a nightmare!’ Josh is laughing now, taking everything in his stride and eventually succeeding in getting Luca strapped in. ‘Okay, let’s go!’ Anna takes his arm as they make their way out into the blistering Las Vegas heat, Josh pushing the stroller in front of them. Externally, he looks calm and collected. Inside his head is a different story. When Anna had dropped the bombshell of Luca’s existence, he’d reacted well. Too well. With everything else that was going on in his professional life, Josh had simply done what he thought was for the best, not fully comprehending what exactly this development would mean in the long-term. He had a son. He wasn’t a free agent any more. He had responsibilities to other people now. For the first few days he’d wondered what all the fuss was about. This cohabiting thing was easy, the sex simply mind- blowing and stunningly frequent. The parenting side, no trouble at all. Why people made such a big deal over it, he had no idea. For the first few days. Then reality bit. Luca needed constant attention and while he loved spending time with his son, there was only so many cartoons Josh could stand in a day. He was exhausted and that wasn’t even considering the time he’d had to spend with Anna, getting to know each other again, getting her moved into the apartment. Then on top of that there was the issue of keeping on top of his business interests, as well as training and travelling for his wrestling career. There just weren’t enough hours in the day. Naturally, doubt was setting in. Had he done the right thing or had he moved too fast? Was he doing it more out of convenience than a desire to be with Anna and Luca? Could he juggle all the balls he had without dropping any of them? For someone like Josh Marquez, this feeling of uncertainty in himself and his abilities was a strange feeling. He’d never once had anything but supreme confidence in himself and yet here he was, questioning every little thing he did. It wasn’t a great situation to be in, and he was determined to get out of it as quickly as possible. Question was, how? *~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~ ‘…48…49…50! Okay, next exercise!’ The sweat has soaked through his white vest, the clammy fabric sticking to his body as he jogs over to the rowing machine that sits in the corner of the gym, a strength- sapping beast in a nondescript package. Then he’s on the machine, 2000 metres of pain ahead of him. After this it’s the Roman chair, then the Marine sets. For most people, the next step is more than likely collapse and exhaustion but Josh is anything but normal, physically and mentally. This workout regimen is relatively new, born out of Josh’s understanding that what worked for him in the past isn’t going to cut it here in 2009. The old ways are not always the best, at least not in the wrestling industry. No, to move on and develop in his career and his personal life, Josh needs a new approach, a fresh outlook. No longer can he be the carefree party guy whose bedstead has so many notches it was beginning to look like a Leatherface victim. No longer can he workout, run the ropes a few times and feel prepared for whatever his opponent throws at him. No longer can he take his spot in the company as a certainty and ignore the win/loss ratio. He needs to adapt. Josh Marquez 2.0
