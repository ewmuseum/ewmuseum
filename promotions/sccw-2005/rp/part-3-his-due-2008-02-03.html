Part 3: His Due

3 February 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/3/08 Imagine that there had been a serious car accident, and that the victim of it needed quick medical attention. Most people would be grateful to receive it, no matter who it came from. 'That is going to leave a horrible scar.' Most people are not Wyatt Connors. This is for the best, really. If they were, imagine how difficult it would be to buy a car. 'My God,' Wyatt muttered as he looked at his stitches in the mirror. 'I could have done a better job, and I wasn't even conscious.' A soft knocking came from the other side of the door. 'Y'all right in there, Reverend?' Mavis called. 'Fine, dear,' he replied, overloading his voice with syrupy smarm. 'I'll be out in a minute.' Every ounce of sweetness in his voice had been taken out of his face; his mouth twisted into a scowl that would make children burst into tears. His finger traced the line of stitches that ran just under the hairline. It was easily concealed for the moment, but would become glaringly obvious the next time he got his hands on some pomade. There were other things to take care of first, of course. Specifically, getting out of this miserable, godforsaken rathole. It hadn't been all bad, of course--he'd been well fed, and the conversation was nice enough, if not intellectually stimulating--but a person can handle only so much pleasantry. If this kept up, he would have to strangle someone. He splashed some water on his face, then dried off. While the hand towel was perfect for wiping away the moisture, it did nothing to remove the look of scorn. He did that himself, once he opened the door of the bathroom and rejoined his gracious hosts. Connors beamed at Mavis Jessup, a saccharine smile plastered all over his face. 'I do apologize, Mavis. But as they say, cleanliness is next to godliness.' 'Oh, I understand perfectly,' she replied, completely missing his insincerity. 'You wouldn't believe how much dirt the boys track into the house.' This earned a snort from Hiram, who was sitting in a tattered easy chair in the living room. 'Don't you go scoffing at me, Hiram! Just yesterday morning, you tracked in enough mud to bury a horse up to his knees.' Wyatt smiled vapidly at the couple's bickering. It was amazing how he could achieve such a disconnect between his inner thoughts and his outward reactions. To look at him, you'd never know that the prevailing thought in his mind was, God, I cannot wait to get away from these people. With any luck, he wouldn't have to wait much longer. He walked over to the kitchen table, and looked at his few possessions that they had found and gathered up. There was his suit, which was now clean...although God only knew what shape it was in. He didn't expect these people to have a dry cleaner nearby, but that didn't stop him from being upset about it. In the meantime, he'd been wearing some of Hiram's old clothes...unfortunately, Hiram had been a strapping fellow in his younger days, and so his old flannel shirt and jeans were a remarkably poor fit. It was a good thing that Wyatt did not also need to borrow underpants; he would have needed a second pair of suspenders to hold them up. The other item belonging to him was the Bible...which, as he damn well knew, did not belong to him at all. He'd have to talk with Seth about it...that, and the one item that was supposed to be there, and wasn't. 'Would you know where I could find your hired hand?' he asked. 'I've been meaning to talk to him before I go.' 'Reckon he's out back, fixin' the tractor,' Hiram mumbled. 'Damn thing ain't run right since--' Hiram's crotchety griping was interrupted by his wife. 'Go? But...you've only been here a few days!' Mavis was quite distraught, so Wyatt did his best to act apologetic. 'I'm terribly sorry, my dear. But you know as well as I do that we cannot deny what we are called to do.' 'But...but...I've told all the neighbors about you! The McCoys have been cleaning up the old church all week! We haven't had a real reverend in years--' 'What about Old Man Henderson?' Hiram piped in. 'It's not the same, Pa. Henderson's done fine, but he can barely read anymore, his eyes are so bad. Besides, he's just been leadin' us in the songs and readin' scripture. That ain't the same as having a real reverend. And now you're just gonna go?' The woman was almost in a state of panic over this, so Wyatt tried to calm her down, using relaxed tones, logic, and of course, laying it on with a trowel. 'Mr. and Mrs. Jessup, don't get me wrong. I am very grateful for the hospitality you have shown me this past week. You have been the very model of charity, and I would love nothing more than to stay here and guide your local congregation. But the truth is that I am expected in Los Angeles. I have been assigned to an inner-city youth group...it's the same kind of thing I did in New York; trying to turn these kids to God instead of gangs.' It was a tricky lie, and Connors hoped that the Jessups wouldn't hear 'inner-city' and think 'not white.' He didn't know for a fact that they were racist, but you could never be too careful when dealing with hillbillies. 'I know you had your hearts set on having me around. But the truth is, those kids need me more than you do. I can honestly say that if your neighbors are anything like you, Mavis, then you don't have much to worry about, as far as your souls are concerned. I'd love to stay and help, but the truth is, you'll be just fine without me.' Fun fact: when someone says 'the truth is,' the statement that follows is almost always false. This is one of the rare occasions where, if not completely true, then it is at least half-true. If he were being completely honest, he would have said, 'you're better off without me.' 'I want you to know that I'm not trying to hurt you, or insult you, or anything like that. I do appreciate what you've done for me. You tended to my wounds...quite possibly, you saved my life. I will always be grateful for the time I spent with you and your husband, but it is time for me to move on. Please understand.' Mavis nodded, but could not conceal her disappointment. She looked absolutely crestfallen. Wyatt tried to look sorry, but it only served to make things worse. Mavis Jessup turned around and ran into the bedroom, leaving a trail of quiet sobs as she went. Hahahahaha. Connors watched her leave, somehow able to contain his sadistic glee. His face was completely sheepish as he turned toward Hiram. 'You understand, don't you? This isn't something I wanted to have happen, but--' He was cut off by a quick glance from the old man. It was not a pleasant one. 'I'll just gather my things and go, then.' # # # It was only a two-minute walk from the house to the rickety machine shed, but Wyatt Connors made it last closer to five. He figured it would be best to give the Jessups time to calm down. Besides, there was definitely something untoward about this Seth person, and Connors was in no great hurry to find out what it was. He just wanted to get the last of his things, and get the hell out of here. Sure enough, Seth was working on an old International tractor. Wyatt nodded in approval; he didn't know much about tractors, but he did know that only communists used John Deere. Quietly, he approached the larger man. Seth worked in a shortsleeve shirt, despite the chilly February weather. The farmhand cut quite an impressive figure--he was in good shape, similar to a Lance Marshall without being freakish...well, as freakish. His muscles bulged as he tightened a bolt with a socket wrench. Good-sized guy, he thought. If I'd run into him five years ago, I could have made some money off of him. His job done, Seth carried his tools back to a nearby bench, and started wiping the grease off of them with a paper towel. Wyatt walked closer and spoke. 'I am told that you retrieved my personal effects from the car.' You had to know when to cut to the chase, and this was obviously not a conversation where pleasantries were needed. Seth looked up at the Jessups' 'honored guest' and nodded. 'I did.' 'I don't suppose you came across a large metal briefcase.' 'Don't know what you're talking about,' Seth replied flippantly. Wyatt Connors did not believe him for a second--lying, as opposed to tractors, is a subject he knows very well. 'Listen, chief. I know you're trying to play some little game here. I'm not interested. All I want is to get as far away from this miserable jerkwater place as I can, as fast as I can. The sooner you return my property, the sooner we'll be out of each other's hair. Deal?' 'No, Wyatt Connors...I don't think I'll be taking one of your deals. Bad things happen to people who do.' Wyatt blinked. So he knows me, he thought. That explains a few things. 'You don't recognize me, do you?' Seth asked. 'Should I?' Seth chuckled. 'No, I suppose not. But I remember you. I remember you very well. You ruined my life.' Wyatt rolled his eyes. 'I'll need you to be more specific.' If you were to write down a list of all the people Wyatt Connors screwed over, he would buy stock in Paper Mate and make a fortune. 'It's not important right now. What is important is that I've got something you want. And if you ever want to see it again, you'll have to play along with...my little game.' Normally, Wyatt Connors was a man of infinite patience. He could wait forever, for just the right moment to execute his plans. But that was when he was still in the game, not when he was almost out of it. He snapped, 'Fuck you in the heart. You will return my property, or I will kill you in your sleep.' The threat was met with laughter. 'You can try, Connors. But I know you're a thinking man. Think about this--one, you can't take me in a fight. I'm a hell of a lot bigger and stronger, and I haven't been in a car accident in the last week.' 'I've beaten longer odds.' 'Two, it wouldn't do you any good. Even if you did, you still wouldn't have your precious case. You have no idea where I have it stashed--and believe me, there are plenty of places to hide something out here. You could look for a hundred years and never find it.' 'Trifling details.' 'Three, people would notice. Hiram's as dumb as a post, but even he'd notice when nothing was getting done around here. And then people would start asking questions.' 'I really don't--' And then he stopped himself. Shit. He's got me there. Wyatt Connors did not like it when people asked questions...and he liked it even less when they got answers. 'Man...if they could see me now,' Seth gloated. 'The legendary bastard 'Wise Guy' Wyatt Connors...and I've got him painted in a corner. I am going to get the sweetest revenge, Wyatt. The sweetest.' 'By making me pretend to be a Bible-thumping God jockey for a bunch of hay-baling yahoos. That's your big revenge? You're an Olympic idiot, you know that?' 'Oh, this is just the start, Wyatt. Just the start. There's a lot more coming...and when it's all over, you're going to wish you'd never crossed anyone, let alone me.' Seth looked down on his much smaller victim and smiled. It was an ugly thing, full of menace and teeth. Wyatt Connors seethed, but gave no show of it. He knew that should tell Seth to take a flying leap off of Fuck Yourself Mountain. He could always replace what he'd lost...if he had the time. But that was gone forever. So close to the finish, and he'd run out of time. Nothing else for it, then, he thought. He stepped right up to the farmhand and stared him square in the chest. Then, he took a step back, and looked him in the eyes. Hey, intimidation is difficult when you're five-foot-seven. 'How do I know that I'll get my property back?' he asked. 'Some of us are men of our word, Connors. Unlike you.' 'Sure you are. All right...when will I get it back? If I'm going to run through your ridiculous maze, I need to know you won't move the cheese at the end.' 'You shouldn't be so paranoid, Wyatt. You'll get your case back, if you play by the rules. And, if you survive.' Wyatt shook his head. He'd been given more death threats than he could count. One more meant less than nothing. At least, for now it did. 'All right, shitkicker,' Wyatt said. 'You're on.' 'I thought you might feel that way,' Seth replied. 'By the way, what's in that case?' Wyatt smirked, but did not answer. The farmhand hadn't managed to open it yet. That was one advantage, if a small one. Seth seemed rather nonplussed about the lack of response. 'Have it your way.' 'Well then. I suppose I should head back to the house and tell Hiram and Mavis that I'll be staying on.' 'Suppose you should.' Wyatt turned away and started the long walk back to the house. After a few steps, though, he looked back at Seth. 'I'm going to break you for this. You know that.' Seth grinned. 'We'll just see about that, little man. We'll just see.' # # # Hiram sat in his easy chair, reading the local newspaper. He liked to read in peace and quiet, and finally got it once Mavis had stopped crying, and busied herself with some household chore. Unfortunately, it didn't last very long. Just on the edge of hearing, there was a noise like a human voice. A very, very angry human voice. Hiram put down his paper, wondering who the hell was making all that racket. As the sound got closer, he could pick out individual words...and sincerely hoped that his wife couldn't hear them as well. Someone nearby was cursing a blue streak, and not being very secret about it. Hiram had never even heard of some of those words, and he'd been in the Army. With a groan, he got up from his chair and left the living room, bound and determined to find out who was responsible. He looked out the window, but couldn't see anyone. Meanwhile, the voice was getting louder. He headed toward the door, figuring he could get a better look from there. He never got the chance. By the time he reached the door, the knob was turning on its own. The moment the door opened, the noise disappeared...replaced by the smiling face of 'Reverend' Wyatt 'H.' Connors. 'Ah. Mr. Jessup, I have wonderful news,' he said. 'After communing with the Lord, I have had a change of heart.'
