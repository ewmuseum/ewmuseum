Appendix A Through E (IG 50)

October 2008

Date: October, 2008 Appendix A :: Mervin Humperdink It started, of all places, at a Denny’s. I’d been home for a few months, but hadn’t really let anyone back in yet. Some of them knew what happened, but those who did got the vibe that I wasn’t really feeling like talking about things, at least not yet. I remember sitting there at the table with Mervin Humperdink, a guy with a catastrophe of a name who also happens to be a raging nerd and my best friend. We’d been keeping the conversation pretty light so far, and while I could tell that he really wanted to know what was up he was cool enough not to say anything, not at first anyway. Merv’ was never the biggest guy in the world. I’d guess he was about five-six, maybe five-foot-seven, and he couldn’t have weighed any more than a buck-thirty. After hanging out with me for the better part of 15 years you’d think he’d have learned how to dress himself, but he always looked the part of the stereotype. I may or may not have been drawing on the placemat with a set of crayons. That part I’m a little fuzzy on. Merv’ had ordered a Grand Slam something-or-other, and he kind of laughed when I ordered my usual: boneless Buffalo wings and a stack of pancakes. I seriously must have the worst diet in the history of professional wrestling. It was actually his idea to head out that day, since it’d been a while since we’d seen each other, but it worked out for the best since I wanted to talk to him about a few things too. I think he was hoping I’d have just come right out with it, telling him my side of everything that happened in Japan, why I’d fallen completely off the map for a while, and where I’d spent the last few months. I had other things in mind. "So I’ve been getting some feelers," I said, "from a company called FUSE." I have this thing about companies who all-caps their name. I’m pretty sure that the only way to say it is to shout it, otherwise they wouldn’t have spelled it that way to begin with. Stylizing be damned, I’m shouting. Sorry. Kind of wandered for a minute there. So Merv’ just sort of nods, but I can see the wheels in his head spinning. The guy’s a certified genius, and was always set for life even without me carting him around the world. Come to think of it, I have no idea why someone in his position would want to spend all their time following around some jackass pro wrestler when he could have very easily set up shop in Manhattan, Silicon Valley, Raleigh, wherever, and made a shit-ton of money in computers. "Well, they used to be called FUSE. I think they’re going with Sin City now. Apparently a few of the old crowd are hanging out there, so it’d be kind of like going back home." I think he was a little surprised at how I said it. Given everything that had happened in the last few years I doubt that getting back into the sport is something that he’d have expected me to be so cavalier about. Ha. Cavalier. A word I’ve never used, ever. Actually, now that I think about it, I was drawing robots on the placemat, but they were like those old school wind-ups, like something out of that "Danger, Will Robinson" show. Lost in Space, or something. That’s when he said it. I honestly didn’t think he had the balls, but sure enough he dropped it on me like, I don’t know, a heavy thing. "Are you sure that you should do that?" he asked me, "Are you sure it’s not too soon? I heard about what happened, Jared. Maybe you should just lay low for a while, and take it easy." If I knew the words to explain what it’s like to think you’ve lost your mind I’d have told him, but unfortunately for me I’ve never been the most eloquent (another word I’ve probably never used) speaker in the world. I remember smiling. I think it made him a little uneasy, because he got all fidgety as soon as he saw it. "But this is what I do," I said, hoping to reason with him, "this is more or less who I am. I’m not saying that I can’t do anything else, but it’s that whole ‘falling off the horse’ nonsense." I hate clichés. I seriously do. "You’re probably right," I went on, "I probably shouldn’t, but at the same time I almost have to." I really hate clichés, even more when I know that I sound like one. I’m such an ass. "I’ve even got an ‘out’ in case things get to be a little more than I can handle. I figure that I might be able to pull this off in such a way that folks won’t realize it’s me, at least not right away, and then I can kind of go at my own pace. If I decide it’s too much I get out. If not I keep going." That’s when he made the mistake of asking me what it was, which only made me smile wider. Watching his reaction was hysterical, even more so after I told him what I was going to do. I’m pretty sure there’s no good way to tell someone that you’re going to dress up like a giant blueberry and throw a crown on your head, and if there is than I’m pretty sure it can’t be done with a straight face. Didn’t stop me from trying, though. Appendix B :: Violent Jesus Anyone who thinks that I’m an unusual guy needs to take a serious look at some of the company I keep. I don’t have the same kind of relationship with Lloyd Sullivan that Merv’ does, but nonetheless I’d say we get along okay, or about as okay as you’d expect to get along with a half-crazy oaf who looks like Jesus and acts like, well, Jesus at age 4. The story with Lloyd is that he used to work for a software company writing code in a cubicle all day. From what I gather he was a religious guy, but not psycho-religious or anything. I guess his company was going through some layoffs and Lloyd was one of the poor folks who got the axe. When he went home that afternoon he caught his wife cheating on him with one of the neighbors, and his brain just sort of went kablooey and replaced itself with a potato. Personally, I don’t blame him. While I haven’t had that whole wife-bangs-the-guy-next-door experience, I’m pretty sure that if I did I’d want my brain to become a starchy vegetable; preferably a yam. Yams rule. We sort of bumped into Lloyd by accident on a run through California in the late months of 1999, and he’s been with us ever since, traveling with us from city to city and living at Merv’s when not on the road. We call him "Violent Jesus", mostly because he looks like Jesus, is known to burst into the occasional tirade, and is awesome when it comes to breaking shit. One time he also transformed water into Kool-Aid. It seemed enough like a miracle to us. It’s because of their weird bond that when it came time to let Lloyd know what the plan was (and that I’d be dressing like a blueberry) that Merv’ did most of the talking. If you think explaining "King Blueberry" to a rational person was tough, try watching a legit genius explain it to a legit potato-brain. "Lloyd, do you remember when we used to travel from town to town and put on shows in front of everybody?" Mervin was always great at this sort of thing, explaining it like you would to a child without being too condescending. "Eugene wants to dance on Broadway!" Here’s the thing about Lloyd: ever since a tour through Minnesota he never spoke for himself as himself, if that makes any sense. See, there was one afternoon when we kind of lost him out in public, and he wandered out into the city. I guess he got his hands on a toy rabbit, a plush yellow one from one of the shops, and he started carrying it around with him. He said it was his herald, or something. Then he went and named it Eugene; started hanging it on a rope around his neck like Flava Flav. Ever since then it’s always been "Eugene wants this," or "Eugene wants that." And on the rare occasion when he’s not speaking for Eugene he refers to himself as "The Me". It’s seriously fucking bizarre. Oh, and Eugene never just asks for anything, he barks demands at you like you owe ‘m money or something. "Lloyd, we’re going to be going on some trips again, like before," he continued. I just sat back and let the master work. Lloyd was confused at first, or about as confused as someone in his state could get. To put it another way Lloyd was to confusion what Spinal Tap albums are to the color black. I’m not sure what Mervin told Lloyd about my situation. Even if he did, I’m not sure that Lloyd would really be able to understand it, or realize what it all meant under the surface. What I do know is that he stared at me for an awfully long time before he said anything else, and it kind of creeped me out, more than a little. I’ve never told anyone this, but there are days – not many, but a few – when I’m convinced that this is all an masterfully crafted act, and that V-to-the-J knows a lot more than he’s letting on. "Eugene can come, too?" And then sometimes I’m snapped right back into thinking we could make quite the French fries from whatever’s in his head. Merv’ was the one who gave him the news, which got Lloyd all worked up. He started flapping his hands the same way I did when I was little, which got me laughing a bit since my mother always told me not to do that because people would think I was retarded. Thus far I’d been holding on to a little surprise, but hadn’t told either of them yet. I figured this was probably the best time to drop the hammer. "Just so you guys know," I said, as calmly as I possibly could, "there’s a catch to this." It was here that Mervin’s brow furrowed. I think he was expecting something like this – he’d known me too long not to, but he was most likely holding out hope. I only wish I was holding a camera, because the reaction was priceless. Lloyd seemed to love the idea, but Mervin, well, if this was Star Trek and he had a phaser it’d have been set to ‘murderyouintheface’. I have no idea why he ever thought that I’d be the only one in this group who’d be dressing like a blueberry. Appendix C :: Lawrence Spiderman If I had to wager a guess, Lawrence Spiderman, my agent, would punch me in the face if given the chance. I realize that he pronounces his name ‘speeder-min’, and that’s all well and good, but c’mon. How can a guy with a name like Spiderman expect me to do anything but give him a hard time for it. It’s just funny, at least to me, though I realize that sort of thing tends to go against any conceivable definition of the word ‘professionalism’. I got his number from the guys at Stracker’s, the school where I got my start in the ring, and our first few conversations were pretty good. I know he’d represented a few of the other guys to come out of Darren’s place, and he had a pretty good reputation from what they told me. "Hi Jared, nice to talk to you," this was the first thing he said to me on the phone, and it was probably the only time he’s ever had that thought. "Darren told me you’d be calling. So, what can I do for you?" For the record, this is not a question that anyone should ever ask of a man who’s planning on dressing up like a blueberry, especially if he hasn’t yet told you that he plans to dress up like a blueberry. It was two weeks of business by phone before we met in person. Spiderman’s office was in New York, but seeing as I wasn’t gainfully employed at the time it was easy enough for me to hop on a train and rocket on down. The train ride gave me time to think about whether I was making the right call or not. I’m pretty sure we know how that turned out. The first time I met him I did it as myself, Jared Sykes, and not under the mask of "King Blueberry". I sat alone in the waiting room, since Merv’ and Lloyd had stayed back in Boston. The girl at the desk – I think her name was Stacey, was kind of cute, and I couldn’t help but notice that she was shooting the occasional glance my way. Maybe it was because she thought I was attractive, maybe it’s because I was reading a copy of Highlights for Children. That Goofus is such a douche bag. Larry smiled a lot that afternoon, at least at first. I think he was excited about the prospect of a new client, as anyone in business for himself would be. "So tell me about yourself," he asked, a question that I think he’d regret in, oh, say, seven seconds. "Well, I’ve been a professional wrestler for the better part of 9 years, held a few titles along the way, and represented the National Wrestling Council as its J-Crown – that means cruiserweight, champion for the second longest reign in history both in days and defenses." Larry just nodded along for a while, but I wasn’t done. "My closest associates are a nerd and a man who thinks he’s Jesus. I used to carry around a porcelain lawn ornament, a gnome, that I called Earl. He was great for bonking people in the head with." He stopped nodding, kind of looked at me funny, but because I’m me my mouth just kept on runnin’. "I had a highly-publicized relationship with a hermaphrodite once, had a trans-dimensional experience involving some guys who called themselves the Monks of Funk, and then Gandhi fought Frank Sinatra on the Astral Plane for the rights to my soul." Larry just kind of stared at me. It probably took an awful lot of willpower for him to keep his jaw from swinging open like a rusty gate. "I’m pretty sure they did it Street Fighter style." "Uhh," his response was about par for the course. Here’s the thing about me: most people would pause at the ‘uh’, but most people probably wouldn’t have confessed all the nonsense that I did. I’m not most people. "But there’s more, and it’s pretty awesome…" I have a serious problem shutting up. "I’m thinking of coming back under a mask. Now I realize that it’s kind of clichéd, and I hate clichés with a passion, but I don’t think anyone has ever done anything like what I plan to do." Larry actually responded to this one, which kind of surprised me, though admittedly his response was pretty short. "And that is?" "Well, I’m going to dress up like a giant blueberry." You’d think it was a miracle that he didn’t kick me out of his office right then and there. "Yeah, I’m going to start calling myself King Blueberry, ruler of the fruit kingdom. And since I figure that since every king needs a court I’m going to pull in two other people, Mervin my personal nerd and Lloyd my personal Violent Jesus, and stick them under masks as well." While I was talking I noticed that Larry would sometimes glance at one of the drawers in his desk. I figured it was probably where he kept the magic button that called security, or maybe opened the trap doors. Hell, if Larry had trap doors I would have immediately made him my hero for life. "I’m going to put Mervin under a mask and call him Blueberry Dragon. I think I’m also going to put Lloyd under a mask as well, except I’ll call him Blueberry Mask 14. I don’t know who’ll get the joke, but the whole thing is basically a send-up of the history of masked wrestlers." Larry swallowed hard. I think he was wondering what he got himself into. He could have fired me as a client, could have had security escort me from the building, but he didn’t do either. Part of me thinks he was honest-to-God curious about whether this would be the magnificent train wreck he imagined. Appendix D :: Alexandra Pierce There are few people in this world that I find as undeniably fascinating as Alexandra Pierce, but there are a few things about her that need to be addressed before going any further. One, her last name is Pierce, and while this may not mean a hell of a lot to most people, in certain circles it’s synonymous with "Hi, I’m a giant asshole." Her brother, Ray Pierce, and the only other Pierce that I’m terribly familiar with, was kind of a prick, and with her older brother being something of a dick it’s no surprise that she turned out how she did. Second, she was part of a group of people who called themselves ‘The Illuminati’. They worked together to close the National Wrestling Council, a company that I had called home for a number of years and the place where I had my greatest success. If it’s any indication, a guy named Wyatt Connors? – also in that group, also kind of a dick. Third, she has the strangest eyes I have ever seen in another person, and I find them to be both terrifying and alluring at the same time. They’re gray, which is just kind of creepy. People tell you that the eyes are the gates to the soul, and if Alex Pierce’s eyes are that colorless than what does that say about her soul, hinging somewhere between light and dark? They have this uncanny ability to give you the feeling that they’re staring right through you, which is also creepy, but it also makes you wonder what’s on the other side. We’ve met a number of times in the last few months, though at times I didn’t know it was her that I was dealing with. Throughout the summer she’d had me, as well as a number of other people, believing that Charlotte Ramone was a real, separate human being. I assume that some people knew the truth, people outside her Dead Man’s Hand, but if they did they certainly weren’t talking. What I find especially ironic about our first meeting, her as Charlotte and me as King Blueberry, is that we were both trying so hard to be other people. I’ll concede that her façade worked infinitely better than my own, and it was so convincing that sometimes I wonder whether there were moments when she believed she was someone else. We were going to be competing in the same match that night, and I wanted to let her know that because she was a woman, and because of what I believed, that I wasn’t going to come after her. She probably thought me weak for it. Had I known then what I know now I wouldn’t have changed anything, and I would have kept my word not to touch her. She actually tested me with that the second time we met. It happened the night that Jonathan Rhine’s career was ended at the hands of Amy Campbell. I had made it a point to talk with her, but this time it was Alex Pierce as herself but I was still King Blueberry. She offered me the LiveWire Championship, and I failed to find any hidden agenda in her words. She asked if I would have extended the same courtesy to her as I did to "Charlotte", and again I failed to read between the lines. I did answer honestly, however. The whole thing fucked me up. I was nervous going in, and she could certainly sense that, and when I left I did nothing but throw up for the next fifteen minutes. Fortunately the third time we met it went a little better, and there wasn’t any vomiting. Because of that third meeting she is now in my debt, as we’d made a bet prior to going into a 6-person match that evening. Because I scored the pin, because I won for my team – with maybe a little help from Lance Marshall, I now have a wish to cash in at her expense. For the first time in who-knows-how-long, Alexandra Pierce is at the mercy of someone else, and I can’t help but wonder what she thinks about that. Twice since it happened that thought’s kept me up at night. Truth be told I wonder what she thinks about anything. I can’t imagine anything as interesting as getting insider her head for a few minutes and wandering around her mind. If there were a way I could Matrix my way into her brain I would do it in a heartbeat. I’m also not entirely convinced that all of "Charlotte Ramone" was an act. Oh, sure, there are rumors floating around about her sexuality, but that’s not at all what I mean. "Charlotte Ramone" was a good kid. You don’t just up and fake that. Nobody is that good. Not her, and certainly not anyone in her organization. Not that I’ve seen, at least. She’s the reason I took my own mask off, why I ended my charade a little earlier than I would have expected. Even though she’s probably got a file on me somewhere (though I doubt she’d ever admit to it), I didn’t think it was fair that if I was going to try and get to know her that I should deny her the chance to reciprocate in kind. I tend to think of myself as a pretty good judge of character, and while I’m sure she’s done some vile shit in her life I don’t think Alexandra Pierce, the woman they call Desade, the Spider in the Web, the Director, and who-the-hell-knows what other clever nicknames, I don’t think she’s as evil as we’ve been led to believe. It’s on her to prove me wrong. Appendix E :: Sebastian Parker "With all that out of the way, there’s only one question that I have." Sebastian Parker was hired by Akane Kawahara, the head of Hawaiian Island Wrestling, a member federation of the National Wrestling Council. He was contracted in the early months of 2002 for the purpose of examining and profiling Jared Sykes, a wrestler soon to be under her charge. Kawahara’s motives had been anything but benevolent, looking instead for a way to dismiss Sykes rather than embrace him into her company. Sebastian now sat in a Starbucks in Cambridge, Massachusetts, sipping a venti soy latte and staring at the man he had at one time been hired to profile. "What’s that?" came the response from Jared, in between sips of an iced caramel macchiato. "You told me how you became this character, but you didn’t quite tell me why." Behind a pair of silver-rimmed sunglasses Jared raised an eyebrow, the corner of his mouth curling into a half-smile. His relationship with Sebastian was an unusual one. In the strictest sense Sebastian was the doctor and Jared the patient, however the two had formed something of a friendship that extended beyond the normal doctor-patient dynamic. It had started when Sebastian had made the mistake of falling asleep on the way from Boston to Portland, Oregon and his shoes had been filled with crushed Twinkies. "You want me to tell you why I decided it would be better for me to hide myself under a mask?" "In a word, yes." Jared sighed, not quite sure if the person asking was Sebastian the doctor or Sebastian the friend, either way there was only one answer he could give. "I was hiding," he said, taking another sip from his drink, "Mostly from myself, if you want the God’s-honest truth." The response caught Sebastian a little off-guard. He’d always known Jared to be painfully honest, which had become evident to him in their first meeting, but in this case he had expected to have to prod a bit more before Jared would cave. "I don’t know," Jared continued, "with everything that had gone on in the last few years I wanted a way to get back into things, but at the same time wearing a mask lets me be someone else. If I’m someone else, if I can take even the smallest piece of myself away, than I don’t have to worry about anything that might go wrong, because it won’t be me who’s responsible for it. You know?" For a moment Sebastian did not respond. "I’m not sure if I should be worried," he said at long last, his gaze fixed on the man across the table, "I’m not sure if I should be concerned about whether you’re doing the right thing. On the one hand it certainly seems as though you’ve made or are making strides towards putting things in the past, but on the other hand it may be that you’re rushing." "How do you mean, ‘rushing’?" "Jared, you’re about eight months removed from a self-imposed stint in psychiatric hospital where you stayed for two months because of an incident that had nothing to do with you, but which for some reason you couldn’t keep from blaming yourself." Jared forcibly pushed his drink away from him, letting the plastic cup slide a bit on the table. His hands fell into his lap as he leaned back in his chair. When he sighed he took no measure to hide his frustration. "You have no idea what that was like, Park’. None whatsoever." "Maybe, but here’s what I do know. I know that you beat yourself up over it, unnecessarily, for a long time. I know that you’re not over it, and knowing you I’m pretty sure that it’s not something you’re just going to forget someday." Sebastian took a sip of his coffee before continuing. "Look, I’m not trying to be a hardass about this, but I’ve seen what goes on where you are. We have a relationship, a professional one and a personal one, and all I’m saying is that puts me in a position to maybe be a little more concerned about that than most people. And not for nothing, but if what I keep hearing about this Pierce woman is true, than I’m going to need to re-up my blood pressure medication." Jared chuckled, a move that Sebastian found to be quite interesting given the conversation at hand. "What?" "I wouldn’t be too concerned about it, ‘Park," Jared smiled as he took of his sunglasses, setting them on the table before him. Bright hazel eyes locked their gaze on Sebastian Parker, "When it comes to Alex Pierce I have a plan." For Sebastian Parker few thoughts were as scary.
