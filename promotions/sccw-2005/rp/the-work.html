Date: when he goes to work Over the course of my life, there have been many things that I've done that I could never claim to be proud of. *During Field Day in fifth grade, I stole Blaine Randell's Oakland A's hat and played keep-away with a few other kids that hated him just as much as I did. I took it to the bathroom and peed on it before giving it back. *My freshman year of college, I actively partook in the planning of a blanket party for a kid we all assumed was gay. By 'we,' I mean 'my fraternity.' And by 'blanket party,' I mean 'hazing.' At least that's what we told the Dean. *I've only had sex four times. Once, the most recent, I actually had to pay for it. It was pretty cheap. *When I was four years old, I stabbed my older brother in the back with a No. 2 pencil. I didn't really think about it at the time, nor have I since. These are just a few of the highlights from a rather populated list, however I am beginning to imagine that these were only preludes to a more absurd string of moral quandaries – preparation for the proverbial main event. I had been working as one Adam Dick's personal assistant for little over a week at this point, and already my conscience had endured many burdens. It all began with my first task, bestowed via a phonecall in the first conversation I'd ever had with the man. 'Huhhh-' 'Davenport?' 'Ayeuh-' 'AM I SPEAKING WITH DAVENPORT?' 'Ge-yeah. Speaking.' It was 4:30 in the morning, interrupting a beautiful dream about Carrie Underwood in the back of a tour bus. 'It's the Eater – your new Employer. I need you, Davenport, and I need you pronto.' 'I'm... I'm sorry, I thought I was to meet with you at eight o'clo-' 'DUTY KNOWS NO SCHEDULE. You've got thirty minutes. Call me when you're at an outside line.' Then the call cut. Despite the highly suspicious and outright bizarre exchange, there was no way of knowing what I was getting into. Had I been told what the work would entail prior to accepting the job, I'd probably considered my reservations. But even then, it'd still pale in comparison when weighed against the paycheck. I tried to assure myself, and I still do – the money is worth it. It's all that could keep me going. I walked half a block down from my complex to the closest Duane Reade's. It amazed me to find that the price of payphones had been raised to fifty cents. Then again, I couldn't remember the last time I even used the primitive thing. It'd only been twenty minutes, but I never took more than fifteen to get ready. Quick shower, a comb through the hair, five minutes to put on a suit. It was an art I mastered when I first began taking classes at 7:30 in the morning during my Freshman year. At the time, I passed it off as efficiency. I don't know if I'd still call it as such. Of the two numbers I had, I chose the one on my cellphone. It was different than the number I'd received from RMG. At first I was unsure of which one to use, but for one reason or the next something told me it was acceptable to call the number from which he called me. As far as I could tell, I was being tested, and in retrospect, I don't think either would have been the right answer. 'THE FUCK IS THIS?' 'Um, Hi, Yes, Sorry-' 'WHY ARE YOU CALLING AT THIS UN-GOD-DAMNED-GODLY HOUR?' 'I, uh- It's Daniel Davenport, sir? You told me to call you-' 'TEN MINUTES FROM NOW. I told you to call me TEN MINUTES from now – A HALF HOUR from when I spoke to you. You trying to earn some brownie points?' 'No, sir, I just-' 'Brushin' yer teeth with cockjuice?' 'Excuse me?' 'TEN MINUTES. And don't call this fucking number again!' That was the second time he hung up on me in just as many phonecalls. There was little more than a paycheck keeping me involved – the jobbed seemed terrible from the get go and was proving even worse with the treatment in itself. My defense throughout all of this being that I was too tired to realize what was going on. Ten minutes passed – ten minutes of me feeling like a creep, posted in front of the brick wall of D.R.'s like some ill-tempered, underaged ingrate, meth'd out of my gourd, waiting for some poor, hapless, lonely asshole to buy me a 32 of Red Dog. Nine minutes until the alarm on my phone went off, and another thirty seconds of waiting before dialing the number. This time, I used the toll-free line given to me on the business card I'd received from RMG. Could have saved the fifty fucking cents for the new cigarette tax... [detail withheld]. [detail withheld] 'Davenport.' He answered rather calmly. 'Yes, sir.' 'You're standing in front of the D.R., right?' It was almost unbelievable. I glanced up at the red-and-blue neon just to convince myself I was actually there. 'Yeah. How did you-' '-know where the closest payphone to your appartment is? Um, Verizon-dot-fucking-com.' Hmph. Fair enough. 'I need you to pick something up for me and bring it straight to my place. This shit's gotta be lightning – no fucking about.' 'Sure thing. Now, when you say your place, do you mean the location we established originally through RMG, or is there some other-' 'Stop being a faggot and just listen to what I'm telling you.' ...Fair enough. 'Inside that Duane Reade, I need you to get me a Plan B pill. And a Mountain Dew. And a pint of Chubby Hubby. And a Plan B pill.' 'You already mentioned that, sir.' 'Just making sure you're listening.' 'Alright. Is that all?' 'For now. Bring it to the Delhomme Plaza on 93rd. Suite 2121. Do you know where that is?' 'I think I can manage, sir.' He took the receiver from his mouth and forgot to cover it, 'Arrogant little prick...' On second thought, he probably didn't forget. 'Fine. You have thirty minutes. And don't try and be cute this time, either, you faggin' ponce.' I can tell he was about to hang up, but he stopped himself in the midst of a panic. 'Whoawhoawait! Davenport, one more thing.' 'Yes?' 'Don't forget the-' '-The Plan B pill. I know, sir.' 'DON'T YOU INTERRUPT ME, SHITHEAD. This is exactly why YOU're taking the orders, and I'm the one giving them.' A moment of silence passed. I wanted to slam the phone down, but in the back of my mind, I knew he was right. An odd reason for that. My mouth hung open regardless. 'Don't forget the Chubby Hubby. You fuck up and bring back Chunky Monkey, I'm gonna spend your first check on a fur-coat made of Chinchillas, parade it in front of PETA drones until it gets dumped in lead-based paint, BURN IT, then force you to inhale the fumes. You get me?' 'I think so, sir.' 'Right. Twenty-eight minutes.' Then he hung up on me, again. It didn't dawn on me until all but one item had been crossed off the list that obtaining the final piece would prove more difficult than simply asking for it. In my groggy state, it didn't quite spring to mind that there were certain requirements one had to meet when purchasing a Plan B pill. The first was the issue of gender, and I wasn't exactly an ideal candidate. When it first occurred to me, I considered calling Mr. Dick and informing him of the law, in case he wasn't yet aware, that only women over the age of eighteen were allowed to purchase said pills, keyword being women. I quickly realized, due to albeit very brief conversations, that if I wanted to keep the job that this probably wouldn't be the best course of action. I quickly guided myself through the possible solutions, none of them pretty or dignified in the slightest. First, I thought about bribing the pharmacist outright. Slapping two-hundred dollars on the table with the proposition that it stay our secret. But then I realized that I came to this exact D.R. for various groceries nearly twice weekly, and that the man would just as soon flag me on some FBI list for pedophilia suspicion the moment I walked out the door. My next option was standing out front like a creep, once again, and praying to God that I could catch a woman walking inside within the next five minutes. A woman with a very, very, very open mind, at that. This plan had the potential to either A) work like a charm, B) get me arrested, or C) get me kicked in the testicles. Statistically, my chances were one-part away from utter shit. Finally, I arrived at a final solution of asking the pharmacist an in-depth and hopefully distracting question – one that would require him heading to the back for thorough investigation – which would buy me enough time to hop over the counter and grab the pill before he returned. With the right question asked and enough luck, there would be no commotion. It was my impression that security guards weren't plastered in front of a terminal of a Duane Reade at 5 o'clock in the morning, just waiting for the first glimpse of trouble to pounce upon. I figured that no one would notice a thing until they took their inventory, at the earliest the next day. Of course, the proximity of my home factored into each and every course of action, but there was little time for anything else. Finding another D.R., even one on the way, wouldn't be possible due to the fifteen minute drive to the Delhomme plaza alone. Then there was the x-factor of the budding morning traffic. Needless to say, I made a choice – the one that I figured would result in the less jail time. Also, the prospect of asking to get a Morning After pill seemed far more dreadful than stealing one, and my actions reflected this outlook appropriately. After recalling some technicality about namebrand Phenergen and the ingredient differences it had with the generic stuff, the graveyard pharmacist disappeared to the back for a good five minutes – more than enough time to poke around, grab what I needed, then bolt out the front door before he even had an answer. It went off without a hitch. Attaining the pill should have been enough of an alarm alone, but again, just like how I'd obtained it, it slipped my mind at the time. Even on the drive over, not once did I find myself pondering the reasons behind this seemingly absurd errand. My cellphone rang again just as I pulled in front of the Delhomme building for the first time, with six minutes remaining. It was my Employer with an alteration to the errand. Again, he demanded I go to an outside line. Of course, I obliged, but what little patience I could sacrifice at such an early hour was near nonexistent. At a payphone down the block, he told me that I also needed to pick up a copy of Twilight on DVD. 'Not that widescreen bullshit, either. Cheap ass mother fuckers trying to rob me of a full screen with their fancy black bars...' I told him that it would be impossible for me to get the movie and return in the original time-frame. 'Ten more minutes,' then he hung up. I was at the nearest Duane Reade's – a different one, of course – swiping my credit card through the RedBox, when my phone rang for the third time. 'Davenport. You still at the bootleggers?' 'Bootleggers? What bootleggers?' 'For the fucking DVD!' 'Oh, no. I'm at a RedBox just down the st-' 'A FUCKING REDBOX?' This was followed by the sounds of thuds, muffled through frequencies of the phoneline. 'Fine, they're your fucking late-fees. Anyways, I also need you to grab Disc 5, Season 4 of One Tree Hill.' 'Where could I even find that at this hour-' 'TEN MORE MINUTES.' Needless to say... click. Despite all of the strange requests, it never even occurred to me what I was getting into. Even taking a half step back and looking at what I'd gathered would be enough to to tell me that something was odd about my Employer. At the time, though, I was more concerned with the constant fetch quests and the abusive phonecalls more than anything else. I told myself that finding his latest request would be impossible as I perused the streets, and that if I received any guff for it that I'd spit in his face and walk out the door. Or slam the phone on him, which seemed all more likely. As fate would have it, the only Super WalMart on the entire island was little more than a mile away. Luckily, the traffic hadn't emerged from its morning slumber, so I darted over with little regard for speed limits. Locating the 'TV on DVD' section rather easily, I realized that one cannot simply buy portions of a box set; one must purchase the entire package. A little less than a hundred dollars sat in my account, and I was looking at a whooping price of 59.98 for over sixteen hours of Chad Michael Murray delight. It was another moment where I kicked my toe, bit my lip, and suffered for the sins of the almighty dollar. He didn't ask for the whole season, but I figure he'd appreciate getting it. With four minutes to spare, I zoomed back over to Delhomme. It didn't take long to locate his suite in the skyrise – just an ample use of logic. 'You're Davenport?' He asked through the chain-linked crack. 'Unless you've got someone else running around for you.' His eyes squinted through his vinyl mask – I couldn't wait to get a good look of this clown. Suddenly, all of the anger, the spite, the annoyance that had been festering since that first phonecall disappeared when my Employer opened the door to his suite. Gone were all of the notions of quitting and telling him off. I'd all but forgotten the four speeches rehearsed on the multiple drives back and forth. Everything vanished, leaving a gaping hole on my stomach, regret in my chest, and that feeling of 'duh, you fucking idiot' hammering into my skull. Sure, he was a sight himself – standing in his underwear with what looked like a cape draped under his bathrobe, a loosely fitted wrestling mask covering his crooked visage. For his ridiculous appearance, he wasn't near the most curious inhabitant within the dwelling. 'There a problem?' He snapped, just as fiercely taking the plastic bag from my hand. I couldn't respond, though. I was fixated on the young girl plastered to the couch behind him, as lifeless as a sculpture with the exception of her occasionally blinking eyes. 'THE ENTIRE FOURTH SEASON? I only said DISC FUCKING FIVE.' She was a young girl, no more than sixteen by the looks of her. Asian – assuming by her skintone and hair color, Japanese. Instantly, everything became disturbingly clear. 'Fuck, now I'm spoiling the stupid bitch... Alright, Davenport-' Adam Dick saw that I was not looking at him, but past him. He quickly glanced back, then shut the door slightly as if to hide what was going on, save for a spot wide enough for his head. Due of the angle, it did little in concealing the debauchery – I could still see the young lady. Frozen, her bloodshot eyes glued to the television fixed in a direction where I couldn't see the screen, though the distinct cries of Billy Mays are obvious at any decibel. Something wasn't right. Who, in their right mind, finds entertainment in late night infomercials? 'HEY. DAVENPORT.' 'I, I'm sor- I apologize.' 'ON YOUR OWN TIME. YOUR ASS IS STILL MINE.' He shoved a Post-It into my face. Inscribed on it was an address, but I was still concerned with the wellbeing of the girl on the other side of the door. 'Call me at this payphone. You'll receive further instructions from there.' I received a final glimpse of the girl as he widened the door, retrieving his head from the frame. 'FIFTEEN MINUTES.' The door slammed shut. Mumbling through the particle board, I could hear his footsteps across the tiles. 'Nosy little fuck... HEY BABY, I GOT THE CHUBBY HUBBY!' I swear - although I could be seeing things due to shock, delirium, and countless other reasons - in that glimpse, she was beginning to show the makings of a smile. It disturbed me. On the drive over to Midtown, the location of the address, I couldn't get the image out of my head – the poor girl, strung out of her mind, probably. Attached to his sofa, amused by the inanity of useless niche-products, too young to buy a Plan B pill, mildly excited that she'd finally get to watch Twilight. At least, that's who I saw her as. And if that's who she was, I could only imagine what brand of scumbag my Employer belonged to. It nearly fulfilled every prototype for deranged sexual deviants we'd gone over in my Psyche class all those years ago. More disturbing was the fact that I didn't notice it as I ran his errands. He even went so far in stressing my obtaining of the Plan B pill, and I was just as oblivious then as when I got the other pre-teen propaganda. I arrived to the payphone, and I as I parked in front for the remaining three minutes, I seriously contemplated just going home. I thought about calling RMG, explaining the situation. I even considered telling the cops – about the girl, the Plan B pill, everything – but it all came back around to one simple reasoning... The paycheck. When I was first offered this job, the title alone was enough for me to give Frank Gallagher the finger and walk out the door. But he was quick to point me towards the salary. Oh, Sweet Lord in Heaven, it was mighty appealing for a grad student with a mass amount of loans on the horizon. Those bills weren't getting any smaller as long as I stayed unemployed, and at the end of it, I was left with little choice. I reluctantly climbed out of my car and made the phonecall. 'THIRTY SECONDS LATE.' He said upon answering. 'I like your style.' 'Alright, I'm here. But after I'm done with whatever it is you need, I think you and I should have a talk-' 'Yeahyeahyesyeah, you're right Davenport. A good ole' fashion chat, gettin' down to brass tax... Just what we need. But first... this is real important, Davenport.' 'Yes, I understand Mr. Dick.' 'You listening?' 'Yeah...' 'Listen good?' 'YES.' Once again agitated. 'Right, okay... Nevermind the fact that the ice-cream was melted. See, there's these fliers that are posted all throughout Midtown. You should be able to spot a couple right now – bright yellow, black font.' I gazed around the street. 'Yes, I see the ones you speak of.' They were stapled to near every powerline along the road. 'These fliers, Davenport. They're bad news bears. No bueno. See, they're spreadin' fallacies about me that I simply can't have the people reading. A man of my stature... even in a lowly area like Midtown, HAH!' He spared a chuckle, 'Even in an area like Midtown, the right look from the wrong person. Pow, my career is over. Now, we wouldn't want that, would we? How would you get that fat, mac daddy paycheck if your sugar daddy's rotting in jail?' 'I don't know, sir.' 'You wouldn't. That's why I need you to find these fliers and take them down. EVERY SINGLE ONE. Get rid of them. When you have them all, don't just go throwing them in any fucking dumpster, you hear me? BURN THE SHITS. If anyone gives you any trouble or asks you questions, just say that you found the girl. We clear?' 'Found the girl,' sounded a little questionable. But he did have a point about the paycheck. 'I think I can handle that.' 'Great. TWO HOURS.' Once again, he hung up the phone. While his directions were certainly more alarming than the last few tasks he had for me, I could take a certain solace in the fact that I'd be getting paid for my efforts. Sure, the work may have been a bit depraved, but there was a surplus of zeros that went along with it. And then, I almost found a cause worthy enough to throw up for the first time since my Sophomore year. Approaching the closest flier, its depiction became increasingly clear and even more unmistakable. It was an accurate sketch depicting the very same lifeless girl in the Face-Eater's possession. Apparently, her name was Liu Xi Fei. Apparently, she's fourteen years old. This, of course, was all below the bold header that read 'MISSING,' followed by a phone number to call in case I'd seen her.
