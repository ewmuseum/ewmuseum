Date: 8.21.07 The dreary Illinois sky urinated a slow and steady rain just as it had the six days previous. The farmers had been complaining about not getting enough rain this season; maybe this would shut them up. The rain hadn’t stopped for more than a few minutes in a week and it was taking its toll on the landscape. The over-saturated ground had regurgitated any of the water forced down its throat after those first three days. Now puddles collected at every corner, in every dip and crack there was. "This weather doesn’t help me at all." John said as he flicked his wipers on a different level. The rain drops were pushed from his view by the rubber blades. He had entered town in search of Mark, the fire chief. John hadn’t really sat down and spoke to him since the passing of his father, but today was the day he would. And he was sure it wouldn’t start the two off on good terms. John thought that as he drove down one of the main arteries in Mazon, laughing as he finished the thought. He had known this man since he was a baby; he should have nothing to worry about. "Making it easy for me." John muttered as he turned the corner by the fire station. Mark was already at the station, probably on his way home from work. John pulled up in front of the building and took a deep breath as he shut the engine off and opened his door. The rain lightly fell across his body as he made the six step walk to the front door. "Hey John." Mark greeted. Whew. At least he had spoke first. He was seated in the office at his desk, looking through the mail. John stalled by reading the handwritten notes on the whiteboard to his left before turning to look into the office. "What’s up chief?" John answered back. Mark only shook his head in response as John took a step toward the office. He stopped himself and turned sharply on his heels, moving into the bay instead. He walked over and inspected his gear for a moment before finally sighing and heading back. He had to do it; there was no getting around it. He closed the door to the bay and stepped into the office, sitting down on one of the chairs across the room. "Chief I need to talk to you." John said. Mark must have heard the regret in John’s voice because he immediately set the mail down and turned to face him. His eyebrows had folded into a "V" with intrigue. John dropped his glare to the floor, trying to avoid eye-contact for the moment. "What’s on your mind?" Mark asked. John tapped his foot on the floor as Mark looked on. This was disrespectful and John couldn’t keep it up. The foot tapping stopped and John brought his eyes up to meet the chief’s. John saw the concern written on his face. He must have thought it was about John’s father. "I need to take a leave from the department." John stated. To say that Mark was a little taken back by John’s words was an understatement. Mark knew John loved his time on the department and wouldn’t trade it for anything in the world. Mark looked on as if he was waiting for an explanation or something. After a few moments of silence he spoke. "For how long John?" He inquired. John clapped his hands together gently before answering. This was hard. He paused for fifteen seconds before answering, just as his father had all his life. John sighed and looked back to the concerned fire chief. "Indefinitely." He answered. John knew what was coming next and he didn’t want to hear it. It was a prodding question into his life. John’s father had used it numerous times through his teens. Now was no different, whether or not John was close to thirty and the man asking him wasn’t his dad. "What for John? Something you need to talk about?" Mark asked. There it was. Even though he knew it was coming it caught him off guard. John knew what he wanted to say and exactly how he wanted to say it. But this man was his commanding officer, a man who had watched john grow up and for that he deserved the respect that "None of your fucking business." Would deliver. "Personal reasons. I’ll leave it at that." John replied. Mark nodded and put his hands in the air to indicate he was backing off. There; it was done. Now John wanted to leave the awkward situation. So he did. He stood and nodded towards the chief before reaching for the door knob. As he turned it to exit he looked back. "Put in the paperwork and leave it in my mailbox. I’ll sign it." He stated. John didn’t even wait for an objection or a confirmation before walking through the door and shutting it behind him. He wasted no time exiting the station and getting back in the car. The rain had stopped. For now. John sighed and started his car, removing his cell phone from his pocket. Now was the most difficult person to tell. He flipped the device open and dialed eleven numbers. It rang four times on the other end before a gentle female voice answered. "Hey mom. I love you." He said. That was it. He shut his cell phone to end the call and promptly turned it off. He never had the intention of saying anything more to his mother.
