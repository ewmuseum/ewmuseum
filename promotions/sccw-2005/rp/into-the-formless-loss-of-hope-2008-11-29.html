Into the Formless Loss of Hope

29 November 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/29/08 "Your phone was ringing off the hook, so I answered it. It's someone from Sin Wrestling, or something. They want to talk to you about a guy named Jared." Larry Spiderman, the man with the unfortunate name and the even more unfortunate distinction of representing Jared Sykes, smiled as his wife Melissa handed him the phone before stepping out of the room. It was a habit she picked up early in their relationship, and Larry had always found it somewhat endearing that she kept out of his business affairs, never one to ask him about any celebrities he represented. He'd spent part of the previous evening trying to reach his client, mostly out of contempt for having to sit through the reading of what he could only imagine was a narcotic-induced rant on the virtues of South Park. Needless to say, when his wife informed him of the incoming call he was quite relieved, believing that he would finally get the chance to ask Jared a series of questions that ultimately amounted to 'What the fuck is wrong with you?' "Perfect timing. First thing I'm going to tell them is that no-one should ever be subjected to reading anything with Jared Sykes' name on it," he paused, 'Ever." That chance didn't come. "This is Larry," he started, taking the phone from his wife and placing it to his ear, 'What can I do for you?" The voice on the other end of the line spoke with trepidation. "Mr. Spiderman?" it asked, pronouncing the name with the improper inflection that Larry had finally come to accept from folks that didn't know him, "This is Ellen Bishop, I'm with the Human Resources and Talent Relations division of Sin City Championship Wrestling. I... I wanted to talk to you for a moment about a client of yours, a Mr. Jared Sykes." He could sense the nervousness in her voice, but at the time he attributed it to the fact that she was calling on a holiday weekend. "Absolutely. Hey, tell me, have you heard anything from him? I've been trying to get in touch with him for a while now." For a moment there was silence, and Larry started to worry. "Actually," she continued, "Th-there's been an accident, and since you're listed as an emergency contact, I..." Her voice trailed off. Even in a promotion as noted for its hostilities as Sin City, this was the first time she had to deliver such news to anyone. Larry stood in silence while she composed herself, not quite sure whether to speak. "I wanted to... Something happened at the show on Thanksgiving, and Mr. Sykes and his friends have been taken to a hospital in Rochester, New York." His brain started to race, his imagination throwing itself into overdrive. All at once images rushed to his mind, none of them pleasant. He started to speak, but the words wouldn't come. "Sir? Mr. Spiderman? Are you still there?" "I... Yes. Yes, I'm here. I'm sorry, you just... it caught me a little off-guard. Is everything," he stopped himself mid-word, the realization hitting him, "Wait, did you say 'his friends'?" "Unfortunately, yes. Three of them were taken to the hospital. I don't have the specifics of their condition, but... Melvin or Marvin I think is his name; he was in bad shape." "Mervin," he muttered to himself. v "They've been taken to Strong Memorial Hospital. If we receive any new information I’ll be sure to pass it along." "Thank you." His arm moved to his side, the phone now dangling by his fingertips. In the many years that Larry Spiderman had worked as an agent he had never received a call like this. For the most part he had never needed to worry about receiving one, as the majority of his clientele weren’t involved in activities with such a high injury rate as professional wrestling. He didn’t answer when his wife called from the other room the first time. When he didn’t answer the second call she stepped into the living room, a look of concern on her face. "Larry? Is everything okay?" "I’m not… no. It isn’t." "Oh no. What happened?" "I have to go to Rochester for a little bit. One of my clients, the strange guy I told you about, the one who made me read that script, he’s in the hospital." Wide-eyed, Melissa gasped as she cupped her mouth. "Was that the doctor? Did they tell you anything?" "No," he said, sliding towards the hallway, "that was the company he works for. They called to let me know because I’m listed as an emergency contact. To be honest I have no idea what went on. I didn’t get to see the show because I was up all night reading." "Do you want me to come with you?" He let slip a faint smile. "No, it’s alright. I don’t know how long I’m going to be there, or what my role in this is yet, but I’ll make sure you’re in the loop. I don’t expect that I’ll be there over…" He paused, feeling that his phone had started to vibrate. "Hrm, three one two," he didn’t recognize the number, "Might be them again. I should probably take this." His wife nodded in agreement and he made his way down the hall. "This is Larry," was the last thing she heard before he turned the corner into their bedroom and disappeared out of sight. ---=====--- "Lesbian. Find out if anybody on that bus was taken to other hospitals." "He just forgot mine." "No, Thirteen, I just wanted to call you a lesbian." Larry Spiderman glanced at the wall-mounted television as he entered room 314. "What’s this?" he asked, nodding towards the television. "House. USA is running a marathon." Jared Sykes lay reclined on the bed in his hospital room, his arms folded behind his head. His left eye was bruised and swollen, there were small cuts on his forehead, and a mile of bandage was wrapped around his midsection. What parts of him weren’t exposed were cleverly hidden by a green johnny. His face was expressionless, his eyes fixed on the television. When Larry finally glanced in his direction the sight startled him somewhat. "Jesus," he blurted, "you look like hell." "Yeah. Seafoam green really isn’t my color," Jared responded, his voice wavering and devoid of humor. Even if Jared’s body was beaten and battered, his wit remained intact. The joke forced a smile from Larry. "Not quite what I meant. How are you holding up?" "Physically? Vision is a little blurry, got a splitting headache, and breathing hurts like a motherfucker. They took some x-rays, but haven’t told me anything yet. Probably cracked something." Larry gestured toward the left arm of professional wrestling’s Black Sheep, which was taped around the bicep and forearm. "What about the arm?" "Pre-existing. Connors botched a move a few weeks ago." "How are you doing mentally?" "Not gonna talk about it." Larry nodded as he moved further into the room, finally settling in one of the wooden visitor’s chairs adjacent to the bed. Jared’s eyes never left the television. Both men sat in silence for a moment, the only noise coming from the television and the nurse’s station outside the room. It was Jared who broke the silence. "Did they tell you anything when you got here about, you know, Merv’?" For the first time he allowed his eyes to wander away from the television, fixing them on his agent. Larry could see that they were red, bloodshot. He assumed it was a result of tears, but wasn’t about to bring it up. "No, not really. They told me he was in the intensive care unit, and that the area was reserved for family members." "Yeah, I tried to go down there earlier." It suddenly became very apparent that Jared’s words were working their way around the lump that had formed in his throat. "They wouldn’t let me in either. I was able to get a bit of information though, since we were brought in together." "Is he going to be okay?" Tears started to well in the eyes of Jared Sykes, and in an attempt to hide them he quickly rubbed his eyes. Through the emotion a single word escaped. "Coma." Larry closed his eyes, taking a deep, cleansing breath. "I’m sorry." "S-so am I." Jared’s eyes snapped back to the television. Eye contact was something he’d been avoiding since he first woke up and became aware of his surroundings. "Lloyd made out okay," he continued, "But he opened his mouth and they rushed him right down for a psych evaluation. Probably better that way. Won’t have to worry about what might happen to him." Another round of silence fell over the room, and the realization hit Larry Spiderman like a truck: he was not equipped for this, and he feared it was starting to show. "Say something, dude." The comment caught Larry off-guard. "I… uh…" "Anything. Please. If it gets quiet, then my mind wanders. If my mind wanders, thoughts creep in. If I start to think…" Jared’s right hand rubbed first across his upper lip, then across his mouth as a whole. Again he was trying to hold back a very obvious, very deep, depression. "I read your script." Much to Larry’s surprise his statement earned a chuckle. "Heh. Sucked pretty hard, didn’t it?" "It was… different. I’ll pitch it first thing next week, when everyone is back in the office." At this point lies were much safer than the truth. "Actually," Larry continued, "there is something that we should probably talk about, but I’m not sure now is the right time." "You have my full and undivided attention. Like I said, anything as long as it keeps my mind from wandering." Larry reached inside his coat, producing a white envelope and from it a folded fax. He stood from his chair, stepped to the bedside, and carefully laid it for Jared to read. "I got a phone call on my way over here. How’d you like to work for Mr. Cantrell?"
