Phoenix Rising

23 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/23 It had been several years since Seymour Almasy's Manhattan apartment had been transformed into the premiere warroom in professional wrestling. GTT5 in 2005, to be precise. Almasy's GTT6 tenure had been too wrought with emotion and impetuousness for him to establish his usual level of tournament preparation. The No Limit Tournament, it seemed, would make up for that particular lapse in concentration. Seymour, after all, was a man who had taken a briefcase containing dossiers on his CW2 competition to dinner with his wife. Somehow, she hadn't divorced him over this. A man who had protested what he considered unfair bracketing treatment by taking his case directly to PTC headquarters. Tournaments had been a huge part of the Protagonist's life. Arguably, he had first made his name in GTT3, winning his bracket and only falling to the Primetime Championship Wrestling World Champion. He had won the Primetime Invitational Tournament and the first PTC Cruiserweight Tournament. He possessed a semi-final appearance in GTT5. He held various tournament victories and strong placings in promotions around the world. In short, Tournaments (he always wrote the word with a capital 'T') were more responsible for the legend of Seymour Almasy than anything else. There were only nineteen dossiers lying strewn throughout the Almasy apartment in 2009, and the piece of posterboard that he meticulously wrote the brackets on wasn't quite as big as it usually was, but Seymour could have cared less. It was Tournament time again, and amongst the nineteen names was a depth of competition that not even GTT could match. Amy Campbell. Steve Knox. Wyatt Connors. The Illustrious Face Eater. Dusk. Phillip Kennedy. Jay Phoenix. The list read like a who's who of SCCW and PTC talent, to say nothing of the possible dark horses that arose in Tournaments. It was the last name who he was most concerned with, of course. Almasy entered the No Limit Tournament as the eighth seed, setting him on a collision course with the twelfth seed...who happened to be *the* most decorated tournament competitor in Primetime Central's history. 'Probably Kensington's fault,' Almasy mused, flipping through a folder of information on the Eternal Flame. 'He's still got his panties in a twist that I'm not jumping for joy at the prospect of joining Dusk on his underplanned, idealistic crusade.' By Seymour's line of thinking, there was no frickin' way that Jay Phoenix was a twelve seed. Really, seeding around the tournament was strange, but he chalked it up to allowing two men to pick their seeds. That, Seymour figured, and Kensington disliking him, 'He can put me in the bracket of death all he wants,' Almasy vowed, gazing at a picture of Jay Phoenix. 'It's happened to me before. It'll happen again. Unfortunately for him, I'm going to fight my way out of his best intentions.' Dr. Nigel Kensington, to Seymour, was only the latest figurehead to stand in his way of tournament glory. The nature of Tournament was such that favorites would often be booed by a sizable amount of the crowd. Seymour himself had felt such backlash many times, particularly in GTT4, when the underdog Craig Goat had upset him in the fourth round to the ovation of a capacity crowd. The Final Fantasy, a man who relied so deeply on the support of the crowd, was hurt by these fans. The Protagonist, however, would be and was less susceptible to that problem. He'd managed to prove that to himself by walking out on the man who had saved him from what could have been a seven on one beating. Seymour had heard the boos then. Quite honestly, he couldn't blame them for it. Seymour Almasy didn't do things like that. 'I never asked that asshole for help,' Seymour groused. He had come to the ring set to deal with Desade and Aimz by himself. He would have taken his beating and moved on. But Dusk was insistant on playing the hero. No, not even a hero, Seymour thought. Dusk was in this for his own purposes. He didn't have aspirations so noble as to make SCCW safe for everyone else. Craig Maloof wanted to take down Alexandra Pierce for personal reasons. Personal gain. Dusk was an anti-hero. He wasn't Jonathan Rhine. More gallingly to Almasy, Dusk insulted men like Jonathan Rhine. Men like Lance Marshall. And yes, men like Seymour Almasy used to be. The Lost Soul openly claimed that such people were too nice. That fire had to be fought with fire. That the only way to destroy tainted human beings was to taint yourself. If Dusk was right, Seymour believed, it was better to remain neutral. At least that way, he'd be able to look at himself in the mirror. If being a hero meant purposefully drawing blood, meant stooping to kidnapping and brutality and tactics that people like Desade and Rich Rollins used...there were no heroes at all. There was simply evil and morally justifiable evil. In such a world, there was no good, save in the individual hearts and morality of men, women, and children. In such a world, the Final Fantasy was dead and buried, Just as he should be. ------------------------------------- Much as he was loathe to admit it, the temptation was there. Seymour wanted to argue otherwise, to try and deny the fact...but the Protagonist was a much more honest man than the Final Fantasy ever had been. The Protagonist never dodged harsh facts. He met them face on. And the harsh fact was that Seymour Almasy wasn't confident that he could beat Jay Phoenix in the middle of the ring, one on one. He wasn't in his prime anymore. That fact had been made clear by pretty much everyone in the sport. Most of them thought Almasy should have retired for good a long time ago. Just enjoyed the money he'd made and find a way to move on with his life. Phoenix, one could argue, wasn't either, but that only made it *worse*. A not in his prime Phoenix was the sort of man that Almasy *thought* he should be able to beat. It was his first huge match in some time. Of course he was nervous. If you weren't nervous before a big match, you either believed you had no chance to win or you were dead. The worst part about his temptation was that he was almost positive giving into his temptation would give him what he wanted. It would give him his sought after victory over Jay Phoenix. It would earn him a round two match against Lance Marshall. It would give him a chance at No Limit superiority. And all he had to do to get that which he wanted most was to accept an offer. An offer from the woman who held Sin City in her grasp. Theoretically, if he took it, Seymour Almasy and Alexandra Pierce would be even. Realistically, he knew that the odds of that being true were slim to none. --------------------------- There were some wrestlers who couldn't remember their first matches. Others who forgot just who they took some regional title off of three years in their career. Seymour Almasy was not one of them. He had a relationship with the media staffs of every promotion that he was in. He liked pictures, and movies, and documentation of his career. One of his favorite scrapbooks, in fact, consisted of nothing but before-the-bell pictures of Almasy's biggest matches. Pictures of Seymour Almasy battling the UWF legend Tsunami Oni, the Devil of the Deep Blue Sea. Pictures of Seymour nose-to-nose with the Negasonic Lupine, Karina Wolfenden, in the finals of the PTC Cruiserweight Tournament. Even pictures of the once Final Fantasy mid-ring, across from Rich 'The Renegade' Rollins, both men's faces contorted in twisted, hateful sneers. On glossy picture paper they looked back at him, reminders of what he once was. Reminders not of what he could be, but of how *good* he'd been. The Final Fantasy was dead, but the skills he'd possessed in those days remained, waiting to be tapped into. A match with Mr. Ashe was one thing. A handicap match turned tag match against Aimz and Desade, good though they were, was along the same lines. A match he participated in, tried to win, but ultimately would shed no tears about losing. This was a different creature entirely. This was Tournament. Almasy was famous for having issued the soundbyte 'I simply don't lose in tournaments' in the first round of GTT4. Some of the luster had come off of that statement since, but when made, Almasy had been 14-1 in tournament competition. Moreover, this was Jay Phoenix. The Eternal Flame. His fellow tournament afficionado. Almasy knew from his experience that it took a certain type of competitor to thrive in tournaments. One had to be able to adapt to any situation, be prepared for all sorts of opponents, and be able to do this in short turnarounds. One also had to accept the inherent pressure that came with tournaments. Most tournaments were single elimination. One loss, and it was all over. Nerves of steel were a prerequisite. Phoenix had all of these qualities in spades. Like Seymour, he had more than his fair share of championships, but Jay loved tournaments. He'd been in every GTT. Been a finalist in two of them. Thrived on the pressure, danced his way through tournament field after tournament field. And like the Protagonist, the Eternal Flame wasn't exactly pleased with his current state of employment. C.P. Cantrell had used a quirk of contract law to force Phoenix to return to work for PRIME. The Phoenix had, in some sense, been caged. The No Limit Tournament was Phoenix's chance to fly free. To soar to the top of the mountain one more time. To prove to the world that he could excel in a promotion he'd never been in -- and those were rare in Phoenix's illustrious career. Flipping to the end of his album, there were several places left for more pictures. More battles. More crowning moments. This would be his first in Sin City. Soon enough, there would be a new picture here, the first of 2009, with two tournament specialists across the ring from one another. Two men looking for a new beginning, a resurgence, an ascencion to glory. The question remaining was simple. Whose phoenix would rise again from the ashes?
