The Dance of Joy

Date: 09/29 - 10/01/08 Monday, September 29, 2008 1:14am EST Jared Sykes, regardless of his reputation, was anything but dumb. He may have done some dumb things in his time, but he was anything but a dumb person. He preferred the term "harmlessly eccentric". When the match was over he’d made his way quietly to his locker, careful not to attract attention to himself. He quickly and discretely collected his things, opting not to shower at the arena. Given that it was less than hour by car from Manchester to Boston, he assumed his other traveling companions would understand. He ushered Mervin and Lloyd into his dark orange Honda Element (the vehicle of champions), and didn’t even speak until they’d pulled onto interstate 93 headed south. He did however smile an awful lot. When he crossed the border between New Hampshire and Massachusetts, he took the first exit available and pulled off into a twenty-four hour gas station, throwing the car into park but leaving the engine running. Only then did he finally say something. "I pinned Amy Campbell, I pinned Amy Campbell," he sang, the way one child might taunt another over a new toy. Mervin Humperdink, the man who’d been masquerading as ‘Blueberry Dragon’ for the better part of the summer, facepalmed as Jared jumped from the driver’s seat, still dressed in his wrestling attire save for a change in his footwear and a dark gray hooded sweatshirt. "I pinned Amy Campbell. I pinned Amy Campbell." Ah-cha-cha cha cha cha! The singing was soon joined by dancing, but fortunately there was no one around to witness this fiasco beyond his traveling companions and the middle-aged man behind the register inside the station itself, though he was too occupied with his magazine to pay much heed to the dancing wrestler that was making a spectacle of himself in the parking lot. "I pinned Amy Campbell, and now Desade owes me. Nyah nah-nyah nah nyah-nyah." Inside the car, Lloyd "Violent Jesus" Sullivan had started rocking back and forth in time with Jared’s song. In his hands Eugene the stuffed yellow rabbit danced as well. Jared remained oblivious as a red Ford Focus full of college-age girls pulled into the gas station, the driver hopping out to fill her car. From inside Focus came giggles, but the dancing man either didn’t care or didn’t notice as he pelvic-thrusted his way through the beams of his headlights. Mervin, a little thirsty and desperate to get away from the scene, quietly snuck out of the car through the open door. His right hand served as a makeshift blinder to prevent the sight of Jared, now doing his best Alex Wright impersonation, from burning itself into his brain for the rest of his life. He didn’t run, since he figured that would have been rude, however he did walk at a decent clip. "Hey." The voice startled him enough that he glanced quickly at the car full of girls, some of whom were now taking photos with their cell phones. He tried to ignore it and press on. "Hey!" No such luck. "Yeah, hi," his response was curt, as he was on a mission. That mission: to get his soda, get back in the car, bury his face in something and pretend this wasn’t happening. "Is that your friend over there?" she didn’t even try to mask the amusement in her voice. "Nope. Don’t know him." Mervin kept walking. Fortunately there wasn’t enough light in the parking lot to show just how red his face had become. "You sure? He’s waving at you." Mervin’s shoulders slumped as he turned in the direction of the car. No longer dancing, Jared was now making the international gesture for ‘get me a Gatorade’ which consisted first of making a drinking motion, then of pointing at himself wildly, and culminating with rubbing his stomach. Defeated, embarrassed, and painfully intimidated by the opposite sex, Mervin turned to face the car full of girls. "Please," he said, almost pleading, "please don’t post this on Youtube or anything." 15 minutes later… "I really hate you sometimes." Though they were back in the car and back on the highway headed home, Mervin was still getting over his embarrassment in the parking lot. "Aw, what’d I do? Was it the singing? Maybe the dancing? I bet it was the dancing." Jared, for the most part, was undaunted. This was par for the course with Mervin, as while the two were close, he never really got into the whole ‘nonsense for its own sake’ routine that Jared seemed to perpetrate on a daily basis. "Was it the girls? Please tell me it wasn’t the girls." "Well…" "Dude, they were, like, twelve. Besides, no need to get down. This is a day for celebration!" "What exactly are we celebrating?" Jared only sighed. "Did you not hear the song? Do I have to sing it again?" "No!," Mervin all but shouted, before he quickly reeled himself back in, "I mean, um, it’s okay. I get it." "But that’s only part of it my padawan of perpetual purity," said the alliterative asshat, who also happened to be driving the car, "we have a wish to cash in on, ‘like a fucking genie’." A very unconscious Violent Jesus’ weight shifted, pinning Mervin against the interior wall of the car. Mervin tried to struggle, but not being very strong did nothing but give Lloyd a better angle from which to drool on him. "So, yeah, about that," came the reply from the backseat, from a source pinned under the crushing weight of Jesus, "any idea what you’re going to ask for? I mean, it’s pretty open-ended, right?" "I’ve given it a little thought." "Maybe you should banish her from Sin City, or make her split up the Dead Man’s Hand. Heck, you could even get that bracelet for a shot at Amy." "Eh, I guess. I mean, all that stuff is well and good, but…" "But what?" Other than a few snores from the sleeping messiah, for a moment the car had fallen silent. "Wait," Mervin’s words bordered on accusatory, "You’re not going to… you know… with her, are you?" "Dude, no! Are you kidding me? That would be the world’s worst celebrity couple in the history of ever. I don’t even know what you’d call that. You can’t really ‘Bennifer’ our names." "Sure you can. There’s ‘DeSykes’." "Which makes it sounds like we’re hacking off parts of me, and that would be a bad thing." "There’s ‘Syke-Ade’." Jared exploded in laughter, and the car veered out of its lane as a result. Not wanting to look like a drunk driver, he quickly engaged his directional and merged into the lane to which he’d drifted. "That’s a terrible name." "Why? I think it has kind of a ring to it." "Merv’, I love ya’, but ‘Syke-Ade’ sounds like the worst idea for a powdered drink ever. That would be like if Kool-Aid man burst into your house, and while everyone was all ‘Oh yeah!’ he murdered your family. Murdered them with death." Mervin, who had been taking a drink from his bottle of Wild Cherry Pepsi, promptly spat it on the floor in surprise. "You know, people really need to stop with the fluids in my car." Wednesday, October 1, 2008 11:47am EST The car rolled to a stop in front of the loading bay of a small warehouse on the outskirts of Medford, Massachusetts. The building was by and large unmarked except for the sign next to the door that read Suite 27-10. For a moment Jared Sykes drummed his fingers on the steering wheel in time to the music coming from his radio. The song concluded, he turned off the engine and stepped into the brisk autumn air. From the back seat he pulled a black messenger bag adorned with a red strap, and slung it over his shoulder. As he walked to the sole entrance he pulled his cell phone from his pocket and turned off the power. After fumbling with his keys for a moment, he found the one that unlocked the door and he stepped inside, throwing the light switch as he crossed the threshold. "Honey, I’m home." For the last few years Jared had been renting this space, using it as storage for items he collected through his career. He’d been inspired by an episode of The X-Files, ’The Erlenmeyer Flask’, but instead of bodies in tanks the room held a collection of anodized aluminum cases, each one containing a relic of his past. A case near the door caught his attention. Kneeling, he brushed the dust away from an etched nameplate bearing a single word: Earl. A nostalgic grin found its way to his face, and when he spoke it was if to an old friend. "Hey, man. Been a while, huh? Think you’re ready to come out and play?" He stood slowly, patting the case gently before turning his attention to the rest of the room. In a far corner of the room stood a table on which rested a few Styrofoam heads. The first was the home of a brown ski mask adorned with a red stripe across the forehead and the letters ‘SCCW’ sewn to the forehead in felt. The mask was a throwback to a brief time in his career when he made the transition from the National Wrestling Council’s version of SCCW to Minnesota State Wrestling. Next to it was a black and red mask, last used almost ten years prior as part of the ‘WannaBehemoth’ costume. "Hi kids," Jared said to the heads, reaching into his bag, "Brought you a new friend to play with." His right hand held the most recent mask he’d worn. He smiled as he dressed a third head with it, leaving the back untied. King Blueberry had come home, finding a resting spot here among memories.
