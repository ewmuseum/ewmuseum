A Perfect Family: Losing a Match, Gaining a Daughter.

1 January 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01.01.08 'I lost.' 'Perfect' Raymond Pierce was not happy a lot, not anymore. He wasn't happy because he wasn't winning. He wasn't happy because he'd taken a chance in his life and it hadn't pay off at the Young Lion's Cup. He wasn't happy because he was forty years old and his best (only) friend was a skinny loser by the name of Joe Bob Smith. He wasn't happy because he was sitting on a queen-sized bed in the dark at a fucking Holiday Inn. He wasn't happy, and he couldn't remember the last time that he was. Maybe when he was NWC Tag Team Champion with Damon Hayes... 'I LOST!' Raymond threw the covers back from the bed in a fit of violent pique. No, he probably wasn't happy then, either. The room was nicely appointed, if small, its two beds separated by a darkly stained nightstand, on which had been tossed the entire contents of Raymond Pierce and Joe Bob Smith's pockets. There were two wallets (one snakeskin, one Italian leather), forty-nine cents in change, three sticks of gum, an albuterol inhaler, a pack of Virginia Slim cigarettes, a backstage pass to the Frank Erwin Center, an airplane-sized bottle of whiskey, three packets of mayonnaise, and a packet of soy sauce, if you're keeping track at home. Joe Bob Smith was a scrawny, long-faced bastard who, when he was a kid, used to dress up as Huckleberry Hound without much need for makeup or costuming. He was also the only Raymond Pierce fan remaining in the universe. He watched sadly as his icon crumbled on a polyester comforter in one-half of an adjoining room, because that was all they could afford. Pierce lied face-first in the pillows (which are blessedly soft, he would have to admit), his blond hair (which is NOT RECEDING AT ALL) mussed from an evening of battle that lasted just a few minutes before Joshua Kosidlo pinned him. His robe had been discarded in the corner and he was wearing a pair of silk pants and a frown. 'I LOST!' he said for the third time, the first muffled by down feathers. 'But...' Joe Bob offered. 'You can win again. I talked to Mr. Duluth, and they're booking you in a four-way match -- ' 'So I can lose to three other people? Out of the question!' Pierce sat up and he brushed his hair out of his eyes, which were wild. Wild with ideas. 'I know -- I'll call Andrew. He'll give me my old job back. The company's stock must have plummeted in my absence! There'd be... things... that Andrew can't do... without me!' 'But you have a match.' 'Nonsense! I'm a successful businessman and a scholar. I can't possibly be called upon to wrestle! That's preposterous! Joe Bob! Get me the phone!' Joe Bob moved across the room, picking up the telephone from the nightstand, and handed it to the Impeccable Warrior, now beside him. Raymond dialed a number he knew all too well. ***** Three days later. Raymond Pierce was exhausted. Sweat clung to his body, his breathing was shallow, and in his shaky grip was a bottle of sports drink. He sat on a wooden stool, a heavy bag mere inches away. Cupped against his ear was a cellphone. 'Tell him! Tell him it's his brother! I know where all the dirty secrets are buried! I know where she is! I will tell them! I will tell them all!' Pierce took a slug from the bottle. 'Fine, just have him call me when he gets off the phone with Jeb.' Raymond slammed the phone shut, standing and slapping it onto the seat of the chair in his place. He took a wild swing at the training bag nearby. He missed. Stalking over to the throng of young people, Pierce corralled a limp that only sometimes bothered him. He favored the gym's owner, a large black man with a gray-tinted crewcut, with a tight smile. 'You understand,' Pierce said. 'That as an eleven-time world champion, two of them in the backwards federation known as FUSE, and as the ONLY man alive who knows what FUSE stands for... I am gracing this hellhole with my very presence. That, say, you -- ' Pierce pointed at a scrawny kid in a blue-and-white wrestling singlet. ' -- should be wiping the sweat of greatness off me, because you will learn more from me than you have ever learned from your trainer.' The barrel-like black man, whose name was Barton Marquis Franklin and who unfortunately now had to take credit (if that was what it could be called) for training the new Young Lion, climbed up the ringsteps. 'Get your ass into the ring,' he said. PRP looked dubiously at the 350+ pound man in the middle of the ring, turning over his shoulder. Joe Bob Smith was there (Joe Bob Smith was always right over his shoulder). 'Where's Steve?' Pierce whispered. 'He's shooting a pilot,' Joe Bob replied. 'Some kind of... I don't know... action-adventure thing. Said he was sorry, but that he was going to make millions.' 'He has millions! This man is going to legally kill me!' When someone opened the door to the BMFGym, it was usually thrown open, flung open by a prospective new student who wanted people to see him. This time, however, it was only opened a crack, barely enough to admit the person stepping through. 'No, just... y'know... do what you do.' Joe Bob rubbed his boss' shoulders. Pierce nodded a little. 'When that phone rings and my brother begs me to come back, you stop this right away. I won't be able to save Pierce Industries if I'm hospitalized by this Neanderthal.' 'Yes, sir, Mr. Pierce.' Raymond stomps up the stairs, making a show of getting into the ring. Behind him, a smallish woman, maybe 5'4' tall and lucky if she was a hundred pounds, holds onto the steel handle of the thick metal door to prevent it from slamming behind her. The girl (because, really, that's what anyone would call her; she looks like a teenager, all fresh-faced and naïve) had a bob of light brown hair and would probably be called 'cute' rather any synonym of 'beautiful.' She wore a knee-length denim skirt and a white blouse with flared sleeves. Raymond Pierce climbed into the ring and he stood opposite the mountainous trainer. He rolled his neck, cracking the vertebrae and he lifted his hand for a test of strength. Joe Bob Smith watched from the floor by the apron, as he always did. Franklin flexed his fingers, locking them with Pierce's. The Flawless One immediately collapsed to his knees, letting out a shriek of pain. Joe Bob slapped the mat, impelling his (dare we say?) friend on. Barton shoved Pierce away, relinquishing his grip. Raymond clutched both hands against his chest, whimpering as the large man stepped in closer. The girl weaved her way through the throng of people, all of them quite enjoying the so-called Impeccable Warrior's humiliation at the BMF's hands. She tapped Joe Bob Smith on the shoulder nervously. 'Hrm?' Smith asked, turning over his shoulder. 'Can I help you?' 'I...' She twisted her hands in front of her, whispering. 'I think so.' The crowd fired a mighty roar, as Barton scooped Pierce up and pressed him overhead. Joe Bob looked back towards the ring, his jowls dipping in a frown. 'I'm sorry, I'm in the middle of something,' he said apologetically. 'Can this... can it wait?' The young woman rocked forward on her tiptoes, cupping one hand around her mouth as she whispered something to Joe Bob, whose eyes widened. 'Stop!' he shouted, waving his hands frantically. 'Stop the sparring session!' Smith clambered up to the apron, gesticulating with his arms. 'Put him down! Please, sir, put Mr. Pierce down!' Raymond, still held over Barton's head, glanced frantically at his underling. 'Did he call? Did Andrew call? I knew he would! I knew that my brother couldn't possibly exist another day without the services of -- ' 'He didn't call, sir.' Franklin set Pierce down, grunting with the effort. Raymond stumbled backwards, the ropes holding him up. 'Then what was so important?' 'Please, Mr. Pierce,' Joe Bob urged. 'Please, just come this way.' Raymond Pierce stopped in front of the kid in the blue-and-white singlet, handing him a towel. 'I believe we talked about this?' The young man frowned, but he wiped off the former NWC champion's face. PRP slapped the kid's cheek, gently. 'You're learning.' 'You're an ass,' the kid said. 'Ordinarily, I would teach you one of the forty-eight hundred moves I personally invented and have loaned to other wrestlers -- everything from the hurricanrana to the Superstar Sidekick! But I have some business to conduct.' The Impeccable Warrior tossed the towel over his shoulder, striding across the gym to the secluded area where Joe Bob Smith stood. Raymond smiled at the young woman, seated on the blue workout bench, her legs crossed demurely. 'Now, Joe Bob,' Ray said. 'Please don't tell me you stopped my sparring match -- and right before I was able to take the advantage on that overgrown slug -- just so I could meet a groupie.' The girl stood up, brushing her hands nervously on her skirt. She extended one to the Flawless One, smiling slightly. 'I don't think I qualify, really,' she said quietly. 'My name is Samantha Foster.' Pierce took the woman's small hand, bringing it towards his lips with a small smile. 'I, uh... I think you know my mother,' Samantha continued. 'Alexis?' Raymond let the hand go before his lips touched it. He recognized the name, all right. 'You're... Alexis Foster's daughter.' Alexis had served him as secretary, valet, and Damon-Hayes-distraction for a number of years, even before he was in the NWC. Joe Bob stood nearby, a dumb grin plastered to his lips. 'Yeah, but... actually, that's not really why I'm here.' Samantha cleared her throat, looking away. She tucked a lock of brown hair away from her face. 'I... gosh, I don't know how to say this. I mean, how do you say something like this, right? I've thought about this and thought about it.' 'Go on,' Joe Bob prompted softly. 'Just tell him, like you told me.' She nodded, twisting her hands in the hem of her shirt. 'I think... I think you're my father.' Perfect Raymond Pierce was a lot of things. But he was rarely at a loss for words. The Impeccable Warrior, He Who Never Errs, The Flawless One, the nine-time NWC World Heavyweight Champion, the two-time FUSE Universal Champion, the three-time FUSE Custom CHAOS champion (some of these titles were even true!) ...stared blankly. His mouth worked, but no syllables came out. 'Isn't it GREAT?!' Joe Bob Smith asked, wrapping his arms around Samantha and Raymond Pierce. ***** Four hours later, Raymond Pierce was on the phone again, this time to a woman in Florida. 'How could you not tell me?' he asked. Several hundred miles away, a coffee-skinned beauty sat in a booth in a steakhouse, with a cellphone tucked under her ear. Alexis Foster, like Raymond Pierce, had seen better days, but the former beauty queen had held up quite well. Her dark, curly hair hung around her shoulders, and even that frown couldn't mar her features. 'Look, I said I was sorry, Ray,' she said. 'I didn't tell either of you. I thought it would be best if Sam grew up away from all of your...' 'All of my what? All of my money? I would have provided for her!' 'You would have? Really, Ray? You wouldn't have turned me away? You would have brought up our daughter as a monster, and I wasn't going to have that.' 'So you decided not to mention it? I am Perfect Raymond Pierce! Do you realize what I could have done for a child's life?' He shook a fist, but she didn't see it. 'She would have been a spoiled brat. Now she's a fine young seventeen-year-old girl. Consider yourself lucky she decided to come see you.' 'I have not forgiven you for this, Alexis.' The woman smiled sadly, but he didn't see it. 'I know, but you probably will.' 'Probably.' Raymond paused, peering out from the kitchenette of the hotel room. Joe Bob and his daughter were laughing. He smiled a little. 'Are there... are there pictures? Like when she was younger?' Alexis paused for a long moment. Her eyes welled up, and she flicked water away with a finger. 'There...' she cleared her throat. 'There are some.' 'You'll send them to me.' It wasn't a request. Raymond looked back into the kitchen, grabbing up a glass. 'You'll send them to me and then I might consider forgiving you for the outrage of having my child and not letting that child be perfect.' 'Yeah,' she said. 'I can probably manage that.' 'I'm wrestling...' Pierce trailed off as Samantha stepped up to the counter. 'Who're you talking to, Dad?' she asked. His grin returned. 'No one.' He lifted the receiver. 'I have to go.' He slapped the phone closed, setting it on the counter. 'Are you hungry?' 'I could eat.' 'See that, Joe Bob! She eats food! My daughter eats food!' He slapped Joe Bob on the shoulder, hard enough to spill the smaller man ahead a step. 'Let's go find something to eat, then I'm going to win this match against -- who did you say it was?' 'Jadian Bridden, VON DOOM, and RYUJIN, sir.' 'Why are you shouting their names?' 'I'm not, sir. That's the way they're supposed to be pronounced.' 'Kids today must be hard of hearing. All the rock and roll music, probably.' 'Yes, sir.' 'Let's Go Away.' ***** At a steakhouse in Tampa, Florida, Alexis Foster dabbed her eyes with a red cloth napkin. She set it down beside her salad plate, which she'd only picked at. On the other side of the table is a small black envelope. 'When will you people ever leave us alone? What do you want?' Across the booth from her was a slightly built man with slicked-back black hair and a pale face seemingly perpetually set into either a slight smile or a deep scowl. Tonight, it was a slight smile. The man wore a black turtleneck sweater, and was carefully working on his filet mignon, cutting it into tiny, tiny pieces. As he leaned forward, the man's flat gray pentagram medallion swung forward. A stone cane with a meticulously carved hawk's head laid on the wooden booth next to him. 'I'm here about your daughter, actually. But don't fret, little lady,' Mr. Hawke said, his soft voice making the knife-wound words chipper. 'This is where it gets fun.' In precisely twenty-two minutes, her cellphone will ring. Mr. Hawke will be long gone by then. But right now, he's sent the waiter for a doggie bag.
