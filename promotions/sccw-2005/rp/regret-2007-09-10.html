Regret

10 September 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 10, 2007 The word regret is defined as ‘a sense of loss, disappointment, or dissatisfaction’. There are few things in my life that I honestly regret. I never have, and never will regret love. Yeah, I believe in that sappy saying. It’s better to have loved and lost, than to not have loved at all. Love will change your life. When Janet died, she took a piece of me with her. I considered joining her, believing nothing was worth my being. I’d like to believe something stopped me. I found love again. She wouldn’t have wanted me to. Life had a purpose. No, quite frankly I was too much of a bitch. I couldn’t go through with it. I drug my feet slowly through this sludge I called a life. Rough times. Cheap highs. But I never touched another woman. Call it love. Call it depression. Call me gay. To hell with you. It doesn’t matter. And then, I met her. Cassandra. Cassie. The very woman who changed my life. She made me feel whole. She gave me a purpose. She made me feel again. And now, she may very well walk out of my life forever. * * * * "Don’t go." My heart tells me to go to her, while my thoughts tell me to run. Instead, my feet once again remain stuck to the floor, roots sprouting and driving deeper with her every step. She gets closer, and closer, until I’m sure I can count every one of her eyelashes as she bats them up at me. I raise my arms, a meager attempt to keep her at bay. Instead, she slips inside them, content in my grasp. "Please don’t leave me again. Don’t leave us." My head snaps up, drawn to the open door behind her. Nothing. Darkness. Those gentle fingers brush my cheek, pulling my lips to meet hers. So softly. As she pulls away I can feel her breathing lightly against my bare chest. "I knew you’d stay." She thinks she’s won, only she doesn’t understand. I have no choice. Sometimes the line between need and want is very fine. Sometimes, it’s indistinguishable. Only how to tell her? You’ll hurt her. Surely she already knows, deep down. She just can’t bring herself to admit it any more than I can admit it to her. A bit of hope always remains. Hope for the future. Hope for a family. Hope for us. The typical American dream. A little house with a picket fence. A dog running free in the yard. A few kids of my own. Cassandra, the beautiful wife. Family. Fuck the fence. Fuck the dog. Fuck family. Andrew and Melissa. "You know I can’t stay." She pulled away before I could, tears filling her eyes. The smack from her slap seemed to echo through the apartment. Instinctively, I reach for her, longing to feel her warmth in my arms again. She steps back, crossing her arms across her chest. The standoff is only just beginning. "I love you." * * * * "Another one over here, boss." I had found myself in a dark, dank little bar just around the corner from my apartment complex. Not normally much of a drinker, tonight I was making full use of my new discovery. Behind me stood an old fashioned juke box. Lushes and barflies kept the songs coming. One of the women winks at me as she grinds another on the dance floor. Hell, what have I got to lose? Everything. Shit! "Hey baby? Gonna sit there watching all night, or are you going to buy me a drink?" That’s how it started. One drink turned in to two, followed by three, which turned in to five. It’s a miracle my barstool swayed with me. I fumbled with my wallet, slapping a bill on the bar without bothering to see what it was. Her hand crept up my leg, settling on the inside of my thigh. After throwing back another, I leaned in to her. Our noses brushed. My hand was on the back of her head, pulling her towards me. It was only then that I heard the throat clearing behind me. "Mayson?" Shit, shit! I could feel her nails digging in to my shoulder as she spun me around in my stool. The world turned upside and before I knew it both women were staring down at me. Am I spinning, or are they? Two sets of arms reach down, then stop short. Cassandra’s eyes seemed to shoot daggers at the younger woman, chasing her back to the dance floor. "What are you doing?" Getting to my feet, I stumbled in to her arms, causing her to sag underneath my weight. She flings my arm across her shoulder, a poor attempt to distribute the extra weight. Slowly we stagger towards the door. Sweat poured out of my pores. My stomach tried to escape through my throat. Yet, she was there. Holding me. Loving me. Forgiving me. * * * * "You can’t leave them here like this." The beginnings of an all too familiar argument. An argument she still believes she can win. This time, she’s decided to try a gentler approach. Trying to butter me up, so to speak. Her fingers are magic as they run across my tense muscles. All stress and pain seem to melt away. I close my eyes and lower my head, to allow better access. I can feel her smile. Those soft, angel like hands make their way down my shoulders, and across my arms before settling against my chest. Those lips brush against my neck, turning my body to jelly. She twists a strand of my hair around her finger, as she whispers in my ear, sending goose bumps across my entire body. "I love you." I know. "I want you to stay." I do too. "Forget the flight" If only… * * * * He hadn’t left her side. Not in days. Not since War In Washington. Drew blamed himself for what happened that day. For not swooping Lissa up and running. For taking a stand. For confronting him. Sean Sterling. Me? Hell, I blame myself. The sick bastard almost killed her. She’s only a child. A child I put in danger. It was my actions that brought this upon them. Not their own. They were just children, doing as they were told. Put in a bad situation, at an even worse time. Regret. "Uncle Mayson?" Andrew’s shaky voice snapped me from my thoughts. Slowly I walked over and placed my hand on his shoulder. His gaze never left her. It had been a rough night for Melissa. I thought she had nightmares before, but this was ridiculous. She rarely slept longer than an hour or two at a time. Drew normally slept slumped down in the chair beside her. What kind of man could do this to a child? To children? I did this. "What are you going to do? What ARE you going to do? "I don’t know, kid. Why don’t you go grab something to eat? I can take over watch for a bit." Andrew nodded and started for the door. He turned and narrowed his eyes at me. As he raised a finger in my direction, his voice came out gruff. "Don’t you dare leave her." My eyes began to water as I struggled to nod, but he was already gone. Left me alone with my thoughts. Alone with my criticisms. I don’t deserve them. Any of them. Pain follows me. I have nothing more to offer. Nothing left to give. * * * * The things a man will do for love. For the woman he loves. For his family. Forgiveness can be a powerful weapon. A useful tool if used to its full capacity. Unexpected forgiveness. Unwilling forgiveness. Forgiveness none the less.
