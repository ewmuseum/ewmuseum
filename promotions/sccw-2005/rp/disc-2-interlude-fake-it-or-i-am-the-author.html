It was always fucking raining in Seattle, a bunch of ants scattering under the sprinkler, red and black umbrellas failing to shield them. I ran, raindrops splattering, thunder booming around me. I am the thunder. Lightning brightened the night sky, the Space Needle sticking up like a proud middle finger in the skyline's hand. I ducked down against a chimney, momentarily fearing discovery, but it quickly seemed safe. I'm too careful, I know, but she made me this way. I am the lightning. I pulled the grappling line from my belt, wrapping it around a satellite dish atop the building and I jumped. Gears whirred as the rappelling line lowered me down the slick glass side of the building. It was only a few floors down, but it's always enough to bring my heart into my throat. My destination reached, I peered through the mirrored glass. Within, the fluorescent overhead lights were out and, I gather, the smoke detectors had been disabled, because that looked like a gods-honest fire in the center of the room. Within, figures in enveloping, hooded robes knelt around the guttering flame, hands clasped together in supplication. An office door opened and she strode out, her robe starkest white while the others were dressed in black, sewn-in rhinestones catching the firelight. They winked at me knowingly. Her eyes were empty pits of gray, her hair like her scalp bled across her shoulders. She smiled -- or, rather, her lips curved in a slightly pleased fashion, as nothing she did could ever be called a 'smile'. From the sleeve of her robe, she pulled a wavy-bladed athame that gleamed silver in the flickering firelight. Her hands were ever-wrapped in black leather straps, and she held the blade by its point. The knife's hilt was a crucifix, I realized, the Son of God graven upon it. I pressed my ear to the window, but I did not need to hear her words to know what it was she said, as I had knelt beside her when she recited these words. 'And so,' she intoned in a voice that would forever call nightmares to my mind and yet whose sensual undercurrent could not be denied. 'We consecrate this land for our people, the True people. And so we consecrate this earth in the name of Those Who Understand.' Movement came from within. The largest of the figures lifted his head, scruffy square chin briefly visible in the light. I felt rather than saw his eyes narrow in the firelight. Something moved in the shadows and instinct screamed in my ear. I pushed off the building as a barrage of machinegun fire shattered the glass over my head. Jagged pieces of dull gray rained around me, but the bullets just kept at it, cutting the air, cutting the silence, and, more importantly for me, cutting the line that held me several dozen stories over the rain-soaked pavement. Thread-by-thread, the cable snapped. I had no time, no time at all. 'Fuck me,' I said as the two halves split apart, one snaking along the building's side. The other came with me as I fell. I am the rain, too, I guess. * * * * * Crystal Light and the Dark Crystal The blue-white glow illuminated the pale features of the dark-haired teenager behind the computer screen. That's what the red neon sign in the strip mall said. It didn't announce a name or say that it was 'Ted's Game Emporium,' probably because its current owner was the third that ran a tabletop game shop here. He was the second to use the name, though his name wasn't Ted. The windows were nearly papered over with posters -- for Magic The Gathering, for HeroClix, for Dungeons & Dragons 4th Edition, Exalted, and more, all vying for the geek-dollar of the Emporium's customers. This made it difficult for Lauren to see into the building, but it became quite obvious that there were more people inside than the store could actively hold. Part of this was that Tuesday was the shop's late night, when the new games and books came in. It was also Warhammer night, where the shop's regulars hauled literal boxes of tiny miniatures to the shop and stage epic battles with rules that, quite frankly, she hoped she was never quizzed on. The rulebook read like a VCR instruction manual. Written in Chinese. The fact that Ted's Game Emporium was also holding a signing by the author of 'Banditelli' was just likely to add to the chaos -- though, honestly, many of the gamers' reaction to his book was an epic-level eyeroll. Lauren rolled her shoulders forward, pushing into the shop. The bell attached to the door jingled softly as she stepped in, and a balding gentleman in a Blackhawks sweater smiled at her. 'Welcome to the Emporium,' he said. Lauren smiled nervously in return, but she didn't offer a reply. She felt him watching her as she lingered at the New Releases, haphazardly tugging the Star Wars Knights of the Old Republic Setting Book off the shelf. At least she knew something about Star Wars that she could spit out if someone approached her. Of course, when she paged through the book and saw a bunch of pictures from... what, a comic book or something? Whatever it was, it certainly wasn't pictures of Ewan McGregor, so she hastily put it down. The man she'd been sent to find was sitting testily behind a table that was far too big for the tiny stack of paperback books to his left. He was far prettier than Lauren expected, with nicely coiffed hair and a silk shirt that clearly put him above the 'dirty t-shirt and torn jeans' set that frequented this place. In their defense, however, Lauren (she'd decided her name was 'Harriet' tonight) was dressed similarly. She felt positively unattractive, but since her activities for her mentor rarely relied on the same enticements as her sister, it hadn't occurred to her to 'pretty it up,' as it were. Now she wished she had. Letting her Doc Martens scuff the tile floor, Savant shuffled towards the stack of books, black-nailed hand pulling open the cover, which depicted a man in a long leather trenchcoat. She snapped her gum. 'What is this, some fiction for a new cyberpunk game?' The author (well, the writer, since both he and the woman knew he didn't actually write the thing) scoffed, turning away. The man in the Blackhawks sweater jogged over, his cheery grin only earning him a brief glance from 'Harriet's' red-painted eyes. 'It's a new novel,' Blackhawk said. 'I think it's awesome. Full of action and international conspiracies and all that shit.' 'Uh-huh.' She chewed her gum in her front teeth. 'This isn't a book store, though.' 'I'm... I'm expanding,' the hockey-lover (whom Savant knew to be the owner) said, somewhat defensive. 'I figured, since Shadowrun 4th is a decent seller, we could get some books in, for inspiration or whatever.' 'Yeah, I guess.' Lauren wasn't sure whether Quinn would be honored or offended that 'Harriet' was based somewhat on her mentor's daughter. 'Look.' The Ghostwriter was foreign -- Lauren pegged him as British. 'Do you want a copy or don't you? I haven't got all night.' Lauren didn't know whether it was instinct or just dumb luck that raised her eyes at that point to where the Dutch door of the back room was swinging open. The tiny woman with frizzy blond hair and sharp, elfin features drew her gaze across the shop. 'Pass,' Lauren said off-handedly, trying really hard to make her fleeing to the door unnoticeable. 'Guen FUCKING Maddox watching Ghost,' is what the message said. 'Not to mention your plan for Jonny.' 'I know.' The Director's voice was, if anything, even quieter and even colder than normal. The greeting card had a message scrawled on it in red, blocky writing. 'Stay away from my people, Director Pierce, and I'll stay away from your daughter. Seemed like an easy rule. Too bad you had to go and break it. TTFN, Guen.' Katsidy waited for her friend's reaction, the porcelain visage of the Spider limned by the light of the rising son. She received nothing but a cold-eyed stare. 'You know what needs to be done.' 'You're sure.' 'I am.'
