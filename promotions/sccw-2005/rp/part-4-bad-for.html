Date: Today Friday, September 18th. 10:17 pm. Chicago, Illinois. He left the restaurant, still holding the to-go box that the serving girl had insisted on giving him as he left. Three steps later, he had dropped it in a trashcan. He'd opened it first, allowing the contents to mix among the other refuse, and making it less likely that anyone would try to eat it. Five steps after that, his pace quickened. He was in a full run by the end of the block. He didn't stop running for an hour and a half. # # # Sunday, September 20th. 11:30 am. Chicago. The only sound to be heard in the offices of R.W. Chandler that morning was the rustle of newspaper, as Deacon Dale prepared himself for a full day of football. Not that he'd get to see any of it, of course; he was only in the office because he'd been called to a meeting...and that meant the early games were a wash, but he'd get to catch the Broncos game if he was lucky. 'Not like they're worth it this year,' he grumbled. 'Fuckin' McDaniels.' He idly looked at his watch. 'Quarter to noon, Sunday...just about burger time motherfuckers.' Deacon leaned back in the leather chair and put his feet up on the desk. He'd had precious little time to relax in the last few weeks, with all the preparation they'd been doing for the big match at the pay-per-view. It seemed a little odd to Deacon that he had to deal with so much stuff when it wasn't even his fight, but that's what it was like working for Connors. Things usually didn't make sense to anyone but him. He was halfway through another article tearing into Jay Cutler when he heard footsteps outside the door. He quickly took his feet off the desk and sat up straight, just in time to see 'Wise Guy' Wyatt Connors enter the room. Connors had been even busier than his minions lately, but if the stress was wearing on him, he didn't show it. As Connors entered the room, his icy stare focused on Deacon, and it did not waver for a moment. 'Good morning,' he said tersely. 'Mornin'.' 'Where's Trevor?' 'Food run.' Wyatt nodded. 'Sunday, isn't it. Very well. I'll be in the back, Deacon. Let me know when it's burger time motherfuckers.' 'Will do, chief,' Dale said, and went back to his newspaper. 'And Deacon?' 'Hmm?' 'Don't put your feet on my desk again.' He would have answered, but Connors was already headed toward the back room. Dale's eyes followed until Wyatt closed the door behind him. Deacon sometimes wondered what was back there. Wyatt never let anyone in, of course. He'd made a big show about how it was off-limits, and that anyone who tried to enter would be fed to the starving wolves he kept in there. Deacon didn't believe it for a second, but still had yet to even place his hand on the knob...just in case. He'd just started reading the previous night's baseball scores when the door swung open again. This time, the man entering the room was T.J. Ratigan, the man who put 'filthy' into 'filthy assistant.' His hands were full of paper bags, leading Deacon to wonder just how the hell he opened the door. 'Burger time, motherfuckers!' Ratigan shouted, and strode into the room. He dumped the food unceremoniously on the desk, knocking over the pencil cup in the process. He quickly grabbed something out of a bag and shoved it into his face, barely taking the time to unwrap it first. Within seconds, his chin and cheeks were covered in ketchup, mayonnaise, and a hint of bacon grease. 'You took your time,' Deacon groused, and started rifling through a random bag. 'If you're that hungry, you can eat a dick,' he mumbled through his lunch. 'Anyway, traffic's nasty out there. Where's the boss man?' 'In the back. Which one is mine?' 'Probably the one that's got the dog shit in it,' T.J. answered, and went to summon the Scorpion. Deacon replied, 'I don't even know why we let you handle the food,' but naturally, Trevor was no longer listening. Burger in hand, he'd taken a few steps toward the door to the back room, but found that the closer he got, the slower he moved. Like his partner, Trevor had often wondered what Connors kept in that secret area. He and Deacon had a few conversations about it over their occasional post-workout beer sessions. The big man figured it was all the information that Connors had gathered over the years, whereas Trevor held with the theory that it was a dead hooker. 'What the hell are you doing?' Deacon asked, just before unwrapping a cheeseburger. Ratigan waved him off, and tiptoed toward his destination. As he reached the door, he put his ear against it. He listened for a moment, and then reached down to turn the knob. Just before he touched the metal handle, the door opened. Ratigan pitched forward slightly, so that when the door swung shut again, it slammed into the side of his head. 'Gah!' he shouted, and staggered backward, allowing just enough room for Wyatt Connors to slip through the door and step into the main room of the office, all without stepping into the food that T.J. dropped on the floor. 'There,' he said, smugly. 'Maybe now you'll know not to stick your nose where it doesn't belong.' Wyatt then joined his other minion at the desk, where their lunch had already been spread out. 'That one's yours,' Deacon mumbled through his burger, and pointed at another wrapped sandwich. 'Hamburger, no cheese, no trimmings, and a water.' Wyatt turned the sandwich over in his hands, as if he was inspecting the wrapper. 'Now that we're all here, I'd like to go over what happened in Oakland.' 'That was not my fault!' T.J. shouted, still holding the side of his head as he picked his burger up off the floor. 'I was confronted with a bitch of legendary hotness.' 'Mm-hmm. And what happened after that, Trevor?' 'Umm...dunno. But I don't remember not fucking her, so I'm pretty sure we totally did it.' Wyatt Connors does not shake his head at his young charge, because if he did that every time Trevor said something stupid, his head would have fallen off by now. Instead, he calmly unwrapped his food. 'What likely happened is that she struck you on the back of the head so that she could rifle through my personal belongings without interruption. I believe I had been quite clear about how important it was that you did not let such a thing happen.' 'Sorry,' T.J. mumbled, and actually did seem to be disappointed in himself. 'It's not entirely your fault, Trevor,' Wyatt answered, as he made a full inspection of the sandwich itself. He peered closely at each half of the bun, and each side of the meat. This drew a rather strange look from Deacon...a look that did not go unnoticed, as Connors quickly switched targets in his inquisition. 'I'm even more displeased with Deacon for abandoning his post...and for such an obvious trick, at that. Tell me, Deacon. Did you do any 'ass kicking' at the show?' Deacon's face turned slightly red. 'No. None of 'em knew what I was talkin' about. And then that punk gopher squirmed away from me.' 'I see. And at what time did you figure out you were getting conned?' 'Right about then. I headed back, and T.J. was out on the floor.' 'I see. So what you're saying is, I asked a pair of total fucking incompetents to guard some very important information. Pictures that, in my hands, would have ruined other people's lives...and in other hands, would probably ruin mine. Glad to know that we're on the same page here.' Deacon wanted to protest, but knew enough to shut his mouth. Trevor did not, so Deacon quickly clamped his hand over Ratigan's mouth before he could draw the boss' ire. Strangely enough, that ire did not come. Instead, Connors sighed. 'I suppose it's my fault. After all, I did only let you in on the game plan shortly after we entered the building. In front of about a dozen people. Many of whom had cameras.' As he spoke, Wyatt walked around to the other side of the desk and unlocked one of the drawers. From there, he produced two small packets; one of salt, the other of mustard. Slowly, the wheels in Deacon's head started to turn. 'Why would you do that?' he asked. An equally incredulous Trevor asked, 'You keep condiments in your desk?' 'Subterfuge, gentlemen. Simple subterfuge,' he answered, and emptied the packets' contents onto the beef patty. 'You keep mustard in your desk for subterfuge?' 'No, I told people in public about the pictures for subterfuge. I knew that if Desade would jump at the chance to recover them...although I must admit, I'm a little surprised that she went for it. She had to have realized that I would never bring something that important to a place I knew she was going to be. I'm a little surprised she used Shaw; I hadn't realized they were working together again. Still, it's those little curveballs that makes things interesting.' He seemed oddly chipper about the whole thing; tossing it off as a casual comment while he inspected the seal on his bottle of Dasani. 'The point is, it left me free to enact my own plans without interference.' Ratigan stammered, 'Umm...Wyatt? Got a question.' 'For the last time, Trevor, forget about the mustard.' 'Nah, nah. It ain't about that. What are these pictures you're talking about?' Connors smirked as he twisted the cap of his bottle. 'That's right. You weren't there. You decided getting food was more important than honoring commitments.' 'So, I gotta commitment to food. Now what are these pictures about?' 'Oh, that. Nothing important. Just a few snaps of our esteemed champion and the Director in a...let's say, a compromising position.' It's a well established fact that T.J. Ratigan is not all that bright. It's not just a dearth of knowledge; he also lacks the willingness to learn. If a subject isn't in one of his five fields of interest (girls, wrestling, rock music, cars, and girls) then he's probably not going to bother. If it does fit one of those categories, however, he becomes Bob Woodward. Such is the case now, where he may not be able to tell you what 'esteemed' means, but he's got a pretty good idea about 'compromising position.' 'Dirty pictures?' 'You might say.' 'How dirty?' 'I could break the internet with these pictures.' Trevor's imagination, unused and grown over with cobwebs, suddenly sprang into action. This left him unable to speak, which came as a great relief to the other two. 'So, you pulled a fast one on Desade. That means you still have the pictures, right?' 'Sort of. I've got them hidden safely, but it will be difficult for anyone to get at them for a while; even me.' 'OK. So...what are you going to do with them now?' Wyatt pursed his lips in thought. 'I'm not sure. They can still be used as a bargaining chip, but their value has decreased somewhat since the Oakland show.' 'How come?' 'The damage has already been done,' Connors answered. 'I'm sure you've seen the show by now; you saw what Crisis walked in on. Really, Amelia and Alexandra did all the work for me; I feel a little bad taking credit for it. I should still be able to make a decent return, though. Someone will pay very handsomely for them; of that, I have no doubt.' 'So, you're not going to post them online or anything?' Wyatt's face wrinkled with disgust. 'Of course not. I'm not Lane Stevens, for God's sake.' Deacon shrugged, and took another bite of his double cheeseburger. Connors, considering the matter to be over, sat down in his chair and prepared to eat his own lunch. Meanwhile, Trevor's brain had cooled down to the point where he was actually able to talk again. 'Let me get this straight,' he said, prompting an annoyed grunt from the Scorpion. 'You have pictures of Desade and Aimz doing the nasty, right? And from what you're sayin', it sounds like a pretty nasty nasty, too.' Wyatt nodded. 'Reasonably nasty, yes.' Ratigan pointed at his boss, using the hand that still had his burger in it. Ketchup dripped down his wrist. 'You have famous ginger lesbian porn.' 'If you want to put it like that.' 'And you won't share?!' Wyatt shook his head, because sometimes these things can't be helped. 'Trevor, I need you to focus. The whole point is that I needed something to use against the champion, and--' But there was no chance of shifting T.J.'s focus now; he was like a dog chasing a car. He stood directly across the desk from Connors and put his hands down. This had the added effect of smearing Trevor's greasy sandwich all over the desktop. 'You're cleaning that up. I hope you realize that.' 'What do you want.' The look on Trevor's face was deathly serious...or at least it would be, without the condiment marks. 'Don't be an ass, Trevor. We have work to do.' 'I will give you my right arm.' 'Pictures wouldn't do you any good, then,' Deacon quipped. T.J. threw his burger at him, and then went back to haggling with Connors. 'I will give you all my paychecks for the rest of the year.' Wyatt shook his head. 'Pass.' Trevor thought for a moment, as he tried to think of something, anything, that Connors might want. When he'd thought of it, he pointed his finger into the Wise Guy's face--with the clean hand, thankfully--and made his pitch. 'I will start behaving like a responsible adult.' Wyatt had been ready to dismiss it out of hand, but Trevor's offer gave him pause. 'Now, that is tempting.' Deacon nodded in agreement. 'You'll never get a better deal than that.' 'I'll take it under consideration.' Wyatt reached out to pat T.J. on the shoulder, but thought better of it; he doubted there was any part of the man that was not covered in something unpleasant. 'For right now, I need us all to be on track. Also, Trevor, I will need you to clean off your hands.' Trevor looked down, and wiped his hands on the front of his Slayer t-shirt. Wyatt pretended not to notice. 'There's a lot riding on this one, and not just the title. What happens to Amelia Campbell could affect the entire power structure of SCCW. If she falls, then Desade might lose her spot at the top...and I don't have to tell you what that means. Amelia is the key.' 'I have already cost her something vitally important--her balance. Essentially, my efforts have given her a good hard shove out of her comfort zone, and if her past history is any indication, her attempts to right herself will lead to disaster. The resulting downward spiral is inevitable, and inexorable.' Connors stood up from his desk, making sure to hold the attention of both Dale and Ratigan. He could not stress enough the importance of what they were doing; of how the plan worked. The why...that didn't matter as much. 'That doesn't mean our work is done, however. The title match at Equilibrium carries a great deal of significance as well. The Universal Title is one of her most prized possessions. She has held celebrations over the longevity of her reign. She has even renamed it, in a manner only slightly less ridiculous than that Blackberry person. If she loses that as well, it will certainly hasten her downfall. Then again, if she wins, she may cling to it--the only certainty in her uncertain life--at the cost of other things she may hold dear. Things which might have saved her. It will be fun to watch, regardless.' 'Hold on,' Deacon started. 'If you win either way, then what's the point? Why bother getting your head kicked in?' Connors' lips twinged briefly into a smirk. He liked that about Deacon; he was just smart enough to ask questions...but not smart enough to ask the right ones. 'Out of the two options, I much prefer the first. It has the added benefit of increasing my own position of power within the company. More importantly, it is more likely to produce a favorable outcome. If taking the Universal Title away from her does not give us total victory, then it will at least put us close.' 'How do you know when you get this 'total victory?'' Deacon asked. A wolfish grin spread out over Wyatt's face. 'When there's nothing left to take.' Later that night, Deacon would admit over a few beers that Wyatt Connors' smile was 'creepy as all hell.' Trevor would laugh, and call him a pansy. One thing he wouldn't do was disagree. # # # Monday, September 22, 2009. 3:27 am. Chicago. There are a lot of things that sound like a good idea after you've had a few beers. Unless these things were suggested by T.J. Ratigan. Then, you need to switch to whiskey first. 'I take it all back. This is the dumbest idea you've ever had.' 'Quit being a pussy and get your key out.' That should explain why two men, covered in the stench of drunk-sweat, were skulking around the front door of an office building at half past three in the morning. The larger of the two fumbled briefly in his pocket for a key and tried to insert it into the lock. 'Straight and steady, Deke. Just like slippin' it into your mom.' 'Say that again and I'll put you through the goddamn window. I shouldn't even be here.' 'Hey, is it my fault he won't give me a key of my own?' 'It is, actually.' By now, Deacon Dale had managed to unlock the door, and held it open for his partner in crime, T.J. Ratigan. From there, they made their way to the stairs as quietly as possible. It wasn't strictly necessary, since there were no guards or security cameras--Wyatt Connors never would have set up shop here had that been the case--but they were still careful not to draw any undue attention. Snoopy passersby or night owls in other offices might call the police, and nobody wanted that. As they headed up the stairs (damn him for getting an office on the fourth floor) Deacon kept grousing. 'Anyway, you're not going to find anything. He said those pictures were in circulation.' 'Yeah? Since when d'you believe everything he says?' Ratigan snapped back, as he leaned against a wall for support. 'He's probably got those shots in that room of his. They're probably plastered up all over the walls as part of his creepy-ass Desade shrine.' 'That is not something I need to think about.' After a few near disasters, they arrived in the hallway that led to the office of R.W. Chandler. Once more, Deacon produced a key from his pocket. This time, he hesitated before trying to unlock the door. 'I still don't think this is a good idea,' he whispered. Can you imagine how pissed he'll be if he catches up snooping around?' 'How's he gonna know? The lights are off, the door's locked, and his car's not outside. Nobody's gonna catch us.' 'I don't know...' 'Oh, for fuck's sake. You wanna know what's in there just as bad as I do. Now pull up your skirt and open the goddamn door.' Deacon wanted to walk away. He knew that this was a huge mistake; worse than the time he'd agreed to work a Shakespearean actor gimmick at a show in Butler, Mississippi. But he also knew that, against all odds and previous experience, T.J. Ratigan was right. He did want to know what Wyatt Connors was hiding. Slowly, Deacon turned the key inside the lock. He noiselessly pushed the door open, allowing Trevor to pass through, and then gently guided it back into place behind him. There was a very soft click, but nobody could have possibly noticed. Guided by the scant light from the streetlamps below, T.J. and Deacon tiptoed across the room. Trevor reached the door first, and stood square in front of it. He slowly eased his left hand toward the doorknob, and then held out his right hand toward Deacon. Dale nodded, and Ratigan counted with his fingers. One. Two. Three. With a quick motion, he flung the door open. All he could see was darkness, and a slight glint of metal. Metal that came at him very fast. 'AAAAAAAAHHHHHHHH!' T.J. Ratigan howled in pain, which mostly covered the buzzing sound of electric current, and he collapsed to the floor. Dale quickly looked for a weapon, but found none. Against his better judgment, he stepped into the small room and blindly fumbled for the light switch. Once he found it, he turned to face the assailant. The man was short, skinny, and huddled in the corner. His only clothes were a pair of black sweatpants, although a suit hung from an iron bar that was wedged into the rafters. The man brandished the handle end of a plastic spoon at Deacon, and his hands were shaking in terror. Deacon knew exactly who the man was, and he still couldn't believe it. 'Wyatt?' The man in the corner lowered his spoon and looked closer. Recognition dawned in his bleary eyes. 'Oh, thank God. It's just you two.' Deacon looked around the rest of the room, in a desperate attempt to make sense of it all. It didn't help. The space was smaller than a room in a bad hotel, or a dorm at a small college. Beside the doorway, there was a wastebasket full of empty soup cans and plastic flatware. In the far corner, a single-size bed with thin sheets and a lone pillow. Beside the bed, a series of boxes, bottles and jars, all containing various things. Hair coloring. Vitamin pills. Wrinkle cream. On the far size was a small alcove which contained a toilet and, presumably, a sink. Obviously not enough space for a shower. Deacon's mind reeled, and not from the alcohol. There were a hundred questions bouncing around his head, but only one that he could word properly. 'What the fuck?' Connors scowled. 'You know, Desade's been trying to track me down for years, and she's a professional. How is that you two assholes keep doing it by accident?' He glanced toward Ratigan, who was still convulsing on the floor. 'Suppose I should turn that off.' He leaned down to where he had dropped the taser, and flicked the switch. 'You...sleep here? In the office?' 'Sometimes,' Wyatt answered, in typical cryptic fashion. 'What, one night a week? A couple? You stay somewhere else on other nights?' 'No, I always stay here. Sometimes, I am able to sleep.' Ratigan moaned weakly from the floor outside. Connors tried to walk past the big man, but Deacon grabbed him by the shoulder and spun him around. Instinctively, Wyatt jumped back against the wall, defensively holding out his spoon. 'Jesus Christ. I knew you were crazy, but this is...well, it's fucking crazy.' 'Maybe. But it beats the morgue.' 'You are not making any sense at all.' Deacon wondered if maybe Wyatt was fucking with them again. He'd been known to do that...although, not like this. He'd seen a lot of emotions from Connors, both real and faked...but he'd never seen this full-blown state of panic. And if any doubt remained, they were erased by his next words. 'You don't understand. She...' Wyatt swallowed hard, and choked out the full sentence. 'She found me.' 'What? When?' Deacon, high school dropout though he was, at least knew better than to ask 'who.' 'A couple of nights ago. She knew where I was going to be, and she was waiting. We talked, and...and I think she put one over on me. I don't know how, but she did...and that's the worst part. Not knowing. Now, I...fuck. I need a drink.' Connors started looking around the room for something alcoholic, but nothing seemed apparent. Dale's throat tightened; it was almost like he was getting caught up in the sweeping wave of Connors' paranoia. 'Relax, Wyatt. She's not here. It's just us. You're fine.' He wasn't sure what effect he wanted from saying those things...but he knew that the effect he got, wasn't it. Wyatt grabbed Deacon by the shirt, his eyes darting wildly between Dale's face and any spot where someone might be hiding--which, to Connors' mind, was everywhere. 'Fine? Fine?! You still don't get it! That's how I'm still alive, Deacon--because she can't find me, and I know how she operates. As long as those two facts hold, then I know I've got nothing to worry about. And now, I found out that they're not true anymore. What the hell am I supposed to do now?' Deacon shoved him backwards, just as an attempt to put some distance between them. Connors stepped back, stumbled, and sat down roughly on the bed. The manic anxiety drained out of his body, replaced by a sense of exhaustion and despair. 'What am I supposed to do...' Their eyes met for a moment, but Connors quickly looked away. Even though he stood nearly a foot taller, this was the first time Deacon had ever been able to look down at him. Wyatt Connors had never seemed so small.
