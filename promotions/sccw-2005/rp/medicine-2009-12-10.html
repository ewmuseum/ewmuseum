Medicine

10 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/10/09 The game had been a lull until this point as Thad adjusted a brown mesh hat with a cursive letter D pressed on the sponge-like base. Just beyond the right field foul line, the board read a 1-0 score – Dixie Fence, Inc. leading over Ted’s Carpet Service Emporium thanks to a lead off solo home run in the bottom of the second from his star pitcher. Now in the fifth, Coach Denver knew that the defensive effort his boys had displayed could collapse at any point, so now was the time to start generating a little cushion for the final inning to come. "Big Stick!" Thad followed his yell with a sharp whistle to get the attention of the kid standing on the edge of the batter’s box. The white pinstriped uniform displayed "MILLER" on the back with a large brown number 8 just below it. Miller turned, and emulating a pro, angled the bat with both hands at the bottom and adjusted his gloves while eying the signal. Thad then went through the motions – most were gestures made up on the fly. From an arms-crossed position, he extended his left one outward and slid a palm down from his shoulder to his wrist, then brought the right hand up to finagle the bill of his cap, then touching his ear-to-nose and then to opposite ear. Miller, a wiry youngster with thick orange hair spilling out the front of his batting helmet, nodded back and took his position in the chalk rectangle. Left ear touch, hit-and-run. Miller was pretty bright and one of the handful Thad could count on to follow his hitting signal. Thad’s attention then turned quickly to the robust kid with the freckled face and horn-rimmed glasses pressed against his eyes. While Number 4 looked like your average husky dweeb, he could normally put a hurting on a baseball unlike anyone else his age. Not to mention, he had a decent amount of speed for his big-boned frame, too. He’d turned a crushing line drive into a triple thanks to some hustle and a nervous reaction from the opposing team’s right fielder. Although there was only one out, Coach Denver had decided to take a gamble. Little League coaches who played it safe didn’t win pennants. "Tum-Tum," Thad barked, calling the kid by the nickname he’d given him at the start of the season, "full speed ahead!" The pitcher for Ted’s Carpet Service Emporium toed the rubber on the mound, then took position for his wind-up. Although he was the shortest one on the field, the boy had nasty control and above average speed and was one of the best hurlers in the county for his age group. Unfortunately, Thad knew the politics amongst the coaching clique, and he knew the kid would be overlooked when it came time to pick the All-Star team due to his poor hitting ability. It was something that Thad sympathized with – he’d received the same treatment despite being a golden glove second baseman when he was his age. Winding up, the pitcher let the first one loose: right across the plate but high for a ball one. Be patient, Thad thought, telepathically speaking to Miller. If there was one thing he’d stressed to these boys, it was the importance that the pitcher had to beat you – you didn’t have to beat him. As the smack of the rawhide echoed from the kid’s glove, Thad diverted his eyes over to the bleachers, about half-full with parents, small children, and a few kids in uniform either early for the next game or just hanging around out of boredom. He glanced over naturally to his wife, who was clad in denim and a bright yellow Cal U. T-shirt talking with one of her fellow teachers on the grass just next to the stands. Resting on the top bench behind her was the familiar white baby carrier with Thad’s future lil’ slugger inside. He dreaded the day he’d be coaching that one, as he wasn’t used to softball-style pitching. He made his mind up before she’d been born that Camille would be a tomboy. THWHACK! A tempting scorcher low and away was too much for Miller to resist. Swinging with a Ty Cobb-style grip, the fastball blazed past him. "STRIIIIIHHHH!" the umpire cried out, and the scoreboard operator flipped the metal switch to light up the first dot underneath "STRIKE" out in right field. "S’alright, s’alright," Thad reassured his hitter, keeping his confidence up. "Make him throw to you now." Behind the dugout, a curly-haired dirty blond inhaled on a Doral, making a slurping sound through a gap in his teeth. At least they were what someone would call "teeth," as they actually looked more like corn kernels coated in slightly-melted butter. A fading Coors Light logo on an oil-stained T-shirt and a pair of Realtree camouflage pants rounded out his appearance along with a pair of Wal-Mart brand boots covering a pair of certainly well-groomed feet. Considering the ball ground was the centerpiece of the rural community, the man didn’t look a bit out of place amongst the other spectators who ambled around the fences. A regular "Rusty" or a "Jim Bob," peering down at (one of those newfangled) cellphones, nodding at a text message on the screen. The next pitch came just as fast as the last one – although it got away from the righty as he released it and flew just out of the reach of the catcher’s mitt. Tum-Tum’s first instinct was to charge at the plate, but Thad’s yell was as effective as an Army drill sergeant. "Stay put!" Coach Denver cried, and the kid – "Rogers" now visible from the back view as it was pressed on his uniform – retreated back to third. A questionable move, though, as the catcher had a little difficulty getting to his feet to retrieve the ball even though the pitcher had left tread marks to get to home plate for the cover. The cherry of the cigarette separated itself from the butt as the dingy hand of the prowler flicked it skyward. Placing the phone into his pocket, he made his way unassumingly toward the bleachers where Ted’s Carpet Service Emporium. It was as if God controlled his head with marionette strings, as Thad turned instinctively to see the man stalking toward the spectators. His creepy swagger gave off a magnetic pulse that skated across the field and surrounded Thad with an omnipotent presence. The umpire had lowered his arms, signaling the athletes that the game was back in session. Miller tapped the edge of the plate twice with the fat end of the aluminum Easton bat, then bent his knees and prepared for the pitch. The enemy on the mound gazed dumbly at the catcher as he stood straight and adjusted his fingers for the impending curve ball. Stopping only feet from where Camille lay, the man pivoted on the heel of his boot and connected with a horrific stare directed towards Thad. It hypnotized him, and all color began to melt starting with the top of the sky and rapidly collecting into a clear puddle at his feet. However, everything else continued as if the balance remained perfect in the world, although Thad did not notice his other surroundings. He stayed fixated on the man who smirked at him, his mustache hairs looking painted on like a Wooly Willy board. The man abruptly turned ninety degrees to his previous direction. His hand now freshly stained yellow from the tar and nicotine grabbed the carrier in one motion. He lifted the child up to smile at it, then brought Camille up to his chest and held her tightly. Thad immediately tried to made a beeline for the other side of the field, but his knees buckled and snapped both legs in two, sending him collapsing face-first into the dusty diamond. A bolt of adrenaline surging through his veins alleviated the pain of his broken bones, but he could only watch as the disgusting man leaned down to kiss his daughter on the forehead, then look up to smirk at him tauntingly. "You MOTHERFUCKER!" Thad screamed, as though his limbs were chained to four wild horses running in opposite directions. However, his words merely spilled out of his mouth in the form of worms escaping back into Hell through the Earth. He dug his fingers into the dirt, trying to drag himself hopelessly toward Camille, but his muscle tissues began to unravel like disintegrating rope. Miller pulled the pitch high into left-center – the total opposite of what the signal called for, and the pop fly was easily caught and converted into a double play. Tum-Tum had turned around moments too late to beat the throw. Around the circumference of the filed, you could hear the applause of the spectators. Thad’s wife turned to look at him, completely oblivious to the kidnapper running at full-speed into the wooded area with Camille in his arms. It was then that everything moved in slow motion. His players returned with heads hanging down into the dugout, ignoring their writhing, colorless coach on the ground who strained his throat to capture anyone’s attention. Thad examined the lifeless stare of his wife, and suddenly her porcelain face began to crack open, revealing the decayed complexion of her true state. She moaned, and it was the most awful sound he had ever heard in his life. It increased in volume, and as it magnified, the colors slowly began to spread back into the scenery – filling the sky a bright red. The ground shook and subsequently grew softer. It inhaled, and began to suck Thad into it. By this time, though, he had given up the fight. ----------------------------------------------------------- "You’re sure about this?" The pace of the posed question came slowly and laced with grunting sounds. "Well…yeah. I mean, the burden’s more on you than anything, since it’s one extra mouth to feed. But, it might do a little good, you know?" It was all for show. Thad Denver’s loose underhand grip on the bar was more so for show rather than it actually providing a legitimate aid. With the sleeves cut out of a large black Cher: Do You Believe? Tour 1999 tee, Thad peered down at the pointed brow of the mammoth pushing up his eighth rep of 385 with no signs of slowing down. "Couldn’t hurt, I suppose." Again, it was a wonder that Lance Marshall could respond with such a massive amount of weight in his hands, pressing the bar skyward for the tenth rep and slowly bringing it back down again. "That’s five left," Thad proclaimed, reminding him of the count. The Lion blinked, causing the beads of sweat to roll down the indentations on the edge of his cheeks. He continued the bench press set as Thad focused on the spot that he knew Lance probably wouldn’t need. "You got this." Lance merely grimaced in response, pushing up the bar for four…three……two………one………… "Hold." Thad placed his palm about three-quarters the distance from the pegs on the bench to Lance’s chest. Lance’s skin flushed to a pinkish color as he counted silently along with his training partner for the muscle exhauster. At five, Thad "assisted" him, easing the barbell and its eight plates onto the stirrups and exhaling a breath of relief as he sat upright. "Nice job," Thad acknowledged, handing him a transparent lime-green water bottle. Marshall took a sizable gulp, with a splash of the liquid spilling on the collar of his Lakers shirt. Without waiting for a response, the junior of the duo set to work on removing two of the 45s. Flipping the safety lock on the clip, Thad eased off the first plate on the left. "Thanks," Lance responded, tightening the lid on the bottle and tossing it gently beside the adjacent bench. "So," he continued, "you didn’t tell me how the gig went." Thad stayed stoic as the clunking of steel on steel provided him a second to answer. Taking the second 45-pound Frisbee off the bar, he brought it to the holder and slid it on. "Not bad," he lied. "What’d you play this time? I know the folk stuff doesn’t go over too well with this crowd." The youngin’ known by wrestling fans as Spacely could improvise with the best of ‘em. "Alternative covers. It’s a standby when I don’t know what the crowd’s gonna like. Opened with Dinosaur, Jr. Closed with Soul Asylum. "Cool," was Lance’s response. He’d heard Thad’s rendition of "Runaway Train" before the two were even introduced at an impromptu mixer just after the Rushmore show in April. The truth was, Thad didn’t have a performance in the Los Angeles area – and he hadn’t had to do a paying gig since Vegas. Thanks to the new investors who saw fit to give the talent a very generous pay raise, You Idiot’s Favorite Son now knew for the first time what it was like to pay cash for FANCY things. The best part was that he no longer had to share two-star hotel rooms with production assistant Steve Darlesi. This was fine with him, as he'd rather watch his own mother be decapitated by Al Qaeda than to smell another one of Steve's farts wafting around like a methane ghost. No, Thad had come for one reason: a little cricket perched on his shoulder, tugging away at his collar and instructing him to do the right thing. Normally, that pesky conscience was easily forgotten with a couple of bong rips, but thanks to a rumored "wellness evaluation" on the horizon, he’d laid off the grass for a few weeks. Not surprisingly, it had done wonders for his sense of motivation, too. 'How far's your house from here?' Thad asked, re-attaching the clip onto the bar to hold the plates in place. Mentally, he began to psyche himself up for the big lift. 'About ten minutes. Not too far,' Lance replied, rising off the bench to allow Thad to take his position. The wild-haired blond took a seat and went to the back, looking up at his task and taking a giant breath. 'Lemme get this up then, and we can call it a day.' A lot of people -- including the man standing over him -- used workouts as a form of escape. It was a way for people to focus on nothing but the contraction of their muscles, making sure to breathe properly and use the correct form on their exercise. For Thad, lifting weights didn't help him forget any of the problems that bore down on his mind, and that didn't bother him in the least. Frustrations, fears, and dreams were like a very powerful form of steroid for the kid. Lance, a man who had virtually been like a road father to him, helped ease the bar down in preparation for Thad's first rep. One. The first push was for him: always the easiest, and always when you felt the strongest. To the mentor he looked up to, Thad imagined Lance Marshall's strength never waning throughout the countless number of presses he'd done his entire career. Still he kept going, never showing weakness in the face of danger. Two. The second was for Lane Stevens. In lieu of his first rep being for someone he strives to be like, this was for someone he hoped he'd never resemble. Playing a bad guy on television must have been fairly easy for a sociopath in real life. Three. And a little piece of Thad's heart to Wyatt Connors, who's kick to his best friend had almost set off a very short fuse. While Thad wanted nothing more than to beat his face to an unrecognizable pulp off-camera, it'd be bye-bye job. He'd get his chance for revenge at some point. Four. Mackenzie Malone, but more specifically, his long-standing issues of trusting women. He'd trusted only one his entire life, and the consequences still polka-dotted themselves into his nightmares. Honestly, his biggest dilemma was whether or not he should 'fake' letting her in for the sake of attempting to have sex with her. She seemed sweet, though -- then again, so did Kathryn Shaw. Five. Alex Pierce. His left arm started to quiver, prompting Lance to check the bar. 'I'm good,' Thad strained, a three-second burst of sudden energy giving him the ability to complete the rep. Six. Amy Campbell. As much as he hated it, he was starting to come around to the idea that there was more than meets the eye with her. However, being a nineteen-year old know-it-all made it hard to sway his opinion. She had maybe said two words to him his entire career in Sin City, so the notion that she was merely an arrogant bitch was dominant in Thad's view -- but it wouldn't be the first time he'd misjudged someone. Seven. Jared Sykes. Thad would be the first to admit that things between he and Jared had gone way further than they should have. He knew exactly why they did, however -- the two were virtually the same person in different bodies. Like him, Thad had generated a character that utilized comedy to mask their introspective images of sensitivity. Funny, emotional guys ultimately desired one thing in life: to be loved not by just a few, but by everybody. Which boiled it all down to the real reason that the shit had hit the fan: jealousy. The night immediately after the brawl, Thad had an epiphany as he nursed a bruised elbow at the sink of his hotel room. His selfishness had blinded him into thinking a friend's judgment was impaired. It was true; Thad did not trust Amy, Pierce, or anyone affiliated with the locker room cancer known as the Dead Man's Hand. But, he did trust Jared. And if it did turn out to be a setup -- Thad knew that regardless of their little spat, he'd be one of the first ones out to his aid. Regardless of that, though, by upping his max on the bench today, he still wanted that Gateway strap. Eight! Thad's last bolt of power came from a wagging tail and a face that was permanently friendly. The one soul on this planet he knew would never let him down. A once-abandoned puppy who just so happened to have a much higher purpose in life... ...even higher than being voiced by Rip Torn in front of a wrestling audience. ----------------------------------------------------------- Casa de Marshall wasn't anything incredibly unique, but Thad smiled as he parked his black Santa Fe onto the curb. Someday, he'd have a crib similar to this one: moderately sized in a nice neighborhood with a pool in the back and a basement doubling as a rec room. However, the pool would definitely have a water slide going into it, as well as a SUPER-SPRINGY diving board perfect for impressing babes by doing double gainers off of it. And the rec room? The pool table was standard, but Thad would be sure to have a couple of real arcade games in it: Turtles in Time, Galaga, and that one where you stand on the stationary skateboard and get seriously bad ass air. Either that, or he'd build a sweet tree house home, like in Swiss Family Robinson. And then he'd get his fucking swerve on. 'Let's go, buddy boy.' Opening the hatch on the back of the SUV, he watched as Quasar immediately leapt out and sniffed around the well-kept lawn of Lance's front yard, then promptly relieved his tiny doggy bladder onto it. Thad shook his head and couldn't help but laugh: he really had to pee, too, but didn't have the luxury of lacking dignity. Straightening out the tuck line of his teal-colored Lacoste polo, Thad started for the front door. 'Why, hello!' After a ring of the doorbell, Thad was greeted immediately by the friendly and vibrant face of a woman he'd never seen before. 'You must be Mrs. Marshall,' Thad addressed warmly, adding a tinge of Southern drawl to his voice. He had a knack for getting mothers to love him -- which would explain the pastel polo tucked into his snazzy khakis. Charm wasn't dead. 'Indeed I am. And you must be the young man Lance said we'd be expecting. Ted, is it?' She had a persistent glow of her own, and Thad immediately recognized where Lance had inherited his understanding nature from. 'It's actually Thad, ma'am, and it sure is a pleasure makin' your acquaintance,' he beamed, taking her left hand into both of his. 'My apologies, Thad' Anna remarked, slightly embarrassed at her snafu. 'I'm dreadfully sorry for getting your name mixed up. You don't even look like a 'Ted' at all! Won't you come on inside? Lance should be down any minute.' 'Sounds great! C'mere, Quasar,' Thad called out and whistled as he stepped through the doorway heading into the living room area. 'Well hello there, you beautiful thing!' Anna cooed, bending down and giving the golden retriever a scratch on the top of his head. Quasar panted in response, positioning his body against her legs in his own show of affection. Seated in a recliner and immediately putting down a copy of the Los Angeles Times was a quite subdued yet still respectfully intimidating man. Rising to his feet as Thad paced toward him, the father of his wrestling mentor greeted the kid with a grin and an extension of his right hand. 'Spacely, I presume? William Marshall!' 'You got it right, sir! Friends call me Thad,' he replied, meeting the retired officer's hand with a nice, firm shake. 'Pleasure to meet you, sir.' 'Likewise,' the elder Mr. Marshall said, patting him on the shoulder. 'How's things with you?' 'I can't complain at all, sir. Any day I'm not getting beat over the head with a chair is a great day.' 'I can imagine,' William acknowledged. 'But I hear my son's been running you ragged with his P.T. program, so that can't be much better, eh?' Thad laughed. 'He said that? He's just mad that it only took me a month to be able to keep up with him.' Both men chuckled at the exchange -- each being quite familiar with Lance's sense of pride in the gym. While Mrs. Marshall remained entertained massaging the ears of the long-haired canine sitting on the decorative floor rug, the sound of footsteps drew Thad's attention to the stairs. 'Where'd you get that shirt? Nerds-R-Us?' Thad smiled up at Lance, who had a curious yet mellow little Zach Marshall in tow, peering his head in between his dad's leg and the staircase railing. 'Actually found this in your dressing room last show,' Thad fired back playfully. He then turned his attention toward Zach. 'What's up, dude?' Zack smiled out of politeness at the man he'd met once before and had seen do crazy flips onto Xander Searle three times! 'Hey, Mr. Thad,' he quipped solemnly, as Lance led him in front down the stairs. Then, Zach noticed the other guest. 'QUASAR!' he yelled, a brief burst of glee as he darted down the remainder of the steps and nearly slid into the dog, who in turn began to lick the boy's face with excitement. Lance, William, and Anna Marshall could not (nor did they try to) hide how overwhelmed with the amount of joy they felt. It was the first sign of happiness they'd seen out of Zach since he'd been brought home. As for Thad, he knew then that he'd made the right decision to come here. 'More ice cream, honey?' Anna asked, reaching out for the empty bowl sitting in his lap with the spoon resting inside it. 'Nope,' Lance responded. 'I was talking to our guest, son.' 'Why'd you say 'honey,' then?' Thad laughed out loud as he shook his head. 'No thank you. I am stuffed from that delicious ham, Mrs. Marshall. Thank you so much for the meal!' Anna blushed. 'You're very welcome, dear.' It had been a good three-year stretch since he'd had a real home-cooked dinner (or 'supper' as he called it) and Thad had put down enough to feed the Chinese army for a month. Even his hyper-metabolic rate wouldn't do much in preventing at least a pound being added to Jason Myers' note card at Sin on Spike. Patting his stomach, Thad spent the next hour on the couch in the midst of a pow-wow with the Marshall clan (sans Alanna.) He listened with intent as William shared stories about his military service and a few of Lance's childhood anecdotes -- providing some great fodder for embarrassing blackmail opportunities: including Lance's 'potty accident' at a Sears one Christmas when he was six. Meanwhile, Zach stayed busy on the floor, entertaining Quasar by playing a game of tug-of-war with a length of rope. Zach had earned the dog's undying loyalty by feeding him pieces of ham from the dinner table. The clock eventually reached 9:00. Bedtime. For Thad, that is...as he had a promotional shoot in Vegas at five the next day, so a long drive awaited him in the morning. He stood and stretched his arms wide, announcing his departure to the family. 'Welp, I hate to leave such a fine bunch, but I gotta get going,' he proclaimed, giving a nod over to Lance who returned the exchange with a wink. Zach immediately clamored up to his feet, quick to express his disappointment. 'Aww man! Can't you stay for a little while longer? Pleeeeeeeeeeeease?' 'Now Zach,' Lance reprimanded. 'Mr. Thad's got a busy day tomorrow and needs to get home.' Thad shrugged his shoulders at the kid and made a goofy face. 'He's right, buddy. I wish I could stay, though.' 'I understand,' Zach said, defeat laced in his voice as he turned back to Quasar to say his goodbyes. 'I trust you're gonna take good care of Quasar though, right?' Lance beamed, and the smile was contagious as it hit the faces of the rest of the adults in the room. Zach, not sure he'd heard 'Mr. Thad' correctly, turned around with a little glimmer of hope in his eye. 'Whaa?' Thad grinned. 'Well, if I'm going to give him to you, I have to know that you're gonna take care of him. Otherwise, I guess I can take him back with me...' 'You mean...' Zach's tiny little voice began to get louder and louder with excitement. 'You mean...Quasar's my dog now?' The man known as Spacely (Thad to his friends, Mr. Thad to his friends' kids), gave him a nod. 'Just as long as you'll do me a favor.' Zach raised his arms in triumph as he smiled, showing a gap where one of his baby teeth had recently fallen out. 'Anything!' 'Just let your dad take him to shows,' Thad stated. 'Ya see, I'm nothing in the ring without my talking dog, ya know?' A squeal of glee erupted from little Zach Marshall as he ran over to Quasar and embraced him around the neck. Quasar then rolled over onto his back, which prompted the two to begin to 'wrestle' around the living room floor. Thad decided to stay for a few extra minutes, as he and Lance began to do their best impressions of Dave Gibson and Eugene Ware. Zach Marshall wins by pinfall, due to the Belly Scratch Sleeper Hold. Minutes after his goodbyes to William, Anna, and Zach, outside the Marshall residence, Lance walked the kid from Georgia out to his car still parked on the curb. As the two stood at the edge of the driver's side door, neither could tell each others' facial expressions due to the street light above them being out. 'I...I honestly can't thank you enough,' Lance said, as Thad turned to face him. Thad looked down to the ground. It was a guy thing -- he didn't want his guiding light to see him shed any tears. 'Winning eight-million world titles ain't shit compared to what that felt like to make him smile. Thank you for letting me help, man.' Lance certainly didn't need for his Lion-In-Training to see waterworks fill up his eyes. 'You did more for my spirit than you did for him, kid.'
