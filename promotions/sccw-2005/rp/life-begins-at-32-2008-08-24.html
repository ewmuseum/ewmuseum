Life Begins At 32.

24 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-24 A groan came from the pile of sheets and blankets making up the king-sized bed of room 580 of the Baltimore Marriott Hotel. The room's extremely annoying alarm clock was whining a mile a minute, 7:00 AM on the dot, which meant that the occupant of the room was supposed to be getting up, like, right this instant. 'Ugh. I don't have to be on the plane for another three hours, why the fuck'd I set the alarm so early?' A hand emerged from the bedding, judging the distance from the alarm clock. Nope. Too far away; he'd have to get up to turn the damned thing off. Shit. Groaning loudly once more, one Phillip Kennedy threw about a pound of bedding off of himself, rolled to his right, and angrily turned off the offending alarm. Sitting up, he grimaced in pain, before looking at himself in a nearby mirror. He had to admit, he looked good, for the most part. A far cry from what he used to look like during his poker career, in which he'd sported an impressive beer gut. Well, more of a Jack Daniels gut, but it works the same way. Kennedy frowned, though, at the sight of shades of purple and black all over his ribcage. 'That lesbian can't weigh more than one-fifty soaking wet,' Phillip moaned, rubbing fingertips over the offending bruises, 'and it's been almost two weeks since she hit me with the Lesbosault 9000, so why the fuck do I still hurt?' He sighed to himself. Waking up without Katsidy was never quite as much fun as waking up with Katsidy, who usually was wrapped around him in quite pleasant ways. Mornings were getting harder and harder, particularly after difficult matches. A long, hot shower was likely in his future (yet another activity that was far more fun with Katsidy), as was a good morning meal. But before all that, Kennedy had a kingdom to survey. The kingdom he'd been but a subject in when he was a young man. With one hand wrapped around his ribcage, the Strength in Numbers Champion looked out his window, and down at the city below. A city that he'd lived in for 25 plus years. A city that he now had nothing left in. ======================== 'Mom, Dad...there's nothing for me here.' Robert and Lori Kennedy looked at their son from their seats on the Kennedy family living room couch. Under his arm, he carried a hard hat from his construction job. The scowl on the young man's face seemed to indicate the esteem with which he held his job. 'Nothing,' Robert asked, shaking his head. 'You've got a good job.' 'A good job,' Phillip repeated, snorting. 'I make more money in my HOBBY than I do in my real job.' Lori scowled at that. 'Your hobby playing poker on the internet six hours a day,' she groused. 'The bank book doesn't lie, Mom,' Phil replied. 'I'm good at poker. Really good. And I can make money playing it without busting my ass at a construction site 9 to 5...or working at some shithole deli.' Husband looked at wife. Wife looked at husband. To them, the concept was foreign. Work entailed you getting up for nine in the morning, and going home at five in the evening. You busted your ass, whether physically or mentally. Work wasn't supposed to be ENJOYABLE. When you were a kid, you harbored dreams of it being fun, being a football player or something. That wasn't reality. 'Mom,' Phil continued, 'I've made ten thousand dollars in the past month playing poker. Ten thousand dollars. That's two or three times more than I make on the site.' 'Then quit and play poker if you have to,' his mother almost sobbed. 'Why do you have to move to Las Vegas?!' So that, then, was the issue. Phillip looked his sobbing mother in the eyes, remaining as calm as he could. 'Because that's where the money is. I can play online until I'm blue in the face, but I'll never get the same respect and money that the pros do. Vegas is the capital of the poker world.' Robert embraced his wife, her head resting on his shoulder. This was all foreign to them. Something they couldn't comprehend. Three generations of Kennedy had made Baltimore their home. Baltimore was good enough for them. It always had been. And now, their only son, the man who would carry on the family line...wanted to go to Las Vegas. Sin City. The place where prostitution is legal, and dreams go to die. 'Promise me you'll come back if you go,' Lori whimpered, her husband's attempts to console her only sending the poor woman deeper into despair. 'Promise me you'll come back in one piece!' The future Big Stack merely rolled his eyes. 'Mom, I'm going to Vegas. It's probably SAFER than Baltimore. This city has some really scary parts, you know.' 'But it doesn't have flashing lights and loose women,' she retorted, almost immediately, as if the retort had been planned. 'I'll be fine, Mom,' he insisted. 'I'm 26 years old, for Christ's sake. Don't worry about me.' And with that, he was back halfway up the stairs to his room, to pack, visions of the glory of Las Vegas dancing in his head, and visions of despair dancing in his mother's. =============== A smile quirked the corner of his mouth. 'I wonder how Mom would have taken to Kat. Probably not well...' Then again, though, Kathryn Shaw was an excellent actress when she had to be. Extremely convincing. With the proper act, his parents would have been thrilled that he was with a talented, beautiful, classy woman. Of course, they wouldn't know what went on behind closed doors, but really, what parent wanted to know their child was dating a probable nymphomaniac? It made him sad, in some ways. He was by far the most successful member of his family. He didn't have to work 9-5. He wasn't stuck in some godawful industry. He was doing something he loved, and he had a girlfriend for which the word 'no' didn't often exist. Even with all of that, he knew in the pit of his soul that Robert and Lori wouldn't have approved. Phillip could never have taken them to a show, even if the car accident had never happened. Because they would have seen what he did for his success, for the golden title belt he wore around his waist and his victories in the ring. They would see him as a member of the despicable Dead Man's Hand, one of the fingers of Alexandra Pierce. They would see his girlfriend as a wanton slut, his allies as desperate people who would go to any extreme to win. They would see their precious child as a dishonorable jerk. For most people, the death of one or both parents is a life-shattering event. The sort of thing that reduces you to counseling, or tears, at the very least. For Phillip Kennedy, the death of his parents allowed him to finally be the person that he truly was. It didn't take much longer for him to make the phone call. Just a few numbers punched into the phone for room service to bring a Western omelette, home fries, toast, and orange juice. Sure, the price was exorbitant compared to what it'd be at a diner, but what did it matter? He was young, now. Y'see, age is a number. It will be said of Edward Lambert when he passes away that he died young, because he didn't make it to 60 or 72 or whatever the average male life expectancy is. Fact is, that's bullshit. BloodAngel is an old man. Kennedy knows it. One look in the man's eyes confirms it. He's been through Hell in his time on Earth, fighting battles he can win, and battles he can't. He's 38. Kennedy himself is 32. But the difference is night and day, Phillip realizes. The Big Stack's worries are gone. Sure, he worries about Lambert beating him up. He worries about the enemies of the Dead Man's Hand. He worries about the Dead Man's Hand itself. But he doesn't have to worry about a family that couldn't deal with who he was. He doesn't have to worry about poker games gone bad in seedy parts of the Strip. Phillip Kennedy is a TV star on one of Cinemax's highest rated programs. He has two golden prizes wrapped around him: one a wrestling championship belt, and the other one of the hottest pieces of ass he's ever seen in his life. He was living his dream life. That matters a Hell of a lot more than the number of rings one has on one's tree. 'When I was young,' he snorts, shaking his head as he throws himself back into bed, reaching for the remote to flip on some TV and wait for his breakfast. 'Fuck that. I'm younger now than I've ever been.' And as our hero settles down to watch some paternity tests on the Maury show, there is only one question remaining. Will he continue to feel like such a young man after his elder gets through with him?
