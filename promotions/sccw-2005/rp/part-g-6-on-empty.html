Date: Today Plans are important. If a plan goes right, it can help you overcome insurmountable odds. If it doesn't, it can destroy you. The most important aspect of any plan is knowing when to abandon it. Don't be afraid to cut your losses when you come across something you don't like. Plans can be replaced, but there's only one of you. # # # May 2, 2010. 1:38 pm. Venice, Italy. Wyatt Connors was greeted by a swift knee to the groin. As he crumpled to the floor of the van, he caught a punch to the side of the head and fell over. It was a much kinder greeting than he'd expected. 'It's a...pleasure to see you...too, Cecilia,' he wheezed. Cecilia Sicarii--commonly known as 'Rat'--stared down at the Wise Guy with a look that might have been pleasure or anger...or both. She brushed a strand of brown hair out of her face and said, 'Don't act like you don't deserve it.' 'I've never...' he paused for a moment while he pushed himself back into a kneeling position, accentuating the movement with a heavy grunt. '...I've never had...much interest in...in getting...what I deserve. Try to...avoid it at...all costs.' Once his vision stopped blurring, Wyatt was able to get a better look at his surroundings. The van's interior was a light blue, much like the outside of the vehicle. 'Boy, this takes me back. Same van and everything.' 'You remember that? That was six years ago.' 'You never forget your first time...getting pulled into an unmarked van...and assaulted,' he quipped, which earned him a shove to the floor with one of Sicarii's thick, heavy boots. Connors looked up at his assailant while he waited for the pain in his genitals to subside. Above her trademark boots were a pair of gray jeans and a black t-shirt. Her face was marked by her wide nose and crooked mouth, which currently bore a sort of half-smile, half-snarl. The years have been kind, he thought, which was a nice way of saying that she hadn't gotten any uglier. She leaned down toward the Wise Guy, her long braids swinging free and brushing his face. 'Cut the standup shit, Connors. Why'd you call me?' The corner of Wyatt's mouth twitched towards a smirk, but he suppressed it, figuring that was a good way to lose teeth. 'I'm not sure. Probably for the same reason you slipped your phone number into my pocket, back in Chicago.' Sicarii bared her teeth with a smile that was less than amicable. Some time ago, she'd followed Connors and his minions to a bar in Chicago...and while they were leaving, she surreptitiously snuck a business card into the pocket of his overcoat. 'That's when I figured you needed help. If you were on your game, you'd have spotted me inside of a minute.' 'Be fair. I'd have expected Roderick to wear a dress in public before you.' Cecilia struck him across the face with a backhand. 'You don't get to make jokes, 'Wise Guy.'' She used his ring name with so much derision, she might as well have spat in his face. 'Not after what you pulled on me...twice. We lost one of our best operatives in Los Angeles because of you! What makes you think I'll ever help you with anything?' 'Again, I point out...' Connors began, every word bringing the taste of warm blood to his tongue. '...that it was you who contacted me in the first place. If all you wanted was to attack me, why didn't you do it then? Or at any point since?' Anger flashed across Cecilia's face, but she quickly regained her composure. 'Because I knew it was only a matter of time before you got yourself in shit so deep you couldn't weasel your way out. And believe it or not, we still might have use for you, so we'd kinda like to keep you around.' 'I'm disappointed you think so little of me,' he answered. Wyatt knew that Sicarii wasn't telling him the whole truth; there was something she was holding back. That could wait, though--while Cecilia's comment about Connors being in trouble wasn't the whole truth, it wasn't at all false. 'I know you've been jumping at shadows for months now. And I also know that you're the one who called me three weeks ago, lookin' to make a deal. So spill it, Connors. What's your deal?' Wyatt nodded, and struggled to a more upright position. 'Is it safe to talk in here?' Sicarii rolled her eyes--Wyatt's paranoia was amusing at times, but annoying at others. 'Yes, goddammit. Nobody's listening in, and Pierce doesn't automatically know when someone says her name.' 'It's not Pierce I'm worried about.' That got the Rat's attention. Wyatt Connors' fear of Alexandra Pierce was widely known and well deserved. If someone spooked him bad enough to push her down to second on the list... 'Who is it, then?' 'Shaw. She's been hounding me for certain...information lately.' 'Y'mean those dirty pictures you bought?' Wyatt chuckled lightly. 'Those are the ones. It seems Kathryn wants them for her own purposes. I considered accepting her offer...but the price was too high.' 'She couldn't afford them, huh? That doesn't sound likely.' 'It isn't. She was prepared to give me anything...I just couldn't afford to take it.' Sicarii exhaled slowly through pursed lips. 'Bet that didn't go over well.' 'Not at all. She threatened me, next of course...destroy my hired hands, expose my secrets. Not anything I'd get concerned about, normally.' 'But?' 'But...she knew one of the names.' Cecilia cocked an eyebrow. 'Which one?' 'McGwire.' Even in the dim light inside the van, she noticed the pallor of Connors' face. The diminutive woman smiled. 'Good ol' McGwire. I remember that one.' # # # August 3, 2006. 10:51 pm. Toledo, Ohio. Keith McGwire was almost asleep when the knock came at the door of his motel room. He was almost out the window by the time the visitor spoke. 'Mr. McGwire? It seems we're having trouble with your card. Would you mind coming to the front desk and helping us resolve the matter?' Not a chance, McGwire thought. He knew a trap when he heard one...and besides, he paid in cash. After a few seconds and a muffled 'Fuck it,' the intruder thrust a heavy boot into the door, tearing it free of its hinges. Four people piled into the motel room, each one armed. Two shotguns, one submachine gun, and a 9mm pistol. Keith decided to risk the second-story drop, but a hand caught the back of his shirt just before he could leap. He tried to wriggle free, but the hand jerked him back into the room. Someone--not the someone who had him pinned to the floor--spoke. 'Evenin', Keith. Nice night for a stroll, don'tcha think?' The voice was female, but very harsh...just like the person it belonged to. Cecilia Sicarii looked down into the face of her captive. Even with a face she knew that well, she could still barely recognize him. His hair was longer, with streaks of gray, and his jaw was covered with a thick stubble. 'That beard ain't workin', by the way.' 'Which is why I grew it,' the man snapped back. Cecilia frowned; she'd almost hoped McGwire would stick to the lie...but he refused to be pinned down. 'If that's how you want to play it, 'Keith,'' she muttered. 'You got two choices. You can listen to what I have to say, or my friends here can find out how high you bounce.' He shot a glare up at her and made a futile effort to escape the grip of the much larger men. She crouched down next to him and gave him a crooked smile. 'What's it gonna be...Wyatt?' Keith McGwire--or rather, Wyatt Connors--scowled. 'Fine. What do you want.' She smiled, and waved off her backup. As they retreated to the door, she plopped herself down on the bed. The body armor was a departure from her normal attire, but her braids still trailed behind her. 'You ain't an easy man to find, chief. You go to ground like damn few I've ever seen. Fact is, I can only think of one better...and we want you to flush her out.' Connors grimaced. 'You mean...' 'I do. None other than Alexan--' 'Ah!' he interrupted, looking nervously over his shoulder as he did so. 'Not out loud. Not around me.' She rolled her eyes and continued. 'Not too long after that stunt you pulled on her--and I still got a few things to say about that--she circled the wagons an' won't come out for nothin'. I figure we gotta hang out a pretty good carrot for her to go after...or, a great big piece of shit she'd love to bury. That's where you come in.' 'Careful with the flattery,' sniped Wyatt. 'You'll turn my head.' 'So I'm thinkin', you crawl up outta the sewer...maybe get involved in the wrestlin' again; that's always good for drawin' attention. She comes out after you, 'cause holy shit does she still have a serious mad-on, an' we'll step in.' 'Mm-hmm. Can't say I care for this plan very much.' 'Thought y'might say that. Lemme just remind you that I still owe ya one from the computer raid last year. So it's either this, or a little somethin' I like to call the 'shotgun wedding.'' Sicarii tapped her hand against the barrel of her weapon. 'And I'll tell ya this much for free--you are not gonna make for a beautiful bride.' Connors glanced around the room for means of escape, but found none. At least, not in the room. 'Let me think about it. I might be able to come up with something.' 'You got it, chief. Just try to make the call as soon as possible. Oh--and just so you don't get yourself lost in thought, I'll have these guys keep an eye on ya. Sound fair?' 'Wonderful,' grumbled Connors. 'I've always worried that my resume wasn't varied enough. Now I can add 'bait' to it.' 'That's the spirit, Connors!' Cecilia beamed. 'We'll be back in the morning. Boys?' Cecilia Sicarii walked out the door, but not without a backward glance toward the Wise Guy. Whatever she was looking for--fear, despair, a sign of compliance--she didn't find. A moment later, she was gone, as were her cohorts. Taken up defensive positions around the hotel room, no doubt. Wyatt Connors rubbed his hands together, his mind already buzzing with thoughts about the new task at hand. There was so much to do--and if he was going to take part in this ridiculous scheme, then he might as well do it right. He had to make himself look presentable again, for one thing. Shave, haircut, new suit. And of course, he'd need his lackeys. But first, there was one important call to make. He reached under the mattress and came out with a prepaid cellphone. He quickly dialed a number and waited for the pickup. After a few rings, he got an answer. 'Hello,' he said to the person on the other end. 'No--yes, I know what time it is. This is se--would you shut up? This is serious.' A wolfish grin spread across his lips. He'd found his escape route at last. 'Let me speak with Andrew Pierce.' So many people had tried to paint Wyatt Connors into a corner. They just couldn't get him to stay there. # # #
