The Letter

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Last Week. The letter was received on a Tuesday, less than 24 hours before the SCCW champion came to Kindred Spirits and less than 37 before the Spider in the Web abruptly took a leave of absence from the Order of St. Julian. It would later be blamed for her departure, just a few words inked on a single page said to have impelled the Director to step down. These rumors were only partially true, and they were only partially false. It was typed in twelve-point Courier New font, its big, bulky black letter staining the crisp sheet of egg-white 8.5' x 11' parchment. The letter was precisely, even meticulously, folded into thirds, crease so sharp it could cut through the pretentiousness. Even so, Alexandra Pierce's employees couldn't even pull a partial print off the letter, and even the envelope had no tells, simply a post office box number in Dayton, Ohio that didn't actually exist. Four hours after the postman delivered the envelope (and only after it had been thoroughly poked, prodded, scanned, and analyzed its contents), the letter found its way to an antique, Civil War era mahogany desk in Alexandra Pierce's private quarters. The desk's only ornamentation was a single, unremarkable picture of a singularly remarkable young girl, and it hadn't even had that until Amy Campbell insisted upon it. At this moment, the letter was attached to a solid steel clipboard resting on the tightly bunched muscles of Pierce's thighs, her leather booted ankles crossed beneath her. It was the seventh time she'd read the letter, stone gray eyes barely moving as she absorbed more than read the words. Around her daughter and especially around Amy Campbell, there were times Alexandra was vibrant and alive, but moments like this, Desade cast a pall over Pierce, quieting her movements and muting her emotions. It was a defense mechanism -- some people got angry, but Alex closed down in on herself, bottling it all away. The letter did this to her. This is what it said. * * * * * Dearest Alexandra: You think this is a game? You think I'm just some wrestler; sliding into your pink wig, your rainbow mask, and your garish wardrobe to, what, make a name for myself? You think I'm hitching onto your four months of lies, manipulations and staged reality shows? It's more than that. But you should be honored – you *deserve* more than that. I am not Charlotte Ramone, no. But she is a symbol of all that you have done. The world you have built for Sin City rewards liars and fosters distrust. You could take this letter to Nigel Kensington – hell, you could have Dave Gibson read it live on Temptation – and no one would believe it. They expect you to lie to them. They expect you to play with their emotions. Even your own Hand privately doubts it, but they will play along. Even the women in your life will doubt these words – but they will always doubt because they know – they know a – you are a monster. You've ruined lives. You've crippled people. For years, you have done horrible things for the benefit of no one. All for the sake of your games. Now you will play my game. You will play your human chess with me. Elijah Hawke (an overwrought alias if I have ever heard one) has long been your bishop, striking from odd angles. It was he that I removed from the board first. And now I will linger on the periphery. I will dance for these people like a monkey. I will give these people the blood and circuses they desire. You said it yourself, Alexandra. You said it to Dusk. You want to stop me? I dare you to try. And one day, I will take from you as you have taken from so many. I will cost you something you hold dear. Then – and only then – will you truly reap what you have sown. Until that moment, I think it is time for you to beware of shadows, Alex. They move when you're not looking, too. La Sombra. * * * * * Thirty-seven hours later, Alexandra Pierce crept away from her estate, unsure if she would ever return. But before she left, Desade completed three separate tasks. The first was a single phone call – not to her protégée or her right hand, but to one of her worst enemies. Because even enemies treat for favors. And I was in enemies the Spider trusted.
