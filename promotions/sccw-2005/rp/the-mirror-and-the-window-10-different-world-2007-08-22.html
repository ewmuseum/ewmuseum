The Mirror and The Window 10: Different World

22 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/22/07 10.1 the mirror: split These past few weeks you have spent most of your time at work trying to save Timo Bolamba from himself. He has spent much of that time splitting himself between two personalities: a good, hard-working fighter; and a rotten, low-down cheat. Most people around you have been confused about this, how he can so easily jump between the two sides of himself. After last night, you completely empathize. You’ve always felt that you had two separate lives too, although not nearly as drastic as Timo. You merely felt that with all the support you were getting for being a stand-up guy in FUSE, your actions outside of the ring paled in comparison. But honestly, when it came down to it, you weren’t really being split between two people; you were just feeling an understandable difference between being the people’s hero and being a normal person. But now you’ve gone and done it. If you weren’t a divided man before, you certainly are now. It’s quite clear that you, Jonathan Rhine, are either a fantastic athlete who hates cheating and who is genuinely concerned for other people’s well being… Or you’re an asshole who cheated on your girlfriend with your therapist. 10.2 the window: par for the course "Oh shit, oh shit, oh shit, oh shit," Jonathan murmurs as he drives home slowly. This isn’t about obeying the speed limits or being a good driver; this is about dreading coming home to a woman who is his everything after he’s been with someone else. He ignores the honking from impatient cars and digs for his cell phone. He presses a few buttons and waits to hear the voice of one of the last people he wants to talk to right now, but the only one who can help him. "Hey, Foster. It’s Jon. I’m in deep shit." "What’s up?" asks Foster good-naturedly. Jon puts the phone on speaker and sits it on his dashboard as he drives. "I messed up big time. I…I cheated on Katie." There’s a pause on the other end as Jonathan tries to decide what face Foster is making at this time. He decides it must be one of indifference as Foster calmly answers, "With Sarah Riley?" Jonathan sighs. "Yeah." "Okay," Foster says after another brief pause. "Okay? Okay?" Jonathan starts to panic, shouting a little bit at his mentor. "It’s definitely not okay. I cheated on my girlfriend. I cheated on her with the woman you told me to see. This isn’t okay!" Foster laughs on the other end. "No, it really is, Jonathan. You’re beginning to realize the differences between the world you used to live in and the world you live in now. Sarah Riley is simply a right of passage." "What do you mean?" "Well, I sent you to her so you could find this out for yourself, but obviously it took you a few weeks to do so." Jonathan opens his mouth to answer but Foster continues talking. "What have you been doing with her until just now?" Jonathan looks confused. "Uh…talking to her? Getting my problems off of my chest?" This laugh is even louder, and it causes Jonathan to cringe. "You’ve been using her as an actual therapist? Jon, you’re a class act, I have to give it to you. I’m pretty sure no one’s actually came to her for verbal therapy sessions since she got her psych degree." Jonathan finally pulls over the car and parks, then picks up the phone. "That’s not important, Foster! I don’t care if she’s usually a whore for wrestlers, or if she sucks at being a therapist. I care about Katie. I care about what I did. What am I supposed to do about this?" "Well," Foster says, his voice once again becoming calm and serious, "you basically have a choice of three options. First one is you can tell her what you did, explain that it was a lapse of judgment, promise you’ll never do it again, and hope she believes you. Second is you can not tell her and let it eat at you alive until one day you blow up from having to hold it inside for so long." Foster pauses again, and when a few moments pass with no sound other than the loud humming of passing cars, Jonathan nearly shouts, "And the third?" "The third is to just let it slide and live life normally, knowing that this is part of the business you chose and it’s just going to happen again, so accept it and move on. Like I said, Jonny, you’re in a different world now, and these sort of things aren’t terrible transgressions, or sins, or anything like that. These things are par for the course. So live with it." Jonathan’s eyes close with the weight of this statement. Foster finishes, "Now, I’ve got to go. Me and Magen are going out to dinner. Make the right choice, okay?" And Foster hangs up, leaving Jonathan with nothing but the cars on the road for company. 10.3 the mirror: different world A different world? You’re not really sure you understand it. Is being a wrestler really all that different than being in any other profession? You don’t really think that there are rules that permit adultery, no matter what you do for a living. Foster’s just been jaded for so many years that he’s accepted some people do that. Some people get affected by the business and do things to hurt the people they love. Well, you don’t want to do that sort of thing. You don’t believe in this different world, and you know for damned sure you’re not going to live there. You’re going to do what you’ve been doing this entire time: make your way through both stages of life, doing your best to make decisions that don’t hurt anyone and atoning for when they do hurt someone. Foster made it seem like there was only one choice to make, and he’s right. Except it’s not the same choice he’s thinking. You’re not going to live with it, you’re not going to accept it and move on. You’re going to tell her. You’re going to ask for forgiveness, and you’re going to prove that you can be in this business without having to live in a world of corruption, lies, and adultery. Because you’re different. 10.4 the window: let loose Jonathan Rhine puts a water bottle over his head as he steps out of his car, using his free hand to rub the moisture all over his face. After making his hair wet, he uses a towel to wipe off the extra water, then throws it over his shoulder. Grabbing his duffel bag, he enters his new home and tries to find the woman who he owes an apology to. "Katie?" He looks around in the front room and sees that the lights are all off, then proceeds to the back room. Opening the bedroom door, he sees her curled up in a ball at the corner of the bed, reading a book. Upon seeing him enter, she smiles and sits up. "Hey, babe. How was the workout?" "It was good," Jonathan says, sighing. Then, he grabs her wrists and kisses her forehead. "Look, I wanted to tell you…" "No talk. Cuddle," she says in tired baby-talk, pulling Jonathan towards her and holding him. "I just want to hold you and know that I’m with a superstar." Jonathan arches his eyebrow. "A superstar?" Suddenly regaining energy, Katie smiles. "Oh, yeah. I was reading some internet articles today and everyone’s got you as a favorite to win both of your matches and go on to face the champion. It seems that everyone can’t get enough of you." He smiles, hugging her close to him. "I guess. I’m not really into all of that press, though." "But you should be. I mean, the way you’re touted on here, I expect you to come home wearing sunglasses and playing some sort of entrance theme. You’re so humble, in fact, that I think it’s a little unhealthy." "Oh?" is all he can muster in reply, turning Katie around so she can see him. "Well, yeah. I mean, you’re Jonathan Rhine." Katie begins speaking as if she’s recited this speech. "You haven’t lost a match yet, and you’re this huge superstar in the wrestling community. But you try so hard to not let it affect you or get you bigheaded. I say just splurge once or twice. Accept who you are: the beginnings of a wrestling icon." Jonathan laughs. "Wow, you’ve been getting a lot into the business, haven’t you?" She looks off into space as she begins talking, obviously thinking of all of the things she’s researched while he was gone. "It’s really interesting, actually. I mean, there’s an entire community of people who appreciate you, and they aren’t the fat, ugly, hairy fans who shout for you at every show. There are actually smart people who see your appeal, and they write well. It interests me. So maybe you should live into it a little." "Live into it?" Jonathan’s dense answers are finally starting to get Katie exasperated, and she sighs as she looks at him. "Be that superstar! Be the guy who everyone wants to see. You’re so focused on not losing yourself to your job that you’ve become this tightly-wound, overly-anxious guy who doesn’t enjoy himself anymore. I mean, every time you’re at home you seem like something’s eating at you." Jonathan sees his chance and tries to take it. "But there’s a reason for that. See, I…" "Listen, Jonny," she says, using her finger to hush him, "I know you’re a good guy, and you’d hate to be one of the wrestlers who think they are God’s gift to the world. I don’t want you getting like that either, but what I’m saying is you won’t. Things are changing for you, and it’s like you’re in a different world now than you were before." Katie doesn’t notice Jonathan freezing up at this sentence and continues. "But just because you’re in a different world doesn’t mean that you’re a different person. You’re still that good guy, and enjoying what you’ve earned doesn’t make you any less of one. So let loose once in awhile and don’t worry about everything so much." She closes her eyes and buries her head into his chest. "Okay," Jonathan says feebly, looking down and kissing her forehead. 10.5 the mirror: accept you You want to tell her. You want to force her to listen as you confess what you did, you want to let out all of your problems and all of your fears, and you just want her to listen and forgive. You know it wouldn’t be easy, and you’ve definitely got an inherent fear that this confession would be the end of things between you. But it’s the right thing to do. And as she just said, you’re a good guy. You shouldn’t have a problem telling her the truth, telling her what’s right. But you just can’t. After everything she just said, after she finally started to accept what you do, you can’t give her more reason that the job is unhealthy. You have done everything in your power to acclimate her to wrestling and failed, but three hours on a message board full of smarks has turned her in that direction. And you’re not about to ruin that. You’re not withholding the truth to save your own skin. You’re not doing it to protect her. And you’re certainly not doing it because you think it’s the right thing to do. You’re doing it because, for the first time that you can remember, your girlfriend isn’t forcing to in some way to be one person in the ring and one person out of it. She is finally at a point where she can accept you for what you do, and you’re not about to ruin it by telling her something that’s only going to upset her anyway. But you want to tell her. That has to count for something. 10.6 the window: uncomfortable Half an hour later, Katie has finally succumbed to sleep. And Jonathan is in the kitchen on his cell phone, eyes closed. "Hello?" he hears a tired voice answer, and upon hearing the voice his eyes open in a flash. "Hey, Sa…I mean, this is the all-night gym, right?" he says, looking over his shoulder to the closed bedroom door. She may be sleeping, but still… There’s a pause on the other end before Sarah Riley’s somber voice answers. "Jon, I’m really sorry about earlier. I didn’t want to make you do that, but I just…" "This is Jonathan Rhine. I’d like to make another appointment for a personal trainer, if I can," he says, once again closing his eyes. "You…you want to see me again? Are you sure? I don’t want to make anything uncomfortable for you, and I definitely don’t want to come between you and…" "Next week would be fine." Then, dropping his voice to a whisper, he says, "Everything’s fine, just see me next week." "Okay," she answers, the joy obvious in her voice. "Jonathan…I really am sorry." "No problem. Well, see you then. Bye." Hanging up the phone Jonathan looks at the closed door again, half expecting to see his girlfriend standing there, upset. All he sees is the door, however. He turns around to lean against the fridge and sees a picture of him and Katie, his arm around her, both smiling wide enough to crack the lens. Smiling a painful smile, Jonathan covers up the picture with his hand and leans his head against the fridge. 10.7 the mirror: both true "You’re in a different world now." "You’re still that good guy." You’re not sure how both of that statements can be true. But you feel like both of them are. No matter how much you wanted to deny it, you are in a different world. Even if you don’t want to be, the fact that everyone – your trainer, your girlfriend, your fans – wants you to be makes it difficult for you to stop everything from changing. And maybe all the changes aren’t bad; like Katie said, you’re getting to be a superstar now. Maybe it’s time to splurge. But not in the way you did. Still, you feel like you are a good guy. You made a mistake, you fucked up beyond what you ever thought you could do, but you’re remorseful. You feel bad about it, and you don’t want to hurt Katie ever again. But you’re still going to see Sarah Riley again. Seeing Sarah doesn’t necessarily mean you’re doing a bad thing, as long as you can stop yourself from making the same mistake. However, you’d be lying if you said you won’t be tempted again. You’ve come to discover that maybe those two sentences can both be true because you’re in this different world: maybe in this world, someone can do something that’s considered a horrible thing, but still be a good person because overall his motivations are with well-intent. Maybe you get a pass this time because you’re Jonathan Rhine, because you’ve gone through one of the hottest debuts in FUSE’s history and sometimes things like that happen to a superstar. Or maybe you’re deluding yourself. Either way, you’re going to try out this world for just a little, to see if it’s worth living there. You just hope that nothing happens to your home on the old world where you’re gone.
