Date: Today "As you can see sir, there is nothing left." The monitor displays video footage of a rather damp, black and gray cavern. A single solitary white light plays back and forth across various sections of the dark interior. Gently and slowly the video probe pushes forward slightly to reveal a large dingy grey-white mass that looks rather like cauliflower. This obscene growth covers the majority of Edward Lambert’s left lung. The lady in the green Doctors scrubs reaches over and turns off the small monitor that rests on her desk and turns to the seated man before her. She pushes the glasses perched precariously above the bridge of her nose up with the index finger of her left hand. "I’m sorry Mr. Lambert, but with the advancement as far as it is there is next to nothing that can be done at this point. The malignancy has pressed past the point where we can choke it off via drugs. If you would like, you can to stop taking the Avastin and Eloxatin. They can’t do anything further to help any longer." Normally at this point patients with terminal cancer would be on the verge of tears. Some of the strongest men and women have shed tears when told that they would soon be face to face with the grim reaper. Edward Lambert simply nods his head and stands up. "Well that’s one thing that will make me feel better. Thanks Doc." His tall form turns and opens the glass paneled door and walks from the office like a man on a mission. * * * The home of Edward Lambert is a nice one. It sits on an acre of land slightly outside of the city limits of Lexington Kentucky. The gated community where his house sits is older and most of the home owners in the area consider themselves the "elite" of the town and state. Edward has owned this house and lived here for nearly fifteen years. He has lived in the Lexington community for thirty three of his thirty eight years and his name is well known in the community. Certainly not everyone likes him; in fact, a large portion of the more well to do loathe him. The old money families in particular detest him, as do the horse farm owners. As Edward puts it, "Fuck them." As with any home owner, when you have lived in a home for any length of time, much of his personality has been put into the house. The living room for example has a large screen television. It’s not the newest model, but it’s not the oldest. The chairs are leather and older, as is the couch. The pictures on the walls, the hardwood floor, the paint, practically everything about the house shows wear, use, and life; much like the owner. Edward Lambert sits silently in his office, his feet propped on the desk before him, a burning cigarette clenched between his lips. Smoke wreaths his head as he sits in silence and puffs the smoke like an old fashioned freight train. Finally, with a sigh he grinds the cigarette out in the overflowing ashtray before him and stands. He jerks one of the drawers on the left side of the desk open and from inside pulls out a small mahogany case. He trails his fingers across the top of the case for a moment before putting his thumb to the pad on the case, and after a faint "beep" he flips the lid open. From inside he withdraws a small brass key and puts it in his pocket before walking around the front of the desk and leaving the office. Down the long corridor he walks before finally, he comes to his destination, the single door of his home that remains locked at all times; the door of his basement. The door is solid oak, three inches of solid oak in fact. The hinges hang inset into the door jam and the lock is stainless steel and titanium. With two turns of the key the door opens, and Edward steps inside closing the door behind him. Beyond the basement door a short flight of stairs made of poured concrete with walls of the same pay no attention to him as he walks down them carefully. The twenty steps take him to a bend and then another door. This door is made of solid iron and steel bars. When Edward purchased it from a federal prison doing remodeling five and a half years ago he never mentioned what he planned to do with it. Three solid locking bars bar the door from opening. The same brass key undoes the titanium bolt locks that hold the bars in place. There are banks that do not have as much security as this part of Edward Lambert’s house. "DO NOT FUCKING MOVE!" Edward’s voice is harsh from the years of smoking. Most of the time his shouts are one of anger and rage, this yell is a simple command; one that has been obeyed by it’s target for the majority of the five years Edward has been coming down here. The look on Edward face is stoic, he alone of his family knows why this door remains locked, and he alone in his family has stepped beyond its threshold in the last five years. From his pocket of his shirt he pulls out both a pack of Lucky Strikes and his monogrammed Zippo. Moments later, smoke pouring from his nose, he slides the steel and iron monstrosity into the wall bracket and steps through. The floor of the elaborate cell for that was what it truly is, consists of nothing but bare cold concrete. A section of the concrete in the center of the room is covered by a small threadbare rug. The walls consist of the same poured concrete, unlike the rest however they have been painted a simple powder blue that stretches from the floor to the corrugated steel ceiling. The room contains four pieces of furniture, and those are not what one would call quality. A simple bare mattress holds a pillow and threadbare blanket, a miniature fridge that has a combination microwave and toaster oven sitting atop it, a beaten up reclining chair, and a small television are all the amenities that the prisoner within a cell like this would have to comfort them. However, there is no prisoner within this cell. In fact, from the state of dust that covers the contents of the cell, it’s obvious that any occupant has been gone for at least a month. Edwards gaze flicked to the small alcove that holds the toilet, shower, and small sink; it to holds no sign of occupancy and his mouth curls up in a snarl. "Desade." His harsh voice trembles with pent up rage and hate. Edward is worse than most people. Most people do what’s right or wrong because either of their moral compass, or simply because they want to. Edward however is not a hero no matter how often people cheer for him. A hero doesn’t cripple people simply because they are in his way, a hero doesn’t maim the innocent. He’s more like a bad guy with some good intentions; Edward uses the wrong to do what his own twisted moral code considers right. That’s why he hates Desade, not because she fucked him over. Edward hates Desade because she did to him what he has done to so many others, and now she has done it again.
