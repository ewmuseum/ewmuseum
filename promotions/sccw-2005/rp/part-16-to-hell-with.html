Part 16: To Hell With

Date: Today There were responsibilities. He needed rest, to recover from his injury. He needed medicine to keep the pain at a manageable level. He needed to talk to his wife, play with his son...take another moment to let them know that he loved them, because he knew he wouldn't have many of those moments left. Instead, all Edward Lambert did on his first day home was stare at a three-by-five inch index card. Kia had found it in his bag when he brought it home from the hospital. Not knowing its significance, she tried to throw it away. He nearly re-broke a rib in an effort to stop her. After helping him back into bed, she stormed off in frustration, leaving the former World Champion to his 'Roy Neary moment.' He knew he was being crazy, but he just couldn't shake the feeling that there was something important in those words. 'For, behold, the darkness shall cover the earth, and gross darkness the people: but the LORD shall arise upon thee, and his glory shall be seen upon thee.' 'He that loveth not knoweth not God, for God is love.' 'The words of wise men are heard in quiet more than the cry of him that ruleth among fools.' 'Ye also, as lively stones, are built up a spiritual house, an holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God by Jesus Christ.' Those four phrases were spoken by Connors backstage at Defiance II. Not only that, he wrote them down, to make sure that Edward would get the message. It's a message. He's trying to tell me something. Despite popular opinion (and all evidence to the contrary,) BloodAngel is not a stupid man. He is able to think a problem through when he can be bothered...and there is something about Wyatt's message that bothers him a great deal. He knows that he is at war with two old enemies. There is Wyatt Connors, of course, although nothing like the Connors he remembers. But they've done battle a few times, so Edward Lambert knows Connors about as well as anyone else. Mostly, he knows that ever since All In, Wyatt Connors hasn't been himself. Instead, the former Wise Guy had been acting like another ghost from Edward's past...one that he knew far, far better than anyone. Reverend Ibrahim Seck. They'd known each other for more than a decade, and had hated each other from the first day. Ibrahim saw BloodAngel as a vile and unrepentant sinner, whereas the Reverend drudged up unpleasant memories of Edward's own Catholic past. What followed was several years of bloody conflict, with the only respite coming when Seck found different targets. It ended about seven years ago, when BloodAngel kidnapped Seck and imprisoned him in the basement. But of course, that story has been drudged up many times. The fact remains, BloodAngel knows his adversary like no one else could. That's why he knew that Wyatt Connors' visit to the training room wasn't just for the purposes of taunting. He was trying to pass along a message...and the Bible passages were the clue. You see, Ibrahim Seck was a renowned religious fanatic...but in all the years Edward had known him, not once had he ever quoted scripture. Edward smiled. It looked like he'd finally get some use out of the Good Book after all. # # # The Decency Compound. A spiritual retreat, whose residents pursue a closer relationship with the heavenly Creator. Right now, they're so far off the path that not even God can see them. For example, there is the Compound's public figurehead, Wyatt Connors. A man whose talent for lying had gained him fame and fortune...but he had never told a lie as big or as sinister until the Crusade began. The worst of it was that he believed every word of it. Immediately, the so-called False Prophet began gathering people to his cause. Many of them were sheep, ready to offer their souls to the first tin god that smiled at them. But there were a handful of others--most of whom were now Connors' personal soldiers in the ongoing war against sin. They did not come to this place of their own free will...and now, they had no free will to speak of. Not every soul in the Compound was sworn to Wyatt Connors, of course. There were two who lived outside his influence. They were known as Seth and Abigail, and it was widely known that the two of them did unspeakable things using his p... Using his penis. He knew about their filthy, sinful acts; their duplicity. Of course he knew--ultimate knowledge came with the territory of being God's second son. Nothing went on in the compound that escaped the attention of Reverend Ibrahim Seck. There would someday be a reckoning for the man and woman who flagrantly sin in this place. But that day is not day...for the great Reverend has more important things to attend to. Specifically, a young man named Colt MacReady. The young man sat on the floor, staring blankly forward. Ever since his arrival, he'd been fed a steady diet of faith and Kool-Aid, just like everyone else. However, Colt was not part of the congregation, so to speak. He was not even part of Wyatt Connors' own inner circle. As such, he was kept here in Ibrahim's own room, away from the weaker wills and lesser souls. Ibrahim hoped that, through proximity, this lad could reach an understanding with the Lord that the rest were incapable of. Reverend Seck was not trying to give the boy deliverance. He was trying to give him transcendence. He looked down at the boy from across the room, still seated in his wheelchair. He barely spoke above a whisper, but his voice felt like it crossed the room as if carried by thunder. 'Where is your father, Colt?' 'Hmm?' Colt murmured. His ice blue eyes were glazed over, like almost everyone in the Compound. Still, there was something in there. A spark of life. Of defiance. 'You were adopted, were you not?' 'Hmm.' A statement more declarative than inquisitive. 'Yes. The MacReadys gave you a foster home. I'm sure they were...nice people.' A noncommittal shrug from the boy. He was the proverbial tameless horse on arrival--it had taken nearly every member of Connors' foot soldiers to hold him down. Even the most defiant spirits hold only for so long, though, and by now he was as docile as a lobotomized sloth. 'Yes, I know. But they weren't your parents. They were merely caretakers. Nothing can take the place of a true father...someone to provide guidance. Someone who will teach you what it means to be a man...and what it means to be a man of God. Did they teach you that?' 'No.' Seck knew there was something special in the lad, though. He'd known since he first saw him, all those years ago. Something he could nurture...encourage...help to grow. Something he could use. 'Of course not. How could they? You only belonged to them according to the laws of man, not the law of God. So tell me...where is your father?' 'I...I don't--' The conditioning had gone on for several weeks. Far longer than anyone else had required--even Wyatt Connors himself. It wasn't that the boy was particularly resistant, although he did have a stubborn streak...but the Reverend knew all too well how important the boy was. He was the key. With him, Seck would be able to claim that which he desired most. Revenge. 'You never knew your biological father, did you?' A tear rolled down the boy's face as he choked on an answer. 'You've never met him--you don't even know if he is still alive. He left so long ago...before you were born. Before your mother began to show.' 'He doesn't even know that you exist.' A brief flare of anger fought through the haze in the boy's eye. 'How do you--' 'I know a great many things, child. My own Father has blessed me with many gifts, ultimate knowledge being foremost among them. I will tell you something now of great importance, Brother Colt. The ultimate truth about the man who gave you life with his own great sin.' Colt MacReady raised his head slightly, still not daring to look the Reverend in the eye. But it was he who spoke next, and not the Reverend...and his whispered voice cut the air like an angel's sword. 'It doesn't matter.' The answer surprised the Reverend, but it was a most pleasant surprise. Most people had to be led down the path to salvation...but now that he had been shown the path, the boy was able to take steps by himself. 'The things of the world will crumble to dust. Flesh will wither, blood will dry. But the spirit...the spirit lives on forever. You know it, lad. I can see that you know it. Tell me...who is it that has concerned himself so greatly with your soul? Who gave life to your salvation? Who pulled you out of the pits of hell?' 'You did, Reverend.' Ibrahim smirked. The boy was his. 'Now then, child. Where is your father?' The boy looked up for the first time, finally meeting eye-to-eye with the Reverend. He saw a smile as wide as the ocean; a heavenly face surrounded by a ring of light. He saw the Second Son of God, seated on a golden throne. Slowly, he crawled across the floor, his teardrops splashing on the carpet. When he reached the other side, he curled up at the foot of the wheelchair, like a dog before his master. 'That's right,' the Reverend whispered. 'I'm here.' # # # Edward knew right away that there was a connection. There had to be. Wyatt Connors wouldn't have gone to all that trouble just to spout nonsense. And here was where his knowledge of Connors came in handy. Wyatt Connors--'Wise Guy,' as Edward knew him--was famous for a great many things. He was an unrepentant liar and cheat, with a talent for elaborate plans. That was the thing--Connors wasn't motivated by the same things as most wrestlers. Didn't care about victories or titles. He had no desire to show his wrestling skill, and not just because he didn't have any. He did, however, need to show that he was smarter than everyone else. Honestly, it was as if he was physically unable to be straightforward about anything, instead choosing to merely allude to his deeply-hidden intentions. Like when the Illuminati destroyed the NWC--Wyatt Connors sold the whole plan by pretending to be on the side of the angels...and he spent the better part of a year planting a secret message that explained where his true loyalties lay. For someone like that, making a secret message using only four Bible verses ought to be a cinch. Now all Edward had to do was figure out what the message was. As it turned out, he found the right track almost immediately. He'd looked for key words, sure, but there weren't that many that repeated themselves in each verse. Besides, there was something even more obvious. Wyatt had written the text, yes...but he made no mention of the book, chapter, or verse. There was no way that wasn't intentional. There was only way to find out, and that was to look it up from the source. That proved to be slightly difficult, as the problem with being a hardened atheist is that you tend not to keep too many Bibles around the house. Fortunately, there's this thing called the internet, and within moments, Edward Lambert had his answers. 'For, behold, the darkness shall cover the earth, and gross darkness the people: but the LORD shall arise upon thee, and his glory shall be seen upon thee.' 'He that loveth not knoweth not God, for God is love.' 'The words of wise men are heard in quiet more than the cry of him that ruleth among fools.' 'Ye also, as lively stones, are built up a spiritual house, an holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God by Jesus Christ.' Isaiah 60:2. 1st John 4:8. Ecclesiastes 9:17. 1st Peter 2:5. 602. 148. 917. 125. The numbers still didn't make any sense. It could be that he didn't get the whole message...but why give only half, after going through all that trouble? Anyway, it just didn't seem quite right. Maybe if he left out the book designations, and only looked at the chapter and verse numbers. [detail withheld]. Ten digits. Just the right length for a phone number. Edward Lambert made another brief search, and smiled at what he found. Area code 602. Phoenix, Arizona. Home of PUW, a long-defunct NWC region. The place where the Wise Guy got his start in the NWC. BloodAngel wrestled there himself for a brief time. He even won their heavyweight championship...by defeating Wyatt Connors. 'Connors, you sneaky little shit,' he muttered under his breath, and reached for the telephone. Slowly, he dialed the number. [detail withheld]. Ring. Ring. 'Yeah?' The voice on the other end was definitely male, and almost as gruff as Edward's own. 'Talk to me,' Edward barked in response. 'What do you know about Wyatt Connors?' Something else started to gnaw at him...it was important, and on the tip of his tongue, but damned if he could remember what it was. 'I know that I'll split yer head like a cabbage if you don't tell me how you got this number.' He said something else, didn't he? Something about the book of... 'Kings,' he said. 'He told me about the Book of Kings.' After he said it, it started to make sense. Cabbages and kings, like the line from Through the Looking Glass. Yes, Edward Lambert reads. Pick your jaw up off the floor. 'Did he now?' said the other man. 'All right, here's the deal. He never would have given you this number if it wasn't important. Probably too important to talk about over the phone. You got a time and place for a meeting?' The man on the other end asked a few more questions after that, but they barely registered with the man known as BloodAngel. All he could think about was wrapping his hands around the Reverend's neck and squeezing the life out of him. Soon, the man who thought himself God would be challenged by the Angel. And no matter the outcome, the resulting fall would never be forgotten.
