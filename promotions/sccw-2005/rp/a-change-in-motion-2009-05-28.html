A Change in Motion

28 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Temptation: Provo The blistering sun had no friends, only enemies. Beads of sweat rolled off his back as the large, heavy metal shovel weighed down his massive arms. A mound of dirt rested to the side of the man and he added onto it with another stack. His right arm brushed aside the sweat as his focus remained on the task at hand. The man continued to work a feverish pace and the people of the village just watched as he showed no signs of slowing down. A few people in the background chatted about what the man was doing as they had been instructed that the man would be staying with them for the upcoming week. No one had bothered talking to him quite yet as his demeanor and stature intimidated most in the village. Whatever his intent was in this place they intended to let him finish out his project and then let him go on his way when he completed it. The man knelt down and grabbed the bottle of water next to him and squirted a few ounces of water into his mouth while the sun continued to assault him. From one of the houses appeared a man, no taller than six feet, and his eyes instantly landed upon this man while he lifted another mound of earth and placed it into the designated pile. A few of the other villagers looked at the familiar man and watched as he approached the mysterious man who had been given no name other than ‘friend’. The other man though had been known to many as someone who pushed the boundaries and asked questions until he was satisfied. A mysterious person in the village had greatly intrigued him. “Hello, my friend,” the man said as he approached the mysterious man. “My name is Alfa. What is yours, friend?” Yet, the man didn’t offer a response as he continued to diligently work on the project at hand. Alfa looked at the man and his eyes narrowed before he looked at the mounds of dirt, the holes in the ground that this man had created. “What is it that you’re building here?” Still no response. Alfa though refused to give up as his long limbs stretched out and his eyes wandered across the land as various people had emerged from their houses. Alfa had been the first person to approach the mysterious man while the others feared him or resented him for stepping onto their grounds. “You know, people in this village, they talk about you. Some say very mean things about you, but I defend you. Say there must be a good reason why Sphinx has welcomed you into our home,” Alfa continued to speak. The man finally looked up at Alfa and acknowledged him. “Did you say your name is Alfa?” the man asked and Alfa eagerly nodded his head. “Life has plans for you then. You will do no good to yourself by being seen here talking to me.” Alfa though persisted as he shook his head. “What’s your name?” he questioned the man once again, but before the man was given the chance to answer, a convoy of cars and trucks pulled into the village as gunshots rung in the air. The man held onto his shovel, but stopped digging as his eyes focused on the group as they entered the village in a cocky manner. Quickly, everyone in the village rushed out of their homes as the various Jeep trucks circled the entire village. Alfa looked poised for an attack, but the man placed his hand on Alfa’s shoulder to calm him. “Where are our vaccinations?!” a man howled from one of the trucks. Alfa approached the Jeep, but immediately stopped in his tracks as a gun was pointed at him. “Everyone knows the deal. You bring us the vaccinations and we in return, let you live and protect you. Yet, it seems like while we live up to our end of the bargain, you people have failed us.” Alfa looked at the man who held the gun. “I know you. You used to play with my brother.” “That was a long time ago,” the man offered in response as he showed no emotion. “Silence!” the man in the Jeep yelled as he looked over and saw one of his men talking to the villager. “What is it that you found so important to talk about, Abdi? Please, tell me!” Abdi looked over at the leader with frightened eyes and the mysterious man knew that things were about to get ugly in just a few moments. “Kosey, please. Please, it was an honest mistake,” was all the man could say before the hollow end of a bullet shattered his skull and sent brain matter flying in every direction. The man fell out of the Jeep and bled on the dirt below him. Kosey looked around at the people with a grin on his face. “This is what shall happen if I come back tomorrow and find that my vaccinations are not here. You people are only here because of my mercy and will. Defy me again and you will find that my mercy and will is not as grand as you’ve come to expect. Do you understand me?!” he asked them, but no one answered him as they looked back in fear. “Do you understand me?!” A few people in the crowd murmured as Kosey looked around. Alfa’s eyes focused on the boy who now laid dead at his feet, snapped out of his daze and looked at Kosey. “Sir, you show us nothing but fear and contempt. You come here and you demand things that we don’t have. You take away our medicine which is the only thing that can possibly keep us healthy. There are kids dying in the infirmary as we speak and you want to take their only chance at surviving? Where’s the mercy in that?” his words stung as a few of the villagers looked on in surprise. Kosey’s eyes glazed over at the teenager before he began to laugh. “My child, what is your name?” Kosey asked Alfa and Alfa just stood there resolute. “I am not your child,” Alfa responded defiantly. A few of the other villagers could see the anger in Kosey’s neck as his hand itched vigorously. “You do not understand respect, boy. Those children have been damned by God himself because of your defiance to me. Grovel at my feet, boy, now,” Kosey demanded of Alfa, but Alfa showed in his eyes that he had no intent on doing as Kosey had told him to do. The anger had now stretched to Kosey’s face as the leader wasn’t used to being defied so openly. With one swift movement of his hand, Kosey pulled a gun out and pointed it at Alfa’s head. Yet, the young teenager showed no fear in the face of death. “Do it, boy. These people, your people, they will not care if you die at my hands, but I’m giving you the chance to see how merciful I am,” but once again, Alfa showed no signs of bowing at Kosey. Kosey then cocked his gun and it looked like life would end for the young man before it had even begun. “Enough,” a voice came out of nowhere. Kosey’s eyes wandered over to the mysterious man who held a shovel in his hands. “There will be no more bloodshed here, do you understand me?” Kosey glared at the man for a moment, but there was something that unsettled the normally fearless leader. Kosey looked at Alfa and nodded his head. “Fine, but I will be back tomorrow for that medicine. Have it or you will have tested my will far more than is normally granted,” Kosey informed the surrounding group before he hopped back into his Jeep. With that, the vehicles drove off as the mysterious man went back to work on the project before him. Alfa stood there and looked at the man who had so easily dismissed the dead boy that laid on the ground next to him. Alfa went to move the body, but was halted in his attempt. “Leave him there,” the man said in a low tone to Alfa. The young teenager glanced up at the man and nodded his head. In this very moment, Alfa had learned not to question this man. To scare a man like Kosey, it took a man with great power or composure. From looking at the mysterious man though, he offered none of those things. “Thank you,” Alfa said as he referred to the man saying his life. The man thought nothing of it as he placed his shovel on the ground and dusted off his hands. “You have a future ahead of you. Don’t miss out on it,” the man said before he offered his hand. “The name is Craig.” Alfa shook Craig’s hand. “Nice to meet you, Craig.” *** The small village was home to a hospital that could break anyone’s heart. Beds were strewn all over the place as people of all ages laid in them, ravaged by diseases and illness, or missing limbs and fading light. Most of the people in this hospital though are children or teenagers due to the way that life in Africa works. Death comes faster when you’re older and taunts you while you’re younger. In one corner of the hospital, a child no older than eight years old is trying his best to get out of the bed. He’s missing one leg though. “Come on, I know you can do it,” the man who stood across from him urged him on. The child looked at the man and pain was written all over his face. For a long minute, the child just looked at the man who was trying to help out and noticed something quite odd about the man on this particular day. “Your eyes are red,” the kid said before he laughed out loud at the unique thing about this man. Craig just nodded his head as he figured that sooner or later the kid would ask him about it. Just about everyone remarked about the strangeness of his eyes whether they were an adult or a child. Craig snickered at the suggestion as he waved at the kid to keep trying to get on his feet. “Thanks for pointing that out, I had no idea,” Craig said sarcastically as the kid continued to smile while he tried to get out of the bed. “Now, focus. It’s going to hurt and it’s going to suck a lot, but you can get up. I know you can. If I can have red eyes, you can walk with a crutch and the reality that you only have one leg.” The kid though seemed to be stuck mentally. “It just hurts!” he whined and Craig decided to give him a little push. He grabbed the kid by the shoulders and helped him towards the edge of the bed. The child looked over at Craig and closed his eyes as he felt his good leg hit the ground and the cold concrete gave him a chill up his spine. Craig continued to balance the kid before he handed him his crutch and put it under his arm. “There you go,” Craig said as he watched the child get used to the awkwardness of having a crutch and not a leg there. Yet, as he started to walk he could see the smile on his face as he felt like the world ahead of him wasn’t lost at all. “I can do it!” the kid remarked in an excited tone and Craig could only smile as he nodded his head. The kid continued to walk around and got faster in his pace as he began to feel it out. “Told you that you could do it kid,” Craig told him as the child walked back towards him. “Let me ask you a question.” The child shrugged his shoulder as he looked at Craig. “What’s up?” the child asked Craig. “What happened to your leg?” Craig responded with his question. The child took a moment to think about it. “I don’t really remember. I just remember Jeep’s. That’s all,” he said in a soft tone as Craig nodded his head. “Thought so.” Before Craig could say anything else though, a voice came from behind him, and Craig immediately spun around. “So, what are you doing here?” the man asked Craig before Craig figured out how it was. As Craig took a long look at the man and shook his head. “You know, Sphinx, I really thought you would’ve asked me that question yesterday before you said I could stay here for as long as I needed to stay,” Craig said in a hushed tone as the two men began to walk away as one of the nurses came over to tend to the child who now walked around the decaying hospital. They walked to a table before they sat down and looked at one another with this long gaze of two friends who hadn’t seen one another in quite some time. “The reason I let you stay here isn’t contingent on why you’re here. You asked me if you could stay and that’s all I needed to know. Yet, I know you better than most people in this world. There’s a reason why you’re here and I thought I would just ask why,” Sphinx said in a very even tone to Craig. “I just,” Craig began before he gathered his words. “I needed a chance to do some good in the world. There’s only so long that you can feel the blood on your hands before you feel compelled to actually do something about it. The past months have seen so many people around me die that it felt like that’s the only thing I would ever see again. I needed to feel like I’m still capable of changing this world into a good place.” A small curl of Sphinx’s lips revealed that he was smiling. “You needed to feel like you were still human in a world that you felt like had died on you. Understandable,” Sphinx offered Craig who couldn’t find comfort or solace in those words. Craig’s eyes looked down at the table as he felt this wave of emotions overcome him. “Alexandra came and saw me,” Craig informed Sphinx. “I’m sure that went over rather well,” Sphinx said in a sarcastic tone, but he immediately could tell that Craig wasn’t in the place to joke about this. Craig took another moment to gather his thoughts as he sat the table. “She asked me to stop following her. To give her a chance at happiness. It’s made me wonder if I’m the reason for the way that she is,” he confided into Sphinx who immediately shook his head. “Alexandra is the way that she is because it’s who she chose to become. No one person or one event changed everything, though it might’ve been a catalyst. I believe though that fate dictated who she would be and she bought into it. Like she always has,” Sphinx responded in a much quieter tone. “Do you think she’s ever capable of true happiness? Real love?” Craig didn’t know that answer off the top of his head. “She thinks that she’s found love. Shouldn’t I want her to be happy?” “You should. I think in a way though, you’re skeptical,” Sphinx began. “In the end, you know the kind of person that she is. If she loses that happiness then she will be right back where she began. A woman with a black heart who is fueled by hatred tenfold from previous.” Craig still wasn’t certain though. “She left the Order of St. Julian.” “Well,” Sphinx thought about it for a moment. “That’s not good. You know they’re not going to be quite happy with that and are going to do everything in their power to either bring her back or destroy her.” “What should I do though? Should I help her with that happiness? I mean, Quinn… and her…,” he began to say until his voice trailed off. The decisions that rested in his hand were not easy ones in the least bit. Sphinx put his hands on top of Craig’s as the two men looked at one another. “God is going to put you on the path that he intends for you to go down. Let the Order of St. Julian come to you if that’s what God want,” he told Craig and could tell it wasn’t a good enough answer for the doubts in his mind. “Are you so doubtful of her happiness because it’s not with you?” “I don’t know,” Craig responded in that soft, hushed tone. Craig then took another moment to himself. “I saw Cozen.” Sphinx’s eyes immediately grew wide. “I hate that you call her that. I’d much prefer if you call her by her real name,” he informed Craig. “I used her real name a few years ago and the pain that she went through was truly heartbreaking. Her real name brings her back to reality, makes her see the world that has abused her for so long, and I can’t do that to her any longer,” Craig confided in him. “What did she show you?” Sphinx asked him and Craig frowned. “Death and destruction, the end of the world at the hands of Alexandra.” Sphinx took a moment to take all of that in. “Then, I can see the problem you’ve ran into.” Craig nodded his head. “I think you’re here to do great things. This village, they need you more than anything,” Sphinx began. “I think you could teach Alfa a thing or two.” Craig though wasn’t a huge fan of that idea. “He’s not ready.” “With you though,” Sphinx began. “He could be.” Craig scoffed at the idea. “He doesn’t deserve this hell of a life. No one does.” “Yet, it’s destined,” Sphinx told Craig and the red eyes faded into darkness. “Well, it sucks to be him then, doesn’t it?” Craig said as he knew the hell that the young boy was in for. *** Acres of trees seemingly surrounded the small village; an oddity considering the vast world considered Africa as nothing more than a barren wasteland. Craig’s eyes scanned through the trees as there was a large heavy axe in his hands. Morning would come before long and he knew that when the sun came out it would be as unforgiving as it had been the day before. His eyes landed upon a small tree that he knew he could make good use out of as he slammed the sharpened blade against the base of the tree. A few more hits and the tree gently fell to the ground. He began to lift the tree over his shoulder when he sensed someone else in the forest with him. Slowly, Craig lowered the tree as his red eyes can the ground around him and felt the seismic change in the wind. In one swift movement he pivoted towards the person and threw his axe as hard as he could. The blade landed firmly in the bark of a tree as the man who stood next to said tree stood frozen in his spot. Craig watched as Alfa glanced over at the axe and wondered to himself if Craig had missed on purpose. “That--,” Alfa began as he grabbed the handle of the axe and jerked it free of the tree. “—that was amazing. Though, I think and hope, you missed.” He then offered the axe back to Craig who graciously accepted it as he dragged it back to the tree had just chopped down. “I figured it would be you. Saw you looking out at the forest as I approached it this morning. Knew your… curiosity… would get the best of you,” Craig informed Alfa who eagerly shook his head to confirm Craig’s suspicions. Alfa looked at the tree that Craig had chopped down and knelt down beside it before he lifted it onto his shoulders. “Well, that comes with a degree of relief to know that you didn’t intend to kill me right then and there, my friend,” Alfa responded with ease. Craig looked at the young teenager and shook his head. “You’re no good to me dead,” Craig replied. Alfa and Craig began to walk through the forest as Craig’s eyes continued to scan the landscape to see if there was anything else he felt he could bring back with him. “So, I think I owe you my sincere thanks for saving my life yesterday. They were going to kill me, I think,” Alfa told Craig, but the older man just shrugged his shoulders in response. “Honestly, it was nothing. Those guys, they need someone to put the fear of God back into their hearts or else they do whatever the hell they please,” Craig said in response as he felt pleased with the work he had done this morning. They continued to walk through the forest, back in the direction of the village. “How do you know so much about things like that?” Alfa questioned Craig and Craig offered a smile in return. “It’s just what I know, I guess,” Craig replied and Alfa just left it at that. He knew that there was much that Craig would hide from him and figured it was fruitless to try and pry such information out of a man who had saved his life. As they began to reach the edge of the forest, they saw billows of black smoke as it rose from the village. Craig’s eyes narrowed while Alfa’s grew large as they emerged out of the forest and into the village. In the distance, a house burned uncontrollably and as Craig scanned the field before him he knew that the men from the previous day had returned and wanted their vaccinations. Unfortunately, said medicine had yet to reach the village and deep down, Craig assumed the thugs knew as much. “Oh no,” Alfa said in an exasperated tone. Craig’s eyes became a blazing red as his fists clenched. “You see that house? Go down there and help whoever is there. Round everyone up and make sure everyone stays safe,” Craig told him, but Alfa remained frozen in his spot. He glanced over at Craig with this fearful look in his eyes. “What about you?” he asked Craig and the rugged man had plans of his own. “Don’t worry about me. Put the tree down and run as fast as you have ever ran Alfa. The people of your village need you right now,” Craig yelled back at Alfa who immediately listened to Craig and proceeded to run down the hill. Craig’s posture was much different as he walked down the hill as his eyes remained focused on the Jeep that he assumed was Kosey’s. As he reached the grounds of the village, a man approached him, and Craig wasted no time as he threw his elbow into his windpipe and heard it crack. As Craig pushed the man onto the ground, Craig’s speed changed to a jog before he jumped onto the back of one of the Jeeps and slammed his fist into the jaw of another man and then kicked another thug in the face with the sole of his boot. Gunshots fired off in the distance as Craig exited the Jeep and his eyes remained focused to determine the sound of said gunshots. He rounded one of the nearby houses and slammed his knee into the femur of another man before he heard it crack and Craig sent him crashing to the ground. Craig turned around and found Kosey as he slammed his knee into the back of one of the villagers. In his hand was a rifle that he intended to use. Craig immediately knelt down to the ground and searched the man who screamed for mercy from his broken femur to find a knife that resided in one of his pockets. Craig grabbed it and launched it through the air before it landed in the hamstring of Kosey who immediately howled in pain. Craig ran across the round before he slammed his knee across the jaw of Kosey who collapsed to the ground in a world of pain. “I told you,” Craig began to say as Kosey tried to fight back to his feet only for Craig slam his boot into his face. “I told you, Kosey, that there would be no more bloodshed, and what did you do? You laughed in my face. Let me ask you something… do you think I fear people like you? Do you?!” Kosey spat the blood that pooled inside of his mouth onto the clay-like dirt before his eyes moved to his assailant. “I think you live your life fearing each and every day that you run into someone like me. You think if you kill me this will just end?” Kosey asked him and Craig shook his head before he backhanded Kosey across the face. “Wrong answer. I’ve seen people much worse than you crumble under my weight. I wanted a chance to do some good and instead, I ran into a man who doesn’t understand the word respect. A man who is the very epitome of the word coward. You should’ve taken the hint to stay away and instead you showed me that you can’t follow simple instructions and veiled threats,” Craig launched into a tirade against the vicious leader of this ragtag group. “Do you know why your men don’t attack me? They value their lives, something that you obviously don’t.” Then, in one swift movement, Craig wrapped his hands around Kosey’s head and twisted it as hard as possible until he could hear the bones as they snapped in Kosey’s head and the leader fell flat on his face. Alfa raced over to Craig right afterwards as Craig looked down at the lifeless body of Kosey. “Everyone’s safe, Craig, just like you requested,” Alfa informed Craig who simply nodded his head. Craig looked at the man and noticed something odd that was partially covered thanks to the shirt Kosey had been wearing. Craig knelt down to the ground and rolled up the sleeve before he found something he hadn’t expected while he looked for a Greater Good in Africa. “Fuck,” Craig said as he looked at Alfa and the young teenager could see the slight fear in Craig’s eyes. “What is it?” Alfa said as he looked at the man and noticed a brand or tattoo of some sort on the man’s forearm. At a closer look, Alfa read some words off the tattoo, but nothing made sense to him. “The Order of what?” Alfa asked him and Craig stood up on his feet as his eyes looked around with a frantic look to them. “The Order of St. Julian. He was with the Order of St. Julian.”
