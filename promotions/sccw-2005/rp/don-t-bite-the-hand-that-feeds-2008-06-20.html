Don't Bite The Hand That Feeds

20 June 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 20, 2008 A cleaning crew was working in an old warehouse near the Ambassador Bridge in Detroit, Michigan. Some men swept, while others painted and are taking down faded posters. A new set of lockers were being installed near a ring that was stripped down to its metal structure. Superman has his Fortress of Solitude. Batman had his Batcave. For Jay Draven, this was his sanctuary. Fans of Draven from his time in the NWC would recognize this as his original school. Before he set up shop in Arizona years ago. The faded posters were being replaced by framed replicas. Replicas of the titles that Draven held throughout his career were encased in glass and steel. The actual MSW Tag Team Title belt that he purchased from the former owners resides next to the actual NWC North American Championship belt that he was able to claim in an auction early last year. "Mr. Ryan, we’re on schedule for the work that Ms. Durant wanted us to do." "Good, good." Draven continues to look on as the workers continue to bring the warehouse to its former glory. Not an easy job to do, as the building had been neglected over the years. The Detroit Tigers baseball cap does little to hide Draven's once blonde hair, which now sports a new blackened hue to it. "Must be nice to have an ex-wife that still likes you." Draven turns around to find standing there his ex-tag team partner, former brother-in-law and, most importantly, best friend, Mike Durant. Years away from the ring didn’t affect the size of the man that was once known as Legion. "True." "Divorce from Serena final yet?" "Not yet. There are a few more things that have to happen before that becomes reality." "Sorry, man." "Don’t be." Draven and Mike start to walk through the warehouse. "How did you manage to keep this place through all three divorces?" "Mike, you forget that this warehouse isn’t considered a marital asset. I bought it the year before Kelly and I got married the first time. That, plus the fact that the Callion family also owns 50 percent of the building helps out as well. "You re-opening the school? Moving back to Detroit?" "Moving back, yes. Re-opening the school, I doubt it." "Then why go through the trouble of renovating it. Why not just sell it?" "Because, Mike. You know as well as I do that the ghosts of events past that reside here won’t allow that. There was more to this building than training others to be wrestlers. There was more to this building than a place for me to rehab my injuries. "This place, it’s a part of who I am. Even when I lived in that nice house in West Bloomfield, this was more like home for me. You can’t forget who you are. You told me that once." "Rhetorical nonsense." "Is it? Seems to me that you lived that. Every day you went through your pain of not being able to have a kid. You and Melanie tried for years." "Jay, please don’t." "Mike, what I’m saying is that through all of that, through all of that pain and heartache, you remained Mike Durant. You are a loving and supportive husband. You could have returned to the ring full time years ago, but you didn’t. Because, you defined yourself as a husband first, and a wrestler second." "What are you getting at?" "I’ve never defined myself as anything other than a wrestler. I’ve lost three marriages to this business. Yet, I can’t seem to find it in myself to walk away from it." "Jay…" "Mike, this is my home, more than anywhere else I’ve lived in my life. It’s taken me this many years to realize that. You’re at home where you’re at. I’m at home where we are standing." "What if Edie doesn’t approve of this?" "What?" "What if Edie Raymond doesn’t approve of your home being a once dilapidated warehouse?" "Mike, why would she even care where I live?" "Well, I saw the way that you looked at her on TV last week." "Edie and I are friends, nothing more." "Yeah, last person you said that about, you eventually married." "Mike, don’t you think I know this. Difference is, by the time Serena and I had gotten married, my marriage to you sister was already over, for a second time. It didn’t work between us, no matter how hard we tried. I didn’t give up on Kelly, she gave up on me. "It was for the best. Otherwise, we wouldn’t have been able to be the friends that we are now." "Jay, I know you. I know that there is no way that you would be able to be ‘just friends’ with a woman. Remember when we first teamed together. It was a different woman in every city." "Mike, I’ve changed. Why can’t you understand that? I have got two kids now. I’ve spent years trying to figure out who I am. I’m 38 years old. I’ve been doing this for almost 20 years now." "Yet you haven’t learned that there’s a time to stay and a time to walk away. Your time in the spotlight has past. You know what you’ve become. You’ve become that which when we got in the business you said that you hated. "You’ve become one of those guys that just can’t hang up the boots. Your career is over, Jay. You just don’t know it yet." "Fuck you, Mike. Fuck you." "Admit it, you have become irrelevant." "Get out of here, Mike." "Fine. I’m not going to sit around and watch you destroy your life because you don’t know when it’s time to give it all up." As Mike Durant turns and walks away, Draven walks over to the ring. He places his hands on the cold steel and looks around at the building that is being fixed up around him. "I knew you would be here." Draven turned around to see who said that.
