No Such Thing As Easy Money

13 August 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/13/09 Sitting on one of those wooden stools that swiveled around, Darius Drannen took a healthy swig of his pint of ice cold beer, the filled glass leaving a white foam on the top of his lip as he lowered it back down to the bar. It was midday, but the tavern was almost packed to capacity. Apart from the bar stools on either side of Darius, and one two-person table tucked near the very back of the room, right next to the bathrooms, there wasn't a seat left in the place. Apparently, some important match was being telecast on the big screen television sets scattered throughout the joint, attracting every soccer -- sorry, football -- fan in the city to come and cheer on their squad. He glanced up at the smaller television set above the bar. The score was currently zero-zero, but Darius had no clue the significance of the game, or who either team was that were playing. Soccer wasn't his sport. He enjoyed a lot of action, and sitting through a bunch of nothing-nothing games wasn't his idea of entertainment. He swiveled in his chair to survey the crowd of patrons, but couldn't see the individual he'd come to meet. They'd agreed upon a 2:30 pm meeting, and the last time he'd checked his watch -- which was about five minutes ago -- it was almost 3:00 pm. Swiveling his chair around to face the bartender, he shook his head in annoyance and went back to nursing the rest of his beer. As he waited, his mind went back to about a week ago, when he, his friend Cade Carson, and their hired companion Thibbledorf 'Rumple' Stitskin, had braved one of the worst snow storms in recent history in order to visit with an old acquaintance of Cade's. He chuckled, recalling Rumple's constant bitching over being left in the dark about their destination. He wasn't sure why Cade refused to tell Rumple where they were headed. After all, Cade had told Drannen when he'd called to see if he was free for the afternoon. So obviously, secrecy couldn't have been the reason. Truthfully, Cade's reason didn't matter to him. Drannen enjoyed the fact not knowing where they were going was a constant source of frustration for Rumple. And the more Rumple got agitated, the more Drannen found it amusing. But that didn't mean Drannen understood the point of the excursion. It wasn't because of the bitter cold, or the terrible road conditions they'd had to endure on the drive out there. Rather, it was because Drannen had never understood why Cade placed so much faith in one man's opinion. Even if that man just so happened to be a highly-regarded priest. Drannen wasn't religious. It just wasn't in his blood. Something about worshipping something you couldn't actually see, and following the doctrine of a book that might or might not have been written by someone playing a massive practical joke on the reader, just didn't jive with his way of thinking. He was a practical man by nature. If you wanted to convince Drannen of something, you needed to be able to provide him with physical proof of its existence. He wasn't someone who'd simply take your word for it just because you appeared sincere when you said it. To his way of thinking, the word 'faith' meant the same thing as the word 'gullible' did. And despite his numerous faults, gullibility was not one of them. 'Ah, there you are,' a familiar voice broke through the general murmur of the various soccer fans cheering on their respective teams, shaking Darius from his reflections. He swiveled his chair around, and was relieved to see the individual he was supposed to meet a half an hour ago, had finally decided to show up. 'You're late, Martell,' Darius said. 'I know, I know,' Jean Guy Martell apologized, holding up his hands in a gesture of surrender. 'I can explain.' He was a middle-aged Caucasian male with skin that looked as though he'd spent way too much time in a tanning salon, and was known for his numerous contacts throughout the city. If you needed information about someone, something, or somewhere, he'd be the guy you go to -- for a price. More often than not, that price was worth every penny. 'May I?' Jean asked, indicating one of the empty stools next to Darius. When Drannen nodded, the man took a seat. He turned to the bartender, 'Same as whatever he's drinking', then turned to Darius and said, 'You'll like this.' 'We'll see about that,' Darius answered skeptically. The man ignored Darius' skepticism and continued. 'Remember that guy I was telling you about the other day? The one who was bragging about winning big in some underground poker game?' 'Luc Bellemore?' Darius offered. 'That's the guy,' Jean confirmed. 'Apparently, the people running the poker game didn't like the attention his mouth was attracting, especially from the local authorities.' Darius nodded. 'I can see how that could piss them off.' 'Exactly.' Jean took a sip of his beer before continuing. 'Anyway, seems poor ol' Mister Bellemore didn't get a chance to blow much of his winnings before he had an ... unfortunate boating accident.' Darius raised an eyebrow. 'Boating accident?' Jean grinned mischievously. 'He kinda got run over by a boat ... twice. After he'd been pushed in the water. With his arms tied over his head.' 'Ah,' Darius said. He tapped his chest. 'Interesting story and all, but what does any of this have to do with me?' Jean's grin grew wide enough that it threatened to swallow his entire face. 'Like I said, Bellemore didn't get a chance to spend more than about a couple of grand of his winnings before his, uh, untimely demise. So,' he leaned close to Darius and spoke in a conspiratorial voice. 'there's about forty eight grand sitting in a briefcase in his hotel room, just waiting to be claimed.' Darius frowned. It took him a couple of seconds before the implications of Jean's words sunk in. Then he shook his head vigorously. 'No way, Jean,' Darius said. 'Why not?' Jean protested. Darius leaned close to him, pointing emphatically at him with his index finger. 'Because I don't want to end up like Bellemore. That a good enough reason for you?' Jean leaned back in his chair, and waved away Darius' concerns with a gesture. 'It's not like that.' Darius blinked. 'Oh, no?' 'No,' Jean said. 'See, the guys that Bellemore won the money from have a reputation of being honorable -- at least as honorable as someone who does what they do can be -- business men, and they want it to stay that way. If word gets around that they go around killing anyone who wins money from them, it'll sink them. So you see, Bellemore didn't get whacked because he won money off of them. He got whacked because he had a big mouth.' Darius still looked skeptical. 'What that means is,' Jean explained, his voice dropping dangerously close to that of an adult talking to a slow child for Darius' liking, 'they likely don't care what happens to that money, now that Bellemore's been shut up for good.' 'In fact,' Jean tossed a beer nut in his mouth, then continued, 'They'd probably be just as happy to see the money disappear as well. After all, no money, no motive. Makes sense, don't you think?' Darius was shaking his head. 'I don't know. This just seems too ... too easy.' 'Exactly!' Jean exclaimed. 'It's easy money. You and me can split it, fifty-fifty, so long as we get to it before anyone else does.' Polishing off the last remnants of his beer, Darius took a deep breath, then sighed it out. This wasn't the smartest thing he'd done in his life, he knew. But, if Jean's right not only about the money, but about all of it, then it was just as Jean had said. Easy money. 'Alright,' Darius nodded slowly. 'I'm in.' ~ ~ ~ On the drive to the hotel that Luc Bellemore had been staying at, Darius stared out the passenger window, caught up in reflection of events during the past couple of months. He was oblivious to Jean Guy Martell's questionable driving skills, as their car wove in and out traffic, narrowly avoiding various fender benders, navigating its way through the congested streets. His friend Cade Carson had made a decision about which wrestling promotion he would sign with, finally choosing one offer among the numerous he had received. Darius was both happy and proud for his friend, who would now get an opportunity to make a name for himself, potentially becoming a big star, and in the process, allowing him to finally escape the demons that seemed to follow him wherever he went. But what he'd yet to tell Cade was that he, too, was close to finalyzing an agreement with a wrestling promotion, based out west. Negotiations were still ongoing, but after all the Ts were crossed and Is were dotted, he'd be beginning his own wrestling career, trying to make his own name for himself. Normally, Drannen would be ecstatic. Ever since he could remember, wrestling was always something he'd wanted to pursue as a career. It's a bit cliche since everyone and their dog have been known to make the same statement, but what other job actually allows you to beat up someone else and get paid for doing it well? So the problem wasn't that Drannen was unsure about signing the contract to become a professional wrestler. The problem was, if Drannen signed the contract that he carried in his jacket pocket wherever he went, that'd mean, at least for the bulk of the week, he and Cade would be miles apart. It's been us against the world for so long, that I don't even remember what it's like to be on my own. Drannen took a deep breath and let it out slowly through his nose. He knew that negotiations would be continuing for at least for the next couple of months. That gave him two months to figure out what was the best way to break the news to Cade. ~ ~ ~ 'This wasn't one of your better ideas, Martell!' Darius said as he dived behind the overturned table, Jean a fraction of a second behind him. They flinched at the impacts against the wooden table top, which was just thick enough to shield them from the bullets being fired in their direction. Wood splintered, and tiny cracks formed in the wood where the bullets had burrowed their way almost all the way through. Jean nodded vigorously. 'Yeah, no arguments here.' Darius scowled, as he kept his head down to avoid getting it blown off. 'What now?' 'Uh,' Jean's eyes roamed the room. 'Give me a sec.' A loud explosion-like sound made both men instinctively duck. Shotgun blast. Apparently they'd brought the big guns out. 'Think fast!' Darius yelled. 'Don't rush me!' Jean responded in a high pitched, panic-filled voice. 'I'm not the one rushing you,' Darius indicated in the direction of the gunfire. 'They are.' 'Okay, okay. How about this,' Jean said, his voice making it obvious he was making it up on the fly. 'We split up and head for the car. You go that way,' he indicated a route that would lead Darius to the bathroom -- and a dead end, 'and I'll head out there,' indicating the partially opened window that lead to the balcony. Darius shot him a dirty look. 'Don't even think about it.' Jean gave him a sheepish look, shrugging. 'You got a better idea?' Darius gritted his teeth. Actually, he didn't. Scanning the room, he saw just how bad their predicament was. They were trapped deep inside Luc Bellemore's hotel room, well away from the exit to the hallway. To their left was the door that lead to the small bathroom, which had no other windows or doors. To their right was the balcony, with street level being a two-floor jump. Both options would leave them in the open for far too long. Standing in the doorway, guns blazing, were two men who Darius didn't recognize. He didn't even have a chance to get their names. As soon as they'd entered the room and caught Darius and Jean in the process of rifling through Bellemore's belongings, they'd pulled out their guns and opened fire. Darius exhaled in frustration. 'We're fucked.' 'Maybe we can just wait until they run out of ammo,' Jean offered hopefully. Darius shook his head. 'We need to act now.' If there was one thing Darius was glad for, it was the fact these two goons intent on riddling their bodies with bullets, weren't too bright. 'Once they realize we're not shooting back,' he continued, 'they'll move inside the room and finish us off at close range.' Before either of them could decide on their next course of action, they heard what sounded like two muffled shots, then the sound of two bodies slumping to the ground. Eyeing each other with confusion, each waited for the other one to tell him what had just happpened. They got their answer when they heard the sound of footsteps approaching, and a female voice with a hint of an English accent call out to them. 'You can come out from there now.' It sounded like amusement mixed with teasing when she said, 'Nobody's gonna hurt you.' Standing cautiously, Darius stood in momentary shock at the scene in front of him. The room was a mess, tables and chairs overturned, bullet holes in various pieces of furniture, and two large men with guns lying beside their prone bodies, smoking holes in their temples. And standing with a casual air, as though she were simply waiting for the bus to arrive, was a small woman in her late twenties-early thirties, staring back at him. She was dressed in a form-fitting gray business suit -- jacket and business skirt -- which did a wonderful job of accentuating her curves, and her auburn hair was pulled back in a tight bun, a few loose strands sticking out. In her right hand she held the silenced pistol that had just saved their lives, and in her left she grasped the briefcase with Bellemore's winnings. Darius noticed Jean slowly rising to his feet, his arms held up in a gesture of surrender. He scowled at him. 'Put your arms down, you idiot.' Jean did so, prompting an amused giggle from the woman. Darius turned his attention back to her. 'So, what now?' He gestured to the gun in her hand. One corner of her mouth curled up in a grin. 'Now, you boys get out of here, and pretend you were never here.' 'Just like that?' Darius asked skeptically. 'Just like that,' she responded in a cheerful tone. 'What about these two?' Darius indicated the two men with expanding pools of blood around their heads. 'Leave them to me,' she said. She waved her gun hand in the direction of the door. 'Now, go.' Jean didn't need to be asked twice. Nodding his gratitude, he made his way at a half-walk, half-run and only turned back when he noticed that Darius wasn't following him. 'Darius? You heard the woman. Let's get out of here!' Darius followed him, but at a slower pace. He kept his eyes on the woman as he passed her by. When he reached the door, he turned to her and said, 'Thanks.' She nodded in response. 'What about you?' Darius asked. Again, her lip curled back in an amused grin. 'First, I'm gonna get rid of these two. Then, I'm gonna go on a spending spree.' The money! In all the excitement, Darius had forgotten the reason they'd come to the hotel in the first place. He glanced down at the briefcase, a part of him wondering if there was any plausible scenario in which he'd be leaving the room with the briefcase full of cash in his possession. Realizing there wasn't, he nodded slowly and turned for the door. Jean followed him, the reluctance in the man's step mirroring Darius' own feelings about leaving empty handed, especially after what they'd just been through. You win some, you lose some, Darius thought with bitter amusement. He just hoped that today's bad luck wasn't a sign of things to come for him.
