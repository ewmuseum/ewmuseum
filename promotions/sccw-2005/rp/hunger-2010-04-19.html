Hunger

19 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-19-2010 I need the hunger. BAD. I need the hunger if I have any hope of passing the test this week. At Defiance, Ryan and I had the better wits and the better timing necessary to pull out the win. In Tokyo, I utilized a greater mind than those three otherworldly beasts under the guise of men. Granted, much better timing was necessary THAT week since I ended up eating a Powerbomb, but I repped my crew and we walked out victorious. But in Iraq? I have no Ryan Gallway to tag out to and no Mack Brody to hide behind. It was time to employ everything Sonny imbued into you and show people that you WEREN’T the failure you were pegged as. But how can I hone such a desire? How could I find in my soul the passion necessary to outdo Xavier Kannon, a man whose entire empire has been founded on breaking other’s necks and spewing venom like a cobra? To be more depraved than Terrance Kingsley, a man whose hobbies include playing with animals and barbed-wire neckties? To have more to prove than Jared Sykes, a man just one belt away from being SCCW’s only Grand Slam Champion, a man with nothing to lose and everything to gain and all that clichéd mumbo-jumbo? To outlast everybody else in this Battle Royale tired of being laughingstocks or looking to give their careers a swift kick in the ass? Could I, Frank Silver, actually summon up the gumption… nay, the sack to do any of this? Honestly, I couldn’t tell you. My time has been poured over two years into making The Heirs of Wrestling more than a catchy tag line. We were gradually becoming names. In this last year, our time has been divided between traveling for SCCW and for Empire Pro Wrestling, traveling between the United States and countries I can’t even pronounce. We’ve continually busted our asses to back up one-hundred percent of everything we say. But tonight, this was all on me. Do I really have it in me to do any of these things? To crush the dreams of guys like Jared Sykes? …Actually, that one’s not so bad. You could easily classify me as an asshole, but rarely am I physically vicious. Pure violence was for brutes. Trickery was for the mildly cunning and in just a few years time in this business, I’ve straight killed with mental aptitude. In Iraq, everything needed to come out of the bag. I needed to recall every last vile thing I’ve ever done if it meant becoming Adrienne St. Germain’s Chose One I need to be hungrier than guys like Matt Elias, James von Doom, Mitchell Quinlan and Legion who are probably tired of being my Twitter punchlines. I need to want it more than Jared Sykes. Most importantly, to win, I needed to be more cold-hearted than Kingsley and Kannon combined. But if I can tear the brass ring away from the hands of seven other men and claim it as my own, it would all be worth it. It’s time to be hungry. It’s time to eat. ****** Edgewater Hall Galveston, TX tSC House Show July 8th, 2008 "OW, OW, HE’S GRABBING MY HAIR, DO SOMETHING!" I did my best to appeal to the otherwise unsympathetic official, clutching a clump of my beautiful brown locks to act out Adam Gravender’s indiscretion. "ARE THERE NO RULES IN THIS RING ANYMORE, YOU BARBARIAN?!" Yeah, Patricia Grayson had a reputation as a genuine frostybox and instantly warned me not to press my luck. Pfft, whatever. My conscience was Teflon and her judgment wouldn’t stick to it. Totally unrelated sidebar: MAN, she had some sweet cans. I would gladly do her and never call her again, were she not Coral Avalon’s sloppy seconds. Eyes having locked onto my target, I barged right past Grayson and towards Adam Gravender. A future title match against Steve Knox was up for grabs and I’d be damned if some goody-two-shoes Boy Scout got to parade around with it. He was stunned, the sharp burn of an eye rake searing his nerves as I rolled him back into the ring. "Check this out, Sugartits," I snarled back at the referee as I slammed a knee right in between Gravender’s pretty-boy mug. Because I was awesome like that, I stared straight into a group of kids specifically holding up "Adam Gravender Rules!" signs, hoping my smugness would burn into their young, impressionable minds. "One!" she screamed while I grabbed a far leg, keeping a triumphant pose. This was MINE to hold on this night. "Two!" Annnnndddd… "THRE-" I shut my eyes, waiting to hear the beautiful hand of hand hitting canvas a third time. The third time never came. Instead, the crowd exploded, watching Grayson break out of her sickened autopilot just in time to keep her hand from striking the mat unjustly. "Two" she declared for an elated crowd, conducting music to their ears. If it wasn’t the sound of my entrance music playing my victory, it was just noise pollution. My eyes grew wider as Adam crawled over to his stomach, refusing to yield. He’d been somebody that tSC fans had kept their eye on, waiting for that big break-out moment. "No…" There was no way this cocky little shit was robbing me blindly. While the groggy Gravender remained stranded on a knee, I pounced, ready to take my pound of flesh, raining down endless blows to the back I’d worked over all match. "IT’S ALL MINE!" I screamed for all within earshot. "IT’S ALL MINE!" I was clouded in a red mist, having to be forcefully dragged off of Adam, but not before I tried to fuck up his back a little more, throwing a couple punt kicks for good measure. I wouldn’t be caught dead hitting a lady, but Grayson was quickly making me rethink my stance on that subject. "YOU NEED TO KEEP YOUR FILTHY, STD-INFESTED MITTS OFF ME!" I seethed, still riding the roller coaster’s apex of an adrenaline high. "DO YOU NOT KNOW WHO THE FUCK I AM? I AM FRANK PIERCE! SONNY SILVER’S PRODIGY. THAT FRANK PIERCE! I’M THE CLOSEST THING TO ROYALTY THIS FUCKING PLACE HAS!" "You’re a douche, that’s who you are," she fired back with a much more calm, dry tone. "BITCH, IF YOU GET IN MY WAY AGAIN, I’M GOING TO-" I had no idea where any of this was coming from, but I was frothing at the mouth. Quickly biting my tongue, I took a moment to focus on the task at hand. Unfortunately, I saw said task at hand crash right into my peripheral vision. The next thing I remembered was leather boot greeting ribcage and the sound of guttural breathing in the air. The next thing I knew, the crowd of fifteen hundred bloodthirsty fans spun all round me, then they shot upwards. Then the funniest thing happened: the back of my head whiplashed against the mat, almost snapping my neck in two. The throbbing continued to pulse in tune with the "Gravender" chants that echoed throughout the tiny, yet packed venue. Next thing I knew, something dark and fuzzy passed across the arena lights high above. Before I could register anything more, my lungs had been robbed of precious oxygen with an elbow being driven dead-center in my chest. I felt a body on mine, trying to keep me and my dreams of success restrained. "ONE!" "TWO!" "THR- OHHHHHH!" No, no, no, it was going to happen. Not like that. A pair of musky, sweaty hands tore at my hair, but that trick thumb acted up, catching Gravender right in the eye socket. Staggering up and over to my prey, I couldn’t help but break out into a hysterical fit at the sight of his woes. Everything that Sonny taught me, everything that he’d imparted into me these last eighteen months was going to pay off. I knew how to work this crowd and I had them all in the palm of my hands. It was amazing. With a handful of Gravender’s blonde locks, I tugged him into a front chancery and clamped his neck to sustain the advantage. I locked him up and- Al I could hear was a crowd screaming. Before I knew it, I felt the tug of Gravender pull me back body by the back of the head. The crowd couldn’t possibly see it after he hit the move, but in between our own labored breathing and the adulation of the crowd, his next words were the antithesis of the brave face he put on for the crowd. "You don’t belong in my ring, you fucking orphan." Those words rattled through my brain, not really sure I’d heard correctly. But by the smug look he shot me before keeling over and taking in the sympathy had me heated. Gravender seethed between pants before he started to climb the top rope. He was trying to finish me off with that stupid Moonsault finisher and put the nail into the coffin that was my dreams of success. Despite his reputation amongst the fans for being one of The Squared Circle’s premier attractions, nobody got to see the real side of him. He was a worthless waste of human life that kindly shook your hand then talked shit about you as you went about your business. He didn’t give two shits about any of these fans, all they did for him was give him his paycheck. Autograph sessions were a hassle and a chore for him. I decided right then and there I didn’t have to take this, not from anybody. I’d show him where I belonged. As he finally made his way up to the top of that rope, he prepared to pop off a move that he’d hit hundreds of times in his storied career. A move he’d used to perfection; one he’d savored the very reaction of the crowd with. He prepared to ju-NO. Not tonight. I knew he could feel my hands wrapping around his body. I knew he could feel my body leap back, taking him with me from ten feet in the air. I KNEW he could feel his vertebrae chipping up and clashing against one another. I knew that for him, the lights were out. ****** Greatwood, TX Harvard Avalon Wrestling Academy January 8th, 2010 Now why would I, a jet-setting twenty-three-year-old with money stacks counted by inches want anything to do with some backwoods shithole like this place? Well, because to me, it was where Frank Pierce stopped being a young child with stars in his eyes and became… you know… FRANK PIERCE. More importantly, this was the very place the Heirs of Wrestling were founded. The long and the short of it was I downright LOATHED Greatwood. But this place – this specific training facility – it was different to me. It was a lot more than just some dingy old piece of shit that would give somebody tetanus just by sitting on the dilapidated bench outside the doors. This was the only place that took a chance on me after I gave Tokyo PRO the old heave-ho (or they did to me, but this is MY story. Eff you.) This, along with the shows it ran under The Squared Circle banner, laid the groundwork for life I now led. The facility opened shortly after the death of tSC founder, Harvard Avalon, as a haven for people from all walks of life that wanted to enter the tumultuous world we lived. Right now, it was little more than an inconvenience. "Fucking hot," I groused to Ryan, stepping out of the cab and into the blistering dry heat. With my duffel bag slung over my shoulder, I couldn’t help but feel surlier than usual. With every step, I could feel my own displeasure grow as I approached the building. Behind me, Ryan was glossing over his iPhone, probably beating Mack up in Scrabble. I tried it out once. Gave up, though. I had to try and explain to Ryan that "Aurturbur" was indeed, NOT a space monster he’d slain on planet Zanzibar or some shit, therefore not a word. You ever done that? It’s like trying to explain Calculus to a child with Down Syndrome. So, pretty much, James von Doom. Don’t get me wrong. I’d enjoyed my time with tSC. But what I didn’t have a fervor for was stepping back here at this school. It reminded me of times when I was nobody. Back then, I was a kid with a stupid pipe dream. A kid with a stupid pipe dream and skills, yes, but a kid still. No importance to anybody. It’s downright amazing what changes I’ve gone through in two years time, but I can’t imagine just being another face in the crowd, another moron traipsing about with everybody else. My entire life now was designed to stand out. My suits were custom-tailored, each one designed to every specification in my favorite shades of blue and black. My Gucci shoes complemented my outfits and each one of those motherfuckers were polished within an inch of their lives. Frank Pierce and Ryan Gallway were not unmemorable schlubs walking the streets of some dive in Texas. We were THE stories, the men that mattered. And that’s what, ultimately, brought us back here to our proverbial roots. The last year, we’ve had the tSC Tag Team Championships and they haven’t done shit about it. We had some commitments to finish up, but hell if we were going to lose the straps to whatever morons they were going to throw at us. Kiddies out of training to some big, steroid-jacked assbags. They could line them up and we’d knock them down. Grudgingly, I raised my right hand and knocked on the front door. Moments passed and nobody came. The wait was excruciating. For Ryan, this was cake. He’d get to show off the flippity-floppy shit he was good at and put on a clinic. Me? This was hell. As I considered excuses to recant this entire trip and turn right around, the door opened slowly. The blonde locks, ones I’d pulled on. The blue eyes, ones I’d poked in the past. Oh, I knew that face. God, deity of choice, anybody but him… "Ryan?" he spoke up, glancing down at Ryan. "You look well! It’s been a couple years." Then, of course, he had to look up right at me. "Oh. Hello." I fought valiantly to prevent the forming scowl from overtaking my face, but as he glanced up at me, I couldn’t help but feel my stomach doing more Barrel Rolls than the Blue Angels. "…Hey, Adam." Adam Gravender, once upon a time, considered to be one of tSC’s kingpins until some fuckface ended his career. Rumors were abound that the Avalons gave him a job as one of the head trainers after he recovered from his broken neck. "Hello, Frank," he replied with a simple, terse response. Quickly changing the subject, he turned to Ryan. "I keep hearing good things about you two. It’ll get great to see you both in action. Follow me and I’ll show you around." Adam led the way into the building. Ryan followed shortly behind with me at the rear, keeping a distance. I couldn’t help but find myself appraising the man. For the most part, he looked the same as he had those few years ago. Still well put together. Still walking with the air of dignity that identified him as Adam Gravender. Away from Adam’s ears, Ryan looked back at me. "Hey, Frank, that was old Adam, right?" he inquired. "Yep. THAT Adam. Left not long before you came aboard." I gave Ryan the skinny on Adam some time ago, so there was no need go into further detail. No need to drag this turgid welcome out any longer than it had to. "Boy’s taking care of himself." "Yup." "Where are you two competing nowadays?" Adam called over the shoulder. "Last I heard, you guys were with EPW or some place, yes?" I tried to force the bile down my throat, but thank the Lord, Ryan responded. The only time you’ll ever hear me use those exact words in that context. "Yeah, Empire Pro Wrestling, "Ego Buster" Dan Ryan runs the shit. Legend around these parts, too. It’s a little more sports entertain-y than I’m accustomed to. VERY different from Tokyo PRO and tSC, but it pays the bills, so we’re not complaining. We start with Sin City Championship Wrestling here in about a week, too, and they’re going on a World Tour. That’ll kick ass." "Nice, nice." He chuckled a low, rich laugh, then realized he’d have to acknowledge my presence as well. "Frank, I’d heard you recently lost your uncle. My condolences." "Thanks," I belted. I was directing that mainly at my uncle Leo, who left me twenty million reasons to thank him. The three of us remained silent before we turned a corned and began down the hallway that led to the ring where we would start our sparring sessions. Once inside said ring, I realized just how much… well, nothing changed. Aside from the walls. They were a more horrible off-white than before. "Brand new paint job," Adam said, giving us the grand tour as he gestured around the gym. "…And yeah, that’s about it." He chuckled at his own joke, to which Ryan and I replied in kind with half-hearted, awkward smiles. "We’re hoping the next few shows rake in some cash. tSC’s kind of hurting these days. Attendance hasn’t been so good since Steve Knox, Coral Avalon, Inoue Doi and all of the favorites the crowd came to see took off for greener pastures… or more insane, in Steve Knox’s case." "Dude, I’m gonna go put my stuff away, I’ll be right back," Ryan nodded. Taking off down the hallway towards the locker room, this left me with the unfeasible task of being left alone with a man I’d injured close to two years ago. The question that had been on my mind since this trip was book could no longer go unasked. "So, Adam…" And here came the words. "How’s your neck?" I barely forced out, casting my eyes downward at the floor. No need to see what reaction that was going to bring up. With a quiet sigh, Adam looked right back up at me. "It’s… fine, actually. See, one of the interesting things about the line of work is when injuries happen that end our careers, you tend to have some extra time on your hands." "Yeah?" "Yeah. Life, success… karma. I put you through lots of unspeakable bullshit back in the day. I’ll be honest… back then, I was a different person. I put all the new kids, you especially, through the wringer to see if their hearts were really in all of this. But after I got dumped on my head, I had time to reflect. Insurance covered the medical bills, Rosemary hired me back on after my recovery to look after the day-to-day affairs of the school. I even help to teach the trainees on occasion. That’s why I keep myself in ring shape." I had no idea what to say to him. Sheepishly, I spoke out. "I honestly don’t think I’ve done a superplex of any kind since that day." "Hey, no need to apologize. It was the heat of battle. Accidents happen, Frank. If anything, I should apologize to you. As I said, I was a different person back then. I hated the sport because it took time away from my family, but it’s how I paid the bills. I took that out guys like you... I hazed you and other kids. Took their shit while they showered and locked their belongings outside. Left them with the tab after we all went out for after-show drinks. I think I may have pissed in somebody’s bag once, too." MY bag, you asshole. "Yeah," I replied, voice harsh and starting to remember all the reasons I hated this prick. "It happens, especially with the style that we teach here. That’s the great thing about the past. You learn from it and move on. It is what it is. I’ve moved on and so should you." I fell silent for a few moments, thinking about exactly that. I couldn’t help but focus in on the walls around me. Everything about this place… I hated. I hated it ALL. I was nothing here. I was nobody, treated like nobody, forced by pricks like him to BE nobody. I turned my attention back to him and there he was, smiling back at me, putting a hand out. That lame sympathetic look on his face. "Deal?" I looked down, unsure of whether I could say ‘deal’ to the man front of me. I ended this guy’s career because I was pissed he treated like the dirt beneath his shoes. He didn’t give me time of day back then, and when I saw that he was going to shake my hand, I panicked. Before I knew it, fist met face and Adam Gravender was sent flying backwards, hitting the canvas with a dull thud. As he stared up in bewilderment, clutching his jaw and wondering what the hell just took place, I saw Ryan dart into the gym. "DUDE, WHAT HAPPENED?!" He blurted out, duffel bag still in hand. I couldn’t be a party to this place any longer. One thing that Adam said rang true. The past was the past. I had no reason to sit here any longer. I wanted the life I had now. I wanted all of it. Flashbulbs, limousines, championships. This life was officially over for me, now and forever. I picked up my bags, but not before I left one bit of parting wisdom to Adam. "You don’t belong in my ring, you fucking hack." I tossed my half of the tSC Tag Team Championships right as his feet before trudging away, eager to have the last word. Ryan tried to keep up with my angered gait, but by the time he could, we were both out of that dingy little gymnasium. Subsequently, I explained to him that this was for the best. What happened there was bullshit where we weren’t subject to the scrutiny of others. We weren’t in danger of being hazed by a bunch of locker room watchdogs trying to protect their spot. Like him, I yearned for bigger and better things. No. Now, I was hungry for them.
