(This One) The Beginning to an End

undated

Date: Before I. PRECINCT EIGHT Detective Landry tossed the manila folder onto his oak desk, where it skidded to a halt. He ran a hand over his weary eyes, his thumb and index finger pinching the bridge of his nose as he exhaled deeply. He’d been at the precinct since six, a solid two hours before anybody else had arrived. With the Roger Dodd case fresh in his mind, he’d decided to revisit a few old files and see if anything would fall together. Nothing ever did, or at least it never seemed to. Even ten tumultuous years on the force hadn’t been enough to wear down the Desert Storm veteran and, despite the gray specks emerging in his tightly trimmed hair, another ten or twenty appeared possible. The detective plopped into the leather swivel chair in front of his desk and, with an outstretched arm, pulled himself in tighter to the desk. He slid the manila folder close and flipped it open. What he found staring back at him was a man, or more like monster, that he despised with every inch in his body. The black and white photo was of Sal Esposito as he left a New Orleans night club. There were dozens of photos clipped inside the folder and each one showed off his lifeless, dark eyes. Whether it was a photograph or in-person, his eyes always looked the same. The eyes of a cold blooded, calculating murderer, who had a chokehold on New Orleans. Buried feelings swirled through Landry’s thoughts. How fulfilling it would feel to wrap his hands around Esposito’s throat and slowly choke the life out of him. Landry’s fists, knuckles already turning pale, grew tighter and tighter. He’d look at up at him with those dark eyes, mouth open, and lungs gasping for air, but no relief would come. His lifeless eyes would quickly become just that and his serpent tongue would remain motionless. No more Sal Esposito meant a better life in New Orleans. Bad memories would be buried along with that heartless prick. Landry’s trance was broken by three raps on his glass office door. "Come in," Landry announced, his attention snapping toward the door as he closed the folder and pushed it away. The door swung open and in walked a younger Caucasian man, a badge and sidearm hanging from the brown belt holding up his blue jeans. His attire was much more casual than Landry’s slacks and tie. The young dark-haired detective ran a hand through his shaggy hair before addressing Landry with a shake of his head. "Let me guess, partner, you’ve been here since seven?" "Six, actually," Landry responded, throwing a wink at his younger colleague. "Anything new on the Dodd case, Messana, or are we a bit too hungover to work today?" Messana, hands on his belly, feigned a hearty laugh. "Weeell, both. Officers went on a call to the Magnolia Housing Projects early this morning. Just so happens that they ran into Roger Dodd. Some break, huh? Anyhow, I figured we should head down there and take a look." "And how is Mr. Dodd?" Landry asked with raise eyebrows. "Sorry to say, the officers weren’t the first guys to run into him apparently," Messana said solemnly. "Like I said, I figure we better run down there. Mrs. Dodd and her sister-in-law are going to identify the body in the next few hours. Sounds like Mrs. Dodd was pretty hysterical when she heard the news. Guy had a great job, wife, kid … why he’d get into gambling and shady company is beyond me." Messana shook his head, his eyes scanning Landry’s reaction. Landry nodded along with his greenhorn partner. "Agreed, damn shame his family has to suffer," Landry said, shaking his head. "Give me a second to tie up a few loose ends and I’ll meet you outside." "Sure thing, Jonah." Detective Messana turned around and exited the office, leaving his partner to privacy. In truth, Landry didn’t have any loose ends to tie up. He’d put on a façade for his partner, but his armor was only so thick. When you’re a detective, you try to mask your emotions as best as possible. There was no need to let Messana in on his personal history, which would only open up Pandora’s Box. Landry reopened the folder on his desk to take one last look at Esposito. With a burning anger building in his abdomen, he leaned back into his chair and took a deep breath. "I’ll see you soon, Sally." II. DON'T KILL THE MESSENGER "Goddamnit!" Tommy Campanelli didn’t know what was worse, watching a mafia hitman being plugged in the head or an angry Sal Esposito slamming a vase into the wall mere inches from his head. Tommy’s wide eyes watched his boss, or everybody’s boss for that matter, pace back and forth in front of him. Both men were shaking uncontrollably – Tommy C shook in fear and Sal, red in the face, was shaking like an angry bull. Esposito, eyes locked on the floor, finally stopped pacing. For a man that’d just been pulled out of bed at two in the morning, Esposito had revved to life rather quickly. Zero to red lining in a matter of seconds, in a matter of words. He had still been in his boxers as he rumbled down the stairs with his black silk robe floating behind. First, he’d been pissed that Tommy C would dare impose on him at this early hour, but that took a backseat to what he heard next. The words "Carl Santagata is dead" had thrown Esposito in a rage. He’d gone from mildly upset to using Tommy C as vase target practice in the blink of an eye. Campanelli had heard stories about Esposito, but he’d never seen anything up close and personal. He assumed this would be the last time, one way or the other, he ever witnessed Esposito’s temper. "I’m not mad at you, Tommy," Sal said through rapid breaths. Tommy C nodded his head furiously, not an ounce of him believing his boss’ words. Esposito continued, "I just want you to tell me how the FUCK my good friend, Carl Santagata, died." His cold eyes locked onto Tommy, who shifted back and forth uncomfortably. "Some bum shot him, Mr. Esposito." Sal scowled at the younger Italian. "Some bum shot him? That ain’t enough, Tommy. I want to know what the fuck happened, where the fuck you were, and why the fuck you were there." Esposito was growing impatient and the tone in his voice made it clear. "We were down in the Magnolia Projects because somebody called Mickey Scalese and said they’d seen that Roger Dodd down there. We get a room number and everything. So I meet Santagata down there, just to be an extra set of eyes. He tells me to wait in the car and he heads over to this room. Out of nowhere, just when he’s about to kick this door down, this fuckin’ bum or crackhead, whatever the fuck he was, pulls a piece on Santagata. He fuckin’ shoots him … shoots him right in the head. I’ve never seen somebody pull a gun that fast … it was too fast." As Campanelli’s gaze fell upon the floor, Sal absorbed the information he’d been given. For Esposito, being the head of any organization meant having a plethora of conversations with employees and he vaguely remembered Tommy C mentioning somebody owed Scalese big. It was hardly worth losing a lifelong friend and a damn good worker like Carl Santagata over. "You get a look at the shooter?" Esposito inquired. "Too dark," Tommy C muttered, shaking his head ‘no’ as well. Esposito moved closer to the flustered youngster, resting a thick hand on his shoulder. "I gotta ask, kid. What’d you do when this joker shot Carl?" Tommy’s nerves were on full display as he looked into Esposito’s dark, unforgiving eyes. "I got outta there and called Rizzo-," Campanelli answered, but found himself cut off by the boss. "You got outta there? My friend Carl gets shot in the head … and you just tuck tail and ran?" "You didn’t see this guy, Mr. Esposito. He woulda done me, too," Tommy shrilled. Tommy Campanelli didn’t see Esposito’s free hand until it was too late. The fist hit him in the nose and, with the help of Esposito’s hand on the shoulder, sent Tommy C to the floor. "You chickenshit fuck! I oughta kill you myself! You don’t run away when somebody in your family gets shot … you go kill the prick that did it," Esposito roared at the youngster, who had blood trickling from his nose. "Get up, Tommy," Sal ordered, and his demands were immediately met by a scrambling Campanelli. "Call Rizzo and my brother and tell them to meet me at the club around eight. Go over to Scalese’s tomorrow and tell that little bastard I want to see him ASAP. Now, get the fuck outta my sight before I decide you should join Carl Santagata," Esposito finished, his index and middle finger "crossing" at the mention of the departed. Tommy C scurried out the door as quickly as his legs would carry him and closed the front door behind him. Sal took a deep breath and rubbed his eyes with his palms. The mafia boss staggered over to the stairs and headed back to bed. He’d sleep, much like his childhood friend Carl Santagata slept, only less permanent, and then he’d meet with the brain trust in the morning. Somebody was going to pay for Santagata’s blood; the important question was just who that somebody was. III. MAGNOLIA HOUSING PROJECT - CRIME SCENE The Magnolia Housing Project, or more specifically the crime scene, looked more like a circus than a police organized lockdown. New Orleans media outlets had gotten a whiff of the double homicide and were crawling around the yellow police tape. Crackheads and curious bystanders watched on with equal interest. It felt like all of New Orleans had come out for the festivities. Landry and Messana popped out of their silver Ford Crown Victoria and slipped underneath the yellow barrier. An approaching uniform, an African-American man, was met with two mechanical badge flashes, something the two had perfected over time. "This is nuts," the officer commented. The partners were less concerned about the officer’s analysis and more interested in checking out the crime scene. They moved past the uniform and immediately began scoping out the surroundings. After spotting the body outside the room, Landry and Messana walked over toward the deceased, resting inside a black body bag. Landry knelt down and unzipped the top to reveal the occupant. Landry, thoroughly surprised, looked up at his partner. "This isn’t Roger Dodd," he stated bluntly. He looked back down at the lifeless man with a bullet hole through his forehead. Roger Dodd was overweight and balding. This guy had thick black hair, a hard jaw, and was fit as a fiddle. While the deceased looked familiar to Landry, he most certainly wasn’t the hard luck accountant. Messana shrugged at his partner, who was mildly amused by his younger colleague. "Hey! Hey buddy!" Messana called out to the officer that they’d originally blown past. As the black officer approached, Messana continued, "Who is this guy? And where is Roger Dodd?" "There’s another body in that room," the officer replied, pointing toward Room 9. "I don’t know anything about either victim … just working crowd control, boys. Detective O’Neill is in with the other body, ask him any questions you might have." Landry zipped up the bag and headed toward the hotel room with Messana following closely behind. There were half dozen others inside the room - two had cameras and others stood in a group around the body, which was in an unzipped body bag. The body was indeed that of Roger Dodd. The former accountant had been shot in the chest once, unlike the other victim who had been popped in the head. Landry confidently walked toward the small group of chatting policemen and interrupted the conversation. "Which one of you is O’Neill?" he asked. A man, two or three inches shorter than the six-foot Landry, stepped forward. His expression was all-business and, despite his short frame, the man carried himself with much physical confidence. "I’m Detective O’Neill, and who might you be?" "Detective Landry ... this is my partner Detective Messana," Landry answered, his right hand showcasing his partner. "Mind bringing us up to speed, Detective? We know this is Roger Dodd, but who is the other stiff?" Landry was a real bulldog, but a little professional courtesy could get you far. O’Neill was less than impressed, but wore a false smile anyhow. "The name’s Carl Santagata." Landry responded with a nod. He knew he recognized that face, but couldn’t put his finger on it. O’Neill continued, "Santagata is a well-known Mafioso and we found a gun near his body. From what I can tell, it looks like Roger Dodd got himself into a sticky situation and Santagata was going to teach him a very fatal lesson." Landry and Messana nodded along, but Messana had a question. "So what … Santagata shoots Dodd and then takes a bullet, too? Doesn’t make sense." Landry shot his partner a "That’s what I was going to say" glance. "I don’t think Santagata shot Dodd. Santagata’s gun was fully loaded and he was turned away from the door. Looks like somebody shot him from outside before he ever went in. As for Roger Dodd, he was arranged quite peacefully. Clean death, arms crossed over his chest. I doubt Santagata gave a shit how Dodd was left. We’ll have a better idea what happened when we get the tests back on the rounds discovered in the bodies." "So there was another shooter," Messana said to nobody in particular. O’Neill nodded. "That’s what it looks like. I’m all finished here, fellas, but go ahead and take a look around." O’Neill turned and left the room as Messana and Landry huddled near the body of Roger Dodd. The half dozen room occupants were gone, leaving the room to the late arriving detectives. "What do you think, Jonah?" "Dodd owed big … Santagata was sent to kill him." "By Sal Esposito, you think?" "Probably," Landry quickly replied with a grimace. "Who shoots Santagata then? And, if Santagata didn’t shoot Dodd, who did?" "Russians?" Landry’s reply was more a question than an answer. After seeing his partner not ready to answer, Landry continued, "Could have been Russians, maybe Cubans, but how do they find out about Dodd and why kill him? Probably Russians." The detective paced around the room, his right hand rubbing his chin. Messana, mulling over his partner’s queries, replied, "No clue, maybe he saw too much, but what I do know is that Esposito is going to retaliate. New Orleans will turn into a fucking warzone if Esposito responds like we would expect. The guy’s a ruthless animal." "Yes, yes he is," Landry agreed, that feeling of anger building up again in his stomach. IV. NEW RUSSIA Word traveled quickly in New Orleans, which meant that Alexander Romanov IV had heard about the Santagata shooting before the bodies were cold. Romanov, his long black hair secured in a ponytail that fell between his shoulder blades, was a peaceful looking man. He occupied a plush leather chair while he drank his morning coffee, a routine he followed each morning. However, on this morning, he was joined in his study by his right hand man, Maksim Grigorovich. Maksim was tall and slender, clean shaven and his hair razor short. His wardrobe consisted of a black suit; in fact, the man rarely wore anything that wasn’t black. Romanov, legs now uncrossed, placed his coffee cup on the table next to the leather chair. "Maksim, dis shooting in Magnolia is no good for anybody." Maksim closed his eyes and nodded in agreement. "You must tell me … we had nothing to do with dis, right?" Romanov searched Maksim for an answer. "Nothing, boss. We’re following da plan dat you laid out." Silence hung in the hang between the right hand man and his boss. As his boss massaged his temples, Maksim cleared his throat uneasily and looked around the immaculate study. There was a large desk in front of a large landscape-style window, and several bookcases lined the walls. "How did dis happen?" groaned Romanov, index and middle fingers still massaging his temples. "I don’t know. Vhat do you tink Esposito will do?" Alexander Romanov IV’s eyes instantly shot up, his fingers still pressed against his temples. "The man has no civility, Maksim. He’s a Neanderthal with many guns. He’ll go after somebody, but who I don’t know. Dis Santagata was a big player in the Esposito Family, which means that Salvatore Esposito is currently in a rage. He’ll strike back quickly, and he’ll make his shots count." "Den we attack first!" Maksim shouted, slamming his fist into his leg. Romanov shook his head. "You surely have a war on your hands then. We must meet with Esposito and try to make sense of this." "You just said yourself dat he’s a Neanderthal … I doubt he’d meet with us," Maksim argued. "It’s worth a try, Maksim. Better dan a full scale war breaking out in New Orleans. Perhaps, he’d think logically. Combine our numbers to figure out what happened. We can both prosper in New Orleans … it’s possible." Maksim relented with a nod. "I’ll get something setup. I just want you to know dat I don’t trust dis Esposito. ‘is temper is predictable, but it makes ‘im a very unpredictable man." A knock on the study door captured both men’s attention and the door was pushed open without any approval or acknowledgement. It was Serena Romanov, Alexander’s daughter, who slipped between the opening and into the study. Alexander greeted his seventeen year-old with a loving grin as she walked toward him with purpose and plopped down into his lap. She was a beautiful young lady with dark, curly hair and a slender frame. "Busy?" she chirped, planting a kiss on her father’s cheek. "Never," he replied warmly. Serena beamed at her father. "I just wanted to let you know that I’m going to the mall with Allie and Lisa today." "And you’ll be back when?" "Later," she replied with a smirk. Being daddy’s little girl had perks and getting away with what she wanted was one of those perks. Serena hopped off her father’s lap, gave him another kiss on the cheek, and waved goodbye to Maksim, who returned a half-hearted wave. As his daughter headed for the doorway, Alexander watched her closely until she was gone and turned back to Maksim. "You’re right about one thing, Maksim. Esposito is an unpredictable man," Romanov stated as he looked out the study window. "Send two men to watch Serena when she goes to the mall. If Esposito retaliates before we meet with him, he would try to hurt those closest to me." Maksim nodded emphatically. "Good idea. I’ll put Vassily and Sergei on it." "Thank you," Alexander responded as he stood and patted Maksim on the shoulder. He strolled toward the landscape window that overlooked his vast property with much more trouble than he had started the morning with. Maksim retired and left his boss to his thoughts. V. SAINT OF ... As darkness fell upon New Orleans, the man in the black fedora settled in for the night. He owned no home of his own, but had been staying with a hooker named Jennifer for several months. The apartment was small, but nice enough for a working girl. Jennifer, dressed in short athletic shorts and a white tank top, sat on a couch watching late night news on a New Orleans affiliate. The top story was still two murders in the Magnolia Housing Project. She pointed the remote at the television set, shut the unit down, and turned toward the kitchen, where Saint sat at the island table. Jennifer, a fair looking girl, ran a hand through her wavy blonde hair. "The news said both those guys had families. Even showed Dodd’s wife and son on there … they looked real broken up." "They’re better off." It was the blonde hair that always set it off. She had blonde hair, too. Even now, he could see her face with those curly blonde locks falling around a defined jaw line. A bright New Orleans sun radiating behind her, but her smile was what truly shone. He remembered when he first met her, when he’d fallen head over heels for her, and the day he married her. The best days of his life. "Do you think it’s fair for you to decide?" she asked. Saint looked down, his black fedora completely covering his face. "To decide who lives and who dies?" "No, to decide who loses their families." "I didn’t get to choose when it mattered most … I’m making up for lost chances." His stunning wife, the sun still over her shoulder, smiled as their younger daughter ran into her arms. The burning sun quickly engulfs the wife and daughter, replacing them with the image of a burning car flipping through the air. The car comes to a violent halt, slamming to the street below. "You know I understand, but does that really make it right?" Saint stood up, his eyes focused on Jennifer as he strolled over to the couch. Two gravestones, side by side. They disappear into a burning flame. "It’s not about right or wrong anymore. It’s about revenge … justice … personal satisfaction." "I just don’t –," she paused; they’d had this conversation before. "I just don’t understand how you can take on swarms and swarms of men. They’re going to come after you with everything they have." "I’m stronger on my own," he replied, sitting down beside her. "That doesn’t make any sense." Again, the image of his wife and daughter burn through his thoughts. Two, gorgeous gals who meant the world to them. "They have families. Family is their weakness. I’ll do to them … what they did to me."
