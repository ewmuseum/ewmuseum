The Inevitable 2: Oh, Grand Confusion, Lend Me Your Ears!

26 September 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Whenever. I woke up in a field of tall grass, buried in a blanket of weeds and dead leaves. My head hurt. My body felt sore. My mind felt vacant. The sun was just beginning to rise up over the horizon, piercing the shadows with rays of light. As I stood up, I wondered out loud, “Where the hell am I?” I couldn’t remember anything about where I was or how I got there. Looking down, I noticed my clothes were splattered with dried blood, some my own and some not. How long had I been here? What happened? In one hand, my fingers still clutched tight to a small handgun, jet black and shining in the morning light. With my finger on the trigger, my wrist tense, I held it outward after a few moments and tried to fire it off, only to hear the click of the empty chamber. I dropped the gun to the ground and began to step forward, only to hear a faint coughing a few feet away. A few steps later, I came to find Him laying on the ground, coughing up blood with six bullet holes in His chest. He grinned up at me as I stared down at Him, more confused now than I had been since the beginning of all this. With those yellow teeth, that foul grin, His hair matted to His forehead by blood, He whispered out in between gasps and coughs, “Morning, Sean. Sleep well?” And that’s when I saw the gun in His hand, pointing straight up toward me. --- Foul Thoughts and Terrible Musings Heaven is a tourist trap. People get distracted by the idea of it, so their entire life gets set off course in an attempt to make it there. They waste a half a tank of gas only to find out it really wasn’t all that special and God is really just some backwoods hick with a good way of making business. By the time you load yourself back into the car, you realize it’s already too late to get to your real destination. You’ve wasted an entire life on something that was too good to be true. Good for you. I decided at an early age that I wasn’t going to bother with religion or God or whatever. I decided from an early age that I was just going to enjoy life and all it’s evils. Do what I went when I want, selfish or otherwise. I figured, this was the only life I was ever going to have. Why waste it on some ancient doctrine that makes little to no sense? Sometimes, though, late at night, when I’m trying to get some sleep, but insomnia is getting the best of me, my mind wanders and I begin to think too much into the possibility that maybe I’m wrong. Maybe I’m the one who has wasted half my life on the stubborn idea that there is no such thing as God. Maybe when I die, I’m going to get to heaven and be greeted with a chorus of laughter before I get sent plummeting down into the Hells below. Thing is, and I’ve just started to realize this, I’m horrified by the thought of dying. I’m horrified by the thought that there may be an afterlife. Theologians don’t know nothing about my soul, but I don’t know much about it either, so I guess we’re even. Here’s hoping I didn’t put my entire fortune on the last horse around the track. It’s funny. You never really think about things like this until you have a man pointing a gun at you. --- My eyes went wide, my body went still, my mind stopped working. All the cogs stopped turning With my mouth gaping open and my hands raising into the air, I stared down the nose of the pistol. He grinned wide from behind the chamber, propping Himself up on one arm as stared up at me, both of us silent, if only for the moment. As birds sat on their birch boughs and insects scurried in the dirt below, I prepared for death as best as anybody could. When He spoke, His voice sounded like a broken accordion. He wrestled with himself to force the words out from His mouth. In between bloody coughs, he told me in a whispery voice, “So, you thought you could betray me and get away with it, eh, Sean? Thought I’d get knocked off that easily? Well, let me put it to you like this: Get fucked, Sean.” Still and quiet, I couldn’t muster up the nerve to think of a reply. What was I doing here? How did I get here? What had happened? He said, “If I’m going to die, I’m taking you with me.” And that’s when the bullet came driving through the air, aimed straight for me. How did I get here? --- The Night Before My breath was laced with Listerine, my head crowded with a thousand insignificant thoughts. He had teeth like God’s shoeshine, grinning wide to show them off as he stamped his feet on the wooden boards, sputtering out nonsense from behind a baby blue acoustic guitar. He was a waste of time with a cowboy hat on top of his head., dressed in pleather and speaking with a fake accent in between songs. He was just giving the crowd what they wanted: A stereotype, to point at and sing along with. Bars are burdens on nights like these. Why I even bother to come to them is beyond my comprehension. They unhook the jukebox so and push some sorry sap on stage. They call it entertainment, I call it torture. With that yokel voice sing-songing it’s way to my last nerve, I ordered up another bottle of beer to down my gullet. My uncle was a drunkard and he used to tell me when I was a kid, “Boy, the best way to bury your burdens, be it a broken heart or a dead dog, is to drink, drink, drink until you can’t drink no more!” Words to fucking live by, eh? So here I sat, in some hole in wall bar in Pittsburgh, drowning my sorrows as per usual, wasting away my whole wallet on warm beer and cheap liquor. What a life, what a life. The walls were decorated with memorabilia from one of the local colleges. A pinball machine sat lonely in the corner while the roughnecks stood around pool tables, smoking and drinking and laugh-laugh-laughing with a group of insidious bar whores. The type of girls who never outgrew the eighties. With big hair and too much makeup, terrible fashion sense and Valley Girl accents. My cell phone had been ringing off the hook with people trying to figure out where I was. People calling to tell me about my parent’s untimely death, not knowing I was there when it happened. People calling to see if I made it to Pittsburgh alright. And, of course, He was calling to rant and rave about going back in time to kill my parents. Needless to say, I didn’t answer any of the calls. It’s times like these it’s best to be by yourself. Or surrounded by strangers who couldn‘t give a shit less about you. Take your pick. This wavy haired angel was whispering in my ear with a syllable lisp, but I acted like she wasn’t that. She eventually walked away with a weird look on her face, went off to her friends. I heard her whispering a few feet away to some half-assed hussy, saying, “What a weirdo.” I couldn’t agree more. Most these people are the products of a short love with a long divorce. Most of them hate their parents. Most of them are failures in everything they do. Most of them aren’t who they want to be and, in all likelihood, will never be. So they come to these sleazy bars and try to make themselves feel better by surrounding themselves with people who are bigger losers than they are. I’m no exception to this. Light another cigarette. Down another bottle of beer. At least I’m better off than most of these mother fuckers. Right? --- An hour later… These streets are wrought with diseased old prostitutes, turning out the same old tricks they have been for the past thirty some odd years. Cigarette buttes and passed out junkies fill the gutters, shaking and shivering in the cold night air. Summer was making it’s final stand before the cold rushed in. Breathing smoke from the driver’s seat, drunken and dizzy, praying that no goddamn cops would be making the rounds on this side of town. Too many nights were beginning to seem identical to this, where I would drive drunken through the dead hours of morning, blinking into the rearview mirror at all the prostitutes standing on street corners, dressed in faux fur and high heels, too much makeup. Some people call them cheap whores. I call them a way to pass the time. How to Fight Loneliness. Take Two. If you’re feeling kind’ve lonely and the you’re missing that certain someone, there is nothing in the world that can heal a broken heart like a decadent and depraved night with a hooker. Make sure she doesn’t have a penis before you get her in the sack, otherwise you might be biting off more than you can chew. Fair warning. I pulled up on the curb, driving real slow as she walked up to the car, all smiles with glitter gleaming on her cheeks. She leaned into the window and listened as I explained exactly what I wanted. With the exchange of currency, she climbed into the passenger seat, and we sped off into the night… --- In a shitty hotel by the interstate, another hour later… Her arms around my neck. Her head on my shoulder. Her eyes teary and tortured by a thousand lonely nights without me by her side. Her makeup was running rivers down her cheeks, her slender frame pressed up against me, and me pressed up against the wall. She said she missed me. She said she still thought about me constantly. She said she couldn’t live without me. And she said she loved me. Her name was Judith. At least that’s what I paid her to tell me. I can only imagine what was going through her mind right about then. She must have thought I was crazy as I held her, whispering, 'I know, baby.' Saying things like, 'I love you. I’ve missed you. Never leave me again.' In her sarcophagus heels, her skirt clinging to her thighs, her arms littered with tract marks and heroin sores, she did exactly what I paid her to do. She acted like Judith for me. Most men, I would imagine, wouldn’t pay a prostitute to act like their dead girlfriend for a night. Most men, I figure, they can settle for a five dollar blowjob in the backseat of their junk heap of a car. Most men, I figure, this thought wouldn’t even cross their mind. Then again, most men aren’t on the verge of insanity. Her name was not Judith. I had never met her in my entire life. I would never see her again after tonight. But for now? For this moment in time? For a few hours? I had her back. Is that really so insane? --- Kurt Vonnegut once said, “I want to stay as close to the edge as I can without going over. Out on the edge you see all kinds of things you can't see from the center.” I wish I had found this quote sooner. This seems to be the fatal flaw in the logic of my life thus far. I left the center and went charging full force towards the edge. But when I reached the edge, I wasn’t satisfied with the view from afar, oh no, no, no. I wasn’t satisfied with sitting there, staring down into the lands where dreamers and schemers and fools made their keep. No, that wasn’t enough… so I went ahead and threw myself over, without giving it a second thought. Down, down, down into the bowels of insanity. Down toward decadence and depravity. Where rivers ran with alcohol and trees bloomed with drugs. Where all the world’s best poets, all the best philosophers, and all the best idiots found their way to success. Funny thing is, now that I’m falling, all I can really do is stare back up at the edge that I fell from, that cliff that I went careening off of without even thinking, and all I can really think about is how I wish I had a safety rope to climb back up on or a net to catch my fall. It’s sad. Now that I’m falling, I really miss the view from above. Most people have trouble admitting that they’re insane. They deny it adamantly, and if you happen to mention it in a way that suggests they’re a few cards short of a full deck, they’re liable to go ape shit crazy and rip out your jugular. But me, so far, I haven’t had much trouble admitting it. That, of course, is not to say I’ve come to terms with the fact. I mean, why deny something that’s so damn obvious? I know that there’s something terribly wrong with the way I think. I know that there’s something terribly wrong with the things I do and the way I perceive reality. But to deny it? Well, if I were to deny it, what would that say about how I perceive myself? Whether or not I’m insane, I’d like to think I have a good idea of who I am, even if it’s not who I want to be. Whoever said you’ve got to roll with the punches was exactly right. You’ve got to roll with the punches and kick against the pricks. Problem is, even rolling, you can only stand so many blows before you get knocked out. And what happens when there’s more pricks than kicks? Ah, life. Funny fucking thing, isn’t it? Real fucking funny… --- In that same shitty hotel by the interstate… There was a grin and a grope. A kiss and a moan. Sweat and grinding and all the usual. It was when I punched her that the sex appeal seemed to lose it’s balance. With a shocked expression and a loss of breath, she stared up at me as my knuckles dug into her stomach. This prostitute, this whore, this fucking nobody, her eyes were wider than the moon in the sky. She stared up at me with a sudden looks of contempt, fear, and trepidation. And me? I looked at her banefully, viciously, evilly. That wicked grin spread across my face, my eyes glazed over this something inhuman and terrible. Another punch. This one made her gasp for breath and double over as I whispered down at her, my voice cruel and soaked in malice, “Fuck you, you stupid whore.” She sputtered out, her voice coming out wheezing and pathetic, pleading helplessly, “Please… stop…” Between coughs, she managed to gasp out, “W-Why are you doing this…?” I sneered. I laughed. I pushed her away from me, down to the patterned carpet. She fell on her back, laying and staring up at me as she shuffled across the floor, trying to get as far away from me as she could. I simply shook my head, nearing her real slow, real menacing. I said, “Why am I doing this? A better question would be, why shouldn’t I do this?” With the curtains drawn shut and the door dead bolted, no one could see the tears streaming down the prostitutes face, besides me, myself, and I. No one could see her fear laced expression. No one could see her backed up against the wall, holding her neck as though scared I would cut it. No one could see the way she coughed and wheezed, trying to catch her breath. With words like weapons, I said, “I paid you to act like my dead girlfriend. I paid you to be Judith Kindle for a night. Well, this how I used to spend my nights with her. Throwing punches and calling her names while she rolled up into a little ball in the corner, cowering and begging and boohoo-fucking-hoo. What? Didn’t I mention that part?” My hand struck her across the cheek, sending her tumbling over onto her side, sobbing her eyes out as my fists fell. Total control. Total power. A feeling I hadn’t felt in what seemed like ages. And right then, at our greatest moment together, as the blood began to roll down from her nose and lips began to crack and bust beneath my fists, there was loud knock at the door. At first, I was reluctant to stop throwing punches. It was like coming out of a trance. I sneered and tossed a look over my shoulder at the door, yelling out louder than I intended, “Who the hell is it?” With a voice thick with a cigarette smoke, He replied through the door, “Who the hell do you think it is? Get your shit together and get out here. We’ve got shit to do tonight. You can get laid later.” With a sneer, I looked back to the prostitute, bleeding and crying and sniffling as he covered her face. I leaned in and whispered, “You got off fucking lucky, bitch.” Without another word, without another punch, I stood and put on my jacket, leaving him trembling in the corner. For a moment, I felt like I was in control again. --- The bullet pierced my shoulder, sending a shockwave through my arm as I staggered backwards. My lips parted and my breath came out thin, my eyes wide, my life flashing as his head steadied itself and began to tighten on the trigger once again. When you’re staring into the face of death, you’re breathless. There’s no brilliant flash light. You don’t see your life flash before your eyes. You’re just breathless. And right about then is when you realize how much time you wasted. You realize how much of your life you’ve invested into trivial bullshit. You realize how meaningless everything is. You realize how you wish you would have done things differently. You realize you never followed through on any of your dreams of ambitions. You realize how worthless you really are. You realize you’re about nothing but a name on a tombstone. You realize that no one will remember you one hundred years from now. You realize it was all just a flash in the pan. You realize you blew your chances. You realize you fucked up. And most of all, you realize how much you regret all of this. He said, with a certain amount of undeniable contempt in His voice, “I made you, boy. I gave you the power to do what you did. If it wasn’t for me, you’d be nothing right now and you know it. I made you a god, and you betrayed me. And here we are.” I didn’t remember any of this. I didn’t know what had happened. I didn’t know what was going on. Confusion. Bewilderment. All the usual symptoms. His voice lacked any sense of humor. No sarcasm. Nothing joking about it. He told me, “Last night, I made you a god. I took you back in time and we killed your parents before you were born. Now, you’re without beginning, Sean. You have no end. You’re immaculate. And what do you do? You shoot me.” I was too horrified to reply. All my words came out in vowels. The look of puzzlement on my face must have clued him in. With that grin spreading across His face, His finger tightening on the trigger, He said, “What? Don’t you remember, Sean? Still in shock? Oh fucking well. Goodbye.” And with His dying breath, with all the final power He had left in His body, he pulled the trigger. I winced. I cowered. I felt like crying. Now, call it fate, if you want. Call it divine intervention. Call it motherfucking serendipity for all I care… but as He fell over, slumping to the ground, the chamber of the gun fell on empty. An unsatisfactory click. No bullets. Nothing. Just a click. A great, big, anti-climax. When I realized I wasn’t dead, I looked cracked my eyes open slowly and looked around. Down at the corpse of a man who said he was me, from the future. Up to the sky, where a morning sun rose quiet. To my shoulder, still stinging from the bullet wound. And all I could do was sigh, hang my head, and start walking, more confused now than I was at the start of all of this. But apparently, according to a now dead man, I was a god. Everything has it's own payoffs, I suppose...
