Market Value

24 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/24/2007 Indianapolis, IN Located in the heart of downtown Indianapolis, the Canterbury hotel is a little different than your average Holiday Inn. There is a complimentary limo service around the neighborhood, a five star restaurant, and many other amenities that financially healthy visitors have been enjoying since 1983. The establishment is, in a single word, classy. Saul stood in the parking lot of the previously mentioned establishment, and despite the fact that it was over eighty degrees in Indianapolis on this day; he still wore his usual black suit. His black hair was in a ponytail, and his scarred face was contorted into an impatient grimace. Dark shades covered his malevolent gaze. He had been waiting for several minutes, and finally a 2001 Pontiac Grand Prix pulled into the parking place a few feet from where he was standing. The driver side door swung open, and out stepped a man that seemed to be in his late 20’s. He had a buzzed blonde haircut, goatee, and had a polo shirt tucked into khaki shorts. Saul lip synched the words "douche bag" upon seeing him. "You must be Saul," the man said, and advanced towards him with a hand extended. "I’m Brian". Saul shook the hand unenthusiastically. Brian worked for an online wrestling newsletter called "The Daily Dropkick". This publication had gained more and more credibility over the years, and has settled into its place as one of the best sources for inside information in the wrestling business. "You’re just as lifeless in person as on TV," Brian offered, with a smirk. "I take no particular joy in attemping to destroy a wrestling company, I just get paid very well to do so," Saul responded, and motioned for Brian to follow him. * * * Many of the suites had their own outdoor deck, and the former livewire champion made it a point to frequent his. "The River Rat" Lane Stevens sat at a table covered in shade by a large umbrella, staring off into downtown Indianapolis. The glass in front of him contained a half finished mai-tai with an umbrella sticking out of it. He wore a button-up white silk shirt, with white dress pants. Some of the hotel staff joked that he was as blinding as the sun. Lane had been alone with his thoughts for some time before the sliding glass door that lead to the hotel suite opened, and Saul emerged in said opening. "Brian Halloway is here," Saul said simply. "Neato," was Lane’s casual reply. He took another sip of his mai-tai. Saul vanished, and moments later Brian emerged with a notebook and pen. The writer stood cautiously in the doorway for a moment, before closing it and advancing to the open seat at the table. "It’s nice to meet you Mr. Stevens." The River Rat took another sip, and after a ten second pause all he could come up with for a response was, "yeah". Brian nodded for a few seconds, attempting to put his next sentence together. "Well I’d like to begin today," Brian started, "by thanking you for granting me and my publication an interview today. Interviews with men like yourself, men that don’t have a problem…expressing themselves, has been a big part of our success." "By expressing myself, do you mean acting like a huge douchebag and causing some kind of controversy that leads to more hits for your website and in turn more advertising dollars?" Lane quipped back, almost instantly. Brian stopped for a moment, wondering how to answer that question. "Yes," the writer said, "yes I suppose that’s partly what I mean." "Oh, cool. Cause I’m in a real douchebag mood today," Lane Stevens replied, and cracked his first smile of the day. Brian opened his notebook. "What can you tell me about the masked man that appeared with you on this last week’s Killzone? You didn’t mention him during the in-ring whatsoever," Brian asked, pen in hand. And then Lane cracked his second smile of the day. * * * Wednesday night – after the show. The man in black had just put his mask back on, and fastened it. He shook hands with Clinton Sage and Lane Stevens, and began to head for the door of Sage’s dressing room. "Keep that thing on all the way home, and make sure you aren’t being followed," Clinton commanded. "And shower in it too!" Lane added, and chuckled. Sage rolled his eyes, and the masked man was gone. The Seventh Sin paced for a moment, before voicing the question that had been on his mind all night. "Can we honestly trust him?" Clinton asked. "Well, can he honestly trust us?" was Lane’s question. And they shared a laugh. * * * "You can call him The Scourge," Lane said. Brian scribbled this and a few other observations in his notebook. "What would I call him if I could see his face?" the writer asked, tentatively. The River Rat laughed aloud, taking a few massive drinks of his mai-tai. He appeared to be studying Brian as if he were a new species. "So you think we’d dress him up in a mask, in an outfit that covered most of his body features except for height…and then a couple days later I’d just spill the beans to some wrestling newsletter smart mark? Come on baby, you’re here to hype the Affliction machine, not spoil everyone’s fun." "Fair enough," was Brian’s reply. "I could drink this shit for breakfast," Lane offered, staring at a glass that only contained chunks of ice. Brian ignored that comment, and instead asked: "Recently things have heated up a bit between you and Jeb, and the former tag champs Aimz & Crisis. Is this a personal feud, or a professional one?" The River Rat shrugged his shoulders and said, "Seems to me it’s a little of both." "Mind elaborating?" "If I was a super hero…I would be the elaborator!" Lane yelled in response. It was obvious that he was buzzing a little bit at this point. Brian’s brow furrowed. "Well, when we entered the tag division, we didn’t get much attention at first. Despite all the shit talking…and this and that, it seemed like none of the fatal faction tag-teams really knew who Jeb and I were. So the obvious solution to this was to find the most visible half of the tag-team champions at that time, and throw her head first into a wall and beat her up." "The only solution I can think of," Brian added, somewhat sarcastically. "And, for whatever reason," Lane started, "Amy and her husband have gone and taken this personally." Stevens shrugged, and attempted to look as innocent as possible. "Well, as Darcy said on the last edition of Killzone…it was her big night, you did ruin her big night," Brian said, scribbling something in his notebook. "Women wrestling for major titles is nothing more than a publicity stunt, and while protecting my friend Mr. Sage’s interests I might have accidentally saved this federation some embarrassment. I am entirely certain the management’s thank you note to me has been lost in the mail somewhere." "You don’t think that Amy winning a championship, and defeating the odds against a ruthless group of opportunists would inspire both ratings and a wider female fan base?" Brian stopped writing to ask. Lane picked his teeth and said, "Nah". "That’s all you have to say in response to that?" Lane rolled his eyes. "Bitches watch crappy lifetime movies, dude. Next question." "Fair enough," Brian answered, sounding a bit frustrated. "During your verbal sparring match on the last edition of Killzone, Amy said the worst that you can do is cost her matches. You run with a group of people who seem to be capable of far worse things. So what is your response to that statement?" A vacant smirk slowly crept onto Lane’s face. His eyes were off in the distance, staring at the intense traffic that had a way of materializing in the middle of the day in downtown Indianapolis. "Well, if costing her a match wasn’t such a big deal, those two lovebirds wouldn’t still be bitching about it. But I can assure you, Mr. Halloway, we have a few other tricks up our sleeve. I told her not to pursue this any further, and it looks to me like she is hell-bent on ignoring some friendly advice. We have ruined anyone who has come up against us, just look at where Covel’s career has landed. Do you see McKail around here anymore? I’m not quite sure what makes her think that her precious tag-team will be any different. Dominating jerk offs like The Alliance for a few months has warped her perception of this business, and her place in it. Her place in this business is accompanying her hack of a husband to ringside, and putting on some goddamn make-up." Brian looked at Lane slack-jawed, a little surprised at the comment. He expected him to be a little less of an asshole than on television. Finally, after a pause, Brian said: "The Affliction seems to be on a recruiting drive lately. It seemed at first that your group was like so many that are started in professional wrestling, that talk a lot about dominance…and changing the landscape of wrestling, but your group lately seems to be very intent on doing just that." "Take a look at the roster, Brian, you can already see subtle changes. The strong-willed are being broken. There will come a time when this place is filled with men who have sold their soul to Mr. Sage, or men who are too busy worrying about their own business to see their federation crumbling around them." "Have you sold your soul to Mr. Sage?" the writer asked. The River Rat chuckled aloud and answered, "I think I got market value." * * * Downstairs in the hotel lobby, Saul held a cellular telephone to his ear. "You will find that Affliction membership has many benefits, I would like to go over some of those with you now," he said, with dead eyes and a blank slate of a face. End.
