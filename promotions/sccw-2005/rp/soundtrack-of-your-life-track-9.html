Soundtrack of your life: Track 9

Date: October Matthew Good Band’s "Symbolistic White Walls" (Last of the Ghetto Astronauts, 1995) In the mists of steam and that ‘just less than scalding’ hot stream of water I played a game of find-the-bruise. It was a game that I did not particularly like, but had become more and more common to these post show nights. Add the empty motel room and lonely night, and it had all fast become a routine and my least favourite part of this job. Somehow I figured that this must have been another self-imposed measure, for I doubt any of my contemporaries would be crawling into a bed by themselves. It was in that steam that I had forgotten that I would not exactly be alone either. No, tonight I slept with an icon of my failure, that unsuccessful experiment. Happiness is a fish that cannot be caught. I cannot be bought or bartered for. It would not be forced into existence. And I hung my hypothesis on what I understood as a simple truth: Happiness can be stolen from you, should you let it. And there I was, backing up that ramp with the leather and metal object that he held so dear. He walked into the face of his enemy to retrieve it. He cheated to obtain it. He did it because of what it meant to her, so I thought that my act of thievery would be enough to have him follow after, again looking to face an enemy to retrieve this title for her. But she drew him away to do other works. Yes, it was the happiness of Jay Bridden that I looked to target, and I think I still will. I have seen what he was capable of doing with a clear mind and no motives. Maybe if he was taunted enough he would charge at this matador. But now that he held over me the option to never defend that title, it could just be that the outdated model that lay on that strange bed would be as close as I came. It might be that if I was to possess the same morality of Kennedy I would have the direct method to raise his ire. Julissa, the Minx, you are safe, for now. But still a chess piece to put in play should it come to that. And I must be the only red blooded male in the United States at this moment that could think of Julissa Minorez in the shower and be preoccupied with vengeance, not self exploration. But then again it was a spot of pride to not fall to the same base thoughts that Eugene Ware was broadcasting just an hour ago. ---- Then I stepped out of that shower into the cold basement draft. It was something that needed to be done, to wash away the flight, but damn I hated this part. And I stood just removed from the womb-like shower stall now desperately trying to remove every last drop of water from my naked body. I never had the patience or the will to get that job done thoroughly, and when that ‘good enough’ point was reached, I rushed to put on the gitch and jeans, then sprinting up the stairs to somewhere warmer. At the top of that flight of stairs I was greeted by one set of eyes too many. I shot a nervous grin to both of my parents, and then finally bowed my head to the thirty-something in a neat blazer. The entirely formal apparel was my first hint that she was the real estate agent, while my second was her rush to get back to talking business. It was that she took a glance in my direct as I left the room that had these chapped lips curled up. It was in my room that I sat on the bed and contemplated just how much longer I would be leaving my mess here. And how much longer I would sit and wait for this answer to be made for me. This place was becoming more and more of a house with each time I made it back here, and to have that woman present just tore this place down that much more. A pair of socks and black golf shirt later, I re-emerged before my parents and their guest. "So, Mom, Dad, what is it that you guys were up to this fine day?" I asked, wicking away the water still behind my ears. "Well Mitch," my mother started shifting her focus from me to the red blazer, "we were going to start looking around at places." "Oh, right! That would explain what you are doing here, Miss…" I trailed off. "Ferguson. Shania Ferguson," she stated extending a hand toward my direction. I didn’t take her handshake. "H1N1, don’t you know. Can’t afford to let myself become a victim of that, and you really can never be too cautious," says the man with the least amount of respect for his body in the room. "Oh, of course," she said with the hint of humiliation, retreating her hand back to her side. "It was nice to meet you, but Mister and Missus Quinlan, we really should be getting out to those open houses, and on the search for your next home." Nothing wrong with this one, I refused to relent to myself. "Well Mitchell, we will be going then," my mother started in with a hug. "We should be back later this afternoon, but there is some left over lasagne in the fridge if you get hungry." "Ma, I travel around the continent for work, I think I will be able to handle myself for one night’s meal." "Alright," my dad chimed in, "take care of yourself sport." And after his gentle shot to my shoulder the happy trio exited. And again I was left alone in a (soon to be) foreign set of walls. But know that I think of it, I could eat. ---- "Oh, I am sorry," I offered, previously distracted by the wine coloured walls, or more so the televisions that lined them. "I will just take a Diet Pepsi please." "Okay, one Pepsi, and one Diet Pepsi coming right up," the waitress said, departing our table. "Hate to see her go, but love to watch her leave?" Tim enquired, not looking at me, but instead the rather shapely figure squeezed into a pair of black pants and t-shirt. I think that he might lose his head if he ever happened across Kathryn Shaw. "So," always a great segue, "things still going good with, hey don’t hate me for this, but I have kind of forgotten her name already." "Eh, no offence taken. Things went south. She up and split. And now I am free to guiltlessly stare down the fabulous sights that Boston Pizza has to offer," he said, before digging his face quickly into the menu as our waitress had approached, beverages in hand. "And here we go," as she carefully placed first Tim’s and then mine. "Are you guys ready to order, or?" "We could just use another minute," Tim said. He was usually the default talker of us two. "But congrats, you have got to be the quickest waitress we’ve had. Thanks." Hey, I did say usually. "Well, uh, thanks," she said, blushing somewhat, gathered herself, and set about the next dining party. "When do we ever need to look at the menu Tim?" "Well, you know, I like to have my variety." "Variety? Hmmm. Let me guess here: you are thinking about Beef Dip sandwich with the side of Garden Greens?" "Ah, shove it. It is always nice to see the new and improved prices." I couldn’t actually believe it, but he was doing his best impression of a man who hadn’t made up his mind, flipping between the pages until finally setting it to rest and taking a big gulp of carbonated sugar. "And I suppose you are just going for the chicken fingers again?" "Hey, nothing wrong with routine," I shot back. And dug a little further into the events that I had been too busy to take heed while they happened, which seemed to be too much these days, "So, if you don’t mind me asking, what sent whatsherface packing?" "I’ll just sum it up to the difficulties of being a good Catholic boy." "She wasn’t about to wait until you hitched the knot to do the hanky-panky?" "Bingo," he said, looking past me to the Yankees v. Angels on the big screen. Bottom of the seventh and the Angels just turned another double play. The small percentage in the establishment not agonizing over another Maple Leaf loss is a gasp at the third defensive gem in as many innings. Leaf fans… We sat in silence, enjoying the fall classic, and waited for the server to return. Eventually she did, and we placed our orders, and again, we sat in silence. It was the silence of two people who have had the same conversations many times over. We both knew that the night held many Seinfeld quotes, snide comments and sports talk. It was the same thing we did every time we convened. But that silence spoke volumes and told me all I wanted to know. My brother was keeping himself well, and we still could share this comfortable silence. It was a few minutes, and a few bits into the meal when my unsettled mind sought soothing. "Hey, Tim what was it like to make that move out on your own?’ "Well," he started, halfway through a mouthful of leafy greens, "it was an adjustment for sure. To tell you the truth, it still isn’t something that I have totally come to terms with myself. But I know it was something that I had to do. Never have regretted it. It is a step that added a tonne of responsibility on my shoulders, but the freedom that came with it was more than worth it. I take that you are still not knowing what you are going to do when ‘Rents give up the ole homestead?" "You could say that. I mean, I don’t know. I am spending more and more time away from home as it is with work, and I have been told that all I really would need is just an apartment or condo," I said, before being interrupted. "You must have enough cash these days to get that condo down in Myrtle, no?" the interruption. "Ha, not quite rolling in as much green as you think. You are paying for this by the way. But yeah, I have been looking at places. They are all just white walls, tiny spots, nothing really giving off that ‘home’ feeling. I don’t want to spend my time in motel rooms around the nation and come home to one, ya know?" "And you would have to do your own laundry, make your own suppers. I know those are tough to give up, and really, that’s why you see me back home more often than not," He said, taking another bite full of Roast Beef and French Bun dipped in Au Jus. I nodded and gobbled down another buffalo sauced finger of my own. Commiserating never his strong suit, I decided to let this issue pass by and cast my gaze back to the flat screen. It was after A-Rod’s late inning heroics to tie the game in the bottom of eleven that Tim, long since finished his meal, gave up on the night. "Mitch, it was good man, but these Yanks are killing me. A man’s gotta get up and work in the morning. This thing is probably going to fifteen. So, I bid you a good evening sir," he said, sliding out of the booth and toward the entrance. "Good evening sir… HEY! The CHEQUE!" I shouted through the now half empty bar at the freeloader. He just waived and left me sitting. Figure I owe him this one at least, I guess. And I thought that if I was to chase after him they might accuse me of ditching out on the cheque myself. I only steal title belts though. So I sat in the booth and downed my fourth Diet while the Yanks decided that they did want to take the 2-0 lead. The game ended, and I paid the bill, and sat watching the post-game for good measure, giving away my cover as a guy with nothing to do in the morning. Eventually I got a little attention from our well-tipped waitress. "Why the long face, champ?" she asked, sliding in across from me. "You don’t look like a Halo’s fan." Nah, not a champ, just the title holder. "Good game and its nothing like that." "Well what is it then? Maybe I can give you a little advice and you will let us close up." It was a rare occasion when my conversation with a woman was not just a producer barking at me to get to spot or a crazed redhead with a Louisville. "Just got pretty big step ahead of me is all, ain’t really certain how I should go about it," and believe that I was just as surprised that I had warmed to the early twenty bartender. That costumed goof you saw at Sin*Con was just an over excited fan who got an all access pass. "Shoot kid, we want to get home before sunrise." "Sure, I suppose. Gotta get myself a new place of residence, moving away from home." "Short on cash? Gave a generous tip in that case." "Well, nah, cash ain’t really the issue." "In that case, you should have given a better tip," she said, with a cute smile. That brunette hair framed an angelic face. "The parents are kind of selling our place, giving me the old heave ho." "A fucking tragedy, buttercup," she said, with the confidence of already receiving gratitudes. "You are what? Twenty-five? And still living with mommy and daddy? Sprout some hair down there and make a move." Suddenly that angelic face had become yet another woman telling me what to do. But I took the hint, shook my head and that was out of there. ---- "Hello," the other line answered. "Mrs. Ferguson?" "It is, and how are you Mr. Quinlan?" "The other Mr. Quinlan, and I’m just calling because I think I have something you could help me with. I am going to put an offer in, and them commercials selling you folk as necessity have plum got me thinking I couldn’t help but get it without your help." "I have no time for jokes Mr. Quinlan." Her voice was short, snappy. I’d imagined I was talking to another upset female. "No jokes ma’am, just money. That is why you work, yes?" "I am listening." "This house, I want to buy it." "Which house?" "This house, my parents’ house, but I don’t really want them to know just yet. So I will have you put in an anonymous bid on it, okay?" ---- I wore that same grin as I do every time at the discovery of a new dent when I left for the airport, my parents happy to know that they have received enough of an offer to expand their search, if not expedite it. I left knowing that I would be coming back home. It was about damn time that I made that move to secure my own happiness.
