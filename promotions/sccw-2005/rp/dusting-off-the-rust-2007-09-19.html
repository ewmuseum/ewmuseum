Dusting off the Rust...

19 September 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 19th, 2007 I lay alone on these nights and wrap my ears in headphones that seem ridiculous. Gargantuan, black and noise-cancelling, they cover half of my head and offer the opposite of my slim white iPod. But I wear them anyway, because I'm alone and don't have to care what I look like - as if I ever do - and because I need the absolute silence they offer to put behind the music that my mp3s will provide. You can't listen to songs like these with background noise. No, not the lonely morning songs. Not the things I listen to before and during my restless sleep in these blank hotel rooms. In the daylight hours and in public evenings, I'm the loud punk rock song. I'm Sid Vicious, I'm Mike Patten - I'm spitting in your face, slapping a doctor and smiling about it. But this is the me that people like Lane Stevens could only dream of seeing. This is my weak moment - laying here, sleepless. I thumb the iPod's control wheel for a few seconds, but the random clicks of menu selections - or anything, for that matter - can't reach my ears until I decide. These headphones and this empty room provide the exact silence I need when I turn on the kind of song you could create or extinguish life by. The perfect combination of notes is even rarer than the comfort they create, and that's why we can all relate to those few soft songs. I don't care if you're Hitler incarnate - you've got a slow jam or two that can tug on even the rustiest of heart strings. When I grew up a little, I realized that my tastes didn't have to be defined by the pace of my lifestyle. Not everything had to be hard, fast and filthy - I deserved to slow down every once in a while. I found my 'off-switch' in the voice of a friend of my oldest sister, a lady named Sarah McLachlan. This silence, when broken by the quietest of songs, serves as my strength. It slows all of my thoughts, giving me the precious minutes I need to organize the mess that stirs between my ears every day. I have to lay here and do the work that this business demands outside of the ring - the phone calls, the articles, the fan mail and a plethora of other irritants. At the very least, I plan when I'm going to do those things, and tonight they've taken a back seat. I'll start working when I arrive at the arena tomorrow. I'll go early and hide out in the change room they assign me. For now, I'll try my hand at sleeping alone. I don't know why I resign myself to typing away in this journal every night. After all, it's not like anyone can see it... but it holds some significance to me despite that fact. Writing this lets me reconnect and collect myself, and gives me something to look at when times will inevitably seem impossibly tough. I read back on my thoughts during times like these, actually. Earlier, Darcy got up in arms about the announcement of my match with Jonathan Rhine, one of my Highwaymen teammates. I'd expected that much, but what he later said about my fight with Lane Stevens rang way too true: 'You go on your magical crusades to do whatever's on your mind at the time, and end up in the gutter over it. Face it, you use the excuse of failure to keep yourself slumming at the point where you don't need to take responsibility for anything, because whatever you do can just be considered fighting tooth and nail with nothing to lose and everything to gain.' So here I am. My husband's at home and I'm alone on the road, slowly becoming accustomed to the idea of making a friend or two to watch my back around here. No more Fatal Faction. No more titles. I've entered my crusade against Lane Stevens, Clinton Sage and everything they stand for with absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain. But this time, I'm not going to hide my actions behind the excuse that I'm 'allowed' to fail just because I'm already at the bottom. If I fall, I still have the entire main event, mid and low card to catch me. Now, it's just a matter of keeping up with The Affliction and besting them before they can pick off the new team that's built itself around me. -Amy CHAPTER 1: (PPVP) aka POST PAY PER VIEW PERIL ================================================== Amy Campbell stepped through her front door with her head cast down. She looked as if someone had just run over her cat and, in fact, there was the little glint of a teardrop hiding in the corner of one of her eyes. Normally, she would've taken her sneakers and restricting belt off within two feet of the door, but this time she was heading inside with a heavy heart. Darcy was hunched over their kitchen table when she found him, sorting out airline ticket printouts with his laptop beside him. He was likely planning their trip to the next event, without any clue that he wouldn't be accompanying her. He grinned when she entered the dining room, but it wouldn't be long before the smile was wiped away. Amy sat across from him, looking as if she'd seen a ghost, and threw her hands in the air. ''I've gotta know,'' she began and paused to heave a sigh, ''What did it?'' ''I already told you... Reaper did it. I bought him the damn scratching post like you said, but like I said, he prefers to claw up nice things,'' Darcy answered. He could tell that she probably wasn't in the mood for idle banter, but without her attempting to be slightly more forthcoming a sarcastic retort was all he could offer. ''We'll have a moment of silence for my favorite leather couch later,'' Amy shot back, ''But right now, I've gotta ask about what I just paid your doctor to tell me. What took it out? Was it the pay-per-view?'' She was playing things way too calm, cool and collected. It frightened Darcy a little, because as with most women, you could tell Amy was REALLY pissed when she was calm, having gone past the point of yelling or screaming and straight into that 'I'm so mad, I don't even want to talk to you' zone. Only she did want to talk to him, and that brought a sigh to the surface, with Markson acknowledging that if he didn't have bad luck, he wouldn't have any. 'Oh, my back? Is that what that cold stare's for? Geez, I don't know...' Darcy answered, his voice trailing off. He could already tell this wasn't going to be easy. Even though she was his wife, even though he actually trusted her, openly discussing a subject he'd spent the better part of a decade keeping under wraps was going to be trickier than anticipated. Especially when the answers were just as much a mystery to him as they must have been for her. 'I imagine taking two dives off 30-foot cages might have been to blame. Or maybe it was spending the last ten years getting my ass kicked around the globe. It's hard to say for sure since I never properly got diagnosed when I should have...' Amy was slightly surprised by how fast he gave up his answers. She'd already prepared a rant for when he inevitably skirted the subject with idle chit-chat, and now she was left having to fast-forward her scalding lecture because of her husband's unexpected honesty. ''And that was stupid of you,'' she snorted, ''But you know what really gets me? You tried to hide it from me. It doesn't surprise me - I would've expected anybody to avoid showing weakness - but you hid it knowing full well that I'd dragged you back into wrestling, and that if you got hurt...'' That's when the redhead had to stifle the cracks in her voice. Imagining having the love of her life end up disabled just because she'd challenged and baited him into teaming with her was no light matter. With a cough to hide her weakened vocal chords out of the way, she continued, ''... You knew that if you got hurt, it would ultimately be because of me. And you still went on hiding the injury, gambling on your back, our life and my conscience.'' 'Do you think this is easy for me?' He suddenly felt as if he had bowling ball magically implanted into his stomach. This matter of topic, one that he fought desperately to lock away in the back of his mind, was not easily shared with others. Darcy Markson never cared much for the bowling-ball feeling, and a typical Markson response of yesteryear would have been infuriated at Amy's attempt to pierce through his wall of self-defense, insisted that the decision was his and his alone, and not said another word. But things were different now. This sort of thing still didn't come easy, and perhaps never would. There was, however, nothing left to hide. The ball suddenly lifted. 'I wouldn't have come back if it weren't for you, that part is true,' he began. 'But you forget that somehow, after everything that's happened, I'm still in love with this sick sport of ours. I still don't want to say goodbye, even though I'm one bad landing away from a wheelchair. I always knew this day would come, but that doesn't make this any easier.' Removing his gaze from the vacant stare toward the room's opposite wall, he turned to look at her. 'But the reason we're even having this discussion is because I already know you're right. We're partners outside this ring as much as we are in it. This is your decision as much as it is mine.' ''And that's why you're gonna rest for as long as your doc wants you to,'' ordered Amy, ''Without pulling any funny little stunts, either. You aren't going anywhere near a sports arena. I'm flying solo for a few weeks, and that's the way it's got to be.'' 'Not even for the occasional cameo-slash-making fun of people?' ''I'm not negotiating right now,'' said Amy, ''We can deal with your penchant for public mockery later. And you need to know I'm serious about this. No tagging, no heavy practice and no go-karting with Johnny every Sunday morning.'' He wanted to smile, but forced himself in check. A break from the weekly grind/ache in his back and an reprieve from the ever-lurking shadow of horrific injury was something he was all-too-willing to accept with open arms. But Amy didn't need to know it. 'Alright...' Darcy said, forcing a sigh. '...but you owe me for this.' Amy's head was practically spinning. He seemed a little too okay with things, and while she was grateful, it made her question the severity of what they were dealing with. She sighed, ''Yeah, sure. Thank me later. Either way, I just came home to get more cash... I owe your doctor another hundred for risking his license to tell me that you were lying through your teeth.'' Amy slammed the door when she left, but Darcy knew that she wouldn't stay mad for long. She was more worried than anything. She just didn't know exactly how much reason she had to be worried, and if his doctor was to tell her exactly how bad it was, it could very well break her. That was Darcy's guilt, but Amy's was much worse. While his secrets had come to light, the things she'd been keeping from him - like, say, the whole debacle where she'd terminated her own pregnancy five years ago - were still under wraps and much, much worse by comparison. CHAPTER 2: SINCE YOU'VE BEEN GONE... ================================================== She'd bribed his doctors - charmed them into believing that the man called Crisis actually wanted his spouse to know that he was nearly crippled with a back injury. After all, any other man would have come forward... but this man was a fighter as a career. Their mortgage relied on him being in wrestling shape and, unfortunately, he'd just married the kind of girl who puts her heart before her head. Even if it meant living in a box, she'd never allow him to face a wheelchair. 'I knew I should've married a gold-digger...' he'd thought to himself earlier, when she'd demanded that he remained at home to heal. Sure, he'd been convinced to stay at home, but now there was more to question. With him sidelined, Amy had aligned herself with a group of men he didn't know in the least, and he'd just discovered that she'd be tag-teaming with one of them in the upcoming week - effectively replacing Markson entirely. Incredibly, it wasn't the going behind his back to break the Hippocratic Oath and delve into his personal health records that bothered him. Nor was it the headstrong decision to effectively sideline him. As Darcy stared at his wife's small frame attempting to drift away from the realm of consciousness, it was no decision from the past or very recent present that occupied his mind... but rather, decisions that remained in the future, and the things he felt were boiling under the surface. 'So how long is this arrangement going to last again?' he asked, half-expecting her to ignore him outright. While it was infinitely easier to sleep with him beside her, Amy had found herself praying to fall off the face of the planet at that very moment. Where was an Invisibility Cloak when you needed one? She tried to ignore him, but the little redhead couldn't find it in herself to be quite that rude to the man who dealt with her sharp toenails and cold feet every night. So, she rolled over and bit the bait he'd cast, ''What arrangement?'' 'The one where you continue on the team with another partner while I'm not to even entertain the idea of even showing up at the arena.' Amy sighed and tried to but the subject, not to mention herself, to bed as quickly and painlessly as possible. ''It's not 'continuing on the team'. You and I are still Aimz and Crisis, and we'll ALWAYS be the best team FUSE could hope to have,'' she stated, ''This is a one-off tag match with Rhine, and it's booked because of the stable. With you out of the picture, I need some second line of defense against The Affliction. I mean, you've seen Stevens' fetish for pissing me off... I'd end up in a bodycast if it weren't for the guys I hooked up with to fight them back.'' Darcy sighed. He knew changing her mind was even more impossible than his ability to assertively voice his concerns. Nevertheless, he still pressed the issue. 'Look... just be careful, alright?' he answered. 'The thing about hired guns... well, you don't really know who else they might be working for.' ''I've got you watching my ass, don't I?'' 'Yeah, from a fucking TV screen,' came his terse reply. 'I know you catch me screaming at the TV during Raiders games from time to time, but believe me... it never works.' Amy laid back with a sly grin, ''Sure it does. It gets my attention, at least.'' ''And look...'' she continued, patting the cold side of the bed next to her, ''No matter what, you know who I trust and listen t-- well, maybe not listen to -- but you know who I'd take a bullet for before any of the guys you see me in the ring with, no matter which side they're on. In a way, they're all the same to me - just potential enemies compared to current enemies... but you're the only constant, and I'll always turn back to you. You and I have something that the rest of the jackholes in this business don't - a partner that they can rely on through absolutely anything.'' She smiled again, ''Besides, these rings don't mean nothing. At the very least, you'll need a judge and a team of lawyers to get me off your back.'' Taking the hint, he joined her in their kingly slumber suite. Fortunately, at least for her sake, he trusted her judgment far more than she ever would his, should roles have been reversed. But he still felt it important to remind her... 'Just remember...' he told her, after a beat. 'If it were me going it alone, in a business comprised almost overwhelmingly of single women, you wouldn't have taken this nearly as well.' ''AHA!'' she laughed triumphantly, suddenly springing up from under the covers, ''So it is a jealousy thing! I knew it! You think I'd actually leave your gimpy ass for one of The Highwaymen? Come on, you know me better than that.'' Suddenly, Amy's eyes lit up. Darcy knew the look, too - she'd clearly just been blessed with a rare gem of humor... something the endearing little redhead tended to lack. She giggled, ''At least, I won't be leaving until we get a stable-wide locker room. Once I see their cup sizes, it could be any man's game.'' Of course, that joke came off about as funny as one of her usual 'Your mom' barbs. 'Do it,' Darcy said, unable to fight a grin of his own at his own forthcoming joke. 'Do it...and I'll be in the next celebrity 'Girls Gone Wild.' Hey, isn't your sister about to turn 18?' ''Nice, fuckwad!'' Darcy's chest was going to be bruised for weeks after taking the hit he just had. Amy had decided to punch him about as hard as she could from her side of the mattress. But when Darcy recoiled by playfully giving her a headlock, she shuddered. ''Oh Jesus,'' Amy grimaced, ''My sister coming of age... now that's what I wanted to be thinking about in my bedroom...'' Darcy loosened the headlock, as if to invite the horrible brutality that was sure to come. 'Umm... where did you think we'd be filming the video?' Amy shook her head and sighed lovingly, ''Fuck you. I can't even hit you twice for that because it's not fair to cripples. Punk.'' Within a solid thirty seconds, they were cuddled up and speechless. That was the beauty in the relationship between them, and it always had been - one minute, they could be trading barbs while the next second would find them in an embrace. Those qualities - that wordless communication - helped make Aimz and Crisis a force to be reckoned with. But with him in the picture for so long, Amy had forgotten how to communicate with other, less intimate partners... and that could pose a problem for the Highwaymen's resident mouthpiece. CHAPTER 3: FOR HIGH TIMES TO COME... ================================================== He couldn't sleep a wink, and the voice outside his door hadn't been doing anything to help matters, so Rhine had decided to venture out of his hotel room - not something he was exactly a fan of when he was already so tired that he could barely move. The voice persisted, and as he got closer to the door he could recognize its wording... ''Busted flat in Baton Rouge, waitin' for a train - and I's feelin' near as faded as my jeans...'' It was a woman singing softly. Rhine smiled to himself, easily recognizing one of Janis Joplin's greatest hits as he opened the door - the voice stopping just as abruptly as the knob turned. He got what he hadn't been expecting, to say the least. When Jonathan poked his head through the doorjam, it took only a moment for his eyes to find that there was a tiny woman leaning against the hallway wall. With her knees curled to her chest, she was carefully watching the screen of her Treo cell phone, thumbing idly at its keyboard. She must have stopped singing when she heard the door open, he thought to himself. ''Amy?'' She quickly pocketed her phone and watched Rhine as he closed the door behind him and made his way toward her, smirking. 'This guy's a little too friendly,' she thought as he sat down beside her eagerly, but she kept that observation to herself and plastered a smile on instead, ''Oh, hey.'' ''Did you lock yourself out, or...?'' he queried. ''I didn't think anyone'd be around. Most of the time, people are at lunch or doing whatever business it is that they do,'' Amy replied, ''So I chill in the halls.'' ''And you always sing 'Me And Bobby McGee' while you're at it?'' So he had heard her. Amy blushed slightly before she answered, ''I just hum shit when I'm bored... hope it didn't piss you off.'' Rhine grinned. ''Not at all!'' ... And then he started making mistakes. ''It was kinda cute, if you ask me.'' ''Cute?'' The Red Raver's eyebrows shot up, and her entire body visibly tensed, ''No. I don't do 'cute'.'' It was nice of her to try so hard to maintain her image, but Rhine couldn't help pointing to one of her slender arms. ''You've got Darcy's name tattooed on your wrist, and it looks like it's in his handwriting, too,'' he chuckled, ''If you ask me, that's pretty cute. But if it helps, I wasn't expecting Janis Joplin in the hallway - I had you pegged as more of the type who goes into a dance club and screams Slayer songs just to piss people off.'' Amy grinned and cocked her head to one side, mumbling a sarcastic 'Awww'. ''Slayer songs... That's the sweetest thing anyone's said to me all day. I mean, next to the guy who screamed 'SHOW US YOUR TITS' out of his car when I was getting my Red Bull at the corner store.'' ''Anyway,'' she continued, ''Shouldn't you be working out or something? Or whatever it is you clean, friendly fellas do to kill the time...'' It wasn't hard to tell that the redhead was in her usual antisocial mood, but Rhine had sensed an easy 'in' - and not the type that most men would've thought of. He wanted to bond with his stablemate, whether she trusted him or not. So, he cast his line... ''I know you're trying to blow me off, but it ain't gonna work - 'cause I've got some questions...'' ''What, like what I'm wearing to the ring tomorrow? Don't worry, I learned my lesson a couple of weeks ago,'' Amy said, ''The good guys aren't supposed to freeboob it. I promise I'll wear a bra this time - even though they're uncomfortable as all fuck - just for you guys. Oh, and because Darcy wasn't really happy about people being able to tell how cold I was or wasn't during our on-camera work...'' ''Err, no.'' Jonathan raised his eyebrow, ''I actually hadn't noticed the bra thing but, uhhh, whatever makes you happy is fine by the rest of us on that front. I'm not exactly gonna check.'' He'd managed to pull a laugh out of her on that one, before he quickly wiped it away by trying to get personal, ''I was gonna ask... why Joplin? I mean, what you were singing while I was trying to nap. You don't strike me as the 1960s type of girl.'' ''When I was a kid, my dad sometimes had to cover for the couriers who worked for him. He'd take me along in the van listening to the classic rock station, and I had this wild, crazy-ass red afro hair... he'd tell me I looked like a littler, prettier Janis Joplin. I grew up singing along to the radio when they played her stuff, and she just sorta became this hero to m--- wait a second...'' Without even thinking, Amy had blurted out her answer, but she was quick to reprimand herself. She didn't trust her stablemates yet and, as far as they were concerned, she should have remained the tough bitch of the group. Now, she found herself giving up personal answers to questions without thinking - which is exactly what Rhine had wanted. She could tell he was trying to get to know her, and she hated letting people do such a thing. ''... Nope. No more. Forget what I said. I lied. I was singing that because I'd just heard some old lady playing it in her room.'' ''You know what?'' Rhine grinned, ''I think you might warm up to the boys yet.'' But then, he committed a cardinal sin, ''It might surprise you to hear... but you're actually a nice girl. I know what you like to say, but badasses generally don't pass the time sending text messages and singing songs they remember from their childhood in hotel hallways.'' ''Jon,'' she began, ''You really need to back that train up. I'll get used to you, and you'll get to know me... but it's gonna be a learning experience. The lesson you're learning today is that making assumptions about me usually ends up being nothing more than annoying.'' He still smiled, despite Amy's best mean face, ''Whatever you say. But tell me this: Do big, tough chicks eat lunch? There's a buffet downstairs, and I think we could both use the company.'' Amy couldn't help but sigh. He was trying, god bless him, but Rhine had already gotten too far into what she considered personal territory. She resolved to eat lunch with him, but planned to flatline any further questions he had. ''I could eat,'' she grumbled, ''But no more trying to get too friendly with me. I don't deal well with kindness.'' She paused as he helped her to her feet, ''But I've gotta ask... you said you were trying to take a nap. If I my random singing kept you up from that, what kept you up last night?'' Rhine simply chuckled, but Amy continued, ''No no, if you can ask me shit, I can shoot back. Have you got a secret partying side, or what?'' ''That would be an 'Or what','' he offered, ''I sleep in the afternoons, I just have a hard time adjusting to the time changes... so sleeping at night isn't exactly a hobby of mine.'' ''Then we should hang out tonight.'' ''I'll take it you don't sleep much, either?'' ''I sleep whenever I get a chance, whether it's on a floor or in the backseat of a car... but it never seems to hit me after dark,'' said Aimz, ''And without Darcy here, it's impossible to sleep at night, so I wander around and discover new things about wherever I am. I guess I could use the company when I'm walking around after dark...'' She stopped herself, ''Wait. That was more personal drivel...'' As they entered the elevator that lead to the hotel's dining room, Jonathan and Amy smiled to themselves. For once, Aimz seemed to be making a friend on the road that she didn't have to worry about sex, drugs or anything remotely exciting with... and Rhine was getting a rare opportunity - peeking into the person that his team mate truly was. Generally, Amy hated letting anyone know that she was anything but the biggest, baddest, meanest, craziest hardcore bitch you could meet. As a member of The Highwaymen, though, she seemed to be slowly growing comfortable with the idea that her peers could get to know her. It had always felt uncomfortable to give people information about herself - which is why it had been so easy for strangers to believe any rumor about her that they're fed - but she was growing to trust the men protecting her career at that moment. Honestly, she had little choice but to trust them - as they're the only thing keeping The Affliction from breaking her into a thousand pieces. As he disappeared into the elevator next to her, Rhine finished his last thought. ''Whether we hang out or don't and whether you like it or not, you're gonna have to trust me this week.'' Maybe it was time to open up to The Highwaymen... but it was definitely NOT a time to show anyone else any weakness whatsoever, so she'd continue keeping herself to herself. Is it so bad to make friends on the road? If not, then why did it feel like she was doing Darcy Markson an injustice?
