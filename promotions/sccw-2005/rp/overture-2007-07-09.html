Overture.

Date: 7-9-07 There is always some madness in love. But there is also always some reason In madness. -Nietzsche *** ONE. Power. Slow motion, dead silence. Her body went limp as soon as my fist landed on her jaw, a string of bright red blood shooting from her lip and across the kitchen , spinning and reflecting the bright lights on the ceiling. She fell graceful, beautiful even in this moment of weakness. With her eyes rolling back in her head, her mouth parting just enough to let out a quiet sigh, she hit the ground and her head bounced on the cold linoleum, blood still dribbling down from her lips. With the man in the moon as my only witness, I stared down with eyes piercing and proud, a slow grin overtaking my face, everything silent except the incessant hum of the air conditioner. Her name was Judith, and I loved her, in my way. *** TWO. Reunion. Earlier… I spotted her across the hotel lobby, beyond all the FUSE employees and fans waiting at the front desk, standing alone by the elevator with a suitcase in her hand. Her hair messy, her body twisting in nervous anticipation, wondering whether or not I was really going to show up. As the sun began to climb down from the sky outside, I began to cross the lobby floor, pushing my way through the crowd. Suitcase in one hand, a pack of cigarettes in my other, I approached Judith, still unaware of my presence. Judith, my childhood sweetheart. Judith, the only girl that ever mattered. Judith, my love and my whore, forever. Judith, standing and waiting for me, her prince, her savior, her everything. We’d known each other since our teenage years. For me, she was the shining light at the center of the hometown I hated; the only thing there to help me keep my sanity. My muse. My lover. Mine alone. For her, I must have been the opposite; the dark light at the center of it all, her way of escaping from the normalcy she’d come to accept. I was like nothing she’d ever seen, a total departure from her sheltered life. Her perfect prince, come to whisk her away into darkness. Her immaculate savior, coming down from heaven above only to take her to the depths of hell below. I was her ruin. Her undoing. Her parent’s nightmare put to flesh. When I left our hometown of Manchester, Tennessee, she was the only person who showed up at the bus station to say her goodbyes. Everyone else was happy to see me leave. They called me the curse of the town, but to her, I was the blessing. Since then, we’d seen each other off and on, whenever I came through Tennessee or whenever she could afford a plane ticket to wherever I was. We’d seen each other a month before I was hired by FUSE… and now that I was in Memphis, we were eager to see each other again. As soon as she saw me, she ran for me, her arms spreading out with her suitcase dangling wantonly from her left hand, a smile overtaking her face. Imagine all those old Hollywood romance movies that were in black in white where the woman runs to her man in the train station, wrapping her arms around him and leaping into the air as some corny orchestra music swells in the background. For a second, nothing else matters as they hold each other in their embrace. The world doesn’t exist. It’s just them, together, with all worries put to the backburner. For a moment, we stayed comfortable in our silence, but suddenly her words came out muffled by my shirt. "You’re late," Whispering, "I didn’t think you were going to come," Sighing, "I’ve missed you." "I know you have," I replied, voice soft, gentle. An amazing contrast when compared to the rest of me. Her eyes, a blue as brilliant as the ocean itself, peered up at me, curious. "Have you missed me?" Her voice came out hesitant, but anxious for my reply; even though she already knew what it would be. This was always how we met, and this was always the reply she received: 'No.' Disappointment. She sighed, nodded, and pressed her head back against my chest. Whether or not I missed her didn’t really matter. Besides… she knew I was lying. *** THREE. Control. Coughing hard, she rolled over onto her back, looking up at me with those brilliant eyes now afraid. She was a deer in the headlights as she scooted across the floor, scrambling back against the refrigerator door, her busted lip wibbling, tears beginning to form beneath her eyelids. She would never get used to this. My voice came out doused with poison, "Judith…" I stepped toward her as she tried her best to back even further from me. Crawling, hands and knees, across the linoleum, looking for some sort of way to escape. Frantic. Gasping. Scared. Her knees weak, she was unable to make herself stand up. She reached up, clutching at the side of the counter, pulling herself up quick. "What’s wrong, Judith?" She didn’t say a word as she looked around her, cornered in this tiny hotel kitchen. Amidst the gentle paintings hanging on the wall of fields filled with flowers and leaves falling from trees in a peaceful park, she panicked. "Don’t you love me anymore, Judith?" She bowed her head, biting her lip, the dam breaking and tears beginning to flood her cheeks as I stood only inches from her. I reached a hand out, running it across her cheek, wiping away the tears. I lifted her chin and she looked at me, those eyes wide. She couldn’t bring herself to speak, so I spoke for her. "You love me." She nodded, breathing heavy. "I’m the only person who matters to you." She nodded, biting her lower lip. "And you forgive me." She nodded, reaching a hand up to wipe a stray tear away. "You know I only do this to you because I love you." She nodded once more as I leaned in close, kissing her busted lip. She relaxed into me, her lower lip still wibbling as she closed her eyes. I pulled my lips away slowly and whispered against hers, "…now don’t you dare scream." My fist caught her again, this time in the eye, turning one of those brilliant blue eyes black. I gripped her by the hair, pulling her head toward mine, forcing her to kiss me as she cried. She did love me. I was the only person who mattered to her. She did forgive me… but she knew better than to think that I only did this because her loved her. She knew I did this for myself, and myself alone. For ego’s sake. For power’s sake. For cruelty’s sake. For that feeling of control. That feeling of importance. To know you not only own yourself, but somebody else, to the point that that somebody else will deal with whatever you throw their way. To know that you are your own god, and someone else’s god. And besides. You’ve never truly lived until you’ve made someone else feel dead inside. *** FOUR. Solitude. Her lips parted like a canyon on fire, painted red with a mixture of lipstick and dried blood. Smoke rose out of those canyons, out from her mouth, and up toward the cold night hanging overhead. The stars reflected in the swimming, and from our balcony, it looked like a thousand lights dancing at the bottom of the water. She looked to me out of the corners of her eyes from behind a mask of cigarette smoke, her eyes painted black with bruises and her hair even messier than usual. "So," She said, words soft and as delicate as a porcelain doll. "Is this how it’s always going to be?" I looked back to her, my body leaned up against the railing, my own cigarette dangling half burnt to ash between my fingers. "What do you mean?" "I mean what I said," She looked down toward the ground below, avoiding eye contact with me. "Is this how it’s always going to be? Between you and me, I mean. Am I always going be the victim? Are you always going to be as much my tormenter as much as you are my lover?" "Are you always going to be foolish enough to stick around?" I smiled, looking away from her as well, up toward the moon. "We all have our demons. We all have trials and tribulations. Yours just happen to involve the man you claim to be in love with. Don’t hold it against me." "I didn’t say I was holding it against you," She whispered. "I asked if it was always going to be that way from now on? Things weren’t always this way, after all… You weren’t always the way you are now." "Honestly, Judith. We’re not kids anymore. The boy you loved is long gone," I sighed out smoke, the smile disappearing from my face. "You should feel grateful that I even give you a moment of my time, even if it isn’t in the most hospitable of ways. Most people, I couldn’t be shitted to even acknowledge… but, if you want my honest answer, then it has to be yes. I can’t see myself ever treating you any differently than I do now." "Oh," There was a long moment of silence as our ashes fell down toward the pool of stars. She finally sighed, "Then I suppose I can deal with only having my dreams." "Dreams are only realities outcasts." "That doesn’t mean I don’t cherish them." She paused. I couldn’t think of how to reply. She said, "My dreams are the only place I can be in the past. The only place I can be with you without having a black eye. Even more so, the only place I can be truly alone. The only place where solitude isn’t so hard to find." "Then maybe you should go get some sleep and quit complaining to me about how much your life sucks, don’t you think?" She smiled. I don’t know why, but she smiled and leaned in, kissing me on the cheek. She whispered four words, as gentle as ever. "I love you, Sean." And then she slipped back into our hotel room, leaving me alone, my cigarette burnt down to the filter. Her name was Judith, and I loved her too, in my way.
