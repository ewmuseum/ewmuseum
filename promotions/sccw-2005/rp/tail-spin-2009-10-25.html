Tail Spin

25 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/25/09 For someone in this business, there was nothing more refreshing than going into work and leaving sans aches and pains. Being on the receiving end of Julissa Minorez's heel probably should have left its mark, but Thad carried his red-and-black travel bag over his shoulder and walked out of the Convocation Center feeling pretty damn great. Quasar's mood seemed to coincide with his, as the usually oft-reserved dog leapt around Thad's path and nipped playfully at his outstretched hands. Thad encouraged his aggressive behavior by tapping at the sides of his furry face in succession, causing Quasar to dart his head to and fro. When they had reached the exit, Quasar seized the temporary moment of distraction and chomped down on his owner's arm, lightly gnawing on it as if it were a rawhide toy. Thad ignored him as he pushed the door open and walked into the rear lot. Small cliques of no more than two or three to a group dotted the white cement curb. It was made up of mostly crew members and production assistants, but Thad also noticed a couple of members of the talent roster scattered around the area. Ron, a key grip and veteran of the staff who'd been around since the FUSE days, gave a friendly wave and a chuckle toward the kid who's platinum blond hair poured out slightly from the rim of his worn-out Braves cap. 'Better watch out, Thad,' he called out. 'Whatsa matter? You not feedin' him enough or somethin'?' 'Nope,' Thad grinned, walking toward him and another forty-something donned in gray work pants and a tucked-in SCCW T-shirt. He was a bigger guy who's name Thad couldn't remember. 'You wanna take him home with you for the week?' 'Shit,' Ron scoffed, reaching down to pat the golden retriever on the head. 'Old lady'd kill me if I brought someone home better lookin' than her.' An eruption of laughter rumbled from Ron, Thad, and the unknown ring-constructing warrior standing just a few paces from the exit. The loud cackling of the third man was enough to draw attention from the surrounding groups. One individual in particular took notice, watching Thad's body quiver with laughter. Only hours ago, she had been the recipient of an on-screen hug from the little moon man, and until now she hadn't seen him without the paint on his face. Turning to her old friend, a little upturn formed on her lips as she motioned her hand toward where he stood. 'So cute. He looks like a twelve-year-old without the make-up on.' Katsidy smiled and nodded, simultaneously eying him up and down. 'And a fifteen-year-old with the make-up. It'd be like a teacher-slash-student role play.' Mackenzie Malone arched an eyebrow and frowned at the notion suggested by the perpetually horny Shaw. 'Kathryn, jeez...' 'What?' She shrugged her shoulders and rolled her eyes slightly in defense. 'At least with the paint on, I think that's legal in a couple of states.' 'I can promise you that's not an interest of mine. Seriously,' Mac continued, pausing momentarily to de-wrinkle the sleeves of her black cardigan, 'I need another affair with a wrestler like I need a chair shot to the head.' Kat grinned. 'You know the latter's coming soon enough. Might as well go for broke.' Mackenzie released a deflating sigh. She touched her fingertips to her brow and shook her head. Even though it had been over a year since any interaction had occurred with her, two things hadn't changed about Kathryn Shaw: she was still very pretty, and her mind still functioned as though it belonged to a puberty-stricken boy. It was amazing that she got along as well as she did with the majority of women she worked with. Mackenzie didn't know if that was because her whorish traits caused her to be less judgmental to the rest of her peers, or if it was just impossible to hate someone who's hair was so bouncy. Although she had presumably cut off all ties with the Dead Man's Hand, Mac and Katsidy had one of those sorority-type friendships that would probably keep them connected for a long time to come. 'Even if it were tempting,' Mackenzie hypothesized, 'it's been a few weeks now and not even so much as a friendly wave when we're backstage. It'd be a miracle if he even acknowledges me when he walks out to his car tonight.' Katsidy blinked, and thoughts flashed in her facial expression. 'Hm,' she mused, 'probably one of those shy types. I mean, have you looked at yourself lately? No wonder he hasn't talked to you! His tongue would roll out onto the floor!' Kenzie smiled and reached across to give Shaw a hug. 'I love you.' Kathryn squeezed back, then gave her surrogate sister a big smooch on the cheek. 'I know! But to be honest,' she continued, cupping her face in her palms, 'he's an awkward kid who plays an awkward kid on TV. The closest type of communication to someone as beautiful as you was probably a signature from the prom queen in his high school yearbook.' Mackenzie's cheeks turned pink. Whether it was her gushing due to the compliment she'd been paid, or the fear that Katsidy was about to go in for a French kiss, we'll never know -- because a friendly voice is there to interrupt their intimate moment. 'Woof!' No matter how many awful acts you may have perpetrated in the name of the Director, one thing remains constant when it comes to women (and the majority of men not named Wyatt Connors): animals greeting you unexpectedly will make you giddy. 'Hey there, handsome boy!' Mackenzie cooed, as both she and Kathi dropped down to one knee, lavishing Quasar with all the attention that his doggie heart desired. 'Oh goodness! You a sweeeeeeeeeeeetie, yes you is!' Kathryn's imitation of a gravel-throated cuddle monster was rather cute, if not slightly entertaining. She held Quasar's face with her hands much like she had with Mac only seconds before. This time, though, we knew a make-out session was inevitable. 'Eww!' Katsidy squealed as the retriever covered her nose with puppy drool. Not to be outdone, Mackenzie wrapped her arms around the dog's belly and embraced him, resting her head on top of his and lightly giving him a squeeze. 'So snuggly!' As she opened her eyes and looked up, she grinned at the sight of the dog's owner who observed the display of affection with a sheepish grin on his face. 'Hey,' she greeted Thad, releasing the beast and collecting herself as she rose to her feet. Thad raised a hand up to wave. 'Hey,' he responded. 'Um, you've got, some uh, dog hair...' He reached out toward her to brush the dander off her sweater, but then immediately retracted back bashfully. If he could exit his body and front kick himself in his own ass for that smooth move, he would've gladly done so. 'Oh, thanks!' Mackenzie made a half-assed attempt at shaking the dislodged fur off her cardigan. Internally, she chuckled at the unexpected gentle Southern twang in his accent. You'd have never known it listening to him in character. 'Sorry he's bothering ya'll...uh...you ladies.' Mackenzie found it even more amusing that he tried to cover up said accent. 'He won't do this when I take him to the park and try to meet girls that way,' Thad joked. 'Why have him, then? I thought that's why all guys took their dogs out in public,' Mackenzie responded, motioning to the positive effect in action as Katsidy ignored the two, still beaming over the four-legged blond. 'Only reason I've got him is to save money on the cost of a blanket.' 'Awwww,' Mack cried, reaching back and giving the dog another friendly shooka-shooka on his ears. 'But wouldn't buying a blanket actually cost less?' 'Uh...' Thad stuttered, tripping over his brain-to-mouth decoding like it were an obstacle on Double Dare. 'I...yeah. I should stop trying to be clever, like...ever.' He removed his Braves cap and brushed his hair back with his hand, then readjusted it back onto his head. Mackenzie just grinned at the kid. The Sex Kitten was absolutely right: he was just an awkward boy playing an awkward character on television. It was admittedly very endearing, and a refreshing change of pace as compared to 95% of the smooth-talkers involved in this business. 'Sorry, that came off as bitchy, didn't it?' While she had pretty much pegged him in a matter of moments, Thad was having a time of it trying to figure out Miss Malone. Or was it Mrs. Malone? His hat-removing motions allowed him the secret opportunity to glance at her hand for any sign of a ring on her finger -- one of the first things he did when he was physically attracted to a woman. However, as much as he'd done this, he always forgot which hand was the correct one. He also didn't know whether or not a rock was necessary for it to be a wedding ring. Sure enough, on her right fourth finger was a piece of jewelry, but it was one of those rings that looked snake-like and decorative. Probably a 'miss,' but he wasn't too sure. 'I think 'snarky' is a better term for it, so nah. No harm done,' Thad responded. Although the gears continued to turn, he did not particularly feel like analyzing his brand new Jane Jetson any further, so he sought to keep the chatter light-hearted. 'You have any pets?' Mackenzie's face soured a little, and a frown formed on her lips. 'I had a kitty cat until about three months ago. Mr. Pickles. But he got out of my house one day and never came back,' she explained. Jesus fuck, Thad. Great choice on the topic. 'Wow,' he stammered, this time reaching out to grab her hand but AGAIN stopping in midair yet trying again to make it seem casual. 'I'm sorry I brought it up.' In a move that startled him, Mackenzie not only noticed his attempted comfort gesture -- but grabbed his hand that hung in the space between them and squeezed it. 'It's okay,' she smiled. 'I didn't have a chance to neuter him, so he's probably a pimp to all the strays in my hometown.' Thad chuckled, letting the hand-holding linger just a little longer before they broke free. 'He's a regular 'Cat' Williams now.' Somewhere in the distance, the sound of crickets were interrupted by the sound of a car crashing into a streetlight. 'Once again,' Thad said, face-palming himself, 'jokes plus me equal Enola Gay. I think I'm gonna get out of here before a big hook comes out and pulls me off the planet Earth.' Thad then walked over parallel to Mackenzie and bent down to address the happy couple in front of them. It appeared as though Kathryn was attempting to teach Quasar to do the hand jive with the way she held his paws in front of her. Thad fished into the flap of his duffel bag and retrieved a Beggin' Strip, then held it in his fist. 'Give him this,' he instructed Kat, nodding toward his hand. She held her hand out and watched the treat fall into it, then lovingly cooed to Quasar. 'How much do you love mommy?' She affectionately asked, holding the salty horse byproduct out. Quasar licked it off instantaneously, and the chomping sounds resonated within a seven-mile radius. 'I mean, how much do you love Auntie Kathi?' Quasar's ears and eyes suddenly perked, which led him to begin pacing around the vicinity with his nose to the ground. 'Where you goin', boy?' Thad cried out as his dog sniffed every person in sight. Realizing that retrieving him would be a physical task, he smiled at both Mackenzie and Kathryn and motioned backwards with his thumb. 'Sorry ya'll, but I better go get him before he runs off or something. Nice seeing you two,' he said, tipping the bill of his had like a cowboy and turning quickly on his heel. However, Mackenzie wasn't quite done with him yet. 'Wait!' Thad spun back around, revealing a quizzical look. 'We should...well, we should get a drink or something tonight. You know, for characters' sake,' she suggested, sure to remember to disavow any social implications of the offer. 'I, uh...' Thad pondered, then quickly came to a resolution. 'Can I get a rain check on that? Tonight's kinda bad, and I have a long drive ahead of me, but I'll give you a call soon and uh...yeah.' By the 'uh...yeah,' Thad had already started to pace backwards and turn 45 degrees before Mackenzie could muster out a reply. 'Okay,' she answered. And then, he was out of earshot, walking quickly in the direction of his dog, who had commenced to watering a nearby bush in one of the small islands in the parking lot. Kathi, standing behind Mackenzie, leaned against her and placed her chin on her shoulder. 'There ya go. He'll call you,' she assured, hugging her from behind. Mackenzie stood there, a little dumbfounded at being flat out rejected like that. It wasn't that she thought he wasn't busy, but it was something else... 'How's he going to call if he doesn't have my number?' ----------------------------------------- Know those songs that you hear again for the first time in a long time, and you wonder why you ever forgot about it to begin with? Then, you're just entranced, and you listen to it about fifty times in a row -- you'd listen more than that if you weren't worried that it'd just make you end up hating it. Arlo Guthrie's cover of 'City of New Orleans' was currently that song for Thad Denver, as it looped continuously on his iPod on the hour and thirty-minute drive from San Antonio to Austin where he was staying for the remainder of the week. The song had really stood the test of time, and it described him...hell, everyone who had a nomadic career...to a T. 'I'll be gone five-hundred miles when the day is done.' Eight minutes into the ride, Quasar had already fallen asleep in the backseat. It was incredible the way that a dog's mind functioned. Eat, play, sleep, repeat. What was even more interesting was the way they reacted to simple vocal triggers and their relativity to commands. Quasar, not unlike many semi-celebrity pups, would sit when you told him, 'Sit.' He would bark when you told him, 'Speak.' He would jump and bark when you announced, 'Treat!' So many human words were conditioned into his peanut-sized brain, it was a wonder his above-average intellectual capacity didn't afford him the capability to talk (in real life, of course, not via voice-over from Rip Torn.) If he could talk, Thad wondered, he would have had to fight back tears when he heard the hope in his voice as he asked 'Mommy?' back at the arena. Once Shaw had uttered the word 'Kathi,' Thad knew exactly why Quasar had walked off -- Kathleen. He thought mom had come back. Eat, play, sleep, repeat. The two were one in the same, with the only difference between Thad and the dog was one that the human envied. Seconds after the incident, Quasar had found his tail an interesting distraction and began to chase it momentarily -- then forgot all about the mention of Mommy. Ten minutes later in this rental Subaru, nothing was able to distract Thad enough to forget about her. Not even the tail of Mackenzie Malone.
