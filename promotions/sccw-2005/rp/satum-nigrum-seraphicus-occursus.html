Satum Nigrum Seraphicus: Occursus

Date: Pre-Mask Disclaimer: This is the first part to a three-part series that will culminate at the Pay-Per-View at the end of the arc. This will coincide with the storyline, as well, giving insight to the on-screen actions. / The constant buzz that comes from standing still in one of the world’s most popular airports swarms around the head of the young, blond man sitting in a row of seats just next to the United Airlines gate waiting for service to Hong Kong. He sits next to a black canvas backpack and he waits his turn to get out of this God-forsaken town. Leon looks to his watch and rolls his eyes, Twenty minutes. He scans over pages from his copy of ‘Art of War’ by Sun Tzu. A woman sits down next to him, of Asian descent, and glances in his direction. He nudges away from her, keeping to himself, and shields the title from her. She smiles at him, mildly, and then opens up her purse to stash her ticket. "What are you reading?" Her accent, thick and natural, makes it a bit difficult to understand. He nods a few seconds later, gesturing he doesn’t quite understand. "You’re book. What is the title?" "The Art of War," he says with a nod and smile, "have you read it?" "Oh yes, many, many times," she smiles and nods, letting some of the hazel beauty shine through a slender slit. "My grandfather used to read it to us every time he visit from Tokyo." "You live here?" he stops himself. "I’m sorry. I thought…" "I live in Hong Kong?" she giggles, waving her hands around. "No, not anymore. I was sixteen when father move us here. Better life, he say. Better opportunity." "Right. Better life," he adds, through a sarcastic grin, and nods at the people around him. "Tokyo is where my better life is. Getting’ out of this dump." "To China?" she interjects, missing his point. He smirks at her, amazed, and runs a hand through his wavy, blond hair. He rolls his eyes at the thought of going into the whole spiel for her in the next forty minutes. "Since I can remember, I’ve been good. I help others, I am kind, and, most of all, I do what I feel the Lord wants me to do. Now…now I just can’t help but feel I have a calling, a duty to fulfill. Something that will, some day, might be able to help people. I know it’s weird…but," he takes a second to think, "I’m working on it." She stares at him, fighting back a giggle, and smiles at him with her eyes. She looks down, trying not to make it too embarrassing for the boy. "Is that why you read Art of War?" Looking up, her eyes lock with his, and she removes the stray strands of black hair from her face. His face opens up a bit and smiles. "Yes," he says, easing the tension, "that is why I read it. I also feel like it could help me, down the road. I probably will need it after I’ve been in Hong Kong for a few weeks." "A few weeks?" she shakes her head, laughing at the thought. "A blond, American boy like you? More like a few days.." "Oh, oh!" His face wrinkles at her joke, pulling his head back, and snorts. "Who knew Chinese girls had such senses of humor?" She replies with a sarcastic smile of her own and sticks out her tongue a bit. Her face is porcelain, placid, and calm. Seamless and without a scratch, it crosses the young man’s mind that it is impossible for her to be from the meanest city in the world. Full of criminals, ninjas, and ruthless Japanese mobsters – nothing beautiful could come out of that pit without having something wrong with her. "So," she cuts into his thought with a soft, sweet voice. ‘Do you have a name?" He looks at her. For the past two days, he’s been planning to use an alternate name, a moniker, for people he felt should remember him. Unfortunately, this girl is the first he meets on the journey, and his courage isn’t quite what it needs to be. "Leon," he bows his head, "Leon Christmas." "Hi, Leon," she says with a smile before pointing to her chest softly, "I’m Li." Leon looks toward her hand and notices the cleavage below. His eyes grow in size, narrowing in on his first glimpse at flesh in quite some time. "Li. That’s a nice name." Leon pauses, thinking about what the old boy from Colorado might do in this situation; punish himself in his mind for letting his eyes stray from this heavenly creatures face. A woman willing to waste her time and talk with him; one, in return, that is worth talking to. "That’s a beautiful necklace," he says, looking at the Chinese charm dangling from her neck. "What does it mean?" His mind runs away with itself, undressing her with his eyes and then even imagines her naked, legs open, and body popping up over black silk sheets. Leon immediately flinches at the thoughts. He sits up, adjusting his position, and then comes back to the real world. "Good Luck," she says, still smiling. Good Luck, Leon thinks to himself. His mind begins to entertain the thought of putting his hands all over her bare skin and touching each inch of her surging body. "It’s very nice," he says, fighting back all the new emotions brewing beneath the cool surface of his ice-blue eyes. My son, he hears from above. Have you forgotten that sins of the flesh are the gateway for the Darkness? This is a test from Lucifer himself. Choose wisely. Leon looks to the ceiling of the airport, noticing the skylight that lets the sun beam in from above. The rays hit the floor in front of them, but just as soon as Leon notices, they wash away due to the clouds rolling in. Thick, fat, bubbly clouds that mean only one thing...Darkness // Leon steps off the plane. On his right arm is Li and the other arm is his backpack. The two stand in the middle of the airport just after freshly landing on Chinese soil, look to one another with uncertainty. "Where do you go now?" Li asks, holding onto Leon’s arm. He looks around, smiling at the fact he is out of the place he despises to the very core of his soul. "Anywhere." "You are not ready," she says honestly. "You will get hurt." Leon looks at her with a glare of disbelief, "And you’re the expert on this how? I don’t remember asking you if I was ready or not." "My grandfather," she says, gesturing to the book still in Leon’s right hand, "the man who read that book. His house is not far from here, ten minutes." Leon looks at her for a long three seconds of contemplation. He takes one last look at her and then steps out with his right foot. "I’ll be fine on my own. Thanks for everything though. Oh yeah," Leon stops, looking back to her one more time, "Good luck." Li watches the blond boy take his chances and walk down the corridor, tightly gripping his bag in his right hand. Leon thinks about running to the heart of the city and finding a gang to join. Then he thinks about finding a job. The last thought is food, and how in the hell he’s going to get any now that he’s left the only person he knows in the treacherous capital of China. I am with you, my son, Leon hears the Lord say as he steps onto the escalator. I will carry you. I am your strength. I am your savior. Believe in me and I grant you eternal life. Be righteous. Be good. Leon throws a lot of responses around his head a bit before he stops and stares ahead, gripping his bag even tighter. After an awkward moment of peace comes over the young man, he finally drops his lower lip and adjusts his focus onto the Heavens beyond the concrete walls around him. We’ll just call this Holy Justice. /// Three weeks and a day into this journey, Leon Christmas is feeling the effects of not eating on a regular schedule. His hands tremor, his eyes, dim, and he moves around like a zombie looking for its’ next to last meal. His black backpack still hangs over his shoulder, though not as safe as it was. He lurches around the corner. "What the?" he whispers to himself, ducking back into the shadows. A sign of malnourishment, the delusion and loss of reality is showing through all senses. Leon doesn’t get all the way down that Dead End but is well on his way to becoming a street walker in downtown Hong Kong. He has another idea. He locks onto a food cart moving up the street. Fruits are the main item for sale. The man moving it is much smaller than Leon and he sees that within seconds. This causes his eyes to zero in on the weak limbs of the cart runner. Be righteous, he hears, gazing to the stars. Be good. Leon slowly begins to stalk up the street as his body adapts to a new feeling he has never felt before. This feeling consumes him as he nears the cart. He can taste the apples and strawberries. He can smell the juices running down his face and can imagine throwing the pits into the bushes. The feeling comes over him like a tidal wave. The feeling of being helplessly powerful takes over his limbs before Leon realizes he is running at the cart. His fist crashes into the back of the small cart as he leaps at it desperately. The wooden frame crashes onto the rear end, tossing the Chinese man off of his feet and trapping him on the cart. Leon opens his now empty backpack and fills it with whatever he can; carrots, apples, and even lettuce. He thinks to himself how he would never let this happen to him and then, before the job is done, he looks at the man. "Oh Christ," Leon says. Yes, my son? "Not you!" he snaps, again glancing up to the skies, "What have I done?" Leon shuffles around, looking at the man who is holding his ribs and looking down at the piece of wood that stuck him on the impact of the cart. Leon’s first reaction is to run but realizes that, if he does, this man will die if he doesn’t get help. A short, cautious few steps make enough sound to fill the silence of guilt. Leon rushes toward the man, who buckles in fear of another attack that could kill him, and then removes a knife. He holds it with caution. The weary eyes of his audience follow the shiny blade wherever it goes with wide eyes. Leon then grips the rope restraining the man to his cart and cuts it, quickly, before he takes off toward the shadows. "Sorry!" he cries, running into the alley, before crouching behind a dumpster. 'I'm really sorry!' Leon watches the Chinese man gather his product. He remounts his cart, moving it down the road and on his way, thanking the Lord for letting him live one more day. Leon stands up and walks into the light the street lamps drop on the blacktop in the dead parts of Hong Kong. "Now, get his arms!" Leon hears from behind before trying to scramble away. To no avail, he gets a few seconds to run away before a duo of quick and agile men drag him down to the ground with a loud OOF!. A third man slowly steps out form the darkness and stands above Leon, who tries to fight his way free but can’t get an inch. "You are... very brave," he starts with barely audible English. "You quick, strong, fearless." The man kneels down to inspect the captive. "But very, very dumb." Leon adjusts his look from scared to angry. His first move is to look him in the eye but then squirms some more, inching himself forward, and then is nose to nose with the bald Chinese man. "You forgot to tell your men to grab my legs." A long pause comes between Leon and the bald man, locking eyes and waiting for either one to move first. Leon spins around, kicking one man in the jaw and then comes to his back. He flings out his right foot again, kicking in the bald man’s knee, and then looks up to the big man above him. Leon then hears a click and cold steel press to his temple. "Don’t move mother-" he hears but then doesn’t as the steel leaves and drops to the ground. Leon turns to spot a tall man whose face can’t be seen from the angle. The street lamp highlights the massive heap at both of their feet. "Umm," Leon looks at the body below and then at the blade in the big man’s hands. "Thanks?" The dark figure laughs aloud, "Thank you. I finally got to hit somebody." "So who are you?" Leon gestures to the guys around him, "Some kind of ninja?" "Another thing I wanted to talk to you about," the man says. He takes a step back and stretches his arm out, opening the back door of a black sedan. Inside the car, causing Leon to drop his jaw is the girl form the plane, Li, sitting in a black satin dress with those chopstick things in her hair. Her lips pop out to him with red lipstick. "What is she doing here?" Leon says, looking at the big man who is chuckling and moving toward the driver’s seat. The American looks on with his face spelling CONFUSED. Li leans back and reveals an older looking man. He has a long face and a strong jaw. Het adjust his tie while looking at girl next to him. He smiles, politely, and looks at Leon. "Please," the old man says, "get in." //// The car moves patiently through the city streets of Hong Kong. The back seat, which happens to have a reverse bench for such occasions and to fit more…comfortably, holds three people. Leon, looks at the other two. "Leonsan," the old man speaks, looking out the window on his side of the car, "do you know who those men were back there? They were my men. In fact, my son’s knee was the one you so graciously kicked in just now. Thank you. I will be paying for that, I am sure." Leon takes a deep breath as he shuffles to sit up straight. He looks at the woman and then the man, in fear for his life, "Oh, oh shit. I, I didn’t… he was your son?" "Your language," the old man says, waving his hand toward the girl next to him. "Koji is my brother," Li interjects. "And now you sit here, before me, with a whole lot of explaining to do," the old man pauses to glare at Leon, making him extremely uncomfortable. "My daughter says you have high hopes to become a warrior, to learn how to fight. You think you can just come over here, get a piece of paper, and then go back to where you came from? Then you will call yourself a warrior?" Leon looks at Li, uncomfortably, and glares at her, "No, well, I— look, it’s just that… I’m sorry. I’m tired, I’m hungry. You’re daughter-" "What about my daughter?" the old man barks. He looks to her with a mighty scowl. She turns away. Leon stops looking at the beautiful girl across from him, bowing his head. "Nothing, sir." "Is that what you think?" the old man says, sharp and precise. "You may come to my homeland and learn the ways of our warriors, only to go back and exploit it for money and fame? Or do you want to learn the ways of a true warrior?" Leon looks up, briefly at Li first but then at the old man. "I do!" "If you do," the grey-hair shouts, "you must follow a set of rules that all are expected to live by." Leon stops to think for a second and then looks at him again, "A set of rules—like the Commandments?" The old man reaches out, grabs Leon by the collar, and wraps his arm around his neck. He then drives the edge of his free hand into the ribs of Leon. The blond man winces, bruising immediately, and his eyes lock on with Li’s, whose stare on, horrified. The pain subsides when he finds Li’s eyes looking at him; telling him, in a way, that he will be alright. "Rule number one," the man says, pushing him back to his seat, "No questions." Leon looks up, holding his ribs, and scowls at the duo sitting across from him. He opens his mouth, lowers his brow, and lets the fire growing inside almost come out in full force. His eyes narrow as the old man goes on without a second’s hesitation. "Questions are a sign of the weak," the old man begins. "The quest for knowledge is one of patience and purity. Those who ask questions are impatient, impure, and cannot understand the true meaning of the answer unless they find it themselves. What people do not understand, they question. What people question, they fear. And when people fear something… it consumes them. Fear opens the cracks of our mind, body, and soul…to darkness." "A warrior’s strength comes from knowledge, certainty, experience." He simply stares ahead at Leon, waiting for nothing and no one. "One must have knowledge of the surroundings, knowledge of the enemy, and most importantly, knowledge of one’s self. A warrior’s mind has no room for questions, or queries, nor puzzles or trivial matters. A true warrior knows the path, regardless of the outcome." Leon smiles, cunningly, "You’re damn right I do!" "Rule number two," he says, slapping the man across the face with a whack like a wooden paddle would make. "Always maintain virtue." "Virtue?" Leon says just before the man reaches out, grabs his collar, and slaps him twice with the fat part of his hand. "Rule One, Leon," he softly reminds the young man, "Remember Rule number one." Leon smirks at Li before sitting back, winking at her while her father isn’t looking. Leon looks to the old man, scanning his strong arms and upper torso, and then the rest of the car, and its’ fine leather and luxurious seats. "So now wh—I mean, yes. I am ready. I want to learn the ways of the warrior." "So be it," he says. "Know this, my pupil; this path is one that lasts for life. Are you willing to learn?" "Are you willing to teach me?" Leon asks. "Yes, Leon," he pauses, putting the shiny Zippo back into his pocket. "I am your Teacher," the grey-hair says, right before slapping Leon twice more on his rosy red face. He frowns and sits back to enjoy his fine cigar. Leon sits up with a bright red face. "You," the Teacher says through a raspy exhale. "You are my student." ///// Good morning, my son, Leon hears, startling him from his peaceful slumber of maidens and glory. Opening his eyes and looking at the sunrise before him, Leon feels the warm rays wash over him as they pour over the tree line. He looks around, rolling over to notice a pile of fresh clothes on the chair at his bedside. He stands, pulling the pants from the bottom of the pile and admires their spacious and roomy feel before slipping them on. He wastes no time putting the shirt on. The rattle of the knob spins Leon on his heels to watch the door swing open. "Breakfast is in five minutes," Li says without making eye contact. 'I suggest you be downstairs in three." She leaves just as soon as she came. Leon lowers his brow and looks at the shirt near his knees. He throws it over his head like a cape and pulls it down. Looking in the mirror, he admires how it suits him – which it doesn’t. He kicks his feet around after sliding them into a pair of black slippers. His eyes begin to wander around the room. Home? he contemplates. ////// Until Next Time
