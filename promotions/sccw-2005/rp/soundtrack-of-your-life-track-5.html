Soundtrack of your life: Track 5

Date: Today Matt Mays and El Torpedo, "Tall Trees" (Terminal Romance, 2008) July 24, 2009 The skies hold no interest, just clouds and pollution. When I first started with this company, the idea of flight was coupled with such emotion that I did not know if it was because of anxiety or just simple childlike excitement that made my heart skip a beat or two. But that wonderment fled fast and gave way to the dread that is attached to choirs and a distain for over processed, under proportioned food. I thought about how great it was to be an hour away from home, and how dreary it was that I was due back on to the sheet metal tempter of fate in few short days. I thought about several things that were not related at all to the task of driving. It was always a great isolated environment in that car, but was gone in flashes and blurs. It might have just been one blink, who knows, but there I was, making that oh so familiar left turn on Ninth Ave. And there it was, the rubble strewn to the sides of a now dirt road. The daunting ruts and random pot ditches dared me to drive that last stretch home. Fuck, I hate losing dares to inanimate objects. I abandoned my car up the street, in a spot I would be able to see from the kitchen, and carried my luggage and self that last block. How awkward it was to walk along a disappearing sidewalk, made only so much more annoying by the actions of a careless alien. He zoomed down that road he had no business being on with a speed that I hate to see even when this thing was paved. I made the final yards to the driveway in a cloud of dirty that resembled Pig Pen. I stumbled up those steps and into the backroom, where I promptly unloaded those bags from my shoulders and stood looking out of screen windows. I cast a gaze out to the humble yard out back. Its size was nothing too impressive, punctuated with two trees, but damn if it wasn’t home. That little piece of turf, that dirt you call home, even if my name isn’t on the deed. So many times I had wished I saw the Atlantic from my backroom, but the truth was I wouldn’t have it any other way. But you get bored quick when you look out to the same old sight, and look into back yards of neighbours I cared not to know. I jutted my hand into my pocket and yanked out the keys that had so many times in my travels stabbed me in the leg, and remind me of home. But why, I ask, is it that whenever you are busting to use the facilities does the key ring become the most difficult game of match? I slipped inside with the greatest of hurries, and tended to the business at hand. The cool water that I tossed over my face, after washing my hands was just about as relaxing and bearing of relief as the previous minute. But the thing about relief is that it gives way quick to whatever issue decides to give you worry. At the present, that worry was that of the lacklustre response to my arrival. I had noted that as the frequency of my time away from here there was less hoopla surrounding my return, but I wasn’t even getting a hello. I think they expected me home today. "Hello," echoed back to me, and nothing more than the sound of my own voice bouncing about the hallway. The silence of this place was much more commonplace since Tim took up and found himself a place to call his own. Communication here, these days, were far less frequent and often left on a note on the fridge, so to the kitchen I went. Right where I thought it would be, the ever wise wipe board spoke: "Gone to Tim’s for BBQ. You should come over when you get home. Love Parents." I opened up the contents of the ice box to grab a drink and ponder over the situation that presented itself. In the moment of decision to conquer thirst, a cheap powdery beverage was the answer. The simplicity of its chemistry had only heightened its tastiness: water, sugar, and red And then it hit me; Fuck this. Why is it that this thing has become a Rosetta Stone in dialogue with those two? When did we become vessels passing by in a foggy seascape? This was not what I chose in pursuit of happiness. Why did they deem it so damn important to cut me out, now? I steamed out of the kitchen, thinking it best that I leave my thoughts along with the leftovers of meals I was not apart of. It was to the basement and a quick workout and shower combination. The sweat poured and I cooled down. It is this that gets me away from myself. It is this that gives me the feeling that the man I see projected on the screen in SCCW replays is not the same as the reflection in it when I shut off the feed in frustration. But when he died away with the television, I remained on the other side. I am the calm, reasonable one that gets to regret the impression that I am giving anyone. But still thinking that I am giving any impression to so many as this has given me the opportunity to do so is enough to give goose flesh. It was a pair of envelopes on the table that stopped my second exit from the kitchen. Pink envelopes tend to do that to a guy who lives in an economic terror. Sure enough, upon a quick inspection, the first tiny parcel was made out to my name, and in a handwriting not uncommon to first graders, or people writing with the wrong hand. With hurried eyes I started into it. "Dear Mitchell Quinlan, Yada-yada-yada … big fan ... yada-yada … thank you. Signed, some random chick." And it was obvious who wrote this. It is the same guy who went to the extreme of paying fifty five cents for a stamp two years prior. It is the same guy who will be treating me to whatever animal he has burning on the barbeque. I had to go there now. I never bothered with the second. ----- It was a short walk to Tim’s. He enjoyed the independence of owning his own set of four walls, between which he ruled, and the convenience of walking back to his parents place to dine on their food, our food, whenever he cared. Yeah, he did it right. Five blocks south, two east. Everything is measured in a radial distance from home. Quite the crowd he had gathered here, I thought passing by the cars in double file along the street. All hope for a quick dine and dash was all but dashed on that sight alone. And sure as shat as I got closer I heard the sounds of boisterous laughter too loud to be natural. We didn’t know anyone that funny. "Hey! Look who decided to make it over!" my dad proclaimed already one beer too deep. There went the sneak attack, for that moment at least. "Hey, hey! Psychster," my brother greeted me as I stepped over his three and one half foot fence. "How was the flight?" "Ah, you know. Entirely too long, and with the same quality food they serve our boys out in the desert." I said, flashing him a smile. He led me past a group of people I should have known, but would be screwed to name, all of whom had a face full of hot dog or hamburger. I must have made some face, displayed my disappointment at the dolling out of the food before I had a chance to get any, and the doubt that these fiendish feasters had left a scrap for me. "Don’t look so sad," Tim started, "we saved you a dog. We all know how much you love wieners." There was that charm; the ability that your brother has to stick a barb an inch deep and smile. "Hardy-har-har…ass. Oh, by the way, your write-like-a-little-girl classes are showing through. Really, another fan mail?" "Well, actually, I had a hand in that one." I didn’t recognize the voice, other than that it did not belong to anyone whose balls have dropped. Turns out it was because she didn’t have any, or so I hope. From behind my brother appeared a slender and slight creature that I cannot recall seeing before. Hers was a face full of character upon which sat a plume of light brunette hair. A black Zeppelin tee was stretched tight to her pot belly, and she donned a pair of loose, stone washed jeans, which Tim had found a place to put has hand. I think I was getting the picture here. "Honders, this is," Tim started. And yes, he was referring to me. In his infinite creativity, he saw fit to call be by a thousand and one names, not one being Mitchell. But he was cut off during the introduction attempt by the girl too impatient or too proud to be introduced by anyone else. "This is," she said with a smile. Onion stuck in her teeth and all. First impressions, eh? "Joanne. And you must be the famous spandex superstar." A lot of spunk to this one. The jet lag was hindering my ability to draw that quick comeback and shut her up. All I could think was to shut her up the old fashion way. That wouldn’t do then. Just take the jab, and wait for the opening you need. "Mitchell." And I bowed my head. "But really though, you should think about a different material. Looks like you are smuggling produce on TV." "How the hell do you get S-C-C-Dub? Cinemax ain’t exactly basic cable around here." "A girl’s got to have her secrets." "Just one of them being satellite TV," Tim chimed in, before forcing into my hands an overly crisp hotdog, and buggering off to his hosting duties. "Thanks," I muttered, shuffling away from Tim’s - well, I still really don’t know what to call her - and over to the few faces that I did recognize. Tim had managed to fill his back yard full of people, and damn if all I knew was the folks. Work associates, and neighbours I assumed. And all people that I didn’t care to talk with. "So, how was this trip?" my mother asked, seeing my face for the first time in two weeks. She didn’t need to know that I was left lying on the locker room floor. She didn’t need to know that I got my revenge, albeit with some collateral damage. She didn’t want to know what I did. "It was fine. How has everything been back here?" "It has been normal," she reassured, and proceeded with some story of work and her lack of enthusiasm for her help. To this my father chimed in about how hard his work had been. Long story short, five minutes of bitching passed, and my burnt dog was already working its way through the digestive system. For all that they complain of it, I wonder what they will talk about when they retire. This is where, when we did talk these days, our conversations ended. I cannot believe that no matter what I am facing in my life, they have always been through it, only it was worse, and now I am going to hear about it. In the face of the unknown, I had no supporting cast. Just about the only way I guess I would want it though. I’d have had my food. I’ve talked with the ones I wanted to, that I could. I just wanted to get back to my own bed and crash. I made no outro speech, just vanishing through the house. When I did get home, I did not drift so peacefully into a slumber as planned. No, it was in this quiet, the uninterrupted silence, that my mind plagued me with such thoughts. What is the shelf life for a happy family dynamic? Whatever it is, I cannot help but think ours has past the expiration date. Mom and Dad had become impossible to talk with. Tim was no longer under the same roof. Good for him though. He kept his sanity. And now this . . . girlfriend? Hell, I suppose I might look at myself in this entire deal. For the past two years now it has been work, train, and the weekend getaways to the armpits of Ontario to apply the craft. Now that I was on the other side of the continent didn’t help either. Was this really the hidden costs of following a dream? I tossed in that bed until I thought it was no use, I was going to be awake forever. And then my parents came back. They called out for me. I kept those eyes shut and prayed for morning. ---- I sat in peace. I breathed in the brisk morning air. I meditated about all that this place meant to me, and what loosing it would mean. "Hey Von." I didn’t expect him to be up this early, let alone to seek me out here. "Tim, ‘morning," I offered, turned my head to see his outline in shadow, with the lazy sun hanging behind him. "Quite the Houdini you pulled last night. Had a lot of people there excited to meet you." "Another group of jerkoffs looking to test themselves against a wrestler?" There was nothing kind in the words I spoke, and I knew it. I regretted it as the conversation carried. "Not at all, you are the closest thing these people have come across that could be considered a celebrity." He walked slowly to me, looking about him as he went. He couldn’t help but soak this place in for himself. "So, you are sitting in the yard of the elementary school on a Saturday, in the summer?" "Yeah, when you put it like that, I guess I realize that I am a total nerd. But why are you here then?" "This is where ‘rents said I could find you. And besides, you aren’t the only one who has a few memories about this place." Tim had this smile crawl over his face as he looked at the basketball nets. "Me and Sears are still the kings of this court," he said with a chuckle. "I remember getting a few W’s in this place, myself. I think the last one though was when you and I took on those two randoms. It’s been a while." "But it feels just like yesterday, ya know?" Here before me stood my twenty seven year old brother, and the 12 year old that reigned over this school yard fifteen years prior; two beings occupying the same space at the same time. "Yeah, I do. Hell, just over there," I lifted my arm pointing to the school and the window into the library, "I can still remember chasing Ryan down, scrambling for a basketball rolling on the ground, and he gets the bright idea of trying to kick it off the little bit of wall." "And he put it through the window?" "That he did, got in some shit for it too." Some time past before either of us parted our lips to speak. We just took the time to enjoy the place. Amaze at just how big it seemed when we were but wee children. Remember the limitless potential that each kid had when they started here. Think about the good friends that grew into strangers. "It all went too fast," I said, not remember just how slow time moved between recesses. I just wanted to be back there. Be that kid again. Life was so much simpler back then. "Yeah, it did, but we don’t have it so bad now," the wise older brother spoke. "You got that wrestling gig going for ya. Now don’t tell me that you are not having fun with that?" "Yeah, and you have the job, the house, the . . . girlfriend?" He scratched his hairless chin before answering that one. Made me wonder for a moment if he even knew what this girl was to him. "Too early to use the big G word, Psychster. Had a couple of dates with Jo, and not much more than that." I left that one alone. I didn’t find it the right time to tell him just what I thought about it. I just let the awkward silence wash over me, waiting for him to change the subject. "So they are tearing this place down in October," he obliged. I rose to my feet and spun myself about for the panoramic nostalgia. I didn’t want to believe that I was loosing this place. But the next generation was already erected in the fields, and it was only a matter of time. How I hated the thought of loosing the school. The school I have had little to nothing to do with since I graduated. The school that served as little more than a milestone. I mourned the loss of a monument of so many childhoods, but selfishly I only cared that it was one to my childhood. The conclusion of my wallowing was that this really meant nothing to me, just another change. Just another change, and with all these changes, this place was look less and less like home. "Let’s go home and see what Mom and Dad have for breakfast," Tim offered, waiving his carte blanche for food poaching, and putting a hand on my shoulder. "Suppose I could go for round two." And so we went, and left behind the elicitor of memories made founder through the haze of time. ------ He ate his fill and made out like a bandit, leaving me here with them. I think he might have actually enjoyed that I was running into the same problems of connection with them as he did. And he flaunted his escape. "Mitch, could we talk for a minute?" my mother asked. "And maybe not have you run away to your room, or computer for a change?" Had it been that obvious to them too? "Of course," I responded and grabbed a seat in the Lay-Z-Boy next to her couch. It is a weekend, please don’t talk about work. Even though I had the sneaking suspicion that the familiar topic would again be drudged before me, what with her recent telephone conversation with Dad, who spent this and every Saturday morning hard at work. "I want you to know that your father and I have been talking and," she started, with he brows arching before deciding that there was no way to ease into this. "Ever since Tim moved out, we have realized that this place is too big for the two of us. We don’t want to work these crappy jobs forever, and by freeing up the capital we have invested in this house, we would be able to run our own little business. You remember us talking about it, opening up the little meat shop, living above it, leaving behind the retail business." I sat in that chair, and my jaw sat on the floor. "What are you trying to tell me, Ma?" I knew what it was that she was saying, but I refused to make sense of it. "Your father and I, we are going to be selling the house," she said, actually smiling at the promise of freedom that was on the other end of this tunnel. I couldn’t help but think that someone was being left out of this thought process. "But, what am I going to do?" "Mitchell, for the amount of time that you even in the country, I think this decision will have the least impact on you. You will have a bed with us as long as you want one. Tim has a spare room that he has already told me he would be willing to let you take. You will not be forced to find your own home." "But this is my home," I said frankly. "This is just a house," she tried to reason with me. I wasn’t buying. "This is my home," I repeated like the child who has never moved that I was. "This is where I grew up. For all the capital that you and Dad have invested in this place, do you not think that I have invested more in experiences and memories? Does it mean nothing that for every minute I am away from this place my only thought is how I cannot wait to get back? This place is more than four walls; it has more meaning than that." "Mitchell, we have a lot of memories of this place ourselves, but our memories are not tied to the house. It is just the setting where they happened. We remember them in our minds. You will not lose those memories, and you will make more somewhere else. I wish you showed a little of this passion for the place when it came to cleaning. But Mitchell, we have an opportunity here to be happy, and we will not let you chain us away from that. Please be a little more considerate than that." She had made her mind up before she even thought to let me have a whiff of the great schemes she had brewing. There was no use in arguing, and I knew it; that is why the disappointment set in. I laid back, taken over by a hopeless despair. It felt like I had been hit by a tonne of bricks. My breathing felt choked. "So this is it, I am losing home," I expelled with such little enthusiasm that it was barely audible.
