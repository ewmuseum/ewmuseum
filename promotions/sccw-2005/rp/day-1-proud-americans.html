Day 1: Proud Americans

Date: Today The Ambrose Papers: Volume 1. Proud Americans. May 2008. In which, two friends set out on a strange and horrible journey. "I hate to advocate drugs, alcohol, violence, or insanity to anyone, but they've always worked for me." -Hunter S. Thompson --- Hello, Space Cowboy The peyote kicked in around dawn, just as the sun was beginning to climb up over the horizon. Jeremiah was off to the side, staring up into the sun like the sky was about to fall, while I sat on the ground, my head in my hands, trying to prop up my mortal remains. The desert was hotter than usual, and I could hear voices in the back of my head, like ancestral chanting, Indian war cries, and the devil howling at the moon. Jeremiah looked over and said, "You alright, man? You don’t look so good. Maybe we should get you to a doctor." His voice came out all warbled and his eyes appeared to be bulging from his head. I couldn’t save him now. He was too far gone. We had been driving for three days straight, no sleep, no rest, with a trunk full of drugs and a briefcase full of liquor. We were running on fumes, decadent and depraved, living off the idea, the myth, and the mystery of the great southwest. This was the American Dream. This was our own personal manifest destiny. Some people spend their entire lives trying to find it, tying themselves down to a religion or a belief, but we found it without God. "Jerry," I muttered, "This is it. This is what we’ve been looking for." The desert stretched out, seemingly endless, with a parade of cactuses lined up along the horizon. We were somewhere outside of San Antonio, but it seemed more like we were all alone in some vast wasteland. We were the heroes of those black and white westerns. I was Clint Eastwood and he was John Wayne. I held up my gun and fired a round in the sky, telling God if I had to die then I would be taking him with me. For the first time in a long time, I was damn proud to be an American. "Vic," Jerry said, coming out of his daze and plopping down beside me. "Vic, listen." "What the fuck do you want, you goddamn heathen?!" My voice came out angry, laced with scorn, but such is the nature of the mind on drugs. You lose yourself. There is no such thing as self control, just a stream of consciousness. It’s like an out of body experience. You’re watching yourself, but you’re not the one behind the wheel anymore. Let go. Quit fighting it. Sit back. Relax. Take the ride. "Vic, listen… I’m dying, Vic. I’m fucking dying, I’m sure of it. Jesus came and told me. Said my time had come. I think I need to get born again, Vic. I think I need to get baptized," He was spouting off nonsense, his head full of weird thoughts he couldn’t quite work out for himself. "Vic, listen, I need you to baptize me. I need you to save my soul. I don’t want to go to Hell, Vic. I don’t want to burn in He—" "Jesus fucking Christ, man! Get a hold on yourself!" I stood to my feet, lightning fast, slapping him across the face with a backhand before reaching into the backseat of our convertible and pulling out a bottle of Jack Daniels. "Here, drink this. Baptize yourself. God’s from Tennessee, you know. He’s been drinking Jack Daniels since the beginning of time. It’ll save your soul faster than any priest of preacher, I can promise you that." Jeremiah hiccupped and giggled, taking hold of the bottle and popping off the cap, throwing it back as I knelt down beside him, wide eyed and watching the brown liquid disappear down his gullet. "Whoa, whoa, whoa, pilgrim. Hold your horses. That’s God’s elixir you’re drinking. It’ll fuck you up, man, and don’t you even think for a goddamn second I’ll save your sorry ass then. I’ll leave you here for the vultures! Let ‘em pick your bones clean!" Jerry sat the bottle aside, "Aw, c’mon, Vic… You wouldn’t do that to your best friend, would you?" I stood, shaking my head and leaving him to drink to his heart’s content. I looked around, breathing heavy and foaming from the mouth. Where were we? How did we wind up here? What were we trying to do to begin with? I sat down on the hood of the car. It all started three days prior… --- The Alamo "We’re going out west, Jerry. Pack your things." Kentucky was on fire, hot with all sorts of weird denizens from all corners of the United States, come to watch the 134th Kentucky Derby. Drunken madness ran rampant in the streets. Riot squads ran around like chickens with their heads cut off, scared shitless of whatever strange forebodings these miscreants had brought to their fair city of Louisville. Good, god fearing people cried in the streets, clutching onto their children, praying that these trying times would pass without much incident. It was like The Nazis marching into France all over again. These beer goggled conquerors were here to do the devil’s bidding. Mass genocide and strange hysteria was right over the horizon. Hell would follow with these dirty bastards. "What the fuck do you mean we’re going out west?" Jerry asked, between gulps of beer. "The Derby is in two days, Vic! We can’t leave now! They’ll crucify us if they catch us trying to skip town!" "We’re going to San Antonio, Jerry. We’re going to see The Alamo," My voice came out, but it wasn’t my own. The words were skewed and angry, bitter and gone mad. "Pack your things and tell your wife to keep the doors locked while these madmen are in town; we don’t want her getting raped and murdered, after all. We’ll be back in a week, and no less. She can’t come with us, she wouldn’t understand. I’ve got the car filled up with all the drugs we could ever possibly need, so get your shit together and meet me in an hour. If you don’t show up, so help me, I’ll cut your fucking balls off. We’re on a mission from God, Jerry! I’ll be damned if you fuck it up!" In my head, the ghost of the night before was howling over an acre of bones, breathing electric light and hissing static. I was still waist deep in my trip. The acid wasn’t agreeing with me. The chemist who made the stuff told me to avoid any stress and stay as calm as possible, but it’s pretty hard to stay calm when you’re having open-eye hallucinations of your dead grandmother at the edge of your bed, a knife between her fangs, threatening to cut open your chest and rip your heart out with her teeth unless you ‘go home’. I had spent the previous night hiding underneath my covers, eyes glazed over and drooling all over my sheets, with visions so wild and mesmerizing that I lost track of where fiction began and reality ended. Bad trips come in different flavors. Sometimes, it’s something as mild as seeing the world born and realizing the meaning of life, much to your horror. Other times, you think you killed a man in cold blood and you start trying to hide the non-existent evidence. But every now and again, once in a blue moon, when the planets are aligned and you’ve got The Fear creeping up in your very soul, a bad trip simply fills your head full of bad ideas and weird intentions that cause you to spend the next couple of days in a post-traumatic frenzy. My bad trip was one of those rare kinds. I came upon the notion that I needed to go home, back to Texas, back to San Antonio, back to The Motherland that birthed me. Something in my heart of hearts told me that I needed to see The Alamo, where folklore and legend meet with the devils of truth and history. I needed to stand on the same soil as Davy Crockett and maybe, just maybe, I’d drop dead there alongside his very bootmark, and Jerry could spread my ashes along the walls. Call it some bad karmic shift, or some foul nostalgic longing to go back to where my formative years took place. Either way, I had to be there. Some sinister urge was drawing me back. I was chained to the place by my very soul. We set out around noon, when Jerry was already out of his mind with a mouthful of opiates and I was at my best, my hands clutching the wheel and my foot pounding at the pedal. We were taking a trip to the heart of the American Dream. --- Blood Sport, Strange Musings, and The Proud Highway Day One We stopped somewhere in Tennessee to go hunting. It was a bad idea, but Jerry kept complaining that he was starving, and in the mad rush to get a hold of as many narcotics as I possibly could in a single day, I forgot to pack any food whatsoever. I sat on the hood of the car, smoking a cigarette and waiting for some backwoods retard to come storming out of the bushes to sink his fangs in my neck. I gave Jerry the gun. He stumbled off into the woods by himself, firing off round after round at shadows and tree trunks. There is nothing in this world worse than a man in the depths of an ether binge, raving mad and hungry for blood sport. I shouted out, though by then he was too far away to hear me, "Be careful, you sorry bastard! God only knows what kinds of vicious creatures are waiting to suck your blood dry out here…" This was a place of evil. You could hear the wind howl through the trees, and with the right kind of eyes, you could see the shadows for what they really were. Beasts. Monsters. Demons. Death itself, come to lead you to your grave. My mind was racing and my heart was beating a mile a minute. We were too far off of the highway. If we died here, we’d be murdered along with our names, forgotten and never found. Not even God could find us here. We were alone, but how alone were we? Jerry came stumbling back out of the woods, blood dripping from his chin and his eyes in some frantic rage. He was holding the corpse of what appeared to be a small animal, maybe a squirrel or a rabbit, but god only knows what it really was. "Are you done?" I asked, and he didn’t reply. "Goddammit, I said are you done, you sack of pig shit?!" He seemed to snap out of his daze, dropping the corpse and waltzing over to the car, real slow, wiping the blood from his mouth and licking his lips. "Vic… what the fuck are we doing here?" I shook my head, tossing my cigarette butte to the ground, "Who knows. Where did you put my gun?" "…what gun?" He asked, getting more confused by the second. He wasn’t all there. Neither was I, but at least I knew a little bit about what was going on. "Fuck it!" I cried out, slapping him on the back. "We’ve got to get out of here. No time to find it. Every fucking thing in this jungle is going to be after us soon, Jerry. You killed one of their own! Get in the car, Jerry. Get in the car before the death cults find us." I helped him into the passenger seat and got behind the wheel, but not before reaching into the glove compartment to get a quick dose of high powered blotter acid. We left, with Hell nipping at our heels. God only knows how close we were to death. By the time we were back on the road, I was seeing things that weren’t really there. Some weird insanity was making its home in the forefront of my brain, and it was too late to stop it now. All I could do was scream until my lungs gave out, and pray to whatever higher power that was listening to save me from myself. Jerry fell asleep against the window pane, snoring as loud as an elephant and muttering something about doctors disguised as journalists and Samoan attorneys. The road seemed to stretch on endlessly, but I knew it led somewhere, and anywhere was better than Tennessee. It was a depraved wasteland, chocked full of debauchery and backwards traditions. This was where animals and men were on the same level, fucking in the same barns, living in the same houses, being buried in the same graveyards. This was where the village retard was the town mayor and the smartest man was held down, just for having some intellect on his side. I held nothing but the highest contempt for the people who made their home here, and I’m sure they held me in the same light. We drove for hours before we finally crossed over the state lines, into Arkansas. Arkansas wasn’t much for aesthetic beauty, but it would treat me better. The American Dream was still alive and well in the south, flourishing off the idea that the white man still had a hold on the top of the totem pole, even though in reality, the totem pole had already fallen over ages ago. I had to make sure I kept my mouth shut while driving through this forgotten corner of the United States. This was where people like me went to die and never be heard from again. There is no such thing as Southern hospitality. It’s a myth, a complete frock, bullshit sprinkled in sweetness, to lure unsuspecting, god fearing folks down like lambs to the slaughter. Cannibalism comes to mind, but so does rape and murder. God knows what really happens. God knows what would really happen to us. "Desolation Row" by Bob Dylan was playing on the radio. The day had given way to night and we were slowly approaching our second day on the road. I wanted to go faster than the tires would allow me to go, but the harder I pressed the pedal, the more I seemed to slip in and out of consciousness. There was a maddening rush deep within my bones, some ancient fury that told me where I was going and what I was doing. I could feel my brain boiling in my head, my eyes bulging out as far as they could, my teeth chattering and my whole body shaking. This wasn’t an overdose, it was something far worse. This was the fear setting in. This was the horrifying aftermath that only a man in the depths of a serious drug collection can truly comprehend. Your mind goes numb. Your body loses it’s cool. Your whole life flashes before your eyes, but you’re not dying, you’re just coming down. The only cure is time or more drugs; the more the better. I reached for the glove compartment, opening it and ripping at its contents with some savage anxiousness. Where was the acid? Where was the weed? What happened to all the coke? For God’s sake, what about the shot bottles of whiskey and the rag dipped in ether? I couldn’t even find my cigarettes. My fingers were trembling, my eyes were watering, a deep and unsettling depression was crawling up my spine… And that’s when I saw the red and blue lights flashing in the rearview mirror. --- And The Horse You Rode In On. My eyes started burning. My lips started to quiver. My heart felt like it was ready to explode from my chest. Could this really be the end? Jerry was still asleep. Best not to wake him up. Who knows what kind of maddening thoughts were making their way through his head. One wrong word and he could get us both filled with holes. The police in these parts weren’t exactly known for their hospitable treatment of sapsucking carpetbaggers like us. I had to stay calm, take a deep breath, and pray the fear would settle down long enough for me to get us out of this mess. I pulled to the side of the road, trying my best to keep my composure. My whole world was spinning recklessly. All of realities laws and rules were being flushed down the shitter. I couldn’t breathe. I felt like, at any moment, my head was going to melt and my whole body was going to go into strange convulsions, like some sort of mystic Native American ritual gone horribly wrong. Stay cool. Remain calm. Answer any questions like an upstanding citizen. The long arm of the law could only reach so far, after all. Minutes passed, but it seemed like hours. This cop, he was playing a waiting game. He must have known what was going on here. He was waiting for me to make a bad move. He was begging for a chase, praying I’d get brave and turn the key, press the pedal, and go off flying down the interstate, but I knew better. This was neither the time nor place for drug induced courage. Submit or die, dammit. Submit or die. When he finally got out of his car, pointing his flashlight like some sort of beacon of dread and prejudice, I could tell by the way he walked toward the car that he was just a slobbering imbecile with the badge on his side. Fat and grotesque, he walked with a waddle, a sort of swagger that only the worst kind of power can give a man. "License and registration, please," His voice came out in an almost comical tone, like some terrible parody of that all too well known Southern accent. "Yes sir," I replied, quickly enough. I couldn’t let him know that I was nervous. I had to make him think I was perfectly fine, perfectly well equipped with the capacity to be behind the wheel. As I reached over to the glove compartment, I realized it must have been by some grim fate of God that the drugs were missing. Otherwise, as soon as I opened it, he would have put a bullet straight through my head. "You’ll have to forgive my friend, sir. He suffers from a severe case of narcolepsy. He falls asleep at the drop of a hat. He’s been out for hours. I’m sure he’s fine, though." In reality, the prospect of Jerry being dead wasn’t too farfetched. I’d heard barely a peep out of him. Was he breathing? How long had I been driving with a corpse by my side? "Victor Ambrose, eh? Your license is from Kentucky. What are you doing in these parts?" His voice was thick with scorn, some deep rooted hatred. He didn’t say it out loud, but you could tell just by the way his eyes were fixed, the way his expression fluctuated between emotions. "You got family out here?" "No sir, I’m just passing through, on my way to San Antonio," I could see the snarl of his lips, the hiss underneath his breath. "Going home, you know." "So, you’re a Texan? Hmph," His voice lowered as he slid his hand through his greasy hair, licking his lips, "You know why I pulled you over, son? You were swerving quite a bit back there, and ten miles over the speed limit. You in some sort of rush?" "Just tired, sir. I’ve been driving since the crack of dawn. I didn’t realize how fast I was going. I’m just a good, God fearing American like yourself. Honest," What was I saying? My words weren’t my own, but they were still coming out. I was sure he had me on all fronts. He was just playing a game. He was waiting until my guard was down, and then he would strike. "Well, I’ll tell you what, boy. I’m feeling generous. What goes on this report is what goes in the books, you understand? I’m going to write that I pulled you over and advised you to go to the next rest stop you see. Get a good night’s sleep and try again in the morning, do you understand?" There was a smirk growing on his face. He had me backed in a corner. He knew just as well as I did that I couldn’t stop now. There was no way. "Yes sir, I hear you loud and clear," Beads of sweat were forming on my forehead. I could feel the night air whipping at my back. When he went back to the station, he’d be telling all his cop buddies this story. How he pulled over some hippie loser and gave him a warning, out of the kindness of his heart. He’d tell his pastor. He’d tell his wife. He would brag and be proud and he’d laugh his ass off. "You won’t have to worry about me." He nodded and handed me back my license, that serpent smirk still on his lips. "That’s what I like to hear, boy. Now, you be careful. Make sure you see that there Alamo while you’re in San Antonio. I hear it’s something else," He winked, he laughed, I laughed with him. This was the worst part, the most embarrassing. "And don’t be a stranger, you hear? Come back to Arkansas anytime you want." "I’ll make sure I do, sir. This is a beautiful state, full of beautiful people. Thank you, sir." As he walked back to his car, horse laughing and swaggering like he was God himself, I muttered ‘fucking pig’ beneath my breath and turned the key in the ignition. There is nothing more hideous and demeaning than a man in the darkest depths of a power trip. I’d have to bypass Arkansas on the way back through. Who knows what sort of terrors he’d have set up for me on my way back. I’d remember his face. But that didn’t matter. The show must go on. --- Death and Taxes. (In which, Victor Ambrose questions himself and those around him.) The whole world is going to hell in a handbasket. We’re all falling apart. We’re all struggling to survive. We’re all scavenging for our next meal. This is the future, brother, and it is murder. In this year of our Lord, we’re all starting to wonder what we’re even bothering for. What are we striving to accomplish? What are we dying to achieve? Where’s the finish line and were we even close to it to begin with? I can’t answer these questions. I’ve had my own troubles, my own life to lead. There was a time where you could strike sparks anywhere. With the right kind of eyes, from the top of the highest mountains, you can still see those better days. It wasn’t so long ago, but it seems like a lifetime. Strange memories from a stranger time. Call it a subculture or call it some sort of mass hallucination, but there was a time and there was a place where everything stood still and you knew, in your heart of hearts, that it was all going to be okay. It’s hard to think back to those times without feeling a tear or two stream down my cheeks. It’s funny to imagine how a single day in a three part road trip can make you realize just how close you really were to changing the world, and just how brilliantly you failed. Maybe I’m lying to myself, but I seem to remember it all like it was yesterday. It was a very special time, different from the rest of history, some offset once in a lifetime experience that I’ll never forget and never know again. While Bush ran rampant in the White House, there were riots in the streets and everyone seemed to know exactly what was right and what was wrong, where to begin and where to end. Call it chaos if you want, but I call it the start of something. There was madness in any direction, frantic living in a desperate time, but you could sense in the air that the times were changing, and so were we. The hipsters wrote "California or Bust" on their rear windows and the crooners all sang outside of the pool halls, singing songs of hope instead of songs of sorrow. You could feel the world turning, and it seemed like it was turning faster than it actually was, or it least that’s how it felt in my mind. But I remember that sense of triumph. We knew, undeniably, that we would win. We would overcome the forces of Old and Evil, start a new empire, raze the old one to the ground, and overthrow whoever stood in our way. It could have been the drugs or it could have been simple childish thinking, maybe a mixture of both, but God knows it was there and God knows we all felt it. And that, I think, that was the handle. That was what it was all about. Some childhood dream, impossible but everlasting, a sense of inevitable victory. We had the momentum, we had the numbers, and we would prevail. Strange memories, indeed. I can’t find a single person anymore who remembers it like I do. Maybe I dreamt it. Maybe it was all a figment of my imagination. Maybe the people I knew and the people I loved never existed, it was only me, lonely and crying, wishing America would burn and fold in on itself. Maybe, just maybe, it’s all the sloppy afterbirth of some drug addled failure’s shattered mind. But I don’t think it is. I pray it isn’t. If it is, why am I even bothering not to go ahead and shoot myself now? Strange memories from a stranger time. Nervous thoughts and nervous longings. The wind has gone cold and Jerry is still asleep. The radio fell silent and the mountains are starting to fall flat, replaced by nothing as far as the eye can see. I can feel the desert, it’s calling me home, back to all I forgot and all I never wanted to know again. All that I left and never meant to go back to. America is still burning, I can feel it in the air, but maybe someone else can extinguish it better than we did. We’re just vagabonds; we live this close to death. --- Next Week!: Day Two of a Three Day Journey. Bad vibes and serious voodoo! Weird thoughts and druggie mishaps! Death and destruction!
