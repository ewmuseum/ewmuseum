Date: Today Friends are usually more trouble than they're worth. They expect to be treated better than your enemies, for one thing. And every once in a while, they will ask you for favors. Worse still, there may come a time when you will need to act against them. Could you bring yourself to stab them in the back? Would you hesitate? Would you really lose everything for the sake of a friend? The important thing to remember is that loyalty is a curse, no matter which way it goes. If you don't think you can screw over a friend...then screw them over harder, and make them an enemy. Sometimes, this is the best way to survive. Sometimes, it is the only way. # # # April 11, 2010. 3:15 pm. Tokyo, Japan. The staff and roster of Sin City Championship Wrestling had voted unanimously to spend a few extra days in Japan, rather than continue directly to Iraq. This came as a surprise to exactly nobody--in fact, most of them had made their plans in Tokyo before they even knew the travel schedule. All around the city, representatives of SCCW were taking in the sights, sounds, and flavors of the Japanese metropolis. Even 'Wise Guy' Wyatt Connors would have enjoyed his day off, were it not for one thing. Wyatt Connors does not take days off. That meant it was business that brought him to Kyuubei, a sushi bar in the Ginza section of Tokyo. He had already finished his lunch, but there was still no sign of the other interested party. He checked his watch with every sip of tea, as if it would bring the meeting closer to its beginning...and therefore, to its end. Wyatt refilled his tea cup and took another look around the restaurant. Despite his comments on the previous broadcast, he enjoyed visits to the island nation. For one, he was of average height here. Also, it tended to be easier to spot those who had sworn his destruction. It was much tougher for them to blend in here, as most of Wyatt's enemies tended to be American. Or Bolivian, but that's a story for another time. At any rate, Connors didn't expect the enemies to come from the shadows, as the person he'd agreed to meet preferred to stand out. As usual, it wasn't the click of the stiletto heels that drew his attention. It wasn't the honey-sweet voice, the tawny hair, or the slinky dress that hugged the curves of her body. No, it was the sudden trace of lavender in the air. 'You're late, Kathryn,' he said, not looking up from his tea. In fact, his hand clutched the cup tighter than before. In one smooth motion, Kathryn Shaw rested herself in the chair opposite Connors. 'Only by a few minutes, darling,' she cooed as she looked at the menu. 'I'm famished. What are you going to have?' Wyatt chanced a look at the Sex Kitten--he knew full well what could happen if you stared. 'I've already eaten. The shigaraki is quite good.' Shaw pouted a little, before giving way to confusion. 'You've finished already? How long have you been here?' 'An hour, at least. Figure I'd eat early; that way you wouldn't have a chance to poison the food.' 'Oh, Wyatt. You're so cute when you're paranoid.' She tried to pet his hand, but Connors jerked it back, causing a small amount of tea to splash onto his jacket. 'I don't suppose we could move on to business,' he grumbled as he wiped the stain with a napkin. 'Always so serious. You should relax a little, you know? All that stress is bad for your heart.' 'So are knives,' he snapped back. 'My boys told me you wanted to talk.' 'Well, I'd like to do more than chat, of course, but we both know that's unlikely.' She was looking down at the menu, but not really at the words. Like everything else with Shaw, it was more about the appearance of her looking down, adding something to the lightness of the words. 'They were quite rude in Hawaii, you know. Girl comes by to have a little chat with an old friend and gets rebuffed like that... she might develop a complex.' 'I'd ask you to send me your therapy bill, but I'm no fool.' His body relaxed a little, and he leaned back in his chair. He looked like the very picture of tranquility, except for the fact that he kept looking around the room for the best escape routes. 'Besides, they were only trying to save you a little time. It was true that I didn't want to be disturbed, which is why I watched the program from my hotel room. In San Francisco.' 'I'd ask why you were so far away, but... I don't really care.' That line draws her eyes up, her smile just a subtle crimping of pillowed lips. 'I was hoping we could do business. The kind of business we both know I'm fond of – the seamy, sordid, dirty stuff.' Connors gave a curt nod. 'Go ahead.' 'I seem to recall you have a set of photos. Pictures I'd be very interested in obtaining.' Connors arched an eyebrow. Some time ago, he had acquired photographs of Alexandra Pierce and Amy Campbell in what he called 'a compromising position,' and what T.J. Ratigan called 'doing the nasty.' It had been part of his plan to assault the personal life of Campbell, who had been his opponent at the time. As it turned out, he didn't even need them--she destroyed her own life before he had a chance. 'Thinking rather big, aren't we? I suppose I shouldn't be surprised.' 'Now, we both know I always thought big, but Lexi was the one who always said, 'No, Kathi.'' A touch of scorn slipped into her words. 'Now there's no one to say no to me anymore. Except, perhaps, you.' 'That's true, I can. And I just might, at that. I've grown rather fond of having an insurance policy against her. Why would I want to give that up?' 'Because we both know that pictures of her and Amy aren't actually an insurance policy.' The second time Kathryn lifted her eyes, there was no tease in their hazel depths. 'Lexi is busy being a nice person, but once she's given up or been patted on the head enough... she's going to come for all of us. Do you really think she'll stop because you might reveal pictures of the thing she dreams of at night?' 'Not really. But sometimes it's fun to pretend. All right, so you want the pictures. What's in it for me?' Her chuckle was rich, light. It reminded of a soft, gurgling stream. 'You know how many times I've been asked that? More than you can imagine. But you don't want any of the same things they do. In fact, I've known you for, what, ten years? Eleven? And I still don't know what would entice you to part with those photos. You're a very inscrutable man, Wyatt Connors.' The laugh that followed sounded bright and happy. They both knew it wasn't. 'So name your price. Money? Power? Protection? Some little thing, whose important is only revealed sometime later, and then, boy, imagine the look on my face? I don't know what you want, Wyatt, but I know you want something. You probably decided what it was after your boys told you I was looking for you, but we both know you know what it is.' Connors thought for a moment. He wasn't entirely sure what he'd accept in exchange for the pictures, although there was no reason to let Shaw know that. Instead, the thought that came to mind was what they had cost, and what he had gained. One night, Desade had arranged a meeting, wherein she demanded that he give her the pictures. Then she asked. Then she begged. Desade--the woman who had probably not shown one ounce of mercy in decades--begged. She had even reduced her demands--rather than handing them over, she merely wanted Connors not to put them on display for the world to see. Wyatt Connors had trained himself to sniff out lies, and there were almost none better. Yet when he looked at the Director that night, he only saw the truth. Not in her words, but in her eyes. It was a sight that only a handful of people in the world had ever witnessed--Alexandra Pierce. Not as the Spider, nor the Director, nor Desade...but as a human being. Her true face. He wondered if he could see the same thing from Kathryn Shaw. He wondered if he wanted to. 'I'm sorry, but I may have to pass on this, Miss Shaw,' he said. 'The price is just too high.' 'Oh, come on. I have a mani/pedi appointment this afternoon, and I don't have time to negotiate with you.' Wyatt's response was little more than a shrug, but even that set Kathryn's eyebrows to knitting. 'You're not untouchable, you know.' Again, her voice was light, airy. 'Ooh, sizzling teriyaki on a platter. I think I've made my decision.' She slapped the menu closed, setting it gently at the edge of the table. 'What was I saying? Ah, yes. You're not protected, Wyatt. Two goons who share half a brain between them, your little knot of secrets... give me a day and I can get back to you, well before you've had an hour to prepare. Give me a week, and you'll sell them to me for a pittance.' Connors' mouth curled into a smirk. Threats were more familiar ground. 'My goons are a little more capable than you realize.' Very little, he added mentally. 'And if you dispose of them, it's not like I can't get more. This business pretty much has hot- and cold-running goons. As for my...'little knot of secrets?' Your people have been trying to untangle that for years, and I doubt you're any farther than when you started.' 'Yes, but it'll be annoying for you.' She leaned forward, swatting her napkin in his direction. 'Do you really want to bother trying to stop me?' 'Not particularly. But the fact is, those photographs are mine, and shall be used or not used as I see fit. I highly doubt there is anything you can say that will change my mind.' A smile stretched the Sex Kitten's Cheshire grin. 'Oh, Mr. McGwire... one day, there will come a time where I remind you of that statement.' Wyatt Connors' hand tensed up on the cup of tea. It was very slight, and most people might not see it even if they'd been looking right at it. But she saw it. And she knew. 'I'll be in touch,' he said, and stood up from the table. 'I'm sure you will.' It was off-handed, as if she'd dismissed him. She even turned in the chair to seek out a waiter. But he knew, even as he slipped towards the door, that she was still watching. # # # January 19, 2005. 2:36 pm. Los Angeles, California. It was like walking into the building for the first time, all over again. In a way, it was. Wyatt Connors had worked in the offices of CSP, Ltd. for almost a year...but now, he had his eyes open, and knew his associates for what they were. Thieves. Kidnappers. Murderers. Monsters. Over the last several months, he'd had a series of clandestine meetings with Cecilia 'Rat' Sicarii, a former associate-turned-enemy of the others in the group known as 'the Illuminati.' In those meetings, she told him about the Order of St. Julian, and the true nature of Alexandra Pierce. She told him about the danger to innocent lives. She told him about the danger to his life, and that caught his attention. Finally he agreed that Something Had To Be Done. He wasn't sure when he agreed that he'd be the one to Do it, but Sicarii and her boots could be very convincing at times. The plan was very clear. Earlier in the day, one of their people would hack into the CSP computer system and extract the files, then put them on Connors' personal computer. At 4:05, he would start printing some files and burning the rest to disc. The whole process would take about forty-five minutes, allowing him to cover his tracks and walk out of the building at his usual time, so as not to draw suspicion. From there, he would call Cecilia from a secure line and arrange the pickup. It was a reasonably simple plan, with only two problems. The first was that, under normal circumstances, the Los Angeles branch office did not have access to the files that were needed. They had to wait until a connection with the main servers was made, and that wouldn't happen until someone with the right clearance needed access. Someone like Alexandra Pierce, for example. That was why this day was chosen for their operation. Pierce met with Connors over coffee in the early morning, where he bored her almost to tears with information about forthcoming DVD releases. She remained attentive through his spiel about the History of SCCW and Best of GCW releases, but her focus waned a bit during NEWS' Top Stories, and Connors would swear he saw her stifle a yawn when he brought up the Lucente Cup Collection. The only moment she seemed interested was when he mentioned he had a line on footage of the missing Across Enemy Lines 3 main event, and she told him not to bother. Their conversation finished, she went about the rest of her business--much of which involved avoiding Connors so that he couldn't talk to her anymore. With Pierce out of the way, Connors was free to wait for the rest of the plan to kick into place. Sometime after noon, Pierce had accessed the protected data banks, and Sicarii's mole had acquired a veritable hoard of information. This information was then sent to Connors' computer, where he could take care of it. This is where the plan ran into its other major problem. Wyatt Connors. For one thing, he was a bit more knowledgeable about the CSP computer and security systems than he'd let on. He also had a fairly detailed understanding of the company's financial structure. Specifically, where the cash was. And how to get it. That's why he'd been typing away at the computer for the last twenty minutes...and why each passing second brought with it increased urgency. In the original plan, the main reason why he was to wait so long before printing was that Pierce was planning to leave the building at half past three, so that anyone who noticed the breach would report it to security instead. Connors could lie to security. But Desade? 'C'mon, you bastard...' Wyatt muttered as he watched the printer spew out page after page. He had to leave almost half the information behind; speed became more important than quantity. Besides, he needed all that time for a side project. Transferring money from the CSP accounts, into his own. His eyes darted back and forth from the monitor to the clock. Ten pages to go. He brought up another window, which showed the security feeds inside the building. One camera showed a young woman running up to an office door and reaching for the knob--but she suddenly halted, and knocked instead. Even the interns knew better than to barge in on Alexandra Pierce. 'Shit!' Connors whispered, and went into action. He grabbed whatever documents he could hold from the printer tray and stuffed them into his briefcase, and did the same with the CD that he'd finished burning only a moment earlier. There was no time to erase his work from the computer, so he tipped it on its side and poured a nearly full cup of coffee into the case. As soon as the sparks started flying, he made a beeline for the door. Alexandra Pierce missed him by eighty-two seconds. Wyatt Connors went down the stairs, often trying to jump from one landing to the next for the sake of speed. As soon as he reached the lobby, he bolted through the door toward the main entrance. He saw the receptionist's cheery face, and one or two security guards, listening intently to their ear pieces. He bolted past the desk, shouting, 'Callingitearlyhaveanicenight!' at the girl who hadn't even managed to speak her greeting yet, and went out the door. The security guards were only a second late in catching on, and gave chase...but that one second was enough. Connors didn't see the car when he left the building, but he didn't stop running, either. He just headed straight out into the street...and as he reached the curb, a black Lincoln with tinted windows screeched to a halt beside him. Connors leaped through the open window and landed in the back seat, leaving the guards just a step behind as the car tore away from CSP Ltd., never to return. Connors took a moment to catch his breath, then looked up at his savior. 'Cut it a little close there, didn't you?' His eyes met those of his old friend Harley Roebuck in the rearview mirror. 'You said 2:41, and that's what you got.' 'Point.' And that wasn't all he got, either. He walked--well, ran--out of the building with enough evidence to put Alexandra Pierce away for life about three times over. And over two and a half million dollars. All the while, across town, Cecilia Sicarii waited for a phone call that would never come. # # # April 12, 2010. 9:15 am. Tokyo, Japan. Deacon Dale and Trevor Ratigan didn't catch up with Connors until the following morning, when they met at Tokyo International Airport. Amazingly, Ratigan spotted him first--Connors was sitting on a bench, reading a freshly-purchased copy of Newsweek. Deacon noticed the Wise Guy a moment later, and was about to say something, but Ratigan clamped a hand over his mouth. 'Dude,' he whispered. 'We cannot pass this up.' 'Mmmfffm?' 'Just...don't say anything. OK?' T.J. let go of his partner, and then bounded off on a circuitous route toward Wyatt Connors. Once he got close, he started walking as softly as possible...and as he did so, he put the end of his right index finger in his mouth. Deacon shook his head, but made no move to help or hinder his cohort. He figured, it was going to work or fail hard...and either way, it should be good for a laugh. Once he was in the prime position, Trevor slowly brought his wet finger up to Wyatt's ear. He got closer...and closer...until he could almost touch it... 'Go ahead, Trevor. See whappens.' Ratigan jerked his hand back, as if he were afraid it might get bitten of. He looked up, expecting to see the well-known icy glare of Connors...but the man hadn't even looked up from the magazine. 'Hold on. How did you know it was me?' 'I've had you both implanted with tracking devices that transmit your locations directly into my brain.' 'Huh. That how come my balls itch all the time?' 'No, that's because you're not careful about who you fuck. Deacon, get over here.' Dale started moving as soon as he heard the order, bringing both his and Trevor's luggage. 'Yeah, boss?' 'I trust you had an enjoyable time in Tokyo?' 'Yeah, it was all right. We met up with Koji, went around town, hit a few bars. He asked how you were doing.' Wyatt nodded. Koji Toyoshima--sometimes known as Clockwork--had spent a few months as one of Connors' henchmen in Seventh Circle Wrestling, after the NWC closed. He'd suffered a serious knee injury and was forced to retire from the ring a few years ago, however. 'What did you tell him?' 'That you're just as much of a pain in the ass as you were back then.' 'Heh. Only just as much? I may have to try harder. Are you both ready for the flight?' Deacon nodded, and rolled Ratigan's suitcase toward its owner. 'Let's roll.' After a mostly uneventful trip through security (where do you even get horseradish in this country, let alone on your passport?) the three of them arrived at the gate for their flight to Athens. Ratigan plopped down across three seats, and Deacon and Wyatt sat down on the other side. Deacon started listing the details of their visit with Koji Toyoshima, but Connors quickly tuned him out. The Wise Guy had a lot to think about. It'd be easier this way. So much easier. There wasn't any logical reason not to go through with it, in fact. Yes, Pierce would be angry...but she'd be much more angry with Shaw and Stevens, after they went through with it. And anything that kept Desade off his back was fine by Connors. Besides, Shaw's threats were not exactly veiled. She'd said that name...of course, Keith McGwire was a red herring; one of many Connors had planted in an attempt to obfuscate his past. But what if next time, she found the real thing? Could she really dig up his private life? Maybe, maybe not. But she could easily attack his charges through their private lives. To be fair, that was part of their job--to be distractions--but that did not mean he welcomed their destruction. Besides, he'd have to cut them loose if they became useless to him, and where would he get more? And if there was even the slightest chance that she could discover his past...wasn't that more than enough reason to play ball? In the end, the choice was clear. There were too many arguments for making the deal...and only one against. 'Excuse me, gentlemen. I need to make a phone call.' He stood up and walked toward the nearest payphone terminal. After a few minutes of hassling with a prepaid calling card, he dialed a number and waited for an answer. 'Yeah. It's me...Connors. Who did you think? Listen, I want to make a deal. I'll let you know more when we get to Italy.' He waited through the other person's response, then finished, 'Nah, let it be a surprise. We'll talk later.' Connors hung up the phone--halfway through the other person's 'goodbye'--and went back to the terminal. From a distance, he could see Deacon and Trevor fighting over something like small children. 'What the hell is going on?' he snapped, bringing their eyes up to him. 'Deacon won't let me look at the picture,' T.J. whined. 'I told you, not until you wash your friggin' hands, first. They're covered in soy sauce. Anyway, you only want to see it so you can drool over Koji's wife some more.' Wyatt rolled his eyes and went back to his book, in an attempt to tune out their bickering. But there was a moment...so brief that unless you'd been there to see it, you'd never know it happened at all. But for one split second, there was a clear message in Connors' eyes. I'm sorry. Because that woman he just talked to on the phone? It wasn't Kathryn Shaw. # # # When you make a career out of breaking the rules, it becomes even more important to uphold the ones you make for yourself. 'You are beholden to no one.'
