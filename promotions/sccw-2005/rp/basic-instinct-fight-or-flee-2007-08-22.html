Basic Instinct: Fight or Flee

22 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08-22-2007 He felt the punch. Not much before that mattered, only that for one instant, one moment in time, he was alive again and he wanted for more than just to live in the excess of the world he had surrounded himself in. Staggering back he left his guard open and caught a sharp kick in the ribs. This was it, for the first time since he could remember, he was being outclassed, and there was nobody to blame it on. Trying to fight back, he swings wildly, like a madman. He hopes only for one to connect, but the air makes a much less tangible target than his opponent and before long, he begins to breathe heavily. The air tastes of sweat and salt. The blood that trickled down his cheek was warm and inviting. He felt another punch, then felt no more. ***** In the great many days leading up to the event that was to befall Timo Bolamba, he had traveled a path not many men would consider worthy. Lying, cheating, and stealing victories were his methods of mayhem. When he faltered from this path, there was always the path of excess, living beyond the life of a man his age and traveling back into his early years again. He had it all, women, cars, booze, whatever his greedy wheels desired. Still a hole was left in his life, one that no amount of fun could patch. On the morning just after he had lost his last fight in FUSE, "The Dastardly Bastard" walked alone on the streets of Motor City wondering to himself. "Is this really how it was all supposed to go?" the lonely man mused. For all his bravado and success in the past, he had not been able to pin anyone’s shoulders for longer than two seconds in quite some time. It was supposed to be an easy path to the top, and awaiting him was the rival he had searched for his entire life: Clinton Sage. Instead he was bridled with the task of facing Jonathan Rhine, and he failed to achieve a victory there as well. This was inconsequential to The Bastard, as he had convinced himself that he threw the match to get a shot at Sage. What was it they say about people who lied so often they eventually believe their own lies? Regardless, he had failed. Not only did he fail against Rhine, but also against Sage. Everything was there, a perfect plan, a perfect move, but there was one element he had forgotten about. This was no longer his world. The world of professional wrestling had long been steeped in tradition and all the normal ballyhoo of a man testing his mettle against an opponent, but no longer was it the case. Everything these days was about advertising dollars, product placement, and the almighty draw. Clinton Sage was a draw, perhaps the biggest. Jonathan Rhine was now a draw as well. Fans lined up in droves to see their favorite wrestlers perform, and it made Timo sick. Inside, The Bastard seethed with envy that any man should want to spend more time staking out a possible autograph from a low level chub rub such as Rhine versus a seasoned veteran like himself. The entire world he had known was gone now. He was alone and naked in a place that did not understand him. What’s worse, that maddeningly difficult optimist had now made it his personal mission in life to revert Timo to the annoying state he once lived in. Things just were not adding up to the potential that he had envisioned himself living up to, and now, more than ever, he had given himself a reason to hate those who stood in his way. "I should just retire," he thought deeply. "Maybe they are right, maybe the game has passed me by and this entire world has changed in my absence." He felt pressure creep around him every day now. The stacking of mixed emotions made him crumble though he could never show it. Above all else, he felt a mixed rush of wanting and disillusionment all at the same time. Blindly walking forward, The Bastard happened upon a small building not far from his old gym. He had seen the area a few times before and never actually bothered to stop, even though he had been interested in the place for several years. The sign read Jiu Jitsu. A familiar tingle ran through the spine of The Bastard, though he knew not why. He had remembered that several base techniques and training sessions his former self had put their body through referenced this style though, so being a curious party, he opted to stroll into the club. The air inside smelled deeply of hard work and effort. He crinkled his nose as the wafting aroma pandered to his senses. The Samoan looked about with intrigue as several young men flailed about in assorted grapples, clinches, and holds. He felt a base sense of pride dragging him towards the ring, though he knew not what the feeling was for, or what it longed to transfix to. "You…are you new here?" a voice shot from the left. Immediately, his hand flew to his breast pocket and produced a set of Maui Jim glasses. Slipping them on quietly, the Samoan turned to meet the voice, only to stare down at a small Latino man, probably in his late forties. "If you are interested in joining, you can come back for the beginner’s sessions later in the evening." The man insulted Time’s credibility. The Samoan looked down to a balled fist and then around the room at the glaring eyes of the men as they stood around him. He felt their penetrating glare, their hard edged desire to fight him…and he knew why. He was a veritable living legend in Motor City. Finally, he parted his lips and returned the conversation with the smaller man. "I think you have me confused with someone else sir." The Bastard nonchalantly glanced at the students. He felt their eyes upon him as a youngling deer feels the eyes of the wolf in autumn. The man had finally made it to within distance and opened his invitation further. "I do not believe I have sir." This was the only reply Timo received. This bristled The Bastard, for he now knew the man must be playing in jest. Who amongst this room of nobodies could look upon the well to do Samoan and not know his name? He was not only a hero in this city, but a national figure! "Ah, I see then." The Bastard smiled. "So then I am to assume you know exactly who I am, but refuse to acknowledge the fact that I breathe a higher quality of air than you do?" The man smiled, and slightly bowed. "You sir, disgrace us with your presence. You do not understand the martial arts, and you never will. For this reason, I cannot train you." He sullenly looked away. Curiously, The Bastard smiled. He looked around the room, and slowly removed the glasses from his face. The pictures upon the wall, the lackluster paint job, the blue mats…it all made sense. He had been here before. More importantly and precise, perhaps…The Silencer had been here before. Finding himself at a rare loss for not just words but also eccentricities, The Samoan slowly nodded as the sensei made it clear he was not wanted. "I see." Pausing slightly, the eyes grew more intense. "Pray tell then sir, what must a man do to become welcome in an establishment such as this?" The man merely smiled. "Around here, we fight. Do you think that you can still prove you are man enough to fight a member of the gym, or do you need to belittle us with your fancy words and silk shirts?" The old man had a point. A slow nod signaled that perhaps there was more to the new arrival than had previously been established. For reasons Timo did not understand, he began to slowly take off his wristwatch. Shortly afterwards, he loosened the first button on his expensive silk shirt. Slowly, perhaps methodically, he slid the buttons loose until the final one had been pried from its purchase and the garment was thrown to the ground. Stepping solidly onto a blue mat, he looked across to see a smiling man probably ten years younger than he was. He felt the rush of adrenaline as the nerves began to run their course. The smell that stung his nostrils had faded and all that was left now was the taste of wild uninhibited freedom. Even as the young man circled, and Timo instinctively began to feel the balls of his heels lift and he moved more out of pace and timing than thought. It wasn’t fear or even nerves at this point that assailed The Bastard, but a feeling that was long bereft of the wily Samoan. Finally the monotony ended with a short jab that caught the Samoan by surprise, followed by a quick snap uppercut he barely avoided. Timo stepped backward, and counter punched, missing by a narrow margin. "The kid is fast. I might have hit him ten years ago." He mused as he circled again. A leg strike sends a shockwave of memories through the mind of the larger and older man. He remembered how many times his legs had been targeted by his enemies, and the terrible pain he had endured because of it. It was almost at that point that he felt the overwhelming urge to run from this man, or even to cheat and win. Who should care but Timo about the dignity that is bestowed if he wins? And that’s when it happened. ***** He felt that punch. His mind swirled with vague recollection of moments in the past, how he would escape the onslaught of a younger, faster, and fresher foe. He barely felt the barrage that followed, and in the coming moments before the final blow, he felt not pain…but humility. As his opponent closed in, his eyes had gone black. The second punch had connected hard, and he felt the room spin for a moment. A trickle of blood fell down and landed with a splatter upon his Dockers slacks. He did not feel pain. He did not feel the urge to cheat. He did not feel envy. He felt only one thing as his enemy closed in on him even now… The urge to fight back. The young man was fast. The young man was fresher. The young man wanted it, but after that second blow landed, he did not want it more. Timo brought the young fighter into a closed guard and locked his feet together. The younger fighter tried several close punches, but could get no power behind them. In a moment of frustration, the young man left his arm open, and that moment was all it took to bring a man back to life. Without thinking, The Samoan rolled into an armbar and lifted his hips, applying massive pressure to the appendage of his foe. Having no choice but to tap out, the young man did so, much to the chagrin of the room. A snarling, chest heaving, bright eyes monster stood to look at the rest of the room with ferocity in his eyes. No man would dare step closer to the foe that stood before them, and with good reason. Finally, the instructor bowed and looked up, facing his foe eye to eye. "So I see you have not completely left us." The Samoan paused to wipe the blood from his brow, and then stooped to pick up his shirt. A lesser man would have ridiculed the dojo. A lesser man would have challenged the sensei. A lesser man was not that which stood in front of the class this day. As The Samoan walked to the doorway, he was halted by the prompting of the teacher. "You cannot run from what you are Timo Bolamba. If you remember nothing about today, remember that. When you remember yourself, come back, and we will continue the lessons." A slow stream of blood ran across his face. The air from the slightly propped open door clashed with the decades old funk of hard work and effort in the building. A slow creak marred the perfect quiet as Timo opened the door. He looked back just once at the room of students wanting for their approval. In their eyes he saw the same hope that was in the eyes of Jonathan Rhine. And Timo smiled just before the door closed behind him.
