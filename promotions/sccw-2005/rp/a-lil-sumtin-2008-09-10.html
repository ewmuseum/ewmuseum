A Lil Sumtin

10 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9-10-08 Small red and blue lights shine through a pale cigarette smoke fog, cutting beams in primary colors that drop into a pool on a wooden stage. Little tiny multicolored butterflies, tethered by a translucent cord of light to a rotating sphere inset with rainbow lenses, dance around the walls and the floor and the ceiling, random at first glance but eventually settling into a long-term pattern. The heavy beat of drums is palpable, reverberating through the air in waves from speakers stacked taller than a man can jump, standing on either side of the long stage. Thick, fat bass and wah-wah guitar punctuate the music, drive it smoothly along like a serpent propelling itself through water. It's engineered to be as seductive to the ears as the main attraction is to the eyes, and it plays its part well. There are serpents here. Not the voice of temptation, but certainly the image, they walk along their route from pole to pole, stopping along the way to doff an article of clothing or partake of the offerings of their fans. Tall, short. Large-breasted and small-breasted. Various states of undress. All dancing sinuously their hour on the stage; some for the money, some simply for the sway they hold over their audience. A simple strip club is the setting, of course. Quite the large one, though it certainly doesn't hold as much class as it was designed for. Many of the patrons are somewhat dirty from hours of grueling manual labor, still dressed in their work uniforms, and the bills they pass the dancers as they strut by are equally greasy. Occasionally, a barely-dressed woman will make her way out through the obstacle course of tables and chairs, chatting up the observers, and once she finds one willing, takes him away from his party and seat toward a steel door with an 'employees only' sign on it. In the entry hallway, against the wall near the steps to the table floor of the club where most of the patrons are sitting, there's a rather large fellow pressing himself up against the wall so as to avoid incoming patrons -- not large as in rotund, but large in the way that years of physical training provides, the sort that makes a man appear as if stuffed into a three piece suit like a second skin. In fact, the suit looks so out of place in these surroundings that several of the patrons have taken to staring at him, or pointing him out, their conversations taking a turn for the derisive. 'Ha, look at that rich guy,' they say, immediately reducing him to a generalization in their minds. He and his fancy clothes and cigar represent everything they've been brought up by indoctrination to believe they would have, but could never hope to reach. He is an invader, stepping into their territory demarcated by the amorphous rules of a social cold war that nobody likes to talk about directly, but always alludes to. He runs a hand through his neatly-trimmed, layered red hair, halts the motion of his arm halfway to stare at his watch. The hawkish features of his face, while sculpted marble in youth, show the signs of middle age as he wrinkles his nose in disgust before taking a puff from his cigar. Despite the slight disdain in his expression, something that when shown usually makes a person less attractive by several degrees, he is still quite stately and handsome. And of course, when you take away the generalizations and the fact that he's standing quite near the entrance of a tawdry strip club, there aren't many who could say they recognize the gentleman. It's Michael Minor, former wrestler, and he's looking for someone. Some time passes. He remains passive, arms folded across his broad chest, occasionally looking toward the bar or surveying the tables, but quickly looking away when his gaze reaches the stage and the group of women hugging poles, removing their clothes, and accepting money from men in the front row with a collagen smile and a silicone jiggle. Soon, it's apparent in his eyes, narrowed to little slits with tiny flecks of pale green iris showing through, that he's getting tired and frustrated. That's when he gets the tap on the shoulder. It's Jadian Bridden. Mikey takes a look at him without moving a bit, then cracks a wry smirk. 'I take it you didn't find him.' The younger man shakes his head, giving out a bit of a snort, and a light kick to the wall from a booted foot for good measure. 'What can I say, I keep getting distracted.' 'Just like I figured. You could probably search all the bars within twenty miles of here, but it wouldn't do us any good, Jay...when he doesn't want to be found, he doesn't want to be found.' Mikey nods his head slightly in the direction of one of the topless waitresses. 'You forget a few things about him, Jay. For all intents and purposes, he tries to maintain as few personal connections as possible. He doesn't have a lot of friends, he ignores his family for the most part, and...well, you've seen the way he treats Juli.' 'Yeah, he's not real happy with us right now. But it's not like we're going to toss him out on his ass, you know.' 'Worry,' Minor says, shaking his head. 'He's realized that the only thing he has left in this life – now that Julissa is ignoring his calls – is gone. He didn't have anything other than the gym, and now you technically have that too.' 'He still needs to sign it over to us.' Bridden answers. Mike flashes a quick smile to the waitress. 'He'll die first.' 'If that's the way it has to be,' Jadian sighs, then starts walking over toward the bar. 'I just hope that we find him before Juli does... She's having one of her weeks.' Mike continues to enjoy the view. 'Oh, great, we better hurry then.'
