Part 6: Is In The Details

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Today Then. Late February. Captor and captive had come to an...understanding. All that was left was to finalize the details. So, that's what Wyatt Connors and Seth were doing on that brisk Sunday morning, just before church. Wyatt walked out of the Jessups' home dressed already in his best--only--suit. Hiram and Mavis were still having breakfast, which allowed Connors a few minutes alone with their farmhand without the chance of an interruption. He was sure he'd get enough questions from Seth; he didn't need any more. After about thirty yards, he came to where Seth waited, leaning against the truck. The big man idly twirled the key ring around his right index finger and smiled at the approaching Connors. 'Mornin', Reverend. Hope you slept well,' he said. 'Choke on shit,' snapped Wyatt--ever the diplomat. He reached into his right pants pocket and fished around briefly. Soon after, he extracted his hand, which was now holding a folded sheet of paper. He extended it toward Seth, who snatched it rudely away from the man who would soon be known as the True Prophet...and soon after that, as the False one. The farmhand unfolded it and read the contents. Just as Wyatt had thought, the questions began almost immediately. 'This is your list?' Seth asked. He wasn't sure what he expected, but it wasn't this. 'Yes. That should do it.' 'What the hell is all this stuff for? Bicycle tubes? Rope? PVC pipe?' 'I haven't had a match in almost a year. I'll need to get back into fighting shape. I realize that a gym membership is a little much to ask, but I'm sure we can MacGyver a few resistance-based devices with the proper utilities.' 'Do you really need that much Gatorade?' 'I prefer to get it in bulk. And try to get lemon-lime. The fruit punch stains your teeth.' 'How about the hair dye?' Wyatt frowned. 'How about you stop asking so many stupid questions?' Seth shrugged, and stuffed the paper in his pocket. 'I still don't know how you expect to pay for all of this.' 'Don't worry,' came the reply. 'I've got a plan for that.' There's that smirk again. # # # 'Another fine service today, Reverend,' Cyrus Henderson muttered through his loose dentures. 'But was it really necessary to hold the Offering four times?' There was a small murmur of agreement from the others, but Wyatt Connors silenced them all with his best holy smile. 'It's for a worthy cause, Brother Cyrus...and what better use is there for money than helping those in need. After all, is it not written: Give 'Til It Hurts?' Despite the financial concerns of the congregation, it had been a fine service. In fact, it might have been the best one yet--there had been even more sheep in the flock this week, and they all hung on his every word. He hadn't been sure if he could get away with labeling Chinese food as a sin, but he managed to pull it off. Never underestimate the power of jingoism, he thought. Naturally, all of the new churchgoers wanted to stick around and talk to this amazing new reverend...asking him all manner of questions, prompting him to respond with all manner of lies. Yes, he'd been overseas on a mission. No, he'd never met the president. Yes, he'd preached in Enid, but that was a long time ago, and it was only once. No, he never had the pleasure of eating at Wally's Meat & Beer's new franchise just outside of Enid. Eventually, their curiosity sated, the crowd dispersed. This left behind only a handful of people who had been there for the beginning of this grand revival...Old Man Henderson, the McCoys, the Widow Simmons, the Jessups, their farmhand Seth, and of course, the good reverend himself. Seth nodded discretely toward Connors. It was time to make the announcement. Wyatt began by clearing his throat, thereby drawing the attention of all gathered. Not that it was necessary, as they generally had at least half an ear on him whenever he was around...but Wyatt does so love his theatrics. 'Folks, I'd just like to say what an immense pleasure it's been, spreading the word of God among you. Looking into your faces every week, it reminds me of why I started doing this in the first place.' The immense pleasure I get from laughing at fools, he thought. Unaware of his snide internal monologue, the other churchgoers were touched by the sentiment they thought they heard. 'Oh, the pleasure's been all ours.' 'You've been a blessing sent from God.' 'I don't know how we got along without you.' Wyatt rolled his mind's eye at them, but his own actual eyes were still bright and friendly. 'I've come to realize that what I needed most was to get back in touch with the base. After all the time I spent in New York, watching the worst of humanity every day, trying to save who I could...it feels good to be around people with strong faith and morals. Recharge the ol' spiritual batteries, if you will.' There was another round of agreement, thanks, and general glad-handing. Wyatt held up his hand to put it to rest, though, as he had something important to say. 'But as they say, all good things must come to an end. I've thought about it, and prayed about it, and I think I know what God wants me to do. I must go back out into the world...I must find the worst of the sinners, and show them the way to redemption.' A small murmur tried to run through the group, but it's tough to get a good murmur going without a lot of people. Still, Wyatt knew he would have to do some sweet-talking before this was all over. 'You see, there is a company out there that operates a sort of traveling entertainment spectacle. They are desperately in need of a spiritual advisor, as not only the performers but also the fans of this...thing...are all bound for the lake of fire. I feel very strongly that I can help these people right their ships, so to speak, and turn back toward the straight and narrow path.' It was the Widow Simmons who spoke first. A bit of a surprise, since she'd always been a very quiet woman; very shy and reserved. He'd heard that she'd been like that, even before her husband's death. 'But...' she repined, '...what about us?' He held out his hands in a defensive posture--but not too defensive. He wouldn't want them to think he was up to something. 'Please, please. Do not think I am abandoning you. The truth is, you fine folks don't really need me...you'll be able to find your way to heaven without my help.' 'Oh, but you can't leave!' Alice McCoy cried out. 'Just look at all the good you've done, in such a short time!' 'My dear Alice,' he said, somehow trying to speak God's own words with a forked tongue. 'I'm not leaving you. I could never leave all of you. In fact, you are all a vital part of my new mission. 'Huh?' asked Alice's husband, Buford. Quick on the uptake, that one. 'I have been given a special opportunity, to bring the Divine Truth to many people, in many different cities. However, there is a great deal of time in between events...at least a week, and often more. Long enough for me to come back here afterward. You wonderful people will give me the strength, the resolve, and the faith I need to go wading back into the pits of hell again, hoping to bring one more poor wretched sinner into the light.' 'Wow,' the Widow Simmons whispered, and the McCoys seemed similarly impressed. Hell, even Hiram was almost smiling. It was amazing how quickly they all turned around on this. All of them, except for Mrs. Jessup. As soon as Wyatt Connors said he was leaving, she turned away. And even though his words were meant to bring reassurance and comfort, all they brought her was despair. She knew that it was selfish of her...that it must be some kind of horrible sin, to keep the wonderful new reverend all for herself and her friends. But she couldn't help the way she felt. She tried to stem the tears, but to no avail. She just didn't know if she could go through all this again. # # # Mid-March. It was almost time for the grand return. Wyatt had gotten himself closer to his fighting shape, if not quite all the way back. Since Seth was going to hound Wyatt's every step anyway, they went ahead and made him Connors' official bodyguard. The wrestling tights had been delivered, exactly to specifications. The new ring name had been chosen, although it was one that Connors was not at all happy with. Still, the farmhand had been insistent on it. They had also chosen a new name for Seth, as Wyatt felt that the farmhand's real name wasn't 'churchy' enough...no matter how many times he was reminded that the name itself comes from the Bible. All the paperwork had been filed, and the debut was planned. It would happen at the St. Patrick's Day Show--he was ready to go a week earlier, but there was no way on this earth or any other that he would go to a wrestling show in Seattle. Now, as the fateful day approached, there was not much left to do, but film the promotional video. It had been Wyatt's idea to feature Seth--soon known to the wrestling world as Jeremiah--as the main focal point for the camera. After all, nobody pays attention to a man barely five and a half feet tall, not even if he's wearing a mask. Connors would do all the talking, of course, as well as the fighting...but a psychological edge of any kind is a good thing to have. He might have kept it longer, had it not been for Smitty Duluth. But we're getting ahead of ourselves. At the moment, Wyatt Connors was putting the finishing touches on his public verbal beratement of RYUJIN, who was not long for SCCW anyway. 'The True Prophet is the wrath of God, made flesh. He can reach into your very soul and crush it. Perhaps he does so to punish you for your evil deeds. Perhaps he does it to prevent you from committing more. It is not for me to say. The Lord, as it is said, works in mysterious ways.' 'We know not where it may end, but it begins on St. Patrick’s Day, and it begins with RYUJIN.' 'You may take solace in the knowledge that we will remember your sacrifice, even if nobody else will.' With that, the smaller man fades back into the shadows made by his larger associate. Now that there is no other voice to distract you, your attention is once more fully focused on the hulking monstrosity. He stares back into the camera, as if he is trying to see you own sins, so that he may punish you for them. Then, the picture slowly fades to black. That, of course, was the end of what you saw on TV. It was not quite the end of what happened in the tiny studio in the SCCW home offices. The lights came up, and both men walked toward the camera. The smaller man--wearing a mask for his own protection--pressed the 'stop' button twice--first to stop the record, and then to eject the tape. A quick trip to the editing room, and the first words of the True Prophet would be ready for public viewing. The two men turned toward each other. The big man had a very satisfied look on his face, while the small man's displeasure was quite obvious, even through the mask. Jeremiah spoke first. ''I think it went well,' he said...not because he believed it, but because he couldn't wait to see what the response was. The masked man's reply did not disappoint. 'I think it was the stupidest goddamn thing I've ever been a part of.' Unless you had been in the room, you wouldn't believe it was the same man who spoke on the tape. The voice had more bite to it, as well as a hint of sarcasm and bitterness...in the same way that a Girls Gone Wild DVD features 'a hint' of nudity. Had there been anyone else listening, they would have known in an instant that it was Wyatt Connors under the mask. 'I'm sorry you feel that way, friend. I was hoping that you would embrace your role.' The farmhand-turned-bodyguard put plenty of concern in his voice, in the hopes of really winding up his 'boss.' It worked, as Wyatt leveled a glare on him that could melt a bus in half a second. 'You say that as if I had a choice in the matter.' Jeremiah grinned, like a satisfied tomcat with a feather in his mouth. 'That's true. You don't.' And that, dear viewers, is the True Origin of the False Prophet. # # # Now. He was getting closer...but so were they. The Scarred Man had agents everywhere...of every age, color, and sex. He could trust no one. He stayed away from the interstate, and from large gathering places. When in town, he stuck to the alleys. When in the country, he walked through the farmlands. His feet were blistered and sore, but he could not stop walking, nor was it safe to ask for help. After all, was it not the Apostle Simon who said, 'You got to walk that lonesome valley...you got to walk it by yourself.' No. No, that was the Kingston Trio. Close enough. Step after step, mile after mile, he trudged. Even when he was too tired and hungry and pained to continue, he dug down into his soul and found the strength. Yes, this was a great tribulation...but it was far preferable to the one he had escaped. The captivity. The shame. The Scarred Man. 'Get your mind off the pain,' he whispered--there was no one around for miles, but words could still carry to his enemy's ears. 'Think of something else. Think of...think of her.' The angel. Her kindly face and warm smile shone in the sky, brighter than the sun. Suddenly, the pain and hunger and all the other wants of the human body felt smaller. More than food, or water, or rest, or medical attention, he needed her. To nurse his wounded soul back to health. He would not be afraid of any demon, not even the Scarred Man...not when such an angel beckoned for him to return. His pace quickened, and his steps were almost lively. Heaven waited before him...and hell came behind.
