The way it has to be (w/Jeb Stewart)

17 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/17/2007 There are a lot of classy restaurants in Washington DC, a lot of places you could eat an exotic and expensive breakfast. The International House of Pancakes was not one of those. FUSE commissioner, Smitty T Duluth, typically enjoyed a little fine dining. But today was not typical. There are some pieces of news you can receive that have a way of nesting inside of you, and laying eggs. And speaking of eggs, there are three of them in front of Smitty right now. These eggs are scrambled and accompanied by bacon, hash browns, and wheat toast. He lifelessly plays with his eggs with a fork, envisioning the look on his daughter’s face on the last edition of Killzone. How could his little girl be capable of even tolerating a man like that? Certainly it wasn’t a sudden thing. One doesn’t just meet someone like that and go head over heels in love, its imposs… STD’s thoughts were interrupted by a man clearing his throat. Smitty looked up to find a face even more joyless than his. * * * "I don’t really dislike ‘em." "That’s not the point." Welcome to a studio in Washington, rented out for the day for a purpose that it has never been used for: to cut a wrestling promo. Although, this promo will not start for some time. There are still issues being…worked out. In front of a plain white backdrop, "The River Rat" Lane Stevens and Jeb Stewart sat on stools. The former was dressed in a white button up silk shirt, and white dress pants. The latter wore his black and red Affliction tee, and jeans. A couple cameras were facing them, yet to be turned on. The lights were dimmed in the half of the room that housed all of the equipment. "Why is that not the point?" Jeb asked, sounding a little confused. "Do I even want to know the point?" Lane sighed, and replied: "Aimz and Crisis think they are fighting Lane Stevens, and some gentle giant that would rather take Amy Campbell out for ice cream than lay a hand on her. Part of being a huge ass motherfucker, is intimidation. Part of winning is intimidation…a part of the formula our tag-team has been lacking. What you were brought in the fold for, is intimidation." Jeb nodded. "Let me make this clear. If you aren’t able to do the job of enforcer in the manner that Sage requires, we won’t be able to pay you the kind of money you are currently receiving…and of course there’s a domino effect, and a loved one suffers unnecessarily." Jeb closed his eyes, and an image of his mother lying in bed flashed. There are tubes coming out of her. Lane reached out, and put a hand on his partner’s shoulder. "This isn’t coming from me buddy, I’m only the messenger," The River Rat added sympathetically. When Jeb closes his eyes again, a thin smile flickers across Stevens’ face. "Darcy Crisis told me he doesn’t give a damn what happens to your Mom, but we do," The River Rat stated, his hand still resting on Jeb’s shoulder. Stewart’s eyes popped open in an instant, his face a mask of rage. * * * The face that Smitty looked up at was none other than the Affliction’s business associate, a man of money and contacts. Saul’s scarred face portrayed no emotion other than a dull pain. The grimace that lingered there was not voluntary, but more like a stain that would not come out. He extended his hand to the commissioner. "A pleasure as always, sir," Saul offered coldly. Duluth did not shake the hand. He simply paused his chewing for a brief moment, stared at the hand as if it was an alien creature, and resumed chewing again. Saul sat down in the booth across from the commish. "The political climate is changing here," Saul started, looking out the window into a busy street, "and I can feel that a stand is about to be made." Still, Smitty says nothing. "I understand that in these new strange times, you might feel…a little more biased than usual," Saul said. "Ya think?" Smitty finally said. "Look, what the fuck do you want?" Saul is not bothered by yelling. His employer, an odd man by the name of Artur, puts him through much worse. "Just to let you know that your daughter is not with Clinton now as a means to get at you. As irrational as it may be in my mind, he loves her, and he has spent some time now ‘courting’ her. You have an opportunity now to make things right." "And just how might I do that?" Duluth asked, his voice full of suspicion. "Side with us, side with The Affliction. Side with your daughter," Saul pleaded. "Aimz and others are planning something, and you know as well as anyone that in the end they will not win. We…always win. Mr. Stevens has authorized me to give you quite a bit of money, and the opportunity to meet with your daughter." The commish seems to take a minute to consider this. "Well you can tell Mr. Stevens that I have authorized him to walk off a fucking cliff, and get dead," Smitty responded, his voice trembling. Saul smiled politely. "Why go down with the ship, Mr. Duluth?" he asked, just as politely. "Because the ship has FUSE written on the side," Smitty started, "so please…let me be." "Very well," Saul said, "enjoy your breakfast. Oh, and by the way, if you were to help ensure Lane’s victory in the tower match somehow we would…" "GET…OUT," Smitty barked, drawing attention from the restaurant. The Affliction’s business associate got up out of the booth, and was quickly gone. STD was left with the remains of his breakfast. He wiped a tear from his cheek. "Jackals," he said aloud, softly to himself. "Goddamn jackals." * * * "The thing about cutting a promo," Lane said, "is you can’t just make your point…you have to make it in compelling fashion. It’s like dropping a leaflet with a bomb attached. Right after they get the message, their arms are going one way and their guts are going another. You can explode their world with the truth…or, petty jokes about how they suck a lot of dick." "Well, that’s lovely," Jeb replied sarcastically. There were now men behind the cameras, seemingly ready to start filming. "This screen is blank now," Lane said, gesturing at the plain white backdrop behind them, "but when we send it to Aimz & Crisis, we’re gonna get the geeks to put a lot of shit there. And all you got to do is read off the teleprompter." Jeb fidgets uncomfortably. "You ready to do this thing?" Lane asked. "I guess." * * * Fade up. This is a promo. Jeb Stewart stood in the foreground, still looking uncomfortable. In the backdrop we see larger than life video clips of Aimz in a Lane Stevens camel clutch, followed by Darcy Crisis being put in a choke lift by Jeb, etc, etc. "Hello, Darcy…hello girl who is unclean," Jeb starts. "CUT!" Lane yelled from off camera. "The teleprompter says skank!" "Yeah, I know but…" "SKANK!" Lane shrieks. Jeb sighed. * * * Take two. "Hello Darcy. Hello....skank. Say, you know how many Amy Campbell’s it takes to screw in a light bulb? One to screw in the light bulb, and uh…" Jeb pauses for a few moments. "Two more to stand around and suck a bunch of naughty parts." "DICK!" Lane screams from off camera. "SHE SUCKS DICK!" * * * By about take thirty six, Lane agreed to let Jeb cut his own promo. There were no flashy images in the background this time, only the white backdrop. Jeb sat on a stool, and avoided eye contact with the camera. He spoke quietly. "A lot of people think I’m just a big dummy, and I understand why ya’ll think that. But I’m smart enough to know that there’s nowhere else I could be getting paid this much money. Factory work don’t pay nothin’ like this. All my bouncer jobs, they ain’t paid nothin’ like this either. A lot of people also assume that I’m the nicest guy ever, just cause I don’t like hitting people if I don’t have to…and I talk quiet. Well, I ain’t no asshole, but I can tell you one thing. I’m no saint either." Jeb cleared his throat, and finally made eye contact with the camera. "I used to be a bouncer at a little bar called Tully’s about a year ago. There was this fella who came in all the time, always looking to cause trouble. Now I always refrained from really harmin’ this fella cause he had a hard life, his parents both died when he was a little boy…he was never much good at football or any of the sports he tried out for when we was in high school. He carried a kinda anger with him. Well one day he came in that bar and he spit in my bosses’ face, and told him old Jeb ain’t gonna do shit. I threw him out of that bar and he landed funny, collided with the bike rack and he don’t walk so good anymore. He don’t walk at all anymore. I don’t take no pride in that, but it’s the way it is. Darcy…Lane wouldn’t lie to me. And he told me you said somethin’ mean about my momma, and I don’t understand why you did that. She’s a sweet woman, and never in her life did anything to you." Jeb inhaled sharply, trying to keep it together. "I ain’t gonna hurt you just because of that, I’m gonna hurt you because I’m supposed to. I’m gonna hurt the both of you because that is my job. I might hurt you a lot. I won’t be takin’ pride in that either. It’s just the way it has to be." Jeb walked off the screen. We fade to black. * * * But we are not done. Lane Stevens was pissing in one of the studio’s bathrooms. He had one hand on his Johnson, and another on his cell phone. On the other end, Saul said: "Duluth rejected your offer." Lane nodded, as if Saul was right in front of him. "I figured, had to give it one last shot though." "What should I tell The Scourge? Is plan B operational?" Saul asked. "Don’t you have a better name for it than…plan B?" Lane replied, laughing. "I don’t concern myself with flashy names, sir," Saul said coldly. "Fair enough. It is operational." End transmission.
