Road Trip

Date: Today "I do believe Nick that you and I will be parting ways as of today." The gravelly voice of Edward Lambert is far harsher than the normal tone that he uses with his personal biographer. Typically when he holds videotaped conversations and monologues to be shown to his son his tone is typically cordial, and for his, almost friendly. Currently however his tone is one that many people would recognize; angry. "I don’t understand Mr. E. You told me you wanted me to record everything for you, if I’ve made a mistake with the equipment or a video…" Edward waves his left hand above the desk in the universally known "cut" motion. "That’s enough Nick. The problem isn’t anything with your videography skills; it’s more in line with your extra curricular activities. As of… well, I’m not quite sure when, those activities have cost me something rather important to me." Edward reaches to the nearly overflowing ashtray on his desk, lifts up the latest in his long line of cigarettes, and takes a deep draw. "We’re done Nick. Go back to Chicago. I doubt that I will contact you again but I may. We will have to see." * * * The house that Edward Lambert calls home has seen many leavings over the years. For many years Edward would walk out of the door on a Friday only to return on the following Monday. For years his weekly routine was the same; Kia would wave to him as he drove away, each time knowing that while it was a dangerous life, it was never going to be the last she saw of him. In light of his medical problems, this has of course changed. Rather than remaining home, Kia now accompanies her husband on each of his trips; their home watched under the careful eye of neighbors or his foster brother Dashielle Fairborne Jr. This trip however Edward will take alone. "You can’t come with me Kia, not this time and not where I’ve got to go." The look on Edwards faces one of stony resolve. "I’ve got to ensure your safety, you have to remain here." Edward stands dressed casually in a pair of blue jeans, cowboy boots, and a simple white t-shirt. On either side of him rest two bags, one a moderately sized suitcase, the other an overstuffed duffle bag. Kia for her part stands with both hands planted firmly on her hips and a scowl on her face. "Edward Carter Lambert, if you think I am about to let you run off over god knows where doing god knows what with god knows who you are in for a rude surprise! I haven’t been married to you for this long to not know how your brain works, and if you think for one instant that I am going to stay here while you ride off and do something as idiotic as you normally do then you have another think coming!" Kia’s long brown hair whips in the mild breeze as she stares at her obstinate husband. "Kia…" Edward sighs, "Listen, I know that I haven’t always been truthful about some things I’ve done, and I know that you are worried about the cancer, but trust me this time. I’ve got to do this alone, and nobody, not Dash, not Alan, nobody can help me out with this. I have to do it alone." "No Edward, it’s your turn to listen to me. For years I have watched as you did your level best to ensure that you made it into an early grave. First it was the drinking, then the wrestling, and all the myriad of other idiotic things you have done with your life! I will not have you running out on me or Drake now, god knows how long we will get to have you around, and I am not about to lose one second of that time!" Kia’s tone is one of fury; unfortunately it has been directed at her husband far too often over the years. "If you think for one second that I am going to sit out whatever your latest idiotic scheme is, then you… you…" Edward sighs and stares down at his wife as she slowly winds down from her tirade. "Are you done Kia?" "I haven’t even gotten started!" "I’m not going to win this argument am I?" Kia crosses her arms. "Not a chance in hell." "Right," Edward sighs again, "Go inside and pack about four days worth of clothes, we’ll go when you are done." Kia’s left hand comes up from her hip and sticks straight out at Edward, palm up. "Keys. Now." Nodding slowly Edward pulls a ring clip from his belt and hands the keys to his black Ford F150. She puts them in the pocket of the jeans that fit her hips snugly and stands on her tip toes to give him a peck on the cheek. "Thank you Edward." She then slaps him smartly across the face. "Now give me the other keys." "Goddamnit." * * * "WHAT?!" Kia’s voice is shrill in to the ear, her voice having become more so over the past few hours of this extended road trip. Years ago, when Edward first made this trip down the interstate he did so with loud music blaring. Lynyrd Skynyrd, Metallica, and Rammstein all helped to build his mood to a violent crushing rage as he drove. Of course, the case of beer that he would drink each trip didn’t help his attitude as he sped down the road to Ponca City, Oklahoma. The music that issues forth from the custom speakers inside the truck instead put forth Gretchen Wilson, Big and Rich, and Cowboy Troy. Edward Lambert hates Country music in the same way that most men hate a prostate exam. "Goddamnit" he thinks to himself. "Of course I have to explain this shit, exactly what I didn’t want to fucking do, especially not now." "It’s really simple Kia. There used to be a man living in our basement, he’s gotten loose and I don’t want him to end up hurting himself so we have to go get him back." Edwards tone is conversational and to the point. "Edward, I have been inside our basement, the only things down there are the water heater, that old weight bench, some ratty furniture, and your wet bar. Unless of course you are talking about the basement that locked door of yours leads to, you know the door Edward, the one that you never let anyone other than yourself into; the door that is locked up like Fort Knox." "Err… yes Kia. That door." "So, somehow, inside the door that only you have a key to, we had a house guest?" "Uh huh." Typically Edward has plenty to say, his reticence at this time serves simply to show his guilt. "Edward, how long did we have a house guest you never told me about?" "Here it comes." he thinks. "Oh, not that long." Kia turns as much as the seatbelt will allow her to and focuses the cold stare that most husbands dread on Edward. Her tone when she again speaks is syrupy sweet. "How long Edward?" "Uh… I think it was January the seventh or eighth..." "Edward…" Kia’s tone could freeze the core of the earth. "Seven months?" "Uh… of two thousands and two." "WHAT?! Who have you been keeping in my basement for six and a half years?!" "Funny you should ask Kia, that’s a really long, convoluted, and very strange question with an even more convoluted answer." "Well Edward," Kia waves her hands at the roadside flashing past them on the interstate. "It looks like we have plenty of time for you to tell me!" "Yeah, yeah it does." Edward reaches for the pack of Lucky Strikes sitting in the cup holder in the dash of the truck, and after fishing one out he lights it with his ever present Zippo lighter. Finally with a long exhaling sigh he speaks again. "You remember when I bought that new Toshiba wide screen for the basement? The one that I told you had a bad screen, and had to go back to the store?" "Obviously I do, that’s the television sitting in the living room." "Actually, inside the first box was Ibrahim Seck, there wasn’t any television." "IBRAHIM SECK WAS LIVING IN MY BASEMENT?!" "Yeah, that’s pretty much it." "Jesus Christ Edward, what were you thinking? Kidnapping him and keeping him in the basement? Did you even think about what you were doing? What were you going to do with him when you die?" "Yes Kia, I thought about it for a long time." "AND?" "I was going to brick the fucking wall up and seal that fucker down there where he couldn’t hurt anyone else ever again." He takes another long draw from the cigarette clenched between his fingers. "I had planned to throw Desade down there to join him if I got the chance. I figured leaving those two bricked up in a tomb was the best thing I could ever do for humanity." "Damnit Edward." "Oh I know, it was a bad idea when I came up with it Kia." "And you still plan to go through with it?" Edward looks across at his wife, and the grin that crosses his face can best be described as feral. "Oh yes Kia, I most certainly do."
