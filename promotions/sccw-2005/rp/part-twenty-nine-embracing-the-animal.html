Date: 6/22/10 "How do you solve a problem like a busted up champ? Well, if you’re SCCW, you manage to turn it into your newest main event and, hey, everyone’s a winner! With Lane Stevens riding the bench after the injury he suffered at the Amherst show (word has it no one knows just how long Mr. Stevens may be out of action), the PTB at SCCW (acronym overload!) have decided to run an emergency overhaul of their next Sin On Spike megacard and run an emergency one-night tourney for the Universal Title." "Looking at the folks signed up for the tourney, it looks like Ess-Cee-Cee-Dub is taking this pretty seriously. Kingsley and Kannon are getting their shots at the belt, appropriate given the impact both men have made in the federation as of late. Connors is always one to keep an eye on and the duo of Pierce and Aimz help fill out the crazy bitch quotient that every tourney needs." "It just wouldn’t be a tourney, though, without some weird ass choices being thrown into the mix. This is the biggest stage Jared Sykes has ever played on and I’m not all that sure he’s ready for it. He’s got the goods, no doubt about it, just hope he can sack up and step up to the level of everyone else in the tourney. I’m surprised to see Baron Von Blackberry in there as well, to be honest…he’s never really been pushed as a Universal Title level contender." "But if Sykes and Blackberry are a bit strange, the last contender in the tourney is looking more like a pity pick with each passing day. Coming off a frankly embarrassing loss last week (not to mention the beatdown at the hands of Xavier Kannon), I’ve gotta wonder what the hell Lance Marshall is even doing competing for the title at all. Yeah, he’s got a hell of a history and all but that’s old news…and when he actually did manage to get his hands on the Universal Title, he held it for all of about thirty seconds." "Note to the folks in charge of SCCW: if you don’t want this tourney to blow up completely in your face, ditch Marshall and fill his spot with someone else. Someone who won’t embarrass you…which means pretty much anyone else on the roster. It’s time that Marshall and his fans realize that the Lion’s best days are done and he needs to retire before he gets permanently crippled. The cat’s been declawed, y’all, and he needs to step down before he gets put down." -Excerpted from the Terry Hoffman’s June 18th posting to Wrestle Speak: The No-Holds-Barred Wrestling Blog- ---- "Shit." The thick Texas drawl wrapped itself around the obscenity, stretching the word from one syllable to three. The body attached to the voice shifted slightly in the armchair, the heavily muscled frame nestled in among the Italian leather. One large paw seemed to swallow the mouse, a thick finger casually turning the scroll wheel as the image on the computer screen slowly panned downwards. The slight frown on the man’s face gradually deepened as he read the words on the screen, a full blown scowl slowly blossoming on his features. "Is there a problem, Mister Landry?" Where the first voice spoke of the heat of Texas, the reply carried with it the air of breeding and class, of speech lessons and formal English pronunciation. Its owner was a tall man with slicked back blonde hair and piercing green eyes. His face was statuesque, as though a sculptor had worked with all his skill and craft on a block of marble and the face had been what remained. His suit fit him precisely (he had paid the tailors handsomely to ensure that), the materials costly and only slightly flashy. The tie was silk, the shoes Italian leather. He carried himself with the grace and poise of a man who knew exactly how to get what he wanted…no matter who or what stood in his way. But then, when you were the man in charge, appearances were everything. "No problems, Mister Atherton, sir," replied the man in the armchair. Colton Landry stood up from his position in front of the computer, stretching his muscled arms above his head. "Just some jackass commentator on the internet being smart." "As much good as the Internet has done this corporation, Mister Landry," replied Mister Atherton, "it has also proven itself to be the most fertile breeding ground for idiocy that the world has ever seen. It seems that everyone who has the bare intelligence needed to operate a computer…and as some of our employees have shown us, it isn’t much…has decided to have their say on the World Wide Web. How does that saying go, Mister Landry?" "Opinions are like assholes, sir. Everyone’s got one and most of them stink." "A bit crude but very apposite," replied William Atherton. He paused, glancing at the form of his assistant. Most, he realized, would have looked no deeper than Colton Landry’s physique, dismissed Colton as vain and obsessed with his appearance…assumed that the presence of muscles had to equal the absence of brains. They would not have seen the diligent employee, the man who was often the first to arrive and the last to leave, the keen and analytical mind behind the fearsome frame. William was glad he had not been the one to make that mistake. Despite his statement, Colton Landry still looked troubled. He scratched idly at the back of his head, just under the brim of his Stetson. It was a signal William recognized. "I see," said William, "that you have failed to convince yourself. What’s troubling you? Are you regretting your earlier decision regarding our upcoming endeavour?" "Not at all, sir," Colton replied, "I stand behind my decision one hundred percent. But perception is as important as truth. What they’re saying online…it’s not true but that’s not gonna stop people from thinking that it is true." "Say something enough times and it becomes truth, eh? How familiar I am with that. And you are indeed right, Mister Landry. Indeed, Sarah Palin would hardly have a career otherwise." "Please don’t mention that bitch’s name in my presence," purred a voice from the doorway. "Language, Ms. Russell," snapped William. Everywhere Veronica Russell walked, it was with intent. So it continued as she stalked her way into the room, making her way towards the two men. Instinctively, Colton took off his hat and held it to his heart as Veronica came towards him. She still managed to do it, Colton thought, every single time. She didn’t try like some of the women he’d seen, the one who wore their shirts too tight or their skirts too short. Ronnie, by contrast, had gone for a perfectly fitted Gianni Versace suit, the professional effect ruined only by the three inch pair of Loboutins she was wearing. So why, Colton thought, did she always manage to make him so nervous? "Ma’am." Ronnie walked over to Colton, somehow managing to balance herself enough on her heels so that she could give Colton a chaste little kiss on the cheek. "Ooh," she purred, "aren’t you sweet. But I’ve told you before, Texas, it’s Ronnie." "Yes, ma’…I mean, Ronnie." "Mister Landry was just telling me about some…issues…that he was having with our plans." Ronnie turned her attention to William, a look of mild confusion on her face. "What, he didn’t tell you about the plan?" "I was getting to that." "A plan," answered William. "Oh, I do like how that sounds. Do you have a plan, Mister Landry?" "Yes, sir," Colton replied, "I do." ---- "Now, are you sure you’ve got all of your homework, Zach? "Yes, Daddy." Zach Marshall squirmed slightly in the passenger seat of his father’s car as they idled outside the entrance to school. Although he didn’t have the words for it, Zach had been deeply appreciative of how his mother and father had taken care of him ever since…well, it was something he and Mommy and Daddy didn’t talk about very much. Ever since it had happened, they hadn’t let him out of their sight. Whether it had been at a doctor’s appointment or school or that other doctor where all he did was talk…if they could be there, they were. There had been days when Zach had half expected to see his father sitting next to him in class. And Zach had loved his father for it. At first. But he was feeling better now (not like he once did but better) and they were still acting like it was the same day. He could never go over to friends’ houses, had to miss two birthday parties because his mom and dad couldn’t be there…and the other kids were starting to call him a baby. To his face, even! And he hated that. "Now, you remember that Mommy will be here to pick you up, right? Make sure to only go with Mommy, don’t let anyone else take you home, don’t.." "I know, Daddy! I’m not a baby so stop treating me like one!" Zach couldn’t believe it when he said it, immediately wished he could take it back. He’d never talked to his father like that, ever. He braced himself for what he knew had to be coming. Daddy would be angry with him, punish him, maybe take away the new Lego Harry Potter game that Zach had been wanting to play…but no. Instead, he just looked at Zach like he was sad. "I’m sorry, Daddy. I didn’t mean to yell. I didn’t mean to make you sad." Lance Marshall smiled weakly at his son. "That’s okay, champ, I know. It’s just…I want you to be okay, you know?" "I know, Daddy. I’m sorry." Lance reached over and ruffled Zach’s hair gently, giving his son a quick peck on the cheek. "I know, champ. It’s okay. I’ll see you when you get home, okay?" "Okay, Daddy." Zach opened the door and clambered as quickly as he could out of the car. Just before turning away and walking towards the school, he turned to his father and said "I love you, Daddy." "I love you too, champ." Lance sighed as he watched Zach walk away, joining some of his friends by the school gate. Zach was right, ultimately. They couldn’t stay hostage to what had happened, couldn’t let themselves be shackled by fear. Zach was a tough kid and he was trying, as best he could, to move beyond what had happened. He just…couldn’t help wanting to protect his son, cocoon him away from all the things that could hurt him out there in the world. But that never really went away, did it? It didn’t matter if they were 6, 16 or 66…they would always be your children and you would always want to protect them. Lance winced as a bolt of pain shot from his ankle up through his leg, an aftereffect of Xavier Kannon’s attack at the last show. He knew he could work through it, if necessary, and he was determined not to give him even the slightest satisfaction. But damn if it didn’t have its bad moments. Lance made a note that he would have to get Marco at the gym to look it over, see if there was anything they could do to rehab the injury. Good thing he was going there anyway. ---- It took Lance a moment to realize that something was different when he pulled into the gym parking lot. Jerry was standing outside by the entrance, scanning the parking lot for someone or something. Usually, Lance realized, he’d have to wait until he was inside before he was greeted by Jerry and his rude T-shirt of the day. Lance pulled into the first empty spot he could find, noting that Jerry’s gaze stayed fixed on the Cayenne from the moment he saw it. "Hey, Jer," said Lance as he hoped out of the car with his gym bag. "What’s up?" Jerry (today’s shirt: F-CK: All that’s missing is U) walked over to meet Lance in the parking lot. "Glad I caught ya, bro. Wanted to give you a heads up before you went inside." "Why, what’s wrong?" "There’s this new guy," answered Jerry, "in town for the NPC show. Will probably win it, way he’s looking. Anyway, he’s been talking some mad shit about everyone, you in particular." "Me?" asked Lance. "Why me?" "Dunno, man. But the minute he found out this place was your regular, he’s been talking some mad smack. I’m talking stuff I’m not sure I want to repeat." Great, Lance thought, another little problem to deal with. "Thanks for the heads up, Jer. What’s the kid look like so I’ll know to stay out of his way?" "’Bout your height, just a little smaller than you. Blonde, almost buzzcut. Sounds like he’s from Texas or some shit." "Thanks, man. I’ll keep an eye out." ---- Twenty minutes ago "Hey, Jerry?" "Yeah, kid?" Jerry looked over at the voice that had called his name, the one belonging to the new kid from…fuck, Texas or Oklahoma or some shit like that….who was now calling him over. Nice enough kid, tended to get in, do his workout and get out. "There a problem?" "Nope. I just need you to do me a favor." "What’s up?" Jerry watched as the kid reached into his gym bag and pulled out his wallet, slowly taking out a fat wad of bills and counting them slowly in his hand. If he wasn’t just showing hundreds on the outside…fuck, there had to be at least five G’s in there! Jerry took a step back when the kid tried to pass the wad of cash his way. "Whoa, kid, hold on. What’s this about? Before you say anything, I just wanna let you know: I’m not doing anything illegal, I can’t hook you up with gear and I for damn sure ain’t gonna fuck you." The kid laughed. "Nothing illegal, don’t need gear…we’ll see about the fucking. This is Lance Marshall’s place, though, right?" "Yeah, so?" "Good. The money’s to make sure you tell him something. You’re gonna need to be very specific and very exact." "Kid, that’s a one, maybe two hundred dollar deal. What’s the rest of the cash for?" "Oh, that’s to cover the damages." ---- "Lance Marshall is, fundamentally, a good and decent man." "Public knowledge, Mister Landry, and nothing I did not already know." "What he also is, sir, is an absolute animal inside the ring. Or at least, he used to be. I have seen him come damn close to ripping guys in two on live TV. He’s a good man outside the ring. It’s what makes him a good husband and a good father. But we need to get the animal back." "So, Mister Landry, what exactly did you have in mind?" "Something very public, sir. Something no one will be able to ignore." ---- "You’re telling me not one of you fucking Hollywood faggots can spot me?" It hadn’t been hard to identify the new kid, Lance thought. The constant stream of curse words had been a definite sign, the Thick Texas accent had been another. And if he’d somehow managed to miss those, Lance thought, the kid’s apparently fail proof knack for pissing off everyone within a ten foot radius would certainly have sent up the warning flag. So, as per Jerry’s warning, he’d done his best to stay away from the kid. Not that it had actually stopped the kid. He’d managed to toss off the odd nasty remark Lance’s way which Lance had done his damndest to ignore. Typically, Lance thought, it wouldn’t even have been that hard. But, thought Lance, his focus had been wavering a lot lately…and the kid’s remarks were finding their way in, getting under his skin. And they were non-stop. "What’s the matter, pussy," sneered the kid once again, "that bulldyke you married to cut off your balls?" That’s it, Lance thought, sloppily reracking the dumbbells he was using and stomping over to the bench press bar where the kid had been getting in a chest workout. "What the fuck is your problem?" Lance bellowed. The kid stood up, stomping over to meet Lance halfway. The two titans stood toe to toe, sizing each other up. "My problem, "Lion", is you. Can’t believe you still have the nerve to call yourself that especially after Kannon just made you his bitch." "You have no idea what you’re talking about." "Aw, is the big bad wrestler gonna cry. Is the tough guy gonna go off and be a pussy because I hurt his feelings? Fuck, I thought Sykes was the only one of you guys with a vagina." Forget it, Lance thought, this guy wasn’t worth it. "You know what, kid? Fuck you. Come back and talk to me once your balls have dropped." Lance grabbed his gym bag from off the floor and made a beeline for the exit. "Hey," screamed the kid from behind him. "Don’t you turn your back on me, faggot! Don’t you fucking turn your back on me!" Lance was almost at the door when he heard a voice he didn’t recognize, one with a thick English accent, yell out his name. That was all the warning he got before he felt the kid’s fist make solid contact with the back of his head, sending him staggering forward. ---- "Yeah, just what I thought, you faggot. One punch and you go down like a bitch. Maybe I should go and fuck that wife of yours, show her what a real man feels like…" Robert Spencer maneuvered himself forward through the crowd as best he could, making sure that the iPhone 4’s camera (the boss had managed to snag all of them release models early) had the fight in view. He knew Colton could take a hell of a beating and could dish one out in return (their practice sessions had been proof of that) but he still wondered if Colton really knew what he was doing this time. He’d seen the look on Marshall’s face before, a look he tended to refer to as MWTKM (or motherfucker wants to kill me, to give it its full name). If Colton wanted an fight, bloody hell, was he about to get one. But his instructions had been clear. Keep filming, keep getting it all on camera, only step in if it really did look like Marshall was about to kill Colton. So that’s what Robert Spencer did as Colton and Marshall started trading blows, knocking seven shades of hell out of each other in the process. Not wavering for a moment. Not even when Marshall sent Colton flying through a plate glass window. ---- Etienne shifted slightly at the sound of broken glass, lifting off slightly from the car roof he had perched himself on. Sounds, he thought with a smile, like things were going even better than expected. He laughed slightly as he jumped from car roof to car roof, allowing himself the luxury of the odd little flip as he did so. It wasn’t like anyone was going to be watching him, he reasoned. Stopping on a car roof about ten feet away from the action, he took the phone from his pocket, set the video record mode to high definition and pointed the camera directly at the action. Everything had to be taken into consideration, Etienne thought, every angle covered. ---- The fight ended up lasting about ten minutes. Any shorter, Colton thought, and it would have screamed obvious fakery. Any longer and it would have killed the message it was trying to convey. So for the first five minutes, he had done his damndest to stand and trade blows with Marshall…and it had been just as hard as he’d imagined. They were both doing some damage, sure…there were black eyes and busted lips aplenty and he was going to be feeling that spear that had sent him through the plate glass window for a little while. It hadn’t take quite as much effort as he’d expected to start winding the fight down, making sure his punches were just a little too sloppy, leaving openings for Lance to get through. One particularly hard right uppercut had sent Colton to the floor, a turn that Lance had followed up on by delivering several kicks to his ribs. After the first few kicks, he could hear some of the crowd pulling Lance back, yelling at him to stop, that the kid was done, that he had learned his lesson. Robbie’s voice, he noticed, being right at the front of the crowd. And as the crowd placed themselves between the two men, trying their damndest to calm Marshall’s bloodlust, Colton took a moment to relax. No one was looking at him and that was fine; no one needed to. Besides, they would probably find it slightly confusing that a man who’d just gotten his ass kicked was lying on the ground with the biggest smile on his face. ---- The conversation with the cops hadn’t lasted very long. The kid was refusing to press charges (too embarrassed, the cops guessed) and, despite his willingness to face the consequences, no one was particularly eager to arrest Lance. So, after making Jerry swear to him that he’d send him the bill for the window, Lance had gotten into his car and driven off. He hadn’t gone home, not at first. He couldn’t. He wouldn’t know how to explain this to Alanna, how to tell her about what had happened. Instead, he had driven to Griffith Park and spent some time on the grass in front of the Observatory, taking some time to collect his thoughts and figure out his next move. By the time he got home, he no longer had any need of an explanation. YouTube had taken care of that for him. ---- "Cor, you are gonna look a right mess tomorrow, cowboy, and no mistake." "I’ll live. Did you guys get the footage uploaded?" "Yes. It’ll take a few minutes for YouTube to process it but it should be online shortly." "English please, Frenchie! The only words I understood in all that was YouTube." "He said that everything went according to plan, Robbie. God, I hope so." ---- He’d thought that everything would be over. That with the footage of the fight online, everything would be in tatters: his life, his career. That hadn’t happened. In among the "Obivsouly fake!~" and comments which had proven not everyone should own a computer, there had been comments he hadn’t expected. That had been impressed with him. That had thought he’d done a good job of taking the kid out. That he’d looked cool. It was only that night, as he sat on the deck looking out on the stars and pondering the bizarre turn the day had taken, that one specific moment from the fight came into sharp relief. He’d had the kid in a headlock and the kid had whispered something at him. He hadn’t paid attention at the time, far more concerned with getting his pound of flesh from the kid. But now, with the distance of time and perspective, the remark suddenly rang in his ears as clear as a bell. "Marshall’s capable of being an animal inside the ring, sir. But that side of him…he’s terrified of it. I want to get him to embrace it." The kid had looked up at him and whispered: "Now there’s the guy I wanted to grow up to be." And before he could stop himself, before he was even entirely sure what he was doing: Lance Marshall began to laugh. ---- We now return you to your regularly scheduled lives, already in progress.
