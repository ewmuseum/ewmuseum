I Get it From my Daddy

undated

Date: 7-16-10 She stood in front of the mirror, her heart pounding in her chest as a lone tear trickled down her cheek – it may have been the only one she had left. Julissa Minorez ran her index finger over the purpling bruise on her ribs. Things were getting more and more out of hand, and she had no idea how much longer she would be able to keep them from getting worse. For now, she would just have to be smart about how she went about things. She lifted the white summer dress with big bright sunflowers up by its spaghetti straps, away from the pile she had accumulated on the bench to her left. The four by six dressing room had become her home away from home for the last five or six minutes, but any longer and the attendant would wonder what was taking so long. "Hey, Sandra," a South Jersey accent echoed into the room from the small slat at the bottom of the door. "How did that dress look?" Sandra... She wasn't even sure what had made her use that name. Maybe it was a subconscious decision because of everything going on with Jay at the moment. She couldn't be certain, but it was strange to hear someone call her by Jadian's mothers name. "Looks fabulous," she replied in a voice that she felt was a bit more Anna Faris than Snookie. "I'll be right out." "Okay." Julissa removed the dark blue denim skirt she was wearing, the matching off-white lace panty and bra combo the only clothing she had on before she slipped the white dress up over her head and let it form to the curves of her body. She lifted her arm and made sure the bruise was unnoticeable, then grabbed the pile of clothes and tucked them under her arm as she grabbed her small purse with the other hand. She backed into the door to open it and let herself out of the small confined space, then proceeded to walk over to a red basket with other clothes in it and dump her own pile in along with the rest. "Didn't find anything you liked?" the attendant with a fake smile asked as Sandra tucked a stray strand of straight brown hair behind her ear. The hair was not her own, but she always managed to forget after a few minutes. "Not really," Sandra replied with an insincere smile of her own. "But it ain't as bad as the stuff I saw at Wal-Mart... You see where my friend headed off to?" The attendant with the red vest over a black button-up polo shirt pointed nonchalantly. "Towards the shoes, I think." "Thanks," she replied before heading in that direction. "It's alright, she can be a crafty little bitch when she wants to be." Jadian Bridden rolled his eyes as he spoke into his cell phone. "She's in the wind now, so just go back to where she left the car and sit on it." It took less than ten seconds for him to flip the phone closed and toss it across the room. He was angry, but not because they had lost sight of Julissa. He never expected them to keep up with her, because she was far too smart for them. Shaw had taught her how to seduce a man with a mere bat of an eyelash, but it was Desade that had taught her to blend in and disappear like a chameleon. She was good at what she did, and he admired her for that. It was the fact that she was lying to him that made him want to drive his fist through a wall. "Trouble with the wifey?" Jadian turned his attention to the scantily clad woman stalking her way across the room, the short red dress looked more like a child's tank top than a woman's choice of apparel. She went by the name Katsidy, but those that know her well knew her as Kathryn Shaw. "Nothing I can't handle," he answered, as she approached him with a glass of red wine in her hand. The index finger of her other hand glided around the circumference of the rim, as her eyes locked on his. "Did she disappear again?" Kat bit lightly on her bottom lip before tipping the glass toward the midnight red shade of lipstick that glided smoothly on her lips a few moments before she stepped into the room. "Not in the mood, Kat," Jay said as he avoided making eye contact. "First time I've ever heard that from a man," she responded with a smidge of venom in her eyes. "Yeah, well, I'm not one of your marks." "Surely not one of mine." She didn't have to explain what she meant. "Like I said, I'm not in the mood." He tried to walk away, distance himself from a conversation they had a few weeks ago, but he knew she was not going to let this one go. "So you're just going to keep playing stupid?" "What am I supposed to tell her?" Jay turned to face her, his lips pressed together tightly as he sighs through his nostrils. "Not like I can just tell her that I want to know where she went because the guys I had following her said she disappeared." "The fact that she shook them, means she already knows, Jay." She folded her arms across her breasts and tilted her head a bit. "The only one looking like a fool here is you." "Well, she's not just going to tell me the truth anyway, so I'm going to keep trying to catch her in the act of whatever she's up to." "I guess," she threw her arms up in mock defeat, then walked over to where he was. "Do me a favor and stop using amateurs though, I know someone that may be just as good as Juli at blending in." He watched her slip two fingers between her cleavage, then withdraw a slip of paper and hand it to him. "Who's this?" "I don't think you'd recognize her if you saw her," she allowed that flirty grin to dance across her face. "But maybe you'll remember her name..." He looks down at the name written above the ten digit phone number – Adelina. "Wait, isn't this..." She was already gone. She couldn't remember which one of them had decided on meeting out in the open, but as she walked down the literature aisle of the store she couldn't help but wonder she wore a disguise at all. She noticed the blonde woman a few aisles down avoiding eye contact, almost as quick as the photo lab clerk did. She approached from her left, and Julissa couldn't help but smile when she saw the clerk shift in his seat and start to fidget with a few photo envelopes waiting to be picked up. "Were you followed?" Julissa kept thumbing through the paperback book in her hand, a novel about a Demon's memoirs by some woman named Tosca Lee. Seemed interesting enough, something she might actually buy before she leaves. "Of course," she replied without a glance behind her, she knew exactly who the woman was. "Jay's had Lane's men trailing me for the last few months, why would he stop now." "And he hasn't questioned you about your whereabouts, knowing that you've evaded them?" "Hasn't even brought it up," Julissa placed the book back on the shelf, then grabbed another. "He knows he shouldn't have anyone following me, and if he brings it up I'll just tell him that I'm still a little on edge and figured you had sent someone to hurt him through me so I ditched them." "And you think it would be that easy?" "He loves me, why would he doubt what I had to say," it was that comment that made her feel slightly ashamed for allowing him to lay his hands on her. Still, she loved him and felt that the phase would eventually fade out. It never occurred to her that most battered women felt that exact same way. "I don't know what is going through his head right now," the woman said as she moved alongside the Mexican Minx. Same could be said for the woman, who's red locks had been tucked perfectly inside the wig full of golden curls. "But I know I don't like where it is headed." "I think you've just been in this business too long and you're reading too much into it," Julissa replied as she set the James Patterson book back down on the shelf in front of the one just like it. "He's just doing this to get inside your head... He's harmless." "Maybe you missed the part where he attacked me and manipulated my daughter into agreeing to a match with him," Alexandra Pierce turned to face Julissa. "I would hardly consider that harmless, child." "I told you what happened with his mother," Julissa became defensive, as much of a jerk as he had been lately, she still cared deeply for him. "How would you feel if someone you had finally begun to put your trust in just abandoned you when you needed them the most?" Pierce looked away, and Julissa glanced after, suddenly afraid she'd missed someone. She missed the faraway look in Alex's eyes. "Ask Amy how she felt when she learned what I'd done." "Your relationship is what started all of this in the first place," Julissa scrunched up the bridge of her nose as she pursed her lips and smacked her tongue across the back of her teeth. "So excuse me if I don't rush over and let you cry on my shoulder." "Nor would I expect you to," Pierce replied, struggling to stem the flow of memories that begged to resurface. "I appreciate what you are doing here." The compliment was quiet, and they both knew they were just empty words. "It is comforting to know that I have at least one friend left. But let me assure you that you are the last person I'd break down and cry like a blithering idiot to." "Of course not." Juli's lips twisted in her smile. "We'd get fucking hammered." That drew a soft laugh from Pierce, though Minorez couldn't say if it were real or not. "What's done is done now, and we must press on. As much as I will always love her, it's best... best for her this way." Their path seemed haphazard, two women browsing paperbacks. Pierce lifted a copy of Twilight, thumbing idly through, her brows up and her cheeks flushed. Juli wasn't sure if she was supposed to like it or not, but turned to peer over the other woman's shoulder. Times like these, the layers the Spider was cocooned in were amazing; it was a skill Julissa had always wished to be able to do. "When did you become an expert on smart decisions?" Alex asked lightly, but the smile didn't reach her eyes. 'How did you manage to trip over your crutches – crutches you don't even need, mind – and end up with a hand print on your arm?" "I told you he was a bit rough the other night when helping me up," she thought it was anger that began to burn her cheeks, but it was embarrassment that had reared it's ugly head. "I should have watched where I was going." "I know you don't believe I'm ignorant," Alex began, replacing the book on the shelf before grabbing a magazine from the rack. "You would have to be a fool not to see it, and you are not that. Far be it from me to offer relationship advice, but I truly have no idea what you're still doing with him." "I'm more than capable of handling whatever it is you think is going on between me and Jay if I need to," She placed a hand on the Spider's shoulder. "But that isn't the case. When Lane got hurt, Jay became a kite blowing in the wind... unsure which direction it would take him." "As with everything, it is blowing him in my direction." Alexandra dropped the magazine back into the spinning circular rack. "I know you think you can control this, and I promised you the opportunity to try." She lifted her eyes, and even with the quiet blue contact lenses, there was simply no mistaking the threat of that gaze. "But I will not allow him to harm my daughter." "I'll talk with him," she replied, biting on her lower lip as she realized just how hard that would actually be. "He knows this is a bad idea, I know he does." "Perhaps you do not know him as well as you think." Pierce drew her iPhone from the purple handbag draped over her shoulder, pulling on a curling smile. She held up a finger. "See that you learn to." Alex stepped away to answer the nonexistent call, and though the words she said stung, Julissa was not going to let them deter her. She knew all she needed to know about Jadian Bridden, and that included why things had changed in their relationship. He was his father's son after all. Sandra Bridden had planned the evening out perfectly. She fed the kids early, had them showered and in bed by seven, and started making her husband's favorite dish soon after they were tucked in. She had made up her mind earlier that day that she wasn't going to let her marriage fall apart, and it had nothing to do with the fact that they had children together. Truth was, she loved him just as much now – if not more – than when they first started dating. A friend of hers had given her the number to a priest from the church she attended, he was someone the two of them could talk with in order to save their marriage. "Did I just walk into an episode of 'Leave it to Beaver' or something?" Jason Bridden asked as he stood with his left shoulder butted up against the door frame. "I was trying to surprise you," Sandra replied as she wiped her hands on the red and white checkered apron wrapped around her waist. "Oh, I'm surprised alright," he chuckled softly. "Cleanest I've seen the house in a while." She fought back the desire to snap at him, she had worked so hard to just let him ruin it now. "But I've got plans to grab a few beers with the guys." She felt a knot in her stomach. "But I –" "But you what?" There was a smirk on his face that his son would eventually inherit. "I went to a lot of trouble to do this for you," she replied, the urge to just break down and cry begging to be released. He walked over and grabbed a piece of chicken from the pan, it seemed as if all of this amused him. "Yeah, well, you'll have to excuse me if I'd rather grab a couple of beers with my friends. I'm sure you'll be willing to put out when I get back." "It's not about that and you know it," she swallowed the lump that now sat in her throat. "I wanted to talk to you about something." "How about we do this tomorrow?" "Look at us, Jason," she approached him, practically begging for him to take her serious for once. "What's happening to us... to our marriage?" "You tell me," he laughed before taking another bite from the chicken strip in his hand. He enjoyed this far too much. "I was talking to Bertha, and she thinks –" "You talked to Bertha about what?" he asked as he grabbed her by the arm and yanked her closer to him. "I told her we were having problems, and she said that maybe we should talk to someone about it." "Problems," he raised his voice slightly. "You know what my problem is, it's having a wife that thinks it is okay to open her mouth to her friends about shit that doesn't concern them." "We were just talking," she could not hold back the tears any longer. "Yeah, and now she's probably just talked to half the damn neighborhood about it, too." he tossed her against the counter, and she collapsed to the ground soon after. "Why can't you just keep your mouth shut, huh Sandy?" He was next to her in two strides, and it took less than a beat for his arm to swing and slap against her face. "No," she screamed, but he didn't hear it. He was already lifting his arm to take another swing. "Dad..." Jason Bridden turned slowly, his chest heaving with every breath. There stood his son, Jadian, with tears in his eyes. It was a moment that should have broken the man's spirit, but that had been broken long ago. "Fuck this," he seethed. "I don't need this shit right now." Jason stormed his way out of the house. He didn't see his son stand there in shock of what he saw, and he would never realize that he ended up blaming his mother for not having his father around.
