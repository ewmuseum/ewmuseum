Date: 01/02/08 Alone. So Alone... When you’re a kid, you never want anyone to know that it bothers you. When you’re a kid, you never want anyone to know that you cry yourself to sleep every night. When you’re a kid, you never want anyone to know that most of the time you can’t even fall asleep because of the beatings you’ve taken. When I was young, I never wanted anyone to know that the reason I wore hand-me-down clothes was because I didn’t have anyone who cared about me. When I was young, I never wanted anyone to know that the reason I wore sweatshirts and sweatpants in the middle of the summer was because I needed something to cover the bruises from all the beatings. When I was young, I cried in my room every night. When I was young... I was always alone... I open my eyes and I feel like I am trying to see through a kaleidoscope. My vision is blurry and even the dim light of this small space hurts my eyes. I try to wipe the blood and sweat away from my face, that’s when I feel the harsh touch of the handcuffs against my wrists. I shake my head, trying desperately to shake the cobwebs out of my head. I open my eyes again and try to find out where exactly I am. That’s when I start to feel the sick feeling in my stomach. My tongue starts to wander through my mouth. I can feel the inside of my mouth, and the results are quite disturbing. The inside part of my cheek is cut and bloodied, it actually feels quite a bit like raw hamburger meat. The blood from the inside of my mouth is starting to build up. I can feel it swishing around in my mouth. Now I don’t know about you, but I don’t really like using my own blood as Listerine. The blood is starting to build up in my mouth and I have no choice but to spit it out. The idea of swallowing mouthfuls of my own blood is even less appealing than the mouthwash thing. I spit the blood out, and I can only imagine the grotesque image of what my mouth must look like right now. Judging by the amount of blood on the ground, I can guess that my teeth must be completely crimson and my lips covered in dry blood. The feeling of nausea continues to wash over my body, the sick feeling in the pit of my stomach is beginning to cripple me. I try to lean forward as much as I can, the flex in my stomach makes the sickness subside, if only for a few seconds. My head feels like a ton of bricks, and I have a really hard time focusing on anything for a long period of time. I sit up, only for a moment, it only takes that long for the sick feeling in my gut to come rushing back. I lean my head forward once again, hoping desperately that the sickness will go away. No such luck this time. I keep my head leaned forward, but can’t manage to hold back the sickness. I taste the awful flavour of bile rushing into my throat. The sound of my heaving and vomiting echoes through the empty space that is my current prison. The stench picks at my nostrils and it makes the sick feeling even worse. The good news is that my sight is starting to return to normal. The bad news is that I’m still handcuffed to this fucking chair, and have no idea where the fuck I am. To add to the fact, I can’t really remember anything about how I got here, a sure fire bet that I’ve been drugged. Feels like sort of a weird thing for me to say. When you’re someone who most doctors and physicians would say suffers from a substance abuse problem, being drugged up isn’t something too out of the ordinary. But this time, it’s definitely not my kind of drug, and it definitely wasn’t my idea to take whatever the fuck I’m on. My vision is finally getting back to normal, which is good because I really need to figure out where the hell I am. I also need to figure out how to get the hell out of wherever I am. I look down at the chair that I am handcuffed to and I begin to wonder how I am going to get out. An even bigger problem is that I’m having an even harder time figuring out how I got in here. My head starts to feel light, I begin to feel faint. A feeling of numbness begins to rush over my entire body, it feels kind of like a wave washing over me. My head feels extremely light, I start to get the feeling of pins and needles all over my extremities. The darkness continues to come over me, getting worse and worse. My head feels like it’s filled with helium by now, and the black starts to set in. My mind begins to fade away. Before I can even try to think about how to stop it, my entire body goes limp. Unconsciousness sets in. I like to think that every single kid has hard times when they are younger. The truth of the matter is I’m pretty sure that most of them don’t. I think that most of them live happy lives, normal lives. Lives that are filled with smiles. Lives that are filled with memories... happy memories. I shouldn’t feel too bad. I have lots of memories, there are a lot of things that I’ll never forget, no matter how hard I try. Unfortunately those things that I will never forget, all of those memories, they are bad memories. They are awful, horrible, scary, terrible memories. If you were to run a slideshow of my childhood, I would bet that there are a good amount of people that would either throw up or quit watching it before it ended. Slide number one, childhood. Everything starts off normal, everyone smiling, everyone happy. I was an only child and I was showered with love and attention my entire childhood. You can see the smiling faces of my parents. They look so happy... so care free. Their only worry seems to be my happiness. My mother, her raven black hair shimmers in the sunshine. Her soft skin makes her face seem like she’s radiating. Her aura engulfs everything around her, lighting up the rest of the picture. My father’s big hands grip me and hold me steady on his shoulders. The smile on his face paints a picture of raw happiness. His skin is tough and weathered, he looks like a warrior who has seen many battles. In his arms there is nothing that can hurt me. Me. That’s the final piece of the picture. My smiling face beams with happiness, my smile; missing some teeth. My hair is messed up, strands hanging in front of my face. Together we are happy. Together. Cue slide number two, we’re in the backseat of a 1995 Ford Tempo. I’m in the backseat, tears flowing down my face. There are pieces of shattered glass scattered all over the backseat. The look on my face is that of sheer terror, it wouldn’t be hard for you to tell what’s going on in the front seat. My parents sit there, together in the front seat. Gone are the looks of happiness and joy, replaced by looks of terror and the contorted faces of pain. Their faces are pale and lifeless, their mouths hanging open and twisted. My father’s face is covered in blood and broken glass, the interior of the car is beginning to crumble in, crushing everything inside. My mother is an absolute horror story. Gone is the aura of happiness and beauty, now her face is a massacre. If you had to use one word to describe her it would probably be horrifying. The other car was out of control. The driver was drunk, he fell asleep at the wheel. The car hit us at over 100 kilometres an hour. The steel crumpled in. The glass shattered. The cries of fear and pain echoed out through the night. Now, take one final look at the picture, the image of my parents lying lifelessly in the front seat. The image of me crying in the back seat, the stare of my father’s lifeless eyes is something that I will never forget. Take one final look at me, crying in the backseat, all by myself. Alone. Cue up slide number three. You can see the image of me and my uncle posing together. On the surface you would think that we were happy. Me the grateful and thankful foster child. My uncle, the loving and caring foster parent, taking care of me in my time of need. If you only glanced at the image, that’s what you would see. But if you looked harder, what you would truly see is a deranged old man. A haggard, old, drunken, bastard of a man. A man that in order to feel like a true man had to beat the shit out of his eleven year old foster child. If you looked harder, you would see the monster for what he truly is. If you looked even harder, what you would see is a boy all alone. Alone. My head rolls around and I begin to feel my eyes open up. I can feel my body coming back to life, albeit an extremely painful life. I open my eyes trying if I can figure out where I am once again. My head still feels heavy and the drugs are still fucking my body up. I struggle to get my hands free, but I still can’t move. I sit here. Trying to get myself free. I sit here by myself, wondering if I’ll get out of here. But, right now I just sit here. Alone
