Part 5: And The Deep Blue Sea

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Various Then. Mid-February. The whole community--such as it was--buzzed with excitement. Have you heard the new reverend? He's wonderful, isn't he? Knows his Good Book, yes he does. It's a miracle, straight from heaven. It seemed that none of them could stop talking about the church's new reverend, Wyatt Connors. And that was all well and good...as long as none of them started talking to wrestling fans. As it turned out, there was an upside to this 'more or less cut off from civilizaiton' thing. Wyatt didn't have much to be thankful for these days, but he was very happy that none of these people had basic cable. Still, it wasn't all kittens and rainbows in the world of Wyatt Connors. He was still stuck on a farm in the middle of nowhere. The culmination of his career was still gone. He still was subject to the whim of some musclebound jackass who could leave him to twist in the wind at any moment. His life still depended on his ability to preach about a God that he didn't have much faith in, or use for. As he was about to learn, things could only get worse from here. Wyatt sat in the living room of the Jessups' home; the owners having long since gone to bed. He sat quietly at the dining room table, nursing a cup of coffee and trying to figure out just how the hell he was going to get out of this mess. Sure, he could just bolt out the door right now, never to be seen again. There were three problems iwth that plan, though. The first was that he still didn't know for sure where he was. Sure, he could find 'west,' but he had no idea how far he was from the nearest payphone. Besides, he only had about a hundred bucks on him...not enough to acquire transportation. The second was his missing case. He had no idea where it might be, and hadn't had much time to search. When the local yokels weren't talking his ear off about religion, Seth was keeping a close eye on him. He rarely had time to himself...and most of that was spent frantically cooking up another steaming pile of bullshit for the next sermon. If it came down to it, he could leave it behind...but then all the lies, the cons, the backstabbing and double-dealing...it would have all been for nothing. The third was Seth. Even now, Wyatt felt like the farmhand was watching...and laughing. That was the worst part...he'd been caught in more than one tight spot, but he'd always managed to get out of it. And now, this troglodyte was succeeding where others...where better had failed. 'Good evening, Reverend. I trust you're doing well?' Wyatt snapped his head around and saw Seth, the Jessups' farmhand. He sneered and asked, 'What do you want?' A smile played around Seth's lips. 'I've got a surprise for you.' 'You've decided that I've learned my lesson, and will now return my property and let me leave.' 'Heh. No. Nice try, though.' Seth pulled a sheet of paper out of his back pocket, and handed it to Wyatt. The smaller man unfolded it and looked at its contents. It was a flyer for a wrestling show, from a company calling itself FUSE. 'What the hell is this supposed to be?' he asked. Seth grinned widely. 'That's the next part of my plan.' # # # Last week. Elsewhere. He looked out over the city. The bright lights twinkled, like stars in the heavens. But he knew better. He knew that down there was hell. Down there was the Scarred Man. He wasn't down there anymore. He was free. But he was also far. There were many miles between him and the wizened angel he had seen in his visions. But he knew that he must get back to her, no matter the cost. She needed him...so did the rest of them, but her most of all. 'I must prepare myself for the journey,' he muttered. He looked down at the few meager items that had come into his possession. A hunk of bread, an old cup filled with water from a public fountain, and a handful of quarters. He felt pangs of guilt as he looked at them, since they were not technically his. He had not paid for the food and water, nor had he given anything of value in return for the money. In fact, what he had done to get them was commonly known as 'stealing.' But he needed these things. He had to get back. Dad would understand. He picked one of the quarters from the pile and held it up. The light shined on the coin, and he watched it reflect off the image of a scissortail flycatcher on the back. He could almost hear the bird's song...calling him home. Calling. Yes. Calling. Before the journey, he must prepare the way. He would tell the people of his Second Coming. And then, he could save them. # # # Then. 'You're going to make me wrestle again? Are you fucking kidding me? What's next, you're going to have me teach monkeys to juggle?' 'Shut up, Connors,' the farmhand retorted--sharply, but quietly. 'You obviously think this is some kind of joke. It's not. You are going back into wrestling, and you're going to do it for FUSE.' 'You suck at this. I've seen better revenge plots in Disney films.' 'There's more than a few people in that company that would love their hands around your neck, you know.' 'The same thing happens when I go to Wendy's. Big deal.' 'Including some old friends of yours...remember, from the NWC?' 'Really,' Wyatt responded flatly. The truth is, he hadn't looked very hard at the flyer...just saw the name of the company, and a few words about 'championship matches,' and drew his own conclusions from there. 'Of course. There were a handful of them who managed to revive their careers, after all.' Wyatt looked down again, and nodded. 'Yeah, I recognize a few of them. I'm surprised some of these people aren't dead.' 'Like BloodAngel?' 'And Pride. Wow...they gave Slick Willy a manager's license? Doesn't speak too well of this Rollins kid, if that's the best he could do. I've heard good things about Rhine; one of Foster's, if I remember.' 'Anyone else?' 'Hunter Sabuani? Holy shit. Last I heard, he was in jail.' Then, he gets down to the bottom of the page, where Smitty likes to put the blurbs about other things you can expect to see on the show. Specifically, he gets to the one that begins, 'Monet has been revealed as...' Connors' face went ashen, and his whole body tensed up. It's not sure how much farther he read, but a safe guess would be 'another two words.' 'No.' 'What do you mean, 'no?'' 'I mean fuck you a thousand times. I will not come within three states of that company. I would sooner be dead.' 'It may come to that.' 'You can stick your head in a bucket of shit if you think that--what?' Then came another sinister smile. Wyatt made a secret pledge to put some holes in it before he was done. 'Well, if you don't want to bring the good word of the Lord to FUSE...then I can just tell the locals the truth. The truth that you're a renowned con artist, here to give them false hopes based on their faith. They'll probably kill you, and bury you in the fields. The greatest mastermind of our times, murdered by rednecks...and nothing to mark the grave.' For some reason, three words from an earlier conversation come back to Connors' mind. 'If you survive.' They seemed ridiculous at the time. 'You wouldn't.' 'Yes, I would. So would any one of the thousands of people you've screwed over...but I'm the lucky winner, aren't I?' Seth sat down in a wooden chair and leaned back, looking as satisfied as a sailor leaving a whorehouse on a Sunday morning. Only now did Wyatt understand just what kind of trap the other man had put him in--faced with possible death, or a fate that was most likely worse. If he wasn't scared out of his mind, Wyatt Connors would have been impressed. # # # Now. There are certain dangers that come with a life in the service of God. One of the most sinister is the Afternoon Coffee. It starts just after the service, with several well-to-do (or, what passes for well-to-do around here) ladies would try to lure the unsuspecting man of the cloth to their homes, so that they could bore him to death with weak coffee, questionable desserts, and banal conversation. This week, that particular honor went to Alice McCoy, who lived just a few miles down the road from the Jessups. Her husband, Buford, was also there, along with Old Man Henderson...presumably to talk about corn prices. But really, they were there to chew the fat with Reverend Connors, just as much as the ladies were. Since he began preaching for the rundown old church, he had become something of a local celebrity. The size of the congregation had almost tripled in just under four months, as more people wanted to find out what this 'new reverend' was all about. This popularity also led to these wretched gatherings, where Wyatt would dunk stale cookies into weak coffee and answer their inane questions, all the while daydreaming about smothering them with pillows. But Mavis loved these gatherings, and he didn't have it in is heart to tell her, 'Die already.' Also, Seth had been Very Insistent that he should go to these things. So, here he was. Cyrus Henderson spoke slowly, with a voice even more creaky than his bones. 'Excellent service today, Reverend,' he said. Wyatt didn't really hear him, fixated as he was on the old man's false teeth. What the hell are those things made of? Moss? He merely nodded and smiled in response. 'I liked what you said about those movie people out in Hollywood the best. About how they're doing their best to turn us all into sinners.' Wyatt nodded, but now his mind wasn't quite as absent. He hadn't said a word about Hollywood today. Today was those pinko liberals. Hollywood was three weeks ago. So, either Henderson was trying to trap him in a lie, or he was starting to lose it upstairs. Either way, he'd have to be careful around the old man. He cast a sideways glance at the old man while he took a bite out of the cookie he'd been dipping into his coffee, in a feeble attempt to get flavor from either one of them. 'Are you married, Reverend?' Mrs. McCoy asked. Wyatt was so thrown by the question that he nearly choked on his soggy oatmeal cookie. He coughed for a few seconds, then Buford clapped him heavily on the back. 'Thank you. Well, Alice...the Lord's work generally keeps me too busy for that sort of thing. Perhaps someday...honestly, for now, it's enough to see other people experience the joy of holy matrimony.' Ladies and gentlemen, Wyatt Connors: master of 'laying it on thick.' 'That's so true. You know, Buford and I have been together for forty years.' 'That's wonderful,' Wyatt said, his voice dripping with smarm that nobody caught. 'You two look so...' ...alike. Is she your sister? '...happy together.' 'Oh, we are. Aren't we, Buford?' She looked over at her husband, who seemed to be nervously eyeing the door, as if he were getting ready to bolt at a moment's notice. She paid him no heed--after all, inattentiveness is the key to a lasting marriage, as we all know. 'It hasn't always been heaven on earth, I admit, but we do love each other very much, and as long as we hold on to our love and our faith, we'll be--' Thankfully, Alice was interrupted by a cacaphony of rapidly-ringing bells, coming from another room. 'Oh dear,' she fussed. 'I suppose I'd better get that.' It took a moment for Wyatt to realize what it was. A telephone. He hadn't heard one since the accident. It wasn't until just now that he realized how strange that was. Perfect. I can ask to borrow their phone. Place a call, and someone will be here to get me within the day. Nothing to hold me back except-- Except that he was still missing some important property. And that he'd ordered that his network of contacts be dismantled. Fuck. The others sat in relaxed silence, while Alice's voice drifted in from the other room. 'Hello?' 'Yes, I know her.' 'Oh, I don't have to! She's right here! Would you like to speak with her?' A few moments later, Alice McCoy peeked her head around the door. 'Mavis, honey? It's for you!' Mavis stood up from the table and walked toward the hallway, strangely nonchalant--as if she constantly receives calls in other people's houses. 'Does that happen a lot?' he asked Buford. 'Oh, once every couple of months or so. She and Hiram don't have their own phone--Hiram won't stand for one in the house. So some people call here, and then we take a message for her.' That knowledge didn't surprise Wyatt very much...he had recently been made aware of Hiram's aversion to 'gol-danged newfangled doohickeys.' 'My, my,' he responded. 'That's quite neighborly of you.' Buford shrugged, then turned back to his coffee. # # # Then. Wyatt's mind raced...but for the life of him, he couldn't find a way out of the situation that didn't involve stabbing his adversary with a pitchfork while he slept. There was nothing else for it, then. Wyatt steeled himself, calming his nerves. He no longer thought of this as a threat. This was business. 'All right,' he said. 'I'm in. With conditions.' 'Conditions?' the other man scoffed. 'You're not in a position to negotiate.' 'I don't care. If I'm going to be in the same state as...her...let alone the same building, I want to at least have a fighting chance.' The farmhand's mouth grew a tiny smirk. 'All right. What are your 'conditions?'' 'First: I'm going to need some supplies.' 'Like what?' 'I'll make a list. Most of it is wrestling gear...tights, boots, pads...mask.' Seth arched his eyebrow. 'A mask? What do you need a mask for?' 'So that I don't get strangled to death the moment I set foot in the building. I know that more than one of these people will be chomping at the bit for the chance to tear me apart. I would at least like to get my bearings before that happens.' 'And where will you get the money for all this?' 'Oh...I'm sure I'll think of something.' Seth pursed his lips as he tried to get some clue of what Connors was up to. All he got was a stare filled with ice. 'I don't like the mask, but the rest of it's fine. What else?' 'I assume you'll be tagging along on this little venture?' 'Right. Someone has to keep you from causing too much trouble.' Wyatt chuckled without humor and said, 'Well, there are going to be certain situations where you will have to defer to my judgment.' 'What kinds of situations are those?' 'I'll let you know.' 'Oh, no. I know you, Connors. You could get away with murder if I gave you a leash like that.' 'Listen. If I have to do this, then dammit, I'm going to do it properly. I'm going to need some leeway so that I can do the job.' 'All right. I'll tell you what...I'll give you a little bit of slack on your leash. Just remember that I can always step in if you go too far.' 'I can live with that. Do we have a deal?' Seth chuckled. 'No. I know better than to make deals with you. Let's just say we have an...understanding.' The two men stared at each other from across the table...Seth looking a great deal more pleased than Connors. Still, it was almost as if they were equals. Almost. # # # Now. She'd only been gone for about five or ten minutes...but when Mavis came back into the room, there were tears streaming down her face. Immediately, her friends rushed to her aid. Alice took Mavis' hand and led her to a chair, while Buford poured her a fresh cup of coffee. 'Oh, you dear thing, what's wrong?' Alice asked. 'Is it Hiram? Is he all right?' 'He's fine,' Mavis answered, although it seemed like she was doing it from another place entirely. 'Everything's wonderful...' Naturally, a response like that is only going to be met with confusion. 'Mavis, honey,' Alice asked, 'What in the Lord's name are you talking about? Who was that on the phone?' 'It was...it was him...' Mavis' eyes sparkled. There was no sorrow, in her voice, or in her tears. 'Who?' 'Our old reverend...he's coming back home...' It was like time had frozen. While Mavis continued to cry her tears of joy, her friends stared at her, too stunned to even move, This was good...because if they had turned around, they would have seen the look of terror on the face of Wyatt Connors.
