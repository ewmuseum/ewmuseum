Noise

17 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 17 September Make noise. Bellow at the top of your lungs, shout from a rooftop, scream from a mountain, and let the whole world hear you. That is what it takes, to reach the zenith. But I do not seek such accolade for the sake of prominence, no; I need to be at the top, for the sake of economics, for the sake of survival. Make noise... I’ll make some fucking noise. One of the great features of the Foggy Dew bar was the huge level of surplus storage space. A room lay vacant in the basement of the bar. Dualta knew this as he had worked there when he was younger, when his uncle owned it. However, the room had found itself a use. Dimly lit, due to no sunlight, musty, due to lack of ventilation, but big enough for its new primary purpose, and away from prying eyes. An old, dilapidated table sat centre stage. Six chairs surrounded it, in five of them sat eyes of intrigue, eyes of hope. These eyes focused intensely upon Dualta Sheridan, his eyes in turn where desperately trying to give signals of confidence and belief. They believe in me. They believe this can work. But what if it can’t? What will fate bestow upon us then? Concentrate Dualta, concentrate! Dualta stood beside a board, pinned up onto the walls. On it were plans, bank statements, drawings and most importantly a business plan. He spoke with as much buoyancy as he could muster. Everyone in this room needed to be behind him. "First things first though, we need to set up base in Manhattan, right in the middle. Fifth Avenue, Seventh or around Times Square, it needs to draw the crowds and it needs to be profitable. If we start big we end bigger." Round the table sat the people who could make it all happen, the people that could reignite the dwindled torch of the Sheridan Empire. Firstly there was Sean, Dualta cousin and his best friend, he was there because he would believe in Dualta no matter what, and he had a lot of intelligence and common sense. Then there was Sean’s father, Paudy. Paudy was first and foremost a businessman, and he understood how things work. Beside him sat another of Dualta’s uncles, Dermot. Dermot was the former owner of the very bar they sat underneath. He, along with Paudy were the only Senior members of the family who still had the fight left in them to rebuild what had been lost. They were willing to go to whatever extremes that were necessary, this was the opportunity they had been waiting on. Next was Ciara, Dualta’s older sister. The reason she was here was simple, she was an accountant, and she could sum up the financial sustainability of the venture Dualta put forth. She was not a practicing accountant as her business had mysteriously been ‘burned to the ground’ around the time of the Odessa Mafia takeover, currently she worked in a bar making most of her money of tips, saying it was a reality check for her would be an understatement. Lastly there was Frank Kelly. Frank had been a close friend of the Sheridan family for years as well as a business associate. He had a doctorate from Harvard business school and knew the feasibility of any property put in front of him, he was not far of genius. Dualta’s eyes were fixed on Frank; the room was silent besides the shuffling of documents as they were passed through Frank’s hands. He gazed at them through his thick, rounded glasses. "Well, all in all... I think its plausible," Dualta tried to hide his incredible surge of relief, "the best property in terms of location, space and price is that next to Flash Dancers south of Times Square, but of course you will have to get the money together, no bank in the city will give you a loan." "I’ll get the money. " Dualta didn’t know if he believed himself. The relief that shone through so evidently on Dualta’s face now passed through the rest of the room like an electric pulse through steel. Part one of my plot rendered, though any languid disregard for detail could subsequently end all budding hope. "I’ll get on the phone to a couple of contacts, see what we can do with this place, God Almighty will they be surprised to hear my voice." Paudy spoke as if his world had been turned around, as if everything was okay now, that scared Dualta. "We’ll need to keep this as low key as possible, if the Odessa’s find out we’re fucked, I’ll try to set up some form of security, keep us under the radar." Dualta had always known Dermot as a realist, but now even his optimism had ascended. Ciara and Frank began to trash out fiduciary details at the end of the table. Dualta’s eyes were fixed on his sister, she was probably the most beautiful girl in Queens, she worked hard and she made it to the top on her own. Now she lived a life of squalor all because of her surname, it wasn’t fair. She had done anything and everything for Dualta his whole life, it was payback time. Sean sat alone, not saying anything and not looking at anyone, emotionless. The past year had affected him more than most. Dualta wondered if he was taking it all in, trying to register the thought of returning to his old life, or if he just did not care anymore, if he had been devoid of all feeling, all passion, not capable of processing the prospect of hope. What depth of hell has his soul been cast through, for that is not the sight of a man of vitality, who would sooner go to war with the devil himself than have an ending of clemency and defeat. That is not my cousin. More talking was done as the finer details of the potential acquisition were discussed. A plan was in place that could be the launching pad for the return of the Sheridan’s atop the lavish heights of the New York underworld. It was where they belonged, and it was all they knew. Everyone packed up and slowly vacated the room, the mood had improved, even laughs were shared, they really believed. Ciara came over to Dualta. She was twenty-nine years old, six years Dualta’s senior. Her hair was long and black and her eyes were big and brown, she looked through them, at Dualta, waiting for assurances. "Do you think this will work?" Her voice was unsteady. "I know it will." He didn’t. "When are you leaving for Boston?" "Tomorrow morning." Conversation was straight to the point; they weren’t as playful as they used to be. "Get some sleep." She made her way towards the door. "On and Dualta..." She stopped and looked back at him, her eyes large and hopeful. "Good luck." She said this not as a message of encouragement, but as a plea, a plea to save her and her family from the paucity that had become their lives. Dualta could see it in her face, and it broke his heart. She left. He struggled to come to terms with it. She had looked after him her whole life, she was his big sister, when did he become the custodian of her fate? All his life he had been looked after by his father, his sister, his Uncles, even his cousin’s. He had it all and he didn’t have to lift a finger if he didn’t want to, because he knew that if he fell someone would be there to catch him. Not anymore, now they all relied on him, and the weight of that thought was too much for him to carry. She was my Guardian Angel, my Gabriel. Her hands healed my wounds and revitalized my mind, what do you do when the saviour needs to be saved? Only Dualta and Sean remained in the room. Dualta was unsure if Sean even knew where he was, his eyes had not moved since he arrived. "Get out of here man, get some sleep." It was all Dualta could think to say. "It’s not gonna work, is it?" Sean’s eyes still didn’t move, but they were the first words he had spoken all night. Dualta contemplated the comment, he had put on a front to everyone else, he had to, but he could not do that with Sean. "I don’t know Sean." Sean rose from his chair, his back hunched and his face pointed south. No more words were spoken as he left the room, quietly shutting the door behind him. A sigh was released from Dualta’s mouth as he raised his hands up to his face wiping off the sweat that had accumulated. He left the room, taking one last glance at the plan that held the fate of his and so many others’s lives before switching out the light. He did not leave the building however, for there was another room in the basement. Dualta entered. Over the past few days he had made this room into his gym, his home and his temple of reflection. In it were the standard workout apparel and weights. On the wall however is where the interest loomed. Nineteen pictures of nineteen people. Dualta had spent hours every day staring at these pictures because they were the final hurdle in his task. Adam Calloway, Andre Jensen, Austin King, Simon King, Josh, Elliot Rollins, Hiro Saotome, Paul Searle, Sean Sterling, Jimmy Bonafide, Hank Cobb, the Codemaster, Bryan Dawkins, Dusk, Enemigo I, Jason Natas, Christian Novak, Connor O’Reily and Union Jack. Dualta had nothing against these men, he did not care whether they came from PRIME or SCCW, he felt no loyalty to either. He just knew that he would rip through everyone of these individuals; he would kick, punch and bite anything that stood in his way, because he needed this, more than any of them. They were wrestlers but he was a fighter, he was born for this. In such a contest there is no time for suplex’s and submission, there is only time for strikes. Smack... one down, bam... another, until no more are left. He had went to Temptation last week, and he fought, and he won, and he went home. Whilst everyone else was involved in one of the biggest media stories in the country. He didn’t want to be involved, but now he had to. Publicity was not something which Dualta longed for, but whatever way he could make money was fair game. He stared coldly at the faces in front of him. You all believe in the agitation of media glorification. But you are false idols; you are all but mortals in a game for Gods. As you revel in the lights of stardom, giving the crowd what they want, I will sweep from under the radiance and tear down all of you. On Thursday I can’t be mortal, and I’m coming for you. You think you can stop me? You think you can win? But I put forth this question to you... you and whose army? Time to make some noise.
