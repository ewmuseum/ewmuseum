Part 1: Newark to Richmond

Date: Today Ding! The elevator door opened, and Deacon Dale stepped into the lobby of the Newark Airport Hilton. He knew he was running a little behind, even though he'd showered and dressed in a hurry. In fact, he'd almost left the room before he noticed his blue t-shirt was inside out. Bad enough that he was wearing yesterday's blue jeans, but he hadn't had the chance to wash anything since he'd arrived in Newark. Dale hoisted his black gym bag over his shoulder, then maneuvered his rolling suitcase toward the front desk, and looked at the clock above it. Two minutes after ten--a little late, but it couldn't be helped. That didn't matter to Trevor Ratigan, who stared at him with a reproachful glare and half a jelly doughnut stuffed into his mouth. 'Mff--' he began, before spitting the half-chewed pastry into his hand. 'You're late.' 'Had to do my stretches,' Deacon answered. 'If I don't, I can barely move. You know that.' 'I know 'yoga' spelled backwards is 'oh, gay,'' T.J. replied, and shoved the doughnut back into his mouth. He sat back in a chair, next to his own luggage. If Ratigan was worried about his own laundry, you couldn't tell--if his Dale Earnhardt Jr. shirt and Levi shorts were cleaner than anything in Deacon's pile of dirty clothes, it wouldn't be by much. 'No it--oh, fuck off.' Deacon rolled his eyes and turned toward the clerk. 'Checking out,' he said, and placed the keycard, along with his American Express, on the desk. The clerk, a young, bright-eyed brunette named Melanie, smiled at him. 'I hope you enjoyed your stay, sir,' she said, and processed his card. 'Yeah, it's a nice place,' he said. She gave no response; her friendly greeting was just part of the job, nothing more. That didn't keep Deacon from taking a look, of course, but only a quick one--by the time her eyes were back up, so were his. 'Sign right there, please,' she said, indicating a line on the bill. As he did so, Ratigan walked up beside him to see what was taking so long. He coughed loudly in an effort to draw attention to himself, but Deacon ignored it as he finished his signature and grabbed his card. 'Have a nice day, sir.' Melanie gave a warm smile, which Deacon returned. 'Jesus,' T.J. groused. 'You're looking at her like she was eight.' The smiles faded, giving way to looks of confusion (from Melanie) and anger (from Deacon.) 'He's a convicted pedophile,' Ratigan continued. 'He's in therapy for it, but we still have to tell people. Court orders and all.' 'I'm not--' Deacon protested, but Melanie had already taken a few steps back from the desk. By now, her confusion had turned completely to horror. Deacon growled and followed Trevor, who had already bolted for the door, giggling madly. As soon as Deacon was outside, he swung his gym bag at Trevor's head. Ratigan ducked the attack, but not the abuse that followed. 'You fucking shit! What did you say that for?' 'Relax, Deke. She wasn't going to fuck you anyway.' He was still grinning, and why not--he'd pulled a great prank and, thus far, had gotten away with it. He nimbly dodged another swing. 'When the taxi gets here, I'm slamming your head in the goddamn trunk,' Dale barked. 'Then we'll see how funny--' A third voice interrupted his tirade. 'As amusing as this is,' it--he--said, 'we do have a schedule to keep. Both men turned to face the speaker. They knew by voice that it was 'Wise Guy' Wyatt Connors...which is good, because they might not have known by the look. Wyatt Connors wore suits. He always wore suits. It was always the same suit, too--black jacket and trousers, gray shirt, red tie, black shoes. The only times he didn't was when he was wrestling or taking a shower (and they couldn't be sure about that last one.) So when they saw him in brown shoes, khaki pants, and a green polo shirt...well, the confusion was understandable. On anyone else, it would be business-casual. On Connors, it looked like what he'd wear to mow the lawn. 'Uh...' Dale stammered. When you worked for Wyatt Connors, you had to get used to surprises, but this caught him well off guard. 'What's going on? I thought you were supposed to meet us at the airport.' 'Change of plan,' Wyatt said. His mood was remarkably upbeat, considering how Sin On Spike V had gone for him. He'd lost a match against a long-time rival, and then saw his current primary target of scorn, Jared Sykes, win the High Rollers tournament and the SCCW Universal Title. One would think he'd be more upset...which only shows how much one knows about Wyatt Connors. 'I took the liberty of renting a car, which we will drive to Richmond. It's a six-hour trip, hardly worth the trouble of flying.' 'Shotgun!' Trevor shouted, and ran toward the car. He did not know which car he was supposed to be running to, but that did not slow him down. Deacon shuffled his feet a little. He had, on rare occasion, questioned Wyatt's plans in the past. It never went over well. However, the flaw with this one was so obvious, he felt the need to point it out. 'Umm...sir?' 'Yes, Deacon?' Wyatt replied. 'You want to take a six-hour trip,' Deacon said, trying to break up his main point into smaller bits, like a parent would do with a small child's steak. It wasn't so that Wyatt might understand--no doubt the Scorpion knew perfectly well what he was doing--but if Deacon made it simple enough, then maybe Wyatt could explain it to him. 'That's correct.' 'In a car,' he continued. 'Yes.' 'With him.' Dale pointed in T.J.'s general direction. 'I understand your trepidation,' Wyatt answered with a smile. 'But I have my reasons.' Deacon tried to think of a reason why one would want to spend any amount of time, let alone six hours, in a car with T.J. Ratigan. He came up blank. Connors sensed his minion's bafflement, but offered no explanation. That, at least, was familiar ground--Wyatt Connors explained things about as often as he wore things other than suits. 'Anyway, it'll be good to get out on the road. See the country, and all that.' Deacon nodded, but still had his concerns. 'Might not be a good idea, sir.' 'Why not?' 'Well...there's people out there, in the country. Some of them may know you.' Wyatt smirked. 'Something we'll have to deal with when the time comes. Just like Trevor--after all, we can't leave him behind.' 'You sure?' Deacon asked. He was joking, of course. Maybe. Connors chuckled, then turned back over his shoulder. 'It's over here, Trevor. The one I'm leaning against.' Ratigan darted back through the parking lot, until he reached the car Wyatt had identified: a dark green 2002 Chevy Malibu. 'Nice wheels,' he sniped. 'Borrow 'em from your grandma?' 'I chose it for its practicality, not its style,' Connors answered as he opened the trunk. 'That's obvious.' Ratigan dumped his bags in the trunk, leaving Deacon to shift them around so that he could find space for his own. After Dale had finished organizing the luggage, he came back around to the driver's side. He gave another look of concern to his boss. 'Chin up, Deacon,' Connors said. 'Can't be as bad as all that.' # # # 'Maybe this wasn't such a great idea after all,' Connors muttered--not that anyone, even himself, could hear it. By the time they reached Camden, New Jersey, Wyatt couldn't decide whether to push T.J. out of the car, or just swerve into oncoming traffic and end everyone's misery. It wasn't the smell, although that was bad enough. T.J. Ratigan earned every inch of his nickname, 'Rotten,' partly for his attitude but mostly for his hygiene. Today, his own personal odors mingled with those from the state of New Jersey, and almost canceled each other out. Almost. The real problem was that, since Wyatt had his hands full with driving, this allowed Trevor unguarded access to the radio. This resulted in a steady stream of either southern rock or hair metal, turned up far too loud, and accompanied by the song stylings of Ratigan himself. An all-too-familiar guitar riff played, and Ratigan pumped his fist in excitement as 'That Smell' played for the fourth time in the last hour. (There is a little-known corollary to the Caine-Hackman Theory which states: at any time, you can find a station playing either Lynyrd Skynyrd or Motley Crue.) 'Whiskey bottles and brand new cars!' Trevor sang, causing another wince from the other people in the car. At least he was getting the words right this time, but that was small comfort next to the fact that he couldn't carry a tune with a forklift. Connors sighed, resigning himself to the noise pollution. His alternative wasn't feasible--the traffic on the interstate had slowed to a crawl, so pushing T.J. out now would cause minimal damage. The northbound lanes were just as bad. At this speed, the most serious thing a collision would lead to was an argument. The solo kicked in on the song, prompting Ratigan to abandon his tone-deaf yowling in exchange for air guitar. His frantic gestures and wild-head thrashing jostled Connors on several occasions. Wyatt leaned back and addressed Deacon in the back seat. 'Would you kill him, please? I'll give you a quarter.' Deacon snorted. 'I'll give you change.' That, at least, drew a chuckle from the Wise Guy. All right, so maybe he hadn't picked the best travel companions. It wasn't like he had others to choose from...and although flying would have been faster, more convenient, and far less annoying...he had his reasons. The first reason, although maybe not the primary one, was that he had a lot of business to discuss. Doing so was much easier with a captive audience. # # # Deacon's turn at the wheel came just after they passed Wilmington, Delaware. Wisely (heh,) Wyatt decided to turn the radio off. After an hour or so of sweet, blessed silence, Connors brought up the affairs of the day. 'Wanted to congratulate you two on a job well done in Newark,' he said. This brought out quizzical expressions from his charges. Trevor broached the subject first. 'But...didn't you kind of get your ass kicked? Like, first round and out? Didn't think you'd be real happy about that.' 'I might be disappointed, if becoming champion again had ever been my intention. Naturally, I would have preferred to defeat Ms. Pierce, but that I did not is immaterial. The two of you held your own against her for some time, which is nothing to scoff at. In the end, she had to scheme to win--an exemplary job of scheming, credit where it's due--but not before we did enough damage to keep her from reaching the final. So I'd say you did your jobs rather well. As for the rest of the night...well, I got everything I wanted.' A sly grin spread across his face. Deacon and Trevor shared a glance through the rear view mirror. Working with Wyatt Connors left them feeling confused most of the time. As mentioned earlier, he rarely explained anything..but somehow, when he did spell things out, they often made less sense than before. Taking their puzzlement as acceptance, the Wise Guy continued. 'New business. I know you boys have been wanting to see more in-ring action, so I've signed you to a match against the Heirs of Wrestling at the next show. Sadly, it is a non-title match, but a victory against the new SIN champions will go a long way towards establishing the Conspiracy within SCCW.' The pep talk did not bring visible excitement to his lackeys, which did not surprise Wyatt. Still, he expected better than a nervous cough from the back seat. 'Something you wanted to say, Trevor?' he asked. 'Yeah...' The Bad Apple paused awkwardly, as he was about to venture into another unpleasant territory: making suggestions. 'Me and Deke was talking, and we kinda thought maybe we could...y'know, get a new name for the team.' Wyatt arched an eyebrow. 'Really.' 'Well, that's what we were talkin' about.' Ratigan felt his mouth go dry--he didn't like making the pitch, but he knew Dale would never have the guts to do it. 'I mean, the Conspiracy...that was all three of us, right? And I figure you want us to do the tag stuff so that you can do your own thing. That's cool and all, but if it's just us, then we should have a name that's more...just us, y'know?' 'I see. Did you brainstorm any possible replacements, perchance?' Wyatt's voice carried a tone that was half patronizing, half annoyed--like he was proud of you for coming up with a thought of your own, but disappointed that it was so stupid. All that, of course, went right over Trevor's head. 'Got a couple. How about 'Sex-Nuts and Retard-Strong?'' 'It's descriptive, I'll give you that.' The Rainmaker seemed mildly amused, which probably meant he was dying to let out a healthy gut-laugh. 'Which one of you is 'Retard-Strong?'' Ratigan smiled like an idiot. 'Ain't it obvious? I'd be 'Sex-Nuts,' so that only leaves Deke.' 'Yeah,' Dale quipped back--opening his mouth for the first time in half an hour. 'If it was just 'Retard,' they'd think the team was two of him.' T.J. gave Deacon a quick smack on the back of the head, and then leaned back. Deacon would have to try hard if he wanted to reciprocate, and he was driving. Once he was safe, Trevor went back into his pitch. 'All right, you don't like that one. How about 'Ten Inches of Dynamite?'' 'Is that supposed to mean what I think it does?' Connors' answer came with a small amount of shock. He'd expected it to be much more vulgar. Ratigan shrugged. 'Probably.' 'You want to name your team after your penises,' Wyatt said, shaking his head in disbelief (although, not that much disbelief. This was Trevor, after all.) 'One question--and I only ask this out of morbid curiosity--is that supposed to be an average?' 'Total. Eight of mine, two of his.' T.J. grinned again, as Deacon whipped his head around and gave him a sharp look. 'Bullshit,' Deacon snarled. 'How do you know? You been checking me out in the shower? Homo.' Ratigan let out a smile that bared his yellowing teeth, proud as he was for achieving the pinnacle of wit--for him, anyway. Deacon retorted by giving a quick yet forceful tap on the brake, causing Trevor to knock his head against the seat in front of him. 'You dick!' Trevor shouted as he sat back, rubbing his forehead. If Wyatt Connors had any less self-control, he probably would have been beating his head against the dashboard and laughing. Instead, he tried to get the conversation, such as it was, back on track. 'The idea has merit, I must admit, but it calls for something a bit less...vulgar. More recognizable, too...after all, the Conspiracy is a name that carries some familiarity. Perhaps referencing something in popular culture, a television show, a song, a movie...' 'Y'mean like Inglourious Basterds?' Connors smiled a bit more. 'Something like that, but maybe not that one specifically. How about...the Dirty Rotten Scoundrels?' Ratigan's face was a mask of incomprehension. 'Maybe Dale knows that one. He's old.' 'That was from the late 80's, you turd,' Deacon snapped. The rebuke slid off of T.J. like he was coated in Teflon. 'That's pretty good, though. That could work. I mean, I'm already Rotten. We just have to make Deke 'Dirty.'' 'How so?' Connors' grin almost cut his face in half, which should have been a warning. If it was, Trevor ignored it as he did most warnings. 'I dunno. Maybe have him cuss more. Quit shaving for a couple days before every show. Maybe paint up his tights a little, so it looks like there's dirt on 'em.' Ratigan had wandered blindly into the trap, and all that remained was for Connors to spring it. 'Inspired suggestions, Trevor. Why, you make it sound almost like...a gimmick.' 'I dunno about--oh, you fucker.' Realization dawned on Ratigan, just as he spied Deacon's white-knuckle grip on the steering wheel. Everyone in the car knew that if Deacon Dale let go of the wheel at all, it would only be to wrap his hands around somebody's neck. If either of them had mentioned the bunny suit, there would already be blood. Wyatt's amused grin belied his calming tones, but he knew it was time to defuse the situation. 'I think, perhaps, that you should stay with the current team name for a while. At least until we've had some time to think of reasonable alternatives. Don't you agree, gentlemen?' Deacon's snarl could have meant anything, but his grip relaxed, and he wasn't strangling anyone. Both good signs. Trevor nodded emphatically, but said nothing...thereby showing that he could, on occasion, learn. They sat in uncomfortable silence for a few minutes, waiting for Deacon's anger to reach acceptable levels. Deacon was the one who broke it. 'Baltimore's comin' up,' he said tersely. Wyatt nodded matter-of-factly. 'We'll get around the city, and then stop for lunch, I think. In the meantime, we have a few more things to go over. Specifically, what to do about Jared Sykes...' For the rest of the conversation, Wyatt's outward demeanor was serious...but inside, he was grinning like a jack-o'-lantern. He'd forgotten the sheer joy of pushing people's buttons just for the hell of it. It was another one of life's simple pleasures with which he wanted to reacquaint himself. That was the second reason--in no particular order, of course--why he'd been insistent on the new travel arrangements. He'd been outside of the world for too long. So much of the last few years had been spent in hiding; skulking like a tiny scavenger who needed to eat, but was afraid of drawing attention from other predators. Because of this, Wyatt felt he had lost his touch in certain matters. That was going to change, though. Like so many other things, that would change...and not for the better. # # # As previously mentioned, Wyatt Connors had his reasons for organizing the Conspiracy's life on the road. He'd willingly divulge the first reason, if anyone asked. The second...he might admit it, given enough time and the right circumstances. There was a third reason, though, one that he'd never admit to anyone. Rest assured that if you knew what it was, you'd never believe it. Stranger still is the fourth reason, which Connors won't even admit to himself.
