Date: 6/10/08 As if you couldn't see it coming from a mile away. You can call it a fall from grace. You can call it Newton's law of physics. "A physical body will remain at rest, or continue to move at a constant velocity, unless an outside net force acts upon it." Or, if that weren't apt enough, we could go with the second. " Rate of change of momentum is proportional to the resultant force producing it and takes place in the direction of that force." Kind of like hitting a brick wall, one would suppose. What you should call it, though, is a fucking tragedy. The signs were there. Even though Elliott hadn't seen the move that would eventually put him on the shelf for nearly two months, there were hints. Cosmic forces were in affect, and if he had just noticed the hair standing at attention on the back of his neck, or the... fuck, just that feeling. It's as indescribable as deja vu or vertigo, but it's something everyone is familiar with. That... Fuck. Double fuck. He should have seen it coming. It's not like it was some centripetal force, tearing him apart from the inside. It was an external force; a living, breathing, shitting prick by the name of Wyatt Connors who did what countless others didn't have the skills or the balls to do. Elliott knew that the move would hurt even before he landed; little did he realize just how much it would hurt. His neck and spine compressed. His brain rattled against his skull. .oh god. The first thing he did when he woke up was vomit. They released him from the hospital after a day of neurological and orthopedic tests. The results were fortunate; merely a mild concussion and a sprained neck, but the doctors told him that to return to a profession as dangerous as his would take up to three months. Elliott had warned them to do everything in their power to allow his return to take place quicker than that, but the know-it-all doctor told him that a rushed return may incite further injury. So, at the behest of a manager taking every precaution with his most precious commodity, Elliott took time off. Too much time off. The first few nights were the worst. It was recommended that he didn't sleep alone; that part he didn't mind too much. What did bother him was the nausea. And the dizziness. And the neck spasms. As he vomited the rest of the days dinner in to a wastebasket close to his bed, he hoped and prayed that it would be over soon. He hadn't been able to keep down a meal in almost a week, and sleeping at night was next to impossible, even though bed rest was highly recommended. He couldn't drive, he could hardly walk, and bathing himself was a life-or-death situation. "Ugh..." he grumbled, rolling back to a place in bed that didn't irritate his back. As long as he kept it relatively straight, he could get through the night with the help of a fistful of painkillers. "You okay?" a voice whispered to him in the darkness. It was obviously feminine, but had a rasp that was the result of sleeping with her mouth wide open. He didn't respond; instead, he stared at the ceiling, dim moonlight casting eerie shadows across it. His head was swimming. The ceiling moved in waves, like ripples in a pond. Soon, he heard the labored breaths of Hannah Rose next to him, short, concise, and to the point. After so many days, the room began to feel like a prison, suffocating him. At this moment, he begged for death to come quick and painless. .beep. The messages hadn't been checked for days. Hannah was now out of town, unfortunately drawn away by some sort of art gallery showing of her work on the opposite side of the country. Elliott was left on his own, as his manager was... off doing managerial tasks, he could only assume. He hadn't left his bed the entire day, outside of using the bathroom, or trying to force down whatever substance his stomach deemed acceptable. At around three in the afternoon, he decided enough was enough. He hit the button on the answering machine, then shuffled his way towards the couch. He sat down, gingerly, with a sigh, and the couch sighed as well under his weight. The first three messages were from McDormant; something about a sermon and an arrogant prick, maybe the title of a novel he should read, it was really hard to understand him when he got excited. Regardless, it was the fourth message was the one that caught his attention. "Hello, Elliott.." The voice started off quiet, reserved. It almost took him awhile to recognize it, but when he did it was a voice he was far too familiar with. "I'm calling because we haven't spoken in awhile." It was the voice of Doctor Thapes. His mind immediately rushed back to their sessions a few months back, and how those months felt like years ago. He didn't remember specifics of them; faint glimpses here and there, maybe, but nothing specific came to mind as far as what had happened inside of her office during the long, numerous hours he had spent there. He remembered the color of the carpet, and how the couch creaked and groaned under his weight, but nothing more tangible than that. "I want to talk to you about a case I'm working on right now." It wasn't the sessions that he remembered. Instead, it was merely the message that he now carried with him. "I would like you to come back to the clinic for a little while. I know your last session didn't go very well, and you don't have an overall fondness for the work that you did here, but the girl that I am now working with... well, I don't really know." Her voice trailed off. He clung to every word. "She reminds me of you in a way that I can't explain. You just have to meet her. I think that if you came down here, just for a little while, and spoke to her, it would be of great benefit for her, and possibly yourself as well. I know you're busy with your wrestling nonsense --" That's what she called it. Nonsense. "--but I would greatly appreciate it if you could spare a few hours of your time to help me with this one." There was another pause on the line. "Please. Call me soon." And with that, the phone went dead. No more information other than the few words she left on his answering machine. Leave them wanting more, as the adage goes, but Elliott had absolutely no desire to know any more. Working there had been excruciating, and left him feeling that there was nothing he could do to be of any benefit to these people. Could it? .reunited. There's no place like home. There's no place like home. There's no place like home. And to Elliott Rollins and William McDormant, this small, dirty, hot diner was home. The aroma, the atmosphere, all of it lends itself well to the idea that Pittsburgh truly is blue collar at its finest. The two men sitting in the booth are by far the most attractive people in the room, but only one of them is thinking about fact that at the moment. The other is stirring his coffee mindlessly, his cigarette burning, the smoke billowing upwards. "I can't believe I spent six days following that fuck around," McDormant spits vehemently, chewing at the end of a cold French fry as he speaks. "You would not believe some of the shit that was spewing out of his mouth. Just gobs and gobs of horse shit dribbling from that fat face of his, clinging to his chin... it was fucking disgusting, really." McDormant looks around, realizing his surroundings fit that adjective as well. "All I'm saying," speaking to nobody in particular, "is that the next time I see that guy, I'm going to punch him in the dick. Right in the dick. Cock uppercut." McDormant's trailing eyes finally meet Elliott, who is still distracted by his coffee and cigarettes. "You are one mopey motherfucker, you know that?" McDormant rhetorically asks. Elliott's eyes drift upward with no particular interest, and then back down to his coffee. "This is the first time I've been out of the house in ten days," Elliott replies coolly. "So?" McDormant replies. "You should be stoked, not mentally drowning yourself in your cup of java. I guess that makes sense." His eyes drift to the ceiling, then snap back down again. "You realize what you have coming up for you, right? You have the chance of a lifetime this week. You get to make Wyatt Connors look like a putz, and you get a shot at All In. You should be effing ecstatic." If McDormant knew about the phone call Elliott had received earlier that week, he may be a little less abrasive. "I am. I'm totally stoked, dude. I'm just..." Elliott pauses for a second. "To be honest, I'm just sick of talking about work. I'm sick of thinking about it all the time. I can't sleep at night because I can't stop, and this sport is the only thing I have to think about. All the fucking time." "So?" McDormant scoffs. "You're a professional wrestler, what else do you need?" "Something," Elliott sighs. "This sport doesn't define who we are." McDormant smirks. "Speak for yourself. You're talking to a guy who has orgiastic fantasies involving often-times sexual misdeeds with golden belts. Like, sick shit." His eyes drift off again, likely recalling those long nights alone, surrounded by his replica world titles. "Accept your role, and you'll sleep better at night. God knows I do." And he does sleep very well. Elliott just bites his lower lip. He still hasn't looked up from the table. "Maybe I just don't have it in me anymore. I thought this was what I wanted, but maybe, I don't know... fuck, maybe there's a reason I was never successful. Maybe I don't deserve it." He pauses again. "Maybe I don't want it." McDormant's wandering eyes are now solely focused on the man across from him. His eyebrows are furrowed, and his ears are trying to recover from the words he just heard. "Please tell me you're joking." Elliott doesn't tell him anything. McDormant slaps the table, hard, snapping Elliott to attention, as well as the other patrons in the dim diner. "Listen to me. You've worked so Goddamn hard for this. I've seen it, and trust me, I don't pay compliments very often, but I've been impressed. Everyone has. You've done everything right up to this point, worked so hard, and you're not even sure if it's what you want?" Elliott still doesn't respond. "I swear to God, sometimes I feel like I'm dealing with a fucking child. And to be honest, and I'm going to be very frank here, I don't give a shit what you want. I could give a shit less if you wanted to run off and join the Peace Corps. You're here, and you're on the verge of becoming the biggest star in this industry. If you fuck that up now, I will personally – look at me – I will personally make sure that you walk out of Temptation a fucking eunuch, you understand?" Elliott doesn't respond, but he understands. "This is about more than just you," McDormant continues. "I've put my entire career on the line, supported some fucking nobody and helped bring him through the ranks, it wasn't all me, sure, but I did my fucking part, and if you give it up now, it's like personally spitting in my face. The last guy that spit in my face got put in the hospital, so I'd advise you, unless you feel like making a return visit, you get whatever questions you have answered, and quick." Elliott looks up. He simply nods. .snap. He felt his neck crack. It wasn't particularly pleasant, but adrenaline is a funny thing. With adrenaline, you could pretty much break your spine in six places, but as long as there was enough of it going through your body, you could tolerate yoga. Here he was, the proud owner of a compressed vertebra, minor concussion, and a severe neck sprain, and somehow, despite it all, he managed to get up. He managed to take the maneuver in stride, and even though he knew he was hurt, he found a way to win. Through all of the trials, he succeeded. Being a wrestler may not define who he is, but who he is defines him as a wrestler. And all be damned if they didn't believe that Elliott Rollins is a fighter.
