Chapter 3: Rumors, Bargains, and Lies

15 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12.15.2007 3.1 - I Will Make You Pay Like with any sporting or entertainment venture, the wrestling superstars of FUSE often received both email and actual mail correspondence from the fans whose money Smitty T. Duluth used to pay their salaries. Many of these letters were well thought-out; say what you might about the Internet Wrestling Community, there was a large contingent of people who watched FUSE programming with an eye of supposed expertise. Others were... not as well formulated. Smitty's marketing department had a file for marriage proposals they had received for Kansas-Marie Pride and Amy Campbell that filled two entire filing cabinets. The common wrestling fan was never known for their intelligence, however, so some of the letters were a little out there. Clinton Sage and Lane Stevens had routinely received threats of bodily harm when the federation passed through the Deep South, but they were considered more of the 'drunken ramblings' sort than the 'get the police on the phone' sort. So when the first threat to Duncan Drake came in, the people Smitty hired to read the mail just sort of shrugged their shoulders, chalked it up to the young kid starting to do things right, and filed it away. In the coming months, a lot of questions will be asked of a lot of people. One of these questions will be, 'Could any of this have been stopped?' The answer to that is, 'Yes.' For starters, they should probably have actually read the letter. Dearest Duncan Drake: For what you have done, I will make you pay. For all those you have hurt, I will make you pay. For all the people you have thrown away, I will make you pay. For the lies you have told, I will make you pay. You will not see it coming, but I will make you pay. You will not expect it is coming, but I will make you pay. I will make you pay for the pain you have caused my family. I will make you pay for the tears my mother has cried. I will make you pay for the way my father's smile never quite reaches his eyes anymore. I will make you pay for their suffering. But, most of all, I will make you pay for Kelly's. I will make you pay soon, Mr. Duncan Drake. XOXO The letter sat unread in the Death Threats and Various Threats of Bodily Harm file for well over a month. * * * * * 3.2 - Climb The Ladder The motel room was dark (because it's rude to have your lights on at 2:30 in the morning), but 'the darkness' is no fun to describe. The room got that way because of thick, heavy purple and blue curtains, drawn across its only window to blot out the starlight and the streetlight. Only the barest crack of fluorescence crept in across green carpeting, barely reaching yellowed wallpaper. Twin brass lamps, one bolted to the wall over each nightstand, sat untouched. The only other light in the tiny roadside inn came from a small orange bulb plugged, bare, into a socket beneath a vanity mirror in the bathroom. As with most of these rooms, the bed was the centerpiece in this one (because most of them are used only for the express purpose of sleep). It was a well-worn queen-sized beast whose scattered sheets bespoke the only activity that had taken place herein in many, many days. Atop this morass of twisted, tangled, and sweaty cloth lied two twisted, tangled, and very sweaty bodies. These two could not see each other in the shadowy room, but when you lie that close to someone else, sight is not necessary. 'Johan?' a woman asked, her voice soft, the words mumbled against flesh. 'What does your tattoo mean?' A man groaned quietly, a large, chiseled silhouette arched away from the bed. 'It's kanji. Ahhh. It's like... like a ladder. 'Intellect leads to strength; strength leads to victory.' It's all about strengthening the mind to strengthen the body, and, through both, strengthen the spirit.' 'So it's entirely unlike the philosophy of, like, ninety percent of men in the world.' The woman's laugh is a warm 'nng' sound at the back of her throat, her lips against the ear of the tattooed man, Johan. His return chuckle was raspy and labored. 'Y-yeah. You could say that.' There was the sound of lips brushing bare skin, barely audible over the hum of the air conditioner. 'Well, I must say. Your philosophy? Has served this body very... very well.' The soft scrape of lips was quickly replaced by the hard, rat-a-tat-tat clicking of tightening handcuffs. Johan grunted, then there was a clatter of chain on headboard. 'Hey!' he objected. 'These are kinda tight, babe.' The woman ignored him completely, not bothering to dress as she crept across the motel room. Her silhouette slid free of the snarl of bedsheets, padding across the carpet to where a pair of men's slacks hung, discarded, over the back of a chair. 'But your mind? Maybe it hasn't been served as well,' she said, her voice steeped in scorn. 'I think you're supposed to start with the intellect part of the ladder and work your way down the ladder. Not the other way around.' The woman retrieved a sleek black smart-phone from the left pocket of the slacks. Without turning on the light, she pulled the stylus free, sliding the point through the phone list. She settled quickly on a number. The phone rang only once before it was answered; in the quiet room, the speaker's bright voice is almost audible. The woman's groan definitely was. 'You know who this is,' the woman said, her voice losing warmth with every word. 'I have your man in a very precarious position for him, and, while I'm sure you don't give a fuck about what happens to him, I'm pretty positive he does. We need to talk. You have... say, an hour?' She hung up, spinning on one heel to face the Tattooed Man. Dark hair, as dark as midnight, fluttered out behind her. 'You've got an hour,' MacKenzie Malone said, surveying the scene. 'I hope your people check their messages as much as I do.' * * * * * 3.3 - Dinner with Maxwell Emerson Drake II It was said by many reporters that Duncan Drake never seemed to get nervous. That he was always a cool, calm customer, ready to take whatever the world dishes out and come back with shit-eating grin. Usually, they were right. However, when it came to this house, built on stilts and overlooking the Pacific Ocean in beautiful Malibu, California, even the Hollywood Hellion had to take a breath to gather strength. Truthfully, to call it a 'house' was not doing it justice. In a city full of multimillion-dollar, palatial mansions, the home of Duncan Drake's father, the so-called 'Old Dragon', stood out in terms of sheer ostentation, from its imported wood finish to the marble pillars that held the building up in the face of California's notorious mudslides. The house itself rose in the center of a one hundred acre estate, tended by a staff of two-dozen gardeners. Inside, the three of them sat around an antique, 17th century Louis XIV table that Maxwell Drake only had 'special occasion' dinners at. They ate pheasant and drank wine that had aged longer than MacKenzie Malone had lived. They traded light, meaningless, bullshit small talk, because that's what one does with one's parents. But, honestly, Maxwell Drake had not invited his son and his son's girlfriend to his home for pheasant and wine and small talk. 'So, son,' Maxwell said, wiping at the corners of his salt-and-pepper goatee with a deep red napkin. 'How is that professional... fighting thing going for you?' Those who had watched the younger Drake on television to this point had likely made some rather harsh judgments about the man's character. Things like, 'Man, that guy sure is a fucker' and 'Someone should punch him pretty hard -- in the face'. As with many young men, however, Duncan was a little more subdued when he was around his father. 'Eh, it's going okay,' he said. Maxwell was amused. 'Just okay?' 'It's... I mean -- it's a lot harder than I thought it would be. I got the first guy, right? And that seemed easy. But then there was this battle royale thing and --' Drake blew out a sigh. 'You didn't win, I take it.' Maxwell's words were cool, his gaze level. Though he and his son both had brown eyes, no one would ever accuse the elder Drake's gaze of dancing with the kind of warm humor Duncan's often did. His were cool, frozen, like a log after the fire went out. 'Uh, not really. I, uh... finished second. Got beat by this other guy, who, like, is a better wrestler right now.' 'I see.' The two words were cooler by degrees, and Duncan reacted as if he'd been slapped. 'Are you still attending your training classes with Mr. Franklin?' 'Yeah.' Duncan's reply was met with his father's painfully empty stare. It was a stare he could not meet. 'Most of them.' MacKenzie Malone could not stay silent any longer. She slid a hand onto Duncan's knee and turned her face to his father. 'He's doing quite well for himself, Mr. Drake. Duncan's entered into a tournament that's got some of the younger talent in the federation and we're both pretty excited about --' 'That's all well and good, Miss Malone, but I have told my son on many occasions exactly what I think of his current choice of vocations.' 'Dad, I --' Duncan tried to interject, but the elder Drake was not finished. 'You work for an organization that prides itself on having a man who earned his fortune in the adult film industry. You've chosen a vocation where men will walk to the ring dressed as clowns, where grown men tattoo their names on the thighs of their foes' girlfriends on live television because they believe 'that will show them'. This is not the business for a Drake to find his way in the world.' 'But it... it's what I gotta do,' Duncan said. 'It's like... yeah, I could go into your business, but... you and I both know I'm shit with numbers, and I'd only ever be 'Maxwell Drake's other son'. And the acting? Hey, that was great, but there's this visceral enjoyment, this power I get from standing in front of all those people and telling them to fuck the fuck off.' 'Eloquent as always, I see.' Maxwell removed a set of reading glasses from the breast pocket of his charcoal gray vest, dusting them off on his lapel. 'What would your mother think, I wonder?' He did not look up to his son when he asked this question. 'Mom's not here,' Duncan responded, pushing his chair from the table. The Hellion stalked from the room, not looking back. His face was pinched closed. 'Children never quite learn not to be their father's son, do they?' Maxwell Drake asked. The question was not directed to MacKenzie Malone, and she was smart enough not to answer it. * * * * * 3.4 - The Man with the Hawkhead Cane When the knock came at the door, it was almost four o'clock in the morning -- a full ninety minutes after MacKenzie made the call. Now in navy blue sweatpants and a gray University of Arizona Wildcats t-shirt, the Belle padded to the door on bare feet, turning over her shoulder to grin down at Johan, still bound to the bed. 'See? I told you they weren't going to let me cut your dick off,' she assured him. The Belle took a glance through the peephole, and then turned back to the bound tattooed man. 'Although this isn't exactly promising for you.' 'Told you they'd send someone for me!' Johan spat. 'Best not stand in front of that door, lovely -- a shotgun blast would cut you square in half, cheap door or no --' MacKenzie cut off Johan's hollow threat simply by pulling the door open. There was no man with a shotgun on the other side; in fact, the man on the other side was not armed at all. He was a smallish man, perhaps five-foot-ten or eleven and not more than 180 pounds, with black hair (though not as dark as MacKenzie's own) slicked back away from a scowl that would not go away and eyes that were sunken down behind circles that seemed permanently etched into his face. The man dressed black-on-black, sharply creased slacks and a turtleneck sweater. A dull silver, pentagonal medallion hung around his neck, with whatever symbol originally etched upon it long since faded away. What was most noticeable about this man, however, was the cane he leaned on. It was one solid piece of dark brown stone, its head carved meticulously into a hawk's head, its eyes red, and its mouth open. If one looked closely, they might make out individual feathers along the crown, each one lovingly crafted. 'Do shut up, Johan,' the man said, his voice gently tinged with an accent MacKenzie could not quite place. He never looked to the bed, however, his eyes focused solidly on Malone. 'If he'd not gotten himself into this mess in the first place, I could have waited for his report. Now it will probably end up reading, 'Met with subject, delivered message. Against all advice given to me by my superiors, got conned into fucking subject. Was tied up and humiliated until heroic man with cane came to rescue.' Not exactly the kind of report that will get a man a promotion, do you think?' 'You're not exactly who I expected,' MacKenzie said. 'If that was the first time I heard that one, I'd give you a booby prize,' the man with the hawkhead cane snapped. He lifted the cane to point at MacKenzie's chest. 'But since you already seem to have that, I probably would have had to take it back.' Mac folded her arms. 'Forget it. I should just go to town on your boy. You people obviously don't think he's important...' She put one hand to the door, pulling it closed as she turned. 'He's not,' the man with the cane said, pressing its tip to the door to prevent it from closing. Without the stick to hold him up, he teetered badly to one side. 'He's just a driver who we sent to deliver the message. The message is still important, MacKenzie. Even moreso now that you've proven yourself capable of taking it out on the messenger. You've impressed us. Now go on and don't take any unnecessary chances and you'll get what you want.' The man dropped the tip of the hawkhead cane back down to the concrete and limped down the hallway of the roadside inn. MacKenzie swung her body out of the room, grabbing the doorframe to holler after him. 'How will I get in touch with you? I don't even know your name.' 'I mean to keep it that way,' the man said. 'As I'm rather not fond of ending up tied to beds in strange motel rooms.' * * * * * 3.5 - That 'How They Met' Question, Round Two Maxwell Drake turned those cold brown eyes on the woman in the Zac Posen tailored pencil skirt and black chintz Galactica jacket. 'What is your story,' he said -- not asked. 'And do not tell me you met at a death metal concert. My son hardly seems the type.' 'I... shouldn't Duncan be here for the third degree?' 'This is not the third degree, Ms. Malone. Believe me, you have not seen 'the third degree'.' 'I... I was -- am -- a hair stylist. I worked at this little shop in Glendale, and Duncan's agent -- ' 'The Slug, yes. I am familiar with him.' 'Noah, yeah. Noah brought him in with one of his girls, and I worked on her hair. We talked, some, about stuff.' ''Stuff'?' 'You know. Hollywood things. Life, gossip, movies. That sort of thing.' 'And then you decided to come to be his wrestling manager?' 'It's not as simple as that.' 'I would certainly hope not.' 'I... Duncan would bring in people. Girls, mostly, though he even let me style his hair once. I swear he'd pay them just to come in so he and I could talk.' 'How utterly romantic.' 'It was. He is. One of them... One of the guys Duncan brought into the salon was a fan of professional wrestling. I'd only seen it a few times, back in the mid-90s when, like, everyone was watching. Duncan loved it immediately, I could tell. It was like he said, the visceral immediacy of the reactions, you know? And he's not that bad at it. If he's given a chance, he...' Maxwell held up a hand to forestall further commentary. 'This is no longer a referendum on Duncan Drake as a professional wrestler. That is a discussion for another time between he and I, a discussion you do not get a say in.' 'No, this is a discussion on my fitness to be your son's girlfriend,' she said calmly. Maxwell's grunt was neither an assent nor a denial. Duncan Drake's voice rarely matched his father's for indifference, but when it reached the table from the other side of the room, it was positively icy. 'That ain't a conversation we're having, either, Pops. C'mon, Kenz. We've got places.' MacKenzie stood gracefully, smiling quietly at Maxwell; it was not unfriendly. 'I'm sorry, Mr. Drake,' she said. 'You do have a lovely home. I would like to visit again someday.' She ran to catch up with the Hellion, heavy boots clattering against the hardwood floors as she went. In the hallway, MacKenzie met Duncan at the massive front door. He was grinning. 'You okay?' she asked. 'Me?' Duncan responded. 'Oh, yeah. I pissed in an antique vase in Dad's bathroom. Figured, hey, if it works for that Gunnar guy, it'd work out all my anger issues, right?' Mac did not smack Duncan in the arm. At least, not too hard. * * * * * 3.6 - The Art Lovers Maxwell Drake awoke everyday so he was available during the opening of the London Financial Markets. Given that he lived in California, which is eight full hours earlier than Greenwich Mean Time, the Old Dragon saw the sunrise from the wrong side many, many nights. So it was not insomnia, nor was it guilt at the way he had spoken to his son and to MacKenzie Malone that roused Maxwell from a restless slumber just before two the following morning. Rather, he had overslept, a fact readily apparent from the dark scowl with which the elder Drake regarded the red digital numbers on his clock. Maxwell Emerson Drake II was a man who could be called a lot of things, but one such as he did not blame a clock for its failings, and neither did he truly fault his son for the argument that resulted in his sleep being restive. These things were merely obstacles, not villains, and, for twenty years, no obstacle had stood for long in the path of the Old Dragon. When Maxwell rose from his sleep, he did so alone, though unlike many who slept alone in king-sized beds, he only used half of the thing. His staff joked -- very quietly -- that the other half of the bed would have gathered dust over the past four years if they hadn't made it everyday. Maxwell slid his feet into black slippers, and had barely stood from his bed before the intercom on the wall beside his bedroom door buzzed for his attention. 'Mister Drake,' came the crackling voice of his housemaster, Gregory. 'I trust I have given you ample time to awaken.' Maxwell never knew how his ghost of a majordomo did those types of things; Gregory was always fixing problems Maxwell never even knew existed before they became crises. There were days the Old Dragon only heard the balding Scot through the ubiquitous intercom speakers that littered the compound. The elder Drake pressed the 'talk' button on the egg-white speaker box. 'You have, my friend,' he said. 'Now why are you still awake at this ungodly hour?' There were those who said that Maxwell Drake treated his servants better than his children; like those that joked about the dust on the other side of his bed, they said these things very quietly, and they always made sure to be out of earshot. 'Because you are awake, sir,' Gregory responded, matter-of-factly. 'And because you have a guest waiting for you in the study. A female guest.' Maxwell Drake did not blame Gregory for not awakening him earlier -- no man would wake up the Old Dragon without thinking about it long and hard. Nor did he comment at the arch humor in the Scotsman's suggestion at a woman's visit at this hour. He merely pulled on a blue button-down dress shirt and last night's tan slacks and briskly made his way down the hall. Recriminations were for lesser men. Maxwell Drake's study was his personal sanctum away from the world. It was where he kept his private art collection, featuring several priceless pieces. It was also the nerve center of the Drake Family business empire. For a guest turned loose inside? He would forgive himself for how hurriedly he opened the door later. Within, the fireplace had been stoked -- its dull oranges and reds provided the only illumination in the room, and Maxwell had to squint against their glare. Even with lidded eyes, the Old Dragon knew every inch of his hoard. He recognized immediately that his high-backed, forest green recliner had been moved from its spot in front of his desk, and that set him to doing a short mental inventory of all the items in the room. That cataloguing stopped abruptly, because Maxwell Drake was a man that could be called many things, but he was still a man and he was not cold enough he could ignore the sight of two tanned legs slithering over the armrest of that stolen high-backed recliner. The woman's legs were bare from at least the middle of the thigh down, they were tanned to a warm cocoa brown, and the feet they ended in were wrapped in pearly white, strappy pumps. Maxwell's guest crossed her feet at the ankle, and, after a moment, a carmine-nailed hand bearing one of his crystal goblets, half-full of rich burgundy wine, draped across her thigh. 'Max, darling,' the woman said, her voice rich and warm, like a gentle summer rain shower. 'I thought you were never going to wake up.' She did not turn to face him. 'Forgive me,' Maxwell said, covering his confusion with his brusqueness. 'If you'd had the courtesy to call ahead, perhaps.' 'Then you wouldn't have seen me. I would have gone to all this trouble for nothing. You know a lady gets dressed up in large part for her man.' 'Madam, I am a married man, and I do not even know you.' The woman pointed her toes as she laughed, a delighted sound filled with promises. 'But I know you, Max. I believe we have a mutual acquaintance. A fellow art lover.' The hand with the wineglass gestured up to the wall, to the painting that hung there. 'La Femme à la Robe Verte, no?' she said. Her French was flawless, as smooth as if she'd been speaking it her entire life. 'It's amazing how many people just choose his Water Lilies, because they're so very pretty.' The last word came out scathing. 'You know your art, miss.' Maxwell strode to the recliner, brushing the back of his hand across the headrest. 'That piece must have cost you a fortune.' 'I doubt you came here to talk about paintings.' 'No,' her voice was almost regretful as she handed the wineglass up to Maxwell. 'I did not. It is lovely, though.' 'Then what is this about? Money?' Maxwell drained the entire glass in a single swallow, his lips pulled back from his teeth as he sighed in sadness. 'What has my son done now?' The way he said those two words -- 'my son' -- was a sad combination of disgust and regret, not the tightly reined emotional front Maxwell showed to Duncan. 'Duncan?' A listener could positively have heard the woman's nose wrinkle with her grin. 'It's funny you should mention him. He's the whole reason I'm here.' 'Believe me, you're not the first half-dressed woman to come to me for money, miss -- what is your name, anyway?' Maxwell set the goblet on the countertop, turning towards his desk. 'Or do I really want to --' Maxwell stumbled as he walked, catching himself on a table of knickknacks. 'Careful, Max,' the woman said teasingly. 'I don't want people to think I got you drunk so I could take advantage of you.' The Old Dragon turned, his eyes wide, his jaw working uselessly. The only sound that came out was a cotton-mouthed cough. 'Drugged you into unconsciousness, sure,' the woman said, kicking her feet off the armrest and flowing to her feet. 'But not that I got you drunk to take advantage of you.' Maxwell fell gracelessly to his left, landing hip-first across a Napoleonic coffee table. The wood did not break. 'Oh, you can just call me, 'Trouble,' sweetheart. You and me -- we're going to become fast friends.' Trouble slinked across the study floor, one foot in front of the other, deep red skirt swishing around her thighs. She retrieved a sleek black smart-phone from her purse and dialed a particular number as she squatted down beside the unconscious Old Dragon, laying two tapered, beautifully manicured fingers across Maxwell's neck to check his pulse. 'Tell Hawke it's done,' was all she said when the operator picked up.
