Only a Few Hours Before Sunlight

24 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/24/07 They watched as he got into his car, gathered tightly together atop a tall oak tree. A fierce glee rose within them as they lifted off, cawing into the night. Joshua glanced through the window to notice the ebony birds before starting the car. Crows were just a normal part of central New York, so he paid them no mind. He backed the Mustang down the driveway, his black duffel sitting on the seat next to him. With a quick turn of the wheel he was out into the street and on his way to the airport. They followed him closely behind, their murder growing with each passing street. Before long the murder consisted of fifty crows or more. Joshua paid them no mind, he was too caught up thinking about everything else, and enjoying the smooth driving of his Christmas present. As a champion it was his duty to defend his title when and where it was deemed necessary. Though he wasn’t very fond of leaving his family during the holidays but it was something he had to do. At least Christmas was already taken care of, that alone made him feel a bit better about doing this. The dashboard clock read 11:49 P.M. His eyes ached and burned, his tear ducts had been in overdrive but even they could not ease the pain. Tear ducts could never correct a lack of a sleep, and they sure wouldn’t have made his son any better. His little boy Jason was fresh out of the hospital for the eighth time since his birth. Honestly, sometimes Josh was convinced the boy would be the death of him. This most recent time the doctor’s thought he had an RSV infection, turns out it had just been a cold. Another case when the boy had made him worry more then he had too. Kids had a way of doing that, this one especially. After three long days in the hospital for observation Jason was allowed to go home, so Joshua had brought him home packed up and prepared to head out. Angel had begged him to sleep some before leaving, but he had wanted to get to London early. If he caught the flight out of Syracuse tonight then he could be in London about a full day before the event started. He had never been to London before, and wanted sometime to look around before putting his title up for grabs, then even if he should lose his title the trip wouldn’t be a total disappointment. Leaving Rome on 365 he was the only car in sight. Usually on a Friday night the roads running to the bigger cities would be filled with people looking for a night of fun, tonight however it was a long lonely stretch. The State route had been maintained well, only a light drift of snow brushed along the asphalt, which provided well enough driving conditions. For Central New York, this was much better then you could hope for. Behind the Mustang the murder continued to follow, unbeknownst to the late night traveler. Had Joshua noticed them still, perhaps he would have stopped the car, turned back and returned home for a good night’s rest. Perhaps then in the morning he would have rescheduled his flight and just dealt with getting to London later then he wanted. But, Josh didn’t notice them, so he kept driving. The wind began to pick up some, and snow once again began to fall. This didn’t disturb him any, living in the area he became accustomed to these conditions. The snow was soft and flaky, not the type you could pack into neat snowballs where Josh and his daughter could sit outside building forts and making a game of it. No, this was flakier and lightly textured. He was coming up on his exit when he saw the first car pass him. Slowly he pulled out to his left passing another car, with another set of headlights off into the distance. Behind him the murder flocked together soaring off ahead of the car, still Joshua paid them no mind, his attention was on the road not the sky. This road wasn’t as clean as the State route he had been on, still pretty manageable however so it really didn’t bother him. He pulled up to the toll both and the man handed him his ticket. As Joshua drove away the toll both operator peered into the sky and noticed the murder following Joshua. The sight alone sent a chill down his spine. There was something eerie about such a large murder, maybe it was superstition, maybe it was just nerves but personally he didn’t care for this sight. Joshua made his way down the thruway as traffic began to slowly pick up. Compared to the State route the thruway was pretty packed. At least in the eastbound direction, Joshua’s two lanes were very empty. The murder put some more distance between them and Joshua, this time he noticed them. He couldn’t get an accurate number this time of the night, but his guess would have been a good sixty or more and they appeared a good fifteen to twenty feet ahead of him. Chalk it up to sleep deprivation, or even how occupied his mind was with other things but the sight of the crows didn’t register anything for him, already he had forgotten the murder outside of his house. He passed a sign telling him a service station would be coming up on his right in another mile and a half. Josh would have to stop and see if they were open, he hadn’t realized it before but he really needed to piss. The murder released a unified cry swooped around and came back together heading straight for the Mustang. Fifteen feet. Joshua noticed them and for a brief moment he was convinced he had fallen asleep at the wheel and that he was dreaming. Ten feet. The murder drew closer, and Joshua reacted. His instincts pulled the car to the left hard, the thin layer of snow gave him less traction then he expected and he hit the median head on barreling over the small knoll and straight into the eastbound lanes. He slammed hard on the brakes. Bad idea. The car skidded, fish tailed and landed in the furthest lane just as a Dodge Dakota crashed into the passenger side. The Mustang rolled six times before finally landing on its roof. The smell of the Dakota’s burnt tires filled the air mingling with the chokingly thick black smoke of the Mustang. Other motorists stopped their cars joining the driver of the Dodge as they approached the wreckage. Someone hollered to call 911, as the murder watched lined across the guardrails of the westbound lane. They cawed together excitingly, while the motorists stood in horror of the crash. After the first minute passed and no one climbed from the Mustang the driver of the Dakota along with two other men approached the car. The Dodge driver dropped to his belly and wiggled through the driver’s window of the Mustang. Gummy pieces of glass littered the asphalt around the car, but the driver was more concerned about the Mustang then himself. Inside the car Joshua wasn’t conscious. The driver pulled him out as the other two men helped position him thirty or so feet from the Mustang. No sooner then Joshua had been laid down on the asphalt the Mustang burst into flames, followed shortly by an explosion. People shouted, and people hollered in a panic while Joshua lay upon the thruway staring at the back of his eyes oblivious to it all. As the State Troopers and the ambulances showed up the murder lifted off of the guardrails and sailed off into the night. Had the motorists not known better they would have sworn the birds were angry, but birds didn’t think like man. The murder put many miles between them and the accident. There was death to bring yet tonight, and only a few hours before sunlight.
