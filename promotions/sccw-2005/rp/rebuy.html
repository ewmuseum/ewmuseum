Date: 7/24/2010 July 3, 2010 The sportsbook wasn't what one would call one of Phillip Kennedy's more common haunts in any given casino. Phil was a realist; he absolutely considered what he did at one point for a living gambling, but it was gambling that he had a say in. With sports, one never knew. Star players tore their ACLs. Upsets happened. To Kennedy's mind, $100 spent on a sports bet was $100 that could be turned into more at the poker table, or even at the blackjack table, if it was the rare day Kennedy wanted a break from the grind of tournaments and cash games. Under most circumstances, the Stack wouldn't be caught dead at the sportsbook. Tonight, though, he'd been knocked out of yet another event at the World Series of Poker, and it was UFC 116. Add to that the fact that the sportsbook had a bar, and taking in a night of fights seemed like a far superior idea to trying to grind it out in a cash game for the rest of the evening. 'Another fuckin' suck-out,' he grumbled, under his breath. 'I'm having unbelievable luck.' Kennedy's World Series of Poker was going even worse than it usually did. Phillip's situation was unique in that he was both a former poker professional AND a well-known professional wrestler. That meant that people who could take a shot at the Stack at the table often did, logic or cards be damned. There was no way he'd put any money on the fights; MMA was way, way too much of a crapshoot for his betting tastes. But he'd be lying if he said watching sporting events at the book wasn't often better than watching it at home. Being around a couple dozen people with monetary interest tended to raise the excitement level a few notches. Having finished his day at the tables, Kennedy had left his black cowboy hat in the hotel room. Being recognized was nice sometimes, but after a long day of getting sucked out on, his desire to be recognized was slim to none. That explained his outfit: an Arizona Diamondbacks baseball cap and t-shirt, along with a pair of jean shorts. With the brim over his face, he looked like any of a number of similarly dressed casino denizens. Clad in his baseball best, he sauntered over to the bar, plopping down some cash for a bottle of Budweiser. 'Thanks,' he said, as the transaction went with usual Vegas speed: the money was put in the register, change given, and the bottle of beer handed over within a few moments. Phillip couldn't help but laugh when people talked about the pace of New York City life; by his reckoning, nothing was faster and looser than Sin City. The book was already loud with people watching the event, allowing Phillip to slip through most of the seating area to a small table in the back corner. Given the size of the giant wall TV that the event was being shown on, sitting up front wasn't particularly something he cared about. Dressed down, in his own corner of the sportsbook, Kennedy had the freedom to be another ordinary Joe watching a night of fights like anyone else, with a Budweiser in his hand. 'Hey,' Kennedy heard a voice bark at him. 'How the fuck ya doin'? It's been months, my man!' It wa a voice that made the Big Stack do a double-take. Poker and wrestling didn't collide in too many ways when he was busy living one life or the other, but in this case... In spite of himself, Kennedy chuckled. 'Not bad, Michael. Been playing a lot of poker and drinking a lot of beer.' From his seated position, Phillip looked up at the former rising mixed martial artist turned pro-wrestler, Michael Vain. As always, Vain was the best dressed man in the room, his wrists and neck dripping with ice. Kennedy didn't know how Vain got his hands on such finery given his income. Kennedy also didn't make it a point to ask such questions. 'Sounds like my kinda life,' Vain barked, slapping Kennedy on the back and taking a seat at the Stack's table. 'Well, except for the beer. I've got refined tastes. Give me the Goose any day of the week.' On that note, Vain turned to a wandering waiter, and growled out the word 'Goose' while flashing the waiter a bill. Given his marching orders, said waiter headed off in the direction of the bar. 'But yeah,' Michael continued, his drink order completed, 'SCCW's got me down in their training facility, gettin' me ready for the ring. Ain't easy to resist the urge to punch a muhfucka in the face. They frown upon that sorta thing, apparently.' Vain clucked his tongue disapprovingly, looking up at one of the sportbook's mammoth TVs. 'Makes me miss this sometimes,' he said, as Chris Leben and Yoshihiro Akiyama got ready to kick off round one of their fight. 'Oh yeah,' Kennedy said, suddenly remembering. 'You used to do MMA. Ever wonder if you could have made it to the UFC?' 'All the time, man,' Vain replied, snatching his bottle of Grey Goose vodka from the waiter and slapping a $100 bill in the waiter's hand. 'Keep the change,' the burly ex-mixed martial artist said, taking a hearty swig straight from the bottle.' 'How the fuck do you do that,' Phillip asked, shaking his head at the taller man's ability to swig straight vodka. 'Easy, Phil,' Michael grinned. 'I'm a man, and that's what men do.' Man or not, Phillip was content to sip away at his second Budweiser of the evening. 'What brings you here though?' he asked. 'You told me you weren't a gambling man.' 'I'm not,' Kennedy's former bodyguard replied. 'I came here to watch the fights, and I saw your ass hidin' over here in the corner, so I figured I'd come over and say hi. You dropped off the fuckin' map, man. No 'hey, Mike, I'm gonna take some time off' or nothin'!'' Vain grinned at Kennedy, but there was a touch of actual hurt in the bald man's eyes. 'Sorry,' Phil said, looking at Vain from under his cowboy hat. 'Never really meant to go away. Just got my ass kicked by Jonathan Rhine bad, went to the doctor, and he told me that I had to take actual time off. Really, he told me never to come back, but like Hell was I listening to that. One month turned into two, which turned into three. I was back at the poker table on a regular basis. Wasn't getting hurt anymore. It was kinda nice, really.' 'That mean I ain't ever seein' yo' ass in Sin City again,' Vain asked, shaking his head. 'Or are you still pacifyin' the doctors?' 'I don't know,' Kennedy admitted. 'I'm here in Vegas until I get eliminated from the main event of the World Series of Poker. After that, I've got time on my hands to think about it.' 'Yo' ass needs to get back, on the double. Company's down a couple of its top guys. Lane retired and vacated the strap. Xavier's been MIA for a few weeks now. They're having a tournament for the belt as we speak.' 'Shit,' Kennedy swore, shaking his head. 'Lane retired?' 'Was a shock to everyone, yeah.' Vain took another hard swig from his bottle of Goose. 'For the first time, well, evah, SCCW's short on assholes. We got Wyatt, of course, and that Kingsley guy. Bridden too, and the Heirs, but...yeah. I'd step up myself, but they ain't think I can be trusted to not get DQed every time out yet. If yer gonna go back, now's probably the best time.' Placing his bottle of beer on the table, Kennedy shook his head. 'Maybe, but I imagine most of the people who hate my ass are still there.' 'Oh yeah,' Vain agreed. 'Alex is still there, plugging away against Bridden. Amy's still there. I don't think Sykes likes you much either. Umm, who else...' 'Is Lance there still,' Kennedy asked. Somehow, when he thought of SCCW, he generally saw two figures: the white pantsuited form of Alexandra Pierce, and the walking slab of granite that was SCCW's resident Lion. 'Oh yeah, still hammering away as always. He's a stubborn man, that one.' 'That he is,' Kennedy agreed, a grin coming to his face. 'He and I always had a...I don't want to say friendship, because it really wasn't, but...' 'Ya respected each other,' Vain concluded, with a nod. 'Anybody could see that.' Phillip didn't speak for a few moments, turning over an idea in his head. Finally, Phil nodded at his friend. 'I'm gonna go get some fresh air, Mike,' he said. 'You gonna be okay here with the fights?' 'Fuck yeah,' Vain said, grinning. 'These middleweight faggots are about to start the third round. After that, it'll be Brock and Carwin, so your ass better hurry back!' As much as Kennedy wanted to see the two gorillas slug it out for the UFC Heavyweight Championship, there were more important things on his mind right now. 'See ya in a bit, then,' Kennedy told his former bodyguard, before disappearing out of the sportsbook and into the jingling, blinking madness that was a Las Vegas casino. 'Reminds me of being in the damned ring,' Phillip mused, as he weaved his way through slot machines and blackjack tables. Poker was usually set off in its own room, away from the madness. Somehow, it was a more civilized game due to that, a far cry from its Wild West reputation. Poker had been commercialized, sanitized, and put on television for the masses. Sort of, Kennedy knew, like a lot of pro wrestling had been. 'Maybe that's why I miss it so much. Gambling at the poker table's just risking cash or chips...' Risk was, essentially, what Phillip Kennedy thrived on. Risk and adrenaline. Going all-in for all your chips wasn't the same as chairing Alexandra Pierce in the skull and seeing what happened. Once upon a time, when one played poker, you weren't just gambling your money. You were gambling your freedom, if the game got broken up by the authorities. You were gambling your health and life, if you beat the wrong hothead with a revolver. That was why, no matter how much he thought about it, he couldn't make his return for the corporate worlds of PRIME or GCW, with their better healthcare, better road accomodations, and bigger paychecks. There was only one place for Phillip Kennedy, a gambling man born a hundred or so years too late. That place was SCCW. A wry smile curved the Big Stack's mouth as he stepped free of the slot machines, and entered the giant lobby. A few more steps and Phillip was through the glass doors, out into the dry heat that permeated Las Vegas even at night. Kennedy fished in his pocket for his cell phone, frowning at the lack of reception. He took a few steps away from the casino until the reception bars returned. 'Well,' he told himself, under his breath, 'here goes nothing.' Searching through his contacts, Phillip found the number of the one man in Sin City that he was on decent terms with, and punched the 'send' button. A few rings later, and someone picked up. 'Hello?' 'Lance,' Phillip said, unable to help smiling at the familiar voice of the former SCCW Universal Champion. 'It's Phil. How goes?' Thirty minutes later, Phil's mind had been made up. ----------------------------------------------------- 'Dude, you fuckin' missed an insane fuckin' fight!' Michael Vain exclaimed, upon Kennedy's return. 'You're droppin' more f-bombs than Dana White,' Kennedy observed, sliding back into his seat. 'What happened?' 'Carwin came out like a bat outta hell and dropped Brock!' 'For real?' Kennedy couldn't imagine the gigantic former pro wrestler getting dropped easily, even if Carwin had Oldsmobiles for fists. 'Yeah, and then he started raining death, but Brock covered up. Ref almost stopped it a half-dozen times, but Brock defended until Carwin gassed out from so many punches. End of the round, Carwin was done. Brock took it in round two--actually, they're showin' a replay right now. Look up there.' The Big Stack looked up just in time to see Brock Lesnar wrap his mammoth arms around Shane Carwin's neck, before hopping out of the mount position to tighten up whatever sort of hold he'd managed to ensnare Carwin with. As Carwin tapped out, Phillip turned to the man dubbed the 'Pain Train,' the result of the fight itself no longer of any importance. 'What the Hell was that? That move Lesnar finished him with?' 'Oh, the arm triangle,' Vain asked, shrugging. 'It's a jiu-jitsu sub. Good thing for a wrestler to know, since you can use your power and size to lock it up.' The wheels were already turning in Kennedy's mind. 'Can you teach it to me?' Vain shook his head. 'You think my ass knows any submissions? Fuck no. I punch a muhfucka in the face until they ain't movin' no more. I do know a couple guys at the gym I used to train at that know BJJ though, so I can hook ya up if ya want.' The Pain Train reached for his cell-phone, and fired off a quick text message towards that end. By the time he was halfway done typing it, Vain looked up and grinned. 'I'm gonna assume this interest ain't cause you want to learn BJJ...' Phillip Kennedy fixed his former bodyguard with a broad grin. 'You'd assume right,' Kennedy stated. 'Also, go tell SCCW brass that you're gonna need to be coming back on the road, effective immediately.' The bottle of vodka in Vain's free hand was placed on the table just so that the bodyguard could rub his palms together, menacingly. 'That's the kinda shit I like ta hear, Phil. Cause when ya got me on yer side, I promise one thing for alla yer opponents: PAIN!' The final word was bellowed loudly enough so as to get a few odd looks. Vain shot glares back, as if to say 'yes, I am a loudmouthed motherfucker, and you will sit there and like it if you want to be chewing your breakfast tomorrow.' 'Just finish sending that message,' Kennedy insisted, shaking his head a bit at the eagerness of his bodyguard. 'I want to come back to Sin City with an edge. Lane or no Lane, that place is full of people who want to take my head off, and no doubt a bunch of newcomers who'd be happy to make a name off of taking me out.' Vain grinned toothily in response as he pushed the 'send' button on his phone.. 'Ain't that how it always is?' 'Yeah,' Kennedy agreed, remembering his days climbing the SCCW ladder, 'and I wouldn't have it any other way.' Whether the former Gateway Champion would be so happy a few weeks later, when his opponent would be announced as El Hijo del Senor Ass? Anyone's guess, but signs point to 'no.'
