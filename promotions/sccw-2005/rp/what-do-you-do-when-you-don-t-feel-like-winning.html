What do you do when you don't feel like winning?

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Today Excerp from a forth coming rp.. since I don't want to win, and Luke don't either. "Deconstruction Site" It was a novelty to gaze into the empty stands that just a half hour past was filled with eager and vocal fans. As intimidating as it could be at times to step out before them, I think I struggled harder that night to stay put in the scene of the crime. Ghosts haunted the Hofheinz Pavilion, one of which appeared eerily similar to myself in full on attire. Yeah, you could say that I was far from the task at hand, or unaware to even the calling of a man strides away. "Mitch," Dave called out to me, "You awake over there? I mean come on man; you are making us look bad here." Seeing that he had indeed seemed to pull me back to Earth the man of many hats went back to the tearing down. This was the work that was never glorified in our profession. The viewers at home sit and marvel at men and women who are tasked to work no more than an hour a night and know nothing of effort required to set the stage on which those players play. No one commended the guys who take apart the ropes, except for this night. "Hey guys, you did good tonight," was the bellowing of the "King of Old School" himself, Dave Gibson. He appeared to be retrieving something left at the broadcast table, but I think he was just as in awe of how large it all looked. That never fades. "Hey Mitchell, you just got to forget about it," the advice I hadn’t asked for. "Kid you are doing wonders in there. But for me, would you lose that damn temper? It is not exactly a pleasure having to call to the crowds around the world that you tapped out." He was right. Of course he was right. He had reached heights I could only hope to see, time permitting. There was little in my situation I would care to do other than say, "Yeah, thanks. Have yourself a good one Gibbo." He cringed a touch. I didn’t think the nickname premature or too particularly awful; must have been some nerve I never knew existed. A quick recovery and his round of goodbyes to the crew around me, and Dave Gibson was off to his own corner of the continent. "Hey Steve, great to finally see you back out here. The kid’s been stuck in his head, so we got a little slack that needs picking up." Dave more than enjoyed his role of general to the disassembly line, but did he really need to go and call out my contributions like that? Did he see any other of the ‘talent’ lending a helping hand? "I would have been out here sooner," he started, cuing my grin, "but that kid there thought it would be funny to put my stuff in the girl’s shower room." This was the other part of this business that you never saw: How road weary men made themselves amused. And I should say that Steve knowing I to be the culprit just further intensified my urged to chuckle. This was the best rib I had yet to pull, so excuse me if I wanted to enjoy it. "Hey kid." Why do they always call me kid? "You know that’s not funny right?" Dave asked in a pseudo-serious manner. "Nah," Jamie chimed in, taking the volley for all that it was worth, "its friggen hilarious!" "Hardy-har-har! Get you kicks in now gentlemen, but need I remind you that payback is a bitch." He said it with such conviction that I knew he heeded not to the theory of ‘…a dished served chilled’. "So, we going to hit the bar after this?" Jamie asked me. He asked me this question every week. Every week I explained to the boys I didn’t drink and just wanted to get back home. He happily asked once more. His hazel eyes glimmered with the hope that this losing streak might encourage my willingness to abandon my straightedge life. Every week I would say no, and be on my way far away from here. I would rush to escape to my music, and transition myself between the two selves. Lately I had been wondering if I really did need to become the well intentioned but frustrating son quite so quickly. Tonight I knew that I would need to be the confident man just a little while longer than I would care for. Tonight I need to let this loss consume me. Let the taste of defeat swash about my mouth. Trust it is vile, bitter, and most unappealing. Let me endure this for a time and then resolve to never again visit upon me. "Yep." It took him aback. I think I saw everyone around display some form of surprise. "You ain’t shitting with me?" he didn’t believe me. "Not at all Jay. Tonight we descend on a local tavern for some beverages. You gents can get shitfaced and give me a laugh. I could use one about now." With that we all picked up the pace. The light at the end of the tunnel was just that much brighter. And when all was said and done a handful and some piled into alternate modes of transportation with an agreement to reconvene at a place called "Griff’s" at the recommendation by our native Houston zebra, Ray Thomas. Before I made my phone call to a cab, I had a wrong tonight I needed to right. The extension of an invitation I prayed could give me the chance to remedy. ----- Yeah, I suck.
