Simply Better

15 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/15/09 The sun was bright in the sky as Xander Searle stepped out of his Taxi Cab and set foot outside of a car dealership in Beverly Hills. Back at his new apartment, his sparse belongings sat in boxes. They were remnants of a former life. He really didn’t need them anymore. Now, he needs a new set of wheels. Even if he wanted to bring the Jeep that he was borrowing from Jason Ryan, he wouldn’t have brought it with him. It was another reminder of the life that he left. The life that he didn’t want to have anymore. He looks back towards the cab driver and hands over the fare. The driver takes a look at the amount that’s in his hand as Searle walks towards the door to the dealership. "Jackass!" the cabbie yells out, seeing that Xander gave him just enough to cover the fare, and didn’t give a tip. Searle enters the dealership, and is quickly greeted by one of the sales people that was on the floor that morning. "Welcome to Jim Falk Lexus. My name is Thomas, and let me know if you have any questions about anything." "Sure." Searle starts to look around at the different cars. He’s saved up his downside guarantees ever since he started in SCCW, and was now going to spend it. "Was there a particular vehicle that you were looking for?" Searle ignored him. He was taking a look at the IS F that sat on the showroom floor. He was impressed by the fact that it was a performance model, but he wasn’t sure if he wanted to go with that, or since he was now going to live in Southern California, if he was going to go with the SC that was next to it. He liked the idea of having the convertible. At 6’8, it’s hard for him to find a car that fits him just right anyway. "So, what are you, 6’6?" Thomas asked as he continued to shadow Xander. Still, Xander ignored the salesperson. "You know, with the leg room that you would get with either one, you wouldn’t have to worry about banging your head when you’re in the car. You just have to be careful when you get in or get out." Xander places his hand on the glossy black paint that covered the IS F. He uses his fingertips to feel every little curve and edge that is in the body. "These cars are as smooth as glass, and handle better than a Formula 1 racer." Thomas was trying to push the virtues of buying this car on Xander. He wasn’t moved. "So, should we start up the paperwork to get you financed for this fine machine." Still, the 19 year old Searle didn’t respond. "Well, when you make your decision, I’ll be over there. Just call for me when you’re ready." Thomas walked and started to talk with another customer that entered the dealership as Searle walks over to the SC that was also on the showroom floor. Xander opens the door and gets a look at the interior, finally sitting down in the seat. The look that is on the faces of everyone that is in the showroom is priceless. Many of them can’t believe that this person, wearing jeans and a black t-shirt, has the audacity to do this without asking for permission from the employees of the dealership. That is the great thing in Searle’s mind. He doesn’t have to worry about what everyone else thinks about him. He doesn’t care that what he is doing is considered uncouth. "Sir, was there anything I could help you with?" a man in a rather dapper suit asked as he walks up to him. It wasn’t Thomas the salesman. "Yes, I would like…a latte." "Sir, this is a sixty six thousand dollar automobile that you are sitting in?" "Really? You mean to tell me that this isn’t a Miata." The person that’s obviously the dealership’s sales manager didn’t take too kindly to this little attempt at a joke, as he walks even closer to the door of the car, and opens the door. "You see, sir, we have policies here at this dealership. One of those policies is that customers aren’t to enter the vehicles without the permission of one of the members of the sales staff. Are we clear on that?" "Well, I know that I don’t like you. Send Thomas back over, I’d rather talk to him." "Sir, we do reserve the right to refuse service at this dealership?" "Really. I’m pretty sure that if I decided to go down the road and buy a different car, that your owner is not going to be happy that you let a sale for seventy grand go out the window." The sales manager kept his eye on Xander as he steps out of the car. Xander walks up to the sales manager, and the nearly foot difference in height is noticeable at this point. Xander takes his sunglasses off and put them in his pocket. "Are you going to be the one to tell Mr. Falk, or whoever owns this place, that you let a potential buyer walk about without buying one of your cars because he didn’t like to play by YOUR rules." "The rules, sir, are here because we aren’t a Ford dealership. Buying a car like this isn’t like you are buying a Taurus. This, sir, is a status symbol." "Yeah, the status that says that I am better that you. Which I am. " "You are what?" Xander gets a smirk on his face. "I live a life where I live by my own rules. The consequences don’t matter, because as long as I can live this life, there is nothing that can stop me from attaining that which I desire. "You see, I don’t fear the reprisal of those that are above me. I can operate my life with clear autonomy. There are no rules, there is no fear. "You live to make the sale. I don’t. I’ve already made the sale. I’ve already made the decision, which you are going to have to live with. You need to ask yourself is the decision I made going to take my money out of this dealership and going to another dealership where I will spend the money that I have on a car… "Or is the decision that I’ve chosen between Matador Red Mica, or Tigereye Mica." The boldness in the soliloquy rocks the sales manager. He’s never been talked to like that, ever. Not even by the people that are above him. "Sir, I don’t think you know who you are dealing with?" Xander puts his sunglasses back on. "Yes, I do. You’re afraid of life, that is why you live and die by what your bosses tell you. You’re afraid that if you lose this life, that if you lose this meager salary that you get here, if you lose the commissions that you make from every car that you and your people sell, that you are going to lose your identity. "You are a scared little man that is going to try to do whatever it is that you need to do in order to make the sale, because that’s what you are taught to do in life. You are taught to cow tow to the whims of every customer that walks through that door. You are taught to treat them like they are the most important person in your life. "The fact is that you weren’t taught anything that you didn’t already know. You are an insignificant little man whose only role in life is to service those that simply are better than you."
