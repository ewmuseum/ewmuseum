Trashed And Scattered (PART ONE)

11 February 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 11th, 2008 An unconventional pro wrestler's outlook on life, love and the World... Of Warcraft. In a San Diego bay Guitar Center, Amy Campbell strums a vintage Les Paul unplugged. It's time for her to unwind after a rough showing at her company's ESPN show. She's thinking of buying a new guitar to aid in her escape from the loss she'd just suffered, but doesn't trust her ability to play enough to plug the guitar in and test its tone in front of the more serious players working in the store. After fumbling over what she thought was a simple blues scale, she repositions her hand around the neck of the instrument - much like the neck of an opponent - and finally squeezes tight enough to eek out the right notes in succession, thrashing through what sounds like an unplugged version of a Metallica solo until she realizes that she's gained an audience, where she almost immediately drops the ball and her chops once again fail her. She later walks out of the store with an expensive new toy and a smile on her face. An unsteady start, a slick moment of victory and then another rocky step; That's just the way things have been going lately. Amy is a little too hyperactive to stay in one place or a specific career path for very long, and that might very well be why she's not holding a title belt when she spends her day with us. Fortunately, it doesn't seem to bother her in the least. She grins sheepishly when discussing her hobbies, which range from being a part-time wannabe rock star to - get this - an avid World of Warcraft fanatic. And while most celebrities, pseudo-celebs especially, are chomping at the bit to discuss the details of their work and accomplishments to any fan within earshot, this 24 year old isn't having any of it. ''What are you listening to lately?'' she asks the first person who recognizes her as we walk back to her car. He's wearing a Dillinger Escape Plan t-shirt, and approached her politely for a picture after realizing that he'd just passed someone who is, according to him, 'One of the only real chicks on TV today'. ''I know there are some good newer bands out there, I just can't seem to find them.'' The young man is quick to hand over a sticker with his band's myspace adress. While it's not exactly what she'd planned for, Amy accepts it with a friendly nod and a couple of random questions to show some semblance of interest. No doubt, she is interested. As socially awkward as her normal demeanor tends to be, the redhead knows how to speak to people -- a skill she no doubt picked up and used in what she calls her 'tougher years'. In the past few months alone, this girl's had a lot to overcome. Her husband, who we'll get to later, got re-injured and consequently put out of action, leaving one less soldier at her side. She hooked up with The Highwaymen, a group of self-proclaimed heroes against the evils in their company, and put an even larger target on her back for the vultures that had nested back in the shadows. One of those vultures was a man by the name of Lane Stevens, who had never given in to the subtle charms of the sober, much more subdued Amy Campbell that he'd finally gotten to meet and instead marched against her based on his hatred for what he'd seen in the years prior. He made her life a very open living hell for a long time, citing his motivation as simply being irritated by her presence. He attacked her because of her staying power, attempting to beat out of the redhead the very thing that made her who she was -- the ability to walk out of almost any situation and keep her eyes ahead of her. 'Quit' doesn't exist to her and thanks to that, her personal life and career both survived an almost endless public parade ofher past mistakes. As persevering as Aimz is, her fans are even more forgiving. ''The people who stay with me are the fans I care about,'' she explains while checking her Audi's GPS unit for directions to our lunch, ''Not the guys who drool over me on the internet and ditch the thought of me if I get a tattoo they don't like. I love the people who wear the t-shirts and come to the shows to have a good time and scream. Even though nobody on the face of the planet should look up to me, I especially love the girls who I can set some kind of an example for by being real. I am who I am, on camera and off. I don't project something that I'm not, and I don't think anybody should feel that they have to.'' She continues, noting ''I enjoy what I do, because I'm getting paid to do something that comes naturally and relieves some tension. When I get a chance to relieve that tension standing across from someone who I genuinely think is a fucking jackoff - like Lane Stevens or Jacob McKail - then all the better, but you'll NEVER see me trying to play a role on TV for the sake of a crowd reaction or merch sales. Even though I'm with The Highwaymen, I know I could never get away with convincing people that I'm as righteous as Jon Rhine. At the same, no matter how pissed off at someone I might get, you won't find me going to the lengths that someone like Lane Stevens would. I guess I'm a PR nightmare because I fall in that easy, in-between zone.'' That statement alone might have you turning the page. You're thinking that this womans sounds uninspired, flat even. If you're a fan, you know better. Just as she is in the ring, Amy Campbell is the consumate posterchild for the bipolar; She maintains a fairly relaxed exterior while peppering her conversations and actions with devil-may-care outbursts. What's been a fairly demure interview takes a twist when she stops in the middle of traffic and gasps, having heard the intro to a timeless song on the radio that had been turned down to allow for our conversation. In the blink of an eye, she's got the volume at an inhuman level and shrieks along to Guns 'N Roses' hit 'Sweet Child O' Mine', proclaiming 'I do a mean Axl Rose when I'm hammered.'. Not that she's driving drunk - we can only assume that she's discussing her impersonations during karaoke nights gone by - but she is shredding all hope of this being another tame, management-driven article. Once again, it's become difficult to attempt separating the person we see in the ring from the person who brakes for 80s cock rock. This journalist could spend pages delving into her psyche to decipher why, but it's much easier than that. Simply put, Amy Campbell and 'Aimz' aren't two different people. It wouldn't be a stretch to say that she was born to be a character. You can't catch lightning in a bottle because it's impossible to predict its next move, and the same can be said for Amy. This is the Aimz that fans have come to love. She approaches and makes you feel welcome, sliding gently into conversation. Once you're at ease, her world opens up and the rollercoaster ride starts. Our coaster just pulled over to the side of the road for an impromptu air guitar jam session, but we were soon back on the rails, and back to the quieter side of Campbell. She throws a half-hearted apology for the G'N'R heartattack she just gave me and quickly gets back to her driving, pulling haphazardly back into the fast lane without signaling. Almost everything this woman does gives insight into herself, and that could very well be why she claims that she can't be anything but 'who she is' -- who she is isn't exactly a secret after knowing her for five minutes. Though that kind of honesty would normally be considered a social handicap, with Amy Campbell it translates into a soothing charm that she can seemingly turn off and on like a lightswitch. She speaks with the accent of a French Canadian immersed in Americana, and that lethal combination is topped off by natural beauty that no amount of ink can take away from, no matter how hard the lithe Canuck might try. She doesn't have the mug of a woman who's been to in-patient rehab, much less a girl who gets pummeled on a weekly basis. To top it off, she doesn't need that nose ring or any of her tattoos to set herself apart from others, unless she's standing next to David Bowie, because she was blessed at birth with one of the most interesting genetic 'defects' you'll ever see. Her mismatched eye colors make her literally one in a thousand and, that blaze of red hair aside, are her most unmistakable feature. At one point, I made the mistake of asking about her past relationships. Not that it was mysterious territory for such an open book, but it did bust the flood gates down. With more tales of cheating and drunken one-nighters than a five year subscription to Cosmopolitan, it's not hard to imagine how Amy got to being a former drug addict, divorcee, near-mother and a locker room legend for falling easily into the trap of any man who smooth-talked her into believing the lies they used to get a piece. All of those things earned Aimz a reputation that wasn't exactly becoming of a young woman, but there are a few developments that have effectively 'saved' her: Maturity, the acceptance of her family, a few good influences at her side in The Highwaymen and, most importantly, the presence of Darcy Markson. ''I think when I got married, it was the greatest day of my mom's life. I don't think she's gotten me into a dress since a terrible incident in Sunday school when I was like eight -- this red velvet and lace number... it's painful to think about.'' When we've stopped for our lunch, and once she's ordered the biggest cheeseburger she can find, the redhead makes sure our table's ketchup bottle is prepared for her meal as she recalls that day. ''I've got this ridiculous paranoia, like some kind of abandonment complex or something, so once I'd said yes to Darcy's proposal, there was no way in hell I'd let him wriggle out. He didn't even want to get away from me or out of the engagement, which still confuses me because really, who in God's name is twisted enough to look at my dumb ass and think 'wife'? Either way, I don't think any of it seemed real, because my only other experience with engagement or marriage or ANY of that was this terrible Vegas fiasco a few years ago. When it came down to it, we had this big match coming up - I don't even remember what it was, but it was for FUSE - and he decided that it'd be fun to take a week off in the Dominican to relax a little.'' She doesn't realize it, but there's a smile creeping onto her face. ''Anyway, he brought the dress. It was this thing I'd been forced into for his best friend's wedding two months before that, so I didn't think anything of it except that maybe Darcy thought it'd be some sick joke. He told me we were gonna go downstairs and crash some kind of fancy function, and bribed me by telling me all about the fantastic bar and comped drinks - not that I was an alcoholic or drunk then but hell, we were on vacation. Needless to say, I got downstairs and my entire family was there. He even flew the pastor from the church I'd gone to as a kid up, but that had more to do with my mom having a deathgrip on Darcy's balls than any request of mine. Badaboom, badabing, we got married. I had a couple of drinks in me, and that's probably the only reason I ended up in the dress in the first place.'' ''Shit,'' she laughs, ''My mom's gonna kill me when she reads that. Wait, can I say 'shit' in a magazine? 'Cause I'm pretty sure I've sworn throughout this whole thing...'' Her cursing can be forgiven -- we're not tight-lipped about what we print. And in a feat worthy of the Guiness Book, her meal's lifespan is under ten minutes from the time it arrives at the table. When asked to give a ballpark figure of how many calories she just shoved straight to her hips, Amy sticks her tongue out in defiance. ''Probably a few trillion.'' Isn't that a little unhealthy for an athlete? ''I think I said it earlier too, I'm not Jonathan Rhine. I can't stand having to watch every little thing I eat, 'cause I like food. I have a hard enough time keeping the other unhealthy things I enjoy out of my life. Compared to what I used to put in my body, food's the least of my worries -- I give myself wriggle room and a pat on the back for it. Now that I'm fairly sober, my body must think it's on vacation... I don't notice a cheeseburger here and there, and it doesn't affect what I do in-ring, so why worry? If I go to the doctor and she bitchslaps me for getting fat or being near a heart attack, I'll gladly snip what I eat here and there. In ten years, if I don't watch my diet, I'll end up looking like Rosie O'Donnell... so why not enjoy my young metabolism while I can?'' She makes a good point, but I can't help noticing that we've gone through the day so far without mention of a gym in her schedule. ''I ride bikes for fun. When Darcy's doing his little workout, I'll toss in a half hour or so of whatever he's doing if I'm awake. I kind of randomly train in jiu jitsu and mixed martial arts, but that's not a daily thing. As long as I've got the adrenaline and stamina to kick the snot outta someone, I'm not going to bother and wear on my body by doing some massive five hour hellride every morning just to be able to tell you how many reps I can do.'' It takes a moment for her to digest what she just said. ''Oh God, I'm the laziest wrestler since Bam Bam Bigelow.'' Though she might think so, nobody else seems to. Amy Campbell is as known for being a work horse as she is a party animal. Though 'work horse' might be the wrong term -- she's of the mentality that if wrestling becomes work, she'll no longer be a part of it. What earns her a decent reputation for attending events and giving her all in the ring is a genuine love of fighting. If there was a UFC for women willing to pay for her to live, she'd probably join it. ''I don't think I could handle MMA competition, just because I don't like my entire life becoming some fitness machine.'' Or not. Either way, she's made her name based on a lot of things - partying, looking the way she does and many other not-so-fantastic features, but Aimz is also well known for throwing absolutely everything she has into a wrestling event. She doesn't back off, even in the face of men like Lane Stevens who have been bent on turning her into just another chest-heavy female wrestling throwaway. Her Highwaymen counterparts have spoken highly of her, with FUSE's Universal Champion calling the redhead, ''The scariest and somehow nicest person I've met in a long time, and a miniature monster if something gets in her way.'' If that's true, then Malachi and Andre Jenson have their work cut out for them when they face the team of Aimz and Jonathan Rhine this week. Both are coming off of losses at FUSE on ESPN, and Amy particularly doesn't like to lose. ''I try to block the fact that I just lost out of my head, so I don't take it home with me. During my issues with Lane Stevens, I brought too much of my in-ring mindset home because of how deep he was getting into my life, and I was making it hell for myself 24/7. I'm trying not to do that anymore, but I guarantee that when I get off the plane this week, my opponents will have giant red targets painted on their foreheads.'' When discussing her Highwaymen cohort Rhine, who she teams with at the coming event, Amy's curiously mute. He's been going through some notable personal problems, straining his relationship with his girlfriend, and Campbell's good friend, Katie Malick. ''Let's not go there,'' she sighs, visibly growing upset, ''Jon's my partner this week, he's my friend... I'm not gonna speculate or throw my opinions out there right now. I'm not into throwing anyone on a cross unless they deserve it and so far, he doesn't. He needs room to deal with whatever he's gotta deal with.'' Though that clashes with something that a camera crew recently picked up in whispered words from Amy to Jonathan, we're not TMZ.com and won't push the subject further. It should be noted, though, that when the meal is over and the digital recorder is paused so we can get back to the car, the redhead sighs. ''My loyalty there lies with whoever gets hurt, and so far Jon's the one dishing out the hurting. I'm hardly impressed seeing one of the guys I'm on the road with and respect doing what I think he's doing.'' (Continued on page 78)
