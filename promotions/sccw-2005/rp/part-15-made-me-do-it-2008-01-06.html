Part 15: Made Me Do It

6 January 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/6/08 Today was the day. Wyatt Connors stood before the gathered masses of the Decency Compound, who all looked at him with expectant eyes. This was to be his most important sermon to date. He stood at the front of the hall, alone. Not even his trusted lieutenants were by his side--they had been charged with a holy mission by the great Reverend Seck himself. That shouldn't have any effect; after all, Connors was certainly no stranger to public speaking. Yet he approached the pulpit with apprehension...for today's sermon was of a far more personal nature. None of those in attendance would truly understand what it meant, and those who would recognize the significance would never hear it. That didn't matter. The actions that had been taken were justified, and someone had to know why. Wyatt scratched at the side of his pompadour and took a quick look at the congregation. He felt his throat dry up, and a strange buzzing noise in his ear...but a large swig of Kool-Aid from a nearby glass solved both of those issues. He began hesitantly, almost as if he was nervous. 'Many years ago, there was a man who wrote a poem. That man was John Milton, and the poem was Paradise Lost.' Already, he could tell that the followers were not completely receptive, conditioned as they were to the theatrics and interminable shouting. Connors soldiered on in the face of their confusion. 'The subject of that poem is the Fall of Man...the long, sordid tale of how Adam and Eve came to be tempted to sin, and were cast out of Eden. Most of the story is told from the viewpoint of Satan, the fallen angel who was responsible for that temptation, thereby bringing sin and death into the world.' 'The beginning of the story concerns itself with Satan shortly after the fall, where he rises from the lake of fire to claim dominion over the realm of the damned. It is here that Milton writes his most famous line.' 'Better to reign in hell, than serve in heav'n.' Wyatt paused a moment to let that sink in, and refreshed himself once more. 'As you all know, Lucifer's great sin was that of pride. So great was his arrogance that he could not bear the thought of being less than anyone, even the creator of the universe. Even ultimate defeat could not silence his vanity, as with Milton's words he claimed that he preferred to remain forever apart from God's presence, and instead rule over a land devoid of joy or hope. Those words have been the rallying cry of the selfish for centuries.' 'I know of a man who has taken this idea to heart. He calls himself an Angel, but make no mistake, he is a creature of hell through and through. Not once has he ever spared a thought for others, caring only for fulfilling his own base desires. His code, his mantra, his raison d'etre...is taken from the words of Milton.' 'Rule Or Serve?' # # # 'I am going straight to hell for this,' Jeremiah muttered under his breath. He wasn't completely up to date on his scripture, but he figured that any task requiring five men to wear ski masks and hide in a van could not end in anything other than eternal damnation. The trip from the Compound to Athens, Ohio felt about twice as long as it actually was. Jeremiah--and it was strange how he now thought of himself by that name when he was on the road--should have been used to it by now, with all the recent SCCW shows on the East Coast and in Canada, but this one was especially bad, because Connors wasn't there. Usually the False Prophet would be the focus point for all the crazy that went on...but without him, it all seemed to land squarely on Jeremiah. That included Illuminado and Angel Verde singing hymns very loudly, in Spanish, and off-key. Every twenty minutes, Caravan would ask a philosophical question that would tax the spiritual knowledge of anyone less than nine years old. After a while Jeremiah would blurt out, 'It's God's will,' every time the big man opened his mouth--making for a rather awkward moment when Caravan said he had to go to the bathroom. Worst of all, Neophyte sat stock-still in the front passenger seat, staring straight ahead for the entire drive. Jeremiah wasn't certain that the kid had blinked, let alone slept, the whole time. It took every ounce of willpower he had to keep from steering the vehicle off the side of every bridge they crossed. And yet, he would gladly do it a hundred more times if it meant not going through with the task that lay before him. They'd parked the van by an apartment complex that was mostly populated by Ohio University students. Classes hadn't restarted yet, but there were still a few students that had already come back from Christmas break. One of them was the reason they'd come all this way. 'All right, guys...Brothers, I mean. Remember the plan?' he asked, and was answered with affirmative murmurs. They'd received some information about their target from a washed-up old curtain-jerker with a stupid name and a thick German accent. It took some work, but eventually Jeremiah figured out the important details. The target lived in this complex; no roommate or steady girlfriend; at least, not one that stayed over a lot. Worked at a supermarket, and usually walked home, even in winter. Jeremiah checked his watch, which read a quarter to ten. If everything went as normal, he'd be coming home in about five minutes. 'It's time. Places.' The other four men immediately sprang into action. Neophyte exited the van and ran halfway down the block, clutching a pamphlet in his hand. Angel Verde and Illuminado took up a position on the sidewalk, directly across from where the van was parked. Caravan sat in the van right by the back doors, while Jeremiah remained in the driver's seat. 'Won't be long now,' he said. Sure enough, just a few minutes later, a young man approached the block. Time would tell if this was the right man or not...and if it was, then all that remained was-- 'Uh-oh.' Jeremiah whipped his head around. 'What do you mean, 'uh-oh?'' 'Uh-oh,' repeated Caravan, prompting Jeremiah to clamber to the back of the van to get a better look. 'Uh-oh' was never good, and it must have been obviously bad if Caravan noticed it. The kid was tall, and a little lanky, with short-cropped light brown hair. He had a little muscle to him, but didn't really look all that tough...which came as a bit of a shock, honestly. It was tough to tell right away, but his face did match the picture they'd received from the German. Yeah...that was him, all right. No cause for concern there. The girl hanging off his arm? That was another matter. 'Shit!' he whispered, and craned his head so that he could get a better look. He managed to spot Jimmy, who had (fortunately) already lifted the ski mask so that it no longer covered his face. The junior member of the Crusade was already approaching the couple, which meant all Jeremiah could do was to open the back doors to the van and hope for the best. 'Excuse me, folks,' he heard Neophyte say in his best 'friendly' voice. 'Would you be interested in learning more about our Lord and Savior?' 'Screw off, pal,' the young man barked, and tried to walk past. Neophyte backpedaled to keep pace, still offering his pamphlet to the young couple.' The girl gave her friend a semi-playful elbow and a sharp reprimand. 'That's not very nice, Colt.' 'Well, I had a hard enough time staying awake back when I actually went to church. I don't need to hear about it where I live, too.' He tried to shoulder his way past Jimmy, but couldn't quite get past. 'Sir, I assure you that the Church of Decency is a progressive, forward-thinking entity, dedicated to applying the Lord's teachings to modern life. If you would like to take this pamphlet, you can learn all about--' Just then, all three of them came into full view of the van. That was the cue. 'Honey,' the girl said, 'go ahead and take the pamphlet. What's the harm in--HEY!' Quick as a flash, the Luchadors For Jesus had grabbed both of them, pulling them apart. Angel Verde hauled the girl to the ground, while Illuminado was trying to drag the boy--Colt--to the van. He wasn't having much luck though, as his early efforts drew a sharp elbow to the face. 'You get your goddamn hands off of her!' Colt shouted, desperately fighting out of Illuminado's grasp. 'Shit,' Jeremiah muttered, and pulled his mask down over his face. 'Let's move.' He and Caravan hopped out of the back of the vehicle and grabbed on to the young man's arms. The girl screamed, 'Colt! Look out!' Before he could protect himself, though, the two big men were already dragging him away. 'MELISSA!' 'COLT!' Underneath his mask, Jeremiah rolled his eyes. Can't take much more of this soap opera bullshit, he thought. They threw the boy into the back of the van, as Illuminado, Caravan, and Neophyte piled in after him. Angel Verde gave Melissa another shove to the ground, and followed. Jeremiah started to follow suit, but turned around to face the girl. She was scared nearly out of her mind, but was already starting to inch her hand toward her purse. 'I wouldn't do that,' he growled. 'We hear so much as one siren tonight, and you'll never see your loverboy alive again. Got it?' She nodded weakly, leaving Jeremiah satisfied enough to head back for the vehicle. As he crawled through the back, Angel started up the engine and tore off across the lawn of the complex. There was a jolt as the van dropped off the curb, but after that it was smooth sailing. Jeremiah turned to look at their captive, buried under the mountain of flesh that was Caravan. Illuminado and Neophyte helped pin the kid to the floor, allowing Caravan to clamp a big, meaty hand over the captive's mouth. Just for a moment, Jeremiah's eyes locked with those of the boy. There was fear there, and some confusion, along with a bit of pleading...but mostly, there was anger. A rage that turned his blue eyes hard, cold, and piercing. Caravan couldn't have knocked him out fast enough. 'Yep,' Jeremiah said to nobody in particular. 'Straight to hell.' # # # Was he getting through to them? He couldn't tell. He hoped...he prayed that he was. 'To this day, people continue to use this same infernal logic to justify their sins. From the first fallen angel to the man who claims to be one, they disguise their selfishness as independence; their weak wills as rebellion. But they continuously overlook one important point.' 'There are no masters in hell.' 'Every single soul in history, saved or damned, pledges its allegiance to something. Those who possess true wisdom pledge their wills to God. 'Satan's fall was not caused by a rebellion, but by subjugation. So beholden was he to his pride, that he had no choice but to make war with the Almighty. Later, he would become the ruler of hell...but if he possess power there, then why does he stay? He stays because he cannot, he dares not disobey his pride.' Wyatt took a quick look over the crowd, taking note of some of the faces. Some of them looked interested...riveted, even. Others had a glazed-over look. Seth's friend--Abby something--looked positively horrified. But here and there, a few tiny, barely perceptible nods. Some of them got it. They understood. 'It is the same with this false Angel. He is a man of countless vices, and refuses to give them up, even though they destroy him. Alcohol has poisoned his organs, yet his gluttony will not allow him to give it up. Cigarettes will, quite literally, be the death of him, and yet he continues to smoke them out of pride. Wrath has twisted his mind to the point where he no longer notices the pain he causes himself, or those who love him. He has fallen, much like Satan...yet never was he cast out of heaven. This man chose to fall. And now, he is little more than an animal, controlled not by his intellect, but by his urges.' 'That is what happens to his kind...it is what might happen to any of us, but for His grace. They follow the earthly rather than the heavenly, the base rather than the divine. In doing so, they lead themselves astray...and into eternal bondage.' His confidence grew as more members of the flocked properly received his message. Doubt tried to crawl its way back up, but it was quickly washed down by divine providence and red Kool-Aid. 'Every damned soul is subservient to their own animal desires. They turned themselves away from the Lord for the sake of carnal pleasures. And though they follow their pride, or their lust, or their wrath...make no mistake, they are no more masters of themselves than we are of the Almighty.' 'The question is not 'rule or serve,' as some would have you believe. We are servants, all of us. The only difference is between our chosen masters, and the nature of our service. There will come a day of judgment. On that day, those of us who devoted ourselves to Him will be rewarded, while those who followed themselves will be punished.' 'The real question, then, is rather simple.' 'Would you be a servant...or a slave?' 'I want you all to know that this man who has chosen to debase himself at all turns...I think of him as I do of all sinners. I wish to show him how to break the bonds of sin. He cannot do this by himself--in fact, struggling in solitude will only strengthen the chains. He must give over control willingly; he must devote himself completely to his holy master. Only then will he truly become free. Can you see that?' A small murmur of affirmation. A few nods, a few scattered calls. 'Will you help me show him the way? Will you lend me your strength, just like He has done for all of us?' 'Yes!' came the answer. This was more familiar territory for them. 'Together, we can take his soul out of his hands, and put it in the Lord's! Will you help me?' 'YES!' 'Save him from sin!' 'SAVE HIM!' 'Save him from slavery!' 'SAVE HIM!' 'SAVE HIM FROM...' 'HIMSELF!' they answered, all with one voice. 'Hallelujah!' he cried, and they responded in kind; their praise like the thunder that followed the lightning of his sermons. The hall erupted in shouts of rapture, which continued long into the night. Connors watched their jubilation, and tears of joy streamed down his face. They understood. They were united in purpose. They would not fail. 'Thank You,' he whispered in prayer. 'Thank You for them...for all of them.' His chosen few were not there to join in the celebration, of course. They had been charged with a sacred quest...and by this time, those brave few had already succeeded or failed. As for the rest of the congregation...Wyatt could never tell them what the mission was, but he had done his best to let them know that it was right. No, not right. Necessary.
