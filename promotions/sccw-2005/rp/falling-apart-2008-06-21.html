Falling Apart

21 June 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-21-2008 In the pro wrestling arena, he is known as Jay Draven. One of the surliest men ever to walk the halls of any arena. His career has been full of triumph and heartbreak. Success, and failure. But he was no longer in the wrestling arena. Instead of staring across the ring at an adversary, he was staring across a table at a lawyer. In this room, Jay Draven didn’t exist. Instead, he was Jason Ryan. A father, and soon to be an ex-husband. "Now, Mr. Ryan, I want you to know that after taking a look at everything, I don’t see how the judge is going to return a favorable verdict for you. You have some tough claims coming against you from Mrs. Ryan." Jason stared right at the lawyer. "She wants me to give up the life that I’ve lived most of my adult life. A life that brought me into her world." "Mr. Ryan, she’s claiming irreconcilibe differences. Something that I’d be hard pressed to dispute considering the fact that this is only the second time I’ve seen you since you hired me as your lawyer." "What do you want me to do, Mr. Corliss? Hide while my life falls apart? I don’t think so. I can’t go to my boss and say, ‘Smitty, my life is in the crapper, can I take a vacation?’ The business I’m in doesn’t work that way." The lawyer that Jason had retained had never dealt with a professional wrestler before, so he didn’t know what to think of his client. "Mr. Ryan, I can understand, the pain that you are in. I too have gone through this process." "Don’t patronize me. You can’t understand what it’s like to go through this a third time." "No, but I have been through a divorce." "Tell me something, how many times have you been married? I’ve been married three times to two women. Twice, I’ve gone through the ringer. I don’t want to go through that again. You got me?" "Well…." "I know that because of what I do for a living, I will not be able to get joint custody of our daughter. I understand that no matter what choice I make, that it’s going to be the wrong one." "That’s not a good way to look at it." "You tell me then, what is a good way to look at it? If I don’t wrestle, I am not true to who I am. I am a wrestler, simple as that. I tried to be a manager, I tried to stay retired. Neither one of them brought me any sense of myself. "But in order for me to be myself, I have to give up my marriage." Jason doesn’t want to hear any more from the lawyer and storms out of his office. What he doesn’t notice as he exits the building are the set of brown eyes that are watching him.
