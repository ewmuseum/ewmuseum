Why? Part Four: True Intent

3 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/12/2007 DEFIANCE! You see the word graphically superimpose itself upon the widescreen TV which the rather upscale drinking establishment you find yourself occupying has hanging prominently upon the wall directly behind the bar, numerous liquor bottles flanking it neatly. You see the hype commercial for the upcoming FUSE pay per view promptly follow and you allow the briefest of grins to creep cross your face and don‘t even try to hide it. The title seems apt, perhaps to you more than others. You betrayed your friends, your fans and then you joined forces with Clinton Sage; a man once considered your bitter enemy and greatest rival. You became the Scourge to all of FUSE. You did this, defiantly, and you did it because it was what you had to do, or at least, that’s what you tell yourself. The truth of the matter is more convoluted than this and you don’t much care to unravel its complexities in the least. The TV diverts away from the hype piece and refocuses itself upon the Affliction and their illustrious history within the organisation they’ve made their own. You bear witness to everything they’ve accomplished and you find yourself admiring their undeniably substantial achievements, if not their dubious tactics. And that’s why people such as Ethan Frost still ask you the question that has plagued the locker room and the journalists alike for the better part of a month. They do not, they cannot, understand you or what you‘ve done. Clichéd as it sounds, for them the world is usually easily definable, everything is black and white, good and evil. But not this time. You finish the whiskey you’ve been nursing for all of three minutes and order another. The alcohol doesn’t entirely absolve you of your problems, but it damn sure helps. Then, you feel a gentle hand caress the back of your neck and shoulder and smell the sweet aroma of Jasmine that to you become synonymous with only one person: Caroline. "Could you turn that off please, bartender?" Caroline asks, taking the barstool next to you. The bartender looks at you questioningly, looking for you to give him the okay to proceed. You’ve been here drinking and throwing your money away for the majority of the day and evidently he feels somewhat loyal to you. With little hesitation you nod and the bartender obliges. You stare at her for the longest time in silence and you realise you could do it all day with little chance of boredom ever being an issue. Her beauty astounds you and for the hundredth time in as many seconds you wonder just what she see’s in you. After all, you’re nothing but an alcoholic wrestler who’s constantly on the road and maintains a consistent penchant for depression, doom and gloom. You realise that no other woman would’ve endured some of the shit you’ve put Caroline through for even half the time you’ve had together and you get a little more depressed. You’re the scum of the Earth and everyone but Caroline can see it. You take another mouthful of whiskey and allow the alcohol to numb the pain some. But it doesn’t help all that much. It’s not long before Caroline feels your eyes burning into the side of her head and she turns to you and establishes eye contact, that same thin-lipped and graceful smile populating her face which warms you more than the liquor ever could. "See, despite my alleged dubious actions I still command respect…" You tell her, allowing the sentence to be somewhat open ended. It’s supposed to be your feeble attempt at humour, but as Caroline’s smile evaporates within the blink of the eye, you realise she’s in no mood for crappy jokes. "You’re mistaking respect for greed, Jacob," she tells you. "With you sitting here propping up the bar, he’s liable to double his daily profits within the hour." You glare at your whiskey and nod, but what else can you do? As usual, she’s right. "What can I get you ma’am?" The bartender asks, sensing a lull in the conversation. "Just water, thanks," she responds. You finish your drink and hold the glass up for the bartender to take away and refill, which he does without even posing a question. "Is that why you did it, Jacob?" Caroline suddenly questions, taking you by surprise. So much so, you pretend not to know what she’s taking about. You have know idea why you do this. "For the respect?" Your whiskey and Caroline’s water arrives. You don’t waste any time getting to work on it. Out of the corner of your eye, you see the disgust spread itself across Caroline’s face and although you feel bad about it, you do not stop. You can’t. Not anymore. "Did what?" You play dumb for a little bit longer, the ignorance is bliss. Caroline rolls her eyes. "Joined the Affliction! Did you do it because you thought people would respect you?" You shake your head and allow a brief chuckle, unsure how much of the merriment is due to the alcohol. "Hell no." Caroline sighs. "Well, at least you’re not deluded as well as drunk!" "I wouldn’t be so sure," you reply, taking another sip of the bitter alcoholic beverage. "After all, there was a point in my life where I thought I deserved you, I thought that I was capable of changing for you…" Her sweet smile returns and she places a gentle hand upon your forearm. "I know that’s your idea of romance, Jacob, but you’re changing the subject." She’s too intelligent for you to simply divert with mere kind words alone and you really should’ve known that. "If you didn’t do it out of respect," she begins, "then why did you do it at all?" You sigh. If you tell her the truth, you’ll hurt her, perhaps even lose her. If you lie to her and she can tell that you’re lying to her, then you’ll lose her for certain. But there is no hesitation for you. She deserves the truth at least and there’s nothing to gain by lying. You turn away, too ashamed to maintain eye contact with her. "I did it because of Mary?" "Mary?" She repeats, a little shocked. "You’re late wife and my sister?" You nod. "Yeah." Confusion saturates Caroline’s face. "I don’t understand, what does she have to do with this?" "The methods in that the Affliction utilise are questionable at best, I think you‘ll agree," you tell her, "but you can’t argue with their results." "Results?" She repeats, clearly taken back. "You’re doing this simply to win? You’re doing this for the glory? This is madness, Jacob! This isn‘t you!" "This is me!" You reply. "And it always has been!" Caroline rolls her eyes. "The promise you made to Mary on her death bed? Jesus Christ, Jacob I thought we’d been over this already! Yes, she made you promise on her deathbed to go on and succeed in the business you love, but we both know she only did it to make sure you carried on with your life whilst she was gone! You must see that!" You don’t answer, because you can’t. You don’t know how to respond to her. Caroline has always had such high expectations of you, but you had no idea her expectations were this high. "Perhaps," you reluctantly concede, but add, defiant to the end, "but I’m going to keep my promise regardless." "And you’re going to do it like this?" She asks. "Throwing in with a bunch of thugs and Neanderthals?" You finish the rest of your whiskey in one mouthful and concentrate on the pain as the liquor burns the inside of your mouth. "Right about now, seems like the only way to me." "The only way? Are you serious? You can‘t tell me this is what you truly want, Jacob…" "It’s not," you tell her, "but this is far from a perfect world." She shakes her head in frustration, all her arguments spent. Almost. "So you will keep your promise? You will do all you can to succeed in the wrestling industry, even if that means selling your soul to the devil, but will you not honour the true intent of the promise? You will not live your life as Mary truly wanted you to?" You stare at your empty glass and the bartender quickly reaches to scoop it up, but you grab his hand firmly and shake your head. He leaves the glass be and you turn your attention back towards Caroline. "I didn’t say that," you tell her, dismounting your stool. "I just gotta do one thing at a time, is all." Caroline nods with acceptance, if not with understanding. "I won’t be here waiting for you when you’re ready, you know…" "Perhaps," you tell her with smile, "but I doubt it." You leave.
