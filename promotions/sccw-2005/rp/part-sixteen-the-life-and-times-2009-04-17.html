Part Sixteen: The Life and Times

17 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/17/09 There have been many attempts throughout history, in both the highest of prose and the crudest, most stumbling attempts at literature, to compare the life of man to the telling of a story. To compare the peaks and valleys of a man’s existence to the twists and turns of a novel. That speak of us all being the main characters in our own novels, filling out the chapters of our lives until our tale reaches its end. But, however poetically stated it may be, there is one major problem with that idea. Real life bears very little resemblance to fiction. It’s certainly nowhere near as neat and tidy. The villains often ride off into the sunset, the heroes often suffer and stories do not often end happily ever after. We demand that our stories be tidy, that they follow certain conventions, that they not allow anything to happen that questions our ability to believe in them, that throws us out of their cozy fiction. Life is random. It is messy. There are no heavily foreshadowed plot twists, no last page saves, few victories snatched from the jaws of defeat. Sometimes, it is all we can do to make it from one page to the next. And we never see the ending coming. It is five weeks after the funeral. Five weeks since life as he knew it ended, five weeks since he ceased understanding how his body continued to live at all. Five weeks in which an aching, hollow despair had turned into a gaping numbness in his soul, an ache that felt as if it was consuming him by degrees. He knew that he was wallowing in his despair, knew that he was acting in a way that would have infuriated her. She would have been shouting at him, angry at him for allowing her absence from his life to make him so miserable. He didn’t care. From that first moment he had seen her on the beach, he had known…known that she would be the one he would spend the rest of his life with. The one he would marry. The one who would be the mother of his children. The one he would grow old with, establishing their own branch in his family’s large and complicated family tree. And now…now that story had ended too soon, the final chapter coming too quickly. This was not how things were supposed to end, this was not how their lives together were meant to finish. They were meant to see Zach become a husband and father himself, meant to spoil their grandchildren rotten. Not this. Her presence at the house that day did not surprise him. In truth, he had been anticipating (or, to be more accurate, dreading) this moment for every minute of the past five weeks. She had been at the funeral, of course, making sure that he was aware of her presence but going no further. That had not surprised him…she had been a part of their lives for too long for him not to know at least a few things about her. Not least of which was the fact that she knew very well where the line was in any given situation …and when stepping over it was not the wisest of decisions. Now she stood on his doorstep, bearing a brown paper bag in her hand. He had never told her where he lived but it didn’t surprise him that she knew. Even though she no longer favored the white pantsuit that had been her signature wardrobe choice, even though she was no longer the woman feared and loathed throughout the sport he had once called his profession…she had spend too much time, too much effort cultivating her mystique for it to ever truly leave her. "Alexandra." He greets her at the front doorstep, dressed sloppily in a T-shirt and sweat pants. It has been two days since he last left the house. "Hello, Lance. May I come in?" "What are you doing here?" It has been years since he has thought of this woman as Desade. But some scars never truly leave you. "I’m here to pay my respects." "You had your chance to do that at the funeral. And please don't act surprised, I knew you were there." "I’ve also brought you and Zach a few things." She holds up the brown paper bag, bearing the Zabar’s logo, up for inspection. Before, he would question her presence here, her motives for coming, what weapons she might bear in such innocent looking packaging. Today, though, he lacks the energy to fight. "Come in," he says, sweeping his massive arm as he steps aside to let her in. She automatically heads to the left, making her way to the kitchen where she places the bag down on the counter top. It is then that she allows herself to truly take a look at him. The tired, red-rimmed eyes, dark circles prominent beneath them. The slight growth of stubble on his face, the wrinkled clothing. She bites her tongue. "Where’s Zachary?" "In his room, taking a nap. I think it’s the first decent sleep he’s had since that day." "How are you doing?" A scowl crawls into place upon his features. "Look, Alex, let’s not pretend that we’re friends. I don’t know why the hell you came but you can let yourself out." He starts to walk away, irritated. Her voice stops him. "I hear Zachary’s quite the athlete, under consideration from a number of top schools." "He knows he has to get the grades, too. Sports can only take you so far." "Perhaps I could pull a few strings, whisper in a few ears." She should know better than this. "No." "Excuse me?" "I said No, Alex. I wanted nothing to do with you while Alanna was alive and I will be damned if I allow my son to…" "Dad, stop." Zachary Marshall pads slowly into the kitchen, bare feet slapping against the hardwood floor. He stands tall, handsome and strong, the best of his mother and father made flesh. He walks over to Alexandra Pierce, placing his arms around her and giving her a kiss on the cheek. Many of the people who knew the woman Alexandra Pierce used to be would have had a stroke on seeing her return the gesture. "Hello, Aunt Alex." (As descriptions go, "aunt" is woefully inaccurate. But as the truth lies somewhere between miniseries and "how much time have you got", "aunt" will suffice for now.) "Hello, Zachary," Alex answers as the two disengage. "How are you?" "Okay. Dealing. Dad’s been cool about things," Zach responded. "Not that it’s any of your business." "Dad, please. " "I thought you were taking a nap." "Couldn’t sleep," Zach answered. "Thought I’d grab something to eat. I came in just in time to hear you guys arguing." "I’m sorry, Zach. Alex was just leaving." "I was just telling your father," Alex retorted, "that if there was anything I could do for you, any strings I could pull…" "NO, Alex!" "Dad, come on," Zach responded. "Everyone else is pulling every trick in the book to get into a good college. If Aunt Alex can talk to someone, maybe help me out a little, why not? It’s not like she’s going to keep me there, right? I have to do that on my own." A smile crossed the face of Alexandra Pierce. "You truly are your parent’s child, Zachary." "Thanks. I’ll take that as a compliment." "It was meant as one." "Thanks again for coming, Aunt Alex. Give Quinn my love, okay? And give Amy my best." Zach ignores the sour expression coming from his father as does Alex. "Thank you, Zachary. I will." It is the day of the funeral. While Lance Marshall will be able to tell you that this day occurred (and he will have plenty of physical evidence of that), he will not be able to hold on to one single memory of the day. Future attempts to recollect this day will find nothing more than a patch of his mind filled with a grey haze, a thick sea in which memories and recollections sink to a murky death. Perhaps it’s for the best. The service is packed, far more so than Lance even anticipated. Every member of their respective families that could attend has done so, all offering Lance and Zach their words of comfort and support in between their own outpourings of grief. Lance marvels at Zach’s strength and poise as his son warmly greets every mourner with a smug, a kiss, or a handshake. Lance is vaguely aware of himself talking and interacting with people but as for specifics…they elude him. The idea of their former co-workers showing up to pay their respects does not surprise him…the sheer number of them that appear, however, stuns him. Men and women that he has not seen for years come to pay their respects. Jonathan and Katie Rhine come to share their grief. Wyatt Connors expresses his sorrow at Lance’s loss and even appears to mean it. Kathryn Shaw is, of course, present, looking devastated. And so, Lance notices, is Alexandra Pierce, standing towards the rear and flanked by Roderick Ashe. Lance notes that the rest of the Hand is absent this day as is Amy Campbell…and sends up a prayer for small mercies. They speak of her for hours. Of her strength and tenacity. Of her fearlessness. Of her tenderness and good heart. Of how she loved her husband and son more than anything in the world. Of how much they all loved her, respected her, admired her, even feared her. Of the mark Alanna Marshall left on the world and how she will never be forgotten. Then Lance, Zach, her brothers and Lance’s two eldest brothers carry the coffin to the burial plot, watching silently as Alanna’s body is lowered into the ground. One by one, they offer Lance their condolences and place flowers into the grave. One by one, they all disappear as they day marches slowly on, headed either towards the Marshall home or to their own homes. Soon, only a small group remains. Still, Lance remains rooted to the same spot he’s been in four hours, staring downwards towards the grave. Zach pauses at the limousine door, staring at his father with a look on infinite tenderness in his eyes. He knows how much today is killing his dad, knows that no matter how bad he feels his father feels infinitely worse. He turns towards the rest of the party gathered around the limo. "I gotta go get, Dad. You guys go on ahead, okay?" Kia Kupalo steps up, taking Zach’s hand in hers. "Let me talk to him, okay?" Zach just nods, wondering what memories this day must be conjuring in Kia’s mind. "Sure." A small breeze blows around her as she walks towards Lance and Kia feels the chill in their air that signals rain is on its way. She continues her slow pace towards Lance, not wanting to push him into anything. She just takes a place next to him, placing her hand in his and standing there quietly for a few moments. When Lance finally speaks, his voice is thick and hoarse from crying. "How did you it, Kia?" "What?" Kia asks. She knows what Lance means…but she also knows he needs to ask it himself. "When Edward died…how did you go on? "I wasn’t sure I could at first. I wished I was dead for…oh, probably a good solid week after the funeral." "What changed?" "I realized I had to be there for our son. That I couldn’t afford to let myself wallow in my grief and anger forever, that I needed to keep on going." Kia turns her face to Lance. "Edward didn’t want me to spend the rest of my life miserable. And I know that Alanna wouldn’t want that for you. So, we do what we have to. We get up, put one foot in front of the other, and go on living. Because there's nothing else we can do. Because we’re survivors. And that’s what we do." Lance Marshall outweighs Kia Kupalo by a ridiculous amount and is considerably taller than Edward Lambert’s widow. But at the moment, as the rain began to fall slowly onto the cemetery grounds, it was the larger man who looked oh so small. It is the final day of Alanna Marshall’s life. She knows this with a certainty that she has never felt before in her life. She has fought against the disease claiming her body as long and as hard as she can…but there is no longer any fight left in her. She knows this and she is unafraid. She looks at the two men seated at her bedside, who have stayed with her here for the past few months. At the strong, solid face of her husband crumpled in pain and fear at the thought of her death. At the handsome young man that she will always think of as her little boy, crying and begging his mommy to hold on. She knows they will hurt. She knows there will be pain and for that she is so very sorry. But she knows that her memory will not always be painful and that, with time, they will remember her with smiles and laughter. Most importantly, she knows that she has done everything she could for these two men. She loves them both and they her. With that final thought, Alanna Marshall dies. It is the day everything begins. "I’m so sorry, Mrs. Marshall. The test results came back and they were positive." For we rarely get the ending we deserve. We now return you to your regularly scheduled lives, already in progress.
