Date: Various Voicemail received Monday, September 21 at 11:43am Hey, Cal’. It’s Jared. Umm, just wanted to say hi and see how you were doing. Give me a call when you get this. I’ll be around. Talk to you later. It was the first time that Jared was making a pay-per-view appearance since March, and to celebrate the occasion Darren Stracker had decided to order the show for his students, gathering everyone together in the lounge of the school he operated. It was a fairly small room, filled with hand-me-down furniture in various states of wear. The walls were adorned with photos of students past, some black and white, some color, all framed. Nothing matched, but that’s what made the room comfortable. The usual crowd had gathered. Vinnie, normally loud and beyond obnoxious, had mercifully stuffed something in his mouth to prevent ridiculous outbursts. They watched as the opening credits ran, transitioned to a well-produced video package, and then transitioned immediately into the opening contest. It was only when Jason Myers made it halfway through his introductions and his voice was accompanied by the sound of a guitar did Justine Calvin decide to quietly and discretely rise from her seat, sliding out into the hallway. The sweatshirt she wore, a hooded Boston Red Sox number, didn’t belong to her. She told herself she wore it because it was big and baggy on her small frame, and that it was more comfortable than anything else she owned. In truth it was the scent it carried, the scent of its owner, that drew her to it and made her feel at home in it. When she took her seat on the edge of the ring at the heart of the gym she pulled the fabric closer to her, closing her eyes breathing deep. Darren had watched as she’d slid out of the room having expected her to return fairly quickly. It was only when she didn’t that he took note of who was on the television. That’s when he decided to politely excuse himself and track her down, figuring to be a buffer of sorts. He found her in the gym, away from the crowd and with her eyes gazing listlessly at the floor. "Hey. Is everything okay? Why aren’t you inside with us watching the show?" "It’s nothing. Just needed a break for a second." Her smile was weak, but she’d been around Darren Stracker long enough to know that it wasn’t going to fool him. "You know how those asses can get." "Maybe, but those asses haven’t said anything, and I couldn’t help but notice when you left." It was all the confirmation she needed. Darren wasn’t buying it. "There still something going with you two? I thought you got past all that." "Don’t worry about it, D. It’s nothing. It’ll work itself out eventually." He sighed as he stepped to where she sat, taking a seat next to her on the edge of the ring. She turned her head in his direction, smiling again. She hoped it would be all the reassurance he needed to leave her alone, but unfortunately for Justine Darren wouldn’t have any of it. "Well," he said, grinning. "Maybe you two should just screw and get it over with." Justine quickly vaulted from her seat and pulled the hood of the sweatshirt over her face. Behind her makeshift mask her cheeks had darkened, taking on the color of the shirt she now used to hide them. The realization of what he’d just said struck Darren like a gunshot. Immediately his own cheeks flushed and his mouth went suddenly dry. Darren Stracker did not do "embarrassed" particularly well. His first stammered words were closer to grunts and groans than coherent thoughts. "I… uhh… I didn’t… wow. I mean… wow." She didn’t say anything, and with nothing to play off of he was left to fend for himself. Apologies seemed the best place to start. "Holy shit, kid. I didn’t know." Whatever shock she wouldn’t read on his face she could certainly pick up in his voice. "Honest-to-God, I had no idea. He never said anything. I mean, not that he would, but… I’m really sorry. That was a dumb thing for me to say." "It was the second time." Her tone was quiet, and though she kept her back to him Darren picked up on it nonetheless. "Really, I had no idea. I wouldn’t have said anything if I’d known." "It’s fine." "I may be overstepping my boundaries here, and please, tell me if I am, but," he paused, waiting to see if she would cut him off before he could continue. Only when she answered with silence did Darren decide to proceed. "It doesn’t look fine from where I’m sitting." "Yeah, I’m a horrible liar." "Come on, that’s not what I meant. If everything was fine then we wouldn’t be having this conversation, and I wouldn’t be apologizing for putting my foot in my mouth a minute ago." She flipped the hood of the sweatshirt back, planted her hands firmly on her hips, and turned to face him. Some of the color had left her complexion, but enough remained to indicate that she was still at least mildly embarrassed by the situation. "You seriously had no idea? None whatsoever?" "No, not at all." He paused again, bobbing his head lightly as he weighed the potential consequences of his next statement. "Well, I mean, there were whispers about it. Nothing recent, though. Mostly it was when you two were students here." Slowly she stepped back towards the ring and leaned against the nearest post. She still wouldn’t make eye contact. "Yeah, it happened then, too. This was the second time," she repeated, louder this time. "And then you two had a falling-out." Darren drew a coarse hand the length of his face, stopping at his goatee to scratch. "Or is that why you had issues?" "Might have had something to do with it, yeah." "I’m assuming it was on him, given that you punched him in the head when you saw him a few months ago." "Actually," she said, finally raising her eyes to meet his. "I punched him because I knew he was around and I thought he was ducking me. But to be honest, he kind of had every right to. I was sort of a bitch about it back then." "Mind if I ask what happened? And again, stop me if I’m out of line here." "No, it’s fine." She pushed away from the ring post and once again took a seat on the edge of the ring. "It’s weird to talk about, is all." She was about to continue when from the lounge came the obnoxious voice of Vinnie, whose last name Justine still could not remember. "Oh my God, his ass is so pale!" he shouted, temporarily drawing the attention of both Justine and Darren away from their conversation. "I’m afraid to know what that means," Darren said, finding it hard to suppress a chuckle. "Yep." "I’m sure we’ll find out eventually, whether we want to or not." The "hrmph" she offered was about as close to a laugh as Darren expected to get, but it was still better than what he’d seen thus far. It took her a minute to refocus, having been thrown so wonderfully out of whack by Vinnie’s earsplitting outburst. "I didn’t handle things very well the first time," she continued, hopeful that she wouldn’t be interrupted again. "I kind of disappeared on him, and by the time I was ready to try and deal with things he was gone. I still owe him an apology for that." "Nah, that was a long time ago. You were both kids." "No, I kind of lied to you a minute ago. When I smacked him a few months ago it wasn’t just because I knew he was ducking me. I think part of it was me being pissed at me and using him as a punching bag. Wasn’t very fair of me." "Honestly? He probably doesn’t care. Something like that isn’t going to get to him, not for very long anyway." "Yeah, but then I got drunk that night with my brother, took a cab to Jar… to his house, and threw up in the hall." "Ah." "Yeah." She’d broken eye contact again, and from what Darren could tell seemed to be fixated on a dark spot on the wall about three feet up from the floor on the far side. "What are you doing to deal with it this time?" he prodded, more confident in testing the waters now that she’d started to open up. "Aside from spending quality time with your friend-slash-boss to vent about it, of course." "Not much different, to be honest. He’s called a few times, but I never pick up. Can’t even play the messages." She dug her hands into the shirt’s single pocket and leaned back against the ropes. A bit surprised with herself for being so open with this, Justine simply shook her head and sighed. "It was different this time. It started with a fight, you know. Like, I’d been giving him a hard time, just sort of joking around, but he seemed to take it really hard. He called me out on it, which is why I’m not so sure about what you said. I think it did get to him. We each said some things, really harsh things, and then just… He was trying to leave, and I stopped him." The silence between them was thick, broken only by a raucous cheer erupting from the lounge. Both heads turned in the direction of the door as Vinnie burst through, waving his left arm triumphantly. "Woo, new champion! Little guy gets it, bare ass and all!" he shouted, taking a large step towards Justine and Darren. "Vinnie!" Darren barked, bringing the young man to a screeching halt. "Yeah boss?" "Back inside." "Yeah boss." Dejected, Vinnie immediately turned and headed back into the lounge. "I’m seriously going to kill that guy one day," Justine added weakly. Darren grinned as he rubbed his hands together. "I’m going to be honest with you here. The two of you, you and Jared, you’re as close to family to me as anyone I’m actually related to. I give you both a hard time, but it’s because I look out for you, and I want you to be happy. I’m not going to lecture you, or tell you what you need to do, because it’s not my life and it’s not my decision." "But?" "Come on, kiddo," he said, rising to his feet. "You already know the rest. Match is over. I think it’s safe for you to come back inside now." Darren grinned over his shoulder as he started his walk back to the lounge. It took her a minute to motivate herself, but Justine wasn’t far behind. She managed to sit through the rest of the show, even going so far as to laugh once or twice during a lively if not unusual game of Rochambeau. She made a mental note when she saw him speak to Jonathan, wondering if his intent to fix things was somehow directed at her. And though she’d never admit it, when she saw him speaking to that woman near the show’s end something turned in her stomach, green-eyed and envious. She still wouldn’t play his voice messages. Voicemail received Friday, September 25 at 4:17pm Hey, it’s me again. Sorry to be a pain in the ass. I’m just starting to get a little paranoid, like maybe you’re freaked out about what happened. I’m driving from Cali to Vegas with a car full of lunatics, so… (unintelligible mumbling) Gabe says hi. Just, umm, just give me a call. Whenever. I really want to talk to you about this. "Fuck me, I’ve got a line and a half." Jared Sykes peeked his head through the curtain that separated him from the rest of the Tucson Convention Center during the second day of the inaugural Sin*Con. He quickly drew the curtain shut, thinking he’d been spotted by a nearby group of fans. Larry Spiderman, his agent, had made the flight out from New York to take in the sights for himself. Thus far he was quite impressed by the offering. "You sound surprised," Larry said, sliding his hands into the pockets of his slacks. "I guess." Jared dared another glance into the convention hall, where it seemed the attendees were multiplying by the minute. "More concerned, since if I’m going to be signing autographs with my right hand all day again that means my plans for the evening are shot." Had the sights beyond the curtain not occupied Jared he might have noticed his agent’s pained grimace. "Is this another one of those jokes?" "Yes, Larry. Yes, it is." "Do you think maybe once we can have a meeting without them?" "No, it’s tradition now," the Black Sheep joked, finally stepping away from the curtain. "Like Christmas." Jared paused, seemingly staring off into space. Larry knew that look all too well. It was a thing of fear. "You know, I probably could find a use for multicolored lights." The scowl Larry wore was quickly joined by frantic waving. The mental image his client had managed to conjure was neither pleasant, nor was it something he was likely to forget in the near future. "Alright, no more. Anything else and I’m leaving." "You can’t leave, Larry. I need you to stay close. If today is anything like yesterday with so many people running up and telling me that I should ‘bring back the strawberry’ I’m going to need you here to bail me out of prison." "That’s not exactly something I’d look forward to doing." "No, you’re right." Like Dorothy in Oz Jared simply could not resist the allure of what hid behind the curtain. "’Sides, I’m too pretty for jail." There were days that Larry Spiderman wondered just how much of this was real and how much was a show put on in his honor. After all, his client was the same man who first arrived in his office dressed as a blueberry. Certainly this was not the kind of thing that a man, a fully-grown adult no less, would do simply because they found it amusing. Would they? "Okay, Jared, stop it." Larry did his best to swat his client’s hands away from the curtain, shooing him as far from it as possible. "I didn’t fly out here from New York to watch you play peek-a-boo with a few thousand people. Oh, for the love…" No sooner had Jared been separated from his previous plaything did his hands search out a new one. This time it was in the form of his phone. And while Larry couldn’t see exactly what Jared was looking for, what seemed apparent was that either he couldn’t find it or didn’t like what he saw. "Do I have to take your phone away?" "Will I get it back for good behavior?" "Are you capable of good behavior?" "No sense in it anyway," Jared muttered to himself before handing the device over to his agent. "Here." It was with a fair amount of trepidation that Larry took the phone. Why would he hand it over so quickly? A hundred thoughts raced through his mind. Is it going to shock my hand? Is the cover going to fly off and unleash a half-dozen spring-loaded plastic snakes? Do they even make phones that do that? With a shake of his head the cruelly-named agent took the device and placed it in the breast pocket of his shirt, where it would remain for safe-keeping. "Is everything alright, Jared?" he asked, afraid of what the question might unleash. "You seem a bit more distracted than normal." "I’m sorry. There’s just so much going on here that it’s tough to focus on one thing." It was a lie, but the Black Sheep wasn’t about to admit as much. It was true that his mind was elsewhere. It was not true that his mind was wholly focused on the cavalcade of fans that had flooded the building since the early hours of the morning. "I’ll give you my attention from here on out." "And no more jokes?" "Let’s not push our luck." "Fair enough." "So," Jared said, folding his arms as he leaned against a nearby wall. "What are we talking about?" "Well, for one, somehow you’ve managed to get your hands on another new belt. Congratulations, by the way. I’ve typed up a list of a few radio call-ins that I’d like us to do, there’s even a few local stations on here. Some show called The Morning Sickness with John Holmberg actually wants to bring you in studio, God help them." "Adult contemporary?" "I believe it’s rock station. I could be mistaken." "Sounds swell." "There is also a little matter of – just hold on while I find it." Larry Spiderman was very much entrenched in the old school. He never brought a laptop to meetings like this. It was always a manila folder with printed sheets of paper, each one numbered and indexed. Jared often mused why Larry didn’t have more paper cuts. "Ah! Here it is. Something about a GTT. Do you know what that stands for? Nobody I’ve asked will tell me." Jared simply shrugged. "Okay then. So there’s that, and I also wanted to run tomorrow’s match with Phillip Kennedy by you, so maybe if we could…" It was when Jared rolled his eyes that Larry briefly snapped out of business mode. "What?" "Can we talk about something other than wrestling?" "I-I’m sorry?" "I know it’s your job, and mine too, but it’s pretty much the furthest thing from mind right now. I’m surrounded by it and I don’t even want to think about it." It seemed a strange request, even considering its source, and though he felt he should push the issue Larry Spiderman was sure of one thing: he would not win an argument with this particular breed of stubborn. "What, pray tell, would you like to talk about instead?" "I don’t know. Comic books. The movies. How people around here are so hellbent on telling me that ‘it’s a dry heat’ like it should mean something. The atomic weight of cobalt. Basically, anything but wrestling." Larry pondered the notion for a moment. It may not have been a cause to argue, but that didn’t mean he wouldn’t negotiate. "Okay," he acquiesced. "But on one condition. We’ll discuss whatever it is you want to discuss now, before you have to go out there, but when your time at the table is done we’re getting back to business. I will even give you your phone back." "Fine, deal, whatever. Can I get that phone now, though?" Larry slid his hand into his pocket, retrieving the phone and handing it to his charge. Jared reached out to take it, but before he could close his fingers around it Larry quickly pulled it away. "And if you try to run off or disappear in the crowd I promise I will have you fitted for an ankle bracelet or one of those microchips they put in dogs." It was only under duress that Larry handed the small black device back to his client, curious as to why Jared wanted it so badly, and terrified of what nefarious ends he might use it for. The Black Sheep waited until his agent had strolled off out of sight before he let his smile drop and his façade melt away. He clicked the phone’s scroll wheel to illuminate the display and then frowned at what it showed him. Zero missed calls. Zero new messages. He paced a slow circle, alternately inhaling and exhaling deep with each step. He knew what had to be done, what had to be said, he just dreaded doing it. A few more hours couldn’t hurt. I’m guessing that since I haven’t talked to you in three weeks that I probably shouldn’t get my hopes up about hearing from you again, so… I, umm… (hushed) Fuck, I’m so bad at this… I just… I think I’m really going to miss you.
