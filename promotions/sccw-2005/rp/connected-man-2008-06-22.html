Connected Man

22 June 2008

Date: 6/22/08 The wind was a torrent of darkness among the gusty trees, The moon was a ghostly galleon tossed upon cloudy seas, The road was a ribbon of moonlight over the purple moor, And the highwayman came riding— Riding—riding— The highwayman came riding, up to the old inn-door. ------------------------------ After SIN on SPIKE! He lay on the bed in his hotel room, unwilling to move, unwilling to focus on the television, unwilling, really, to do anything but revel in the memories. 'Good God,' was about the only thing Phillip Kennedy could muster. He would be sore for days, by his current reckoning. For a man who hadn't had sex in longer than he cared to remember, Katsidy was an oasis in the middle of a desert -- if that oasis happened to feature a casino with a five star hotel, three Michelin star dining establishments, and a masseuse. His head was on the pillow, and if he closed his eyes, he could still smell her perfume, clinging to his skin and clothes. He knew that he should probably shower, but relishing the afterglow seemed far more optimal at this point in time. Kennedy had no delusions about what had happened. She was Katsidy, the Sex Kitten of the Dead Man's Hand. She worked for Desade. Sleeping with him had a strategic purpose. And he didn't give a damn. Somehow, his initial plans of engagement with the Hand all went to crap when confronted with a beautiful woman who knew how to dress and act according to a code designed to break him, a code that he didn't even know. Yeah, in retrospect, she'd totally played him. Like a fiddle. Made him dance to her tune. But was it such a bad thing? He had been living in fear of the Dead Man's Hand. Worried of how Alexandra Pierce could saunter into his life and leave it a shambles. And now, he'd finally met a finger of the Hand, and it had slept with him. Sure, maybe it was part of Pierce's plan to hurt him...but why would she? He was a rookie. He was malleable. He was a potential ally, if courted properly. Maybe that's what this way. An attempt to win him over. He could deal with that, he decided. But as he lay in bed, quite enjoying the aftermath of being Kat's ball of yarn for the evening, he had no way of knowing that two weeks later, his saga would continue. And continue with stakes that were, as they often seemed to be in SCCW, raised. ------------------------------ June 19, 2008 'Are you out of your poker playing MIND?' Las Vegas was Phillip Benjamin Kennedy's sanctuary. When not on the road, he found himself more often than not hopping a plane back to Sin City to play some cards, drink some beer, and clear his mind. With him on this particular occassion, watching as Kennedy dove into a buffet plate of macaroni and cheese, mashed potatoes, and barbecued chicken, was one of Mr. Kennedy's numerous poker acquaintances, a fellow young semi professional going by the name of Louie 'The Lip' Lucchiani. Said semi-pro alos happened to be looking at Phil as if a third ear had sprouted out of the middle of his face. 'I really don't know why you're so bent out of shape about this, Lou,' Kennedy proclaimed, chomping away at his chicken. 'You make it seem like I've made the worst decision of my life.' 'You HAVE, you moron! Look at the people she works for! They're not the sort of people you want to be involved with!' Louie, it should be noted, followed Kennedy's career. Probably, Kennedy tended to believe, in order to follow in his footsteps one day. Phillip sighed, putting down his fork to look his friend in the eye. 'Look. You're not working for SCCW. I am. There comes a point where you've got to make decisions. Besides, what was I supposed to say, no? She's smoking hot...not to mention that if I *DID* say no, you think she looks like the kind of person to take no for an answer?' 'Well, no,' Lou had to admit, 'but you just shoulda--' 'Shoulda what? Ran away from her?' Kennedy is quiet, as his friend gives a helpless sort of nod. 'I know you think I'm an idiot, but look. The Dead Man's Hand is a fact of my life now. It's a fact of life for everyone who walks into the Sin City lockerroom. You can't avoid it. For every name I know that works for it, there's a cameraman or an SCCW employee I don't know who also does.' 'So fine then!' Lou all but screamed, throwing his hands in the air. 'Talk to Katsidy! Complement her dress, tell her she's hot, whatever! You SLEPT with Desade's right hand!' 'Actually, Lou, there wasn't too much sleeping going on.' 'Whatever! But God, gotta admit, I really hate you right about now...' Kennedy gave a pleased sort of smile, reaching out to pat his friend on the shoulder. 'I know what I'm doing. You think I don't know why she slept with me? She's keeping me within arm's reach, in case I'm 'needed'. That Pierce woman has a mind like a steel trap. And with the events of last week's show, with the Highwaymen being back, seems like a good strategy, try to tap into some of the new blood.' In that sense, it was all quite simple. Business, plain and simple. Pleasurable business, though. The sounds that woman made when in the throes of ecstasy should have been criminal, by Kennedy's judgment. Louie was still unconvinced. 'Yeah, and so you get called on to go do some business. You're like a connected guy in the Mafia now, Phil! Bottom rung of the ladder, they'll send you out to do all sorts of dumb shit.' Kennedy picked his fork back up, to spear some cheese-covered pasta. 'You look at that as a negative. I look at it as a positive.' Phillip scratched at his five o'clock shadow, prickly from an all-night session of poker at several Strip casinos. ''I'm not a target. The Highwaymen think of the Hand, they think of Savant. Katsidy. Hunter. Desade. They don't think of me. I'm under the Hand's umbrella, but I'm not holding it up.' 'Yeah. That's why Sean Sterling's fighting you this week. Because obviously, it's got nothing to do with the fact that you're a known associate of Desade's merry band of sociopaths.' Kennedy clucks his tongue at his friend, shaking his head and pointing a finger as if lecturing a child. 'Again, you look at it as a negative, I look at it as a positive. I'm absolutely convinced Smitty booked this match for two reasons. One, he wants Sean Sterling in the big match at All-In so that there's a Highwayman there in case the Hand gets in. And two, yes, he wants to 'teach me a lesson', since god knows he can't actually punish the Hand. So he comes after me.' 'I fail to see how anything you just said proves me wrong.' 'Louie, I'VE GOT THREE MATCHES AS A PRO!' Kennedy's voice raises just enough for nearby tables to hear, shrug, and move on. 'Three matches! I've got no business in this qualifier, from any logical measure. So why am I here? I'm here because STD, in his 'infinite wisdom', has decided that Sean Sterling can wipe the mat with me.' 'Um, can't he?' Kennedy, of course, shoots his buddy a deathglare. 'That is unimportant. Fact is, being a connected guy with the Hand's got me a shot at a shot at the top prize in SCCW. Simon King, Tina Davis, King Blueberry...they ain't got that, do they?' 'Well, no, but they're also not going to get their heads taken off by a pissed off Sean Sterling, either.' 'You're a real optimist, Lou, ya know that?' 'Hey, I've busted out of tournaments when I thought for sure I'd win. Poker's got a way of takin' your optimism and flushing it down the crapper.' Kennedy sighs, but reaches into his pocket, pulling out a piece of mail. 'I've got twenty bucks in slot comps from the Venetian, Lou. THink I'm gonna go over there, blow it in ten minutes, then play some seven card stud. You in?' Lou shakes his head. 'Naw man, at dinner break for a 500 buyin pot limit Omaha tourney. Might actually make some damn money.' 'No problem then,' Phil says. 'Make your money. And don't worry about me. I'll be fine.' Lou, of course, doubts this quite seriously, but he shrugs. 'Whatever you say, chief.' 'Listen, Lou. It's just like playing poker. I've got my chips, everyone else in Sin City has theirs. I'm making my reads. Eventually, I'll have to push all my chips into the center. Go all-in. And when I do, you can be damn sure I'll have the nuts.' With a flourish, Kennedy leaves, off to the Venetian for more booze and gambling. It is only once Kennedy is out of earshot that Louie sighs softly to himself. 'Kid's got spunk, I'll give him that much.' A few moments later, he has dug into his pocket, pulling out his cell phone to punch in a series of numbers that he knows by heart. The conversation that he has with the party on the other end will remain lost to the sands of time. Use your imagination. ----------------- 'Back, he spurred like a madman, shrieking a curse to the sky, With the white road smoking behind him and his rapier brandished high! Blood-red were his spurs i' the golden noon; wine-red was his velvet coat, When they shot him down on the highway, Down like a dog on the highway, And he lay in his blood on the highway, with the bunch of lace at his throat.' -- The Highwayman, Alfred Noyes (1906)
