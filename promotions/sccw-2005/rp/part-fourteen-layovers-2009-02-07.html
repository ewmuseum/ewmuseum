Part Fourteen: Layovers

7 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/7/09 There’s a saying: travel broadens the mind. While there is some truth in that, what the saying neglects to do is define just what exactly it means by travel. In fact, there are two very distinct stages of travel. There are all the things that you do once you arrive at your intended destination. When you go and see new places, meet new people, try new things, eat new and interesting foods (or is that just me?) All the things that you spend hours boring people senseless about, sharing vacation photos and stories. Let’s call it the "destination", for short. That is what most people think about when they speak of travel broadening the mind. It’s all the cool stuff, the adventure that comes just from being somewhere different to the place that you call home. What most people don’t tend to mention is the other part of travel. Let’s call this the "journey", for short. The times when you spend hours on your duff on small, uncomfortable plastic chairs waiting to be herded into large metallic tubes with wings…there to spend even more hours sitting and waiting. Flight delays. Loud, screaming kids. Lousy airplane food. Thanking g-d for portable electronic devices. Because the uncomfortable truth of the matter is this: in themselves, airports are some of the most boring places in the world. That’s why your average airport is packed to the gills with shops and restaurants (or, if you happen to hit Vegas, slot machines): to distract you, to allow you to believe that an airport is not all about sitting on your ass with an iPhone and a cup of coffee. Or, if you happen to be one Mister Lance Marshall, an iPhone (currently getting weather updates), a large cup of coffee from Starbucks (steam still coming off the top, Lance marveling at the fact that the Dallas-Fort Worth airport could somehow sustain fifteen separate Starbucks locations), and copies of the newest issues of Q (Lance had never really "got" The Killers and was reasonably sure he never would) and Empire (Christ, The Hobbit wasn’t gonna be out for another year or two!) Lance didn’t tend to make much use of airline first class lounges. He’d never really seen the point of them and, to be honest, he tried to spend as little time at rest (both in airports and in life) as he could get away with. But with the way things had gone so far today, having a little peace and quiet would end up doing his head some good. The feeling had started from the beginning of the day as he gazed out of his hotel room window at the New York skyline. The biting cold had started to creep in around the hotel’s central heating system and the clouds hung dull, heavy and grey in the air. Everything just seemed to be moving incredibly slowly, from the service at breakfast to the hotel’s checkout procedure. Lance quickly found himself feeling incredibly grateful for Steve Jobs’s little inventions. He was fairly certain that music was the only thing that was going to be keeping him sane today. The cab ride to the airport had proved him right. Miraculously, it had been uneventful (we are talking about New York City cabbies, after all) largely because traffic had been moving so damn slowly. It was as if the cold, which seemed to be everywhere (at least according to CNN) was leeching the strength from people’s bones, causing them to addle about in a fog. JFK had been little better. The first flight had ended up being delayed for two hours for unspecified reasons (American Airlines staff showing little interest in actually informing people why they would be stuck at the airport for longer than anticipated). That, in turn, had caused what had been a comfortable amount of time to catch his connection in Dallas to turn into a mad sprint through the airport…only to be informed that his connection was also to be delayed, this time due to "mechanical problems" (with airline staff again failing to be terribly specific). Lance had been told that the flight would be on its way "soon". That had been half an hour ago with what looked like at least another two hours to wait. Picking up the iPhone, he closed the weather alert feature and switched over to the actual phone, dialing the familiar number without even needing to look at the keypad. The phone on the other end answered after two rings. "Hello?" "Hey, babe." "Hey, honey. Where are you?" "DFW. Specifically, the American first class lounge." A sigh. "Shit, not again. How long is it gonna be this time?" "They won’t say. They just keep telling me ‘soon’." "Fucking idiots. But you’re okay, though?" "I’m good. Bored as hell but I’m fine. Hey, is Zach around?" "Yeah, he’s in the other room watching Ben 10. Hang on a sec…" Lance could hear his wife calling out to his son, "Zach, come say hi to Daddy," the rapid patter of feet on hardwood and suddenly there was a breathless voice on the other end of the line going "Hi, Daddy!" "Hey, sport. How are you?" "I’m good, Daddy. I miss you, though." "I know. I’m sorry it’s taking me so long to get home. Tell you what, we’ll do something nice to make up for it when I get home. Something cool." "Like what, daddy?" "Whatever you want, champ." "YAY!" "But you gotta let me know what it is when I get home, okay?" "Okay, daddy." "Can you give the phone back to mommy now?" "Okay. I love you, daddy." "I love you too, champ." A short pause as the phone is handed over, then… "Whatever you said, it did the trick. His smile’s so big it’s about to stretch off his head." Lance smiled himself at that. No matter how bad a day could be going, just knowing Zach was okay to make everything else bearable. "Don’t wait up for me, okay? I don’t know how long this is gonna take." "Okay. Just take care of yourself." "Always. Love you, babe." "I love you too, hon." He’d managed to make the cup of coffee last for a full half hour, idly flicking through the reviews section of Empire while sipping at the steaming beverage. It was halfway through the review of The Spirit that Lance’s train of thought was interrupted. He knew the other person was there before he heard them. In his line of work, being able to feel the physical presence of others was a definite occupational advantage. "Excuse me, sir, is this seat taken?" The accent was thick and unmistakably Texan. The body attached to the voice was gesturing towards the chair across from the one Lance was sitting in. The young man (G-d, thought Lance, how long has it been since I’ve been this guy’s age) was huge, looking about six feet five inches and easily about three hundred and twenty pounds. The youngster’s face broke into a wide grin as Lance looked up from his magazine and Lance couldn’t help thinking that he looked familiar. "Holy fuck! You’re Lance Marshall, ain’t ya?" Lance couldn’t help but smile at the youngster’s enthusiasm. "That’s me. And nope, chair’s not taken. C’mon, sit down, take a load off." The youngster sat down, enthusiastically pumping Lance’s offered hand. "This is so cool. I didn’t think I’d be running into a celebrity today or anything." "Trust me, kid, I’m nothing special." "Come on, I’ve seen all the SCCW shows. You’re really damn impressive in the ring." He added, "Some of my friends say you remind them of me, a little," with that look that adds ‘and I think they’re talking complete bullshit but it’s flattering all the same.’ Lance chuckled slightly. "Very cool. Want an autograph or a picture, prove that you saw me in the flesh?" "Oh, sure!" The youngster fished out a notebook and a pen from the backpack he was carrying, handing them over to Lance. "Just make it out to Jake." And it clicked. A mention on ESPN, a piece on rivals.com…little bits of information stitching themselves together… "You’re Jake Modetsky. First Team Offense, All-American." Of all the things Lance Marshall had managed to do in his lifetime, making Jake Modetsky blush had to rank as one of the most unique. "Yessir." "Hey, be proud of that. That’s something pretty special." "I am, sir. If I remember right, that’s something we have in common, ain’t it?" Lance nodded, memories of his high school days flashing through his mind. "So, lemme guess, campus visit?" "Yes, sir. I’ve had a lot of schools trying to sign me, going down to check out USC for a few days." They sat there for…well, neither of them could say for how long, exactly. They spoke of college, Lance stressing the importance of education but also the need for living a balanced life. Jake told Lance of his plans to balance his football career with a business degree, how he planned to eventually head back home to Odessa and run his own business. They spoke of sports, with Lance sharing a few SCCW backstage stories with Jake that had the young man’s eyes going wide with wonder. They spoke of music, with Jake hipping Lance to a few new bands he wasn’t aware of and Lance doing the same. It was just after Jake had managed to get a passerby to take a picture of the two of them with Jake’s phone that the intercom buzzed into life, calling Jake’s plane for its boarding. Jake turned to Lance, once again vigorously shaking the older man’s hand. "Thanks again for the time, sir. It’s been great." "No problem. Knock ‘em dead at USC, man." "I will, sir." And as Jake walked off, backpack slung over his shoulder, he turned his to Lance and said "You know, sir, some of us know who the real champ in SCCW is." With that, he was gone, making his way towards his gate. Lance sat back down, the smile lingering on his face. Yeah, sometimes things did end up working out for the best... We now return you to your regularly scheduled lives, already in progress.
