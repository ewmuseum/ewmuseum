How Jason O'Neil Says Goodbye.

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Today --I-- I said, you're going to betray me tonight, aren't you? She said nothing. I said, you're going to betray me. I know it. After everything I gave you, you're going to forget it, aren't you? She said nothing. I said, you're going to forget. I know it. After all I've done for you, you're going to say goodbye, aren't you? She said nothing. I said, you're going to say goodbye. I know it. It doesn't matter if it's Malachi, Clinton Sage, or Joshua Kosidlo, does it? She said nothing. I said, yeah, it doesn't matter who it is. I know it. You have disaster in mind for me, don't you? She said nothing. I said, yes, you must. I know it. You're going to make me blow out my knee tonight, aren't you? She said nothing, because fate is silent. --II-- I stared at her for a long, long time. I hadn't seen her in days, but, there she was. Her supple curves, her unwavering heart. She seemed to smile at me as she moved in the slight breeze, shaking subtly, swaying hypnotically. I smiled at her, and approached. She saw me, immediately. 'This... this is goodbye, I think,' I said as I put my hand on her, gently. 'I... I don't know if I have it anymore. I don't understand it. I love you, so much. It pains me to even consider it, but... I think it's goodbye. Forever.' She said nothing, as she stood silent. This wasn't the first time I'd spoken to her like this. She was used to it from me. But, this time, something was different. The spark was gone, and she knew it too. She seemed to know it was coming, and rather than fight it, she accepted it. I ran my hand on the hem of her skirt, and smiled at her. 'We had some good times, didn't we?' I laughed, a tear falling off my cheek. 'I always had you by my side. I always knew you'd take me back, whenever I needed you. You had... have... no, had my heart for a long, long time. I remember when I first saw you. It was love at first sight. I knew I needed to be with you, to be one with you, to understand you, inside and out. And over time, we did become one. 'Remember when I accused you of cheating on me with Deville? It took a long time for you to forgive me for that, no matter how much I apologized. I just got so jealous, and you were so... It doesn't matter. It doesn't matter anymore, and it didn't matter then either. I was stupid and silly and childish. I'm sorry.' Though no one was around, I felt embarassed. My cheeks turned red, and I looked away, wiping the tears from my eyes. 'But, it's just not the same anymore. It's not me anymore. I thought I wanted you again, but, I was wrong, and I'm sorry. I'm sorry I got your hopes up again. I'm sorry that you let me in, one last time. And I'm sorry that you accepted me so willingly. This... is goodbye.' I turned, and I walked away. She said nothing as the final tear fell down my cheek. I never expected a word from her, anyways. She said nothing, because wrestling rings are silent.. --III-- And so, it came to pass. Jason O'Neil walked away from the sport that made him famous. The sport that he'd loved since he was a child. He walked away, found a woman, and finally settled down. Amber was her name, and she had red hair, and large breasts. They were happy together. Soon, they had a child, Christina Marie O'Neil, and their happiness grew even more. She was small, red headed and perfect. Her eyes were blue, and her skin pink. Even her cry sounded happy, as she wailed away, waiting to be fed. She grew up as fast as children do, and in the blink of an eye, she was gone off to college. Christina had another sister, Morgan Taylor O'Neil, with brown hair and brown eyes. Her cry wasn't as happy, but, she was a good baby, for the short time she was a baby. She grew up to read a lot, and appreciated Chaucer, Thoreau, Austen and Plath. She studied Shakespeare, Poe and David Ives. She loved to laugh, and soon, she too left. Morgan had a brother, Angelo Tyson O'Neil. He was strong, even as a baby, with a grip that could dent a Buick. They called him Bam-Bam, and soon, just Bam. He grew up strong and he grew up fast, with them Ohio girls on them Ohio summer nights. He played football, and started both ways for St. Xavier. He had a great smile, and soon, he was off, fighting in a war. Jason smiled every day. He smiled when his kids graduated college, he smiled when he gave his two daughters away to two decent men, to start their own families. He smiled when he shook his son's hand. He smiled on the off chance people recognized him from his 'glory days'. He smiled when the call came in that his wife had lost her two year battle with cancer, because it's what she would have wanted. He smiled when he saw her face one last time, as they lowered her into the ground, his children and grandchildren around his seventy-two year old body. He smiled when the doctor looked him in the eye and shook his head, as he hit the age of eighty-five, his body old and tired, ready to rest for the final time. He breathed his last breath and smiled at Christina, holding her hand. On May 9th, 2063, ninety-two year old Jason O'Neil was interred into Lakeview Cemetary, next to his wife. Newspapers ran obituaries, and thousands who still remembered, bowed their heads. In bars across the country, arguments broke out about the glory days, and how Jason O'Neil, one cold day in December, found the fire again, and went on to become exactly what people wanted him to be: Their champion. --EP-- Jason O'Neil is a lot of things. Handsome, smart, cunning, and, most importantly, manipulative. Think that was goodbye? It is. Way ahead of time. When I go, I'm gone. But, not without that Universal Championship around my waist. And I start again with Malachi. Now, is not that time, because time is on my side. Yes, it is.
