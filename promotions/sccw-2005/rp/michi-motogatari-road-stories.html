Michi Motogatari ('Road Stories')

undated

Date: A day after Temptation There were exactly three aspects of my job, a professional wrestler, that really sucked. The first was the time investment. I spent two to three days physically preparing for the next show, probably another two on travel, and the rest of the week is spent lounging around whatever city I found myself in. I made my residence in Dallas, Texas, but I promise you that I spend very little time there. You always need to work out and stay in shape, regardless of age and how long you've been in the business. I'm closing in on a decade, here, and I still have to prepare a lot even if my matches got a lot shorter because of my recent inclusion in the Livewire division of Sin City Championship Wrestling. My fiancee understood that I spent a lot of time working towards my job, even if I technically only worked maybe a handful of minutes each week. That being said, I should probably spend more time with her that didn't involve being confined to a cramped car or aircraft for hours on end. The second was the risk of injury. Being injured was absolutely the worst possible thing that can happen to you as a wrestler, short of death, drug addiction, and arrest (not necessarily in that order). In the course of my career, I've had four concussions, severe lacerations in more than one location on my body (though mostly my head, but there was one match where I was just covered in cuts), a dislocated shoulder, several cracked ribs, bruised ribs, and a torn abdominal muscle. ...Looking back at my list of injuries, I'm convinced that Inoue has caused the majority of them. All things considered, though, I'm pretty fortunate as I know my injuries could have been much worse. The worst part can honestly be the fans, because they've gotten harder and harder to please. Nowadays, younger guys would just do absolutely crazy things to half the reaction. I've had a few opponents who just wailed on me because they think that fans will like it more if it's 'real'. At least until I asked them politely to stop it. It surprises me right now that my list of injuries aren't worse just because of that. The third and last would be all of the travel, especially when you work independently like I've been doing for the past two years. In fact, of the three aspects of my job that really suck, I've always considered this one to be the absolute worst one. You either flew or you drove, and I've had more than a couple of problems with both of those options. I've probably logged a lot of frequent flyer miles just from my tours of Japan, although I might have attempted to convince an airline or two that making a career out of jumping off the top rope should count towards my mileage. To this date, I have never successfully negotiated this. That's the last time I believe in those old cliches. Anyway, I hate flying. My fiancee can attest to this, but I get really uncomfortable if I have to sit in a cramped airplane for twenty hours, and start to want to find any excuse to stand up and stretch my legs. Strangely, I feel fine when I'm in a car that's crowded with multiple wrestlers. That being said, car rides can be abysmally boring, particularly in the Midwestern United States where there's nothing but plains and farmland for miles on end. Montana is even worse, because there's nothing particularly exciting about Montana. It's just... miles and miles of Montana. I've been through there recently, so believe me when I say that Montana really sucked. There were very few ways you can withstand these kinds of trips, and one of them happened to be the people you travel with. I've had some great travelling companions and I've had some really bad ones. My favorite had always been Marcus Dox, who's probably the biggest idiot I knew, but was also an amusing idiot and a lot of fun to be around. Of course, even though I call him an idiot, he's the one with the eighty billion world titles while I sit here with none. You know, that just makes me want to reevaluate my whole life. I've also had some pretty bad travelling partners. While Inoue Doi's the most unpleasant person I've ever met, whose only goal in life seemed to be to make my life that of suffering... she's not the worst and as long as I don't interrupt her conversations with Annie, she's actually somewhat pleasant. Actually, one of the worst travelling partners I've ever had was Steve Knox, whose ego couldn't even be contained in this solar system, much less inside a car. Steve couldn't go five minutes without reminding me that he's 'awesome' or 'the son of a legend'. I had exactly one car ride with him, filled with nice little egotistical trips down the memory interstate with minimal rest stops in between. I never did it again, but we don't exactly get along, anyway. On the other hand, though, the worst road trip I ever took involved Bruce Shanahan, or Violence Jack as most have come to call him. He made a pretty big name for himself throughout promotions like RPW and PRIME as this cult leader for some crazy religion involving Cthulhu, with his 'cult' being known as 'the Sect'. I briefly shared a car with the guy in 2004 out of necessity, because everybody else I usually travelled with was doing something else at the time and couldn't be bothered to give me a lift. The trip went from Louisville to Nashville, and while I don't remember how long the trip actually lasted, I do know that it felt like it lasted eighteen years. While he might have seemed sincere when he offered to take me to Nashville, I found out quickly that Bruce was about the same guy in real life as you see on television. That is to say that I spent the majority of the car trip battling Bruce on the Cthulhu Mythos and the other Lovecraftian themes that have become part of his persona. Considering that Ethan Knight and Horace Tully, his acolytes in the Sect, were in the car with me, it turned into a three-headed sleeping godfest and the whole trip felt more like they were attempting to convert me to their ways than helping me get to the next show. By the time we got to Nashville, I wanted to get as far away from those three as I possibly could. I think it took me a week to mentally recover from the shock of that trip, and Marcus probably still suggests to this day that I should have another car trip with Bruce and his flock of lunatics. I swear to God, that man was a sadist. Or, at least, he just enjoyed watching me squirm. These days, I share my car with different people than I'm used to. They're not violent, they're not particularly egocentric, and they're not going to convert me to some evil religion based on slumbering unspeakable horrors. What they will do, however, is bicker and argue like a married couple, at least for the first two hours of our trip between Oakland, California and Las Vegas, Nevada. The only thing I could think of at first was 'this is going to be a long trip'. Joining me in the front seat of my rental car were Jason Myers, Sin City Championship Wrestling's ring announcer who normally didn't travel with us. Jason was one of those folks that liked to hitch a ride with whoever would let him, which meant that he never had a consistent road companion. He was like that. If I recall correctly, he travelled with Mr. Monday and his daughter the previous week. Jason didn't like to talk about himself much, but he did like to listen to the stories of the other wrestlers. The other two men in the car with me were, unfortunately, the bickering couple. The first man was Jared Sykes, a professional wrestler whose only goal in life seemed to be to make as much a fool of himself as possible. He was the first King Blueberry, with the second one, Gabriel Simon, being the other man in the car. From what I knew of their relationship, they've known each other for more than a decade, and Sykes really seemed to take some sort of perverse enjoyment out of annoying Gabriel. Their current argument has lasted more than an hour, originally over whether to stop by a Taco Bell or a Burger King on our way to Oakland. Later on, though, their argument developed into something that had nothing to do with either Taco Bell or Burger King, or in fact, nothing about fast food in general. 'I'm telling you, Gabe,' Jared started with a faux whiny, almost sing-song, voice, 'We need to be Green Lanterns. All of us.' Even out of the rear-view mirror, I could visibly tell that Gabe had long since lost his patience with his counterpart. His face was turning red and I could barely catch a glimpse of what I presume to be a throbbing vein over his brow. Words were failing Gabe, whether it's because he was so annoyed that he couldn't formulate any or because Jared had suggested something so astonishingly ridiculous that his brain hit the brakes and called for a time out. Since my trip with Bruce Shanahan had at least proven that it would take more than the ridiculous suggestions of Jared Sykes to crack the downright Asgardian walls of my sanity, I offered a simple question, 'Even Myers?' 'ESPECIALLY Myers!' was Jared's response. I swear I could actually hear Jason's head do a double-take, 'Wait, what?' Jason hadn't exactly spoken much since the car trip began, so finally hearing his voice two hours into the trip was like a breath of fresh air to us. Maybe he saved his really good voice work for his ring announcements? He's pretty good at his job of yelling out our names, after all. I think it's at this point that Gabe finally found the words to object to Jared's admittedly bizarre suggestion, 'What the hell? Green Lanterns? Are you goddamn nuts!?' It wasn't that he was angry, it's that Jared seemed to know exactly how to push Gabe's buttons. A certain inflection in his voice, a certain choice of words, or just flat-out ridiculous suggestions... Jared knew every trick in the book in order to have fun at the expense of others, usually Gabe. When I think about Jared's relationship with Gabe, it makes me reflect on my own road trips with Marcus. 'No, hear me out. Imagine the four of us, armed with power rings, fighting off the forces of evil! We could wear matching costumes and everything!' Jared said, now with a more excited tone in his voice as he outlined his latest master scheme. I could tell in the rear-viewing mirror that Jared was using head gestures to help make his point, even though only Gabriel could see them with anything resembling reliability. Unless he wanted me to crash our car in the middle of central California, anyway. 'I dunno, green's not really my color,' I said, with a chuckle, 'I'm more of a blue guy.' 'Why the hell do you want to be a Blue Lantern, Avy?' Jared blurted out, after a momentary pause. He made it a point to use a nickname for me that I'm sure he thought was really clever, at least until I told him after this trip that people have called me that before. He seemed a bit down after that. 'Well, the blue would match my eyes. Obviously.' I said, sarcastically. Actually, up until I became a nut in a blackberry mask who ran around doing stupid crap like declaring the Livewire championship to be my son all day long, I was pretty well-known for using blue for my ring gear. Of course, it's because I just really like the color blue, and not because it matched my eyes. `Cause the eye thing was kind of nuts. 'Oh, well, of course.' Jared said, seemingly understanding my point of view, no matter how sarcastic it was. After a short pause, Jared decided to keep going with his strange fixation on the Green Lantern, 'But yeah, we could make folding chairs made out of green energy and just smack people with them. It'll be great.' 'Alright, then... where would we get the power rings, Jared?' I asked, good-naturedly. I'll be honest, the idea of matching costumes didn't the least bit bother me. I already made my living wearing spandex, another costume wasn't going to change anything. Besides, people used to think I was gay, and I'm still scratching my head as to why they thought that. Jared opened his mouth, and I could see in the rear-view mirror that he had his finger up. He didn't utter a word, and he just held that pose for a good three seconds. It was probably in that three seconds that the hamster that I assumed turned the wheel within his brain finally had to stop running on its wheel and go 'wait, what the hell am I doing?', because Jared finally realized in that span of time the fatal flaw in his plan, 'Uh...' Gabe finally woke up from whatever stupor he was in during this entire conversation, 'You don't have a damn clue, do you?' 'Nope.' our deflated friend said. I could hear the disappointment in his voice. I let out a sigh, as an awkward silence filled the car. Actually, it was the first period of silence at all since this trip began. It was peaceful, but also, admittedly, fairly boring. The only sounds present were the fading signal from one of Oakland's classic rock radio stations, which I had set to a fairly low volume, mostly to annoy Jared who wouldn't stop trying to reach up between the seats and mess with the radio. It made me glad that Myers took shotgun. Then Jason Myers finally opened his mouth and said more than two words at the three of us, 'So, you guys have been wrestling a while?' 'Eleven years.' Gabriel said, simply, which was followed by the equally laconic response of 'ten years' from Jared. 'Eight years, here.' I answered. Actually, it'll be nine years by December. Time sure flies when you're a professional wrestler, doesn't it? 'So, you guys have been around for a while, then?' Myers asked. I could already tell what he wanted to ask before the words even escaped his mouth, 'Have any good memories of that time? Road trips, a funny story, that sort of thing?' I decided I'd better say something quick before Jared decided to take over the conversation for the next hour, 'Sure. I've got a couple.' Apart from a few weeks after I've had my concussions and a couple of months after I tore one of my abdominal muscles, I really haven't had too many actual breaks from my life as a professional wrestler. That meant that I've spent a lot of time in the company of other men who've lived the same life as I have, which meant that I've been a witness to many bizarre incidents. One came to mind immediately, back when I was still a relatively high-level star. I took a deep breath, and began my story, 'So, it's five years ago, and we're in Cleveland for a show. Our promoter, who was hiding the fact that he was terminally ill at this point, decided to hold a party for our promotion lasting six months. We all holed up in some dive just outside of town, and the majority of the roster got blitzed. I was the designated driver for a number of guys, mostly because everyone knew that I didn't drink, not to mention that I was living in Cleveland at the time. So, it comes time for me to take some of the guys back to my place for the night. So it's me, Osyrus, Longshot, Deft, and the Kitagamis... all piled into one car. Of course, Ossy was insisting that he was capable of driving, and it's really hard to convince a seven foot man built like a damn skyscraper to actually let the sober guy take the wheel.' 'So, Ossy takes shotgun, and the other four guys are all crammed into the back seat. I'd like to think that everything about the actual trip was normal, at least until I started to come near a Taco Bell.' I said, and then I paused. It was mostly for the dramatic effect. I figured my audience would appreciate it. 'Then... Ossy goes nuts.' 'He goes 'nuts'?' Myers asked me, seemingly interested in the story. 'Yeah. He turns to me and goes,' I start, and then I paused to go into my badly-done Osyrus impersonation, 'Yo, Avy! Avy! We gotta stop at the Taco Bell, man! I love that little dog!' 'I, of course, remind you that Ossy had enough alcohol to make an elephant drunk, so needless to say that he wasn't entirely acting like his usual self. Normally, I'd have just ignored Ossy and pressed on with driving, but then Deft, or Eddy as I call him, decided to speak up. `Cause, I dunno, maybe he just wanted to see me crack under the pressure. And he goes,' I said, and then I paused to go into my even worse Deft impersonation, 'He just wants to meet the dog, don't deny him that!' 'Now, I sat at the stoplight, which felt like it lasted ten years. The Taco Bell is up ahead. And there I am trying to convince a black, completely blitzed, seven foot tall, mountain of muscle that the Taco Bell dog doesn't actually *live* in the Taco Bell.' I explained. I wanted to use hand gestures like Jared was earlier, but I found it vitally important to keep my hands on the steering wheel. You know, for everyone's own safety. 'So, that's when Akage Kitagami decided that he absolutely had to leave the car. He just opened the passenger side door, I still don't know how, then he crawled over his cousin Hiro, and then literally exited the car practically standing on his hands. He gets to his feet, and then, I guess, goes to do what his intention was the whole time. He zips open his pants and just takes a massive leak on the tire of the car parked adjacent to mine.' I continued, ending my last sentence with a chuckle as I remembered the scene. Although I wasn't even done with the story yet, 'So, the guy gets out of his car, and it's at this point that I realize that he's a police officer.' 'Oh, geez.' was Myers' reaction. 'Yeah,' I said, agreeing with Myers' more stunned reaction, 'So, first thing I do is I turn my car off and then step out. Of course, the second I do is when the officer calls out to Akage while approaching him. So, Akage turns to the guy and starts pissing on his shoes, all while grinning like a fool and saying 'Hey, Occifer!'.' I say with a sigh, remembering that idiot, drunken look on his face. 'I shout at Akage to get back into the car and apologize ridiculously to the officer, explaining to him that I'm the designated driver. He looks me over, I guess he recognized who I was or something... but before he has a chance to say anything, Ossy gets out of the car and starts power-walking right to the Taco Bell. All the while shouting something like...' I said, before I went into my bad Osyrus voice, 'I'm gonna shake paws with that dog!' I heard some snickers from at least Jared Sykes in the back, though I wasn't sure about Gabe's reaction yet. Myers had such trouble believing the story that I was telling, but he was definitely laughing the hardest at it. I paused, letting the absurdity of my story sink in, before I concluded, 'The officer just watches this seven foot juggernaut go running up to the Taco Bell, turns to me, and advises me to get everyone home before they cause any more of a ruckus. So, he basically lets us all off with a warning and drives away.' 'He just lets you walk?' Sykes asked me. 'Yeah. Don't ask me how that worked, I'm still scratching my head at that.' I told Jared. I sighed and continued, 'So, I get back to my place after I retrieve Ossy from the Taco Bell. Longshot is completely passed out, probably because the kid couldn't hold his liquor to save his life, and I literally had to carry him from the car into the place. Ossy just curls up next to the bushes outside my house and falls asleep right there. I actually still remember what happened the following morning when he woke us all up with this scream that's something to the effect of...' I paused and went into my bad Osyrus impression, ''WHY IN THE HELL AM I IN THE BUSHES!?'' 'So, anyway, I get everybody in. Eddy immediately flops down on the futon, before I can even pull out the bed, and just passes out right there. So, the Kitagamis and Longshot all have to sleep on the floor. And Eddy... well, his snoring could literally wake the dead.' 'You mean,' Sykes interjected in this excited tone, and I'm already hearing the groans from Gabe, 'He snored so loud that there was a zombie outbreak at your house?!' 'Yes, Jared,' I started in my usual sarcastic manner, 'And we had to board up the house and stock up on booze and guns. ......Anyway, back to reality, here.' I could hear Jared's disappointment before I launched back into my story, but I ignored him, 'So, I'm about ready to go to bed. It'd been a long day, after all. I'm in my room, and right when I'm about to drift off to sleep... I hear this LOUD crash that jolts me awake. So, I shoot out of bed and go to the source of the crash, and I find that the Kitagamis have started having this miniature wrestling match right in the middle of the living room. Hiro has this nosebleed from taking this face-first bump into my coffee table, which they collapsed in the middle of this impromptu wrestling match. Akage Kitagami's trying to hook in what I consider to be the worst small package in human history, because over the course of trying to hook it in, Hiro's pants got yanked down just enough to tell that what they say about Asian men wasn't always true.' Myers' laughter was encouraging, while I notice that I was slowly tickling Gabe and Jared's funny bones as well. 'All the while, Akage's shouting something at... well, I guess it was me, since I was the only coherent person in the room. 'REF! REF!' I guess he wanted a referee to make a count. Mind you, Akage's shoulders weren't even down and he was trying to squirm out of his predicament, while I was mourning the loss of my coffee table.' I said. Looking back, I think it would've made sense to have dropped to my knees and screamed 'YOU MANIACS!' in some weird Planet of the Apes moment. 'So, trying very hard not to stare at Hiro's member, I get down and I make a count. And that loser, Hiro, kicks out at one just so he can adjust his pants. Then he gets right behind Akage and literally tries to tear his pants off just trying to catch him with a schoolboy. Since I'd already met my quota for seeing another man's junk for one night, I fast-counted Akage and went the hell back to bed before Akage could even get up and question my refereeing skills.' I said. I paused, and Myers turned to me, 'And then?' 'Then Ossy woke us all up by yelling, and as happy a drunk as Ossy can be, he's pretty grumpy when he's hungover. He practically breaks my door down getting into my place, and he's pissed. Livid, in fact. He's looking at me like I wouldn't let him inside, and he's throwing every curse in the book. I think he even said something about a rusty trombone and 'crawling inside my ass'. All this nasty stuff. So, he gets done with his tirade and he's got no more words left to shout at me with. So then I go, 'So, does that mean you don't want pancakes, Ossy?'' 'Ossy just kinda stops, stares at me for a moment, then he says something like,' I said, and here came my bad Osyrus again, ''Hell to the yes, I want pancakes!'' I think we just lost Myers there. Sykes seemed amused, too, and I guess the best I was gonna get out of Gabe was a big smile, 'And that's just one story. I probably have a mountain of others, but I'd rather not spend the whole time talking by myself.' It was quiet for a bit. From the rear-view mirror, Sykes seemed... almost serious. It was rare for him, at least when around me. I guess he liked to hang around with people who were usually pretty normal when away from the ring, or something, so he could act like a goofball. After a long pause, in which Sykes allowed Myers to calm down a bit, he got my attention, 'So, Avy.' 'Yeah, what's up?' 'You seem to be getting used to the whole Blackberry thing.' he said to me, 'Enjoying it, are you?' I scoffed at the guy calling himself the 'Black Sheep', and took a glance back at him, if only for a moment. After putting my attention back to the road, I responded, 'Yeah, right. I carved out a nice reputation for myself as one of the best wrestlers in the world, and now I wear a dumb mask, act like a retard, and wrestle eight minute epics with a green rookie with a limited moveset. I just want to lose the mask, be who I am, and let it be that.' Jared thought about it, 'I dunno. You're kinda boring when you're not in character.' 'Pardon me for being boring, then.' I said back. I sounded a little more harsh than I probably intended. My comment caused the car to be silenced, other than Myers' random chuckling. After a short silence, I heard Gabe sighing in the back of the car, 'You're pretty short-sighted for a veteran, you know. As long as the gimmick gets over, it doesn't matter who you are. Right now, you're getting over, so why spoil it?' I hate it when these jerks were right. Grudgingly, I nodded and spoke in a defeated tone, 'Yeah, you're right.' I happened to glance in the rear-view mirror and I saw a sight I really didn't need to see: Jared Sykes grinning like he's the damn Cheshire Cat. This was not a good sign. In fact, this was NEVER a good sign. 'Okay, is it my turn to tell a story? Because I've got a good one.' Sykes said with an excited tone. Gabe looked like he was going to be sick, leading me to conclude that the more excited Jared was, the more irritated Gabe became, 'So, there were these live-action roleplayers...' I sighed. We were not getting to Vegas fast enough.
