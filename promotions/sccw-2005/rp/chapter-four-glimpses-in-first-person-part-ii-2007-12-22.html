Chapter Four: Glimpses In First Person, Part II

22 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12.22.07 4.6 - Mr. Hawke - The Bad Guy Sometimes I hate being the bad guy. Okay, that's almost never true. I rather enjoy being the bad guy. I rather enjoy the moments like this one where I can stand over a defeated opponent and wave my fingers in his face. It's just a shame this one didn't know he was defeated quite yet. 'Do I find hot women arousing?' Maxwell Drake tried to sneer at me, but, really, I've been sneered at by better. 'What does this have to do with anything?' 'I just thought it was interesting, given that you had a half-naked knockout in your study, and she was ready to go -- or, at least, you certainly had to think she was. But you stuck to your dead wife rather than jumping the, uh, bones of the willing woman. It's quite admirable, really.' 'Yes, well, I still ended up in the wine cellar. So much for being admirable.' 'You were ending up here anyway, Mr. Drake. I just think that Ms. Trouble would have probably been willing to give you quite a bon voyage before you took a nap.' 'Your point, Mr. Hawke? Or was this just to ridicule me for not taking a roll in the hay with your friend?' 'Ridicule? That was the furthest thought from my mind. Your wedding vow was until death do us part, but you've taken that a step beyond, my friend. And you should be commended for that.' 'Again,' Maxwell's teeth came together. 'I ask you if you have a point here. You said this was about my son.' 'And I would not lie to a man so devoted to his family.' 'What are you going to do to him?' Why do they always skip ahead in the book? Is there no one with the patience to enjoy a good story? 'I'm afraid I cannot answer that question. Because this...' I rose creakily to my feet, trying very hard not to let the hatred at my crippled condition overwhelm my role in the drama. 'This isn't about what we're going to do to your son.' 'Then what is this about?' 'This is about what we're going to do to you, of course.' Maxwell's eyes got bigger -- it was fractional, I'll give him that. But it was still a widening, and it never fails to occur. 'You said this was about Duncan.' 'It is.' 'Then why are you doing this to me?' 'It's all about the -- ' The bolt on the door behind me was thrown open again. I spun around, cane jabbing out in front of me. Sometimes I hate being the bad guy. But not tonight. * * * * * 4.7 - Maxwell Drake: Unexpected Guests 'Can no one give two gentlemen have some peace and some quiet to discuss the joys of marriage?' Hawke sounded annoyed at the interruption, and his brow furrowed as a slight feminine form appeared in the crack of the doorway. I could not help but smile, as any dissension between my captors could spell only good tidings. The woman was businesslike in dress and manner, her blonde hair pulled back in a tight bun. Her outfit spoke of a secretary or office staffer -- knee-length black skirt, white button-up blouse (done all the way up), and sensible, flat-heeled shoes. I only hired women who wore flat-heeled shoes to their job interview. Let every other American businessman have a walking sexual harassment suit with short skirt and impossibly tall high-heeled shoes. Mine will actually get work done. 'Mr. Hawke,' the woman said softly. 'Yes, Celeste?' Hawke cracked the butt end of his cane against the hard ground, his posture erect, commanding. Hers was docile; she could not look the man in the eye. 'Ms.... uh, Ms. Trouble asked me to let you know that your other subject is here.' 'Excellent.' The smile that slithered across Hawke's face was most unsettling. 'Tell her I'll get to them as soon as Mr. Drake and I are finished discussing the intricate interplay of marriage.' 'Uh?' Celeste clearly had more of an issue than just the arrival of another prisoner, but she was uncomfortable discussing it in the open. Whether her uneasiness came from my presence or Hawke's own, I was not sure. I chalked her up as a possible ally in my eventual grand escape plan. She was one with some backbone, since she made the man with the cane limp almost all the way up the stairs to meet her. Either that or I scared her, and either option worked for me. I strained to hear their whispered conversation, but only caught a few words, most from Celeste. 'Busload.' 'V.I.P.' 'Nowhere else.' 'Insists.' I was not sure if Hawke even said anything, to be truthful; all he did was turn his back to me, moving slowly up into the doorframe. Celeste retreated into the room beyond, chancing only a single look over her shoulder. I tried to look as pathetic as possible 'It looks like you'll be getting a guest, Mr. Drake,' Hawke called over his shoulder, one hand on the doorframe. 'But don't worry, I hear she's another pretty girl. Just think real hard about your wife's rotting corpse and I'm sure you'll be fine.' He rapped on the door, and it opened a crack to let him out, then further when they tossed her in. The girl was a redhead -- young, probably no older than my son or his girlfriend, in heavy combat boots, ripped blue jeans and a black t-shirt for one of those bands Duncan used to play at obscenely high volumes. Metallica or Master of Puppets -- the shirt referenced both, and I never managed to keep track of which band he liked that year. They tossed the girl callously into the room and slammed the metal door behind her. In a flash, she was on her feet, banging fists bound in front of her by nylon rope into the door. ''Ey!' Great -- and she was British. 'Y'cain' jus' lea'me in heah! I got bloody rights, you muppets!' Tiny, black-nailed hands and heavy, steel-toed boots thundered against the doorway. No response came. 'They're not going to respond to you, miss,' I said finally -- the only person being tortured here was me. The girl spun around, lifting her hands in front of her, defensively. 'Who're you, then?' 'My name is Maxwell Emerson Drake II. Like you, I'm being held against my will.' 'This's some kinda cock-up! I ain't s'posed ta be 'ere!' 'I do not believe either of us were destined for this. I assume you ran afoul of a woman somewhere?' 'I did. I'm watchin' Man U on the telly, an' there was this blond tart there. We were takin' the piss outta her, let me jus' say, then I went out for a ciggy, right? And the next thing I know, I woke up in this yank's trunk.' I nodded. 'I had a similar misstep with regards to this woman.' 'Wha' do they wan' with me? I'm jus' an exchange student!' 'I do not know. As you are not, as yet, strapped to a chair, come over here and I will attempt to unfasten your bindings, miss?' 'Oh. Me manners're horrible. Name's Kelly,' she said. 'Kelly Carpenter.' * * * * * 4.8 - MacKenzie Malone: The Real World In the real world, I hate my hair. I know. It's crazy, right? Whenever you think of MacKenzie Malone, you're thinking, 'ZOMG! She's the black-haired bitch!,' right? But it's such a pain in the ass to make sure the roots don't come out, and to make sure it's that shade, right? Guys don't understand these things. They think that women take a long time getting ready because we're 'lazy' or we 'can't decide what to wear.' In reality... okay, well, in reality, there is a lot of truth to that, especially the 'can't decide what to wear' part, because that's hellishly hard. I was blow-drying my hair when Duncan called, so that took some effort to complete (good thing he made me set up a special ringtone for him -- 'What A Man' by Salt-N-Pepa). And then there was the disaster of choosing an outfit. Usually this wasn't so bad -- we'd get in his car, we'd go to a store, I'd buy a dress. But I had to pick from the stuff in my closet, y'all. And I had to do so without turning my bedroom into a sty, since Duncan would be here soon and I didn't want him to see that my apartment was a disaster! So this amounted to a whole lot of 'No, that doesn't go, chuck it in the closet and make sure the door closes.' Finally, I decided on something that was stylish, attractive, and didn't make my butt look like someone jammed an air hose in it. It's amazing the things that Prada can do. I flipped on the TV while I was waiting for Duncan and I saw the news on the television. I... I don't know what to say here. I freaked out a little bit. I did something I shouldn't have. I dug under my bed and I found a telephone I'd stolen from a tattooed man I'd fucked for the express purpose of finding out removing him from my life (yeah, call me bad names, I don't care). I ducked down on the other side of the bed and I called a number that the man with the hawkhead cane had told me not to call unless it was an emergency. He answered before it rang once. 'Ms. Malone -- what's going on? Spring a leak? Tie your shoelaces together? Accidentally shoot the President of the United States?' I really don't like this guy -- but I think I'm not supposed to. 'Because I can't think of any good reason why you should be using that telephone, nevermind pressing buttons on it. I will have you know it has a detonator charge in it. Not a big one -- it won't blow up your house or anything. But it'll blow off a few fingers that deserve much better.' 'You let me keep a phone with a BOMB in it? What's wrong with you people!' I was a little miffed, you might say. 'That was a joke, MacKenzie.' 'Oh.' I didn't laugh. 'What have you done?' 'Today? I've insulted several men, belittled both my partners' theories, sat outside an ice cream shop for several hours, and several other things that are, quite frankly, none of your fucking business. So if you want to ratchet your accusations down to a specific, we can discuss it. If not... well, I'm a busy man, Ms. Malone. Signal pirating is an exhausting business, especially with the pirate in chains and all.' 'I just turned on the news. I saw what you did, you smarmy little shit.' The man on the other end of the phone was nonplussed at my outrage. 'Everything that occurred today occurred as a result of consequences. Every action in this life has a reaction. A pushed lever moves every one of my steps. Duncan Drake is responsible for some pretty... well, some pretty horrible things, MacKenzie. I'd advise you to stay away from him.' 'You know I can't do that. This is my place.' 'She had a place, too, MacKenzie. A warm place, a loved place -- surrounded by friends and family. Now that's not exactly happening any more, is it? She eats out of a fucking bag and breathes thanks to a machine.' The man with the cane shifted the phone; I could hear it moving. He took a long breath. 'So, yes, I'll do exactly what my contract specifies in regards to Duncan Drake. And you shouldn't worry your pretty little head about your boyfriend. Put this telephone away and do your part when you're called upon.' 'But he's coming over in less than an hour! How will I tell him?' If he was here, the man with the cane would be shrugging. 'That's really not my concern. I just do the job as the client specifies. Everything else is out of my hands. Good night, Ms. Malone. Keep in mind -- if you so much as think about calling the police or going to Duncan with what you think you know, remember that envelope you're hiding under the floorboard in your bedroom is not the only one I have.' I didn't realize until later that he knew exactly where I kept everything. My voice was quiet. I sniffled, wiping at my eyes. 'I know. I'll...' But the line was already dead. There came a knocking at my door. Rap-rap-rap, BANGBANGBANG. 'Mac? Yo, Mac, it's Duncan. I'm sorry I'm late. I'd tellya I brought flowers, but... y'know, I didn't.' I padded over to the door, flipping off the TV. The crawl across the news was reloading. 'Maxwell Drake, CEO of The Drake Group...' the story began before the TV went dark. I pulled the door open, attempting to pull on a smile. 'Duncan, hi,' I said. 'Hey, babe,' he said, already in mid-rant before I'd fully opened the door. 'I'm thinkin' we go catch a movie, maybe get that seafood, but the restaurant says it'll be a couple hours before they can get us a table, and I can't raise my dad to ask him to pull some strings. So I figure we bail on that, get the movie in, maybe some pork ribs and then we come back and you suck on a cup of my balls.' His grin is usually charming, despite itself. He squinted at me. 'You okay? Seem a little... eh. Maybe we skip everything else, and we just stay in tonight.' I decided to let the grin do a little bit of the work for me, prowling over to him. 'After I just spent all this time getting ready?' I asked, letting a teasing smile I didn't feel climb onto my lips. 'Fuck, no, sir. We are going to be seen tonight, if I have to rob a hobo to do it.' 'That's my girl!' The real world could wait until tomorrow. * * * * * 4.9 - (Intermission from the Future, Two) Grandma - Wake Up, Dear You have to wake up. I know it's probably easier out there, wherever you are. Life is hard - you ain't got to be my age to know that. People don't really understand what they got till it's gone, do they? You're momma and your daddy and them, they in here a lot. I see your momma cry like those tears ain't never gonna stop, and your daddy... well, my son's trying to hold everything together. But that was always your job, Kel. You had your problems -- it's my experience that most young people do -- but you were our glue, and without you around, everything's come undone. I know a lot of folks come in here, and it's good they do. You oughtn't be alone; your grandpa spent a lot of time in a place like this towards the end, and it was always important to me that he never got lonely. But what really chaps my hide is what's said inside these walls. Child, there's a storm blowing in -- I can feel it in my old bones. People I love are going to do some horrible things; they're becoming people I don't recognize anymore. I can't stop it, I can't do anything about it, because we're still a family, and in my day and age, this is how a family treated each other. But you can do something. You can wake up. Tell them to stop, to rethink what they're planning. This Drake kid... I understand what he's done is unconscionable, and I cannot forgive him for it. There's nobody that can. Nobody but you. Wake up. Wake up, before the people you love become people they hate. Wake up and stop the wrong choice from being made. Before it's too late, I'm begging you to wake up. Just... wake up, Kelly. Your family needs you. * * * * * 4.10 - Maxwell Drake: Escapes I probably looked like I saw a ghost. This is not how I expected to go out. 'Wot?' the girl asked, her heavy-soled boots thudding across the concrete as she walked behind me. 'Y'eat some bad fish?' 'I... I knew a person once by that name.' 'I don' think it wus me, luv.' Kelly stooped down behind me, working at the straps that held me in the damned chair. 'No,' I shook my head a little. 'I don't believe it was.' 'Oi, these straps're tight're than a bishop's twat,' the Brit said, grinning a little as she pulled one free. 'There we go!' I tightened and relaxed one fist, trying to get the blood flowing again. I could feel my frown etched into my face. Oh, how I wanted to punch that scowling bastard with the cane. I realized Kelly wasn't working on my other arm and I glanced that way in consternation. Bound wrists filled my vision. 'This'd go loads faster if y'could undo me wrists.' I nodded, lifting my hand to the knot. It was not as strong as the straps -- obviously, it had been done in haste when they transported the woman. The three minutes it took me to unbind her felt like an hour, and I knew that Hawke would return at any moment. 'Wot'd they take you for?' Kelly asked, crouching on the other side of the chair. Now with her hands unfastened, the woman was making short work of the strap holding my other arm to the chair. 'F'rgive, but I swear I seen y'before.' 'I'm a businessman, but my son is quickly becoming famous -- though, for what I couldn't tell you.' 'Y'gotta kid? I had a daughter.' 'Had?' 'Lost her. She... she died. Car wreck.' 'I am sorry for your loss.' The woman came to a crouch between the door and I, her nimble fingers going to work on the leather that bound my ankles. She had a tattoo on the small of her back, and as she reached forward, I could make out the swirling red and blue Celtic symbology, but I never knew how to read them. 'Tramp stamps,' my son called them. 'Was a car wreck, year back,' the woman said, thankfully distracted by her work. I couldn't bear blubbering women. 'So y'said ya had a... got ya, y'bastich...' One of my legs came free. 'A son?' 'Yes. Two, actually. Maxwell III and Duncan.' The woman's unknotting paused; she looked up at me, her thin brows knotting. 'Wait. Duncan? Like, Duncan Drake?' 'Yes, that would be him.' I can never quite get used to that reaction -- wait, that thing is your son? 'If you'll forgive me, we can compare family stories after I'm free, I'm sure that...' THUNK. The bolt opened. The fluorescent light above the door flickered back on. 'A-ha!' Kelly exclaimed, tugging a piece of leather free. I was free. I leapt to my feet, grabbing a nearby wine bottle as the door came open. Kelly stood a hair behind me, peering over my shoulder as Mr. Hawke's silhouette loomed at the top of the stairs. 'Mr. Drake, Ms. Carpenter?' Hawke called. 'I had better not find you two in flagrante delicto down here, or I will be very disappointed in Mr. Drake. Or, who knows, perhaps he's just melted for Ms. Carpenter's attitude and thick British accent.' 'I am free, you arrogant, unctuous jackass,' I hissed. 'You have two choices. You can either stand aside and let the lady and I go, or I will bring this building down around your head.' 'There's a third choice, actually, but it's not time for that, Mr. Drake. So what I'm going to do is... I'm going to choose option B for now. I'm going to lock this door again, then I'm going to go get myself a posse of really large gentlemen, and we'll see how long a 54-year-old man will stand up to... whomever it is that Trouble uses as bouncers.' The door slammed closed again, the bolt thrown behind it. I let out a breath, trying to relax. 'You knew my son?' I hoped the change of pace would distract Kelly from panicking, though I feared our end was not far off. No answer came from behind me. 'Kelly?' The bolt was thrown again -- damn, that was quicker than I thought the Hawke man would be able to hobble off for help. But I had to make sure the girl was okay, that she hadn't fainted dead away or something. The last thing I could afford was having to carry her out of here or something. I heard the door open as I glanced over my shoulder. Kelly stood silently behind me, as she had the entire time. No sounds came from the staircase, so I turned fully to the girl. 'Kelly, we have to go!' I shouted, as blackouts and freezing up was not uncommon in the field. I grabbed her by the shoulders and shook gently. Kelly smiled. 'I knew your son,' she said quietly. I should have known there was something wrong immediately. Her accent was gone. I was caught unprepared and open, my hands on her shoulder as she stepped under my guard. There was a lightning-like flash and the faintest sound like chiming bells. I felt a sharp pain in my gut; something hot and white flashed behind my eyes. Kelly grabbed me by the back of the head, rocking forward to her tiptoes to hiss a whisper into my ears. 'I knew your son,' she repeated. 'He's the one that killed me.' I felt her small hand retract. I heard something slip free of my skin, so sharp I hadn't even really felt it enter. I felt the second blow, though, and the third and the fourth and the fifth, each coming in rapid succession. She eased me into the chair -- I didn't realize my legs didn't work any longer. My hands would not close, my voice would not scream. All I could do was stare blankly ahead and try to hold my guts in. I bled out on a fucking wooden chair in a fucking wine cellar, and the only people who saw me die were two fucking sociopaths. This is not how I expected to go out. * * * * * 4.11 - The Orchid: Fanatic Sir Winston Churchill once said, 'A fanatic is one who can't change his mind and won't change the subject.' I am not a fanatic. Yes, I walked into that room with the express purpose of getting Maxwell Drake's hopes up, to make him think that an escape was imminent, before I crushed those hopes under my bootheel. Yes, I walked into that room with a wig on my head and an accent I'd borrowed from an old friend, and I lied to a man who wanted nothing more than to go home. Oh, and, yes, I'd stabbed him five times. I cannot forget that. But I did not do so because I am some crazed fanatic, hell-bent for blood and vengeance. Nor did I do so because I hated Maxwell Drake. I did this thing that you probably think is horrible, because it was an ugly, necessary thing, and I am the person that is called upon to do the ugly, necessary things. Our client wanted Duncan Drake destroyed, and Maxwell Drake was a casualty of that path. If it had been one of the others... if it had been Trouble or even Hawke, it might have shaken their belief. No, I am not a fanatic. But I am the Orchid. I will never stop believing. END VOLUME I
