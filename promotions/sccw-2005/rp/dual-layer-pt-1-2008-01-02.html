Dual Layer (pt.1)

2 January 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/02/2008 "I killed my mother." The words clung to the back of his throat, digging in their claws. It may have been the most difficult thing he’s ever said, but for a man who doesn’t speak much, the words that come from his mouth tend to carry much more weight. Those words, however… "How’s that?" Margaret Thapes had always been confused for a cold, calloused woman. She wasn’t the kind of woman who her clients ever felt at ease with, or close to, but they continued to come to her because she offered good advice at a reasonable price. They had her all wrong, though. Margaret Thapes was, in fact, an extremely compassionate woman. Everything her patients told her, she felt. Every dark secret she knew was carried around like luggage. This is why when Elliott told her what he did, she felt like he had thrown a brick at her chest. They sat quietly in her office. The surroundings were the same, but this visit felt vastly different. The oakwood table, the dim lamp in the corner of the room, the stacks of books piled everywhere; it was all the same, but now, instead of an office, it felt like a confessional booth. Elliott had taken a knee. When the words left his mouth he immediately began to cry; gently, at first, but it quickly turned in to violent bouts of uncontrollable sobbing. "Elliott?" She said it softly. She didn’t want to force anything, didn’t want to prod, but in her heart she knew she was on the verge of something very important. For a woman accused of feeling no emotion at all, the man kneeling before her was guilty of just the same. Until now, that is. She stood and walked slowly from behind her desk. Her heels dug in to the soft carpet as she made her way towards Elliott, trying her best not to alarm or upset him. When she was standing over him, she wasn’t sure what to do. All she did was look down at him, and for the first time felt like she understood him, even though his words were unclear. The man before her was a broken man. She dropped down to a knee, putting her arm around his shoulder. She felt him flinch at first, but then tuck himself further in to her arm. They just sat there for a moment, quietly. .and now for something completely unrelated. "I booked you for an interview." William "Slick Willy" McDormant was a man fond of putting others in uncomfortable situations. He wasn’t particularly fond of those situations himself, but he certainly liked watching others cringe and shudder under the pressure. This may have influenced his reasoning when he set Elliott up for an interview at FUSE. "Why?" The two sat at the same coffee shop they had met before. The two of them, by stereotypical standards, looked out of place. Elliott looked like he had just left a metal show, with his black ‘The Red Chord’ tee shirt, faded denim shorts, and black, unwashed hair that was tied in back with a hair tie. McDormant, on the other hand, looked like he had just finished signing a multi-million dollar deal. Maybe he had, who knows? All we know is that he looks like the pinnacle of professionalism in his suit, tie, and forty dollar haircut. In this coffee shop, however, the general consensus was dread-locked hair, Birkenstocks, and an overwhelming smell of incense. Welcome to California. "Why do you think?" McDormant just smiled as he watched Elliott squirm in his seat a little. "You need to get the word out there. You had a hell of a showing at the Young Lion’s Cup, and we need to build on that." Rollins wasn’t convinced. He thought his in-ring display was enough, but apparently the man who was now entitled to manage his every move had differing opinions on the matter. "You’re Elliott fucking Rollins," McDormant continued, unconcerned about profanity in a public place. "The antihero. The man the fans have elected to defeat the evils of guys like Paul Cain and Duncan Drake, the man destined to destroy the scourge of sin festering in FUSE." He was also fond of alliteration. "The only problem with that is…" he paused, actually contemplating putting it delicately, but didn’t bother. "The only problem is you’re pretty fucking dull. Yeah, you’re solid as Hell, and quite possibly one of the better wrestlers this place has seen, but it’s not just about that. You need the personality to match it, so next week we‘re going to go down to Hollywood and visit an image consultant…" It was too much for Rollins to keep up. Since McDormant had been hired, it was a flurry of autograph signings, walking the streets, shaking hands with people and kissing babies. He felt like a politician, not a man hired to destroy people inside of a wrestling ring. McDormant continued talking (which he is very, very good at), but Elliott’s mind wandered to other places. He was no longer in the coffee shop. He was elsewhere. .elsewhere. "She used to sleep on the couch all of the time." By now, Elliott Rollins had managed to calm down some. He was still obviously upset, but he was composed enough to at least talk about it. It was a struggle, but he was able to get the words out. Margaret Thapes sat at his side, something she had never done with any patient before him, and nodded her head. "She’d just sit there," he continued. "All day, every day. She would wake up, watch television, take her pills, and fall asleep." He paused. The blinds were usually closed, but today they were open, so he stared out of the window, allowing memories from ten years ago to come flooding back to him. "What did she have?" Margaret asked. "M.S., fibromyalgia, depression. A bunch of stuff," he said, rubbing his hands together. "I think most of it was in her head." Thapes nodded. "The mind is a very powerful thing." "Yeah, well, whatever it was," he said somberly, "it was real to her. She just sat there every day, depressed, and when her back started to hurt or her muscles ached she would just take another pill." His eyes met Thapes’, who was struggling to keep herself together. "I guess that’s kind of why I chose to come here. You don’t just toss prescriptions around, but her doctors, they did." She felt he was skirting around the subject, so she pressed on. "So tell me what this has to do with what you told me." "It has everything to do with what I told you," he responded, a little more angrily than he had intended. She flinched slightly, not at the animosity in his voice, but at the fact that she was the cause of it and it was something she had been trying to avoid. "So anyway, I left one night," he continued, "like usual. Went out to a show with a couple of friends. Nothing abnormal about that. When I came back she was asleep on the couch. Again, nothing abnormal about that either." He paused again. This is where it started to get tough for him. "So I went to wake her up and try to get her back to bed," he said, his voice slightly above a whisper. "She was breathing really heavily, like, strained, you know? But I didn’t really think anything of it. I just tried to get her up and I couldn’t. She looked up at me and mumbled something, real briefly, but that was the most I got out of her." Thapes nodded again. "And then what?" "And then I went to bed," he said flatly. "I went to bed, but I was just so… frustrated, you know? I was pissed. I was pissed because she wouldn’t get up, I was pissed because of this stupid fucking illness, just… everything. And I hated her for it. I hated her." He started to choke up. "And I hated what it had done to her. I hated the fact that she wasn’t the same. She used to be so happy, you know? And now all she did was sit there on that fucking couch all day, and I hated it." He just shook his head, his eyes on the floor. "I hated it," he choked out, "and I prayed that she would just die already. That it would just all end so she wouldn’t have to be miserable anymore." He didn’t say anymore. He didn’t want to. Thapes, on the other hand, wasn’t done. "And then what?" He looked at her. "And then she died." .snap. William McDormant is far from a patient man. He is also not a very pleasant man. He also wants the attention to constantly be on him, and when it’s not, he will do anything in his power to make it so. That is why when Elliott Rollins’ attention drifts from him, William began snapping his fingers annoyingly. He can also be very annoying. "What?" That annoyance in Elliott’s face as he snapped at McDormant. "What do you mean ‘what’?" McDormant snapped back. "I’m talking business here and you’re fantasizing about God-knows-what. If you want this to work, rule number one is: pay the fuck attention." He also likes to swear. William McDormant is a lot of things. "Sorry," Rollins said, submissively turning his attention to his cooling coffee. He stirred at it mindlessly as McDormant continued on. "So," McDormant continued on, "we’ve got something good built. Now it’s a matter of slapping on a pretty coat of paint." Rollins just rolled his eyes. Thankfully his head was hung too low, or McDormant was too busy ranting, to notice. "That’s what the image consultant is for," McDormant said. "I think with a good gimmick you’ll be golden." "What’s wrong with the one I have?" Rollins replied meekly. "…" responded McDormant. "You don’t have one. I know you think the whole ‘I’m brutal metal fucker’ thing is cool and all, but it’s really not. You need something to make you stand out, or else you’ll be standing out in the streets clutching your Quiet Riot LP’s." McDormant was also a little out of touch with the music industry. Elliott Rollins was just a little out of touch. McDormant watched him as he began to drift away again. When he was about nearly out of it, McDormant reached across the table and slapped him across the face. "What the fuck?" Rollins snarled, his head snapping to attention. McDormant just smirked, which pissed Elliott off even more. "What’s your problem?" Rollins snapped at him. "What’s yours?" McDormant asked back. "We’re here talking about your career, talking about what’s going to make you the best thing around, and you act like you couldn’t give a shit less. What‘s rule number one?" Rollins just shook his head. "Sorry," he said submissively. "What are you thinking about?" McDormant asked earnestly. "Honestly?" Rollins replied. "No, I want you to lie to me," McDormant said with a smirk. "Honestly…" His voice trailed off. .honestly. "I killed her." He looked at her. He wasn’t crying. He wasn’t anything but calm. It seemed as if he had finally come to some sort of realization. She looked back at him, struggling to remain as calm as he was, and realized that the man she was looking at had been fighting these demons for many years. "I killed her. I prayed that she would die, and she died." This one caught in the back of his throat. "I’m convinced that she knew," he continued. "I’m convinced she knew that I no longer wanted her around, and that’s why she finally let go. That’s why she stopped fighting. She knew that it would be easier for her, easier for everyone, if she was dead." He sighed. "I was wrong. It wasn’t easier." She looked at him empathetically. She didn’t know what to say, so she said the only thing she knew to say. "And how did that make you feel?"
