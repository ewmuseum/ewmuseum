The Engine

23 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/23/2007 Arlington, TN – last week It was a typical Saturday afternoon for Jerry and Audrey Stevens. Jerry was watching the Braves take on the Marlins on TBS, and dozing off every couple innings. Audrey was reading the opinion section of the paper, and getting herself worked up about all kinds of political issues. Jerry would always know it was time to avoid her when he could see her with this section of the paper, shaking her head repeatedly. She’d say things like, "I can’t believe they get away with this crap". And, "isn’t there anyone left that gives a shit?" And a lot of other stereotypical remarks uttered by frustrated Americans. After she gave her head a good shake, Audrey stood up and walked into the living room. Her voice was intentionally loud enough to wake a person up. "Did you get the mail yet?" Audrey asked. There was no answer. She sighed and walked out of the room, and eventually pushed the screen door that lead to the outside world open without losing her forward momentum. Outside the sun was shining down on this peaceful suburb of Memphis. The River Rat’s mother walked down her driveway and reached their mailbox. Upon opening it up, she quickly shuffled through her mail. There was a catalog, a couple bills and… She paused. There was a letter from her son. This was a rare occurrence indeed. * * * Washington, DC – right now Lane Stevens walked out the glass double doors of the Marriott hotel with a cell phone in his hand. There was a gym bag slung around his shoulder. What he said into the phone is, "What kind of a name is Bridgette? Is she like 60?" On the other end Saul said, "No sir." Lane shrugged. He was standing on the sidewalk in front of the hotel, dressed in a black "Affliction" tee and tan shorts. He checked his watch twice in the span of about 15 seconds. "You really think I need an agent?" Lane asked. "We feel that your career is on the verge of reaching the next level. And if my contacts are right, FUSE is about to become a lot more of a hostile environment for our cause. You will need someone to represent you for not only endorsement deals, but with the front office. Someone to fight for you when you are too tired from the more literal battles," was Saul’s reply. "Whatever," Lane said, "I’ll meet the bitch." "My employer will be most pleased," Saul said. "I’m going to head to the airport now, catch ya later sunshine." Saul did not reply, he simply hung up. Lane closed his phone, and put it in his pocket. A few moments later a limo pulled up to the sidewalk. * * * Last week Audrey Stevens nudged her sleeping husband multiple times before he finally sat up on the couch. He looked startled and confused, pretty typical for someone who has their nap unexpectedly cut short. In her hands was an envelope that was ripped open, and a few pieces of notebook paper. Her eyes were red. "What’s wrong?" Jerry asked. "It’s from Lane," was all she could get out. "What does it say?" She began to read it to him. * * * Now. Audio feed. "I apologize for how shaky the view is. You can’t always get a camera-man at the drop of a hat." That was the voice of Lane Stevens. Video feed. We fade up to find that we are in the back of a limo. The camera is sitting on the seat across from Lane, and shakes every once and a while. We are staring at Lane, who seems relatively relaxed. The gym bag he had earlier is sitting at his side. "Hello, FUSE. How’s it going? It’s me, Lane, one of those guys with the cool shirts," he motions to his Affliction shirt, "and the dick up your ass." The River Rat waves. "This is a promo. You’ve seen a million of them before, hell I feel like I’ve cut a million of them in my wrestling career by now. But ya know, I’m not going to act like I’m above the moment. I’m not going to act like this is another day in the office for Lane Stevens. Because that would be dishonest, and as I’m famous for saying…honesty is the best policy." Lane smirks. "I have supported The Affliction’s endeavors from the birth of our organization. It was my deal with Smitty T Duluth at Afflicted Glory that helped Clinton Sage retain the world title. It was my ability to reason with Lance and Travis Reed that got them to turn on Ethan Frost, and break his arm. I am a team player, ladies and gentlemen. I may not be the racing stripes on the car, I may not be the flashy feature, I may not be the leather seats, but I make this car go. I am the goddamn engine. One thing I have never done, however, is win a world title. In fact I’ve never even been in a world title match. I’ve no grizzled veteran, but this is not my first promotion. I’ve been at this for a little while. I understand my time will come, but some weeks I’m more patient than others. I’m not feeling all that patient this week." * * * Last week. Audrey read the letter aloud to her husband. "Dear Mom and Dad. I know you don’t watch me wrestle all that much nowadays, as on separate occasions you have each in your own way expressed that you are ashamed of my actions lately. You have expressed shame in the man I have become, and I suppose I do not blame you. I am not the good little boy who made Mom call my apple juice ‘cactus juice’ because I watched a lot of western cartoons. Nor am I the innocent young man who thought that he could one day be a football announcer, and practiced by himself in his room with the volume on the TV down during Hawkeye games. No I have seen the real world, and it has chewed me up and swallowed me. It has crapped out a real asshole. You are not blameless for this." * * * Now In the limo, the promo took a brief pause as Lane communicated with the limo driver. "Yeah, you have to take a right at the third stop light. Jesus Christ. Seriously, how much do you get paid for just having to know where the fuck you’re going?" The glass between the driver and Lane closed. The River Rat sighed, and turned his attention back to us. "So who stands in my way in what has to be the biggest wrestling match of my life? Who will enter Thunder dome with me? Some old faces, some new faces, and some retarded faces." Lane mimics what he considers to be a retarded face, it is not pretty. "We got Timo Bologna, a relatively confused soul. Whether it be sitting around and trying to act evil with Tanaka, or being saved by Rhine with ‘You were meant for me’ by Jewel playing in the background…I can safely say there are few people in this life who I have ever found less interesting. But I’m not going to spend too much time talking about you Timo, because if you should perhaps get close to winning this tower match I think it will suddenly dawn on you that you are a midcarding piece of shit, and at midnight your carriage will turn into a pumpkin or something. Then we got Sean Sterling and Mayson Colby. These two are talented enough, I guess…but I’m relatively sure they might be a little distracted. In the middle of the match I think one of them is going to cut the other one’s head off, or maybe set them on fire, I’m not sure. It’s one of those blood feuds. Craaaaaaazy." Lane glances at a small piece of paper; he’s probably just reading names off at this point. "Ethan Frost is the one person Thunderdome that holds a win over me, so congrats on that. Your prize is I’m going to break your fucking arm again." Lane smiles thinly. * * * Last week Audrey continues to read the letter. Jerry now looks fully awake. "Do you remember when I just got my driver’s license? You would only let me drive down to the grocery store. You wouldn’t even let me drive into Dubuque to see a movie with my girlfriend. I have always felt one step behind. Maybe right now I’m making up for lost time. But despite all of this, I still want you to fly down to Washington. It is, as of now, the most important night of my life." * * * Now. The promo continues. "Prince Pride, Paul Cain, Kid Cool, Czar…" Lane does a double take. "Who the fuck is Czar?" He just shrugs, as there is no one around to answer the rhetorical question. "There’s a lot of people here who I am pretty indifferent about. I will scout you, and scout you well but, let’s be frank, you probably aren’t going to win this thing. Tough break, maybe next time. Which brings me to my dear friend Amy Campbell." You had to know this was coming. "It will be the second time we will clash that night, and I know you’re all jazzed about it aren’t ya? You dislike me very much, and I can assure you…the feeling is quite mutual. Our two tag-teams have been bloodied each other our fair shares. But you know what? Your husband is not going to be in Thunderdome. This cage is no team affair, and the last time I checked, you haven’t faired all that well in those kinds of situations lately. The kind of situations where you have to rely on your own ability. Because while you may be a talented little girl…you’re still just a little girl, in a big cage. You might be useful though. Maybe before all the men do the real fighting, we can all use you for a little relief…and we can repackage the first part of the match as ‘steel cage gangbang 7’. Lane winks. "Last, but certainly not least…Jonathan Rhine and Jason Cruise, a couple men whose souls are not for sale." The River Rat claps slowly. "Impressive, really. You have both risen very quickly to the top of FUSE without taking any shortcuts. I’m just going to say bravo for now…because I don’t want two men who are as talented as you to be mad at me. After all, I’m just Clinton Sage’s lackey after all, and could not possibly defend myself in that cage against such thoroughbreds." Lane cackles loudly. "Our true battle awaits gentlemen, and as big as this cage match is…I sense the road ahead will be far more eventful. But I’m getting ahead of myself. Instead I’ll just say to all of the Thunderdome participants…good luck, and keep your eyes on the prize." The River Rat pulls Clinton Sage’s Universal Title out of his gym bag, and shows it to the camera for effect. The Seventh Sin let him borrow it for the sake of this promo. "I’m going to turn the camera off now, so that you can leave me to prepare for the biggest match of my life. Don’t let the cold hard reality that I’m going to win hit you on the way out." Grinning, Lane Stevens reaches over and turns the camera off. We fade to black. * * * But we do not end. We go back to last week, where Lane’s mother is finishing his reading of the note out loud to his father. "But as much as I want you to fly down to Washington, part of me knows I know that you cannot. Part of me knows you must not. I am ready to do horrible things to reach my goals, and you do not need to see these things. I used to be embarrassed by your shame, it used to make me feel bad about myself. But I can no longer tolerate it. It sickens me. You sicken me. I have a new family now, and I do not need you anymore. Love, Lane" Audrey Stevens dropped the letter. * * * The limo is parked outside of Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport. The River Rat had been waiting for about fifteen minutes before the driver opened up the door, to let in his guest. It is of course not his parents, but instead a woman who looked to be in her early thirties. Her black hair was up in a bun, and she was dressed professionally. She wore thin framed glasses. "You must be Bridgette," Lane offered. "Bridgette Masters," she replied, and extended a hand. He kind of liked the idea of having an agent. "So, you think we can do a wrestling storyline where we have a lot of sex?" Lane asked. "Only if we do one afterwards where I cut your balls off," she replied, smiling politely. "Fair enough," Lane said, and chuckled aloud. "So sell me on you, and what you do." "Have you ever thought about endorsing your own brand of steel chairs?" she asked, immediately getting down to business. His eyebrows perked up.
