Cycles

18 November 2009

Date: 11/18/09 "Best to keep things in the shallow end, cause I never quite learned how to swim." – Maynard James Keenan * * * Laird, Ohio – sort of. In made no sense. But for whatever reason, Regan Guest didn’t even question it. She could visualize herself as she was now: mid twenties, long black hair, a statue-esque woman. Yet here she was, in her old neighborhood, a place she had not seen in over fifteen years. It was sunny the way that a postcard could be. Little girls wished for their wedding day to look like this, while little boys thought of opening day in the majors. Even though she was not a child, it felt as if people regarded her as such. Even though she was taller than the crossing guard she walked past, the woman’s words made her feel much smaller. "Now you be careful dear." She walked a little faster, past two old men sitting outside a diner. They were playing checkers and regarded her with toothless grins. She felt like she should hurry, that her Mom would be angry if she was late. There was only a faint voice in the back of her head reminding her that her mother was dead. That voice was brushed aside and forgotten; seemingly whisked away by the fall breeze. She stepped around papers that were blowing around at her feet, but paid them no mind. Up ahead she could see a road sign for Clayton Drive. It was the street she had grown up on, and suddenly her legs were carrying her faster! At first these old sights and sounds were comforting, but there was something slightly off about them that she did not care for. Regan would give anything to be inside her house. On her left was a sign for the Laird Christ Lutheran Church of Rebirth, only the last word was blurry. It was…actually blurry. As she got a few steps closer, it suddenly popped into focus, and the word had been replaced. The word was now "Stagnation". It wasn’t graffiti, it wasn’t vandals. The word was actually engraved. And she walked faster yet. Regan couldn’t help feeling alone. She had tried so hard for years to make everything work on her own: the restaurant, and going back to school, and taking care of her father, but she did not feel independent anymore. Not in this place, whatever this place was. All of her failures were pressing down on her now. She was practically running when she turned the corner onto Clayton Drive. The pieces of paper were still blowing around at her feet, but she still did not bother to look at them. The stench in the air, a familiar stench, was becoming almost a physical force, repelling her from her destination. And it was darker now, where had the sun gone? Her thoughts were coming too rapidly to process. Flickering in her mind was the female crossing guard, and the one discolored tooth she had in her mouth. She had seen that tooth before. Regan’s breathing became quicker, and she broke into a full out run. But alas, that run only lasted about a fourth of the block before she came to a dead stop. Clayton Drive looked exactly how she had remembered it, with the exception of the beige two story house that she grew up in. It had been replaced by a gray building. There was black lettering scrawled on the side that said, "Maybe he’s always been here". A crude arrow was drawn higher up on the wall, leading to text that said: "Or maybe not, let’s not get worked up about it". It was the same building that Dennis Darkhat (or Bernie Blackcap) inhabited. Different names for different games he had said to her once, and she didn’t know what that meant. She didn’t understand a lot of the things that the loan shark had said, and while she was able to pay him, she didn’t much care. But now, things were different. He was here. He was everywhere. She looked to the left to see her neighbor’s family beagle; she had believed his name was Pudge. It was lying on the side of the road gutted, his insides strewn about. It occurred to her that she had never seen anything more senseless in her whole life. "Get your ball," she pleaded at him desperately, in the process of hyper ventilating. "C’mon get your ball. We need to get out of here." His eyes still looked up at her, hopeful. This loan shark, this traveler, he was finding his way into the cracks of her mind. Places that he had no business. Up ahead a cowboy hat sat on her old mailbox. And on top of that hat was a note, balancing perfectly despite the breeze. Regan knew it was for her, and only her. She contemplated walking towards it for a moment, the curiosity almost overwhelming the rest of her sensibilities for a moment. But instead she turned and ran the other way, as fast as her legs would carry her. And the sky rumbled with satisfaction. * * * Regan sat upright in the hotel bed, still breathing heavily. Part of her wanted to keep on running, but the desire to not look like a complete crazy person won out in the end. Before recently, she could not remember having bad dreams since she was a little girl. The noise of her breathing woke up the other occupant of the bed. "Hrmph," Lane Stevens mumbled, and groggily pawed at her powder blue night gown. She slapped his hand away, which caused him to finally open his eyes. It was still dark, a little after four in the morning. "I was on a sail boat," he said dreamily, still sounding drunk from the night before, "I like sail boats." "Yeah well, my dream wasn’t like that at all." She turned away from him dramatically (something she immediately scolded herself for) and laid back down. The River Rat was not oblivious to her mood. Even in his most whacked out state he could pick out some of the most subtle details, although there was nothing subtle about this. In the darkness he smiled to himself and scooted his way over to her side of the bed, wrapping an arm around her. He had never "cuddled" with her before. When Stevens spoke again, his tone was even softer. "In a few hours I’ll run and get us breakfast. The tiny pancakes." Silver dollar pancakes were perhaps Regan’s favorite food. But just the same, she did not acknowledge his statement. The River Rat lifted his head and put his chin on her cheek, and inspected her face. "You want to go shopping tomorrow?" he asked, now intent on solving this puzzle. "Why are you being nice to me now?" she shot back sharply. This was not the calm, cool, and collected woman that had made a deal with Stevens on his balcony. This was not the woman that had traded sex for protection. The River Rat seemed to ponder that for a minute. "I dunno, you can’t really say whether you like being nice or mean until you try both. Then you’re just being close minded." She hadn’t the faintest clue whether he was serious or not, but it seemed possible that he was. "You’re a fucking lunatic," Regan whispered, with a mixture of warmth and malice that the written word cannot do justice. "Well," Lane said, "you’re fucking a lunatic, so…glass houses, and all that." Desade’s most brutal soldier, her warlord, watched the corner of Regan’s mouth like a hawk until he saw it begin to twist into a smile. Satisfied, he laid his head down on the pillow and closed his eyes. Maybe it wasn’t even her that he liked; maybe sometimes he liked surprising people. Lane wasn’t even sure. But he did know that tonight, he liked this. Who can say about tomorrow, he thought. In a few moments he was out again. Lane would dream of sailing on a boat to a wonderful place that would never allow his kind to come ashore. * * * A digital video disc was delivered to Foster Nackedy’s hotel room in St. Louis. This was its contents. Nothing, and then… Audio feed. "I still think about the Highwaymen, sometimes." If you watch SCCW TV, you know this voice. Foster knows this voice. "I think about the way that Jon inspired people, the way the tide turned, the look in Amy’s eyes when she really started to believe that the world could just…change. But guys like you and me bucko, we know the truth, don’t we?" Video feed. The same hotel room from our previous scene, only it is daylight. Lane and Regan had a suite at the Sheraton Plaza, and it was certainly not cheap. Unlike the Lane Stevens hotel rooms you may have heard about, there is no empty booze bottles or pills strewn about. All of the pictures are sitting neatly on the wall, and there are no condiment stains on the wall. After Regan’s bad dream, he decided to not get on her last nerve by creating his usual mess. Sunlight touched every corner of this room, another rarity for Mr. Stevens. Oh, and speaking of Lane, he was sitting on the edge of the bed directly in front of us, adjusting the camera. The River Rat was dressed in a white hotel robe, and looked to be completely clean shaven for a change. "We know that all we are looking at are cycles. People like Rhine show up, get everyone all jazzed…eventually someone like Alex learns how to bring him down a few pegs. Maybe eventually Alex gets too comfortable, someone comes along and fucks up her shit. Blah, blah, blah." He leaned back on the bed, putting his arms behind his head. This was a man that looked comfortable in his own skin. "Will good ever triumph in pro-wrestling, forever vanquishing evil so that we can be left with boring mat wrestling and faggot ass sportsmanship? Will Alexandra ever push this fed so far to its knees that everyone gives up, and Dusk doesn’t bring in a parade of lemmings to march off the cliff every few months? The answer to both of these questions, is no." He shakes his head as if to re-iterate the point. "The solution then, you may ask? Don’t risk much. Fuck, I’ve been in the thick of this thing ever since I entered FUSE. I’ve been on the ground, the front lines. And like you, I know where all the pieces are. Live to fight another day, so when the next Rhine comes along, you can stand behind him too. It’ll be good TV. Because your golden boy, your protégé…he’s burning out. He even fell for the bit with Katsidy, for god sake! Surely you had to know that was a bad idea. And when his silly tiff with Kennedy is over, we all know he’ll come for Alex, and we all know how that’ll turn out. Shit, he may be the best damn wrestler I’ve ever seen, I’ll give credit where its due. But he can’t handle all of this. The pressure, the fame, the women…" He smiled then. It was a cruel smile. "Or, OR…" Lane sat up suddenly, with the excitement of a young boy. "You could come work for me. Jeb Stewart flew the coop, I could use a soldier with a good head on his shoulders, someone I don’t have to mold so much. You’re already bitter and everything, shit I can use that!" He giggled then, a sound that was somehow crueler than his smile. "Anyway, think about it. Desade says she’ll throw a jacket in with your name on it and everything! Now I’m only going to make this offer once, so give it some thought." Desade’s Warlord reached forward, and before he turned the camera off he looked directly into the lens. "I’m not a liar, Foster. I’m just the bearer of bad news." Stevens made a comically sad face. "But hey, don’t take my word for it. Just look into your golden boy’s eyes sometime. See for yourself." Wink. Fade. * * * Regan sat in the hotel lobby, in the middle of a row of red chairs. Her head was buried in a Newsweek. The way she looked from a distance, was like a lonely traveler on a layover. She was dressed in jeans and a gray hoodie that belonged to Lane. There was nothing sensual about her appearance on this day, even her lips were chapped. A bellhop pushed open the door, holding it open for the brunette that passed through, pulling thin, fashionable sunglasses up into her short, yellow-brown hair. She walked like she was floating, her sky-high heels barely touching the floor. The man holding the door watched her surreptitiously. The man behind the front desk... less so. The way Kathryn Shaw looked from a distance was like a fantasy walking around in reality. The dress she wore was Frederick's of Hollywood's idea of business casual, tan skirt with a notch cut out of its hem, low-cut blouse with a short, pink jacket over it. The no-prescription eyeglasses she replaced the sunglasses with were on the edge of sassy. She looked less like a secretary, more like a porn star on a way to a naughty office shoot. As Shaw paused in the door, her eyes adjusting to the lower lobby light, she caught sight of the girl huddled in the lobby and flowed that way, as if by accident. 'Regan!' Kathryn beamed. 'Fancy meeting you here!' Regan looked up from the magazine, and willed her face into a polite smile. It wasn’t that she disliked Katsidy; certainly she had Shaw to thank for the fact that she was not homeless. As a former protégée, she had picked up more than a few useful tricks over the years, some of them as effortless as breathing. But she couldn’t help but get the feeling that every gesture that this woman made to her was part of a prank she would never understand. When they had first met Regan felt like a doll that Kathryn was using for dress-up. "Heya Kat," she offered, suddenly feeling smaller. "Lane’s upstairs, making one of his videos. I feel kinda silly being in the same room." 'Oh, right. I forgot about that.' Shaw's grin hooked across her lips. 'You should have been there back in the day. Everyone did them. Like... everyone. You should really learn how to be mean to people.' The Kitten dropped into the seat beside Regan. 'What do you know about Jonathan Rhine?' 'Which one is he again?' Kathi couldn't help but laugh, crossing her legs in a long, lingering motion. The men at the front desk swore she did it in slow motion. 'You'll learn, I guess. Just like you'll learn when you're supposed to, like, hit them with things, too.' Regan immediately cringed. "Look, I’m sorry, it’s just I thought this would all be easier and…" She didn’t really have a game plan of where that sentence was supposed to go, so it just withered and died. "Don’t apologize to me. In fact, might want to save it for Alex," Katsidy mused, her attempt at good humor somewhat transparent, "she cared about that match more than Laney, I’m sure." "All is well that ends well, right?" Regan asked, referring to Lane Stevens’ GTT victory over Clinton Sage, and immediately felt stupid. She had no idea how to act around this woman. Not unless you learn your lesson.' She slid a teasing smile over to the counter, but lowered her voice. 'I was supposed to distract the referee so you could get the spray out. That's why you were carrying the stupid thing to start with!' 'I said I was...' 'I know, you're sorry. Look, I got you this job, Ray. Don't fuck this up, or I'll end up looking bad.' "Just been a weird couple weeks. I’ll be a real cunt again in no time." Regan chuckled at her own statement, and was relieved to hear Kathryn do the same. You'd better be.' Despite the lightness of tone, Shaw's eyes made the threat a promise. 'The next time, I'll make you prove that.' 'Your cruel... device! Your blood... like ice!' The muted growl of Alice Cooper's 'Poison' emanated from Kathryn's clutch, and the Siren hooked a pink Bluetooth over her ear. 'This is Kathi, how can I make your day?' Shaw stopped, touching Regan on the knee. 'As a matter of fact, I'm here already, Lane. Just having a chat with your Girl Friday here.' The laugh was light, mocking. 'You're about a half-hour late for that, baby. We'll be right up.' 'What was that all about?' Regan tried not to sound interested. 'Oh, he wanted to know if you were going to get me off. I told him I'd already gotten off before I left.' Shaw rolled her eyes as Guest reined in her shock. 'I'm totally joking. He hung up after I told him we were down here. Said we'd saved him a call.' Kathryn stood smoothly, offering the crook of her elbow to the thin girl. 'Smile for the cute boy at the counter as we leave.' Regan did as she was told, and felt confidence begin to swell in her again. And when they passed a folded up piece of paper on the ground that reminded her of the one in her dream, she simply walked past it and paid it no mind. * * * Across the lobby two pairs of eyes watched the girls leave. "Where’s Stevens?" "Oh, he’s here. They’re off to some fancy meeting." "When we gonna fuck his shit up?" "Whenever we want, my friend. Whenever we want." One of the men itched the tattoo on his upper arm; it was of a black cowboy hat.
