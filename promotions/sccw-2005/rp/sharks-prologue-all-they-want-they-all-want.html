Sharks (Prologue): All They Want, They All Want

Date: Dual Halo RP March 11, 2010 The pub was called Charlie McGee's, and all it wanted was to be the best dive bar in town. It was a rough-and-tumble place where the wooden furniture had been scarred by brawls, the mugs had never seen the inside of a dishwasher, and the servers had skin like leather or were missing teeth, or both – even the women. The sign said, ''Est. 1898,'' and for most of the bar's 112 years of existence, it lived up to its reputation as the place to be on the wrong side of the tracks (or the place to avoid, depending on how one looked at it). In the late 1970s, however, everything changed. A rash of home invasions in the neighborhood led then-owner Charles the Third (uncle of current owner Charlene) to slash prices for off-duty police officers. Since that day, the place became somewhat notorious as a cop hangout. It still hadn't shed its rather unsavory image, however, and rumors always swirled about Internal Affairs keeping on eye on the place. That didn't keep the customers away – Bernie Dixon counted at least four of his colleagues enjoying a Guinness and the Sharks game, and that didn't even include the two that shared his table. (Though one was the medical examiner, though he supposed she didn't count.) Newly minted Lieutenant Bernard Dixon looked like nothing more than a man shaped from Play-Doh and then left out in the rain. His head was lumpy, eyes just a little too close together, his forehead just a little too big – he liked to say it was because of his big brain, though no one who talked to him actually believed that. Dixon's promotion – earned on the back of his discovering Detective Helena Maddox was apparently on the take – was fresh, less than a month old, and he hadn't yet taken the opportunity to purchase a proper ''detective suit'', so he still wore his rumpled, out-of-style dress clothes, the same ones he bought almost a decade earlier. ''It's like... it's like they don't have a clue – not a damn clue – what kind of cancer Helena was,'' Bernie was saying. It was his favorite topic. ''I keep tellin' the captain... every one of her cases needs to be reopened, starting with the Fox murder. Maddox is... that cunt fucked this entire department. She fucked us good.'' Bernie smiled to the side for the sake of the diminutive, bespectacled blonde seated at his left. ''No offense,'' he said quietly. ''It's fine.'' Christina Metcalf was only the interim coroner, transferred from Sacramento after San Leandro's regular medical examiner was injured during a daring midnight raid of the morgue, one that resulted in the theft of the corpse of Lauren Fox – wrestling's ''Savant''. The girl hardly seemed old enough to serve as proper CSI, but her résumé was stellar. She graduated at the top of her class from the prestigious University of California at San Francisco School of Medicine at the tender age of 24, and she had several glowing letters of recommendation from the brass in Sacramento, espousing her dedication and gushing over her attention to detail. Not to mention that she was hot and could hold her booze, Bernie thought. Christina had become his regular drinking buddy these last few weeks, ever since she'd pointed Dixon to the envelope full of jewelry secreted in Helena Maddox's locker. He never imagined a coroner could look that good with the lab coat off, but Metcalf cleaned up nice, and Bernie wouldn't deny he'd had daydreams about doing things one shouldn't do in the morgue – dreams she didn't exactly discourage. ''It's not like I'm not used to that kind of salty talk,'' Christina continued, her smile emerging briefly from behind the cloud of her frown. ''I just... I can't help but wonder if they were, like, colluding.'' ''Who?'' ''Maddox and that creepy Pierce lady. I mean, I don't know Helena the way you guys do, but do you really think she'd... she'd have 'fucked', as you put it... I mean, do you think she'd have done it all by herself?'' ''Oh, yeah.'' Dixon nodded firmly. ''She's as dirty as they come. If we just had something to go on, Captain Danvers would have to listen.'' ''Well, I'll keep looking,'' Metcalf promised. ''I'm still sifting through Darryl's notes. Did you guys know he used some kind of cipher?'' ''No shit?'' Dixon smiled as Christina half-turned to face him, one jean-clad leg only just touching his. ''I mean, I knew he was a freak – most of you guys who like digging around inside dead bodies are.'' The grin that touched Bernie's lips was teasing, but it still drew a playful swat from the girl at his side. ''So, what, is there some kind of secret decoder ring?'' ''Not funny, Bernie.'' Christina smiled around the mouth of her beer bottle. ''I feel like we should be doing something other than drinking beer and bitching about things, is all.'' ''Well, what'd you have in mind?'' ''I don't know, that's just it.'' Christina dropped the bottle onto the table, her thumb skimming through the condensation building on the side. She lifted her thin smile up to the third person at the table. ''Well, well, I never thought you were going to get off the phone!'' Roland Morante made a sound that was half a grumble and half a sigh. ''Laugh it up, Metcalf,'' he grumbled. Morante had a smile that could still make ladies weak in the knees, but that was about his only trick anymore. His hair was thinning, his gut growing, and his attitude wasn't getting any better. These were the moments that the detective relished – ones away from the prying eyes of the precinct, especially his nagging partner. ''They say it's all gone, Bern.'' ''All of it?'' Bernie Dixon's eyebrows lifted. ''Fuck.'' ''All of what?'' Metcalf's smile might have grown curious, but it was buried too quickly in the neck of that beer bottle to be sure. Roland rubbed at the back of his neck, fiddling with a wedding ring that no longer held any meaning for him. ''I tell you about the blonde I busted?'' ''Do you mean 'busted'-busted, or just arrested?'' The coroner's sandy brows quirked in humor. ''Because I think we've all heard the rumors.'' Roland shrugged one shoulder, and Christina's good humor faded with the drop of his eyes. For him not to respond to her gentle needling meant something serious. ''I was... I was down at the Pawn Club, and some chick picked Kenny Truong's pocket.'' ''Aw, man.'' Dixon frowned. ''I told you not to go down there. You know Truong's bad business...'' ''I know, I know.'' Roland blew out a sigh. ''Sometimes a guy doesn't have a choice.'' ''Truong?'' Metcalf arched an eyebrow. ''I've heard that name before.'' ''Eh.'' Dixon waved a hand through the air. ''Smalltime chink thug. Or is he a Jap? I lose track. He's involved in some shit, mainly gambling dens, opium importing, fencing... that kind of garbage. I keep tellin' Rollie here that he oughta stay away from that kind of stuff.'' ''I know, I know...'' Roland shifted uncomfortably, changing the subject in an effort to gain some momentum. ''Point is, we caught this hot little blond number, tits out to here, dress that was basically just a handkerchief. Had her, had the wallet with her prints on it, had it all. Sure, she was just a pickpocket, but it felt nice. Felt like the old days, before... before I was on the pills.'' ''Aww,'' Christina cooed. She reached across the table, touching her small hand to his. Roland's smile oozed across his lips, like a slug pulled away from his teeth. '''Cept she's gone. Some big, blond fucker bailed him out, and the evidence? Ken Truong's wallet? Gone. Fire in the evidence room – in that particular part of the evidence room.'' He held up his phone, shaking it. ''That was them. They figure it was some kind of firebombing.'' ''Over a wallet?'' Dixon's brows shot up. ''The fuck?'' ''Yeah. You'll never guess whose business card the girl had on her.'' ''Maddox?'' Morante nodded. ''Fuckin' Maddox.'' ''You think Maddox is involved in this?'' Christina prompted. ''Maybe even Pierce?'' ''I figure...'' Roland rolled his head on his neck on his shoulders. ''I figure there's something rotten goin' on with that girl, yeah?'' Metcalf whirled to her feet, both palms on the table. ''Then you guys have to do something, right? I mean, this is like every movie. The new detective teams with a disgraced colleague to root out a conspiracy in a quiet little suburban town.'' She grinned, wide and charming. ''Sure, you don't think of you guys as heroes, but I'd be your plucky sidekick – the kind of role Reese Witherspoon would play. And really, when the bad guys have tits out to here, you're at least going to get played on Cinemax.'' Bernie Dixon chuckled, but he was clearly considering it. ''I always wanted to be famous.'' ''And I still wanna be the best,'' Morante put in. ''See!'' Metcalf beamed. ''You guys want things. You're dynamic personalities – charming and witty and smart. And—'' She cut herself off with a lopsided smile. ''I clearly have had a little bit too much tonight. And the only solution to that is another beer. You guys want anything?'' Bernie had his chin in his hand, lost in thought, his index finger tapping his lips. ''Dixon!'' Morante nudged the new detective. ''Lady's talking to you.'' ''Hrm?'' The other man blinked, straightening. ''Nah, I'm good, Chris.'' She was still smiling as she turned, meandering back to the bar. Morante watched her go, murmuring softly under his breath. ''That is a girl who knows how to wear a pair of jeans.'' ''You really think we can do this?'' Bernie asked. ''Like she said? Track down what Maddox and that Pierce girl? Find out what they're doing?'' ''Yeah.'' Morante's shot glass paused in front of his fish-like lips. ''I mean, you've gotta admit... some really weird shit's been happening in our city, man.'' Dixon nodded. ''Weird fuckin' shit,'' he said emphatically. ''I'm pretty sick of it.'' Roland raised his glass in agreement, throwing back the shot. He winced at the burning in his throat, looking back to Metcalf, chatting up the barkeep. ''You an' me, famous detectives?'' he mused. ''Not such a crazy dream.'' ''Nah.'' Bernie Dixon popped his knuckles, one finger at a time. ''Not so.'' If they only knew. Her name was Quinn Gregory, and all she wanted was a few minutes more of sleep. The smell of bacon stirred the sixteen-year-old from her sleep, a smile drifting onto the girl's features. She rolled over, lifting a slender hand to scrub the sleep out of her eyes. ''Mom?'' the girl asked the air, her voice half-swallowed by a yawn. The sound disturbed a tiny, orange kitten from its perch atop Quinn's hope chest, who unfurled from his tight ball with a mewl. His name was Hermes, and he arched his back as he stood, hopping from the chest onto the bed – then onto the girl herself. ''Herm!'' Quinn whined, easing onto her back. The kitten trotted up the lanky teen's body, standing proudly on her sternum and announcing himself with a hollow meow. The teen dropped her hand onto his forehead, thumb rubbing along his forehead as she tried to get her bearings. The large, aquamarine numbers on her alarm clock proudly proclaimed the time – 6:28 AM. Her alarm wouldn't even go off for almost forty-five minutes. ''Do you know what's going on?'' Quinn asked the kitten, but she only got a louder purr in response, which turned into a yelp as Hermes leapt off, dropping down to the plush, blue-gray carpet. The girl sat up, hands on knees, tar-black hair dangling before her eyes. She straightened, arching her own back in a mirror of the kitten's motion. One hand smoothed her hair back as she plucked her black cat's-eye glasses from the nightstand, easing to her feet. ''Coffee,'' Gregory mumbled, staggering for the door. The moment she pulled it open, Hermes trotted between her ankles, racing out into the hallway. ''Well, hello there, kitty!'' Quinn knew that voice, even if she didn't quite recognize the tone. ''Are you hungry?'' Hermes answered with a defiant meow, and a drum roll of cat food spilling into a plastic bowl followed, hiding the self-proclaimed Devil's Daughter's quiet footsteps as she padded up the hallway. Blearily, Gregory crossed into the kitchen, her hands on autopilot as she pulled down a Jabba the Hutt coffee mug and filled it with piping hot brew. Her mother didn't say anything -- she knew better than to disturb her daughter before the routine was complete, a tiny teaspoon of sugar dropping into her morning cup of Joe. Quinn turned, sea-green eyes slitting shut as she lifted her mug, inhaling deeply. Her fist sip was greedy and not very ladylike, but that flood of warmth (and caffeine) was the only thing able to prop open her eyes long enough to meet her mother's gaze – or whatever passed for it this morning. When she moved to Oakland a year or so earlier, it hadn't been years of separation or Alexandra Pierce's strange double life as Desade that Quinn had difficulty dealing with, it was the so-called Spider's propensity to live in someone else's skin. Pierce never seemed quite comfortable just being herself, and, for a long time she ran through personas the way most people changed clothes. While the habit faded during Alex's yearlong affair with former SCCW Universal Champion Amy Campbell, it was coming back more and more since the pair split in early February. All in all, if forced to pick one of her mother's former vices to return, Pierce's identity crisis was far preferable to many other options. Quinn was practiced enough that she could usually pick a few telltale features out of whatever face the Spider used – high, chiseled cheekbones or the tiny bump along the bridge of her nose where it had been broken and didn't reset properly. Certainly, few of Pierce's colleagues would be able to identify the blonde at the stove, scooping a frying pan of scrambled eggs onto a flowered plate. It was the little details that sold the masquerade. Her shoulder-length, rust-red ringlets had been replaced by a drab, dishwater blond ponytail, and the infamous stony-gray gaze that earned her the nickname ''Medusa'' had gone green, but that wasn't the extent of it. There was a small beauty mark penciled just below her right cheekbone, and a mark for a wedding ring she'd never worn had been carefully marked into her finger. Pierce wore baggy, dove-gray sweatpants and a soft pink babydoll tee, a far cry from the snow-white pantsuit once synonymous with her name. ''Which one is this one?'' Quinn asked, levering herself into one of the tall stools that surrounded the kitchen island. ''I'm not sure yet.'' Alex added three slices of crisped-to-perfection bacon to the plate and set it before her daughter. ''She feels like a Christie or a Colleen.'' ''Ooh, Caroline!'' the girl suggested. ''Hmm.'' Alex brushed her hands along the front of the t-shirt, her fingers touching the space where the ring should have been, a nervous gesture only part of her realized was affected. ''Caroline could work.'' ''What's the occasion?'' The younger Pierce was in many ways a natural at the business her mother had worked so long to excel in, but one thing she could never quite pull off was quieting the nerves in her voice. ''I was thinking of going for a run.'' Behind her daughter and slightly turned, the Spider's smile was unreadable, but her light tone could only dance so much around the fact both women knew – ever since Amy Campbell left her, driven away by actions so unconscionable they gave Desade nightmares, the GTT7 Champion had pretty much become a homebody, crushed by the loss of the first person she'd ever given her heart to. ''Really.'' Quinn struggled to pull back the doubt from her voice, to mute the fear that there was something going on her mother couldn't share. ''Yeah.'' Pierce's long fingers spread the blinds that hid the window over the sink. ''I felt out of shape in Seattle,'' she said. ''I've grieved enough.'' ''So does this mean you're back to you? Or... does it mean... does it mean, that... that... you know.'' The disguised Spider turned her head only a few degrees, and even with brilliant green contacts hiding the granite chips of her eyes, her gaze was enough to pin the sixteen-going-on-forty-year-old girl in place. ''Does it mean that I'm Desade again?'' The gentlest curve of her eyebrows softened Alex's entire face. ''No. I'm not going back to that place. I'm just going out for a run.'' ''I can come with.'' Quinn shoveled the eggs into her mouth by the forkful. ''I mean, school doesn't start till eight.'' ''I'd like to go by myself, if you don't mind.'' The blind snapped shut as Pierce released it. ''Thought I might stop by the office for a while.'' Her lips creased just slightly – more the afterimage of a smile than a real one. ''I'm sure the place has gone to hell these last few weeks.'' The dark-haired girl's head bobbed in a nod. ''A little. I've been by after school a couple times, but... yeah. I never know what to throw out.'' Pierce strapped a black iPod Nano onto her upper arm, tightening the Velcro. ''I'm not going to get some frantic call from the truant officer, am I?'' ''No, I'm totally going. They're holding auditions for the school play tomorrow, and I'm not about to have detention on my record.'' Alex snatched a pair of headphones from the countertop. ''What play are you doing?'' ''Les Miz.'' Quinn lifted her head, tucking a long lock of tar-black hair over one ear, a nervous habit she picked up from her mother. ''I'm trying out for Eponine.'' ''I didn't know you could sing.'' The Spider was meticulous; she'd even dyed the brows that rose at the thought. An honest smile broke free from her controlled expression. ''If you get the part, I'm absolutely coming. Keep me posted?'' ''You bet.'' With that, Alexandra Pierce pushed out into the chilly Bay Area morning. She never turned on the iPod – it, like much of the outfit, was just for show. His name was Antonio Vigzoni, and all he wanted was for the owner of this office to show the fuck up already. Vigzoni was the type of man that used to be called ''swarthy'' before that word gained negative connotations, with sun-kissed skin and a lopsided smile. On his good days, he reminded people of another Antonio – Antonio Banderas, with his oozing charm and thick accent. Today was not one of those days. He sat behind the wheel of a clunky, 1980s sedan, his mahogany-brown hair out of its usual tie and hanging loosely over his sculpted features. His breath was ragged, the gleam gone from his eyes. The car radio was on – not because Antonio actually liked smooth jazz, but because he needed something to focus on other than pain and his increasing worry. The strip mall was like a hundred others in the city; it had a dollar dry cleaner's, a Subway, a dentist's office labeled only as ''Dentist,'' and a florist's. What made this one special was the office at the far end that didn't yet have a neon sign on the marquee. From the outside, you'd never know what happened behind that door, and, in fact, Vigzoni nearly missed it. The frosted glass door simply read ''API Investigations,'' with a phone number underneath it. The shades were drawn, the lights were out, and there hadn't been movement from... well, from anywhere in the parking lot, really. The Subway would open soon (who knew they served breakfast?), but the employees only shuffled in, stifling their yawns and not even engaging in conversation with each other. No one noticed the early-model sedan, or the man behind the wheel. Vigzoni glanced at the clock on the console as it ticked over to 6:57. Almost an hour since he'd parked, ninety minutes since he'd sent the email. Antonio couldn't be sure the old protocols were still being followed, he couldn't be certain that she'd even listen. But he didn't have anywhere else to go. The smooth jazz station went to commercial, and the man reached out to the dial to find something to focus on. His hand shook, a tremor that ran all the way up his arm. He drew his hand back into his lap, closing his fist over it, his brow furrowed. ''Fretta, ragazza,'' he whispered. ''Fretta.'' Antonio wasn't sure when he drifted off, but the hollow thokk of knuckles on glass jerked him upright in his seat. ''Eh?'' He caught a glimpse of the clock as he snapped his head to the window. 7:25. ''Are you all right?'' the girl was asking. ''Mister, can you hear me?'' Vigzoni squinted, having difficulty focusing. Was that Alexandra? He thought so – maybe – but it'd been so long since they worked together. Antonio rolled down the window, smiling. ''I am sorry?'' he asked. ''I did not hear.'' ''Oh, awesome. You're not dead,'' she beamed. ''You don't look so good – and that accent! Are you French? Like Rousseau on Lost?'' Ah, the pass phrase – so it was her. He allowed himself to relax, released a breath he wasn't aware that he'd held. ''Like Rousseau, sí.'' ''Look, I'm a temp at this office over there... you want to come in and, like, have some water or something?'' ''It is always the same with you, isn't it, Alessandra? Always with the lies and the parts.'' Vigzoni's lower lip quivered just slightly. ''We do not have time for you to play-act for sleepy sandwich artists or whoever it is you think may be listening to this conversation.'' ''Fine.'' She straightened, some of the slack coming out of her posture. Without asking, she pulled open the driver's side door. ''But we're doing this inside.'' Antonio groaned softly as he clambered to his feet, his unsteady hand clutched against his stomach. His steps behind Alexandra's were slow, almost shambling; an onlooker might say, ''Hey, look at that zombie guy following that girl'', if they were paying attention. No one was ever paying attention. Pierce had always been faster than he was; Vigzoni made his living off his charm, whereas she made hers based on her talent. His slow progress earned him a sharp look and a hidden wave from the Spider, and he sped as much as he could. Alex turned the key, pushing into her office with a quiet sigh. The interior of the API office was tasteful, well-kept, and almost disgustingly organized. The receptionist's desk sat empty – no fulltime secretary had been hired, and there was little indication the Spider even considered it a priority. Part of the waiting room was like a shrine built around a small television, but Vigzoni thought it wise not to ask, following the blonde into the inner office. ''This is not what I expect from you, Alessandra,'' Antonio said, settling into a high-backed chair as the Spider crossed behind the desk. ''What did you imagine?'' Pierce raised her eyes to him only briefly; if she noticed his pallid, worn complexion or the tremors that shook his right hand, she didn't show it. A small stack of messages waited beside the telephone, but she didn't so much as flick through them as she took her seat. Vigzoni half-turned in the chair, giving the place another once-over. ''It is too kitschy, too... too cliché. Alessandra Pierce does not work out of a strip mall.'' ''She does now.'' Her tone sharpened, only noticeable to someone who'd known her as long as Antonio had. ''It's been a while, Antonio. I must say you've looked better.'' He coughed into his hand. ''It is just a flu bug. Not important.'' ''What is important?'' Pierce folded her hands atop the table. ''As I said, it's been some time, Vigzoni. You're lucky the email address you used is still one I check.'' ''I know.'' He sunk back into the chair, stifling another coughing fit. ''I came across information. Something I thought you should be aware of.'' ''Well?'' She lifted one hand, just far enough to gesture for him to continue. ''What is so pressing? As you can see, I have quite a bit of business to catch up on, and, as nice as it is to reminisce, not every memory I have of you is a good one, so you might want to—'' ''It is about your daughter.'' Pierce straightened noticeably in the chair, her chin lifting to allow the full brunt of her glare to fall on him. ''What of her?'' ''You know that Margaret Winters... that your mentor, ah, she bred you. That your little girl is the product of a conscious desire to produce such a child.'' ''I know this.'' Alex bit off the words more than pronouncing them. Over the last year, she'd gone out of her way to shed the ice queen façade she'd used to protect herself for so long. The clipped speech, the carefully protected body language, and the emotionless voice – all of it was part of her past, one she was trying desperately to run from. Every once in a while, however, the old Spider – the one they called ''Desade'' – came out. Threats to her daughter were the surest way to make that happen. Her posture was suddenly perfect, thin lips pressed together in a line. Vigzoni was unmoved. ''Ah, but do you know everything?'' He raised a finger. ''You think the child was to secure an alliance – a father would do anything for the mother of his child. I am...'' He convulsed in another fit of deep, full-body coughs. ''Antonio?'' Alexandra's brow wavered more than arched. ''May I get you something?'' ''No, no.'' He waved a hand. ''Grazi, but we must finish our business first.'' ''As you say.'' Pierce inclined her head. ''Please, continue.'' ''I am led to believe that your girl was Margaret Winters' endgame, not a means to it.'' Alex's palms were pressed to the blotter on her desk, hard enough that the skin around her knuckles had started to whiten. ''Kieran Whitechurch is under the impression he is her father.'' Vigzoni's smile was a nasty thing. ''Never heard of him. And I do not know the truth in any event.'' He waved off the question. ''It is not important.'' ''It is important to me.'' If the chilly veneer was what lied under the surface, then the burning rage that coiled at her core burned just beneath that. ''Damn it, Antonio. I'm trying to give her a normal—'' Vigzoni leaned forward, right elbow on his knee. ''Scuttlebutt says Margaret is back.'' ''Scuttlebutt is distressingly correct in this instance. I am unsure what she is planning, but... yes, she is back.'' Pierce's tone was scathing, her hands no longer flat on the desk, but balled into fists. ''Antonio...'' Her brow furrowed, and for a moment, he was certain the green-tinted contacts would simply be melted by the charcoal gray they concealed. ''Allow an old friend his fun.'' The man's coughing returned. ''I think I will take that water now.'' Alex flowed to her feet, visibly gathering herself as she crossed the room. ''I know you like your wordplay games, Tony, your... your cute turns of phrase.'' She filled a paper cone with water. ''But you called me.'' ''I did, yes.'' His smile had lost some of its luster, but it was bright enough as he accepted the cup. ''Thank you.'' She didn't return to her chair, simply leaning against the wall. ''Now you've implied there is something more to my daughter's conception, that the man who has confessed to being Quinn's father may not truly be her father, and then you change the subject?'' The Spider consciously softened her expression – or, more properly, she put on a mask of gentility. ''You can understand why I'm concerned here, can't you?'' Vigzoni didn't have the weighty glare that his old associate did, but it was like an ice pick through her hasty pretense. ''Don't try to work me, Alessandra. It is beneath you.'' He took a long, noisy sip from the cup. ''They are connected, you know. Ms. Winters' return and my questions about your daughter.'' ''If you mean to tell me they are still interested in Quinn, that I should... should be doing something to keep her safe...'' ''I am saying that Quinn is not the only aspirant for whatever Winters is planning.'' It was as if the breath caught in Alex's chest, as if her heart skipped a beat. ''There are others?'' Antonio nodded, his Adam's apple bobbing. ''Couples brought together to have a child for whatever mad game she is playing, children born the last fifteen years all across the globe.'' ''You're certain of this?'' His laughter came from somewhere deep inside him. ''As certain as I can be.'' ''How?'' Her head was still down, but her eyes flicked up. ''How can you be so sure? I killed Maggie...'' ''I think by now you know that isn't true.'' His lips pursed in consideration. ''I am sure because I...'' Vigzoni's face had always been remarkably malleable to his whim; when he forced a smile, she only knew it was fake through long years of exposure. ''I've been poisoned.'' She pushed off the desk, crossing to him. ''Poisoned?'' ''Decker.'' He said it as if it explained everything. Her brow furrowed. ''He's dead.'' ''As dead as Margaret Winters, yes.'' Another fit of coughing claimed him, and Vigzoni pulled a royal blue handkerchief from the inside pocket of his coat. ''I do not know what their plan is, Alessandra. I came to you because you have a stake in this, because I know what you want.'' '''Want'?'' She chuckled, a bitter sound. ''I never get anything I want, Antonio. If I can find out what she's planning, what it has to do with my daughter, maybe... maybe Quinn can have the life I've never been able to enjoy. Tell me what you know about this plan. I'll do the rest.'' He nodded, his trembling hand fumbling with the flap that covered his pocket. ''She calls it... damn, I can't get it.'' Alex fell into a light crouch at his side, slipping a small girl's diary from the pocket. The book wasn't very thick, sky blue with a ribbon tied around it. Alex raised those falsely green eyes to meet his bleary brown gaze. Vigzoni dropped the cloth from his lips, forcing his face into another smile. ''I thought it was... befitting.'' There was blood on his teeth. ''She calls it Fagin.'' ''Antonio?'' Dyed blond brows nearly flew off her face. ''Tony, are you okay?'' ''I think... I think perhaps you will want to call an ambulance now.'' His eyes rolled back into his head. Alexandra Pierce was on the phone immediately. She didn't play a role when she dialed 911, didn't force tears or desperation into her voice. She didn't have to. Her name was Julissa Minorez, and all she wanted was a good deal on some shoes. No, that was a lie. So much of her life was, these days. If someone held a gun to Julissa's head, she'd say... she wasn't sure what she'd say. Probably that she wanted it all back the way it used to be. Back when this was about the wrestling and not all of the other garbage that had come along afterwards. Minorez lied on her stomach on a plush, king-sized bed in a hotel room just outside Las Vegas, facing the foot of the bed as she aimlessly surfed the web. Her feet were raised, her ankles crossed and heels resting on what could only be called an ''apple bottom''. It was a remarkably girlish posture for a woman whose reputation would paint her as a whore, whose wardrobe on SCCW programming was often little more fabric than a bath towel. Even then, all she was wearing was a lacy demi bra and matching thong, but she'd long ago learned to overcome her shyness in the name of getting what she wanted. The shower ran in the next room, her boyfriend, simultaneously SCCW Gateway and LiveWire Champion Jadian Bridden, taking a long shower after they christened the hotel room. He'd been so withdrawn as they approached the Defiance 3 event – she'd hoped that a particularly vigorous romp would bring out the man she knew, not the man that he was becoming. ''You want me to join you?'' she called. He didn't answer, and the brunette frowned, perfectly arched brows cresting over her hazel eyes. He didn't trust her anymore – they didn't talk as much as they used to, didn't have their little, shared moments, and he'd developed the unfortunate tendency of sleeping on his side, so she could no longer drift off on his shoulder.
