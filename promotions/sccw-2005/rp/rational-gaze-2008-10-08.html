Rational Gaze

8 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/08 "You can’t lock yourself in there forever, you know. Eventually you’re going to need to come out." Mervin Humperdink considered himself to be a fairly reasonable man, the sole exception being the company he kept. "Nuh unh!" At times Jared Sykes was anything but reasonable. At times his behavior could be politely described as childish, and in the crudest of terms as "fucking retarded." The expression on Mervin’s face as he pounded against a locked bathroom door seemed to indicate that on this day he felt it closer to the latter. "I’ve got plenty of water. I can stay here forever." Mervin’s shoulders drooped. He’d been on the losing side of this argument for the last ten minutes which had seemed like an eternity if only that he really had to pee. "Jared, please," pleaded Mervin, "it was just a match." "You don’t get it, do you?" The response from the other side of the door was muffled, yet quite belligerent. "I was so close. I practically had the thing won. I mean, yeah, Charley’s a tough guy and all, but still. So close!" If he didn’t think it would take him thirty minutes to get home, Mervin would have been in his car and on the way there. If he was any stronger, he would have tried to force in the door. Since both of those were out of the question, he did the only thing he could: gingerly bounce from foot to foot in an effort to keep from wetting himself. "These things happen, Jared. You can’t… oh man… you can’t treat it like it’s the end of the world. Now can you please open the door?! I’m dying out here." "Losing straight up is fine, dude. I can live with that. It’s the damn interference that pisses me off. He had no business being there. None whatsoever." Mervin winced. A drop may or may not have escaped him. "Look, Bridden is an asshole. We both knew that. He’s probably still pissed that you made him dress up like a peach. Now if you’ll just come out…" Mervin exhaled a sigh of relief as he heard lock disengage; a moment later Jared peeked his head through the partially open door. "Bridden? Who said anything about Bridden?" The full-bladdered nerd raised his eyebrows in surprise. "Then who are you," he paused, as the realization sunk in, "You. Are not. Serious. If this is about the thumb wrestling, so help me…" Jared opened the door fully, stepping through it and out of his friend’s way. Regardless of how angry he was at being detained Mervin took comfort in knowing that he could now empty his bladder. Jared started off down the hall on his way to the kitchen, likely in search of something to munch on. "Ethan Peters is a dead man." Jared was already in the kitchen fishing through drawers when Mervin left the bathroom. "I suppose you’re also upset about the bagel?" Jared didn’t bother to look up when the question came. Instead he simply shuffled through the various culinary instruments, taking a moment to pick up and examine a partially melted spatula. "Did you have to bring that up? That one still kind of stings." Mervin turned slowly to face the door jam and gently bounced his forehead against it a few times. "Do I even want to know what you’re looking for?" he asked, still rapping against the wooden frame with his skull. "A knife. Can’t frickin’ find one. I feel like I’m cooking with the Dead Man’s Hand. It’s like ‘Where’s the knife? Where’s the knife? You know the knife is here somewhere.’ Damned kitchen elves must be stealing the silverware agai-ow!" He pulled his right hand back sharply away from the drawer, shaking tender fingers, "I think I found one." "To what exactly do we owe the pleasure?" For a man in his forties Darren Stracker was still in impeccable shape, and if the fading colors in his hair hadn’t betrayed his age it would have been quite difficult to glean his maturity just by looking at him. Darren Stracker had once wrestled on the American independent wrestling scene under the guise of ‘The Prophet’, one of countless men to use that name at one point. He was talented, a second-generation grappler, but he never made much of a name for himself as a wrestler. His legacy came from the people he and his father helped to train. "I’d almost forgotten what it was like. You know, to be new at this." Jared Sykes was one of those people. He’d slipped into the Stracker School of Wrestling virtually unnoticed, and had taken a seat on one of the benches that ran along a wall near a practice ring. The hood of his sweatshirt was pulled low so as to cover his face, but it hadn’t worked, and so far twice the in-ring session was disrupted by glances in his direction. "What’s with the newbs?" Jared continued, his eyes still lingering on the ring. Darren stepped over to the bench, quickly tying back his shoulder length hair in a ponytail. "I’d say some of them are probably starstruck. The others are probably dumb enough to think they could take you. We do have a few like that in this season’s class." "How’d they know it’s me?" Darren could only laugh. "Probably because they all walk by your section of the display at least twice every day. You can take the hood off, you’re really not hiding anything." "Can I at least keep up the illusion?" "Fine by me. At least you’re not wearing the Groucho glasses this time." Under the hood there was laughter. "But seriously, Jared, what’s up?" Finally he pulled back the hood, an act that brought all action in the ring to a grinding halt. Darren caught this quick, and was on his feet and marching to the ring. "All right, kids," he barked, "yes, we have a celebrity today, so instead of standing around and staring why don’t you folks actually do something. I doubt very much any of you wants to get stretched in front of our esteemed guest." It took a moment, but business in the ring returned to normal. Darren strode back to the bench to the anguished cries of Baby’s First Backbumps. Jared fought hard to choke down a laugh. "I need your advice, D. I have something I want to run by you." "This isn’t about that dinner thing with the redhead, is it?" "No, nothing like that. It’s about the lanky kid." "The one that Draven guy pulled from the crowd? Seriously, talk about a lack of professionalism." "Yeah, he’s the one. Paul’s his name. I’m not quite sure what to do with the kid." "What do you mean? I didn’t think he was your responsibility." "No, he isn’t, but I do kind of have to babysit his ass in a few days. And while I’m sure he’s probably a decent enough guy, let’s just say I’m not looking forward to it. I mean, you’ve been doing this a while, you know. What the fuck would you do in this situation? The guy has next to zero real-world experience, and was pulled out of the crowd by someone who should have known better. What the hell am I supposed to do here?" Darren scratched at his goatee a moment, mulling over the situation. "I hate to say it, but I honestly don’t know what to tell you, other than to make sure he doesn’t get himself in over his head." "Dude, we’ve got Wyatt ‘Punch ‘Em in The Dick’ Connors and a guy who played table tennis with my head. This isn’t exactly a walk for me. I think we’ve shot right past ‘in over his head’ and gone straight to ‘hey look a shark-OHGODMYLEGS!’" Jared sighed as he pressed the palms of his hands into his eyes; working them in small, slow circles. "He’s going to get hurt, D. It’s going to be on my hands again." "You can’t hold yourself responsible for what happens." "How the fuck can I not hold myself responsible? I’m the only one on that team with any sort of cred. He was pulled out of the crowd and then this became his life. He doesn’t have the benefit of a real school, or teacher, or a guy like you to make sure he doesn’t royally screw himself." "You don’t think you could help him out? Help him get his footing?" The Black Sheep took a deep breath and exhaled slowly as he leaned forward, resting his elbows on his knees. "In case you haven’t noticed," he mused, "I’m kind of a mess. Kid could do a shitton better than me. Don’t forget," his words came slow, labored, "I killed a guy." Darren’s brow furrowed, and he took a moment to rub his eyes and choose his words before answering. "What happened with Kageyama was a tragic accident, and while what happened between the two of you may have cut short his career, it was not what ended his life. Neck injuries and heart conditions are not connected, Jared, no matter how much you beat yourself up over it." Jared had pulled his hood back over his head and slumped back against the wall. He didn’t answer. "You want to do this kid a favor? You want to show him what can happen to people, even people who are prepared?" Stracker continued, "Show him the tape."
