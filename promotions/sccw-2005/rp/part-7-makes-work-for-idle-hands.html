Part 7: Makes Work For Idle Hands

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: this week Happiness can do strange things to a person. Often times, someone who suddenly becomes happy begins to lose track of what is going on around them. They're off in their own little world, humming tunelessly and smiling like an idiot, completely unaware that everything around them is going completely to shit. It becomes like pulling teeth, trying to snap them back into reality. All this explains why Wyatt Connors has spent the last twenty-three minutes, clearing his throat in the center of the living room. He did it to get the attention of Mavis Jessup, who was a flurry of activity--sweeping here, dusting there, rearranging things...all the while wearing an inane grin, like she was all doped up on Xanax or something. Eventually, he had to grab her by the arm and pull her to a stop. 'Hmm?' she asked, as the rest of the world faded back in around her. Wyatt managed to swallow his intense exasperation. 'Excuse me, Sister Mavis, I was wondering if I might have a word.' 'I'm sorry, Reverend. But there's just so much to do! Everything needs to be perfect for his return.' Capitalization can be tough to pick up in conversation, but Wyatt was almost sure she said 'His.' 'I understand your excitement, my dear,' he said, trying not to let his frustration show. 'But I would like to speak with you on a few matters.' 'Oh, don't let me stop you,' she said, and turned back to her dusting. 'Right,' answered Wyatt, knowing full well that Mavis wasn't listening. 'Well, I'm sure by now you know of the injury that our dear friend Seth suffered while we were on our mission. Got hit by a bicycle messenger; hurt his knee pretty bad. He'll have to stay off his feet for the week, so he won't be able to help Hiram with the crops. I am sorry about that.' 'Mm-hmm.' 'Also, I feel the need to commune with the Lord for a few days. As such, I ask that I not be disturbed. It will be a very intense spiritual discussion, and if I am to receive as much of the Lord's wisdom as possible, I will need to be left alone.' 'All right.' 'Finally, your dress is on fire.' 'That's nice.' Yep. Not listening at all. Wyatt Connors rolled his eyes and left, leaving Mavis Jessup to her cleaning marathon. She'd been like this for days, and would continue on this way for several more. The funny thing was, she didn't feel the least bit tired. Not that it mattered if she had, she'd have kept going anyway. He would be home soon...and everything needed to be perfect. # # # As soon as Wyatt was out the door, he ran like hell to the nearest outside building, the machine shed. Seth was laid up in his own room, nursing his injured knee. Hiram was going to be too busy with the farm, with Seth's injury effectively doubling his workload. Hopefully, Mavis would pass the word around that he was to be left alone. For the first time since he landed in this backwoods community, he was free to do what he wanted. That amounted to two things. One, find his property. Two, get the hell out. He figured he could sneak off with the truck in the middle of the night, dump it at the first opportunity, and find some other mode of transportation the rest of the way. But, first things first. Feverishly, he began rifling through everything in the building. Cabinets, bins, toolboxes and piles of bolts and screws...everywhere it was possible to hide a briefcase, and at least half of the places it wasn't. Had the floor not been made of concrete, he would have tried to dig it up. He wasn't at all careful about where things ended up after his searches, but time was of the essence, not subtlety. Besides, what the hell did he care if Hiram got all pissy about his nails being in the wrong order? Connors didn't plan on being around to hear it. For three hours, he rifled through almost every inch of the machine shed. Had anyone been watching, they'd have likened the scene to the proverbial chicken, sans head. Of course, they wouldn't see the method to his madness. Most people didn't. All of his method and madness came to nothing, however. After all that time and searching, all he ended up with were hands covered with dirt and old grease. No matter. There were plenty of places to hide something, but he had a week to search for it. He tried to dust off his hands as he headed to the barn. # # # Six hours later, Wyatt found himself nailing floorboards back into place. How the hell was he supposed to know Hiram had fixed up the barn over the winter? He certainly couldn't tell by all the musty hay and disused horse tack in there. # # # Another entire day spent searching. First, the machinery. No inch of the tractor or the pickup was left untouched. He was tempted to try and disassemble the engines, but he couldn't find a decent wrench. Apparently, some jerk had made a mess of Hiram's tools. After that, he poked around the old outhouse that hadn't been (officially) been used in a decade, and he even poked around Mavis' flowers with a thin metal pole. Nothing. # # # Hiram sat atop his International tractor, watching Wyatt Connors wander aimlessly through the field. The reverend seemed to be very preoccupied with the ground, and not at all with the plants ground out of it. 'What are you doing out there?' 'I'm trying to hear the Lord!' 'I can hear him! He says get the hell out of my alfalfa!' # # # The lawn. The garden. Under the stairs. On the roof. Beside the cistern. IN the cistern. Ditches. Culverts. The burn barrel. The garbage pile. The shower. Nothing. # # # It hadn't been one of the Reverend's more memorable sermons, but even gifts from heaven are likely to have an off day every once in a while. Besides, he was understandably preoccupied. After the service, he asked that the Jessups leave so that he may commune with the Almighty. They agreed, and said they'd come back and pick him up at sundown. In retrospect, he wished he'd asked them to leave a sandwich. It took all nine hours to comb over the church and its grounds, and every second of it was wasted. There was nothing in the rafters, or the pews, or the pulpit. He even went out into the small graveyard, but found nothing apart from old stones. He thought about digging up one of the graves, but he knew there was no point. None of the dirt had been shifted in years; the grass had all grown over the plots. Anyway, he didn't have a shovel. The Jessups' old pickup rumbled back shortly after darkness fell. They soon found Connors, who had still not found his salvation. # # # The house. Most of it had been gone over, although he had to be careful about it. His own room and the bathroom were easily covered, but the living room and kitchen were trickier. He had to follow around a completely oblivious Mavis, rifling through the places she had just straightened up. She reorganized the pots and pans three times without catching on. The Jessups' bedroom wasn't much of a difficulty either; he just picked a time of day when both of them were too busy. It was rare that anyone bothered going into the basement or attic, so he had no problem there, either. That left only one room in the house. He creaked the door open and peeked inside, doing his best to remain silent. The first thing he looked at was the bed, which was occupied by a very large man. The man's breath was steady, and his chest rose and fell with regularity. He's sleeping. Good. Connors tiptoed into the room, quietly and slowly. He knew the big man was a heavy sleeper, and wasn't likely to get up in a hurry with his knee injury. Still, he had to be careful. There was only going to be one chance at this. He snaked his way over to the closet and ever so softly slid the door to the side. It was dark, and identifying anything was difficult, but he managed to make out a few things. Clothes, mostly, and some boxes. But there in the corner, there was a pile of laundry that looked a little too big. He cleared away some of it, and could not believe his eyes when he saw-- Click. 'Surprise, surprise.' --the lights come on. Wyatt nearly jumps out of his skin at the sudden brightness. He grabs the nearest thing he can find to defend himself and wheels around to face the speaker. That speaker is Seth, who is lying on his side, wide awake. 'Well, Reverend. How goes the search?' he asks. In a fraction of a second, Wyatt's face is the picture of innocence. 'What? What search.' He spares a glance to his hand, which is holding a pair of Seth's dirty underpants. 'Igg,' he mutters, as he tosses them aside. Seth sits up in bed, and points to the wall...specifically, the part of it that is made of transparent glass. 'You see that right there? It's called a window. I can use it to see things that are outside. Things like some squirrelly little fuck wandering all over the place.' And once again, as they oft have recently, Wyatt's best-laid schemes have gone awry. He put all of his eggs in one basket, only to find that his 'faithful bodyguard' has taken a giant shit in it. 'You haven't found it yet, have you?' Seth asked, although he already knew the answer. Connors snapped, 'I don't have time for games anymore, fathead. Where is it.' 'With an attitude like that, you're never going to find it. Looks like your plan to get me out of the way has fallen through.' Wyatt raised his hands defensively. 'Hey, I didn't plan that. That was your own fault--you shouldn't have laid your hands on her. And you really shouldn't have called her a whore.' 'Don't you even try it, Connors. You know goddamn well that she was supposed to take me out, or you wouldn't have pushed Rhine out of the way. Anyway, it doesn't change anything. You're still stuck here, with me...and with SCCW.' Cue complete freakout in five...four...three... 'WHAT?!' Whoops. Little early there. 'I can't go back now! Do you have any idea what I've done? That bitch will have me picking turnips with a stepladder by the end of the show!' 'Picking what?' 'This was an all-or-nothing deal. If it didn't work, I'd get nothing, and now I'm fucked. Do you know how fucked I am? Imagine a hooker who just set some kind of world record. That's how fucked I am!' 'And whose fault is that?' Oh, the pain. Wyatt's oldest--only--friend, Logic, had been turned against him. Despair began to sink in. 'Now then,' Seth said, attempting to restore calm to the proceedings. 'I think you should quit your tantrums and go find someplace else to be, before the Jessups hear you. I'm pretty sure that preachers aren't supposed to curse words. Especially not that many.' Wyatt muttered something under his breath, which caused Seth's smile to grow even wider. 'In the meantime, you've got other problems. You know how Mavis has got all excited about the reverend coming back? That's not too far off now. What are you going to do when he shows up?' Further despair. Connors looked like someone just ran over a puppy. Actually...more like someone else just ran over a puppy. 'He's going to know you're a fraud, Wyatt. He can probably tell just by looking at you. Hell, he probably knows who you are.' 'Not necessarily! Maybe he's taken a vow of not watching wrestling!' He was grasping at straws, but that's to be expected. What would you do, if you were drowning in a world of shit? 'Good luck on that one. It looks like we've come to the end of the game, Connors. If you go back to SCCW, Des--' 'DON'T! Don't say her name!' he shouted. This was followed with a whisper. 'She's attuned to it...she'll find me.' Incidentally, this was not the first time he had said this about a woman. Seth laughed at his frightened pleas, relishing in the panic on Connors' face. 'She's going to tear you apart. If you stay here, they're going to find out you've been lying to them all this time, and THEY'RE going to tear you apart. And you can't leave, because whatever I took from you is obviously too valuable. Face it, 'Wise Guy.' You're done.' He knew it, too. All those years of planning, scheming, hiding...all of it would come to nothing. It didn't matter if it was the power-mad Machiavellian woman or the semi-literate combine jockeys; he was a dead man. Broke. Powerless. Alone. And there, in Wyatt Connors' darkest moment, was a ray of hope. It was a thin ray, and it was a false hope, but you take what you can get. 'Ah! But what about you?' Confusion reigned. 'What?' The hopelessness on Wyatt's face turned to confidence. Not quite arrogance yet...but it'll get there. 'So, the instant this guy sees me, he's going to know I'm a fraud. What do you think he's going to do?' Seth began his clever retort, but Wyatt never gave him a chance. 'He's going to tell everyone. Then they'll come after me. They'll ask questions like, 'how could you do this to us?' And you know what I'll tell them?' No answer came from the farmhand; he was still in the face of the Smirk (TM). Yes, Wyatt Connors holds the trademark on Smirks. 'I'll tell them it was all your idea.' It was now Seth's turn to open his mouth in stunned, horrified silence. 'You were the one who told them I was a reverend in the first place. You supplied the Bible with my name in it. You were the first one on the scene of the accident. For all they know, this was all some plan that we cooked up together, to rob them off all the money they keep in their coffee cans.' 'That's bullshit.' 'Of course it is. But I don't have to fool you...I just have to fool them. Do you want to take that chance?' As it turned out, Seth didn't much like the shoe being on the other foot. His faced twisted into a snarl, he growled, 'Get out of here.' 'Sure. But I'll be back to speak with you later. I think our 'understanding' has almost expired...and now it's time to make a deal.' He left the room, still smirking. For the first time in their adversarial partnership, Wyatt Connors had the upper hand. It wasn't much, but as stated before...you take what you can get.
