Part Nine: Warning Shots

17 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/17/08 Logic would have told him not to let himself get so emotional. Logic would have told him that the words being spoken were meaningless, only a cheap and obvious attempt at manipulation by a gentleman working the situation as much for his own benefit as anything else. Logic would have told him that anything said by Desade, by Cantrell, even by Shakur…that no matter the substance, they were just words, granted only whatever power he chose to give them. Logic, however, was currently taking a back seat to the urge to break shit. Sleep had come fitfully the previous night when it had bothered coming at all. There would be a few hours of rest at a time, at most, and then something would jolt Lance into drowsy wakefulness. A twinge of pain from his ribs. A troubling dream. A stray thought which would buzz its way around and around his head like an angry wasp until all other thought was drowned out. When the alarm had buzzed at eight o’clock, as it did every Saturday morning, it had come as a blessed relief. He had barely mustered up the energy to kiss his wife good morning as she woke and had come about as close as anyone could to drowning while standing up in the shower. Even Alanna cooking breakfast for him, which would normally be greeted with a shower of praise, merited only a quick "this is great, honey" and a frenzy of eating as he shoveled the food into his mouth. It had been a few hours later, as early afternoon saw Lance idly flicking through the college football offerings on television, when Alanna had finally decided to confront her husband. She had hoped, ideally, that Lance would simply snap out of whatever mood he was in or, if necessary, confide in her as to what was bothering him so much. Instead, he’d chosen the third option and elected to sulk in silence. She knew it was as much to protect her from any flare ups of anger as anything else…and given the events of the most recent editions of ReVolution and Temptation, he’d certainly had cause to be upset. It still didn’t keep it from working her nerves, though. Taking care to be quiet as she slowly slide her shoes off, Alanna slowly made her way across the floor until she reached her husband’s perch on the sofa. Sliding into the plush leather of the sofa, she snuggled into the warm bulk of her husband’s seated form. Automatically, his eyes never breaking from the TV screen, Lance wrapped his free arm around his wife, drawing her close to him. For a moment, they sat in silence, each of them drawing strength from the other’s presence. But only for a moment. Alanna eased slightly away from her husband, turning her head to look at him directly. "Not that I mind college football on ADD, exactly, but could we talk about what’s bothering you?" "Nothing’s wrong. I’m fine." Alanna shook her head. "Bullshit, babe. We’ve been together way too long for me not to know when you’re in a mood. Right now, all of this? Is a Grade A, full-blown, prize winning stink. So fess up, what’s wrong?" The TV shuts off with a quick wink of the image, the controller dropping flatly onto the sofa cushion. Lance’s eyes finally break from the TV, his body turning to face his wife’s. He waits a moment before speaking, careful to make certain that his words were as controlled as he could make them. He would never raise his voice to his wife…and, to be quite frank, hard won experience had taught him the dangers of doing so. "I’m pissed." "No duh, babe," Alanna replied, "but about what, exactly? I didn’t even think Devin Shakur had done a piece yet." The mention of his opponent at Cataclysm merely occasioned a sigh from Lance Marshall and the rubbing of his temples. On the list of reasons for this particular strain of funk, thought Lance, Shakur was currently residing…ooh, somewhere in the mid-twenties. "Not Shakur, then. We know that Desade spouts bullshit as easily as breathing so that can’t be it. Hell, we’re almost used to Alex’s little games by now, as scary a thought as that is." Alanna paused for a moment before continuing. She now had a fairly good idea of who had managed to get under her husband’s skin so badly, knew she was taking a risk even by saying his name. "Is this about what Cantrell said?" Lance’s answer is instinctive and drowning in venom. "FUCK Cantrell." "Not with Tila Tequila’s cooch," answered Alanna, "thanks all the same." "The fact that Cantrell would even have the NERVE to put me on the same level with Desade," fumed Lance. Alanna marveled to herself that, if this were a cartoon, now would be the point where steam would literally be rising from her husband’s head. While she could certainly understand why he was angry, Alanna also realized that her husband wouldn’t be of use to anyone in this mood. "Babe, Cantrell’s just stooping to Desade’s level here, maybe even more so. At least Alex believes most of what she says but Cantrell…he’s just condemning all of SCCW so that the rest of PRIME can have a convenient focal point to rally against, a big bad enemy that they can go forth and smite. He didn’t mean anything against you, personally." "Sure as hell felt like it at the time," Lance retorted. "Definitely felt like it when Shakur started swinging that bat around." "Come on, babe. Getting hit by Devin Shakur is like getting hit by a Chihuahua. I’m surprised you even felt anything." One look from Lance was enough to let Alanna know that she had made a bad choice of words. Okay then, she thought, time for Plan B. "Why don’t you get out?" Lance’s right eyebrow raised in confusion. "Excuse me?" "Get out of the house for a bit," his wife responded, "do something to blow off some steam and snap you out of this funk. Hit the gym, go for a run, I dunno. But just sitting around here and moping isn’t doing either of us any good." Lance sat for a moment, pondering over his wife’s words. Slowly, a look of realization began to dawn on him. She was right as she usually was. There wasn’t necessarily anything wrong with being pissed…but when it started making you shut down, something needed to be done. He turned to his wife, giving her a full-on smile for the first time that day. "Y’know, babe, you’re right. I’ve got to shake myself out of this. You okay with me taking some time today?" "Don’t worry about a thing," Alanna responded. "I’ll pick up Zach later and I’ve already got dinner planned out. You just go ahead and do what you need to do, all right?" Padding over to where his wife was standing, Lance takes Alanna in his arms and gives her a long, slow kiss. "You know," he says as they part, "you’re the best." Alanna’s smile was soft and flirty. "Duh," she replied, giving her husband a playful swat on the arm. "Now go." Ten minutes later saw Lance dressed and in the Cayenne, making his way down the 5 towards FIRING-LINE. As he slid into the first parking spot available, he tried to cast his mind back to the last time he had made it down to the range…and came up blank. To his surprise, he had readily taken to firearms instruction at the Point, his instructors saying he had a real aptitude for it. He wasn’t exactly what you’d call a firearms enthusiast (and even if he was, Zach’s birth had absolutely erased the possibility of having guns in the house). Still, there were those times when the urge to explode felt so great that loosing off a few rounds was the only way to really vent his anger. Besides, there was something about holding an expertly crafted weapon in his hand, feeling the recoil travel along his arm as the bullets exploded out of the chamber, that just felt indescribably…powerful. Jerry at the counter waved to Lance as he came in. "Hey, Mister Marshall. Long time no see. You want me to set up the usual for you?" Lance marveled that, even with the sheer number of folks that had to pass through here, Jerry still remembered who he was, let alone his choice of gun. The idea that Jerry would not find it terribly difficult to recall the preferences of a man who made his living on television never occurred to Lance. "Yeah, Jer, same as usual. How’s things been, man?" "Oh, can’t complain. Just gimme ten and I’ll have your lane ready." Ten minutes later exactly (Jerry being the stickler for timeliness that he was), Lance stood at the end of the lane, a paper target dangling in the background. He went through the safety procedures, making sure his ear and eye protection were secure, making sure the gun was free of any mechanical faults or potential glitches. Satisfied, he slammed in a clip and slipped back the safety. Arm raised in front of him, sights set on the target. Lance’s breathing slowed, his awareness of everything around him slowly drifting away. He tried as best he could to push everything away, to free his mind of everything but himself and the gun. But his mind would not stay clear today and, as he slowly squeezed the trigger, a name came unbidden to the forefront of his brain. Devin Shakur. Lance cursed, feeling his arm drop as the gun fired. His right arm now looked as though he was auditioning for the next John Woo movie and the target…well, the intent had been to aim at the chest. As it stood, the bullet had ripped through an area that would have proportionally been equal to its groin. Fitting, given what a dick Shakur had proven himself to be. All ego and sneer, Lance spat inside his mind, crowing like the cock of the walk when he has the Universal Title in his possession. But have someone take it away from him and what happens? He’s reduced to a sniveling mess, a man who had just barely managed to squeak out a win in his last match while looking fat and out of shape. A sniveling little lap dog, ready to spring forward with bat in hand whenever C.P. Cantrell pointed his hand at someone and yelled "Sic’ Em!". And still…still the oddsmakers and the pundits and the gossips had Shakur coming out ahead. If they were feeling generous, some would add that Lance might "make it close". A feral, nasty little grin crossed Lance’s face as he thought of all this. The gun rose again, almost of its own accord, another bullet ripping through the same spot as the first. A message obviously needed to be sent, Lance decided…and if Devin Shakur had to be the vehicle of that message, then so be it. Lance’s arm rose again, this time aiming for the target’s head. Another name leapt to his head as he squeezed the trigger, rage threatening to blot out his vision as he emptied the clip into the target. Four shots, all dead center of the head. Desade. For a woman who liked to imagine herself smarter than the rest of SCCW, Lance thought, Desade seemed to be playing an awful lot by her emotions lately. Sure, there were ways to justify seeking vengeance upon your enemies, ways of portraying "getting your own back" as a cold and rational decision…but gloating over the fall of your enemies? Exulting in the pain of others, letting their misery be your pleasure? That wasn’t smart fighting…hell, anyone who’d studied even a gnat’s ass worth of Machiavelli knew that much. Then again, he was having trouble understanding anything Desade did these days. He understood that their conflicting senses of morality would keep them from agreeing on…well, anything…but letting her daughter into her life? Posing for months as Charlotte Ramone? What the hell had any of that been about? Lance’s arm now stood level as he aimed at the target’s head, the gun pointed at a point smack dab in the center of his head. Another name came to mind as he squeezed, four shots ripping through the target. C.P. Cantrell. Cantrell imagined himself as Ozymandias, thought Lance, dubbing SCCW the alien that he would set among PRIME so that his squabbling roster would unite against a common enemy…and if some had to suffer along the way, more’s the pity. The problem with Cantrell, thought Lance, is that his grand plan wasn’t working as well as he thought. It was obvious that not everyone had been drinking the Kool-Aid, not everyone thought of SCCW as one monolithic evil entity. Lindsay Troy had been great to him and Alanna as had Chandler Tsonda. And as for Cantrell’s belief that he was in control? Well, Xavier Kannon proved that to be a lie. The whole force of PRIME massed at Temptation, aiming to send a statement…and Kannon ends up making the biggest statement of all, betraying his entire federation to stand at Desade’s side. Not that the decision had surprised Lance much. Desade tended to enjoy working with the morally dubious, the ones who didn’t mind bearing the stain upon their careers and their souls. As Lance pulled out of his parking space and set the Cayenne to drive, he reviewed the time he had spent at the firing range in his head. It had helped to a degree, he thought…but it had also reawakened some thoughts that had been sleeping in the back of his mind. If he was going to go into Cataclysm at full strength, he needed to be free of distraction. He needed his mind focused. So, he pulled over and punched in the address for the Farmer’s Market over on West Third Street into the car’s Sat Nav system. Blasting the hell out of a target had its place, Lance thought, and it had certainly helped him work out a great deal of aggression. But there were very few things in life, he thought, that couldn’t be solved (or at least seriously improved on) by a chocolate shake at Bennett’s. As the Sat Nav pinged to begin its guidance, a thought crossed Lance Marshall’s mind. If it’s a war with C.P. Cantrell wants, then fine. But I’ll be damned if I end up being one of his casualties. We now return you to your regularly scheduled lives, already in progress.
