Feed the Animals, Act Four: Time

undated

Date: When. My name is Sean Sterling. I’m not a good man, by any stretch of the imagination. I wrestle to make a living. Famous sometimes, but forgettable, for the most part. I’ve never had a single regret in my entire life. Up until now. --- ONE. Sights From the End of the World. I had a dream last night about the end of the world. The sky all went to melting, clouds crashing into the sun, planes spiraling down and dissolving into dust. The city sighed, it’s skyscrapers waving with the wind, as people leapt to their deaths, screaming until they hit the concrete. The sewers all muddled with a thousand lonely suicides, billboards leering like makeshift grave markers, silhouetted by the sunset. The flags all hung dead from the top of their poles and I stood amongst the ash and the rubble, crying for my own peace at mind. The skyline looked beautiful on fire, twisting metal reaching up towards the drunken sky like hands lifted up towards heaven. We were all praying now, whether silent or out loud, while we waited for the white horse of Death to descend upon us, like a fever, and pluck our souls from our bones. Here’s to nothing and here’s to no one, because there was no one left to raise the glass to. I saw every sin I’d ever committed stretched out at my feet, like shadows in the evening sun, taller than the tumbling buildings and darker than even the end of the world. As the sound of gunfire rang out through the streets and the stars all started to fade to black, I closed my eyes and I leapt from the tallest height I could find. And I fell, and I fell, and I fell. …and then I woke up. Cold sweat and tangled blankets, heavy breath and eyes wide open. The television set hissed static as I looked to the window, just to make sure everything was still there, and it was. And I sighed. And I fell back to sleep. --- There’s something going on that I can’t quite make out. Something rooted deep in my bones keeps waking me up at night. Call it a premonition, call it what you want. It hovers over me like the Ides of March or the Sword of Damocles hanging from a string. It’s everything I can do to keep myself from thinking about it. Nervous tics and too much coffee. My ashtrays are overflowing and my head is spinning dizzy. Something’s wrong. This isn’t guilt and this isn’t just a reoccurring coincidence. This is fear, as obvious as the ‘o’ in God. --- TWO. Sounds From Childhood. Things were easier when I was a kid. Life was simple. Living on a farm where the grass was taller than me, dressed up in torn blue jeans and a Lynard Skynard t-shirt. I had a reputation for being somewhat of a troublemaker, but nobody really seemed to care. A cell phone was a tincan on a string. The tooth fairy brought me money every couple of months. Christmas was for presents, not for lonely drinking in the back of a bar. Simple. Take it easy, never worry, life is forever. I grew up in Manchester, Tennessee. It was nothing but shitty farmland an hour away from Nashville up until some huge music festival decided to buy some land down there a few years back, but I was long gone by then. All I can ever remember are those lazy summer days, long and everlasting, and those sleepless winter nights, staying up late to watch the stars. First kisses, skipping school, dreaming of rocket ships and faraway places. My father was a preacher and my mother was a housewife, but they’re gone now. Sometimes, I still think of the Sunday sermons, my father standing at what seemed like the top of the world, screaming out hellfire and brimstone, telling stories of hell and rumors of war. I always watched wide eyed and scared to death. I couldn’t make out the truth from fiction back then. He screamed, with a voice like a wall of fire sweeping out over the congregation, his forehead dripping with beads of sweat and his cheeks gone red with anger, pride, guilt, and faith. It was in that small white chapel that I first met John Dent, just as wide eyed and horrified amidst the shouts of faith and the words of glory. We sat beside each other in Sunday school, coloring pictures of Jesus and talking about the future. John Dent always had huge dreams, a planned future as bright as the moon. It seemed like he had had them ever since conception. While the rest of Manchester’s youth dreamt of being astronauts or adventurers, John Dent said he wanted to be a lawyer or a doctor or even a scientist. So, when the rest of Manchester’s youth grew up to be meth chemists, cheap hookers, and complete failures, it’s no wonder that John Dent ended up being exactly what he set out to be: A success. --- If there’s one thing that can be said about mankind, there’s nothing kind about man. I’ve smoked all my friends down to the filter. Even the top of the world is just as lonely as the bottom. At least at the bottom, you have people who are just as bad off as you, people you can form some friendship with. Here, all you have is backstabbers and golddiggers. Fake friends and false advertisements, people who just want to live easy off of your success. I don’t even have much in common with the people who make up the bars anymore. I’m a face on a television, a false prophet for mainstream American media. I’m everything I never wanted to be. The only people I have anything to do with anymore are the people I work with, and even they don’t have much to do with me. Rhine never calls to go out drinking. I haven’t seen Rollins in weeks. Everyone else either hates me or is scared to death of me. All I have is the demons I meet at the bottom of beer bottles, or the devils disguised as angels who come to me whenever I’m high. Prostitutes aren’t worth the money anymore and I fuck things up with whoever I form even the slightest bit of a friendship with, no matter how superficial. It’s times like these I pull out the old family movies or the scrapbooks of old photos. Sat back and watch or finger through with a sigh. Sometimes, I miss the things I never appreciated. Misery is the river of the world. --- THREE. Mr. Tambourine Man. New York City. October 31st, 1998. Eighteen years old, barely old enough to call myself a man. It seems like forever ago, even though it’s not even been ten years yet. John Dent and I stumbled out of a back alley bar that didn‘t bother to ID, singing Bob Dylan with our arms around each other’s necks, looking as mad as hatters. "Hey, Mr. Tambourine Man! Play a song for me! I’m not sleepy and there is no place I’m going to!" We slid down the neon sidewalks, depending on each other to keep from falling in the gutter, attracting angry stares and muffled laughter on every street corner. It was a different time, a better time, before New York was a safe place, when it was filthy, but fine. Before billionaire mayors, convinced they were kings, turned the place into a family amusement park. I mean all disrespect. "Hey, Mr. Tambourine Man! Play a song for me! In the jingle-jangle morning, I’ll come a-following you!" Headlights looked like diamonds and skyscrapers seemed to shudder, the cracks in the sidewalks tripping our feet. Lost for the big city, occasionally hailing for a taxi with no luck. It was freezing cold, snow sputtering down from the smog clouds overhead. Hipsters smoked outside of CBGB’s, huddled together for warmth, gazing at their shoes and trying to look cooler than they actually were. "Though I know that evening’s empire has returned into sand, vanished from my hand, left me blindly here to stand, but still not sleeping!" Junkies stumbled out of alleys, itching and twitching, fueled by an angry fix of heroin, grinning like jester’s in a throne room. Homeless didn’t bother to ask us for change, because they knew we were too drunk to listen. Occasionally, they would even stumble with us for a few blocks, singing along. "My weariness amazes me! I’m branded on my feet! I have no one to meet in the ancient empty streets too dead for dreaming!" Occasionally, John would fumble with the lyrics, but my recollection remembers nothing but perfection from my mouth. It was a perfect night. No one bothered us and we didn’t bother anybody. It was like the whole of New York City dropped it’s angry façade and decided to be friendly, to everyone and anyone. Even the cops just shook their head, letting us walk on by. "Hey, Mr. Tambourine Man! Play a song for me! I’m not sleepy and there is no place I’m going to!" This was years after we colored pictures together in Sunday school, years after we shared our parachute dreams. John Dent was about to go to college to get his doctorate, and I was about to waste the next few years of my life, in and out of inebriation, spending each morning in a hangover and each night trying to wash my own uselessness away. This was our final road trip, our final time seeing each other for years, and admittedly, our final days as friends. "Hey, Mr. Tambourine Man! Play a song for me! In the jingle-jangle morning, I’ll come a-following you!" And I’d be a liar to say I didn’t miss it. --- Another dream about the end of the world last night. This time, John was with me and it wasn’t quite as horrifying as it was before. We stumbled through the ruins, covered in ash, our clothes torn and tattered. The whole world moaned like a bitch in heat, the moon glowing red in the sky. Cars littered the streets, abandoned. One still had the engine running and Bob Dylan sang out of the radio, mixed with static. Even he didn’t matter now. John looked at me and he said, "These are truly the end of times." And I said, "Yes, they are." The neon lights had all faded out and the awkward glances from passerbys didn’t exist. It was just us and the whole world stretched out at our feet, forgotten by God and unwanted by Hell. So we stumbled, and we staggered, and we sighed. A black crow snuck through a hole in the sky and landed at our feet, cawing and clawing at the dust on the ground. And Death came with it. --- FOUR. And The Skies Went Black. I woke up staring down the barrel of a Colt .45. As the sun came up over the city, shining in through the hotel windows, John Dent stood over my bed, his hair gone unwashed, a five-o-clock shadow taking over his face, and his fingers quaking on the trigger. "John," I said, my eyes slowly going wide and my voice barely above a whisper. "John, you don’t have to do this." "Just like you didn’t have to do this to Brenda, right?" John muttered out, his voice already going tearful as he placed the barrel against my forehead. "And don’t you even fucking think about saying you didn’t do it. I know you did." There was a moment of silence as I took a heavy sigh. "I won’t lie, John. Yes, I killed Brenda. Yes, I did it just to spite you. Yes, that’s a terrible excuse… but you don’t have to do this. I’ve been thinking, John…" "I don’t give two shits what you’ve been thinking about, Sean. You ruined my life. You ruined me. And why? Why the fuck did you do it? Just to spite me, you say? Just to get your little shits and giggles off of making someone even more pathetic than yourself?" "I don’t know why I did it, John. I don’t… I made a mistake. A huge mistake. Sometimes my mind gets the best of me." "Fuck you! Fuck you, Sterling! You just shut the fuck up right now and listen. I don’t give a shit what you’re reasoning is. I don’t care if you’re schizophrenic or bipolar or just half-retarded. It doesn’t fucking matter," This is the part where the levees finally broke and the tears started streaming down his cheeks. "All that matters is what I’m about to do. I’m going to throw you in the back of my trunk, drive to some secluded place, and kill you. Do you understand that? You’re going to die, Sean. Just like Brenda." "John, listen…" "No. Get the fuck up. If you make a fucking noise, I’ll shoot you in the lobby. We’re taking the back exit." It’s funny. As he led me down the staircase, across the parking lot, and finally into the trunk of his car, all I could think was, whatever happened to my childhood? What happened to us? Who am I? Who is he? And then it hit me: I fucked everything up. --- Laying in the trunk of John Dent’s car, curled up in a ball with my hands tied behind my back, I kept thinking to myself, how the hell did I end up here? I suppose a legacy of shitting on other people who didn’t deserve it caught up with me. John Dent. Brenda Dent. Marilyn. Judith. My parents. The list goes on. I’m not going to place the blame on drugs or drinking or even depression. Fact of the matter is, I’ve just made a lot of mistakes in my life. The sad part is, I never regretted a single one of those mistakes up until now. Give any man a little time to reflect and he’ll realize just how many times he’s fucked up. This trunk is cold. It’s dark. I can feel every bump in the pavement and every stop sends me flying from one side to the other. What a proper way to die. --- FIVE. Feed the Animals. When the car stopped, I could hear John walking around to the back, taking his sweet time, crushing fallen leaves beneath his feet. I would be a liar if I said I wasn’t scared. When the trunk opened, sunlight beamed in as I looked up to see John Dent, silhouetted, leaves falling from the trees above him. I took a deep breath, and so did he, as he pulled me to the trunk and helped me to my feet. "This is it, Sean," He muttered out, his nerves finally catching up with him. "This is it." I could tell that he had been crying. His cheeks were red, his words coming out harsh. I stood in silence, closing my eyes as he led me further into the woods. When we stopped, he lifted the gun and placed it against the back of my head. His finger quaked at the trigger, his head hung low, his breath heavy. I could tell he was just as scared as me. There was nothing but silence between us. No words and no motions. Just two men, both falling apart in their own ways. Every passing moment felt like a lifetime. Every second that he didn’t pull the trigger felt like forever. And finally, there amidst the falling leaves and the glaring sunlight, John Dent broke down. Tears began streaming. His hand began to tremble. His mind finally caved in on itself. I opened my eyes slowly as he lowered the gun. I looked over my shoulder to see him lazily falling to the ground, wrapping his head in his hands, crying and muttering to himself. I knelt down slowly and spoke softly, my hands still bound. "It’s alright, John. It’s alright…" When I looked up, I looked dead into his eyes, and I saw the end of the world. I saw the broken dreams of a broken man. I saw all of my mistakes, all of my regrets, and I sighed. "C’mon, get up. Let’s go get some drinks," I muttered. "They’re on me. We’ve got some catching up to do." We both stood and he untied my hands. We walked to his car in silence, and we drove back to the city just as silently. We found the closest bar and we drank and we talked and we stumbled home together while the rest of the world laughed. We even sang Bob Dylan. --- SIX. Moloch. Mount Vernon Baptist Church. Manchester, Tennessee. 1987. We were coloring. "Do you think we’ll be friends forever, Sean?" I looked up, smiling. "Best friends, John. I promise." He smiled and nodded as we went back to our coloring, as our conversations went back to outer space, cowboys, and dinosaurs. If I had known what the future would truly hold for both of us, I wonder if my answer would have been the same? Sometimes it’s easy to forget who your real friends are.
