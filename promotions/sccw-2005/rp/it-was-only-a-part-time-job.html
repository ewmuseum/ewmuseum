It was only a part time job

2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/6/08 The idea of a life changing event occurring in an individual’s life is not new, in fact, some people have more than one in their lifetime. These events are often determined by said individual; for instance, when a man with low self esteem and no direction gets married that person will consider the marriage a life changing event. Sure, some let outsiders decide if the event is life changing but the only one who really knows is the person it happened to. Half of the time they don’t put two and two together – about the event – until it’s so far in the past that it is almost an afterthought. Life changing events are remembered in a movie format. This doesn’t mean there are actors or there is a convenient angled shot that shows the sadness or excitement on a person’s face. Every instant, every word, every movement of a truly life changing event is remembered. April 10th, 2008 was a day that changed the life of Hiro Saotome…but we’ll get to that later. Hiro Saotome is twenty eight years old, he is married, graduated from college and is attending graduate school at night while teaching 8th grade US History during the day. Hiro is a good guy, he’s honest, loyal to a fault, confident but not cocky, overly logical in his thought process and for a little over five years of his life he was a professional wrestler. Getting a job in professional wrestling is like getting a job in porn, you take an odd job that relates to the field (bodyguard, security guard…stripper, escort…etc, etc) and somebody notices you. If you’re taking the jobs to get you into these situations then odds are you don’t have the money to really support yourself so you’re always open to new opportunities. Luckily for Hiro he didn’t have to suck any cock to get into wrestling. Hiro Saotome was "discovered" while he was working as a security guard for a indy wrestling that happened to put on a show in Eugene, Oregon. Saotome walked on to the University of Oregon football team and got some of his schooling paid for and in many ways it was a cake job for him. Run some scout team offense, show up to meetings and you’re making close to twenty four thousand dollars a year. When all of that money is going to tuition it can make it a little difficult to eat, drink, party or – most importantly – impress the ladies. Being an athlete allowed Hiro to get jobs pretty easily but who wanted to do a 9 to 5 at a pizza parlor? So when the opportunity to play security guard at a pro-wrestling event showed up it was easy money. * * * "The Pit," otherwise known as MacArthur Court in Eugene, Oregon, is the home to the University of Oregon basketball team. Compared to other Pac-10 schools the court is small and raucous. The seats are close to the floor, the gym is constantly hot because of its antique build and the ceiling is low enough where you could swear that if a ball was thrown high enough it would hit it. Still, the building is properly maintained and supported by Nike so everything is old yet immaculate. Traditional yet cutting edge. In only a few hours the golden hardwood court is covered with black and blue mats and in the middle of the pads is a wrestling ring. Inside the wrestling ring, on the sweat and blood stained canvas are three letters, MSW. In a few hours there will be more sweat and possibly some more blood staining the canvas. "The Pit" will be full (hopefully) and people will be enjoying a few hours of entertainment. That’s why being a security guard at pro-wrestling is perfect for a college athlete, who is really going to go nuts at a house show? If you’re a well known athlete people will be more focused on you then the show anyways. You never know, if the promoter knows you’re an athlete he might even slip you an extra twenty to do something in the show. Collins: So you play some football for the Ducks? Kevin Collins is the road show director for Minnesota State Wrestling. Collins in a lot of ways is a soccer mom for pro-wrestlers. He makes sure they’re at the events on time, he makes sure they get paid (the equivalent of an ice cold orange wedge) and critiques something he really doesn’t know much about. The only difference between Kevin Collins and a soccer mom is that Collins is responsible for putting on a good show. Sure, the wrestlers are doing all the work but Collins is responsible for advertisement and "booking" the matches. Hiro: Yeah, I play some receiver and safety. Collins: Both ways? Hiro: I walked on so it’s wherever they put me. Whatever pays for tuition, you know? Collins: Yeah, I hear ya. Anything for a buck. You play any other sports? Do anything else athletic? Hiro: I practice Shoto-Kan and, um, play some intramural sports. Collins: Really? Hmm. Kevin Collins is what you would expect a professional wrestling road agent to look like. His face is worn, bags under his eyes and the hint of gold on his teeth from his favorite dinner, cigarettes. Thin, stringy, peppered hair is pulled back in a pony tail that only accentuates the progressive hair loss from the widows peaks. The months of eating take out, smoking, drinking and being on the road has made his body soft and his skin glossy…like a pig. The only thing "classy" on Collins is the black MSW embroidered polo shirt that is tucked into his snug black jeans. Collins: One of my guys is banged up and really shouldn’t go tonight. How would you like to win a match for the U of O? Hiro: Seriously? I, uh, don’t know how to wrestle. Collins: You don’t have to! Just some of your karate and our guy will take the fall. You’ll get thrown around a little bit but you can take that. I’ll even give you an extra fifty bucks on top of what you’re making doing the security gig. Standing across from Collins is a twenty year old Hiro Saotome. His 6’3" frame is lanky yet defined, no matter how form fitting the "Ducks Football" shirt you would never think of him as a wrestler. Hiro’s hazel eyes are not hard, threatening or even intimidating…they’re calm, inquisitive and warm. Hiro: How much do I have to do? I won’t be in there long, right? Collins: Like ten minutes tops. You’ll get called out, get into the ring, he beats on you a little bit then you come and do some fancy karate. All you need to do is make your feet and fists touch him and he’ll do the rest. You have a finisher in mind? Something flashy? A sheepish smile comes over Hiro’s face, running his hands though his short black hair he shrugs his shoulders. Collins allows for a few moments of silence before slapping Saotome on the arm. Collins: Just do something flashy. Don’t worry about it you’ll be fine. * * * Hiro Saotome never intended to become a professional wrestler but it was a hell of a lot more enjoyable then riding the pine in football. The only problem was if Hiro quit football then how was he going to pay for school? He only received money while he was on the team, meaning Hiro had to try out for the team every year. The thought of asking his parents for money to pay for school, again, so he could have some fun was not the biggest problem…MSW is a traveling show. The decision was simple. Finish out football, get the degree and if there was a life in wrestling at that time then why not give it a shot. The way Hiro thought of it was like any guy would be in his twenties. You’re done with school, don’t want to get a job right off the bat, you’re single and want to party. Now most parents would encourage their child to get a job, make some money and be a productive American. Luckly for Hiro Saotome, his father Genma, was an adventuring soul. The relationship between Hiro and Genma was close. Hiro considered Genma to be his best friend, role model, mentor and hero. Hiro knew his father had lived all over the world, working various jobs and gaining various life experiences, still when the time came to tell his father that he wanted to pursue professional wrestling for a little bit there was some serious apprehension. Genma still lived in Japan so anytime the two spent together was treasured and Hiro would never try to bring an ounce of drama home. The phone conversations, though, while the two were as close as father and son could be…could get rather heated. Hiro: Hey dad, what’s going on? Genma: Oh not much. Just watching some TV and annoying your mother. She thinks taking food out of a box is cooking. It’s re-heating but it’s not worth arguing about. Hiro: Tell mom I said hi. I thought she was making you healthier meals now? Genma: She is but she took a day off that’s turned into almost a week. Hiro’s father is a diabetic and is in his sixties which is not a good combination when you’ve had the disease for almost half of your life. This didn’t stop Genma from working out or leading a productive life, though, as he would work out daily and do everything he could to fight the disease. Still, with the relationship between Hiro and his father…and Hiro’s protective nature…information like that was not easy to take. Hiro: Doesn’t she understand that you need her to make healthy meals to keep you around longer? Genma: Yeah she understands she just needs to rest. She’s cooking three meals a day… Hiro: She’s retired and doesn’t do anything, it’s her job to help you. Genma: Find any teaching jobs yet? Genma would never sell his wife out. He knew that eating healthy and leading an active lifestyle is important to his life but he wouldn’t get as worked up about the "little things" as Hiro would. This train of thought was hard for Hiro to grasp let alone accept. Why would you take your life so lightly? Hiro: That’s what I called to talk to you guys about… Genma: You found a job? Hiro: No. I was actually thinking of taking some time off and getting a job…relax for a little bit before I get into the real world. Genma: Nothing wrong with that. What are you thinking of doing? Coming home? Hiro: Well, remember how I told you about how I made fifty bucks being a wrestler? I was thinking about doing that. Genma: Wrestling? The hint of laughter in his voice is hard to miss. Genma is a caring and loving father, a "do as I say, not as a I do" or did, father. It was his goal to give Hiro the best life possible, free of problems and mistakes that he went though. Hiro: Yeah, I mean if I don’t get into it then it’s not a big deal. I just don’t want to get into teaching yet. Genma: Don’t do that, Hiro. You’ll break your neck or screw yourself up. Just get in the classroom and start making some money. You’ve got to get your masters still, don’t wrestle, just keep your nose to the grindstone and keep working. Hiro: This coming from the guy who moved to Italy when he got done with college. Genma: Times were different. Just because I did that doesn’t mean you need to. Hiro: You turned out pretty good in life. I’m not going to become a professional wrestler for my career, hell I don’t know if I’ll even get hired…just something to do… Genma: It’s a stupid idea. The tone of Hiro’s voice fills with anger. His lips purse and his eyes harden. Only his father could push these buttons. The only thing Hiro was looking for was acceptance, like most children. Genma: You need to grow up and… Before Genma can finish Hiro slams his cell phone shut and throws it at the couch. Lips pursed, jaw clenched, fists balled and eyes shooting daggers though the phone which is now buried in the couch cushions. Hiro: I don’t need your fucking permission. You don’t even let me finish… Tears begin to well up in Hiro’s eyes; finally he blinks which causes a few tears to run down his cheeks. Hiro: I’m not going to break my neck… These are tears of anger, not at his father but at his father’s words. He could never be mad at his father, Genma has devoted his life for his only son and Hiro knows this. While he might not be able to relay the message all the time Hiro tries to look past the gruff and no nonsense attitude and find the caring and loving father that just wants to see his son succeed in life. Hiro always finds that version of Genma, sometimes he wishes he could talk with that version.
