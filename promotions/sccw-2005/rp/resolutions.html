Date: Today Amy's log, star date... uhhh... 2008. Hey future self, I hope you aren't too embarassed by these stupid journals that you sat up at all hours typing. I'm trying to bring a close to the events of our daily life in this moment by documenting what I consider to be important, and rambling about the rest. Hopefully you don't think that 'This Amy' was an idiot. We both know that typing this pointless information has been like therapy for me, and I'm not gonna stop doing it - no matter how it might sound when I read it back in a few years. This week was wild, for sure. I don't have a title anymore - I blinked and it was gone. I just couldn't find my motivation. That didn't help the atmosphere at Christmas, since I was supposed to be at home as the victorious hero who finally got her life on track. Whoops, sorry mom! 1.01In his eyes - Darcy _______________________________________________ Amy isn't the only one who likes rambling on her laptop. She doesn't even know I'm typing this. It seemed like it happened in another lifetime, even though it had taken place in this calendar year. Perhaps that’s why I still remember it all quite vividly. Standing center stage inside that ring; a microphone in one hand, a gleaming belt of gold in the other. I can hardly call it my life…it isn’t the same, and we’re not the same, and especially since I still recall having one prior to signing up for all of this. But I have given my life in pursuit of this trade and the glory there within. And in exchange, I had but one humble request. Rather, a fervent prayer – that I would be allowed to reclaim my life, and my very soul, when I knew it was time to walk away. I just never thought it would be this day. And I certainly never thought my prayer would fall upon deaf ears. The gold I carried with me, just like the all-too-similar shine of the golden bond I wear now on my left hand, I shared with my bride-to-be. The roller-coaster ride of my professional career had taken me on more ups, downs, twists, turns, loops and free-falls than I could ever chronicle with true justice, but this was the moment I would remember best of all when revealing to my posterity just what it was like to be strapped in to that coaster. There had been bigger stages, brighter spotlights and more…prestigious awards, if you want to call them that. Yet I am no fool. I owe everything that I am to my partner in life, as well as in crime. And to simultaneously possess the ring and the belt, to know that together, we were inseparable and unstoppable….well, I can scarcely wrack my brain to ponder a more beautiful thought. I lied. I am a fool. My odyssey throughout this business is well-documented, as is the wife’s. That’s why people even care about us at all, isn’t it? Isn’t that why people bother following our careers from one brand to the next? Not fans of the sport itself, which is an art form spanning generations, born far before my own existence and one that will carry on long after my name is last whispered, but those who have marked us out as individuals, taking each harrowing victory and painful defeat equally as harder, sometimes harder, than we take it ourselves. Somehow these people, those who have witnessed my odyssey as well as participated, both directly and indirectly….again, perhaps this is because I am a fool, but I feel and have always felt that they’ve dutifully tuned in because they care about me, at least on some level. I only wish the same could be said about the lords of my most recent professional venture. To them, I was just another golden boy with a checkered past who could earn them some extra cash by being hammered through the gauntlet one more time. Even though I only set foot through their doors with one singular purpose in mind, I was foolish enough (are we sensing a pattern yet?) to believe that the name Crisis meant something more than dollar signs and ratings points. When I go to bed at night, I have nightmares. It's the same one every time. I'm fifty years old, I'm confined to a wheelchair. There's dust everywhere. Stacks of unpaid bills. I haven't eaten in days. Sitting at my feet is my five-year-old son, who looks like a goddamn twig. He's got tears in his eyes. He looks up at me and says ''Why, Daddy? Why can't we eat? Why can't you play with me?'' And all I can tell him is ''Well son, the wrestling business isn't exactly the most stable enterprise there is. And no matter how hard Daddy pushed himself, every time he had to go some place new the assholes in charge made him start all the way over. I tried to be a good guy and earn it all over again, thinking one day that I'd climb out of the hole of destitution us mid-carders are buried in and make the big bucks in the main event. But my call never came, and one day my spine just gave out. I'm sorry, I'm so sorry...'' And he cries, and then I cry…and then I wake up in a cold sweat. With my microphone in hand, I told the people exactly that. It was then, suddenly, that I knew I’d hit my wall. I've broken my fucking back for this business. I actually went through with it and married that feisty little redhead, the once ever-present thorn in my side who I couldn’t stand but couldn’t stand to be without. It won't be long from now that some little Crises are on the way. I'm thirty. Thirty! In all my life, I never truly imagined what it would be like to see three decades go by and still be standing. And that was the undeniably key – still standing. I wanted to keep standing. I want to not be in constant pain when I’m chasing the future Crises around the house. I want the smiles of a new family facing the world for the first time. And that meant a major gut check. It was time to trade in the keys, to give up fame for future. While every part of me wanted to spit in the faces of those who dared not to care about what I’d been through, and climb one more latter toward immortality…I knew I could climb no further. I had nothing left to give. It was time to go home. Little did I know where home would turn out to be. 1.02. Home Again. ______________________________________ Darcy Markson awoke from what sure seemed like the longest nap of his entire life. Today is still Tuesday, right? he wondered silently, feeling as if the prospect of either several hours or several months fleeting away were equally plausible. Waking back to life meant waking his muscles along with it, as Darcy went about by extending his arms as far as they could reach. As his left arm reached out toward empty space he was instantly reminded that he was the sole occupant of his sleeping chamber, but as his hand gravitated back to earth it grazed against a shockingly warm area of his bed. Confused, Darcy weighed the possible solutions to the scenario: Was Amy here recently? Did her flight get cancelled? A quick roll to his left, however, solved the mystery. Reaper, Darcy’s midnight-hued feline friend, leapt gracefully from the rug onto the bed, evidently returning to the spot where he had been slumbering alongside his companion. A greeting from Reaper never failed to lighten Darcy’s mood, and this was no exception. Yet the reunion was bittersweet, as he was once again reminded that his wife was nowhere to be found. The early months of marriage, predictably, had been quite far from the smooth sail he had long, and admittedly quite foolishly, hoped for, which only became exacerbated by prolonged absence from each other’s company. His last appearance on television, a shocking return after pledging never to so much as set foot in a FUSE ring again, saw his attempt to save Amy Campbell from certain disaster result in his own disastrous arrival on the wrong end of a punishing spinebuster. And while this predicament would normally fail to earn the envy of even the most adamant masochist, many onlookers cringed with worry as yet another blow had been dealt to a spine that was seemingly held together by masking tape and fervent prayer after the fateful freefall from atop the structure that bore the name of the Atlantic Wrestling Club’s final main event. Amy had seen enough, and in the name of Darcy’s own good she forbid him to even think about returning to avenge the despicable attack. A younger, less-experienced, less-married incarnation of Darcy Markson might have protested Amy’s steadfast decision. But the crosshairs that were seemingly tattooed onto Darcy’s lower spine were certainly no secret, and taking that kind of risk would be far better saved for a moment more monumental than exacting revenge on the latest in the long line of those who had sought to make his place on the injury shelf bronzed, dedicated and most certainly permanent. There was only one problem…a problem that Darcy weighed against his will, tightly forcing his eyelids shut as a painful trip down memory lane ensued. In Darcy’s absence, the woman far better known as Aimz had fallen in with a group known as the Highwaymen. It seemed more often than not that factions dictated the course of wrestling greatness, and this foursome proved no exception. And while Darcy’s venture with FUSE appeared to be nothing more than a dismal fizzle of a finale, the career of his counterpart appeared to be only just beginning. With three times the protection of her near-crippled husband and a golden opportunity to shine, Amy Campbell finally found herself on the cusp of greatness that had eluded her since their days together at the old Pier Six. Truth be told, Darcy couldn’t be prouder, and knew of no one more deserving…but the one thing he didn’t know is where he fit into this equation. He winced as his eyelids fused even tighter. There was, unfortunately, a precedent to all of this. Following their joint departure from Pier Six Wrestling after Darcy’s first major back injury, suffered from his first thirty foot plummet into near-oblivion, the two had been set to embark on the independent circuit as a team, hoping to claim that impossible dream that had only a few scant months ago been realized. Unfortunately, only half of that team ever made it there. Unlike the fall at Triangles, where Darcy miraculously (please believe the author’s use of the term, which is most certainly not loosely) recovered in time to reclaim the summit and its ultimate prize, he was simply unable to withstand the initial, devastating shock to his spinal column. Nor was he able to cope with the fact that the girl who had arranged his exodus from Pier Six was now going to continue on without him, leaving him out of work and out of hope. One day he could take it no longer, and told her as much. What followed was the biggest fight they’d ever had. She walked out of his house; he walked out of his own continent, disappearing into the brush of the Brazilian rainforest, practically disengaging from civilization entirely. It had taken another miracle, one Darcy still struggle to understand nearly every day, to bring him out of the jungle and lead him down the path to reclaim the girl who had now become a woman, and would one day become his bride. And not only this, but the career that had been left in shambles and the dream of athletic success long left for dead had been reclaimed. A beautiful thought, one would think, until considering the notion that history now appeared poised to repeat itself. The timeline wasn’t quite identical, but all the major events had arrived for the party: A championship victory against all odds came with the liquor. A freakish fall from atop a cage brought the chips and dip. The DJ’s equipment came courtesy of his and Amy’s departure (albeit forced) from the company that had contributed so much to their notoriety. And of course the magical white pony, the ingredient needed to send any good party over the edge, appeared in the form of Amy’s continued success in foreign waters while Darcy was left to languish at home with nothing but Reaper and a wrapped-up wrestling career to keep him company. Darcy shook his head vigorously, blinking his eyes back to life as he shook the thoughts from his head. Events may have unfolded in a chillingly similar fashion, but the key difference in this saga was clear – its protagonist. Darcy was only 23 when he had been forced into early retirement; too young, too headstrong…and admittedly, too stupid to come to terms with his unfortunate predicament. He couldn't be sure if it was early in the morning or the middle of night, but a nervous twinge shot through Darcy Markson when he heard the front door close. Most people assumed that he and Amy traveled together, and as a result wouldn't be home a lot of the time. Those people could easily be on their way inside for the plasma screen they kept in the living room. He started to sit up in bed, but the nervousness was gone after a very silent, breathless moment where he listened to the intruding footsteps. They were light thuds, but gave the impression that the person attached wasn't exactly light on their feet. Not one step was hesitant, and that's what brought on his sigh of relief -- those footsteps belonged to someone notoriously clumsy, but also someone who could step into a wrestling ring and act as if she'd never tripped over herself as a kid. Reaper recognized the steps before even Darcy and shot out of bed, tip-toeing through the slight opening in the bedroom door. The voice that responded to his greeting meow eased Darcy back against his pillow. ''Hey, Reaper!'' she half-whispered. There was a clearly relieved undertone in her voice when she continued to ask ''Where's the papa?'' He could only assume that he woke up to the sound of her car in the driveway, because there was no other way to time something so perfectly. As had been the case for many weeks, she finally arrived at the times he'd started to miss her most. His workaholic, his champion... his redhead. She looked like hell when she poked her head into the room, peering through the darkness to see the state of her husband. Though she couldn't tell if his eyes were even opened, because hers weren't done adjusting to the lack of lighting in their bed room, Amy quietly removed her jacket and slid onto the foot of the bed, looking like she could fall asleep there and then. Though she still couldn't see, she could hear and feel Darcy rustling around under the blankets. The mattress would rise and fall when he shifted his weight to sit up to see her, and that's when she knew he was really there. In this case, there's no room for 'I'm sorry about working so much' - they've both been well aware of the consequences of leading profitable careers. Instead, her first words are both much simpler and far more negative than an apology. ''I missed you.'' She sighed, ''I missed you way too much.'' The redhead smiled sheepishly, ''And I know I say that every time, but I mean it now more than ever.'' Sure, it was enough to make even the most diehard Dashboard Confessional fan want to throw up. No, there was simply no denying that Darcy and Amy Markson were as emo-tastic as their critics would charge. But while onlookers might find it revolting, he could think of no reward worth trading in exchange for the feeling in his stomach upon hearing those five delightful words. ''Shouldn’t you be off main-eventing somewhere?'' he asked the bedroom’s co-owner, hoping she could see the smile on his face meant to partner his sarcastic tone. ''You’d think that, wouldn’t you?'' came her sharp reply as she dropped her seemingly ever-stuffed suitcases near the doorframe. ''But it would seem a certain overzealous husband of mine went and got himself spine bustered on my behalf…and now revenge will have to wait until after I came home to make sure he was okay.'' Deciding that there was no sense keeping her in the dark…Darcy flipped on the bedside lamp within his right arm’s reach, bathing the room in the early, dim glow of the eco-friendly fluorescent bulb. Indeed, Amy did look like she’s been on a plane for weeks on end, while forced to go fifteen rounds with an early 90s-era Tyson who forgot to pack Zoloft in his carry-on. And yet still, easily and overwhelmingly, she was the most beautiful girl in the room, with no one even approaching a close second. ''I can’t speak on behalf of the rest of your overzealous husbands…'' Darcy said, as he rolled his eyes. ''…but I can assure you that *~I~* am perfectly fine. Maybe even good enough to-'' ''Don’t even think about it,'' she interjected, cutting him off before he had a chance to re-negotiate his in-ring capabilities. ''As far as I’m concerned you’re good enough to stay in that bed and wait for me to get there.'' Darcy raised a brow. ''You need to unpack right this very second?'' ''Just need to check some e-mail. I’ve got an important eBay bid going on an actual pair of Johnny Cash’s black jeans.'' Darcy loved many things about his redhead, but chief among them was her propensity to participate in late-night, frivolous, and almost always alcohol-induced online auction purchases. A great joy had been brought into his life when Amy introduced it to him a few months ago, and a fair amount of income had been burned in pursuing this new hobby – although Amy clearly remained the bar to which he could only hope to raise himself. A chance to own a piece of the Man in Black’s actual black was far more awe-inspiring than Darcy’s latest eBay steal: a set of Ginsu knives. They can cut through shoes! he remembered telling himself, only noting later that he had no shoes in need of cutting. ''Good God, Darcy… did you really have to put me on the Amelia Touros mailing list? I think my ovaries are crying.'' Darcy’s eyes shot open. A proud member of said list for his favorite female balladeer, the alert sitting in Amy’s inbox either unlikely managed to elude his own, or it had only just arrived. ''Just trying to expand your horizons Amy…'' he replied, doing his best to contain the excitement that Amelia had released her first e-mail update that he could remember seeing or hearing about. ''You’ll thank me later, I swear it.'' ''You know, you’re not doing ANYTHING to help your cause against all the people who are convinced you’re a woman,'' Amy said, glancing through the rest of her inbox and slapping the monitor of her laptop closed, evidently finding nothing else worthy of note. ''And how, exactly, am I supposed to defend myself when I’m not allowed to make any public appearances?'' Darcy asked in kind. ''I’m not really injured per se, so much as running out of lucky breaks…but that’s how I’ve felt for like ten years. Really, I’ve felt like a million bucks all week.'' ''Is that so?'' Removing her midnight-hued hoodie/sweater concoction, Amy slid over toward the edge of the bed, gliding toward her ''ailing'' husband. ''I know a doctor or two who might feel otherwise. You’re more than welcome to try convincing them otherwise… but before you do, how about you try convincing me that your back can handle a little pressure?'' Darcy knew where she was going with it but, as always, there was a catch. As with everything in the world, pleasure didn't come without a cost. ''Just don't think you're getting a free pass,'' she chuckled, '''Cause you've gotta rub my back before anything else gets touched.'' Women. My turn! ________________________________________ Once you commit to spending an eternity together, it seems that the need to stuff your schedules with one another becomes a lot less urgent. I think Darcy saw that flaw a few months ago and, in a small way, I knew it was there too - but we both really realized it when we'd spent a day in our own home and had hardly seen one another. I spent the day sending him all over the place while I made phone calls to remind people where they'd be getting drunk New Years Eve. He brought home some of the liquor I'd requested, but I made him watch the random basket of precooked Asian cuisine I'd bought to put out in case someone craved a spring roll or potstickers when they got wasted. Why make him watch food that's already cooked? I'm not sure, but Reaper's habit of hunting inanimate foodstuffs probably had something to do with it. I left so I could go get more liquor... just to correct what I saw as mistakes in his first round of purchases. I know, it's the normal married couple pile of crap, but that's not how we're supposed to be. By the time I realized that we needed the alone time, we were halfway through a holiday party with our closest friends. 1.03. New Years Rockin' Eve. _______________________________________ ''To the cutest, coolest couple I know... at least, the cutest couple I'm not a part of,'' Johnny chuckled when he realized he'd almost forgot his entire marriage in one slip, ''Darcy, Amy... best party in a long time. Nice work with the, uhh... the stuff I'm about to take a shot of.'' While Darcy's best friend had, as usual, opted to get shitfaced that evening, Amy and Darcy were each on respectively different paths. Amy held glasses of wine throughout the night, none of which were emptying even at a snail's pace, and Darcy had gone the route of the occasional beer. Mainly, he was toting around a Dixie cup full of soda - a cup he didn't go out of his way to declare as alcohol-free... after all, it was a party, he couldn't look like he wasn't even trying to catch a buzz - that'd give the wrong impression. After the speech and the ding of midnight, the couple met one another in the kitchen. Secret rendevous? Not so much - but the looks they'd been giving one another throughout the night spoke volumes... they wanted the same thing. Amy spoke in low tones, and gestured to the other room where the party was raging. ''Offer cab money, anything... let's just get rid of them.'' ''But we need an excuse to hustle them away just after midnight on New Years, Ames.'' said Darcy, ''They're gonna want to party all night.'' ''We'll get them to move it to a bar, or Johnny's house,'' she suggested, ''We can tell them my parents are due into the airport in the morning or something.'' Darcy smirked at his desperately plotting wife, ''There's always a last-minute backup plan-slash-lie. That's the girl I married.'' He put his arm around the anxious redhead, smiling to himself. Finally, she was seeing his side of things. He could tell she craved the time alone together. Maybe - just maybe - Aimz was finally getting tired of the rat race. ----Finish it, Amelia------------------- I am a bitter legionnaire whose motivation is derived from negativity. This doesn't make me a bad person by any stretch of the imagination, because it's not 'that' kind of negativity that I thrive on. While I hurt people for a living, I generally don't set out to ruin their lives. I feed off of the negativity that's thrown at me by others, not from myself to the masses. For some reason, though, none of this helps me to sleep better at night. I enjoy hurting, and I seem to gather my motivation to better myself or best everybody else by the challenge that being disliked presents. Does that make me some kind of masochist? Maybe it just means that I care more about how I look to others than how I see myself - but then again, if that was the case, I wouldn't currently be analyzing my thoughts and wearing sweatpants. My New Year's resolution is to give myself a kick in the ass, in the ring and in my marriage. I'll start new patterns. More time at home, balanced with more time training. I'll start 2008 by giving Darcy the version of me that he deserves to see - not the mess he married. And as far as FUSE goes? I might not be able to make many enemies to motivate me right now, but I'm sure gonna try. Just wait and see, self. Just wait and see. (I'm back! Expect better work in the coming weeks. :))
