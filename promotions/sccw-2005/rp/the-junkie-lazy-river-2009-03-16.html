The Junkie: Lazy River

16 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/16/2009 The junkie lived in his head, rambling on. "It feels like a…like a lazy fucking river." "So you’re comfortable then?" "There is not a writer in the world good enough to describe this. Maybe dig up Hemmingway, see if he can do it justice. And give him some cologne, dude probably smells like shit." "So that is a yes?" Walter Halvorson was having a quiet dinner at home. His wife, a round woman with a round face, studied him as he jabbed tentatively at his baked chicken. It seemed to her that he was checking it for signs of life. He had not even touched the green beans or mashed potatoes. His head was clearly somewhere else; perhaps he was in one of his moods that he described to her when they first began dating, twenty six years ago. He had said that sometimes the thought of conversation, even with the people he loved the most, made him want to throw up. This made her laugh at the time, but as the years went on, and she became one of those people that he loved, it became less amusing. Or maybe, she thought, he had just had an odd day. "I’m just peachy. Say, you got time for a joke?" "I’ve got to be heading home, my wife will have dinner on the ta…" "So these two kids are walking home from school right?" Wrestling fans, even the most hardcore ones, wouldn’t be able to pick Walter out of a lineup. He was a tall and skinny man in his mid forties. He wore glasses and dressed plainly, never drawing much attention to himself. His hair was already beginning to gray, just as his father’s had done at this age, and his father before him. Even when he yelled there was a softness to him, as if he was just trying out for a part in a play, and in a moment he would be back to his old self. He donated money to the United Way every year, and went to church every Sunday. He had never performed a hip toss or cut a promo, he had never raised a championship belt in the air. And he was fine with that. He didn’t need the fame, or the silly drama. But almost any wrestling fan would be able to pick out some of his associates, some of the men and women he had worked for over the years. These were not always the most admirable men and women. "So anything new?" Tamara finally worked up the courage to ask, trying as best as she could to not sound concerned. "Same old, same old I guess," Walter replied, and finally took a bite of his chicken. She forced a smile. Walter looked right through her, through the present, and saw a man lying on a bed, with a morphine IV stuck in his arm. Walter could hear his voice, clear as day. It was a slow stammering voice, a product of the drug’s potency. Occasionally the man would open and close his hands very slowly, and this seemed to please him enough. "They’re just walkin’ along, having a conversation about god knows what. Stupid kid bullshit probably. They got these Transformers backpacks, and their mothers just love the crap out of these brats, ya know?" In the present, Tamara’s eyebrow arched in concern, but her husband was stuck in the past, in this afternoon. He was stuck in the presence of another one of his duties. Another one of his tasks. Another one of his secrets. And in the past, this junkie won’t shut up. "So this truck comes around the corner, not paying attention to a goddamn thing. Hits these little buggers head on, they never had a chance. Their school books go flying, they are dead instantly. All kinds of little kid pieces everywhere." "I thought this was a joke you were telling?" "You just need to relax fella, let me finish the thing." Walter gave a perfunctory nod to Tamara after taking a few bites, as if to say ‘well done’. She returned the nod. A picture of them on their wedding day was hung on the wall behind her, and as she moved slightly it came in and out of view. He could remember trying to impress her, and constantly over analyzing everything he had said. Walter, the sappy fella that he was, used to chart their phone conversations based on quality with several variables. Including how often she laughed, whether plans were made, and so on. It was that same diligence and attention to detail that has made him a useful tool for certain professional wrestlers over the years. "So when the parents come down to ID the bodies, they break down. They cry and cry. It’s a real heart wrenching scene. Goddamn Sunday night Hallmark movie of the week shit. Holy hell this morphine…you gotta try this." "No thanks." "They will never have kids again, and their relationship will slowly disintegrate under the weight of such a tragedy. Isn’t it just a hoot?" "I would love for you to explain to me the humor in this." "Straight for the punchline, I like your style Walter." In the present, he watched her try to think of what she wanted to say to him. She had seen this look before. She stared at her feet, and her mouth moved slightly, almost rehearsing what she wanted to say. But just as per usual, standard behavior really, it melted away. This big thing she had to say, what seemed like the most important thing in the world to her just a few seconds ago, was gone. He didn’t even wonder what it was, but he remembered what it was like to wonder. "The real joke here is if you’d put one fourth of the effort you just put into the fake outrage you had about a story I made up into your home life…maybe you wouldn’t be so ashamed of yourself. Or maybe you’re just ashamed that you don’t care, maybe that’s it." "You just met me." Tamara stood up, and took her half finished plate into the kitchen, avoiding eye contact with her husband. He was far away, and she knew it. "Does that wife that’s waiting on you have any idea the kind of people you associate with? The kind of things that you’ve allowed to happen?" "My wife is none of your business." "These are all answers from the good guy manual, only that’s not you at all, is it? Maybe you should invite me to dinner. We can fill her in on the whole thing! I’d like a pork roast." Walter stared down at his own half finished plate, and sighed. The junkie had gotten to him. "I’ve done a lot of bad things in my life, and I think I’m just getting started. I’d like to say there was a grand reason behind this…but its’ just that shitty people pay so good, ya know? They got all this money from all their shitty enterprises. Hell you probably know that better than me. The difference is I’ve come to grips with who I am. I like morphine a lot…so, here I am, at the expense of your employer…enjoying it. It’s just that simple." Just then, Tamara walked back into the dining room, carrying a cake pan. She had swallowed her doubt in the kitchen, and her face was amiable again, although Walter could tell by her eyes she had been crying briefly. "I made marble cake with chocolate frosting, your favorite" she said. "My favorite," Walter repeated, and tried to smile. "I get depressed sometimes, sure. I am privy to certain things, certain undeniable truths that most people tend to ignore. But sitting here Walter…I’ve never felt better, maybe no one ever has in the history of time, I swear to God. I wish you could float down this river too." Walter cut into the cake with his fork, and raised the cake to his mouth, and for a millisecond he forgot about everything that had weighed down on his soul this day. Taste buds were telling his brain that the cake was delicious. "I wish you could feel this peace."
