She catches me staring -- she always catches me staring -- and she makes a show of checking her teeth, but we both know why, and it doesn't take her long to shove me in the arm. ''Quit it!'' She's whispering, afraid she'll wake my daughter. I don't have the heart to tell her that a nuclear bomb couldn't wake Quinn. ''You're freaking me out, Alex.'' ''I cannot help it. I am happy, and I... don't really know how to deal with it.'' I shrug, sinking back into the couch cushions, and I know my eyes are glittering over the rim of my wineglass. There's something playing on the television behind Quinn, but I muted it when she fell asleep -- I don't watch TV, not when the floorshow shifts its bare feet out from under it to rest them on my lap. ''You should be happy that Quinn's here, because she's the only reason you-know-who didn't put up a fuss about me coming down early with her, missing a day off with him.'' She tosses the leftover turkey toward the wastepaper basket and, fortunately for the cleaning staff, the discarded poultry meets its mark. ''Shouldn't you wake her up and get her to bed? And whose room is this, anyway? I know you got two, because the idiot at the front desk apologized that my room was on a completely different floor from the ones my friend and her mother were staying in.'' My brow wrinkles, the corner of my lips turning downward before that smile comes back to her face and I know she's paraphrasing the clerk's words. ''It's my room. Please, like I would leave her the big room when I am supposed to be showing off?'' I'm smiling, and I can't help it. ''Part of me just wants to let her sleep there, at least for now. She looks...'' My eyes drift to my daughter, curled up under a blanket on the floor. ''She looks peaceful. I wish I slept like that.'' There's a moment of silence in the room, before Amy speaks again, softly. ''I wish you slept like that, too.'' The words draw my eyes back to her, and she's quickly grinning. ''Because you're totally a cover-hog, I mean.'' That's not what she meant, and we both know it. My gaze slips back to my daughter (my daughter -- sometimes I have a hard time imagining that I had any part in creating someone so fabulous), and I feel her fingers pull the a strand of hair out of my face. ''I like seeing you around her,'' Amy admits. ''You're much... more centered.'' ''Am I? There's something about her that makes it hard for me to be mad.'' I pat her calf. ''You being here helps some, too.'' Amy caught me staring again; I could tell from the scowl that threatened to break up her smile. ''C'mon, we don't wanna get caught.'' She pulls her feet from my lap (I'm struck by how warm my legs are when she moves), shifting slowly and quietly back to a solitary position on the seat, gesturing to Quinn. ''And you can't let her sleep there all night, she'll have back problems. I should probably go, anyway...'' ''No fair using reverse psychology on me.'' I pivot to my feet, nudging the diminutive teen on the floor. ''Quinn. Quinn, dear, you should go to bed.'' ''Wha?'' She blinks lazily, reaching blindly for her glasses on the table. ''I swear, I had the strangest dream.'' ''I told you to lay off Ashe's sweet potato casserole.'' Far be it from Amy to not offer her commentary from the peanut gallery. ''Anyone who eats vegemite on anything but a dare should not be allowed to cook.'' ''He's from New Zealand, not Australia, Aimes,'' Quinn mumbles as she climbs to her feet. I find myself smiling again and have to consciously restrain myself. ''Like, way to be an ignorant American.'' ''Come on, up you go,'' I say, ignoring Amy's mock hurt look. Quinn shuffles towards the door, pausing as she looks over her shoulder. ''Mom?'' she asks, and I know my brow has arched before I open my mouth. She darts across the room, wrapping spindly arms around my waist. ''Thanks,'' she murmurs against my shoulder. ''I really had fun tonight, and I know I was kind of mean earlier.'' I wrap my free arm around her shoulders. ''I had a good time, too.'' ''Maybe next time, you'll let Amy finish that story about Jason Cruise at the bar?'' I press my lips together in a line, but I don't mean it. ''I doubt it. Now go, off with you to the -- '' The girl is across the room, offering a second hug to Amy; she looks just as surprised as I am. ''Happy Thanksgiving, Amy. Tell Darc I said the Stars still suck.'' ''Judging by the cursing whenever the TV's on or his laptop's open, he already knows.'' Amy smirks, jokingly kicking Quinn in the rear on her way out the door. She turns her head to me, putting on a decent act for the sake of my daughter. ''I'm gonna take off soon, too. Just need to go over a couple of things, should only be a few minutes. Unless you're going to bed too, that is.'' ''I'll probably be up for at least a little bit,'' I say as Quinn shuts the adjoining door behind her. ''You've got a good kid, Alex.'' ''I don't know what I did to deserve it, though.'' I'm wandering back to the couch, fingers skidding over Amy's shoulders. ''I really am glad you came. It meant a lot to her.'' It meant more to me, but I don't think I have to say that. ''Up for a nightcap before you head out?'' Amy slouches near the door to Quinn's room, placing an ear to it and holding her breath to listen. When the TV flicks on (even I can hear it across the ring), she likely hears blankets rustling (I am not the only cover-hog, and I believe Quinn described a king-sized bed to herself as ''wicked''), she takes it upon herself to lock the door - hoping Quinn is too distracted to hear the click and question why we bother locking it against her. The light returns to her eyes as she looks back into the room, taking strides to launch herself onto the couch beside me. Unzipping and tossing her graffiti-tagged hoodie onto the bed was the last step to ultimate relaxation for the little redhead. ''It's sweet that you think I'm going anywhere but yeah, either way I'm up for a drink.'' My fingers dance over her jawline as I step past, and she stops me to rub her thumb over my knuckles. It's a breathless gesture, as I am sure she is no different than I, and is somewhat eager to simply be able to touch me. ''Never has a meal been so much fun and so torturous.'' I'm squatting beside the wet bar as I speak. ''What kind of tiny bottle of liquor do you want?'' I'm perusing the selection, so I don't see her slip off the couch or dart to me. I do feel it when she tackles me to the ground, pinning me on the plush carpeting with a soft cackle. ''Hi,'' I say with a grin, plucking a bang of her own out of her face. ''Hey.'' She's grinning now. ''You looked like you could use a little bit of run-jump-tackling.'' ''Ah.'' My grin can't help but match her own. ''So what you're saying is...'' ''Is that I now have everything I need in easy reach -- booze and the babe.'' ''And what do you intend to do with these things?'' ''I'm still weighing my options.'' I chuckle, but it's deep and in the back of my throat. It's been twelve days since Mayhem tossed her around the ring now, and her ribs are clearly bothering her less, my fingers dancing along the hem of her tanktop. Lord knows what I want. ''See, I...'' The first kiss is barely a peck, gentle and mild. ''I've kinda spent all day thinking about what I'd do if I got you alone for a few seconds.'' The second one is harder, mashing her lips into mine. Her voice drops an octave, nearly a growl. ''But I accidentally trashed the list I wrote, so I'm winging it. Feel free to lend a hand, my memory kinda sucks.'' The third kiss -- I would be lying if I said I remember it completely; call it lack of air to my brain, because I swear she's trying to suffocate me with her tongue. Not that I find myself caring. I still hear the door open, softly -- call it years of practice. The main door never did get locked, and the step-step-thump of a three-legged walk could only mean one man, his soft voice putting more meanings than should be allowed into the word ''Ahem.'' Amy's frowning as she pulls up, though I will note she doesn't move off, just glances at the scarecrow of a man I'm watching upside-down, lowering her lips to my ear. ''Let's get rid of him. I promise I'll help you hide the body when we're done.'' I should be ashamed that the thought makes my grin quirk, but we both know he's smarter than to intrude unless there wasn't another option. ''What is it?'' I ask, curling an arm around Amy and tipping us both to the side. Elijah would apologize, but all three of us would know he would not mean it. ''Phillip Kennedy is in the lobby and has asked for an audience.'' ''Son of a -- '' Amy is gearing up to deliver a tongue-lashing. ''Okay, let's get rid of him. All three of us can hide the body.'' ''No.'' I clamp down on Amy's waist, holding her hips against mine. ''It is the night before Thanksgiving, I am here with my family, and I will see Phillip tomorrow in Rochester. Is Kathryn injured?'' ''He did not mention such.'' ''Then it can wait until tomorrow evening. Send him away.'' ''What do you want us to tell him?'' Hawke asks, both hands clutching the head of that cane. When Amy smiles, all three parties know no good can come. ''Tell him she's busy trying to get poor Amy Campbell drunk to seduce her. Discretely, of course.'' ''Yes, because either of those tasks are so terribly difficult.'' I lunge up and nibble the side of Amy's neck, causing her to giggle. ''Tell him anything, Elijah. But in the name of all that's holy, get rid of him.'' Hawke's lips press together as he turns and I find myself remembering his face on a chartered plane to Milan with Miranda in his arms. He had less wrinkles, then, but life had been kinder to him. ''As you say,'' he murmurs, hobbling away. I cannot consider my old friend's heartache for long, however, but that is only because Amy's fingers are quite dexterous, and I look down, getting only a glimpse of that grin before it's engulfing me again. ''I believe we were composing a list...?'' Amy asks airily. ''Yes, we were, but you can't leave me like this on the floor all night -- I'll have back problems.'' ''You'll have back problems anyway,'' Amy smiled mischievously, ''On a floor, a couch or a bed, my nails can still be sharp when they want to. But you're lucky -- you've got all three options thanks to your excellent taste in hotels. Or we could just go back to watching TV...'' My growl is answer enough. If this were a movie, we would stand up and fall delicately back into bed, arm-in-arm. Life doesn't work that way, though, so when I clamber to my feet and pull her with me, we're suddenly a tangle of arms and legs, a tornado expelling clothing like refuse. ''Do your worst.'' I try to sneer, but it's hard to pull off. ''You asked for it...'' In the end, my life is no different than anyone else's. Sure, it's complicated, it's scary, it's raw, and it's powerfully, breathtakingly, heart-wrenchingly emotional... But no matter what you call me, I would not give it up for anything. * * * * * ''Can't seem to shut it off - this thing I've begun And it's hard to tell - just where it's coming from And it's hard to see - what I'm capable of And it's hard to believe - just what I've become.'' -- ''Me, I'm Not'' by Nine Inch Nails from the album ''Year Zero'' available now on Interscope Records.
