Limbo

9 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/9/08 June 26, 2008 Temptation Aftermath of the Phillip Kennedy/Sean Sterling Match 'Ugh.' It was a short, but heartfelt sentiment from Phillip Kennedy, lying flat on his back after taking Sean Sterling's patented Hail to the Thief corkscrew Shooting Star Press. A quick glance to his right confirmed that the cameras weren't rolling. Whether this was due to a commercial or something happening backstage, he didn't know. All he cared about was that this would give him a modicum of privacy as he groaned, and rolled towards the ring apron, clutching at various pained body parts. 'Fucker hits like a truck,' was about all he could mutter, under his breath, grabbing his cowboy hat from ringside as he retreated. 'YOU'RE A MANWHORE!' *clap clap clap clap clap* A quarter of the way down the aisle he paused, looking up at the Memphis crowd, which was slowly but surely taking up the chant. 'YOU'RE A MANWHORE!' *clap clap clap clap clap* 'YOU'RE A MANWHORE!' *clap clap clap clap clap* 'YOU'RE A MANWHORE!' *clap clap clap clap clap* 'Damn right I am,' the Big Stack called back, defiantly. 'Got more ass that night than most of you inbreds'll see in six years, if we don't count your immediate families!' 'BOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOO!!!!' Even as he scowled his way down the aisle, opting to ignore the rest of the jeers and catcalls, a slight smile was threatening to escape him. Sure, he'd lost...but there was always something to throwing back the proverbial middle finger in the face of the masses. There would be other matches. Other chances. And what was it Savant had said to him again, a little bit before the match? He wasn't going to win. She'd been right, now that he thought of it...though it hadn't been for lack of trying. Kennedy had seen Sterling, poised, and ready to have his head taken off. Sterling had ducked. Luckily for him. A quick duck of his own behind the curtain had him face to face with a couple of Sin City doctors, who looked at him expectantly. 'I'm fine,' he insisted, pushing past the medical professionals towards what he wanted to see the most right now: catering. Yup, covered in sweat, exhausted, and ready to just sit down and rest for the remainder of the evening, all Phillip Benjamin Kennedy really wanted was a nice turkey sandwich, a Coca-Cola, and maybe even a bag of Baked Lays. For the record, items one and two were present, but the third had to be compromised: pretzels were there instead. No biggie though, right? 'Get some chow in me, watch the rest of the show, then I can head back to the hotel and get ready for three hours of internet poker before bed. Got a $200 sit and go. Now, if only I'd won that match, and/or had a Sex Kitten here, this'd be a perfect night.' With catering empty, Kennedy unloaded his food on one of the tables, sat down in a chair, and put his feet up. Taking a satisfying chomp of his sandwich, Kennedy glanced up at one of the numerous TVs that always seemed to be backstage at SCCW events. 'Desade probably insists on it,' he whispered to himself, darkly. She, of course, was the leader of the Hand. The group that more or less was trying to take the company over. The group that was, it seemed more and more lately, more or less succeeding. And, equally importantly, the group he was getting to know more and more by the week. Katsidy first. Then her sister, Savant. There were more, far more members lurking out there -- even if he restricted himself to the wrestlers and Desade's direct associates. The more SCCW he watched, the more he participated in, he was coming to realize that the Spider in the Web was an incredibly apt name for the woman in the annoyingly starched white pantsuit. Which gave him pause. It would give anyone pause. 'Can't do a damn thing about it when I'm talking to them, though,' he mumbled, disgustedly. He had tried to stand up to Savant a bit more than Katsidy, which had met with varying degrees of success. It was still hard for him to believe that the woman would come to him of her own free will with no prompting -- hard, but still plausible. Which was the inherent problem. A little plausibility goes a long way when weaving a tapestry of bullshit. If Desade was behind everything...that meant that everything that had happened thus far in his relations with the Dead Man's Hand had a point. That thought, alone, was enought to make him shiver a bit. He had never met the woman. Only seen her on television, and she carried herself with the confident air of a supermodel mixed with the swagger of a Mafia don. He was, however, torn from his reflection by the shrill, obnoxious sound of the voice of a former SCCW Universal Champion, issuing threats in her usual arrogant tone of voice. 'Aimz.' With that, his eyes were drawn straight up, to the Red Raver holding one lesbian lover of Charlotte Ramone by the hair. 'Aw shit. Here we go..' The backstage party, it seemed, had broken down. And moments later, Kennedy watched a martial arts weapon of some-sort crack across the birthday girl's back, setting off a melee within the once happy party setting. This, Phillip Kennedy decided, almost immediately, was not good. Not good at all. 'Hoo boy, looks like the shit's gonna hit the fan tonight. Now, do I want to be around for it or not?' Rather quickly, he made the decision with a chomp of his sandwich. He would stay. As he expected, it didn't take long once the main event began. The events played on the catering room screen as if in fast forward, quicker than he could react. The Hand was in the ring and on the attack before he could take another bite of turkey sandwich. Jay Draven was standing beside Charlotte Ramone before he could compute the entire clusterfuck. And then it happened. Draven had one of the Hand up, poised to deliver his signature Spinebuster. A move that, by the way, had beaten Kennedy in his first SCCW match. Katsidy had leaped on, trying to prevent Draven from executing the move, but the Modern Day Warrior was too strong. Spinebuster. Sandwiching Katsidy between man and canvas. Wide-eyed, Kennedy made an instant, snap decision. He was going to go out there, into the raging maelstrom, and strike Jay Draven down like an avenging angel. Well, an avenging angel who was ticked off that the woman he'd slept with had just been hurt by a son of a bitch who'd beaten him in his first match. Shooting to his feet, Phillip grabbed the chair he was sitting on, snapping it closed. He was going down to the ring to crease Jay Draven's skull with a steel chair. What happened after that...he didn't know. It was entirely possible and probable that Charlotte Ramone would be all 'Hey, that wasn't very nice taking out someone who's helping me' and attempt to take him down. But since he had a chair, Kennedy figured he could be all 'Sorry, bitch, but I've got a chair!' and go all THWACK, and it would be awesome. Now armed, he looked back up at the screen, only to see a rather different scene... the Highwaymen cornering Savant in the middle of the ring. 'Shit, shit, SHIT!' he bellowed. The odds were now bad. No. Strike bad. Atrocious. God only knew if he could even GET to Jay Draven to smack him in the skull with a chair. No, that was unlikely, but he had a new objective now. 'Got to get her out of there!' Even with a chair, he doubted he could take out the Highwaymen. Fuck, Jonathan Rhine was still going with one friggin' eye! But Phillip didn't care. Something inside of him had snapped. Katsidy and Savant were the only--he didn't quite dare call them friends yet-- people he knew in the company. The only people he knew anything about. Fuck the consequences. It was Whack-A-Mole time for as long as he could keep swinging the chair. Sure, when he got it taken away from him, he was fucked, but he could deal with that. Maybe he could buy Savant time. And then, *IT* happened. For the first time since he'd understood the seriousness of the discussions that he had been having with Katsidy and Savant, the leader of all things Hand was on the jumbotron, cold, uncaring, and laying out a plot that was equal parts Snidely Whiplash and evil bitch. 'She...wouldn't.' And then, the immediate edit. 'Would she?' The standoff was on, between Desade on Jumbotron and Rhine in arena, but standing even stiller than either was Phillip Benjamin Kennedy, steel chair dangling from one hand, partially eaten turkey sandwich on the table, and horror in his eyes. Thankfully, he wouldn't have to find out..even though his stomach DID lurch violently when he saw the empty stretcher ejected from the ambulance. The chair fell from nerveless fingertips, clattering on the floor as he continued to watch until the feed went dead. The image of Krissy's face, battered and beaten, would haunt him longer than he cared to think about. As his stomach lurched again, he realized that it wasn't just a one time deal, and that a bathroom was going to be necessary. One problem. No bathroom near catering. 'FUCK!' Frantic eyes darted left and right, to no avail. The glowing red EXIT sign seemed to offer his only escape out of the stuffy catering room, and he took it, charging out what ended up being the side door of the arena and a small group of stairs. He only made it three steps further, just enough to get him away from the bottom stair, when the cold sweat began to break out on his body. Around the corner, SCCW fans were filing out of the arena, left and right, stunned by what they had seen...unable to believe the atrocities that the Dead Man's Hand seemed willing to perpetrate for the sake of...what exactly? A title? A goddamned professional wrestling title? Something bigger? He wasn't sure. Not at all, especially not now. 'Yo, Kennedy! Autograph?' A teenaged fan held out the program from the evening's show, and a small sharpie marker for Phillip to use to sign. Kennedy held out a hand, and shook his head -- and it was only then that the fan saw the deathly expression on the Big Stack's face, the pallor there, the far off stare in his eyes. 'Dude, you okay?' Said fan was fortunate enough to dive out of the way just in time, as three bites of turkey sandwich led the flow of everything Kennedy had eaten in the past day and a half out of his innards. 'Siiiick!' With that, the fan was gone, to leave Kennedy to his vomiting. Fans returning to their cars quickly moved to either side of the wretching wrestler, whose second wave of nausea was forcing more bile from a stomach that he couldn't quite understand how had anything left in it. Perhaps predictably, a moment or two of dry heaving was next, before he forced his arms to hold his weight up on hands and knees so he didn't fall face first into a puddle of his own puke. 'No poker tonight,' he managed, wiping snot and vomit from his face as he shook with disgust: at the Hand, and at himself. 'What the fuck am I doing here? What the fuck kinda person am I?' ==================================== One evening later... Before that night, he'd known exactly what the fuck kinda person he was. In fact, he was drunkenly explaining it at this very moment, to a bartender in one of Memphis' trendier watering holes. 'Yeah, I'm an asshole, man,' the Big Stack admitted, clinging to a beer and a shot of Jack Daniels (probably not the best way to keep from vomiting again). 'I'm the type of guy who'll steal his best buddy's girlfriend. What I'm not, though, is the kind of asshole that drugs a girl's drink.' To explain in a slightly less disgusting manner, he was a jerk...but not a total jerk. He had values and beliefs, even if they were slightly self-centered and fucked up, and some things were sacred to him. Temptation, if nothing else, had taught him that there was seemingly nothing sacred to the Dead Man's Hand. 'What the fuck, man. What the fuck...' This time, the words were directed inwardly. He'd seen a potential murder plot unfold, on live television...and before it had begun, he was going to jump into the ring to save the day for the people who the plot was meant to benefit. The notice slipped under his door the next morning, when he finally woke up after feeling like he wanted to die for most of the evening, was of little consolation. 'A Livewire Title shot. It's a poker playing asshole against a self-righteous faux-prophet. Who the Hell is everyone going to cheer for?' The shot of JD was gone, thrown down the Stack's gullet in record time. Several other shot glasses sat near it, in perfect formation. Sure, alcohol wasn't the best way to deal with problems...but few men had problems quite like Phillip Kennedy's. For the first time in his career, he wasn't looking forward to going to work -- not even with a title shot. 'Maybe I'll just go straight to the locker-room. No walking around backstage. Might run into someone.' 'Someone' could be any of a number of people that he didn't think he could face right now, who all, coincidentally, collected a paycheck from Alexandra Pierce. Island music seemed to be pumping through the club, loud enough that it was making it harder and harder for Kennedy to concentrate on his own problems. Really, though, that was what island music was supposed to do. Make you feel like you were on vacation. Make your troubles go away. 'OKAY BRUDDAS AND SISTAHS,' the DJ's loud voice boomed, 'IT'S TIME FOR DA LIMBO CONTEST!' 'A friggin' LIMBO contest,' Kennedy asked himself, in disbelief, even as he turned to look down at the dance floor. It took him about five seconds to see the possible appeal in this limbo contest, as the contestants all appeared to be rather good-looking women. 'Score,' he whispered, rising from his seat, throwing a couple of bills to the bartender, and wading his way through the club crowd to try and get a better vantage point. Soon enough, as promised, the traditional chant broke out, and the women began to wiggle their way underneath the limbo stick, being held by two rather pleased looking young men. 'HOW LOW CAN YOU GO? LIMBO!' 'HOW LOW CAN YOU GO? LIMBO!' 'HOW LOW CAN YOU GO? LIMBO!' After being impressed at the first woman's ability to somehow slither under the limbo stick while still wearing stiletto heels, the irony of the refrain struck him. 'HOW LOW CAN YOU GO? LIMBO!' 'HOW LOW CAN YOU GO? LIMBO!' 'HOW LOW CAN YOU GO? LIMBO!' Another gorgeous co-ed made her way underneath, but it no longer mattered. The words were in his head, the question seemingly more applicable to Sin City Championship Wrestling than to this dance. 'HOW LOW CAN YOU GO? LIMBO!' 'HOW LOW CAN YOU GO? LIMBO!' 'HOW LOW CAN YOU GO? LIMBO!' 'How low CAN I go,' he wondered to himself. The LiveWire Title was at stake. Wyatt Connors was, by all accounts, as big a fuck as Kennedy himself. Maybe a bigger one. No one would likely shed tears if Kennedy screwed Wyatt over en route to becoming the LiveWire Champion. To survive in SCCW, you had to be one of two things. You had to be a principled individual. A Highwayman. Or you had to be a scumbag. And, though scumbags like himself and Connors called Sin City home, the scummiest of the scum was the Dead Man's Hand. Men and women that he was becoming acquainted with. And, if he didn't fuck up too badly, it was starting to look more and more like the Hand would come to him with tasks. Contracts, if one took the cynical path. Had one of those tasks been laid before him? Connors was a good competitor...but no Sean Sterling. Was this, unlike last week, a match he was meant to win? The sick feeling in his stomach told him that he was likely to find out the answer at Temptation in Arkansas, whether he wanted to or not. A shapely shoulder brushed against his, effectively snatching his attention up towards the attractive blonde who had bumped into him. Phillip opened his mouth to speak, but she was quicker than he was. 'You should give it a try, Mr. Kennedy. I'm sure everyone's quite curious as to just how low you can go.' She was gone before he could form an effective retort. Any retort, really. He was left to blink in the woman's absence, contemplating just what the Hell had happened. One thing was immediately clear: their meeting had not been an accident. He'd been bumped far too firmly for that. From that deduction, others followed quite logically: it wasn't a random woman who wanted to find out how low he could go, and it had absolutely nothing to do with limbo. It had to do with the LiveWire Championship wrapped snugly around Wyatt Connors' waist. And it had to do, of course, with the Dead Man's Hand. What didn't?
