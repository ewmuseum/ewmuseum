The Junkie: True Story

21 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/21/09 3-22-09 When I told Walter, my old grumpy bastard babysitter, that I have kept a journal for years the look on his face was one of surprise. What kind of things could I possibly write about, he probably wondered. How he probably thinks it goes is "ruined another life today, mwahahahahaha" or some shit. He probably can’t even imagine me doing mundane tasks like brushing my teeth, or eating breakfast. To him I am a cartoon character, a parody of a parody of a parody of a villain. Eh, it’s a fair assessment. But what I am trying to achieve with this journal, is complete honesty. Because typically, the only true honesty that one ever comes across is in their own head. But sometimes, when the moment has passed you by, memory and truth can become dated and stale…like milk. The truth on paper can endure. It’s also bizarre, scary even. Like this: last night I realized that in my whole life, there have probably only been like three people that I have ever met, whose passing would have a legitimate effect on me. Everyone else I would probably just act more devastated than I really was, just to get sympathy or affection from those previously mentioned three people. You can’t just say these things at a party. In fact you can’t really say the truth anywhere. * * * "It’s the truth," Walter Halvorson said into his cell phone, "I’ll be home by five." A pause. "Love you too." He closed the phone, and put his BMW in park. A lot of people had paid for this car, he figured. A lot of people had bled for it Walter exited his car, and shielded his eyes from the sun with a free hand. He stared up at the beige condo and silently shook his head. From the outside this place looked almost as nice as his. A friend of his employer used it as a winter home, an arrangement was made. He had made hundreds of arrangements over the years. Walter was dressed in a tucked in white button up shirt and dark pants. To the untrained eye he was just another yuppy in yuppy town. Henderson, Nevada certainly had no shortage of these. He walked up a set of stairs before reaching the front door. After a few courtesy knocks he inserted his key into the lock, and frowned upon realizing that the door had been left unlocked. Moments later, he was inside. Walter did not lend advice unless it was asked for, and if his employer were to ask him if all of this was a good idea, if this junkie was worth the risk, he would have said no in a very succinct and diplomatic way. Walter could not imagine this scenario coming up anytime soon. So today he ran his errands like a good boy. "Anybody home?" Walter called out, and examined a half empty bottle of gray goose vodka. If someone were to ask his opinion, he’d say that this won’t end well. * * * Walter came by the place again today. Took me grocery shopping. It made me feel like I was five years old again and my Mom was putting whatever I wanted in the cart. I’d whine until I got Count Chocula, and she’d make me promise not to tell my father. After that he took me to the gym. It was a little odd, running around again, being physically active. Fucking arm drags, what the fuck. I almost broke my arm right off messing around in the ring with some kid that Walter paid in cash. I’ve never felt so out of shape in my life. I kneed that little shit right in the head while attempting a leapfrog. Come to think of it, maybe the leapfrog should be my new finisher. It was quite a recruitment speech I got from that old turd a few weeks ago, but I could tell his heart wasn’t in it. I’ve been doing some thinking lately, about why it is that they want me around. I think part of it is simply that they can afford it. Having a wrestler with any notoriety at all taking orders, doing the laundry or whatever, it sends a general message to the federation. It says I fucking own people, and if you don’t watch your step you might be bought and sold tomorrow. It’s the kind of strategy I’d employ, if I was some big important person. But there’s something else to it. Something worse. Like she knows something is coming, something horrible. And maybe they’ll need someone a little more detached from the human race to deal with some of it. To take out the trash. Either way, I don’t much care. And hey, I guess that’s kind of the point, right? * * * After taking the junkie to the grocery store and the gym, Walter still had other errands to run. A manila folder sat in the front seat of his car, and it seemed to have a presence about it, a personality even. It seemed like just as much of a passenger as the junkie that had sat in its place minutes earlier. Its crazy but it seemed to Walter that this folder was staring at him, accusing him of a hundred things. He did not know its contents, but he had a few guesses. And it wasn’t that he wasn’t permitted to look, either. Enough dirt had accumulated on Walter over the years that his employer didn’t mind him being in the know about most anything. He just didn’t want to know. The BMW pulled up to a stoplight. A car stops because our foot hits the brake. Our foot hits the brake because our brain tells it to. Our brain tells it to because we obey stop lights. Stop lights obey a computer system. The computer system obeys programmers. And so on and so on. As high up as you go, you’d be hard pressed to find anything that actually has its own cause, its own inspiration. Walter and I both know this, in our heart of hearts. He’s ashamed of it, and I just think its fucking hysterical. The light turned green, and off he went. Walter was getting close now, probably about a half mile. He was starting to get the butterflies in his stomach. Because he did not look in the envelope, because he really didn’t exactly know what this was about, he considered that there might be a bit of danger to this. It certainly was not beyond his employer to be involved in something shady. But if there was danger, certainly someone else would have been chosen, right? I mean just look at me, he thought. His own pathetic physique was a sudden comfort to him. I like this journal shit. But I don’t think I’m going to go write the next great American novel or anything. Writers, they only got one real story, and they spend their whole career repackaging it over and over. Hell, all people are the same in that regard, but if you’re not a writer it just isn’t as noticeable. Well journal, here’s mine. Here’s my one true story. Walter took a right on Aberg Ave, and another quick right into a parking lot. In science class, 7th grade, we were discussing the rate that certain species grew. And I raised my hand, because a question suddenly popped into my head. I wanted to know how rocks grew if they weren’t alive. Everyone started laughing, naturally. A real chucklefest. The parking lot was empty. It had previously been used as customer parking for a small coffee shop called "Lazy Jane’s". The coffee shop had since gone out of business, and a for sale sign hung in the window. Walter inhaled deeply and exhaled loudly. What my father had done when I was little was show me a small rock, then he’d water it like it were a plant. A few days later he’d just show me a larger rock and presto, the illusion was formed. Why did he do this? I’m not entirely sure. Maybe he wanted to play god, maybe it was just innocent fun, I don’t know. I’ve thought about asking him, but I doubt I’d get an answer from him. He might not even remember. And besides, we’re not exactly on speaking terms these days. He sat in the parking lot for around five minutes before he had any company. A tope Pontiac Grand Prix entered the small parking lot with music blaring. It was loud enough that even though neither car’s windows were open he could make out the sound perfectly. It was "Happiness is a Warm Gun" by the Beatles, a song he had not heard in a decade. He rolled the passenger seat window down and kept his eyes dead ahead, preparing himself to act cool. Walter couldn’t imagine "acting cool" ever coming natural. He heard the song get louder, which to him meant that the other half of this meeting had opened their car door. After another deep breath Walter turned to the right and nodded at the man who was now approaching his vehicle. The man bent down at leaned his head into the car. He was younger than Walter, but not by more than ten years. He had long hair and darker skin. To Walter the man looked like a Hawaiian gangster. "Hi there," Walter said. "Good morning," the man grunted. "Almost four in the afternoon," Walter replied, and immediately felt stupid. "Yeah." A moment later, Walter handed him the folder. The man put an envelope on the front seat and without saying another word he walked away, got back into his car, and departed. Walter just stared ahead at the abandoned building, hearing the Beatles get farther and farther away. What had just happened bothered him, but he wasn’t sure why. Did it bother him because he had no idea what kind of domino effect he had just triggered, or did it bother him because he didn’t really care? An interesting question, really. This girl Becky I had study hall with, later on in the day she asked me why I could ever think such a stupid thing. I wish I could go back in time and laugh at her, tell her that one day she’ll probably hate homosexuals because an old book told her to. Or buy designer jeans because of some TV ad. But instead I was embarrassed. Instead I just answered her question as best as I could. Walter Halvorson pulled out of the parking lot, and was on his way home. He pulled out his cell phone, scrolled through a few contacts before finally reaching his employer. It rang a few times, before going to voicemail. "Hello Miss Pierce, Walter here. The exchange went fine. Oh, and about the problem child…he’s a little rusty, but I think he’ll be in ring shape in a couple weeks. If you have any questions give me a call. Thanks." My father, I said, suddenly feeling myself shrink. My father told me so. Walter saw a traffic light turn yellow, sighed, and stepped on the brake.
