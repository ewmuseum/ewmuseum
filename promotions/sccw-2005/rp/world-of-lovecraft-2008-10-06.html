World of Lovecraft

6 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Oct 6. Starbucks Coffee Boston, MA Immediately following Cataclysm 'I do wish that you would have told me Mr. Kannon was planning on betraying us. It seems that he is quite good at betraying me in particular.' Reginald Victor Lovecraft sits on one side of a small, cramped table in the popular coffee establishment, sipping delicately at a double espresso designed to keep him awake long enough to have this conversation and tend to other business before falling asleep. 'Hey, Reg, I had no idea. How's I supposed to know C.P. Cantrell would go off the deep end and mortgage his company in a futile effort to win that match?' William Shane Powell sits on the other end of that small, cramped table. He is working on a venti cafe mocha with whipped cream, shrugging apologetically. 'If it makes you feel any better, we could always go watch that tape where we put Eleanor in the Executioner's Chair. That always improves my mood when Xavier's all bitchy at me.' In spite of himself, the Elder Statesman of Sin City smiles. 'Thank you, William, but that won't be necessary. Kannon's selfishness aside, it was quite the spectacular night for us. The Dead Man's Hand was dominant, and your client got almost everything he could have wanted out of the loudmouth who decided to declare war upon us.' Powell scratched at day-old beard stubble as he took another drink of his coffee concoction. 'Yeah. I really am sorry about the Kannon thing, though. If I'd have known, I'd have told you.' 'That's bullshit,' Reginald said, 'and we both know it.' Caught red-handed, Powell could only sigh. 'Yeah,' he admitted, letting his own mind wander back to the days when he and Lovecraft had been dominant tag team champions, alongside Xavier Kannon as a part of Primetime Championship Wrestling's 'Front Office' stable, a stable broken up in large part by Kannon's greed and lust for even more power. 'The PCW business still bothers you after all this time?' Powell asked, knowing the answer. 'I got over it by the time we were in OSW. Besides, you hold a pinfall win over the guy.' Lovecraft raised what was left of the espresso to his lips, downing it in a small gulp. 'Of course it bothers me. I am competing again. And as a competitor, I cannot help but wonder how dominant we would have been had things continued on their natural course. You draw your paycheck from the man. Obviously, we see things slightly differently.' 'And quite a nice paycheck it is,' Powell grinned, 'though I doubt Desade's paying you in beer and pretzels.' 'Oh, she isn't,' Lovecraft acknowledged, 'but there are days I feel like I earn every penny. Usually, those are the days that my fifty three year old body wakes up after a match and wonders why its owner is so very foolish.' 'So, that'll be tomorrow then,' William smirked. 'Can't say I miss getting my ass kicked, that's for damned sure. My mouth always was my best attribute. Though...I do have to admit to being slightly jealous of you. The Dead Man's Hand has more hot women than PCW ever did. All I get to stare at nowadays is Eleanor, and not only is she taken, she's as annoying as ever. Never did think I'd meet a woman who was too dumb for me, but she qualifies in first place.' Lovecraft favored his ex-partner with a thin smile. 'I'm sure that Desade could always use more well-connected invididuals on her side. I could speak to her if you like...' 'I don't think that would go so well after Cataclysm,' Powell interjected, 'on either side. I appreciate the offer though. I'll just watch Cinemax with spare sock in hand like I do every night anyway.' 'I should introduce you to Eugene Ware, our commentator,' Reginald mused. 'You two would likely get along spectacularly.' With that, Reginald rose from his seat, looking down upon the man with whom he had traveled the roads for years, spending hour after hour with in rental cars and in airports around the world. 'Take care of yourself, William. For now, I have business to attend to.' William looked up at his once-partner, and nodded. 'You too, Reg. Don't work too hard, your heart can't take it.' Reginald sighed as Powell smirked. Some things, Lovecraft knew, never changed. And whether he'd ever admit it or not, he was glad that William S. Powell was one of them. ------------------------------- Immediately following Temptation 'Geez,' the brash young man that was Lovecraft's *current* partner said, looking down at the piece of paper that the Elder Statesman carried, 'that sucks for you. I've got an easy job this week. All I have to do is referee.' Phillip Kennedy favored his partner with a sympathetic look, reaching out to clap the older man on the back. The piece of paper, of course, was the lineup for next week's Temptation, which would feature Lovecraft teaming with Hiro Saotome against the equally unlikely duo of Lance Marshall and Hunter Sabuani. It would also feature Amy Campbell vs. Sean Sterling, with none other than the Big Stack himself making his long-awaited (mainly only by himself) refereeing debut. For his part, though, Reginald did not seem especially perturbed. 'I am a professional, Mr. Kennedy, above all else. I have no reason to attack Saotome. He was simply in the wrong place at the wrong time when he stepped into the ring with me. As long as he understands that, he will find me to be an exceptional tag team partner, just as Powell and yourself have. Any dysfunction that we may have will be greatly exceeded by Marshall and Sabuani, whose hatred dates back far longer than you would care for me to dictate.' Phillip nodded. As of late, it seemed that the Hand was holding him in higher esteem than it had in a while. 'So while you take care of that, I'm going to do a refereeing job that will make Ed Hochuli seem competant.' Reginald chuckled. 'It's a lot less fun to be blatantly biased from the start, I've found. More entertaining to be down the middle at first. Besides, that will allow you to pat down both competitors for weapons, a task I imagine you might enjoy when it comes to Mrs. Campbell.' 'Yeah, maybe,' the Big Stack admitted, 'though, if I listen to everyone on the internet, wouldn't I get a disease from that?' 'If you listened to everyone on the internet,' Lovecraft retorted, 'your liasons with Ms. Shaw would have resulted in you having around one-hundred separate venereal diseases by now.' 'Nope!' Kennedy beamed. 'I'm all clean. Just had my physical the other day and I'm as fit as a fiddle.' 'That is good to know,' Lovecraft replied. 'Especially if the Kent Fusiliers ever find time in their busy schedule to give us a defense of our Strength in Numbers titles.' Phil shrugged. 'Who cares if and when we defend them? The fact of the matter is we're the champs. Less we have to defend them the better.' Lovecraft shook his head, much like a parent chiding a son. 'That is where you are wrong. It is far more effective to defend the titles and win than simply not defend them. Defenses yield hope for those who stand against us. And every time we win, every time we crush another of their heroes, every time another of our plans come to fruition, they are robbed of that hope.' The smile on Lovecraft's face by this point had turned cruel. 'That, Phillip, is how the Dead Man's Hand operates. Think of our leader's deception of being Charlotte Ramone. No less than six or seven people were involved in an incredibly detailed ruse that resulted in the banishment of Jonathan Rhine from this company, the elevation of the Dead Man's Hand in the eyes of the wrestling world, and Desade holding the Big Slick bracelet.' Even Lovecraft, who had seen much wrangling on the political plane in his lifetime, and seen many, many elaborate schemes both succeed and fail, was very impressed with the Ramone ruse. Phillip Kennedy, though, seemed less so, sadness seeming to spread over his face. 'Phil,' Lovecraft asked. 'What's wrong?' 'I just realized something,' the Big Stack admitted, feeling somewhat silly that it had taken this long. 'You know the Heaven & Hell match I was in?' Reginald nodded, wondering for a brief moment where the Hell this was going, before making the obvious conclusion along with his charge. 'Charlotte Ramone eliminated me from that match...' 'She did, yes,' Lovecraft said, forcing himself to smile. 'Now that you know what she is capable of, be glad that shall be the only time you find yourself across the ring from our fearless leader.' 'I had no friggin' clue,' Phillip groused. 'My ribs hurt for days after that flippy-floppy thing she did.' 'If it makes you feel any better,' Reginald offered, 'I was not informed of the ruse either.' Kennedy did a double-take, looking at his partner with genuine shock. 'You didn't know, Reg,' the faux-cowboy asked. 'I thought they told you everything...' Lovecraft shook his head. 'No, like yourself, I am on a need-to-know basis with everything that happens within the Hand. True, I need to know more than you do at this time, but I do not know as much as you believe I do.' Reginald still knew a lot, of course. Knew things that would probably at the very least make Phillip Kennedy question his path in life. It went without saying that those things would never be revealed, but Lovecraft was still Kennedy's greatest source of information within the Dead Man's Hand. 'What about Savant,' Phillip asked, and Reginald nodded. 'She knows far more than I do,' Lovecraft stated. 'Probably, she knows as much as anyone not named Desade. It is good that you are speaking with her more often now. You should continue to do so. She is far more qualified than I to help you unlock your full potential.' Kennedy looks questioningly at his partner. 'Full potential?' Reginald Lovecraft nods. This question he has a ready answer for. 'Right now, you are acting like Sean Sterling, no offense meant. You popped out of a coffin and attacked Sterling. Such direct approaches are effective, no doubt; Sterling has made a successful career out of similar such basic atrocities. You are capable of handling yourself as a member of the Hand physically. Lauren Fox will train you to be a member of the Hand mentally. Desade is special, her ability to plot and plan is without equal. But someday, if you listen to Lauren, you will be able to carry out similar plots with aplomb. And then, partner, you will be a *truly* dangerous man.' It is Reginald's turn to clap his partner on the back. 'Listen to her. For now, I must go. I have business to attend to.' Phillip watched Reginald shuffle off, likely in the direction of the exit to head for the rental car Lovecraft drove. No matter what city they were in, the car was always red, Reginald's favorite color. Left to his own devices, Kennedy leant against the backstage wall, reaching up to massage his forehead. 'Never realized being a shithead would be this Goddamned complicated.' A thought suddenly comes to Stack's head, and he smiles, screaming after his partner. 'Hey, Reg! Make sure to kick Hunter in the nuts for me!' -------------------------------------- The advent of the laptop and the Blackberry meant that if one so chose, they could remain plugged into the world at all times. In spite of his advanced age, Reginald V. Lovecraft prided himself on being technologically up to date. When he went on his walks, he did so with the musical accompaniment provided by his iPhone. He carried various gadgets and gizmos designed to make communication easier. And though communication indeed was easier in 2008, the news that he recieved through such devices was as bad as it had ever been. 'Thank God for Desade,' he murmured to himself, in the privacy of his hotel room. 'Being a Republican in 2008 is no way to make a living.' Lovecraft had weathered the storm of the midterm elections in 2006 relatively unharmed. Whether Democrats or Republicans were in power, there was always corruption to be found, always officials who would be on the take. But now, Wall Street was going down, and it was going down probably because of bad decisions made by people who should have known better. Lovecraft had no evidence, but he imagined that there was money changing hands to help grease the wheels. And now, the economy was, by most measures, in the toilet, or well on its way there. Whether accurately or not, it seemed that the ruling party was being blamed for the economic troubles. 'Ugh,' Lovecraft moaned, continuing to read doomsaying editorial after doomsaying editorial on his laptop. 'We are *so* boned. So, SO boned.' Barack Obama's cult of personality didn't help. Nor did the fact that, in a desperate bid to wrest publicity from the Illinois senator, John McCain and the Republican party tapped Sarah Palin as the vice-presidential candidate for the ticket. 'McCain's in his 70s, the census says he could die any day now, and we pick an inexperienced Alaskan governor to be our vice presidential candidate. In-frickin'-credible. Oh, for the days of Ronald Reagan. Hell, for the days of Bush 41 at this point...' It wasn't necessarily Palin herself. She certainly had energized a majority of the party, and given a couple more years, would have the experience to be a truly dangerous candidate. But to Lovecraft, picking her severely blunted the Republican argument against Obama's lack of experience. Moreover, Lovecraft saw her as a female George Dubya: equally folksy, equally anti-intellectual...and equally capable of shoving her foot in her mouth in front of the world. 'Gonna be a brave new world out there come January, I can feel it,' Reginald groaned. An Obama administration would probably be the most difficult environment for Lovecraft to conduct business in that he had ever experienced. And yet, he knew, the corrupt would always be there. It would just be more difficult to find them, and if caught, Lovecraft knew that he would likely spend a good chunk of the rest of his natural life behind bars. Such were the dangers of the new world. A vibration at his pocket led to Lovecraft reaching down into his suit pants to come up with his Blackberry. Raising it to his ear, he spoke in his usual controlled, strong tone. 'Reginald Victor Lovecraft speaking.' A feminine voice spoke into his ear from the electronic device. 'It's Katherine,' the voice said, seemingly distressed. 'Are you still in town?' 'I am, yes,' Reginald said, voice softening. 'Do you need my company?' 'Yes,' came the voice on the other end of the phone, and Reginald nodded. 'How soon can you be here?' 'I'll be there in five minutes,' the female voice promised. 'Marriott hotel,' Reginald said, quickly. 'Room 1209.' With that, the call disconnected, and Reginald looked at himself in the mirror behind the desk he sat at. Even in his younger days, he had never considered himself truly handsome, much less now, with his hair long past graying and now starting to recede. That didn't matter now, though, much as it bothered him personally. The woman on her way to his hotel room didn't care about such things. Nor had she ever, for that matter. They were co-workers. Technically, at one point, she had interned for him when he worked for the office of the governor of Georgia. She had since gone on to a successful life as a bureaucrat, constantly resisting Reginald's offers to use his power and positions to get her a better job and place in the state of things. The only thing that she would accept from him was companionship. Katherine Willis was a thirty-seven year old bureaucrat, deemed past the marriageable age by most who knew her, deemed too plain looking by the rest. Reginald Victor Lovecraft was a fifty-three year old professional wrestler, former politician, and lobbyist who was well past the marriageable age for a man who wasn't sitting on tens of millions of dollars. They had much in common. Both were intelligent and crafty, though Katherine applied her traits for the good of the world, Reginald for personal gain and arguably, evil. Moreover, they both had in common the simple fact that they were both desperately, depressingly lonely. Lovecraft's eyes went to his watch, watching the seconds tick away slowly, far too slowly. Reginald loved his work, far more than most would imagine he should considering the morally questionable material he dealt with. He was fine with it for the most part, until he put his head on his pillow at night and was left to confront the fact that he was almost morally bankrupt and alone in the world. Having to babysit Phillip Kennedy wasn't especially helping the alone bit, either. Especially when he'd have to leave the hotel suite because Kennedy and his Sex Kitten were fucking. At least, Lovecraft mused, he'd learned his lesson, and had convinced SCCW to book them separate quarters at the hotels. Lovecraft wasn't jealous of Kennedy. Not really, anyway. Katsidy was slightly too trashy for his tastes, Savant too young and punky for a fifty-two year old man. Though, he realized, with a grin on his face, given the chance in his younger days, he'd have been as enthusiastic as the Big Stack was. 'Wonder if he'll ever figure it out,' Lovecraft mused, darkly. Reginald certainly hoped not. Dealing with a jilted and hurt Phillip was not a task he wanted on his plate. He much preferred the idea of teaming with a Kennedy, properly given the Kool-Aid, and made a powerful and loyal ally. He was jarred from his thoughts by a knock at the door. 'Coming,' he called, trying to keep the utter joy out of his voice as he rose from his chair, making a quick stop in the bathroom. He filled one of the glass cups with water, taking a pair of small blue pills from a vial. With a moment of mourning for his former self, Lovecraft downed his security measure, and made his way over to the door. He threw the door open, and before he could quite react, there was a woman a foot shorter than his six feet seven inches clinging to him. 'K-Katherine,' Reginald stammered, trying to pry the woman away from him just enough to look at her. 'Is everything alright?' 'I had a bad day at the office,' the younger woman said, willing herself not to cry. 'They...let me go. Said they wanted to hire someone younger.' Lovecraft growled. 'I'll take them to court on age discrimination,' the Elder Statesman stated, face severe. 'They probably just want to hire someone they think they can sleep with. Assholes.' She shivered against him, looking him dead in the eyes, lip quivering as she debated asking the question she had promised herself a thousand times she would never ask. 'Reggie,' she began, hesitantly. 'You know how you...always offered to get me a job if I wanted one?' Reginald Victor Lovecraft nodded, a grin tugging at the corner of his mouth in spite of himself. 'Shall I put my connections to work, Miss Willis?' She nodded once, unable to bring herself to do anymore. 'Don't be ashamed,' he whispered, running a hand through her long, black hair. 'In the world we live in today, sometimes, connections are all we have.' Unable to quite bring herself to look at him, her slender hand reached out for the hallway's lightswitch, obscuring the pair in darkness.
