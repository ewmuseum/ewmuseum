First Thing's First.

16 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12.16.2007 He hadn't felt this good in a long time, and in the same instance, he hadn't felt this miserable. The adrenaline from his victory had kept the pain to a minimum, but now that he had been home for a couple of days, he was beginning to feel the full effects of the cheap shot taken by Paul Cain. His spine throbbed, and the blood pushed its way towards the skin and formed patchy bruises up and down his back. He couldn't even say it was worth it. Don't be confused. The victory was worth every ounce of pain his body had to endure, that wasn't the question. The question came in the form of why someone like Paul Cain, of all people, would interrupt at the end of the match and drive a thick steel rod in to his vertebra. That much he didn't understand. The possibility hadn't crossed his mind that he had made such an impression so quickly that people would take to post-match beatdowns to get themselves over, but he assumed it came with the territory. He would be sure to get to the bottom of it, though. In time. First thing's first. .Who the fuck is this guy?. "Language, Elliott." Dr. Margaret Thapes sighs. He had been inside of this office many times over the two-plus years he'd been a client, and even though there was no sign of them on the door, he knew the rules when he entered it. This did not stop him from taking liberties from time to time, although he did his best to respect them. "This --" "I can speak for myself, doctor." The man Elliott was referring to with his question clears his throat and steps further in to the room. Dressed in a three-piece suit, he is the symbol of professionalism, which is ironic for anyone that knows his past. He adjusts his tie, fashion apparel he was not accustomed to, tugging at it with a single finger and loosening its hold around his neck. "The name's William McDormant, and I'm better than you." He smiles, his gums lined with teeth that could be made of pearls. Rollins raises an eyebrow at the smug sonofabitch that has just entered the office of Margaret Thapes. "That still doesn't answer my question," Rollins says, gritting his teeth. "Well then allow me to elaborate," McDormant says. "I'm the man who is going to take you to the next level, kid. You're young and you're tough as Hell, but that'll only get you so far. Your grandma here hired me to make sure that you utilize it." Thapes furrows her brows at that last sentence. "I think I'll do just fine," Elliott insists. McDormant nods. Sandpaper couldn't remove that smug look from his face. "Fine, yes. You did just fine last week, but unless you're satisfied with merely being sufficient, then you need help. That is why I'm here." "And what says you're qualified?" Rollins inquires. "History," McDormant says. "Oh yeah? And what have you done?" Rollins continues, rising now. As he does, he clutches at his back, wincing in pain. "Slow down, pops," McDormant says, backing up just slightly. Rollins rises to full-size, which is about four inches taller and 20 pounds heavier than the wolf in sheep's clothing standing in front of him. McDormant holds a hand up, easing Rollins out of his fury. "Answer the question," Rollins hisses. "I've fucked super models," McDormant says. "That's what I've done. What have you done?" Rollins grunts. "Exactly," McDormant continues. "You haven't done shit except win some clusterfuck battle royal and get a DQ victory over that genetic mutant that spawned from Tony Pride's testicles." McDormant instantly shudders at the thought of the aforementioned testicles. Thapes shudders at the language being used inside the confines of her office. She reaches in to her desk as she stands. She walks over to the two men, her back arched in perfect posture. "Here," she says, handing McDormant a ruler. "I'll be outside when you two are done." McDormant just gives her a 'what's this for?' look as she exits the room, but as immediately as the door shuts, his attention is back to Rollins. "If I wanted you here I'd have hired you myself," Rollins says. "No you wouldn't have," McDormant responds. "Despite what you may think, you're an asshole. And you're very, very stubborn. You're not the type to ask for anyone's help, whether it be me or Rhine or anyone else." He smirks. "In that way," he continues, "you and I are very much alike." Rollins just snarls as he sits back down. "You don't know anything about me." McDormant follows suit, sitting down behind Thapes' desk and tossing his feet up on the oakwood table in front of him. He has no need for an ottoman. "You're right," McDormant says with a nod. "I don't know anything about you. You're an enigma." McDormant's voice drips sarcasm. "To quote Eugene Ware, of all people, you're a troglodyte. I'd be willing to bet that woman standing outside of that door doesn't even know anything about you." He puts his feet back down, leaning over the desk. "But I'm not a man of knowledge, Rollins. I'm a man of instincts, and instincts tell me that you've got what it takes to be great. You could be at the top of that crumpling mountain called FUSE before you even knew it if you'd just accept the gift you've been handed." "And that would be?" Elliott inquires. McDormant leans back now, folding his hands behind his head and smirking once again. "Me." .rewind. He had walked in to Defiance with no enemies. He walked out of Defiance with three. Paul Cain, Duncan Drake, and the LiveWire champion, whoever it may be when his turn came to take a shot at the title. He would deal with them in order, or at least he planned to; but as a friend of his had recently said, plans are subject to change. "A lot of people have tried to do some horrible things to Elliott Rollins tonight, Eugene..." Paul Cain was the first on the list, because his impact was felt most immediately. Elliott tended to be an even-tempered man, but when someone goes out of their way to cheap shot him, he's not one to take it smiling, and that much was proven at the pay-per-view. He would deal with Cain as swiftly as the metal made contact with his back; as swiftly and as bluntly. "...but not a damn one of them have succeeded in stopping him." Duncan Drake was merely a nuisance. Throughout the course of the match the animosity between the two was inescapable. It was around the time that Drake wrapped a plastic bag around Elliott's head that he knew, despite Duncan's skittish and arrogant demeanor, that this was a man capable of dangerous acts. Or maybe it was the woman pulling the strings that made him capable of such acts. While his ability was questionable at best, Elliott knew in his heart that this wasn't the last time their paths had crossed and he may, in the long run, pose more of a threat than the pipe-wielding man that preceded him. ".mih gnippots ni dedeeccus evah meht fo eno nmad a ton --" "-- not a damn one of them have succeeded in stopping him." And that brings us to the LiveWire champ. Elliott had earned his shot, and he would sit patiently until his number was called. When that time comes, he will do everything in his power to bring gold around his waist for the first time in his fairly lackluster career. There was an order of things, however, and Elliott would be sure to follow it. ".mih gnippots ni --" "-- in stopping him." First thing's first. "Yet. They haven't succeeded yet." .pulse. Elliott Rollins checks his. His accelerated heart rate may be the result of the man sitting across from him, or the three cups of coffee he's ingested since the two have sat down at the coffee shop they now find themselves in. It's a typical chain coffee shop, with overpriced coffee and underpaid employees. Rollins and his recently-acquainted 'manager' William McDormant sit at a table near the window, bright Orlando sunlight blasting their faces. "So how'd you meet the old lady?" McDormant asks, feigning interest. "The yellow pages," Rollins says. He takes another long sip from his cup. "The question is, how did you meet her?" McDormant merely shrugs. "Well it's not like she got you to fly across the country on a whim," Rollins continues. There has to be a little more to the story." The original 'Hollywood Hellion', William M. McDormant, turns his eyes to Rollins. "I owed her a favor. Let's just leave it at that." Rollins nods, as McDormant shifts his attention back to the passing traffic just outside the window. "You know," McDormant continues, "you should be thankful. She could have asked me for anything, but instead she asked me to help you. She must like you." Rollins wasn't so sure, considering the arrogant bastard she set him up with. "What makes you think I need help?" Rollins inquires. "What makes you think you don't?" It was a hypothetical question that McDormant didn't need an answer to. He already knew. "With that win you just put a target on your back, and if you don't believe me you've surely got some bruises you can use as evidence." Rollins grimaces slightly, not at McDormant, but at the lasting memory of Defiance. "Now if you don't want my services," McDormant continues, polishing off the last few drops of his coffee, "then I can walk out of that door and you'll never see me again. I've already been paid, whether you take advantage of it or not is up to you." Rollins is hesitant, but asks anyway. "What did you have in mind?" "I don't want to make any commitments without yours," McDormant said, his brash bravado replaced by calm intensity. He is, as stated before, a man of business, and he takes his business very seriously. "I want you to commit, right now, to doing whatever I tell you. I don't want you to ask questions. I've been successful for many years, and if you buy in to what I can teach you, I promise you'll be facing Jonathan Rhine in the new year." Rollins senses the seriousness with which the man across from him speaks, and for some reason, it is far more convincing than any he has seen in FUSE thus far. He nods. If he and the Devil were to meet at dusk on an isolated dirt road and make a compromise for his soul, this is the same sort of feeling it would have. "When do we start?" A lump forms in the back Rollins' throat as he finishes his question. "At the Lion's Cup," McDormant says, that same smirk now returning. He had inked the deal. .broken record. "How was Pittsburgh?" You can't discredit Margaret Thapes for her effort. She tried, really tried, to get to the core of Elliott Rollins. She had dedicated over two years of her life, and in that time, and even before had never met such an interesting specimen worthy of brain-picking. Unfortunately for her, every tool she had to utilize was rendered useless by Rollins' complete inability to cooperate. She had once convinced herself that she had made one too many enemies in the past, and Rollins was her penance for her lack of discretion in lives past. Rollins was convinced of exactly the opposite. "How do you think it was?" Before Elliott met Dr. Thapes, he had only hoped that he had made enough enemies throughout his short stint on Earth. He made a point to drive to the market every day, only hoping that someone had planted a bomb underneath the vehicle. He turned the key, and with it, nothing. He was invisible. He couldn't make friends, and he couldn't make enemies. Thankfully, there was always tomorrow. "The reason I asked," Thapes says, her monotone voice never faltering, "is that I would be able to avoid making assumptions." Rollins sighes. He can't wait to get back to the parking lot. "It appears we are following a trend," she continues. "I ask you something, and instead of answering it, you only follow it with more questions. I'm afraid we'll never get to the bottom of why you're here if you won't cooperate." She was disheartened. She had thought throughout the past few weeks they had made headway, but every time she made that assumption, he would take four steps backwards. "I buried my father," Elliott says blankly. "How do you think it made me feel?" "I'm becoming more and more convinced that you feel absolutely nothing, Elliott." Rollins tries to swallow, but the knot in his throat has only grown larger. There is silence. Long, empty silence. "I killed my mother." Rollins' eyes leave the floor and search in the dim light of the room to meet Thapes'. When they do, her eyes are like a textbook, reading every emotion that can pass through the human mind. She isn't sure what caught her off-guard most; the words, or the fact that Elliott actually said them. This would certainly make their next few sessions very, very interesting. But for now, first thing's first.
