The beginnings of change?

23 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 23, 2007 I’ve always wanted to feel like my life had a purpose. Like I had a purpose. For being. For breathing. For living. Once before, I thought that I had. Only after it was too late did I realize that I had been fooling not only myself, but those around me who I card about. Who I loved. Who loved me? You can love with all your heart. So much so, that it hurts. But how do you know if that love is ever truly reciprocated. How do you know the other’s inner thoughts, feelings, or possibly demons? You don’t. Love’s a scary thing. A person must be secure in themselves in order to put so much on the line. Yet, it’s necessary. When loved ones are taken from you, you never truly stop loving them. Although, an empty feeling remains in the heart. A feeling that you aren’t sure you’ll ever be able to replace. In a way, you long to replace it. To feel that love once again. But, you’re torn. If you ever feel that way again, will it be betrayal? A sign of moving on? Or perhaps, weakness. Is it possible to love something, or someone, so much that it not only hurts yourself, but those around you? Is it possible to regain lost feelings, or to be given a second chance towards not only love, but life itself? If the second chance is taken, will the cycle be broken, or are you doomed to simply repeat life’s mistakes? This I wonder. * * * * I tried to quietly open the door, but flinched as the loud squeal gave me away. She was sitting in the kitchen, focused on the book in front of her as I walked in and closed the door behind me. As she spun around her brown hair glinted in the light. I couldn’t help but smile, but nearly choked trying to force words from my constricting throat. "Thank you." Carefully marking her place by folding over a corner of the page, she set down the book and walked over to me. I could feel my heart beating, trying to burst through my chest. My palms were growing sweaty. My head tries to turn, searching for the door and some sort of escape but my feet are rooted to the ground. Time seems to move in slow motion. Her blue eyes twinkle. Hips sway. Arms open wide. And there she is. Hugging me. God, I feel as giddy as a schoolboy sneaking his first kiss in between classes. But, I like it. But she’ll only get hurt. You’ll only hurt her. The sweet smell of her citrus perfume floats up, filling not only my nose, but my thoughts as well. My eyes close as I wrap an arm around her, holding her close. Never wanting to let go. I can feel something overwhelming growing inside of me. How much more pain are you willing to cause? How much more suffering can you take? Opening my eyes with a sigh, I push her away. As she looks up at me, her blue eyes are full of hurt and confusion. Hell, at least it isn’t of betrayal. Not yet anyway. "What’s wrong, Mayson?" Her voice sings in my ears. I want nothing more than to look in to her face and bask in the feeling of love. Of being needed. Wanted. Instead, I divert my eyes towards the ground and force myself to sound gruff. "Nothing." As her soft hands reach up and grab a hold of my face, pulling it down to hers, I don’t fight it. A part of me wants to roughly grab her and toss her out, screaming obscenities, ensuring that she would never come back. That I would never be the cause of her hurt. Her suffering. The other part of me melts as her lips push themselves onto mine. Scooping her in to my arms I want nothing more than to make her every dream come true. To protect her from all the evils in this world. You’d have to protect her from yourself. * * * * I had woken up in a panic. Frantically, I searched the apartment. Everything was in its place, and nothing seemed out of the ordinary. Except, my life hadn’t been ordinary. Andrew and Melissa were gone. I desperately made my rounds, checking doors and windows, only to find everything not only closed, but locked. No one had gotten in, so they must have gotten out. I could feel my temper flare up inside me as I stormed towards the door. It bounced off the wall loudly as I flung it open. I nearly plowed down the bodies standing on the other side before recognizing Andrew and Melissa. Both children were beaming, smiles wide across their faces and each held a small grocery bag. Behind them stood a young woman, smiling slightly but looking confused. I grabbed the children by the shoulder and herded them in to the apartment behind me before heading out in to the hall myself and slamming the door behind me. I could hear the children’s calls of protest, but I pushed them to the back of my mind. Puffing out my chest and crossing my arms, I could only think of one thing. Who the hell does this woman think she is? She looked me up and down, and placing her hands on her hips, seemed unimpressed. "What kind of father lets their children wander around San Francisco at that age?" Her attitude had taken me aback. Now, more than ever I wanted to know who this woman was, and where she had come from. Not that I would give her the satisfaction of knowing any of that. "I’m not their father. I’m doing my best to look after them and give them a decent life. If you’d prefer, I could send them back to live in the drug hell I pulled them out of. No? Didn’t think so." As her eyes drifted to my chest, I suddenly become very aware of the scars crisscrossing my body. Wishing I had thought to grab a shirt to go along with my sweatpants, I shifted my weight from foot to foot. "It seems like you’ve had a bit of a rough life yourself." I started to tell her that my life was none of her business. That she didn’t know the first thing about what I’ve been through. That she never would. I’d like to think that something, somewhere told me not to. To be completely honest, the only thing that stopped me was the door opening behind me and a small hand tapping me on the back. "Uncle Mayson, breakfast is ready! Miss Cassie, come on! It’s ready!" I turned around to protest, but my opening had closed. Andrew and Melissa were back inside, and expecting their guest for breakfast. The woman behind me cleared her throat, and I turned around to find her hand extended towards me, a small smile creeping across her face. "My name’s Cassandra and I believe I have a breakfast invitation." My eyes darted between her and the outstretched hand. With my fists balled, I tried to move, but my arms wouldn’t cooperate. She waited for a moment, and I watched as the smile slowly left her face. "Perhaps another time then." As she turned around to leave, I found myself reaching for her. I was suddenly desperate to not let her leave. As my hand closed around her arm she jumped. That was the first time she looked deep in to my eyes, and I felt something I hadn’t felt in a long time. "No, please stay." And she did. * * * * Her name was Janet. She was my life, my love, my high school sweetheart, and I thought, my everything. Only a small percentage of high school romances last. Everyone believes that theirs will be the one that makes it. The one that beats all odds. They’ve found true love. Have they really? Or are they yet to discover what true love really is? You know one of those sickly sweet, loving couples that make you want to puke? Yeah, that was us. But there was a problem. Janet’s father was sort of a business tycoon. He was the type of guy who had a different car for each day of the week, and had his choice of more than a few vacation homes. In fact, we would have never met if she hadn’t begged and pleaded to attend a public high school. He had high hopes for his baby girl, and wasn’t quite sure I was the one to deliver. I’d taken a part time job working as a dishwasher in an overcrowded restaurant, scraping things the resembled baby food off of plates for a next to nothing wage. But finally, I’d gotten together the money. One day, just before graduation, I made the trip to a pawn shop up the road, and bought the ring I’d been eyeing for months. That night, at a picnic under the stars, I asked her to marry me. Cheesy, I know, but she said yes. I felt as though I was on top of the world. That is, until the next morning. In the morning I received a phone call from her father. He must have called me every name imaginable. Every name, except son. I was threatened with the police, offered money to just go away, and finally was demanded to return his little girl, "or else". Of course, I was upset, but after a few days I got over it, thinking everything would blow over in time. One night, I got a frantic phone call from Janet asking me to meet her at the park. At our spot. I assured her I would be there shortly, and what I saw when I arrived changed my life forever. Standing the edge of the bridge was Janet. Her hair whipped in the wind, and a tear fell down her cheek. She gave me a meek wave, and blew me a kiss. Then, she jumped. A beastly growl escaped my throat, followed by screams of onlookers as I dived for her. Her body bounced in the dirt before mine did. My tears streamed on to her body as they pulled me away from her. Her eyes are still pierced in my mind. The look of love. The look of hope. The look of betrayal. * * * * I tell myself that this time things will be different. This time, I’m in control. We’re in control. I’m no longer a child. Yet I can’t help but to feel like one. I’m giddy. I’m hopeful. I’m happy. I’m loved. And I’m scared to death.
