We're all special

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: when it happened August Monday was considered a rough and tumble sort of guy. The kind of person who walked through something, instead of around it. Augie had the potential of being a monster but a stint in the pen in the duration of his youth straightened his arrow, so to speak. But he was a monster nonetheless with the capability to literally bring any man to tears. Or worse. Sin City Championship Wrestling considered him to be one of its toughest competitors, only to be rivalled by "the Lion" Lance Marshall. The pair had clashed once before and it was almost as if the gods themselves were belting one another. Thunder claps arose as fists struck flesh. Lightning struck as the two goliath men locked horns. A duel in the future was on the cards and was guaranteed to be a show stopper as they collided, along with their female counterparts, April Monday and the venomous Desade. These days, Monday spent his time adoring his daughter and grand son outside of a wrestling ring. Right now, he rough and tumbled with the twenty-month old. Adam veered around a corner, alluding the roaring August as he stumbled from foot to foot, pretending to be a grizzly bear. The kid’s squeal was saturated in joy and laughter as he rounded the corner, grabbing it with two chubby mitts and peering around with sheer adoration for his grandfather. "EEEEEEEEE!" he squealed as ‘Pop’ drew nearer. ‘My Sin rep’d be ruined if they saw me carryin’ on like this,’ thought the Raging Fear, ambling around the corner, glancing around for the twenty-month old. He spotted two little feet beneath a curtain and stifled his laughter as best he could, trying to hide it amidst another amiable, bear-like growl. Fleeing from the scene, Adam tried to escape the clutches of his Pop but one massive, tattooed forearm reached out before him, scooping him up easily and held horizontally to the monstrous Augie’s chest. With the index finger on his right hand, August easily brushed his t-shirt aside and tortured the child by dragging his grizzly beard back-and-forth along the bare skin of the toddler. He squealed with laughter so high-pitched every dog in the neighbourhood’s head exploded in unison. August couldn’t help but chuckle. The kid’s laugh was infectious and Augie’d caught the disease. The pair of them laughed hard. Twisting within his Pop’s grasp, Adam contorted his body to wrap a tiny arm around his Pop’s neck, pulling in close to his body and resting his head on his Pop’s shoulder. His tiny wing-span tried to surpass the girth of August as he hugged but was only reachable to August’s shoulder width. The tiny hands patted Augie reassuringly. For who it was reassuring for was another question entirely but to say August’s heart wasn’t melted by the sign of affection would be an outright lie. His grizzled face embraced the love his grandson was giving him and August closed his eyes, enjoying the moment. Holding Adam out, with a hand in each of his armpits, August looked him in the eye. "Don’t grow up, kid. Bein’ a grown up sucks ass. I mean… butt. I mean… aw shit. I did it again." "Butt. Butt." The toddler repeated after his Pop. The eyes of the Raging Fear bulged as he pressed Adam’s back to his chest, locked him against it with one massive forearm as his free right-hand covered Adam’s mouth. Panic stricken, August glanced about for the entrance of Adam’s mother, hoping she wouldn’t be privy to the profanity he’d been teaching to her son inadvertently. "We don’t say that, Adam," Augie told the toddler under his breath, trying not to draw attention to the situation. Adam squirmed in August’s grip, wriggling and writhing and making it nigh on impossible to restrain the toddler. He put him down and the toddler set off, making tracks at the speed of light as a silhouette passed the laced curtains that were designed to give a little privacy to the lounge. That silhouette headed right for the front door and the familiar ding and dong of the doorbell brought the Portland Powerhouse to greet his unknown guest. Pressing an eye to the peephole, August looked his visitor up and down. New York City oozed from the man’s suit as he glanced around the neighbourhood, running a hand through his short ginger hair before ruffling his fringe. Always on the toes of dressing to impress. Snapping the deadlock free, August pulled the door back unveiling himself. "Who are you?" "You always greet your guests that way?" Augie shrugged. "I don’t know you so you ain’t a guest ‘til I says so. Right now… you’re a stranger trespassin’ on my property ‘til I know otherwise." His right hand pushed into his jacket producing a little leather wallet. He flipped it open, holding it out for August to inspect. "Fed, huh?" The man nodded. August read the words beside the man’s picture, which must’ve been taken before the man had been moved into whatever division he was in now that had aged him beyond his years. The man was still handsome nonetheless. The positive between the picture and the man that stood before August was the man in the flesh appeared more confident than his doppelganger in the photo. "Finn Roberts? Why do I know that name?" Finn grinned. "I spoke to your daughter a little while back." August Aaaaah’ed the Aaaaah of a man beginning to understand. "I gotcha now. The one who gone and ruffled her feathers ‘bout Brock." "You know Brock?" "Dickhead… you wouldn’t be here if you didn’t know the answer to that question." Roberts chuckled but the chuckle was the resonance of a man’s patience wearing thin. "May I come in, Mr. Monday?" "Mr. Monday is my father." "Can I call you August?" Monday stared him up and down, running his hand over the length of his face and down his grizzly beard, before giving it a satisfied tug. "Guess so. Come on in." August stepped aside, letting the Federal agent into his abode. Immediately, the cogs in Finn Roberts’ head began to grind as he assessed the situation. You learnt to do that as an agent. It became second nature. First, you assessed all valid exit points. Windows were no exception to the rule. He’d had to lunge through a plate glass window once before thanks to the misguided lifestyle of a young Pitbull pup and his crackhead master. Second, you sized up those in your presence. Reassuringly, Finn pressed his hand against the semi-automatic handgun in its holster against his chest subconsciously. August noticed. "You plan on shootin’ some one, city slicker?" "No sir. Just habit." August grunted, leading the way into the lounge room. Roberts followed, eyes darting to-and-fro as he soaked in the scenes. Halting in mid-step, August spun to face the agent. "You take a seat and I’ll fix us a drink." "I’ll take coffee." Augie shook his head. "You’ll take Jack." "I’m on duty." "You’ll take Jack or you’ll take leave, sunshine. What’ll it be? Two fingers or fuck off?" Finn raised a hand, two fingers extended and a grin shining behind it that was unavoidable. As gruff as August Monday was, he grew on you by the second unless you posed a threat to him. Roberts had decided as soon as the behemoth had opened the door that he would best attempt not to pose as such to the man. Cupboards doors banged and clattered as August collected two glasses and a bottle from his stockpile of Jack Daniels. Swung open the freezer and pulled out a tray of ice cubes, tossing a couple into each tumbler with a jingle on arrival. Carrying them into the room, August never took his eyes off the Federal agent, sitting in his armchair. Of all the places for him to sit. He handed one to Finn and remained standing a moment, trying to figure out where it was he would take a seat since his was already taken. He sighed to himself and sat on the couch, staring Finn dead in the eye. "This ‘bout that phone call?" Roberts nodded. "Why didn’t you come forward?" "Why should I?" "This is a homicide investigation." August pfffft’ed at him. "Do I look like a cop to you?" "You know what I mean." "I sort shit out my way, Roberts." Monday seemed to grind against the patience of Finn with his anti-authoritarian perspective on the situation. "Special Agent Roberts. I’ve worked hard. I think I deserve the title." "I don’t see nothin’ special ‘bout you. Yer just a fancy copper far as I’m concerned. Ain’t no different to the fucker that tickets my truck. You just chase other tails, right?" Roberts gritted his teeth. "Do you understand what it means when the Federal Bureau of Investigation gets involved in a murder investigation, Mr. Monday?" "Means the feller is special. Jus’ like you." "I don’t like your attitude. Maybe I should hold you in contempt." Again with the pffffting. "Maybe. Maybe if I said you had a beautiful body you would hold it against me. Faggot. Come in my house and lay down the rules. I ain’t doin’ nothin’ to fuck up your investigation, Roberts. Yer just a big time city slickin’ copper with a chip on his shoulder coz he can’t get the job done. Can’t put the puzzle together? Thought you would be able… seein’ yer special and all." With a thunk of his tumbler, Roberts rose to his feet with anger coursing through his veins at one hundred mile an hour. Monday met him on a vertical base and rage level. Two stubborn males ready to fight for their territory. A little piece of August respected the man for having the gusto to speak to him like he did. The other part wanted to smack some understanding into that thick skull of his. "You’re withholding information, Monday. That’s a federal offense." "I ain’t withholdin’ nothin’, sunshine. I told you I got the call when you asked me." "You should have come forward." "Why?" Monday’s hands balled into fists. "So you can look ol’ Brock away like an animal? Another fucken merit badge on yer cub scout uniform, SPECIAL AGENT? I know how it all fucken works, Roberts. You get scared by a man ya don’t understand and yer boys put more bullets in him than yer allowed to legally carry." Huffing and puffing, like two buffalo having tried to show their dominance over the other, Roberts and Monday were wired and ready to punch on. But the realisation dawned on Finn Roberts that he and August had similar agendas. Just different endings to their stories. "You’re not trying to hide him?" "Shit no, asshole. I wanna help the boy. He’s confused. He ain’t right. That ain’t the Brock I know, Roberts." Slumping back into the armchair, Finn scooped up his Jack Daniels and took a swig, letting the elixir burn its way down his oesophagus on track to his gut. He sighed the sigh of a man who felt like he was taking two steps forward and three steps back. "He’s dangerous, August. You know what he’s been doing, right?" "He’s been loppin’ the hands off’f whores. I know that. It’s just… I know it ain’t him." Roberts shot him a confused look. "You know what I mean. Physically… it’s Brock, right? But there’s somethin’ that got dislodged in his fucken head or somethin’. It ain’t the Brock me and Ape know." "BUTT!" screamed a tiny voice. Both men were startled by little Adam, who’d wandered in sometime during their argument. He raised both arms above his head, like an order more than a request. "UP!" August complied and picked the little boy up, hoisting him onto his hip like he were the centre of his own investigation. "See this boy, Roberts? This is Brock’s son. This is a kid who has lost his daddy in more ways then we could ever know. It ain’t right for a father not to be a part of his kid’s life. It just ain’t right. You can take my fucken word as Bible on that." "He’s going to go to jail, August. We both know that." Monday nodded. He knew the score. But he knew how these things turned. When suspects were considered armed and dangerous there was little expectation any siege (should it turn into one) wouldn’t end in the suspect’s departure from this earth. "I know that, Roberts. I just wanna make sure he goes to you on my terms." "What’re they, August?" Turning to stare his grand son in the eye, August sighed with the heaviest of hearts. When he turned back to stare Roberts dead in his eye, Finn could’ve sworn he saw a tear forming in those piercing green eyes. "That he lives so we can help him." "I don’t want him dead." Roberts put the tumbler back on the table and held his hands up, palms out like a symbol that he were telling the truth to God himself. "I promise August. I just want him off the streets." "And I just wanna help him." The right hand of Finn Roberts slipped into his jacket pocket again, rummaging on the inside until it had found what it was after. He passed it across to August who accepted and examined it. It was a business card. "You call if you’ve got any information." Finn held his right hand up, shaping his thumb and pinky into a phone shape. "Any time. I need you on this, August. We can’t help Brock if we’re not on the same page." Monday slowly nodded. Adam lunged for the card and shoved it in his mouth, biting down on it. His baby teeth pierced through the card with ease and it soon soggied up and turned unreadable five chews later. Roberts headed across the room, hand already inside his jacket again. Crossing the kitchen to the fridge he snatched up a magnet and placed the second card beneath it. "Any time, August. I mean that."
