Part 10: Knows You're Dead

16 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/16/08 The voice of Wyatt Connors boomed out above the gathered crowd, without the help of external amplification. 'If anyone here has any reason why these two should not be married...' Seth could think of plenty of reasons. Fortunately, he had the good sense not to say them out loud. He stood at the edge of the crowd, which had gathered at the church for a wedding ceremony. It seemed that love found its way to even the most emotionally stifling places, as this was the third such event in as many weeks. Wyatt Connors had been all too happy to give his blessing and conduct the ceremonies themselves, which for some reason always involved a lengthy sermon on the evils of penises. 'Sally, do you take Brad to have and to hold--BUT ONLY HIS HANDS!!--for better or worse, in sickness and...' Seth shook his head and walked away, completely unnoticed by the masses. They were too intent on the Reverend and his spectacle--and oh, how the Reverend did love his spectacle--to spare a second glance. In a way, it was somewhat comforting. Ever since the perimeter fence was finished, most of the Compound's residents had come out of their worker-bee mentality, and were now managing a level of interaction beyond asking for more nails. By the time he got to Hiram Jessup's old tractor, he could hear a burst of applause from the gathered witnesses of the happy event. He let out a pained sigh in response. Didn't Connors know that he was supposed to keep all the women for himself? That was how these things were supposed to work, anyway. But then again, you could never count on Wyatt Connors do to things properly. Still, he had to admit, Connors ran a tight ship at the Compound, even without marrying half of the eligible women. There were just over a hundred people now; the migration had slowed, but a new pilgrim would show up once every few days. Within a day, they would be incorporated into the larger group; usually with no more preparation than a refreshing glass of Kool-Aid and a few kind words from Reverend Connors. After that, the new members would be right alongside the old ones, performing the daily chores needed to keep the Compound running. A hundred souls, eager to please. All of them under the direct control of Wyatt Connors. Actually, that wasn't quite accurate. There were three people in the Decency Compound whose will did not belong to the False Prophet. The first of them was Seth. Not that it hardly mattered, since he followed orders so as not to arouse suspicion. That was the difference between him and the rest of the flock--they obeyed out of love, and he out of fear. Not much of a difference, when you thought about it, but enough of one. Seth had, thus far, managed to avoid the tainted beverage that everyone else drank like it was straight from the golden cup. He'd seen what it did to those who drank it--it subjugated them, turned them into thoughtless sheep. He had no intention of going that way, and so he remained one of the few people in the Compound whose thoughts remained his own. Unfortunately, most of those thoughts tended to be the same thing, running over and over through his brain. 'I am so very, very fucked.' # # # Later. After the wedding and a brief celebration, Reverend Wyatt Connors slowly made his way back to the home of Hiram and Mavis Jessup, the family who took him in and told others of his good works when he first arrived. They would not be home, of course; Mavis was in charge of the refreshments at the dance, while Hiram was playing the fiddle for the band. No, Wyatt was headed there to talk to someone else. A man named Ibrahim Seck. Seck was second of the three not controlled by Wyatt Connors. Many years ago, he had been a simple country preacher whose faith was only dwarfed by his ambition. He'd gotten his start in this area, a loose collection of farms in rural Oklahoma. After giving them his particular style of gospel, he moved on to become a televangelist in nearby Ponca City, and his sphere of influence grew. He entered the world of professional wrestling, which allowed him to touch even more souls. By the time he reached his peak in the National Wrestling Council, he had built a small army of followers inside the arena, and a quite large one out of it, all designed to bring glory to God...and by extension, to Ibrahim Seck. Then, it disappeared. So did he...but not by choice. It was the Scarred Man. That demon straight from hell defiled the sanctity of Seck's own home and stole him away. Seck was locked in a dungeon for years...so many years. His grasp of sanity was torn away--and keep in mind, this was a man who regularly claimed to be Jesus' younger brother, and that the devil lived inside everyone's penis. He was freed from his prison by an unknown benefactor earlier this year, and from the very moment he stepped out of his cage, he thought of only one thing: returning home. Not his place of birth, of course, but to the place where his holy empire began...and would begin again. Wyatt Connors knocked on the door of the guest bedroom where Seck took residence. It had been two months since his Exodus, but Ibrahim was still not strong enough to visit himself upon his congregation. Instead, he got his information from Wyatt Connors. 'Enter,' his voice creaked, and the door opened. Wyatt stepped into the room, walked toward the stuffed chair where Seck rested, and bowed his head. 'His blessings be upon you,' Connors whispered. 'And you as well. Look upon me now,' came the reply. Wyatt lifted his head and gazed at the Brother of the Messiah. His dark hair had turned gray and fallen out in clumps during his captivity, and was only now filling back in. The skin on his face and hands was cracked and dry, and his eyes were sunken in. Those same eyes burned with a madness brighter than the light of heaven, hotter than the flames of hell. Ibrahim smiled, showing off his teeth: all capped, and gleaming white. 'Tell me, Reverend. How goes the good fight?' 'Excellent, Master. Brother Macarthur has been a great help at the Compound, and I know that he will be invaluable in battle as well. With him, in addition to the others, I feel we are almost ready to truly make our presence known.' 'Almost?' 'I have an eye on another possible convert...I think this one will be the key. He has the fire in his heart, I have seen it...all he needs is direction.' 'When will you be ready?' 'Our next opportunity is in less than a week. The Crusade will make its move then.' 'Marvelous. I don't need to remind you how important this is.' 'Of course not, Master. The world needs to be prepared for His return.' Seck gave a slight nod of approval. 'What other news do you carry from the land of Sin?' he asked in a harsh whisper. 'It seems they have stepped on the toes of another group of heathens, which has led to the predictable major event between the two promotions that will serve no purpose apart from assuaging the egos of the winners and lining the pockets of the people in charge.' 'Those poor fools. Only concerned with money. Tell me, are any of my disciples participating in this display?' 'Yes. I've been assigned to a tag team match against a pair of what passes for 'stars' in the rival company.' 'And your teammate?' Connors shrugged. 'It hardly matters, does it? Some recalcitrant spirit who refuses the truth. Who can stand against the Crusade, whether they are temporarily with or against me?' 'You?' 'And you, of course. I only meant in the context of the match.' 'I see. You do not think I will have a presence there?' 'Well...of course you will, Master. The love of you and your brother guide us in all that we do.' 'Correct. You would do well to watch your tongue in the future.' 'Of course, Master. Thank you.' 'You may go now,' he hissed. Connors left the room, bowing in deference all the while. Ibrahim's mouth turned into a wolfish grin, baring more of his immaculate teeth. The world had forgotten his name and his works. Oh, sure, they remembered some of it. They remembered all the parts that were cute and funny. They remembered the terrible wrestling, and 'Oh The Shame, The Palpable, Palpable Shame.' But there was more to Ibrahim Seck than ridiculous catchphrases and punches to the genitals. They forgot about the stalking. And about how he had abducted and brainwashed almost a dozen wrestlers in the New England Wrestling Syndicate. They had forgotten about just how devious a maniac he was. That would change. With Connors serving as his Metatron, his message would spread far and wide. Thousands--millions--would heed his call. His empire would return, stronger than ever... ...and with it, he would storm the gates of heaven itself. # # # Jesus Is Coming. Look Busy. That was the key. If you looked like you were in the middle of something, nobody would ask you to do something else...usually something far less enjoyable than whatever you were pretending to do in the first place. That, in turn, was why it had taken Seth a week and a half to change the oil on the tractor. The job gradually got bigger and bigger, with this part needing to be cleaned or that one replaced. The tractor hadn't even need an oil change in the first place; he just didn't want to spend another minute talking with the others. They talked now, of course, but they usually only ever talked about their beloved Wyatt Connors. Seth wondered for a moment if it was true what they said about how man makes his own hell--and just who said it, for that matter--then turned back to his work. The tractor was actually outside the perimeter fence, giving Seth an extra amount of precious distance from the rest of the congregation. He tended to bury himself in his approximation of work, just so that people would leave him the hell alone. But like the great prophet Mick once said, 'You can't always get what you want.' The air was pierced by a sudden cry. 'Aaah!' Seth snapped his head toward the shout--it came from the one road that led into the Compound. 'Hello?' he called out. 'Ow ow ow ow ow ow ow...' the voice moaned. Female, and obviously in pain. Seth dropped his tools and immediately ran toward the sounds of agony. It must have been a quarter-mile down the road before he came upon the source: a young woman with a large backpack, lying in the gravel and clutching her ankle. 'Are you all right?' he asked, a stupid but almost inevitable question. She snapped back, 'No, I'm not! I wouldn't be hurt and on the ground if I was, would I?' 'I suppose I deserved that,' he replied, and walked closer toward her. Soon, he was standing right by her, but as he reached down toward her leg, she shied away. 'Hey! What do you think you're doing, buddy?' 'Relax,' he said, holding his hands before him in a gesture designed to induce calm. 'I only want to see if it's broken. I know some basic first aid.' That seemed to assuage her fears, as she then allowed him to examine her leg. Apart from a few hisses when he prodded around the ankle, nothing seemed to be very serious. 'There doesn't seem to be a break, but you might have a pretty nasty sprain.' While he was down there anyway, he figured he'd take a closer look. It was a very nice leg; well tanned, recently shaved, and there was another one that looked just like it. They went up at least as far as the bottom of a pair of denim cut-offs. They might have gone farther, but his search was interrupted by an impatient throat-clearing. 'Ahem,' the woman said. 'The problem's at the other end of the leg.' Seth grinned sheepishly. 'Sorry,' he muttered, then stood up. 'Well, nearest hospital is a damn long way from here. But we've got some bandages; we can at least get you patched up before we send you on your way. How does that sound?' The girl gave a frustrated sigh. 'I suppose it's going to be the best offer I get. Fine. If you'll just give me a hand, I can--hey!' she cried, as the much larger farmhand-turned-henchman scooped her up in his arms, and began to carry her back to base. She tried to give protest again, but her complaints were answered before she could make them. 'Quickest way back to the camp,' he said, quickly stepping toward the front gate of the Compound. It wasn't an easy trek--the girl was slightly heavier than she looked, and the backpack didn't help matters at all--but it wouldn't be long before they arrived. 'So...what brings you all the way out here?' Seth asked, hoping that conversation would make the trip easier. The girl flipped her shoulder-length, dirty-blonde curls to the side, giving her a better view of her transportation. 'I finally got fed up with the hypocrisy that my family lived with every time they went to church...and I heard about a sanctuary out here in the sticks. Someplace where people really understood what Jesus was all about, you know? A...sort of a Paradise that hadn't been Lost yet.' Seth pursed his lips in thought. Literature had never been his strong suit, but he seemed to recognize the phrase. 'That...sounds familiar. Dante?' 'Milton, actually.' Seth was taken aback a little bit--so much that he almost tripped on some loose rock. Someone was actually using their brain? Here? 'What's your name?' he asked. 'Abigail Proctor. Call me Abby; all my friends do.' 'Hi, Abby. I'm Seth.' # # # As soon as they arrived inside the Decency Compound, Seth ducked quickly into the first aid station, which seemed to be undermanned today--small wonder, since the chief nurse just got married. 'I've got someone who needs a bandage on her ankle, and probably a crutch too,' he said. Unfortunately, there didn't seem to be any nurses there to hear him. There was, however, a person in there of clear authority. It was Wyatt Connors, who for some reason was staring blankly at the wall. 'Are you all right?' Seth asked. Wyatt barely seemed to notice that someone was talking to him, but he still managed to answer the question. 'I lied to him,' he whispered. 'And I don't even know why...' Remember how we told you that there were three people in the Compound not under the complete control of Reverend Wyatt Connors? The last member of that triumvirate is probably the most important--if not to the Crusade, then certainly to the False Prophet himself. In fact, Wyatt Connors would gladly trade every his lordship over every other person in the Decency Crusade, or in the entire Compound, for the full control of this one man. That man's name? Wyatt Connors.
