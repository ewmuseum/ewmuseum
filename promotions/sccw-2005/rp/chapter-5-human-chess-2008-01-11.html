Chapter 5: Human Chess

Date: 1/11/08 5.1: The Future - Lyle Desmond and the Key to the Art Collection Sometimes, you get in over your head, and sometimes you live over your head. For longer than he could remember, Lyle Desmond had been in the latter category. Ten years ago, Lyle was just a hotshot straight out of journalism school. He had aspirations to be a newsman, to tell the world some hard truths. He'd be the next Dan Rather, the next Walter fucking Cronkite, man. But in order to any of that shit, he had to eat. So when a man named David called him all those years ago with an easy job that paid well and had health insurance, Lyle jumped at the chance. He figured it'd be easy money to follow around some overgrown, jacked-up idiot, screaming about how they were going to beat up some other equally overgrown, equally jacked-up idiot. Ten years later, Lyle stood in front of a dark blue door with an omega symbol painted across the front in graffiti, and he regretted that decision. He regretted it quite a lot. The people he'd met... Lyle didn't know men and women like these people existed. And he did not want to know, but he couldn't unlearn what he'd learned. Lyle's thin fingers trembled as he felt blindly for the lock that the long iron key he held would open. The light next to the door was broken, shattered white glass ground up in the olive carpet beneath it. When he'd gotten the voicemail some four weeks earlier and he'd heard that voice, Lyle knew he should have just fucking deleted it. But there was always a story there, and all the message gave him was an address and instructions to bring his camera. So, yeah, he'd followed the directions and he'd gone to the address. He was damaged goods now, besides -- just another crackpot conspiracy theorist. It's just that his theories were a based a little more on fact than most. In the end, Lyle Desmond never meant to film the videos that would end up being re-cut and aired on Showtime by the signal-pirate Monet. But then, he didn't mean for a lot of things to happen that did. It was thirteen days ago that Lyle had visited Pembrook Asylum to visit the person FUSE fans knew only as 'Monet,' getting a visit from a rather perturbed doctor and a key instead (the footage of which just aired last week). It took Desmond time to get up the gumption to go; he was afraid of what he would find behind the door with the omega symbol scrawled on it. The key slid easily into the lock, throwing the bolt open with a heavy thump. The door opened soundlessly, without the creak that Lyle expected. Inside, it was just a hotel room, though there was no furniture -- no bed, no nightstands, and no dressers inside. The walls were painted an ugly shade of yellow, but the color was almost completely gone, covered by photographs that were plastered to the walls, to the ceiling, and to the floors. Some of the pictures were old; some were distressingly new. Colored lines connected the pictures, drawing a haphazard map of relationships that Desmond didn't understand. He recognized some of the names -- 'Prissintino,' 'Nackedy,' 'Duluth,' 'Campbell.' Some lines were solid black, some were a livid red, some were acidic green... as Lyle tried to follow them, it seemed there were as many colors as there were in the rainbow. Quite frankly, the cameraman couldn't make hide nor hair of all the interplay. But he knew someone who could. Lyle Desmond had been treading water for so long just to keep from drowning that he'd forgotten what it felt like to stand on solid ground. During his time swimming, however, some of the sharks swimming with him had grown... if not fond of him, then at least accustomed enough to his presence not to shred him at first sight. Lyle pulled his cellphone free of the leather carrying case he wore at his hip and dialed a telephone number registered to a home somewhere in Trinidad. The number was busy. 'Fuck, man, answer your line.' He shot a nervous look over his shoulder, turning the phone 180° to the side and snapping a series of pictures of the walls before he slipped out. He was unwilling to risk getting caught here. Every once in a while, Lyle Desmond thought he finally had his head above water. This was usually when another wave hit him and nearly dragged him under. He'd just found his life preserver. * * * * * 5.2: The Present - Dearly Beloved Maxwell Drake's funeral services were a gala event, a veritable who's who of the media elite gathered to pay their respects to a man that not one of them liked. It took place on a dreary Saturday morning in Malibu, a few dozen of California's most influential citizens in their best mourning clothes, huddled under umbrellas as they listened to eulogies of the Old Dragon. The speeches given on that day used words like 'opinionated' when their speakers meant 'kind of an asshole,' and 'steadfast' when they meant 'stubborn as a fucking mule.' The gathered flashed plastic smiles for the television cameras, they espoused their sadness and outrage at Maxwell's tragic death during a mugging, and, all the while, the media talked. Pundits speculated about the division of the Drake family fortune and they took note of how little Maxwell Drake III and Duncan Drake spoke to each other. All the while, the only person with an inkling of what actually happened sat to Duncan's left and quailed with doubt. MacKenzie Malone had not heard from Mr. Hawke since the afternoon she'd learned of Maxwell's death. She didn't really want to call again; she was honestly afraid to learn anything more than she already had. Duncan was unusually quiet during the ceremony, with his eyes focused on his feet most of the time. His shirt collar was unbuttoned and his tie was loose. 'Hey,' Mac said, sliding her hand across the Hellion's thigh with a small smile. 'You okay?' 'Hm?' Duncan looked up. He took her hand in his, squeezing lightly. 'I'm fine. Just... thinking about stuff.' Her eyes widened. 'Duncan -- ' 'No jokes, Kenz. Not today.' She tucked her chin, looking away briefly. She blinked a couple times, lifting her free hand and dabbing gently at her eye with the back of her forefinger. 'Sorry.' 'S'okay. Just... it's been hard.' 'I can only imagine that losing a family member would be hard on anyone.' She wouldn't look at Duncan's face when she spoke, but he was looking down again. 'I'm... I'm supposed to be this asshole, right? I'm supposed to be all loud and brash and whatever. But I can't... I can't figure out how to do that, y'know? How can I be that guy after I leave here?' MacKenzie didn't have an answer that would satisfy the Hellion -- not today, at least. She squeezed his hand and slid to her feet, stooped over so the people behind her could still see. 'I'm... I'm gonna go get some water. Everything will be okay. You know that, right?' 'Yeah, I suppose.' MacKenzie strode briskly to the outskirts of the ceremony, her head low to avoid onlookers and she made a call on her cellphone that she knew she probably shouldn't have made. Only this one didn't go to a man who went by the name of Mr. Hawke or a woman who called herself Trouble. This went somewhere else entirely, ringing only once before a gentle-voiced lady picked up. 'Banner Desert Samaritan Hospital, how may I direct your call?' 'Room 14-101, please,' MacKenzie said. There was a long pause from the operator. 'There's no one registered in that room, ma'am.' 'If you could ring it anyway, like it was, I don't know, your fucking job or something, that'd be spectacular.' With a sigh just on the border of 'I'm this close to hanging up on you, bitch,' MacKenzie's call was connected. The Belle of the Ball clambered up on the hood of a parked car, lying back to stare up into the gray sky. Her pitch-dark hair tumbled down around her head. 'Hello?' It was a man's voice that answered, tired and angry and, well, mainly tired. 'It's me,' MacKenzie said gently. She would later blame her messed-up mascara on the rain. * * * * * 5.3: The Past: Just a Car Crash Away The car was brand new at the time, a cherry red, 2006 Ferrari F430 Spider, optioned out to the gills. It was a beast of a convertible, aerodynamic and with a low center of gravity, and some of its modifications were later determined to not be exactly street-legal. Its racing tires were new, and they gripped the freeway easily. In truth, it was a splendid machine; it was responsive, maneuverable, and ohmygod aitch-oh-double-tee hott. The passenger was a twenty-three year old blonde girl who told people she was twenty-one who'd given her name to the driver as Dani but whose driver's license said, 'Kelly Ann Carpenter.' She also wasn't really a blonde, because this woman was made of lies. The driver didn't much care, though, because he knew her line of work called for certain things. She was a fine-looking specimen as well; her dress was colored a few shades darker than the Ferrari and cut a few inches shorter than was proper. She was hiding something much more important than just her real name. The driver was a young man, himself having seen only twenty-four summers. He had liquid brown eyes, a sharp smile, and short, spiky brown hair. Back then, he bleached the tips a pale shade of yellow. The man dressed like he had money, in silks and fine designers, but then he drove a Ferrari, and was there any more ostentatious way to announce one's wealth? The way the driver maneuvered that car was reckless and it was reckless -- he was hell on wheels. Actually, Duncan Drake would more properly be termed a Hellion on wheels. 'Damn, I love this car,' Duncan said, pressing smoothly down on the clutch and whipping the stick into fifth gear. 'I mean I'm sure you're great too, hon, but I really love this car.' Kelly straightened in her seat, turning to face Drake as she curled one leg across the leather seat. 'Were you planning to stick your dick in the exhaust pipe?' she asked. 'I might.' 'Until you're sure, hon, you might want to remember how much you've paid for my company.' Duncan's grin was a slash of lips. 'He's gonna shit a brick,' he said. 'I mean, she's gonna laugh her ass off, but he's gonna flip the fuck out.' 'Tell me again why you're doing this? You're Duncan Drake -- can't you just call one of the studios if all you need is a girl to play a role in some practical joke.' 'Some practical joke! You don't understand -- it's all about the realism in order to make shit like this work. Besides, you may be called to do stuff that... let's just say it's probably against the SAG rules, and most actresses I know would probably tell me to go fuck myself.' Kelly Carpenter never wanted to be a whore; few girls set out with that kind of career path in mind. But while she had the looks and she always dir well in the Muncie Southside High School's yearly production of Our Town (she even got mentioned in the local paper once!), there are a thousand girls just like her who move to Hollywood every day. When, like she did, they fail to land the big role, the lucky ones end up with a day job or back on a bus home. The unlucky ones end up much like Kelly Carpenter; they do what they must to survive. She'd tell herself at night that she was still acting, that Dani was just a role, that the wide-eyed newcomer to the skin trade that she put on every night for the men who paid her boss a lot of money for the pleasure of her company wasn't her, because that kind of innocence is bred out of a girl real quick, especially at the Cherry Wood Lounge. 'Well,' she said with mock artlessness. 'My boss told me to make sure to do everything in my power to make sure that you were satisfied with the Cherry Wood's services, so I doubt I'll be telling you to go fuck yourself.' The sound of a zipper being tugged open was not a sound she was unfamiliar with; she heard it even over the hum of the engine. 'Did you say anything?' Duncan asked. 'Think you did.' Kelly did not sigh, though she certainly wanted to do so. 'Uh,' she said instead, tucking her chin as if she were blushing. But she wasn't doing that, either. 'I may have.' She unbuckled her seatbelt, drawing her knees up underneath her. Deft, long-nailed fingers slipped into Duncan's lap. When the black Ford Expedition plowed into the passenger's side of Duncan Drake's shiny new, cherry-red Ferrari F430 Spider, neither the passenger nor the driver were paying enough attention to notice it. Not that they would have seen it, as it came from off the roadside, and its headlights were dark. The sportscar was hit solidly in the side, crumpling the door beyond usability and shattering the window. The car's tires squealed as the impact first turned the Ferrari sideways, then flipped it up to its side. Inertia and momentum working together make for a bitch of a team, and the wind lifted the sportscar into the air, sending it hurtling through the air end over end at more than seventy miles per hour. When this was reported later, the media's attention would focus on how well the F430's safety harness worked, as Drake only suffered a fracture to his collarbone and a severe case of whiplash, and on how police never located the SUV that hit the car. Drake would be lauded as a victim. Teenaged girls cried on MTV, worrying about his condition. No mention was ever made of the professional escort who went by the name 'Dani' who was thrown from the twisting, twisted car as it flew down the highway or the fact that the paramedics later told her family that she was very lucky that she survived at all. Her family accepted a very large payout from Duncan Drake's family to keep their secret, to keep quiet about the phone call the young Hellion made from their daughter's cellphone after the crash. They had to delete any record of the call from the phone and give Maxwell Drake's people the SIM chip. None of the Carpenters mentioned their daughter's secret to Maxwell's attorneys. No one but them would ever know that Kelly lost her child in the accident. * * * * * 5.4: The Future - Correspondence and Dinner Date: Tuesday, January 08, 2008 14:14:37 Eastern Standard Time From: [detail withheld] To: [detail withheld] Subject: Re: Monsters I think they're both monsters, and it's TOTALLY your fault. She fast-forwards through a lot of the stuff, though she thinks Jonathan Rhine is just dreamy. Her twin sister always tells her there's no accounting for taste. * * * Meanwhile, there was much diabolical plotting to be had at the Sizzler. Mr. Hawke and the woman called Trouble were enjoying a light salad bar. It was not the type of place the man with the hawk-headed cane usually dined, but Trouble said it reminded her of her roots and that she'd lived in Nevada so long she'd become spoiled by the buffet. He just nodded, fairly sure she was testing her ability to lie to him, but deciding it wasn't worth pursuing. '...some reticence there,' Trouble was saying. 'But you had to expect it. You can't give someone everything they want without them realizing there's nothing left.' Mr. Hawke's fork stabbed viciously at his greenery. 'I told you I was concerned about the closeness factor. There's a certain amount of variables we won't be able to control if the pretend becomes the real and fairytale romances suddenly seem possible.' 'I'm pretty sure it will be fine.' Trouble picked at her food, circling it like a hyena harried a gazelle. 'We can't afford for you to be 'pretty certain.' I've already spoken to Orchid.' Trouble would not meet the man's gaze. 'Without me? You know this is my play, dammit.' His smile was tight as he cut down a piece of chopped steak. 'I told you I was concerned about the closeness factor. I meant with you, as well.' 'What's her plan?' 'It's being taken care of. The Specialist was already in place.' 'You had no right, Hawke.' Trouble's voice is a weapon, just the right mix of breathlessness and husk, like carrying around a tease in a bottle. But right then it was strained to its breaking point. 'You had no fucking -- ' The 1812 Overture interrupted their argument; Hawke slid his cellphone from his breast pocket and snapped it open. 'How's the happy family?' He was one of those people who never said, 'Hello' to anyone. 'Oh, I'm sure it's just fucking wonderful.' He snapped his fingers, beckoning to Trouble, pantomiming writing in the air. She produced a pen. 'I can't believe he just left the bill laying around. Mm. Uh-huh. So it's exactly where we figured.' Trouble's eyes lit up, her earlier anger fading. She leaned forward, elbows on the table as Hawke wrote down whatever his caller told him. 'Got it. I'll have a team there in two days. Yes, we'll accompany them personally.' He smiled, and that was not a nice expression on his thin features. 'Well, you knew what would be asked of you, no matter how long it took to find this. Burn it down, burn it all down around them. Scorched fucking earth. The old fashioned way.' He snapped the cellphone shut. 'We're a go.' * * * * * 5.5: The Present - Giving Thanks MacKenzie didn't know why Duncan wanted to see her, but she always made sure to be dressed up when she did. She'd picked stonewashed designer blue jeans -- the kind that cost three hundred dollars to look like they cost twenty -- and a button-down red blouse this time, her tar-dark hair pulled back and clipped by silver. As she pulled into the lot outside the Hellion's condo, something flared her suspicion. She peered around the parking lot, her green eyes flicking over Duncan's three cars before landing on the one that was out of place. She immediately knew from the license that it was a rental, a forest green four-door sedan, parked between the GTO and the Caddy. Malone slid out of her truck as quietly as she could, slipping off her high heels as she hit the ground. She padded, barefoot, across the parking lot towards the Drake condo, staying low. It might have been paranoid, but after what had happened to Duncan's father, she wasn't taking any chances. Not with these people. '...disturbing you or anything, am I?' Living in Southern California meant Duncan didn't have to worry about heat, even in the dead of January. A woman's voice reached from the den's bay window. The voice had a breathy quality to it, an eagerness that painted its speaker as kind of young. 'Nah, I'm... I was just waiting for my girl.' That was Duncan -- she'd know that voice anywhere. He sounded sort of uncomfortable or perhaps distracted. MacKenzie stepped closer, her need for secrecy not as important now (though she never could say with the people she found herself tied up with). She ducked down under the lip of the window, deciding she'd give it a count of five until she peeked her head up again, unless it sounded like she'd been seen. 'Oh,' the girl said. 'You have a...' There was a pause here, a word caught in the woman's throat. 'Uh, a girlfriend? Will she mind that I'm -- here?' MacKenzie was about to look, but she froze in place, suddenly actually interested in the answer. When it came, Duncan's response was a hiss of a whisper and she barely caught it. 'I wasn't gonna tell...' He drew out the word. 'Good,' the girl responded. 'I mean. I'm just...' Duncan coughed lowly. 'I'm just thanking you for the chance to be in your... mmm, party at Uproar.' 'With your friends.' 'With allllll of my friends.' MacKenzie Malone peeked over the edge of the windowsill, dreading what she would find. Duncan Drake's den was his playroom, with two pinball machines and an actual version of the old Pac-Man and Capcom X-Men video game against the wall. He had a foosball and an antique pool table, the latter of which was his pride and joy. He'd hung a stained glass light over it and had his friends over quite often for drinking and drunken pool playing. Duncan was sitting on the pool table, facing away from the window, braced back against his palms, looking at the ceiling. There was no sign of a woman in the room. MacKenzie ducked back down and crab-walked away from the den window, only coming fully to her feet when she was sure she was out of sight. She headed for her car, brushing off the seat of her pants. As she moved, she dialed Duncan on her cellphone. She did not look back to see if he answered. He did not. 'Duncan Drake's Daily Message of Awesomeness,' Drake's voicemail said. 'If Awesome and Duncan Drake were in a race, Duncan would win, because Awesome would be like, 'Dude, I can't beat that guy.'' There followed a long beep. 'Dunc, it's MacKenzie,' she said as she slid her shoes back on. 'I'm running a little late, but I should be over soon. I really look forward to seeing you -- after the week we've had, we need to do something fun. Maybe we should catch a movie? Talk to you soon, sweetie, bye.' Perhaps surprisingly, Malone was not crying as she sat in her pickup and waited, quietly chewing on the inside of her cheek. * * * * * 5.6: The Future - The Pembrook Fire This coming Sunday morning, there will be a throwaway story in a British newspaper. The editor will have put it there just to fill some column space. The reporter will only have written it only for the byline. Neither will consider the story very important; it's very likely neither man will think about it again after the newspaper goes to print. Most readers won't even look at it, but four very different people will take notice immediately, and eventually, it will mean a lot to many American professional wrestling fans, though they will never know it happened. A woman in San Diego that reads every major newspaper in the world will read this story, but she will not learn its meaning for some time, and when she does, it will not mean the same thing for her that it does for everyone else. A man in Park City, Utah will read this story, and his head will fall into his hands, because he will know the woman he loved (loves? he isn't sure) succeeded in her quest where no one thought she could. He will stub out the end of his cigarette, only to light another, and he will wonder when he will have no choice but to stand against his love when her only crime is belief. Another man, this one in Trinidad, will read this story and he will have packed everything essential from his beachside bungalow in thirty-seven minutes. He will be on the first flight off of the island in eighty-one minutes, and he will intentionally miss his connection, losing himself in the great melting pot of the United States. He will do these things out of habit, and because he will soon be a target. The fourth man who reads this story is named Lyle Desmond and what happens when he reads the story is... well, it's a tale for another time. This is the story in question. For the most part, it is true. Fire At Pembrook Asylum; Eleven Inmates Feared Trapped Inside Penbryn, Wales Officials are investigating the cause of a mysterious blaze that swept through the notorious Pembrook Asylum early last night. The fire started just after six o'clock in the evening in the north wing, which houses the asylum's now-condemned chapel, and ripped through much of the deteriorating structure before local area firefighters were able to stem the tide. Rescue teams have pulled seven bodies from the gutted out remains of the building, identified as either staff or inmates. Pembrook Asylum rose to some notoriety in the early 20th Century as rumors of mistreatment arouse in Walter Johansson's book Sound Mind, Sound Body, and its patient count has dropped precipitously over the last century, from nearly 500 in 1907 to just eleven according to the Welsh government's records. Officials state they will continue their search for survivors, but that they are not holding out much hope. Reports of foul play involved in the fire would not be confirmed at press time. -------- The night before the story broke, the figure known simply as Monet stood on a hardscrabble hillock overlooking a burning mental institution. The night air, tinged with greasy gray smoke, wafted through Monet's bedraggled, shoulder-length hair, but the stench was not even paid a second thought. It was the signal-pirate's first taste of fresh air in twenty-six months, three weeks, two days, eight hours, and three minutes; it was meant to be enjoyed. The three-pronged stride of the man with the hawk-headed cane -- step, stab, step -- drew Monet's attention, the fire's light dancing across pale, pale alabaster skin. 'Time to go,' Mr. Hawke said quietly. 'You've got a lot to catch up on and not a lot of time to do it.' No one who saw the man would recognize him as the acid-tongued, black-clad specter that did very bad things in Monet's name. Gone were his perfectly pleated black slacks and his tar-colored turtleneck, replaced by flat gray coveralls whose nametag declared him to be 'Ted.' Monet nodded, turning reluctantly away from the burning building. Deathly silent steps carried the pirate like a ghost in Mr. Hawke's wake, pausing as he opened the trunk of a 1979 Chevrolet Caprice. 'Your chariot awaits.' Hawke gestured expansively with his cane. The old clunker was hitched to the back of a beaten-up blue tow truck parked alongside the road. Without comment or complaint, Monet climbed into the trunk, hair a mass of dark tangles fallen across a frigid smile. Hawke tossed his cane in as well before he slammed the trunk shut, limping to the driver's door. The tow truck will be found Monday morning, abandoned in a junkyard, though it will have been cleaned professionally. It will never have been reported stolen, but that is likely because its owner, Theodore Valentine, was currently lying dead of a heroin overdose in a motel room in Swansea. Teddy was last seen leaving a pub with a woman his friends recall as a 'punked-out knockout,' but they only remember the green highlights in her hair, her nose ring, and that they could see nipple through her Sex Pistols t-shirt. Teddy's body will be found tomorrow morning; to their never-ending regret, his buddies never see the chick with the too-tight Pistols tee again. Alone in the dark, locked in the trunk of a car being towed down a Welsh highway, Monet slept peacefully, paying no mind to being cooped up. The promise had been fulfilled. The signal-pirate was free. When a master chess player makes their first move towards checkmate, it's called an opening gambit. This night was Monet's. Pawn to Queen's Bishop Four.
