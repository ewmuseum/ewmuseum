Scratching the Surface: Wealth & Taste

Date: Today Prologue: Of the Diary of Leonid In a climate-controlled room hidden in the sub-basement of a manor in the wilds of New Mexico, there exists a cabinet said to house some of the greatest secrets known to man. Conspiracy theorists call it the Shadow Cabinet, and inside, one can find everything from the truth of the Roswell landing (it's not as exciting as you think) to the details of intelligence operations never declassified to notes on scientific experiments that would boggle the mind. Also included in this collection are several books of supposed ''prophecy,'' beginning with those written by Nostradamus and his contemporaries and continuing with a legion of geniuses and madmen over the last twelve hundred years. These words are predictions of an indeterminate, even contradictory, nature – some spoiled by the mutable nature of the future, others simply by the vagaries of the human psyche. Among them is a small book, bound in leather, no larger than a Victorian era chapbook, each page jammed with hundreds of words, written in Russian and printed in a tiny hand. This book, called the Diary of Leonid, is not well-known or well thought of by scholars – not even the type who hold to such fanciful things as ''prophecy''. If only someone read the Diary of Leonid these last few weeks – so much could have been prevented. Could still be prevented. Alas, such is the way of prophecy. * * * Translated from the Russian: ''There once were Two – Man on a black horse, Woman on a white – And Death rode beside Them. Together, They were Balanced, happy, and hale. Apart, they reveled in Sadness and Hatred. They circled each other like vultures spitting fire, unaware of the Truths both hide. ''There will come a Time when He wallows in misery, and She soars with Happiness Unimagined. Then shall come a Snake in the form of a Swan, with one chance to Bite with Poison well-aged.'' -- The Diary of Leonid, p. 18. One: The Way The Story Goes Then. Ann Arbor, Michigan To call the Bloody Vine a ''nightclub'' was something of a misnomer. They said the place used to be a pawnshop, but a tragic armed robbery left the store's proprietor dead and led to the inevitable sale of the property. No one can quite agree on exactly when the robbery occurred, and the veracity of the tale is questionable at best. One thing was for sure – the club looked like it'd been converted from a pawnshop, its bartop a former display case, and bottles of expensive liquor housed in the same steel cages meant to hold firearms. The rest of the place was decorated like some strange hybrid of a funeral home and a medieval castle ballroom, with lots of hanging tapestries and lit predominantly by a series of wrought-iron candelabras. Low, growling music was the order of the day, slightly outdated selections of everything from Nine Inch Nails to Type O Negative to Ministry and beyond. It felt overdone, like a pretty, Goth-flavored candy shell without anything underneath, designed to attract the kinds of rebellious, black-clad teenagers who rebellious, black-clad college students. For the most part, it failed spectacularly, with crowds that could barely be called ''sparse'' on its best nights, nevermind a half-hour after opening on a brisk Sunday night. And that was before the gunfire. Kathryn Shaw ducked down behind an old wooden chair, squatting as best as her skirt would allow, both hands over her head. ''Fuckfuckfuck! They're shooting at each other! This was supposed to be a milk run!'' ''She's... I'm not sure where she is.'' Jonas Stryker whispered in her ear from hundreds of yards away. Kathryn would never get used to these earbuds. Whatever happened to a goddamn headset? ''You're not sure? You promised me that we had this!'' She ducked down, running unsteadily in sky-high heels as she darted behind the bar. ''I thought we did!'' Stryker was across the street in a van marked ''Addams Plumbing Co., and he spoke as he climbed over the driver's seat into the van. ''Just stay down and let them work it out, then work your magic on whoever wins.'' ''Are you CRAZ—'' She hunkered down smaller, trying to make herself invisible, shout morphing into a whisper. ''Are you crazy? How do I spin that? 'Hey, I like your gun, are you packing heat for me?''' Stryker paused as he tugged a pump-action shotgun from his bag. ''That'd probably work on me, you know.'' Inside the bar, Shaw couldn't keep the grin from spreading across her lips. ''That's because you're an easy mark, now someone do something to get me the fuck out of here that doesn't involve me having to take off my—'' The front door to the Bloody Vine had been painted red, but the job hadn't been touched up in years, and when the motorcycle boot connected with the old wooden door, flecks of dried paint flew off along with the dried splinters. The noise was enough to draw a chirp of shock from the brunette behind the bar, and the two pairs of shooters stopped firing just long enough to look that way – it would've been dumb not to. The tink of metal on wood was the only warning before the smoke grenade split the pair, obscuring the vision of all of them as the figure in leather stepped forward. ''Teddy Haynes!'' It was Stryker's voice, and it drew a smile from Shaw behind the bar, especially since no more bullets had been fired since he made his appearance. One of the men – the one who, ten minutes earlier, found himself in the crosshairs of Kathryn Shaw's attentions – raised his head, pointing his gun somewhere in the smoke. ''Who wants to know?'' ''My name is Geoff. I was hoping we could be friends. But I can't be friends with men who are shooting at each other, so—'' ''They were shooting at me, man! Lenny's been hit, and these guys won't even tell me why!'' ''Shout out the location of the perps, Kathi.'' Stryker's whisper impelled her forward. ''Are you crazy? You've got this one under control.'' ''Just do it.'' Shaw crawled behind the bar, avoiding the broken glass from shattered bottles as much as she could. ''And you guys?'' Stryker called out into the bar. ''Whatever your problems are with Mr. Haynes don't matter nearly as much as I have been paid to bring him in, so maybe we can make a deal.'' ''Two shooters,'' Kathryn was prone on the floor of the bar, trying not to think about what else had been spilled where she was lying. ''One looks kind of hurt, about five feet in, uh... at about ten o'clock from me. He's a big target, flattop haircut. Ex-military. The other guy is still crouched behind one of the chairs, but I think he's got a knife or something, which is weird.'' Neither of the shooters spoke, and the leather-clad Stryker suddenly burst into a run, leaping onto a barstool and using it as a launching pad to take him onto the bar right over Kathryn's head. Abandoned glasses and bowls of peanuts fell aside as Jonas ran heedlessly into the thick of it, leaping off with the butt of his gun leading. ''Let me know if the other guy moves.'' The blow took him behind the ear, spinning the large man around. The man went with the blow, but Stryker fell into a crouch, and the clumsy punch whistled overhead. Shooting up to a standing position, Jonas used the motorcycle helmet as a weapon, driving it into the bridge of the attacker's nose, staggering him backwards. A standing front kick to the chest drove him further back, and the man with the flattop stumbled to a knee over the assault. Jonas stepped up onto the man's knee, driving his leg into the man's jaw. It was over, just like that, and Stryker spun through, leveling his pistol at the copse of chairs where the thin man with the knife was waiting. Or, rather, should have been waiting. ''He was right there,'' Shaw said. ''I was watching!'' '''Sokay. Gimme a Vicki here.'' Stryker swung around, pointing his gun into the booth where Haynes hid. ''You want to come out now.'' ''Don't do anything stupid, man.'' Haynes set his gun down on the table. ''The cops are on their way, so let's not get crazy.'' ''I do not have time for this. You're coming with me, even if I have to make you.'' ''What are you going to—'' Kathi heard the bang of heavy-soled boots down the bartop, and she didn't have to fake her squeal as a gloved hand wrenched her to her feet. ''Don't shoot, don't shoot!'' She waved her hands towards the booth. ''Ted! Teddy, please! I don't know what kind of shit you're into, with the guns and the, uh... shooting and whatever, but I don't want to die because I thought you were cute.'' ''All right, all right!'' Haynes slid out of the booth. ''Don't hurt her, guy.'' He raised both hands, trying to smile. ''I'm going with you. Not raising a hassle or a—gahk.'' That was when Teddy Haynes' neck sprouted a hole, blood rushing out of his mouth as he slumped to the ground. The man that stood behind him was thin, built like a scarecrow with a smile that had too many teeth. Just a few wisps of hair clung to the top of his head, a pair of small spectacles perched on his nose. The sudden violence forced Shaw to turn away from the scene, turning her face away and into Stryker's shoulder. ''Jesus, Jojo.'' ''Hello.'' The man waved a long-fingered hand. ''You ladies are very curious, I must say. I think...'' He beamed a smile. ''I think we should go have a chat, shan't we?'' ''Yeah? How about Jojo kicks your ass again, you wannabe Batman villain?'' Katsidy slid between Stryker and the man, bowing up. ''I truly doubt your friend wishes to continue our hostilities, miss. So put the claws away. Now that I know you were faking – a terribly convincing routine, believe me – I think that means you are a target now, hmm? If we were to get in a fight, I think you might get scarred up.'' ''Take me, leave her.'' The leather-clad figure spoke, but it wasn't Jonas Stryker's voice. Pulling off the motorcycle helmet, a tumble of red curls fell over Alexandra Pierce's shoulders. ''I'll go willingly.'' ''Sorry, princess.'' Jonas Stryker was still in Shaw's ear. ''Shoulda told you that wasn't me.'' ''Oh, good.'' The man sheathed his knife along his forearm. ''That battle with Mr. Haynes was quite exhausting, believe me.'' Shaw looked between them, slinking forward. ''I like you knife,'' she purred. ''You packing heat for—'' ''Go back to the van.'' Alex put her hand on the Siren's shoulder. ''I'm going with him.'' ''You don't have to do this, Lexi. I'm sure you can take him.'' ''Maybe, but he is correct – you would be a target, and I cannot have that. Margaret would have my head.'' The man quirked an eyebrow at the name, but made no mention of it. ''Come now. Send the harlot away, and let's have a chat.'' Kathryn walked away reluctantly, scuffing her feet on the floor. ''What's your name, anyway?'' The man slipped a pair of handcuffs from... Kathryn could not tell where, a tattoo of a spider on his right hand that winked like a gemstone in the candlelight. ''Call me Scratch, sugar.'' He bound Pierce's wrists behind her. ''I hate to see you go, but I love watching you leave, so... go on. Shoo. Off with you now.'' Two: Introspective Twaddle Now. She was sure her iPhone was mocking her. With everything Alexandra Pierce was involved in – from complex confidence schemes to her participation in the most prestigious high-stakes match in Sin City's history to her nine-month affair with possibly the most famous woman in the sport -- it would be hard to imagine the Spider in the Web to ever be bored. Sitting in the coffee shop at an Oakland-area Borders, Desade may have argued with the term, but the truth was... she was fucking bored. Her move to Oakland had been a spur of the moment decision, or at least as close as Alex ever came. Most of the time, she didn't regret the move, since her New Mexico estate was, by design, ruthlessly devoid of any sign it was hers; she'd used it twice in her professional wrestling career as a stand-in for another location. On this particular lazy Thursday, just a few days before she stepped into the Dome of Doom, Alex kind of missed it. She could have lost herself in research or gotten into an argument with her Cambrian Cambridge or offered her services to her colleagues for one of the games they were playing. Anything to avoid that phone. SCCW's visit to Albuquerque hadn't been uneventful; aside from Amy's victory over Dusk and her own defeat of an injured Phillip Kennedy, the after-show event at the Fairgrounds ended with the two redheads hanging precipitously from the seat of a Ferris wheel as she'd asked Amy to... To what? Pierce wasn't really sure. It hadn't been a proposal, not really; Alex wasn't stupid enough to actually ask Amy Campbell to marry her, since that would have forced the Red Raver's hand, pushing her into making a decision Alex feared inevitable. But the ring burned in Pierce's pocket, even now. She didn't want to put it down, as if tucking it in a drawer would turn Campbell's desire for time into an outright refusal. Her reluctance to call the woman she loved only made things worse, and it was kind of embarrassing. Originally, she'd wanted to avoid pressuring Campbell, unsure if it was possible to have a conversation without the specter of that awkward moment on a Ferris wheel spoiling one of the few happy things left in her life. In hindsight, it was selfish of her, and Alex couldn't help but imagine Amy was somewhere, similarly staring at her Blackberry, just as scared to call. Her mother's wedding ring loomed over them, and Alex felt increasingly cut off from the world. Her daughter was in summer school. Her best friend was in love with a man Alex wanted nothing more than to destroy – a man she's actively attempted to hospitalize. Her prized pupil had twice attempted to murder her girlfriend, and, as punishment, Pierce locked her in a cargo container with minimal supplies for nearly a week. Her only remaining allies were the Black Ops (whom she had almost nothing in common with), her new student Donnie Bell (whom she couldn't trust as far as she could throw), and Lane Stevens (who... was Lane Stevens). So it was that Alexandra Pierce ended up alone in Seattle's Best Coffee Shop, with a dog-eared copy of Susanna Clarke's Jonathan Strange and Mr. Norrell in her lap, hoping the dense text would distract her from the temptation to make a call that could simply not go well. It was a war she'd been fighting for a week, but the closest she'd come to breaking down was a brief text message sent the day before. ''Back in town. Sorry I haven't been around. We need to talk about the match.'' Alex felt badly lying about her ''out-of-town'' trip, but it was easier than admitting the (rather pathetic) truth – that she'd spent the last week ordering in and watching Spooks on DVD, silently willing the phone to ring or a knock at her door. For the first time in some fifteen years, Alexandra Pierce was lonely. The mornings were the worst. When she moved to Oakland, Alex upgraded from her simple queen-sized bed to a California King, since Amy was a notoriously restless sleeper and the extra bit of space made them both more comfortable. But Alex herself hardly moved when she slept, and without Campbell beside her, the bed was a huge empty space. The Spider coped the only way she knew how; if Alexandra Pierce was miserable, she determined she'd simply stop being Alexandra Pierce anymore. The last few days saw a succession of different women emerge from the Shadow Woods condo, created out of whole cloth in a vain attempt to forget herself. She'd been a mother (complete with a baby carriage) and a substitute teacher, and, today, she got a job as a cashier at the local Safeway. Becca Livingstone was a clueless, sort of bumbling girl with a lisp, but she was perfect, as the store gave her busy work to keep her occupied. She'd spent the morning stacking cans of green beans, humming softly to herself, and very nearly forgot the mélange of awkward nervousness and looming sadness. Until, that is, Darcy Markson wandered down her aisle clutching a black basket. He raised his voice. ''Miss?'' he asked. ''Where are the replacement mop heads for the Swiffer Sweeper?'' Alex froze, staring dumbly. A can spilled from her fingers, clattering on the floor and rolling out of easy reach. Dimly, she saw a box sticking out of his basket, and though she could just read the first four letters of the brand name, ''T-R-O-J'' opened up a new pit of hell for her. Tears welled in her eyes, all her fears crashing down on top of her head. Briefly, she entertained the thought that Markson was cheating. That he had some other missus on the side, someone easier for him to deal with. But that might not have been the case, and she couldn't exactly ask the question. So she ran, throwing her store apron at her manager as she darted out the door. It was safe to say Becca wouldn't be welcome any longer, after barely a day. But Alex didn't care – she was on autopilot, ending up at this café without even changing, trying to lose herself in the novel and mostly failing. ''I should just call her,'' she whispered. ''The longer I wait, the harder it will—'' ''It is you!'' Alex hadn't heard the man's approach, and cursed herself a fool – there were still plenty of people that would love a distracted Spider, people who'd leap at the chance to take a potshot at her and wouldn't care about whatever circumstance led to that opportunity. People like the thin scarecrow of a man circling her table to perch in the chair opposite the Director. Scratch hadn't seemingly aged at all since their encounter all those years ago, still with a pea-shaped head and tiny spectacles to hide his mouse-like eyes, still with the oddly-gleaming tattoo on his right hand. Pierce shifted, hand falling to her shoulder satchel/laptop case. She never left her condo unarmed, even in her distracted state, and if it came down to it, she'd defend herself. He clucked his tongue. ''Alexis, if I'd wanted you dead, you would be dead, believe me. Years ago, not a messy splatter on a bookstore carpet. At least grant me a modicum of the respect I am owed.'' Alex dropped her hands into her lap, affecting the calm, measured façade she'd worn as a shield for well over a decade. ''What do you want?'' ''Would you believe I happened into the same café you were reading at?'' He smirked at the flat stare she gave him in response. ''Ah, I didn't think so. I came to see you, naturally.'' ''Why.'' It wasn't quite a question. ''To ask you the same thing you never answered long ago.'' Three: Questions Without Answers Then. Gauging from the hum of an old water heater and the dripping coming from a leaky pipe, Alex figured she'd awoken in someone's basement. She barely remembered actually being rendered unconscious – just a flash of foul-smelling cloth pressed to her nose and mouth. She awoke... well, she wasn't sure how long ago it had been, but it was long enough she'd lost count of her heartbeat. Pierce was bound hand and foot to a metal chair, blindfolded and gagged with a damp cloth; they'd changed the washcloth gag twice since she came to, each time squeezing a new torrent of viscous, rancid water into her mouth. She wanted to spit it up, but the cloth prevented that, and she had no other choice buy to swallow – and cause her stomach to revolt. Even the gag-change wasn't done with any kind of consistency, and she was never given chance to get her bearings. The worst part was that none of them spoke or even acknowledged her presence. Alex tried every trick Margaret Winters had ever taught her, from insults to tears to wild sexual promises, and she got no response – not even a change to the regular sound of her breathing. The closest they'd come was when Alex screamed her throat raw, and the extent of that notice was just a hard yank on her gag when they retied it. So when the cloth was peeled off her eyes and out of her mouth, Alex winced from the sudden change. The guards, two men built like kites, unbound her wrists as well, and they set a small, flowered Dixie cup on a card table erected beside her. Pierce drank its contents down greedily, a part of her aware that it could have been almost anything – and not sure she cared. ''Why are you here?'' The question came out of the blue; Alexandra hadn't even seen the balding man across the table from her until he spoke, his voice a ragged sigh. ''I'm so—'' Pierce swallowed, her own voice hoarse. ''I'm sorry?'' ''It isn't really a difficult question, Alexandra.'' His slight smile only danced out for a moment before retracting, like a striking snake. ''Why are you here?'' ''I am here because this is where you brought me.'' She tried not to notice that the man knew her name. ''That is why you are in this building. But why are you here?'' ''I do not understand.'' ''You will.'' Again, that tight smile crinkled his lips. ''Every end also has a beginning. I never understood – could never have imagined, really – that this would be yours. Somehow, I pictured you springing forth fully formed from Maggie's forehead like a modern-day Athena.'' ''Do... do we know each other?'' Alex squinted, her eyebrows knitting together. He didn't seem familiar. ''After a fashion, Miss Pierce. I think I know just about everyone. I'm just not sure if they know me.'' Alex closed her eyes, blowing out a sigh. ''I'm not really in the mood to be taunted. Why don't you come back when you're ready to have a proper discussion?'' ''You want me to stay, Alexandra.'' One eye popped open. ''I rather doubt that you know what I want.'' ''Would you believe me if I said it was written on your face, in the pulls and twitches of your cheeks, the narrowing of your eyes. The tilt of your head. You are intrigued, I think. Considering.'' ''Or I'm having a stroke.'' Scratch finally let his smile go, and it was not a pleasant thing. ''But you haven't answered my question. ''Why are you here?'' ''Because there is nowhere else I should be.'' Scratch clapped his hands, fairly leaping to his feet. ''By George, I think she's catching on.'' Four: Answers Without Questions Now. ''You can't be here,'' Alex said. She couldn't look up – no, she wouldn't look up. ''Not now.'' ''Where should I be, dear? It seems you've made Oakland into your very own playground, a hub for connecting flights for your girls.'' ''Tch.'' Alex scoffed. ''They refuse to let me be happy.'' ''Or maybe they refuse to let you lose yourself in the illusions you surround yourself with. You know, for someone who plays with lies as much as you do, you've got quite a few wrapped up in your head. Wish I could help with that.'' Alex carefully closed the novel, a bookmark holding her place. ''You can. You can leave me to it – it's the same request I made of Lauren and Kathryn.'' ''And they listened so well.'' Scratch mimed a yawn. ''Whatever happened to Alexandra Pierce? You used to be someone, you coulda been a contender.'' ''I do not want to be a 'contender'. I never have. All I want is my own place in the world. Everyone else gets a chance to be happy. Why don't I get a chance of my own?'' ''People like you and I... we aren't meant to be happy, Al. You gave that up, remember?'' ''I was twenty years old, Scratch.'' The thin man raised his hands, slipping up to his feet. ''Hey, now.'' He pushed his chair with a gentle shove. ''How about I go? Don't want to stay where I'm not wanted.'' ''I think you better had.'' He grinned, again the smaller one, just a flicker on dry lips. ''Just remember, Piercey. You've gone and made a lot of enemies in these last few weeks, a band of folks who'd like to see you torn down in your little 'wrestling' game, and more yet who'd like to see worse done outside of it.'' ''It's not a position I'm unaccustomed to.'' For the first time, Alex met the man's stormy gaze. ''Or a price I'm unwilling to pay.'' ''All right, all right. I get it. You're a girl in love.'' Scratch retreated into the magazine racks. ''But... seriously. Whatever happened to Alexandra Pierce?'' Five: The Cavalry Then. ''You're still not making sense.'' ''Do you think Margaret Winters recruits every waif who picks her pocket?'' ''Do you think she doesn't?'' Pierce folded her arms over her chest. ''How many have tried?'' ''Well... you've got me there. But you'd like it to be more. You'd like to be something, to be special. Like Andrew and Raymond are special.'' ''You know an awful lot for an assassin in a bar.'' Scratch's shoulders quirked in a shrug that was more of a shudder. ''Maybe it's my job to know things. Sure would help in my job with the killing people if I knew who'd come to their rescue, wouldn't I?'' ''Maybe I do want more.'' Alex's admission was reluctant. ''Is that so wrong?'' ''Don't want to do the grunt work all the time. Like picking up dudes in a bullshit Goth nightclub.'' ''Don't remind—'' It was like she suddenly remembered where she was – and who he was. ''We're not having this conversation.'' ''Really? Because I've been sitting here for a little bit, and I'm pretty sure we've been talking for a little while now.'' Pierce turned to the left, narrowing blazing gray eyes on the man across the card table. ''You aren't going to get any secrets from me.'' ''Now, Alexandra, after all our talk, do you really think it's secrets of your little Order of Hypocrites I want?'' ''What do you want?'' Only half of Scratch's smile slipped free of his iron grip. ''Funny, I was about to—'' An explosion resounded overhead, dust shaking loose from the rafters. Alex's well-trained reflexes snapped her eyes up to the ceiling. ''What the—'' ''That would be your friend Mr. Stryker, I believe.'' Scratch's unholy smile wouldn't stay in place. ''He brought a grenade launcher to suburbia – can you believe it? Welp...'' the man stood, brushing white dust off his slacks. ''I guess the cavalry is here to see you off. Good chat, good chat. We should have another someday.'' ''I do not think you'll leave this...'' She blinked, her eyes dilated ''This room. You did something to me. Drugged me.'' ''Of course I did. You knew it when you drank it.'' Scratch tipped an invisible cap. ''As for me escaping... well, why do you think you're here?'' The last time he asked it, Alex was pretty sure Scratch was being rhetorical. Six: 415 In Progress Now. ''Hey.'' She vaguely heard the sound of fingers snapping, somewhere in the back of her head. ''Hey, lady!'' Alexandra Pierce's eyes snapped open as she shot out of her slouch as she came to grips with her environs. ''You're still at the coffee house. Don't worry your pretty little head, Teach.'' ''Donnie?'' Her newest student's cheeky grin locked onto his lips – well, it rarely ever let go, really. ''In the ever-loving flesh.'' ''What are you doing for? How long have I been unconscious?'' ''Barkeep – barista, whatever. Lady behind the counter says it's been an hour or so. They didn't want to wake you – apparently, you're kind of scary. Who knew?'' ''How did you know where I was?'' Her voice was still thick with sleep. ''That hawk-nosed bloke. The one with the cane? He apparently has some kind of chip in your arm or something, 'cuz I asked him as soon as I, well... soon as I needed to find you and he punched in a code in his little... computer thingy. And boom, there you were.'' ''There's a tracker on my car.'' She rubbed her eyes. ''Why?'' ''I fink it's probably so they can keep track of where you are, Teach. I mean, you're kind of an important person in these people's lives.'' ''No.'' She met his gaze without fear, nothing like her conversation with Scratch. ''You said you needed me. I doubt it was sexual—'' ''Well, it could've been. I could be here, pining away for—'' ''And you're rarely early for your lesson. So what has happened.'' ''Well, it was just a little thing when I went over there. It's a little more important, because the big guy, he apparently... he likes to listen to the police radio.'' Alex nodded slightly. ''It helps him to relax. I know this.'' ''Appears there's been some kinda fracas at the Shadow Woods condos. So I'm to hustle you over there to clear things up right away.'' That's all it took for Alex to pop out of the chair. ''What kind of fracas?'' ''A throwdown. A kerfuffle. You know, a big old fight. Two redheads.'' He paused. ''How many violent redheads you guys got going on around here?'' Alex was already moving to the door. ''One more than I need. You're driving.'' ''Right. Because you're sleepy.'' Alex held open the door. ''What was the other thing?'' ''That?'' Bell shrugged his shoulders, stepping past Alexandra and hitting the keyless entry button for his car. ''It's nothing. Just a question about all these weird names you all use for cons. Is there a cheatbook?'' ''You should know I don't put things on paper. Now, drive.'' Donnie nodded, sliding into the driver's seat and turning the ignition at the same time. He hoped the envelope in his back pocket didn't crinkle too loudly. He'd ask about the letter some other time. It is when They find the Third that—'' (Sentence is cut off; no copy exists with it completed.) -- The Diary of Leonid, p. 19 (pages 20 and 21 are missing)
