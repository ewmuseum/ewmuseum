Kaimaku ('The Curtain Rises')

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Before Sin*Con Professional wrestling's about showmanship, and we wrestlers put on a show to entertain crowds. You hear that music going, and your blood pumps through your veins. You anticipate that sound. You anticipate the pyrotechnics, the strobe lights, the mists of the fog machines, all of that leads up to the performance. The ring is a different world from the area surrounding it. It's such an open space, yet it's so confined. The air feels stagnant, yet soothing. There's thousands of people in the building, all cheering or booing you for everything you do or don't do in a match. For the longest time, I always believed the match and building up to it was the most important part of my job. I was a diligent student in this sport, to the point that I considered this to be my purest artform. People think I'm one of the best in the continent, actually. I try not to let that get to my head. Anyway, for however good I was in the ring, there was always something that held me back. I was never quite certain what it was. I clearly had the talent for this profession, but I was missing something important. Something meaningful behind what I was doing that would reinforce my exploits inside the squared circle. And somehow, I don't think learning how to dance was it. Yeah, for some reason, I was asked if I knew how to dance a few days before Sin*Con. When I answered in the negative, of course they had to send the best dance consultant on the Sin City Championship Wrestling roster to teach me dance moves. Naturally, when I heard the name 'Rick Malloy' escape the lips of the person on the other end of that wonderful phone call I had, I quickly put the kibosh on the whole idea. I already cut a promo in rhyme in this company, what more did these guys want from me? Well, anyway, they didn't exactly give up on me with the idea. After that phone call, I got a second one about two hours later. Apparently, they found someone else to take the role. They conveniently neglected to tell me who exactly it was until he showed up at my hotel room's door. So, when I saw that idiotic half-smile on the face of Jared Sykes after I opened my door, the level of panic I felt reached critical mass. You could understand why I promptly shut the door in his face and hid in a distant corner for a good five minutes. Okay, I didn't actually do that, but after today, I really wished I had done that just to see if Jared would give up and go away. Not that he would, of course. 'Okay, we've got a lot of work ahead of us and no time to do it, so let's crackin’, Cory!' Jared said as soon as he stepped into my room. I was about to close the door back behind him, when another man, an African-American, that I'd never seen before simply brushed his way inside, as if the door was a mere annoyance rather than a physical obstacle that I so desperately wanted to put between myself and anything strange and unusual. I turned to catch a glimpse of the man, and I swore for a moment that my entire world was an afro. The man who walked in had this big, almost perfectly symmetrical afro on his head. I swore that even if you could drop him on his head, his afro could cushion the blow. He turned to me, his sunglasses blocking his eyes, and he seemed to examine me for a few seconds as I closed the door and faced the two intruders in what I wanted to be another peaceful day. I didn't recognize the other man and I was so thrown off by his appearance and his manner that I was forced to pay attention to Jared, if only so I didn't have to pay attention to *him*. Either way, I heard the afro man speak, 'Yo, Jared, is this the guy you referred to as 'an uncoordinated mass of humanity unfit for the medium of dance'?' Okay, now I *had* to pay attention to Jared, if only because he deserved a particularly dirty look. I guess it had some kind of effect on Jared, because I saw him nervously adjust the collar of his T-shirt. I'm guessing that like most things he did in life, he didn't exactly think his cunning plan all the way through. Perhaps it's his good fortune, then, that the man in the afro was just as socially awkward as Jared was. It wasn't the way he spoke, but rather, it was the way he conducted himself. He didn't so much stand there as he did sway from left to right, as if he was hearing a rhythm that nobody else could. It boggled my mind, and that was before he said anything else. 'Name's Charley Crisp.' he told me. He held out his hand for a high five that would never come. It took about ten seconds and one of the most awkward silences I'd ever experienced for him to take the hint, and he frowned when he took it. Somewhat sullenly and downcast, Crisp turned to his cohort, 'I can see why you needed me for this one.' 'Yeah, he's, like, the opposite of hip. I guess that would be unhip? Maybe anti-hip?' Jared said. He looked at me as though he was actually disappointed in me. How did I become the bad guy in this? 'Anyway, it's something that needs to be fixed for the sake of his future here.' 'So, wait, my future is dependent on whether or not I know how to frickin' dance?' I asked. Saying that outloud only served to reinforce exactly how ridiculous it was. Well, okay, it sounded ridiculous to me. With these two brain surgeons in front of me, though, I think the point of my comment was lost. Especially when they both simultaneously responded to me, 'Yes.' Dammit! 'So, let's get this started!' Crisp said with an excited tone. He stepped to a spot in the room where my luggage sat and very unceremoniously booted it several yards out of the way and into the wall, where it made an obnoxiously noticeable 'thunk' sound. It annoyed me. 'Hey!' I shouted. It's one thing to barge into a guy's hotel room and demand that he learn to dance. It's another thing entirely to kick their belongings into a wall, 'Don't do that!' I don't think Charley really cared, or at the very least, he seemed to just want to do what he came here to do... to hell with me. Maybe he was just bitter about the high five thing, though. Either way, he gestured at me to come forth and show my moves. Now, being a professional wrestler meant already mastering a kind of a coordinated dance. I wouldn't say that I could appear on 'So You Think You Can Dance' and strut myself, but I did have a good idea of what my body can do. Somehow, though, I don't think showing off my moonwalk was a good idea. Mostly because of the laughter I got from it. 'Man, Jared, you said he was awful, but this...' Charley said, in between his laughter. He didn't actually finish his statement before he just resumed his laughter. You know, I could take being made fun of. Really, I'm not that thin-skinned. What I couldn't take was these two barging into my hotel room and just spending more of their time insulting me than actually helping me. I turned around and started to leave, but Jared stopped me. 'No no no, don't leave.' he insisted, as he took hold of my shoulder in order to keep me from leaving. Against my better judgment, I did not shrug off Jared and keep walking. Instead, I turned around and allowed myself to hear him out, 'We're just trying to help you out. Add a little spring to your step, you know? More bounce to the ounce. More rhythm to the… What the hell rhymes with rhythm?" He turned to Charley for reassurance, but Charley just shrugged. "Now, people have described my own dancing as being octopus-like...' ...Was that supposed to be self-deprecating, or did he really consider that to be a compliment? '...So, naturally, I brought in a ringer, which in this case means ‘a black guy’. This man here, he’s better than I could ever be. Taught me everything I know.' he finished. Well, at least he's self-aware of himself. I sighed to myself. 'What do you want from me, Jared? You know how much I don't like this ridiculous, humiliating gimmick.' I said. I will admit that this whole 'Baron von Blackberry' thing had been good for me in a small way... the fan reactions I got had been much stronger than even my professional peak as a top national star. When I stood toe-to-toe with Jeff Garvin despite probably being overmatched at that stage of my career, people stood and cheered. When I said something dumb as the Baron, people stood and cheered louder. I've been cheered louder for doing a stupid punch that I stole from a video game character than I'd ever been cheered for hitting a Ratings Spike. Somehow, I think I'm stuck with this Blackberry thing for a while. 'I just want you to follow along with Charley, okay? He knows what he's doing.' Jared said. I found that increasingly difficult to believe. I sighed, threw my hands into the air, and gave up getting out of this with my dignity intact. If they wanted me to dance at Sin*Con, then by God, I'm going to be the best dancer there ever was. 'Alright, man,' Charley said, as he stepped in front of me with this wide smile, 'For this one... it's just a jump to the left, alright?' This was going to be a long day.
