The Mirror and The Window 5: family dinner

14 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/14/07 5.1 the mirror: leaving unannounced You have to admit, moving away was the best thing you could’ve done. For the first few weeks it was hard living with Katie. She did different things at different times, and it was difficult to get used to having someone else to share the room and all of the other mundane duties that go hand in hand with daily life. But after that happened, and everything calmed down, you’ve discovered that it’s actually really simple and enjoyable living with your girlfriend. What isn’t simple, though, is dealing with your family. You aren’t particularly proud of the way you left New Orleans. Leaving without telling anyone good-bye is something that can stay in someone’s memory for a long time. Hell, the Baltimore Colts left for Indianapolis unannounced in the 1980s and the media still torches them for it. And your family is light years worse than any media, you found out when you tried to make a routine family visit. 5.2 the window: prodigal son "Mom, the prodigal son has returned," says Brandon Rhine sourly, giving his brother one icy glance before leaving the door open and allowing FUSE’s Custom C.H.A.O.S. champion to enter the house. Jonathan nods, accepting the reaction, then walks into the kitchen to find his mother. "I’m surprised you came," she says dryly, mixing vegetables in a pot for the weekly big family dinner. In Jonathan’s family there was never a set schedule with all of the different times, but every Tuesday the Rhines set aside anything they have currently happening and have a big dinner with all of the family members. It used to be a time of great happiness, but it looks like all of the happiness packed up and moved when Jonathan did. "I love Tuesday dinner," Jonathan says uneasily, looking around at the house that looks the same yet has changed tremendously since he has left. Trying desperately to break the awkwardness that has pervaded the family, he asks, "How is everything going?" "Fine," his mother says, looking up at Jonathan with a very emotionless face, one that almost makes his step back in surprise. "We’re all fine, we’re doing just great, and we will continue to do fine. Now if you excuse me, dear, mom needs some personal time with the cooking. Run off and go see the brother and sister who’ve missed you so much." 5.3 the mirror: balancing act It kills you to see your mom act this way, but you understand that whatever feelings you’re having, she’s feeling ten times worse. Your mom has turned her thirty-two years of marriage and her twenty-seven years of motherhood into a circus-style balancing act. For longer than you can remember she has acted out the conflicting interests of all of the parties close to her so she didn’t get in trouble with either of them. Any time there was a problem between you and your dad, your mom played the peacemaker who wouldn’t take either side, and any time she had to take a side she was heartbroken. When you were twelve, you had a fight with your dad about quitting the football team. You were tired of getting roughed up, as you hadn’t hit your growth spurt yet and were the smallest member of the team. Your father yelled, screamed, and hit you to express his anger. When you went to your mom to complain about it, you saw that he had gotten to her first. Because she looked at you with an expressionless face, and she told you that you would do best to do what your father said. Later she told you how much it broke her heart to tell you that, to firmly take a side in an issue that she knew would hurt you. But she felt that, after all that balancing, she had to stand up for something. She had that same face then as she does now, and you know that it breaks her heart to freeze you out like that. But it still doesn’t mean you’re okay with it. 5.4 the window: not family Brandon and Jessica Rhine are talking in whispered voices when Jonathan enters the room, remaining in the door frame and soaking up the cold stares. "Listen, guys…" is all Jonathan can get out before Brandon begins talking. "No, you listen. I know you’re going to say you don’t like getting these looks, or having mom treat you badly, or whatever else you want to bitch about, but frankly I don’t care. Because you deserved it. As soon as you packed up and moved without telling anyone you were leaving, you wrote a check in the amount of ‘infinite hatred from the family you should have supported.’" Brandon looks at Jessica, who at 17 isn’t nearly as strong as her brother or mother in this issue. "I miss you, Jonathan. We all do," she says, looking down at her feet. Brandon looks at her and sighs, shaking his head. "Maybe we do, but it’s only because you tucked tail and ran. You’re angry now, probably thinking that you’ve come back to us here to spend time with us and all we’re doing is yelling at you. Thing is, you made this choice. We are a family. No member of this family has ever left this state in three or four generations. We are born in Louisiana, we raise a family here, and we die here. Because family is important. Family is togetherness. And right now you, and that slut you moved in with…" A tear drops down Brandon’s eye as he concludes his speech, "…aren’t family." 5.5 the mirror: Louisiana You honestly can’t believe what he’s saying. And it’s not even the comment about you not being family. It’s not calling your girlfriend a slut either. It’s the fact that he thinks living in Louisiana has been your family’s choice. Every generation has stayed in this state, as your brother is proudly saying, because they didn’t have the means or the opportunity to escape it. You can’t wrap your head around the fact that he genuinely believes that Louisiana is a great state to live in. It is at this moment that you realize everything at once, and it hits you with a force that almost sends you from the room. You were their means and opportunity, and they’re mad because you didn’t bring them along. It all makes sense now. All of the training, all of the pushing you to be everything you wanted to be, was all just because they were tired of living in the trap of a city and they wanted a way out. And now that you have made strides in something and have done well enough to afford to get out of this place, they wanted to come with you, and the fact you only brought your girlfriend makes them feel abandoned and lost. And they’re dealing with it by giving you cold stares and snappy comments? Well fuck that. You exit the room with the determination and speed you use when running down to the ring. You’ve got to find your father. 5.6 the mirror: pet project "You wanted me to do well in wrestling so I could buy a home for you in some other state, didn’t you?" Jonathan’s eyes narrow as he finds Greg Rhine, who was also whispering something to Jonathan’s mother. Greg looks surprised as he backs away. "What are you talking ab…" "That was how it was supposed to happen, right? You push your son into doing something that you couldn’t do when you were my age, so you can get out of this city and stop your harrowing 9-to-5 and I could bring the family to a better place because you couldn’t do it for us?" Jonathan ignores his mother’s pleas for him to stop, because he’s angry and on a roll. Greg Rhine stands as tall as he can and gets in his son’s face, a snarl forming around his lips. "Don’t you dare talk to me like that. You can’t…" "Tell me it’s not true then. Tell me you didn’t want me to buy you guys a house and move you near us in Indianapolis. Tell me that, and I’ll apologize and leave." Greg looks uneasy now, and he looks to Sylvia before nodding. "Well, isn’t that how it’s supposed to work? We raise you, give you everything, and you reward us by bringing us along for the ride." "No, that’s not how it works! You don’t sit here feeding something to me for years pretending to foster my interest in something only because you weren’t good enough at it yourself. You don’t raise a child without letting your intentions known, and you definitely don’t turn the entire family against him when he’s trying to finally get out on his own." Jonathan sees the color drain from his father’s face, but he doesn’t stop or slow down. "You act like I’m some horrible son who doesn’t care about his family at all but you’re really only mad because you feel like I’m a pet project that didn’t turn out exactly as you had hoped." Greg is speechless, and Sylvia Rhine begins crying. She catches Jonathan’s eyes, and he gives her an expressionless face in response. "I’m leaving." As he leaves, Greg finally regains his speech, shouting after, "Fine! Leave your family! Choose your girlfriend over us! The ones who raised you, who loved you. I hope you’re happy." 5.7 the mirror: half true To be honest, you actually are. Katie had told you that your family had a bad agenda, but you never wanted to believe it. Knowing it for a fact now is very freeing, and you can finally smile as you make your way to the car. You hated giving your mom that look, and you can’t help but feel as if you burned your bridges when it comes to the only people required to love you. But right now you feel like they never did. You have been a good man in your young life, and you had always believed that it was a result of your parents raising you to be like that. But when you see your brother, you know that’s not exactly true. The truth is you are a good man, and that is why your parents tried to use you as a pole vault to success, because they knew you would let them. And now that you’re not, you’re the family enemy. But it’s something you can deal with. Your father’s words ring in your head as you drive the rental car to the airport, ready to get back to the only thing in your life right now that makes any sense. How he thinks you’re abandoning the family and choosing Katie over them. It’s only half true. It’s true that right now you are choosing to go back home to someone who makes you feel good rather than stay at a stuffy dinner with people who make you feel guilty. But you aren’t abandoning them, you convince yourself. You’re abandoning the lies, the pressure, and all of the other things that came with being your dad’s project. And now that you know it was true, you have no problems walking away. Maybe at another time you can mend fences, but for now you’re walking away, knowing exactly what you’re doing. And yes, that does make you happy.
