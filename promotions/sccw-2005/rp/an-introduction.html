Date: Today A hotel room has seen it all. Sometimes they hold the tale of happiness, of families venturing to a new location for the first time. Sometimes they have the tale of sadness told in them, an abused wife staying just to get away from her husband. Tales of ecstasy, tales of boredom, each room always had quite a few stories they could tell. This specific room, with the soft light emanating from the corner would tell the tale tonight of indecision and alcoholism. The glass bottle was silent on the table, inside of it sat the amber liquid Native Americans had said was "fire water." Whiskey wasn't a liquid for many social situations. Sure Jim Beam came to the party, but he was usually hanging out with the guy trying to fight everyone there, or the guy hanging over the toilet vomiting his innards into a porcelain bowl. Luckily for Clayton Byrd this wasn't a social situation, and he knew better than most that Jim Beam was nobody's friend. Clay Byrd, a long shot to be here by any means. It had been a few days since he first stepped foot in the Sin City backstage, the sight didn't sit well with the big Texan. He rubbed his hand over his mustache letting the liquor go from his hands to his pants before licking his lips to make sure it was clean. Working in independent wrestling promotions, Clayton Byrd had seen his fair share of problems. He'd walked into places that had a bunch of maniacs running around, but he never had to stick around long. Work the show and get out, that had always been his motto. No sense in trying to build a following in New York City, those city punks wouldn't like a man like Clay anyway. He never had to deal with the problems for long, he'd show up, try to right the wrongs and walk away whether the job was done or not. A vast majority of the time, the job was done. This place though, there was no walking away when the situation got hard. He'd signed on the dotted line for the third time in his life, neither time ended well, and he imagined this wouldn't either. It never ended well for a man like Clayton Byrd. Sure, walking the independent wrestling scene since 1990 he had made some acquaintances, some he'd even reckon he'd consider friends. In front of Clayton sat a deck of cards laid out in eight piles, he loved Solitaire. It was something you could do by yourself, you didn't have to gamble having your body hurt, or having your feelings crushed. You could sit in silence, with your best friend the bottle and play away into the early hours of the morning. He rubbed his temple then ran his hand through his greasy brown hair. He hadn't showered today, he didn't know if he would. Going to bed half inebriated, and waking up with your head throbbing would do that to you. His son hadn't called him since after the show, he remembered the conversation pretty clearly still. -- "So Dad, hows the new job going? I saw you punched a guy." Clayton chuckled into the receiver. His son didn't even sound like he was born in Texas anymore, the accent completely washed away from conversations with other high school freshman in upstate New York. "I reckon it's goin, not exactly what I figured to be gettin into goin on forty, but life's a cunt what can ya do? Enough about me, how are you 'n your Mom doin?" Clayton responded, his drawl especially after a few drinks got increasingly more prominent. "Pretty good, Mom and Chad are at the store. I'm starting at linebacker on the freshman team, and I have a date for homecoming. So yeah, it's going pretty good," Jake responded. Clay would quit everything to hear that voice everyday. He only saw him a few times a year on the circuit, especially considering his mother was the epitome of evil ex-wife. Sharon and Clay weren't exactly a co-operative couple when it came to parenting. "Nah footballs all well and good, but is she a good look'n girl?" He heard Jake snicker and it made him smile, that laugh melted him. He missed his boy, oh how he missed his boy. "She's not the greatest Dad, but she's alright," Jake loved his father. He wished his mother would let him go out on the road with him during the summer. He loved the atmosphere, the big Texan going out and locking up with random bad guys in high school gymnasiums. Plus his Dad always let him have a beer with the guys afterward. "How're her tits?"Jake busted up laughing immediately as Clay began howling himself. The laugh of a child was infectious, especially when its your own child's. "You're a pervert," he said, still laughing. Clay chuckled a bit as Jake finished. "Anyway, Mom and Chad are in the driveway, I should probably get off of here." "Prolly so, she'd be purdy pissed if she heard us talkin' bout teenage gals tits and all. See ya later, Love ya." "Love you too" -- That last part was always the most important to Clay. The reassurance that his son loved him more than that asshole accountant Chad Holisting. He had never done anything to hurt Jake, but he had married his ex-wife. Which meant guilt by association. Signing on the dotted line the second time in his life was probably the worst decision of his life, marrying Sharon when they were twenty wasn't really an intelligent decision. Clay had dreams, Sharon had goals. Clay liked playing it all by ear, Sharon liked yelling in his ear. Clay liked liked counting the stars, Sharon liked counting dollars. The two couldn't of been more different, but in 1989 it seemed like a good idea. Clay was in his second year of college at Texas, and so was Sharon. He played defensive end for the Longhorns, and Sharon had always loved dating a star. From high school to college, she followed Clay like a puppy. He had a plan, he was going to be a big time football player. Unlike most high school kids with dreams of the NFL, Clay actually had the athletic ability and the drive to do it until spring practice going into his junior season. Occasionally his knee still popped a bit after a rough beating, signing on that dotted line didn't work well for him either. Blowing out your knee in spring practice meant you were cut so that scholarship could go somewhere else. Sharon pushed Clay to try to get back on the team, instead he dropped out of school and started training to be a professional wrestler. Everyone had always told him that he and Stan Hansen looked a lot alike, Sharon figured he'd do alright. Clay finally went out working the independents in year four of their marriage, by year five Sharon had hooked up with the local accountant and left with Clay's son to New York while he was away at a show. He figured it was because Sharon assumed he'd of killed Chad, which was true, he'd still love to knock a few teeth out of that city boy's pretty mouth. Clay picked up the glass bottle and swirled the amber liquid before pressing it to his lips, indulging himself with a little more of the devils poison as his father always called it. Thinking about his life always made him want another drink. He imagined it was the same reason that drove his father to the drink. Why sign on the dotted line after all these years? Even now it bugged him, signing that dotted line put a person like Clay knee deep in a big steaming pile of shit. Part of him wanted to just stand up and skip town, another part of him wanted to go out there and show these big bad villains exactly what big Texas could do. Thinking + large amounts of alcohol = Bad choices It was an age old problem, people thinking when they had poison pumping through their system. From the greatest heroes, to the greatest villains, many of them had done it in their time. From casual sex leading to pregnancy because you were to stupid drunk to wear a condom, to coming up with the bright idea to bomb Iraq into a dust bowl. Clay imagined most of these ideas were thought up in alcohol induced hazes like the one he was currently experiencing, that's why he had to see it for himself. The big man unfolded himself from the small hotel chair and walked over to the bed and picked up his black cowboy hat. Sliding it on his head, he brushed his hands down his black t-shirt straightening it up a bit before throwing on his cowboy boots. He slowly made his way to the door, between his right foot being asleep and the alcohol he figured it was better to take it slow. -- Arenas are awkward buildings, when all the people are in the building, lights shining they can get pretty warm. But once all that body heat has time to leave they go back to their usual chill state, and in a state as far north as New Hampshire the arena bordered in the fifty degree range. Compared to Texas at this time of year, this was like being in the north pole. Clay shivered as he sat on the steel entranceway, legs extended watching the stage hands rip down the Temptation set around him. Wrestling shows were much like circuses, they just didn't bring their own tents. Everything would be boxed up before the end of the night and shipped off to the next location. Clay would hop the next flight, train, or bus to the next location. He liked traveling alone like most other things in his life. The people working around him were comforting though, just regular guys who all had simple enough jobs to make a simple living. "I nevah liked it simple," Clay mumbled to himself. He hadn't even walked down this ramp but he already felt a unique closeness to it. The cold steel felt remarkably good against his exposed arms as he laid down. Elevating levels of drunken stupors could cause a severe warming feeling, and Clay surely knew that as the back of his head hit with a thud on the ramp. The big Texan could feel the despair, the anxiety here still. This ramp had watched it all happen, they say objects and things don't remember anything. The arena in Ottawa will never forget Rhine, and neither would this ramp. To the casual on looker it would have been presumed that something immensely great had happened that night on Temptation. That the battle against all things evil had finally ended, which in a way it did. He tilted his hat over his eyes, sometimes in the battles against good and evil the players changed. Jonathan Rhine was a hero from another age, one where you didn't have to stoop down to evils backstabbing ways. Now though, Clay knew things were a bit different. This evil in 'the city' as the doctor called it, was something you couldn't just come out and try to punch in the face. You have to walk on the edge, being careful not to fall off into the pit of blackness yourself. It takes a special kind of man to do that. Would Clay Byrd be that man?
