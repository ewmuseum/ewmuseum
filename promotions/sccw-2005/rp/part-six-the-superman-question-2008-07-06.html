Part Six: The Superman Question

6 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/6/08 Why do we continue to believe? We are greeted with evidence each day that should turn our beliefs into ash. The headlines of newspapers trumpet stories of corporate malfeasance. Tales of man’s inhumanity to man blare out from our televisions, spun from the mouths of expertly coiffed, brightly smiling news anchors. Athletes resort to performance enhancing drugs or break the bonds of marriage. Pundits call for a return to the "gold old days", conveniently forgetting the racism, sexism, xenophobia and casual hatred that made sure that the good old days, in the words of Mister William Joel, "weren’t always good". Such is simply how the world is, some sigh. Man is a base, vile and degraded creature and will always be so. Life is but a minefield, full of traps to be carefully avoided. These are not the ones to be feared. Pitied, perhaps, or regarded with a sigh and a shake of the head. But not feared. There are others who would argue that man’s darker side is not something to be avoided or guarded against. That, instead, it is something to be cherished, even harnessed. For it is only by embracing the venal, baser, darker side of our nature that anything can be accomplished. You can get what you want, they argue, but only if you step on everyone in front of you to get it. In the face of all of this…in the face of those who would argue that the very concept no longer has any real meaning… …why do we still believe…in heroes? The actual act of throwing up had not taken Alanna Marshall very long at all. Just a few quick heaves and most of that day’s lunch had come spilling into the toilet bowl. That had been five minutes ago. She now sat on the floor next to the toilet, the sound of the flushing water echoing in her ears. She raised herself up as quickly as her shaking legs would allow, placing her right hand against the bathroom counter to steady herself. A quick flick of the faucet (her shaking hand making it take a few seconds longer than normal), a quick squirt of toothpaste onto the toothbrush, a quick run of the toothbrush under the stream of water…and Alanna began to vigorously brush her teeth, desperately trying to get rid of the taste of vomit that still lingered in her mouth. The image that had brought her to this place…well, that wouldn’t be so easy to get rid of. She had tried contacting the home office of PRIME for a tape or DVD of the show containing…most folks were now just referring to it as "the incident"…but only managed to get as far as the secretary at the main office. Said secretary had been…well, "incredibly rude" was currently the winning description (although "raging cunt bag" was a very close second), snottily informing Mrs. Marshall that as she was not a current employee of PRIME, she had both no reason and no business asking for any record of the show. The secretary then proceeded to hang up on Alanna in order to further emphasize her point. Alanna had slammed the receiver down in response, muttering a few choice curse words under her breath. Her next step had been to turn on the computer located in the Marshall’s den. Ten minutes later, she had the PRIME show in question downloading while she took care of a large pile of mail that had managed to accumulate over the past few days. She had continued to take care of the mail as the show played on the monitor, sparing it the occasional glance just to keep track of how far into the show she had watched. No offense to the roster of PRIME, she thought…but they weren’t why she was watching the show. It was, now that Alanna thought back on it, a bit startling as to the strength of her reaction. It wasn’t as if she hadn’t known what was coming. Rhine’s injury had been the talk of the locker room, after all. And gore wasn’t exactly an issue for a woman who had once marathoned through all four of the SAW films. But watching Katie Malick stumble, watching Xavier Kannon grab her shoe and plunge the heel into Jonathan Rhine’s eyeball… …she had only just managed to make it to the bathroom in time. The noise of the water splashing into the sink nearly masked the knock at the door, so soft was the knock. The voice behind the knock was small and filled with worry. "Mommy?" Alanna quickly turned the water off, grabbing a towel and drying her face. She walked unsteadily over to the bathtub, lowering herself down gingerly until she sat on the edge of the tub. Only then did she call out to her son. "Come on in, baby." The door opened slowly, cautiously. Zach Marshall walked in, dressed in jeans and his favorite Bugs Bunny T-shirt. He walked over to where his mother was sitting, taking a place next to her. He looked up at her with an expression of the most infinite love and concern, causing Alanna to marvel at how even the slightest look from Zach could make her so completely happy. "Are you okay, Mommy?" Alanna mustered up as much of a smile as she could manage. "Yeah, baby, I’m okay. It’s just that…well, I just found out that a friend of mine got hurt really badly. And I feel bad about that." Zach wrapped his arms around his mother, clinging to her as though she was his lifeline to reality. "I’m sorry you feel bad, Mommy," he said, his voice muffled against Alanna’s shirt. "I hope your friend gets better." "Me too, baby. Me too." For a blessed moment, nothing is said: Zach clinging tightly to his mother, Alanna gently stroking her son’s hair. Finally, it is Alanna who breaks the silence: "Hey, I’ve got an idea. Y’know what’ll make us feel better?" Zach breaks the hug and looks up at his mother. "What, mommy?" "Ice cream." Zach smiles, a smile that stretches across his entire face. "Yay!" "Just go and get ready, baby and we’ll leave in a few minutes. Okay?" Zach is already halfway to the door. "Okay, mommy," he replies, as he scrambles quickly out the door and towards his room. Alanna raises herself back up gingerly, checking her appearance in the mirror. The taste of vomit no longer stings in her mouth, the redness in her eyes down to a level that she could attribute to exhaustion. She was by no means certain how long it was going to take before she was okay. But for her son’s sake? For her son’s sake, she could damn well fake it. If you were to ask Lance Marshall what music was currently playing on his iPod, he wouldn’t be able to tell you. It wouldn’t be because of any lack of familiarity with the music. The main reason would be that Lance had simply chosen the folder on his iPod marked "Big and Loud" and then placed the device on random. Lance Marshall wasn’t looking to put all that much thought into his choice of music for this workout. If he was being completely honest, Lance Marshall would admit that he wasn’t really looking to think about anything at all. He had arrived home to a note from Alanna saying that she was taking Zach out for a special treat and would be home in time for dinner. He had placed the note back on the counter with a smile. He had then headed over to the computer to check on his email. It was then that he noticed what had been left playing on Windows Media Player. He rewound the file, noting that it had been the PRIME show where "the incident" had occurred…dreading what he was about to see. When "the incident" had played itself out for a second time on the Marshall’s monitor, it had been the taste of bile that Lance Marshall had found himself pushing back in his throat. Lance found only one thing running through his mind on infinite loop: a deep, abiding desire to find Xavier Kannon and punch him in the face until there was nothing left of it but pulpy, red meat. The fact that Lance had no idea where to find Xavier Kannon was completely irrelevant. Instead, Lance found himself driving to the gym, his mind making its way through the Los Angeles streets on autopilot, "the incident" playing itself out in his head again and again. By the time he pulls into the parking lot, he finds himself coming to determined conclusion: he wants this image out of his head, gone. It is why he finds himself lifting heavier than he has in months, pushing himself until every muscle in his body is screaming in agony. It is why the music blares out from his iPod loud and harsh, the sound burrowing its way into his head. It is why he roars with each rep when normally he takes care to be as quiet as possible. All in an attempt to shut out the world, to put his mind on autopilot, to lock out everything else and turn his focus solely inwards. It didn’t work, of course. It never does. Why do we continue to believe in heroes? We do not stick someone’s poster upon our wall out of a desire to live vicariously through someone else’s life. We do not stand rapt in front of the television every week due to some supposed belief in our own inferiority. We do not hold long, involved arguments in which facts are cited and statistics are quoted because we have no real meaning in our lives. When our idols show themselves to have feet of clay, we do not simply sigh and accept it as the way of the world. We are disappointed. We are angered. We feel betrayed. For in a world that screams at us that we are nothing but base, vile, degraded creatures and we should just accept our fate… …we cling to our heroes… …we believe in our heroes… …because, through their example, they allow us to believe that we can be better.
