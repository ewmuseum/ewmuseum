Date: Today September 19, 2008 'Phillip!' The tone in Kathryn Shaw's voice did not sound pleased. Not one little bit. For his own part, Phillip Kennedy was wondering what he'd done. He was somewhat relieved to see the Siren clutching his wallet (luckily for him, there wasn't much of value in it), and glaring at him. 'Why do you have a picture of another woman in your wallet?' Said other woman happened to be the ravishing Anna Kournikova, tennis player. Also probably better known for being really hot than her tennis, but such is life. 'Oh, Anna.' Kennedy smiled. This was a question he had an answer for. 'In poker, we call the hand of Ace-King the Anna Kournikova because it looks great but never wins. It's a reminder to me, of sorts. No matter how good something looks, there's alway the chance it turns out badly. Gotta keep on my toes.' Katsidy pouted, though more out of the potential that he'd figured out 'something' than the actual presence of Kournikova herself. -------------------- Present 'What in the name of Phil Hellmuth are you trying to pull?' The Big Stack was, to put it mildly, not in the general best of moods. This was because of his habit of watching SCCW's Temptation broadcast each and every week. He did this because, quite frankly, it seemed like Desade, Katsidy, Savant, and everyone else in the Hand did in order to get an advantage on their rivals. Usually, he enjoyed the broadcast, took some notes, and moved along. This week, though, he had seen a televised war of words between Alexandra Pierce, leader of the Dead Man's Hand, and David Walter Smith, his own agent. And so for the second time this week, David found himself looking down at an angry figure that didn't seem to want to hear any of his explanations. 'I, Mr. Kennedy, am not trying to pull anything. What you saw on Temptation was me exercising my duties as your agent, and Ms. Pierce being extremely unreasonab--' Phillip shot his agent a withering look. 'She could have you taken out in the blink of an eye, you know that, don't you?' Kennedy, by now, was quite confident of the Hand's capabilities. After all, it counted amongst its membership the Stack himself, along with Jadian Bridden, Mayhem, and the Director herself, to say nothing of the dozens of nameless and faceless people under the Director's direct employ. 'I know this, yes,' David replied. 'But think for a moment how ridiculous that is? All I asked her for was information. About you. About her organization. And she was *extremely* loath to give me even a scrap of information. Not only that, but she threatened me.' 'Because you threatened her first!' Smith shook his head. 'No. She threatened me implicitly. Regardless, Phillip, what was I to do? Back down from her?' Kennedy nodded, and David only shook his head more vehemently. 'I will not back down from Alexandra Pierce, Phillip. I have no duty to her. My responsibility is managing your career the best way I can, and I cannot do so without knowing exactly what lies behind the dog and pony show that she is in command of.' A small smile quirked David's mouth. 'Besides, I think she gets off on people backing down from her.' 'You're going to manage me into retirement at this rate if you keep ticking her off, Logic,' Kennedy retorted. 'I'm a big boy. I can handle myself.' 'You are, and you can,' David replied. 'For the most part. I will also admit, unlike most, that you are more intelligent than people give you credit for. Your error is not in a lack of questions, mostly. It is in not asking the right questions.' The Big Stack's dander was up, a look of offense on his face as he asked the obvious followup. 'So then, brainiac agent of mine, what are the right questions to ask?' 'There are many possible questions you could ask. For example...have you ever asked Kathryn anything about what she does when she's flying all over the country -- without you?' 'We talked about this already,' Phillip said. 'She promised me that she's not cheating on me.' David remains stony-faced, though he sincerely doubts the Sex Kitten's faithfulness to his client. Though Desade and Logic are opposite in many things, they both believe that Kennedy finding out any lack of faithfulness on Kathryn's part could turn Phil into a ticking, uncontrollable time bomb, 'Perhaps not. But have you ever asked what she IS doing? You two are dating. Dating couples have a sense of trust with one another. They allow one another to know important things. She knows much about you, but really, how much do you know about her? Or Savant, for that matter? Or Desade?' Kennedy's mouth opens to fire back a retort, but he cannot voice one. 'Meh. Maybe I should ask some more questions. Or at least have a chat with the Director.' Kennedy frowned. Desade was not the easiest person in the world to speak to. 'I am not telling you what to do. Contrary to Pierce's belief, I do not care whether or not you are allied with the Dead Man's Hand. Being a member of the Hand has numerous benefits. I am, however, asking you to think for yourself and ask questions. You are a member of the group. Not its pet. You profess yourself to be the King. Be King Solomon, not King Lear. Be Louis XIV, not Louis XVI. If you wish to be a monarch, be a strong and informed one.' Kennedy nods, a bit too quickly. A question dangles on his lips for a brief moment, the asker too hesitant to ask until David helps him along with a friendly (as friendly as he gets, anyway) nod. 'Do you really believe they are hiding something from me?' Smith favors the poker player with his best poker face. The answer is, of course, yes, but a blatant yes might make Phil clam up, or react unpredictably. A qualified yes was needed. 'I cannot say for sure. All that I can say is that Alexandra Pierce wanted me to go away. Very, very badly. As I told her, I am well aware that she has numerous balls in the air. I may not be a particular ball that she finds threatening, or I may in fact be. Regardless, I am another ball that she must keep in the air, one that she would rather not have to juggle. I will continue my inquiry to the best of my ability while minimizing her ire.' If Phillip actually believed his agent, he was doing a very good job of not showing it. ---------------------------------------------------------------------- Las Vegas, Nevada Sometimes, the poker room at the Bellagio or the Rio simply wouldn't do. Sure, you had a nice variety of people at the table, but even if there were fish, they were mouthy fish. Arrogant fish. The sort that didn't make for a fun experience. So it was a smaller table that Phillip Kennedy found himself at, in the Treasure Island casino. Smaller stakes than he played for usually, but a bunch of people had gotten together and agreed on the terms for a sit & go, twenty dollar buy in, winner take all. Laughable stakes, but Phil didn't care. Gambling was gambling. The single table event had barely started, three hands in, and Kennedy hadn't gotten shit to play. Looking down, he found himself both out of position and in possession of a 74 offsuit, which he quickly discarded into the muck. Adjusting the brim of his omnipresent Stetson, he leaned back and watched to see if there'd be any action at the table. A middle-aged looking guy matched the big blind, casually. A youngish kid folded in turn, followed by what Kennedy knew was inevitable when playing: some dumb asshole who went all in on the first hand, usually with a mediocre hand. 'Glad I'm out of this one,' he thought to himself. 'If I had a real hand, I might have to call him, and God knows I'm not winning that battle.' Fold. Fold. Fold. 'I call.' The words were spoken by a feminine voice. Unusual in and of itself; in spite of the poker boom, men were still a far more common sight than woman at the poker tables. Kennedy found his eyes trailing up from the felt to land upon the woman. She was tall. How tall, he didn't quite know, because she was seated. Jet black hair framed her face down to her shoulders, with streaks of red cutting through like angry gashes. She was dressed in what appeared to be a catsuit of some kind, the material he wasn't sure of. 'Christ, she looks like she's out of one of Charley Crisp's wet dreams,' he thought to himself, that thought not meshing well in his head with the fact that she was admittedly attractive. The outfit bothered him though. Was it part of a ploy to grab attention? She wasn't using any of the tricks that amateurish women seemed to like to use at the tables. No flipping of her hair, no winking, no blown kisses, nothing. The initial caller of the blind folded, and it was Dumb Asshole vs. Mystery Woman, heads up. Asshole flipped over an Ace-Jack suited, spades. Not a bad hand at all, but not something you'd go all in on first hand against eight other people. Mystery Woman revealed...two jacks. Hooks. A notoriously hard hand to play. He evaluated the move in his head as he watched the flop fall. Calling an all-in early with Jacks was risky...but if she made the read that he was on a marginal hand, perhaps trying to make an early play with a low pair, she'd have him dominated. Even if he had Ace-King or Ace-Queen, she'd still be a favorite against the two overcards. A risky move early...but had she picked up a tell, a read of some kind? Turn. Blank. River. Blank. And just like that, Asshole was out of business and the table had its first real chip leader. She favored the table with a small half-smile as he left. No 'oh golly gee, I won a hand.' No dinner theater. Just a confident air about herself. That was always dangerous. And sure enough, she started picking off players like a sniper. Her reads were impeccable. Not once did she put herself in with the worst hand. Of course, not once did she get outdrawn, as so often happened in poker, but getting in with the best of it was the best you could do most of the time. Kennedy himself was only able to take out two players, and most of his damage was clearly being done on hands that she didn't play. It didn't take long for him to realize that this was going to be two-handed rather quickly. And thus it was that the mystery woman rose, when it was down to two, to take a seat directly across from the Big Stack. There was no dealer, so they would take turns dealing the cards, as had been done all night. A small array of twenty-spots lay on the felt around them in what Kennedy saw as a mockery of the WSOP tradition of putting all the money on the table for the final two. The money didn't matter; it was victory that counted for him today. Just as it was for anyone with a dream of capturing a bracelet, and poker immortality in the process. 'You're good,' he found himself saying, looking her dead in the eyes, trying to pick up a tell, anything. 'Likewise,' she responded, in an even tone of voice. There was something about her voice that got to him. Something different from the women he spoke to on a regular basis. It wouldn't be until later, much later, that he'd realize what it was. Not until he was back in his hotel room, a hundred plus extra bucks in his pocket from the game. Not until she had smiled at him in a clearly disarming way and told him that they would see one another again. And not until she had left something on the table. It took him a few moments to identify it then, and it rested on the dresser of the room now. It was a tarot card, seemingly painted by hand. Kennedy knew little about the art of tarot, but he had heard various things in passing. He didn't know whether to be insulted by the card that had acidentally or purposefully been left on the table. The Fool. The first of the Major Arcana. On the table as well had been left the final cards played that evening; the pair of queens in her hand, and the Ace-King offsuit in his. The board read 29JK3, rainbow. A pair of kings was enough to win. Anna Kournikova, on this day, had finally triumphed. Just like always that night, though, she had gotten in with the best hand. He'd been outplayed. The hundred bucks plus would help his wallet, though his pride was a bit hurt.
