Episode 1.6: 'It Was the Start of a Beautiful Friendship.'

Date: IG46 vs. Chris Storm & JA REC 06/18/2008 19:43:11 CDT Caption: Tomatillo Italian Grill & Eatery They say laughter is the best medicine; it is the brush that knocks away the cobwebs of the heart. They say a spoonful of sugar will make the medicine go down. The people who said these things weren't sitting with the pink-haired woman, elbows on the oaken bartop, half-gloved hand curled around a tumbler glass full of amber liquid. Her name was Charlotte Ramone (professionally, they called her the 'Punk Rock Pixie,' but most of her friends just called her 'Charlie'), and she was having kind of a bad night. Charlie's sense of style has always been...we'll go with 'questionable', but in black combat boots, red sweatpants, a black sportsbra, and a beaten leather jacket, it's...well, it's a wonder that the barkeep served her. (How many pink haired girls wearing plastic, Rip Hamilton-style face shields do you think they serve at the bar at an Italian restaurant, after all?) 'Come on, Charlotte,' our cameraman cautioned as the slight girl tipped her head back, guzzling the glass's contents. 'That's your third glass and we've been here for ten minutes.' The speaker, longtime viewers of the As-Yet-Untitled Charlotte Ramone Reality Show Project will tell you, was that of the much more reasonable half of the twin set of Charlotte Ann and Edith Jillian Raymond. Edie (who'll tell you that only their mother calls her 'Edith' anymore) was Charlie's business manager, executive producer of the Reality Show Project, and the pink-haired dynamo's second-best friend (and third-worst enemy) in the world. Which one she was at this point was up for debate. 'Fuck off, Eed,' Charlie said - her voice has been described as 'Demi Moore after a bender' or 'Kathleen Turner waking up from a decade-long coma,' with all the hoarse and breathy tone without any of the inherent sensuousness. 'It's a spaghetti restaurant. It's not like I'm taking up space, y'know? It's bad enough you made me put on the mask so you could shoot some footage, don't make me stop boozin' it up.' Edie Raymond-Ellis did a remarkable job as cameraperson (to the point that a viewer rarely noticed her presence), but sometimes, her duties as 'sister' overwhelmed those with the camera. She reached out, laying a hand on her sister's shoulder. 'Charlotte.' Edie was the only person in Ramone's life who used her given name that often. 'I know your girlfriend's ex just popped up out of the blue. I know they're out there and they're having some ravioli or whatever. You. Should go. Talk to her.' Charlie looked over her shoulder; her pink hair swished along the jacket. 'I don't know.' 'I've seen the two of you together, Charlie. And I've seen the two of them together. I think you know which one works better and I think we both know where Krissy's heart lies.' 'Yeah, maybe. They just seem...matched. Like Bree's a better choice for her than I am.' The frown was evident in Edie's voice. 'That's a bunch of nonsense. It's bullshit, is what it is.' Charlie turned back to the bar, resting her elbows on its scarred surface. She dropped her head, fingering the glass. 'Maybe. Or maybe I'm still just a waitress, like I was back before...before my hair was even pink.' Charlie's kid (by 53 minutes, dammit) sister's hand retracted, disappearing back behind the camera. 'That would have been, when? Oh-Four?' The Pixie was silent for the span of a few heartbeats. When the barkeep slid down the bar in front of the nearly motionless woman, she just shoved the glass at him in mute demand. Finally, she spoke, voice hardly over a whisper. 'Has it been that long already?' 'Well.' The grin was audible in her sister's voice. 'I was looking through some old home movies the other day and - ' Ramone's head snapped up, wide green eyes focusing past the camera - on its operator. 'No way. No. Way.' 'What? They're cute videos, Charlotte!' 'You're not putting them on the show!' The declaration was forceful and louder than it probably would've been if she hadn't already put away three glasses of whiskey. 'We talked about this, E. This show is supposed to be about me being kickass - I've got the qualifier with Jay-Jay Draven, then I guess they picked me an' Geritol to try to rip away Stormykins' belt.' ''Geritol'?' Charlie lifted her head slightly, peering at her sister (and the camera) from the corner of her eye. 'Man, can we cut that out? It's fuckin' stupid. I shouldn't talk shit when I'm getting wasted.' 'You know I don't edit these things - ruins the whole 'reality show' concept.' Ramone snorted. 'By making me look stupid?' 'Especially then. That's always a good time.' 'You're gonna put the video in anyway, aren't you?' 'Pretty much, yeah.' Charlie pulled at the sleeve of her jacket back, glancing at her Mickey Mouse watch. 'Fuck, I have to do that call-in thing to Wisconsin. Tell me why I'm doing it again?' 'Because Global Championship is up there and you need the nice-guy points.' 'Shit. You know how many gay jokes I'm going to have to put up with?' Edie did not answer. 'You have the number?' 'I...' She trailed off. 'I think it's out in the saddlebags.' 'You always bring that shit in. Ah, well. I should stop drinking anyway. Let's go.' 'I'm fine in here. May have some crème brule.' Charlie sighed. 'Fine, fine. Just leave Bree and Krissy alone.' The camera slid to the entrance to the dining room as Charlie stumbled-slash-meandered to the door. 'No problem.' Fade down. 'I'm Charlotte Ramone and I'm gonna rock your Kasbah!' The footage that's spliced into this episode of the Ramone Reality Show doesn't begin like the other bits. Sure, it still says, 'REC' in the lower corner, but those letters are white and blocky instead of red; the date is also written out in the upper left ('April 11, 2004'). Such is technology. When the shot opens, the much-less-high-powered video camera was pointed at the side of an old gray house, aluminum siding faded under the relentless assault of the sun's angry light. A blue tarp was hung along the side of the building, a wooden stool sitting unevenly on the lawn. The woman who stood in front of the backdrop was probably the Punk Rock Pixie, only there wasn't any pink hair (it was brown then), no bad fashion sense (well...at least, it wasn't as pronounced as it is today - the tie-dyed t-shirt and ripped denim shorts were pretty atrocious). In lieu of the Kickass Plastic, the Ramone of yesteryear wore a pink mask clearly converted from an old Catwoman costume (complete with the 'ears' chopped off); it still covered her face adequately, though - that was all that mattered. She leveled a finger (at this point not painted a ridiculous color) at the camera. 'I know what you're thinking. 'But she's just a giiiiiirl. Why should I let her into my wrestling school? What's she gonna do, kick guys in the shins?'' Ramone asked. 'And I'm sure, Mr. Bolton, that some - OK, most - of the guys in your school are bigger than I am. Some of them are faster. Some have even probably got some real fighting experience or whatever.' Ramone spread her hands, encompassing her own form. 'You know what you don't have at your school?' Her fingers pointed to herself. 'Me. That's what you're missing. Charlie Fucking Ramone, Bad to the Bone and the Girl that Makes You Wanna Write Home. I - ' 'This isn't working, Charlotte,' our cameraman said. 'You just don't scream 'hot chick heel'.' Again, it was Edie Raymond manning the camera for her sister. 'And you haven't explained to me what the mask is for. You're not a luchador.' Ramone unconsciously adjusted the modified mask. 'I think it makes my look kind of unique. And besides...if I suck at this, I don't want the people at the diner to know that I'm a pro-wrestler. Ruins my hardcore cred.' 'You don't have hardcore cred. You...work at a diner.' 'Shut up, Edie. We still filming?' 'Never stopped.' 'OK. Rewind this and I'll start again.' 'No, keep talking - it's going so well.' The eyeroll was audible in her sister's voice. A steely look came to Charlotte Ramone's eyes. 'I'm so kicking your ass later. Just do what I say.' The camera shot tilted down for a moment, then back up, focusing dead back on the woman who would become the Punk Rock Pixie. 'Okay, done,' Edie said. 'Really? That was fast.' 'It's a new camera. Action.' 'I'm Charlotte Ramone, and I'm gonna rock your Kasbah. I know what you're thinking, big man - what's a pretty girl like me doing in a mask? I can't have these precious good looks marred by a - ' Uproarious laughter emanated from behind the camera. 'I'm sorry, I'm sorry,' Edie said, waving her hand (her fingers appeared in the shot briefly). 'We've gotta start again. 'Precious good looks'? You've gotta warn me when you're doing that shit.' 'Forget it, Eeds. My shift starts in an hour; I need to get changed.' 'Will that girl you like be there?' Even through the pink Catwoman mask, the blush that came to Charlotte Ramone's cheeks was clear. 'I don't want to talk about it. She's with someone. Just...stop filming. We'll do this again later.' 'You should just be yourself.' Edie swung the camera down to her lap (anyone who cares can tell their friends that Edie Raymond used to wear blue jeans). 'My 'self' is boring. I'm just a girl, you know? There are guys winning world titles out there. Global titles. Titles I want to win. If I'm 'just myself,' I'll be lucky if I'm the person who'll be taking the pinfall in big, important matches.' 'Whatever, Char. Go get changed. I'm hitching a ride. I have some errands to run.' Charlotte nodded, nearly bounding towards the patio built attached to the back of the house and in through the sliding glass door. The camera spun towards her sister. Edie Raymond doesn't appear on the show much; she's not pretty like Charlie's girlfriend and she's not witty like Charlie herself - the mousy look with flat brown hair and thick-framed eyeglasses comes back into style the fifth of Nevurary. 'Mr. Bolton,' Edie said into the lens. 'My name's Edie, and that girl you saw...she's my sister and I love her dearly. Which is why I'm sending you this tape and not the one we'll be recording later. You see, as far back as I can remember, my sister's wanted to be a pro wrestler, which is - I know - a strange dream for a young girl to have. But while her friends were playing with Barbies, she was playing with...well, she was playing with Barbies, too, but she was pretending they were Wendi Richter. I'm not going to pretend she's going to be your best student, I'm not going to pretend that she's going to be a world champion someday, but...please, sir, give her a chance.' With that, Edie cut the feed. REC 06/14/2008 20:36:31 CDT Two women (a brunette and a blonde) sat in an isolated booth in an Italian restaurant. Both were dressed in expensive dresses, and they were laughing. Neither was aware the Sony 1080i High-Definition Digital Handheld Camcorder of DOOM! was filming them. God bless the high-powered zoom feature. 'I can't believe it's been so long since we've seen each other,' the blonde said, knifing into a pork chop. Her name was Krissy Jamison, and she was the live-in girlfriend of the Infinite Gauntlet challenger (and all-around kickass babe) we saw earlier. 'I know - it's all my fault.' The other woman's name was Sabrina Yates; she's Krissy's ex and that's all you need to know to understand this. 'Can you forgive me for...for everything? For disappearing?' 'Of course, silly. It's not like I didn't play my own part in that. We were both young and stupid.' Bree slid her hand across the table, brushing Krissy's wrist. 'Don't start thinking that you're at fault nearly as much as I am, Kristina. I was an addict, I was...I was kind of a wench, actually.' Jamison's knife and fork paused in mid-stroke, her blue-blue eyes locked down on the other woman's hand on her wrist. 'May-maybe a little.' Bree's gaze focused on Krissy's face; the blonde wouldn't look up. 'You know I didn't just come back because it's your birthday this week,' Bree said quietly. 'Yeah.' Krissy's voice was almost silent - the fact that the Camcorder of DOOM could pick it up over the ambient noise of the dining room was a testament to the amount of money that STD Entertainment, Inc. had put into its purchase. 'You said that you and Derrick had gotten into a fight and he'd threatened to - ' The camcorder zoomed in on a wrist as Bree's thumb brushed across the joint tenderly. 'We fought because of you. Because a part of me wants it to be 2004 again.' The silverware clattered against the ceramic plate as the blonde scooted out of the vinyl booth, demurely keeping her skirt from riding up. 'I have to...I have to use the ladies' room. I'll be back.' The pace that Krissy Jamison struck as she fled the dining room was not quite a run, but it was damn close. Edie took a step back into an alcove, trailing Krissy Jamison's progress; note she turns left, not right - towards the sign that says, 'Restrooms/Telephone,' then swung back into the room. Bree Yates pulled a black Razr telephone up to her ear, dark eyes darting to the left and the right. 'Elijah?' she said. 'Sabrina. You would not believe - ' Her gaze slid up, locking dead-on with the camera. 'Blast. Got to go.' 'Shit!' Edie said, slumping down around the wall. A moment later, she panned the camera up as a woman in a red cocktail dress, whose smile down at the younger Raymond sister was patient, but not terribly kind. 'Edie, it occurs to me that I haven't had a chance to catch up with Charlotte's family now that I'm back in town. Why don't we have a chat?' Bree extended a hand down to the woman, which Edie used to pull herself to her feet. 'Good to know you're still kind of a bitch, Sabrina,' Edie said. 'I have no idea what you're talking about, Edith.' She gestured towards the dining room. 'Now. I have some lasagna that's getting cold. If you could turn off the camera? I'd really prefer this not be on Charlotte's little show.' Cut down to static. The much-less-clear footage was spliced in here; according to the corner of the screen, it's still April 11, 2004. 'Shit, I hope this is working.' That voice was definitely Charlotte Ramone's, laced with worry. This time, the camera wasn't shooting an awful promo in front of a blue tarp. It was at another restaurant - some kind of diner, obviously, nearly barren of customers, with Formica countertops and red vinyl stools. The shot was focused haphazardly and kind of lopsidedly on a corner booth, where two women were chatting (if by 'chatting,' you mean 'kind of arguing a lot') at low volumes. 'I'm just tired of dealing with all of your manipulative bullshit, Bree,' the blonde said, her back to the counter we appeared to be shooting from. 'I want my own life for a change, not to be second-fiddle to your career.' Sabrina Yates grabbed at Krissy Jamison's wrist - but unlike when she did it four years later, the brunette's grip is kind of forceful, almost enough to bruise. 'You don't get to decide when it's over. No one but me gets to decide when it's over, Kristina. Do you realize what I've sacrificed for you? I am supposed to be the kind of girl that men want to sleep with, but they see me with you and - ' 'And they want to fuck us both; don't give me that crap, Sabrina.' Krissy snatched her hand away, retracting it to her chest. 'I still love you, I just need...I need time to be me.' 'Fine.' Sabrina slid out of the booth, leaning down over the side of the booth that the blonde sat in. She braced one hand on the table and one hand on the booth. 'Hold this, Jose?' Charlie asked softly. The camera passed from one person to another; this angle was noticeably higher than when Charlotte filmed; thick fingers appeared briefly in the bottom of the shot. 'Just keep the camera pointed at Table Seven.' 'You need me, Kristina,' Sabrina hissed. 'You need me to keep you in this lifestyle. You need me to keep you on your feet, and you need me because I'm not sure that you - ' A woman in a blue waitress outfit appeared onto the frame, brown hair bound up in a short ponytail, her back to the camera. 'I'm sorry, is there a problem here, ma'am?' Charlotte Ramone to the rescue! Bree looked over her shoulder. 'No, we're fine, miss,' she said with a thin smile. 'Wasn't...really talking to you, but thanks for the update.' Charlie's head turned down to the blonde, who looked very small with the statuesque model standing over her. Bree grabbed Krissy by the wrist again, tugging. 'Yeah, see, we were just leaving. You won't be getting a tip.' Ramone nodded sharply, hair waving. ''Sokay, I'm one of those weird waitresses who gives tips, actually.' Even Sabrina Yates was smart enough to know what comes after a setup line like that; she let go of Krissy's wrist, spinning blindly with a very girly right hand - one that Charlie Ramone was able to smoothly duck under. The woman who would become the Punk Rock Pixie one day came up out of her crouch, elbow leading, and caught the brunette under the chin. The force was enough to send Bree stumbling (especially in those heels), landing backfirst across the table, her head clattering against Krissy Jamison's plate of pancakes. Syrup smeared in Bree's mahogany brown hair. Blood came to the corners of her lips. 'You hit her!' Krissy Jamison said. 'Yeah, happens when you're being a bitch,' was Charlotte's response. She extended both her hands to the blonde. 'Come on, she's not going to be happy with either of us.' 'I...' Krissy looked dubiously from Sabrina, rolling to her side, to the woman who'd saved her. 'I don't know.' 'Last chance, lady. You can live in someone's shadow forever or you can take a chance and live your life.' Charlie tried not to wince when she said it; it sounded like a line she'd been saving since the two squabbling women first came into her section. Krissy's French manicured hands clasped Charlie's; the woman who would one day claim the last name 'Ramone' (but was still then just plain-old 'Raymond') gave a mighty tug. The two made their way to the door of the diner quickly, both looking over their shoulder as Sabrina sat up. 'Jose, tell Maggie I'm taking my break. And make sure that Edie gets the camera back!' 'Krissy!' Sabrina called after them. 'Krissy, come back!' The brunette spun, listening to the soft jangle of the bell above the diner's door. Charlotte Ramone's motorcycle was kicked into gear. 'Kristina, please...' This was about when Jose found the stop button; he probably had some more food to cook, after all. The following is audio-only. 'You are listening to Bob Onan on the HOG!' Thunder crashes. '102.9 FM, Everything That Rocks Milwaukee! And we have a special treat for you tonight, fans! Joining us via the telephone is Sin City Championship Wrestling sooooopah-star and kickass les-bean, Charlotte Ramone!' 'Charlie, please,' the gravelly voice of the Pixie said. 'Where are you right now, Charlie?' 'Honestly?' 'Hells yeah! I think the fans in Milwaukee want to know what kind of lesbianish things you're doing.' 'I'm sitting on my motorcycle outside an Italian place.' 'Did you...eat the clams marinara?' Onan asked. They played a rimshot sound effect. 'You're funny.' Charlotte's tone made it quite obvious that she did not think Bob was funny at all. 'So, Charlie, you're wrestling Chris Storm and...uh, my notes say some guy named 'J_A' coming up here soon, right?' 'Jerichoholic Anonymous, yup. For the Infinite Gauntlet title - the biggest match of my damn life. These guys have been champions for, like, ever, and I've only been in the business a couple years. You don't believe that Chris Storm's a phenomenal talent, I invite any of you to go see the GCW show coming up next week, and you can see JA in Empire Pro and A1E.' 'But you're not a GCW wrestler, isn't that right, Charlie? You work for this Ess-Cee-Cee-Cee-Cee-Cee-Dub place - that might be too many 'C's'. How's it going - ' 'It's going OK. I've got a big match myself an' - Krissy!' The receiver appeared to have dropped away from her lips. 'Krissy, over here!' 'That's your girlfriend, right?' But Charlotte Ramone wasn't listening to the interviewer anymore. 'Kris...' 'Hey!' the other woman's voice was faint, off the mic. 'What are you doing here?' 'I was getting drunk. So I could talk to you.' 'Charlotte?' Bob Onan rarely felt like a second wheel - he was Milwaukee's most happening rock & roll DJ. 'We're kinda doing an interview.' 'You don't have to get drunk to talk to me,' Krissy said. 'And you are not riding home if you are.' 'Ms. Ramone?' Bob was appearing to flail slightly. 'Could you, maybe, answer the question?' 'I dunno,' Charlie said. 'You just seemed so happy and I didn't wanna f-.' 'Okay!' the DJ interjected. 'We're gonna...let's play some AC/DC while our subject works out whatever her problems are...' 'Dirty Deeds,' baby. Oh, yeah. The classics. 'I feel stupid!' And we're back to the spliced-in historical footage. This is dated 'October 20, 2004.' Tall, military green lockers lined the walls of the room, an oaken bench bolted to the ground. Krissy Jamison sat on the bench, barely in frame, as the camera pointed towards the closed door. 'I think you look great,' Krissy promised. 'And, besides, you wanted something that'd make your look unique, remember?' 'But it's so pink!' Charlotte Ramone whined from behind the door. 'Just get out here, Charlie,' Krissy said impatiently. 'Don't make me drag you; you know I will.' 'I'm...I'm not feeling too good. Maybe I'll just no-show and let Darryl win that way.' 'Charlotte Ann,' Edie said from behind the camera. 'I flew all the way out here from business school to record this moment. You are not going to let some overgrown lug beat you in your first match, are you?' 'But he's like four times my size!' It was...OK, it was a whine - wanna make something of it? 'I just graduated from T-Bolt's wrestling school, how am I gonna beat somebody like that? Let's just go home, and I'll dye my hair back to brown. I'm sure that Jose will take me back at Tom's if I apologize for elbowing...you-know-who...in the face. Then everything can go back to the way it was and ow-ow-ow-OW! Leggo my ear!' While Charlotte Ramone was making her excuses, Krissy Jamison had stood, stalking across the room. Her high-heeled shoes clicked on the tiled floor, which would have made anyone with more perception skills than your average block of wood aware of her approach. She shoved the door open and grabbed her...gonna go with 'friend'...by the ear, pulling her out. It was like Charlie was the Beta Version of the Punk Rock Pixie; the hair was vividly hot pink, shoulder length and kind of frizzy. The outfit was much less extravagant, though - just a sky-blue singlet with a hot pink picture of a hand curled into the devil's horns on the chest. 'You,' the blonde said. 'Are wrestling Darryl 'The Barrel' Farrell. He's a nice guy, but, forgive me, he wasn't talented five years ago. Go out there and make an impression on these people, then come back here and make an impression on me.' 'I wi-wait.' Charlie turned to Krissy, one hand resting on the shorter blonde's hip. 'You mean that's a yes? You'll go out with me? I thought...after Bree, I thought you were taking a break from the dating thing.' Krissy smiled softly, looking up into the hot pink facemask of the would-be Pixie (much more luchador mask than the one she wore today - how she came to wear plastic face shields is...actually a funny story, really). 'I...was.' She reached up, brushing the frizzy blonde hair out of Charlie's wide green eyes. 'But then I figured I was fooling myself, you know? That...we already spend so much time together and you're so great to me. I might as well, right? It's not like you're gonna hurt me.' 'Never.' Charlie put a half-gloved hand on the side of the blonde girl's neck. 'I'd never let anything happen to you.' 'Then...yeah.' Krissy smiled shyly. 'Let's go out and have a drink after the show if the big fat guy doesn't beat you up too bad. That should give you reason to not let that happen, shouldn't it?' Garbage's cover of the Ramones' 'I Just Wanna Have Something To Do' boomed through the cheap PA system of the high school gymnasium as a burly gentleman knocked on the door (barely visible behind them in Edie's shot). ''Scuse me,' he said - not terribly kindly. 'Don't you kids know when your cues...' He trailed off, looking at the blonde and the pink-haired dynamo, in each other's arms. 'Are you two...you know...together?' Charlie protectively ferried Krissy behind her. 'None of your business,' Ramone said. 'I'll give you an extra five hundred if she comes out there with you. A grand if you kiss.' 'Not a chance. My relationship is not for - ' Charlie will tell you she'd have trailed off her for saying the word 'relationship,' even if Krissy hadn't interrupted. 'Chuck, wait.' That interruption was soft, but it impelled the Pink & Green Machine (who wouldn't earn that particular appellation for a number of years). 'We need the money, don't we?' 'Maybe - the academy really cleaned us out.' Krissy reached up, adjusting the fall of the cloth mask over Ramone's face. 'Then what's the harm.' 'I'm not going to let them exploit - ' 'They're only exploiting if we let them, and we're only using what already exists. Besides, if I'm going to accompany you to the ring when you win the world title, I should probably learn how now.' 'You sure?' Charlie put her hand to Krissy's cheek, brushing her thumb along the cheekbone. 'I think I'd follow you anywhere.' 'Made your mind up?' the promoter asked, as if he hadn't heard the soft conversation. 'I got a crowd of fifty or sixty people there who're looking to see what you can do, girly.' Charlie turned, dropping her hand to her side. 'We're in - just for the accompaniment. We'll have to see about the kiss.' 'Awight.' Edie zoomed in as Krissy Jamison laced her fingers through Charlotte Ramone's and the pair moved past the camera and out the door. The brunette filmmaker turned the camera back on herself one last time. 'It was the start of a beautiful friendship...' Fade to black.
