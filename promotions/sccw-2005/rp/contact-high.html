Date: Various Friday, February 26, 1999 Approximately 5pm EST He drove a beige '87 Dodge Shadow with a temperamental cassette deck and a bizarre stain on the back seat that he passionately claimed was in no part responsible for. It may not have been the fanciest ride, but it was all that Jared Sykes had, and he had done his best to clean it up and make it presentable for the evening. His time as a student at Richard Stracker's school for professional wrestling (it would later be inherited by Richard’s son Darren) was coming to a close, and as was customary among students their graduating was to be celebrated by an act of tradition and copious amounts of alcohol. The group, of whom there were six in total, had rented a few hotel rooms that would be used to shield their intoxicated exploits from prying eyes. WZLX, a local classic rock station which also owned the distinction of being America's very first classic rock station, was currently in the middle of a Pink Floyd block, and Jared happily drummed his thumbs on the maroon steering wheel in time with 'Money' as he drove. Fortunately there was no one else in the vehicle to 'enjoy' his particular vocalization of the song. By the time his car pulled up to her house, Justine Calvin was already bounding halfway down the walkway with a navy blue Nike duffel bag slung over her shoulder. Jared had only enough time to reach over and unlock the door before she was tugging at the handle to be let in. 'So how's 'The Dart'?' she asked, sliding the seat of the two-door vehicle forward so she could throw her bag in the back. 'Still sketchy?' 'It's not a Dart,' he corrected. 'It's a Shadow.' 'Jared, if it was a Shadow it would be black, or gray, or some other dark color. It's a Dart.' 'It's okay, baby,' he said to the car, rubbing his hand across the dashboard. 'She doesn't mean it. You have to be nice to her.' He turned his attention to his passenger. 'She's a little sensitive.' 'You're insane.' 'Cal’, please,' he mock pleaded, gesturing with his head towards the dashboard. 'Not in front of the baby.' It was the kind of back-and-forth that gave most people the impression that they were a couple. Where there was one there was usually the other. She would tease him and he would tease her right back. Occasionally they would argue about something silly or trivial, but it was never serious. And while they were never actually a couple, at least one of them wished they were. 'So what's the plan?' she asked, changing the subject. Though they hadn't known each other for very long Justine was quite sure that if left unchecked Jared would continue to talk about his car, or any inanimate object, as though it were a real person with real feelings. 'When are we meeting up with everyone else?' 'I talked to Cameron earlier, and he's picking up everything from the liquor store. I guess we're meeting at the hotel around seven to check in and drop stuff off, and then we're heading to the water from there.' 'Cool.' She took a quick look at the clock on the dashboard. 'So we've got almost two hours to kill. Think we can stop somewhere? I'm starving.' 'Yeah, absolutely.' Jared smiled as he turned the wheel and brought the car back out into traffic. Time with her was something he cherished, all the more so when he didn’t have to share it with anyone else. ~~~~~~~~~~~ Saturday, September 19, 2009 Sometime after 11pm EST One of the first things that Jared when entering an unfamiliar building was to imagine what it would look like as a level in a video game. He'd think about enemy placement, what parts of the architecture would make for good natural cover, where power-ups would be located, and what the flow of the level would be like. It was something that he did fairly regularly but never told anyone about for fear of what they might say (as if they weren't whispering enough already). Tonight was different. Though it was only the second time he had been in Justine's apartment building, the first being earlier during the night when he picked her up, his attention was drawn elsewhere. 'Your dancing wasn't that bad.' 'Oh man,' Jared laughed, covering his face in feigned embarrassment. 'Please apologize again to your grandmother for me the next time you see her. I feel so bad about accidentally knocking her over.' 'She's a tough old lady, I'm sure she'll be fine.' Justine raised a playful eyebrow. 'Besides, I think she has a little crush on you.' 'Yeah, that was kind of creepy. Didn't you tell her you were 'spoken for'?' Her giggling mixed with the sounds of footsteps on marble tile as the two made their way to the elevator. In any other situation Jared might have made a mental note about how the far hallway would be an excellent vantage point from which to launch a stealthy ambush, but that particular quirk would be saved for another time. 'Speaking of which,' he continued, slinging his suit jacket over his right shoulder. 'How'd I do? I didn't overstep any bounds or break any rules, did I?' 'No, you were absolutely perfect.' 'So you think your brother bought it?' 'Yeah, I don't think he'll be trying to set me up with any of his friends again for a while.' She adjusted the hood of the Red Sox sweatshirt she wore, borrowed from Jared when the fall air had grown too crisp for his suit jacket to be warm enough. 'I owe you huge for this.' 'Nah, it's fine. That was actually kinda fun, and your bro' and his wife did a nice job with the wedding.' The duo ascended the three stone steps that brought them to the elevator door. 'You should have seen Cam's wedding a few years ago. It was gorgeous what he and his boyfriend did. Everything was covered in flowers.' For a moment the Black Sheep stood in stunned silence, not quite sure he’d actually heard that. 'Cameron is...?' 'Gay? Yeah. You didn't know?' She waited for Jared to nod his reply, seemingly too surprised to verbalize it. 'Yeah, it's actually why he stopped wrestling. He was afraid that if he came out while he was still working that nobody would want to get into the ring with him.' 'That's pretty fucking awful.' Her explanation seemed to snap Jared out of his temporary haze. 'I'm kinda bummed I didn't know. Cam' and I worked together for a while after Darren and Rich set us loose on the world, and he never told me.' The doors to the elevator slid open, allowing both Jared and Justine to step aboard. With the press of a button they were on their way to the sixth floor. 'I don't think he wanted anyone to know, not while he was still in the business.' Her words were meant to be reassuring, but the Black Sheep couldn't help but feel a bit slighted. 'I get it, but it's not like I would have said anything. My dad -- my biological dad -- he came out about eight years ago, so I know where the guy's coming from.' Now it was Justine's turn to be surprised. 'Wait, your dad?' 'Yeah. He called my sister and I over one day, sat us both down and told us. It was kind of a trip.' 'That's got to be a tough conversation to have. I can't even imagine.' 'It actually wasn't so bad.' Jared shrugged and offered a smile. 'I mean, if anything I know what it's like to have people think that what you do is fucked up, so at least on some level I can relate. Plus, he's my dad. It doesn't change anything. He and I have our issues, but for different reasons. Every time I talk to 'm, doesn't matter what it's about, conversation always ends up being about me and my 'wasted potential'.' The elevator slid to a quiet halt at the sixth floor, the doors gliding open. Jared gestured to the door, allowing Justine to step through first and like an obedient puppy he followed close behind. 'Wow,' she said, reaching into her purse for a set of keys. 'I guess there's a lot I don't know about you, huh? Not really surprising though.' The key turned in the lock and Justine turned to Jared, grinning. 'After all, you're the Invisible Man.' In the last few months since they'd reconnected she'd said things like this before, and regardless of her tone it was never pleasant to hear. The first few times she said it he played along, figuring that given the amount of time they'd been apart she was more than entitled to express her frustration, but lately the words seemed to carry more weight. To Jared it was like getting punched in the stomach. He decided to use this moment as a chance to bring up his displeasure. 'That's actually something I wanted to talk to you about...' It wouldn’t be very long before he started to regret it. ~~~~~~~~~~~ Friday, February 26, 1999 After 9pm EST On an obscure part of Revere Beach in northern Massachusetts a small fire was keeping six twenty-something’s warm in the cold winter air. 'No fucking way.' 'But if you've never done it, Jared, then how do you know it'll be that bad?' 'I've never been fisted with a boxing glove before either, but I think we can all agree we wouldn't need a live example to accept it as a bad thing, right?' 'S'not as bad as ya' think.' Roderick Frost’s tone was too quiet and relaxed for anyone to hear, as was normally the case. He proudly smiled to himself, but it faded once he realized that no one had heard him. There existed a tradition at Richard Stracker's school for professional wrestling: the fall/winter classes would celebrate their graduation by stripping down to their underwear and jumping into the frigid waters of the Atlantic ocean. It was a rite of passage that started thirty years prior when Mitchell Anderson, who was something of a risk-taker to begin with, was dared by his classmates to take the plunge. In recent years with the addition of more female students the tradition was expanded to allow for bathing suits, but everything else remained. Jared Sykes wanted nothing to do with it, though his own reservations had little if anything to do with the temperature of the water. The task to get him into the sea was left to Cameron Smart, a leviathan of a man at over 6 feet tall and pushing two hundred and eighty pounds, most of which was muscle. By nature he was fairly gentle, something of a teddy bear, but tradition was tradition. If he was going in the water, if anyone was going in the water, than everyone was going in the water. 'C'mon, man,' he said, taking his first imposing step in the direction of his much smaller classmate. 'We all know it's going to be cold as hell, that's the whole idea.' 'It's not the water that bothers me.' They didn't need to look any further than his attire to understand the truth of his statement. While everyone else was huddled up in hats, gloves, jackets, and scarves Jared stood seemingly unphased in jeans and a hooded sweatshirt, the sleeves of which had been rolled to the forearm. 'Jesus, man, don't be such a fag.' Maxwell Barton III wasn't much bigger that Jared size wise, and while he wasn't generally regarded as a bad person he tended to operate without a filter, the result of growing up an only child in a well-to-do family. 'Doesn't matter what it is, Jared,' Cameron continued, stepping closer to the miniscule cruiserweight. 'Everyone's going swimming.' The sand at Revere Beach in Massachusetts was solid, owing to the sub-freezing temperatures of the average New England winter. When Jared backpedaled, looking for an escape, his heel struck a outcropping of sand. He lost his balance as a result, and before he was able to steady himself landed firmly on his ass. Cameron used the opening to dash forward, pinning Jared to the sand before he could recover. 'Besides, you don't want to look silly in front of everybody, do you?' Jared raised an eyebrow. 'Dude, do you even know me?' It was a point that Cameron, and everyone in fact, had to concede. 'All the same, you know the drill. Won't let you be the only guy in thirty years not to go through with it.' Jared tried to squirm, but a forceful paw held him in place. He was defeated, resigned to his fate, and he didn't like it. Not one bit. 'Alright, fine,' he spat, 'I'll go in the damn water.' 'Good boy.' 'Just promise me I can keep my shoes on.' Kelly Taylor, one of two girls in the graduating class, was next to speak up. 'The's the dumbest thing ever. Why the hell would you want to do that?' 'I'd really rather not say.' Kelly shook her head, turning her attention back to the other girl in the group, Justine Calvin, with whom she had been in the midst of a private conversation. Though she didn't say it, Justine found the entire situation terribly amusing. Even under the weight of a behemoth like Cameron Smart the spectacle of Jared Sykes was something to behold. 'You want to tell me?' asked Cameron, easing up a bit on his grip. 'If it's personal I won't tell anybody.' 'I'd prefer not to.' 'Or you can go in totally bare-assed naked. Your call.' Perhaps it was the fear of facing the chill water without any scrap of clothing, perhaps it was the thought of what that water might do to his exposed anatomy in front of someone for whom he had caged feelings, but whatever the reason it was enough to bring Jared to an immediate decision regarding his fate. 'Crabs!' he blurted, loud only enough for Cameron to hear. This was something of a shock to Cameron Smart, a man whose in-ring nickname of 'The Devastator' did not go unfounded. He pulled his hand away from Jared who wasted no time in creating a bit of distance between them. 'What?' 'Ever since I was a little kid I've been afraid of 'em. You know how some people just flip the fuck out when they see a spider or a snake? I'm the same way, only it’s with crabs.' It seemed to Jared at first that Cameron was having a hard time trying to decide whether he should laugh, or... laugh. 'What, did you get pinched when you were little?' 'No, never once in my life. It's just one of those things. Can't really explain it.' 'You understand that they're tiny, right?' Cameron tried to rationalize. 'Like, you're way bigger than they are, man.' 'You ever see someone freak out over a spider?' 'Yeah.' 'Did it matter how big the spider was?' 'Well, no, but...' 'And they don't even have claws, or body armor, or little nasty faces.' It only confused Cameron more when Jared’s descriptions were accompanied by rather bizarre gestures and facial expressions. 'You're a weird guy, Jared.' Cameron rose to his feet and offered his hand to Jared to help lift him back up. With both men standing, Cameron turned his attention to the group as a whole. 'Alright folks, you've all heard how this works. If you're not wearing a bathing suit under your clothes then you're going in with just your underwear. Going to be plenty of time for getting warm back at the hotel.' Jared tried to steel himself against what was to come, but when Justine stepped to his side in preparation to disrobe any thoughts of the ocean quickly left him. 'What was that about?' Her voice was gentle, reassuring. 'Oh, it's stupid. You'd probably laugh.' 'I won't. You can tell me.' Jared sighed, pulling off his sweatshirt and tossing it to the ground next to the fire. 'I have this weird irrational fear,' he said, glad that his blushing was camouflaged by the stinging cold of the wind. 'I kind of have a problem with crabs. They scare the shit out of me.' He waited for her to laugh, braced himself for it, but it didn't come. Instead he felt the light touch of her hand on his arm. 'Don't worry,' the lilt to her voice was somehow reassuring. 'I'll protect you.' ~~~~~~~~~~~ 'Rick. Yo, Rick.' Of the entire lot of them, Cameron Smart was the only one who hadn't had anything to drink. He figured that given the nature of some of the people he was surrounded with that it might serve everybody well if at least one person remained in their right frame of mind. It didn't hurt that Cameron was something of a physical specimen, so if he need be he was fairly certain he could pick up and carry anyone who became unruly. Except perhaps for Kelly Taylor. She looked like a kicker. Not drinking also had the added benefit of keeping him safe from his friend and future tag-team partner Roderick Frost, who was busy entertaining himself with a magic marker and a very drunk, very passed-out Maxwell Barton. 'Hey, Roderick,' Cameron pressed, this time accentuating his words with a nod in the direction of his friend. 'Sup.' 'What the hell are you doing?' ''S'it look like I'm doin'?' Cameron leaned forward in his seat, lifting himself up slightly to get a better look at Roderick's masterful marker work. 'Looks like you're drawing dicks on his face.' Roderick scoffed, as though Cameron had just insulted his integrity. He pursed his lips and shook his head, the motions exaggerated in accordance with his current level of intoxication. 'Psh, naw,' he said at last, glancing down at his own handiwork. 'This ain't drawin'. This is art. This is the male form in all its power and veiny, visceral glory. You see this? This is where it all happens, man. Where life comes from. We’re makin’ babies.' For a moment there was silence. The normally reserved African-American had drawn the attention of the room as he waved his marker through the air as a maestro before his orchestra. 'Aight, so maybe I'm drawin' fuckin' cocks on 'is face.' There was laughter among the group, and a few spilled drinks as a result. That's the thing with drunk people, Cameron thought, they can't do anything without making a mess. Seizing the opportunity, The Devastator figured he would help the situation along with a little more levity. 'Hey, Rick,' he continued, 'Why don't you tell Jared what you told me earlier.' 'What'd he tell you earlier?' Jared asked, still barely sober enough so as not to be a total slurring mess. 'What'd I tell you earlier?' Roderick added. 'About your nickname. Tell 'em why you're calling yourself 'The Iceman'.' Had Roderick smiled any wider it was likely his jaw would have come unhinged. Most people assumed that when Roderick Frost took the ring name of 'The Iceman' that it had everything to do with his surname, or the fact that when not drunk he was quiet, reserved, and a generally calm person. Such was not the case. 'It's cuz I make yo' nipples hard.' No sooner did the words pass his lips did Roderick start to laugh, quite proud of his joke. Cameron simply smiled, having heard this particular line more times than he cared to count. Jared half-choked on his beer, a few drops dribbling from the corners of his mouth onto his shirt. Seeing this made Roderick laugh all the harder. Neither of the girls seemed to register Roderick's comment, having instead occupied themselves with a conversation told in hushed whispers. This did not go unnoticed by the only sane man in this particular asylum, and Cameron slid into a seated position on the floor next to Jared. 'They're talking about you,' Cam said, his voice low so as not to attract attention. 'Who are?' Jared asked, watching as Max subconsciously tried to swat away Roderick's marker from his cheek. Roderick giggled, and went right back to drawing. 'Cal' and Taylor,' Cameron continued. 'Don't look, or at least don't make it obvious, but every so often one of them looks over at you.' 'Shut up. You're drunk.' 'I'm serious. Watch. Make like you're looking at art class over there, but make sure you catch it out of the corner of your eye.' Jared was skeptical, but complied with Cam's request nonetheless. He turned his eyes back towards Roderick's impressionist marker experiment, but kept his focus on the two girls. It took a minute, but sure enough he caught Justine steal a quick glance in his direction before immediately turning her attention back to Kelly and whatever secrets they were sharing. 'See? I told you, man.' Cameron's smile was proud. 'She's into you. You should totally go for it.' Jared felt his pulse quicken. He and Justine had been fairly close since the start of their training together, and while he'd always wondered if there was -- or would ever be -- something between them he was always oblivious to anything that could be construed as a sign or a signal. Even in the face of secret stories and stolen glances he wasn't sure he was ready to accept a mere look as hard evidence. 'You're insane,' he said, taking a drink from his beer to quench a mouth suddenly gone dry. 'Oh, c'mon,' Cameron playfully chided, his words followed by a light elbow to Jared's arm. What are you talking about?' 'Exactly. Maybe there should be, if you follow me. Maybe you two should - see, she just did it again.' He watched as she danced through a minefield of empty bottles, each step bringing her closer to him. Jared raised his own bottle to his lips, hoping to find a piece of solace there, but it was empty, leaving him no alternative but to cope with the impending situation. 'Hey, Cam',' Justine said, now only a few feet away from them. 'Hey, Cal',' Cameron responded, and for a flash Jared hoped that perhaps she was there to see Cameron. This was immediately followed by an equally brief, but undeniably powerful wave of jealousy. 'Do you mind if I borrow Jared for a little bit?' she asked as she slid a few stray hairs behind her ear. Her question had the unknown side effect of immediately quelling and renewing any of Jared's fears. 'I left something in his room that I wanted to grab real quick.' Cameron smiled pleasantly, knowing full well what was taking place. For his part Jared looked visibly confused, as though his mind was preoccupied with proving the laws of thermodynamics. 'I can give you the ke...,' Jared started to say, before a much harder elbow from Cameron set him straight. 'I mean... uhh, yeah. Sure. Let's take a walk.' Jared pulled himself to his feet, set his bottle down, and dusted off the back of his pants. Still very unsure of how to conduct himself, he slid his thumbs into a pair of his rear belt loops. 'Um, after you.' Justine turned and headed for the door with Jared in tow. He cast a quick glance over his shoulder just long enough to catch an enthusiastic thumbs-up from Cameron. The distance to the room shared by Jared and Maxwell was short enough, preventing the walk from being too awkward for either he or Justine. That didn't stop him from having his own private panic attack as they traversed the thirty feet that separated the door they now stood at from the one they just left. Jared fumbled in his pocket for his key. 'So what do you need to get?' he asked, the first words either of them had said since leaving the room shared by Cameron and Roderick. 'Oh, just figured I should grab my bag. You know, since Kelly and I are staying together.' 'Right.' The lock disengaged with an audible click. Jared cast the door open, stepping aside and holding it open for Justine to enter first. Only a single lamp, casting strange shadows across the walls and floor, lit the room. He followed a few steps behind her, scanning the room in an attempt to not make eye contact, and it was only when the door swung shut and latched behind him did he say anything, hoping to break the quiet tension. 'Pretty funny about what Roderick did to Ma...' A surge of heat rose in his chest as she turned abruptly and pressed her lips to his, stealing his words and his breath in a single stroke. He had no idea how long it lasted whether minutes or seconds. The world around him had ground to a halt. When she pulled away at last is was though a surge of electricity had struck him. Every nerve tingled. Every hair was on edge. He feared that his heart might try to burst forth from his chest. Her smile was shy in the silence that hung between them. He opened his mouth to speak but could find no words. The desire to kiss her again was overwhelming, and as he could not suppress it he conceded to it, gently placing his hands on her waist and pulling her closer. Any fear he felt was gone as if suddenly and entirely erased. The world was new again, and he wanted nothing more than to drink it in. 'What the hell do you want me to say, Jared?' Justine was barefoot and pacing, having kicked off her sandals a half hour ago before things intensified. 'I’m sorry I brought it up. He extended a hand palm-out, but she withdrew at his touch. Sensing that this was indeed a losing battle he pursed his lips, nodded softly, and eased away, each step gaining ground on the door. 'I’m sorry,' he said again, moving towards the chair on the back of which his jacket hung. 'I shouldn’t have brought it up.' It was only when his back was turned did she speak again. 'It was just sex, Jared. That’s all it was.' Her words ground his pace to a halt. He paused for a moment, knuckles tightening on the back of the chair. Whether she realized it or not she’d found the chink in his armor, the missing scale on the underside of neck. Worse, she’d poked it with a very, very sharp stick. 'It wasn’t just anything.' He didn’t turn to face her, not at first. His grip on the chair tightened, his knuckles whitening from the pressure. To most people Jared Sykes was comic relief. The general belief seemed to be that he operated on only one gear, at one speed, and was always 'on'. In truth the smile was a façade. A cleverly crafted defense mechanism honed through years of practice against trials too painful to recount. It hid the hurt, the sadness, and the anger. Right now Jared Sykes was angry. The smile was gone. 'I didn’t catch that.' Her statement was honest. His first words were hushed, a result of having to check the emotions bubbling to the surface. 'I said it wasn’t just anything,' he repeated, his words now louder. 'Jared, it was…' She didn’t get a chance to finish. 'No,' he interrupted as he turned to face her. 'Whatever you’re going to say, whatever you’re thinking of saying, don’t. You tell me that there wasn’t ever anything there and we’ll both know you’re lying. I ate two knees and punch in the fucking face the first time I saw you a few months ago, and that damn sure wasn’t over nothing. Nobody reacts that way to something they claim is trivial.' The intensity of his response caught her off guard. Awestruck, her eyebrows arched and her jaw fell slightly agape. In the time that she’d known him she had never seen him angry. But she was something of a scrapper herself, and would not go down without a fight. 'You know what else normal people don’t do, Jared?' She took a step forward, brazenly folding her arms across her chest. 'Normal people don’t carry around busted-ass photos for a decade.' Now it was his turn to be surprised. 'I mean, really,' she continued, smirking malevolently. 'That’s just sad.' 'How the hell did you know about that?' 'Well, for one, you just told me. Then again, I might have done a little snooping when you weren’t paying attention. Guess you’ll never really be sure.' He clenched his jaw, trying to crush one set of teeth with the other. 'That’s awesome, Cal’. Really, it is.' His gaze narrowed. It was his turn to smile. 'Okay then, explain what kind of person shows up wasted at someone else’s house all purrin’ and ready to go. I mean, if you want to talk to someone about ‘sad’ there’s a mirror by the door you could put to good use.' Justine felt as though the wind had been knocked from her. Unable or unwilling to respond she simply took a single step back, turning away from him. They stood in awkward silence for what felt like an eternity. 'I should go,' he said at last. His voice had lost most of the animosity it had only moments ago carried. This was not the outcome he wanted. He slid his jacket from the back of the chair and stepped to the door. He fingers were barely to the doorknob when the weight of her spun him against the wall, pressing and pinning him there. 'I’m sorry. Cal’… Justine, I’m so sorry. I didn’t mean to…' Jared found himself unable to speak. Her kiss was deep and passionate. 'Shut up,' she said softly, pulling away for just a moment. 'Just shut the fuck up.' She kissed him again. This time he kissed back. He knew better. He wanted to believe he knew better. The last time that he allowed this to happen a ten-year schism and a fight only minutes old were the result. Unfortunately, he was the thrall to an emotion designed to eliminate both logic and reason. His grip loosened and his coat slid to the floor. It was the first of many clothes to follow. ~~~~~~~~~~~ In the span of a mistake twice repeated He winced as a thin ray of sunlight snuck through a gap in the curtains, landing across the upper half of his pillow. A weak yet agitated groan signaled his displeasure at being pulled out of a restful slumber far too soon. Jared rolled onto his right side, reaching out with his left arm to where she lay, wanting to pull her close and enjoy these last few moments of blissful haze. All he felt was a cool sheet and empty space. He sat upright and opened his eyes, but recoiled as a dull ache sped through his temples. Gingerly he tried to rub away the pain and the sleep, but was only successful at the latter. 'Cal'?' he asked to the empty room, hoping that a voice unseen would answer. When no response came he begrudgingly swung his legs over the side of the bed and rose to his feet. The light in the bathroom had been left on, and at realizing this he felt a sense of hope sweep over him. It was quickly doused when he turned the corner and saw the room empty. It was only when he turned and surveyed the entirety of the space did the truth sink in. She was gone. She had left him. There would only be heartache to come.
