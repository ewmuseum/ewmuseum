The Perfect Family: The Lies We Tell

Date: Today Reunited, and It Don't Feel So Good What is it about hospitals that make them so damn disconcerting? They're supposed to be places of healing, places where doctors can practice their craft to make people better. Instead, people within these walls end up, more times than not, feeling worse when they leave. Maybe it's the meticulously pristine environs, bleached white by a flood of fluorescence. Perhaps it's the fact that the place is full of the sick, the injured, and the dying that helps. It was at the University of New Mexico's own hospital that Perfect Raymond Pierce awoke to the sound of bleeping machines and the soft voice of his daughter Samantha, somewhere nearby. '-- idea how we got so lucky,' Samantha was saying, though he saw no one nearby. 'But she always said that it's better to be lucky than good.' There was a short pause as Raymond stirred, blinking drowsily to drag himself out of unconsciousness. 'No, you're right -- it's almost always better to be lucky and good.' The Impeccable Warrior made a pained sound at the back of his throat, drawing his daughter's attention. She looked over her shoulder from her station by the window (it's devilishly difficult to get cell reception in a hospital). A brilliant smile ballooned onto her cherubic features as she held up a finger. A thin, candy apple red cellular phone was cradled against her ear. 'Dad finally woke up, Stace,' Sam said, her tone brightening up noticeably. 'No, I should be able to go on the boat trip. But I have to go now, okay? Mm-bye.' She closed the phone against her thigh as she slid it into a pocket of her jeans. Samantha rushed to the head of the elder Pierce's bed, brushing brown bangs from his face. 'Hey, you...' She smiled. 'You gave us quite the scare.' 'Nng. Did we win?' Pierce had teamed with Duncan Drake a day earlier at Uproar, a match that had ended when Duncan abandoned Raymond, watching from the rampway as the Perfect One was dropped sickly, headfirst, on the turnbuckle by Elliott Rollins. Raymond Pierce doesn't remember that, though. 'No, Daddy, you didn't,' Samantha said softly. Tears welled in the young girl's eyes. 'Man, I'm so sorry I made you talk to a jerkface like Duncan Drake... this is all my fault.' Raymond reached across his body, pulling all kinds of wires with him, to pat his daughter's hand. His smile was small, tight -- pained. 'It's okay, princess. Elliott Rollins is a scurrilous knave who was overwhelmed by the bright light of He Who...' Pierce devolved into a fit of coughing. 'Ah, you know the rest.' Samantha threw herself back into the chair by Raymond's bedside; her mood seemed to have brightened, just from his return to consciousness. 'I've been thinking about how we can get your revenge.' The elder Pierce stared up at the ceiling, shaking his head. 'Don't worry about that. Not right now.' 'But I've made a notebook!' 'I'm too tired to read it right now. Why isn't Joe Bob here? Tell him he's fired.' 'He went to request some videotapes for your next match. Well, that's where he said he was going. I think he went to hang out with that Eugene Ware guy, who's lurking in the cafeteria.' Pierce opened one eye. 'My... next match? But I can feel my brain poking the inside of my skull.' 'Nonsense,' Samantha smiled beatifically. 'You're Perfect Raymond Pierce! You're the Impeccable Warrior! Sure, your comeback hasn't been exactly... stellar... yet, but that was before you met me, and you don't get to go back to your nine-to-five yet! So I... put a book in for it. Did I use that word right?' She sighed, plucking at some lint on her leg. 'I mean to say I got you another match.' He turned his head towards the seventeen-year-old girl, seated prettily at the edge of the cushion of the plush chair. 'Y-... wait. You got me another match?' 'Mr. Duluth - sorry, he said I should call him 'Smitty' - he gave me a manager's license, remember?' 'But that was just so you could sit at ringside. Honey, I know you're trying to help, but, Daddy's got a really big headache now, and I don't think that the guy you got me a match against will enjoy it when a forty-year-old man teaches them what wrestling is all about. I think I should just... lay here... this week. Just for a little bit.' The girl turned her head to the side, looking away briefly. 'When I was a kid... I was a bad kid. Mom and I had it kinda rough, since she wouldn't tell you I existed.' Ray opened his mouth to speak, to defend himself; Samantha threw up her palms. 'I'm not saying anything bad, Dad. I'm just setting the scene. This one time, I was on a girl's softball team, and we lost. I mean, we got stomped. I wanted to give up softball; hell, I wanted to give up sports altogether. I just wanted to sit in my room and play Nintendo. Do you know what Mom did?' 'She got you into wrestling so you could follow your dear old dad's footsteps?' The girl laughed, leaning forward a little to rest her elbows on her knees. 'Almost. She signed me up for field hockey. And soccer. And basketball. Because you can't just run from your problems.' 'Actually, that works pretty well, from my experience. There was this time with Damon that he -- ' 'Dad.' It's not very often that a child scolded their parent. 'Can we be serious for a second? I know you need this. When you face these guys and come out on top, you'll feel better.' Her smile becomes cheeky, sort of impish. 'I daresay you'll feel Perfect.' There was a soft golf clap from the doorway, drawing both father and daughter's attention. Standing in the doorway was another man; a man with light brown hair, styled as meticulously as his suit, which was a blue so deep, it may as well have been black. The man's smile was thin and unamused; a storm lingered in his eyes. His name was Andrew Pierce; he was Raymond's little brother, a former NWC Tag Team Champion in his own right, and he was not a happy man. 'Brava, bravura,' Andrew said. 'Exceptional speech. Really, it was. I was almost moved to tears. The trouble is, this fucking farce is over. My brother is coming home.' *************** What Do Your Dreams Say About You? Raymond Pierce dreamt often and, moreover, unlike many people, Pierce had an impeccable memory (naturally), so he remembered most of them. The large majority of the dreams were, of course, visions of his own past glories. Victories over 'Sexy' Steve Sire, Damon Hayes, Soul Shadow, 'Peerless' Hunter Sabuani, Damon Hayes, Eugene 'Sparky' Robinson, and Damon Hayes... whether real or imagined, these moments tend to dominate the sleep of He Who Never Errs. The nights he hated, though, were those he dreamed of his sister, Alexandra. He would ream of her empty gray eyes, her clotted blood hair, and her smile... fuck he hated that smile. He would be having a pleasant dream (hey, he was a man, sometimes he would be having a very pleasant dream), and she would worm her way into his subconscious. He remembered dreaming about some coffee-skinned twins he'd seen on a boardwalk in South Beach, but the waitress at the restaurant in his dream wore Alex's smile. He recalled shopping for a new house (a better one than Andrew's), but, in his mind, his real estate agent had bloody red hair. In his nightmares, everywhere Raymond Pierce went every face was his sister. Everyone was Desade. The dreams had stopped for some time, stopped after he and Andrew finally, finally won, defeating their errant blood once and (he thought) for all, as long as they kept sending money. When he worked at Pierce Industries, that bill was the first one he paid, every month as his first order of business on the first day. It had stopped the nightmares, at least for a time. Lying there, in a hospital bed at UNM-H, Raymond Pierce imagined a nurse, thin as a rail in turquoise pants and a pretty flowered top changing his IV fluids. The girl had dark red hair, like waves of blood that fell to her shoulders. He envisioned waking up from a pleasant dream and his nurse looking over her shoulder. She smiled down at him with an expression devoid of caring, her empty gray eyes not even flickering a little as she opened a jagged knife and sliced neatly through his breathing tube. Raymond saw himself thrashing like he was watching from outside his body, unable to breathe as his sister looked on impassively. He found himself facedown in his pillow when he woke up. *************** And Now For a Comedic Interlude On the right, a long-faced man with a nervous expression picked at a plate of meatloaf. His name was Joe Bob Smith, and he was here because, God help him, the other man at the table was the only one who he talked to about his life. On the left sat a fat man with slick-backed hair, waving a chicken finger as if it were a pointer. His name was Eugene Ware, and he didn't come to these armchair psychiatry sessions to offer advice. He came because someone else was buying him food and he was a cheapskate. This cannot end well. Joe Bob and Eugene go back quite a long way, back to the days of the National Wrestling Council. There, they had some infamous tussles while Joe Bob's mentor/father figure/dictator of all things Raymond Pierce was teaming with Pierce's own nemesis Damon Hayes (whom Eugene was totally stalking at the time). 'I said,' said Eugene, jabbing the chicken at Joe Bob. Little gobbets of sauce (buffalo and ranch, both) dotted the table as they talked. 'That's great, but why aren't you sitting on my face? And she did! How great is that?' Eugene laughed, a braying sound. 'Yeah, it's great,' Joe Bob responded distractedly, one cheek buried in his hand. 'What's the matter, Jim Bob? You look like this cat I kicked once.' 'It's... it's Ray.' 'Man.' Ware blew out a sigh. 'That guy is pretty awesome, but you sure are up his ass a lot.' Some people eat one French fry at a time. Others were like Eugene Ware, for whom the term 'bite-sized' meant, 'Whatever would fit in my mouth.' 'It's just... he's just found out he has a kid, right?' 'I saw her. She's pretty hot. I mean she's pretty hot whenever it's legal for me to say that. So you have nubile teenaged flesh hanging around. What could possibly be wrong with that?' 'I don't know. It's just... he has this tendency to overcompensate for things and, don't get me wrong, it's great that he has something real in his life that he can shoot for. I just hope he doesn't overdo it.' 'You want my advice?' 'Not really.' Joe Bob looked away, towards the elevators, like he knew he should go check on his mentor. 'Tough shit, you're getting it anyway. My advice is... buy smaller towels for Ray's place.' Smith looked back, raising bushy eyebrows. 'What?' 'You know, so when she's done taking a shower, her towel barely covers that pert little apple bottom, and then she has to bend over and you can see her hootenanny.' 'She's still just seventeen, Eugene.' 'Really? Are you sure? I would've thought she'd have turned twenty by now. It feels like I've been imagining her naked for so long.' 'I'm sure. She hasn't aged at all in the last five minutes. It's only been twenty-four hours since you last saw her.' Ware waved a hand through the air. 'Doesn't matter, really, except as a preview of what I'll be wanking it to later. My point, I guess, is don't take all this shit seriously all the damn time. Either it works out and you slip her the Little Joe when she turns eighteen so you can be part of the family, or it doesn't and I'll let the Ware-aconda take a chunk out of her.' 'Uh. Thanks, I guess.' Joe Bob Smith turned his head slightly to the side, staring at his friend-turned-enemy-turned-friend with some amazement. 'No problem!' Ware said, dunking a hunk of fried chicken meat into some buffalo sauce. 'Bringing the perspective is what I do. I ever tell you they want me to write a self-help book? Shut the Fuck Up and Ten Other Easy Lessons I Learned: A Guide to Life, by Eugene Ware. Oprah wants me - on her show and for reals. True story.' 'JOE BOB SMITH!' The booming voice of the head of Pierce Industries security caused Raymond Pierce's assistant to jump, diving under the table. 'Don't tell him I'm here!' Smith begged. 'Psycho' Sam Leever, some six-foot-eleven and still over three hundred pounds of muscle well into his thirties, stalked through the hospital cafeteria, wild and dark hair streaming behind him. Leever (tag-team champion with Andrew Pierce almost a decade ago) was a whirlwind through plastic chairs and Formica tables, stopping in front of the spot where Eugene Ware was enjoying his juicy, juicy chicken bites. 'Ware?' 'That's me,' Ware said, his mouth full. 'You will tell me where Joe Bob Smith is, or you will choke on bits of fried food.' 'I don't have any idea where he is,' Eugene said. He pointed straight down to the tabletop, however. Leever pulled Joe Bob Smith out from beneath the table by his shirt collar; the long-faced man pointed a thick finger at the FUSE color commentator. 'Traitor!' Joe Bob spat. 'Benedict Arnold!' 'Yeah, yeah,' Eugene responded, dunk-dunk-dunking his chicken. 'You'll get over it. Remember: shorter towels!' Joe Bob just glared at the fat man as he was dragged away, Leever like a caveman with his prize. Eugene swirled his piece of chicken through the air towards his mouth. 'Here comes the airplane! Heeeeere comes the airplane! Vrooooom!' *************** Stacey's Mom (Has Got It Going On) Three days after Andrew Pierce and Sam Leever crashed the party at the UNM Hospital, Raymond and Samantha Pierce had settled back into life in the confines of the elder Pierce's palatial Florida estate, the only difference in their lives being the presence of Andrew Pierce's security detail and the man formerly known as 'The Hurricane' abjectly refusing to let his brother even consider wrestling Andre Jensen and Cyrus Raynes in just four days. 'Why doesn't he just make you quit?' Samantha asked, curling her legs beneath her on Raymond's leather couch. The younger Pierce wore beaten, faded jean shorts and an old, oversized white t-shirt she wore tucked down around her knees. The shirt read 'LSW - Lone Star Wrestling' in red lettering and was clearly not hers. 'Because I still own fifty percent of Pierce Industries.' Like many men his age, Raymond Pierce had a favorite recliner, deep brown and with a divot where his rear-end was usually planted, as it was right then. 'Then why don't you tell him to - pardon my French - fuck off?' 'Because he still owns fifty percent of Pierce Industries. It's a delicate balance. Like a dance, only no one knows the steps.' She tucked a lock of light brown hair behind her ear. 'It seems weird that you let him run the business.' 'I've never had cause not to.' An impeccable shrug lifts his shoulders. 'Andrew's got a head for that kind of stuff - all I know how to do is win championships.' Samantha grinned, cheeky and bright. 'He's only got, what, one? I can see why he retired. He...' Chimes rang in from the foyer. Well. Not chimes, though it was set to music. 'Go---A---Waaaay.' Imagine the NBC chimes - with the little peacock? Then put in those two words in place of the bells. That was Raymond Pierce's doorbell. The Impeccable Warrior himself frowned at the sound of the bell. 'Who is bothering me at this hour?' 'It's, like, six o'clock in the evening. Don't be such a fuddy-duddy.' Samantha glanced towards the door, sliding up from the couch. 'Let me get it.' 'No.' Ray flipped the wooden lever at his side, lowering the footrest. 'It's my home and I will not have my daughter play maid. That's what we have Eric Rage for. Unfortunately, today is Eric's day off.' Raymond walked across the room, flipping on the hall light and opening the door. 'What do you...?' On the other side of the door was a woman in a navy blue dress with a scooped neck and knee-high black boots with dull silver heels. Her black hair was tinged red along the roots, swaying haphazardly in front of her ginormous brown sunglasses. She smiled broadly for Raymond as he opens the door. 'You must be Samantha's dad,' she said by way of introduction. 'She said you were quite a character.' Pierce protectively clutched the door, keeping the woman from coming into his abode. 'And you are?' he asked, his brows knitting in lieu of his arms folding. 'I'm sorry.' She extended a hand; Pierce did not take it. 'Elisabeth Richmond, Stacey's mother? You can just call me Liz. I led the boat trip that Sam was supposed to go on last weekend.' Samantha poked her head around the corner into the foyer. 'Ms. Richmond? Is that you?' Liz rocked forward onto her tiptoes, looking over Raymond's shoulder. 'Yes, it's me! You left your sunglasses in my car, and I figured since I was going to be in the neighborhood, I'd just drop them off.' Samantha came down the hallway, shoving her father out of the way. 'Dad, quit being such a dick. Liz is kinda totally awesome, and you should be nice to her.' Pierce glowered, but he stepped back. 'Say, Samantha,' Liz said, stepping past Ray into the room with a smile. 'Stace's friend Megan had tickets to a concert tonight, but she's come down with the flu. I'd love it if you came.' There's only one thing like a teenage girl's smile when she wants something from her father; it's all sugar and spice and everything nice, just like the song goes. Combine it with the puppy dog eyes and most fathers are doomed, unless they've had years of practice in resisting it. Perfect Raymond Pierce had known he was a father for about two weeks and was, thus, utterly doomed. 'Daddy, can I go? Pleeeease?' 'I don't know. Is this thing chaperoned?' 'Well, I'll be there,' Liz said smoothly. Pierce gave a doubtful look to the woman's wardrobe. 'And there will be at least two other parents besides me.' 'Well...' 'Pleasepleasepleasepleasepleasepleaseplease?' Samantha put in. 'Okay, then. Just make sure you're home by, like, 9:30 or whatever your mom said would be a good time.' 'Absolutely. We still have to figure out how to sneak you out of here and get you to Tucson!' Samantha brushed past her father, padding quickly for the guest room, now for all intents and purposes her room. 'Let me get changed quickly and - Dad, could you come here for a second?' 'Of course,' Pierce said. He turned to the other woman. 'Don't think you can take anything. I have a security system in place that monitors the precise location of everything in my home.' The Pierces met in the hallway. 'What's wrong?' Raymond asked. 'If you don't want to go, just say the word, and I'll throw that ho out of my...' 'She's pretty hot, isn't she?' '...What?' 'Ms. Richmond. Liz. She's pretty hot.' Samantha padded on to her room, her woebegone father trailing in her wake. 'You can go ahead and say it. You don't have to worry about me being too young to hear it.' The Impeccable Warrior turned over his shoulder, looking back at the woman, marveling at the championship belt replicas in his trophy case (all eleven of them). He shrugged his shoulders. 'She's... yeah, I guess she's attractive.' 'You should ask her out.' Samantha was surely grinning as she was changing, that was clear from her tone, although Ray couldn't see her. 'She's divorced, but she's awesome, and she's not bitter about it or anything.' 'I'll... I'll think about - I'm not really ready to date, Sam.' 'You're never going to 'be ready'. At least let me invite her and Stace to the show.' 'But Andrew...' 'Remember what I said about pardoning my French? Tell him to do that.' Raymond sighed. 'I'll think about it.' 'Okay. That's all I ask.' Samantha reappeared, having changed into cigarette-leg blue jeans and a peach-colored, spaghetti-stringed top, grabbing her leather jacket from its hook on the wall by her bedroom door. She rocked up to her tippy-toes to press a kiss to her father's cheek. 'Bye, Daddy,' she said. Samantha darted for the door, passing Liz on her way. The other woman turned, smiling for Raymond. 'Good night, Mr. Pierce.' 'Raymond,' he found himself saying. 'Ray.' 'Goodnight, Ray,' Liz said with a little curl to her smile. 'You have a lovely home.' 'Thank you,' Raymond said with a tight, uncomfortable smile. 'Go Away.' *************** Matthew 4:19 Liz walked (casually meandered, really) down the stone walkway outside Raymond Pierce's estate, her black Coach bag dangling carelessly from one hand. She never looked back as she walked, not even once, even though she knew he was watching. The first step to being a good fisherman, her father always told her, is to never let the fish know there's a boat on the water or a hook in the sea. When she got to the end of the walk, Liz stopped, bending one knee slightly to rest her pocketbook on. She tossed her black and red hair to one side as she dug for her car keys. Framed by the white pillars at the end of the walk, the sequins in her blue dress catching the sunset, she chanced a smile back towards the house. A curtain rustled; a shadow retreated. Liz's smile stretched further when she turned her head back towards the car, cat-like. An onlooker might have even caught the tip of her tongue between her teeth. 'Come on, Ms. Richmond!' Samantha sounded impatient, leaning against Liz's slick, sleek black Lexus SC convertible. 'We'll be late!' 'Girl...' Liz produced the key, opening the locks by remote. 'You know I'm late for everything.' Samantha tossed another wave to the door, where Raymond was, Liz knew, still watching, before the teenager tossed herself bodily into the car as only a teenager could. Liz slipped into the car slower, more gracefully. The dying sunlight glinted off the silver of her bootheel as her foot snaked into the vehicle. 'Liz,' Samantha said as she popped open the door to the glove compartment. 'Are you trying to seduce my dad?' She favored Liz with a wide-eyed, innocent expression as they pulled away from the curb. Liz sat up in her seat as the convertible drifted to a halt at a stop sign, brushing at her bangs in the rearview mirror. 'Why?' she asked. 'Do you think he believed I was really interested?' Samantha retrieved a small, green and white box from the recesses of the compartment: a package of Virginia Slim 100s. She packed the box against the heel of her hand with a sharp grin. The second step to being a good fisherman is that one must cast a great number of nets. This rule was taught to both Liz and Samantha, but not by their fathers. 'Why not?' she said, tugging one of the cigarettes with her lips. 'He's fucking bought all the other bullshit I've been feeding him.' This cannot end well, either.
