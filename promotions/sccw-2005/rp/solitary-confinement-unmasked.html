Solitary Confinement (Unmasked)

Date: Various Lately, it seemed, the road was a lonely place to be. For the better part of six months he’d traveled on his own, afraid to put his friends back in harm’s way. It wasn’t safe anymore. Maybe it never was. Rochester had been proof enough of that. The thought had occurred to him that perhaps it had all been nothing more than a remarkable string of good fortunate, but ever since the events of the previous Thanksgiving Jared Sykes wasn’t planning on testing that luck again anytime soon. Mervin wasn’t the only one he refused to travel with. Lloyd "Violent Jesus" Sullivan had been sidelined as well, and though Lloyd was considerably stronger than Mervin and capable of handling himself, even his role had been passed on to a stand-in. What would people think, he wondered, if I told them Ultimo Pancakes worked a camera when not on screen. It wasn’t just the road that stirred these feelings in him; Sin City itself seemed different. Though the overall climate was much milder than in months past, there was no one on the roster who he connected with, no one that he could talk to, not since Charley left. He found no friends there, only a collection of people who tolerated him to varying degrees out of professional courtesy. Lately, he thought, life in general felt pretty lonely. At the end of the day, when he packed his bags and went home, sure he had friends to occupy his time, but what was he going home to? Jonathan had Katie. Charley had Leyla. Even Alex and Amy had each other, if you believed the rumors. And Jared? He had… It was a thought that for the past few weeks had kept him awake at night. Lying motionless in bed, counting the ridges of the hotel room ceiling until his eyes lost focus, and then his mind would start to wander. Every night he would lie there, and every night he would invariably come back to the singular thought around which his mind had been consumed since he saw her through a window. He’d convinced himself that it was hopeless. She’s married, he’d tell himself, or at the very least is involved in a serious relationship. There’s no room for me. He also knew that inevitably he’d need to move beyond his own fears and insecurities and confront her, if only to give some closure to the idea of what could have been. One day had turned into two, then three, and then a week. Gradually the weeks had passed, and still he’d done nothing. Uncertainty had turned into self-doubt, which had blossomed into fear, and no amount of waiting did anything but reinforce in his own subconscious that it was already too late. A blueberry mask only lets you hide from so much. Tomorrow, he’d tell himself. The next time I’m home I’ll go and say hi. Fifty tomorrows had turned into yesterdays. ------------------------------------------- 11 years ago He stood against the wall taking it all in. He’d always imagined the places where wrestlers trained to be more impressive than this. Sure there was a ring, some mats on the floor, even a few heavy bags and some free weights, but it all just seemed so mundane. For someone as normally mouthy as Jared, new places always made him uneasy, so when more people started to file in over time he gradually slinked to the back of the pack where he could be alone. He wasn’t the only one who felt this way. "Hey," she said softly, taking a spot against the wall a few feet away from him. "Not quite what I expected, you know?" "Yeah, I know what you mean." She was cute, he thought, definitely not the type of person you’d expect to see wandering around a place like this. He was almost ashamed that he felt a little sexist wondering what she was doing here at all. This was a place for men to get sweaty. He turned against the wall, a thousand questions running through his mind. In truth he’d never been very good around girls, even when he was at school and relatively popular. The opportunity though was too good to pass up, and if nothing else would give him a great story. "Oh, fuck," he said, looking past her to the pair of figures who stepped out from a room at the far end of the training space. It was not quite the first impression he had hoped to make. "I’m sorry?" He gestured with his head at the shorter of the two men approaching the new batch of students, his face a mask of displeasure. At the other end of the room Gabriel Simon had spotted him. When Jared gave a half-hearted wave, Gabriel just scowled in response. "I know that guy. He was a year ahead of me in high school." "Oh, that’s cool that you know someone. That’s better than me, at least." "He’s a dick." "Oh." "I’d bet you anything that if they need volunteers to beat on, I’ll be the first one they call up." "Nah, I doubt that. Why would he make you look bad?" He thought about telling her of the time when, during a varsity wrestling practice, Gabriel had pulled his right arm out of the socket. Ultimate he thought better of it. She was cute, after all. No sense in looking weak in front of the cute girl. "We’ll see," he said instead, sliding his hands into his pockets. "Hey, what’s…" he started, before a bellow from the taller of the two men, Darren Stracker, drew the attention of the room. "Everybody over by me, now!" and the room wasted no time in responding. "I guess this is it," Jared mused, pushing himself off the wall. A tug at his sleeve brought him to his halt. "I’ll go with you," she said, loosening her grip. "You’re the only person I know." Two hours later Jared found himself against that same wall, only this time he was seated, sweat had pooled under both arms and around his collar, and his left eye had started to swell. "Wow, I can’t believe he actually did that," she’d hung by him most of the session, save for the few times he was called away to serve in a demonstration, and then again now to get an ice pack. She knelt as she offered it to him, taking a seat on the ground nearby. "I tried to tell you that he’s a dick," he joked, hoping the ice would help stave off the swelling. For someone who’d just been punched in the face, he seemed to be taking it remarkably well. "And I totally should have made that bet." She laughed, though whether it was out of pity or because she thought he was funny he couldn’t tell. "What would you have asked for?" His eyebrow (the one that wasn’t slowly swelling and covered in ice) arched at the question. His mind started spinning, the gears going into overdrive dreaming up the possibilities. For someone as inept with the opposite sex as Jared, the question couldn’t have been better. Unfortunately, his answer was seriously lacking. "I dunno, probably something stupid." And then it occurred to him, they’d been talking on and off the last two hours, and he didn’t even know her name. "Wow, I’m sorry," he continued, his face reddening, "I’m such an ass. You’ve been so cool to me, and I don’t even know your name." "Justine," she said, "Justine Calvin. A lot of people just call me Cal’." He shifted the ice pack to his other hand, and extended his right hand to shake. "I’m Jared Sykes. Most people just call me Jared." It was a terrible joke, and he knew it, but as they talked the mounting pain in his left eye seemed less and less significant. "What does he call you?" she asked, gesturing in the direction of Gabriel. "Things I can’t repeat in public." She laughed again, and the pain faded. More and more he was beginning to enjoy her company, but beyond that he felt comfortable, less insecure, and there was no need to put on a show. It was a far cry from what he’d been used to. It could have been something special. ------------------------------------------- Lately, she thought, it was getting hard to concentrate. The students never noticed, or if they did they were certainly weren’t saying anything. They found her scary enough on her best days. No reason to make her any angrier than absolutely necessary. Their friendship had started eleven years ago in part because he’d been punched square in the face, the more she thought about him, and the fact that he’d been to the gym where she worked and it had been kept a secret made her want to put a modern spin on that experience. She wasn’t even sure how she felt about him anymore. Ten years ago there were feelings, but that was before he disappeared off the face of the earth, before he went on the road and never looked back. And while she wondered what it would be like to see him walk through the door, secretly she didn’t even know what she’d say. Why did he disappear? Why did he never reach out? Why would he hide the fact that he’d been showing up on days he knew she wouldn’t be in? She’d glance over her shoulder towards the door, most times trying to hide it from prying eyes, and at the end of each day she’d think maybe tomorrow. Fifty tomorrows had turned into yesterdays.
