Feed the Animals, Act Two: Fairweather Friends

Date: Whenever. ONE. The Favorite Son. To call him a friend would be nothing short of ludicrous. More like a rival or an enemy, an acquaintance who I would rather kill than keep in touch with. But even so, that didn’t stop me from going out for a beer with him whenever I was in town. His name was John Dent, though he preferred to be called Johnny. He was Manchester, Tennessee’s favorite son. While the rest of my generation went on to build meth labs in their trailer parks or sit on their front porches, drinking from dusk until dawn, Johnny Dent grew up to be a success. Good grades, eight years in college, every award you can think of. While the rest of us were fucking our lives away, Johnny was off making something of himself. I won’t lie and say I wasn’t at least a bit jealous of him. We sat in a bar in Nashville, drinking bottle after bottle, catching up on old times beneath the wail of a blues guitar. With a cross necklace hanging from his neck and his balding head covered by a Yankees cap, he coughed and cursed the rain outside, saying it gave him a real bad cold. Said that even though he felt like hell, he couldn’t pass up a chance to meet up with the one and only Sean Sterling. He’d grown fat, spoiled by his own riches. Even though he was ugly as shit, he’d managed to score a trophy wife; a real young piece of ass, ten years his minor. That wasn’t all he had going for him, though. He had it all. The house in the hills, the white picket fence, the newborn baby, the puppy running in the front yard. The so-called American Dream. "Listen," He told me, between sips of the cheapest beer he could get away with buying. "Sean, I want you to know, even though we never saw eye to eye on most things, I just want you to know, man, I love you like a brother. I love you like a goddamn brother." He put his arm across my shoulder, his drunken stupidity making him sneer and laugh, smelling of sweat and alcohol. I just nodded, "Yeah, man. Yeah." The stray dogs howled at the moon outside, the rain came tapping down on the streets below, and I called the bartender for another shot of whiskey. Best of times and worst of times. You know how it goes. --- TWO. Trading Liquor for Blood. We stumbled down the sidewalk in a drunken bliss, with John still mumbling out liquor lacedwords of adoration, and me still nodding in some half-assed agreement. The city shined brighter than the stars in the sky, neon lights flooding the alleyways and the gutters in a flood of color. "Listen," John muttered, grinning from ear to ear like a serial killer who just got away with murder. "Sean, you know, I mean, you know… I know I’m more successful than you and… I know you’re an insect in the corporate world, I mean, compared to me and all, but, y’know, I mean, y’know, I envy you, man." I stopped dead in my tracks, cocking an eyebrow and looking at him like he was insane. Involuntarily, my hands were already balling into fists. "How do you mean?" "I mean, y’know, you’re pretty worthless, man. You’ve got no reason to live, no one to live for, no real significance in this world. I mean, you just float along, man. Go with the flow. Bid sweet fuck all to everything. It has to feel good to be so insignificant, yeah?" I gritted my teeth, feeling the veins in the top of my hands pulsing with some unexplainable anger. And he laughed. He laughed like he hadn’t said anything wrong. He laughed like he didn’t insult me, at all. He laughed and grinned and patted my shoulder, saying, "Man, we should do this more often." There on the cracked sidewalk, with the neon lights and the porn palaces standing like towers of sin alongside us, I could only muster up one thing to say, "Fuck you, man." I caught his jaw with my fist, sending him spiraling down to the sidewalk, with a busted lip and a look of surprise. Without missing a beat, without sparing a word, he pushed himself to his feet and threw his own right hand, catching me in the gut and causing me to drunkenly stumble back against a brick wall. I tried to muster up some witty come back, but ended up just lurching over and puking into the gutter while he wailed on me with weak punches that somehow sent shockwaves through my drunken veins. I don’t remember what happened next. A whirlwind blur of neon lights and bloody fists, with John Dent walking off to call a taxi and me crawling off to pass out in a nearby alleyway, bleeding from the forehead and feeling an ungodly hangover building up in the pit of my stomach. When morning came, I cracked open my eyes to the sight of the sunlight glaring into the alleyway, the smell of piss and garbage surrounding me, cars all whizzing by and people walking like they held some sort of authority. I stumbled out of the alleyway and nearly fell back down on the sidewalk, sick to my stomach with a headache like the Second Coming of Christ bursting and burning. Call it an overreaction, call me blinded by anger, but suddenly, John Dent’s head would have looked very nice on a pike in my front yard. Gritting my teeth, balling my fist, walking down the sun swept streets, I grinned a little to myself. Three cheers for sweet revenge. --- I am a terrible person. This is fact, this is known, no surprises there. But, just for the record, just for the sake of being clear, I do the things I do for the good of society. The world needs something shocking in this day in age. Without someone like me, what would keep the good guys good? Who else is going to keep the world in line? I’ve seen the lowest lows and the highest highs. I’ve crawled through the back alleys at dawn, coughing and wheezing, looking for an angry fix of whatever drug I could get my hands on. I’ve been the center of attention in mindless parties, celebrated for nothing and loved for anything. I’ve been everything and nothing, the brightest star in the lowest pits of hell. Call me what you will and say what you want about me, but don’t kid yourself. You know you’d be the same as me, if you could get away with it. I’m everything you want to be and more. I do the things people wish they could do, and I do it better than they could ever dream. Yeah, I’m a terrible person. But then again, aren’t we all? --- THREE. The Trophy Wife. Her name was Brenda. Blonde hair. Blue eyes. An affection for vodka in bars she’d never step foot in, unless you told her to charmingly enough. Your typical gold digging slut. Easy lay, but not much else. All the beauty in the world couldn’t make her worth my time. The thought of revenge, however, could. I grinned at her from across the table, offering a wink and downing another shot of vodka, asking her as if I was actually interested, "So, you’re John’s wife, yeah? Good guy, good guy. We’ve had our arguments in the past, but he’s always been alright in my book." "Yeah, John’s okay," She replied between sips of a White Russian. "I mean, he’s not the most charming man in the world, but we get by." "Hey, we’ve all got to make due with whatever God gave us," The flash of a grin, my every orifice oozing with the sort of bewitching allure that I only dusted off in my most desperate attempts for pussy. "For example, God gave you an upper hand when you put you together. While he was giving the rest of us scraps from the trash bin, he must have slaved over you for weeks. You’re nearly perfect." She smiled, blushed, bit her lower lip. All the usual flirtations. "Thanks, Sean. You’re sweet." "I try." Revenge schemes are best put together with a mind as wicked as all the demons in hell. You have to think out every single way to destroy a person, to disassemble and decimate them. It’s a lot of work, but it always pays off in the best of ways. This was step one. Seduce his wife, take her home, have her buried in my bed sheets by the end of the night. All you needed to was a particularly stupid woman, enough liquor to make an alcoholic go to puking, and the comfiest bed you could get your hands on. Simple. "Listen," I practically purred, leaning across the table just enough to force her to look directly in my eyes. "What say we get out of here? I’m staying in this hotel up the street with all the luxuries of this place and only half the filth." She smiled, raising a drunken eyebrow, "What kind of girl you take me for, Mr. Sterling?" "Simple," I replied, in a matter-of-factly way. "The type of girl who would never turn down a drink with a gentleman willing to provide them." "You hit the nail on the head," She replied, biting her lower lip and standing, grabbing her purse as I began to lead her toward the door. "You better have vodka." "Brenda," I replied, grinning over my shoulder. "I have more vodka that God has angels." --- FOUR. Any Port in a Storm. Her nails down my back. Her sweat on my skin. Her lips on my neck. Her voice rising all the way to Kingdom Come. I grinned to myself, pushing in and out, maybe even laughing a little beneath my breath. Not because of what a fool this girl was making of herself, not because of how easily she was being used for my own selfish purposes, but because she had no idea, whatsoever. It went on for hours, the empty bottles of vodka left forgotten on the kitchen table, the sounds of a televised war blaring in the room next door. A dead moon hung in the sky outside, all the stars fading in and out as the clouds drifted and doled on with their wretched existence. From the balcony of the hotel, you could see off all Nashville. From the neon streets where country music still had a name for itself to the dirty alleyways where junkies dragged themselves through the dirt, needles in each arm and no trace of their previous lives. As the city wept, decadent and depraved, Brenda Dent was falling for a man who didn’t love her, not even in the most miniscule of ways. As she laid beneath me, gasping into the pillow, gripping at the sheets, in absolute bliss, she couldn’t have even begun to suspect what was really going on. Ignorance is bliss, I suppose. When it was all over, we found ourselves standing on the balcony, smoking, wrapped in dirty blankets and still gasping for air. She was all star bright and tongue tied, grinning to herself, giggling like a schoolgirl who just had her first kiss. I went on with the act, smirking knowingly down towards her, smoke escaping from my nostrils. "So," I said in a voice as seductive as Prince Charming himself. "I bet John never did anything like that, did he?" Wordless, she shook her head, giggling and sighing, the perfect picture of a woman satisfied. As the night grew darker still, all the lights of Nashville casting a halo over my head, I wondered if she’d even considered who else had been in the same position as her. I wondered if she even considered the things I could, and would do, to ruin her, just for the sake of getting revenge over something as silly as a drunken fist fight. I wonder if she could even begin to consider my real intentions. We’re all born mad. Some remain so. --- FIVE. Confessions and Consequences. I sat at a café, watching the world pass by, sipping on coffee and waiting for my food to arrive. Brenda had left in the morning, still giggling and grinning, more enamored than she had been in a long time. When she left, I dropped the act and rolled my eyes, stumbling off to bed to fall asleep for a couple of hours. No dreams, nightmares or otherwise. Just a peace at mind that I hadn’t felt in years. Somewhere in the city, Brenda Dent was making a confession to her loving husband, tears streaming down her face as John’s eyes went wide, his mouth hanging over. I could see the look on his face. I could feel the horror and the anger pulsing through his veins. Maybe he was right. Maybe I am insignificant, compared to him. Maybe I am an insect, waiting to be squashed by a world I never asked to live in. All the alcohol in the world couldn’t excuse him for telling me that to my face, though. Call it jealousy, call it some half-assed envy, but I'll be the first to admit that this had all been building up since childhood. This wasn't just about a drunken insult, this was about a life in his shadow. What he failed to understand, however, was how easily I could bring him down to my level. What he didn’t realize was just how easy it really is to destroy someone’s life. He didn't know just how far I would go to prove a point. But what I didn’t realize was how it wasn’t quite over yet… Nothing is ever that easy, is it?
