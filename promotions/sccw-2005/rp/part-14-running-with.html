Date: Today The Farmer And The Viper Once upon a time, there were two men who hated each other very, very much. Their names were Ibrahim Seck and Edward Lambert. They had numerous battles between them in the ring, but behind that was always the threat of something worse. Ibrahim wanted to 'save' Edward Lambert, in the same way he would 'save' Wyatt Connors later on. He also wanted to 'save' Edward's family in the same way. Lambert--known in the wrestling community as the hyper-violent BloodAngel--did his best to end these threats, but Seck was nothing if not persistent. In the end, their matter was not settled in the ring, as Seck suffered a career-ending injury while fighting a wholly different crusade. However, Seck found a way to continually harass Lambert, even from outside the spotlight. It had gone too far--even for BloodAngel, a man who used 'too far' as a starting point. That is why, six years ago, Lambert kidnapped Seck and locked him in a secret cell in his basement. But of course nothing lasts forever, especially when they pertain to professional wrestlers. Six months ago, Ibrahim Seck woke to find that his cell was no longer locked. He never knew who had done it, nor had he asked--he merely thanked divine providence and headed for the hills. But where could he go? His followers were gone; without their master, they were sheep left to the wolves...and besides, anywhere he could think to go, Lambert would have been waiting for him. Unless he went farther back. Before BloodAngel...before wrestling. Back when his ministry was just beginning. It took him two months, hobbled by a bad leg and addled by madness, but he eventually made his way to a small farming community a few miles from Ponca City, Oklahoma. The people there greeted him with open arms for the second time in their lives; they had been his congregation when he had first joined the service of the Lord. They offered him a place to rest, warm food, hot baths, and unbeknownst to them, protection from the thing he feared most. He repaid them in full, of course. He removed the worries and cares of the world, leaving only thoughts of spiritual fulfillment and devotion. True, he used tainted Kool-Aid and brainwashing techniques to do it...but a mind is a small price to pay for salvation. Soon, the word had spread among those seeking spiritual enlightenment, and the congregation grew, and the Decency Compound became a paradise, free of woe, want, and pesky little things like independent thought. That all came to a halt a few weeks ago, when Seck's appointed messenger, Wyatt Connors, told him that Edward Lambert was still looking for him...and that he was close. Reverend Ibrahim Seck stared into the mirror and wondered what happened to those carefree days, when he was bringing sinners into the embrace of his big brother Jesus. His reflection looked worse than it ever had before--sure, the skin was finally healing and most of his hair had grown back...but there was one word, one emotion, written into every single line on his face. Fear. Ever since he received that horrible news, panic clutched at his heart like a drowning man to driftwood. No man deserved to be put through hell twice, let alone the second Son of God. He thought of his flock; the people who had stayed in this miserable corner of America after he left, going purely on faith. He thought of Mavis Jessup, whose home he lived in. The first person among them to truly believe in him...and whose indefatigable spirit had led him back home. He thought of all those wonderful, pious people...and how he'd sacrifice every last one of them if it meant keeping the Scarred Man at bay. 'The boy,' he whispered to the man in the mirror. 'The boy is the key. But how do I find him? It's been so long...' 'You took precautions, didn't you?' came the answer. That answer might have come from him, the mirror, a voice inside his head...to tell the truth, he couldn't be certain. Not that it mattered. 'Of course. We were watching him...but it's been so long. After what Bl...what B--what he did, I lost track. I lost track of them all...' 'Have faith in your chosen followers, Reverend. They have faith in you.' 'Yes, I...yes. Thank you. Of course.' It was possible that the boy was still being watched. It was just a matter of finding out for certain. 'Yes, I sent someone to watch the boy. It was...who was it?' Seck tried to remember, but the information was frustratingly elusive. 'It must have been...oh, damn! Don't you remember?' he snapped at his reflection. It gave no reply, but something else did: a soft knock came from the door. 'I AM NOT TO BE INTERRUPTED!' Seck yowled, but the door opened anyway, revealing the fresh face and glazed eyes of Jimmy Dawes, the latest recruit of the Decency Crusade. 'My humble apologies, Master,' he said, 'but Sister Mavis has prepared your breakfast.' The Reverend blinked, as if he had to try harder to process the words. Once their meaning had dawned on him, he nodded politely. 'Ah. Bring it in, by all means.' Having devoted several days to winding up his paranoia, he had not realized how hungry he was. Brother James (that's Neophyte to you SCCW fans) brought in a tray, containing four pieces of dry toast and three large glasses of red Kool-Aid. Ibrahim greedily clutched one of the glasses and poured it down his throat, as the liquid within overflowed and spilled out of the corners of his mouth. He sighed with satisfaction--things always seemed much clearer after slaking his thirst. 'Thank you, Brother James. May His blessing be upon you.' 'Thank you, Master,' James answered, in a very flat voice. Seck took a bite of his toast, and James turned to leave. Before the young man had exited completely, Seck called him back. 'Cuff oo femn imn Buvva Wiya, peez?' 'Excuse me?' Ibrahim swallowed, and tried again. 'Could you send in Brother Wyatt, please? I have an important task for him, and I need to speak with him straight away.' 'Of course, Master,' James replied, and disappeared through the door. Ibrahim munched on another piece of toast. Old memories were starting to take shape...men he used to know, important assignments they had been given...the answer was there, somewhere. All he needed was to find out who had it...and how to contact that person. He picked up the second glass of Kool-Aid and took a sip; as he did so, the fog of his memory seemed to lighten even more. It wasn't one of his most trusted followers, he knew that. 'Calvin, maybe? No, Calvin would have been no help...handy when it came to the wrestling business, but otherwise useless.' 'What about Scott?' came the helpful voice again. It was beginning to sound like him, so he chalked it up to thinking out loud. Seck chuckled at the thought of his old friend, a reformed Liverpudlian soccer hooligan. 'No...Scott never would have left my side for long enough to do the job.' 'One of the others? Richard? Mortimer? Wilhelm?' 'Wilh...WILHELM! That's it! Oh, you marvelous provider of dialogue, you've done it!' 'Master?' Seck almost answered, before realizing that it was someone else's voice. Then it occurred to him that the speaker was addressing him, so he should answer anyway. 'Yes? Come in.' The door creaked open, and a man stepped through. It was 'False Prophet' Wyatt Connors, ELITE Champion and the man who was chosen to deliver the great Reverend Seck's message to the people. He was dressed simply, in a white short-sleeved shirt and black trousers. He held a copy of the Holy Bible in his hands. 'You sent for me, sir?' he asked, with a hint of trepidation in his voice. His last few meetings with the Master had not gone well. 'Brother Wyatt, I have had a revelation. A message that needs to be spread. Please direct me to Sister Mavis' telephone.' Connors looked around, nervously. He couldn't lie to the Reverend, but telling the truth was just as unpalatable. 'I'm afraid I have some bad news, Master. Brother Hiram and Sister Mavis do not have a telephone.' 'What?!' Ibrahim shouted, causing Wyatt to cover his head instinctively. 'What do you mean, they don't have a telephone? That's preposterous!' 'As you say, Master. But they do not have one. The nearest available phone is at the McCoy residence, a mile or so from here.' 'I know where it is!' he snapped, even as his faulty memory banks reminded him that he should have known all this already. Wyatt offered, 'If you like, I can take you there. It would be no trouble at--' 'No, no, that's all right,' Ibrahim answered, maybe a little quicker than he should have. There had to be another way...one that didn't involve leaving the house. He couldn't risk getting caught out in the open, not with Edward out there looking for him...not to mention the members of the Decency Compound. How would they feel about a messiah that couldn't walk? 'Do you have one of those...cellular phones?' 'No, sir,' Wyatt answered, 'but many of our members had them when they arrived, before they divested themselves of their possessions. I believe they are still on the grounds, although they might be difficult to find.' 'Excellent. I would like for you to procure one for me. Use your trusted assistants if you have to, but I need it today. Understood?' 'Yes, Master.' 'Good. Now, go. And may His blessing be upon you.' 'Thank you.' Wyatt bowed his head respectfully, and left the room. Ibrahim smiled. A plan formulated in his brain...one that would allow him to defeat the forces of evil, once and for all. Somewhere out there--possibly just a few miles away--Edward Lambert was looking for Ibrahim Seck, planning to return him to captivity. And the Second Son of God would be damned if he'd let that happen. Kindness is wasted on evil. # # # The Frogs Who Desired A King Once upon a time--specifically, a year ago--there was a man named Allan Bowdry. He had been working as a farmhand (using the name 'Seth' for some unknown reason) and, to his estimation, he hated Wyatt Connors more than anyone. He was wrong about that, of course. He wasn't even in the top thousand...but that's beside the point. The point is, Seth hated Wyatt Connors a whole lot, and spent many a day dreaming of punishments, should they ever meet again. Back in late January, he finally got the chance. Wyatt Connors had been traveling across the country when he wrecked his car on the same plot of land where his secret 'admirer' toiled. When he woke up, he discovered that the people who rescued him--a fiercely religious couple that went by the name of Jessup--were under the impression that he was some kind of reverend...and that his only possession had gone missing from the car. Both of those were Seth's doing, of course. He'd managed to paint Connors into a particularly dangerous corner--the man known as 'Wise Guy' was forced back into wrestling...in a company where there was no shortage of people calling for his head. Connors couldn't leave, either, not while Seth had his briefcase. Seth had no idea what was in it--he never was able to crack the six-digit combination--but whatever it was, Connors hadn't been willing to disappear without it. It had been great fun, watching the 'False Prophet' wriggle like a worm on a hook. Between watching Connors get what he deserved, and throwing a monkey wrench into any plans Wyatt tried to cook up, Seth had a great deal of fun. Then, came Reverend Seck. The Decency Compound. The Kool-Aid. The kidnappings. Before he knew it, Seth found himself in just as deep as his prisoner, and just as unable to extricate himself. Suddenly, it didn't seem so much fun anymore. These days, Seth went about his business, doing his best not to attract attention. If he kicked up too much fuss, someone might notice that he was one of two people in the whole compound who weren't under the influence of the tainted Kool-Aid. To complicate matters, he'd taken quite a liking to the other person who hadn't been brainwashed, a girl by the name of Abigail Proctor. 'I have got to get out of this place,' he muttered to nobody in particular. He refused to say such things out loud, because everyone knew what happened to dissidents. Actually, nobody knew what happened to dissidents, because nobody ever dissented. Nearly everyone went around, blindly following orders and talking about how great Reverends Seck and Connors were. Half the time, they reminded him of zombies. He occasionally wished one of them would start shouting 'BRAAAAAIIINNNS!' At least that would have been interesting. For the last several days, Jeremiah had been doing odd jobs around the Compound, mostly by the perimeter fence. Today was no different, as it found him by the west wall, testing the fence for loose boards. To the untrained eye, it looked like he was fixing the fence. To the trained eye, it looked like he was checking for escape routes. He found a plank that seemed a little loose, and tried to rattle it around a bit. Unfortunately, it seemed to be relatively sturdy. That was no good--you needed a place where you can get out quickly, with minimal effort. Sure, there was the front gate--much easier, and much quicker--but it also ceased to be an option the day they started having people serve guard duty at nights. Something had seriously spooked the Reverend, and the man had reacted by overreacting. It wasn't just the guards, either. Nobody was allowed outside the compound except on official business. All vehicles were to be kept outside the walls, but all gasoline was kept inside. True, these measures were meant more for keeping people out than keeping them in, but a cage is a cage, no matter which side of the door the lock is on. A few feet from the last loose plank, Seth found another one. This one had a little more give to it--a little work, and you could fit a medium-sized person through. That didn't help Seth, who was quite big, but it was a start. This wasn't the first time he'd thought about escaping, and it wasn't likely to be the last. There were three things that kept him from actually making an attempt, though. The first was the new security measures--the sentries and all of that. Made things a lot more difficult. Then, there was Wyatt Connors himself. Seth was actually starting to feel sorry for the man--Wyatt Connors, a man who didn't deserve to go to hell, because it wasn't bad enough. But this? To see the man have his entire identity stolen from him, replaced with something else? Turned into a mindless sheep? Nobody deserved that. Well...maybe Wyatt. But that didn't make it easier to watch. And out of some kind of misplaced guilt, Seth found it hard to run out. Sure, he could do it if the situation arose, but he'd feel bad about it for quite a while. The third reason--and most important--was the person walking up to him right now. He didn't even notice anyone was there until she swatted him on the butt. 'Hey!' 'Hey, yourself.' Abby Proctor smiled as Seth turned around. She threw her arms around Seth's neck and leaned in for a kiss. He reciprocated, but also checked to see if anyone was watching. The people in the Compound liked love just fine, but if the reverend caught you making a public display, you were in for a good twenty-minute lecture on how your penis was leading you to hell. 'How are you holding up?' he asked as he set her back down--kissing a girl more than a foot shorter than you requires some lifting. 'All right. I miss our...field trips.' She playfully twirled a lock of her dirty blonde hair between her fingers. 'Me too, baby. God, do I miss those.' 'Well, hopefully the new rules are just a temporary thing. If not, we'll have to work something out.' There was that smile again. Ever since that first time in the wheat fields, that smile drove him crazy. 'Well, we have to be careful. You know how the boss gets.' Abby rolled her eyes. 'I'll try. So...what is it you're doing out here?' 'Testing the fence. There's bound to be a good weak spot somewhere.' 'Weak spot? You mean, for sneaking out?' 'I mean for escaping. I just have the feeling that things are going to get worse...and I think someday, we'll have to make a run for it.' Abby cast a skeptical glare at him. 'Paranoid much?' 'Just thinking ahead, babe.' Seth went back to work on the fence, pushing against the planks to see if they held. Abby followed, listening intently to the crunch of the dying grass beneath their feet. If she noticed the third pair of footsteps, she never said anything. When they got close to Seth and Abby, the person who owned those footsteps said, 'Excuse me, Brother Seth.' The big man turned his head, and found himself matching the gaze of Wyatt Connors, who was still carrying the Bible in his hands. 'What do you need?' he asked. 'I have a special job for you and the others. Round them up, and take them...' He trailed off, noticing Abby's presence for the first time. 'Hello,' he said, flatly. 'Hi, Reverend,' she responded. 'Would you please excuse us, Miss? I have business to discuss with Brother Seth.' She started to protest, but Seth gave her a silent nod. Don't make a fuss, he though, and she seemed to have gotten the message, because she smiled and left without another word. As soon as she was out of earshot, Connors turned back toward Seth. 'That's better. Now, I need you to round up the others--James, Macarthur, Pablo, and Jorge. You are to go into the storage area, where we keep the personal effects of all our members. Reverend Seck would like a cellphone.' Seth barely stopped himself from asking why. It's none of your business, he told himself. Out loud, he said, 'I'll get right on it.' 'See that you do. I would join you, but I have important work of my own to do. Good day.' As Connors went off on his way, Abby returned and stood next to Seth. 'So, what was that all about?' she asked. 'Oh, just some stupid thing he wants us to do. Apparently, he needs a cellphone.' 'What for?' He shrugged. 'He didn't tell me. I didn't ask.' 'Pretty weird, though. I mean, what's he need with a cellphone? I don't even know if you can get reception out here.' 'Not good reception, but it's possible.' 'Hmm. I wonder...' Seth cocked an eyebrow. 'Wonder what?' 'Oh...nothing. I just thought...y'know, maybe you could...get my cell while you're looking?' She grinned sheepishly. 'Mm-hmm. And what do you need a cell for?' 'I wanted to send a message to my mom. Tell her I'm doing fine, and all that.' 'I thought you said you didn't like your mother.' 'I do like her. I just can't stand her. If that makes any sense.' 'Nope.' 'Well, anyway, I'd like to make the call. It's no big deal if you can't.' He shrugged. 'I'll give it a shot. Can't make any promises, especially if Wyatt's in there with us.' 'Yeah, I understand. Is it just me, or is he getting even more crazy?' She stared out after Connors, watching him approach the crowd by the main building. 'And what's that he's got in his hands?' 'Bible. Why?' 'Huh. Now that's really weird.' Seth gave a confused look. 'He's a reverend. He preaches. He's got a Bible. What's so weird about it?' 'Well, that's the first one I've seen since I got here.' 'What? That's ridiculous. They've got 'em in the church, right?' 'I've been to the services, Seth. There's not a one to be seen.' 'That can't be. Someone's got to...' But the more he thought about it, the more it seemed that Abby was right. He hadn't seen a single Good Book since Seck arrived. Whenever Connors conducted a service, he never had one up with him, and there weren't any in the crowd. 'Wow. That's fucked up.' 'I know, right? Who ever heard of a religious commune that didn't have the books?' Seth looked back into the main area of the Decency Compound. Somehow, it seemed a little darker than when he'd last looked. 'I really have got to get out of this place,' he muttered again, and headed out to find Brother James. 'So...what's your phone look like?' Be careful what you wish for. # # # The Crow And The Pitcher Once upon a time, there was an evil man. No...that's not quite right. Doesn't give you the whole picture. This man spent many years doing terrible things. He swindled his way to success. He helped destroy an empire with lies. He took a lot of money from a lot of people. He betrayed every person who was foolish enough to trust him. He promised rain, and delivered heartbreak. And he loved every second of it. So, it would be more accurate to say: once upon a time, there was a wicked man. His name was Wyatt Connors. Wyatt had a very successful career as a professional wrestler, owing mostly to his penchant for creative dishonesty. He was an even greater success behind the scenes, as an agent--his clients would usually get paid far more than the promoters would have liked (and often, more than the clients deserved.) He gained political power, which he used to perpetrate schemes that would net him even more money and power. Sure, there were repercussions at times. But he always made sure that, when there was a bag, someone else was holding it...and all the money had already been taken out. Then, one day, he decided he'd had enough. He quit his job in the World Wrestling Alliance--leaving his teammates high and dry on the night of a title match. He had gathered the few earthly belongings that he intended to keep, got in a car, and disappeared forever. Or at least, that's what he tried to do. The car he drove had different plans, as it blew a tire, causing his car to crash into a tree. He was rescued from the wreckage by a nice couple called the Jessups, and their farmhand--who, as luck would have it, was one of the many people who had been screwed over by Wyatt Connors. Connors had no idea, of course, because he never kept track of that sort of thing. It would be impossible to count all of Wyatt's enemies; numbers do not go that high. Still, this one lucky foe had the chance to put the screws to Connors like nobody else ever could. In time, Wyatt lost everything. His freedom, his belongings, his anonymity; all gone in the blink of an eye. He was forced into SCCW, home of numerous stars that would love nothing more than to play polo with his head. Lance Marshall. BloodAngel. Desade. Wyatt played along with Seth's game, telling himself that he was biding his time until he found his possessions. But he never got the chance--before he could escape, another man came to take something else away. Reverend Ibrahim Seck, the prodigal son, had returned to the place of his spiritual birth. His first act upon returning was to save Wyatt Connors...from himself. A steady diet of brainwashing and Ibrahim's special Kool-Aid had turned Connors into a new version of Seck, suppressing his actual personality--that of the manipulating scumbag. Trading a bastard for a monster. Ever since then, Wyatt has been first and foremost among those spreading the word of Ibrahim Seck to the nation. Still, there is the saying. 'You can't keep a good man down.' That saying applies even more to bad men. The orders had been given. The instructions had been carried out. The Decency Compound was working as it should. Finally, Wyatt Connors had a little time to himself. He sat in a well-lit corner of a dark room, holding only a pencil and a Bible. The inside cover bears his name, but it is not his signature--a very obvious and half-hearted attempt at forgery, but good enough to convince the locals. It was the same book that Seth had produced when he first convinced the Jessups that Connors was a man of the cloth, all those months ago. Craning his neck so that he could catch the light, he leafed quickly through the gold-edged pages. They were flying by too fast for him to actually be reading them, but he was keeping an eye on something. Silent words escaped his lips as he scanned the pages. Every once in a while, something would catch his interest, and he would fold down the corner of the page. Then, back to flipping he would go. It took him the better part of an hour to get through the thirteen hundred pages, but eventually he did. Then, he flipped back to the dog-eared pages, looking for what caught his eye the first time. Sometimes he would make a pencil mark next to the verse in question; other times he would pass it over. After the marks, there were circles for the choicer phrases, then underlines for the best of the best. At long last, he had what he needed, and closed the defaced copy of the Holy Bible. If you were to ask him why he did that, he would not tell you. Not because he doesn't want to, but because he doesn't know. The False Prophet has no idea why those specific verses are marked. But the Wise Guy does. Those verses mean his salvation. They might also mean his destruction...but that's a small price to pay. Necessity is the mother of invention. # # # The Lion And The Mouse. Once upon a time, there was a man who was a terrible wrestler, but an excellent manipulator. His name was, and still is, Ibrahim Seck. One of Seck's best known stunts was during his run in the New England Wrestling Syndicate, where he built an army out of the company's 'talent enhancement' performers. Such men as Ricky 'Retread' Rogers, Morty 'Taxman' Weinberg, and Kevin 'Floggy' Loggins--people who aren't even household names in their own houses--were once enthralled by great Crusader for Decency. They were brainwashed and manipulated into doing his bidding, usually with disastrous results. It also marked the career high point for all of them. They were at ringside for main events! They were on pay-per-view matches! People knew their names! Once, a fan brought a sign that said 'FLOGY RULZE!' Loggins nearly burst into tears. It didn't matter that both words on the sign were spelled wrong. He had a fan, and it was Ibrahim Seck who made it possible. So, naturally, when Ibrahim Seck needed someone to undertake an important duty, his old Crusaders were always there for him. He wouldn't use them for important duties, of course. They were still a bunch of screwups. That doesn't matter, though. What matters is that they were there, just in case. As it turned out, one of them was needed. And now it was time to find out if he had performed as hoped. Ibrahim Seck sat in his chair, while Brothers James and Macarthur rifled through a pile of cellular phones. 'Gentlemen,' he pleaded. 'Time is of the essence.' The problem was that most of the phones had been sitting idle for some time, and their owners had come to the Decency Compound months ago. Most of the phones have lost their charge, and most of the rest have been deactivated due to the fact that nobody in the Compound pays their bills. Eventually, though, James found what they were looking for--a pay-as-you-go phone with some time left on it. After a brief tutorial and two wrong numbers, Seck was finally making contact with one of his men out in the field...any more, his only one. Once fat loser wrestler, no longer a wrestler, a man by the name of Wilhelm Gobstopper. 'Hallo?' said the voice--complete with thick German accent--on the other end of the line. 'Brother Wilhelm? It's the Reverend. Reverend Seck.' 'Oh ja? Rewerent! It is so goot to hear from you again!' Seck gave an enthusiastic thumbs-up to his new charges before answering his old friend. 'Remember what I asked you to do for me? I asked you to watch the boy. Do you remember that?' 'Oh, yess! A wery heppy day it wass.' 'It pleases me to hear it. Now then...HAVE you been watching the boy?' 'Umm...vell...' The hesitation in Wilhelm's voice was obvious. Seck started to go red with anger. 'Oh, Wilhelm. You didn't lose him, did you?' 'Maybe. A little. But--' 'I'm not interested in your butt, Brother Wilhelm. This was my last chance, and you've blown it! OH, THE SHAME! THE PALPABLE, PAL--' 'But...but I know vhere he iss! I fount him again!' '--PABLE SHAME! Wait, really?' 'Yes! He is enrollet in Ohio Uniwersity. He lives in bik dorm buildink vit girlss.' Seck frowned. Boys and girls living in a large building together? That could only lead to trouble. Still, his displeasure was easily outdone by his elation of knowing where the boy was. 'How did you find this out?' 'I saw him on my vay beck from ze Schnitzel Haus!' 'This is wonderful news, Wilhelm! Wonderful! Your place next to me in heaven is assured, I swear to you.' 'Oh, tenk you, Rewerent! You are a great men!' 'It's true. Now, listen carefully, Wilhelm. Here's what I need you to do...' Seck spent the next five minutes giving further instructions to Wilhelm Gobstopper. The whole time, tears were streaming down his face. 'I've beaten the Scarred Man,' he whispered at the end. 'I've won.' Little friends may prove great friends. # # # The Fox And The Crow Once upon a time, there was a man and a woman who liked each other a great deal. Possibly, they even loved each other. Certainly, they enjoyed having sex with each other, and in the end, isn't that what's important? 'God, I wish I had a cigarette.' Late that night, both Seth and Abby snuck out of the barracks, as they'd done so many times before. They didn't dare leave the compound; not with Pablo and Jorge minding the gate tonight. Instead, they went back up to the wall where Seth had been checking for weakness in the posts that morning. Afterwards, she curled up next to him as they both lay on a blanket that he'd swiped from supplies. She nuzzled her face against his neck, warming herself with his body heat, while he stared up at the stars. She cooed in his ear, 'You're great, baby.' 'You're the best,' he answered. He wanted to just lie here with her, forever. Sure, that would make for a very interesting conversation with Reverend Connors in the morning...but that would never come. All there was was tonight, and her. She could ask him to do anything right now, and he would do it. Break down the wall of the Compound with his bare hands? No problem. Swim across the Pacific? Abby shrieked, as Seth began tickling her with his free hand. She drew back her arms to defend herself, leaving Seth in sole possession of the phone. 'Hey, no fair!' She snatched it away again, as she gave her best I'm-pissed-at-you-but-not-really face. 'You're mean.' 'You are such a girl.' 'And you love me for it.' 'Can't argue with that. So...you want to call your folks right now?' 'Absolutely not,' she said, and tossed the phone aside, next to her clothes. There was that smile again. Beware of flatterers.
