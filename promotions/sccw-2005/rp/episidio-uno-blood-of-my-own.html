Nick Cave & the Bad Seeds – Red Right Hand (Let Love In, 1994) October 23rd, 2008. As gentlemanly as he could, he laid her down on the carpet. Her right arm splayed out beyond her in a pool of her own blood. The carpet was so thick with red that it had grown into black and gently, Brock Shepherd rested her newly defined nub into the puddle precisely. Stepping back, to examine the scene, like an artist, admiring his handy work. Trying understand the context of her lying down and the positioning; to make it look like self-mutilation. He knew there couldn’t be much time before the PD burst through the door and pinned him up against the wall, reading him the Miranda’s and bustling around with their handcuffs. He could see them, the Albany PD, arguing about who got to kick the door in and who got to point their firearm. It made him chuckle as he knelt down beside her. He ran his only hand through her hair before gently, with his index and middle fingers, closing her eyes. "How did it feel?" Brock rose, turning to the mirrored wall beside the cupboard in his Motel 6 room. His eyes met with the man in the mirror, there was no shock in seeing his reflection moving as an individual. Just a sense of welcome. The man in the mirror was not the same as the man who stared into it. For his right arm was whole. There was nothing artificial about his right hand. Aside from that startling fact, there was really only one other major difference. The man in the mirror’s right arm was red. Red with paint? It was too difficult to tell, but appeared as if he’d dipped the arm to mid-way up his bicep in some sort of red substance. Whatever substance was it did not appear to be dry. Silently, Brock answered with a nod. His reflection was pleased with the nod. "She feels," Brock whispered, ashamed of the pride which pumped adrenalin through his body. He was visibly hard in the mirror, finding his eyes wandering down to the bulge which the man in the mirror possessed. "You have done well." "Thank-you." Brock dropped his head, bubbling with so much pride he did not wish for the man in the mirror to see the silly grin it forced upon him. The man in the mirror looked to the door in thought. The cogs were turning to put the plan, which he’d been concocting for so long now, into motion. "They will be here soon." "I know." Brock shifted on his feet nervously, as if he could feel the chase rising in the air. "They will have questions. Are you ready?" Brock drew breath, allowing it to puff his chest up and as he nodded, he expelled the breath letting it calm his rattled nerves. He ran a bloodied hand over his face and through his hair, trying to relieve his tension. "Yes. I think so." The man in the mirror smiled once again. "Good. I’m going now. You mustn’t mention me." He paused as if allowing it to sink into Brock. "To anyone." Brock shook his head. "I won’t." "I will be back for you. Once the coast clears." Again, Brock nodded, this time with a smile. "I’d like that." "I promise." The man in the mirror’s attention was taken by that of the noise outside of the motel room. Brock could hear vehicles coming to a halt outside. Their tyres locking up, momentarily, in the gravel parking lot with a crunching noise. He could hear their footsteps, and those of the owners and other patrons of the Motel 6. Their voices muted through the walls. He knew what they spoke about. They would be talking about the scream and how they heard a "kerfuffle". The officers would be taking notes in a little pad, one of them watching the window all the while keeping a sharp ear to the details the witnesses were giving them. They’d be planning how they’d do it, silently, with the years of training clicking over into gear, idling and ready to be put to use. Brock snapped his gaze back to the mirror, but the man with the red right hand was no longer there, just Brock’s natural reflection. He sighed for a moment, growing used to being alone again. He knew it wouldn’t be for long. He moved to the kitchenette and snorted the final line of coke, pinching his nostrils between his fingers and holding his breath momentarily before he turned to face her again. Samantha Gideon’s life had completely departed her as Brock knelt beside her. He took the cleaver from where he’d left it and pressed her left hand around it, hoping to garner her prints before her hand became to cold to exchange them to the weapon. He let it drop naturally from her grip and lifted her, by the armpits, into a hug and he found a dark place inside his head. A sad place. He thought of never seeing the man in the mirror again. Brock shed a tear. And then another. And by the time the police department kicked the door in, with weapons poised ready to fire, they found Brock Shepherd weeping dramatically at the loss. Samantha Gideon lugged into his lap with his arm holding her to his chest, wailing incessantly. Like love had left him. Episodio Uno Blood of My Own: daddy Present Day. April Monday let herself fall into the leather couch with a plop, the air hissed out of the cushion under her ass. She let her head fall back into the big comfy back and closed her eyes for just a moment. It was the only decent piece of furniture he owned outside of his La-Z-Boy armchair, which nobody dared to think about sitting in. She was glad he’d taken a moment to reflect on the fact that his guests had comfort requirements too and bought that comfy, leather sofa. She could hear him rummaging in his room, probably trying to find a clean shirt. April imagined him picking them up off the floor, giving them a sniff. Sniffing the next and the next until he finally decided which of them smelt cleaner. In the end, he’d do what he always did. Wear a dirty one until they got down the street, where he’d buy another from a K-Mart or some place like that, tossing the old one in the bin. August Monday, her dad, was notorious for being thrifty and frivolous all at once. A murmured voice called through the wall, but the words were inaudible. "What was that?" she called to the next room, sitting up, eyes opened and squinting, as if it would help her understand him more. Augie’s rounded corner, tugging on a shirt with a minor stain that looked like he’d sipped some cola and it got captured in his beard, straining itself down through the whiskers and onto his shirt. "I said, you managed to find a sitter for Adam?" April nodded at him, half-grinning as the thought of being toddler-free was a liberating experience. "Yeah, I wanted to take this seriously, Augie. You know, this is a big thing. I haven’t stepped into a ring for a while," she replied, almost drifting away into thought to recognise that this was actually happening to her. She was actually looking forward to a life all her own. Outside of being Adam’s "mummy". August straightened his shirt out, smoothing it with the palms of his hands and noticing the stain. "Ah shit." With his face bearing a little crimson tinge to it, he sheepishly grinned at his daughter. "We gotta swing by a store on the way." "To get a new shirt?" August nodded. "Yup." And he winked at his daughter. She could only laugh as they gathered their things, ready to hit the road and ready themselves for what could be a monumental step forward in their lives. Their time together had been disjointed. She’d only known her father for little more than three years now. Taking his name all those years ago meant nothing until she’d finally met the man behind it. The story of how he lost her mother was gut wrenchingly awful and he’d never known of April’s existence. He never knew, at the time of the accident, that his beloved Ellen was three months pregnant. His heart could never bear to see her in that coma, hooked up to tubes and machines just so as to keep her alive. Kept alive for the baby growing inside of her. For April. August simply tried to shut it all away. Thing is, he had a whole lot more to deal with, having been removed of his masculinity in that car crash, in the physical phallic sense of it. Having been the driver of the vehicle made it all the more worse for him, being the one who caused the accident. The one who killed the woman he loved. August never spoke of it. He isn’t the kind of guy who pours his feelings out to anyone. As they stepped through the threshold and out into the day, April found herself smiling in admiration of the man who patted his pockets in search of his keys. She admired the way he’d just got on with things when it came down to being her father. No crying over the lost years but looking forward to the years ahead of them. He had that way about him. He just got on with things. "Ready?" She smiled at him coyly; embarrassed she’d been busted showing a sign of weakness like emotion getting the better of her. She was a Monday after all. Monday’s don’t show emotion. "Yeah. Let’s do this." Blood of My Own: daughter Driving down the Interstate, August snatched quick glimpses of his daughter. Her window was wound down and she snaked her hand through the air, enjoying the freedom of the ride being toddler-free for once in her life. April made her days up of being mummy to one Adam August Shepherd, the fruit made up of herself and Brock Shepherd. These days, he wasn’t on the scene, after April got the courts involved to remove his influence. August hadn’t been entirely supportive of her removing the kid’s father but stuck by his daughter nonetheless. All the while, maintaining his friendship with Brock. Everybody needs a friend. God knew, that man could do with some. He felt like closing his eyes, just enjoying the moment of being able to spend some time with his daughter. It was a big step, in both their lives, coming back to wrestling. Competing didn’t mean much to him anymore. At least not until April came up with the idea of them competing together. The thought of doing the one thing which had been a major part of their lives together seemed to make him go all gooey on the inside. The Raging Fear, August Monday, wasn’t used to being gooey and he kinda liked the feeling it gave him. Reaching across, August turned on the radio. It snatched April out of her dream state and she watched as he fiddled with the dial. "Smoke on the Water" crackled through the snow and when August felt he’d got the tuning just right, he sat back, stealing another glance at April. This time, he had one of those reminiscent grins strewn over his face. He bopped his head along to Deep Purple. "Classic. They don’t make music like this no more," he told her. "You sound old." "I am old. And what’s wrong with Smoke on the Water? It’s a fucken classic, Ape." April shrugged. "If you say so," she replied, lacing her words with just a hint of cynicism. "I do say so, ya little blasphemin’ heathen. And you diss Deep Purple again and you might be walkin’ the rest of the way," he said, with a cheeky grin on his face. April didn’t think she’d ever seen her Dad so pleased to be doing anything. He was renowned for being gruff. Not cheeky. And certainly not happy. Even if he did say "diss", sounding far older than liking Deep Purple gave him credit for. Internally, she chuckled and let herself enjoy the music as they cruised down the Interstate, enjoying the sun as it warmed them through the windshield. Plucking a Marlboro Red from the packet in the glove compartment, August patted his jeans pockets for his lighter. "You don’t mind, do ya?" She stifled her laugh. "Oh, you’re askin’ me if it’s alright now?" "Figured I’d be polite a little. Just this once. Even though… I’m goin’ ahead and smokin’ it either way." He flashed a sheepish grin as he lit the cigarette. He drew a plume into his lungs before speaking. "Utah, huh? I ain’t been there in some time, Ape. Think you got what it takes to get back in the ring?" It felt surreal to be asked the question again, so April let it settle on her a little. She’d got so excited about the idea of getting in the ring again she never even bothered to think about if she was actually ready to do it. "Scared, huh?" August seemed to see right through her façade. She nodded. "A little. I guess. Just been …I dunno… so long I guess. GCW was when Adam was in my belly. I guess I just dunno what to expect, Augie." He cringed as she said it. ‘Augie’. It stung like a nettle when she said his name like that. He so desperately wanted her to call him ‘Dad’. It’d mean so much to him if she did. But he’d never ask her too. He was too proud to do that. "I guess I’m a little scared doin’ this without …without Brock, I guess…" she near whispered, trailing off into her own thoughts. Trying to focus on what whizzed by the passenger side window, April struggled to relieve her mind of thinking about Brock Shepherd. Deep down in her heart of hearts… she loved him still. And she always would. Yet, it was obvious to everyone, April included, that he was no good for her. He was like heroin. The more she had of him the more she wanted him, but the more she had of him the more the sickness took over them. Being with Brock wasn’t a good idea for her or their child, April knew that much. Having sensed the mood had grown a little chilly in the car, August turned up the radio as it the tune changed. April’s attention was caught by the song playing. "If that Reef?" she asked, interrupting her own train of thought. August shrugged. "That their name? I jus’ thought this song was catchy. Got a catchy riff, don’t it?" April smiled, not wanting to seem uncool in front of her dad she resorted to a teenage-esque shrug and a "dunno". Taking another long draw on his cigarette felt impressed by his spur of the moment fathering skills to change the mood from somber to smiley. He was getting the hang of this dad business. Blood of My Own: coincidences October 24th, 2008. Detective Renner left interview room C and headed on down the corridor, his mind clouded by the confusion arising within him. He rounded into the kitchenette provided kindly by John Q. Public and began to pour himself a cup of coffee out of the percolator. Black and unsweetened, he took a swig and cringed as it crawled down his throat. The coffee was so bad in this place he had replaced his home brand with the same, just because he’d gotten so used to this muck that regular (good) coffee just didn’t sit right anymore. The kitchenette stank of the cheap coffee and that fried chicken stink of sweat. Some of the guys on the force could do with a refresher course on personal hygiene. They seemed to linger in rooms long after they’d left. He sat down at the small table and rifled through the newspaper. It was already littered with photos of the man he had handcuffed in interview room C. One image was of him raising a wrestling championship belt high above his head. The other was of police hauling him into the back of a patrol car, with blood all over his white shirt. Renner’s career had been fairly quiet in terms of homicide investigations and the last thing the Albany PD needed was a headline that doesn’t go their way. The media were like coyotes waiting for them to fall so they could pounce and eat their fill. A pretty young girl having her hand chopped off and bleeding to death in a Motel 6 out near the interstate was headline news as it was. Then when you add to the plot that the potential murderer is a wrestling star it seems like something the writers of Bold & the Beautiful might very well have concocted. The press swarmed to it like moths to a flame. The only downside was with all this media attention, at the end of it, only one man would feel the weight of all the scrutiny. Guilty verdict or not, the world would weigh heavily on Detective Seth Renner’s shoulders. With a heavy hearted sigh, Detective Renner flipped over the cover page to see if the media had soaked up any crucial titbits he might have overlooked. A friend of his in Boston cracked a case thanks to a pillow case the local tabloid found hidden in a dumpster, conveniently for the reporter to find. Whilst the egg on Boston PD’s face had repercussions, a guilty verdict was found nonetheless and a monster was put away. Renner wasn’t about to make the same mistakes and after skimming through the articles, he was comfortable he hadn’t missed anything those jackals were able to stumble upon. "Media got you by the proverbials, Seth?" called the familiar voice of Captain Lou Rickshaw. He swaggered in valiantly, like he always did, stinking of that cheap cologne his wife bought him annually for his birthday. The same cologne he annually bitched about getting instead of a new golf club. Lou helped himself to the same percolator, swirling it in the jug before pouring it out. He added some cream to his mud, swigged it and near gagged at the very taste. "Eugh… how can you drink this swill?" He took one more verifying swig before tossing it in the trash. "I’ll never get used to that. No matter how hard I try." "It’s decent enough. Coffee’s coffee when you do the real work, Lou," Renner offered, with a wink to voice the sarcasm laced within his comments. Rickshaw accepted them with a chuckle and sat opposite, spinning Renner’s paper to look at the shot of Renner attending the scene with a couple of uniforms. They clearly hadn’t shot Renner’s best side because for an Albany detective, there was no best side at four in the AM. He tapped the paper twice with his index finger, right on an image of the wrestler held in custody performing some sort of move which caused for him to trap the head of another man between his thighs. "You ever seen this guy in action?" Lou asked. Renner responded with a slow shake of his head. He stole another sip of his coffee. "Can’t say I have. All I know is I got me a media frenzy and shit don’t add up." The paper seemed to hypnotise Rickshaw as he contemplated Renner’s words. The detective continued to speak over the captain’s thoughts. "See, he’s tryin’ to tell me he don’t remember nothin’. I guess… I guess I just don’t like coincidences." "Didn’t his tests show high levels of cocaine and alcohol in his bloodstream?" "Enough to make regular guys like us party for a week. Figure it’s likely there might be some sort of murkiness or glaze over it all… but to completely forget about someone dyin’ in your motel room? Just seems… I dunno… I guess it seems-" "Convenient?" The captain shrugged. "So we have a shroud of mystery over this case. That’s our job. We put those pieces of the jigsaw puzzle together and we see the face at the end, Seth. We remove the shroud. Wave the fog. That’s what we do." The captain rose, snatching the paper up and tucking it under his arm. "Don’t let the media get to you, detective. They’re all in their churches praying this feller cut her hand off, just for the awards they might get writing ‘bout it. You fuck their shot at a Pulitzer up and they’ll come chasin’ a cop hangin’." Captain Rickshaw slapped Renner on the shoulder and headed out of the kitchenette, leaving him to his thoughts and conclusions. Weighing everything up on an invisible pair scales. Comparing the clues. Analysing the lay of the evidence and flow of the blood. Sure, these days the PD had all sorts of labs to do the processing. It wasn’t as glitzy as they put it on the telly. CSI wasn’t full of beautiful people. After hunting down the clues all day in the office these people go home to hunt for dragons in dungeons. They were brilliant minds but that was it. They saw the facts. They let science make their decisions for them. Science, like everything was fallible. Except more expensive on tax payer’s pockets. Seth Renner was what the DNA geeks called ‘old school’, he didn’t need something like a computer to tell him who was guilty or not. He used a little something called facts. And right now, the case of Samantha Gideon’s possible homicide and with Brock Shepherd in custody… the facts just weren’t weighing up. Blood of My Own: fuel & fire August let his head hang, just to try and extend the spine, while he filled the car with gas. It was a decent enough drive from Oregon to Utah, and he was beginning to feel the years of wrestling creeping up with him. These days, his knees groaned when he walked and his body whined if he turned sudden. He closed his eyes as the cool breeze lapped at his face, using it to wake himself up a little. April noticed August’s attempt to keep himself awake. "You want a soda or something, Augie?" She peeped her pretty red head of hair out the door. Pondering on it for a moment, as if it might have been an actual important question, August finally came up with his nod. "Get me one of them energiser sodas." "A Red Bull?" "Whatever it’s called. I dunno. Just as long as it keeps my eyes from shuttin’ the few more miles we got left." She left car door open as she crossed the gas station yard, not noticing the faded green El Camino parked by the diner. She never noticed one of the diner patrons catch a glimpse of her within from a window booth, head spinning so quick to the roadhouse door they might have gotten whiplash. April never noticed them spring to their feet, rushing across the roadhouse to greet her. "APRIL!" they cried. "Brock?" Brock Shepherd nodded, his face washed with amazement, shaking his head with disbelief. A sheath of embarrassment crept over April Monday, having been taken by surprise of meeting up with Brock. He was the last person she expected, or WANTED, to see. "Oh my God, what’re you doing in Utah, Brock?" He pushed his thumb over his shoulder, as if pointing back to a day gone by or a group of people. Seeing the only booth with food on the table, April assumed it were a date. "Just Wrestlin’ just finished up a tour. Here in Utah. Did the West Coast." "You’re touring again?" "Yeah." He casually blew off the interrogation. "I’m headed to Montreal now." "Montreal? As in Canada?" "Nah, as in the one right beside Bizbee, North fucken Dakota. Of course, Canada," he said, straight as a die, but the joke was implied and April laughed anyway. "Good for you, Brock." She shoved him in the arm, trying her best to act natural. But April wasn’t genuinely warm. They stood there in an awkward silence, both searching for something to say to the other. Somehow, fate had pushed their paths to cross again but these days April just wished fate would leave them both the Hell alone and keep out of their business. Shepherd seemed to search his mind for the next thing to say, clutching at straws to keep their rendezvous alive. "Yeah, I’ve signed on to do three shows with All-Star Championship Wrestlin’. I guess it’s all slowly comin’ back together for me." "That’s great news." She was as genuine as she could possibly muster, which almost came across as feigned interest. "Yup. Only up from here, Ape." She smiled, nervously glancing around the store for someone else fate might have put in her path, but she was miles from anyone she knew. Brock was just a freak occurrence. "Where you headed?" he finally asked. "Provo. Me and August are-" "Oh, August is here?" He peered out the window to see the monstrous Monday tucking the nozzle end of the gas hose back in its place. "Say ‘hi’, would ya?" "Sure. We’re on our way to Provo." "Provo? Aren’t SCCW there?" He thought on it for moment. "They are. I saw posters." April nodded. The nod flowed on with an impressed whistle from Shepherd as he jammed his real and prosthetic hands into pockets. "No shit. August gettin’ back in the ring again?" "You could say that." She could feel her cheeks flush. Almost ashamed by the pride that had bubbled to her surface. "Actually… we’re doing it together." "You managin’ him?" "Tag team, Brock. We’re tagging." He mustered the best smile that he could. Peering back out at the man the fans knew as the Raging Fear. He seemed like he was hurt by the revelation but tried his best not to show it. Despite trying to mask his emotions, Brock looked different than he normally did. His eyes were cracked with blood shot lightning bolts and large, black bags framed them. He wore long sleeves. Brock never wore long sleeves, not in all the time April had known him. Occasionally, he scratched at one of the arms, without even realising it. Just him being there in general made April feel nervous despite his appearance. "Well, Ape, that’s really great." Brock paused, his mind had wandered onto something and he looked embarrassed that it struck him. "How’s my boy? How’s… Adam?" The embarrassment seemed to fall into place, April understood why he felt this way. He’d forgotten to ask having been so wrapped up in seeing her. "He’s good, Brock. He’s fine." "Does he miss me?" April could feel herself getting warm. "Of course he does, Brock. You’re his daddy." "Can I see him?" April sighed and fiddled with her hands nervously. "No, you can’t. You know that." "Why not?" "Don’t do this." The clerk behind the counter made eye contact with April’s pleading glance. Brock’s face flushed red. April wasn’t sure if it was embarrassment for causing a scene or rage. "Why not, Ape? I just wanna see my son." "Drop it, Brock." April left him standing there, heading to the fridges to grab two Red Bull cans and a bottle of water. Shepherd had stood still a moment, but his pride got the better of him and he began to follow April around the store like a puppy dog. A scorned, psychopathic puppy dog. "I won’t drop it, April. I wanna see my son. I’ll fight this in the courts." "Then do that, Brock. We live in Portland now. We have family there. We haven’t even heard from you in six months and then last week, out of the blue, you call and expect everything to change?" "I need my son, April." She spins, shoving Brock with one hand against one of the fridges. Her index finger pounds into his chest as exclamation to her words. "YOU need him? Adam needs a father, Brock. And you’re no father. Not even close. You’re a parasite. A loser, Brock. That’s what you are. A God-damned-loser!" Neither of them had noticed that frightened by the confrontation, the woman behind the counter had begun wrapping on the window to get August’s attention. He was bemused by it until he noticed the fear in the lady’s eyes. Noticed her gestures pointing at something inside. His gut instinct told him that something was wrong. August recognised the El Camino and ran across the gravel. "Adam doesn’t need a deadbeat dad who pops in and out when he feels like it. Who only plays ‘daddy’ when he wants to. He needs a REAL father, Brock." "I AM A REAL FATHER!" Brock roared, shoving April back before she could prod him again. She staggered backward, her feet tangling up in each other and she fell. One Red Bull can hit the ground on just the right angle to burst it, spraying a fine mist of energy drink over April. The shove had taken her by surprise. She looked up at Brock, bewildered and enraged by the shove. Adrenalin coursing through his veins, Shepherd stood over her, his only natural hand clutched into a fist. He never noticed the jingle of the bell when the door brushed by it on August’s entrance. "I AM A REAL FATHER, YOU MISERLY CUNT! AND IT’S ABOUT TIME YOU PAID ME SOME GOD DAMN RESP-" August Monday grabbed Brock by the throat and slammed him into the fridge, his head hit it with a thud sharp enough to crack it. Shepherd’s eye flickered with wild fire as Monday got up in his face. "BROCK!" he roared, with a vicious index finger threateningly poised before his eyes. "Pull yer fucken head in, man." Shepherd tried to surge his energy and burst off the fridge door, only to be shoved back again. Harder this time. Hard enough to turn the crack into a split. Brock groaned as the impact was made between skull and glass. A dull ache had begun to form in his head from the blow. "Don’t do it, Brock." As if displaying he’d calmed down, Shepherd raised his hands, his natural hand palm out like he were surrendering. Monday released the pressure on Brock’s throat, allowing him to straighten up off the cracked fridge door. Turning his back to the amputee, August gave his daughter a hand up. He snatched the fizzing Red Bull can off the linoleum floor and stuffed it into Shepherd’s chest, forcing him to take it. "You… pay the lady for the door, my fuel and the drinks." Monday pointed at the counter where an anxious clerk awaited. Brock protested like a nine year-old kid. "That’s not fair." "Not fair is you doin’ this to Ape, Brock. Pay it or so help me…" Monday trailed off, figuring he needn’t finish his sentence. He shook his fist as an exclamation. Shepherd began to make way for the counter, stopping briefly. "Sorry, Ape," he admitted, attempting to appear whole-hearted but the meaning behind his apology and the situation seemed to be two different things altogether. April turned her cheek to him, not in the Biblical sense. Making it to the counter, he tossed a wad of bills on it under the piercing gaze of the clerk. Shepherd dropped his head and watched his feet as he walked, only snatching a final glimpse of them as he made his way through the door. Pausing for an instance like he might say something but deciding against it at the last minute, disappearing to the sound of the tiny bell’s jingle. "You okay?" August asked, as he watched Shepherd peel off onto the interstate and into the distance in a dust cloud. She rubbed her tailbone, the pain more emotional than physical. "Fine. Let’s go." "You sur"- "Drop it, August. I said I’m fine. Let’s go already." She pulled another Red Bull can out of the fridge and stormed out of the service station, making a b-line for the car. August rubbed the back of his neck, like it might relieve the tension coursing through his body. He wasn’t sure on what to do. Or if he did the right thing. Was it his place to interrupt? Maybe he just wasn’t getting the hang of this daddy stuff after all.
