Interlude - Charlie Larsson

4 June 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 4th Dawn greeted Charlie Larsson like a punch in the face. The rays of light filtering through the blinds brushed his face, and as he slowly regained consciousness they felt like razor blades sliding over his skin. "Whhthhffggrrr," he said, somewhat hampered by his mouth feeling like it was full of sawdust and socks. Moving his head, Charlie worked out that he was lying on the floor; on a floor which he recognised as being that of his own hotel room. How he came to be there was something of a mystery to him at that time. He felt like he'd maybe drunk a bottle of Jack Daniel's and followed it up with an antifreeze chaser. The walls of his skull were pounding in all directions at once, and his legs felt like they'd been in a blender. This was the mother of all hangovers. And then Charlie remembered something. He'd not drunk anything the previous night.   ***   Several days earlier, prior to the Larsson vs Quinlan title match "One more set of reps," said Charlie. "No... more..." rasped his twin sister, dropping the bar and rolling away from the bench press machine. "You're slacking off training in the week before your title match? "No, I'm working to a plan," explained the Valkyrie. "Quinlan and I are both technical wrestlers, but I have the edge in power." "So work it!" "No! I already have that advantage. I need to work on other areas, such as speed. I need to know that if he switches angles and attacks differently, that I'll be able to fight him off and get the match going how I want it to go." "So what does that involve?" "It involves training on my own," said Suzanne. "On your own?" said Charlie, his tone sceptical. "Yeah. It's not the usual kind of thing I do." Suzanne grabbed her towel and started to dab at her forehead, wiping away the sweat. "You're hiding something from me, Suze." "What?" "I can tell," said Charlie. "You don't have to let me know what it is, but I'm here for you if you need to talk." Suzanne looked at him and quickly weighed up her options. She didn't wan to tell her brother that she was now working for and taking instructions from their father, and that the very people who wanted him dead were the ones to whom Charlie had been talking and making his peace with. "Thanks, bro, but I just need to take some time to get into the swing of my new training. The Livewire Title matches are only ten minutes long. If I don't get my speed right then I could be in control but not get the job finished before the bell rings. It's just a different challenge." "Are you sure?" "Trust me, Charlie. I promise that I'll tell you if anything goes wrong." "Okay sis, but be careful."   ***   Using his hands to push himself to a vaguely upright position, Charlie Larsson reflected that he had rarely felt worse than this. The disorientation he was experiencing was causing the room to somehow spin whilst staying still, and his stomach was feeling all the worse for it. Colours seemed brighter, as if they wanted to burn themselves into his retina and make themselves a little home there. In his time, Charlie had tried almost every type of liquor, and most pharmaceutical means of altering consciousness, but he'd never had a comedown like this one. This was something different, though his brain didn't seem to want to tell him exactly what had happened. "I'd talked with Suzanne..." he muttered to himself, as his tongue finally became unstuck from the roof of his mouth. But that was it; the sum total of his memories of the previous day ended after speaking with his sister after her drawn match with Mitchell Quinlan, and then going back to his room. And then hearing a knock at the door. And answering the d... Charlie Larsson winced as pain shot through his temples like wildfire. Even thinking about answering the door on that day caused him to steel himself and take a deep breath. There was something there, something blocking out his memory, and it was something that hurt. He was going to have to tell Suzie.   ***   The day before, shortly after Suzanne Larsson's match with Mitchell Quinlan "Damn it!" stormed the Valkyrie. "You nearly had him," said Charlie. "Nearly! Nearly isn't enough! Who knows if they'll give me another shot at this, and Quinlan escapes on a fucking technicality." Suzanne angrily ripped the black tape from around her wrists, and sat down on the bench next to her locker. "And don't you even think about telling me that I should have kept to my usual training schedule," she snapped. "Hey, I wasn't going to say a word." "I do things my way, okay?" said Suzanne, looking up at her brother. "I didn't say anything!" "You were thinking it." Charlie looked at his sister, who appeared to be staring right back at him with new eyes. There was something different about her, and it wasn't just that he had been thinking those exact things. "Are you okay, Suze?" he asked. "No! I just blew my first damn title shot, and now I'm gonna have to work twice as hard to get anywhere in this fed." "Look, they can't help but have seen how good you were in the ring. Sure, you didn't get the win, but guys like Quinlan are hard to put down in less than ten minutes. You'll get another shot." "I know," she said, the anger in her suddenly evaporating, causing her to deflate like a mixed metaphor. "I know I'll get another chance, but I've just got a lot going on at the moment." "You can let me in. You know that," said Charlie, quietly. "I wish I could," she replied. "I really wish I could."   ***   Later that evening... "You're sure that he doesn't remember our previous visit?" said Gunnar Arnison. "Do you doubt my abilities?" replied Lars Haldorsson. "Never," said his companion, though he knew that Lars had spoken in jest. "He's almost ready." Gunnar nodded as he padded softly down the corridor, his huge feet making barely a sound as he moved with a grace unexpected in one of such bulk. "Do you feel he is ready?" asked the bear. "He will be," replied Lars. "After tonight, we will have done all we can. After that, it is up to Fate. If it favours us, then the Icelandic Council will live on. If it smiles instead on Magnus, then we die and our country dies with us." "Is there nothing else we can do?" "No, my friend. We have chosen our battle as best we can, but in the end, it will be the children who decide the outcome. Our lives rest in their hands, and in their heads and hearts." "So this is it?" "This is it," said Lars, knocking on the door of Charlie Larsson's hotel room. "This is our endgame."
