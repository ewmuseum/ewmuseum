Three Sets of Eyes

24 August 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/24/09 We have three sets of eyes, but we all see the same thing. If we didn’t, this team wouldn’t have any chance at success. Instead of the unstoppable Superversal Champion, we see an insecure woman who will eventually be conquered by her own demons. Instead of the long-standing Elite Champion, we see a man who has developed such a rigid plan of action that as soon as something goes wrong – and it will go wrong – the plan will fall like dominoes, like clockwork, like open-ended clichés. Instead of the monster and her Dead Hand, we see a woman who might not control anything anymore, and who won’t be able to adjust to the new rules of the game. The trick is fear, and no longer playing into that fear. We do not yield to their supposed power. We do not buy in to their apparent never-ending well of tricks and games. We do not accept the pre-determined supposition that they are unbeatable. We are the Lost Soldiers of the Highway, and this is what we see. JONATHAN RHINE’S EYES The road is already easier than it was before. A loss is a loss is a loss, and you’ve finally accepted that. Your shoulders being pinned for three seconds doesn’t change anything, and neither does the fact that it was at the hands of the woman you have supposedly gone to war with. It’s hard to even think about it in those terms, really, when you realized that none of it matters any more. The posturing, the big words, the booing of the crowd as Foster got attacked by Lane and Alex…all of that was just par for the course. Nothing to cry about, nothing to accept as unfair. You just shrug your shoulders and move on. Because the world didn’t end. Your support team didn’t crumble. The legion of fans didn’t stop cheering. You didn’t suddenly lose wrestling ability, or find yourself without a job. Nothing. Changed. And that’s a very good thing, because you have some unresolved issues that need to be taken care of. --- "Are you ready to talk about it?" he asks. Katie Malick is sitting with her back to him, poring over a textbook, or maybe just using it as an excuse. She tucks a piece of hair behind her ear as she readjusts her chair, taking extra care not to look up as she replies softly. "No." Jonathan Rhine is a patient man; it is part of the reason he has success both in the wrestling ring and, occasionally, at the poker tables. He understands that winning at anything takes time and won’t be achieved immediately. Despite all of this, he is forced to grit his teeth as he moves quickly next to his girlfriend, sitting at the counter-bar contraption that Katie has rooted herself to ever since they started staying in Foster’s guest home. He looks over at her and sees only hair, then sighs and tries again. "Katie, I just want to have an open and honest discussion." He waits for a response which is not immediate. As his patience starts to wear thin, so does his smile. It droops slightly at first, subtly, then begins to turn down as she fails to answer his "Katie?" and his gentle prodding. By the time he stands up, the smile is a faded memory, and it is with a weary grimace that he whirls his girlfriend around and forces her to look at him. However, before he can say anything he looks at her face, and with a swift intake of air he stops. Katie isn’t a very made up girl, usually electing to go for the natural look, so it is a slight shock when she wears any sort of product. What is slightly more shocking than this is when the makeup she does apply is streaming across her face, lines of mascara being washed away by thick tears. Jonathan takes a step back, trying to think of something to say. In the silence, Katie turns around again, burying her face in the book that is serving more as a tissue than as study material. "Katie, what’s wrong?" Jon says stupidly. He steps back, then forward again. After a moment, his newfound impatience takes hold again, and he grabs her once more. Turning her around and staring straight into her flushed face, he says, "Tell me what is happening." After a moment, she nods. "I don’t know. I don’t know. I look crazy, and I know the makeup isn’t doing me any favors." Jon raises his eyebrow as he asks, "Why did you wear makeup anyway?" "I wanted to look good for you, to make up for the last argument." She smiles through the tears, and Jon’s heart breaks a little more. "But then I just…I don’t know, Jon. I’m not happy." The New Life’s uneasiness in this situation is well illustrated: his fists clench and unclench, and he seems to be unsure where to place his feet, shuffling them back and forth on the carpet. He extends an arm towards her to bring her close to him, then drops it at his side. Most telling, however, are his responses: "Um, why?" She shrugs her shoulders and moves past him, wiping her face with her hand. "Because I don’t know what we’re doing here! I don’t know why we’re sitting here in your trainer’s guest house while you do crunches and ring runs until your ribs are bruised. I don’t know why you and Foster are blindly following this woman who tried to convince me you were cheating on me again, and then as an encore poisoned you in your fucking sleep! I don’t know why, after seeing all of the pain and heartache that wrestling has given you time and time again, you keep getting back up and asking for seconds, thirds, fourths and fifths!" She looks at her hand, grimaces, and then wipes it off on a towel. "I just…I don’t get it, Jon. I don’t see the allure that has obviously blinded you." Men do a lot of things that frustrate and annoy women. They disregard their feelings. They focus more on immediate pleasure than thinking about the big picture. But probably what annoys and frustrates women the most is when a woman directly tells her man exactly what she is feeling, and they decide that there must be another issue. "Are you sure none of this has to do with the argument we had before?" Jonathan Rhine isn’t the typical man, but in some cases he fits the prototype pretty damn accurately. Katie looks at Jonathan for a full thirty seconds (which, in this case, is a lot longer than it sounds) before letting loose a small scream, then throwing the towel she was holding at him. "No, it has nothing to do with our argument before, but since you seem so intent to talk about it again, let’s go back there AGAIN! I told you what happened, I apologized for my moment of weakness. What more do you want from me?" The arrival at this new subject puts The New Life slightly more at ease. His shoulders relax as he removes the towel from his face and drapes it over his shoulder, moving forward with confidence and grace until he is directly in front of her. "What I want, Katie, is real remorse. Not this little, ‘I made a mistake, tee hee, won’t you forgive me?’ bullshit you’re giving me. When I did what I did, I was heartbroken. Every day was a battle just to get up in the morning. I felt so terrible for everything that I did to you, and when it all finally came out in the open my apology was pure and genuine. I didn’t see any of that pain from your tongue-in-cheek ‘I’m sorry.’" "Tongue in cheek? Tongue in cheek?" Katie’s nostrils flare, and that is the first sign to Jon that maybe, somewhere along the way, he had made a wrong move. She begins to walk forward, poking him in the chest, as he backsteps quickly. "Listen, Jon, the first thing you should have learned when you joined that stupid wrestling federation is that a lot of people aren’t like you. They don’t have your same morals and rules, and they certainly do not have your same standards of apology. What, do you want me to come with you to the next Temptation, get on a mic and shout to the world how sorry I am? Sorry, Jonathan, but that’s not me. You think your apology was worth more than mine because you said it in front of a couple thousand people? How fucked up are you?" Katie doesn’t wait for an answer as she storms out of the guest house. Jonathan recovers and hurries after her, making it a few feet out of the house before stopping and shouting, "About as much as you are! We’re the perfect couple!" As she gets in her car and drives away, Jon looks down, shaking his head, and goes back inside. Out of the words he is mumbling, "crazy bitch" might be included, although even he isn’t aware of what he’s saying. --- Taking down Lance Marshall? Dealing with Lane Stevens’ insults? The eventual all-out battle with Alexandra Pierce? Those are nothing compared to what you go through every day. Compared to the road you are on now, anything is easier. FOSTER NACKEDY’S EYES What are you doing here? Signing on to a cause you know is doomed to fail. Renting out your home to a introverted couple and a scheming madwoman. Playing the supporting role instead of the star. Getting involved (in whatever capacity) with the first person you cared about since Magen, and the first person who screwed you over since too. Those are all literal answers to your question, but none that make you feel any better. They certainly don’t answer the real meat of the question. What are you doing here? That answer isn’t coming any time soon. And clapping as Jon hits a big move or competing in tag team matches every once in awhile isn’t going to make you feel any better as you wait for the answer. If there is an answer at all. --- "They’re fighting," Shweta says simply, looking out of the window. Foster looks up from washing his hands, then moves over behind his ex-girlfriend to see Katie’s car speed out of the driveway and Jon shake his head slowly. Foster shrugs and moves back towards the sink. "Good for them." Shweta turns around and raises her eyebrows. "Good for them? How is that possibly good for them?" She doesn’t say "or good for us" but from the tone of her voice it’s implied. "Arguments are good for a couple. It’s cathartic to rip each other to pieces, to bring each other down from time to time. It makes you realize how much you love each other." "Is that so?" Shweta chuckles, grabbing a magazine and opening it. "You certainly have a healthy opinion on relationships." Foster grunts as he grabs a package of chicken and rips it open, dumping out thawed legs and thighs on the table. "Makes sense to me. Think about it. Magen and I had fights every day. Big fights, monstrous fights, fights that would make the neighbors call the cops. You and I never fought at all." "And this is supposed to prove something? You and Magen got divorced." Foster points a finger at Shweta, then looks at his hand and laughs, as his finger is covered with chicken fat. Shaking it off, he tries again. "Yes, but Magen and I truly loved each other. You and I did not." Shweta stares at Foster for a long moment, although the gaze is not returned as the former Viper has once again immersed himself in his cooking. There is something in her eyes that he wouldn’t be able to identify if he was looking, so it is just as well that he isn’t. "That’s an interesting view you have, Mr. Nackedy." He grunts again, and for a few minutes there is uncomfortable silence. Shweta is not really reading the magazine, and Foster is barely paying attention to his chicken (as the gratuitous use of Tony Chachere’s cajun seasoning would suggest). After a few moments, Foster asks an unexpected question. "So…she killed someone you loved, didn’t she?" It takes Shweta by surprise; her arm moves suddenly up, taking with it a page from the magazine. The ripping sound makes Foster look up quizzically. Shweta’s face, a mixture of embarrassment and confusion, darkens further. "I…um…yes." "And were you loving this someone at the same time you were fake dating me?" There is only a little shame in her voice as she answers, "Yes." Foster finally decides that the conversation takes precedence over dinner, and he sets the chicken down and moves to the sink. The time it takes to wash his hands isn’t long, but the silence that looms over both of them makes it nearly unbearable. When he is finished, he sits next to her, and she unconsciously scoots away from him. He stares at her for a few moments before finally saying, "That must have been really hard for you, Shway." "Which part?" "Both." She nods, running her hand through her hair. She drops her eyes to the magazine she couldn’t read at the moment if she tried. "It was really hard. I really liked you, Foster. That’s the part that no one gets right when they make stories about it, the girl who is supposed to act like they love some guy for an elaborate plan. They always portray the girl as either a heartless soldier or a weak-kneed schoolgirl who falls in love with the target and has to abandon the plan because she can’t get over her emotions. "The reality was something more in the middle," she says, finally looking up at him. He leans in because her voice is so low. "I liked you a lot, and being in a relationship with you, however real or unreal it was, was a perk of the job. I looked forward to spending time with you, being in your arms, taking a break from being one of the strands in the web. But I was always going to do my job, and my heart always belonged – always does belong – to Kal. Just because I finished the objective doesn’t mean I cared about you any less, and just because I cared about you doesn’t mean I was a bad employee. The fact that I enjoyed doing it doesn’t take anything away from my feelings either. You’ve been around the wrestling business long enough to know that nothing is black and white." Foster nods, then looks down as well. He raises his hand to her shoulder, but in mid-motion he stops and lets it fall by his side again. Then, he sighs. "Thank you for your honesty. It means a lot to me. It…it is hard to trust you right now, hard to know whether or not we’re just walking into another one of D’s overboiled schemes again. But I still do trust you." Shweta smiles, and it is a smile that both pleases Foster and makes him uneasy at the same time. Because that smile is real, it’s genuine, it’s disarmingly beautiful – but it isn’t foreign. It’s the same smile he saw before, the smile that hid all of the plans she just revealed that she actually enjoyed doing. It makes him happy to see that smile, but it also makes him wonder what’s behind it. And this makes him stand up and walk back over to his chicken. She doesn’t say anything at first, only watches him for a few minutes as he finishes up the preparation and brings the chicken to the oven. As he stands up after loading it, she stops him. "Now can I ask you a question that needs an honest answer?" "Sure. You were honest with me." "Did you really mean what you said before, about there being no love between the two of us?" She bites her bottom lip nervously. He smiles, trying to give her the same warm smile that she gives so easily. "Let me know when the chicken’s done, okay?" He kisses her on the forehead and leaves the room, smiling as he does so. --- You don’t know if you’ll ever find an answer for what you’re doing. You still aren’t doing jumping jacks about your position in this whole Lost Soldiers thing. But you might be able to wait for an answer. Because maybe you’ll answer a few other questions along the way. SHWETA KALLEMULLAH’S EYES You always hear about people making plans and then watching things get in the way and saying, "I didn’t expect this to happen." And then they watch their plan fall apart at the seams because of unanticipated roadblocks. Obviously these people did not attend the Alexandra Pierce school of Plotting. You are not only aware that every plan yields unexpected results, you have planned for it. You are not worried about Jon and Katie’s fights, or the weird feelings about Foster that are surfacing. Even though they aren’t technically part of the plan, they are as much a part of the plan as Jon’s muscle, as Foster’s advice, as your brains. And there is nothing to do but shrug your shoulders, let the chips fall where they may, and make the most of every situation. It’s what Alex would do, after all. --- Shweta’s eyes are still a little glazed over as she turns her attention back to the ripped magazine. She smiles as the smell of baking chicken fills her nose, then slowly loses that smile as her mind settles on the events that just occurred. She was sure that Foster’s feelings for her were genuine, and his outright denial of such slightly unsettles her. It could have just been a move to protect his feelings and preserve his machismo, but Shweta, who has always been confident in her skills of perception, can’t quite figure it out. Even so, she can still sense something, and maybe if everything goes well – A knock interrupts her thinking, and after a moment of waiting for Foster to answer the door, she stands up and walks around the kitchen table, flattening her skirt as she approaches the door. A strange chill runs through her body, and she knows who is knocking (or more accurately who sent the knocker) before she answers the door. "Shweta," says Sarah Riley, standing in the doorway expectantly. Her black striped pantsuit is perfectly ironed and pressed in the way that leaves no mistake about Sarah’s current alliance. Shweta Kallemullah had been a soldier of Desade’s far too long for her to be shocked at this arrival. "Hello, Sarah. Working for the Spider again?" Realizing she will not be invited in, the Good Doctor brushes by Shweta and settles herself in the chair in the foyer. "The benefits are nice; furthermore, I’ve experienced a downturn in clients after a nasty rumor surfaced that I fell in love with a wrestler. Everyone decided it would not be prudent to relieve stress with someone who might cause them more." Shweta smiles and leans against the opposite wall. "That’s probably something you should’ve thought about before you let loose that ‘rumor’ on national television under Director’s orders. It’s not easy to get out of the web, as I’m sure you’ve found out." "There are exceptions," Sarah says lowly, and Shweta’s head bows, Kal’s screams once again ringing in her brain. Sarah lets this memory sit in the air for another moment before saying, "You are well aware that this is not a meeting of pleasure." "I don’t think such a meeting would be possible, ho-bag," Shweta returns with a sweet smile. Sarah Riley stands and makes her way to the kitchen, and Shweta follows her slowly. "Desade used a bunch of words about your arrival to SCCW, and she told me to deliver the message to you. Personally I’m done with delivering messages verbatim from her because honestly I went to too many years of school to spend the rest of my life memorizing eloquent speeches that some redhead practices in front of a mirror. So I’m just going to tell you what I got out of her spiel." "Such disrespect, Sarah; you certainly are a badass. You better watch out before your crazy rebellious attitude makes you the next fallen soldier." Shweta is often a master of subtlety, but she makes it a point to coat every syllable in sarcasm so thick it’s nearly visible. "The difference between you and I, Shway, is that I’m slightly dissenting yet loyal, and you’re just plainly dumb as shit. Now would you like to hear the message?" Shweta lets loose an exaggerated sigh. "If I must." "Thank you. Now, as I said, Desade spent most of this morning transcribing my message to you. This is the gist." Sarah Riley clears her throat, then delivers the message with the sweetest smile she can muster. "Your efforts to lead an attack against Desade in SCCW is admirable, and, for lack of a better word, cute. The Director would like to give you one last chance to drop this silly little front and run as far away from Desade and the wrestling world as you possibly can. Despite all of your trespasses against her, Desade kept you alive for a reason, and a simple one at that: she doesn’t really want to kill you. Please don’t make her do something she doesn’t want to do." After a moment, Shweta laughs and grabs a nearby water bottle. Taking a sip, she says, "How long did you spend reciting that one in your mirror?" "A half hour," Sarah Riley shrugs. "Did it show?" "Now, I know that D sent me on these types of missions all the time, but I never really understood them. So now that I’m on the other side I have to ask you. What’s the point in this offer? Does she really think that anyone in their right mind would simply go, ‘oh, you’re right, let me run for the hills now’?" For the first time, Sarah Riley drops all smiles and pretense and lets her true emotions show through. Her lip curls as she shakes her head in disgust. "How did you last as long as you did without knowing anything about this business? Of course Desade doesn’t expect you to give up. This warning isn’t some sort of last ditch effort for us to save ourselves, it is exactly what I called it: a warning. To let you know that when you are broken and bleeding at the feet of the director, you could have avoided everything if you actually used your head for once in your pathetic life. You could have not let Kal die in vain." At the mention of her former lover, Shweta also drops all pretense and raises her arm, slapping Sarah. As the Good Doctor recovers, Shweta says, "Deliver that message to D, verbatim. If I ran away, Kal’s death would still be in vain. But giving me the strength to fight her, fight all of you, will be Kal’s purpose. Now get out of Foster’s house." Sarah puts a hand to her reddening face and suddenly smiles. "I don’t know where you got all of these budding emotions from, Shweta, but they will cause you nothing but trouble. The other reason to these missions, as you call them, is for the Director to see what she is up against. Thank you for proving that it won’t be a difficult battle." As Shweta stares at her coldly, the man who compromised Jonathan Rhine’s morals tucks a blonde strand behind her ear and sniffs the air. "Smells like burning chicken. Good day, Shweta." As Sarah Riley turns on her heel and walks out of the house, Shweta’s eyes widen quickly, and she runs to the bedroom. "Foster! Foster!" --- The unexpected isn’t supposed to be a deterrent, she told you. She told you to accept unwelcome changes and use it as part of your plan. But maybe she was telling you a lot of things because she knew this would happen. Maybe she was lying just because. Maybe you should start thinking about your plan. We have three sets of eyes. We see problems on the domestic front, with an inability to find understanding. We see problems in the short term, putting the goals of the group ahead of individual wants and feelings. We see problems in the long term, coming to grips with exactly what we are fighting and how we will be victorious. We see all of these things for what they really are. And as long as we see them this way, we will not see defeat. We will see the light at the end of the tunnel. We will see tomorrow.
