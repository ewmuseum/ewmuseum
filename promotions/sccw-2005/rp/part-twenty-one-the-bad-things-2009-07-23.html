Part Twenty-One: The Bad Things

23 July 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/23/09 It was at moments like these that Lance Marshall started to truly believe that there was no God. It was never an idea Lance would seriously entertain, to be clear. The part of his brain that had been conditioned by years of Sunday church services and Midnight Mass at Christmas wouldn’t allow it. Not to mention the possibility of a smack upside the head from his father and an admonition to quit talking such foolishness. But right now, stuck smack in the middle of a mass of traffic that looked like it was staying put for the duration, Lance would be damned if he thought the universe was working like it was supposed to. The L.A. freeway system currently resembled nothing so much as the playroom of a child who had very carefully arranged his vast collection of Hot Wheels in a very precise order…and then immediately abandoned them, already bored and moving on to the next diversion. Traffic was moving along at a pace which would have even turtles urging drivers to hurry the fuck up and, not for the first time today, Lance found himself grateful for the well appointed SUV he was currently driving. Give me leather and AC, Lance thought with a chuckle, and I can handle anything. Shame about the radio, though. Oh, it was working just fine…there just wasn’t anything worth listening to. Every radio station seem to have come to a collective agreement that not only would they all play the same short list of songs but that they would do so at twenty minute intervals. He’d already heard the Black Eyed Peas more than could possibly be healthy…and who the fuck needed to hear that stupid "Calle Ocho" song that many times in one hour?! He’d eventually resorted to stopping on stations during commercial breaks as the ads were proving far more entertaining than the music. He was about to change stations yet again when the DJ broke into the end of a song. "Hey, that was Shakira’s new single, She-Wolf. You’re listening to Terry Day at Midday and I’ve got some bad news for fans of the Beastie Boys. Group member MCA, better known as Adam Yauch, issued a statement on the band’s website today that he’s been diagnosed with cancer. Doctors apparently found a tumor in Yauch’s salivary gland." Shit. Lance’s mind couldn’t help but drift to the thought of his wife who was currently lying in a hospital bed, undergoing another round of treatment. He’d desperately wanted to stay with her…but she had all but pushed him out of the room, reminding him that things needed to be done and hey, life didn’t stop just because she had the Big C, you know? "The group is having to cancel all currently scheduled tour dates and has indefinitely postponed the release of their new album, "Hot Sauce Committee, Part One". As part of the statement, Yauch apologized to the fans for any inconvenience." Now that was class, Lance thought. Diagnosed with a major illness and he was apologizing to the fans for inconveniencing them. No need for apologies, Lance thought. Health was more important than anything. "For all of us here at the station, I wanna say…just do you right now, MCA. Take all the time you need to get better and, when you’re ready, come back and kick a rhyme about how you kicked cancer’s ass." Lance laughed at that, the first real laugh he had allowed himself in some time. Damn straight, he thought. "And now, let’s send some good thoughts MCA’s way with a little something from the Boys." Lance wouldn’t have sworn to it but it suddenly seemed as if the traffic began to speed up, formerly stagnant clumps of cars now moving along again at a healthy pace. Almost as if they were responding to the music coming out of the stereo. Despite everything, Lance couldn’t help but smile as the opening notes of the song began to drift out of the SUV’s sound system. #Because you can’t, you won’t and you don’t stop…# #Love, thy will be mine/and make me strive for the glorious and divine…# One of the benefits of a nearly full iPod (only 1 gig of space remaining on a 120 gig model, she marveled) is that you couldn’t reasonably be expected to remember everything that you’d put on there. So, if you managed to find a favorite little early 90’s era guilty pleasure when digging around your list of available songs…well, who was gonna say anything? Besides, it wasn’t like there was much else to do. Alanna sighed as she shifted around in the hospital bed, careful not to jostle the IV line pumping drugs into her body. As much as the song was helping her to relax, the bed was somehow working just as hard to do the exact opposite. It was almost miraculous, she thought. She had experience of hospital beds up and down the country (the business she and her husband were in had seen to that) and she had yet to find one that was in any way comfortable. Lance had left about an hour ago, protesting the entire time. She loved that about him, how he desperately wanted to try and protect her, how he wished this disease she was dealing with something physical so he could take it and beat the shit out of it. But she had been just as firm in reminding him that the world was still turning in spite of everything. Errands had to be run, Zach had to be picked up from school and, hey, didn’t he have a match that he needed to prep for? So he had grumbled and stamped his feet but, in the end, he had gone. Although she would never say so out loud, Alanna was grateful for this for two reasons. The first was that she hated her husband seeing her like this. For someone who had spent her entire life priding herself on being able to handle anything, suddenly being at the mercy of doctors and nurses and drugs…it was galling. Things were largely out of her control and she hated that feeling worse than she could possibly express. The second reason was that she tended to be lousy company during these sessions anyway. Several of the previous sessions had involved Lance taking a book with him as she tended to spend most of the time asleep or on the verge of dropping off. Even now, despite the bed’s best efforts, the combination of the drugs and the song’s beat were threatening to lull her back into dreamland. Her focus was wandering so badly, in fact, that it took until the second knock on the door for her to even register someone was knocking, much less respond. "Come in." "Hey, gorgeous." Alanna tended to joke about Michael O’Connor that his smile entered the room before he did. That had been the first thing she had noticed about him after all; a smile so perfectly arranged and gleaming white that most dentists would be willing to sell their soul to use it in their advertisements. Lance and Michael had first met when both were trying out for a now defunct Los Angeles based wrestling federation. Neither of them had ended up getting hired (a fact Alanna to this day couldn’t understand) but they had bonded during the tryout process and become fast friends. While Lance had stayed in wrestling, Michael had moved down a different path: first bodybuilding, then modeling and some acting. He wasn’t exactly what you’d call a household name but he was doing well and happy. To be honest, Alanna couldn’t help but feel some small measure of satisfaction when she heard the fed the two men had tried out for had gone under less than a year later. Michael continued to flash that trademarked smile of his as he shrugged a black canvas gym bag off of his well-muscled shoulder. With a quick flick of the wrist, the bag landed on the unoccupied hospital bed opposite Alanna. "Hey, Mikey," Alanna drawled sleepily, "Thanks for stopping by." "No problem," Mikey responded. Glancing around him, he added "Hey, where’s the big guy? Getting a cup of coffee or something?" "I asked him to go." Bless him, thought Alanna, Mikey could make even confusion look good. "Wait, what? And why?" "There were things that had to be done, you know," Alanna answered. "Besides, he’s got a big match coming up that he needs to get ready for. He can’t spend his time being worried about me." "He’s gonna worry about you anyway," said Michael, "so that’s a bullshit answer. So, what’s really going on?" Alanna sighed. "I spend most of my time here asleep or falling asleep, I didn’t want him to just sit here and waste his time with me. Besides, what we’re doing is supposed to be a surprise, right?" Michael popped his right eyebrow in response (a quirk that he’d somehow managed to teach Lance) but elected to press the issue no further. Instead, he asked "So, how are you feeling?" "You know me," answered Alanna. "I’m doing great." Michael shook his head. "You get one bullshit pass with me a day, Lani, and you’ve already used yours. Come on, the truth." Another sigh from Alanna. "For real? This stuff is kicking my ass. But I’m gonna fight it and I’m gonna beat it." "Now, that’s the Lani I know and love," enthused Michael. "He’s worried sick about you, you know," he added. "He tries not to show it but he is." "I know," said Alanna, stifling a yawn. "And I hate what this is doing to him." "Hey," said Michael, "worry about you now. Lance will be okay as long as you are. Now, since you don’t look like staying awake for too much longer, how about we take care of what we had planned?" Alanna nodded. Michael went over to the gym bag, unzipped it and removed from it a small camcorder. Quickly turning it on, he took up a position in front of Alanna’s bed. "Now, smile for the camera!" Alanna quickly stuck he tongue out at the camcorder before quickly composing herself. Looking straight into the camera lens, she began to speak. "Hi, babe. Sorry I couldn’t be there with you, tonight…" #Now go to sleep, bitch/die, motherfucker, die…# Lance Marshall had been punching at the heavy bag steadily for the past thirty minutes and, if he went to form, would be doing so for at least half an hour more. Sweat was seeping into his t-shirt and his breathing was starting to become a bit more labored. It was getting harder to lift his arms and swing at the bag. Lance Marshall paid attention to none of that. Because when it came to motivation, imagining Amy Capmbell’s face on the other end of his fist was doing a hell of a job. If he were to slow down and think about it logically, Lance knew that he shouldn’t be allowing Aimz to get to him this badly. She was an irritant, like a tick or a mosquito; something that got under your skin and annoyed you but was ultimately meaningless. But logic, at this moment, could go shove it. It wasn’t as if she was the only person in the Dome that he would have issues with. Spacely was an unknown and required some research. He and Phillip Kennedy weren’t exactly the best of friends but at least they understood each other, neither trying to bullshit the other into believing that they were something they weren’t. And Desade…well, anyone who didn’t know the nature of the relationship between Lance Marshall and Desade by now wasn’t exactly paying attention. But Aimz…Aimz infuriated Lance to an irrational, almost obsessive degree. Here was a woman who had managed to rack up a nearly year long reign on the back of Desade and her army…yet crowed that her wins were due to her ability. Here was a woman who delighted in screwing people over every chance she got…yet tried to portray herself as the injured party. Here was a woman who had seized the moral high ground when Jonathan Rhine had cheated on Katie Malick…when, with her past, she and the moral high ground shouldn’t have even been on speaking terms. Hell, given her present she had no right to be acting self righteous. The rumors around the backstage that Aimz was back on hard drugs had only grown louder ever since she had decided to ally herself with Desade. And if they only knew what Desade and Aimz were really up to backstage… Oh, wouldn’t that be fun, whispered an evil little voice in the back of his head. Get Darcy alone in the back one show, have a quiet little whisper in his ear. Hey, Darcy, wouldn’t you like to know what your wife’s been up to when your back is turned? All the times she’s been claiming to be away on work related stuff…and she’s been sleeping with the most hated woman in all of SCCW. After everything you’ve done for her, this is how she repays you? No. Not yet. A weapon this powerful was not something to be used for trifling reason, was not something to be used in a fit of spite or pique. No, something like this needed to be held in reserve, used only when circumstances really demanded it. For now, he would just have to be content with taking the Universal Title away from Aimz…of stripping away from her the one thing that defined her, that allowed her the inflated, deluded sense of self-worth that she possessed. If he was to be completely honest, he didn’t need to be in possession of the Universal Title at the end of the night in order to be happy. It would be nice, of course, but it wasn’t necessary. He could live with losing to Spacely or Kennedy…hell, he’d even be able to swallow down a loss to Desade. Just as long as Aimz exited the Dome with nothing. Not the Universal title, not the Gateway, not the Livewire, not even the SIN belts. Nothing. For Nothing. A fitting end. Lance Marshall continued to pound away at the heavy bag, hoping that with each blow a little more of the anger burning in his chest would fade away…and resigning himself to the fact that it would not. #One, two, Freddy’s coming for you…# "MOMMY! DADDY!" Lance Marshall jerked awake with a start at the sound of his son’s screams. Besides him, Alanna stirred awake, sleep fogging her voice. "Was that Zach?" she muttered. "Is he okay?" Lance placed a hand on his wife’s shoulder. "Stay here, okay. You need your sleep." He placed a kiss on her cheek. "I’ll look in on Zach," he added. "Okay," murmured Alanna as she slipped back into sleep while Zach padded his way out of their bedroom and down the hall to Zach’s room. As he made his way down the hall, Lance found himself hoping that the problem wasn’t related to what had happened earlier that night. Zach had been put to bed an hour earlier and both of his parents had thought him sound asleep. A quick flip through the channels had found the tail end of a showing of "A Nightmare on Elm Street" (the original and best) which the Marshalls had decided to watch, taking care to keep the volume low so as not to wake Zach up. Unfortunately, Freddy’s final confrontation with Nancy had coincided exactly with when Zach had wandered into the living room, hoping to ask his parents for a glass of water. They had quickly turned off the TV once they’d realized he was in the room, gotten Zach his glass of water and sent him back to bed as quickly as they could. But he had managed to see some of the movie and Lance couldn’t help but wonder if the damage had been done. It’s not that he had anything against horror movies, far from it. It’s just that he’d been hoping not to have this discussion with Zach until Zach was at least ten years old. After all, that’s how old he’d been when Charlie had forced him to sit through "The Exorcist" (and boy, had he been punished for that). Zach was sitting bolt upright in bed, the light next to his bed turned on. His body was locked tight, his eyes watery, his hands shaking. Crap, Lance thought, this was not good. He quickly moved to Zach’s side, placing a comforting hand around Zach’s shoulders and ruffling his hair. Zach sniffled in response. "You okay, champ?" Zach turned to face his father, tears forming in his eyes. "No." "Did you have a bad dream?" Zach simply nodded in response. Lance braced himself. "Do you wanna tell me about it?" The words came from Zach in a flood. "TherewasthebadmanfromtheTVandhehadthoseknivesonhishandandhewasgonnahurtMommyandIcouldn’tdoanythingIcouldn’tmoveorscreamoranythingandIwassoscared!" Lance didn’t answer. He couldn’t. Instead, he drew his son to him, gripping him in a tight hug as Zach cried on his shoulder. The sight of his son in such fear tore at Lance’s heart. When he could feel Zach start to relax, Lance put him back into bed and tucked him in. Ruffling his son’s hair again, he whispered to him "I wanna tell you something about the bad man, champ." "What, Daddy?" "See, the bad man only has power when you’re afraid of him. So, if you see him again, I want you to yell at him as loud as you can that you’re not afraid of him and that he should just go away. Cause if he doesn’t, you’re gonna get your daddy to beat him up." Zach giggled. "Okay, Daddy. I will." It hadn’t been that easy, of course. Lance ended up staying at Zach’s bedside for another thirty minutes, talking about school and cartoons and video games and promising that yes, of course he would take Zach to see "G-Force" as soon as he could. Eventually, sleep started to overtake the youngest Marshall and he found himself snuggling into bed. Lance gave Zach a kiss on the cheek as he got up to go. "You wanna sleep with the light on tonight, sport?" Zach nodded sleepily. "G’night, Daddy," he muttered, his eyes closing. "G’night, champ." Lance made it to the doorway of his son’s room before Zach spoke one last time. "I love you, Daddy," he muttered, half asleep. Lance paused, struck. Now it was him trying not to cry. "I love you too, champ."
