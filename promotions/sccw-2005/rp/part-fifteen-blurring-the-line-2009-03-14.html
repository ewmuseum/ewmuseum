Part Fifteen: Blurring The Line

14 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/14/09 There’s an unfortunate misconception attached to the world of professional wrestling. We could spend days discussing this particular misconception in detail, outlining its various nuances and shadings. But, as we have not the time (nor, if we’re being completely honest, the interest), we’ll boil it down to its essential point. Which is, as follows: Professional wrestling has very little to do with "reality". Note that this is not a misconception held by most of the folks actually involved in the sport. Those who make their living in and around the squared circle know full well that there is a heavy element of fantasy involved in what they do. Things are never entirely what they seem. Neither, for that matter, are people. In a business where it seems that every second person is either a criminal mastermind, Bruce Wayne wannabe or embodiment of pure evil, the stench of bullshit can tend to permanently burn in ones nostrils. But, like most things in life, things are never truly that simple. True, some of the men and women traipsing their way across your screens are creations of pure fantasy, created out of lurid imaginings and hot button triggers. Others, however, are very close off screen to how they are onscreen. Sure, certain aspects of their personalities are likely pumped up to eleven when they’re on camera, inflated to near cartoon like levels for the benefit of the audience…but even a vastly exaggerated version of a person is still, to some extent, that person. And if a "villain" displays an unexpected moment of mercy or a "hero" does something truly shocking…such things are quickly glossed over, dismissed and forgotten so that the integrity of the "character" is maintained. The audience continues on, secure in their knowledge of what they believe to be "real". For the audience, believing in what’s shown onscreen is fine. But when the talent starts doing it? Well, that’s just career suicide. Lance Marshall paced towards the refrigerator, bare feet slapping against the hardwood flooring. He paused for a moment in front of the refrigerator, enjoying the unfamiliar sound of complete and total quiet. Alanna was still out running errands while Zach was asleep in the next room. Although he had protested that he was a big boy and didn’t need a nap, Zach had offered Lance little resistance when Lance had tucked him into bed a few moments earlier. Now, as he stood in front of the counter drinking from a bottle of water and flicking through the mail, Lance found his mind drifting again. This time, to work and what had been happening for the past few weeks. Amy Campbell had managed to do it again. He had been in possession of another opportunity to take the Universal Title from her, another chance to strip the belt from someone who was champion in name only. For most of the match, he had dominated Campbell, decisively proving his superiority as a wrestler and competitor. And then it happened. One pop to the junk, one tainted pin…and once again, Amy Campbell manages to slither away with the win. It should have infuriated him. Under any other circumstances, it almost certainly would have. But not this time. For Amy Campbell’s behavior in the aftermath of Defiance had said more about her than Lance could have possibly imagined…to a point where Lance (to his personal shock) felt not just contempt but something close to pity for Campbell. The gossip had gone around, as it would, that Campbell didn’t deserve to be holding the belt. That Lance was the rightful Universal Champion. There had even been a sign or two in the audience to that effect. While Lance appreciated the sentiment, he had recognized it for what it was: an ego boost and nothing more. Amy had stomped out to the ring soon after Defiance, as expected. But she had not stood defiantly in the center of the ring, brandishing the title and telling the doubters and haters that they could kiss her ass. Instead, she had railed and ranted, tried to paint herself as the poor, put-upon martyr who would never be able to do enough to earn the respect that a champion deserved. That had been bad enough. But the whining…that a "champion" would go out in front of the crowd and whine…that had been truly pathetic. It had been that moment that Lance had come to a realization about Amy Campbell, about why she clung so desperately to the Universal Title and did whatever possible to keep it. Because without it, what was she? A sociopathic junkie who treated everyone around her like garbage and expected them to respect her for it? A craven, desperately needy woman who jonesed for the respect and fear of the audience as she had once done for cocaine? Someone who believed herself to be empty without the validation of others? Lance had longed believed all of this of Campbell…and more. But what the night, that extended rant inside the ring had proven to Lance is that not only did Campbell believe it as well…but that she knew it to be the truth. That, coupled with the secret Lance now knew Campbell to be keeping, was probably the only thing keeping Lance from choking on his contempt for Campbell. Lance stopped on a small manila envelope bearing his address and the corporate address of SCCW. He opened it quickly and found inside a DVD-R with a post-it note attached reading: For your attention Five minutes later saw Lance sprawled out on the couch fronting the large plasma TV which dominated his living room, the screen humming into life as the DVD player whirred silently. The image on the screen resolved itself into a man that Lance did not recognize. It wasn’t until the first reporter opened his mouth to ask the stranger a question that he had realized that this was Brock Alyas, the man whom he would be facing in the first round of the No Limit tournament…the tournament that would finally return the belt where it belonged. It took but a moment for that confusion to turn into laughter; a convulsive fit of laughter so great that he did not hear the sound of key being fitted into lock or of the front door being slowly opened. It took but another moment for the laughter to disappear, replaced by seething anger. "You have got to be fucking KIDDING me!" "Swear jar," replied a honeyed voice behind him, a small plastic jar suddenly appearing before Lance’s face. He grumbled slightly but reached into his pocket and placed a five dollar bill into the jar without any further argument. Alanna moved to join her husband on the couch, sliding in next to him and placing the plastic jar on the tabletop. Her gaze moved towards the screen where she caught the end of Alyas’s ramble. "I see you’ve met Brock Alyas." "Yeah. Dunno what the f…what the hell he thinks he’s accomplishing with it." "I doubt he was even aware of what he was saying," Alanna replied. "I hope not. Because, if he was, that was one pathetically tired ass attempt at trash talk." "Trust me," Alanna replied, "that whole I’m so uber-blitzed that the dudes from Half-Baked would be telling him to chill the fuck out thing he’s got going? From what I understand, that’s just standard operating for Mr. Alyas." "What have you heard?" Alanna rises gracefully from the couch, shoes click-clacking along the floor as she makes her way to the kitchen. "You know that whole "No one wants to face me a second time" bit he did at the end?" "Uh huh." "Word is, it’s not because he’s a particularly fierce competitor." "So what is the word?" "Incompetent. Accident waiting to happen. Liable to cripple someone one of these days level bad." "That’s more of a phrase, babe." Alanna’s only answer was the soft thump of a dish towel as it hit the back of Lance’s head. Lance placed the towel on the tabletop, allowing a mock "Ow!" of pain in response. "So," said Alanna as her voice drifted back from the kitchen, "workout week?" "Oh, hell no. Dude may look so smashed that he can’t walk a straight line without tripping but I’m not convinced. Trust me, I’m in the mood to do some schoolin’." The image of Brock Alyas disappears, replaced by a blank blue screen as Lance stops the DVD-R and ejects it from the player. He strides over to the player, placing inside it another DVD which had been resting atop the player. Mission accomplished, he once again took his place on the couch. "Hurry back, babe. I’ve got something I need to show you." In a few moments time, Lance and Alanna Marshall will be paying very close attention to a past episode of Temptation. Specifically, a match between Aimz and Mayhem. Even more specifically, one particular moment within that match…a moment which will help Alanna Marshall realize something that her husband has known for a few weeks now. But that’s a story for another time. We now return you to your regularly scheduled lives, already in progress.
