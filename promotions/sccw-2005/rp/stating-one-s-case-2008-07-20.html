Stating One's Case...

20 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-20-08 "So, how long do you think you're going to be here?" Benito Minorez lifts his head, a string of cheddar cheese dangling between fork and chin. He breaks the line with a swipe of his tongue, continuing along his lips to soak up any sauce left over from his last bite of cheese enchilada. He answers with a shrug of his shoulders, his eyes fixed on the younger looking Latina woman across from him at the table. "I'm not sure," he finally says, before washing his food down with a drink from a tall glass of iced tea. "I never expected this from her, and I'm not sure how Pierce ties in with it all." "So you're just going to keep hiding?" tucking a stray strand of long black hair back behind her ear, the Hispanic woman takes a drink from her own glass – her eyes never waver from his as she awaits an answer. "I'm not hiding," he replies, though his eyes shift away from her glare – the guilt of knowing she is right stabbing him in his chest. "Then what do you call this?" She leans back in her chair, her eyes traveling around the room as she judges the environment. "You didn't come all the way to Mexico to deal with everything, so don't lie to me." "What do you want me to tell you, Lettie?" Benny drops his fork onto his plate in frustration. "I don't know how to deal with this. My daughter hates me so much. She despises me to the point where she would manipulate Jay -- use him to get to me. I know that Pierce is behind it all, but I can't for the life of me figure out why. What's in it for her?" "I don't know," she responds, placing the fork on her plate. "I wasn't around when you and your brother were running around the Council. What I do know, is that your daughter is probably in way over her head if this Pierce woman is as bad as you say she is, and you're here." "Leticia, I love you, but don't sit there and judge me on this. It's my meddling in her life that probably caused this in the first place. I should have just let her live her life." "Exactly," she exclaims. "But now it is too late to go back and change what you did. What you need to do now is protect her from what this woman can do to her. Forget about everything else and just remember she is your daughter." "I haven't forgot that," he turns away from her, embarrassed by the direction of the conversation. "But somewhere along the line I think she did." "Then remind her. Don't sit here in this shithole and pretend everything is going to fix itself, because its not." "I'm not as young as I use to be, Lettie. I don't see how me going back there is going to change anything either." He rests his elbows on the edge of the table, flanking his plate as he stares at his food. "She got what she wanted. The gym is hers to do with as she pleases." "Do you still think this is about the gym?" She pauses after the question, leaving it hanging there for him to fully digest before she asks the next one. "Are you really that blind?" "I don't know what to think anymore." He pushes the plate away, his appetite having left once the conversation had changed from how much she missed him to how bad a father he has been. "I just know that it isn't my place to question her anymore." "So you're just going to keep hiding then?" "I have nothing left to go back for." The gasp rang in his ears longer than it had taken for it to escape her mouth, but he still managed to hear what she said when she stood up and threw her napkin onto her plate. "Well I do." "Dammit." * * * * * * * It had only been a month since the doors and windows of the Minor Wrestling Academy had been boarded up, and yet graffiti already decorated the walls with amateurish tag names and more intricate designs from talented artists that culled their art much like a Da Vinci or Michaelangelo. For some, like the amateurish tag names for example, it was easy to discern who it was that had vandalized the property -- as the names were meant to be noticed and understood. For others, the ones who had taken their time to plan out in advance which colors to use and in which order, it was much more difficult -- as colors blended together in intricate twists and turns like one of those pictures that you need to focus on to see the hidden design. Jadian Bridden stood there in front of the front doors, his hands on his hips as he took it all in. A maroon dress shirt -- with the top two buttons unfastened and the sleeves rolled up to just below his elbow -- hung loosely over the top of dark denim jeans that partially covered black steel toed boots. He couldn't help but feel disgusted about how things had turned out, it was never intended to go this far. "Reminiscing?" Jadian turned to look over his shoulder, where the other half of where the gym had gained its name stood. Miguel Minorez walked up beside Jay and pulled a pair of platinum framed Oakley sunglasses off of his face. He folded them up and stuck them into the inside coat pocket of the white blazer that covered up a powder blue dress shirt – the coat actually matched the snow white slacks that draped around brown loafers like a skirt around the bottom of a Christmas tree. "Just trying to figure out why we haven't heard from Benny." "He knows better than to show his face," Mikey said, taking a moment to scan the artwork that covered the building like a canvas. "He doesn't trust either one of you anymore." "And why hasn't he contacted you?" Mikey smiled, his teeth showing a remarkable resemblance to his coat -- thousands of dollars in dental surgery can do that for you. "Oh, my brother stopped talking to me long before you and Juli started playing with his mind. It would take something big for him to swallow his pride and give me a call." Jadian nodded, Julissa had told him of the falling out between the brothers. Ironically, Desade had a hand in that as well. Though, it was not intentionally, as the two brothers had a difference of opinion as to how to deal with her closing of the Council. "I heard Connors is running around Sin City now too." Mikey's smirk accompanied a head shake. "The band's back together." "Connors hasn't showed his cards yet," Jadian said with a knowing glance, understanding exactly what the comment inferred. "But I somehow doubt he found Jesus." "The guy's a snake, Jay." Mikey told him, though he was sure Bridden already knew that. "Don't drop your guard around him, and never, ever turn your back to him." "Yeah, I figured that." Jay turns to face Juli's uncle. "But I don't think you called me here to tell me that. So... What's up?" "It's about Riley." Jadian threw his arms up, then turned his back on Mikey as he took a few steps. "Are you kidding me?" "Look," Mikey said, extending a hand in Jadian's direction even though he could not see it with his back to him. "Juli called and asked me to take a look at her." "She called you!" He turned back to face the former NWC superstar, his face red with anger. "Who else can she turn to?" It wasn't really a question. "She needed someone to do some digging, and your new friends aren't real fond of putting each others darkest secrets on display." "If you're doing this for Juli," his left eyebrow raises slightly. "Why did you call to meet with me?" "Because, Jay," Mikey walked over to where Jay had stopped and put a hand on his shoulder. "You're the one I'm worried about." * * * * * * * "Wait." Benito Minorez ran up to Leticia De La Garza and placed a hand on her shoulder to keep her from getting into the cab she had called. The little yellow car blew dust from the unpaved road into the air, the puff of gray smoke coming out from the exhaust managed to kick it up with each revolution of the engine. "Que queires, Benito?" she asked in her native tongue, a sure sign that she was angry with him. The old gentleman, with the white wifebeater that exposed his day laborer's tan tucked into khaki work pants, grabbed a hold of her luggage and stuffed it into the trunk of the car. The trunk creaked a bit, the old hinges having gone years without a drop of oil to lubricate them in the blistering Mexico heat. "I'm sorry," he told her, his hand now on her chin to lift her head to look into his eyes. Streaks of tears could still be seen, and she made no move to wipe them away. "I didn't mean what I said. Not the way you thought I did." "I can't do this, Benny." She shook her head, moving away from his grasp. "This isn't the life I expected when we started dating. I know you have issues with your daughter, I understand that." "I didn't-" "Let me finish," she interrupted, her index finger firmly pressed against his lips. "I came with you to Mexico because I thought you needed time to be away from it all. I thought we would come here for a while, then go back once you had time to cool off. I didn't think you would just run away from it all, and now that I know that is what you are doing... I can't do it with you." "Things are different, I know that." "Different?" Tears trickled down rose blotched cheeks as she turned to face him, her eyes red with anger. "You have a daughter basically screaming out for your attention -- for you to just pay attention to her for what she is -- and you run away from it all. What happened to you? What happened to the strong man I fell in love with?" "I..." he choked on his words, his chin nearly tucked into his chest as he stared at his dirty bare feet. "I don't know." "Well," she turned away from him, her hand now on the edge of the open door. "When you find him, have him give me a call." He looked up from his feet in time to watch the door slam shut behind her. The sound of the gravel being crushed beneath the tires as the car pulled away brought tears to flow from his own eyes, though they had been there since she had stood up from the table earlier. They were only going to be joined by more.
