Funeral For A Friend

30 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-30-08 The memories start coming to him. He remembers being in that broken ring in a dark and hot San Jose warehouse. He remembers walking into it, and the first thing that he saw was the trainer using duct tape to fix the canvas. He looked at the clock on the wall above the improvised locker room. It read 8:13 in the morning. "Are you Jason Callion?" The old man in the center of the ring looked up. In front of him was a kid that was no more than 18 or 19, maybe weighed a buck ninety. "Who is it to you?" "My name is Jay Ryan. I was told to come see you if I wanted to try out pro wrestling." The old man looked at the kid. "Jason isn’t here right now. I’m Clay. I help out around here. But I’m going to tell you something. You’re going to need to put some meat on those bones if you want to become a professional wrestler." The kid didn’t move an inch. "I know that I can be good. I know it in my heart." The old man looked up into the kids eyes. "You don’t have it in your soul. You want to be a wrestler because it’s a fast way to earn money." "No sir." "Get out of here, kid. You don’t belong here." Jay Ryan set his bag down, and he stood there. A couple of hours passed, and Jason Callion was in the ring with some of the other students. Finally, he looked over at Clay. "Clay, who is that kid?" "Someone that says they want to be a wrestler." "How long has he been standing there?" "Well, he got here around quarter after eight." Clay looked up at the clock, which now read 3:22 in the afternoon. "About seven hours." Callion held up his left hand, and that brought about a stop to his trainees work. He then exited the ring and walked up to the kid. "What’s your name, son?" "Jason Ryan. But everyone calls me Jay, sir." "OK, Jay, what are you doing here?" "I came to be a professional wrestler, sir." "Is that right?" "Yes sir." ----------------------------------- Now, almost 20 years later, that young kid was now Jay Draven. He was on a plane back to San Jose. He had learned that Jason Callion had passed away. Another funeral that Jay didn’t want to go to. Serena sat next to him. She knew that it was rough for her husband. This was a man that brought him up in the business. Jay even went as far as teaming up with him, as his storyline son. "Honey, are you OK?" The last years of Jason Callion’s life were hard for Jay. They had a falling out stemming from the fact that Jay used steroids to bulk up to 265 pounds and had gotten back with his first wife, who was also a student of Callion’s. They hadn’t talked in years. "Serena, I don’t feel right doing this." "I know. But you have to remember what he did for you. What you did for him, also." ----------------------------------- A year after first stepping foot in the school, the young kid was no longer a 190 pound 5th string cornerback for San Jose State University. Now he was up to 215, and made the decision to leave school. By day, he worked in the school, doing some of the things that Clay had done only a year prior. He did that, because he could no longer afford to take the lessons. He wrestled on the weekends and worked nights at a local warehouse. Jay even lived in a loft that was put together in an old office at this warehouse. "Kid!" Jason no longer called Jay by name, but as kid. Jay looked up from his patching up the ring and saw Callion there with two new students. "Sir." The respect was always there. "This is Mike and Kelly Durant. They’re going to be staying here for a while." Jay pulled himself out of the ring. "Husband and wife trainees. That’s different." Mike got a chuckle from this. He didn’t want to be there. "Actually, we’re brother and sister." Kelly said as she looked at Jay. "I think you need a haircut." Kelly ran her hand through the mop of hair that was on his head, and then across the blonde stubble that was on his face. "A shave too." "So, this is what a California boy looks like." Mike added. "Actually, I’m a transplant. Came here from Michigan." "Really, where at?" "Wayne." "Wow, we’re from West Bloomfield." From that day on, Jay had found a lifelong friend and a first wife. ----------------------------------- Jay and Serena arrived in San Jose. He didn’t pack a suitcase. He was only going to be there for a few hours. He looked out of the window of the airport towards where the General Aviation landing area is, and saw a familiar sight. It was a black and silver Cessna, the logo on the tail said "Damage Inc." on it. "What the…" Before Serena can finish the words that were coming from her mouth, Jay put a single finger to her lips. Jay had paid for that plane, and only once set foot in it. "This isn’t the time or the place, Serena." ----------------------------------- Livingston High School in Livingston, New Jersey was where Jay had arrived. Charles, one of the security guards that Kirk Thornton had hired behind Joey Long’s back was watching a custom made Harley Davidson, as Jay walked towards a makeshift office. The sounds of the Ryan Mercer/Jared Sykes match were audible in the hallway. Jay had arrived at the door of the office, and knocked. "Karl, can you get that?" The door to the office opened and Jay saw Kirk Thornton, the man that gave him a North American Title shot sitting in the office, looking like someone had told him that he was bankrupt. "Jay, come in. Take a seat." Jay entered the office, but this wasn’t like the other times that he had talked to Kirk. Kirk seemed different. Jay just stood there, crossed his arms and cracked his once surgically repaired neck. "Are we going to play this game again?" Jay had no clue what was about to happen. He wasn’t in that great of a mood either, having just lost the North American Championship in another territory. "Kirk, what do you want this time? As you can tell, I really haven't had the greatest week, and I'm not in the mood for any bullshit." "Well, Jay. I think I'm going to be blunt with you. You've made me a lot of money, so I'm going to hate doing this to you." Kirk stated as he slid a folder to Jay. "What's this?" Jay asked as he picked up the folder. "I've been informed that you may have been in violation of the NWC's controlled substance policy. More specifically, the policy about addictions to prescription medication." "What the hell are you talking about?" "Jay, you ex-wife Kelly called me up and said that you were possibly addicted to pain killers and are attaining them illegally." "You believed her? This really is funny." "At first I didn't, but I took a look at our recent insurance records. It seems that in a one month period, you attained 4 seperate prescriptions for pain killers." "I don't believe this." "I don't want to, but I have to look at the evidence that's in front of me." "Bullshit." Jay screams as Kirk stands up and looks him right in the eye. "Jay, I don't think that you really understand the severity of what I have to do to you." "What the hell are you talking about?" "I have to make an example out of you. That's why I've decided to suspend you. You won't be able to wrestle in any NWC-sanctioned events." "So, how long am I suspended for? One month? Two?" "Try indefinitely. The only way that you are going to be able to come back to the NWC is by completing a rehab program." "Well, Kirk, I just need to tell you something. Fuck you, fuck Joey, and fuck the Atlantic. I don't need this bullshit." Jay angrily stomped away from the desk as slammed the door shut as he exited. ----------------------------------- The airport lounge was bustling with people. But it now sits as a place where a moment no one thought would ever happen would take place. Sitting on one side of the table, dressed up in a black pants suit, with her long, brown hair draped over her shoulders is Kelly Durant-Ryan, the CEO and Majority shareholder of Damage Inc. Across from her sits her polar opposite. A former fitness trainer with short black hair, Serena Ryan. They just look at each other. Their only common thread was Jay. "So, Kel, how have you been?" "Not bad. When I found out about Jason, I knew I had to be here for the funeral." "You realize that Jason didn’t like you. The only reason he put up with you was because of Jay." "I’m not here for Jason, I’m here for Jay and for Mike. If it wasn’t for Jason, they would never have been a team. They would never have gone up and down the road as long as they did, as friends, as partners, as brothers." "Kelly, after everything you’ve ever done. You still don’t get it. You might want to think that you’re here for them, but you’re really here for yourself. Maybe you can finally get closure on your failure of a life. The fact that you didn’t lose Jay once, but twice. The fact that even your own older brother doesn’t want much to do with you." "My failure? Listen, I run a very successful company." "Made on Jay’s blood, and sweat, and money." "He chose to give it up, I didn’t force him to." "No, you forced him. You gave him the choice to either give up Damage Inc or to give up wrestling. Even when he retired, Jay couldn’t find himself staying away from the ring." "What do you expect me to do? I didn’t want to see my company go down in flames because my husband at the time would rather have gone around the world wrestling instead of trying to build the business." Before things could get anymore heated, Jay had walked up to the table with a set of keys in his hand. "Now, either the two of you are going to have to get along, or Kelly, you’re going to have to walk to the church. I got the last rental." ----------------------------------- They arrived at the cemetery shortly after the service ended. Jay, his younger brother Dorian, Mike Durant and Jason Callion’s three sons all served as pall bearers, bringing the casket to where it will be laid to rest. Jason’s oldest son, Jayce walked up to Jay afterwards. "Jay, I’m glad you’re here. After what happened between you and father, I was afraid that you weren’t going to come." "I almost didn’t." "I’m glad you did anyway. I think father would have wanted you to have this." With that, Jayce handed Jay a velvet bag. Inside of it was a brittle, old championship belt. "It was his first championship." Jay said as he pulled the belt out of the bag. Jay remembered seeing this in a display case with the rest of Jason Callion’s various title belts. The gold and silver of the plates had lost their luster. "Thanks." The belt reminded Jay what he strived for as a wrestler. That the pain of Jason Callion's passing would eventually go away. He was honored that the Callion family would give the belt to him. It was time to honor their father's memory.
