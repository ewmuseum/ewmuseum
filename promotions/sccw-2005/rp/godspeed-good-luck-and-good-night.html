Invisible Death, Impossible Life. On November 13th, 2056, Sean Sterling died. Though conspiracy theorists and diehard fans may say otherwise, he committed suicide. Above all things, that is fact. A self inflicted gunshot straight from the barrel of a 12-gauge shotgun, rendering Sean Sterling’s facial features indefinable. He left behind a suicide note and it read like this: 76. 26 years past 50. 26 years that I didn’t want or need. Call it an act of cowardice, call it an act of God, call it what you want to call it. I don’t care. Everything was fine and nothing hurt. Good night. It was covered by every member of the press, from mainstream anchors to college kids with a wrestling news website. It was called "The Death of a Legend". T-shirts were made. Bumper stickers were sold. Everyone got filthy rich and lived happily ever after. Sean Sterling was cremated after a traditional funeral and his ashes were released at sea by his son and only child, Victor Sterling. Alongside Victor stood Sean;s second wife, Gwen Sterling. Nobody cried and nobody really mourned so much as they sat solemnly, in silence, wrapped up in their own memories of the man who, once upon a time, called himself The Third Revelation. To some, he was remembered as a monster, marauding and masquerading as a good man. To others, he was remembered as kind and caring, albeit this came with a sailor’s tongue and the ever present possibility of a stab in the back. It really depends on who you ask and how they knew him. On November 13th, 2056, Sean Sterling died. And the world just kept on spinning without him. --- 2. Interview One: Gwen Sterling. The camera pans in on your typical interview room. Fake plastic plants. Dull colors. A picture of Sean Sterling, in his youth, hanging on the wall behind interviewer Dave Hailey. Across from Dave Hailey sits an elderly woman, Sean Sterling’s second wife, Gwen, clutching tissues and occasionally wiping her eyes. Dave Hailey: Good evening ladies and gentlemen, this is Dave Hailey sitting down with the late, great Sean Sterling’s wife, Gwen. Thank you for your time, Gwen. I know that this isn’t exactly the best time. Are you doing okay? Gwen Sterling: I’m fine, Dave, thank you… Dave Hailey: Now, Gwen, most people would say that you knew Mr. Sterling better than anyone else. Towards the end of his life, you were often given credit for keeping him going through the years. Would you care to share some of your thoughts on this horrible tragedy? Gwen Sterling: I’m… I’m honestly still in shock over the whole thing. I mean, honestly speaking, Sean never was the happiest man in the world… even I couldn’t make him that… but all the same, you never see something like this coming, even if the signs are all there. I miss him, wherever he is. I miss him. Dave Hailey: Over the years, Mr. Sterling was given several titles. An innovator, a living legend, a man who would do anything for his fans… however, it was always unanimously said that he was never exactly the easiest person to get along with. Gwen Sterling: Sean was… different. He went about things differently than most people. Some might say that he had a thought process that went beyond what you would expect of just some wrestler. Sean was a difficult man, above all things, and we had our trying times, that’s a given… but he made it easy to love him. He made it easy to… to just want to be around him, always. He certain aura about him that just… it was unexplainable. Dave Hailey: For those watching who might not know, would you care to explain how you and Sean met? Gwen Sterling: It was in 2015, I believe… Sean was still wrestling for SCCW. His first marriage had just fallen through and he wasn’t exactly at his best. He was drinking, constantly. I was working for SCCW, off screen of course, and we met at an employee Christmas party. He was completely different than everyone else there. In fact, the fact that he was even there to begin with seemed absolutely ludicrous. He was sitting by himself, spiking his punch when no one was looking, so I went over and sat down with him, started talking to him… I remember the first thing he said to me was, "Do you want to get the hell out of here?" So, we left the party and… well, the rest is history. Dave Hailey: Certain people, fans and theorists, have put together the notion that Mr. Sterling didn’t really commit suicide, whether they say it’s an accident or a conspiracy. What do you have to say about these claims? Gwen Sterling: (nervous laugh) Sean killed himself, I can guarantee you that much. There’s no way around it, Sean killed himself. Dave Hailey: We’ve all seen the note he left behind, but we were hoping maybe you could shed some light on exactly why he committed suicide. Gwen Sterling: Why? Huh… you know, all things considered, with all of this happening at once, I haven’t even stopped to ask why. I just know he did, and that’s that. The camera pans in on Gwen’s face as tears begin to stream, making rivers of mascara down her cheeks. Gwen: All I know is that I miss him. I miss him more than I can put to words… --- 3. Gwen’s Song I was out getting groceries when the telephone rang. It was Sean. He said, "Gwen, no matter what, remember that I love you." That’s all he said before he hung up. He didn’t even give me a chance to reply. I’m not going to say that those were his last words, but I know that he meant it. I know that much for sure. I thought he had been drinking, honestly, which he hadn’t done in years, so it’s no small wonder that I was more than a little upset. I rolled my eyes and finished shopping without giving it a second thought. It was Sean being Sean, nothing more and nothing less. We would have an argument when I got home and he’d end up sleeping on the couch. That’s how much thought I put into it. I saw the flashing lights as soon as I drove into our neighborhood, red and blue, turning the whole street into a spectacle. Neighbors stood across the street, watching and spreading rumors, asking each other what was going on. The yard was crowded with people, cops and EMTs. The only person I recognized was my son, Victor, standing in the driveway with his head hung low. He never smoked in front of me, but he was smoking like a freight train that night and every night since. I remember that first spark of horror when I saw the stretcher coming out of the front door. I jumped out of the car and ran to Victor, screaming hysterically, asking him what happened, asking him if Sean was okay. All he did was wrap his arms around me and pull me close, whispering, "Don’t worry, mom. It’ll be okay." And that’s when I started crying. --- 4. The Pain of Being a Man. An excerpt from Sean Sterling’s autobiography, ‘The Pain of Being a Man". Chapter 3: The Dark Horse I never really considered myself a wrestler, so much as an actor. It wasn’t some pretentious thing and it wasn’t me attempting to be clever or anything, it was just the way I’ve always thought of it. Honestly, I’ve never enjoyed wrestling. I never watched it, growing up, and I couldn’t tell you the difference between Ric Flair and Hulk Hogan. It was just something I fell into, by some strange hand of fate, and I went with it. It wasn’t about honor or some archaic family tradition, it was just about the money I could make. I’m not going to say I’m a greedy man, but I won’t say I ever felt like donating to a charity either. But I digress, wrestling wasn’t what I did. I fought and I acted like I cared. I went from company to company, ring to ring, and all I left behind was a history of violence, a bad attitude, and a terrible reputation as a grade A asshole. Honestly, though, I don’t regret any of it. --- 5. Interview Two: Jonathan Rhine. Once again, the camera pans in on the Dave Hailey, sitting in the interview room with a one of those big, fake smiles on his face. He looks over to the side, asking someone, "Am I on?", and then nods, clearing his throat. Dave Hailey: Welcome back to our tribute to one of wrestling’s greatest legends, Sean Sterling. I’m sitting down with a man who knew Sean, perhaps, better than anyone else in his SCCW years, another great, Jonathan Rhine. Good evening, Jon, thank you for giving us your time. The camera pans over to Jonathan Rhine, his hair gone gray, but his face still as youthful as it was years earlier. With a patch over his eye and a melancholy smile on his face, he nods slowly with a deep breath. Jonathan Rhine: Thanks, Dave. It’s good to be here. Dave Hailey: Now, Jon, Sean considered you one of his only real friends in the business, and is quoted several times as saying that you knew him better than anyone else in the locker rooms. You two had a short history in SCCW, both being members of the Highwaymen, but in that short time, you seemed to have one of the largest impacts on Sean when it came to wrestling. Jonathan Rhine: Well, Dave, Sean was a strange man, above all things. I loved him like a brother, even though we both had our disagreements and slight arguments over the years. When he first came to SCCW, I passed him off as nothing but some asshole trying to act big and bad for the sake of being shocking, but when he stepped into the ring, he blew me away. The man had genuine talent, which isn’t something you can say about most the kids they throw into the ring these days. Dave Hailey: Did you attend his funeral yesterday morning? Jonathan Rhine: Of course. It was at the ass crack of dawn, which I think was Sean just getting his last asshole laugh, dragging us all out of our beds that early. Honestly, it was one of the happiest funerals I’ve ever seen. I mean, don’t get me wrong, tears were welling up, but everyone seemed happy. Everyone laughed and smiled, talked about Sean. I’d seen faces I hadn’t seen years. They played "Mr. Tambourine Man", and I swear to God, I could almost hear Sean singing along. Dave Hailey: You’re reported as being one of the last people to see Sean alive before his untimely death last week. Do you care to recount? Jonathan Rhine: Well, Sean moved out to Aspen after he retired and I was passing through, so I decided to stop by and say hi. We hadn’t seen each other in a year or two, so he told Gwen he’d be back later and he met me at one of the local bars. Hopefully Gwen won’t see this interview and have my head, but we had a few beers and talked about old times, caught up on the news, discussed politics… you know, the usual. And then he said something I’d never him say before, and I’m still not sure what he meant by it. He told me, "Jon, we’ve shared our thoughts a thousand times with each other, but have you ever noticed the kinds of thoughts I’ve got?" I asked him what he meant, but he didn’t bother explaining himself. He just smiled and nodded, took another sip, and told me, "Don’t worry about it, Jon. Don’t worry about it." Dave Hailey: What do you think the world will be like without Sean Sterling in it? Jonathan Rhine: Sean leveled things out. He was that pessimistic darkness that hovered over all our good hopes, and the world will be a lot less interesting without him. He was too weird to live and too rare to die, God bless his soul. He’s either in Heaven laughing at the rest of us or making the Devil cry down in hell. Wherever he is, all I know is that he’s going to be missed. --- 5. Jonathan’s Song. He met me at O’Reily’s Pub on the corner of Highland and Lane, the night before he shot himself. I remember him telling me not to tell his wife where we were going, otherwise he’d never hear the end of it. He seemed happier than he used to be, which is saying something for Sean. He never really seemed like a happy man. We drank and talked until one. Two old men talking about nothing in particular with nothing but a pitcher of beer and a half a century behind us. He told me he expected the world to end years ago, but that was the only thing he mentioned that was even remotely connected to death. I can’t even begin to imagine what was really going on in his head. It was snowing when we left the bar, just as the night clubs were closing and party girls and bar hoppers were hitting the streets. We had to stop along the way to sign autographs every few steps. Every time a fan would walk away, Sean would sneer and make some snide comment about them. That’s what I always liked about him, he never had a good thing to say about anybody. When I heard the news the next day, I couldn’t believe what I was hearing. Honestly, I still can’t believe it. Sean never seemed like the type of person who would want to kill himself, or even consider killing himself. I guess it’s true what they say. There’s no country for old men… --- 6. Rumors of War The light in the arena fade, a single spotlight shining down into the center of the ring, where Ring Announcer Michael Clarke stands, microphone in hand. Michael Clarke: Ladies and gentlemen… in tonight’s main event… coming in at 225 pounds and hailing from Dallas, Texas…"The Fourth Revelation" Clint Virgil! "Mote" by Sonic Youth hits as Clint Virgil steps out of the back, met by nothing but a chorus of boos as he pauses to pose for a photo op at the top of the ramp. With a self righteous sneer on his face, an egotistical aura all about him, he marches down the ramp and towards the ring, sliding beneath the ropes and popping up just as fireworks shoot out of the turnbuckles. The strobe lights and music fades back down as Michael Clarke clears his throat. Michael Clarke: Ladies and gentlemen… introducing the SCW Ultimate Champion… coming in at 210 pound and hailing from Manchester, Tennessee… "The Living Legend" Sean Sterling! Cue " Goin’ Out West" by Tom Waits, pyros almost immediately exploding all the way down the ramp and ending at the ring, where fireworks burst from all the turnbuckles just as the song starts to pick up. Sean Sterling steps out from the back, and the crowd goes absolutely crazy. Even with his hair graying, that trademark cocky grin remains, spread wide across ‘The Living Legend’s face. Slowly walking down the ramp, staring dead ahead at Clint Virgil, with The Ultimate Championship wrapped around his face, Sterling is nothing if not determined. He climbs up the ring steps and steps into the ring, almost calmly taking to his corner as the lights in the arena come back on. At the side of the ring, the announcers, Gerald Moore and Alex "The Wolf" Cannon., sit at the edge of their seats. Gerald Moore: Alex, this is going to be one of the most historic matches in SCW history! This is the moment we’ve all been waiting for! Alex Cannon: You’re goddamn right, Gerry! If I had to choose between watch between two anorexic porn stars wrestle in Jell-o while letting me snort coke off their tits or this match, well, hell! I might just choose this! The match begins with the ring of the bell. The two men circle each other like vultures, staring right into each other’s eyes, before both go after each other with a classic grapple. The two fight in the middle of the ring, both trying to get a move off, but Virgil ends up overpowering Sterling, pushing him back against the ropes, and bouncing him off into a rough clothesline. Gerald Moore: And just like that, Virgil gains the early upper hand! Alex Cannon: That’s because Clint’s a legend killin’, throat slittin’, city slickin’, American phenomena! He said it himself, true story. Gerald Moore: I don’t even know what that means, let alone why it matters. Sterling falls to the mat with a ‘OOF!’ as Virgil takes the opportunity to make for a quick pin. ON-- Kickout! Sterling throws Virgil off of him, quickly managing to roll over on top of him and start punching. Gerald Moore: And Sterling takes the lead! Sterling’s fists collide with Virgil’s face, a sneer spread all across ‘The Living Legend’s lips as his knuckles go red, Virgil’s face contorting and blood already beginning to trickle down from his forehead. Sterling goes to his feet, lifting Virgil up by his hair. Virgil groggily stands beside Sterling, who continually punches him from time to time, leading him over to the turnbuckle. Virgil leans against the turnbuckle as Sterling goes to stand on the opposite side of the ring. Showing some slight resemblance to his younger years, Sterling goes running across the ring, leaping to perform a dropkick to Virgil’s face. However, at the last second, Virgil moves, forcing Sterling’s feet to impact against the top of the turnbuckle, sending him crashing back down onto the mat. Virgil crouches for a moment, catching his breath as he shakes his head, muttering something to the effect of, "Nice try, old man…" Virgil walks over to Sterling, lifting him to his feet and sliding his head beneath his arm, performing a quick DDT. Virgil goes for another pin, but Sterling once again quickly kicks out. Gerald Moore: Sterling won’t stay down. That’s why they call him The Living Legend. The man build a foundation on blood, sweat, and tears. Alex Cannon: And beating up kids. Gerald Moore: That was a long time ago, Alex. Alex Cannon: Time doesn’t heal all wounds, Gerald. Minute after excruciating minute passes as both men beat each other to the build, the fight moving outside of the ring. Sterling clutches at Virgil by a handful of his hair, slamming his younger opponent’s face into the turnbuckle time and time again. Sterling eventually lets go, sliding back into the ring as Virgil groggily follows. What Sterling doesn’t see, however, is what Virgil is concealing on his right hand… and neither does the ref. As the men meet in the middle for another sparring contest, Virgil tosses a quick right punch, and Sterling falls like a brick. A careful eye would see the brass knuckles, but to the referee and anyone not playing close attention, it just looks like Sterling has finally had enough. Virgil grins, quickly sliding back out of the ring and tossing the illegal weapon as the fans boo and begin to throw trash at him. He pulls Sterling out of the ring as well, slowing exposing the rough concrete beneath the padding. With a cocky grin that resounds the one Sterling is known for, he poses for a moment, before lifting Sterling into the air and piledriving him onto the exposed floor. Again… and again… and again. Gerald Moore: This is almost too painful to watch! Alex Cannon: You know what must be even more painful? Sterling’s head. OH! See what I did there? The match eventually leads itself back into the ring, where Virgil still maintains complete control. As the cameras flash and the lights twinkle, Clint Virgil pulls the final, career-ending act of disrespect. Gerald Moore: HAIL TO THE THIEF! Clint Virgil just used Sean Sterling’s own finishing move against him! ONE… TWO… THREE! Michael Clarke: Your winner, and NEW SCW Ultimate Champion… CLINT VIRGILLLLL! The fans anger can be heard throughout the entire arena, chanting vulgarities as Virgil stands in the center of the ring, grinning wide as Sterling lays in the ring for the last time, defeated and bloody on the mat. As Virgil grabs his title and starts to head for the exit, he stops to spit in Sterling’s face, before he disappears up the ramp and out of sight. As Virgil disappears, a new chant begins out in the audience. Crowd: STERLING! STERLING! STERLING! Sterling slowly pulls himself to his feet, clutching at the ropes for support, blood pouring from his forehead and all down his face as he looks out into the fans, tears beginning to stream down his face and a slow smile spreading across his lips. Gerald Moore: In that ring stands a man who will go down in history as one of history’s greatest wrestlers. Sean Sterling will not be forgotten! The SCW copyright appears at the bottom of the screen and the cameras begin to fade as Sterling lifts his hand into the air one last time, holding a defiant fist high. For the last time, the camera fades on Sean Sterling. --- 7. The Funeral. There he lies. God rest his soul, and his rudeness. A devouring public can now share the remains of his sickness, or his health. There he lay: poet, prophet, outlaw, fake, star of electricity. Nailed to a cross or stuffed in a coffin, burned alive or buried at sea. It didn’t matter and it doesn’t matter, all that matters is, there is lies, and dare he speak again. No preacher, because that would be silly. Just friends and family, enemies and fellow outlaws all gathered together to spread rumors, tell old wives tales, and build upon the legend of Sean Sterling, king of nothing and prince of no one. Jonathan Rhine. Mayson Colby. Clint Virgil. Everyone and anyone in the business, all gathered to pay their final respects. The coffin remains closed, but the very fact that his remains are even here drives a certain aura throughout the whole room, through the air that we breathe, in the ground that we walk. He’s here, and he’s with us, and we know it. The world will undoubtedly spin on and, eventually, Gwen Sterling’s tears will dry up, but for now, she’s the only person in the whole place crying, and understandably so. Tomorrow, when his ashes are spread at see and he’s finally given bad to the blue that birthed us all, he’ll live forever in our thoughts, in our minds, and in history. There he lies, God rest his soul. There he lies. --- 8. Interview Four: Sean Sterling. The following is a clip taken a month prior to Sean Sterling’s death. It is his last known interview. Another typical interview room. Bland colors, some interviewer with a bad haircut, abstract paintings hanging on the wall painted by no-name artists. The interviewer was named Jeffrey Wise, and although he didn’t know it, he was the last reporter to sit down with Sean Sterling. Jeffrey Wise: Sean, thanks for taking the time to sit down with us today. How are things? Sean Sterling: Fine, dandy, peachy keen. All the usual bullshit. Between puffs of a cigar, Sterling struck a typical grin, cocking a quizzical eyebrow as the interviewer fumbled with his note cards. Sterling wouldn’t even have been there, had it not been for the price they were willing to pay. Jeffrey Wise: It’s been twenty years now since you last stepped foot in the ring. Rumors are spreading like wildfire lately that you’re planning on making a return. Any truth? Sean Sterling: Damn, has it really been twenty years? Fucking hell, I need to buy a goddamn calendar, don’t I? Heh… listen, kid, I’m 76 years old. My body has went all flabby and my bones barely manage to drag the rest of me around. There’s no chance in hell of me returning to wrestling. Jeffrey Wise: What about as a manager or announc-- Sean Sterling: I repeat, no chance in hell. Next question. Jeffrey Wise: Um… well, Sean, where are you now? Sean Sterling: Living the good life, where else would I be? Me and Gwen moved to Aspen a few years ago. Got a real nice cabin up in the woods, real nice… Basically, I’ve been enjoying the fruits of my labor. Old age comes quick, kid, trust me. Comes out of the fucking blue and bites you on the ass. If these are really my final years, then I’m damn well going to enjoy them. Jeffrey Wise: And deservedly so, might I add. Sean Sterling: Yeah, sure. Flattery gets you everywhere. Jeffrey Wise: So, what’s next for Sean Sterling? Sean Sterling: Well, I’ll probably live the rest of my life in isolation and eventually die to go meet my maker, whoever that is. I’ll try my best to come back and tell everyone how he is. Look, kid, let’s both be honest with each other here. There’s not much left for me to do. I’ve done it all. So, what’s next? Absolutely nothing. This is the part where I sit back, relax, and watch the world burn. Jeffrey Wise: Fair enough. So, when do you expect to world to start burning? Sean Sterling: You mean it isn’t already? Sterling grins, offering a wink as the camera fades to a commercial… --- 9. Sterling’s Song. Give me the end of it all, let it all fall to shit. Bid sweet fuck all to this world and see what comes next. Kiss me with apocalypse. I sat at the kitchen table, lukewarm coffee by my side and a shot of vodka down my throat. It was snowing, like always; little flakes flickering down outside the window, there and gone, buried beneath themselves. There’s something deep in there about the fall of mankind, but who really gives a shit? Relax, old man. This won’t hurt. Gwen would cry, I guarantee it. Victor would be the first to find me and the first to call the cops. I’d get blood all over the tiling, so Gwen will probably change the linoleum like she’s wanted to for years. If Hell is a metropolis and Heaven is a tourist trap, I’m going to be severely disappointed. The things that go through your mind when you have your eyes completely closed will really make you size yourself up. You’ll begin to wonder where all those thoughts came from, and you’re never really able to figure it out. Then, when you realize that you’re biting down on the barrel of a shotgun, you start to really wonder about yourself. Then, well, all you can really do is laugh. This wasn’t some sort of half-thought notion, y;know, killing myself. I had been considering it for years, I could just never figure out the right way to go about it. I never brought it up and I kept it completely to myself, but the thought was there for as long as I can remember. My finger quivered the gun, my breath gone heavy, and that vodka hitting the pit of my stomach, making me warm all over. I sat on the edge of my seat, almost anticipating what was about to happen, even if my nerves were about to break. I pressed the trigger in slow. I closed my eyes as tight as I could. For a second, I almost thought I could feel that oh-so-cliché single tear rolling out the corner of my eye, down to the cheek, and down to the floor. And then, it happened. Bang. And the world just kept on spinning.
