Episode 2: The Pearl of the Pacific

Date: Today "Where is he? Answer the phone." Edie Raymond-Ellis was not usually an impatient person. But for some reason, today she is wondering where the person that she is trying to get on the phone was at. The sun is now beating down on the pool of the hotel where she, her sister and Krissy Jamison have decided to stay during their few days down in Mexico. "Does the señorita want another drink?" the attendant asked. "Sure." Now, you can see frustration showing on Edie’s face. The look that is in her eyes is that of heartache. Maybe she should have heeded warnings that other people gave her. Don’t get too close to wrestlers; they will only break your heart. Maybe she thought that he was different. After all, she thinks most things through. Then again, she reminds herself, he was married three times. She doesn’t want to think about that. Maybe he’s in Arizona spending some time with his daughter and former stepson. That thought brings about a smile, until she remembers that he told her that they were going to be in Hawaii this week visiting with their grandparents. The attendant brings her another Rum and Coke. Edie looks down into the brown liquid, hoping that an answer would come to her. A tear was about to stream down her face when her cell phone begins to ring. She is quick to answer it, hoping that it he is calling. "Hello." She didn’t look at the Caller-ID. Otherwise, she would have known that he wasn’t on the other end of the line. She hangs up the phone, picks up her drink and walks over to the railing and looks out towards the Pacific. It was beautiful. She knew it. She just wanted him to be here with her. Little does she know that while she calls him, that in reality he isn’t too far away. ------------------------------------ The streets of Mazatlan that were tucked away from where the tourists usually stuck to were crowded. Markets, kids playing soccer, tourists that turned a left when they should have turned a right. One shop though on this street was different from the others. Raúl Montoya was a mask maker in the city also called the Pacific Pearl. For years, he made masks for the Día de los Muertos celebrations. But he also specialized in another art. The mask of the famed Luchadors of Mexico. Very protective of the art was Raúl, and very protective of tradition as well. Any luchador that received one of Montoya’s masks were sworn that if they ever lost the mask in a match, that all masks that he ever made for the luchador would be returned to him. He almost never made a mask for an American. He thought that Gringos didn’t deserve the honor, didn’t understand the tradition that goes behind the mask. The man that entered his shop was different. He proved that he understood the tradition, that he earned the honor. At the time, the man was known simply as el Diablo Blanco. But after proving his worth, he would become el Diablo Mágico. At first, he was nothing more than a Gringo, but after a while, Jason Ryan would prove that he was more to the master mask maker. "Gringo!" the Spaniard greets the man that now known as Jay Draven as he enters the shop. "Maestro!" Draven responds as he picks the rather diminutive man up in a hug that would bring anyone else under homo-suspicion. "I see you are looking well, young man." Raúl always had a way of talking to Draven that would both show him respect and humble him at the same time. Not many people alive could do that. "Me? Look at you, maestro. You look younger than the last time I saw you." "I gave up Red Apple Cigarettes." "Really, and I thought they only existed in cheesy movies." A laugh comes from both Draven and Montoya, as the old mask maker pulls a pack of cigarettes from under his counter and puts one between his lips. "You really should give up that habit." Raúl Montoya had smoked as long as Draven could remember. A pack a day, unfiltered, no menthol. "I will smoke even as they bury me." "Will be sooner rather than later if you keep smoking those things." "I seem to remember a time when you would smoke with me." "Maestro, what I was smoking was healthier, and I wasn’t able to get them in the US legally anyway." "There are plenty of things you can’t have in America legally. Look outside, there are a ton of people that would kill to live in America legally with their families." "Yeah, they want to get away from the Donkey Shows." "You have them in the United States also." "Yeah, but we have to have them in the privacy of our own homes or at a fast food restaurant." "I guess." "So, work on any new masks lately." "Yes. In fact, there is a wrestler that wants to become El Hijo del Diablo Mágico." "Gringo?" "Sí." "He any good?" "Sí." "What did you tell him?" "That he could be Diablo Mistico. I made him a new mask." "At least you are keeping me unknown." "You never lost your mask in the ring. If you ever wanted to return as el Diablo Mágico, I will have a new mask for you." "Gracias maestro." "What brings you to Mazatlan?" "Had some time off, wanted to come down and say hi to the man that got my career off the ground." "It is good to see you." "So, other than making masks, how have things been going for you here?" "Bueno." "That’s good to hear." "Getting older makes things tougher, but I’m getting used to it. My hands aren’t like they used to be. I now use a machine when putting the panels for the mask together." "Just like they did back in the 1890s, huh?" "You really are a wise guy, aren’t you? It’s just getting harder for me. I have the arthritis in my hands." "I remember you once told me that your hands were your life." "They still are. I can use them still, but it’s just harder to make the same quality masks that I used to make, especially with these newer synthetic materials all the Luchadors want to use." "Well, the times are changing, Raul. The times are changing. I remember when I was here; no one was doing moves like they are doing now. It’s hard to keep up with the newer wrestlers coming around." "There is this one kid, Jason, the things that he does in that ring; they would make your head spin. His front roll splash looks as effortless as a Mil Mascaras cross chop." "This I’d have to see." "Kid is wrestling at the Arena Mexico tomorrow night. You could check it out while you’re here, if you have the time." "I might have to Maestro." The water that was crashing against the shore is clear. This is why Mazatlan is an amazing destination for those wishing to vacation in Mexico. Edie looks right into the ocean. Her heart sinks with every wave that crashes ashore. He told her that he was interested in being friends. Maybe she could convince herself that was all that they are. But she doesn’t want to believe that. She feels that they made a connection. She feels one way for him, but she doesn’t know if he feels the same way. ------------------------------------ Draven looks from his hotel room over the Pacific Ocean. Behind him, Lucha Libre is being aired on television. The kid that Raul told him to look at was just wrestling. Draven was impressed by the kid’s performance. Maybe, when the kid got older, he could make the trip to the states and be a major star. The bottle of water that he was drinking is now nearly empty. The waters of the Pacific seemed peaceful to him. The phone to his room starts to ring. Draven walks over and answers. "Hello." It was Dorian, responding to the call that Draven had placed a week prior. The brothers were always close, even though with their ages, it was hard to imagine that they really were brothers. The first time Dorian ever saw Jay wrestle, Dorian was a young man. They both have a picture that they took with their older sister from the Civic Center. Draven listens intently to what Dorian is telling him as he rubs the chain that is around his neck. The medallion that is attached was a gift from Samantha. It read ‘Ryan Family, Today and Forever'. "Yes, Dorian, I cleared it with the front office. I also found out that Caldera Enterprises has cleared it as well since it doesn’t interfere with their television schedule either. Just remember what I said though, you are only coming to be protection when I can’t be there, you aren’t being brought in to wrestle for both companies. Especially with the reputation that Wyatt has built for himself here." The medallion was an important piece of who Draven was. Outside of the ring, that medallion bore a significant meaning. Only three of them existed. One was buried in North Carolina, one was in the front jacket pocket in a locker in Minnesota. Of all his possessions, this medallion was the most valuable. His sister gave it to him a week before she died in that car accident. It was the last time he ever saw her. Draven knew that the only way he would relinquish that medallion was so that it goes to someone important. At one time, he thought of giving it to Michael Shaw. The divorce brought an end to that. Maybe his daughter Samantha will get it. He didn’t know. He misses them. He wishes that they were here with him right now. Michael may not have been his biological son, but he held a bond with the young man that was hard to break. Samantha was his only child, the one that would carry on his name and his legacy. She is the only member of that generation of the Ryan family. She never met her cousin. Draven’s sister was unable to have children. He finds that he is now looking at the picture that he keeps in his wallet. He doesn’t even remember ending his call with Dorian and hanging up the phone. He sits there, looking at the picture, and a tear comes to his eye. Draven lived so long without the burden of kids that when he finally became a father, he thought that would have been enough to keep him out of the wrestling business. But he found himself coming back in order to provide for his children. Serena wasn’t trying to keep Samantha from him, though she is bitter with the fact that Draven returned to the wrestling business. She already found another man to fill the void that was created when she decided to file for divorce. ‘He has a stable job’ Draven thinks to himself, better to provide for the kids. Michael was 18, so really he only needed to make sure that Samantha was taken care of. Draven pulls his wallet back out and puts the picture back in. Another tear starts to stream down Draven’s cheek. The pain is nearly unbearable. He pulls out his cell phone and notices that it’s off. He turns it on so he can book a flight out of Mexico and back to the States. ------------------------------------ Edie is back by the pool. Her floral sarong was flowing in the breeze as she gets in a quick swim. She finally has convinced herself that Jason didn’t care for her the way that she cares for herself. The poolside attendant walks past the pool with another Rum and Coke and places it at the table next to the chair where her stuff was. ‘Edie, you need to remember that he’s just a man.’ she thinks as she starts to finally relax. For as much as she convinces herself of the contrary, there is still that part of her that finds that she wants more with Jason. That the connection she thought she had with him is real. She hears her cell phone ring, so she starts to swim back to that end of the pool. The chlorine that was in the pool has her eyes stinging as she pulls herself out of the water. She goes to answer the phone, but it stops ringing before she can reach it. "Fuck." Edie says under her breath as she reaches for her towel. Before her hand gets to the chair where the towel was, she feels it wrap around her body. She turns around and before she can react he brushes back some of the hair from her face. "You rang?"
