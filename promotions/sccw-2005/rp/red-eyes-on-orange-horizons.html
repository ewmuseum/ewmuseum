Red Eyes on Orange Horizons

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Present Jared was a little surprised when he turned the corner by the brick building that served as Darren Stracker’s wrestling school. The last few times he’d stopped by he’d run into the proprietor lingering on the sidewalk, taking a break from the day’s activities. He felt it even more unusual to not see him today, given that New England had finally come through what felt like a month of steady rain, all the more reason to expect running into his mentor outside. By the time he made it to the door Jared had already pulled his Blackberry from his pocket, casually flicking the small scroll wheel in the center of the device. For the past few days he’d been expecting to hear from his agent, Larry Spiderman, regarding one last press push before SIN on SPIKE 3 and an encounter with Wyatt Connors, but so far none of the details had been passed along to him. The device beeped once, indicating a new text message, but his hopes were quickly dashed when he saw that it was instead from Mervin Humperdink, his closest friend. You having fun yet? It wasn’t Mervin’s normal geek-friendly fare, but Jared thought little of it. His attention was soon drawn to Darren, who had descended from his second-floor office to meet him in the hall. "Didn’t expect to see you here today," Darren said, smiling as he gave Jared a firm pat on the back. "You’re not normally a ‘Thursday’ guy." "Yeah, I know," Jared answered, putting the device back in his pocket, "But Merv’ told me he talked to you a few weeks ago while I was in New York, said that schedules had changed and Thursdays were safe." Still smiling, Darren nodded. "What’s with the grin? The wife throw you a little something last night?" the Black Sheep probed. "Not used to seeing you so, I dunno, happy." "What’s wrong with happy?" "On most people? Nothing. On you? It’s kind of fucking terrifying." At the end of the hall the two men stopped, and Darren draped his right arm around Jared’s shoulder. With his left arm he pointed into the center of the training complex, where an athletic brunette was busy showing a few newer students how not to fall. Jared’s heart dropped. "Aw fuck," he groaned. "You set me up." "It’s been two months kid. I think you’re due." "I didn’t want to do this like… this," Jared pleaded, his nerves suddenly on edge. "I have to be honest, I don’t quite think I’m ready for it. Not yet." "I’ve known you since you were nineteen years old, kiddo." Much larger than his protégé, it didn’t take much for Darren to gently shove Jared closer to the ring, even if he did have his heels dug in. "I know that sometimes you need a little push in the right direction to get you going. Also, there’s one other thing." "What’s that?" he asked, his attention still on his mentor. "She’s staring at you, so it’s not really like you can back out of this now." Jared felt his heart rate increase, and though the color was draining from his face he felt suddenly very warm, as though he could vomit up his central nervous system at any moment. Slowly he turned his attention to the ring, where all action had ground to a halt. Through a few wisps of hair, a pair of bright green eyes had him locked in their gaze. Justine Calvin wore a playfully defiant smirk as she looked down at her former friend. "Wow, you must really fucking suck," she said, stepping towards the edge of the ring and leaning against the nearest buckle. She pulled a few loose strands of hair back, tucking it behind her right ear. "I mean, why else would you be here?" He opened his mouth, but no words came. "Really? You don’t say," she continued, earning a few chuckles from the small collection of students gathered nearby. "Uh, hi," he said at last, finally brave enough to take a few steps forward. "That’s it? That’s all you’ve got? Ten years, and the best you can do is ‘hi’? I’m not gonna lie, I’m a little disappointed, Jared." Her sigh was exaggerated, done for the purpose of being overly theatrical. She placed her elbow along the top rope, resting her chin on her knuckles as she waited for his response. Every time he spoke she responded quickly, spurring him on. "Well…" "Yes?" "It’s like…" "I’m listening." He stepped to the side of the ring, and she crouched down to meet him. "Can we, you know, maybe just go somewhere and talk?" "No," she said, straightening back up. "That’s not going to work. See, you and I have a bit of a problem, and while I think we should probably sort though it, I’ve got a better idea." "You do?" "I do. I think you should climb on up in here, and we’ll play it by ear." "I think I’d rather not," he said, backing away. "That’s not something I’m interested in, like, at all." "Maybe not, but it’s something that I’m interested in." "You realize it’d be kind of one-sided, right? I wouldn’t fight back, or anything." "And you think this bothers me because…?" "You really want to do this?" he asked, stepping closer to the base of the makeshift wooden stairs along the near edge of the ring. "I do," she replied, folding her arms and leaning over the corner buckle. "I really do." "No talking you out of it?" "Not at all." He took a step forward, placing his left foot on the first wobbly stair and extending a hand to the ring post for balance. "You’re sure about this? Like, seriously?" "Just get your ass in here," she shot back, pushing away from the buckle and backing towards the ring’s center. With a heavy sigh he ascended the last few steps, using the middle rope to pull himself onto the ring’s outer perimeter. "Fine," he said, leaning over the ropes. "We’ll have it your way. I just… I really don’t want to do this, Cal’." "Sorry, not an option." She bounced lightly on the balls of her feet, all too eager to fight. "Any last requests?" "Just one. If we’re going one-on-one, then I should be ‘shirts’, and you can be ‘skins’." It was meant as a joke, a means to lighten the tension that had quickly grown between them in the last few minutes, but considering the expression she now wore on her face it was the remark was better suited for a list of "All-Time Backfires". He smiled, albeit weakly. She didn’t. Hell hath no fury like a something something. It would have served him well to remember the rest of the old adage. Whispers and hushed laughter spread through the gathered students like wildfire. They knew what this meant for Jared. After all, Justine was the one responsible for most of the bruises they went home with each day. "No?" he continued, desperately searching for a way out of the hole he’d already half-buried himself in. "Nothing for that one?" Her gaze tightened as she flexed her fingers, balling them into fists and pulling them in close to her. "Oh, there’ll definitely be something for that." "Oh, fuck," is what he thought, but he was smart enough to keep this particular thought to himself. Perhaps it was time for a different approach. He was in the ring now, standing in the corner with a hand on adjacent ropes. "This isn’t going to fix anything. You know that." Slowly, reluctantly, he stepped closer to her, still clinging to the hope that it might still be possible to reason with her. "We can go talk about this like civilized ad…" He was cut short by the right hand careening towards his jaw. He ducked to his left, and that’s when the first shot landed. The sound rising up from the ten or so students who’d gathered was a blend of laughter and sympathetic groans. Her feint was flawless. She knew him well enough to know that he wouldn’t try to fight back, that he would be an easy target. When he dodged to his left she had brought her knee up, forcing it sharply into his ribs. The strike was hard enough to send him to the mat, where he propped himself up on his hands and knees. "Dated a mixed martial artist for a bit," she taunted, stepping away to let him back up. "Learned a few things." "Lucky him," he wheezed, his body trying to suck back the air which had been driven out of him only seconds before. And as his body fought to recover its air, his mind still struggled to wrap itself around the situation. He wouldn’t fight back, his moral code made sure of that. But, he conceded, if her heart were set on throwing punches then he’d take (or dodge, preferably) as many as he could weather. He pushed himself back to his feet, ready for the coming onslaught. Instinct settled in, and as the second shot came in, this time in the form of a left hook, he brushed it aside, quickly sidestepping behind her. Jared hit the floor again as Justine regained her balance, compensating with a kick to the same spot she’d kneed just a moment before. "Ah, fuck," he grunted silently through clenched teeth, pressing his forehead down into the battle-worn canvas. The shot didn’t have the same breath-stealing effect as the last one, but it stung like hell on an already sensitive area. "I remember you being faster," she stepped over to him, crouching low by his head, speaking just low enough so that only he could hear. "But after a decade away I guess it’s a miracle I remember that much, isn’t it?" He closed his eyes tight, his forehead still on the ground. "You’re right," he conceded, rocking back and sitting on his heels, the two now eye-to-eye. "I’m sorry, Cal’. I really am. You want to hate me for it? I won’t hold it against you." It wasn’t what she expected to hear. Her expression softened. Her brow furrowed. She put one knee down on the mat and leaned back, dropping her guard. When her right hand connected with the bridge of his nose the shock of the blow sent him sprawling against the nearby ropes. Once more a mixed chorus of laughter and sympathy went up from the nearby students. Even Darren Stracker, watching this all from behind the glass window of his second floor office, couldn’t help but laugh. "Ah, that wasn’t fun," Jared said at last, sitting back on the mat and leaning against the lower ring ropes. He raised his left hand to his face, and when he pulled back to examine it found a pool of blood in his palm. "Alright, okay," he turned his hand out to show her, his voice tinted both with sadness and defeat. "You win, alright? I fucking give." For a moment their eyes met, and he shook his head slightly. It wasn’t long before he was on his side, rolling under the rope he’d been leaning against and quickly hopping down to the floor. Head down and more than a little embarrassed, he stepped through a gap in the gathered students on his way to the men’s locker room in the far corner of the building. Justine stayed crouched for a moment, watching him as he walked away. Finally she rose to her feet, rested her hands on her hips, and paced a slow circle. "Wow, boss," Vinnie Something-or-other beamed (most people could never remember his last name). He was loud and a little obnoxious by nature, your typical East Boston type. "You really jacked him up good." "Shut up, Vinnie." Her pacing stopped, and a quick glance up at Darren Stracker in his office was met with a grin and a shrug. "Ah, fuck," she muttered, sliding out of the ring and heading towards the locker room. "Take ten, kids," she continued, not looking at any of them, "But anyone who follows me answers to the guy in the office." In this building that was threat enough to put the fear of God into most people. Jared was hunched over a sink with his head down, washing the blood away when he heard the door close. "Well, that was awesome," he said sarcastically, splashing a handful of cold water on his face, "Did you know she hits like a truck, or was letting me figure that out on my own part of the plan, too?" When he raised his eyes and looked into the mirror he’d expected to find Darren, but instead saw the reflection of a petite brunette in sweatpants and a ribbed tank top leaning against the lockers behind him. Once again a wave of embarrassment washed over him. "Jesus, I should just not talk ever." He pulled a series of coarse paper towels out of their dispenser, dried his face, and turned off the water. "I guess I kinda had this coming though, didn’t I?" "Sort of." She pushed away from the lockers, moving in a few steps. "But it’s possible I was a little harsh." "Only possible?" When he turned away from the sink to face her the first thing he noticed was that her hands were still wrapped, and the right one was tinted with blood. His blood. It occurred to him that she could be gearing up to hit him again, though at least this time there’d be no one else there to see it. He eased up a bit when he noticed that she was starting to remove the wrapping as she stepped in closer. "I owe you an explanation, and more apologies than I have the breath for," he was still very much embarrassed by the ordeal, and it showed in his voice. "Where should I start?" She didn’t answer. Instead she stepped over a nearby bench, and before he could recoil her arms were around his waist, embracing him. "Okay, didn’t see this coming." "Can you just shut up for a minute?" Jared was all too happy to oblige, returning her embrace. For a moment they stood in silence, the kind of friends’ reunion he’d hoped for but hadn’t expected. It was Justine who broke the silence. "I’m still a little pissed at you," she said, pulling away and taking a seat on the bench. "I mean, really, what the fuck, Jared?" He took a seat next to hear, straddling the bench. "Like, okay, I get the whole disappearing act all those years ago," she continued, turning her head to look at him. She pivoted slightly on the bench, tucking one leg under the other. "I mean, things were kind of taking off for you, and maybe a little weird between us, so I’m cool with that." Jared only nodded. He thought it better to let her get this off her chest than to start interrupting. Though he felt relatively safe, he was still very much aware of the fact that she’d beat him good only minutes ago. An event he was in no rush to see repeated. "What bothers me is that you knew I was here, and didn’t even stop in to say hello. Hell, Jared, you were coming on days that you knew I wouldn’t be here. I had to construct this elaborate scheme just to get you through the door." Though she tried to hide it, he could have sworn he saw her eyes starting to tear. "That’s the part that really hurts, you know." "Cal’," he started, catching himself before going any further. "Justine, for what it’s worth I’m sorry for all of it." When he spoke next his voice was soft, his tone sincere. "I’ve wanted to talk to you since I knew you were working here, I just… I froze up. I didn’t know what I’d say. I’m kind of awful when it comes to things like this." "I don’t get why, though. I mean, aren’t you the guy cracking jokes on television, in front of thousands of people? What the hell makes one person so scary?" "One person is terrifying. It gets even harder when that one person is someone you were close to, and would prefer if they didn’t hate you. A thousand people is fucking cakewalk, because no matter what I do or what retarded shit comes out of my mouth the chances are that at least one of them is going to like it. Odds are in my favor. The flipside is this, what happened today. I show up here and you punch the fuck out of me." She smiled. "Yeah, I feel sort of bad about that." "It’s fine. And for the record, I don’t think it was ever weird between us, even after… things." She started to respond, but at once their attention was drawn to the sound of the door. Peeking through was Vinnie, too curious for his own good, and whose last name still eluded both Jared and Justine. "Hey, sorry to bust in on ya’ like this," he started, brazenly stepping into the locker room, "but we were wonderin’ if you were gonna come back out, or if we were callin’ it a day." Jared turned his attention back to Justine, touching her arm to get her attention. "You want to let the kids have their space back, and you and I can get out of here? Maybe grab a coffee and catch up a little?" "Yeah, I like that idea." She glanced quickly at Vinnie, still lingering in the doorway, and then back to Jared. "By the way, it’s this guy who you can thank for blowing your cover. Started showing everyone some photo on his cell phone. That’s how I found out." The Black Sheep’s attention returned to the man at the door. For someone who spent the majority of his career as comic relief, it was not entirely common for Jared Sykes to break out the "game face". This is not to say that he didn’t have one, but for most people it was less than convincing. Fortunately Vinnie was not one of those people. "I, uh, just thought it would be cool, ya’ know," the suddenly flustered trainee stammered, hoping that he might earn a stay of execution through good, old-fashioned, brown-nosing. "Not every day I get my picture taken with a big star." When dealing with her students Justine normally adopted the demeanor of a drill sergeant, which is why she turned away and tried to quiet herself when she started laughing. "Okay, so we go get coffee," Jared reaffirmed. "But first we kill Vinnie." * * * The Starbucks was, in short, a Starbucks. Young, 20-something baristas made coffee drinks behind the counter. Hip, trendy music played softly through the in-store sound system. A middle-aged technophile navigated his netbook with one hand while flipping through tracks on his iPod with the other. The two young adults in worn athletic apparel didn’t look at all like they fit in. They’d been there well over an hour already. Jared was on his second green tea and lemonade concoction. Justine nursed a "skinny" vanilla latte. "What made you want to go out west?" Jared asked in between sips from his drink. "Family out there, or just one of those ‘do it while I can’ things?" "Just needed a change of scenery, I guess. Friend of mine had been harping on me since forever to go out to San Diego and be her roommate. Was fun for a little while, but I couldn’t get as many bookings as I’d hoped, and the guy I was seeing turned out to be kind of an asshole." She paused to take a sip of her drink, wiping some of the condensation on her leg after setting the cup back down. "Plus, my brother Dave is getting married in a few weeks, so it seemed like a good time to come home. I’m actually meeting up with him and his fiancée a little later for dinner and drinks." "Oh, so does that mean, like, you’re going back? After the wedding or whatever?" "Nah, this is home, you know? I can’t see myself going anywhere else, not for a while. Besides, The Boss tells me the gig is mine for as long as I want it, and it’s a lot more fun beating up the rookies that it ever was getting tossed around every week." She picked up her cup and stirred the ice with her straw. "What about you? Don’t tell me you’re still living in that same apartment you had when we met." "Nope." "Thank God." "I moved out over the winter. Ended up buying a house near where I grew up." She looked at him, her eyes wide. "Jesus, Jared, that’s pretty impressive. I never thought of you as the type to grow up or settle down someplace." "I didn’t," he smiled back. "I just ran out of places to put all my toys. You should see it. I’ve got a whole wall that’s just Han Solo." "Seriously?" "No, not at all." "Whew. For a minute there I thought we were going to have to stage an intervention." "You’d never make it past my army of nerds." She paused for a moment, a single eyebrow raised. "Are you still fucking with me?" she whispered, not quite sure whether to believe him. "I totally am." She drew her fingers along the sides of her plastic cup, gathering the water that had condensed and settled there. She giggled as she playfully flung the drops at him, and though it was only water he reflexively flinched. "Stop teasing," she joked, pointing a less-than-threatening finger at him. "It’s not fair. I can never tell when you’re being serious." "Sorry, I’m just so used to Merv’ rolling his eyes every time I try to get one over on him. Nice to finally have someone who goes along with it." "I’ll need to have a talk with that boy. Maybe learn his tricks." "And to be fair I do have a ton of stuff you’d probably laugh at, but I keep most of it in boxes these days. Really the only things I have on display are some small-scale replicas from those giant robot cartoons they show in Japan. Brought a whole bunch back with me a couple years ago." "Look at you, Mr. World-traveler. How long did you stay?" "A little less than a year," he said as he picked up his cup, now nearly empty. He gave it a quick shake, hoping to find more liquid hidden among the ice cubes. Defeated, he set it back down and decided he’d get a third soon. She leaned forward in her seat. "Oh, wow. That sounds kind of awesome. You’ve got to tell me about it." "No offense, but…" He squirmed in his chair. "It’s not really something I like talking about. Don’t get me wrong, it’s an amazing place, it’s just…" "Bad memories?" "You have no idea." "It’s okay." She slouched back in her seat. "I won’t press you." "I mean, it’s not like I’m keeping some big secret here, and you’ll probably find out eventually, it’s just one of those things I have to psyche myself up fo…" The ring of his cell phone cut his explanation short. Take me home tonight; I don’t wanna let you go ‘til you see the light… "Shit," he said, reaching into his pocket to retrieve the device. "I’m sorry about that. I forgot to mute the ringer before we got here." He quickly pressed the option to dismiss the call, instead letting it go to voicemail. "Don’t worry about it. I’m just trying to get over the fact that you have an Eddie Money ringtone." "Nah, only for this one number." "Girlfriend?" "Agent." "You have an Eddie Money ringtone for your agent?" At the moment she found it quite hard to stifle a laugh. "That’s… weird, like, even for you." "No way. I’m lazy, and it lets me screen out his calls, though lately I can’t really get away with that anymore. Not since he had to save my job twice." He pushed his chair away from the table, about to stand up. "Speaking of, I hate to do this but I need like five minutes to call him back or I’ll never hear the end of it. Do you mind?" "Not at all, unless you’re using this as an excuse to disappear on me again." Her remark stopped him cold. She was kidding. He knew she was kidding. It still hit like a gunshot, and hiding that fact was tricky. "Jared, I’m just screwing with you," she said at last, trying to ease his obvious disquiet. "You know, for the Han Solo crack." "Oh, yeah, right." He smiled half-heartedly as he stood up. Within seconds he had emptied his pockets of his wallet and keys, stacking them neatly on the table. "Call it collateral." He winked, sliding the stack of his personal effects towards her. "Seriously, I’ll be back as fast as I can." "Okay." He smiled again as he stepped past her, moving to the door. Once through it he went about the business of redialing Larry Spiderman’s number. Jared leaned up against the brick outer wall of the Starbucks waiting for his agent to pick up, and when he did The Black Sheep wasted no time on pleasantries. "You’re going to need to make this quick, Larry," he said, casting a quick glance over his shoulder. "And this really needs to be important. Like, somebody had better be dead." Back inside, Justine was in the middle of an intense moral debate. She’d already brushed aside his keys, taking note of a tattered and faded black and green "MSW" lanyard that looked as though the slightest breeze would cause it to disintegrate. The focal point of her curiosity now lay on the leather wallet before her: black leather, relatively new and with an insignia that seemed familiar but she couldn’t quite place (the Decepticon logo) embroidered on one side. Snooping wasn’t in her nature, but this presented a unique opportunity. Ten years was a long time to go between visits with a friend, especially one she’d been particularly close to, and while she had some reservations about doing it knowing that the details of his life were all neatly packaged before her was too fascinating to pass up. Fortunately, outside the building, Jared was completely oblivious. She took a quick look at his license, noting that the photo wasn’t as goofy as she’d expected, and then her eyes lingered for a moment on the printed address. She hurriedly flipped past it, hoping to find a thin plastic flipbook and the photos it contained, and sighing when she realized there was none. She cast a short glance out the window to make sure the coast was clear and chuckled when she saw him now over near his car, rapping his forehead against the door. Whatever he was discussing, it clearly wasn’t going very well. Justine was about to close the wallet and set it back on the table when something caught her eye. Jagged and torn, the corner of a photograph peeked out through the billfold. Very delicately she pulled it from its resting place, and her cheeks flushed when she realized what it was. "Oh my God," she whispered, but only to herself. It was only a fragment of a photo, cleverly torn from the original whole so that only a very specific piece would remain. It had been taken in February of 1999, but there was no date to identify it, no markings of any kind save for a faded phone number written in blue ink on the back. Justine knew its origin immediately for she was one of only two people it contained. The other was currently outside. She didn’t dwell on it too long, as the fear of being caught with it soon crept up. She put it back in its resting place, as carefully as she’d originally removed it, and tried to compose herself before he could come back in. "Hey, I’m sorry about that," Jared said, bouncing back through the door and over to their table. "He gets really cranky if I don’t follow up with him." He glanced at her, and seeing that her face was still somewhat flushed, became a bit concerned. "Is everything okay? Did something happen while I was gone?" "No, I’m fine," she said, brushing away a strand of hair from her face. "Probably the coffee. Sometimes caffeine does funny things to me." He retook his seat at the table, not quite ready to accept this as truth. "You’re sure?" he pressed, his brow furrowed. "Yeah, it’s nothing." Believing he’d persist, Justine decided it was probably better to change the subject. "So what’d your guy have to say? Anything earth-shattering?" For a moment he eyed her inquisitively. It was obvious that something was up, he was sure of that much, but whether he wanted to press the issue any further was something else entirely. Ultimately he decided to let it lie for the time being. If it really were something important he’d no doubt learn it eventually. "Nah," he said at last, "nothing useful at all. He called me to tell me he’d be calling me tomorrow. Like, what’s the point, you know? Just fucking call me tomorrow." She chuckled, but it wasn’t convincing. "Actually, since we’re kind of on the subject" he said, taking his phone back out of his pocket, "I should probably give you my info." "Yeah," she reached into the messenger bag that was slung over the back of her chair and removed her own phone, "I’d like that." "But only as long as you promise not to punch me in the face the next time we hang out." "I make no such guarantees." * * * Jared had learned early in life that most truths are best wrapped in humor, so when he'd told her that he'd bought his house because he ran out of space to store his toys it was actually quite close to the truth. His home was something of a nerd sanctuary, with two separate areas devoted to his quirky, eccentric interests. The biggest of these zones was his basement, most of which he'd converted into livable space and decorated with a color scheme fresh from the pages of a comic book. It was the second room where he found himself currently, huddled over his desk staring blankly at the monitor. World of Warcraft was something that he'd taken up during his downtime before joining Sin City last June, and then again over the winter during an extended break from wrestling. His guildmates, mostly friends of Mervin, had allowed him into their ranks not so much because he was particularly good at the game, but more out of a sense of obligation to their friend. Jared didn't really feel much like playing, but after the day he'd had the distraction was a welcome one, even if it did mean having to listen to nine other people yapping incessantly through his headphones. Normally they weren't bad, but tonight they sounded to Jared like a swarm of angry mosquitoes buzzing in his brain. Plus, he'd already exhausted his catalog of 'emo' music. 'So what are we waiting for?' 'Steve had to repair his gear, or something.' 'No, I ran out of arrows. I have to grab some more.' 'What kind of hunter runs out of arrows? I swear to God, man, you are fail as hell.' 'Hey, I'm second on the DPS meter, and I'll be right back. Jesus.' 'Whatever, we've still got to wait for Jared to stop being retarded. Seriously man, why is it that every time we run something you have to take off all your gear and dance?' He didn't answer. Really, he was only there in body, as he'd checked-out mentally about six minutes into playing. This may or may not have contributed to the fact that the group had all died, or 'wiped' in game terms, three times in the last hour. 'Uh, Jared?' Still nothing. 'Hey, Merv', wake up your friend. Steve's back and we're ready to go.' 'Sorry,' he said finally, his character still busy dancing across his screen. 'I kinda spaced-out for a minute there. What are we doing again? Something with that Norse Code guy?' Dan, the organizer for this particular raid, was starting to become visibly annoyed. 'Ugh! We're going for another attempt on Hodir, and you're going to try to stay alive this time. One more wipe and I'm seriously going to beg this Connors guy to kick your ass for all of us.' 'Ha! Jared couldn't win that fight with a training montage and a Robert Tepper soundtrack.' 'I wonder what kind of loot he'd drop.' 'Shut up, Steve.' 'Yeah, shut up, Steve.' 'Okay, so everyone know what they're doing on this fight? Jared, you with us here?' Again, he didn't answer. His attention had been pulled towards a nearby window through which he could see a pair of headlights pull to a stop outside his house. 'Jared? Hey, Jared!' In the darkness he could faintly make out the sight of a car door opening and a figure emerging. He removed his headset, tossed it onto the desk, and rose to his feet. When he saw the car door close and the vehicle start to drive away Jared pulled on a nearby Green Lantern tee shirt and curiously made his way to the stairs. 'Shit. I think we lost him.' By the time Jared reached the bottom of the stairs the doorbell was ringing frantically. It was late, after 1am, and as he prepared to open the door the scenarios played out in his head. Something’s gone horribly wrong! There’s been an accident! Oh my God, your house is on fire and aliens are in the pool! 'Uhh... Hi.' He braced for the worst as he pulled the door open, but the worst was not to be found. Jared wasn't quite sure what he felt when he saw her standing on his doorstep. She was dressed in khaki-colored Capri pants and a short sleeved white blouse bearing faint pinstripes of beige and blue. She was barefoot, having instead elected to carry a pair of simple sandals rather than wear them. 'You thought you were getting away from me,' she slurred, the scent of alcohol still fresh on her. She stumbled forward over the threshold all but falling into the hallway. Jared moved quickly to catch her. 'But I'm not letting you run away again.' 'Cal', what are you doing here?' he asked, helping try to steady her on her feet. 'I told you, silly. You were going to run off again, but I thought 'hrm, noooooo', and now I'm here to make sure you don't go anywhere.' She jabbed her finger into his chest to punctuate each word. Her giggling quickly turned into laughter, before it all came to an abrupt halt. 'I'm gonna siddown.' He watched dumbstruck as she clumsily navigated her way to the living room off the front hall, plopping herself down into the nearest chair. Jared stepped to the door, still open, and glanced outside, but there was no sign of the car that brought her here. He closed the door and stood alone for a moment, trying to wrap his head around things. The day had already taken its toll on him, both emotionally and physically, and now it presented yet another challenge. He slid his hands into the pockets of his shorts and moved into the living room as well, keeping a respectable distance. The last time the two were alone with alcohol involved, things had gone... Jared sat down slowly, still fighting to get a handle on his thoughts. 'How did you find me?' 'I'm fucking magical,' she said, crinkling her nose. 'I have my ways.' 'And that was a cab that dropped you off a minute ago?' 'Mmmmm.' 'Um, don't take this the wrong way, but...' 'Buh' what?' 'I don't think that it's a good idea that you're here like this. I mean, let's face it: you, me, and drinking doesn't usually end well.' He got back to his feet, but still kept a reasonable distance. 'Just hang out here for a second, and I'll get my keys to bring you home.' 'Aww,' she pouted, cocking her head to the side. 'You're no fun anymore.' He stepped back into the hallway, pulling his wallet and keys off the console table and sliding them into his pocket. Because she was barefoot he never heard her stand up and creep over to him, but by the time he turned around there she was, less than a foot away. 'Look,' he continued, trying to back away without insulting her, 'It's been a weird day for the both of us. I'm not judging, or trying to be a dick, but I really, really don't think this is a good idea, Cal'.' She was still moving closer, and he was still backing away, but when his back hit the same table he had just pulled his keys from Jared started to panic. 'I missed you,' she said, letting the sandals slip from her fingertips to the floor below. 'And I know you missed me. Don't act like... uh oh.' She pulled away suddenly and quickly put her hand to her mouth. Jared knew immediately what was coming next, but before he could react to it she lost control, vomiting up a bit on the front of her shirt and the hall floor. 'I don't feel so good,' she groaned, sliding to the floor. In truth he was relieved. The floor and her clothes were both messes that could be cleaned-up easily. The same could not be said for what could have resulted had she not been sick. He knelt next to her and extended his arm, giving pause before gently rubbing her back. 'Oh, Jared, I'm sorry.' 'It's okay,' his tone was comforting, no doubt due to the sudden lack of stress the change to the situation provided. 'You can crash here. We'll bring you upstairs and get you cleaned up.' She made it to her feet okay, but wobbled when she tried to take her first step. He stepped in again to catch her, scooping her up off her feet and electing to carry her upstairs. She felt light in his hands, and for a moment he wondered how something so small could hit so fucking hard. 'I'm sorry,' she groaned again, this time weaker. 'It's okay,' he reassured her again. 'It's okay.' * * * The early morning air was thick, humid. Jared slid aside the glass door separating his kitchen from his porch and stepped out onto the wooden surface, his feet now wet from the gathered dew. Gently, he closed the door and stepped to the edge of the deck, leaning his body against the railing as he looked out over his yard, watching as a few fireflies danced in the mist rising from his pool. There he stood, reflecting on his situation, until the soft touch of a gloved hand on his shoulder seemed to pull the heat from body, conveying in no uncertain terms that he was not alone. "I was wondering when you were going to show up," he said to his guest, a smile forming at the corners of his mouth. She removed her hand from his shoulder, letting it fall by her side. She was not a large woman, though she was unquestionably intimidating. The grace with which she moved was almost unearthly, ethereal. She was a master in the truest sense, the least amount of effort always yielding the maximum result. Jared found her scary enough in the daylight, but in the moonlit haze of early morning Alexandra Pierce seemed purely terrifying. "Oh do forgive me," her tone was colder than her touch, and laced with sarcasm, "but navigating through your addled brain is quite the chore. I trust that you do not intend to make a habit of summoning me under these circumstances." "Great," he muttered under his breath, turning his attention out over the yard, "even in my head I can’t catch a break." "Consistency is paramount." "Oh, you heard that?" "I am, unfortunately, a construct of your subconscious mind. Thus, I hear everything you think." "Wow. That sucks." "Indeed." For a moment there was silence between them, but this did not sit well with the Spider, who did nothing to mask her annoyance. "Do you intend to tell me what it is I am here for, Jared, or will I be forced to try and guess your intentions, ludicrous though I presume them to be?" "Well, I figured you already knew." The Black Sheep shifted his position against the railing, turning to face The Director and leaning his elbows against the wooden surface. "I mean, you did just say that if I think it then you know it, or something like that." "Very well then," she continued, her expression hardening, "I will be the devil on your shoulder, provided it expedites my departure from this place." She gently dragged her fingers across the railing, and then pulled her hand away to examine them, rubbing her fingertips and thumb together. "Awesome. So, what ‘cha got for me?" "Nothing you are not already aware of," she taunted, the corners of her mouth curling ever so slightly. "Great. Let’s go in circles all day. You’ll say one thing, I’ll repeat it, and then you can tease me for it. Awesome. Love it." He pushed away from the railing and eased himself to the deck floor, adopting a seated position with his legs extended out before him and crossed at the ankles. "Humor me," he continued, leaning against a nearby support. The lines of her scowl sharpened. "Fine. We shall have it your way." She stepped closer, staring down at him derisively, and clasped her hands together behind her back. "You are a coward, Jared, as you have always been. You hid for well over a decade, from your friend and from yourself. You think that today has somehow vindicated you, helped repair the relationship that you squandered, but would you like to know the truth?" She crouched low, bringing herself but inches from his face. Personal space was not something she ever cared to recognize. "You. Are. Lying to yourself." The words dripped from her lips like venom, and though she wasn’t smiling, not really, she seemed to be enjoying the moment. "All of this means nothing, because when the time comes – and rest assured that it will, you will fall into the same safe habits that brought you here in the first place. You will have your toys, and your house, and perhaps even a few misguided fans, but you will be alone." "Aww, c’mon. You can’t be too hard on the guy." The addition of a new voice captured the attention of both Jared and Alex. They turned their heads, almost in stereo, to the far side of the deck where Charley "Cookie" Crisp sat comfortably in a patio chair. "Seriously, look at him." The other half of the ‘Kings of Pop’ rose from his chair and took a few steps forward into the beam of the deck’s motion-based light, his infamous and pronounced afro casting a shadow that enveloped both Jared and Alex. "He’s kind of pathetic, all sittin’ here on the porch while there’s a pretty white girl, drunk ‘n willing, upstairs in his bed. Didn’t you learn anything from me?" Cookie, Sin City’s resident pervert extraordinaire, simply smiled as he shook his head. "Honestly man, what the hell am I supposed to do with you?" Charley continued. He glanced at his own torso, and then down at his legs, before returning his gaze to Jared. "And why the fuck do I have whipped cream on my nipples?" "His imagination is an interesting place," Alex answered, "I myself am trying to not spend any more time here than is absolutely necessary, else I fear I will have become tainted by it." "So she gets to be her, all scary ‘n shit, and I get the thong treatment?" Charley said to Jared. He then turned his attention to Desade. "You believe this guy?" "Fortunately I do not need to, since not of this debacle is real. It is the only thing that is giving me any comfort at present, since dealing with one of you is arduous enough," she said, rising fluidly, "but both of you at once borders on catastrophically insane." Finally Jared spoke up. "Aw, it’s not that bad, is it? I mean, if you’re playing ‘devil’, then I need someone to play ‘angel’, you know." "And he is the best you can do? Perhaps Charles is correct. Perhaps you truly are pathetic." "Dude, she’s pretty brutal," Charley added, leaning up against the house and folding his arms, an act which inadvertently smeared the whipped cream. Hell, you must be pretty screwed up if you’re imagining me, complete with what we’ll call ‘clever accessories’. You know what she’s going to say, and how’s she going to be all ‘I will stab you to death with my marvelous English’. You know how I roll, and the kind of shit that I’m into, so you know what I’ll tell you to do. Why don’t you just ask yourself?" "There’s a reason he doesn’t ask." The attention of all three people turned to the source of the fourth voice. There, joining them on the porch was a second Jared Sykes, ten years younger, not yet jaded, and untainted by the cruel realities of those unlived years. "He already knows what I’m going to say. He needs to pretend he’s not that guy anymore." * * * He woke suddenly with a sharp inhale, immediately aware of a tingling sensation in his right arm. When his eyes cleared and he was able to focus the realization struck: he’d fallen asleep on his bedroom floor. He slowly eased himself up until he was seated, pulling his legs in close as he took a moment to try and get some feeling back in his arm. Jared rubbed his eyes, trying to clear out the remaining sleep and wondering if he’d simply dreamed the previous night. A glance at the bed was all the confirmation he needed. There she lay snoring softly. For a moment there was nothing else in the world. Wyatt, Sin City, and the Elite Championship: none of it mattered, and he’d have traded it all for more moments like this one. He pushed himself to his feet, careful not to wake his guest, and slid through the half-open door. He passed a mirror in the hallway on his way to the stairs, and gave pause as he brushed a few stray pieces of lint from his shirt. Half his face was red, the result of rubbing against the carpet during the night. Gently he lifted his shirt. A bruise had formed along his left side, the result of two solid strikes from his houseguest. He descended the stairs, hopped into the first floor bathroom to gargle away his morning breath, and then made was way to the kitchen where he had the unfortunate pleasure of forgetting that orange juice makes a horrible chaser for mouthwash. The marble was cool against his skin as he leaned against the countertop, pressing his forehead against the smooth stone in the hopes it would take some of the dry sting away from the rug burn on his face. Soft footsteps in the hallway drew his attention, and when he finally convinced himself to look up she was standing opposite him on the other side of the island in the center of his kitchen, still wearing the tee shirt and mesh shorts he’d helped her change into the night before. "Hey," she yawned, taking a seat on the nearest stool. "What time is it?" "Little after nine. You sleep okay?" "Yeah. What about you? Thought I woke up and saw you on the floor." "Eh, no complaints. A few screwed-up dreams, but fine other than that." For a moment there was an awkward silence between them. She flopped forward, her arms sprawling out on the counter.
