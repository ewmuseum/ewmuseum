Date: For Equilibrium He watched the moonlight twinkling in his tumbler of Jack Daniels before raising it to his lips and letting the warm liquor trickle down his throat. His forearm captured the strays that adorned the outside of his lips and he watched those moonbeams again. Life had become so hectic. He wasn’t sure he was aware of which way was up anymore. The revelation that Brock Shepherd called him to act like his guardian angel to save him from something called the Red Right Hand plagued him every waking moment of the day. It was beginning to interfere with his life. It kept him up at night so the Jack Daniels was one of several servings he’d swallow down in the battle of the fatigue that came with the insomnia of worry. Since April got back from the police station she hadn’t been the same. She’d seemed kind of hollow to August. Distant even. Lost in a train of thought and assumedly never coming back by the look on her face. The nanny she’d get in to watch Adam was in more often while she was plagued by her thoughts. And she hadn’t told August what she’d found out in that interview. The worry in her eyes seemed to have been multiplied tenfold once she’d returned. Those cops had rattled her somehow. Yet even though he knew the distance between April and Brock he had an uneasy feeling in his stomach about what the guy was going through. It was genuine suffering he’d heard on the other end of that cell phone. There was a monster that was haunting him and he needed someone to pull him out of the shadows. As August dwindled on the thoughts more and more it seemed to make sense to him as to why he’d called him for help and not somebody else. Who better to kill a monster than another monster? He wasn’t called the Raging Fear just because of some stupid wrestling gimmick. He was a legit A-1 bad ass motherfucker with a thing for hurting people. It extended further than the ring. He just found some place that paid him to do it. But what was the monster that plagued him? Was it something that he could actually do something about? All of this was coming to ahead as August drew closer to a better place in his wrestling career. Closer to opportunities. But sometimes professional pride had to be put to the side while you dealt with real-life. August knew he’d never be able to compete without facing the demons of his life. Brock was as close to a brother as he’d ever had. He was almost his son-in-law. He was a friend and he needed help. Whether he was the cause of the problem or he was the victim it didn’t matter to August. A friend in need is a friend indeed. Keys rattled at the mouth of the door’s lock and August heard the crunching of the lock’s gears falling into place. The door swung open and April stood there. She was dressed in a woollen coat that came to her knees. Her pale white skin looked all the more pale having been exaggerated by the black bags beneath her eyes. She’d been sleeping about as well as August had. Their eyes met and they shared a moment between them. They each knew the inevitable was coming and they had to swallow their fears and face it. Come face to face with the devil they knew was lurking just beneath the surface. April tugged a stray wisp of hair behind her ear and forced a smile. August did the same. He reached across and patted the cushion of the sofa near his armchair, beckoning his daughter to come sit nearby. Come sit so they could talk. She obliged. They’d been so distant lately a wash of relief overcame August as his feisty daughter sat before him. He took her hand in his free hand, placing the tumbler of sour mash on the coffee table so that he could speak to her properly. Really give her his attention. But the urge for a cigarette was fierce. He wasn’t sure how she’d take his decision but knew he needed to be upfront and honest with her. He’d only just found his daughter. Three years was a short time in the span of a lifetime. He never wanted to lie to her. Giving in to the urge, August plucked a cigarette from his packet and placed it in his mouth. Tugged out his Zippo lighter and flipped the lid, rolled the wheel and put flame to the cigarette’s end. He flipped it shut with a sharp hand movement and stood the lighter up proudly on the coffee table beside the Jack. He took a long draw of courage through that Marlboro Red and closed his eyes as he let the plume of smoke omit from his lungs. When he opened them he was peering into the eyes of his daughter. There was no tension. It seemed like a wash of relief was overcoming the pair of them as they both shared their inner demons. "The Feds told me Brock was killing girls, Augie." August drew back on his cigarette again, having been beaten to the chase of speaking first. He let out a simple grunt that let her know he was shocked but not surprised all at once. "I think he’s coming for me." April tried to fight the tears that were building up, unfortunately not strong enough to fit the solitary tear that forced through the gate and broke down the wall, allowing it’s compatriots to roll down her cheeks together. She cried silently before her father. "And I’m scared." With a nod of his head and another puff of his cigarette, calmness seemed to grow within Augie. He nodded his head again. "I’m so scared, daddy." He stopped mid-breath. His heart stopped mid-beat. The whole world came to a grinding halt and August could have sworn he’d heard the flap of a butterfly’s wing. Maybe that flutter he’d heard was the butterflies growing in his stomach, rattling his cage and shaking the very foundation of one of the toughest men to walk the earth. Those butterflies flapped their wings and reduced the monster to quivering mess. His top teeth bit down into his bottom lip, producing blood due to the bite’s intensity. She called me daddy. He ran that Jack Daniel’s stained shirt sleeve across his eyes, bringing a watering amidst the tears that were trying to escape. Then he grunted and nodded. "It’s okay, darlin’. I’m gonna find him. I’m gonna find him and help him." "I don’t think he WANTS to kill me. I just think he’s confused. He’s not himself. This isn’t Brock. I know it isn’t. I just know it." April broke down into a hysterical sob that shook her shoulders violently. She gagged on her tears and hyperventilated like the three year-old who’d fallen in the gravel. August’s ham-sized hand reached out and cupped the back of her neck, pulling her head into the nape of his neck while he dragged on a cigarette so he could burn off his own overwhelming emotions. He glanced around into the shadows, like he could see the monster that was plaguing a man he once considered a friend. A man he was now vowing to save. "Don’t worry, darlin’. Daddy’s here." He patted her back. "Daddy’s here."
