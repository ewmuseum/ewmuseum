Date: 10/19 One of the advantages to being a cult hero around the world is having a support network. In Japanese pro wrestling, this is somewhat common. Whether members of the corporate world, yakuza, or simply huge fans with money to burn, such supporters provide meals and presents to their favorite wrestlers in exchange for getting to spend time with their heroes. Such gatherings can be fairly low key, or fairly...not. Tonight's falls into the latter category, as sixty or so people have taken over a shabu-shabu restaurant in Shinjuku. They sit at long tables, fifteen or so per, dipping thinly sliced beef, chicken, and seafood into boiling broth with their chopsticks to cook it. Sitting at the head of one of these tables is a most unusual sight, even for a country such as Japan whose very existance is an odd dichotomy. Amidst the sea of Japanese faces sits the restaurant's only Caucasian. His attire is respectable, a fact that clashes completely and entirely with the oversized red afro that sits atop his head. 'Hell of a match today, Takada-san,' the man says, as he dips a slice of Wagyu beef into the broth. 'Couldn't have asked for a better way to go out.' The man seated to Mr. Afro's right, presumably Takada-san, looks similarly worse for wear. Unlike his opponent for the evening, what Koji Takada wears is Western business attire. Not only does it look good, but it covers up a lot of the bruises and scarring. The stitches over Koji's right eye and his general exhausted look remain the key indications that he was in a fight. Not just a fight. Japanese wrestling has had streetfights for years, just like American wrestling. The damage exhibited by these two men can only be explained with one word: Deathmatch. 'It was,' Koji agreed. 'Not bad for my first time with the wire, was it?' 'The wire', of course, was barbed wire. The match in question was a no-rope barbed wire barbed wire board deathmatch. What this means is that you take down the ring ropes, and lace up barbed wire in their place. Then, in two of the corners of the ring, you stand up plywood boards, wrapped stem to stern in the sharp, pointy wire. In short, its the sort of enclosure you'd put cattle in, not people. 'Not bad at all. You don't look you tried to escape from jail, which is always a good sign after one of these. Guess I always miss that memo, though..' Suffice it to say that the Trapezoid has far more wire punctures on him than Takada does. 'Damn good thing I got it, then,' Takada prodded. 'Don't want to look like your ugly ass any time soon. After this I'm headed out into Kabukicho to pick up some bitches.' 'Of the legal or illegal kind,' a voice chimed in. It was the man sitting to the Trapezoid's right, a middle aged, generally inocuous looking fellow that the Trapezoid knew well. He'd been one of the HIT's sponsors for several years. Ishinomori-san loved pachinko, his daughter Miyoko, and good sushi. 'If it's the latter, you should come by my place. We got a new few girls that you'd love.' He was also a ranking member of the Oguchi-gumi crime syndicate. A man who had once confded in the Trapezoid that he liked the American because they were both constantly in fights for their life, and expressed his fervent hopes that one day, he would be fortunate enough to kill someone in the ring. It astonished the HIT sometimes just how many of his fans were yakuza. Most were lower-ranking soldiers. Some were high level officers. Many had the top joint of their pinky finger severed in the rite known as yubutsume. The first time he'd been in a gathering of such people, he had quietly feared for his life. One of the gangsters, though, had taken pity on him, and explained that the yakuza were generally not that bad a sort. Unless, of course, you owed them money or were in a turf war with them. It was different than the United States, that gangster said. The yakuza weren't mindless thugs like the Italian Mafia. Trapezoid didn't know how much of that he could believe, but he did know that in his presence, for the most part, yakuza were just like most other wrestling fans: enthusiastic, loud, and quite often intoxicated. 'So long as they're clean,' Takeda agreed. 'I'm going to be wrestling deathmatches, and ain't good for business if I bleed all over people when I've got diseases.' 'With the amount of women you fuck,' Trapezoid grimaced, by way of reply, 'it'll be a miracle if I don't have herpes or something worse after tonight.' 'Not like it'd matter for you,' came the retort. 'Your dick gets so little use that it's considering open revolt.' 'My girls are all clean and of the highest caliber,' Toru stated, firmly, in an effort to stop the playful argument. 'I would ask you to come as well, Afro-san, but you've made your aversion to my establishment clear on many occassions.' The HIT chuckled at the nickname. 'I feel bad about it, Ishinomori-san, I really do. Just got this thing about prostitution. Maybe it's because I'm from Utah. All that odd morality washing over me. Besides, back there you don't need prostitutes. If you find another girl you like when you're married, you can just marry her too.' Laughter broke out across the table. The Trapezoid had also been surprised to find out that a vast majority of the yakuza he knew were well-educated, well-spoken men, literate in both Japanese and English, and well aware of the world around them. 'I'll ask one last time,' Ishinomori-san stated, 'because you're going back home soon. Stop on by with Takada-san? You can have your pick of any girl in the place, on me. I treat them well, Mr. Williamson. I do not kidnap the women who work for me off the street.' Ishinomori's gaze met that of the Trapezoid, and he nodded firmly. For the Trapezoid's part, he knew Toru was being serious; the older man never referred to him by his real name otherwise. 'His last name's Williamson,' Takada chuckled. 'What's his first name? No wonder he goes by the Trapezoid.' 'It's Justin, and believe it or not, I wrestled under the name for a year or so. I got tired of the afro and hardcore wrestling and elaborate moves.' 'You, tired of hardcore wrestling and elaborate moves,' Koji asked, incredulously. 'You only put them on the map in America. When I first showed up to do a few tours for Blood Alliance Championship Wrestling the dirtsheets were calling me the Japanese HIT.' 'It didn't last long, but it was something different. Live and learn, I guess. After that I came back here to where I belonged.' 'And now,' a voice from across the table called, 'you're leaving again.' 'Yeah. Can't say I saw it coming, though.' That was a vast understatement, to the Trapezoid's way of thinking. 'Why not,' the voice chimed in again. 'You've always been popular in the States. Had matches that make even the stuff here look tame.' 'Yeah, but the sport's changed in America for the most part. Wrestling's trying to clean up its act. Lot of the older audience has moved on to mixed martial arts. I hear that's a subject you all know lots about, considering if I take rumors to heart you own stakes in most of the Japanese MMA companies.' Most of the table chuckled, as if to neither confirm nor deny such rumors. 'But anyway,' the HIT continued on, 'it's a matter of demographics. Most groups want to appeal to the kids, so blood's out, excessive violence is out. Even if a company brought me in and I never swung a single steel chair, the Parents Television Council would have past footage of me on its website so fast my head would spin. I'm infected with swine flu for the purposes of most of the industry right now.' 'But you still got hired by someone,' Takeda said. 'Sin City Championship Wrestling? Never heard of them.' 'They changed their name from FUSE a few years ago,' HIT said. 'They're televised on a premium cable channel in the States, Cinemax, at like, 11 at night, so there's no content restrictions. Company's made its name off being batshit insane. Sex, blood, violence, the whole nine yards. I can't believe I didn't know about the place sooner, or I'd have tried to get in the second I found out about it.' 'They don't have a Japanese TV deal yet,' Ishinomori-san asked, to which the Trapezoid shook his head. 'I know a couple of guys at Samurai TV. I'll see what I can do. It would be a pity to miss seeing you in your element.' 'I don't think they'd mind a new audience. They've apparently been in some financial trouble lately, but they've got some new backers. Part of how they had the money to sign me. Granted, I'm not the most expensive guy on the free agent market, but...' 'You still command a fair price given your fame and talents,' Ishinomori Toru stated. 'Fair?' 'Yes.' The Trapezoid took a break from the conversation to dunk another piece of beef in the boiling liquid. Judging it to be done, he removed it, blew on it carefully, and took a bite. Delicious. 'This place is the best,' Takada sighed, his own plate full of recently dunked food that was slowly being consumed. 'Out of my price range, though.' The HIT chuckled. 'Generally, I'd consider it out of mine, too. Only time I ever come here is when the boys are kind enough to throw me a bone.' Toru grinned, and the Trapezoid patted him on the shoulder. 'Koji, I'll be out of the country for at least a year. The yakuza are going to need someone else to throw their support behind. Why not someone who supports their businesses in Kabukicho?' 'Many of our young men support you, Takada-san,' the gangster to the HIT's right chimed in. 'Given time, you too could rise to the favored status of our geometry-loving friend. He has been competing here for twelve years. You, to my knowledge, have been around three. Things take time, Takada-san. You also have the benefit of being a native.' 'Yeah, I do,' Koji grinned. 'Never did understand at first why everyone loved the gaijin so much.' 'To echo my friend over here, it takes time,' the Trapezoid admitted. 'First few times I wrestled over here, I was just the really odd gaijin with the huge red afro. I got fans because of the fact that I don't especially care much for my body and my ability in the ring. Made a pretty good reputation for it, all told.' The smile the Trapezoid gives is surprisingly full of teeth. For all of his other injuries, he's only missing one tooth. Somehow, it's a molar. It's really best not to ask. 'The evening is young,' Ishinomori-san observed. 'I can tell that my colleagues are getting restless. A toast before we leave?' Flagging down a waiter, he whispered something into the waiter's ear. The other man promptly took off for the kitchen. 'I've ordered us all a glass of sake,' Toru said. 'Something to conclude the first phase of the evening.' Koji Takada rubbed his hands together. 'I do enjoy concluding the first phase of the evening. Means I can get to the second phase of the evening!' 'He's more excitable than usual tonight,' the Trapezoid observed, as a small phalanx of waiters came out, to place glasses filled with fine sake in front of the tables of diners. 'I promised him he could have his pick of the girls at my club tonight for the first time,' Ishinomori grinned. 'It's a considerable upgrade for him. Not that any of the girls in our employ aren't worth it,' he amended, 'but...' 'Say no more,' HIT said, waving a hand. 'I understand. I don't think I've known anyone more addicted to sex than Koji here...' 'Don't knock it till you've tried it,' Koji leered, and Trapezoid was about to launch back with a comment to the effect of that he had tried it, thank you very much, when the middle-aged mobster at their table suddenly rose to his feet, and the tables around them quieted. 'Thank you all for being here tonight,' Toru stated. 'Tonight, we send off a man who has spent the past three years here entertaining us. He has been given the opportunity to return home to do what he loves. On behalf of my colleagues, Mr. Trapezoid, I thank you for giving your all every night for our benefit as fans of your craft. We look forward to your return whenever possible, and will follow you to the best of our ability. KANPAI!' It was, by the Trapezoid's way of thinking, one of the more touching tributes he'd been given. The fact that it was given by a man who had willingly told the HIT that he'd committed two murders didn't lessen that fact any, even if it perhaps should have. 'KANPAI!' Sake glasses went up and down around the room. The Trapezoid didn't drink much, but always partook with his hosts to be polite. That didn't mean, though, that the rice wine didn't make him shiver from the taste. 'You really are a gaijin,' Takada pointed out. 'That aftertaste is the best part!' Chuckles ensued from the rest of the table, making the Trapezoid look rather sheepish. Slamming his glass down to the table, he could see people rising from tables all over the restaurant, presumably headed out for any of a number of activities, legal or otherwise. What the Hell, the Trapezoid thought. I only live once. 'Ishinomori-san?' 'Yes, Afro-san?' 'Your offer from earlier. Is it still good?' The middle-aged man's face broke out into a huge grin. Koji Takada slapped his opponent from earlier in the evening on the back. 'Absolutely. You will not be disappointed.' And to make a rather long story short, he certainly wasn't. ----------------------------- In Brigham Young High School, the Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid was also a legend, but for entirely different reasons. 'Mr. Trapezoid' had started his educational career there as a much beloved substitute teacher who essentially no-sold the curriculum left to him and taught students according to his own system, the Fun, Unviolent, Caring, and Kind mathematical methodology. Yeah, no one can ever really say the Trapezoid is the most mature human being walking the face of the Earth. Parents weren't especially happy with Mr. Trapezoid, but in spite of his odd methods, he got results. Students reported being able to understand Trapezoid's lectures far better than those of their ordinary teachers. Eventually parents came around as well. Thanks to a groundswell of popular support, soon enough, the HIT became a full time teacher at the school, teaching a full load of three tenth grade geometry classes, and one class in advanced placement calculus. Rather than use textbooks that he deemed boring and unsuccessful, the Trapezoid penned his own, got them published, and went before the Utah Department of Education to get them approved for use in the state. In fact, ask the Trapezoid his greatest achievement, and he will not tell you of wrestling conquests or title belts. He will tell you about his geometry textbook. Three years ago, though, the Trapezoid headed to Japan to pursue his other love: wrestling. For three years, the halls of Brigham Young High have been devoid of its legend. The date is Thursday, October 5th. The place is Mr. John Reynolds tenth grade geometry class. Mr. Reynolds has been in a car accident, unlikely to return for a considerable length of time. Today, a legend makes his return to his arena. * * * It's 7:30 in the morning, a Godawful early hour for most people, but the beginning of first period for the students of Brigham Young High School. Twenty-six students sit at their desks, whispering amongst one another. They all know their teacher's out for a while, but they don't know what happens from here. 'Please not Mr. Jenkins,' one says. Mr. Jenkins is, needless to say, one of the school's more unpopular substitute teachers. There are no shortage of those at Brigham Young. Seconds pass, each one ticked off by the clock sounding louder than ever in the mostly quiet room. Just as the clock ticks 7:31, the door flies open, and a voice says 'Sorry I'm late.' Twenty-six sets of eyes turn to the doorway. The man standing there is familiar to them only through the school's lore and legend. Even having never seen him, the red afro atop his head is enough to identify him. Some students have been told stories about him from their brothers and sisters. Others have friends who know him. Now, as he steps into the room, dressed in a button down shirt, tie, and slacks, placing his bag down on the front desk, he removes all doubt by writing his name on the white board in black washable marker: MR. TRAPEZOID. 'Mr. Reynolds sends you all his best. He gave me a call from the hospital yesterday, and he wants you all to know that he's resting comfortably. As for me, some of you might know me from television or siblings who know me, but for those who don't, I'm Mr. Trapezoid, and I'll be your geometry teacher for at least the next few weeks.' The pandemonium that ensues can be heard several classrooms away. 'Please quiet down,' the Trapezoid says, even if he's basking in the applause like it's a wrestling pop, 'and open your textbooks to page 342. We're going to be starting Chapter Seven today: Triangles & You: What You Need To Know.' Unearthing his textbook from his bag, he flips it open to the proper page. The book is illustrated with images of a cartoonish version of himself demonstrating various mathematical principles. A hand goes up immediately, and the Trapezoid is forced to unearth a seating chart from his bag. 'Yes...err, Tom? Sorry, it's going to take a bit for me to get to know everyone's name. I just got told I'd be taking over yesterday.' 'What are you doing back here,' Tom asked. 'You've been gone for...what, three years?' One of the few problems that the HIT had as a teacher was focusing kids who often had questions about his wrestling exploits on discussing math for at least a majority of the forty-nine minute period. 'Well, I've been signed to Sin City Championship Wrestling. Before that, I'd been wrestling in Japan since that was the only place that really wanted to see me perform. Now that I'm back home, I can manage a full course load again. It's a nice deal for me, really, especially since SCCW shows are so late at night.' Abruptly, the bell rang, and students began packing up their books and writing implements. 'Remember to review the chart on page 344 in your book. It's got lots of great examples on types of triangles!' the Trapezoid called after them, as they filed out of the classroom and on to history or British literature or whereever the Hell else their day would take them. Glancing at the clock, the Trapezoid sat down in the chair at his desk. It would be about two or three minutes before the first couple of students trickled in, five before the bell to start the second period of the day rang. Two or three minutes was just enough time for him to marvel at the fact that two days ago, he'd been sitting in a shabu-shabu restaurant in Shinjuku, Japan, dining with a bunch of yakuza. * * * So, just who or what the Hell is the Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid? Is he the innovative, death-defying lunatic that has won titles around the world and pals around with the Japanese mob? Or is he the slightly out of his mind mathematics teacher who helps students understand geometry in what can only be described as a unique way? The answer, of course, is that he's both of these people, at the same exact time. And what, in turn, does THAT mean? Simple. He's gonna kick yo' ass.
