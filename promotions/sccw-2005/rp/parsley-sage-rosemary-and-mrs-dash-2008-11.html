Parsley, Sage, Rosemary and Mrs. Dash

November 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 2008 With a frown on his face and a Styrofoam container full of cold take-out, Jared Sykes plopped himself down on the floor of his hotel room, reaching for the television’s remote control. He entertained a brief thought about why it hadn’t been fastened to a table, the way that most hotel remotes he encountered were, but the thought was fleeting, replaced by a desire to poke at a shrimp with a plastic fork. He sighed as he stabbed it, imagining how it might have tasted when it was hot, fresh out of the sauté pan where it had been cooked. His thoughts drifted to mustard sauce, endless rivers of mustard sauce, and for a moment he imagined himself adrift on a slice of herbed focaccia, gazing at trees of garlic mashed potatoes and parsley. It made chewing his dinner that much more arduous. He was alone in the room, the soft light of the television and the sounds of Reverend Horton Heat and Eugene Ware his only companions. "Oh boy! Get ready for judgment, you smelly heathens!" He watched the opener of the show in silence, stabbing periodically at the contents of the container. He knew that eventually he’d see himself on television, a recount of the evening’s events. He just wasn’t expecting to start so soon into the broadcast. A scowl crept onto his face as he watched himself get out the car, talk to himself as he walked to the other side, and lost the footrace to one of the attendants. Thoughts raced through his head, none of them pleasant. No, you dumbass! Get the door. GET THE DOOR! Ohhh, you look like such an ass. It’s a miracle she didn’t poison the water and leave you to die. For a moment he entertained the thought of stabbing himself in the eye with his fork. Instead he reached for the remote and flipped channels so as not to have to watch himself. The plastic fork probably didn’t have the penetrating power he needed, anyway. He started flipping channels, trying to buy himself enough time before he would put Cinemax back on. If there was an avenue he could take to keep himself from watching himself look like a fool, he took it. By the time Phillip Kennedy and Reginald Lovecraft finished their conversation he had already eaten the last of the shrimp. The remaining potatoes made it through a goofy "static" piece, but didn’t survive much longer than Paul Searle’s conversation with Barry Kitna, and just as well. When Amy Campbell led Zach Marshall to find cotton candy he focused his attention on the television, and he spoke to no one in particular. "You’ve got to be kidding me…" He stood up and sighed before removing his shirt. His left arm, both the forearm and the bicep, had been taped in the aftermath of his encounter with Wyatt Connors and a botched splash. For the first time perhaps in his life he was happy to have worn long sleeves, since it afforded him the chance to hide it. No sense in announcing to the world that he had a hairline fracture of both his left humerus and radius bones. He cleaned up the part of the floor where he’d been sitting while Sean Sterling beat Reginald Lovecraft. He changed into his standard basketball shorts and tee shirt while Steve Knox threatened to drink everyone’s blood. He’d also made a mental note to ask Mervin just what the hell a "luffy" was, given that Knox looked nothing like a bath sponge. By the time Campbell and the youngest Marshall were once again on television, he’d taken up a hawk-like posture on the edge of his bed, and his eyes weren’t looking at anything but the screen. By the time it was over he pressed his palms into his eyes, and he had but one thought: cotton candy. Cotton. Fucking. Candy. "Son of a bitch." A click of the remote control shut off the television, and a quick twist on the lamp killed most of the light in the room. "Canada sucks," he sighed, quite dejected. He threw himself back on his bed, his eyes closed tight, but sleep didn’t come. "You look like hell." "Hey, thanks." Mervin Humperdink sat across the table from his closest friend, Jared Sykes, in a relatively unexciting Mexican restaurant just over the Canadian border. Mervin had found this to be particularly amusing, given that he was relatively certain that there weren’t enough people in this part of the world to substantiate the operation of any restaurant, much less one so specific. Though he had made sure to comment on Jared’s appearance, Mervin was looking fairly haggard himself. His dark brown hair had become completely unmanageable, and given his build he looked very much like an inverted mop. "And for the record," Jared continued, responding to his friend, "between the hair and the glasses all you’re missing is the facial hair and you’d look like a friggin’ cult leader." Mervin just stared across the table, though he couldn’t make eye contact. Jared was hiding his behind a pair of silver-rimmed sunglasses, trying to conceal the dark circles that had formed from a lack of sleep. "So what’re ya’ having, David Koresh?" Mervin shook his head, rolled his eyes, and went back to reviewing the menu. Jared, thinking that it would go unseen, quietly slid his cellphone from his pocket, and flipped the cover open. This was not lost on Mervin. "Who’re you hoping to hear from?" he asked, his eyes raised to peer over the top of the menu. Jared quickly and clumsily replaced his phone in his pocket. It was less than discreet. "I don’t know what you’re talking about," he said, a failed attempt to hide it. "Your phone, you just took it out of your pocket to check for messages. It’s the second time I’ve seen you do it since we got here." "No, I…" "And then there was at least eight or nine times in the car while I was driving, while you pretended you were sleeping." Behind the menu Mervin was smiling. If there was stubble on his face, and if he did look like a cult leader, the smile would have been beyond creepy, since neither of those were true instead it just came across as dorky. Jared removed his glasses, putting them on the table, and then rubbed his eyes, heavy from a lack of sleep. "You seriously think she’s going to call, or text you, huh?" He didn’t need to explicitly define the ‘she’ he was referring to. It was painfully clear to the two of them who he meant. "No, not at all," another lie, "I’m waiting for Larry. He’s probably got some information about the Jewel in the Crown tournament, or something." "My money’s on the ‘or something’, and I seriously doubt that Spiderman would have anything relevant to say about the situation. Not like you haven’t been following it." "Actually, I really haven’t," this much was true, though he could tell that Mervin still didn’t believe him, "Between the shows and the… other things, I really haven’t had much time. I just know that they haven’t needed me until now." "Riiiight, I’ll believe that when… actually, no, I probably won’t believe that. ‘Specially since you haven’t told me how ‘other things’ went." "Dude, you paged me back to the show. Things didn’t really go all that far." "Yeah," Mervin sighed, bringing his eyes back down to menu level, "about that." "It’s fine. I’m over it. Not like there was anything I could do about it anyway, what with my ‘interesting’ relationship with Lance, and all. I try to get involved and he’s liable to build a chain-link fence just to bounce my face off of." Jared took a moment to rub one of the two scars on his forehead, a reminder of the time Lance Marshall personally introduced it to the support structure of a steel cage. "Though I have to admit," he continued, "I might not be the highest on Campbell’s big list of favorite people, but from what I saw and heard she did kind of have a point." An awkward pause followed. Mervin was the first to break it. "Well, it’s kind of shame." "What is?" Jared asked, taking a drink from his soda. "That you didn’t get to play catch with the squish-mitten. That the panty hamster never got to run on its wheel." Surprised, Jared choked on his drink, coughing as he placed the glass back down on the table. "The hell did you just say?" the words leapt from an otherwise stunned Jared Sykes. "What? Isn’t this how you and Charley talk about things?" Jared stared across the table at his friend, realizing where this was going. "Dude, are you jealous?" "No, not at all," he paused, "Well, maybe," another pause, "Okay, yes. A little. I mean, I can understand why. He’s sort of got a knack for this stuff, even if he does have to pay for it." For a moment the Black Sheep sat in silence, looking inquisitively at his friend. "Okay," he said at last, "you want the details? All the details?" "It’d be nice, yeah." Jared again flipped open his phone, but this time it was to depress the power button long enough to turn the device off. The screen now dark, he replaced the phone in his pocket and turned his attention back to Mervin. "Where do you want me to start?" When Lawrence Spiderman called a few minutes later it rang straight to voicemail.
