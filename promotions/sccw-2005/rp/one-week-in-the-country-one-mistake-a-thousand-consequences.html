Date: Today August 15, 2009 10:13 PM Eastern Time Boston, Massachusetts ''Careful now.'' The show hadn't ended when they left; Fenway Park was still packed to the gills with cheering Bostonians. They could hear the pyrotechnics and hard chords from Killean Sirrajin's entrance for the main event. But for these two redheads, Colossus VI was over. Alexandra Pierce was limping as she made her way across the parking lot, leaning heavily on her partner in so many ways. Her whole body hurt – back, legs, and arms – and the trainer (a reprehensible man who went by the name ''Dr. Fihlguud'') had glued shut the cut Hessian's chair opened on her forehead, a pair of butterfly bandages marred the woman's forehead. But the proud woman hadn't said a word since Deacon Dale pulled her out of the ring in the closing moments of her failed attempt to win the Elite Championship. She hadn't objected to being helped to the backstage, hadn't said anything when the trainer saw to her injuries, and hadn't fought the small redhead as Amy Campbell guided her through the back door. She hadn't even smiled when Amy actually made a ''shoo'' motion at Jadian Bridden as the Ace attempted to compliment Pierce's performance. In fact, the first sound she'd made was a quiet whimper, a sharp sound that came mainly from her nose, as Amy helped her into their silver, four-door rental car. ''You're allowed to be in pain, y'know.'' Campbell's sigh was soft as she eased the Spider into her seat. ''But I'm not a doctor, I don't know how bad it is unless you say something... and it's worrying me.'' Alex waited until the door shut, resting her elbow on the locker and her head in her hand as Amy darted around the car. ''I'll be fine,'' she said dourly. ''I've been beaten up before. This...'' She moved her hand up to touch the bandages. ''Is new, but it's my ego that hurts the most. I know I did not get pinned, but that is three bouts in a row.'' When she sank into the drivers seat, the Director's tattooed counterpart spared a small, kind smile. ''One of those was my fault, but none of them count because you weren't pinned. You lost the race to the pinfall, not the match.'' The smile turned to a frown and irritated grunt when she shifted the car into gear. ''God, these rentals might as well be boats. Good thing the hotel's nearby... though I'm thinking the hospital might be a good idea.'' ''No hospitals.'' Pierce half-turned in the seat. ''Trainer cleared me to leave. If there are hospitals, then there will be... people. I'm not really in the mood for people today. And I doubt people are in the mood for me. We'll just go to the hotel or whatever. Come back tomorrow, maybe. Jonathan has a match—'' ''Yeeeeeeah, I'm not coming back to one of these shows. I nearly hung myself in boredom. Ever get that feeling you're completely unwelcome somewhere?'' Amy marveled slightly at the fact that Pierce had grown so used to her driving habits; she no longer gripped the seat for dear life when they merged lanes. ''Besides, I've got plans. We've got plans, actually.'' That perked the Spider up slightly, one brow rising. ''Whatever it is, I'm certain I will...'' She had to force down Desade's diction. ''I'll hate it. Well. I'll pretend to hate it, and then probably secretly love it because you somehow see through to the heart of me every time.'' ''Does the heart of you feel like stopping at Wendy's on the way, then? 'Cause I avoided catering all day for fear of being deliberately poisoned - and you will pretend to hate what I've got in store, promise.'' ''Wendy's... that's the one with those heavenly little pieces of chicken with the Asian flavoring, isn't it?'' Amy shrugged. ''Baconator, Alex. Baconator.'' ''I have never been comfortable with a restaurant with a sandwich named after a Schwarzenegger movie. But lead on, Siegfried.'' Amy's grin stretched slightly as Alex did pull down the handle above her head. For a woman that faced death on a daily basis, there was still nothing quite as frightening as Amy Campbell driving. It was several hours and multiple stops for food later that Alexandra Pierce woke up, bleary-eyed as she focused on the motel outside the windshield. It was nothing to look at, just a two-story Motor Inn, illuminated by pale, yellow-orange streetlights. ''Whuh...'' Her mouth tasted like warm paste. ''Where are we?'' ''Oh, thank God, you're awake. I was going to have to break out the Pink and sing. The drive was kind of boring. Who knew how many little states there were bunched up together up here?'' ''It's New England.'' ''Yeah, but... bah, too many jokes.'' ''How long have I been asleep?'' Campbell glanced down at the clock. ''Somewhere between five minutes and four hours.'' ''And where are we?'' ''Vermont, I hope.'' ''Vermont.'' Alex brushed the hair out of her face, sitting up straight. ''Did you drive us all the way to Burlington?'' ''Sure is what it looks like.'' Amy grinned. ''Did you make a reservation?'' ''Uhh... I didn't want you to wake up, so I avoided talking on the phone as much as I could.'' Alex patted her pockets, brows knitting. ''Where's my ph—'' ''Oh, I picked your pocket when I stopped for a shake at McDonald's. Well. Not picked, so much as it was sticking out and I figured it might ring, and you need your rest.'' Alex brushed her hair from her eyes. ''You stole my phone.'' ''I seem to remember doing that the last time we were here, and the world hasn't spun off its axis or—oh, crap!'' Your palace, such as it is, awaits.'' ''Oh, it's not that you'll be dreading,'' Amy said with a wry grin, picking up the pace as she headed to the front office. They'd both say it felt as if they'd seen the inside of nearly every hotel room on the planet, but this was different. For one of them, it was another world – for the other, a long weekend. The size of a hotel suite, it was infinitely better while being far cheaper; a kitchen island dividing the main area for cooking and eating, a small hallway with a washroom and second, tiny bedroom and then the 'master bedroom', inappropriately placed in the only communal portion of the building, with a tussled bed meeting a window that overlooked what most people would tell you is better than what you'd get in any of those thousands of hotels they knew.
