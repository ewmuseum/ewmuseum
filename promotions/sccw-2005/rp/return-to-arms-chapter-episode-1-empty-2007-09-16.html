Return to Arms Chapter: Episode 1 - Empty

16 September 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/16/07 Our story began in 2002, with a young man named Jason Seymour Wilson. With his childhood friend, Laura Marie Winters at his side, Jason embarked on a quest in the world of wrestling. Dubbing himself Seymour Almasy, he tore through the ranks, winning championships all over the world and the acclaim of thousands of fans. More importantly, he wed his childhood friend, and all was well in the world. But the world, as it is so often wont to do, struck back. Fame took a toll on our hero. The constant scrutiny of the spotlight drove a wedge between him and his wife that ultimately lead to divorce. Working perhaps the sport’s most Hellish ring schedule broke down his body. And his ex-wife’s suicide, aided and abetted by one of his greatest rivals, destroyed his competitive soul. He soldiered on after this, more machine than man, to some success. But Seymour Almasy really died on February 9, 2006, along with his ex-wife. Seymour visited her every week, regretting ever dragging her into his career to begin with. But time flows like a river…and history repeats. Time heals the wounds caused by repeated pounding. Though his body will never be 100%, it is at least at 95%. The reporters have faded away. Certainly, they can return, but Seymour is older and wiser now. He can match them in wits more capably. As for his ex-wife and childhood friend? She is the real reason he has returned; the real reason behind everything he does. So why is it that Seymour Almasy has returned to the sport of kings under the banner of FUSE Wrestling, and in doing so is about to set into motion a chain of events that would defy even the imagination of great storytellers like Shakespeare, Orwell, and Nomura? In the Final Fantasy’s own words, taken directly from the first line uttered in the best damn slumber party ever known as Final Fantasy X-2, "It all began when I saw this sphere…of you." Seymour Almasy: Advance The Return to Arms Chapter Episode 1: Empty In truth, it wasn’t a sphere in the FFX-2 sense. That sort of sphere was the rough equivalent of a videotape. No, this sphere was literally a glass sphere, filled with small pieces of some material and a picture of Laura Winters. Seymour Almasy held it in his hand, shaking it around to set the pieces of material in motion around the picture. In spite of himself, Seymour smiled. She had given him this snow globe just before they parted to go to college, her in Delaware, him in Pennsylvania. It was an arrangement that hadn’t lasted too long before the call of professional wrestling led their lives in a completely different path, but to this day Seymour considered it the sweetest gift she had ever given him. He placed it back carefully on his computer desk. Since his wife’s death over a year ago, he had taken to living in a small one-bedroom apartment in New York City. Anything bigger than that reminded him of the penthouse he and Laura had shared, or their beach house on Long Island Sound. Almasy moved from the computer to head back to the small dining room table he took his meals at. On it was a letter that had already been open, read, and debated aloud a half-dozen times. A letter from FUSE Wrestling. Seymour had always been on good terms with the promotion, having competed there twice. Once, he came to challenge for the PTC Global Title, and once to take on FUSE’s own Tsuyoshi Tanaka. Truthfully, the letter had inspired both anticipation and dread. Anticipation because he had been out of the world of wrestling for so long, and was flattered that it still remembered him. Dread because of all it had taken from him. Still…he could not sit idle forever. His video game systems sat in the corner of the apartment, unused for several days. He had fought through his queue of unplayed video games over the course of a months long effort that culminated a week ago. Seymour Almasy was, effectively, bored. Bored, perhaps, but not without options. Sitting next to the letter on the table was a filled-out application for employment at the Dragon’s Den gaming store a few blocks from his house. It didn’t pay well, but Seymour didn’t care about the payment. He cared because it was something to do, and he would get free gaming product. Free gaming product was always a good thing. He could only sit at home for so long and do nothing. Seymour was a person who liked to be active, in spite of his love for RPGs. The choice then, was simple at its root: Go back to what he once loved that had taken everything from him, or go work in anonymity, where he could avoid the cameras and the prying eyes for the rest of his life. Seymour looked from letter, to application, and back to letter again. The once Final Fantasy had just picked up the Dragon’s Den application when the phone rang. Placing it back down on the table, he went over to the phone, and picked it up, clapping it to his ear. "Seymour Almasy here. " A breathless, frightened sounding voice on the other end of the phone replied "It’s Nameed, from the cemetery." There was a slight, pregnant pause, and then the cemetery employee continued. "We….have a significant problem." * * * * * An hour and a half (and several subway stops, the LIRR, and a taxicab) later, and Seymour Almasy was standing in the office of one Nameed Mohmmad, owner and operator of the Dignity Cemetery in Long Island, NY. Both men sat in chairs, Nameed on the business side of the oaken desk, serious expressions on each man’s face. "I’ll cut straight to the point, Mr. Almasy. What I am about to show you is extremely unsettling, and something that I hope to never have to show anyone." Seymour gazed back at the other man, and took a deep, agonized sigh. "What happened to Laura," he asked, presuming the news that was going to come was not going to be good. "The ultimate, overarching problem," Nameed replied, "is that we do not know exactly what happened. Graves are disturbed every year for a variety of common reasons, but this case does not fit into any of them. " Seymour may have been five-foot-nothing, nearly two-hundred nothing (after a good buffet) pounds, but as he stood up to fix the cemetery operator with a withering stare, he looked much larger and intimidating than he truly was. "So, you’re telling me that someone broke into Laura’s grave?" Nameed winced, but nodded his head in the affirmative. "That is, regrettably, but a small part of the problem. It cannot be understood unless I show you. Come with me, and I will continue to explain. From his side of the desk, Mr. Mohammad rose, gesturing for Seymour to follow. They wound their way out of the small white building, and out a back entrance. "We noticed potential evidence of grave tampering this very morning when we arrived on-site," Nameed explained, as Seymour listened, motions stiff and disbelieving as he followed the cemetery director. "We are well-aware of your visiting habits, and the fact that you place a bouquet of flowers at your wife’s grave weekly. Moreover, you have instructed us multiple times to not remove these until they are no longer attractive. Generally, as you know, we place the flowers in a small glass vase by her headstone. When we came here today at the beginning of hours, we noticed that the flowers and vase you left on Friday were gone." Seymour continued to listen. Had he been alone, he would have been pacing back and forth by now, but he had a destination for his nervous walking now. He recognized the path they were taking, up the main road for a quarter-mile before the inevitable right turn. "We find generally that most thieves don’t simply steal flowers. There also seemed to be evidence of activity around the gravesite. As a precaution, we exhumed the casket, to see if the body had been disturbed. What we found was, well, slightly confusing." Nameed made the right turn into the cemetery proper, weaving through headstones with the grace of a man who did this every day of his life. Seymour’s heart rose into his throat at the sight of a well-polished black coffin sitting next to a large pile of dirt, an open grave, and a headstone that he read as his ex-wife’s. "I warn you," Mr. Mohammad said, gravely, "that this is likely to be shocking to you." Swallowing hard as Nameed moved to the side of the casket, Seymour nodded. "I’ve never seen a body before," he choked, trying to summon together the courage to look inside at what he knew was likely there. "Unfortunately," Nameed said, shaking his head in confusion, "you still won’t have after looking in here." He opened the coffin, and Seymour peered inside to reveal, as Nameed had eluded to, an empty casket. "Where is she," Seymour asked, almost instantly, steel in every word. He no longer got ragingly angry. He didn’t throw temper tantrums like he used to. Instead, he simply glared at the director, wondering why such things continued to happen to him. Wondering who he’d pissed off to deserve this fate. "I do not know," the director said, looking at Seymour curiously, "but there is something more wrong here than the simple fact that there is no body in this casket. Look again. Do you not notice something odd?" Almasy growled under his breath, but did as he was asked. Again, he saw nothing. "There’s nothing in there. It’s an empty casket. Nice, doesn’t even look like anyone was ever in it. Your point being?" Nameed Mohmmad looked at Seymour, swallowing hard. "Think about what you just said. You are absolutely right. This casket looks unused. I have seen cases of grave tampering, even outright graverobbing. In all of those cases, there is evidence left behind. If not bones themselves, at least an impression in the casket of where the body laid. I have had a forensic investigator over, and he has taken samples to be tested in the lab, but I am confident that my professional opinion on this matter is accurate." "And that opinion is," Almasy asked, throat dry, beads of sweat starting to form on his palms. "There was never a body in this casket to begin with. Which, before you asks, means one of two possibilities. Firstly, the graverobbers who were here went to far as to take her entire casket with her body, and replaced it with this one." Noticing Seymour paling even further, Nameed shook his head. "This is beyond improbable. I would wager my career upon that fact." Slowly, the gears in Almasy’s mind began to turn, and his mouth dropped open, eyes widening to saucer-size. "Yes," Nameed said, nodding in response to Seymour’s seeming realization. "The second, and more likely possibility is that this casket was buried as you see it now – empty. Under that theory, I believe that someone took the flowers and vase that had knowledge of our policy. Someone that knew his action would trigger the exhumation of Laura’s casket. In short, putting this all together, I believe that one of my employees knew that we were burying an empty casket over a year and a half ago, and has been wracked with guilt for that long, to the point where he has revealed the truth in this elaborate fashion. I will question my employees over the next few days. If I discover anything, I will let you know." The Final Fantasy, though, barely heard anything after the word "empty". His mind had already begun to wander to a thousand different places, among them his dining room table at home, where the FUSE letter and Dragon’s Den application sat side by side. Before Nameed’s call, he had been seconds away from heading out to go submit the app. Now, he knew, that application was going to sit home and collect dust for the foreseeable future. Seymour thanked Mr. Mohammad profusely, and asked the director to remain in touch with him. Once that arrangement was made, Seymour turned to begin his elaborate public transportation route in reverse. After all, he had a letter from one Smitty T. Duluth to respond to. * * * * * Empty. That one word, five letters long, has changed my life once more irrevocably. It means that, barring shenanigans that an experienced cemetery director has never seen nor heard of, Laura is alive. Somehow. Somewhere. Life has no Phoenix Downs. I know this all too well. There are an impossible number of leads I need to track down. Laura’s mother and father. The hospital that called and TOLD ME, point blank, that she had flat-lined. On that long trip back to Manhattan, I came to the conclusion that I couldn’t just sit here and do nothing. She could be anywhere in the world at all. As good to me as wrestling was, I don’t have the resources to go around the world to search for her in every country on the planet. And that’s how my courtship with FUSE began, through snail mail, and then the phone lines. STD told me he wasn’t going to coddle me. He told me that I’d be put on the road with everyone else. No special treatment. No nothing. I told him that was fine. If I’m coming back, I want to work my way back up. I’ve been out of the sport for six months. By night, I will wrestle in front of loyal FUSEiac crowds and put on the best show I can. I will regain what I once had, what I once was. By day, I will train, and search every city that FUSE goes to, from Kansas City to Kuala Lumpur for a clue as to where Laura is. I will find her one day, even if it means my life. September 21st, it all begins again for me at FUSE’s first-ever Uproar show. I’m headed for the Joe in Detroit to take on Itachi. Gimmick infringement, clearly. I’m an RPG fanboy, clearly Itachi-san’s seen one too many episodes of Naruto in his day. Not that I have much room to talk, but still. I’ve got no beef with him, though. He’s just in the ring opposite me in my return match after six months off, in a match that I must win. Let’s just say, Itachi, that you’re going to need all the Sharingan you can get…
