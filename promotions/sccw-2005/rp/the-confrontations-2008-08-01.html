The Confrontations

1 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/1/08 Part One: The External Confrontations "I know why you’re here." The only sound in the low-lit room is a fan circling on the ceiling, but even that noise is almost able to drown out the gravelly voice. Jonathan Rhine stands under the fan, staring down at a man who is lying in the corner of the room, concealed in shadows. "That’s sort of funny," says Jonathan, "because I really have no idea what I’m doing." "That’s because you’re a doubter. You always have been. Every little bit of success that you have been able to achieve has been attacked by your furious lack of self-confidence. It’s why you won’t ever last." Jonathan’s face hardens, as it does any time he is faced with an opponent. "I lasted longer than you." The shadows snarl. "You think you had anything to do with it? You really think you were the one who made me leave? See, this is why you came. You came because you want to understand how I think. How we think." "We?" "The ones who seem to run around doing whatever we want while you’re restricted by do’s and do not’s. The ones with the power, the fame, the glory. The ones who take out eyeballs, ruin relationships, and especially the ones who tattoo their rival’s girlfriends." The shadows suddenly move, and Clinton Sage steps towards Jonathan Rhine, sneering. "You want to know how the winners think, because you can’t stand losing matches as much as you lose body parts." Jonathan Rhine doesn’t move; he merely stares at the man who kicked out of FUSE wrestling and asks, "So are you going to tell me?" "I just don’t get it, Katie…why am I feeling so conflicted?" Katie Malick and Shweta Kallemullah are walking down a central Indianapolis street with bags aplenty in their hand, as shopping is only second behind Edy’s Slow churned ice cream in separation anxiety suppressant. Katie’s face is concerned but does not match the sheer crestfallen expression that currently sits on the young Indian’s face. She looks at her feet, which causes Katie to spend most of her time either consoling Shweta or preventing her from bumping into people. "It’s normal to feel like this. You and Foster were really close, and just because some bad stuff happened doesn’t make you automatically love him any less." Katie grabs Shweta’s arm to move her through the traffic on the sidewalk, then continues. "I had so much trouble with this when Jon and I first had problems; I couldn’t forgive him for what he did, but I couldn’t stop loving everything about him. It’s just a process that you have to go through." Shweta looks up suddenly, her face full of hope. "But you ended up getting back with Jon. Do you think I…" "I don’t know," Katie sighs. "I mean, it might be unfair of me to speak about it without knowing him so well, but your boyfri – ex-boyfriend tried to get my boyfriend to hurt somebody to the point of paralysis. When Jon made those mistakes, I knew he at least had a good heart. It’s something you have to judge for yourself whether you think you feel safe enough to let him back in." Shweta nods, then looks down again. "You’re right. I shouldn’t judge my relationship based on yours. I mean, you and Jon are the power couple. The guys not even infidelity could break apart. I’m not even sure if anything can break the two of you up for good." Katie laughs. "I don’t know either, all I know is I lo…" She stops in the middle of her sentence as she looks up and sees the woman who almost did break up the power couple. The psychologist is wearing a business top with a striped skirt, and she smiles wholeheartedly as she walks towards Katie and Shweta. "Hi, Katie. And who are you, ma’am? You look troubled. If you ever want anyone to talk to, I might be able to help you." Then Sarah looks up at Katie expectantly. "Uh…oh! Sarah, this is Shweta, a friend of mine. Shweta, this is Sarah Riley, a…uh…friend of Jon’s." "Look at how much you’ve grown since I’ve moved on," says Clinton Sage, walking around Jonathan with an amused smile. Then he walks around to the front and inspects Rhine’s eyepatch. "Nice trophy. See? Look what being a knight in shining armor has gotten you. Watch." Sage takes a step back and spins in a circle, showing off his body; even in the privacy of his own mansion, removed from wrestling for half a year, he is wearing no shirt and slacks, and his body hasn’t lost muscle. "No scars." "Why does it happen this way? Someone like you, who deserves much worse than what ever happened to me, sitting here in his mansion without a care in the world. Meanwhile, I have been assaulted and broken from every direction, and I still haven’t been able to keep my grip on the top, despite how hard I try." Jonathan shrugs, then looks down at the floor. "It shouldn’t be this way." Sage drops the smile and stares down at his former rival. "Have you ever considered maybe you’re taking it all too seriously? I mean, I’m sure the biggest advantage of leaving whatever piece of shit small town you came from to wrestle was that you were going to get paid for doing what you loved to do. And I can tell from the look in your eye," – here Sage chuckles – "that you don’t even love it anymore. So why care so much?" Jonathan shrugs. "I care because I feel like the morals and consequences of general human actions completely flip when we step into an arena. And worse, I feel like everyone is okay with it. And it just doesn’t make sense to me – and I know I can’t change it myself, so don’t bother telling me that. So if I’m going to get what I want, I have to be able to live with the mentality." Rhine then steps up to Sage, looking straight into his eyes. "That’s why I’m here. I want to know how to beat you without being you." Sage chuckles in disbelief. "Are you really that thick?" "Why, because I’m asking a bad guy how to beat a bad guy? What do you care? You’re not wrestling anymore. You just sit in this mansion collecting merch royalties and doing absolutely nothing. You have nothing to lose. You…" "Shut up, you prick, and listen," says Sage quickly, shutting up the former 2-time SCCW Universal Champion. "I wasn’t asking if you were thick because you’re asking me for advice; that shows you actually have a fraction of intelligence. I was asking if you were thick because you’re not even thinking about what winning means. Where are we right now?" Jonathan just stares. "I’m not playing this…" "WHERE ARE WE RIGHT NOW?" Sage roars. "Your mansion," Jonathan says, shaking his head. "That’s right. We’re here. Not in a wrestling ring. Because I don’t do that anymore. In that capacity, you won and I left. Doesn’t that make you a winner?" Without waiting for an answer, Sage nods. "As much as it sickens me to say, you are. You won. As long as you aren’t sitting in a hospital somewhere, needing a nurse to help you shit, you aren’t losing. No matter what sort of freak pirate you look like." "That’s your answer?" Jonathan sighs. "My determination? I can look in the mirror for that answer." Sage shrugs. "You can leave any time you like, but I wasn’t done yet. The other reason you’re thick is that you don’t think about playing to your handicap, and no I’m not making another eye joke. I’m talking about something you have that we don’t." Jonathan chuckles. "Morals?" "Your chain of support. Any time things get too hard for you, you can turn to the people who are dumb enough to care about you. And since you are dumb enough to care about them, sometimes it helps you out." Jonathan shakes his head again and turns around. "So you’re telling me I have to do what I’m already doing. I was right all along: I really do have no idea why I came here." As he reaches the doorway, he hears Sage’s voice. "It has nothing to do with what you’re doing. It has to do with the way you’re thinking. You expect some sage answer that will uncover all the secrets of the asshole world, but that’s not the way it works." Rhine turns around to see Sage right behind him. "The way it works is you stop being a pussy, trust yourself, and trust the people around you. And if you care enough about winning and keeping what you have going for you, then you’ll be able to get through whoever is doing an impression of me right now." Rhine smiles. "You’ll never stop being an asshole, will you?" Sage doesn’t. "You can let yourself out now." As Jonathan Rhine walks down the stairs to exit Clinton Sage’s mansion with no new information, he can’t keep the smile off of his face anyway. Maybe it’s not all so complicated after all. "Uh, hi, Ms. Riley," says Shweta, still not really paying attention to the blonde in front of her. "Dr. Riley," Sarah corrects. "Right. Well, uh…" Shweta looks at Katie apologetically. "I have to go, Katie. I need to call Foster, we have some things we need to discuss. I’ll catch up with you later, okay?" Katie can’t hide the horror on her face, but Shweta doesn’t see it as she grabs her shopping bags and turns up the street, grabbing her phone out of her purse and putting it to her ear for a moment before saying, "Hey, Foster," in a dejected tone. Katie looks to Sarah Riley and says nothing for a moment, then sighs. "What’s wrong?" asks Sarah innocently. "I think you can guess," says Katie, trying to move past the Good Doctor. Because she is weighed down by many shopping bags, however, she can’t outrun Dr. Riley. "Wait, Katie. I think we should talk." "About what?" Katie says, nearly shouts, as she turns around. Passersby start to notice as the New Life’s girlfriend channels some of her boyfriend’s indignation. "About the fact that you seduced my boyfriend then turned on him to join up with the most evil woman in existence? About how you destroyed all the trust we had with a blink of an eye?" Instead of getting upset or angry at these public accusations, Sarah simply nods. "I know, Katie. I’m sorry. I know you’ve never gotten a chance to confront me about it, so you deserve to have the anger, and I deserve to receive it. So go ahead." She motions to a nearby table, and Katie walks over slowly and sits, looking down at her feet. "I just…" after the effort it took to yell at Sarah, and getting nothing but understanding in response, Katie has lost her energy. She finally looks up at Sarah and sighs. "Why?" "What?" "Why," says Katie, a tear starting to form in her eye. "I just want to know why." Sarah shrugs. "That’s not very easy to answer. It was my job, as you now well know that I was more than a psychologist. I tried to be with him, and he wouldn’t accept at first. But…there was something about him. I know you know what it is. Something just drew me to him. He made me forget everything I thought I knew about wrestlers, and about men in general. I guess I was powerless to stop myself." Katie nods, but her expression doesn’t improve. "I guess. I just wish I knew why he did it." "You mean you’ve never talked about it?" "No," says Katie, shaking her head. Sarah raises her eyebrows. "That surprises me. This is something that obviously put a rift between you two, and looking at it from a psychological perspective, it’s really unhealthy for you two not to talk about it. Because you’ll never know if he’s just going to do it again." Katie shoots Sarah an angry look, and the doctor puts her hands up. "No, not with me. I’m not sure he’d even want to look at me. But if you never were able to identify the problem point that led to this breach of trust and infidelity, you haven’t been able to cure it." Katie sighs, battling over her dislike of her confidant versus her helplessness in the current situation. "So what do I do?" "You talk to him. You make sure that he knows the consequences of another incident. And you don’t ignore the little signs that something might be wrong." Katie nods, then stands up. "Well, I should get home and give him a call. Uh…thanks for the advice." "No problem," says Sarah, standing as well. "But before you go, can you tell me where the new Stage store is? I need some new business pants but have no clue where any of these places are now." She motions to the Stage bag in Katie’s hands. "Oh, okay, it’s just three blocks up to Central and then four blocks until you get to the strip mall." Sarah shifts uneasily. "Uh…I’m so bad with directions. Call me a typical girl." She giggles, then pushes her glasses up the bridge of her nose. "Can you walk with me over there? Maybe we can talk about other things bothering you. Free of charge?" "Well…" Katie once again struggles with her conscience before nodding. "Okay, I’ll show you." Part Two: The Internal Confrontations A few days after he arrived home from Philadelphia, Jonathan Rhine’s workout routine is interrupted by the doorbell. "Katie?" he shouts, but gets no response. After the second ring, Jonathan stands up and walks to the door. "Shweta? Uh, Katie’s not home, I think…what’s up?" And then he notices the tears on her face. "What’s wrong?" "Me and Foster had another fight." Shweta walks into the house and sits down on the couch, sobbing. Jonathan looks at her uncomfortably, not sure how to react. "Did he call you?" "Yeah, he told me about it. I told him he was overreacting, and he hung up on me. He won’t answer my calls now." Even through the tears, Shweta looks up, concerned. "Oh, I’m so sorry! I don’t want to come between you and your mentor!" Jonathan shrugs. "It’s not a big deal, he’ll eventually know he was wrong and come around. He shouldn’t be acting so retarded anyway. He always gets protective of the people he really cares about." "I know, but it’s just so…so hard to deal with! I know that you’re Foster’s friend and that I usually talk to Katie, but…can I at least stay here until she gets back? I don’t have anywhere else to go." "Of course." Jonathan looks at her awkwardly, then gestures to his fridge. "Do you, um, want something to drink?" "So then he scanned all of our faces and pointed at me, and he said, ‘You. You wanna fight me in that ring?’ And I was petrified, it was my first day, but I stepped into the ring and beat Foster in about four minutes. He then stood up and glowered for a few minutes before shouting ‘academy is over for today’ and running out." Shweta laughs loudly, so much that she involuntarily snorts. Suddenly becoming more conscious of her actions, she blushes and looks away. "That’s a great story, but I don’t need you to badmouth your friend just to make me feel better. Thanks for sitting with me, though." Jonathan shrugs. "It’s not badmouthing if it’s a true story, and honestly this is good for me too. I’ve sort of realized I need to do more of this." "You mean talking about your friend with his ex-girlfriend?" "No," he says, laughing, "just focusing on the things that matter. I’ve gotten myself all worked up about all the problems I’ve been having with Amy, with Kannon, with Marshall, and I haven’t been focusing on how well I’ve been doing in other matters, like the people around me." Shweta nods, putting her hand on his shoulder. "Exactly. You have a great girlfriend and a bunch of friends who can help you when things are going wrong. You know that I can help you if you ever need it." "Thanks, and honestly if I just work at it I…" The door suddenly opens and Katie walks in, dropping her purse by the door and walking about four feet in before she sees Shweta and Jonathan on the couch, with Shweta’s arm still on Jonathan’s. After a few seconds, Katie turns around, muttering, "Sarah was right." "What? Katie, what’s wrong?" Jonathan gets up off of the couch to follow her and finally catches her as she moves towards her car. "What’s going on?" "We never talked about what happened with Sarah, and I never knew why you left for her. Now I can see that you never needed a reason. You might be the guy that people look up to, Jon, because you’re just a guy – just a guy who can’t deal with commitment, with just having one person. So if you want Shweta, go on and have her." Tears start to fill her eyes as she gets to the driver’s side door and turns around, the cold look in her eyes startling Jonathan. "And don’t worry about her being Foster’s ex at all; it looks like you don’t mind hurting the people who care about you." Then Katie turns around, enters the car, and pulls away. Jonathan watches the street for a moment, completely confused. As he turns around, he sees Shweta standing in the doorway. "Did she think – oh my God, Jonathan, I’m really sorry!" "It’s okay," Jonathan says, slowly walking towards the house. "She’ll come around…I hope." "Well, it’s getting late, and I’ve done enough harm here already. I’ll get out of your hair soon." "Where are you going go to?" Jonathan asks. Shweta shrugs. "I guess I’ll just get a hotel. I don’t feel safe going back over to Foster’s and I really don’t know where else to…" "So stay here," says Jonathan, pointing to the corner of the house. "We have a guest bedroom, you can stay in there for the night." "I don’t know, Jonathan, I can’t…" "You can’t stay in a hotel room is what you can’t do. Look, you said you’d help me if I needed it. Well, if you need help I can give it. That’s how this friendship thing works, right?" Shweta smiles. "You’re right. Thanks. I hope everything works out for you with Katie, and with Foster too." As she goes back into the house, Jonathan sighs and looks at the street one more time. I hope so too, because that chain of support is quickly losing links. Part Three: The Future Confrontations A king size bed is enormous when you’re sleeping in it all alone. Jonathan Rhine is awoken by his phone beeping, and as he blinks away the grogginess he realizes he is at the complete opposite end of the bed. Moving towards his phone, he grabs it and sees he has a text message from Katie. The subject? "Thinking of You." Smiling, Jonathan opens the message. His current thought, maybe everything will be okay after all, is barely completed when he sees the message is not from Katie at all. It is of Amy Campbell flipping him off. This, more than anything is what sets Jonathan Rhine at the edge. He begins breathing heavily, and he curls himself into a ball. Not crying, but not trying to hold anything in, Jonathan rocks slowly for a few moments, thinking of everything that is falling apart. His legacy. His body. His life. As he shudders slowly, the door opens and Shweta walks in, holding a plate of food. "Hey, Jonat – are you okay?" Quickly recovering, Jonathan lays down and slips the covers over his boxers – he already has Katie mad at him for suspicion alone, and he isn’t about to earn it. "Yeah, everything’s fine. What’s, uh, what’s up?" Shweta walks over, puts the tray down, and blushes. "Well, I felt really bad about yesterday, and it was really nice of you to let me stay over, so I decided I’d make you breakfast in bed. It’s the least I could to for a new friend who’s been so good to me." "It was nothing, really, but thanks." He notices that Shweta has her purse over her shoulder and nods. "You don’t have to leave right away if you don’t want to." "I know, but I should. I have to get to work and I can’t just sit around all day unlike some professional wrestlers." She giggles, then pushes the tray towards him. "So eat up, and give me a call soon. If you need me to call Katie to tell her everything was innocent…" "It’s fine, I’ll deal with it. She’s never mad for too long anyway." Except when I cheated on her last time. "Thanks for the food. I’ll see you later." "I’ll see myself out. Bye!" Waving, Shweta exited the bedroom and within a few minutes he could hear her car starting up and driving away. Moving towards the plate of food, he started eating, thinking about how at least he had one person to fall back on for the time being. Shweta Kallemullah smiles as she pulls out her phone, turns on the speaker, and hits "call." After a moment, a soft voice answers. "Shweta." "That’s me!" "What is there to report?" "All good news! The trapeze artist has lost all of his safety nets, and he’s preparing for the fall." Shweta giggles at her choice of words as she suddenly swerves to miss a nearby car. The voice that returns lacks the Indian woman’s mirth. "While I appreciate your enthusiasm and your charming penchant for using code names, there is no need to hide the results of your mission by muddled words. In other words, English please." Shweta doesn’t stop smiling as she responds. "Rhine is very much alone right now; I’m his best friend! Katie left, Foster won’t talk to him, and he hasn’t been able to get in touch with his Highwaymen brethren because…well, that wasn’t really part of my plan. That was just luck, I guess. Either way, Jon’s pretty much lost the one thing that Sage told him to value." "Excellent. And the other item?" "Is currently being consumed right now by the Eyeless Wonder. Your Shweta is a wonderful cook." Shweta kisses her fingers and makes a smacking sound. "He’ll be feeling the effects over the next few weeks, just in time for you to do your super awesome special secret plan…which, uh, you haven’t told me about yet, by the way." "I prefer to keep some parts of my plans to myself for insurance purposes. Your next assignment will be forthcoming." Shweta frowns. "You mean I can’t go back to my vacation? Man, there were at least two cute cabana boys near my hotel who I hadn’t stolen from yet." She gets no answer, and she sighs. "Fine, fine. I’ll be waiting at your beck and call, Ms. D." Jonathan pushes the now-empty tray off of his lap and stands up to go try to get in touch with either Elliott or Sean again. As he moves towards the door, though, he feels an odd sensation in his stomach. As soon as he looks down and touches the problem area, the feeling is gone. He shrugs and goes to the front room. The feeling is most likely nothing more than slight indigestion, because the drug won’t start to show signs for two or three more days.
