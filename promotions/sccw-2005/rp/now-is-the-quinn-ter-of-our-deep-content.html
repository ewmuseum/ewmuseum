Date: Today Originally posted as a private entry at quinnerscircle.blogspot.com Wednesday, July 15, 2009 The Road to Albu... However You Spell It. Haha, I totally just jacked my mom's iPhone. Go me! And the whole world has to answer right now, just to tell you once again – who's bad? Ok, just did a moonwalk in the middle of the NM State Fair. Total dork move? I'm thinking yes. I'm SUPPOSED to be in summer school right now (hey, I suck at math – wanna fight about it?). Instead, I'm totally sitting on the curb here at the fair while two of the most awesome people I know (but that you prolly hate a lot) ride the Ferris wheel. I've got the (stolen – I RULE) phone in my hands and a powerful desire to type the words ''captain's log'' a lot. There's a cotton candy cone (is it called a cone? Gonna go with cone) between my knees and I have had two big ass cokes and a wild cherry Icee. What I'm saying is: Life? You're kinda being awesome right now. Tuesday (so, uh, yesterday? I'm bad with dates too), I was sitting in Mr. Peterson's algebra class, doing what I normally do – which is to say, ''draw circles in my composition book,'' because holy crap dude is boring. Then Gladys from the attendance office interrupted the class (thank God – think I was turning into Shia LeBeouf in Transformers 2 will all sorts of weird symbols written on my eyes). She's all, ''Quinny?'' and before I can help it, I look up – pretty sure it was the ''no, ma'am, I have totally been paying attention and do not have my hand in your cookie jar, please ignore the crumbs'' look. Frantically, I run through anything I might have done, because summer school is bad enough, but *detention* at summer school is probably unconstitutional. She repeats my name with a little pause – ''Quinn... Gregory?'' – because adding my last name will some how make it clear which of the approximately one Quinns in my class she's talking about. Now, I like Gladys – she says hi to me in the hallways, and she'll bring cookies for some of us – but the woman is kind of... as dumb as a box of rocks. Sees about as well as them, too. ''That's me,'' I said finally. ''Oh, dear. I'm so sorry.'' Gladys stepped forward to pat my arm comfortingly, and... I'm just gonna say it. Kiiiinda freaked out a little. It's not like pro wrestling is the safest job in the world, you know? Plus mom is, y'know, mom, and she does... stuff outside of wrestling, sometimes. So I'm only vaguely aware that I'm totally holding my breath. ''Your mother's...'' Just say it, right? ''She's waiting in the... There's been an accident. Can you just come with me, dear?'' JESUS CHRIST WOMAN FINISH A SENTENCE. My feet didn't wanna obey. If mom was there, then it might've been Amy, but then why wasn't Darcy here? Unless both of them were hurt... ''No, they were toh— I mean, they were remarkably close... in their... closeness.'' Ever have one of those moments where you're pretty sure your brain has misfired? Like, where what you're seeing and what you're hearing are two separate things that can't both exist in the same timeline? I think I read that it's a sign you're having a stroke. I'm not sure you can have a stroke at 15, but for me strokey == giggly. The woman standing at the desk *looked* like my mom – carefully styled red hair, shoulders-back posture, white suitcoat. But the voice didn't work – the whisper was too hissy, the cadence was all wrong. It almost sounded like... ''Miss Pierce?'' I'd forgotten Gladys was next to me. The woman turned around and I about died right there. Sure, she tried to maintain her façade, but I know how much those spacer things bug her, and her ability to keep a straight face isn't exactly well-known. ''Oh, thank God you are here,'' Amy said as she enveloped me in a hug. ''It's me, your mother, Alexandra Pierce.'' Almost busted up right there, looking past her to Gladys, who was watching all teary-eyed. ''Uh, hey, uh, Mom,'' I say, returning a one-armed hug. ''Is everything...okay?'' ''I'm...'' I was impressed Amy didn't crack the hell up. ''I am sorry to tell you this, my, uh... my daughter. But your beloved... uncle, Uncle Sam--pson. Uncle Sampson Pierce has died. It was quite... sudden and stuff. I'll need to take you out of school in...'' Her eyes widened, as if she had to think of a place. ''In another city. Far from here.'' Now, the, like, three people who can read this already know that Aimz is a pretty doggone good pro-wrestler, and she's tremendous fun to hang out with (her love for shows like ''Jon & Kate Plus 8'' aside). But she sucks like WHOA at lying. If this was my mom, she'd probably have worked it all out ahead of time, even asked someone else to play convincer if the school doubted her. Amy........yeah, not so much. She gave me this helpless look, the closest thing she ever came to begging. But ''Oh'' was just about all I could manage to start with. Amy buried me in a hug, allowing her to giggle in my ear. (Yeah, the Big Bad ''Superversal'' Champ giggled in my ear.) ''Not Uncle Sampson,'' I continued. ''I liked his, uh, jokes.'' Amy pulled back, her hands on my shoulders. ''I know. It is a tragedy.'' She turned to Gladys, and it was like she summoned up a glare all the way from the tips of her toes. ''May we go? Our flight departs shortly.'' She tried to capture Mom's overly polite diction, but I don't even know how *she* does it. ''Of course,'' Gladys said, waving her hand. See what I'm saying about the box of rocks? ''Go. And be strong for your Mom, Quinny.'' I nod, as gravely as I can manage. By now, I'm pretty sure I am gonna burst. ''I'll try, ma'am.'' It would be fair to say we ''scurried'' out of the school. Also: that we ran a lot. By the time we got to the car, Amy'd stripped off Mom's jacket, folding it over her arm as if she didn't want to wrinkle it. ''Don't even,'' she grumbled. ''Stupid ass school will let Darcy and I pick you up, but we can't rush you out of school for your uncle's funeral? Sheez.'' ''Probably afraid you'd try to kidnap me, I suppose. Especially since I don't have an Uncle Sampson.'' ''I know.'' I love Amy's sunglasses, big bulbous things that take up half of her face. ''I kinda froze.'' She unlocked the car. ''Don't smush the cake with those boots.'' ''Cake?'' I asked hesitantly, but all she did was grin -- that grin that makes my soul cringe. I didn't have a chance to press, because... when Amy's driving, you just learn to buckle up and hold on for dear life. Twenty minutes later, we were breezing through the gates at Shadow Woods, and Amy handed me a party hat. I looked at it like, ''Bitch, what?'' But I said, ''I'm not wearing this.'' I tried to shove the thing back. Amy strapped on her own hat at a jaunty angle, and I'm pretty sure my jaw hit the floor. Those famous mismatched eyes rolled. ''Keep giving me that look and I'll have to apologize to your principal because our flight was cancelled and I figured school was more important. You've probably got a pop quiz tomorrow that you should be preparing for...'' ''Oh, you little...'' I swallowed my grumble. ''Fine.'' So I wore the hat, even took a noisemaker as we wandered over to knock on the door. Now, it probably isn't really shocking, but surprising my mom is: 1.) Difficult, and 2.) Kinda dumb. Even though Amy pressed her palm to the peephole (maybe because of that), I'm pretty sure Mom knew it was us. My proof: she wasn't carrying a gun or anything. (I'm just saying, Aim, you're not as clever as you think). But, hey, we blew the hell out of those noisemakers. Enough that the mighty, stoic Spider In The Web couldn't help but smile – though she tried to hide it. ''What is going on here?'' she demanded. ''Like we wouldn't have figured out you're old now,'' Amy explained. ''Well. Older.'' She slid past Mom, cake held overhead. They passed a liiiiiiittle than you want someone to be your mom, but I don't mind so much because she's the only one who makes her smile an actual smile, not a ''gonna-kill-you-now'' smile.  ''How did you know it's my birthday?'' I remember the grin Amy flicked over her shoulder in response - and it wasn't just because Mom's hand was on her hip (or, at least, I kind of hope not). ''You think you're the only one who can charm people into getting teh sooper seekrit infowz?'' Now, I want to make one thing clear: no matter how ridiculous it sounds (and no matter how much she'll deny it when she reads this), Amy ''Aimz'' Campbell does talk like a lolcat, sometimes.  ''Not at all.'' Mom's slid her fingers into Amy's belt loops by now - not that I'm paying attention. ''I just want to know who it is you charmed.'' ''So you can tell the cute guy who works in Nigel's office who really likes my tattoos to stay away or you'll hit him with a two-by-four or something? No way – he looks like George Clooney!'' ''I hope you're kidding.'' I think her grip on Amy's hips tightened. ''Or I know how to use Google.'' Ames rocked up, kissing Mom's nose. ''But jealous looks good on you.'' Okay, I'm gonna get into this in a few - maybe in this post, maybe not - but I've just gotta say: UN-comfortable. ''I'm standing here.'' I wonder if Amy forgot; she almost dropped the cake. ''This had better, uh... better go in the fridge. It's already sat out for a while I was busting Junior out of the joint.'' ''You didn't do that.'' I tried to be solemn. ''I owe that to the late Sampson Pierce.'' ''Sampson... Pierce?'' Alex turned her attention back to Amy, who'd tossed the cake in Mom's fridge. Not that that's hard - she's seriously got nothing in there. ''Quinn's late, great uncle. What, you'd never heard of him? He passed away - real tragedy. You had to pick her up from school to make the funeral.'' ''I... did.'' Amy might like ''jealous'' on her, but ''confused'' is kind of awesome if you ask me. ''It was kind of the worst thing ever.'' I tossed in an eyeroll, and I got an elbow in the ribs for it from the returned Raver. ''So what are your grand plans?'' Mom folded her arms in that way that said, ''I know you have some, but amuse me by telling.'' ''Welllll...'' It's weird how sometimes Amy is bashful. ''I thought we'd go to a movie and maybe get some dinner. Then I got another ticket for some ungrateful teenager to come with us to New Mexico – I know, not very interesting – and Dan scored VIP tickets to the New Mexico Fair. Shock of shockers that he's useful, I know.'' We went to see Public Enemies (decent movie, though I have to say that, ''master crook falls for girl, gets caught because of it'' is not a plot you want to take Alex and Amy to. Just sayin'.) And that is the (not so) short version of how come I'm here in New Mexico, enjoying my cotton candy while Mom and Ames ride the Ferris wheel. It is not, contrary to popular belief, because I took a wrong turn in Albuquerque. Hahaha, okay, that was bad. They're coming down (fucking finally), so I have to wrap this up now and put the phone in Mom's not-purse, because I totally wouldn't borrow it. July 16, 2009 1:47 AM Originally posted as a private entry at quinnerschoice.blogspot.com Thursday, July 16, 2009 WARNING: I'm about to get all sweet up here. It's way late – I mean WAY LATE – and I should be sleeping. (Though we're in ABQ, and... yeah, not a lot to do). I grew up in Dallas. Mom sent me there because, hey, when you're Alex Fucking Pierce, your little baby girl is a hell of an easier target than you are. When Amy found me and told me my mom wasn't a pharmaceutical rep who lived in Iowa, I was... listen, I was skeptical. I wanted to go home, back to my hard-ass stepdad, back to my boring school and my stupid friends. But they didn't give me any choice, really. And I'm sure I was horrible about it. No kid wants to move across the country in the middle of the school year, abandon everything they know, and move to fucking Oakland. After that creepy Oz dude snatched me, it wasn't like Mom was gonna back down (thanks, Ed Lambert, you douchefuck). But now I'm here. And I'm stuck in the middle of this nightmare of a relationship: Amy's married to Darcy, and I live with them. But she's fucking my mom, which I should find creepy. I should be mad – at Amy for hurting Darcy, at Mom for possibly ruining their marriage, at Amy because she's fucking my mom and that's gross. But I'm not. I know it's not perfect, I know there's no possible way it can end well, but I see these people together and... and I believe that love works. I believe that it doesn't work the way that you want it to. But I believe, you know. Given some of my readership, I won't talk about the marriage. (No need to bum out my vacation!) But I see my mom, and she is everything she appears to be on TV. Tough, stoic, icy, frigid (sorry, Mom, it's true), and kind of mean. And I see Amy, and she's everything she appears to be on TV, too: cocky, emotional, angry, and... well, kind of mean. But together, they're magic. Together, Amy and Alex are a team, a unit. They pick each other up when they fall, they fill in each other's weaknesses. They're yin and yang. Peanut butter and jelly. Bacon and... well, more bacon. I love Darcy. He's a great guy, sweet as the day is long, grills a great steak, helps me with my math homework without making me repeat times tables (yeah, Amy, I get it, you know them in two languages – hooray for you). And he and Ames, they're a sweet pairing. But they're not Amy and Alex. I know it can't work. I know it probably won't work. But I look in the next room, and Mom's sleeping with her arm wrapped around Amy, and they both look content. Happy. Tomorrow, we're going home and I have to go back to school. (Boo!) Mom turned 35 today, but when Amy's around, she seems ten years younger, like none of the bad stuff in her life ever mattered. Amy turns 27 tomorrow, and she has to go back to Oakland – there are about 385246 parties with her name on them this weekend. I wish they could stay here, in this moment, forever. I could just watch them sleep. Two women with remarkably different upbringings, lives, skills, and histories. They're Blair and Serena, both beautiful, both talented. Everything comes so easily to Amy, the world is literally at her fingertips, and I feel like Mom has to plan everything out to get to the same places that Amy just decides she wants to go. You know Ames said she wants to try parkour? Just out of the blue, she's decided that jumping up buildings would be neat. Mom would never do that. They're perfect for each other, and a part of me feels bad, because Darcy is great. But I'd give anything for the kind of happy that they are right now, even just for a minute. After tonight, I just felt like I had to share that. They're lucky to have each other. And I'm lucky to have them. ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ (Posted by Harlequinn at 2:13 AM)
