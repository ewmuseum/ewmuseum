Aftershock, part 1

Date: After SOS5 "Okay, I'm just going to step out for a minute to talk to her. Just sit tight, and I'll be right back." Dr. Grayson smiled as he stepped into the hallway, pulling the door behind him. His shift was drawing to a close. Soon he'd be able to clock out, go home, and enjoy the vacation that was supposed to have started twenty-odd hours ago. Soon the wrestler would be stitched up and sent home. The hospital was largely quiet this evening, free of the previous day's chaos and commotion. July Fourth had seen the same steady stream of accidents; the pattern was predictable. There'd been the burn victims, the drunks, and a middle-aged Italian gent who'd managed to blast the tip of his pinky finger clean off. As he passed the nurses's station, Dr. Grayson marveled at the difference a day could make. Even the waiting room was sparse. A young mother blinked away sleep while her young son used her lap as a pillow. A college-age boy tapped his foot impatiently. A nervous father rubbed his wife's shoulder and reassured her that everything would be okay. Amy Campbell's attention was focused on a red Nintendo DS. This particular session of Harvest Moon had just entered the spring season, and there were turnips that needed planting. Attractive, red-head, video games, Dr. Grayson thought. Just like he described. "Excuse me, Mrs. Sykes?" he asked, extending a hand. Immediately, Amy tensed and looked side to side, checking for someone who could have been Jared's mother. It was a moment before she realized what the doctor had assumed. She'd (not) carefully trained herself to stay cool in these situations, so she made an effort not to immediately correct him. "I, uh. Buh. Is there a problem with Jared's insurance or something?" "No, no, nothing like that." He shifted the chart under his arm and smiled. "I just wanted to talk to you about your husband's condition. Could you come with me, please?" "He's n--" She'd never know exactly why she stopped herself from correcting him just then. Instead of interrupting, she stood. "Are we talking something bad here? I know he's banged up, but I didn't think..." "By and large it's nothing serious. Well, let me correct that. It's nothing that I'd recommend admitting him for, but there are a few things that we'd like you to keep an eye on." He flipped the chart open and thumbed through a few pages. "The index finger on his left hand is broken, and needed to be reset. There was also a dislocation of the fifth metacarpal - the pinky finger - on the right hand." Dr. Grayson dug deeper into the stack of material, retrieving a darkened sheet of film that he held to a nearby light. "I'm more concerned with the shoulder," he continued. "There was some tearing of the anterior fibers of the deltoid muscle. What concerns me about this is that your husband underwent surgery to repair his rotator cuff in late 2002 on the same side, so he's going to need to limit his activity for a while. A few weeks at least, a month might be better. I've already explained this to him, but I wanted to talk to you about it separately. I get the impression he can be a bit stubborn." "To say the least." Amy chuckled. She thought to correct him again, but didn't. "So... he should take it easy on weights or...?" "He should try to keep it immobile, if at all possible. We'll be sending him home with a sling as a precautionary measure. There's been severe trauma to the shoulder in the past, so it's important that this be allowed to heal properly. The general rule of thumb here is that if he has to question whether he should take part in an activity, that he should refrain." "Would surgery fix it? I mean, he's going to fight you because he's in a good place career-wise right now, but... I dunno. If the benefits outweigh the time off, y'know?" Suddenly, Amy shook her head and reminded herself that she wasn't actually Jared's wife - she'd just gone too far to correct the doctor's initial mistake. "Wait. Sorry. That's not my place to push for." "I wouldn't recommend surgery. Not at this point, anyway. As long he's willing to listen he should be fine. If it gets worse, then we might explore other alternatives." He paused at the door and put his fingers to the handle. "He's right inside here." Inside the room, seated on an examination table, Jared Sykes looked a bit like an impetulant child as he tugged at the sling that held his right arm in place. Tape held two fingers of his right hand together, while an Ace bandage and a metal splint kept his left in one piece. A row of fresh gauze was fastened just beneath his hairline. "I feel like a goddamn mummy," he said. "Baww," Amy's smile was one of the sweetest things on earth when she wanted it to be. She strode over and genlty placed hands on either side of his face - careful to avoid the new bruising and any bandages. "The pouty thing looks good on you, but don't be such a baby. How're you feeling?" Jared shifted uncomfortably in his seat and offered up a weak smile. "I feel like a goddamn mummy," he repeated. "I spoke to your wife outside," the doctor said. The only thing that kept Jared's eyebrows from hitting the roof were the layers of padding and tape that held them in place. Unfortunately, his cheeks were clear, so there was nothing to hide the blush. He shot a nervous glance in Amy's direction, but she didn't return his gaze. "Oh. Uh... Y-yeah?" "Mm," Dr. Grayson held the chart against his chest. "Told her the same things I told you. You're going to need to take it easy for a while. Nothing strenuous. Nothing that could re-aggravate your injuries. And, I know nobody likes to hear this, but that also includes any sexual activity. At least for a while." Like a pair of teenagers caught in the act, neither Jared nor Amy said a word. Dr. Grayson cleared his throat. "Well, I'll have a prescription waiting for you at the nurse's station, and I'll be in touch with your primary back home about getting those stitches out in a week or so." "Thanks," Jared said. The doctor stepped from the room, nodding his goodbye as he pulled the door shut behind him. Jared exhaled a long sigh, as though he'd been holding his breath for minutes on end. He slid from his seat on the end of the table, again tugging at the sling. He couldn't remove it here, not without a lecture, but he'd already made up his mind to cast it aside once they reached the car. "God," he whispered. "This is gonna suck." There was nothing particularly special about the Garden State Diner. The name was horribly unoriginal. The decor was 1950's retro. The menu consisted of staples. Even the burger, which they advertised on the windows as being the "world's best", was passably average on a good day. If it wasn't for the simple fact that it happened to be open at 3am, and that his pain medication instructed to "take with food", Jared and Amy would have driven right on past it looking for something better. Against doctors orders, he'd left his sling in the car. His shoulder had started to tighten, so moving it wasn't in the cards. Still, he felt shackled by it, and casting it aside imbued him with a sense of liberation that, given the rest of his physical condition, did more for him than the drugs. What good the burger was doing he wasn't quite sure, but the fries were crispy, and there was enough ketchup on his plate to drown out the taste of most anything. "So, umm..." He jabbed at a pickle spear with one of his fries. "I'm sorry you had to sit through all that tonight. I hate hospitals. Waiting in one... I don't know of anything worse." "Even doctors don't like hospitals," Amy shrugged. "Waiting for you isn't a problem for me. We're gonna have an issue if that sling isn't back on your arm when we get to the car, though. Just because I never obey doctors doesn't mean I'm cool with you doing it." "Yeah?" he grinned. "Eh, I guess you're probably right. And I should listen, after all, seeing as how we're apparently married now." Campbell's eyebrows suddenly darted up. She dropped her onion ring to the tabletop and laughed nervously. "Oooh man... you noticed that. I, uh... see, he kinda... he assumed it, and then I forgot to correct him the first time and... yeah... I didn't lie or anything. I'm not creepy and trying to wife up prematurely here, I promise!" The vinyl cracked as he leaned back in his chair. "I may or may not have told him that, you know, you were. I mean I'm not assuming anything, or... I dunno. It's just... I wasn't sure what they would or wouldn't tell you otherwise, you know? I'm sorry if it put you in a weird spot." "Truth be told, I kinda liked the idea. It... I dunno. I had a husband who wasn't a friend by any stretch, and you seem to be the whole package for me." After that, Amy swallowed hard and decided to work on her Pepsi. "I, uhm. Should I not say that? That was probably a bad thing to say." Even after four months as a couple, and close to a year of friendship, every now and again Amy managed to say or do something that Jared simply had no response for. He'd lock up, stutter, stammer, and beam a vibrant shade of red. This time was no different. He stared, slack-jawed and unable to speak, suddenly aware that he was beginning to sweat. "W-wow," was all he managed to get out. "Sorry." Campbell sighed during her pause. "I know that's the last thing I've probably got any right to say, especially since I'm already sorta the crazy chick with a horrible history. No cause for alarm - I'm not gonna peel your skin off and dance in it or anythi--WOW. Who says that?" She raised her palms innocently. "Really sorry. I won't be offended if you wanna pretend the last minute didn't happen. This is your celebration, even if it's not a whole lot for the moment..." "It's all I need," he said. "All I'd want." "A trip to the hospital and then sitting in a sketchy diner with me?" She snickered. "Sounds like the most magical fucking night of your life. He must have knocked you around hard. Yikes." "Alright, well, the hospital I could do without, but the rest of it? Yeah, you might be surprised. If anything, I'm just..." He sighed. "I dunno." "You're just... not nearly as happy as you should be looking right now. C'mon, you just ran a riot on everybody who's anybody in our business! I know how important this is to you - I'm only gonna be sad if you don't relax and enjoy it." "I'm fine. It's just been a long day, you know? Pretty tired, really sore. Plus..." He held up his hands, still splinted and bound. "Just sorta feel like a mess. I dunno, it'll probably pass. I wouldn't worry about it." "Just remember that it's worth the mess - at least, that's how I see it. You're on top now." "Maybe it just hasn't sunk it yet. It's weird, but I always thought that it'd be different. Right now I'm just... like... it's hard to describe. It's like I don't really feel like me. It just feels... it just feels wrong." "Because of who you had to go over?" "No, I mean, I don't think so." In his attempt to dodge the question - a question whose answer would no doubt earn him a lecture - Jared set about shifting the remaining fries on his plate, arranging them in neat rows away from a blob of ketchup. "You know how people win the lottery, and then they go on television all like 'I can't believe it happened to me'? Figure it's kinda like that. Only without the extra three hundred million." "Probably just the painkillers," Amy assured. "And wanting to get home. Still want me to spend the week? It's not a big deal if you need alone time or anything, I'm not gonna push - it's no problem to switch my flight or anything." "I'd really like the company, actually." He looked up from his potato sculpture just long enough to smile. "There are some people I'm gonna have to see, only because they'll kick my ass if I don't, and really I'm in no position to defend myself. Are you cool with that? I don't wanna throw you in front of a bunch of people you've never met unless you're comfortable with it." "I'm fine with that! We'll all probably have a lot in common, since I'm guessing they agree that I'm not up to snuff when it comes to being good enough for you. Just no ex-girlfriends... that gets hard on the head." She shrugged. "And I get to meet whatshisass... Gabe?" "Okay, so, for the record, I'm taking you to meet the people I care about. I lied to a doctor and said we were... you know... that thing. Pretty sure we can relax on the rampant self-deprecation. Besides, that was my gig for a long time, and I still hold the copyright." He tossed a french fry across the table. "So nyah." She was right, and he knew it. These people, his friends – most of them did held the belief that the pair couldn’t be more wrong for each other. He was the noble nice guy, and she was girl your mother warned you about. But things were different now. She had cleaned up her life. She’d been genuinely remorseful about the things she’d done. And moreover she made him happy. Surely they would see that. "They’ll love you, Ames." He needed to believe it. "Trust me." Jared eyed the rim and wiped the sweat away from his eyes. Against the orders of his doctor, and against his own better judgement, he'd snuck out in the middle of the night to the portable basketball hoop in front of the Hansons' place to be alone with his thoughts. Sleeping had been all but impossible since New Jersey. It was bad enough that his body ached from the beating he'd taken in his quest to win the most coveted prize in SCCW, but now his mind was playing tricks on him. Where he should have been happy he felt only remorse, and a growing fear that introducing Amy to a potentially hostile environment could have dire consequences on their relationship. He dribbled twice, and the ping of rubber on concrete echoed through the humid night air. The Hansons had packed up the card and headed to a family cabin in Maine for the week. The Bennetts, the only other neighbors at this end of the street, were in Florida visiting relatives. Why anyone would travel south in the dead of summer was something Jared just couldn't understand. A surge of heat tore through his shoulder as he released the shot. It sailed just wide to the ride, connecting with the backboard and falling to the asphalt by his shirt. He took a moment to slowly rotate his arm before retrieving the ball. He charged the basket, contemplated leaping, but pulled up short and spiked the ball in frustration. "Fuck," he hissed, and ran the back of his hand across his mouth. The neighborhood was quiet, peaceful. There was never a need to worry about staying out too late, or what he might find lurking in the darkness. This wasn’t always the case. He’d moved into the house during a particularly trying time in his life, when the omnipresent threat of a sinister redhead loomed on the horizon like a storm cloud ready to burst. There was a redhead in the shadows, smaller and gentler than the one he once feared. On sore legs she hobbled up silently behind him, wearing a blank tank top and a pair of Hello Kitty pajamas she likely shouldn’t have left the house in. The hand she laid on his shoulder seemed to take the pain away. "Is this a bad time to repeat what the doctor said?" He tucked the ball under his left arm and lowered his head, but he didn't turn. "Don't worry, I know this is stupid," he sighed. "I just couldn't sleep, is all. I'm sorry if I woke you up. Didn't realize I was that loud." Finally he turned, but kept his eyes low. "I'll stop if you think I should." "Is it clearing up the emo any?" Campbell limped slightly, swatting at one arm to murder a stray mosquito while she advanced on him with a kind smile. "I'm told this is a free country, so you can keep going if it's making you feel better... but I should probably get you to stop soon. I woke up and looked around for you, you kinda freaked me out with the disappearing act." "I'm sorry. I didn't mean to worry you or anything." He stepped away, resuming his dribble for a few paces before turning and launching a jumpshot at the basket. It bricked against the rim, hard enough to wobble the backboard. His shoulder ached all the more for it. The ball rolled to a slow stop a few feet from the curb, but neither made a play for it. "Has it been that obvious?" Amy smirked and pinched fingers close together in the air in front of her face. "Liiiiittle bit. Mainly the part where you don't seem at all happy about winning - well, earning - the top prize in the industry you've spent most of your life working in. And wow, clearly you're not gearing down to pursue a career in basketball..." "Yeah, Charley Crisp and I tried to do the 'White Men Can't Jump' thing one weekend and hustle people. Ended up costing me about a grand." He rested his hands on his hips and set about retrieving the ball. "And it's not that I'm unhappy, it's just... I dunno. It's complicated." "I'll give you a minute to realize who you just tried to brush off with 'It's complicated'." She chuckled and made a swipe for the ball. "I'm the queen of 'It's Complicated'. I'm also the Kobe of ginger chicks from Canada." "I'm not convinced, and... something tells me you wouldn't like the explanation." "Something tells me it's up to me to make that decision." He stood in silence for a moment, mulling it over. "You were supposed to win." Their eyes met when he spoke, and his brows lifted just slightly. "I wanted it to be you. I would have thrown that match, if I thought for even a second that you'd let me get away with it. I would have held back and let you tool on me if I didn't think it'd make you upset. It kills me that I'm the one." Amy's stomach knotted up pretty quickly after hearing that. "Hey... no. Not to minimize what you're feeling, but I've already been there. The point of that match was to see who could get the job done in the end, and it was your time. I didn't give it to you and expected the same from you. That would have been unfair. I told you before it even started that either outcome gave me something to smile about." "But it is unfair. You said it yourself, you've been there. But what else was going on then, Ames? I know you're not proud of those days. You've told me as much. Everything that you've had to go through, everything that you've had to get past... I wanted you to win for all those reasons. Because you've known what it's like to be on top when outside the sport everything was going to hell. I wanted you to know what it's like to be on top with all of it behind you. I just..." His voice started to waver, enough that he let his gaze drop. "I wanted to see you succeed, to know what it felt like to be there when things aren't all falling apart." He took a single step back and let the ball fall to the street. "I got in the way of that, and I'm sorry." "I misused and horribly abused that thing." Amy spoke as she finally snatched the basketball away and began to dribble. "I don't deserve to have it back yet. It's in the cards, just not now. What makes you think I'm not more than satisfied just to see someone who does deserve it, someone who's earned it - not to mention someone I care an awful lot about - with that right now? I tainted my reigns, you're not gonna be selfish and foolish like I was... don't let my presence hurt your time with the title." "Please don't say things like that. You're not hurting my time with it, or whatever. Amy, I wouldn't even have this thing if it wasn't for you. I don't mean that in the 'oh we wrestled two days ago' sense, I'm talking big picture. I wouldn't have had the confidence for it before. I wouldn't have wanted it, because I wouldn't have had anyone to share it with." He shrugged, aware of the stiffness in his arm. "Maybe I just suck at explaining it." "If you've got me to share it with, then share it! Let's not be all sad and down on ourselves." She cocked her arms back and fired a shot. To both of their surprise it kissed the backboard and fell through the hoop. "Except about your basketball skills. Seriously... you can't just hold this shit in, Jared. The last day, I've been sitting around wondering if you're quietly resenting me for something, or if there's something truly awful going on that you haven't mentioned... you can't keep this stuff from me, okay? I'm not one of those chicks who's too busy with herself to listen to what you're thinking. Been there, done that... wasn't especially proud of myself for it." "I'm sorry. I didn't mean to make you uncomfortable, or worry, or any of that. I feel so stupid for saying this, but I'm still sorta new at it all. I was just waiting for it to pass, so I wouldn't have to burden you with it. And not for nothing, but easy on the basketball jabs. Bad shoulder, remember? I got skills... sort of. Maybe." He grinned. "Okay, not really." "You're overwhelmed, but try not to be. Everything's great for you right now. I mean, you could use a better girlfriend, but maybe you'll disagree when you see me GO KOBE UP IN THIS!" She limped a bit as she ran, and though her desire was strong, the truth was that without a ladder or a trampoline Amy simply wasn’t going to pull off the spectacular dunk she imagined. She came up about two feet short, the top of the ball barely grazing the bottom of the net. She was blushing a little when she turned. Jared could barely contain a giggling fit. "Wow. That was... uhh... that was something special." He signaled for the ball, and a bounce pass delivered it. "It's done kinda like this." By the time he realized what he was doing, and what a colossal mistake it was, it was too late. He’d already committed, and his hand was bringing the ball down towards the goal. His hand hit the rim, jarring his previously dislocated finger against the metal and forcing his arm back just a bit. When his feet touched the ground he shrank almost immediately into a tight ball, wincing as he stroked at the injuries he’d insisted on testing. "Okay, that was fucking stupid." "Shit!" Amy immediately went to him, very lightly rubbing the offending shoulder. "That's it, I'm getting some ice. This is a mistake..." "It’s fine, it’s fine. Shoulder doesn’t need it yet." He looked up, smiling again through the pain. "Unless it’s for your ego, ‘Ginger Kobe’." "I was warming up! Just checking... uh... the height of the basket. This isn't standard, is it?" She trotted back to the basket, standing under it to better gauge the height. "Looks a foot or so high to me." "Actually, it's shorter than the ten feet it's supposed to be. The kids who live here are only like eight years old. Their dad lowered it so they could reach it easier." He scooped the ball up off a patch of grass on the sidewalk, and tossed it lightly back to Amy. "So let's see it then, your Royal Airness." "You'll regret this! You might be able to beat me at wrestling, but who cares about that? Being like LeBron James will net so much more in the end... and SPEAKING OF NET--" She dribbled twice and started her approach, biting her lip in determination. Jared came up under her when she leapt, using his left shoulder to brace against her hips and edge her upwards, closer to the basket. He couldn't help but wince a bit. His upper body was already a mess, and he certainly hadn't been doing it any favors by beating it further. He steadied her with his right hand, the tips of his fingers brushing against her side. He craned his neck back just far enough to see two tiny hands triumphantly throw the orange sphere down through the hoop. "ESPN will be calling you in no time." He slowly eased her back to the ground, more for her safety than his own. Her arms fell around his neck, and for a moment he held her just inches from the ground, where the tips of her toes could not reach. His body was tender and sore, but the aches he'd feel tomorrow were well worth it. He held her that way for what felt like an eternity, two confused teenagers slow-dancing in the imaginary gym. "I'll bet." She smiled, speaking softly. "So, uh. How sore's that shoulder, again? Seeing as you've been so down, I was trying to figure out ways to cheer you up. Sadly, like every possibility is against doctor's orders." "Well, I guess it's a good thing I've been paying sooo much attention to what he said then." "You didn't have to catch me, y'know. This is probably the first time in my life someone's been willing to... but you didn't have to." "This is how it works, though." He smiled, and brushed his nose against hers. "We're a team, you and I." "Guys - anyone, really - never say things like that." Amy laughed, pecking him on the lips. "You're so coming back to bed with me. I think we both need the rest, and bug bites from hanging outside on a humid night in New England totally aren't sexy." "Rest? And here I was just starting to get my hopes up." "The rest comes after we work up a good reason to, and only if you promise to wear your sling tomorrow." She said. "Well, actually, I probably won't enforce that rule... but get your ass inside." She took him by the hand, careful so as not to disturb the splint, and led him back towards the house. Jared smiled. His conscience was clear, and for the first time in days he was outside his own head, able to finally enjoy the moment.
