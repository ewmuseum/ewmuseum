Unconditional Surrender

28 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/28/09 Backstage, after Temptation: Maryland went off the air... It had been one of the easier nights Seymour Almasy had ever had in wrestling. Five minutes, if that, on camera with Dr. Nigel Kensington. No dark match before the show to get the crowd hyped, no post show main event -- which was especially good, since he didn't think he could really top Desade versus Kennedy. And he'd made...Yevon, it was almost ridiculous to think about how much he'd been paid for the night to do next to nothing. With duffel bag slung over his shoulder, he was ready to head back to the hotel for a good night's sleep. One of the few things he disliked thus far about SCCW (other than locker room stealing redheads) was the fact that the show went off the air at midnight, It was usually around one in the morning when he got back to the hotel, which left precious little time for late night gaming sessions. 'Come on, man, lie down! It's for your own good!' Almasy's eyes turned to the source of the statement, where he found a gaggle of SCCW medical personnel surrounding Phillip Kennedy. Seymour winced as he looked at the Big Stack, covered in blood, trying to resist being put on a gurney with every fiber he had left in his being. Seymour had been there before. More times than he cared to remember, really. He walked away as the medics continued trying to get Kennedy to lie down. From personal experience, Seymour knew that it was only a matter of time before they succeeded. Better to let the other man have his dignity and pride and not watch it happen. As he lugged his bag towards the back entrance of the arena, Seymour recalled the match in his mind. It had probably been the most brutal thing he'd seen in years, and there wasn't a single weapon involved. Just two people with a Hell of a lot of pride and something to prove. It was the sort of match he'd become famous because of, demonstrating his nigh-legendary ability to take punishment and keep coming back. His ability to fly at points in the contest when he should have been shattered. They were skills that he took pride in, but moreso, they were skills he hoped he didn't have to use again. The Mr. Ashe match had gone a long way in confirming that yes, he could still win without taking quite so many chances. The Evoker had worked beyond his wildest dreams. He hadn't expected his new finisher's first test to be against a 350 plus pound man-mountain, but the knee to the temple had dropped Ashe for three. If it could fell Roderick Ashe, it could take down anyone. Seymour had just about made it out of the arena, when the shout of his name stopped him in his tracks. 'Figures,' Seymour groused, under his breath. 'Always when I'm about to leave.' He turned, to come face to face with one of the many employees of the company. 'Card for next week,' the other man said, handing over a freshly printed piece of paper. 'Straight from Doc Kensington. Good luck, Mr. Almasy.' The employee turned and jogged off in the opposite direction, leaving Seymour standing by the door with what was likely to be his assignment for Temptation: West Virginia in his hands. After all, he reasoned, he hadn't been scheduled to compete tonight. It was only logical that he be booked for the following week. Flipping over the card, he started at the bottom and searched for his name. Nothing. 'So much for getting to work a hot opener,' Seymour chuckled. Truthfully, he loved opening up shows, and trying to put on a contest that would be difficult for the rest of the roster to follow. His name value and career pedigree, though, meant that he didn't get to jerk the curtain with the frequency that he would have liked. Seymour continued to scan up the card. Still no 'Seymour Almasy.' 'Hrmm,' he mused. 'Maybe they've got me against Dusk. Kensington didn't seem very happy with me tonight, and I'm sure he'd like for his golden boy to prove to me just how good he is...' At the end of the day, Almasy didn't have anything *against* the Lost Soul, but Dusk didn't quite seem to understand what he'd gotten himself into, in spite of Maloof's constant insistance otherwise. Seymour was positive that Kensington had no clue, if tonight's conversation was any indication. 'Geez, maybe I'm not booked after all,' Seymour murmured. After all, the only thing that was left was the main event slot, and... Oh, look. There he was. 'Why the fuck would Kensington put me in the main event,' Almasy asked aloud, to no one in particular. 'I've been here two weeks. They've got Lance Marshall, surely he'd be better to put in the main event than me.' At least that confirmed it. It would be Seymour Almasy vs. Dusk. He could handle that; Dusk had eliminated him from PRIME's Dual Halo a few years back. Seymour had a long memory, especially when it came to being defeated in the squared circle, or scary cage structure, as the case was. That belief, then, was why Almasy's eyebrow raised at the sight of the words 'Mystery Partner' written next to his name. 'Mystery partner? They've got me in a tag team match, and they don't have the decency to assign me a partner? I don't even KNOW anyone here yet!' 'Mystery partner', as Seymour well knew, generally meant running around backstage trying to convince someone to go to war with you. He would know; he'd been someone's mystery partner lots of times. So much for a relaxing Temptation. He'd have to spend his time talking to people he barely knew to find a partner to take on...who was it that he was taking on again? He looked back down at the paper, across from his name. Three letters stared back at him. 'Oh, for the love of...' Seymour sighed, looked up for several seconds, then looked back down. Nope, still there. The wailing sound of a siren could be heard, prompting Seymour to look back over his shoulder. Phillip Kennedy had been strapped successfully to the gurney, and was being wheeled towards what Seymour could only presume to be an arriving ambulance. Somehow, as Seymour crumpled the piece of paper into a little ball, and launched it across the hallway in anger, he could only see two images in his mind's eye. The first was of Amy Campbell, perched in the middle of his locker room, arrogant expression on her face. The second was of Alexandra Pierce, covered in another man's blood, still standing after a thirty minute war with a competitor who did absolutely everything in his power to put her down for three. With both images firmly in mind, there was really only one thing Seymour could say. '...Fuck.' ------------------------ Often times, Seymour had been asked why he continued to wrestle. Why his career was going into its eighth year, in spite of the bumps and the bruises, the trials and the tribulations, the heartache and the suffering. He always gave the right answers, too. His love for the business, for one. The desire to be a champion, a role model for children. The fact that there was nothing quite like going down to the ring and hearing the roar of thousands of fans, whether they were cheering for you or calling for your head. All of those answers were the truth, of course. But only part of the truth. The real truth, Seymour thought, as he drove his Honda Accord down the street, turning into the parking lot of the Resthaven Cemetery, was made abundantly clear to him each and every time he made this Godforsaken drive. Almasy put the car into park in one of the lined off gravel parking spaces. Reaching into the passenger's seat, he picked up a pair of roses, one red, the other white. And so...it began. The walk that he made weekly when his schedule permitted. A walk of approximately six-hundred forty one steps. He'd made it enough times to have counted and been reasonably sure of that fact. Cemeteries were the sort of place where dreams went to die. Seymour always walked past people on his march that were learning that fact for the very first time. Children dealing with the first death of a relative. Mothers burying sons. Husbands burying wives. The sounds of sobbing rang out over the tombstones, but Seymour continued to walk with almost laser-like focus. His legs moved on auto-pilot, trying not to think too much. He knew that if he thought about it, he'd cry. He usually cried before he even arrived at the headstone he was looking for. There were any number of things that could set him off, but it usually was seeing everyone else around him so abjectly miserable that reminded him of his purpose. Seymour made his sharp left turn at the same place that he always did. He knew that meant about fifty more feet to the end of the aisle before he would find the reason he came. That reason rose above many of the other stones in the area, a sizeable piece of black marble cut to size, and polished to a perfect sheen. He could see his reflection staring back at him whenever he gazed into the headstone's face, something that never failed to make him feel guiltier than he already did. 'Hi, Laura,' Seymour whispered, laying first the red, then the white rose across the top of the headstone. Taking a few steps back, he kneeled on the earth before the headstone, reading the inscription on its face with the same disbelieving expression that he always read it with. LAURA MARIE WINTERS 1981-2005 LOVING WIFE, SISTER, DAUGHTER Seymour's head shook from side to side, back and forth. It wasn't so much disbelief or denial; by now he knew that this was no dream. It was more the realization of just how different his life would have been if she was still alive. He wouldn't be wrestling now, that was for damn sure. Maybe he'd have come back once or twice for big matches, maybe a PTC battle on pay per view. Full time though, never. He would be at home with Laura. Re-married. By now, the two of them would probably have their first child. If a boy, he'd have likely been named Cid, after Seymour's grandfather. If a girl...well, Seymour would have angled for Aerith, Laura would have smacked him, and they'd have settled on Jennifer or something. His body would likely be in a heck of a lot better shape, too. Almasy's time off over the past four years had given him a second lease on a career, but he still hurt a good chunk of the time, particularly after matches. Four years out of the sport by now, he figured, and he'd probably be just about as good as new. If he closed his eyes, Seymour could see the man responsible in his mind's eye, the uncompromising Rich 'The Renegade' Rollins. Arguably one of the best wrestlers in Primetime Central history. The GTT5 Champion. And in brutal honesty, looking back on it, a man Seymour Almasy would have thought would be above the sort of shit that he perpetrated. Seynour stood, slowly, looking down on the name on the tombstone one more time. No, it should NOT have been this way. But it was, and there was nothing that anyone could do about it. ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- The Journal of Seymour Almasy Written February 28, 2009 Back then, I thought what happened to me was an aberration. The product of the twisted mind of Rich Rollins. A black eye on the sport of professional wrestling. Four years later, and I've come to realize that there is no such thing as professional wrestling. Nor is there that buzzword that so many apologists like to use: sports-entertainment. What happens on television nowadays isn't a sport, and unless you're a sadist, it's sure as Hell not entertaining. Rich Rollins fucked my ex-wife, handed her a gun, and pointed her at me. Sadly for Rich, she ended up killing herself. Not that it mattered. When we met in the ring I was frazzled. Enraged. Really in no condition to wrestle, but I fought that son of a bitch until he pinned me. I thought it would never happen again. Rollins was almost executed, a man who lived a few extra years on a technicality before drugs did what the electric chair could not. And then a year or two later, I watched Clinton Sage brand his name into the flesh of Jonathan Rhine's girlfriend on a FUSE broadcast. I realized then that not only was Rich Rollins not an aberration, he was but the first of many. Flash forward to 2008, and Jonathan Rhine's retired from the sport by the Dead Man's Hand, an organization that clutches SCCW in its iron grip. It has signed contracts rendering it virtually immune to retaliation for its atrocities. Things occur on SCCW television that should result in jail time, and yet the Hand moves onwards, mighty, unchallenged. Unchallenged even by me. I suppose many figure that I'm the exact sort of person who can take on the Hand. After all, I've lost everything. I lost the love of my life, and love of this sport. The wrestling world that I once lived in is dead, likely never to return. It was killed by people like Rich Rollins. People like Clinton Sage. And yes, people like Desade. No wonder there are no heroes anymore. Jonathan Rhine was the last of a dying breed. He sits now in enforced retirement, probably wondering what happened to his sport. The Seymour Almasy the world loved is dead now. That Seymour Almasy would have popped Amy Campbell in the mouth two weeks ago for having the audacity to plop herself in the middle of his locker room for the sole purpose of being a jerk. That Seymour Almasy would go into the match with DNA two on one if he had to, hellbent on victory, hellbent on showing both redheads that their methods do not pay. That good triumphs over evil. What a nice fairy tale that was. To think, we all almost believed it for a while. That fairy tale is no more reality than the RPGs that I devour in my spare time like candy. The real world has stated loud and clear that professional wrestling is dead, and that the age of the asshole is here and now. Even Dusk, willing challenger to Desade, is no white hatted hero. I am now little more than a man trying to find joy wherever it exists. Hoping to rekindle the spark that wrestling once brought me. Being able to line up across the ring from another competitor, and being pretty sure that the worst I'd get is a chairshot to the skull. Those days are dead. So congratulations Rich, Clinton, Alexandra. You won. What we once called wrestling or sports entertainment has been remade in your image. Names such as mine will fade into history before long, remembered only by the nostalgic and the brave. Someday, there will be those who remember the Seymour Almasies and Jonathan Rhines of the world. To those men and women, I thank you. To the rest of you...I understand. After all, history is written by the victors.
