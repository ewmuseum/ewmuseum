Four Men; One Winner

29 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/29/07 [Time: 12:13 AM] [Place: Undisclosed Gym] [People: RYUJIN] -The scene opens up in a nearly empty gym. It would be completely empty if not for RYUJIN. As RYUJIN began to run on a treadmill, a man walked into the gym. This man wore an ill-fitting suit made of a rather gaudy gold fabric and carried a beat up looking suitcase.. He also had a rather horrible comb over, as well as a rather large pair of sunglasses on his face. He approached RYUJIN, his smile revealing a few gold teeth throughout his mouth.- Man: Hey kid, got a minute? -RYUJIN scowled before letting out an annoyed sigh.- RYUJIN: I dunno what you’re selling, but I don’t want any. Man: Nah nah kid, ya got it all wrong! My name’s Johnny Gold, wrestling manager extraordinaire! I’m manager to all the stars, ya see? RYUJIN: Stars, huh? Care to name a few? Gold: Er, uhm, well… I managed a big Russian guy named Ryzen. RYUJIN: Never heard of him. Gold: Whatever, that’s not important. The important thing is I want to manager ya. I’ve seen ya in action in FUSE, I know you’re good. RYUJIN: I haven’t had a match in FUSE yet… Gold: Details, details! Look, this offer aint gonna be on the table too long. You can either take it, or leave it. -RYUJIN stops running to look Johnny over. His eyes seem to settle on Gold‘s briefcase.- RYUJIN: What’s in the case? Gold: Oh, this? This is my information file I got on ya. It has the low down on your opponents for the next match! RYUJIN: Really now? And how did you go about getting such information? Gold: It pays to TiVo, kid. Look, why don’t we talk somewhere a little bit more… cozy? [Time: 2:11 AM] [Place: Undisclosed Denny‘s] [People: RYUJIN and Johnny Gold] -The scene fades back in on RYUJIN and Johnny relaxing at a table in Denny‘s. Johnny has a coffee while RYUJIN sips on some water. Johnny puts his briefcase on the table, opening it up and taking out some papers. He closes the case back up and places it on the ground. He hands the papers to RYUJIN.- Gold: Here ya go, kid. This has information on all three guys you’re facing on the next show! -RYUJIN looks the papers over before settling on the first one.- RYUJIN: Lets see, the first file is on James von Doom, simply known now as VON DOOM. He seems to claim to be a lot of things style wise, on top of being 24 years old. His finisher is the Schwein Redline, which is normally set up by a Shining Wizard. The guy seems a tad old to be a rookie. I’m not sure if he’s one of these guys that takes everything handed to him like an ungrateful bastard, or if he actually appreciates what wrestling has done for him. I’ll give him the benefit of a doubt and assume he actually gives a crap about the state of the sport. He may be the biggest threat to me in this match If I‘m gonna beat VON DOOM, I need to avoid his finisher, the Schwein Redline. I also have to avoid his set up move, the Shining Wizard. It’s a move I myself like to use as a finisher at times due to the unpredictability it offers. The opponent very rarely sees it coming and often has little time to react to the move. The best defense of it is to wear down the legs, making it more difficult to execute. Or you could take a chance and try to dodge it, which is way easier said than done. The Schwein is hard to counter, with the Redline version being even harder to break out of. The goal here is that if he tries to apply the wrist clutch at any time, I need to immediately break it. Gold: Ungrateful bastards? You mean rookies in general? RYUJIN: That I do. Gold: No a big fan of the young ones, eh? RYUJIN: For the most part? No. On rare occasions, you’ll find some who actually care about the sport’s history; Who give their elders respect and respect the sport as it’s meant to be respected. But those are rare cases. I don’t trust Bridden to be the exception. If he proves different, good for him. If not, he’ll be the first I crush on the long road of bringing respect and honor back to wrestling. He hasn’t had to earn jack yet, but he’s here in FUSE despite that. I doubt he even realizes what a great opportunity he has. He’s ungrateful, just like the rest of today’s youth. -RYUJIN looks over the second piece of paper.- Jaiden Bridden, 19 years old. He’s a rookie in every sense of the word and has yet to face a real challenge. He wrestles a very high risk style. That being said, he’s also a bit undersized. This is gonna be a rough introduction into the real world of wrestling for the kid. Wrestling school and the big leagues are two completely different things. I’m not gonna scold him for screwing up a crucifix takedown. I’m gonna beat the shit outta him. No matter how much you prepare yourself mentally for your first match, there’s no way to prepare for the physicality that’s involved. Bridden’s finisher is something of a modified tornado DDT from the inverted facelock position. He has no official set up for it. He also has an alternate finisher that sees him basically drive his knees into the opponent’s back off of a sunset flip. The key to escaping the first move is to stay the hell away from the turnbuckles as much as I can. The second one… Well, I do have a decent weight advantage on Bridden. He’s gonna have to make sure he gets good momentum going if he’s gonna nail me with that. -RYUJIN picks up the third file, looking it over. After flipping the paper over and back a few times, RYUJIN glares at Johnny.- RYUJIN: There’s no damn information on Raymond Pierce. This is just a blank piece of paper with his name on the top! Gold: Eheh, about that… See, I couldn’t dig up any old matches and the FUSE website doesn’t have anything about him. RYUJIN: Fine, whatever. I doubt he’ll introduce a dynamic to this match that I won’t be able to handle. It does bother me that I know nothing of his past experience or even a finishing move. It makes it hard to prepare for a man when even basic facts like that are missing. That’s the life of a wrestler, however. Some nights, you get thrown into the unknown. The best of the best can adapt and overcome. I’ll show I’m apart of that group. -RYUJIN tosses the Pierce file aside, causing Johnny to scramble to get it. As he does, RYUJIN has already put the other two files on the table.- Gold: I dunno, kid. VON DOOM seems to have quite an arsenal at his disposal. RYUJIN: That might be the case. But it’s not like he can expect to hit each and every one of those moves. Not to mention the penalty for missing some of the more ridiculous ones he seems to like to pull off. I’ve seen the guy wrestle in Japan before. I haven’t gotten a good enough look at him to really dissect what he really is capable of or who he really is. I’ve seen things in magazines and read articles online. The main point I understand about VON DOOM is that I can’t trust him. Gold: Well, what about Pierce? RYUJIN: I don’t have any reason to believe he can keep up with the three of us. When you’ve been in the sport awhile, you tend to pick up tricks that help out during the match. You learn counter moves younger guys either have yet to see or have rarely seen. You learn how to take advantage of situations, such as injured body parts and inexperienced referees. These types of things can be taught, but can only be mastered with experience. While I’ve been wrestling for 8 years, I can’t be sure how long VON DOOM or Pierce have been around. But I do know Bridden’s a rook, and I still don’t think highly of Pierce. I think the best strategy is to focus on VON DOOM, going after Bridden if I can get VON DOOM out of the picture. Gold: And Pierce? RYUJIN: I doubt he lasts 5 minutes. I still have some training and strategizing to do. I’m outta here. -RYUJIN takes his leave from the table. Johnny is quick to pursue, waving a contract wildly while yelling for RYUJIN to sign the dotted line to take advantage of his services.-
