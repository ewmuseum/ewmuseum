Date: 6/18-6/23 June 12, 2001 The clouds above Jefferson County looked like campfire embers, dumping sheets of rain as sudden flashes scarred the sky. Her Chucks were tattered and waterlogged, causing them to smack against the gravel as she traveled down westbound Rodney Road. The forest scenery was static. Every step was taking her further away from the broken home and that drunken bastard she had the misfortune of calling dad and the trailer park and the belt he cracked over her thighs whenever he was feeling feisty. Her porcelain skin had long since begun to chaff. The rain was doing her no favors. She’d stolen the camouflage pants from the Salvation Army, and while she wasn’t proud of that, they at least stopped the mosquito bites in the mug of June. Fifteen going on broken. A little girl far away from Kansas. She started as nothing more than a dot on the horizon, but the more ground his broken down Speedster gained, the more he focused on her. Neither of them flinched through the thunderclaps. Once he drew close, he eased up on the accelerator. A greasy mop of amber hair sagged over her eyes. She was just a small little thing. Smoke sputtered from the exhaust as the engine hiccupped and shook. He swung his leg over the side of the bike, walking it along the lip of the road as he tried not to get ahead of her. He was staring and she noticed. She couldn’t make him out save for small details in her periphery. "Where you headed?" He smelled of cigarettes and black licorice. She turned her head to face him. His cheeks were smattered with oil, a long head of dark hair falling to the shoulders of his leather jacket. He looked like every bad boy she’d ever seen, but his hazel eyes were different, comforting. Bubbles floated up her spine. She couldn’t look away. "Nowhere." He smirked at her stammer, causing her apple cheeks to flush pink. She pulled her hood over her head, trying her damnedest to hide. "Just...just...nowhere." "Don’t look like nowhere." "How you figure?" He lingered behind without saying a word. Her lips were buzzing. "Ain’t you a bit young to be riding a bike like that?" His smirk grew with every question she asked. She reminded him of his mother for all the right reasons. "Yeah. Reckon I am. Gonna report me to the law?" The storm rolled past as they walked side by side, the sun smearing the clouds in crayon before petering through. Soon, the humidity would be smothering. "Need a ride?" She drew her chipped nails over her cheeks as she pretended to think about it. "New Orleans?" "Sure. Can get you there. Not straight away, but I can get you there." She wanted to believe her raw feet were the reason she was so willing to take him up on his offer, but the fluttering in her stomach made that notion foolish. She didn’t know how old he was. She wondered if she looked attractive. She was conscious about her lack of make-up. He pushed the kickstand down with the heel of his boot before lending her a hand. "One thing, though." His hand was rough. She didn’t mind. "What?" "Take off your shoes." She furrowed her brow. "And why would I do that?" "My bike. My rules." She tried to look menacing, but her skin was hot and her lips were curved in a constant grin. She didn’t care if he saw it anymore. If his eyes caught hers, every trick she had would come unwound. She reached down with her free hand, untying the knots on her shoes before peeling them off. He motioned for her to give them to him once she did. The laces were soggy. He loosely tied them off on his handlebar and boosted her onto the seat. "Keep your feet on the side and away from the muffler and you’ll be fine." "What should I do with my hands?"
