Aches and Pains

23 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07.23.07 Months ago John had realized his love for the wrestling business. It had gotten into his blood while out of the game of baseball and had replaced the latter as his passion. No matter how much he loved getting in the ring and putting on amazing performances for the FUSE faithful, there was one thing about baseball he could honestly say he missed. You didn’t wake up in excruciating pain every week. Every once in a while he might feel something in his pitching shoulder, but never did he wake up with a sore neck, bruises all over his body and stiffness in his joints. All of that came with the "honor" of being a professional wrestler. July 21st, 2007 11:31 A.M. CST "I’ve got to go Mom. I’m trying to get out of the house." John said into his phone. He walked around his home, holding the cordless phone to his ear. He was clad in a pair of khaki cargo shorts and a collared shirt. His hair was neatly spiked up and supported the weight of a pair of Oakley sunglasses that sat atop his head. "I’m going to see the doctor. No, I’m fine mother. It’s just a routine check-up. It has nothing to do with wrestling." He tried to reassure her. She had always been afraid he was going to seriously injure himself in the ring, so he had to hide the fact that was because of FUSE. Anything John had tweaked or sprained or pulled in previous matches, he just worked through. But after his match with Tanaka, he knew something was wrong. It wasn’t unusual to hurt the rest of the night following a match, but when he woke up nearly two weeks after the fact with the same shooting pain in his neck he knew it was time to get looked at. "Alright Mom. I’m hanging up the phone now. I’ll call you later. Bye." He said. A push of a button hung the phone up and John was headed out the door. Hopefully the doctor wouldn’t give him any bad news. July 21st, 2007 12:18 P.M. CST "What the fuck is taking so long?" John muttered to himself. He laid on the table and stared at the ceiling. Twenty minutes ago the nurse had walked out, smiling. This is what sucked about the doctor’s office. All the shots, pokes and prods didn’t amount to the time wasted waiting to see him. Finally the door opened and a taller, older man entered. "Good to see you John." He said. John extended his hand and had it accepted. The doctor opened the folder and began looking through it. John winced in pain a bit as he turned his head to look at the doc. After another two or three minutes he was done reading. "So here for a follow-up on that pain in your neck?" He asked. John pointed to his neck, nodded and winced once more. The doctor stood and approached John. He gently moves John’s hand out of the way and begins to feel his neck. Even with the soft touch of the doctor, John winced as he negotiated every inch of the wrestler’s neck. "It’s killing me today for some reason." John stated. The doctor watched as John winced again and removed his hands from the back of his neck. John cleared his throat and watched as the doctor grabbed a large yellow envelope from the desk a few feet away. He pulled out a few transparent pictures; x-rays and an MRI. "I’ve studied these things for hours last night and can’t see anything broken, chipped or anything. Best thing I can tell right now is that you’ve suffered a stinger worse than I’ve ever seen." The doc said. A stinger? Nearly two weeks after the pain started? Unheard of. John couldn’t argue. He hadn’t gone through ten years of medical school. John sighed and accepted the early diagnosis as the doctor stuck the x-rays up onto the light board and examined them once again. "So, what does this stinger mean?" John inquired. The doctor began babbling on in medical terms, lasting for nearly a minute. John sat there and listened intently, trying to grasp onto the jist of things, but couldn’t. When the doctor finished, John chuckled and asked for an English version. "I’ve never seen a stinger like this. That kick jarred your neck in numerous directions, which could explain its lingering effects. You’re going to experience shooting pain and numbness in your limbs, but like any other stinger it won’t be serious." He said. John sighed and looked to the floor. "So what does this mean for my in-ring performing?" John asked. The doc continued to look over the images before turning back to John. "Ideally you’d take a break until the pain ceased." He answered. John began to slowly shake his head, refusing the advice. "That can’t happen. I’ve already booked this week and need to get back on top." John said. The doctor sighed as he sat on the stool at the desk and looked to John. John grinned slightly as if to say "Come on." The doctor looked to the floor and began tapping his pen on the desk before looking back up. "I knew you were going to say that. If you keep getting into the ring with this injury the pain is going to stay twice as long. Stay out of the ring and it’ll be uninhibited, untouched in its healing process." He stated. John still shook his head. There was no way John was staying out of the ring. He was a mainstay in FUSE. There’s no way he could take time off. The company needed him, the product required him. At least that’s what he told himself. In reality, he didn’t want to be completely replaced. There were too many young guys waiting to pounce on John’s spot at the top. "Isn’t there anything you can do so I can work through this?" John asked. The doc sighed deeply and then looked at John’s charts and stats before reaching for a small pad of paper across the desk. He scribbled a few lines on the pad of paper before tearing it off and motioning to hand it to John. As John reached to grab it, the doctor pulled back and looked to his patient sternly. "Now this is serious. You need to use this responsibly. Follow the directions. If the pain doesn’t subside, come back and see me. I’ll be forced to take you out of the ring. You got me John?" He stated. John nodded as he took the prescription from the doctor’s hands. The doctor stood and took the medical images from the wall, sliding them back into his folder. John didn’t look at the piece of paper yet, he wasn’t that interested. Probably just some of the extra strength I.B. Profen like he had gotten for his concussion a few months ago. The doctor shook John’s hand and exited the room, followed by his patient. It wasn’t until john stepped out the front door and onto the mid-day Mazon street that he looked at the prescription. Vicodin 750 mg Refill x 3 by 9-21-07 Charles Comfort, M.D That was some strong stuff.
