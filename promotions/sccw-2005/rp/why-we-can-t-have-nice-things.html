Why We Can't Have Nice Things

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10 Years Trenton, New Jersey March 15, 1999 "Dammit, Jared. Where the fuck are you?" Gabriel Simon was less than pleased. Though he’d only been in the business himself for a little over a year, he had already established a pre-match routine which he followed week-in and week-out, never deviating unless absolutely necessary. Searching the backstage area for an obnoxious rookie named Jared Sykes was certainly not part of it. "I swear to God, I’m gonna’ kill him in his sleep tonight." In truth Gabriel never really cared for his younger colleague. The two had attended the same high school, and though a year apart it seemed to Gabriel as though he carried the unfortunate burden of having to deal with Jared at every conceivable opportunity. The two were on the same wrestling team, engaged in some of the same activities, and even shared some of the same friends, but there was something about Jared that never sat right with Gabriel. Things only got worse a year ago when on a cool February afternoon Jared Sykes had walked into Stracker’s School of Wrestling, a bag slung over his shoulder and grinning like an idiot. Now, as he rounded a corner and passed a group of people running down line items on a clipboard, Gabriel dreamed of new and exciting ways to torment his travel partner. If only he could find him. Gabriel exhaled a frustrated sigh, clenched his teeth, and pushed past a stack of lighting equipment that partially obstructed the hall, determined to not only find but punish his target. For the past ten minutes Jared had been going from person to person looking for anyone from whom he might borrow a cell phone. His search had taken him away from the production staff, many of whom either did not have them or were not permitted to carry them, and closer towards the arena’s loading zone. It was there that he was finally able to get his hands on one, borrowed from a man whose nametag identified him only as ‘Bill’. "So, it’s cool with you if I use this for like five minutes?" "Don’t see why not," Bill shrugged, handing over the phone. "I get free nights and weekends, so it’s not like it’ll cost me anything." "Bill, you’re a life-saver." As he was dressed to compete, the only pocket Jared had was on the left breast of his tee shirt, and it was from there that he produced a torn photograph. Turning it over revealed numbers written in blue ink, and as he started to punch the numbers into the phone’s keypad his pulse quickened, his breath shortened, and a thin veil of sweat formed on his brow. "Alright," he whispered to himself, "here goes nothing." For a moment his thumb hovered over the button labeled ‘call’, unable or unwilling to press it. It was in that moment that Gabriel spotted him and, doing his best to contain his obvious frustration, yelled across the loading zone. "Hey, Sykes, what the hell?!" Immediately Jared’s thumb slid over a half-inch and pressed the red ‘end’ button. Quickly he stuffed the photo back in his pocket, and casually tossed the phone back to its owner as he turned to face the approaching Gabriel Simon. "Thanks anyway, Bill. Guess I won’t be needin’ it." Bill shrugged as he replaced the phone in his pocket and stepped away, leaving both wrestlers alone. "Do you have any idea how long I’ve been looking for you?" Gabriel continued. "You’re up in, like, 2 minutes." "Sorry, I just had to… never mind." "Had to what? Make us look like asses for bringing the new guy who can’t make his call time in his first real match?" "Yeah, sorry." Gabriel shook his head and scoffed. "Whatever. Just follow me," he turned and gestured in the direction that he’d come from. "Was it important, because Darren’s probably going to murder you?" Jared’s hand reflexively went to his breast pocket, lightly brushing against the picture it contained. "Yeah," he said, his voice subdued, "yeah it really was." * * * * * * * * * * New York City June 13, 2009 "Dude, this so isn’t my style. For one, it’s earth tones. I don’t do earth tones. Second, seriously, who wears a sweater in June?" "People who’re lucky to still have an agent after all the shit they’ve pulled." "Damn it, Larry." In many ways Jared Sykes was very much an overgrown child, a fact that was at the forefront of his mind as he stood in the anteroom of a small conference center as his agent inspected and adjusted his outfit. "Anything else, mom?" he asked sarcastically, stuffing his hands into his pockets. "Maybe you’d like to lick your hand and pat down my hair?" "Funny," Larry’s delivery was deadpan, a skill that he’d picked up and subsequently been forced to master after a rather trying year of dealing with this particular client. "And can you remind me again why we’re doing this in New York?" "Because New York is, well, New York. More exposure, higher profile, things like that." "But they’re here to talk to a wrestler. That’s, like, a half-step above Major League Soccer on the sports ladder." Jared managed to pull himself away from Larry just long enough to glance into the room where a dozen or so reporters were waiting for his arrival. "Besides, have you seen these people? It’s like the east coast chapter of ‘Hi, I Live In My Mother’s Basement.’ And don’t even get me started on the whole New York-Boston thing." "What’s the New York-Boston thing?" Jared’s expression was one of stunned disbelief. "You’re a sports agent, and you don’t know why Boston guys do press in Boston, and New York guys do press in New York?" "First of all, I’m in entertainment, which doesn’t always mean sports. Second, should I?" The Black Sheep sighed, shaking his head as he paced a small circle around his agent. Ultimately he decided that the issue was not worth pursuing. "You’re gonna’ get me killed, Larry. I swear to God. Just… just tell me you’re not sending me out there in a Yankees hat, and we’ll be good to go." "Nope, no hat, just a few note cards." "Note cards? For what?" "Well, no offense," Larry said, reaching into his briefcase and retrieving a stack of 3x5" note cards. "We’re probably better off if you stick to a script here. I figure folks will want to know why you’re back in Sin City after everything that happened last year, plus there’s been some talk about why you were let go from PRIME. If we go with a script then you can lay that all out on your terms." Jared scowled as he snatched the stack of cards from Larry’s hand. His expression worsened as he thumbed through them, thoroughly disgusted at what was written. "This… it… Larry, this doesn’t sound anything like me." "No?" "No. ‘Due to unfortunate circumstances, I was required to terminate my agreement with Lisa Tyler’ and blah, blah, blah. I’d never say that." "Well, perhaps it’s a little more professional than you’re used to, but it wouldn’t hurt to show people that side of you, you know? We have an opportunity to show the world that you’re multidimensional." "But you left out all the important stuff," Jared protested. "There’s nothing in here about the things people might actually want to know. Nothing about how I ‘Taylor Hicks’ed’ my way into a title shot, nothing about…" Larry held up a hand, cutting him off. "I’m sorry, how you what?" "Taylor Hicks? American Idol?" "Like, the singing show? Did he win this year?" Jared’s hands fell to his sides in defeat. Though he’d been working with Larry for the last year, the one thing that still seemed to elude him was his agent’s inability to grasp or comprehend popular culture. "No, Larry. He won a few years ago. He was the guy that ended up winning because somehow the real best contestant was eliminated a few weeks before the final. He was a likable-but-not-so-awesome ‘lesser of two evils’. Looked sorta’ like a fat George Clooney. Also, he wasn’t Catherine McPhee, which is I think what put him over." "Ah, I think I follow you now. Still, it’s a strange reference." "Yeah, well, I have a lot of free time to kill. Stuff like this just wanders into my head." "I’d have hoped you’d spend that free time getting in shape," Larry smiled, making grandiose gestures with his arms. "Maybe training for your triumphant return?" "Only the right arm, baby," Jared blurted out, not quite realizing what he said. It quickly sunk in, confirmed by the uneasy expression on his agent’s face. "You know, uh, I think it’s probably a good idea if we just pretend I never said that." "Yeah." "Yeah." The two men stood in awkward silence for a moment, neither wanting to look at the other. Their impasse was broken by footsteps and a third voice. "I miss anything interesting?" Mervin Humperdink stepped into the anteroom carrying his cell phone in one hand and a half-empty bottle of Sprite in the other. He fiddled with his Blackberry for a moment, waiting for an answer, and when one didn’t come he glanced up at the two men before him. "Really? You don’t say," he joked, sliding his phone into his pocket. A quick look at Larry was all he needed to identify the cause of the situation. "Alright, what’d he say this time?" "Nothing that needs repeating," came Larry’s response. "Who, uh, who was that that called?" now it was Jared’s turn to rejoin the conversation. "Oh, it was Darren back in Boston. He says he tried to get in touch with you, but you’re phone was off, or something." "What’d he want?" "I’m supposed to tell you that Calvin changed up her work schedule there. He says that if you want to keep going there on days she’s not working that you’ll need to change it up, too. Also, I’m supposed to call you ‘a pussy’, and say that, and remember I’m quoting here, ‘if you had any balls you wouldn’t still be ducking her after 6 weeks’." Jared cast his nerdy friend an irritated glance, but Mervin just smiled back at him. "Anything else, hotshot?" "Nope. That was it, ‘pussy’." Mervin couldn’t help but laugh. "Wow, this is fun. I should talk to Darren more often." "I really wish you wouldn’t." "And I really wish you’d get in there and talk to all those nice people who’ve come all this way to see you." Larry stepped behind his charge, putting both hands against his back and shoving him towards the door. "Alright, Larry. Simmer down," Jared replied, digging his heels into the carpet, "I get it. I’m going. Make sure you take notes, too. This is going to be epic." Professional wrestling’s black sheep cast a mischievous glance over his shoulder as he let his grip loosen, dropping the stacks of cards on the floor before stepping in to face the gaggle of reporters waiting there. Larry looked at his handiwork, now strewn about the carpet. "Mervin?" he said, reluctantly leaning down to clean up the mess Jared had made. "Yeah?" "I think we’re fucked." "Oh, totally," the nerd replied, in between sips of his beverage, "Should be good for a laugh, though." "This… this is why we can’t have nice things." * * * * * * * * * * Cambridge, Massachusetts June 13, 2009 – 20 minutes ago "This is why we can’t have nice things, Darren." "Say again?" "So just when were you planning on telling me?" Justine Calvin was not prone to outbursts, but on this day something had been brought to her attention that she refused to let slide. She stood opposite her former trainer and current employer, Darren Stracker, leaning on his desk and trying very hard to control herself. "Telling you what?" "C’mon, D’. I know he’s been coming here. One of the new kids, Vinnie something, he’s been showing everybody a photo that he took on his cell phone where they’re standing side by side all buddy-buddy." Darren simply smiled as he leaned back in his chair, clasping his hands behind his head. "You know, I had a feeling you’d find out eventually, though I’d really hoped he’d man-up and come see you himself. He’s such a candy-ass." Darren nodded towards one of the empty chairs. "Have a seat, Cal’." She pushed away from the desk and made herself comfortable in the nearest chair she could find. "How often does he come in? Is it often?" She was trying hard, very hard, not to look hurt. Unfortunately, she was failing. "He used to stop by every now and again, usually when something was wrong and he needed advice. A little over a month ago he came in, and we talked about some dream he had that’d really messed him up." Darren paused, trying to determine how to proceed. "You were actually here that day." "Unbelievable," she turned away, and a moment Darren thought he could see a slight quiver in her bottom lip. "I thought he was going to do something, because when I told him you’d started working here he asked for your schedule." "How often since then?" "Once or twice a week he shows up, apparently on days you’re not around. If you’d like, I can smack him around the next time I see him. You know, for old time’s sake." His comment made her smile, even if only for a few seconds. "No, you don’t have to do that." An idea struck him like a bolt from the blue, and an impish grin crept onto his face. "I think I have an idea," he said, reaching for his own cell phone. "Maybe you can smack him around a little." * * * * * * * * * * New York City June 13, 2009 – One hour later '...a 'chaos storm', he calls it.' The session had started strong. Jared was awake, alert, and ready to answer whatever questions the group had for him, even though the idea of going off-script had caused his agent's acid reflux to flare up. He’d answered questions about the Infinite Gauntlet, about his firing from PRIME, and his subsequent rehire by Sin City. He welcomed questions about his giving up a shot at the Universal Championship, and had even coined the term "Patterson’ed" in reference to he and Charley Crisp no longer having the Strength In Numbers Championship. That was an hour ago. Since then Jared had become zoned-out, lethargic, and had spent most of his time daydreaming about what he'd say or do if he ever ran into (or had the balls to confront) Justine Calvin at Darren Stracker's place. It made for some colorful responses to the questions he'd been asked. 'I, uh, I knew a guy named Chaos once. He had a squirrel, a pet squirrel named Fresus. Is it going to be like that?' Confused looks were exchanged among the crowd. This was, quite possibly, the furthest thing from the original question's intent. A few heads turned at the sound of a light thud, the result of Larry Spiderman banging his head into the walls of the hallway. 'Is it going to rain squirrels? Like, a bushy-tailed hurricane of nuts and teeth? That'd be pretty cool.' For a moment the room was silent; the collection of journalists stymied by the nonsensical ramblings of their guest. The silence brought Jared back from his daydream, and he was suddenly overcome by a wave of embarrassment. 'Oh, guess not, huh?' he said, screwing up his face and scanning the room. 'Okay, uh, anyone else?' A flurry of eager hands went up, each one hoping that this question would get a better response than the last few. 'How about,' he paused, surveying the sea of journalists with his eyes before settling on a small fellow with a drawn face and bushy hair. 'You, the… um… scared, gray, alien wearing a bicycle helmet.' A dozen heads turned in unison as the poor individual that Jared had singled out reluctantly stood up to ask their question. The Black Sheep craned his neck to get a better look, and once again was struck by an overwhelming sense of regret. 'Oh,' he said, his voice falling, 'Sorry about that, ma'am.' The woman stood, remarkably composed for someone who had just been insulted, and asked her question. "Mr. Sykes," she started, her voice brimming with derision, "do you honestly think that you’ll be able to get through Lance Marshall, a world champion many times over? After all, you’re only meeting to date didn’t go in your favor, if I remember correctly." Articulate for an alien, he thought. He leaned forward on the table, resting his head in his right hand. Instinctively, reflexively, his index finger found its way to a scar that lay just below his hairline. His finger slowly caressed the mark left on his skin, a keepsake from the match she’d referenced. He opened his mouth to answer, but stopped himself. He was never big on predictions, especially in a company like this with so many variables at play. Regardless of the outcome, he was certain of only one thing. It would hurt like hell. * * * * * * * * * * Trenton, New Jersey March 15, 1999 – 4 hours later Gabriel Simon was starting to feel a little guilty. He’d felt justified at the arena, having been forced to try and corral his rookie traveling partner, but ever since they’d got back to the hotel he watched as Jared Sykes sat silently at a small circular table in the room they shared. No sooner did they arrive at the hotel did Jared take up residence in the corner of the room, bringing the room’s only phone with him. Gabriel had watched as Jared pulled a partial photograph out of his wallet, setting it down on the table next to the phone. That was over an hour ago. "Hey, Jared?" Gabriel said, turning down the volume on the television. "Yeah?" Jared’s reply was sedate. "You alright?" "Sure." Gabriel turned his attention back to the television, where Alton Brown was occupied making biscuits with his grandmother. His attention was only held for a moment, as a growing sense of moral obligation started to take over. Gabriel powered down the television, swung his legs over the side of the bed, and for the first time really looked at his younger colleague. He’d only ever known Jared as something of a boisterous fool, someone whose life ambition seemed to be innately tied to how many people he could make laugh. Depression was not an emotion he wore particularly well. "I’m serious. I’m not trying to be a dick here. Is something up?" "It’s nothing." "C’mon, honestly. This is the second time tonight that I’ve seen you praying over the phone. You had one earlier when I found you in the loading dock, and you’ve been staring at this one ever since we got here." "Nah, you’ve got enough info on me already," Jared turned his head slightly, forcing a half-grin. "Can’t give you anything else." It was time to try a different approach. "Okay. That’s fair. I know that you and I never really got along, but seriously man, I don’t think I’ve ever seen you like this. It’s kind of creeping me out." "Sorry." Gabriel thought about it for a minute, considering how best to deal with the situation. He discreetly craned his neck ever so slightly, and for the first time was able to get a glimpse of the photo, though he couldn’t make out who was in it. "Who’s in the picture?" Jared slouched in his chair, swept the photo into his hand, and presented it to his roommate without a word. "Oh." Gabriel immediately recognized the people in the photo. One was Jared, and the other was a girl he’d seen at Stracker’s. The realization hit like a truck. "Oh. This is the girl from Darren’s place." "Justine." "So are you two, like, you know…?" "Nope." Gabriel studied the picture for a moment, briefly turning it over to reveal the number written on the back. He nodded silently and handed the photo back to Jared. "So what’s the issue? Call her up." Jared furrowed his brow, his expression a tribute to confusion. Gabriel stood up, walked to the table Jared had made his own, and sat down opposite his roommate. "Okay, Gabe, now you’re creeping me out." "Are you going to pick up the phone, or not?" "It’s not that simple. I’m, well… I’m fucking terrible at this." "So I gathered. I’ll walk you through it. We’ll go Cyranno de Bergerac on this one." It was all incredibly overwhelming. As if dealing with the possibility of confessing his affection for a close friend wasn’t difficult enough, Jared now had to contend with a seemingly sudden personality shift from someone who had, until minutes ago, no tolerance for him whatsoever. It was more than he was equipped to handle. "I’m beyond confused right now. Shouldn’t you be trying to throw me out the goddamned window?" "I know, right?" Gabriel chuckled, "I’m sorry, I know I can be a little harsh." "A little?" "Again, fair enough. You’re right, though. I guess I never really gave you much of a chance here, did I? I think I got stuck on the idea of you having always been a giant pain in the ass back in high school." "Uh… thanks?" "Did I mention I’m bad at apologies? I’m serious, though. Let’s fucking do this. Pick up the phone, punch in the numbers, and rock this out." "Yeah?" "Yup." It was a new kind of panic that surged through Jared. Earlier that evening, at the arena, he half-expected to be interrupted. It was for that reason why he didn’t put up much of a fight when Gabriel came to collect him. He wanted to make the call, wanted it desperately, but at the same time it terrified him. Always being a source of entertainment meant he never had to really open himself up to anyone about anything. He closed his eyes, took a deep breath, and placed his right hand on the receiver of the phone. "You can do this. All you have to do is say ‘Hello’, and the rest gets easier." Gabriel’s words were oddly reassuring. Suddenly none of it mattered. He didn’t care that it was late; Justine would be awake. He didn’t care that he was putting himself out there; he knew she’d reciprocate. They were too close for it to be any other way. He pulled the phone out of its cradle, steadied his nerves, and started dialing. He tried to pace his breathing while he waited for her to pick up. "Anything?" Gabriel whispered, and in response Jared simply nodded a negative. A moment later, a sharp inhale and wide eyes were all the visual cues Gabriel needed to know that someone had picked up. What he didn’t expect was for Jared to hang up immediately thereafter. "What? What just happened?" Jared slumped back in his chair, pressing his palms into his eyes. He didn’t answer at first, since saying it aloud would require him to acknowledge what had happened. "Jared? You okay? Did she answer?" "No… Some guy did." * * * * * * * * * * Somerville, Massachusetts March 15, 1999 "Hello? Hello, is anyone there?" he asked into the phone, but did not receive an answer. Believing the line dead, he pressed the ‘off’ button on the cell phone and replaced it on the coffee table. "Hey, Dan, was that my phone?" Justine Calvin asked from the next room over. "Yeah. Don’t know who it was though, they didn’t say anything." "Weird. Probably a wrong number." She stepped into living room carrying two bottles of water. The first she offered to her guest, and the second she kept for herself as she took a seat on the floor among some partially unpacked cardboard boxes. "I just want to thank you again," she said, unscrewing the cap from her bottle. "You know, for helping me move, and all." "Not a problem," Daniel Calvin said, taking a drink from his own bottle. "I have to watch out for my little sister, after all."
