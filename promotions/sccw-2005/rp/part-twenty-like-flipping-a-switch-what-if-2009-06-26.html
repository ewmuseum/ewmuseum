Part Twenty: Like Flipping A Switch (What If?)

26 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/26/09 If ever there was a more perfect day for a wedding, the folks attending the wedding of Lance and Alanna Marshall were hard pressed to think of one. The weather, which had been promising rain all week, had miraculously cleared into a day of clear blue skies and bright sunshine. The grounds on which the ceremony was to take place had been carefully tended and groomed; the seating arranged on the grass in perfect, neat little rows. Nobody was fighting or causing a scene, the relations were all on their best behavior and everything was running like clockwork. Lance Marshall didn’t care about any of that. As he gazed at the ivory clad goddess slowly making her way down the aisle, her father standing proudly by her side, his world began and ended in the eyes of his soon to be wife, Alanna. He had already known her to be the most beautiful woman in existence, the woman he wanted to spend the rest of his life with. Now, though, he could barely keep his breath. She looked, Lance thought, like something out of a fairy tale. A princess. "Who gives this woman to be wed?" asked the preacher. "I do, with all my heart," replied Alanna’s father. Lance took Alanna’s hand in his, gazing deeply into her eyes. He mouthed "I love you so much" at her, a gesture which she returned in kind. As the two turned to face the preacher for the formal ceremony, Lance felt sure that this was the woman he would grow old with. The woman he would have children with. The woman he would die with. "I’d be careful what you wish for, honey." The voice is soft and female, coming from some way in back of the happy couple. If they’re at all aware of what’s been said, they show no sign of it. Indeed, none of the assembled guests seem to be aware of anything beyond the preacher and the happy couple. Which is just how the voice’s owner likes it. "True love. Doesn’t it just warm your heart…or turn your stomach." It’s hard to shake the image of icicles literally hanging off of those last four words. "Such a shame it’s not going to last…well, not this time, anyway." She stands…well, probably at six feet or higher but at a height that most folks would just instinctively label as tall. The lanky, curvy figure is the first thing most tend to note about our mystery guest. The second is the red hair spilling out from beneath her top hat, a shade of red close to the color of blood. As our mystery woman walks languidly away from the wedding, we’re afforded an opportunity to study her form in motion. She’s wearing clothes that, not to put too fine a point on it, are rather unsuited for this occasion. The top hat mentioned earlier caps off a snug, tightly fitted tuxedo and tails which look not so much tailored to fit as painted on. They lead down to a pair of fishnet stockings tucked inside a pair of thigh high black leather boots. The effect is somewhere between dominatrix and circus master. "Guess we know where your mind is. Eyes up here, by the way." Her eyes…her eyes are a dazzling shade of blue, the kind made to either ensnare or bewitch men’s souls. "How poetic. But can we get back to the point, please?" My apologies. "See, I’ve been to this moment a million times or more. The brave knight finally getting his beautiful princess. And sometimes, it works. Lance and Alanna get married, they have a blowout reception, everybody lives happily ever after." "But sometimes, it doesn’t. Lance finds out Alanna slept with one of his brothers. He dies before the wedding. She dies before the wedding. One of her uncles ends up making a drunken mess of himself. You see, folks like to imagine that they’re the leads in their own perfect little movie, that nothing will ever go wrong simply because they wish it to be so. And then G-d or Allah or Buddah or Shiva or Yahweh or whatever the hell you want to call it comes along…and shows them that life just isn’t that simple. That with the slightest little event, everything you thought you knew can change forever." Our mystery woman takes a small sip from the glass of champagne that has somehow materialized in her hand. "It’s not a party without champagne, is it? Now, just sit back and relax. My name is Destine…and I have a story to tell you." The only thing Lance Marshall remembers clearly about the day is the sound of metal impacting on metal, the groaning and tearing noise as the car crashed into their limousine. It had been a beautiful ceremony. Lance had spent most of it gazing into his bride’s eyes, to the extent that he had to be reminded by the preacher that he actually had to speak at certain points during the ceremony. But that moment at the end, the kiss that cemented the two of them as man and wife…that had been a moment, he swore to himself, that he would hold onto for the rest of his life. It bothered him sometimes that he couldn’t bring it to mind any longer. He could remember the two of them inside the limousine. They had been talking and laughing…about what Lance could no longer clearly recall. He could see the image of his wife in her gown, her mouth moving…but no sound came out. His brain had turned the moment into a silent movie. Then had come the impact. The limo spinning out of control, the driver struggling desperately to right the limo until gravity and physics had taken over, sending the vehicle spinning over and over again. Desperately reaching for Alanna’s hand, screaming as her grip slipped from his. And then…nothing. His next memory was of waking up in the hospital, every inch of his body exploding with pain. He gasped, taking in air greedily as though he would never have the chance again. By his side, the sleeping figure of Steven Marshall arose. "Shit! Hey, bro, it’s okay. You’re in the hospital, man, it’s okay." In the hospital? Why the hell am I in the hospital? "We saw it all as it happened, man. Some douchebag in a Beemer ran the red, plowed into you guys going 90. The limo just went flying. The asshole actually tried to run but we managed to cut him off, hold him until the police got there." Where’s Lani? "The doctors say you’re gonna be okay physically. You broke your right arm and you’re banged up all over but you’ll be okay given time." Where’s Lani? "They’re worried you might have suffered some head trauma, though. They wanna do some tests, see what might have happened…" "Where’s Lani?" Lance rasped. Steven Marshall’s eyes started to well up. His voice cracked. "Lance, I’m…I’m sorry." No. "She got thrown from the car. The doctors…they did everything they could but they just couldn’t…" NonononononononononononononoNO! "no.." "I’m sorry, Lance. But she’s…" "NO! Don’t you say it, Stevie, don’t you DARE say it!" Steven just couldn’t stop crying. His big, strong brother, the one he’d idolized all through his growing up…he just couldn’t stop crying. "GET OUT! JUST GET OUT!" "I…I’ll come back later," Stevie stumbled. He walked out of the room and then Lance was alone. Alone. Like he would be for the rest of his life. Lance Marshall started crying then. And he didn’t stop until the pain medication kicked in again and blessed unconsciousness overtook him. The trial was finally held a full six months later, largely due to the efforts of the defendant’s attorney. Ron Coleman was the best justice that money could buy, the sort of man who was very good at pithy sound bites and playing to the media. The fact that it had taken six months for the case to get to court, Ron thought, was a bit of a failure on his part. If he’d been really on his game, he thought, the case would never have gone to court at all. T he prosecution’s case had seemed to have been gift wrapped for them. A young, happy couple whose blessed day was snatched away from them by a drunk. The wife dead, the husband injured. Lives ruined. A future destroyed. All because of the irresponsible actions of his client. None of that mattered to Ron. It would, he told his client, be easy to overcome. Act like you give a shit. Try to cry even if you couldn’t really care. Hell, you saw what the husband looked like, we can turn him into a roid raging wife beater, no problem. And if none of that works…well, I have got other means. What this means for you, bubba, is no jail time. You spend a cushy stretch in rehab, that’s it. Trust me. And so it had come to happen. Oh, it had looked bleak at first. The husband had been an excellent witness, the prosecutor full of fire and vigor, the judge determined to show his client a lesson. But after one brief little session with the judge…well, let’s just say things had started going their way. The judge had started going easier on his client, repeatedly emphasizing how remorseful the alcoholic little shit was. The prosecutor was finding it harder and harder to mount his case thanks to the judge cutting him off at the knees every chance his honor got. And in the end, it had been as promised. His client had given a long speech about how deeply sorry he was. The husband had looked like he was barely keeping from killing him. And when the verdict had been announced… Not guilty by reason of mental defect. To be sentenced to a long-term rehabilitation facility. Damn, he was good. The families had been outraged, of course. He’d expected that. He actually chuckled at the thought of them being so naïve as to believe that justice would prevail. Someone always had to win and someone always had to lose. And when his client signed him on…well, he wasn’t going to be the one to lose. The families were out in front of the courthouse now, giving statements to the press about how outraged they were at the verdict. He was ready for this, too. "I believe that justice has been served now that my client will be getting the treatment he so desperately needs. I am saddened and sickened by the Marshall and Momoa families continued vendetta against my client and ask that they set aside their bloodlust in the interest of justice." No more. Just walk off, leaving the press to scurry after you like dogs chasing a bone. Oh, wait…just one last thing. "You son of a bitch." It was the husband, his face twisted into a sneer. He was furious, watching the man who’d helped his wife’s killer get off with nothing more than a slap on the wrist. G-d, this would be too easy. "Temper, temper, Mister Marshall. But I do wonder…how long would it have been before you turned that temper on your wife?" He was on the ground faster than he’d anticipated, blood pouring from his mouth. The husband was being held back by a mixture of officers and family members, screaming at the top of his lungs. "Officers! I want this man arrested NOW!" The officers merely sneered at him. "Yeah, right." But it didn’t matter. The pictures had been taken, the articles were already being written. And if he had anything to do with it, he knew exactly what those headlines would say. Lance Marshall: Psycho. It was three weeks later when she approached him. The ice cold redhead in the immaculate Chanel pantsuit. She had come to him, offering condolences and words of sympathy. He had accepted them but, in truth, they had meant nothing. Then, she had caught his interest. "I have a business proposition for you. I would give you the opportunity to exact your revenge on those who have wronged you. And in return…" "Yes," responded Lance without hesitation. "I have yet to tell you what I will ask in return," the redhead calmly replied. "I don’t care. I want them all dead," Lance replied. "I want every last one of them to suffer as much as I’ve suffered." "And they will," answered the redhead. "I promise." "Then I’m yours." And that was the day Lance Marshall sold his soul to Alexandra Pierce. The driver had been easy. It had only taken the slightest of pushes for him and his precious BMW to end up wrapped around a light pole. The judge had been a little harder. First had come the photos of his honor and his gay lover with copies sent to his wife and the press. The ensuing scandal had been messy and prolonged…which made his honor’s "suicide" considerably less suspicious. Ron Coleman, however…that one Lance wanted in on himself. It had been painfully easy to lure Coleman into the trap. One of Pierce’s operatives, an attractive young woman named Katsidy, had found it almost painfully easy to seduce Coleman, luring him back to "her place" with ego stroking remarks about his clothes, his car, his money and what she imagined to be his penis size. Lance almost admired the ease with which Katsidy could lie. Once Katsidy had lured Coleman to the pre-chosen location, the two of them had quickly made their way to the bed with Katsidy promising Coleman a night he would never forget. He hadn’t even blinked when Katsidy had pulled out the handcuffs. Katsidy could barely keep from laughing at that. Once both of Coleman’s arms had been securely fastened to the headboard, Katsidy sat back for a moment and admired her handiwork. "Come on, babe," Coleman said in what Katsidy assumed was his best attempt at sounding "sexy". "Let’s get things started, huh?" "Oh," purred Katsidy, "you have no idea." She paused for a beat before adding "You can come in now, Muscles." And it was only when Lance Marshall entered the room that Ron Coleman started to realize the truth about just how much trouble he was really in. Not that it shut him up any. "I see you’ve already started cheating on your poor sainted dead wife, Marshall. How long did it take you to find this whore, you pathetic…" Coleman’s rant was interrupted by the impact of a massive hand against his cheek. He could taste blood in his mouth. "You," snarled Lance, "will shut the fuck up. Tonight, I am going to show you just how much pain you’ve left me in. And I will take every single bit of it out on you." Lance began doing so by pulling a gun out from the dresser drawer adjacent to the bed, placing the gun square against Ron Coleman’s genitals…and firing. "Ooh," giggled Katsidy, "that’s gonna leave a stain." And for the first of many times that night, Ron Coleman screamed. The satisfaction that Lance had expected from Coleman’s death never arrived. But he had made his promise to Alexandra…and he aimed to keep it. It had helped, at first, that many of the people Pierce set him against could hardly be called truly innocent. All had committed transgressions that justified some measure of punishment, all allowed Lance Marshall to try to exorcize his pain and still sleep at night. After awhile, the reason no longer mattered. He had done so much, seen so much…he no longer had any hope of getting out. He had committed to Alexandra Pierce and he would stay loyal. She had given him so much, given him the opportunity to get his vengeance. He owed her everything. It was almost ridiculous, thought Misery to herself. She was certainly capable of taking care of herself. She could probably take on anyone on the roster on SCCW and, if not beat them outright, at least make them pay dearly. So why, then, did the thought of talking to Alexandra Pierce scare her to death? She shooed this stray thought away as she knocked on the door of the dressing room housing the Dead Man’s Hand. After a moment, the door was opened and Misery found herself greeted by the rather massive form of Lance Marshall. Marshall simply stood there, appraising Mercy before answering "Yes?" "I need to speak with Alexandra, please." "One moment." Marshall retreated and, after a moment, returned with Alexandra Pierce following behind him. "Misery. What may I do for you?" "We’ve…the Magnum Opus, I mean…we’ve got a problem. Mayhem’s disappeared on us. No note, no explanation, nothing." "Unfortunate though that may be," Alexandra replied, "how does that concern me?" "We can deal with Vincent ourselves on a personal level. But Mayhem has some professional commitments that he needs to honor and…well, I was told you might be able to help with that." A small smile crossed Alexandra’s lips; a gesture, noted Misery, that just looked outright wrong. "I do believe I may have a solution. Lance," she said, turning to the wall of muscle at her side, "how would you feel being seconded to Magnum Opus for a while?" "It would be a honor, Miss Pierce." "What," asked Misery, "just trot out a whole new Mayhem, just like that?" Lance shook his head. "I agree with Lance," added Alexandra. "Co-opting Vincent’s name would be rather rude and a bit…tacky. What did you have in mind, Lance?" It was now Lance Marshall’s turn to smile. And if Pierce smiling promised bad things, thought Misery, what Marshall’s smile promised somehow seemed even worse. "Just call me Monster." We move from Marshall’s smile to the smile on Destine’s face. And while Marshall was fairly easy to read. Destine is a considerably harder creature to get a grasp on. "I do like a bit of mystery." "See," Destine continues, "the fact is this. The scary part isn’t how things can change so completely. The scary part is that it can all happen so damn easily, with just the tiniest little push. It’s just like…" Destine mimes the act of flipping a light switch. "…flipping a switch." And on "switch", our view turns from the lovely, sunlit day of the Marshall’s wedding… to nothing. Destine: We now return you to life as you know it, already in progress.
