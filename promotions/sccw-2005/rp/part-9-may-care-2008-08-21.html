Part 9: May Care

21 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/21/08 The Crooked House. Three weeks. That was all it took. Seth (a.k.a. Alan Bowdry, a.k.a. Jeremiah) watched in amazement. It had only taken three weeks for everything to go completely to shit. Actually, that wasn't true. It had taken three weeks to reach this particular level of insanity. The 'completely to shit' thing happened within the first three days. Ever since he'd come out here, it had been nothing but a few scattered farmhouses and a run-down church. It wasn't really that much of a 'here' at all--hell, you had to drive five miles to find the nearest asphalt. That all changed on the last day of July, when the Reverend returned...and the Word got out. Within days, the others arrived. First in a trickle, then in handfuls, then in bunches. By now, the area was home to no less than eighty-three people. Most of them at least fifty years of age, but there were a few younger families as well. Upon arrival, they would rush up to the first person of any kind of authority they saw--usually Seth--and ask to see him. No, that wasn't right. They would ask to see 'Him.' Seth had instructions for dealing with these people; he should tell them that 'He' was too unwell for any visitors, but that 'His' blessing was upon them, and that they should go see 'His' disciple, Reverend Wyatt Connors, at the first opportunity. They would, of course, and they wouldn't be seen again until the next day, when it was time for work. There had been a lot of that, too. Work. Usually his summers were spent in the fields, but there had been little time for that with all the construction. Anyone with an able body was sawing lumber, pounding nails, or painting. God alone knew where they got all of the materials, but there always seemed to be enough for what needed to be done that day. When a building was finished, they would begin constructing bunkbeds inside, thereby providing shelter for the weary travelers who came seeking salvation. Three such barracks had been erected already, and a fourth was underway. Also, Seth and a few of the others had begun work on a perimeter fence. This was the day that this miniscule semi-community officially earned its name. 'The Decency Compound.' The Crooked Mouse. Seth stabilized one end of a long board, while five other men drove nails into it. 'Are you guys finished?' 'Casi,' said one of them--a short, thin man with brown skin and dark hair. His name was Jorge Cortez, although Seth found it easier to refer to him by Angel Verde, his luchador identity. Seth nodded, grasping the meaning more from the inflection than from the actual word. 'OK, Verde, but I don't know how much longer I can hold this.' After a few more hammer swings, 'Verde' responded. 'Allí. Debe estar listo ahora.' Seth stepped away from the lumber, and sure enough, it held. That board, along with several others, would serve as the rails along this wall of the fence. 'All right. Let's keep it going, people. The sooner we start, the sooner we finish.' All six of them walked toward another pile of wood, this time planks instead of posts or rails. They all made quick trips, but none of them were quite as fast as Jorge Cortez or Pablo Villegas. They were already hammering by the time any of the others had brought their planks back to the fence. Seth watched them in amazement for a few seconds, before remembering that he was supposed to be working too. Still, he had trouble believing it. Backstage at the SCCW shows, Jorge had a reputation for being a bit of a slacker, which was part of the reason why he was only having his first televised match this week. Yet there he was, keeping pace with Pablo as they went back for another plank. Pablo had changed as well...word in the back was that he was incredibly thin-skinned, and prone to flying into rage at the slightest insult. Once, he even attacked Mayhem--Mayhem, for God's sake--in an attempt at revenge. Yet every time he had suffered abuse at the Compound--not frequently, but more than once--he responded only with a slight nod and an empty smile. It was as if, once the names were changed, the men behind them disappeared as well. It was a little unnerving, to tell the truth. But that was far from the only thing about them that he found unsettling. Angel Verde and Illuminado had returned with them to the compound after All In. It was a rather uncomfortable trip--mostly because of the extra bodies in the pickup, but also because neither man removed their mask. In fact, it wasn't until a week had gone by that they would actually walk around the compound without their masks. A number of travelers asked about it, but then they went to see Reverend Connors. After that, they didn't seem so interested in the matter. Then, one day, they weren't wearing the masks anymore. Seth thought about asking someone about it, but asking questions just didn't seem to be a smart tactic around here. Seth checked his watch. Four o'clock in the afternoon. Time for a break. 'Hey, Loomie!' Pablo--Illuminado--looked up from his work. '¿Sí, Hermano Jeremiah? ¿Cuál es él?' Neither he nor Angel Verde had learned Seth's name, either. 'I need to take fifteen. You're in charge while I'm gone.' 'Por supuesto. Gracias, Hermano Jeremiah.' There was a slight language barrier between himself and Wyatt's other two lieutenants--their English was only slightly better than his Spanish--but with the help of a few gestures, he managed to get his point across. Pablo turned around and barked a few orders in rudimentary English, then repeated them in Spanish to his contemporary, Jorge. The Crooked Cat. Satisfied that things were well in hand, Seth trudged toward the old church that rested in the center of the compound. There was a small refreshments table set up there, with a plate full of sandwiches and a large metal urn. Also there were the Widow Simmons, as well as Mavis Jessup, the sweet old lady he'd been working for and living with for the past few years. Mavis smiled and offered him a sandwich and a paper cup full of something cold, delicious, and red. 'Lovely day, isn't it?' Seth fought off a grimace. It had to be at least eighty-five degrees, and not a cloud in sight. Between the sweltering heat and the manual labor, he'd been sweating like three bastards all day. But of course he didn't say so. Instead, he smiled right back and said, 'It certainly is, Mrs. Jessup. Like a gift straight from the Lord.' 'Oh, it truly is. Bless you, Seth,' she replied, still smiling as he walked away with his snack. Seth kept walking until he reached the equipment storage pile, where the few tools not in use were kept. A few shovels, a posthole digger, and three or four large toolboxes. Seth sat down in front of them, leaning against a toolbox. It wasn't very comfortable, but it was somewhat necessary. The first thing he did was reach into a red toolbox with his name on it. Not that it technically mattered, since all property was communal, but there are rules that go deeper than religion, and one of them addresses the use of another man's tools (this rule, in its entirety: 'Don't.') The item he pulled out was not one you'd expect to find in there, though...a canteen. He unscrewed the lid, and poured a splash of it on the ground. Clear. Good. Seth then raised the canteen to his lips, and took a small drink. He did not swallow the contents, instead swishing it around and then spitting it out. There was no funny taste to it; it was good, cold, clear water. Satisfied, he poured the contents of his paper cup into the dirt. He knew how paranoid he was acting, but it wasn't like he had a choice. As the saying went, 'Better safe than sorry.' In a way, this entire mess was Seth's own fault. After all, he was the one who found Wyatt Connors after the accident. He also told everyone that Connors was a preacher in the first place, and he'd forced Wyatt to play along through blackmail. Seth knew that he was far from the only one who wanted revenge on the former 'Wise Guy', and he had intended on making it last. Seth had been able to force Connors back into wrestling as well...in a place that was home to more than a few people who would call for his head. Wyatt Connors would spend most of his days sweating bullets, either from concern that someone would uncover his fraud, or the ever-present threat of getting stabbed in the throat forty-six times by Desade or one of her minions. It hadn't been perfect, but it had been pretty good. Seth took a bite of his sandwich and looked around. In such a short time, all of that had turned into...all of this. Sometimes he wished he'd gone with his original plan, which was to bash Connors in the head with a rock and bury him in the cornfield. Anyone who knew the man would have said he deserved it. The Crooked Stile. After his snack, Seth placed the canteen back into the toolbox and returned to work. The crew had made some significant progress in only fifteen minutes...and the work was not only fast, but solid as well. He joined them, as they spent the next few hours doing the Lord's Work (by all accounts, the Lord really wanted that fence built.) Before they knew it, a bell began to ring. It was the seven o'clock bell, which also served as the announcement for everyone to put down their tools and come in to supper. Seth looked down at his watch, which read a quarter to eight. He found that oddly comforting...as if Connors was trying to get away with something. He and his fellow workers got into the chow line, along with all the others. He noticed an old man a few spots ahead, and recognized him as his original employer, Hiram Jessup. Jessup's face was red with exertion, and sweat dripped from his brow, yet he still had an odd little smile on his face. Seth shook his head, sadly. The only time he'd ever seen Hiram smile was when the harvesting was done...that, and the one time when Buford McCoy asked him to help burn down a barn. 'Hey, Hiram,' he called ahead. The old man turned around to face him, his face never changing. 'What is it, Brother?' 'We need to tend to the crops yet. Don't want them to spoil in the fields.' 'Don't worry about it, Brother. The Lord will provide.' With that, Hiram turned back around, and moved with the line. 'The Lord will provide.' He'd been hearing that a lot. In a sense it was true, as nobody had gone hungry yet. But for the life of him he couldn't figure out where the food was coming from, or the money to pay for it. Seth hoped he could get permission to go back into the fields during his next private discussion with Reverend Connors. It would provide the Compound with either food or a source of income, and more importantly, it would get him away from these people for a while. They all creeped him out a little, but none more so than the original residents of the area. They had all undergone changes similar to those of Angel Verde and Illuminado, and many of the arriving pilgrims would display radically different personalities on their first day than they did after. But Seth didn't notice the newcomers as much, because he hadn't really known them before they came to the Compound. Not like the locals, who he'd known and talked to and worked with for the past few years. He got a serious 'Invasion of the Body Snatchers' vibe from all of them. Hiram wasn't crotchety anymore, the McCoys didn't argue, and Old Man Henderson didn't tell rambling stories with no point to them. Someone had emptied these people of themselves, and filled them to the top with 'Him.' The line quickly progressed, and soon Seth found himself at the front. One of the barracks served as a temporary mess hall, and all the residents of the Decency Compound--with one exception--dined together this, and every evening. He picked up a set of flatware and a plate, and moved along toward the ladies who were serving the food. The first face he saw was Mavis Jessup, her smile like a ray of sunshine--bright, continuous, and dangerous in cases of prolonged exposure. The corner of her eyes glistened as she handed out cups to the others. She'd been crying again. That hadn't changed from before, although Seth again found no comfort in the familiarity. Ever since 'He' returned, all of her tears had been of joy. 'Good evening, Seth,' she said. 'You look thirsty. Here, have something to drink.' The Crooked Sixpence. Seth sat down in his usual spot, in the back corner. He hated sitting in the middle of the room, and would stand before he'd sit up front. It was vitally important to him anymore that he could see everyone else in the room. Tonight, just like every night, was filled with the clattering of forks and knives, and chewing noises. Seth looked down at his own plate, which held generous portions of chicken, mashed potatoes and gravy, as well as a roll. He was thankful for that; the gravy would keep his mouth from getting too dry. Tuesday nights were the worst; that's when they would serve cornbread. Getting a drink of water was next to impossible, as well. There were too many people watching for him to sneak in a canteen. There were no sinks or fountains. None of the cooled metal containers in the food line held water. His only drinking option was the red liquid in the cup, and there was no way on earth he was going to drink that. You know that 'Kool-Aid' joke that people tell about religious groups, and cults specifically? Seth will never laugh at that joke again. He did not enjoy any particular time spent with the others, but supper was the worst by far. It was the one time of the day when he could really see what had happened to these people, and didn't even have the option of finding something else to do. He had to watch while the others ate in silence--complete silence, not even so much as a 'pass the salt'--and drank heartily from their cups. Their heads would tilt back, and the sweet scent of red Kool-Aid (what the hell flavor was red Kool-Aid, anyway?) filled the air. It would stain their cheeks and dribble down their chins, and with every drink they took, they lost a little more of themselves. Seth had no idea what they put in that stuff, and he had no intention of finding out. He merely ate his own meal, dabbing everything into the gravy, and making sure not to make eye contact with anyone. The Crooked Mile He ate quickly, hoping to get out as quickly as possible. He noticed some of the surrounding people getting up for more of the tasty red beverage, and ate a little faster. Then, he noticed a slight commotion at the far end of the building, and his shoulders sagged. He wasn't going to leave any time soon. On the opposite side of the hall, a single table was cleared off and moved away from the others. Two chairs were placed behind the table, facing the crowd. Only one man appeared to be seated there, but that had more to do with perspective than anything. Even for someone as tall as Seth, it's tough to look across a room full of people and find someone who's more than a foot shorter than you. This problem was remedied as it always was--the diminutive man in the other chair stood up, and then clambered onto the table. Thin, dark hair, well-worn black suit that showed the marks of heavy use (it was the only suit he had, and he wore it almost every day.) He spread his arms open wide, as if to embrace his congregation. He grinned widely, and his eyes carried a fervorous spark. This was the man known in SCCW as 'The False Prophet' Wyatt Connors. Here, they just called him 'Reverend.' 'BROTHERS AND SISTERS!' his voice boomed out, and all eyes turned toward the speaker. Seth made use of the distraction by pouring his Kool-Aid into someone else's cup. 'Good evening to all of you!' Connors shouted. 'And how are you all on this glorious evening?' 'JOYOUS,' the crowd responded, not quite in perfect unison. They didn't shout, but all of their voices coming together at once made for a very forceful sound. 'It has been a joyous day for all of us! Thanks to the Lord's will and your hard work, the Decency Compound should be fully functional in a matter of weeks! Hallelujah!' 'HALLELUJAH!' they shouted...and was it ever a shout. Seth yelled the word right along with them--the worst thing you could do at a time like this would be to stand out--but his cry lacked the passion of the others. 'More and more lost souls have been finding their way to our little slice of heaven, eager for the spiritual teachings of our beloved prophet! His voice will be heard, and His touch will be felt the world over! Hallelujah!' 'HALLELUJAH!' 'The wicked demons that live within us, and stand against us, shall be destroyed in the face of His holy power! HALLELUJAH!' 'HALLELUJAH!' That final echo was the loudest by far. Connors always led off his sermons by firing up the crowd. 'THANK you, Brothers and Sisters, for being together here on this fine evening, and may His blessings be upon you. It fills my heart with rapture to know that so many of you have traveled from great distances, from dens of sin and iniquity, to ask for salvation from the hands of the great Reverend. Once again, He informs me that he cannot be with us in body tonight, but he wants you all to know that he is here in spirit. CAN YOU FEEL IT?' 'YES!' 'CAN YOU FEEL THE SPIRIT!' 'YES!!!' 'HALLELUJAH!' he cried. 'HALLELUJAH!' they screamed back. Many of them began to stand, and more than a few people around Seth had a white-knuckle grip on the table. Alice McCoy looked like she was about to collapse to the floor in a divine fever...or a seizure. They looked an awful lot alike. 'I can feel the spirit as well!' Wyatt continued, and the blaze in his eyes grew from a spark to a full-blown California forest fire. 'All of us can feel His embrace and are truly blessed, and the kingdom of heaven is ours! But there are those poor, sad souls who cannot feel this embrace!' 'No!' came the scream from one of the women...a fairly new member, as far as Seth could tell. 'Yes, these unfortunate masses, the wicked and the damned and the shameful--OH, THE SHAME--know not of His everlasting love, and they are doomed to not know it in the next world! What can we do for these sinners?' 'SAVE THEM!' 'YES! I have been chosen to carry the message of the Lord God Almighty out into the devil's world!' 'HALLELUJAH!' 'I will confront the evil, for they cannot stand in the face of Him!' 'PRAISE HIM!' 'They will believe, and they will tremble, and they will prostrate themselves on the ground before Him! I will save them all, Brothers and Sisters! The hall exploded in a raucous cheer. Those who hadn't been standing before were doing so now, and many of them were waving their hands in the air. Seth, once again, went through the motions until the cacophony died down a little. When it did, Wyatt Connors launched into the next part of his grand spectacle. 'I have brought one such lost soul with me tonight. He was wandering alone in this world before I found him, lost and afraid, without guidance or even gainful employment. My trusted associates and I brought him here, where he can receive the spiritual nourishment he needs. For is it not written that we are to tend to those less fortunate?' Seth started to laugh, but managed to choke it back so that it sounded like a cough. They tended to him, all right. As soon as they'd gotten Mac Deesul backstage, they tended to his head with a crescent wrench. They had to strap him down in the truck bed with bungee cords and a come-along just to keep him from tearing the vehicle apart. Once they got him back to the Compound...well, Seth didn't see what happened to Deesul. Honestly, he was perfectly fine with that. 'I would like to introduce you now to the newest member of our church...Brother Macarthur!' The other man who had been seated at the table stood up. He was a very large man, not quite as big as Seth, but by no means a shrimp. Three hundred pounds, short hair and bushy beard, wearing blue overalls and a white T-shirt...Macarthur Deesul cut a very imposing figure. Although he lost a little bit of intimidation with the vapid grin on his face. And the kind of glazed-over look in his eyes. And the parts of his mouth, beard, and shirt that all looked like part of the same heavy red stain. The tamed beast of a man was met with thunderous applause from the congregation, as well as a few cries of 'Hallelujah!' and 'Amen!' 'I touched him with His holy blessing. I allowed him to drink that which is reserved for the chosen!' Drink? Seth thought. Looks like you tried to drown him in it. 'I have driven out the demons that plagued him, all in His name!' 'PRAISED BE HIS NAME!' 'With His guidance, I will do this for more of them! One by one, until the whole world follows the one true path! I will bring the truth to them!' 'HALLELUJAH!' 'I will rescue them!' 'HALLELUJAH!' 'I WILL SAVE THEM FROM THEMSELVES!' The final cry from the people wasn't even a word, so much as a scream. Alice McCoy fainted, and the others began screaming and dancing around like maniacs. Seth tried to mask the horror in his face, as he looked around at the others, who thought of themselves as 'saved.' Then, he looked up at the Reverend Wyatt Connors, whose own insanity outshined them all. His eyes darted all over the faces of his followers, until they locked with Seth's. Almost immediately, Seth broke the connection. He hadn't been this scared since he was six years old. All this madness...and the craziest part was that one man was responsible for all of it. If someone told you that man was Wyatt Connors, it would not be a surprise. It would also not be the truth. The Crooked Man. After a few more shouts, some dancing, and about a thousand Bless You's, the evening's festivities came to a close. Wyatt Connors left the hall and headed straight for the Jessup homestead, where he was to meet with 'Him.' Although they all felt the presence, nobody ever saw 'Him.' That was an honor reserved for a select few. Wyatt Connors was allowed an audience, as he was chosen to bring the Message to the masses. Mavis Jessup was also allowed to see 'Him.' She had the strongest faith out of all of them, and had always known in her heart that their spiritual leader would one day return. For her devotion, she was given a place at the Master's side. Apart from that, almost nobody was allowed the privilege...and those who were usually only received it once. Honestly, if he didn't know better, Seth would swear that this was some sort of elaborate scam. Unfortunately, he did know better. Just a few days after 'His' glorious and long-awaited return, Seth was given the honor of meeting with 'Him.' This meeting accomplished two things. One, it fully convinced Seth that this was not a con game. Two, it gave him a severe case of what is known in the medical community as 'the willies.' After that, Seth had no desire to ever see or listen to this great Reverend again. Yet there he was, skulking about thirty yards behind Connors as he made his trip back to the farmhouse. He had come a long way just by going along with the crowd, but Seth knew that he'd need a little inside information if he wanted to get out of this mess. Amazingly enough, Wyatt never noticed anything, or at least never gave any clue that he did. He never even so much as turned around...which, given his legendary paranoia, was a pretty good indication of just how messed up this entire situation was. When Connors approached the Jessups' residence, he walked straight for the door, while Seth tiptoed around the outside edge of the house. The room in which the Reverend stayed was on the far side, and the window was stuck open--Seth knew this, because it used to be where he slept. He'd be able to hear everything from there. Seth peeked over the edge of the window sill, but ducked back down as the bedroom door opened. Another look revealed Connors entering the room and kneeling down, head bowed, in front of a chair. 'How are the flock?' the man in the chair croaked. It sounded like the voice of an eighty-year-old man, although he couldn't possibly be that old. 'Excellent, Master,' Connors replied. 'They have given themselves fully to me, as I have to You. New pilgrims arrive every day. I only hope that we have enough space for them all.' 'The Lord will provide, my child,' the Master hissed, while he gently patted Wyatt's cheek. 'The Lord will provide. What of the City of Sin?' 'An insignificant setback recently, but a great deal of progress has been made in Your name. Today I have converted a new soldier for Your army, and soon we will be ready to destroy the evil forces that thwart us.' 'Soon?' 'One more soldier is needed, I believe. A very special case. He is beset by demons on all sides, but I know that he can be saved.' 'Wonderful news, Reverend,' the old man said, this time with none of the spite present during the first time he used that word to address Wyatt Connors. 'That will be all. Bless you, my son.' 'Thank you, Master,' Connors whispered, and he left the room. Meanwhile, Seth's mind was whirling. Another convert? They barely got control of the last one! Besides, he was quickly losing the stomach for this kind of work. Beating people up was one thing, but they had kidnapped a man, for Christ's sake! And he wasn't the first either, as Angel Verde and Illuminado had hardly come willingly. He knew he should have left, but he couldn't resist taking another look at 'Him'--the man who claimed to be 'God's Second Son.' For a moment, Seth saw the look in his eyes--and, to his horror, saw the same look of almost rabid zealotry that Connors had. Only brighter. Much, much brighter. The man's eyes flickered toward the window, and Seth quickly decided that distance was the best thing he could have at the moment. With a speed aided by panic, he barreled away from the Jessup home. # # # The man leaned back in his chair, worn out from the excitement of Connors' news. He was tired all the time now, so very tired. Strangely enough, this man used to be in the wrestling business as well. He'd put together a rather impressive run in the National Wrestling Council about eight years ago, despite possessing not an ounce of wrestling skill. His tribulations since then...the injury, the madness, the captivity...had left his body weak, but his faith strong. Success in the ring did not equal new converts, however. Most of those he'd amassed were just as quickly lost, and he made no progress with the fans. They thought of him as a clown, a source of amusement. His attempt at healing their souls was met with derision and ridicule. For years, the world had considered him a harmless buffoon. They laughed, and mocked, and printed up t-shirts that treated his holy missives as trite catchphrases. Once more would the unrighteous cower before the name of Reverend Ibrahim Seck. There was a crooked man and he walked a crooked mile, He found a crooked sixpence upon a crooked stile. He bought a crooked cat, which caught a crooked mouse, And they all lived together in a little crooked house. -'There Was A Crooked Man', Author Unknown
