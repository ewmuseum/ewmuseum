Theatrics

3 October 2007

Date: 10/3/07 Dawn. Artur Marx sat up in his bed, awoken by the sun creeping into the second floor bedroom of his charming suburban dwelling. It would be odd for many people to see him this way, dressed only in gray boxer-briefs. It would be odd for all the people whose lives he had shattered over the years to see him get out of bed and slowly walk downstairs and pour himself a glass of orange juice. It would be odd for them to see him read the newspaper. Most days his routine was very much like you or I, but today he simply sits up in bed and stays like that, his eyes fixed out the window. He was watching a row of blackbirds, and even though they were far away, they seemed to scatter at the exact moment a voice spoke up in his room. “Looks like you got a lot on your mind, fella,” a man said jovially. Artur did not even turn his head, he knew that voice. He knew it was a figment of his imagination, he had been hearing the voice for years now. After a few moments he finally turned and saw the figure that went along with the voice. The figure was dressed in a cowboy hat, white t-shirt, and jeans. He had so many names. Sometimes he called himself The Traveler, sometimes The Magic Man. He smiled warmly at Artur. It was an odd sort of smile. At one angle it could look as kind as your grandfather, but from a slightly different angle it looked wrong, it looked wrong in a way that perhaps the written word cannot do justice. Worlds were ending in that smile. “Yes, I do,” Artur replied to the imaginary figure. He assumed the figure was imaginary because no one else ever saw him. This situation really didn’t bother him all that much. Artur Marx, the godfather of The Affliction, was very comfortable with his own insanity. “Don’t tell me you’re born again, or some shit like that, like this is some kinda morality crisis,” The Traveler said, and leaned in close to Artur, “I know what you did to those little girls five years ago,” he whispered. And the cowboy laughed. He laughed his fucking head off. “You’re not real,” Artur said, completely calm. And when he turned to look again, the cowboy was gone. Artur inhaled sharply. He had never told anyone about this particular hallucination, and had lived with it since he was a young boy. Artur Marx would get out of bed and pour himself a glass of orange juice, something that many people would probably think sounds a bit too normal for Artur Marx. * * * “Raise me? I’ll raise your fucking mother,” a disgruntled hotel guest bellowed, to no one in particular. It should come to no one’s surprise that Lane Stevens was the disgruntled hotel patron. He was stretched out in a long chair next to the indoor pool at the Marriott. The River Rat was still wearing the t-shirt with the print of Aimz nude on it…in public, and swimming trunks. There was a lap top sitting on his lap, and he was playing online poker. It was $10/$20 pot limit Omaha Hi/Lo in case you were wondering, but you probably weren’t. Nearby, a family was splashing around in the pool, and the father paused to glare at Lane. Stevens did not seem to notice. What did get his attention, eventually, was the clicking of heels that got closer and closer to him. His agent, Bridgette Masters, was dressed more casually than usual. She wore a white t-shirt and jeans. She was paid very well by certain powers that be to represent Lane exclusively, and cater to his unique…disposition. She sat on the edge of his chair. “Aren’t you going to…swim or something?” she asked, looking down at his trunks. Lane looked at her with a blank stare. She simply moved on. “This is your biggest match in the company as of yet, as far as I understand it. Would you like me to try to see if I can get some tickets for friends or family comped for you?” Bridgette asked, with wide eyes. It was as if she was bracing herself for his reaction, something she finds herself doing constantly. The River Rat’s brow furrowed and he looked up from his laptop. “In your short, yet eventful time representing me…have you seen any of my college buddies have a drink with me?” Bridgette does not get time to answer. “Have you seen uncle Mort stop by to shoot the breeze?” She only shakes her head in response, he continues to speak. “I remember when I was about 17, I used to meet girls on the internet…and talk to them over the phone for hours. I talked about every goddamn little piece of my life, and I wanted so bad to make connections. Real human connections. I went out to the bars with my friends three times a week, and had conversations that Kevin Smith probably wishes he would have thought of for some ridiculous movie. But at some point, these things ceased being important to me. At some point my buddy Matthew complaining about how nice guys never get laid, as he re-heated the pizza we got three days earlier and played Halo…made me want to throw up. I was always there for these people, to lend them a hand while they dwelled in their self pity; I was there for them when they realized their best wasn’t good enough. And because when I made the same realization I decided to actually do something about it, to get ahead in this business…and to stuff it up the ass of every fucking promoter who told me I was too slow to be a speed guy, and not powerful enough to be a power guy, they look at me like I’m Satan. My mother thinks I’m the anti-Christ because she saw me hit some jerkoff with a chair, some guy who will probably have title shots handed out to him like they were candy the rest of his career because he has a golden boy build and 17 year old girls want to fuck him.” Bridgette started to speak, but was cut off. “So no, I won’t be needing any seats comped,” Lane said, and smiled wryly, lightening the mood slightly. The River Rat went back to his laptop, and clicked away “I don’t speak much to my parents either,” she said, watching the young family in the pool splash around, “they think that because of some of the people I have represented, that I’m one step away from being a prostitute. My Mother worked for an insurance company, which if you ask me is a lot more of a disgusting endeavor than this.” Lane nodded in agreement, as he counted out a raise in his head. She studied his face as he played his poker game, and every once and a while she found herself feeling sorry for this man. Usually a minute later she would erase this thought, as he would do something rather offensive to her sensibilities. After about thirty seconds of silence, she asked: “So what are we still doing in Indiana, anyway? The autograph signing I got you was days ago.” “Some things you’re better off not knowing,” Lane said with a dangerous smirk. * * * Artur Marx rarely bothered locking the door of his house. For one, many of the neighbors seemed oddly afraid of Artur, despite never having much contact of him. And Shelby was a small sleepy Indiana town, with very little crime. However while Artur was sitting at his kitchen table eating cheerios, it came as no surprise to him when he heard his front door open. It was no surprise to him when heavy footsteps found their way to his kitchen. Before him the Scourge stood, unmasked. “I had been expecting you,” Artur said matter of factly, with no trace of fear. For he knew what was about to happen to him. * * * Fade up. A day later. You had to know this was coming, the Lane Stevens promo. It was prepared and sent out to Sean Sterling and Jonathan Rhine, and a few other notable FUSE members who he wants to absorb his message. Our video feed begins with a shot of Lane standing outside the Mellon arena in Pittsburgh, PA. The traveling Affliction circus has moved on to the site of the event. You can see that it’s a windy day outside by the way that Lane’s “OMG HE HITS KIDS WTF” shirt is blowing. Lane is standing near the front of the building, the turnstiles are in sight. It is about 4 PM, a couple days before Uproar. In the backdrop there is a poster that says, “Rhine vs. Stevens vs. Sterling for the Gateway title – 10/5, tickets still available!” and then it had the FUSE logo underneath. Also visible is a kind looking old man wearing a black jacket that says, “security” on the front. The River Rat seems to be in mid conversation. “So if terrorists attack tomorrow, you gonna be ready man?” Lane said. The old man has a blank look on his face, and stares into the camera. After about fifteen seconds he waves, and then Lane looks into the camera, and begins speaking. “Alrighty. Hey FUSE, what’s happening? I saw you called, yeah sorry about canceling our date the other week and not getting back to you, but as it turns out I was fucking your sister. What’s that? You’re still going to come over here and give me head anyway like a good little slut? That’s nice. And you’re going to buy dinner too? Sweet. Seeya then!” A thirteen year old kid, who Lane paid three dollars, runs by in the background with a banner that reads, “ANALOGY!” “Point I’m making is, we basically take the company’s most popular superstar, we beat the living crap out of him in the ring until he needs a bunch of medical attention…and how am I rewarded? With a title shot against the same wounded superstar the next week! I gotta tell ya, my breaks have come few and far between here in FUSE, but I really have to hand it to Smitty T Duluth on this one. I dig the match, man. In fact on a scale from 1 to digging it…I’m sitting at about…oh, digging it.” A golf clap from the former Livewire champion. “The Gateway title was created to celebrate those on the cusp of grand success, and in case you didn’t know…I’m pretty fucking awesome. I’m so on the cusp it’s ridiculous.” The kid runs by again, and this banner says: IT’S CUSPDICULOUS! “And who else do I have in this match?” The River Rat points down at his t-shirt. “Yes, Sean Sterling…a man who tries harder to be bad than Jonathan Rhine does to be good, and my god, that’s saying something. Seriously, I’m not really sure what’s left for you to do. Maybe you should bomb an orphanage, or you and Michael Vick can go electrocute some dogs or something. At the end of the day, The Affliction has put people on the shelf. We have embarrassed the biggest and best FUSE has to offer. Jacob McKail checks his closet for Lane Stevens every night before he goes to bed, cause I might just pop out…and make him tap out in his jammies. And you, what do you do? You torment Mayson Colby, because you know the only way you can look like a real man, is when you’re dealing with another midcarding chump. This is your chance to look like more than the pissant you are, so good luck Hannibal. You want to try your little theatrics on us, and you watch what happens. Cause we, Mr. Sterling…” The camera comes in on Lane’s face. “…are the fucking bad guys around here, and you would be best served to remember that.” The camera backs up again, and the kid runs by in the background, this time the banner says: BADDER THAN MILK THAT HAS GONE BAD. Lane’s head pivots rapidly, and the look on his face suggests he did not make that sign. Off camera, Jeb Stewart has a good hearty laugh, his first in weeks. Lane regains composure. “Don’t think I forgot about ya Jonathan. I really like your Rhine Rewind move thingy, by the way…tried it out last week on…who was that, OH SHIT IT WAS YOU!” Lane’s face is fake shock. “Yeah, sorry about that. But get rested up boy, and polish up the title. Better yet have your girlfriend polish it up, you took a beating. OH WAIT SHE TOOK A BEATING TOO!” Lane’s face is almost cartoony in its shock. “Bottom-line, I don’t feel like underachieving anymore; hope that’s okay with you guys. Peace out.” The River Rat waves to the camera. And we fade. * * * But we are not done yet. In a hospital room somewhere near Indianapolis, Indiana, Artur Marx’s face is badly damaged. There are tubes going in and out of him to assist in basic body functions. Sitting by his bedside is a note that he started to write to Smitty T Duluth, before passing out again. It says, “You have all been betrayed”. End.
