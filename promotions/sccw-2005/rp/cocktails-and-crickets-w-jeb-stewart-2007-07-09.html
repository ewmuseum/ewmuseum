Cocktails and Crickets (w/Jeb Stewart)

9 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/9/07 Memphis, TN It’s not even the PM yet, and Lane Stevens was having a cocktail. The River Rat and his tag-team partner/stablemate Jeb Stewart were riding in the back of a limousine provided by a Mr. Clinton Sage, so that they may get certain errands done. There was an ice chest packed with various liquor and mixers, a TV/DVD player, a CD player, and most importantly to Jeb…a limo driver. He thought it was pretty damn neat that someone drove them wherever they wanted to go, and he had a hat on, like limo drivers in the movies. "Why are you drinking already?" Jeb asked. Stevens took a second to consider this. He was in the process of downing a Mai Tai that he had mixed himself. His black t-shirt had the word "FUSE" crossed out. "Because we’re taking a detour", Lane answered, and shot gunned the rest of the drink. Jeb’s eyebrows raise. * * * A couple years ago, for reasons relating to his father’s Job, Lane’s parents decided to move to a suburb of Memphis called Arlington. The detour was to their house. "I’d like you to meet my friend and co-worker, Jeb", Lane offered, and pointed to the giant that now inhabited their living room. Stevens and Stewart stood on one side of the coffee table in the living room, while Jerry and Audrey Stevens stood on the other side. There were three couches and two recliners for seating in this room. All of these faced the 30 inch flat screen TV. Lane’s Dad had done well in his business, which Lane could not even remember for the life of him. "Nice to meet you, I’m Jerry." "It’s my pleasure, sir", Jeb answered politely. Audrey said nothing. She simply retreated to one of the recliners, and sat there, not making eye contact with her two guests. The River Rat’s eyes wander to the mantle that hangs over the TV, and he only sees one picture of himself out of about ten that are displayed. After staring at these photos for a moment, with a bit of a lost look on his face, the former Livewire champ takes a seat next to Jeb on a beige couch. "So what are you guys up to today, besides this little stop?" Jerry asked, looking genuinely interested. "Just an errand or two. Some publicity stuff for the event as well," his son said. Jerry smiled sheepishly. "I thought you were anti-establishment guys. Isn’t that part of your schtick?" "Eh, I still don’t mind a few bonus dollars. Say…you got anything to drink?" Lane asked. Audrey Stevens rolls her eyes. Jeb was the only one to see this. "Uh…sure. One sec," Jerry answered after a ten second delay. There was an awkward silence in the room as Jerry went to retrieve an alcoholic beverage at about 1 in the afternoon for his son. Audrey was still saying nothing. Jeb leaned over, and whispered in Lane’s ear. "You not send her a mother’s day card, or somethin’?" The River Rat said nothing. "So, who you kids rasslin’ this week?" Jerry shouted from the next room. Lane grinned and said, "Mayson Colby and Sean Sterling. I’m pretty sure a couple weeks ago these guys were holding up 'will wrestle for food' signs on the interstate and the management snatched them up.' And finally, she spoke. Audrey’s voice was low, and practically seething. "Don’t act like you are cutting some kind of goddamn promo in my living room". "Uh, what?" was all Lane could respond with. Audrey’s voice began to rise. "I haven’t seen you in years; I haven’t even got a phone call. The only way I can visit my son is to turn on the TV, and watch him shame his family." It is a rare moment to see Lane Stevens speechless. His eyes are huge, and his mouth is slightly ajar. Unlike Clinton Sage, he is not a monster. "You have traded in your morals, and for what? To be subpar at your job and to take some asshole’s orders? Jesus Christ Lane." She sighed. Jerry emerged in the room, with a pained expression on his face. He held a weak captain and coke in one hand. "Look dear, we don’t need to get into…" Jerry started. "Shut the fuck up, Jerry," Lane’s mother snapped back. And he did. Suddenly all Jeb Stewart could think about was his own mother. * * * Jeb fell into a rather realistic daydream. He was ten years old, holding a jar up in the air, squinting. There were all kinds of bugs inside. Jeb felt like he had created his own society inside of that jar, and he was a form of deity. His Mom was allergic to cat and dog hair, and also quite poor, so these were the only pets he ever could have. He cared for them greatly, constantly changing the grass while losing as few of the critters as possible. It was a mild summer day, the kind of day that really bad poetry is written about. His mother approached and sat down next to him. He could not remember exactly where he was, so in the daydream they were sitting in a huge open field that stretched on forever. "How’s it going, friend?" his mother asked, with a genuine smile. He always liked how she called him friend. "You want to help me catch a cricket? There’s only one cricket in here and I think he’s lonely" was his reply. She didn’t respond to this question, she simply kissed him on the forehead. He could tell that something was wrong with her on this day, but did not understand what. Years later he would come to understand that this was when she was just starting to get sick. * * * Jeb snapped back into reality. Even though his mother was the one who was sick, he felt sorry for Lane, sorry that he could not have the same kind of bond…or even understand what that might be like. And then, Jeb spoke up, much to the surprise of Lane Stevens. "No offense, Mrs. Stevens, but your son came here because he wanted to see both of you…and he was real nervous the whole way down here. I ain’t the boss of you, but it would seem to me that the proper thing to do is to try to make your son feel welcome. If you don’t see him that often, why would you want to be this mean when he does come around?" Jerry Stevens winces, as if he knows that his wife is about to explode. But then nothing comes out of her mouth. "Lane got me a job, and helps my mother get her medicine," Jeb started, "and that seems like morals to me." For a split second, The River Rat actually feels guilty for having obviously manipulated the giant to this extent. But that second passes. More awkward silence. And finally… "You want a drink too Jeb?" Jerry asked. Jeb exhales. "Yeah, that would be okay," the Affliction’s enforcer answered. Lane and Audrey made momentary eye contact, before he looked away. * * * The two Affliction members were back in the limo again. "Thanks," Lane said uncomfortably. He can’t even remember the last time he said thank you to anyone. Jeb nodded. * * * Inside the FedEx Forum, various members of the FUSE roster were signing autographs and meeting fans before the big show. Lane and Jeb’s booth had not seen much action, and Jeb was beginning to worry about his tag team partner. It was not like The River Rat to be this quiet. Partly because these two men were from the most hated stable in pro wrestling, but mostly because on the front of their table Lane had written in black magic marker "You’re all a bunch of fucktards don’t come over here okay thanks bye". Finally, after waiting there for some time, a teenage boy approached the table with a notebook in hand. He had a Jonathan Rhine shirt on that had been autographed. "Hi," the kid said. "Hello," Jeb replied. "My name is Doug, I was wondering if you guys could sign this." "I would be happy to," Jeb said. Doug nodded, and slid the notebook to Lane who made no attempt to take it. Jeb reached over and grabbed it, quickly signing it. It was the first autograph Jeb Stewart had ever given. "What kind of tag-teams influenced you guys?" Doug asked, adjusting the glasses on his sweaty face. "GET FUCKED!" Lane suddenly yelled. "Who are you, Diane fucking Sawyer? Is this investigative journalism? Are you going to do a presentation on me at school? Make sure to add in said presentation that I think you suck, and another thing…" It was at this point that Jeb realized his partner was going to be A-okay.
