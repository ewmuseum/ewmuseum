Date: too many to list 1 "Leaving" She had been busy. In only a week and a half since both Sarah La Vega and Joaquin Pierre had come perilously close to death, but Sarah had obviously found a way to channel her emotions and keep herself busy. She had morphed back into her old self, or seemed to have. She'd managed to turn herself from a filthy, bedraggled, sobbing mess into a professional psychologist, from a woman badly in need of help to a woman who was offering help. She'd renovated her office suite to the point that nothing looked the same to Joaquin's eyes. As he settled back into one of the waiting room's high backed leather chairs, Joaquin listened to the creaking leather with a large degree of trepidation. After all, she'd been avoiding him ever since the "incidents" as she preferred to call them, and he did not know how his arrival would be received. And now that he was there, it did not appear that anybody was present. His appointment was for nine o'clock, on the nose, and when nobody had come to speak with him by nine fifteen, he opted to simply settle in and wait her out. All there was to do was to take in the design of her "new" office. It was nice, in fact very nice, and he liked it better at first glance than the ultramodern room it had once been. That room had been all odd colors and furniture with impersonal angles, a room that, in Joaquin's opinion, was more suited for some sort of robot than people needing mental help. The "new" one had a soothing quality to it, a quality that immediately brought a sense of peace and harmony to him. The impersonal furniture had all been replaced by the ring of the very same high backed leather chairs that he himself now sat on, and wooden coffee and end tables that looked much like something one would find in a normal living room. The walls, now bearing a soft sage green coloration, also bore hand-painted splotches done with a sponge, and even though he was not normally somebody who enjoyed the color green, Joaquin liked it. The paintings, all of them scenes of nature and beauty, served to enhance the tranquility of the room. It was pleasant. The fountain, however, was a different story. He first noticed it, completely out of place as it was, at fife minutes after nine, though he did not examine it closely until twenty after. There was nothing wrong with it, really, it was merely a simple granite statue of a dolphin. The figure was poised as if it were breaching the water to look around, its tail and lower body coiled beneath the rippling water, but its beak and head protruded skyward, belching water out of its open mouth. But it was the eyes that bothered him. It could have been the byproduct of an increasingly overactive and neurotic imagination, but Joaquin felt that the eyes were the same as... as his eyes. Harrison's. They were black as coal, sinister looking. In Joaquin's mind, they were evil eyes, full of malice and ready to crush his life once more. He had to shake his head and tell himself that that was silly, that Harrison was dead. Yet it was tough to get that though from his head... A quick look up at the clock confirmed his suspicions. Nine thirty. Sarah was a perfectionist when it came to being on time, in fact she could easily have been described as anal about it. She always made sure to be meticulously on time for everything, so after he stared at that creepy black eyed statue for a while (which may or may not have had the eyes of Harrison O'Dell, Joaquin knew that one of two things was going on. Either Sarah was simply ignoring him and hoping he would go away, or for some reason she was simply delaying meeting him. Not how his final day in Montreal was envisioned. Joaquin's purpose was simple, he had only come to say goodbye. His own city, his life, was returned to him, he felt as though he were ready to return to Paris. Yet he didn't want to leave Montreal without saying goodbye to her, to Sarah. There were certain things that people could not go through without forming an attachment, and being kidnapped, tormented, and nearly filled by a monster was one of them. His fingers shook as he punched in the numbers into his small black cell phone, though he didn't know why. It was possible that Sarah wasn't the only one afraid of this meeting... "You have reached Dr. Sarah La Vega," the recording picked up immediately, without so much as a ring. "I am currently unavailable, but you are welcome to leave a message, and I'll call you back as soon as I may. If this is a business call, for therapy or for a consultation, please indicate so in your message." The sharp beep cut the message off, and Joaquin suppressed a momentary urge to smash his phone against the black eyes of that dolphin statue. "Sarah, please contact me. I know you are here, and I'm waiting for you." Contrary to what you may think, his English is not overly accented in real life. The accent you hear him speak with on television is merely a part of the character that Joaquin plays. "We need to talk, Sarah." A thump from the inner office. "When you get this message, I'll still be waiting here. I'm leaving Montreal tomorrow. I'll be going back home, back to Paris. And I don't want to leave without saying goodbye." Now there were voices behind that big wooden door, he could hear their muffled tones, though not what they were saying. Joaquin Pierre was not naïve enough to think Sarah was whispering with a client, and a single glance at the calendar sitting at the empty receptionist's desk confirmed that no patient was scheduled until ten thirty. "Please, Sarah. Just let me say goodbye." He closed his phone, the sharp click seeming awfully echoed in the room. He wasn't overly sure why he was even here, why he felt it so necessary to say his farewell. Yes, she had been his therapist, and yes, she had been drawn into the awful situation with O'Dell because of her connection to him... Did he feel guilty? Was that what had brought him here? He had to admit that that was partly true, though not all of it. He didn't want to put the feelings he felt into words, for they made him nervous, made him feel like he was being disloyal to his late wife merely by thinking them. She was a beautiful woman, that was obvious, but what was not so obvious was her obvious charm, her strength of character. She was, Joaquin felt, somebody totally and completely worthy of being admired, and she was struggling with everything she had to keep going and hold herself together. To escape the terror and dread and urge to give in to that fear. That alone was a big part of the reason why he... Joaquin didn't want to put it into words, but the words were already there in his head. He'd fallen for her, whether or not he liked it. She had saved both of their lives when Joaquin , the fool that he was, had blundered into O'Dell's trap. One of those long, delicate fingers had squeezed that nickel plated trigger and dispatched that monster into the abyss... and now it was tearing her apart. And he wanted to save her. "What happened the last time you tried to save her? What happened when you tried to save Marie?" He knew. One of his many faults was that, for some reason, the women in his life had a way of falling into mortal peril. "You should just leave her alone, Joaquin." But he shushed my inner voice and listened to the hissed voices coming from beneath the carved oak door patiently. And watched that damn fountain continue to vomit tepid water out of its mouth, spraying it at the ceiling until gravity pulled it down into the pool to be cycled back through. "Excuse me, Mr. Pierre?" The voice immediately brought him to his feet, though it wasn't Sarah speaking. No, the speaker was a tall, balding man with a huge handlebar mustache and a tan that was very obviously from a tanning bed (you could tell from the lines around his eyes where he wore sunglasses.) Mr. Tanning Bed was looking at Joaquin like he didn't quite know what to make of the situation, joining the Frenchman with the exact same feeling. "I'm sorry, but Dr. La Vega is not going to be able to make her appointment with you this morning. She is not feeling well." The guy was obviously lying, but he couldn't be faulted for with wanting to protect her. It didn't stop Piere from wanting to run across the room and take him down, tailored suit and all, and smash his face with a fist. "I'd be very happy to speak with you, if you'd like." He indicated the door to the left, the one he had emerged from. "Please, step inside." What choice did he have? 2 "The Flight" Sarah fled down the back stairs of the old Victorian house, the seldom used stairs creaking and groaning beneath her slight weight as she charged blindly downward. Something drove her, though she couldn't put words to it. All she could think of was getting to her car and getting as far away as she could, as far from Joaquin as possible, and doing it as fast as possible while she was at it. It wasn't that she didn't want to see him, no... in fact, a part of her burned to see him, to grab him in her arms and crush him to her. But that would be wrong, really wrong. She knew it was foolish, that Antoine was dead, but she was terrified that, if she gave in to her feelings for Joaquin Pierre, as she very nearly did that night in Harrison's lair, she would turn around and see him standing there, shaking his head. That she would see him cry, as he had on the night five years ago, when she had the affair. Dust from the ancient stairs had settled on her black suit, but she couldn't care less. Her sister's house was only a few blocks from here, she could run there and hide until tomorrow, when he left. If that message had been true. She held her Blackberry to her ear, listening intently as she fled down the stairs, and listened to it again on the landing. And again on the second flight of stairs. She couldn't do this to herself, couldn't beat herself up like this. But her husband hadn't even been dead a year. Not even six months. And Sarah had, without trying to, fallen in love with a man she barely knew. All she knew was that he had rushed into danger, put his life on the line, to save hers. She had admired him when she first met him, for she was a closet wrestling fan, though if you asked her she would deny it, and he was the type of character she always cheered for. But that had changed now. She couldn't bear to watch SCCW Snake Eyes, which had taken place only days after everything with O'Dell had been settled. There had been no doubt in her mind that his match with Corvus would be a war, that there would be damage done to both men. And the thought of him being hurt... the thought of his body being destroyed by The Obsidian Nightmare was too much for her to think. She'd heard, through the grapevine of the Internet wrestling community, that it had been a fine fight, every bit the war of attrition that she had expected it to be. And that, in the end, the man she loved had pulled it out. That Corvus had been defeated, and Joaquin had walked from the ring under his own power. Battered, yet still very much whole. The fact that he had shown up here to say goodbye to her was proof that he was all right. And that he was willing to leave her and go on with his life. She stifled a sob as she held the Blackberry to her ear one more time. "Sarah..." That one word, spoken in his deep baritone, was enough to send her frazzled nerves into overdrive. A tear rolled down her cheek, falling to wet the black silk of her suit in a splotch. Allan would delay him, enough for her to get away. The man was proving useful... he was a good doctor, and more than capable of taking some of her patients off of her hands. And the fact that his office building had been ready to go had been perfect, until Joaquin had booked his appointment. And thus why her partner was delaying the man who was breaking her heart while she fled into the bright light of an early spring day. And why, when she pulled up in front of her sister Anna's she was pouring tears and feeling very, very much lost. What was she to do? 3 'Swimming Against the Tide' He was home. For the first time since the murders of his family, Joaquin Pierre walked into the Paris townhouse where they had lived and loved, and where he had lost. It was exactly as he remembered it, yet the place rang with the echoes of the past, of better times than these. Joaquin shook his head, a little sadly, knowing that he had everything to be excited about right now, that he shouldn't be feeling like this. Would they have wanted his homecoming to be frought with sadness? He thought, privately, that they would not. They would want him to remember the good times, but to not look at the bad. Marie would, if she still lived, tell him to think ahead to Temptation, now only a few days away, and to win it for them. His sons would take turns playing Joaquin, the good guy, on the living room floor, whilst the other son would be the evil, treacherous foe trying to knock their father down. And, of course, in the lofty world of their young dreams, he could never fail. He still had one child, his son Andre. He knew that Marie would want him to watch over him, to keep him safe and make his life as happy as possible. And, he knew, she would tell him to go to the ring in Las Vegas, bring Andre Jensen down onto his bent knees with the Au Revoir, and come back home posthaste. It was all so simple, so easy, when he thought about it, but putting the thoughts into action was difficult. Tommorrow, he would call his son. Then he would train. Then on to Vegas, and a successful match with Andre Jensen... And then what? Joaquin found that he couldn't focus, couldn't plan for anything further than that. He rather felt like he was swimming against a strong current, being back here. Perhaps he was not as strong as he would have liked to believe, perhaps he wasn't ready... But you are here. And you will do this. He needed to make it work. He needed to be that man in the lofty dreams of his children, the man who could never, no matter what the obstacle in his path, fail. He needed to do that, to honor their memory. That was how he would remember, and how he would heal. The last thought before Joaquin drifted off to sleep was of Sarah, her beautiful pale skinned face framed by that long black hair, and a smile creased his features as he did. For a time, he knew no more...
