Part 3: The Family

Date: Today The rain fell heavy on the buildings and streets of Chicago that evening. Somewhere the sun dropped below the horizon, but the storm clouds had made the sky dark long before. Down below, people were scurrying toward whatever shelter they could find--doorways, taxis, or newspapers serving as makeshift umbrellas. It was the kind of storm he remembered from when he was a kid. They'd take out all the pots and put them under the leaks, and the thunder would-- 'Trevor, I don't suppose you could join the rest of the class?' T.J Ratigan quickly turned away from the window and back to reality. He saw the faces of partner in crime, Deacon Dale, and his agent/employer/overlord, Wyatt Connors. He also saw the annoyance in those faces, but paid little mind. He saw that all the time. 'Normally, I wouldn't mind your inattentiveness,' Wyatt said. 'But tonight, I have something important to ask of you, and I would appreciate it if you were actually listening.' 'Yeahsure,' Ratigan muttered, and adjusted his position on the couch. 'I suppose that's the best I can ask for. Now, if you'll allow me to return to the matter at hand...' Wyatt Connors stood up from his chair, and walked around to the front of his desk. 'As you recall, I have recently begun making my play for the SCCW Universal Title.' 'Yeah, I've been wondering about that,' Deacon chimed in. 'You've never been big on titles, I know. What's the deal there?' 'Picked up on that, did you? Good boy, Deacon. You may have a biscuit.' He ignored Ratigan's laughter and Dale's frown, and continued. 'You're correct. This is part of a larger plan...one that will enter the next stage very soon, if all goes well.' 'So what?' T.J. sat up on the couch, so that he could get a better look at the man in charge. 'You got a plan. Big deal. You've always got a plan. What's so important about this one that you're actually letting us in on it?' The interruption brought another sharp glare from the Scorpion. 'I'm getting to that, Trevor. The reason I am bringing you into the loop, is that this plan, if it succeeds, will also bring down a lot of heat on me...and by extension, the two of you.' Even if Ratigan couldn't connect the dots on his own, Deacon Dale knew what it meant. 'You're finally going to go against Pierce, huh?' 'Indeed I am, Deacon. What I am about to do will make me the target of Desade's wrath. It is possible that she will only exhibit that wrath within the confines of SCCW...just as it is possible that I will turn blue, sprout wings, and dole out quarters in exchange for children's teeth.' 'That'd be something to see,' T.J. said, and perked up a little in the hopes that it would actually happen. 'It is far more likely that she will attempt to worm her way into my private affairs, punishing me by going through those people that I care about. I am sure you can see the basic flaw of this premise.' Indeed they could. Wyatt Connors? Care? 'The only known, active associates I have are the two of you. This means she might also try to attack you through your friends and loved ones, in an effort to weaken me. It might not be effective--she knows as well as you do that the second you stop being useful to me, I will cut you loose--but she may do it anyway, out of spite. And before you say anything, Trevor, I must tell you that this is no joking matter.' Ratigan had not planned on saying anything, mostly because he was only barely paying attention. 'Doesn't sound like it. From what you've told us, she sounds pretty nasty.' 'That she is, Deacon...and that's not the full picture. The things I've told you are bad enough, but they're small change compared to what I haven't told you.' '...Jesus.' 'Indeed. These times will be dangerous, and I will be far too busy saving my own neck to worry about yours. That is why I offer the following option. If you wish, you can leave now. I will not act against you, and I highly doubt Ms. Pierce will either--she's going to have enough to worry about. No harm will come to you as a result of my actions...I think.' Deacon cocked an eyebrow. 'You think?' 'I have to admit, I'm not entirely sure what the fallout from this will be. I only know that the best way to avoid it is to be far away from me as possible.' At long last, the gravity of Wyatt's comments sank into T.J. Ratigan's thick, greasy head. He and Dale had known the man for five years, and not once had he ever admitted to not knowing something. 'Hold on there, chief. Are you saying you want us to bail on you?' 'No. I want you to think about it. If you stay, you will almost certainly be a target. If you leave...you may have to leave SCCW altogether.' Deacon Dale sat straight up in his chair, his expression equal parts shock and anger. 'Whoa. We just got here, and now you're asking us to quit?' 'I am merely presenting the facts. If you wish to remain unscathed, your best option is to leave the company. If you stay, you're likely to be a target for her, whether or not you distance yourselves from me. It's unfortunate, I know, but that's the likely outcome.' His remarks had calmed Dale slightly, but the big man still looked upset as he leaned back into the chair. Connors continued, 'I'm merely presenting the facts. The decision, as I said, is yours. You've got--' He pulled back the sleeve of his white dress shirt, and looked at his watch. '--two hours. If you want to leave, make sure you do it by then. After that, it'll be too late.' His point made, Wyatt quickly turned and walked toward the door leading to a back room. He went through and shut the door behind him, leaving his two associates behind. An uncomfortable pause followed, which was broken by Deacon Dale. 'So...what do you think?' he asked. Trevor didn't answer. He just stared out the window, at the gathering storm. # # # KRA-KOOM! A little boy sat bolt upright and cried out. 'Aah!' He looked around for the source of the noise, but of course it was nearly pitch dark. He briefly wished for his nightlight, even though he'd recently announced he was too big to have one. He heard the rapid plink-plink-plink of water dripping into a pot that was already half-full. 'Jus' rainin',' he muttered, and laid back down to sleep. A few seconds later, there was a brilliant flash of light, followed by another KRA-KOOM! that shook the walls of the trailer. The child once again sat up, his eyes now wide open. There was no going back to sleep now. Grabbing his ragged stuffed bear, he slowly toddled out of his room and into the hallway. He didn't know what time it was--that big hand/little hand stuff was too confusing--but he knew that it was After Bedtime. So he stepped as quietly as he could, hoping to avoid getting into trouble. He was doing fine until midway through the kitchen, when he bumped into a chair. 'Huh?' came a voice from the living room. A head emerged from around the side of the recliner, investigating the noise. It belonged to a man in his late twenties, and featured dull brown eyes, a thick mustache, and a long, light brown mullet. He looked like the kind of man who drank Old Milwaukee because he liked the taste. 'What're you doin' up, boy?' 'Can't sleep, Daddy,' the boy answered sheepishly. He padded toward the living room, just as the man in the chair stood up. 'Hold on there, twerp,' he said, and scooped the boy up into his arms. 'You and Herman there have to go right back to bed.' 'Don't wanna,' the boy whined. 'Scared.' 'Scared? What the hell of?' 'Thunder.' As if to emphasize his point, another bolt of lightning struck. The boy buried his face into his father's chest, hoping to hide from the noise. 'Thunder? Aw, hell. Ain't nothin' to be scared of. Just noise. Ain't nobody ever been hurt by noise.' The little boy looked incredulously at his father. 'But it's loud an' scary.' 'But you're a big boy, ain'tcha? You don't need a nightlight anymore. Why so scared of thunder?' The lightning crashed once more, and the resulting thunderclap was the loudest yet. The boy covered his ears with his hands (and his bear.) The father sighed. 'All right, tell ya what. You can stay up with me for a little bit, okay? Don't want to get in trouble with your ma or your sister. Sound good?' The boy nodded excitedly, and his father put him back down on the floor. 'All right. C'mon, twerp.' The two of them went back in the living room, where the television was still on. The father sat down in the recliner, and moved as far as he could to one side so that the boy could sit next to him. 'Whatcha watchin', Daddy?' the boy asked, his eyes darting from his father to the screen and back. 'That's rasslin', son,' he answered. 'It's...well, it's kinda like yer cartoons. There's good guys, an' bad guys, an' they fight.' 'Like He-Man?' The father looked down at his son--and his son's pajamas, which were covered with pictures of the cartoon hero in question. 'Heh. Yeah, like He-Man.' The boy peered at the screen, his interest peaked. The reception wasn't good because of the storm, and the sound was turned down low, but he could still make out the picture. Two big, strong men, only dressed in boots and underwear, tossing each other around. 'Which one's the good guy?' he asked. 'That fella in the black tights. Name's Dan Parker. 'Desperado,' they call him. That other guy in the red, that's Boris Krushov. He's a Commie.' 'An' he's the bad guy?' 'You got it.' The father reached down to the floor and picked up a half-finished can of Old Milwaukee. He took a loud sip, and a little bit dribbled from his lip, and onto his shirt. 'All right! Nice suplex, Dan!' 'Soupflecks?' 'Yeah. That move there, that's called a suplex. Oh man, Krushov's in trouble now.' 'How come they're fightin'?' 'Hmm? Oh. Well, Boris cheated an' won a match against one'a Dan's friends.' 'That wasn't nice.' 'Yeah. So now, Dan's payin' him back. Oh, lookit that--he's goin' off the top rope!' The boy stared in wonder as the man in black tights leaped off the top rope. He seemed to fly through the air, before landing a sharp elbow onto the sternum of the fallen Krushov. As the man in the zebra shirt slapped his hand on the mat, the crowd went wild, and his dad pumped his fist. The child raised his arms up in the air and shouted, 'Go Dan!' 'Shh! Your ma hears you, she'll send you back to bed. Probably after a paddlin'.' 'Go Dan!' the boy whispered. The threat of paddlin' was not one to be taken lightly. 'Probably oughtta send you back to bed.' 'C'mon...one more?' 'Well...' the father began, but caught some key words from the television...'championship match.' 'All right. But just one more, an' then you gotta go.' 'Yay!' 'Shh!' The boy looked cowed for a moment, then whispered 'Yay!' Father and son watched the rest of the program, quietly cheering on the champion as he defended his title. When the show ended, the man lifted up his now-sleepy son, who was moments away from dreaming of hammerlocks and piledrivers. 'Daddy?' the boy asked. 'Think I could be a rassler when I get big?' 'Son, you can be anything you want. Hell...maybe I'll see you on TV, winnin' the title. Won't that be somethin'.' 'Yeah...' # # # Anyone else in his situation might have thought of his family, or his girlfriend, or his physical well-being...and Trevor Ratigan tried to think of those things; he really did. But as he stared at the rain, his mind kept going back to that night. Sitting on the chair, watching wrestling with his dad. That night, a passion for the sport was born...and he wasn't about to give that up for anyone. Certainly not some crazy ginger bitch who had a mad-on for his boss. 'You hungry?' he asked to Deacon, who seemed to be lost in thought. 'Let's go get somethin'.' 'We're supposed to stay here, dummy,' Dale responded sharply. 'Unless you're out.' 'Fuck that. I just want to get some goddamn food. Pizza sound good?' Deacon sighed. Once Trevor got an idea in his head, it took a shoehorn to get it out. 'Just hurry back. I don't want to have to explain to Connors that you're not really quitting.' 'I give a shit,' T.J. said, and headed for the door. 'I'm back when I'm back.' The man once known as 'The Judge' (but not lately, and not by people who don't want to get hurt) shook his head, before shouting, 'No fucking Canadian Bacon!' just as the door closed. Left alone, Deacon turned to his own introspection. He was taking stock of his career, and he didn't care for the way it added up. Twenty years in the business. Two title reigns, both from only two years ago. Numerous injuries, some of which still required surgery. A handful of painful relationships, one night stands, and the occasional dalliance with a professional. A thousand gimmicks, each one more embarrassing and awful than the last. And not once did he ever get what he truly wanted. It was something he'd only ever told one other person, his brother Teddy, on those rare occasions where they talked. Deacon told him what he really wanted out of his professional wrestling career. It wasn't money, or fame, or titles, although those things were nice. All he wanted was for-- 'Well. I know I'm disappointed at one of you. I'm just not sure which one it should be.' Dale snapped his head around, and saw Connors emerge from the back room. 'It seems that Trevor will not be joining us. No doubt deciding that accidentally impregnating his girlfriend is a life plan.' Deacon shook his head. 'Nah, you got it all wrong. He's in; he just ran out for pizza.' 'Ran out for--oh, Christ. I should drop him on principle. Can't even follow instructions.' 'Anyway, if I had a girl that looked like T.J.'s, I wouldn't be here either.' Wyatt scowled. 'Do not attempt to be humorous, Deacon. It does not suit you. We will be receiving a delivery very shortly. One that will bring Alexandra Pierce's world crashing down.' 'On top of us?' 'Most likely. Still, it seems that the plan has worked, and the evidence will soon be in my hands. It seems our man has acquired some photographs of Ms. Pierce in flagrante delicto.' 'Delicto?' Deacon asked, a confused look on his face. Wyatt smiled and nodded. 'Flagrante. It's a pity Trevor's not here...I'm sure he would have given his right arm to see these photographs.' 'Yeah, I...oh. Oh.' That, if you hadn't guessed, was Deacon Dale figuring out what 'in flagrante delicto' meant. Suddenly, they heard a faint knocking sound. A hand rapped against the glass three times. 'Deacon? I believe that's your cue.' The big man got out of his chair and headed toward the lobby of the building. As he left, Wyatt's smile stretched out into a grin. He honestly had no idea how this was going to play out. It was more than a little exciting.
