Betrayed

23 July 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Sin on Spike 3 “Wake up.” The gravelly voice belonged to a man unknown to Craig. His red eyes, somber in defeat, hung perilously towards the forefront of his eye sockets. The back of his head felt like a thumping bass line in a Jay-Z song. So much felt wrong and he couldn’t quite put his finger on it. “Some dreams, Craig, they must come to an end.” The man continued to speak. He didn’t show his face. That angered Craig. He tried to move, but found restraints in his way. Ribbons of rope pressed against his wrists. Too much fighting and the skin would become raw, unbearable. Had to stay calm. “Who are you?” Craig’s voice came out soft, limp. A set of fingers danced across the back of his skull. It only made him angrier. Blackness surrounded him, but he heard the tapping of feet against the hardwood floor. “Names are not important.” “Being a fag is though?” “Touchy, touchy. Need to learn discipline, I see.” BZZZT! Craig groaned as he felt the cold steel removed from his neck. His hairs stood on end as he felt the tingling electricity course through his body like a jellyfish had taken over him. A pair of red eyes bulged from the pain, the thumping bass line only becoming worse in the process. “Did you have sweet dreams? Not this time, of course. Silly silly, like a million rabbits. No, no, dreams last night. Sweet dreams. Dreams that felt real. Alexandra, in your arms, kissing your neck in a way that you thought no longer possible. Oh joy, I said, oh joy.” The nonsensicalness of the man only laid more stress upon Craig. He knew these kind of people, the ones who had no semblance of reality. They only made his job harder. His wrists strained again, but he was quickly stopped by a smack against the back of his skull. “Stop that.” He was right. Craig moved his neck freely, but there was still nothing to look at. A moment of silence passed before he noticed that the man was standing right in front of him. “Do you remember me? Remember of the atrocities that you committed?” “I did no such thing.” Defiance hung like loose beads of sweat from his words. The man laughed, bellowed, a sickening curl. “Not that you would freely admit, of course not, too easy for you to just deny deny deny. I see through your soulless eyes though, Craig. Yes, yes, yes I do. I can see the crimes that you wish you hadn’t committed and the truths that pain you even at this moment. I know of these things, Craig, things that you would rather ignore, but I don’t ignore them. No, I shine a bright, painful, but bright light upon them.” BZZZT! A few more thousand volts surged into his body and Craig’s body jerked violently from the immense pressure. He desperately needed this to end. “You dream of Alex, dream of her in your arm’s, and yet she isn’t. So cute. I play with your mind, but not for the first time. Not the second time either, no, no, no. You forget about me, which is sad. Far too sad. Used to like you. Used to trust you. Yet, you did the unthinkable. You unleashed hell on this Earth and have done nothing to stop it.” Craig wanted answers. “What the hell are you talking about?” His voice roared with anger. A laugh responded. “Alexandra, my dear Franklin. You unleashed her into this world. You gave her confidence, you gave her strength, you gave her power. Before you, she was weak, unwilling to fulfill the potential that awaited her. Then, you came around, and breathed life into her. You were her helium. Heh. Heh. Heh.” Craig struggled against his restraints, his body screaming out to be released from it’s prison. The man came dangerously close to Craig’s face. “You don’t remember me? How sad? She said you wouldn’t remember me. That you had pushed those dark secrets to a corner of your soul that remain untouched even to this moment. It makes me want to cry. Do you want to cry, Craig? Do you?” BZZZT! The pain seared his eyes and it was nearly enough to make him cry. He didn’t get much of a chance to react though as he was flooded with lights, blistering in power. Craig quickly shut his eyes and turned away, hoping to hide from it. Yet, he was surrounded with it. Slowly, his eyes pulled apart and he looked up at the man. What he saw frightened him. Stitches adorned his face in no logical pattern. His eyes were mismatched colors. His lips seemed to be made out of plastic sandwich bags. Nothing about this man seemed recognizable. Yet, Craig knew him. *** Alexandra Pierce stood before her peers and had never felt more uncomfortable. Even though it didn’t show on her face, the idea she had in mind was radical, and it weighed heavily on her mind. “I think it’s time we help people. They need us out there.” Murmurs flew throughout the room like wildfire, from people who weren’t certain of this idea. Alex felt a sharp twinge in the pit of her stomach as her eyes wandered to where Craig was sat, quiet and alone. His eyes weren’t even upon her, but on the ground below. Having been here for the past three weeks, he hadn’t quite found his niche yet. “Alexandra,” a young man spoke up in a calm, confident voice. “We see the passion in your eyes, but this is not our war – nor our world any longer. We were given special talents and a special place in this world, and that is to live far away from the rest of them. They do not understand us and they never truly could.” Craig looked over at him and tried desperately to remember his name. Akin to trying to catch a fish in a pond, it took a moment before he remembered. Robert. Alex stepped forward “So what is the solution that you offer? To just stand idly by and watch as people are devoured, swallowed whole, by this neverending darkness? I don’t know about you, but I know that I cannot stand by any longer and just watch this happen. It might not be what we’re set to do, but I do believe it is what we are destined to do.” “You speak of destiny,” Robert stood this time. An interesting choice as he moved down the steps and looked at her with a fiery hot intensity. “How is it that you know of our destiny, Alex? You read those books and you see these visions, you see a world that none of us see. Why should we trust you?” Craig felt a weird prickling feeling on the back of his neck. He slowly turned to his left and saw a man standing in the edge of the doorway. He had only seen the man a few times before and looked at the tattoo on his right hand. One of a spider. He didn’t know what to make of him. Instead of spending more time on him though, he looked over at Alexandra and found her eyes upon him. “I have seen things that I cannot explain. I know we were brought here for a reason that was greater than this. If we could only embrace it, look inside ourselves, we would all find that we’re meant to fix this world. I don’t know why I feel so strongly about this, but I know that I do.” She paused, her right hand extended, and the very edge of her pointer finger grazed Robert against his chest. “You are able to manipulate the media so that they see anything. You could stop wars with your images, change their entire belief system. All the death would end in just a moment if you chose. So why haven’t you?” “Because, there’s an order to the way the world works. We’re not Gods, Alexandra. We’re just not!” Robert’s voice became raspy, edgier than Craig had ever heard it. He felt a dryness in his throat and wondered what it could be. His knuckles had turned white from gripping the chair in front of his. Craig didn’t like it here. He wanted to be anywhere, but here. Yet, he was compelled to stay here. “We might not be Gods, Robert,” it was the first time that Craig had spoken. “But, are we still not part of this world? You would rather have them devour each other? Why? What have they done to you to earn such contempt? Alexandra speaks of destiny and while I don’t believe we’re destined to do this, I do believe that we have the ability, the free will, to do whatever we want to do. Putting a little good back in the world never hurt anyone, has it?” Alexandra’s eyes sprung to life, all small nod offered in thanks to Craig. Robert looked over at Craig and nodded his head. “Fair enough.” There was fear in those eyes. No one had dared crossed Craig since the moment he stepped foot into the temple. It was almost like they knew him from somewhere, but they couldn’t put their finger on it. Craig hated the thought that fear had won them to his side. It wasn’t the kind of person that he was. Craig looked over at the door again, but the man with the spider tattoo had disappeared. He then looked at Alexandra who felt a warmth come over her. Months later, Robert would be the first one that Alexandra killed when her heart turned blacker then the coal at the center of the Earth. *** “You’re not supposed to be alive.” Robert smiled at Craig. Craig wasn’t in the smiling mood, especially from a pieced together puppet. The taser twitched in Robert’s hand as he moved closer to Craig. There was still too much holding Craig back and he felt the soothing cold metal against his bare chest quickly replaced with another jolt of electricity throughout his body. Spasms took over him as laughter filled his head. “So much fun to watch you In pain. Your pain is almost enough to make me forgive you. Almost. You did so much wrong, so much evil, and your blood is on everything that Alexandra ever did. Scream, baby, scream!” BZZZT! A blood curling scream escaped from Craig’s dry lips. He felt his body continue to move involuntarily and he wasn’t even certain that the taser was going off any longer. He desperately wished it would all end. Deep down, he knew it never would. There was a fine line between controlled and slave, and he knew which side of it he landed upon. “I won’t be blamed for Alexandra’s transgressions!” Craig managed to yell out the words in between the pain that gripped him. Robert shook his head and back handed Craig, leaving a spot on his neck exposed to send a few more thousand volts of electricity through his body. As the pain ravaged his body, Craig found his mind working on a different level, separate of all the pain. The room had gone from white to black, though Robert was still illuminated. “You don’t know the beast you’ve awakened.” Robert teased him like a woman in a strip club, his lips on the very edge of Craig’s ear lobe. A song played in Craig’s mind, one that he hadn’t heard in quite some time. More electricity through his veins and he felt his body starting to shut down. Keep fighting, the song said. Craig didn’t know if he could any longer though. He wanted to be free, but he knew he never could be. The ropes that bound him, they remained not only on his body, but in his mind. “Who sent you?” Craig asked, unknowingly. “That which would love to see you suffer, to break your will, to cease your existence.” The black turned to red. The red turned to blue. The blue turned to white once again. “I’ve spent years, wondering how it would feel to feel your blood against my lips.” Too bad. Craig’s temple exploded as his head slammed into Robert’s. A pool of blood exploded out of Robert’s nose, stunning him suddenly. Craig felt the ropes slowly burn into his skin until they weren’t there any longer. Robert bounded up to feet and drove the taser into Craig’s abdomen. More electricity. It only fueled him. Sparks flew off of his stomach and he felt the song in his head only grow louder. Is this what it felt like when death came? Music played? Craig’s boot smashed against the jaw of the sick man. His right hand wrapped around the fractured throat and lifted him off the ground. Robert looked into Craig’s red eyes and saw a monster there that he hadn’t seen in quite some time. One cracked door later, Robert laid outside of his quiet room, the one place where he could be left alone with Craig. Robert moved quickly and slammed his foot into Craig’s chest. It didn’t even stun him. Robert looked at Craig and closed his mind for a second. Craig felt him moving around in his brain and brought him to a place he never thought he would see. Hell. “Are you ready, Craig?” Robert teased him. No. His fist collided into Robert’s chest and he felt the wood splinter into pieces. Everything disintegrated, and Craig found himself collapsed on the floor. His body was blue, dark enough to be almost black. It twitched without warning and he felt this heavy pressure against his chest. Everything seemed to fade away from him, as if life was fading. “Who did it, Robert?” Craig didn’t know where the voice came from. It was his own. “Alex, did it. She wanted to remind you.” Of what?” “Of what she’s capable of.” The light started to fade back and he felt a hand against his shoulder. Fire entered Craig’s mind as he looked up. A woman stood there, with long brown hair, large saucer-like eyes, and a smile. Craig hadn’t seen her face in quite some time. “Sabrina?” “No.” He reached out to her, but her face faded away until Samantha stood there, holding him. “Craig?” He didn’t respond. Instead, the darkness took him. *** Alexandra lied in bed, a cold wind rustling. She turned over in bed, all her warmth turned empty. Her cold eyes opened as she sat up, the moon shining brightly. As her eyes scanned the room, she found a figure standing at the edge of her bed. If it weren’t for the pair of red eyes looking back at her, she wouldn’t have ventured a guess as to who the person was. “So now we linger around each other’s bed? Going to surprise me in the shower next time?” Her voice had a bite to it. Craig could care less as both of his hands wrapped around the wooden banister. “How could you have done it, Alexandra? You killed them all?” She smiled at him. “You have not seen me in two years, and that’s what you ask me? I was certain that our next meeting would have been a little more dramatic. Did you want to try again?” Both of his fists slammed into the wooden frame, shaking it violently, but the woman who would one day be called the Spider retained her icy calm. “Not quite as powerful as you once were. I think I did my job then.” “Is that so?” His voice told her more than he let on. A white glow appeared where his hands were, and she inched towards her headrest. Not what she had expected. “I killed them because they would only get in my way. Much like I knew you would. There are things in this world, things that I have to prepare myself for, and knowing that you are looking over my shoulder would not help things. I saw your eyes. You lost your way. You lost your faith in me. You knew what I was about to become, and you would go to no ends to stop me. Eliminating them robbed you of your best weapon.” Craig remained in his spot. Alexandra in hers. An age of tension met in the middle of them. “What am I supposed to do now?” She smiled. “I believe 'leave and never come back' would be the correct answer. I get the feeling that you are not about to do that.” “You brought me into this world, Alexandra. I was happy before I met you.” Her fingers snapped. “Don’t feed me that. I gave you purpose. I gave you life. Before you knew what I was capable of, you were just a puppy dog in love. When you found out what you were capable of, you were a God that could not capitalize on his given abilities.” He wanted to yell at her. Fight her. Anything to tear the aggression out of his body. Instead, he remained there calm. “I won’t let you ruin this world, Alexandra.” “Who said anything about ruining it? I’m going to heal it.” “That’s what you think.” “Do not talk to me like you know what I am, Craig. You were flawed, always flawed. I tried to bring the best out of you, and instead, you became this. Not quite darkness, lingering in the light. It sickens me.” Craig smiled. He didn’t know why. “You want to see what I am capable of, Alexandra? Just wait. I’m the only thing stopping you from yourself. You’ll learn that in due time. You will see the faults in your reasoning and it will tear you apart. I promise you that.” He began to walk away. Alexandra opened her mouth to say something, but stopped. He turned around and looked at her. “How is Quinn doing?” She didn’t respond.
