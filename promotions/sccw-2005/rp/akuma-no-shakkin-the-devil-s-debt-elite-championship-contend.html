Akuma no Shakkin ('The Devil's Debt') (Elite Championship Contendership)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 18-19th, 2010 March 18th, 2010; Chiba, Japan I knew that this 'world tour' that we had planned for Sin City Championship Wrestling would become immediately disastrous for myself as soon as I saw the very first date after Defiance. I had spent a long time in Japan over the course of my career. It was my haven, a place I could go to when things for me cooled down in North America. When I lost my job in PRIME, Japan was the one place I could reasonably go to and still attract a crowd. Japanese crowds loved wrestling in a manner unlike most American wrestling fans. They craved the action, the respect, and dynamic between two athletes duking it out in the ring. The sweat, the blood, the tears of each wrestler in the ring were what they craved. It wasn't about the words, it wasn't about the egos or the personalities. It was about the battle, and the passion of being one of those warriors within the confines of that ring. I loved Japan, and Japan loved me. Well, okay, there was one person in Japan who didn't love me nearly as much as I described. Inoue Doi wasn't so much a woman as she was a literal force of nature. She was a half of a foot shorter than I was, but she could hit me like she was Lance Marshall. She went to the ring wearing a Japanese school uniform and she wouldn't leave the ring satisfied until she had to wash the other guy's blood off of it. She hit people like she was kicking their souls out of their bodies. She belittled people, not because she thought she was above them, but because she took pleasure in destroying their rage with her own. She could fight men twice her size and make them look like they, themselves, had been beaten up by someone twice *their* size. I became acquainted with her in the Squared Circle, where she garnered both her fame and her infamy. It wasn't by my choice, either. It wasn't like I really wanted to be considered a friend to a person as impossibly unpleasant to me as Inoue, or that I planned to tame what was essentially a wild animal posing as a young Japanese woman. Inoue met Annabelle at one of the Squared Circle's shows, and they had bonded instantly. I never really asked how this happened, but what I did know was that Inoue acted like a whole other person when around Annabelle. Suddenly, the woman who was known for beating the absolute hell out of any and every opponent she'd ever wrestled had been tamed by a girl who, frankly, had no business being anywhere near a wrestling ring. I'd like to think that maybe Inoue saw something like a normal life in being around Annie, but that would probably be introspective twaddle on my part, anyway. So, when Annabelle found out that the next stop on our tour was going to be Japan, she insisted on exactly two things. The first was that she continued to travel with me so that she could once again visit the country she spent her childhood in. The second, naturally, was to visit her closest friend who lived in Japan, Inoue. The last time I'd visited Inoue Doi's house was about nine months ago, when we had first discussed the fact that I was about to become Baron von Blackberry. At the time, I had immense reservations about playing the role of an idiot, considering that I had a certain reputation of being all about wrestling prior to taking it up. These days, I've begun to have fewer and fewer reservations about it. Inoue lived in the Mihama ward of Chiba, a bit to the northeast of Tokyo itself. Her house was a bit larger than the normal Japanese house, enough so that Annie and I could spend the night there without us getting in the way of the Doi family. Inoue lived with her mother and her older brother, Ryu, who used to be a professional wrestler himself. He even used to be Inoue's manager until Sonny Silver decided to play basketball with his skull a while back, and now Ryu made his living as an English teacher at a nearby high school. My relationship with Ryu Doi was far better than mine with his unpleasant little sister, but I rarely saw him around. He was either a really private person, or he was absorbed in his grading. I wasn't sure which it was, but he wasn't the one who greeted us when we knocked on the door the morning we arrived at the Doi house. Clouds were cast overhead as we exited our rental car, shrouding the Mihama ward in a dull gray color. A light, misty drizzle had already started falling, and I instinctively pulled up the hood of my sweatshirt over my head in response to that. Annabelle lingered in her car to fiddle with something in her purse, so I casually walked over to her side of the car and opened the door for her like the gentleman I was. That and I was sure she'd just stay in there fiddling for ten minutes longer than I wanted to stand out in the oncoming rain. She slid out of the car while simultaneously tying her long hair into similarly long pigtails. I loved watching Annabelle move, it was like a dance only she knew the moves to. Her yellow shawl and her white sundress only served to accentuate her movements, flowing around as she walked and cascading in the wind even when she was still. She liked wearing things that flowed, which might be why she grew her hair long enough to be half as tall as she actually was, and why she'd put her hair into pigtails. Having completed her spontaneous hair-oriented task, Annie turned to me as if she wanted me to approve of how she looked. I nodded to her. She smiled. Inoue Doi was already at the door, leaning against it and looking a little bored. Her hair was in a ponytail, and she wore this white collared shirt with a pleated blue skirt that invoked memories of her days as the School Girl in the Squared Circle. I let Annabelle go in in front of me, partially because she was excited to see Inoue again, and mostly because Inoue wouldn't dare try to throw any kicks at me if Annabelle was in front. 'Konnichiwa!' Annabelle said in her chipper little way. Inoue flashed a rare smile at Annie and the two embraced in a hug. 'Konnichiwa.' Inoue said, in her usual serious tone. While she held on to Annie, Inoue looked up and saw me standing there with my hands in the front pockets of my sweatshirt, 'I see you brought him along, too.' 'Hai.' Annie said. Annie let go of the hug, but still held on to Inoue's shoulders, 'We're getting married after Coral goes through his world tour, Inoue. You'll still be coming to the wedding, right?' 'Sure.' Inoue said, before she had this mischievous grin on her face, 'If anything else, maybe I'll get to kick the devil man in the face.' I sighed, and looked at the more Asian of the two women with what I presumed was my best stinkeye, 'Please don't kick Marcus in the face, Inoue. I don't want him handing me the wedding ring with an even uglier mug than he already has.' 'Shut up, bakayarou. I'll do what I want.' Inoue said in response. Inoue liked to call me 'bakayarou' instead of my given name, an unfortunate designation considering its insulting connotations. It was a word she never used for anyone else except me. If I had my way, Inoue wouldn't even be going to my wedding. There were far too many people going that she'd probably beat the hell out of in a heartbeat, and still others that she'd probably beat the hell out of on principle alone. Unfortunately for me, my bride-to-be considered her one of her closest friends, and as a result, I couldn't argue with her. Inoue was going whether I liked it or not. Annabelle let go of Inoue's shoulders. She still stayed between me and Inoue, however, 'Inoue-san, I brought many gifts for you.' Inoue's face brightened just a little bit more, 'I see. Come in, Annabelle-san. I think we have a lot to catch up on.' She stepped aside to allow Annabelle through, and then she gave me a more fierce stare, 'You. Bakayarou. You get to come in, but I will kill you if you don't take your shoes off beforehand.' 'Yes, ma'am.' I said, deflatedly. At least she wasn't cruel enough to leave me out here in the rain. Although I swore that when she looked my way, I could see fire in those eyes. I did as instructed, taking my loafers off my feet and setting them next to Annie's sandals when I came in. The sweatshirt came off shortly thereafter to give my old Action! Wrestling T-shirt some much-needed air. Inoue's home was quite cool in the springtime, compared to how it felt when I had visited in July, but not cool enough that I needed to be wearing it. Inoue and Annabelle disappeared off to Inoue's room so that Annie could pander her miscellaneous trinkets to her, leaving me in the den. I sat down at the table that was so low to the ground that you had to actually sit on the floor, and thought to myself just how in the world I could be considered a friend to that woman. I let myself fall onto my back, and I stared at the ceiling for a moment. That's when I felt something wasn't quite right with the room. I sat up and looked around, and there he was. Ryu Doi, Inoue's older brother. 'Gah!' I went. I nearly tumbled right back onto my back again. How long had Ryu been sitting in the corner without me noticing? 'Konnichiwa, Avalon-san.' he said to me. Then he spoke English, 'I presume Inoue let you in without a fight.' Ryu Doi was a 30-year-old Japanese man who usually wore business suits, and he used to be a professional wrestler. His was a sad tale of always being in his little sister's shadow. Everything Ryu ever did in terms of athletics, Inoue would outshine him to the point that he was negligible. Martial arts? Inoue had two black belts and I'm not sure Ryu ever made it past a brown. Swimming? Not even close. Wrestling? Well, I'd say that Inoue won several times more championships than Ryu, but zero times zero was still zero. Yet, if he was jealous of his sister, he clearly didn't show it. He always had this reserved, respectful demeanor. I've known this guy for almost seven years, now, and I've yet to see him show anger. 'I had Annie with me.' I said, as a simply response to his presumption. I doubt I'd be allowed inside if I didn't have Annie with me, that's for sure. 'I see.' he said. He adjusted his tie, and I had no idea why he needed to choose this moment to do so, 'Inoue gets along with her very well. Better than most people.' 'Yeah, I know.' I said. I only knew too well how much Inoue hated pretty much everyone else. After all, she had such a nice nickname for me. A silence grew between Ryu and myself. Neither of us were known for our words, after all. It took me a while to figure out something I wanted to say, and even then, I was sort of grasping at straws, 'So, how are you two doing lately?' 'I'm alright.' Ryu said, and he grew silent again. I hate having to prod people to continue, 'And Inoue?' Ryu hesitated before he spoke, 'Inoue... I don't know. Ever since our mother got sick, she's grown distant. Moreso than usual. Won't even talk to me about anything concerning what's going on with her. It's strange, because I'm usually the first she talks to concerning her worries.' I meditated on Ryu's words for a bit, but he didn't have anything else to say concerning Inoue. 'Think something's wrong with her?' was all I could come up with. 'Something's always wrong with Inoue.' Ryu said with a shrug, 'It's just that I don't know what it is this time.' We grew silent after that. * . * March 19th, 2010; Chiba, Japan Inoue's house had a guest room in case of visitors like us, so that's where I found myself sleeping when it was time to turn the lights out. Truth be told, Annabelle and I had been suffering from jetlag, but it wasn't nearly as bad as the last time we came to Japan since we'd been in Hawaii for more than a week before we took our flight to Tokyo. However, we were both all too happy to duck under the covers and fall asleep in each other's embrace. Right in the middle of this bizarre dream where I, as Baron von Blackberry, led an attack on the White House with an army of super powerful clones of Alan Branch, I suddenly woke up for no apparent reason. I guess the idea of an army of Alan Branch was a bit too much for my weary mind to consider. Or maybe I was just slightly startled by a clap of thunder outside, as the storm had grown only worse since we had gotten to Chiba. Annabelle was still flat asleep, the clap of thunder and my being awake doing nothing to wake her. She had also evidently rolled away from me as she slept, leaving me free to simply get up out of bed without physically disturbing her. In a half-asleep haze, I managed to slide my way out of the guest room and half-walk/half-stagger my way to the bathroom. I'll refrain from giving any details of what I did in there, though. When I finished, I heard the sounds of the front door opening and closing in as quiet a manner as possible, and the subsequent sound of someone trying to step through the house while trying to remain as quiet as possible. I left the bathroom and managed to very nearly run directly into Inoue Doi. She shouted in surprise, which startled me just as much as I'd startled her. She did not even hesitate to swing her wet umbrella at me, which I had to deflect as best as I could considering the surprise nature of being assaulted with an umbrella. 'It's me, Inoue!' I shouted, 'It's me!' I think that just made her want to hit me *harder*. The umbrella came around for a second swing and hit me across the side of my head. It didn't hurt me so much as disoriented me, not to mention made me look like a fool as I raised my arm up in futility to block it. She didn't so much yell her next word as growl it, 'Bakayarou.' I don't know how I did it, but I managed to recover my balance. Inoue still held her umbrella tightly in her hand, and it's then that I noticed some unusual aspects of the way she looked. She wore a black leather jacket that I swore that I'd never seen her wear before, and it was even more alarming for me to notice that her black pants were made of leather. Had I not recognized Inoue's face, I might have mistaken her for someone else I knew. What alarmed me most, though, was the white and red tanktop that she wore underneath her black jacket. It alarmed me not because of its style, but because the red in that tanktop was almost certainly not supposed to be there. It was blood. 'Inoue... what the hell?' I asked. I gestured at her shirt, and it caused her to reflexively grab the ends of her jacket and cover up the trouble spot. 'None of your concern.' she said. She tried to walk past me, and I tried to stop her by grabbing her by the shoulder. Inoue stopped dead in her tracks and snapped her head back at me, giving me the angriest look I think I'd ever seen her give anyone, 'Don't touch me.' She smacked my arm off of her shoulder, and she walked away without uttering another word. Just what the hell was that about? * . * March 19th, 2010, five hours later; Chiba, Japan I couldn't tell you how I managed to fall back asleep after my bizarre encounter with Inoue Doi that morning. All I knew was that I was only awakened when Annabelle shook me awake. 'Hey, Coral, wake up.' she said, shaking me. 'Ugghh... five more minutes...' I groaned. I turned over and away from Annabelle. She hummed to herself in an irritated manner and then pushed on my body again. 'If you keep doing this, I'm going to start jumping on you.' Annabelle said. I heard her sigh, 'Do I have to put on the high heels, Coral? I'll do it, you know.' Half-asleep, I had a response for my blushing bride-to-be, 'Won't Inoue be angry with you for wearing shoes in her home, though? She *always* tells me she'll kill me if I wear mine in here.' 'She won't be angry, because she's not here right now.' Annie said. The sound of her voice made it clear that she was a little distressed over this. I was up on my feet so suddenly that even Annabelle was surprised. Hell, I was surprised, too, I promptly lost my balance and had to grab on to Annabelle just to try and maintain my balance. It didn't pan out, actually. I fell backwards and Annabelle fell right on top of me. Having a hundred pound girl fall on top of you wasn't so bad, but having her head smack my own in what could only be described as the most accidental double noggin knocker ever. My vision was white pain for a few seconds, and I could imagine that Annie was the same as me. 'Geez!' she shouted, holding her head in pain, 'I thought wrestlers had good balance!' She slid off of me and onto her knees, still holding her head, 'Ugh. I tire of head injuries, Coral.' 'You've never really had one, Annie.' I said, sitting up and holding my right palm to my head, 'They're even less fun than this, trust me. At least I'm awake now.' Annabelle puffed up her cheeks as she looked at me, a sure-fire sign that she was a little annoyed with me. She removed her hands from her forehead, which now had a very subtle red mark on it that was sure to annoy her even further as soon as she saw herself in the mirror. Eventually, that puffy-cheeked look on her face gave way to a sigh, 'Coral. Inoue's gone missing. We don't know where she went, but she's not in her room.' If getting a headbutt from my fiancee didn't wake me up, I'm pretty sure this will do it, 'Okay, so what happened?' 'I-I don't know.' Annabelle said, her stammer strangely familiar. She got like this when she was really concerned about something. She's stumble over her words. She especially got like this when she first met me, and I must've been a total dumbass to have not seen her liking me coming for as long as I didn't. 'She... she had a fight with Ryu of some kind and stormed out of the house.' Annabelle said, 'I-it was something over a bloodied shirt.' I think I just had a flashback from last night. 'Her blood?' I asked. 'I-I don't think so. When I saw her storm out, she... didn't seem that... i-in...injured.' Annie said. She was getting so flustered that she was having trouble finishing her sentences. If it was enough to upset her, I knew I had to at least put on my best reassuring face. 'Don't be upset, Annie. I'll try to find her.' I told her. Before she had a chance to argue with me, I was already out the door and out of the house, into the streets of the Mihama ward of Chiba. In some ways... no, wait, check that, a lot of ways, I regretted this. * . * Have you ever gone rushing into a city you barely knew to find a friend of yours who may or may not have found herself in some level of trouble? And was this friend of yours a violent, near-sociopathic Japanese girl who took pleasure in seeing you suffer physically? If you answered 'yes' to both questions, then congratulations! You're an idiot! Just like me. I was already a little lost wandering around the Mihama ward, and it didn't take me long to start lamenting to myself just what in the hell I was doing. It wasn't like I was going to find Inoue particularly easily in a city of nearly a million people. It didn't even make sense that I'd go out and look. I had few clues, nothing that would lead me directly to her. As it stood, I think the only reason why I even went out to look for her was simply because I hated to see Annabelle upset. I found myself in some kind of marketplace, or at least an area of the city where there we stores and restaurants. There were people around, most were just content on going about their business. Some of them stared at me, though, since I stood out from being the only caucasian in the area. Given that I'm already used to odd stares from performing under the Baron von Blackberry monicker, I was used to it. A man standing in front of the nearby sushi shop tried to coax me into his shop by handing me a pamphlet, a poor and destitute-looking Asian-man wearing an apron. I took it out of pity for the guy who had to stand outside all day and badger potential customers-to-be. I made a mental note to come back with Annabelle, since she loved that stuff, but it was while I was filing this mental note away in my brain that I caught something in the corner of my eye. On any other day and any other time on this particular day, I might have passed this off and continued on my merry way. Something made me stop. A random compulsion, fate, an invisible wall of force... whatever you wanted to call it. I stopped and turned to my right, where I saw Inoue Doi standing in an alleyway, wearing a white blouse and those strikingly out-of-character leather pants I'd seen her in the previous night. She didn't know I was there, though, and I decided to duck behind a wall rather than come right out and confront her. At first, I didn't fully understand why I did this, but I was somewhat relieved that I did when I saw this big, tough-looking man in a black suit. His hair was wild and untamed and he wore sunglasses that weren't actually covering his eyes. Rather, they were propped up against this guy's almost strikingly massive forehead, which made me spontaneous think about my accidental headbutt with Annabelle earlier in the morning and how it would hurt about forty times worse if it happened with this guy. He was unpleasant, for sure. Intimidating was the word I searched for to describe him. Inoue had dealt with big guys before, but most of them were wrestlers. I could name a bunch of the guys she'd wrestled who were soft and bloated, people whose muscles weren't entirely natural. This guy was a solid brick wall. If he wanted to, he could punch Inoue through the wall and leave a hole in her shape in his wake. Judging from Inoue's body language, this might have been the first time I'd ever seen her nervous about meeting with someone. Well, other than Christopher Sheffield, anyway. The man was initially less interested in Inoue and more interested in looking around. Perhaps he was looking for people spying on them. Like me. Either way, he lost interest in this after a couple of seconds and spoke to Inoue in Japanese, 'What do you want?' His voice sounded like he was gargling gravel. 'Your head.' she said. Such a statement was usually meant to be intimidating, but she was uncharacteristically nervous. Eventually, she added, 'But since that's not happening any time soon... my brother is figuring it out.' 'That would be your failure.' the man said, simply. 'So it is.' was Inoue's response, 'If he knew, he would try to get me out of this.' The man had this sneer on his face that looked like it could remove grease from a steel beam. 'Oh? Good for him. Perhaps later, I can take some of his fingers.' he said. He reached into his jacket and pulled out this wicked-looking knife. From the way it was shaped and the almost ornate-looking handle, it didn't look like it had any purpose except to hurt other people. Inoue backed up. It may have been the first time I had ever seen her actually back away in my entire life. 'N-no.' Inoue stammered, 'He won't know.' 'Good. Don't worry, your 'work' will be over after your next... 'service'. Or if you happen to actually come up with the rest of the money for your... debt. Whichever comes first.' the man said. He put his knife away and then handed her a folded piece of paper. Then he turned around, 'See you around.' Placing his hands in his pockets, the man walked away from Inoue and out the other end of the alleyway. Thankfully, he came nowhere near me. When she was sure he was gone, Inoue started sobbing. It was a sight that I... didn't want to see. I had no idea what compelled me to do what I did next. Were I acting sane and rational, I would have walked away, washed my hands of this, and let God sort this out somehow. Maybe with a good, proper lightning bolt. I don't know. What I did know, even if I didn't really know it at the time, was that my body compelled itself to step out where Inoue could see me and call her name, 'Inoue.' Inoue was startled, and she turned away from me as quickly as she had turned towards me to hide her face, 'Go away.' I was already here. I wasn't going anywhere, 'Are you in trouble?' 'I said, go away.' Inoue said. She wasn't her usual sociopathic self. She seemed legitimately upset. Possibly just from the fact that I was witnessing her actually crying, which I swore to myself she only did when she couldn't beat the hell out of me enough to win a match... something that actually happened the very first time I stepped in the ring with her. She never did it again after that, so I considered it some weird anomaly. 'What, and come back to Annabelle crying?' I asked. I'm not backing down from this, 'Seriously, are you in trouble?' 'This isn't anything you can help me with.' she said. Part of me thought she was looking for another umbrella to beat the hell out of me with, but she simply stood there. 'So, you're in debt from a 'nice person', is that right? Why ever would you get yourself mixed up with that?' I asked. 'You don't understand.' she said, crossing her arms and really struggling to look away from me, 'The hospital bills piled up. We couldn't afford the procedures for mother, so... I had to. What, you're going to judge me?' 'No.' I said, although she could have at least come to someone other than a scary-looking mafia dude if she needed money. I then recalled what the big guy said to her. '......How much?' 'Excuse me?' 'How much is your debt, Inoue?' 'It's none of your business, bakayarou.' she said. 'Just tell me.' I insisted. Inoue sighed. I think she was too emotionally tired to actually argue with me, 'Half a million yen.' I drummed up the sum in my head. At the current exchange rate, that would be a little over five thousand dollars. It wouldn't be difficult to come up with that kind of money over time, unless, of course, you were in a hurry. Inoue was clearly in a hurry. After a while, she added to her statement, 'If I could pay it off, I would have done so already. Now, I'm in debt and I have a week to pay it off. Or else...... well, it wouldn't be pleasant.' I mulled it over in my head. I had an idea that Annabelle and I probably wouldn't like, but the last thing I wanted to do was to know about this and do nothing. 'Well, let's get you home, anyway.' 'What?' she asked. 'Your brother and Annabelle are both worried about you running off like that. Tell them... something. Anything. Doesn't matter to me if you lie or not. I will help you.' I said. I studied Inoue for a bit, and there was a mixture of surprise, doubt, and annoyance with a hint of relief on her face. 'What? How?' Inoue asked. 'I'll pay the rest of your debt. As long as you come up with the money, they don't care, right?' I asked. Well, there went whatever I made for doing the Defiance show. ...Tokyo, too. 'I... no. You can't do that. What about Annabelle-san?!' she asked. 'Don't worry about us, Inoue. I'll eat it in the pocketbook a bit, but it's alright to me if you're not in trouble again.' I said, reassuringly. Truth be told, though, if this girl got into this kind of problem again, I wouldn't be able to help her again. Inoue gave me this strange doe-eyed look that I didn't expect from her. And that's when she lunged for me. I braced myself, because when she lunged for me under normal circumstances, she was looking to take my head off. What came was not a physical assault, but rather, an embrace. I couldn't say anything, Perhaps it's best if I didn't. One thing still nagged in the back of my head, though. I had no idea what Inoue was doing to pay back her debt. Part of me didn't want to know. * . * I returned to Inoue's house with Inoue walking behind me. She apologized to her brother and spent a long time talking with him, though I didn't catch any of that conversation since I went to my own room and broke the news to Annabelle. Although she was initially upset that I was willing to settle Inoue's debt ourselves, especially considering the tight money situation we had on our hands that led to me becoming Baron von Blackberry in the first place, she ultimately was happy that her best friend wouldn't be in that much trouble any more. I hoped not, anyway. 'You always put others before yourself.' Annabelle said, before she playfully smacked me in the shoulder, 'You should really stop that.' 'Yeah, I know.' I said, rubbing my shoulder. She was right, though. I'm not a saint by any stretch of the imagination, but I did care a little too much about the well being of my friends. Even if they liked to pound my flesh into indescribable piles of goo, as Inoue did. I had this weird sensation that some day, that habit of mine was going to be the death of me. I hoped that day wasn't in the near future.
