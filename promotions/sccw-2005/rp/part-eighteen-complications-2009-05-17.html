Part Eighteen: Complications

17 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/17/09 I don’t know what the hell I’m supposed to do now. Hell, I don’t even know how I’m supposed to start this off. I’ve never really been one for the whole blogging thing, y’know? Sure, I’ve done Twitter and myBook and all the rest of the social networking thing but that’s usually been for pretty simple stuff. "Party at Jen’s house, her parents are away!" "Dudes, the new Star Trek movie was awesome!" "How the hell does Zac Efron still have a career?" Meaningless stuff. I’ve never felt the need to share what I was thinking or feeling with the rest of the world because, let’s be honest here, no one really gives a shit. Now I’ve got Doctor Monroe telling me that I need to get my feelings out, that having a journal will help me process everything that I’ve been through lately. Personally, I think he’s full of shit. I know what I’ve been through lately. My mom died three weeks ago and everybody (well, not my family ‘cause they’re cool like that) seems either pissed off or weirded out that I’m not back to being happy and smiling just yet. Principal Adams calling me into his office, telling me how worried he is, how he’s concerned about me, how he thinks I should see the school counselor. Bullshit. He’s just concerned about jeopardizing our winning season and our chance at State. But I do it anyway. I go because…you ever have those days when it’s taking all of your energy just to put one foot in front of the other, where it’s a personal triumph if you just manage to make it through the day? Since my mom died, every day’s been like that for me. And things like complaining and arguing…they require energy that I just don’t have right now, you know? So I just agree cause I can’t deal with the argument. I know Dad’s worried about me even though he doesn’t think I know. We’ve been close as hell forever, we can’t really hide anything from each other. Not that we haven’t tried over the years… Damn, it’s been so long since this all started. So many years that Dad, Mom and I were living with the sword hanging over our heads…until it finally dropped three weeks ago. And I know I shouldn’t think this kind of thing but I can’t help it…did she know it was all gonna end like this? Did she know, when the doctor dropped the bomb on her that the countdown had begun and time was just slowly ticking away? Alanna Marshall has never liked waiting rooms much. They were inevitable cold, sterile, soulless places filled with uncomfortable chairs and out of date magazines. Eight times out of ten, she thought, you were never in a waiting room for a good reason. It was just a matter of finding out how bad "bad" really was. The problems had started a few months back, after a show whose location Alanna could no longer clearly recall. She was used to the standard aches and pains after a show. Every profession had its occupational hazards, she figured, and some were more physically demanding than others. Her typical plan of attack had been to down a few Excedrin, try to grab a good night’s sleep and hope for the best. This time, however, it hadn’t worked. The pains had still been there in the morning and had, in fact, stayed there for several mornings more. Sleep was also starting to become an issue. No matter how much of it she got, it seemed it was never enough. The tiredness wasn’t there all the time…and sometimes, it was just a dull throb in the back of her brain that she could push away with enough effort and caffeine. It had only been when her favorite pair of True Religion jeans had gotten too big for her that she had started to worry. Weight loss on the road was not that uncommon, given how infrequently and badly she tended to eat. But these had been her skinny jeans, the pair she should have been able to fit into within a good five pound margin. So she had gone to her general practitioner for a check-up. Perhaps it was the flu, she thought, or some weird virus she’d gotten exposed to somewhere. God forbid, it might even be that weird H1N1 thing that was going around. But whatever it was, Alanna thought, it was something that could be dealt with. Medicine, rest, maybe a few weeks off…nothing more serious than that. So she had been poked and prodded and tested. When the results had come back, Doctor Cottrone had been…alarmed. Given that Doctor Cottrone never tended to venture into the same neighborhood as alarmed, this had been slightly alarming for Alanna. There had been no mention of anything specific that might have been wrong, no outlining of a course of treatment. Just a referral to a specialist and a request to keep her informed. So Alanna had made another appointment, this time with the specialist. Once again, she had been poked and prodded and tested. This time, there had been a discussion when the results came back. Medical terminology, recommended courses of treatment, urgent need. The same concerned, supportive tone of voice that Alanna imagined he had given all of his patients in this situation. All of the details lost, washed away in a white haze that came over her the minute she heard one word. That word. She had rejected the idea immediately, dismissed it out of hand. She tried to watch what she ate, exercised as much as she could, took what she understood to be all the necessary precautions. There was no way that the end result could be…this. It had been a mistake. Someone had misread a report or switched test results or some other such nonsense. So she had made another appointment, resolved to go through another round of poking and prodding and testing. This time, she thought, they would find out what was really wrong with her. She would take some pills, take some time off and everything would return to normal. She was now waiting for the third specialist to see her to discuss her results. That certainty that nothing serious was wrong was almost dead now, replaced with a growing dread that not only had things gotten bad but that they were about to get a whole lot worse. Once again, the same words had cropped up. CA-125 Test. Keyhole surgery. Possible Mucinous Cystadenocarcinoma. And that word again. "Mrs. Marshall, are you all right?" Alanna blinked, slowly realizing that she hadn’t been paying attention to what the specialist had been saying. She wondered just how long she’d been oblivious and hoped it hadn’t been too obvious. "I’m sorry." "No need to apologize, Mrs. Marshall. Given what we’ve discussed, it’s not surprising. I do have to ask you, though: are you okay to drive?" Alanna shook her head. "I didn’t drive myself here, my car’s in the shop getting serviced. I’ll call my husband, let him know to come and pick me up." The specialist nodded. "That’s good to know. If possible, I’d like to schedule an appointment with your husband. I find that things tend to go better when both partners are involved in the whole process." Hands are shaken, pleasantries exchanged, promises to call and make follow up appointments made. As Alanna walked across the waiting room and into the elevator, she found her mind a whirl of ideas and thoughts. What were they going to do? How was she going to tell Lance what was happening? What about Zach? As the elevator doors opened on the ground floor, Alanna continued on her way out of the hospital, oblivious to the world around her. As she reached into her purse, fumbling for her iPhone, a voice broke through her fog. It was quite honestly the last voice she expected to hear in these particular circumstances. "Lani?" There was a very short list of people that Alanna Marshall wanted nothing to do with at this particular moment in time. Kathryn Shaw was very close to the top of that list. Quite unfortunate, then, that it was the same Kathryn Shaw who was now standing not ten feet away from her. Ah, my "Aunt" Kathy. And by "Aunt", I mean "Friends with my mom since forever when she’s not trying to kill her and my dad hates her guts and I think I’ve heard him use every single variation on the word whore to describe her". But, like most things in my life these days, that’s just way too fucking complicated. So, I’ll go with "Aunt". "Aunt" Kathy’s been in my life since I was a kid. She was the one who gave me the sex talk, y’know? Freaked my parents out big time. Mom wasn’t happy and Dad nearly lost it completely. I can understand that, too, it’s kinda like Guido getting the sex talk from Saraghina in "Nine". But I gotta admit…she knew what she was talking about. I’ve tried a few of the things she told me about and…oh boy, does she know what she’s talking about. The only reason I even know her at all is because she was friends with Mom. I guess Mom saw something in her, something more than the person everybody thought she was. Because if that’s all there was to her…trust me, Mom would never have been friends with her. Get "Aunt" Kathy and my dad in the same room, though… "How the hell did you…no, you know what? I’m not even going to ask." "Everyone on staff needs to get their medicals run by Nigel for insurance purposes. Trust me, Lani, it wasn’t that hard to find you. Now, what the hell is going on?" Alanna didn’t answer, stalling Katsidy with a raised hand as she spoke on her iPhone. "Yeah, baby, I’m done with my appointment. Yeah, we need to talk. I’ll see you soon." Hanging up the phone and slinging it back in her purse, she turned her attention back to her long time…well, we’ll go with frenemy here. "Lance is gonna be here soon so I really wouldn’t stick around." "I’m not going anywhere. Not until you tell me what’s wrong." "Who says anything’s wrong?" Katsidy sighed, a look of pure disbelief crossing her face at her long time…yeah, frenemy works…trying to pass off such an obvious line of bullshit. "Lani, I’m the bullshit artist here. You’re no good at lying, you never have been. And besides, three visits to three separate hospitals in three months? Something is definitely wrong." There comes a point where you just get tired of fighting. Where giving in seems like the only sensible option. So, Alanna Marshall did not give Kathryn Shaw the fight she was expecting. Instead, she told her everything. Her diagnosis, her prognosis, the projected course of treatment, the expected mortality rate. All of it. It was one of the few times in her life that Kathryn Shaw would recall being well and truly speechless. My parents are my heroes, you know? I look up to them, want to be like them. I know every kid says that they look up to their parents but I really did. Hell, when I was a kid, I thought they were super heroes! I mean, there they were on TV, every week, fighting the bad guys. Far as I knew, Mom should’ve been teaming up with Wonder Woman and Dad should have been kicking Superman’s ass! But I remember that day… "Does Lance know?" Alanna simply nodded. "Oh God, Lani, I’m so sorry…" It wasn’t typical of Kathryn Shaw to go for any gesture of affection towards another female, much less one as intimate as a hug. But, at that moment, all of that was lost on Shaw. She moved to embrace Alanna and the two women just stood there in silence. A silence which was eventually broken by a screeching of breaks and a rather loud slamming of a car door. The voice which followed could be heard clear across the hospital parking lot. "Get. The. Hell. Away. From. My. WIFE!" Lance Marshall was not a happy man around Kathryn Shaw at the best of times. The sight of her at moments like these, however, was near enough to send him apoplectic with rage. So, as Kathryn Shaw saw Lance Marshall storming his way across the parking lot, she did the only thing she could think of and positioned Alanna between herself and her husband. "You people have no fucking shame, do you?" Lance bellowed at Katsidy. "Following my wife to the hospital?! Is the concept of basic human decency so completely alien to you that you can pull shit like this and think it’s okay?!" "I am here," snapped Katsidy, never one to be cowed, "checking up on my friend who I haven’t spoken to in weeks. Someone who could use your support right now instead of having you bellow at me!" Lance’s first thought was to lunge for Kathryn, to smack her right across the face and knock her to the ground. But some voice at the back of his heads screamed that unless it was inside the ring, there was no excuse to hit a woman, ever. So he decided to follow through on his second though. As Kathryn Shaw wiped away the strand of saliva from her face, she had the decency not to look surprised. "You people are a fucking cancer!" Lance screamed at Katsidy. "You’re a stain, taking everything you touch and trying to turn it dark and ugly. And you have the nerve to stand here and tell me how I should be acting towards my wife?! You fucking disgust me." "Alanna," he added in a much lower tone of voice, "come on, let’s go. We’ll talk more at home." Husband and wife turned, Lance wrapping his arm around Alanna’s shoulders and pulling her in closely to him. Katsidy simply stared after them, watching the two of them with a look of infinite sadness. When asked if she cried that day, she would of course deny it. I remember what Mom was like when she got home. I’ve always know Mom and Dad to be so strong, so tough, like they could take on anyone. When I looked at them that day, that’s how I knew something was wrong. Before they even told me anything…Mom and Dad looked so fragile. Like they would break if they moved too hard or too quickly. And that scared the hell out of me. They had been home for two hours. The first hour had been spent together. Alanna had been numb, relating everything the doctor had told her. Lance had held her closely, needlessly telling her that he would be there for her every step of the way, that he loved her no matter what, that she would beat this. Lance was now in the study, calling the rest of the family and breaking the news to them. She could hear his voice catch in his throat as he shared the bad news, hear him on the verge of tears as he let the family express their support and comfort as best they could. Laying on the sofa, staring out of the window, Alanna caught herself feeling horrible for causing her husband so much pain. Her reverie was broken by a small, plaintive voice by her side. "Mommy?" Zach Marshall loved his Mommy and Daddy very much. He hadn’t seen them very much on this day and, from the way they were talking earlier, he could tell something very bad had happened. He was scared and he was sad. Alanna could feel her heart ripping in two at the sight of the tears in her baby boy’s eyes. "Hey, baby." "Are you okay, Mommy?" You could lie, Alanna thought. Tell him you’re just tired. Tell him things are okay. Try to spare him from what’s going on. No. Not to my son. "No, baby. I went to the doctor today and…I got some bad news. But I’m gonna fight it and I’m gonna beat it. You wait and see." Zach didn’t respond with words. Instead, he climbed onto the sofa, snuggling up against his mother and wrapping his arms tightly around her as if she would disappear if he let go. Burying his face in her side, Alanna could feel the tears start to well in her eyes as Zach’s tears began to fall. "I’m sorry you’re sick, Mommy." Alanna reached down and stroked her son’s hair. "Me too, baby. Me too." Ovarian cancer. There’s only so much you can shield a kid from. Mom and Dad tried but I heard it eventually. Didn’t understand what it meant at the time, of course. I was only six, for fuck’s sake. But I gradually began to see what those strange words meant. Why Mom was at the hospital so often. Why I never had a brother or a sister. Why things were so weird for so long. They really did think that would be the end of it, you know. Mom and Dad thought that this was just going to be another obstacle to overcome, another enemy to be beaten. I believed it too. My parents were Wonder Woman and Superman, after all. They were not going to be defeated by something like cancer. But what is it they say? Beware the light at the end of the tunnel…it may just be an oncoming train. My name is Zach Marshall and this, like most of my life…is complicated.
