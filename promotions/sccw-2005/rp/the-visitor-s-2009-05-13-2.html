The Visitor(s)

13 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/13/09 "We might die from medication, but we sure killed all the pain." – Conor Oberst * * * It was too surreal to be happening. And yet too vivid to be a dream. The sky was an explosion of orange and red. It looked like a postcard. And like a postcard, nothing seemed to be moving. There were specks overhead that seemed to be birds, frozen in flight. Tall grass sat motionless as there was no wind here. It was a tangible silence, a loud silence even. The landscape seemed to go on forever with no hills or valleys. Lane Stevens was lying in a bed in the middle of this scene, unaware and unconcerned about how he arrived in such a situation. But it wasn’t just any bed, he decided. It was the one his mother had purchased for him after moving out on his own for the first time. He felt held in place by tubes and machines, yet he could see no visual evidence of them. The River Rat was wearing a hospital gown. Scratch that, he was now wearing black track pants and a white t-shirt. "Okay," he said aloud, and the birds overhead began to move. The grass began to sway. Lane had done enough drugs to know that painkillers often gave him unusual dreams, and after a few moments he concluded this was the explanation, the only one that had any merit. A lot of people had a habit of panicking during a lucid dream, but he really reveled in it. If he had the option, he would never wake up. For better or worse, there were only feelings here. No expectations, no consequences. He could get behind that. The Hand’s WMD turned his head to the right, and was only slightly started to see a child standing at his bed side. The child did not seem to have a discernable gender. It wore a Snoopy t-shirt and had a constantly evolving hairstyle. Also, it had no face. "Yep," Stevens started, his laughter echoing into the infinite postcard that surrounded him, "Just your standard faceless freako kid. Why not?" "Why do you joke?" the child responded, "you know who I am." And Lane’s laughter melted away. "Yeah, suppose I do. How ya been?" "I do not know how to answer that." Lane seemed to consider this, and turned his attention straight ahead. But the child was now standing in front of his bed. The hair continued to flicker from blonde to red to black and everything in-between. It changed from long to short and back again. "Not really a…complicated question," Lane finally offered, and he felt himself shrink away. "But I don’t exist," the child said. "I don’t like this anymore," Lane said, looking around frantically. There was so much space, but no where to go. His mouth felt numb when he talked, the words were thick, hard to get out. "They get any doors over there in the uh…in the sky? Maybe I just slip outta here. Maybe just wake up." But the child was not deterred. "Am I why you don’t smile?" "I’m the cocky guy! I smile plenty!" The River Rat fired back, and attempted a smile. But it didn’t work. His face formed a kind of goofy grimace, and he immediately felt self conscious about it. "No you don’t," was the child’s reply. "No real smiles." Eventually Lane’s eyes stopped darting around. He briefly felt himself fighting something. Tears, perhaps? Maybe. But after a deep breath a sense of comfort washed over him. "You’re just a drop in the bucket, kiddo. There’s no other way this could have turned out for me. Would have found something to be pissed at the world about. But I’m over that, ya know? Now stuff like this is only buried in dreams and acid trips. I’m a parody now. It’s easier. It’s better." And then, without warning, Eugene Ware flew by on a rhinoceros. "Yeah, you’re gonna have that," Lane said, and waved at the color commentator. A few moments of awkward silence. "Thanks for visiting," the child said, its features still morphing at a dizzying rate. Before he could respond… * * * …he woke up. And he was still in a bed. Although this one was a hospital bed. He was hooked to machines that monitored his vitals, and an IV that controlled his pain level. Cradled in his left hand was a small device with a red button that would administer morphine as he saw fit (up to a certain point). He couldn’t see it yet, but his face was badly bruised, and scarred over. Under his right eye there was a mark three inches wide that will never heal. The same goes for a smaller mark on his forehead. His eyes scanned the room, trying to remember where he was, and why he was there. He had one visitor. Walter Halvorson was seated in a chair next to the bed, dressed in a navy blue sport coat and matching dress pants. He was concentrating intensely on the day’s crossword puzzle, when Lane’s stirring made him put it aside. "Rough night," Walter said uncomfortably. And then it came back to the River Rat. Through a foggy memory, he could see Amy Campbell mounted on top of him, punching away. He could feel his windpipe being crushed by a paintball gun. He could smell his own blood. Rough night didn’t really begin to cover it. "Alexandra is a little confused why you would pick a fight with someone who you were not allowed to harm. She is willing to look past this incident. She is a gracious and merciful woman, Mr. Stevens." In another time, Lane would have been talking a mile a minute at this point. He would have explained that she found him, and she was looking for trouble. The Hand’s WMD might have called her a few nasty names, like cunt or bitch. He would have called Walter to task on his unconvincing script. Alex is a lot of things, but merciful is not one of them. He would have told Walter to spare the bullshit, and just get down to business, time is money, etc. He most certainly would not have proceeded this way: Lane smiled lazily as his old friend morphine reintroduced itself. "Tell her I’m sorry Walt, must have lost my head." Walter nodded, as if this was the only appropriate response. "But I did not come here to scold you," Halvorson said, now feeling a bit of power. It was a rare feeling for a man like him, so he decided to savor it for a moment. "I came here to inquire about your willingness to partake in some of Alexandra’s other projects. She feels that your skill set could become an asset in certain endeavors. She would have waited until you were out of the hospital, but time has become an issue, and if you are onboard certain people’s services are no longer needed." Lane studied his visitor carefully, cocking his head to the side slightly. That lazy smile was still present. Walter hated this. He hated that a man like this would be trusted to fetch his boss coffee much less take part in… "You hate this," Lane mused. "You hate me. These things that you carry around, these burdens, I have already set them all down. You don’t know whether to applaud me, or throw up." Walter was looking at his shoes. He said, "You will be compensated most handsomely for your efforts, I think you’ll find that…" Lane interrupted: "…and the worst thing is, you know that deep down you don’t really care about these fucking people that you’re hurting, and you don’t know why part of your brain pretends to." Neither man spoke for some time. The River Rat felt almost hypnotized by the steady rhythm of beeps, the only noise in the room. "Whatever it is," Lane started, "I’ll do it." "I can give you a summary first, you don’t need to…" "I’ll do it," Lane said sternly, and Walter shut up. They sat in silence again, before it was broken by a most unexpected visitor. She might have walked in with a smile that only got wider when she saw Stevens in that bed, but something about her posture made it clear that the woman had no honest idea why she was standing there at that moment. But she'd fake it. ''You're not lookin' so hot. Anything I can get you? Hookers, cheese, maybe a Brompton Cocktail?'' Immediately Walter stood up, looking back and forth between Lane and his guest. There was a kind of panic, a frenzy if you will, written all over his face. How on earth would he explain this to Alexandra? "Not here…you can’t…NURSE!" "Zip it Walter," Lane said. He was momentarily avoiding eye contact with his new visitor. "And take a walk." Walter again looked back and forth between the two. The realization that slowly dawned on him was that he couldn’t break this up if even if he wanted to. He started to talk, but decided against it. He grabbed his newspaper, and a moment later he was gone. Lane stared down at his chest and clicked the red morphine button one time, before finally looking up at Amy Campbell. "You win?" ''You care?'' ''Well, you look pretty pissed.'' Amy narrowed her eyes. ''Kennedy got at my husband from behind with a chain. Belt's still mine, and I'm gonna cripple him for it. Familiar promise, huh?'' Stevens nodded, a smirk forming at the corners of his mouth, threatening to break into an all out smile. "Ah yes, the rile up Amy Campbell playbook. It’s been a while since someone checked that out of the library. I don’t get any royalties or anything. You believe that shit?" ''The kind of idiots who'd jack your plays are too cheap to pay anyway.'' She said. ''I'm here because Darcy's here getting checked out. I think Alex might be, too. Figured seeing you in pain would brighten my day a little. Maybe groan a little for me, pop a stitch or something?'' "You could always suffocate me with a pillow for a little while," Lane said, and shrugged. "I know you didn’t come here to apologize, but I’m not mad. Not even a bit. I used to constantly plot revenge, all the fucking time. I don’t really plot anything anymore though, it’s exhausting. I’m not sorry about anything I did to you, and I never will be. But that doesn’t mean its excuseable. It doesn’t mean its right. I don’t even know what that word means." His laughter turned into a cough, and then back into laughter again. "Right. What the fuck is that?" ''The feeling after they said I'd broken your neck.'' Campbell was leaned against the wall, but her arms came unfolded. ''And it was pretty wrong that it felt so right, but the fact that I'm honestly not the least bit sorry about it's sorta shaped the success I've become.'' ''What about now? Does it feel right? Do you feel better?'' Lane had looked her in the eye when he'd said that, and it caused Amy to bow her head before she spoke next. A heavy sigh came first. ''I... I'm not really sure. I don't feel much of anything right now.'' The River Rat studied her then with a faint smile. He thought of several cruel things to say, and let them pass. Instead quite the opposite came out. "This won’t mean much coming from me, probably less than much…but I’m proud of ya kiddo. Everything you said the other night is correct. You have become better than me, better than the chodes in this federation. Maybe I was starting to figure that out back then, who knows. I can see why Alexandra thinks so highly of you." Amy looked up at him suddenly. They shared a knowing look that faded quickly and after that, she wouldn't meet his eyes, but there was a monumental moment; she sat down. Amy Campbell slipped into a visitor's chair in the hospital room where Lane Stevens laid -- and both were still breathing. Still, she wouldn't look up and distracted herself with anything she could - even picking at the edges of the Jell-o cup the nurse had left for Lane. ''You're right, it means nothing to me.'' The fact that she'd addressed him at all put the lie to her words. ''And you seem to see a lot more into why she thinks so highly of me. At least, that's the best I can gather from the shit you started to ask about what my husband did and didn't know.'' There was still no eye contact; it was obvious her wolfen eyes contained a weakness she didn't want observed. "My instincts are all I have left Amy," Stevens started, "but if they’re wrong, they’re wrong." He wanted to keep her comfortable, to keep her in that chair. This conversation intrigued him. "Another one of my instincts says my boss would love to put me in a box and ship me back to Iowa. A lot of people would be insecure about this, a little peeved. I think the whole thing is hilarious. I have a front row seat to a very compelling drama. The only thing keeping me around is that The Hand is running on fumes, and borrowed time. Kennedy has enough of Katsidy’s cunny juice rolling around in his head, the fucker thinks he can lead. He hasn’t said it…but it’s all over his face. I wonder what vision he has for the Hand’s future, other than standing around and looking cocky. This is why I don’t make plans anymore. Because once they succeed…then what?" ''Then you find yourself sleeping with the same person who brings back the guy you'd planned to cripple.'' She still wouldn't raise her head. ''And after you go after him the second time, you find yourself sitting by his hospital bed wondering why you aren't so happy to have hurt him this go around.'' At that, she eased out of the chair. Amy was still clearly aching from her most recent match, or the hundreds before it, but she was just as obviously trying not to show Lane that. The slightly cocky tinge to her accent didn't fade. ''Anyway... I have a husband to get to, and you've probably got some internal bleeding to do.'' The River Rat laughed aloud. "Sure do champ. By the way, loved the paintball gun. Nice touch." In that room, for that moment, you could almost close your eyes and forget that these were two very bad people. * * * As it were these were not the only bad people in the world. A few days earlier, Walter Halvorson encountered a man that really hammered that point home. This is a harmless interlude. The kind of moment that you might skip over right away, and come back to months from now when things have gone all wrong. In fact, there are very few ways for this to go other than wrong. "It’s an interesting dog, Mr. Sharnell." "You can call me Re…eh shit, better just stick with Mr. Sharnell. Don’t want this shit gettin’ too comfy." "Yes sir, Mr. Sharnell." Walter, clad in a pair of khakis and a white button up shirt, looked a tad out of place here. He walked side by side with Reginald Sharnell at the Highstand dog park in Laird, Minnesota. Curiously enough, Walter carried a small plastic bag with him. It was provided at the gate, to clean up the feces of the animal. They gazed ahead at where Reginald’s dog thrashed about in the water. "It’s an Otterhound," Reginald said. All around them were dogs without leashes, and their owners doting after them. Reggie appeared to be only black man, or minority of any kind, in the park. A fact not lost on him. He stood six foot five and was pushing two fifty. He wore a University of Minnesota t-shirt, and black sweat pants. "They got their name from hunting otters, yes?" Walter inquired. He did research on the breed before making the trip, as Alex had mentioned how much Mr. Sharnell loved the animal. "You must be some kinda fuckin’ genius," Reginald mused. They caught up to the shaggy dog. It was black and tan, and seemed to really genuinely love the water. There was a small pool set up for dogs, and a few others played along. Reginald made a clicking noise with his mouth, and the dog leaped out of the water, and followed them along. "You can throw him treats, or whatever the fuck, and it ain’t no substitute for a nice juicy otter, ya hear me? I know a lot of people that act civilized a good deal of the time, like they can take orders and shit. They be on their best behavior, but at the end of the day…they want blood." Me too, Walter thought. "Alex, she’s got her nose in a lot…queen of all trades so to speak. I like that. All else fails, we ask Alex, especially for something on that side of the states. I’m out of the way, out here in bum fuck nowhere, and I like that too. She’s never sent no country club lookin’ motherfucker up here before, especially when the money is right." "That’s the thing," Walter started, and Reggie stopped and looked back at him. Walter thought he looked older than thirty five, by a good ten years maybe. He cocked an eyebrow at Halvorson. "What’s the thing?" "Thing is, the money is too right." And with that, Reginald chuckled aloud and continued to walk. Walter went after him, his heart beating a little quicker than it was previously. "What you’re asking is simple, way simpler than the reward indicates. That’s a lot of…that’s a lot of money Mr. Sharnell," Walter stammered. "Tell me somethin’ I don’t fuckin’ know. Oh, and that’s you," Reginald responded and pointed to the ground where a fresh piece of feces sat. The dog ran ahead, chasing a tennis ball that his master threw. With no questions asked, Walter bent over and grabbed the droppings with his plastic bag. Such is life, he thought. He wondered if the day would come when someone would clean up all of his shit. Walter tried to imagine the size of that plastic bag. * * * An excerpt from Lane Stevens’ journal – May 13th, 2009. Had a dream the other night. A kid with no face visited me, first time in a while. But I don’t feel as if I have the energy or the words to write about that this evening. So alas if you have stolen this journal, and you want to get to the real juicy stuff that psychiatrist hacks will attempt to use to explain my fundamental disconnection from the human species, then skip ahead or whatever. It’ll be a real hoot. Anywho, training for Dusk this week. This is a man that claims that wrestling is about integrity, and respect. After all that I have seen, this notion so laughable I cannot even begin to argue with it. This is a man that has built an entire imaginary world around himself just so he doesn’t have to face the fact that everything he strives to protect is a joke. Most of the fans he claims to stick up for would give anything to have Alexandra’s clout, and once they had it they would certainly use it to crush men like him. But despite all of these things, I can’t help but like the stubborn bastard. I like his motor. No matter how much dumb shit comes out of his mouth, he will bleed for his perfect world, he will bleed every last drop. While I don’t think this is admirable, I do find it most entertaining. I used to be able to get inside of the heads of people like Craig, but I find more and more though, that I can’t even relate. Sometimes I don’t think that I am human anymore. * * * A black sedan sped along the highway, darker than the night itself. Inside, Walter Halvorson pilots this rental car. He is tapping the steering wheel in rhythm to the CCR on the radio. In the passenger seat, The River Rat looked exhausted. His head rested against the window, his eyes were almost rolled up in his head. He had insisted on a pit stop at a bar they passed, and had done several shots with the locals. He had insisted on another pit stop a few miles later, to throw up. Sometimes I feel as if I am only an amplifier, turning disagreements into arguments. Arguments into fists. And fists into worse. A sign up ahead illuminated the night slightly, making Lane Stevens stir. But moreso than that, I feel like a permanent visitor. As homeless as any bum you’ll ever see in your life. The sign says: Welcome to Salt Lake City! I wouldn’t have it any other way. The River Rat closes his eyes, a peaceful smile present on his lips.
