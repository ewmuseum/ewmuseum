Date: 7/4/09 July 4, 2009. ''Turn around and put your hands on the hood.'' The security officer's tone brooked no disagreement, and neither did the firearm holstered on his hip. He thumbed off the snap-on closure, cupping the weapon in the space between thumb and forefinger. Red, white, and blue lights painted the stucco walls in patriotic colors, the nearby hedge drawing oddly shaped shadows. ''What seems to be the problem, officer?'' The man was downright scrawny, his voice was touched by an accent that spoke of his upbringing on the far side of the Atlantic. ''My ladyfriend and I were just enjoying the spectacular scenery in—'' The interruption came courtesy of a forceful hand that dropped him facefirst against the cold metal of the sedan's hood with a hollow thud. ''I asked you to put your hands on the hood of the car, sir.'' ''Don't hurt him, officer!'' A brunette stood awkwardly nearby, nervousness preventing her from nearing the two. ''He wasn't doin' anything!'' Her voice was whspery, high-pitched, and it did not speak well for her intellect. The rent-a-cop jabbed a thick finger at her. ''Please stay where you are, ma'am.'' ''It's all right, love,'' the man said. ''Just let the nice officer do his job, and I'll buy you one of them pretty little fruity drinks you like.'' ''With the umbrellas?'' ''Yeh, whatever you want.'' He managed to nod with a hand on the back of his neck. The man didn't move even when the officer shifted positions to frisk him. ''Well, what do we have here?'' The security officer dipped a hand into the front pocket of the man's slacks, withdrawing a sleeve of dusky pink casino chips, still bound together. The Brit managed to look back over his shoulder. ''I promise I can explain all that...'' The brunette gasped, hand going to her mouth. ''You said you was just happy to see me, Tommy!'' The security guard straightened, pressing the button on the walkie-talkie clasped to his shoulder. ''This is Westgate. I've got him on the north side of the casino.'' The man – ''Tommy'' – turned his bright blue gaze to the brunette, brows lifted in expectation. ''This is when you're supposed to, y'know...'' he hissed. ''Kick 'em in the face an' all.'' His companion said nothing, crossing her arms with a huff of a sigh, her expression not changing, even when Westgate nodded, pulling a pair of steel handcuffs off his belt. ''Fine, fine. I see how it is.,'' Tommy muttered as the other man wrenched him back to his feet. ''Well, you ain't throwin' me under no bleedin' bus.'' He strained to look behind him to the casino's security guard. ''Maggie there ain't a real brunette, Tiny.'' ''Name ain't Tiny,'' Westgate snarled, tugging the man towards the building. ''And I don't rightly care what color her pussy hair is.'' Tommy sighed, shaking his head. ''That ain't what I mean, brother. I mean her hair, it ain't real.'' ''How dare you talk about a lady's hair!'' Maggie all but stomped her foot, fixing Tommy with a hard glare, but the comment had drawn the guard's eyes back to the woman. ''It's a wig, mate,'' Tommy continued defiantly, and he was rewarded with a hard shove that sent him into the wall. Westgate turned, lumbering towards Maggie with his hand falling again to his firearm. ''Ma'am, if you wouldn't mind...'' ''I don't know what he's talkin' abou—'' The brunette sighed, fists coming up. ''Oh, the hell with this.'' She stepped forward into a left cross, but Westgate twisted the opposite way, clamping his hand atop the woman's head. When her momentum carried her forward, he jerked the wig off, the revealed pinned-up blood-red locks dull like rust in the flickering lights. ''I'm—'' Alexandra Pierce flicked her glare from Tommy to the officer as she rocked up to the balls of her feet. ''I know,'' Tommy said off-handedly. ''You're gonna kill me.'' ''This is Westgate!'' The shoulder walkie came into play again. ''Officer in need of assistance!'' Pierce pushed off into a series of smooth, spinning kicks that drove the man back but did little damage. And all it took was a single overhand right from Westgate to send Alex stumbling backwards, spinning down to a knee. She brushed the knuckles of her left hand against her lips as she turned falsely green eyes up towards the building as more security bustled through the door. ''This is not how this was supposed to go,'' she murmured, fixing Tommy (though that was not his name) with her glare. He offered her a shrug and a half a grin in response. * * * * * Twenty-six hours earlier. It was a night like any other night. Most nights, Alexandra Pierce and Amy Campbell, the pair SCCW fans called ''DNA'' (among other, less savory, terms used to describe them), were far too busy to just relax. Alex had her fingers in nearly every pie in Sin City, and the demands brought about by Amy's role as the federation's singles champion were nearly endless. Not to mention that pesky ''marriage'' thing Campbell was still devoted to. Their schedules would be packed to the gills without Pierce's extracurricular activities, which saw the Spider jet-set across the country and around the world for... well, for some things they couldn't very well discuss at a corner booth in a TGI Friday's outside Albuquerque, a rare moment alone for the pair that was secretly (or perhaps not-so-secretly) a couple. ''This brownie thing is ridiculous.'' Campbell did most of the talking – as was usually the case. She waved her spoon as she addressed the other woman. ''Like, it should be criminal for a fucking cookie-cutter restaurant like this to have a dessert this good. You really should have some.'' Pierce leaned back in the wooden booth, lifting a small coffee cup. ''I told you, I'm fine.'' A small smile snuck across the taller redhead's lips, an expression that would have seemed out of place just a few months ago. ''Your loss. Anyway... we need to kick Kennedy in the head again this week. That was a good time.'' ''It was not an unpleasant trip, no.'' Alex set her cup onto its saucer, reaching across the table to lay her hand over Amy's. Pierce hadn't worn her gloves in weeks – even at SCCW's biweekly ''Temptation'' program, the woman they called the Spider only covered her hands when she taped her fists for a match. ''But I'd trade it all for another two days before you go back.'' ''I'd have to see if I could get away with giving that to you.'' Amy spoke with a strained tone. ''But after last week, it'll be tough to excuse more time away, and even you can't squeeze more days into a week.'' ''I know. I haven't found anything juicy on Father Time yet.'' Alex smiled – a dark, sullen expression – as she withdrew her hand a millimeter or two. Pierce never looked up, but a breath later, their server set down the check with a murmured ''I'll be your cashier whenever you ladies are ready.'' The flick of the Spider's eyes silenced the girl, sending her bustling away. ''I just...'' Alex trailed off, staring into the dregs of her coffee. ''We're always so preoccupied. With wrestling. With my work. With...'' ''Each other?'' Alex smiled, just a little bit, but that's all Amy wanted. ''I wasn't talking about that part,'' Alex continued, and her eyes wouldn't come up. ''I end up missing you when you're gone, and...'' She turned on her side of the bench, slipping her wallet from her laptop case – Alexandra Pierce did not carry a purse. Amy's brow quirked, yet she still grinned. ''And you get so lonely, hookers become the only option? Because I'm really hoping this isn't an admission of some sort, and it's more like Alexandra Pierce actually begging for something. It's not like I could tell anyone about it, either – the hookers, they might believe. The begging? Not in a million years. I kinda like that I get the private show.'' ''I'm not really... good at this.'' Pierce unzipped her wallet, thumbing through the contents. ''I have never had a relationship that consisted of... you know, a relationship.'' A considering quiver curled through her brow. ''They're just credit cards, Al.'' Amy dropped her fork onto the table, half-rising to snatch the wallet off the table. ''Ooookay, they're just a lot of credit cards. How many people live in here?'' ''Four.'' Alex didn't demand her property back or reach for it. She just turned her hand over, knowing Campbell would give it back at her own leisure. ''There used to be a fifth, but she died in a boating accident.'' Pierce lifted the coffee cup, hiding her smile. ''You've gotta teach me how to do that,'' Amy chuckled, though wide-eyed as she looked through the wallet. ''I could be recession-proof with five cars and another few flatscreens – all I'd need to do is surrender to the Sith or whatever gets you all of that creepy shit. Is this rude, by the way? I mean, shouldn't Ashe or someone be jumping out of another booth to beat me to a pulp for spying?'' Alex opened her mouth to speak, but Campbell was, as always, too quick on the verbal draw. ''And fuck this, I'd never know who to sign as. We're using my card anyway. I don't like feeling like your trick.'' She finally looked up, pulling her own collection of ID and cards out of her tote bag. ''Actually, is it wrong that I wouldn't care so much about looking like an escort if I looked like a pricier one? I mean, TGI Friday's? Really? I'd get someone to spring for something higher-end... like Pizza Hut or something.'' ''I like normal things,'' Alex said. ''Fancy restaurants and expensive dinners have never really satisfied me. Besides, it's the closest restaurant to the hotel room, and I mean to maximize what little time I do have.'' Amy slid a Visa card into the pouch atop the leather check presenter, standing it up at the end of the table. ''Hey,'' Campbell said with a grin. ''I still have half a sundae to eat, you know.'' Pierce's smile didn't match the fire in those gray eyes. ''I can wait as long as you can.'' The two women held each other's gaze as the smaller redhead picked her spoon back up, perfectly arched brow rising in a silent dare. Neither looked to the thin man with short blond hair, who scooped the check up with a quiet, ''Let me get that for you ladies.'' ''No offense, but I get you a couple of times on a good day. Nice ice cream's hard to come by!'' ''Fine, fine.'' Alex leaned back in the bench as Amy leaned over the brownie-based ice cream treat, taking a long, leisurely scoop. ''I can wait to show you the new outfit I got at—'' Amy looked up expectantly, dessert-laden spoon hanging just in front of her mouth. Pierce's head snapped to the side, gray eyes suddenly narrowing. ''New outfit you got from... where?'' Campbell tried to sound casual, but there was a certain strain behind her words. The taller woman gave no answer, simply sliding out of the bench. ''Just a moment,'' Alex said, starting across the restaurant at a rapid pace. ''You can't start a sentence like that and not finish it!'' Amy hollered after her. ''I'm not coming after you, you know! Just gonna enjoy my ice cream in peace and then—'' When the thin man who'd claimed their check looked over his shoulder and saw the Spider bearing down on him, he broke into a run. Alex's walk became a dash almost before he'd realized it – as if she knew he was going to run. ''Alex!'' Campbell grumbled as she slid out of the booth. ''Seriously! The ice cream!'' Desade was already out the door, however, and Amy gave chase, her own brow wrinkling in confusion as she followed. When the hostess tried to stop her, hands raised, she barely earned a second look. ''Miss, you can't leave without paying for your mea—'' Amy displayed some of the casual aggression that marked her tenure as SCCW's champion as she shouldered past the woman and out into the parking lot, sky gone into an explosion of pink and purple as the sun set. Campbell's mismatched gaze skimmed through the horde of parked cars, catching a swath of blood-red hair behind a sedan's hood. ''What the hell is—'' Amy interrupted her own question as Pierce rotated to the side, her left hand leading. It was a pose the Red Raver had seen too often, especially of late. It was Alex's fighting crouch. Suddenly, Amy's caution and her doubts were forgotten (her dessert, as well), as sneaker-clad feet pounded across the pavement in a reckless, heedless rush to Desade's aid. As she approached, more details became clear. Pierce bounced on the balls of her feet, right hand hanging loosely at her waist. Opposite her was the thin, blond man, the check presented gripped lightly. The man's fighting style wasn't as practiced as Pierce's – his free hand was open as if to beg off. ''You don' have to—'' But the man still stepped forward, swinging a clumsy right hand. Pierce skidded forward, stepping into her block of the punch. She spun with the momentum, catching the man's other hand (the one that held their restaurant check) as it passed. She drove a knee into the man's stomach, staggering him forward and down to one knee. Amy only just got there as Alex stepped over the man's arm, braced between her legs. The ''fight'' as it were, only lasted for a few seconds; the pressure Alex put on his shoulder and elbow drew a quiet groan from the back of the man's throat. ''I give, I give,'' the man said. ''I'll give you back your bleedin' credit card!'' ''Honestly, what the fuck is this mess?'' Amy caught her breath (Alex was curiously calm), standing over the two. ''Who's he?'' ''I'm nobody! Get her off me! She's... she's crazy! Chased me out of the—ah, Christ, woman, let go of my arm!'' ''The truth.'' Pierce growled the words between clenched teeth. She did not release her grip. ''Or I break it.'' ''My name's Donnie. Donnie Bell. I... I nicked your credit card off the table. But I'm gonna give it back.'' He released the leather packet, which tumbled down to the pavement, landing with a heavy slap. ''See? There it is. No harm, no foul. Aggh, I'm sorry! I'm s-sorry!'' Campbell only shook her head, finally crouching to join them while she plucked the card from the packet. ''You were smart enough to know to run from her, and good enough that it might be said you almost got away... so 'nobody' isn't appropriate. How about you come chill in our car for a minute, though? You look a little tense.'' Her eyes finally turned on Alex. ''You can probably let his arm go now. Just sayin'.'' Pierce tossed her head back, hair flying out of her face. ''He stole your credit card, and you're telling me not only to let him go, but we're inviting him into the car?'' ''I could go. Just...'' Bell groaned again, raising his free hand to bite at his knuckles. ''Or I can stay. Staying's fine. I'm good with staying.'' ''The car's a nice, enclosed, very lockable space where you won't draw attention from cops by pinning a guy to the ground and busting his arm like a crazy person.'' Amy was surprisingly assertive, speaking while she held Alex by one arm, yanking her and, by proxy, Bell up. ''And I kinda want to hear what he comes up with.'' Pierce followed that semi-order with a grunt, coming to her feet with the man firmly gripped by an arm that was twisted into an incredibly uncomfortable position. When they got in, she shoved him into the back seat, sliding in beside. Amy slouched into the driver's seat, then leaned forward to access the mechanism that pushed it full-force into Bell's kneecaps. After the initial yelp and what sounded an awful lot like 'You crazy bitch' under the man's breath, Campbell reclined the seat entirely – effectively pinning him in place. Only then did she turn around, propping her elbows near the headrest with her chin in her palm. ''Sooo...'' She tried not to notice that Pierce looked impressed. ''Now that we're comfy, you get to fill us in. I'm sure you're really sorry about that little mishap – especially the part where it looked like you slipped and almost punched Alex.'' ''I am! I am completely sorry.'' Donnie nodded a little. ''It was... wrong of me to steal from such – ah, upstanding? – yeah, upstanding citizens like you and, uh... you and Alex here.'' ''Why.'' It wasn't really a question, and Pierce's frown made that perfectly clear. ''There are much easier ways to make money than risking taking people's credit cards in a restaurant.'' Donnie half-shrugged. ''I suppose. If I wanted to be thuggish about it, I might rough up somebody at an ATM machine or whatever. But just because I have to do this, doesn't mean I have to be one of these dime-a-dozen crooks. Any idiot can wave around a weapon. Takes a real artist to do what I do.'' ''It takes an artist to snatch what might as well be cash from a table at TGIF?'' Amy rolled her eyes. ''Maybe I thought wrong to take pity on you. Ever fucked someone named Kathryn? Or... well, ever fucked something remotely female looking? She blends in easily.'' ''Katherine who?'' Bell's eyebrows bunched up over his clear blue eyes. ''I don't know what you're talking about.'' ''What she's talking about, Mr. Bell,'' Pierce's whisper darkened as her tone became more civil. She slid her hand back into her totally-not-a-purse bag. ''Is the remarkable coincidence of you stealing my friend's credit card. The damage you could do would be irreparable to everything we have worked for.'' ''It wasn't like I was targeting you. I saw two girls eating dessert who – I hope this doesn't sound too blunt – who weren't paying attention to what was going on, and I figured you'd be easy marks. I didn't expect her to notice me, nevermind chase me down and try to rip the arm out of my socket. Still hurts by the way, thanks for asking.'' ''Good.'' From within the bag came Alex's designer wallet, but she never opened it as she turned it over in her hands. Her thumb brushed over a stylized symbol on the front below the clasp, a raised rose bulb. ''Because if this is not random chance, I would have to take measures to teach Shaw a lesson about trying silly games to get one over on me, especially when I have a match with her pet.'' When Alex pulled on the symbol, it broke free, and a slight whirr sounding as a short wire connecting it to the billfold. ''You understand, of course.'' ''Jesus!'' Donnie tried to move, but Amy's machinations still pinned him in place. ''You telling me your wallet has a goddamn garrote inside it! Who are you people?'' ''We're—whoaHEY!'' The man's final chance came to light in the form of the tiny, tattooed redhead helping to weigh the seat down stretching as best she could to grasp the other woman by the wrists. He made a note that neither shied away from the touch. ''Al, no. He's already got enough to go to the cops about you, looking for some kind of payout.'' She'd whispered it into Pierce's ear – another fact that didn't go unnoticed by Bell, though it was neither the time nor the place to enjoy women in close contact. ''He seems smart enough not to be lying. Just relax, okay?'' It might have been Campbell's light touch or her soft words that soothed Alex's nerves enough that she released the symbol, which wound its way back into the wallet with all due haste. ''I don't know any Shaw woman.'' Bell raised his hands again, scooting as close as he could towards the door. ''And I haven't slept with, you know, anyone in months, since you had to ask. I wasn't sent here to steal from you specifically. I was here because I don't have any other damned choice, and because this piece of shit one-horse town doesn’t exactly give a fella a lot of options.'' Bell smiled again, this one wider, his cheeks dimpling slightly. ''And I didn't see a thing, so don't listen to her. I tripped, like you said. Didn't mean to punch at all, didn't see the windy thingy-ma-jiggy in your wallet. I don't even remember what your name is. So how about you just let me go. Or maybe take me over to the Extended Stay America so I can crash and not have to deal with crazy lesbian ladies with garrotes. Not that there's anything wrong with that.'' ''Not.'' Just don't do any permanent damage – especially in the rental.'' ''Wait!'' His lip was bleeding thanks to Amy's right hand, but he wouldn't stop talking. ''Don't kill me, okay? Don't... I'm not the bad guy in this. Just some honest bloke trying to make a living to pay off a series of wagers that were, admittedly, quite badly thought-out in retrospect, but, ultimately, were unfair to begin with. I'm sorry I ruined your dinner, sorry I insulted your integrity, sorry I...'' Tears sprung to his eyes as Alex climbed up onto the seat, her wallet in hand. ''I'm just sorry, okay! Please! I have... I have a kid sister and a puppy and a baby fucking rabbit that will miss me!'' ''Doesn't know that baby rabbits are bunnies. And put your wallet away or I'll have to frisk you for weapons, because the worst thing I'm letting him catch is a nasty beatdown. You're not that person anymore, remember?'' ''Yeah, put your wallet away because you may kill me with it,'' Bell repeated Amy's point, laughing to himself. I'm trying to work it off so she doesn't have to. Just... you can do whatever. Bust my knees, my teeth, blacken my eyes. I don't care. Just leave my hands alone. Being a respectable out-of-towner and all.'' Alex crept closer, her whisper the only weapon she wielded. ''I have a plot of land less than an hour from here, so I am quite familiar with the place.'' ''She went to his casino and she lost big a couple times. Blew all the money she'd saved for her wedding, everything. Guy left her alone to deal with her own problems, you know that?'' If Alexandra Pierce was amused, he certainly couldn't tell. * * * * * It might come as a surprise to many wrestling fans to find Alexandra Pierce and Amy Campbell stalking the halls of a Days Inn in suburban Albuquerque. After all, these were the two of the most famous faces in SCCW – the federation's champion and the woman days away from competing for a shot at the most prestigious title in PrimeTime Central. Surely, they'd be at a high-class hotel (or what passed for one in a town like this) drinking bubbly or ordering room service or something more dignified than dragging a thin, blond man through the halls. ''You girls don't have a better place than this?'' Donnie complained. ''And watch the threads, Red. This is my best shirt.'' ''There's ballsy and then there's stupid. Holding your head high is ballsy. Ordering me around isn't, especially since you just tried jacking my 'best' credit card.'' Campbell shoved the man up against the wall as Pierce slid her keycard into the lock. ''This is expensive silk. It's feckin' Italian.'' ''Uh-huh.'' The second shove sent the man stumbling across the room, and only the bed held him up. Pierce trailed the pair into the room, hooking the ''Do Not Disturb'' sign over the doorknob before letting the door close. ''Now, then,'' she said. ''Tell me again.'' ''Tell you what?'' Bell rolled over, sitting on the edge of the bed. ''About your sister Emma.'' ''Anabelle,'' Donnie corrected. ''She's a good girl, went up to that Indian casino Simon owns, lost a bunch of money.'' Pierce paced close, but she didn't bend over or sit. ''How much did she lose?'' ''She didn't exactly tell me, you know. Ain't like I've had a chance to check her bank statement or nothin'. Enough. An' the bloke gives her a line of credit. Tells her he knows she's good for it. An'... she's got a problem, all right, mate? Is that what you want me to tell you? My sister's a total cock-up an' now she's a crackwhore? Or did you have another clever little question you'd like to try to trip me up with?'' ''I dunno.'' Amy folded her arms. Won't even report it—ow, quit it!'' Amy looked over the man's shoulder, briefly making eye contact with her... let's call them ''confidants''... with one brow lifted. If he has something to prove himself, I should see it.'' Campbell's brows bunched up, just a slight curl of displeasure creasing her forehead. She didn't like it when Alex slipped into some character right before her eyes, but the flick of Pierce's leg as she crossed it was not an Alexandra Pierce move. ''My instructions are quite clear...'' ''I know, I know. Sit here, be good, let Alex be all Mission: Impossible.'' ''Actually, she has specifically requested your help.'' ''...uh, what?'' ''Will you help me? I am in no condition to do this alone, and Roderick's girth alone makes it problematic...'' He'd already begun his slow trek back to the door, laying a bony hand on the door handle. ''I'm not flirting with anyone to get her out.'' But still Campbell rose. ''I'm monogamous. Well, kind of. You know what I mean.'' Hawke's smile over his shoulder was thin. ''It's cute that you think that's what we do.'' He opened the door, just far enough to reach out into the hallway, pulling a garment bag in with him. ''Put this on – it should be in your size.'' He laid the clothing bag on the bed. ''So help me, if there's some kind of hooker dress in this...'' Amy unzipped the front of the bag, but all that was concealed beneath was dark blue fabric – some kind of jumpsuit, perhaps. ''What is this?'' The man with the cane flicked over his wrist, glancing at the face of his watch. ''Either go change, or we can stay here and argue about it.'' ''You're telling me to change into this wizard robe or whatever and blindly come with you.'' Campbell blinked. ''Is this a joke, a test or is Alex actually in trouble here?'' ''Do you know what they do to thieves on a reservation?'' Hawke's lips curled downward. ''Put. It. On. Else I still have Drusilla Devonshire's number programmed into my speed-dial – do you think she's in town?'' She really, really wanted to punch him in that moment. They both knew it, but Amy took the high road and snatched up the bag with a grumble, heading to the washroom to change. ''You wouldn't, but fuck you for using that name at all.'' Elijah's limpid eyes followed her, but there wasn't any regret for using the name on his face. * * * * * The van's only noticeable features were its lack of noticeable features. It was a gunmetal gray conversion model, with a long body and darkened windows, but the cigar smoke wafting from the window was a comforting sign as she climbed into the back, followed quickly (or as quickly as he could manage) by her emaciated bodyguard-slash-babysitter. ''Welcome t'the funtimes.'' Like Amy and Elijah, Roderick Ashe was garbed in a simple, dark-blue jumpsuit, his bucket-like head covered in the same cap as the other two. ''I love this part.'' ''I feel ridiculous,'' Amy grumbled, pushing aside a plastic cart to wedge herself into the uncomfortable bench seats. ''I look ridiculous, too.'' Ashe adjusted the rearview, his murky hazel eyes flicking her way as they pulled out of the motel parking lot. ''You get used to it. Should see some'a the outfits Allie was wearin' when I drove her places.'' ''That's kind of the opposite of comforting.'' Amy fidgeted in the seat, tugging at the button-up collar. ''Is this polyester? I don't think it's breathing. Can you turn up the air conditioning?'' Hawke used his cane as a leaning post, crossing his hands over the head. ''Relax, Amy.'' His voice was quieter than in the hotel room, calmer. ''I'm just asking you to—'' Amy cut in with a frown. ''To help you rob a casino to get Alex out of their custody before they hurt her. And you don't realize how strange that sounds.'' ''We ain' robbin' nobody, 'cuz we ain't thieves.'' Ashe's mushmouth, oddly accented words carried from the front. ''Jus' think'a it like we takin' the money out for a walk aroun' the block.'' ''Look at me.'' Hawke sighed. ''I am not asking you to be Alexandra. Just keep to your business, put the money on the cart, and nod when they bark orders. Maybe smile a little. We pick up the money and put it on the truck. Just like this is your everyday job.'' ''I... I know this is going to sound strange.'' Amy sighed. ''But stealing is way against what little moral boundary I have left.'' Hawke glanced up towards the front of the cabin. ''It might not have been his most brilliant thought, but we are not robbing this casino. We're simply making the man that owns it believe we are long enough for Alexandra to force his hand.'' The van's suspension certainly wasn't the best, and the cart rattled around, but Hawke's eyes never moved. ''We are not thieves, Amy. If it makes you feel better, I'll put you in charge of making sure the money is untouched.'' They jerked to a halt, Ashe stubbing out his cigar in the ashtray in the console. ''We're here.'' ''In or out, Amy. I'm not going to force you, and I can't exactly do this well alone, but I will try if I must. Taking a very bad person's money for fifteen minutes is not theft. It's a necessary evil.'' ''I do this and she'll be able to get out? You promise me that – otherwise, I'm going in throwing punches and getting her myself.'' She snarled. ''I can't believe I let her do something this fucking stupid in the first place.'' ''If you do this, you will give Alex the ability to do what she does better than anyone else in the world: sell a lie and make it true. She's in that building with no leverage, with no ammunition, and no one to back her up except a street con artist more prepared to play Three-Card Monte than to play her games.'' Elijah set his cane down, wincing as he hobbled out of the back of the van. ''I'm going in there. Are you coming with me?'' Instead of words, Amy's feet spoke as they hit the pavement outside of the van. * * * * * ''Where is my money, Alexandra?'' Desade was very lucky Simon Running Bear did not have a weapon on him, because he might have used it then. ''Tell me where it is!'' ''I can honestly say I have no idea, Simon.'' ''I don't care who you are or what I've seen you do – no one steals from me, Pierce. Do you understand me? No one.'' Alex leaned forward slightly, never resting her arms on the tabletop. ''Obviously, they do, or we would not be having this conversation.'' Running Bear grabbed by the front of her blouse, wrenching the woman up to her feet, arms held painfully behind her. ''This is not the time to be glib, Alexandra. You know what has happened, even the slack-jawed thief knows that much.'' ''Hey!'' Bell barked nearby. ''Perhaps I...'' If the position caused her discomfort, the Spider kept it from her face. ''I might be able to assist you in finding it.'' Simon's sigh was sharp, shuddering. ''How much do you want, you bloodsucker.'' ''My price is not monetary, Simon. If I recover your money, all I ask for in return is freedom.'' Simon Running Bear sighed through clenched teeth. ''Explain.'' He knew he wasn't going to like the explanation. And he was right. * * * * * Alexandra Pierce walked alone through the back door of the Sandia Casino, barefoot on the asphalt, her sandals hanging from her fingers over her shoulder. Despite her victory, the Spider did not seem terribly pleased, though her demeanor improved at the sharp whistle that drew her attention. Her men, the Black Ops, lingered near a sedan, Ashe's head encompassed with a cloud of smoke, Hawke leaning on his cane. But even as Pierce made her way towards them, a third figure burst between the two men, driving whatever remnants of her dour disposition into full retreat. Unsurprisingly, the first thing Amy did after the trio dumped the van was to lose the hat, letting her neon red hair fly free. Also predictably, she was speaking as she approached. ''I should kick your ass.''  Those were the first words, uttered very seriously as she stalked to Pierce. The ensuing headlock would have been threatening, if Campbell's arms weren't wrapped around the back of the Director's neck. She didn't even bother checking how far away the Ops were when she mumbled into Alexandra's ear. ''Don't you ever do that again.'' Alex's arm snuck around Amy's back. ''I seem to recall you were the one who encouraged me to assist him. And I did it, and now I'm at fault for listening to you?'' ''You didn't have to take down an entire casino to do it.'' Amy harrumphed, looking past Pierce for the first time, towards the door. ''Where is that jackass? I owe him some serious beatings.'' ''We parted ways after Simon released us. He, ah, asked me to extend his gratitude.'' Only Alex's momentum carried them back to the car, the door held open by Mr. Hawke to allow the pair of redheads back inside. ''I'm mostly glad you are all right.'' ''I'm not. I nearly had a fucking heart attack when Hawke came to the door to say 'There's been a complication', not telling me if you were okay or not.'' Amy frowned. ''And another when I realized you called him instead of me.'' They pulled away from the casino, and, while Amy may have never noticed it, Alex was gratified that Hawke and Ashe managed to stay out of conversations that they had no business involving themselves in. Elijah did turn over towards the two women, waiting for a break in the conversation. ''May be a tad longer to be certain we're not being followed.'' Alex nodded, pulling her leg up onto the seat as she turned towards Amy. ''I did. If I had called you first, let's face it, you would have probably come running, and while I appreciate that, your solution would have been markedly less clean than Hawke's.'' The Spider reached out, putting her hand to Amy's thigh. ''And I would have lost my nerve.'' ''And I wouldn't have had to hear Hawke threaten to replace me with Drusilla.'' Campbell muttered, half-under her breath. Despite herself, Pierce chuckled, which just drew another scowl from Campbell. ''I'm sorry,'' Alex said. ''You know I never would have done that, but sometimes we are presented with limited timeframes, and we do what we must. I'm certain he regrets it. Don't you, Hawke?'' ''Hm?'' The man turned, as if he wasn't listening. ''Ah, yes. Could you imagine me transporting all of that alone, Amy?'' ''I'm enjoying that thought as we speak, actually.'' Alex smiled, turning to drape her legs over Amy's lap. ''Don't blame him. He was just looking out for me in his own way. Just with, you know, less punching than you might have preferred.'' The smaller redhead grunted noncommittally, glancing out the window. She did settle her hand onto Pierce's calf, so she probably wasn't too mad. ''Seriously. You couldn't have just asked the guy to forgive Donnie's debts? Because that would've been, you know, easier.'' ''We're here.'' Ashe threw the car into park. ''Good old Days Inn, ready for whatever lezzie bidness you girls got.'' Amy lifted a glare to Roderick as the big man turned around, but Alex slid her arm around the Raver's waist, coaxing her out of the car. It took a little more of a tug than usual. ''Come on, he's just trying to rankle you.'' The two women took the stairs quickly, up to the third floor – Desade liked rooms on high floors. ''I'll fucking rankle him.'' Amy kept grumbling. ''Fatass sonofa—'' ''Didn't think you were ever gonna get here.'' It was Donnie Bell, up at the end of the hallway. ''Well, come on! I have to take a wicked piss.'' Desade had stopped midway through the hall and set her jaw, almost leering at the Brit. Amy, on the other hand, was far friendlier – she strutted up to him with a smile, though it disappeared when she bloodied his nose with her right fist. ''You dumb sack of dicks.'' The punch hadn't sent him down, but the follow-through kid to his ribs fixed that. ''I convince her to take pity on you and that's what you do? Get her caught?'' ''Ow!'' Both of Bell's hands flew to his nose. ''You hit me! Listen, Susan, I came back to apologize, an' this's how you repay me?'' He leaned back against the doorframe. ''I was all ready to owe you a debt of gratitude for helping me, an' you go an' punch me! Who does that?'' ''I do, clearly.'' Amy charged forward again, but Don's arms came up to block his face, which was all for the best anyway, since her knee was actually aimed to contact his testicles. When they did, he nearly folded in two, leaving her to slap him on the back. ''Out of breath, huh?'' ''I didn't do nothin'!'' he groaned. ''Everythin' turned out right as rain an' she's standin' here. My sister—'' ''What sister.'' It was Pierce, speaking for the first time. ''You know, Anabelle! I got a picture, great girl, she...'' ''She doesn't exist, Donovan. Simon rather reveled in telling me that.'' Campbell had slipped the keycard into their door and grabbed the still-doubled Bell by the scruff of his shirt, tossing him inside. She'd stalked in after a brief look to Alexandra, who stayed behind her. First came another kick to Bell's ribs. ''Is that true? You lied about having a sister in trouble?'' And another. ''What the fuck kind of game is that? You put Alex in danger for someone who doesn't even EXIST?!'' For his part, Bell didn't fight back – not because he didn't to, but because this is what Amy did for a living. ''I didn't expect her to help me! You're... you're girls! I figured you'd lemme go, maybe give me a little bit of cash. What was I supposed to say, 'Oh, shit, since you're gonna help me, I was totally lying'?'' As Amy snarled, Donnie began to get his bearings to stand and she quickly put a stop to it with a sneaker to the side of his head that effectively launched him onto the bed, only to be tossed off onto the floor. ''When it comes to putting other people's lives on the line, yes.'' ''Amy.'' Alex's interjection was soft. ''Fine.'' Bell scrabbled backwards. ''Fine, then. Beat me up. Throw me off'a the balcony if you want.'' ''I'm considering it.'' ''Amy.'' The second one was louder. He threw up both hands, barely able to block a haymaker from the SCCW champion. ''But I came back, didn't I? I coulda run – you two birds are kind of terrifying. But I didn't!'' ''That-'' She staggered over the words between punches thrown. ''Just means you thought you were good enough to have us believing another sob story when you came back, you stupi—'' ''Amy!'' The third was the one that got her attention. ''Little busy, Al.'' Amy grunted. ''Sorry I caused this mess.'' She raised her fist for another blow, but Pierce appeared at her side as if she'd been summoned there. ''You did not cause a mess.'' Alex turned her gaze from her lover to her... whatever it was the man was. ''You are quite talented. Raw, untrained, but you have incredible instincts. I can mold you into something—'' ''Alex, no!'' Amy interrupted. For once, Pierce paid him no mind. ''I can mold you into something special. If you're willing.'' Donnie wouldn't lower his fists, burying his face behind them. ''Does it... does it mean the punching ends?'' ''No!''  ''Yes.''  The simultaneous response had Amy glance to Pierce. ''Don't undermine me in front of this asshole! He needs to learn a lesson.'' ''I agree.'' Pierce's smile wasn't as comforting as Donnie would probably have liked. ''He needs to learn several. And I can teach him.'' Her gaze flicked to Campbell. ''This is a man who lied to me, and I didn't even know it. Think about that for a moment.'' ''I have.'' Campbell folded her arms. ''It means it could happen again.'' ''No... no, it won't.'' Bell smiled through a split lip. ''I promise. Completely, totally promise.'' ''I believe you.'' Alex crossed the distance between the pair, nose-to-nose with the man. ''Because if it does, not only will I not stop her... I'll help.'' ''You might want to rethink this. He was dumb enough to assume my name was Susan.'' Amy pouted. ''Do I look like a Susan?'' ''I've known...'' Donnie was reaching now. ''I've known some lovely Susans. I'll be good, cross my heart.'' Pierce's gray eyes glittered – whether it was with malice or intrigue, not even Amy could tell for sure. For once, the Spider's eyes were not maps to anywhere. ''I leave the choice up to you – I am not going to take him over your objections.'' ''Come on... uh, it was 'Amy' right? Yeah, thassit. Come on, Amy. Give a bloke a shot, hey?'' Instead of speaking, Amy folded her arms and glared - notably at Alex. ''Is that... a yes?'' Bell grinned the most charming grin he had in his arsenal. ''I believe that is a yes... for now.'' Pierce's own smile had its very own message behind it. One that caused Donnie to step backwards nearly as much as Campbell's barrage of fists and feet. ''I think I better just, uh... go. I'll leave my number with the scarecrow-looking chap I saw you leave with.'' That's when Campbell pointed. ''Don't try to disappear, fucko. You've had your chance.'' Donnie offered his most innocent, butter-wouldn't-melt smile, raising his hands as the door swung shut. It was exactly as comforting as it sounded. * * * * * And so goes the tale of how Donovan Bell became Alexandra Pierce's next apprentice. Or one version of it, at least. Later that night, surrounded by three women in a bar. ''So I met these two birds, right? With the red hair and the knockers and the legs that go on for days, you know? An' the tall one, with eyes a bloke could get lost in, she's beggin' me – I mean down on her knees beggin' me – to let her play teacher. Usually, I prefer my girls playin' schoolgirl an' not schoolmarm, but I said yeah, 'cause I felt bad, and...'' It would continue for the rest of the night. They say that understanding is a three-edged sword. There's your side, my side, and the truth. Donnie Bell? He'll be the one holding the sword.
