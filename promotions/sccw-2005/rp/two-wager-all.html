Date: Today Wager All Story Two The Ambrose Papers: Volume 2. Wager All. May 2008. "America - It is a fabulous country, the only fabulous country; it is the only place where miracles not only happen, but where they happen all the time." -Tom Wolfe --- The Highest Bidder There’s a crossroads in Mississippi where a man can go to sell his soul to the Devil. They call it Tchula Junction, a place of desolation and Faustian bargains. It rests on an acre of bones, dust, and foul decay. We took a detour there, to find out if the legends were true, and if they were, maybe we could strike up our own diabolical pact. Jeremiah was worried before we even got there. He kept shuddering and itching like his skin was crawling. From time to time, he would take a deep breath and wipe the sweat from the forehead to mutter something in Spanish, a prayer or a plea, I wasn’t sure which. Jeremiah, despite all his flaws, was a born again Christian, and while I had no need for a deity myself, I could respect that in a man, but not enough to pass up an opportunity like this. The sun was sweltering. The heat was unbelievable. It felt like, at any moment, the whole world could go up in flames, left to burn until the end of time. I always heard from Bible thumpers and Jesus freaks that the world would eventually go through a baptism by fire, when the Anti-Christ reared his ugly head and the end times were upon us, but I never took it to heart. Maybe, just maybe, they were on to something after all. "Jerry, listen," I said, between puffs on my cigarette. "You’re going to be fine. You’re already in the company of evil, so what’s a little harm in being in the presence of Satan for a minute or two? Honestly, you’d think you’re a two year old hiding under a blanket. What the fuck are you scared of? Take some blotter acid and zone out for a few hours. It’ll all be over soon." His voice came out meek, a whisper, distorted by his own fear. He was reverting back to his childhood, his Native language, and the time he spent wasting his youth in the pews of a Catholic monastery in some Mexico back alley. From time to time, it almost sounded like he was crying, weeping. "Padre nuestro que estás en los cielos… Santificado sea tu Nombre… Venga tu reino…" It was pathetic; embarrassing even. I sneered and slapped him in the back of his head. "Jesus fucking Christ, man! Snap out of it!" He just looked at me, all wide eyed and tearful, before bowing his head and going back to his praying. I could see the fear in his eyes. I couldn’t tell whether it was the drugs, the heat, or a genuine concern for his immortal soul that was causing him to go into such hysterics. Either way, I simply shook my head and kept driving. I didn’t have time to deal with him. I had to be at the crossroads by midnight. The prospect wasn’t exactly a big deal to me. Exchanging my soul, what little of it was left, for a monetary gain or some other strange wish wasn’t stricken out by my morals or my mind. It seemed like a plausible means to an end. Times were tough all over, after all, and in those final months of the Bush presidency, it wasn’t like I had much to look forward to as far as a respectable afterlife went. I was half expecting to be assassinated by the thought police any day now, for being a degenerate, a general nuisance in a country that had seemingly washed away all its scum and filth eight years prior. This was God’s country now, after all. It had no room for the junkies and the madmen that made up the better part of the people I surrounded myself with. Jerry was no exception to this, of course, but he kept on clinging on to his outdated beliefs all the same. Some people call me cynical, but I call myself a realist. It was around eleven thirty when we got there. Jerry had fallen asleep, his head against the window and drool dripping down from between his lips. Despite all his fear and hysterics, he still couldn’t turn me down when I offered him three high powered Quaaludes, and Jerry certainly wasn’t the type of man who could keep his consciousness alive and kicking when he got that loaded up. Now, I sat in wait, wide awake with enough cocaine shoved up my nose to make Tony Montana jealous. Reclining back in the seat, I let out a heavy sigh and smiled, looking up at the crescent moon hanging overhead. In that supernatural darkness, you could hear the whispers of a thousand men before me who had given up their souls for whatever material possession they were hoping to receive. The machinery of night seemed to go quiet all around me. The sound of cars, howling canines, and rowdy drunks seemed like it was all a million miles away. A jazz song played on the radio, quiet and beautiful, mysterious and strange, dripping with sex and the sounds of a time long gone. I was waiting for an angel to whisper in my ear, tell me to turn around, that this was not my place, and try to save me from myself, but that moment never came. Even if it had, I would have said no, triumphantly. This would be my favorite mistake, and no one was going to hold me back. Some ecstatic electricity was in the air, some weird silence, the moon burning a hole in the sky. Time crept by like a slow parade, minutes seeming more like hours, and those hours seeming eternal. There was an anticipation, a longing, an almost unnecessary impatience for something I wasn’t even sure was going to happen. It’s unexplainable, at best. I felt empowered, almost, to finally have full control of the choices I was making, for better or for worse, for good or for bad. When midnight rolled around, almost on the clock, Jerry covered his head with his jacket. Even though he was asleep, some weird distress that shook him to his very soul was still rolling high in his head, in his dreams, in his mind. From time to time, I almost thought I heard him sobbing to himself, shaking in his sleep from some tormenting nightmare. It was almost enough to put the fear in me, but I took a deep breath, sitting up straight, and putting on my best deadpan expression. The world seemed to stop spinning. The stars all burned out, fading to infinite nothingness. That foreboding silence only grew quieter, the whole of everything seeming to be muted out by what was to come next. In the distance, I could see a car coming, a Cadillac Coupe de Ville. It drove slowly, and as it neared, the streetlights all seemed to go out as it passed them. I couldn’t tell whether I was tripping or not, but either way, I could feel a slow fear building up in my gut. Suddenly, I almost understood where Jerry had been coming from. Unlike Jerry, however, I had a rule when it came to fear, a motto, a mantra: Never turn your back on fear. It should always be in front of you, like a thing that might have to be killed. So, I stared at the oncoming headlights, my eyes wide open, taking a deep breath as I lit a cigarette. The Cadillac was as black as night, the windows tinted to the point that you couldn’t see what was inside, even if you squinted your eyes as hard as you could. It sat beside my car, the engine still running, sounding more like a howling demon than a purring kitten. I took a long draw off my cigarette, watching in anticipation, holding my breath. When the car door finally opened, a man stepped out, walking toward me with slow steps, calculated and dripping malevolent intentions. When he tapped on my window, I looked up, and I knew for sure that I was looking into the eyes of evil incarnate… --- Dark Was the Night He had a politician’s grin and the type of eyes that burned a hole through your very soul. He was dressed all in black, real classy and sophisticated, obviously powerful in a world where power meant everything. You could tell, just by looking at him, that he was something special, something evolved beyond humanity. His very existence seemed to stand as a monument to all the rancid genes and broken chromosomes that corrupted the possibilities of the American Dream. He was a foul caricature of himself, a man with no soul, no inner convictions, with the integrity of a hyena and the style of a poison toad. Myths and legends die hard in America. We love them for the extra dimension they provide, the illusion of near-infinite possibility to erase the narrow confines of most men's reality. Weird heroes and mould-breaking champions exist as living proof to those who need it that the tyranny of 'the rat race' is not yet final. He defied and defiled all that was known of men, corrupting and concluding everything without even parting his lips to speak. The Devil isn’t a cloven hoofed demon, blood red with horns stretching out from his forehead. He’s a man, a mirror image of you and me, quietly defining and redefining the world from behind the scenes. I rolled down the window just enough to hear him speak. His voice came out calm, quiet, without a hint of evil hidden within. "Are you here to make a deal?" He spoke like a businessman, everything seeming to have a double meaning, some subliminal message hidden deep within the trenches of his words. All I could do was nod my head, somehow awestruck in his presence, a lump caught in my throat. "Good," He muttered, reaching down and opening my door for me. "If you could just step out of your vehicle, we’ll get down to business." "What exactly do I need to do?" I asked, hesitating as I began to step out of the car. "Is there, like, a blood pact involved? Something painful?" Jerry, still in the passenger seat, seemed to murmur something in his sleep, his head still covered by his jacket. I eyed the man standing in front of me as he reached into his Cadillac, pulling out a briefcase as he shook his head, laughing beneath his breath. "Of course not, Mr. Ambrose, it’s as simple as any transaction," He turned to me, smiling. He knew my name without even having to say it. "It will be no more painful than parting ways with a dollar bill. However, you know what you stand to lose, right?" I nodded my head, as solemnly as I could, replying, "My soul, yeah. I’m not using it, so I’m pretty sure I won’t miss it." He cracked that smile again, his eyes seeming to flicker momentarily, "Of course you won’t, Mr. Ambrose. But this also raises the question, what do you stand to gain?" This was the bargaining portion of the deal, the part where we came to a mutual agreement on what he would give me in exchange. I could tell he already knew what I wanted. I could tell this was all just part of the game. He was practically mocking me, but I replied all the same. "Fame. Fortune. A meaning in life. The works," I paused. A realization struck me, a revelation, an epiphany. I realized, despite myself, I wanted the same things everyone else wanted. In a way, that scared me more than standing in front of the Devil himself. The idea that I, a man of truth, had been lying all along about what he really wanted out of life. And I wondered, if only for a moment, what else I had been lying about. I wondered, if maybe, I was just as responsible for the death of The American Dream as anyone else… and then I disregarded it, shrugging it off, and hid it in the back of my mind. But he knew what I was thinking, I could tell by the way he grinned, and I could tell by the way he laughed when I asked, "Is that alright?" "That’s quite alright," He replied, sitting his briefcase on the hood of the Cadillac. Opening it’s locks and looking into it for a moment, writing something down, he closed it as quickly as he had opened it, turning back to me with his hand already extended for a shake. "All we have to do now is seal the deal with a handshake, Mr. Ambrose. From then on out, your soul belongs to me and I make sure you achieve your wildest dreams." I wondered, momentarily, whether or not this was a hallucination. Considering how much acid I had done, considering all the drugs that made their home in my body, I wondered if, maybe, this was nothing more than another wild fantasy. Somehow, I doubted it, but at the same time, I couldn’t help but think about it. I wrapped my fingers around his, giving him my strongest handshake. I could feel something leaving me, something disappearing completely, but I couldn’t quite put my finger on it. I asked, "And now what do I do?" "Nothing, it’s all out of your hands now, Mr. Ambrose," He replied, pulling his hand away and turning back to his car. He climbed into the passenger seat and shut the door. As he turned the key in the ignition, he rolled down his window, that smile never quite leaving his face. "Now, you just wait. Be patient. You’ll know exactly what to do when the time comes. And please, try not to worry too much. There seems to be a myth roaming around that you’ll die when I want you to die. Unfortunately for me, that’s still God’s business. I just handle the messy aftermath. Do have a nice night, Mr. Ambrose. Oh, and please tell your friend Jeremiah that I said hello. He’s a good man, despite his faith." With one last laugh and the tip of his hat, he rolled up his window and started driving off into the horizon, leaving behind a trail of black smoke and foul apprehension. I watched as he skidded off into the night, and I had to ask myself whether or not I did the right thing. As a man who never doubts himself, even in his most trying of times, this came as more of a shock to me than anything. I sighed and shook my head. This life was hardly worth living anymore, let alone figure out. So I closed my eyes, got back behind the wheel, lit a cigarette, took a deep breath, and moved on. Relax. This won’t hurt. Let the good times roll. --- Salvation, Texas We crossed the Texas border from Louisiana by the time the sun was coming up. This was the result of high speed, total disregard for law, and tossing enough pills down my gullet to keep me wide eyed and in complete control of the situation. Jerry woke up a few minutes past 7am, god bless his soul. Wiping the sleep from his eyes and almost immediately reaching into the glove compartment for his bottle of whiskey, I could tell that he didn’t remember the night before, and if he did, he didn’t want to talk about it. We’d leave it behind us, back in Mississippi. This was Texas, the last stronghold for what was left of the dismantled American Dream. Texas was something of a wasteland, barren and impoverished. It was dirty, rugged, with a history made up half of tall-tales and half of the actual truth. The population was known to be savage, holding their heritage next to their heart, and their heart next to a revolver. It was everything I should have hated with every inch of my being, but couldn’t. This was my home, the place I held in the highest regard. Texas was the mother that birthed me and made me, for better or for worse. Needless to say, I was dizzy with excitement. With my giddiness obvious, Jerry couldn’t help but look at me as if I was a madman while I rambled on about the undeniable grandeur of my home state. Where were my cynical insults? Why weren’t my words dripping with venom and disdain? Put simply, he couldn’t understand and he wouldn’t understand. I had gone native. As far as I was concerned, this was where the world began. "How much further do we have to go?" He asked, yawning lazily, stretching out as wide as he could while managing and spill liquor all down his shirt. "Who cares?" I asked, grinning from ear to ear. "We’re in Texas, padre. As long as we’re here, time doesn’t even matter." "Maybe to you," He replied, sneering as he rested his head against the window, staring out at the endless flatlands and tumbleweeds blowing on the horizon. "Personally, I’d like to make it home before too much longer. Unlike you, I have a wife who is probably in a fit of rage as we speak." "Fuck her!" I called out, looking over at him with the most serious expression I could muster. "You can’t expect a woman to understand the beauty of a place like this. This is the final resting place for many a great men, I’ll have you know. A woman, especially yours, could never grasp the full concept of a place as amazing as this." "I’m not even sure I can grasp the concept," He muttered, causing me to imagine daggers going through his chest and coming out the other side. For a man of God, he definitely didn’t know when he was looking at the Lord’s most glorious accomplishment. This was the heart and soul of America. A man who couldn’t understand that was doomed to a life of nothing more than TV and relentless masturbation. Of course, knowing Jerry, he wouldn’t have minded that one bit. I changed the subject before an argument ensued: "I figure we’ll make it to San Antonio by three, maybe four, o’clock. We’ll stop for lunch and then head to The Alamo. I’ll do what I have to do and then we’ll head home," I paused, realizing he didn’t even know what I had planned or why I wanted to go on this trip other than to see the Alamo. It didn’t seem like the right time to tell him, not yet anyway. He would have bailed out of the car no matter how fast I was going, land in a ditch, and get up to run for his life. I couldn’t have any of that; there was no time to hunt him down. "We should make it back to Louisville quicker than we made it out here; no surprise detours or tourist traps. You’ll be lucky if we even stop for a piss break." I looked over at him and realized he wasn’t paying attention. Instead, his eyes were transfixed on an upcoming road sign, the only one we had seen for miles. It read, in bold white lettering: SALVATION, TX 5 MILES "What’s Salvation, Texas?" He asked, raising an eyebrow as he looked over at me, suddenly intrigued by such a peculiar name. I couldn’t help but grimace when I heard the name. It was a blight on the great state of Texas, a cold that was slowly turning into a cancer that could have (and should have) been eliminated years prior. "A place where religious nut jobs, Bible thumpers, and the worst kind of holy rollers congregate to pretend their shit don’t stink," I replied, rolling my eyes without even meaning to. "Apparently, miracles happen there daily. People say it’s where God lives these days, or at least where he takes his vacations. You won’t find anything there other than a bunch of proud Christians waiting for the next naysayer to burn at the stake. It’s what the rest of the country would be like if the Nazis had won the war." I could tell without him even having to tell me, he wanted to go there. I muttered a hushed ‘dammit’ beneath my breath, sneering to myself in that long moment of silence, while he collected his words to make his case. Jeremiah was notorious for being one of the most obvious men you’d ever met, and this time was no different. "Do… uh… miracles really happen there, you think?" "It’s an urban legend," I told him, bluntly. "It’s just a bunch of whackos watching grass grow and calling it ‘God’s love’. You remember Waco, yeah? Well, it’s like that, except they’ve got a whole town to themselves as opposed to a tiny compound." Jeremiah was a man who, once he got an idea in his head, he was relentless. He would fly around your head like a gnat, too fast to swat away and too annoying to ignore. "Well, I mean, what if miracles really do happen there? You know, what if God just likes the place and the people? It’s not out of the realm of possibility." I started to correct him, but then I realized I’d just had a meeting with the Devil the night before, so I was really in no place to strike anything out of the realm of possibility. "Well, we’re not going to find out, so we better not dwell on it, eh?" I shot a look over at him. He already had that pouty, puppy dog face going. That was his control mechanism, and whether I liked it or not, it always backed me into a corner. "Oh, god dammit, Jerry! I’m not going to that fucking cesspool! Do you know what they do to guys like us in places like that? We’d be skinned alive, probably cannibalized, or at least sacrificed in the name of The Father, The Son, and The Holy Ghost." "C’mon, Vic. Just for a few minutes? I just want to see what it’s all about. We can grab a bite to eat, check it out, and get back on the road. Real quick and easy. Besides, from the way you make it sound, if nothing else, it’ll be something to laugh at, right?" He looked at me again with those big, pleading eyes. God only knows why he felt the need to go there. God only knows why he felt the need to put me through it. Even worse, God only knows why, when I saw the turn-off coming up in the distance, I didn’t just keep on going right past it instead of turning on my signal. Jeremiah grinned, "Thanks, man. I owe you one." I was either a glutton for punishment or I was trying to keep my karma in check. Either way, as I descended down that off ramp, descending down into my own personal hell on Earth, all I could do was sigh and admit defeat, reaching frantically for the glove compartment and the bottle of pills within. All the drugs in the world couldn’t save me now. --- God Plays Dice with the Universe Salvation, TX was at the edge of the universe. It existed somewhere between the threads of reality and fiction, drifting in and out every so often. For all their supposed miracles, the place was hardly ever the topic of conversation, just vague whispers and half true rumors. It never got any press coverage and you couldn’t find it on any map. It wouldn’t be too strange to say that the town may have been just one mass hallucination, an imaginary town full of imaginary people. The whole place felt and looked like a ghost town. There was a literal row of churches lining the main street, practically identical save a different shade of white paint for each one. It felt deserted, frozen in time, long forgotten. I hadn’t seen a single living soul. It was quiet, almost foreboding, drenched in dread and some savage fear. We pulled into a parking place in front of an old timey mom and pop shop. It looked like they dealt mostly with antiques, Christian memorabilia, and some weird collection of trinkets, knick-knacks, and junk. The whole town oozed of Americana; it dripped off the walls at every corner, from the rail car diners to the apple pies on every windowsill. The place was completely unburdened by that big city aesthetic and corporate advertisement; the only advertisements that I saw were signboards covered in Biblical scriptures. This was a place where dead souls came to congregate, where absolute truth was a dangerous commodity and outsiders were the enemy. Behind the hospitable façade, tucked away somewhere beyond the backdrop, was a foul, depraved, festering mess of a place. A place with a heart full of hate, all prettied up and disguised as a patriotic utopia. Whether it was a fact or just the drugs talking, I knew I didn’t belong here, and neither did Jerry, despite his religious convictions. I let out a heavy sigh and looked over at him, "Well, this is it. Salvation, Texas. As dry as the desert that it calls home. Can we leave now?" Just as soon as Jerry was about to reply, the town seemed to spring to life. In one fell swoop, the streets were bustling with people, the roads were filled with cars, and the shops were opened for business. It seemed like it all happened in one brief moment. As I took off my sunglasses and looked around, more confused than I was scared, Jerry just laughed to himself as a big, shit-eating grin overtook his face. "See, Vic? This place is great!" I felt bamboozled. In a minute’s time, this place went from abandonment to thriving. There was something here, something that I couldn’t quite put my finger on; something wicked and wrought with vicious intent. "Jerry, I don’t like it here," I spoke out loud, my voice almost rising to a shout. "There’s something not right here. I can’t tell you what it is, but we need to leave. Alright, Jerry? Jerry…? Are you listening to me?!" I looked over only to find that my compatriot, my comrade, my brother-in-arms had already deserted me. He stood on the sidewalk now, smiling in some acid laced bliss, shaking hands with passerbys and introducing himself to anyone who cared to know him, which was just about everybody. I sneered, I snarled, I bit my lip until it bled. My eyes filled up with a boiling rage, gritting my teeth until they were being chiseled down to dust, feeling at any second I could turn into a relentless savage, ready to rip the jugular from the first throat I saw. For a split second, I considered leaving him there, to wallow in his own self-importance and archaic beliefs. If he wanted to be closer to God, then I’d let him be closer to God; these people were going to kill him and send him on to heaven, after all, it was just in their very nature. The way he shook their hands, the way he laughed at their jokes, the way he goochie-goochie-gooed their babies… it was all disgusting and sickening. I felt, at any moment, I’d heave over and lurch all over my lap. He deserved whatever ill fate he had coming to him. I couldn’t tell if my anger was real or just the leftover adrenaline from coming down off my high, but that didn’t matter to me right then. I turned the key in the ignition, I put the gearshift in reverse, and I started to pull out of that pious little corner of Hell as fast as I could, when I was stopped dead in my tracks… Tap! Tap! Tap! It was an annoying sound, accompanied by an annoying voice, right outside my side view window. Tap! Tap! Tap! "Hey! Hey stranger! You ain’t deaf, are ya’?" Tap! Tap! Tap! "Just wanted to invite you to step out here with your friend and let us get to know ya’! Didn’t want ya’ feelin’ all shy and coy ‘round here, we’re just good God-fearing folks such as yourselves! Ain’t often we get tourists ‘round these parts!" I lowered my head and pounded it repeatedly against the steering wheel. They had caught me. It was over. I had reached a dead-end. This was my final resting place. I decided to take it with grace instead of going out, guns blazin’. I turned off the car and opened the door, stepping out as slowly as I could. I was expecting one of these backwoods Neanderthals to take me by the shoulders and shove me up against the wall; give me a blindfold and a cigarette and ask if I had any last words. I felt nervous, introverted, and admittedly, just a little bit scared. I shot a glance over to Jeremiah. He had a crowd of well-wishers gathered around him, a shower of good will and God’s grace. Of course, I was imagining a bullet ripping through his forehead, but I was already the odd man out in this clusterfuck. The lanky bastard who tapped on my window and removed me from the comfort of my car was standing in front on me with his wife, both wearing identical smiles, all dressed up in nice, conservative clothes. I could read who they were just by looking at them. Christians, enjoy coffee in the morning, watch Grey’s Anatomy every night, go to church on every Sunday (whether it’s a holiday or not), and vote Republican in all situations. The only thing worse than a Christian is a Republican, though the two mostly go hand in hand. "Hi there, stranger!" He said in the brightest, happiest, most inviting voice he could. Despite by obvious unwillingness to socialize with these people, he remained undeterred. "Welcome to Salvation. I’m James Arthur, and this is my wife, Dorothy. What brings you two to our little corner of the world?" His words all seemed rehearsed, his movements that of a robot who thinks he’s human. I looked at him, breathing a heavy sigh of defeat as I replied, "We’re just passing through, on our way to San Antonio. We’re going to see the Alamo. No need to sound the alarms, we’ll be on our way as soon as possible." "Well now, there’s no need to be in any sort of hurry! You’re welcome to stay here as long as you want, friend. Your friend is already the talk of the town," One look over at Jerry would confirm that. Women giggled behind him while men nodded their hand in approval, slapping him on the back from time to time and going into fits of horse laughter. It was like they’d never seen a Mexican before, which is an impossibility when you live in Texas. "He was saying your name was Victor. Victor Ambrose?" They already knew my name. Not only did they know my face, but they knew my name. This was a slippery slope and I was falling fast. "Yeah, yeah. That’s my name. Uh… say, hoss, you wouldn’t happen to know where a bar is around these parts, would you? I could go for a beer right about now." "Oh, no, sir. Salvation is a dry town. You’re not going to find any alcohol around here for miles. Though, in a place like this, you won’t need it," Oh, goddammit. He and his wife laughed to themselves, not caring that I wasn’t laughing with them. All I could do was concede, nodding my head and letting out another heavy sigh. "Well, thanks anyway," I muttered, hanging my head low and tucking my hands into my pockets, turning to walk away. I figured, I myswell take a tour of the town I was going to die in, find out where they were keeping the bodies and mass supplies of Kool-Aid. James Arthur and his wife muttered something back in forth, perhaps planning my murder and eventual burial, but it didn’t really matter anymore. Paranoia is an ugly beast. --- Little Miracles I had taken shelter in one of the town’s many diners, sitting at the bar sipping coffee and trying my best to avoid any and all conversation. Hours had passed since the last time I saw Jeremiah; he was most likely lost, probably scared for his life, stripped of his clothes and left to die. I tried not to dwell on it too much. I was expecting death. I was prepared for death. The idea of dying didn’t bother me as much as the idea of dying in such a shithole, stripped of my fanfare and theatrics, all replaced by grisly mutilation and macabre bloodshed. I was considering taking the risk of going back to my car, getting my gun, and going out in a blaze of glory, but it all seemed kind of pointless. What’s the point in wasting bullets when you know you can’t win? My concept of death for a long time was to come down a mountain road at 120 miles per hour and just keep going straight from there, burst out through the barrier, and hang out above the world below. And there I'd be, sitting in the front seat, stark naked, with a case of whiskey next to me and a crate of dynamite in the trunk, honking the horn in a maddening frenzy, with the lights turned on their high beams, and I’d just sit there in space for an instant, a human bomb, and fall down into that mess of farmlands below. It'd be a tremendous goddamn explosion. No pain. No one would get hurt. One massive, glorious end to it all. You’d read it on the front page the next time and say, "Huh. I wish I would have thought of that." Needless to say, that concept was being eclipsed. By some grand paranoia or some weird clairvoyance, I knew this would be my deathbed, and I’d just have to learn to accept that. It was already getting dark by the time that Jerry came stumbling into the diner. He was as pale as a ghost, all star-white and tongue-tied, eyes gone wide and his jaw hanging slightly open. I couldn’t tell if it was shock or enlightenment, but he didn’t say a word as he sat down on the stool next to me. He just let out a sigh, tapping his fingers on the bar as he shook his head from side to side, muttering something to himself. He was trying to figure something out in his head, something big that didn’t make much sense to him, I could at least tell that much. There was a moment of silence before I finally decided to speak up. I tried to sound concerned, but I’m sure it came out sounding more like ‘I told you so’. "Are you alright, Jerry? You look like you’ve seen a ghost." "I-I’m fine, Vic. I’m fine," He muttered, hanging his head and taking a deep breath. There was something calm in his voice, something new. Another moment of silence passed before he spoke up again, "This place is special, Vic. I swear, this place has changed my life." I sneered to myself, running my hand through my hair. They had him. They’d caught him by the balls. They’d sucked him into their little world and now he was one of them. In all likelihood, they sent him here to kill me. There was some irony in that that even I could appreciate. "Don’t tell me you’re buying into this tourist trap," I said, in whisper. "You didn’t join their cult, did you?" "Vic, it’s nothing like that," He told me, looking over at me with those big wide eyes. There were tears forming at the corners, and before his next sentence, they were streaming down his face. He was choking it back, trying to keep from crying openly. "They… I saw… those miracles you were talking about, Vic. I saw them with my own eyes. They took me to one of their churches and, I swear, they made a blind man see. A cripple stood up from his wheelchair and started walking. They spoke in tongues, they… I can’t explain it, Vic. God was there. He talked to me. I heard his voice." I looked at him. I looked at him as hard as I could… and then I laughed. I couldn’t help it, I laughed out loud. "You’re kidding, right?" He looked offended. "No, Vic, I’m not joking. For fuck’s sake, can’t you just be happy for me for once?" He huffed and looked away from me for a moment, breathing a heavy sigh before speaking up again. "I know what I saw and it was one of the most amazing things I’ve seen in my entire life." "Eh, well, I guess if I can make a deal with the Devil, you can have a conversation with God. The world is a strange place," I paused, taking a sip of coffee. "So, what are you going to do now, Jerry? Stay here?" "No, I’m going to finish this trip, I owe you that much," He replied, pausing for a moment. "But after we’re done, Vic, I’m getting out of this game. A few more days of drugs and your special brand of mayhem, then I’m done. I’m not saying it’s the end of our friendship, I’m just saying… well, Vic, I’m going to change some things in my life. I’m going to… um… I’m going to become a preacher." I broke into another fit of laughter while he cursed and insulted me, saying that I wouldn’t understand. We stood to leave, it was time to move on, but there was something hanging over us now, like the Sword of Damocles. The rope was ready to snap and let the blade split us in two, but for now, we’d just have to stay on common ground. I felt a weight in my heart and I knew that I was losing my best friend, but enough drugs can make any heartache subside, if only for a few hours. The world is a funny place, huh? --- The Best Laid Plans of Mice and Men The townsfolk said their goodbyes, hugging Jeremiah as he cried on their shoulders. He thanked them for everything they had done while I sat with the car running, obviously bored and obviously hating every second of this. We left Salvation, TX, letting it become a distant memory, another strange experience in a strange time. We drove in silence, for once not bothering to frantically search the glove compartment for drugs to ingest. Instead, we just drove, watching the moon come up over the horizon and night rear its ugly head. I’d lost track of how many days I’d gone without sleep. We were an hour outside of San Antonio when he stopped at a Waffle House to grab a bite to eat, on Jerry’s request. All exaggerations aside, there is nowhere in the world more decadent and depraved than a Waffle House in the middle of the night. This was where drunkards, freaks, junkies, and all the underbelly of modern day society came to congregate and confess their sins, over a plate of hash browns and half cooked cheeseburgers. Jerry and I were no exception. Sitting in a booth toward the back corner, ignoring the horrifying conversations that were taking place all around us, Jeremiah’s curiosity finally reached its peak with a realization that he had no idea what this trip was set out to achieve. Between sips of his coke, he said, "So, you never really told me what this trip was all about, Vic. Are you going to visit your family or what?" "Yeah, I might while I’m down there, but that’s not the main reason I’m going," I replied as the waitress brought out our food. "I have more important things to do while I’m there." "Is there a girl?" He asked, grinning a little to himself as he began to bite into his cheeseburger, talking with his mouth full. "Some long lost love begging you to lay the dick to her?" "Nah, nothing like that," I replied, shaking my head as I scooped up a spoonful of hash browns. "You should know me better than that, Jerry. I’m not going to drive halfway across the fucking country for a piece of ass, let alone one of my old girlfriends." "Well then, what is it? What are you planning? All you’ve really told me is that we’re going to the Alamo," He looked up at me, raising an eyebrow, hoping for an explanation of some sort. I couldn’t tell if he was ready to hear the truth or not, but it was about to come out either way, regardless of if he was ready to hear it. "What’s so important about the Alamo?" "Well, it’s one of the last remaining symbols of The American Dream, Jerry," I didn’t expect him to understand that. He was, after all, a Mexican by blood and by soul. Despite his racial handicap, he was a good man, but not exactly patriotic, even in the sense that I’m patriotic and especially considering the Alamo was the final resting place for many of his ancestors. It held a sour tone in Mexican culture, I would have to assume. "It’s important. It’s a monument of all that is good and righteous in this country." "Yeah, I get that, but why are we going there? I mean, why drive halfway across the country to see a fucking building?" He laughed a little to himself, taking another bite out of his cheeseburger. "Well, that’s easy, Jerry," I said, looking down into my plate of food as I scraped up more hash browns. "I’m going to blow the place to Kingdom Come." He choked, he gagged, and his eyes went wide in shock, a sudden horror climbing up his spine. The realization of my grim mission hit him like a ton of bricks. He wanted to think I was joking, but he knew better than that. As I looked up at him, his jaw hanging open, all that shock still written on his face, I asked, "You going to eat your hash browns, man?"
