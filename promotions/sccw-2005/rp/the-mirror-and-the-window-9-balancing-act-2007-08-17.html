The Mirror and The Window 9: Balancing Act

17 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/17/07 9.1 the mirror: balancing act You called it a balancing act earlier. Spending your days with one woman and your nights with another. To be fair, you haven’t done anything to the second that would constitute cheating, infidelity, or adultery in any way. But your mind is there. And it’s seeing both women, over and over again. So many times the images become muddled, and you can barely tell who’s who. But still, when you described it before as a balancing act, you mentioned that it was something that might be troubling, but something you could get through. That no matter how much the temptation got, you could continue the balancing act because you were dedicated to Katie and that this other woman was only providing a service. It was a balancing act, but you were supposed to have a safety net. Looking down, you really don’t see any net down there anymore. 9.2 the window: deserve you "You going to your all-night gym again today?" Katie asks innocently, putting her arms around Jonathan as he lies back in his recliner, closing his eyes and pulling her close. He kisses her on the cheek. "I was planning on it," he says, trying his best not to sound unnatural in his delivery. He looks up. "Why?" Slightly disappointed, Katie lets go of the hug. "Oh, nothing. I was just going to cook us a nice dinner, you know…haven’t done anything like that in awhile for you." "Oh." Jonathan sighs, then stands up, shrugging. "Well, I definitely have to go to the gym later, but we can have an early dinner, if you want." "Sure!" Smiling, Katie stands up and walks to the kitchen, humming happily. "Tacos okay?" "Yeah, anything’s fine." Jonathan looks over his shoulder at her and smiles, bemused. After a second, he stands up and follows her in the kitchen, watching her from the doorway. "Thanks. What did I do to deserve you?" Katie smiles. "You happened to be the first cute guy in my register at the grocery store to ask me out on a date. It all kind of went from there." Jonathan laughs. "But seriously, you’re amazing. I don’t tell you that enough." Katie stops unpacking the thawed meat and looks to her boyfriend, a mixture of confusion and pleasure on her face. "Thanks, Jonny. I think you’re great too." Jonathan stands in silence, watching her for another second before nodding and leaving the room frowning. 9.3 the mirror: soulmate First, there’s Katie. The first time you ever met her, you were turned off by her smile. Something about it just drove you crazy. The way her lip curled a little bit at the top just made you sick for some reason you couldn’t explain. Everything else about her was fine, but it was that curled lip that almost turned you away. But you didn’t, and it’s a good thing you didn’t. Now that smile is what you live for. Every day, when trying to survive your self-imposed double life, you yearn for that small curled lip to tell you that everything is fine, that you are still a great person, that you are somehow worthy of her love. Although at the current moment you probably couldn’t get to that point with a thousand of her smiles. She is amazing. She is beautiful. Understanding. Courageous. Katie Malick is your soulmate, plain and simple. Through everything you’ve been through, all the problems with your family, with your friends, with your trainer, she’s the one who has never tried to villainize you or isolate you. She’s been your number one fan, even though she doesn’t like the sport. Katie is your everything. So why are you leaving her for this "all-night gym?" You don’t really have a good answer for that. 9.4 the window: foster "Welcome to the all-night gym, you ready to work out?" Sarah Riley says mockingly, flexing as she lets Jonathan into her home again. The New Life sighs, his face breaking into a shameful frown. "I don’t know, I wasn’t sure what to tell her." "Well, all I know is I’ve had tons of clients before you and I’ll have tons after you, and the excuses don’t get much lamer than that. You should be proud, you’re unique in more than one way." There’s an obvious hint of malice behind that sentence, but Jonathan chooses to ignore it. "So…what should we talk about today?" he asks. "Whatever you want to talk about. These sessions are for you, Jonathan, so talk about whatever you want to. Talk about your job, your home life…whatever you wish." Jonathan nods. "Um…well, I’ve been thinking a lot about my trainer lately." "Foster? What about him?" Sarah Riley leans forward in her chair, a reassuring smile on her face. "Well, I remember coming up from his wrestling academy, and everyone who watched me perform there always said that I was destined to be just like him. We have the same moves, the same fighting style, relatively the same build…after awhile some of the more jealous students started calling me Foster’s clone." "And were you okay with that?" Jonathan shrugs. "Sure. I mean, Foster Nackedy defined wrestling at one point. He was always the good guy, the one who the fans cheered for. And to be called a clone, or even just being his protégé, that was perfectly okay with me." Sarah Riley nods, then cocks her head to the side in confusion. "You said ‘was.’" "Excuse me?" "Was. You said it was okay, not that it is okay. Did something change?" Jonathan thinks about it, then sighs. "Kind of. But I…I have to ask you a question. You’ve talked to Foster before in these same kinds of sessions, right?" Sarah nods. "Yes." "Did he come to you with a lot of duality issues? Like how he can’t balance the greatness of his professional life with the ambiguous morality of his personal life?" Pursing her lips, she says, "Jonathan, I can’t tell you anything that another client told me in confidence. It’s part of my role as a psychologist." "But you don’t have any problem breaking other statutes of the Hippocratic Oath," Jonathan says, smiling. "Why not just tell me if he was having those sorts of problems?" Sarah doesn’t seem to appreciate that comment. "There might be some things I don’t do right, Mr. Rhine, but I will keep at least some dignity in my profession, and I don’t think telling you about my other sessions helps me to do that." Jonathan’s smile quickly drops. "I wasn’t meaning to insult you, Sarah. I just am really concerned about how Foster felt, not only because he’s a good guy, but because if I’m as like him as everyone thinks, then I’m concerned about myself as well." Sarah’s expression softens, and she leans forward to touch Jonathan on his arm. "There’s no need to be concerned, Jonathan. Foster has a lot of issues dealing with what he sees as a split personality, but that doesn’t mean you have anything to worry about." She smiles and squeezes his arm harder. "You are a kind, gentle, wonderful person." 9.5 the mirror: slut And then there’s Sarah Riley. You remember one time when you had just started wrestling school, you saw Foster bringing in a woman who was watching the action, a satisfied smile on her face. She was wearing a long black dress, which you thought was strange for someone coming to watch wrestlers train. You saw her watching you and whispering to Foster while you were in a sparring match, and afterwards they went into a back room together. You didn’t know what to think of it, but your immediate and one-time impression of the woman was that she was a home-wrecking slut, just because of her extravagant dressing and the fact you secretly thought Foster cheated on his wife (at least you were right about that). Well now that home-wrecking slut is your psychologist. And you can’t stop thinking about her. She represents everything that Katie Malick is not. And even though you love Katie, the same is true with her that is true with anyone in a relationship: every once in awhile, you need something different. Something that’s not so routine. And Sarah is anything but routine. Just the fact that she is someone who is supposed to be a wall, an impartial person, makes her so much more appealing. What you found so odd about her that first time you saw her is now what makes you think of her when you’re at home, when you’re in the ring, when you’re everywhere. She’s providing you a service, but she’s also making your life much more complicated. You’d love to just have a girlfriend who you love and a psychologist who you do not think of in any way but professionally. But then it wouldn’t be a balancing act, now would it? 9.6 the window: what’s right "Thanks," is all Jonathan can say in reply. Then, he gulps. "I’m sorry for trying to ask…" "No, I’m sorry for getting offended," Sarah quickly says, pulling back her arm. "I can tell you a few things about Foster. Foster did have problems with this duality that you talk about. He thought he was a good man in a wrestling ring and a bad man outside of it, just like you do. But there’s a difference." She pauses, and when it’s obvious that she isn’t going to readily tell him, Jonathan asks, "And that is?" "He was. You aren’t." Jonathan shakes his head in confusion. "Excuse me?" "Foster Nackedy was and is a bad person. Not only because he was unfaithful to his wife. Foster is an aggressive, mean person, and the only reason he was respected in a wrestling ring was because he was mean to people who were meaner. That aggressive attitude naturally bled over into his personal life, and he suffered because of it. "But you," she continues, once again putting her hand on Jonathan’s arm, "do not have the same personality issues. You are a sweet and generous person, and you genuinely love your girlfriend as more than an accessory or a valet. I could tell the first time I talked to you. So no matter what you do, no matter what insecurities you feel that you’re getting to be like Foster, you’re not. You’re a good person." At this, Sarah leans across the table and kisses him, a short but deliberate kiss that sends him back a few spaces. As she releases, he looks at her, bewildered. "What…?" "I shouldn’t have done that, I know," says Sarah, although she is not moving away from him. "But you are the only wrestler I’ve ever dealt with who has this strong, unflappable commitment to morality and what’s right." Jonathan sputters, "B-but doesn’t what we just did impose on that morality? I mean, that wasn’t right, Sarah." "I disagree," she says, moving to the couch next to him. "I think it was about the most right thing we could have done." She pushes him back and kisses him with more passion, more strength, and he leans back as she climbs over him. 9.7 the mirror: fall And there goes the balancing act. You were supposed to be a good guy. You were supposed to be right. Faithful. Smart. There’s no safety net now. You hope that something else breaks your fall.
