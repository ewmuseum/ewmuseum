Mugshots Make Wonderful 8x10s

21 September 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/21/09 He’d read it almost three hundred times in the past hour. Scrawled with a shank or a smuggled quarter into the cum-colored brick was another’s past time when they too were stuck in this place. Removing the Braves cap from his head and slicking back the split-end ravaged blond locks; Thad examined the wall graffiti for the three hundred first time. SHIT Whether it was an adjective to describe the surroundings or a noun to label it, it certainly had an appropriate place just above the rusty latrine that had last been sanitized during the Prohibition Era. Of course, the cement floors of the cell might has well have been made of porcelain, as the inmate piss had almost tinted it to a dull shine. Piss and something else, too, Thad feared. For the entirety of the visit, he’d managed to keep his asshole sandwiched between the rotting vinegar-stained floor and the rest of his body. Although he was certainly no slouch, he’d rather have to fight a hundred Alex Pierces and a thousand Hessians than test the sex nuts of the stone-faced behemoth that currently shared the drunk tank with him. Luckily, it appeared as though the Mr. T lookalike was not interested in anything but starring at the wall perpendicular to where he sat. The youth longed for a razor or anything with a sharp edge, as he’d been overcome with the desire to carve "YOU SAID IT!" underneath the expletive on the wall. As he contemplated a MacGuyver-style rigging of the shoelaces on his Chuck Taylors and a roach at a standstill ten feet away to make a retractable ball-point pen. It had been a successful open mic showing at Harry’s just toward the end of the Vegas strip. While it wasn’t a paying gig, he didn’t by a drink of his own from the minute he walked onto the one-foot plateau at the back of the bar that served as the stage. "American Pie" seemed pretty suited as an opener to the predominantly late 20’s crowd, and dudes decked in Ralph Lauren chinos and tipsy women breaking out every weapon in their make-up collection began to steadily feed him shots during the thirty minute slot. Miraculously, although red-faced and treading water in an imaginary pool by then, he’d flowed through the final cover (Garth Brooks’ "Oasis," which provided the biggest sing-a-long of the night for the 'redneck chic' crowd) with relative ease. At some point, it was a locked deal that he’d be going home with one particular girl, a bar whore who was infamous for having tried to fuck several musicians and three-fourths of the staff. Thankfully, the bouncer had advised him against it. "She’s a babe," he recalled him remarking as he wiped some stray ash off his King Diamond T-shirt, "but unless you’re carrying already, I’d probably pass if I were you." "Shit." He’d sobered up over ninety minutes ago, and up until now had not taken the time to reflect on exactly how he’d come to wind up in the custody of Deputy Do-Right and the Underage Drinking Task Force. Thad now questioned whether or not being clap-free was worth going to the pokey, and quite possibly ruining his career as a professional wrestler at the same time. Normally, if someone in a white polo and black work pants asks "how you’re doing tonight" at 1:30 A.M. on the sidewalk, you’d stop and address the officer cordially, trying to hide any signs of intoxication, especially if you were two years under the legal drinking age. However, Irish Car Bombs and Brain Hemorrhages have a way of distorting one’s perception, and this was apparent when Thad mumbled out the response of "pretty fucking wasted." Handing the cop Foster Nackedy’s ID instead of his own might have been classified as bad judgement, too. Worst part was that he didn’t even need it, as the fine folks at Harry’s never questioned the hit of the night once on his age. Thad was no stranger to the back of a police cruiser, although this one was more pleasant than the shit box he was put into at the age of sixteen, when he was busted for smoking weed behind the old train depot in Winterville. Surprisingly, his arresting officer ended up being a pretty chill guy, despite having to book him for being a stumbling idiot. "We all used to party when we weren’t supposed to," he explained. "Next time, just get drunk at your own place." "How’m I supposed to pick up women at my place?" Thad slurred, tact still melted into a puddle thanks to his blood alcohol content. "Dang," the cop responded. "Good point." ---------------------------------------------------------- Without a cuddle buddy, a pillow, or even a mattress for that matter, Thad Denver could only hope that one day he’d be famous enough to make the poster that featured celebrity mug shots. He also hoped that his wouldn’t be as disastrous as Nick Nolte’s, who'd looked like he’d been holed up in the Overlook Hotel in The Shining for three months. The sound of footsteps interrupted his train of thought. Accompanying the clomp-clomping of the travelling hooves making their way down the hallway was a birdlike voice, chirping the most beautiful medley of musical arrangement that anyone had ever heard. "It’s just A-NOOOOOOOOOTHA day! For YEWWWWWWWW IN ME! In par-a-dise…paradise, PARA-DISE!" And by birdlike voice, Thad thought immediately of a squawking parrot that had only one level of range: the key of awful. While he definitely had to give credit to the Freddy "Please-Break-A-Thermometer-Open-And-Drink-The" Mercury for remembering the echoing chorus in the Phil Collins mood-lightener, he obviously was not the only critic amongst the jailed. "Shut the FUCK UP!" screamed the American version of Simon Cowell two blocks down, currently being held for a domestic abuse – and potentially an assault charge if the fresh meat was placed in his quarters. As the guard on duty for the evening (a portly guy with a mustache you could hide a small child inside) led him to the front of Thad’s cell, the captive craned his neck forward in the general direction of his antagonist. "Fuck you, you shit-eating waste of a load!" he yelped in response. "Better be glad they’re putting me in THIS ONE or I’d have to come FUCK your FUCKHOLE out of principle, BOY!" "I bet you’d like that, wouldn’t you, faggot?" The kid, who was given a shove through the entrance by the less-than-amused deputy, laughed haphazardly at the response he’d generated. Sticking his hands through the safety window in order for the officer to unlock the cuffs, he offered up another proposition for his fellow inmate. "Know what I’d like? For your old lady to sit on my fucking face and suck me dry, while you’re in the corner crying and stroking with the hand your wedding ring’s on," he cackled. "Then, after I’m done, you go make me a sandwich. ON WHEAT! And if they’re any mayonnaise on that mother fucker, I will shit in your SHIT!" "That’s fucked up, man," the criminal down the hall conceded, mainly just weary of hearing the goofy voice. "Uhhhhhhhh that’s fugged up minnnnnnnnnnnnn," the kid mocked, smiling at Thad with a smug victory pose, left fist pumped into the air. This gave the locked-up wrestler the opportunity to size up his new slumber party arrival. Although the guy sang a fairly familiar tune in the late 80’s (or was it early 90’s?) he didn’t look a day over his own age and could have easily passed for younger if it weren’t for an Amish-style neck beard accessorizing his baby-faced complexion. His light brown hair was of normal length, but pushed up into a pompadour at the front. With his attire consisting of a green Lacoste polo, khaki shorts, and purple flip-flops, his douche bag clothing matched his douche bag attitude. One thing was certain: the kid was way more suitable as jail bitch material than he was. Thad glanced over at his stoic pal on the bench, curious to know what he thought of their new houseguest. As expected, the big guy seemed completely oblivious to the entire situation. Great, Thad thought. Alone in the war on terror. "Fuck yeah! I’m sharing a cell with Billy fuckin’ Idol!" In his best effort to avoid any confrontation, Thad nodded, deciding to play along. "Yeah," he responded in his best Cockney accent, "It’s me! Billy Idol!" The jail guard shook his head and guffawed as he started his walk out of the range of sight. Peering down the hallway to make sure he was gone, the very inebriated frat boy jumped out of the window of opportunity. Unfortunately, it wasn't on the tenth story of a high-rise building. "Rebel YELLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLLL!" "Shut yer FUCKING MOUTH!" Had it been in unison and through the world’s largest bull horn…the resounding statement from the other cell would probably still be rendered ineffective. Momentarily diverting his attention away from the British pop star wearing a Jared Sykes "Black Sheep" tee shirt fashioned in the Minor Threat logo, the prep shuffled over to Bubba the Beast. An uplifted fist offering the man a little "dap" fell upon blind (read: uninterested) eyes, even after an attempt at conversation. "What up, Slim?" Although hammered, the slur-filled dude-bro could still miraculously pick up the vibe of a man who did not want to be bothered. While this was a fortunate thing for him (as it saved a highly potential ass whipping,) it spelled certain doom for Thad, who knew that this cat would not be ignored very easily. Reaffirming these fears, the cellmate stumbled over to where Thad sat, extending his hand downward to him. "I’m Jake," he introduced, actually more cordially than expected. "I never expected to be in the shithouse tonight, much less with a goddamn rock icon!" Thad nodded in response. "What you in for, Mr. Idol?" Jake asked, living up to the stereotype of introductory cellmate conversation. "Mur-dah," Thad answered, remembering at the last syllable that he was the singer of "White Wedding" and not a hick in denial from North Georgia. "Sweet!" Thad couldn’t tell whether or not Jake thought it was genuinely cool that he was talking to a celebrity-turned-killer, or if he was just ignoring him. "Bastards got me for public defecation. Then I threw the shit at them." A softball-sized lump of vomit made its way up Thad’s throat as he realized that he’d just shook this guy’s hand moments ago. Thankfully, he managed to swallow it down and effectively block out the memory as best as he could. For the time being, that is. Memory of the stink-palm would probably come back to haunt him later that morning when he’d be completely sober. "I missed, though. It’s pretty hard to throw feces ‘cause of the texture." "Can’t say I know. Never turd-tossed before," Thad replied, not exactly enthralled on the subject. Unfortunately, Jake seemed all too eager to share his knowledge to the public forum he’d been given. "You’d think that it’s heavier than it is, but that’s the whole magic of poop! Like, this one time I had to deuce real bad, and I got all curious about how much fatter you are when you’re constipated. So, I stepped on the scale. One-seventy, right?" "So then, I plop down on the shitter, and grab Better Homes and Gardens, because I’m at my parents’ house and they consider that boring stuff acceptable bathroom reading. I duke'd and read about organic composting or some shit, wipe my ass like a Charmin bear would, then step on the scale…and you know what it said?" Before Thad could humor that interesting glance into Jake’s personal business with a half-hearted "what?" he was surprisingly beat to the punch. "You don’t lose weight when you shit, stupid ass." Both Thad and Jake looked over at Mark Henry, Jr. as if he’d just awakened from a coma, which was a fairly accurate description since he hadn’t bothered to make any sort of movement since Thad had been tossed in the cell. "At least not enough for yo mama’s scale to pick up." The kid blinked, finger still hanging in the air. "…yeah, you’re right." Defeated, Jake stared down at the cement floor, slowly falling into the same abyss that Thad had underwent – the alcohol starting to evaporate from his pores and the realization of being in jail hitting him like a roundhouse kick to the stomach. Thad sensed the jovial nature of this fellow inmate deflate, and for whatever reason began to feel sorry for him. A man with a tenth grade education had punctured a story he thought would lead to a scientific breakthrough. It was hard to embrace the sudden tranquility, and after a few minutes of silence, Thad wished Jake had pressed on with more intriguing bowel tales. Suddenly, a Christmas light flared over the top of Thad’s skull, and although the chance for a well-placed fist to the nose was great, the risk might be worth the reward. "Hey man," he called out, pointing an index finger at the older hoss across the room. His eyes acknowledged him in return, prompting Thad to follow with the million-dollar question. "Is it true that black guys’ dicks are bigger than white guys’?" The assumed expert on the subject shot Thad a "what the fuck" glare, while Jake darted his eyes back and forth to the other two, a grin spread across his face. "Man," he exclaimed loudly, "I don’t be looking at no guys’ DICKS!" ---------------------------------------------------------- "And then we all laughed about it pretty hard," Thad retold, looking down into the floorboard of the car. Although maybe it wasn’t the best topic of conversation, he figured that the story would have been a better way to pass the time on the ride back to his hotel room rather than thanking him a hundred times to the point of annoyance. The driver just stared ahead, visible signs of sleep still in his eyes – as it was around 7:00 a.m. and the dawn had only managed a few rays from behind the horizon. His response was somewhat of a half-chuckle, half-grunt kind of thing that Thad couldn’t measure. Considering the circumstances, the measurement was probably not in Thad’s favor. ---------------------------------------------------------- Jake and "Rolo" as he had (finally) introduced himself as had turned into quality company after the ice had finally been broken. Rolo chiming in with the occasional "you dumb mothafucka" when Jake would clamor up an observation on automobiles, politicians’ ulterior motives, and women who refused to shave their legs on a regular basis. The kid had calmed down to the point where Thad finally revealed himself to be a second-rate professional wrestler rather than an 80’s pop icon. He saw elements of himself in the guy, and wished both him and the man who didn’t look at other men’s penises good luck in the future. "Oh," Jake called out as the guard unlocked the barred door to release Thad from the clutches of the detox chamber, "I was joking about the shit-throwing." "Really? What’s the real reason you’re here, then?" "Touchin’ butts," Jake smiled, making a grabbing motion with his hands. Thad laughed. "Creep," he mused, walking with the deputy to the front. After the standard issuance of documentation, including a court summons in one month’s time and a receipt for the bail co-pay, he found himself greeting the morning in the empty parking lot of the Clark County Sheriff’s Department. Opening his phone, he saw the notifications pop up: three new texts, two new voicemails. There wasn’t much need to read or listen, as he was fairly sure that the messages were resounding. Sighing, he put his feet in motion as he stared down at the screen of the phone. Thad worked his fingers across the red and black Nokia to summon his contacts. SCCW crew man and travel buddy Steve Darlesi, the mother fucker who ditched him earlier that night, was the first dial. Ring. Ring. Only two, which obviously signified that he’d heard the phone ring beside the bed and had opted to decline the call. "Damn." Immediately, Thad redialed, which resulted in it going directly to Steve’s voicemail message. "Hey, dis is Steve," it blared in a heavy and apparently forced Goombah accent. "I can’t answa my phone right now, leave me a message an’ I’ll return ya call at a moa convenient juncture. Capice?" Thad held the phone away from his ear momentarily and pressed down on the pound key, prompting the beep. Opting to take the passive-aggressive route, he cleared his throat and pulled the cell toward his face. "Comfort Inn wake-up call!" Thad chirped, changing his voice to a more effeminate tone. "Could you please inform Mr. Denver that it is time for him to rise and shine? Oh wait, you can’t," he bit sarcastically, "because he got arrested last night and is stranded at the county lockup. Anyway, if you could pass on the message, that’d be great." "What the heck-a-munga am I gonna do?" Thad looked down at his phone display (a picture of Quasar with a tennis ball in his mouth, no less) and read the time. 6 a.m. Even if it were a travel day, most of the cast and crew wouldn’t have bothered waking until the sun was visible – and that went double for Thad who’d depended on his dog to get him up before noon every morning. If it weren’t for natural talent virtually carrying this kid, no doubt he’d be living in his parents’ basement and working part-time as a barista, sneaking out back to toke whenever business was slow. Cycling through a couple of names in his list, he sighed at the options available who were probably lazier than he was. Looking up, he stared at the empty four-lane highway running north and south, wondering which direction would lead back into the city. At least there, he’d be able to gather his bearings a little better…but the big problem was the distance. He could hoof it, but his mental state last night had prevented him from gauging the mileage during the ride in the cruiser. There was also the option of phoning a cab service, but the only issue with that was the fare. With about twelve bucks in cash left in his Ninja Turtles wallet and an almost certainly negative balance in his checking account after paying his bail, he’d probably get about as far as the end of the driveway of the police station. Pulling his foot behind his back, Thad stretched his quads to prepare for the inevitable hike that awaited him. Maybe some old man would take pity on him and pick him up off the side of the road as he headed to Bob Evans’ for breakfast. Even though he looked fairly dingy in torn Levis and a wrinkled tee, he didn’t appear very threatening or serial killer-ish. If anyone had reservations, he could always rummage through the dumpster and find himself a piece of cardboard and a writing tool. Like Joe Dirt, a "WON’T KILL YOU" sign might just land him a ride. Inhaling deeply, the youngster gathered himself and began to pace forward toward the highway, swearing off alcohol for the rest of his life as his head began to thump like a bass drum. This was due to the combination of a lack of sleep and inadequate hydration. Desperate, Thad flipped open his phone and caroused his contacts one more time, hoping for a better solution than the Homeward Bound-esque journey that awaited him. The highlighted entry stopped his scrolling. At first, he told himself no, that he’d had very minimal interaction with him and that a favor of this magnitude would be rude to ask. Then again, he seemed like a real genuine article, so it might be worth a shot. That, and if he weren’t awake, he could always say later that he’d meant to call someone else, therein avoiding an embarrassing situation. Without giving himself a chance to dwell on it further, Thad moved his thumb over to the green button and pressed down. "Oh shit, oh shit, oh shit," he panicked, as if he were in seventh grade again and had finally worked up the nerve to call Marcia Rickman. She ended up being the only girl he ever finger banged in a movie theater, but the fear of the initial first phone call lasted well after junior high romances had long since passed. Ring. He second-guessed his original second-guess as his heart thumped along with his hangover. All he had to do was hang up, write the call off as if he’d never fathomed making it. Ring. I mean, Thad worried, the guy’s got enough going on right now without having me bother him. What a selfish asshole I am. I’m hanging up. Ring. Though he did say anytime I needed anything, I should call him. Ring. Asleep. Why wouldn’t he be? Why would anyone be awake right now, unless they just happen to be getting out of jail? I… "Hello?" As his airway slowly formed a loop knot, the words travelling up the pipe squeezed through the tightness and just barely fell from Thad’s mouth and into the receiver of the Nokia. "Hey, um…Lance?" ---------------------------------------------------------- No matter how disciplined you are in training regiment, and no matter if working before sunrise was a habit you've had for decades -- mornings are never one's most energetic point of the day. Lance Marshall was not an exception to the rule. Whenever the alarm would blare the awful din once the digital numbers read 5:45, it was a ten minute fight to maneuver toward the bathroom and wash the sleep off of his face. Even after the cold water had hit his skin, it was a larger struggle to put on his workout gear and lumber out of his door (whether it be a hotel room or the one at his home) toward the gym. However, since boarding the tumultuous carnival ride involving Alanna's illness and the meddling of Lane Stevens into his personal affairs, Lance no longer arose at this hour because he wanted to. Now, he did it because he had to. While his frustration often acted as a natural supplement towards his bodybuilding, it was also a way to temporarily escape the mental torture that prodded at his conscience with Lucifer's trident. Lately, he'd developed the tendency to keep his cell within reach at all times of the day. This morning, though, he had hurriedly stuffed it in the side pocket of his duffel bag. Exiting out into the lot and unlocking the door, he tossed the bag just behind the driver's seat. Lance enjoyed his early AM drive in complete silence, and when he heard the faint sound of his ring tone going off, he immediately panicked. There was only one reason anyone would need to call him at this hour. Slinging the Porsche Cayenne into a deserted strip mall, he threw the engine into park and reached behind his seat to retrieve the bag. Fishing the BlackBerry out of the velcro side pouch, the Lion could feel his heart as he looked down. The call was not someone in his contact list. It would be a very rare opportunity for Thad Denver, one he wouldn't even think about until later on in his wrestling career. He'd heard the sound of weakness in Lance Marshall's voice, fearing the news on the other end of the line. This was something that no one in the business would ever think existed. The greeting sounded more like a kitten than the mighty king of the jungle, as only the worst of possibilities circumvented in the mind of Lance Marshall. Tensed up, he awaited the response on the other end with sweat-inducing anticipation. To him, 'This is Thad' were the most beautiful words in the English language at that particular point in time. ---------------------------------------------------------- He didn't have to be a psychic to realize that the 19-year old greenhorn who's day job was to masquerade as Buck Rodgers was beyond nervous. The way he had stammered through an otherwise humorous story was enough to pick up on that fact. Lance had absolutely zero intention to make this ride more awkward than the situation had made it. Right now, and throughout the drive on the way to pick up the stranded kid, he tried to settle an internal debate. Being his senior both in-character and out, Lance knew that there were two options in which to handle this: as a friend, or as a type of father figure. Exhaling, Lance broke his silence after a thirty-second lapse in conversation. 'So, you okay?' Thad responded without thinking of any underlying indications of the question. 'Yeah, just a little headache. Guess I'm going off adrenaline right now though, 'cause I ain't slept a wink.' His Southern drawl had slipped out due to his slightly delirious state. 'It'll pass, I'm sure,' Lance reassured. Taking his eyes off the road, he looked over at him. 'But seriously, you're okay, right? I figure something like that could put a decent scare in anyone who's not a seasoned criminal.' Thad nodded. 'It wasn't as bad as you're lead to believe, ya know? I think the big scare is when they process you, you're fuckin' terrified that you’ll be in there with some weirdo raper-guy.' 'That's when being trained in some form of combat comes into play,' Lance interjected. 'Unless he's like a linebacker, or a viking.' 'A viking? I...' Lance stopped short, shifting his head. A small grin showed signs of forming, but he shook it off to keep the direction of the conversation on course. 'I think the whole waiting game would be torture enough, though,' Lance continued, casting his eyes over to yet another billboard advertising a $3.99 all-you-can-eat buffet, this time for one of those shoddy-type casinos like the one Clark Griswold visited to try and win his bankroll back. 'Sitting there just having to think about everything for hours, because there's no other distractions. That's the real terror of prison.' 'Shit,' Thad chuckled. He immediately cleared his throat, however, not wanting to give off the impression that he was laughing at Lance's notion but rather with it. 'I mean, uh, I've never had to go to jail to get lost in thought. Those things don't just come when there's nothing better to do, for me at least.' 'Theres's always deterrents, though,' Lance interjected. 'You don't have to keep dwelling on it out here, but jail...' 'Nah, man. Not when you're constantly around stuff that makes you think.' There was the opening that Lance was looking for. He shot another glance to Thad and smiled. 'Why drink to the point of incoherence, then? Why do anything where the sole purpose is to relieve stress when you're just going to think about it anyway?' Thad stayed stoic, his gaze shifting from windshield to side window, examining the sparseness of the plain. 'Fills that gap, Mr. Marshall. I'm still thinking when I'm fucked up on something, just not as much as usual.' Lance nodded, his eyes softening a little. He recalled the time they'd first been introduced -- a typical rookie showing his polite nature to the veteran. While he had to admit that although it was just a character he'd portrayed, the kid behind the paint had something else going on in that star-filled, asteroid-shaped brain of his. This was a business that had its secrets amongst its athletes, and SCCW was a shining example. Lance knew that Thad Denver was not an exception to the rule. This was why he'd immediately agreed to retrieve the youngster from the asphalt island. While he could have most certainly been wrong about his hunch -- it wouldn't hurt for him to know he had a real friend in the locker room. Conversely, if he was right, it was better for Thad to open up to him as opposed to someone who would take advantage of his youth and vulnerability. Without reservation, Lance opened his ears and his heart to the Georgia boy known as Spacely. 'What gap?' It may have been the exhaustion of his entire body that forced him to unload the baggage, or it may have been the years of repression -- the absolute desire to just spill his guts out onto the pavement and trace a chalk outline around them. Giving the secret a bullet to the head by the power of admission harnessed in a shotgun barrel. He honestly didn't feel like he could fully trust this man behind the steering wheel, but at this point in time, it could've been anyone within listening capacity. His hands trembled, which was directly correlated with his voice. 'You...you're the kind of guy everyone aspires to be, man. Struggles and adversity, no sweat, right?' Lance shook his head in disagreement. 'It's not that easy most of the time.' 'I know, but...' Thad continued, still tripping over his words. 'You deal with it head-on, so much so as to beat on the door of those...those fuckers who are in this just to ruin someone's livelihood! You're the hero to a lot of folks, and it's 'cause you don't try and hide who you are. You fuckin' fight. And me, I...I just...' Thad stopped suddenly, a wave casting ripples in his stomach. Moving his hands down and clutching his abs, he managed to squeak out the next few words just in the nick of time. 'Pull over, please.' ---------------------------------------------------------- It was a bad morning for this particular patch of weeds on the sight of the highway going into the smaller city just north of the Vegas strip. A mixture of what looked like authentic Louisiana shrimp gumbo fell from the mouth of the bleach blond. Lance was quick on the draw in getting the sports car over onto the shoulder. With a pawlike hand on his back, Marshall waited patiently for Thad to calm himself and extradite any leftover Cajun cuisine still lingering in his esophagus. 'I ran away from something. That's why I'm here, puking in a desert.' Lance said nothing as Thad lifted his torso to a vertical position, slouching back onto the door of the Porsche. 'Your wife, Lance. I know she's gonna pull through.' 'Thanks, buddy,' he sighed, patting him gently on the back. Thad immediately spun to face...well, to face Marshall's chest, but shifted his eyes upward to meet the peepers of the Jungle Cat. 'I mean it. 'Cause she's like you. She's a fighter. And those kind are few and far between,' Thad said, confident in his tone. 'I really do appreciate that, Thad. I know people are kind of 'required' to say it, but truthfully, thank you.' Lance reached for the door handle and pulled it open for the kid. 'Let's get out of here.' Thad nodded in agreement as he eased his leg inside. Lance made his way around, taking in a breath of dry air as a light breeze shook his hair. 'Even if she weren't a fighter, even if she just rolled over and gave up,' Lance called out to him just as he'd started to shut the door, 'I...' '...still wouldn't miss her any less,' Thad finished. ---------------------------------------------------------- The rest of the ride might has well have led them straight up and into the clouds. Both Lance and Thad simmered in an atmosphere that was as refreshing as a sprinkler on a steamy July afternoon. They sat in complete silence as soon as they pulled back onto the highway. It was one of those instances they'd described minutes ago: nothing in the way of distracting them from their own thoughts. However, Lance and Thad were not prisoners inside their own mind, but free men who'd chosen to let these daydreams take over conversation. Thad did not think about Kathleen, minor chords, sky-twisting moonsaults, marijuana, or assholes named Jake who livened up jail cells. Thad instead drifted back to pedaling his bicycle around the subdivision where he'd grown up. Neighbors recognized him as that 'bikin' youngin'.' A red Georgia Bulldogs cap turned backwards covered his goofy haircut, and he showed off to the community by releasing his hands off the handlebars and dropping them to his side. Fearless. A feeling that he wanted back. Lance, too, did not think about the woman he loved. He didn't reminisce about winning championships, the roar of a crowd, or rookies who have the nerve to call at the crack of dawn who needed a ride back into the city. He thought about his son. The tender child who had been so brave through the family's entire ordeal. Fearless. Just like he wanted to be. The Porsche eased into the parking lot of the Comfort Inn, surrounded by the two-story L-shaped stucco structure with white numbered doors patterned twenty feet apart. Lance put the car into neutral, while Thad shifted toward him and extended his hand. 'Thanks. For everything.' Lance just stared at his outstretched hand, making no sign of accepting the shake. Instead, he gave Thad a funny look, cocking his head to the side and raising an eyebrow. 'Go take your dog out. Grab your gear, and meet back here in ten minutes.' Thad blinked, confused. Lance merely smiled back at him. He didn't need to choose between being a friend or a mentor to this talented little mess. 'You'll never drink again after we're through.' He could be both.
