Date: 1/30/08 He was the brand new owner of a shiny old belt. Oddly enough, it wasn't nearly as satisfying as he had thought it would be. He had worked so hard to get here; he had fought through so many opponents and defeated almost everyone of them since his debut, becoming one of the most talked-about newcomers in FUSE. But still, that doubt lingered. Not only was it in his head. It was in everyone's head. Just how good was this guy? Sure, he had been on a roll, but the validity of it was in question. He hadn't beaten that BIG name yet. He hadn't really faced much in the way of proven opposition, but those he had beaten, he had beaten soundly. He showed promise, but little else to test him and his abilities. Then along came Jason Cruise. He had held the belt for four months solid, bringing it from the "lowest tier" belt to one of the more respected titles in the industry. The LiveWire championship wasn't about gimmicks or egos; it was about pure wrestling ability, and in that category, Jason Cruise was more than sufficient. He was, by far, the biggest opposition Elliott Rollins had ever faced. Elliott had made a comment at Uproar, a comment he didn't understand the irony of until after the match ended. "All I can think about is how sorry I'm going to feel for him when I take his title away." The irony being that it hadn't been him who had taken the title away. It was Tony Pride. He hadn't seen the Superstar Sidekick. The spin kick Jason Cruise hit him with had struck him on the bridge of the nose, disorienting him. When he stood, he wasn't sure where he was, much less had time to contemplate why Jason Cruise was motionless on the mat. The only thing he knew to do was climb, and climb he did, nailing the downed Jason Cruise with a corkscrew senton that was ugly, but nevertheless effective. As Malik Johnson's hand struck the mat for the third time, Elliott had a ping of dissatisfaction in the back of his mind, but couldn't really figure out why. All of that was quickly overshadowed anyway, as the roar of the crowd quickly drowned out any question he had in his mind. Then, as everyone watching will know, Duncan Drake decided to make his presence felt, hitting him with tornado DDT on to the red steel of a Radio Flyer wagon. Not surprisingly, he was slightly distracted. It wasn't until after the show that Elliott realized what had happened, and why the victory had been less than satisfying. It was because even though he had won it, he hadn't earned it. Nothing he did resulted in Jason Cruise losing that match; he had Tony Pride to blame for that, and with a man of Elliott's integrity, the victory sat at the pit of his stomach and ate away at him like a parasite. She had rolled around so much he had difficulty even shutting his eyes, much less falling asleep. Add to the fact that she had a tendency of snoring; not obnoxiously so, but it was a heavy breathing that was an odd addition to his usually quiet sleeping arrangements. It was nice, though. He had a warm body to lay next to, and it was something he hadn't felt in longer than he'd like to admit. Late at night was when he missed it most. He was never much of a talker, but it was nice to have someone to share the latest hours of the evening with. Before her, he would sit there, alone, watching whatever came on television to distract him long enough until he could no longer stay awake. He longed for something, but couldn't figure out what it was. Then she entered the picture. Actually, reentered would be a more accurate word to use. She had always been there, lingering, and somehow seemed to know exactly when he needed her most. Now she was here, actually here, sitting in his apartment, eating his food, watching his television, using his shower... It had been unexpected, but a pleasant surprise nonetheless. "Yeah," Elliott said absently, as he sat at his dining room table, shuffling through papers. "I slept fine." Hannah Rose emerged from the hallway, a toothbrush in her mouth, and a smile on her face. She walked over to him and wrapped her arms around his neck. "I missed you," she whispered, muffled. She laughed at herself, and then pulled the toothbrush from her mouth. She walked over to the sink and spit, then put the toothbrush down and returned to him, sitting in the chair next to him. He dropped the papers, and his eyes drifted to her as if he'd just now seen her for the first time. Her formerly blond hair was now her natural brown color; her cheeks were still blushed from the steaming hot shower she had just taken, and the pale skin of her arms glowed under the chandelier that hung overhead. "What's this?" she asked, grabbing the first paper off the growing stack. His dining room table had become a filing cabinet. "Money shit," he said, as he rubbed at his eyes with his thumb and index finger. "Ever since William came along, he's dipped his dick into everything he can. Figuratively speaking." Maybe literally. He thought it was nice; he never felt the need to censor himself around her. She wasn't sure if she found it charming or crude. "Advertisements, investments, merchandise..." his voice trailed off. "This is a lot of money," she said softly. "Well that's good," he said as he stood, "because I'm spending quite a bit of it too. Between that joke of an image consultant, the financial manager we just hired, and McDormant's salary, we better hope that I manage to stay on the roll I've been on or else all of that goes away." "We?" she asked coyly. It didn't dawn on him what he said until she pointed it out. It just felt natural to him. He ignored it for a moment, as he walked over to the refrigerator, plotting the next thing he said in his head as he opened it and took a drink from an open container of orange juice. "Well yeah," he said, as he placed the juice back on the shelf. "I didn't know how long you planned on staying down here, but I assumed it wasn't a weekend visit, and it's not like I'm going to make you start paying rent." He wasn't sure if it was convincing as it escaped his lips. "Maybe you should," she said, and tossed the paper back on the pile. "Why?" he asked, as he hopped on the counter. "Unless you plan on moving in here, I'm not going to suggest you going out and getting a job for a couple of weeks just to toss in a little rent money." His eyes met hers. She had a smile on her face. "Maybe you should," she repeated. He eyed her curiously. "Is this your way of asking to move in?" "Maybe," she said, standing. "I can help with the rent, I can help keep this place clean," she said, as her eyes scanned the small, grungy apartment. "I don't even know what I'm doing tomorrow, though, so I can't really say how long I'm going to be down here." This was all moving too fast for him to even process. "You're coming to the press junket with me tomorrow," he said. She had forgotten, and it looked that way as well. "That's right," she said, as her eyes wandered. There was silence between them for a moment. She was still standing, but she wasn't sure exactly why; he was still sitting on the kitchen counter, as he watched her move ever-so-slightly around the dining room table. She traced the table with her slender index finger, following the grooves ingrained into the wood. "You alright?" he asked. "Yeah," she responded absently. "It's just... everything's pretty intense right now." He scoffed. "Tell me about it." His eyes shifted to the microwave that hung over the stove. It read 3:34. He sighed, knowing that in about a half an hour, he would meet with McDormant over coffee, a tradition that had hung around since they first met, and they would discuss business. He stared at the girl standing in the dining room, and realized that business was the last thing on his mind at the moment. He grabbed a pack of cigarettes on the counter next to him and lit one. "I have to get going. Willy wants to talk ESPN stuff." She nodded, her eyes still scanning the tabletop. "You want to come?" he asked. She shrugged. "Sure." .he said I have a little dick?. "Who's this chick, anyway?" he said, as he pointed rather rudely to Hannah Rose. "That's -" "I'm Hannah," she interrupted. She eyed Elliott for a moment, then extended her hand across the table. McDormant shook it. "Nice to meet you," he said. It was actually quite polite. He looked over to Elliott then. "If you don't mind, he and I have business to discuss. I hope it doesn't bore you." She shook her head. "Good," McDormant continued, and motioned to the waitress. "We need to figure out a way to keep that whorish manager on the sidelines, excuse my language." Hannah shrugged. The voice on the other end of the phone was a familiar one. Recognizable, yes, but it still sounded different. Elliott Rollins hadn't talked to Margaret Thapes since his return from Pittsburgh. She had stopped in at her office, but her desk was emptied and her plaques removed from the wall. Her leave of absence was official, but when she called, Elliott felt that doctor/patient relationship return almost immediately. "I've been..." Elliott paused. "I've been great, actually." "I'm glad to hear it," she said. "I take it Pittsburgh went well?" He nodded, but then realized he wasn't sitting on her couch. "It did. You were absolutely right, as usual." He smiled; despite their sometimes rocky relationship, it was always good to hear from her. "I thought I might be," she said, with a playfully smug tone of voice. "What about you?" Elliott asked. "What about me?" she replied. "How have you been?" he continued. "I stopped by the office the other day. I have to say, it was kind of weird going in there and not having someone ask me how I felt about seeing it empty." He chuckled gently. "I've been fine," she responded. "I just got back from a vacation in Paris, and now I'm going to be doing some pro bono work at a counseling center in town. It's odd, not being there, but I'm sure I'll get used to it quick." "Well if you don't," Elliott smirked, "you can always come sit on my couch and talk to me about it." "I'm sure I'll be fine," she said on the other end. "It's actually been quite therapeutic, for lack of a better expression." She paused. "You know, Elliott, I was thinking that if you ever have some free time, you might want to come down to the center." "To catch up?" he asked. "To counsel," she said bluntly. "I don't think..." his voice trailed off. "I do," she replied. "I know that they're looking for grief counselors, and even if you aren't certified, it wouldn't hurt those people in there to have someone to talk to. I could talk to my supervisor about possibly having you do sessions once or twice a week. It's nice to have someone that knows what they're going through, or at the very least, has had similar traumatic life experiences." He was silent. "You don't have to give me an answer now," she said.
