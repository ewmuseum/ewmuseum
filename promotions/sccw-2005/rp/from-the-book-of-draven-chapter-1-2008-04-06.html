From the Book of Draven, Chapter 1

6 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 6th "Honey, are you coming to bed anytime soon?" Serena asked as she stood in the doorway of the darkened office. The only light that is visible in the room is coming from the desk where Jason Ryan, the wrestler known as Jay Draven, is sitting. A notebook was in his hand, but now sits in the light, the ink on the paper visible under the glare. "Shortly, I want to get this chapter done before I call it a night." Draven is writing a memoir of sorts. Part of that was the reason that he decided to return to the wrestling ring. To give the book an ending that he couldn’t give it before. "You know, that book is going to be the death of you. You’re worried about making it good, aren’t you?" He was reading what he had written on the paper. His glasses rested on his nose as he rubs his temples. His once golden blonde hair was now showing gray near the temples. His best years were behind him in the ring. "I just want this to be a book I can be proud of." "Don’t worry so much about it. The story of Jay Draven can write itself. But I didn’t marry him. I married Jason Ryan. That’s who I want to go to bed with." Draven didn’t remove his eyes from the paper. "Maybe I should give Jay a call. He might be able to help me get through this." Jay Callion trained Draven to be a wrestler. It was partially in honor of him that Draven shortened his first name for wrestling. "He knows better than anyone how I was when I was nineteen." Serena walks over to where her husband was sitting and took the notebook from his hands. She than sat on the desk and looked the man she knew as Jason in the eyes. "My mom has Lily. We have the whole house to ourselves." Draven took his glasses off and sat them on the table. He looked out the window, into the Arizona night sky. "I know. I’m just thinking, though." "And?" "I keep coming back to the question. Why did I become a professional wrestler? I wasn’t particularly a fan of it when I was going up." Draven, upon signing with SCCW started to question his own motives. "I don’t remember that far back." Serena sensed some pain that Draven was in. He didn’t hide it. Years of abuse, and he continued to go back for more. The only time Draven was able to get rid of the pain was when he was writing. Now that he’s stuck, it all came back with a vengeance. "Is this about what Glen said to you?" ----------------------------------------------- A few months ago, Jay and Serena returned to Michigan to visit with his dad and his current wife. Glen Ryan wasn’t the greatest father in the world. Instead of taking care of his son, he sent him to live with his mother and her loser husband. It affected him in such a way that when Jay became a wrestler, he took his stepfather’s name as his own. If only because it was the most destructive name that he could have thought of. Glen never though highly of his middle child. Always thought that he was a loser, just like his mother. Didn’t graduate from college, went into this wrestling foolishness and then dragged his younger brother in with him. It didn’t matter to Glen that Jay was able to take his wrestling career, turn it around and start up a successful company. It didn’t matter to Glen that Jay was starting to become happy with his life, and escape the life that he would have had if he followed his father’s preferred path. That was not the life that Jason Ryan wanted to have for himself. The life that Jason Ryan wanted to have for himself eventually manifested itself into Jay Draven. On the outside, he was a rogue. Didn’t care what anyone had said. Didn’t care what anyone had done. But on the inside, he was still Jason Ryan. A kid born in Denver, raised in the suburbs of Detroit, and unleashed into the world of professional wrestling. He was still the young man that worked hard for his father’s acceptance. He never got it. Not once in his more than 35 years on this planet. His life would have been validated that day if when the walked into the front door, Glen would have said, "Son, I’m proud of you." Instead, he got, "Well, if it isn’t my son…the wrestler." That was the last straw for Jay. He walked out of the house that he once grew up in and vowed never to go back. Not until he earned his father’s respect. ----------------------------------------------- "No, it’s not him. I don’t want to talk about it." "Honey, I know you’re hurting. Is that what this book is about? Earning his respect? I don’t know why it matters so much to you. I don’t know why this book matters so much to you." Draven starts to stand up and accidentally knocks over a folder full of photos that he had gone through. He looks down at the pictures, a little bit of sadness comes to his face. He looks down, and sees Jade and Karnage, two people he enjoyed friendship with while on the road, Leonard Aarons III, the one man in the business that he never wanted to see again, Hiro Saotome, another friend and frequent rival. "I see." Serena hops off the desk and helps Draven pick up the pictures. "Those were…better times." "Honey, let me take care of these for you. You need to rest." A tear rolled down Draven’s cheek. Serena looked up and didn’t see a powerful man, she didn’t see a warrior. She saw a man that was trying as hard as he could to make sense of his life. "No, I can handle it. You go to bed. Lily’s coming home from your mother’s tomorrow, and I still have to pack for LA." Serena put her hand on her husband’s shoulder. "Leave the pictures, sweetie. You need some sleep. You don’t need to let this eat you up inside." Draven listens to his wife and shuts off the light in the office. Tomorrow is going to be a long day for him.
