The Mirror and The Window 7: Sarah Riley

30 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/30/07 7.1 the mirror: restrictions Restrictions. Limitations. Boundaries. You’ve lived your whole life with them. Most people have parents raising them to do certain things properly and not do certain other things at all, but not all of your restrictions were a result of your parents. A lot of them were self-imposed. You turned down drinking every day in high school and college, and not a drop of alcohol touched your lips until it was legal for it to do so. Nothing you did, in fact, crossed the line of legality. Nothing until a few weeks ago, when you had your first (and second, and third, and so on) hit of marijuana. You’ve been thinking about it a lot since then. It was something really small at the time, something you did without really considering it a big deal. But after reflecting on it as a month-old memory, you realize that, barring any violence violations you would have incurred were you not a professional wrestler, that harmless moment was the first time you lived outside of the restrictions and limitations you have lived with for as long as you could remember. And it felt so good. You felt freedom in those moments, and you didn’t stop to relish it amongst all of the things that were going on at that same time. So now, once more stuck in the quagmire of self-imposed borders, you’re longing for another feeling like the weed, something that may be frowned upon by most people close to you (including yourself) but also makes you feel like you’re not imprisoned. There’s some irony in that statement, you’re sure, but you’re too busy sitting in some strange woman’s house late at night to discover exactly what it is. 7.2 the window: whatever you want "One of Foster’s students, huh? Yeah, I can almost tell by the way you look, the way you carry yourself. There must be something they’re putting down in that bayou water," Sarah Riley muses, pouring Jonathan Rhine some coffee, her hand covering the opening on her robe. Jon looks down at his mug of coffee, his face turning red. "Thanks." As Sarah walks back to the kitchen counter, Jonathan takes a sip, then says, "Ms. Riley, I’m not…I’m don’t really know what to…this isn’t what I’m used to, so I’m not very comfortable…" "Call me Sarah," she interrupts, setting the coffee pot down and walking back to the table. She sets her hands on the chair closest to Jonathan and leans over it, fixing him with a penetrating stare. "And don’t worry your pretty little head about the process. I’m a professional, so I can dictate how this goes just fine by myself. You only have to come along for the ride." "A professional…so you’re a prostitute?" Jonathans asks almost crudely. He realizes how it sounded, as he bites his lip and begins to speak fast: "I’m sorry, that came out wrong, what I meant…" "…was exactly what you said," Sarah finishes, smiling. "I’m not technically a prostitute, no. Technically I’m a sports psychologist. But my methods…well, let’s just say that I’m not a strict follower of the Hippocratic Oath." "Oh," is all Jonathan can muster in reply. This nervous reaction makes Sarah smile wider. "Don’t be so nervous, Jonathan. This doesn’t have to be painful. I am here to do whatever you want." She sits next to him and squeezes his thigh. "Whatever you want at all." After a few moments pass, Jonathan shakes his head and sighs heavily. "I have no idea what I want." Sarah nods. "Okay, that’s fine. We’ll figure that out later. For now, tell me about your work environment." 7.3 the mirror: psychologists You don’t know what to think about psychologists. When you were 14, you had your first real relationship. Her name was Jackie, and she taught you a lot about life. She made you feel cool, strong, in control. And when she dumped you after 6 months, you felt like she took all of that with her. When you told this to your mom, looking at her through irritated, teary eyes, she told you that you might want to see someone about it. You adamantly refused. You believed back then that admitting you had a problem that you could not fix on your own was a sign of weakness, that you couldn’t do what everyone else seemingly could. You thought that seeing a psychologist meant you were crazy…a freak. Your belief system on that subject hasn’t changed very much. You weren’t sure of what to expect from this Sarah Riley, but finding out that she’s a psychologist rather than a prostitute actually scares you more than the other way around. At least with a hooker you know what to expect. Part of you is telling yourself to finish the coffee, bid her good night, and leave. But another part of you is telling you to stick it out and see where it goes. You think that part of you is easily influenced by her amazingly attractive looks, but you’re not exactly sure. 7.4 the window: life lesson "So you seem to always be the hero when it comes to the ring," Sarah says, once again piercing through Jonathan with her penetrating stare. "I don’t know about hero. It’s not like I’m trying to be," Jonathan replies, looking down at the table. "But you save people from getting hurt in uneven odds, you try to help wrestlers whose detrimental habits concern you…when you’re wrestling, you’re a really standup guy." Jonathan nods after a second of thinking about it. "Yeah, I guess so." Sarah matches his nod, then abruptly asks, "What about at home?" "Huh?" Jonathan says, confused at the question. "Do you think you’re a hero at home?" He considers the question, then shakes his head. "Definitely not." "So you’re a different person at home than ay work?" "Yes," he immediately says. Then: "No. I don’t know. I’m not sure that I’m different as much as the environment is different. I mean, when I’m defending myself at work I have thousands of people watching me and cheering me on, supporting me. I don’t have that when I’m arguing at home." He chuckles, adding, "Although that might be interesting to see. It’d probably help me win a few arguments." Sarah smiles politely. "Who do you argue with?" At this question, Jonathan’s face becomes contorted with shame. "Uh…my girlfriend. I have a girl that I live with…sorry." This causes Sarah to laugh. "Jonathan, you came to see me. I’m not the one you love, so I’m not the one you have to apologize to." Jonathan sighs, then nods. "You’re right." "She jealous of your in-ring success? Thinks you devote too much time to your job? Thinks that the physicality of the business will slowly drive a wedge between the two of you?" Jonathan looks astounded at her questions. "Yeah, all of them. How do you…" Sarah gives another chuckle. "You’re not the only wrestler I talk to, as I’m sure you know. Wrestler’s wives feel the same sort of detached jealousy, and live-in girlfriends are not excluded from the rule. She has justified feelings, that’s the first thing you ought to know. The second thing you ought to know is that you can’t let her feelings build up into a crusade against you. A lot of wrestlers lose their personal lives because they expect their wives to drop the jealousy thing as more and more money rolls in." Sarah shakes her head, then says in a lower voice, "Let me give you your first real life lesson, Jonathan: women don’t care about the money. They care about how they are seen. And if they feel that they are second on your priority list for any amount of time, well then…" She lets it trail off, and Jonathan finishes it for her. "Then I’m in a lot of trouble." 7.5 the mirror: escalate You are in a lot of trouble. Katie had you completely convinced that, while this was something she was obviously not in favor of, as time passed she would be willing to accept that this was who you are and you weren’t going to change. But if what Sarah Riley says is right… It’s only a matter of time before the situation escalates. But there’s another part of you that doesn’t want to think about that. There’s a part of you that still has hope that everything will be all right. That was the part of you that gushed to Sarah for a long time about how great Katie is, how supportive and nice she is, how sure you are that this is the one. So as you drive home from your meeting with Sarah, plans already made for another visit, you congratulate yourself. You didn’t sleep with her, you didn’t do anything to kill the relationship. You’re all smiles as you make your way into your apartment, because sometimes… 7.6 the window: get out Jonathan is all smiles as he gets out of his car, making his way to his apartment. He opens the door quietly, nervously peering in to see if there are any noises that would signify an alert girlfriend. When he hears none, he smiles wider and sneaks in. Walking to the bedroom in the back, Jonathan is almost ashamed to feel a giddy sense of happiness wash over him. Everything seems okay, maybe it is okay to talk about things. It seems to make him feel better, that’s for sure. Now with all of his problems off of his chest, he feels like he got the release he needed. Now everything is all right. Until he sees the sign taped to his bedroom door. Anyone in this house who happens to have a key but has NOT been present in this house for the past two nights will find the couch more than suitable for sleeping. It takes Jonathan a couple of read-throughs to understand the message, and when he does the smile is wiped off of his face. Grumbling, he grabs the pillow lying against the door and walks back down the hallway. 7.7 the mirror: sometimes …sometimes things work out just fine. Heh.
