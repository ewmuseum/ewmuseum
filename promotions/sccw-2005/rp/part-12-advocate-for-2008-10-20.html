Date: 10/20/08 'Quit stalling, girl,' she whispered to herself. She had an unpleasant task before her, and her mind was searching feverishly for any excuse not to do it. Still, sometimes you had to do the job, no matter how dirty. This flimsy rationalization was just enough to get her moving toward the young white-haired man who was presently milling about in the congregation. If you were to list all of the things that Abigail Proctor wanted to do right now, that list would not contain the words 'talk to Jimmy Dawes.' Really, she didn't like talking to anyone in the Compound apart from Seth, since he was the only one who didn't give her the willies. The problem was that she didn't know where Seth was, and finding out would require new information, and new information would require talking to someone else. Still, she'd have rather talked to someone else. Hell, even Reverend Connors would have been better. James was...creepy. Like, he buried the needle on the Creepy Meter. Abby walked up behind him and tapped him on the shoulder. 'Excuse me...James, right?' He turned around slowly, his white robes flowing in the autumn breeze. 'Good morning, Sister Abigail,' he said in a soft voice. 'How may I be of service?' It was the way James looked at you. Not like he was talking to her breasts; that would have almost been better. He stared, yes, but not at you...more like through you. It was like he was trying to look at your soul. Not to judge it--Judgment Is The Lord's, as he would be quick to remind you--but only to see it. Abby shied away from his gaze. If the eyes were truly a window to the soul, then Abby Proctor, like most people, preferred to keep that window closed. 'I was just...um...I was wondering if you'd seen Seth around.' 'Seth? I'm sorry, I don't--oh. Of course, Brother Jeremiah. I spoke with him earlier today, to inform him of the blessed task that he has been chosen for. He went outside, presumably toward that old tractor he is so obsessed with repairing. Curiously, he did not seem to be enthused with the prospect of a holy mission.' 'Oh...kay. Thanks. I'll go look for him there, then.' She smiled politely, and headed toward the main entrance. 'My pleasure, Sister Abigail,' he said, without raising his voice at all. 'May His blessing be upon you.' He tilted his head to the side as he watched her leave. To the casual observer, it would have looked like he was checking her out. If only. # # # 'Fuck! Fuck! Fucking shitting fuck fuck!' Seth (known as Alan Bowdry to his parents, Jeremiah to the SCCW faithful) was upset. In other news: the sky is blue. Normally, the old broken-down tractor was his one place of refuge from the insanity inside the Compound walls. A place where he could go, sit in silence, clear his head, and get away from the infectious madness that radiated from Wyatt Connors and all who followed him. Judging by the constant stream of curse words, punctuated by the sound of a socket wrench handle banging against the side of the machine, there was no serenity to be found here today. 'Fuck!' he screamed again. The other seventy-three times he shouted it hadn't made him feel any better. But you know what they say: the seventy-fourth time's the charm. He was just about to swear at it again when he heard a voice behind him. 'I knew you liked that rusty old thing, but that's going a little too far.' Seth spun around, finding himself face to face (sort of) with Abby Proctor. She had a playful smirk, half-hidden behind her dirty blonde curls. 'Very funny,' he muttered, and struck the tractor again, although with less spirit than before. 'I've been looking for you all morning, Seth.' She pursed her lips, remembering what James had told her earlier. 'Or is it Jeremiah?' He thought about telling her that it was Alan, actually, but there was no point in confusing the poor girl further. 'Who told you about that?' 'Jimmy.' Seth shuddered. 'That guy is...weird.' 'You don't know the half of it. Now, are you going to tell me what all this is about, Irving?' 'I can't really--Irving?' 'Hey, if you're going to go around calling yourself different names, I'm allowed to give suggestions. Now, spill.' 'It's...complicated.' 'A lot of things around here are complicated. How complicated is this?' 'Ever watch wrestling?' 'Oh. That complicated.' 'Right. Well, you know how Connors has a few guys around here who seem to be in charge? Me, Jimmy, Macarthur, and the Mexican guys. Well, we all work for this wrestling company...' 'Let me guess. Wyatt's your evil manager?' 'Not quite. He's actually the wrestler; the rest of us work for him.' 'What? Wyatt Connors? The guy's, like, forty pounds! I bet I could take him!' He laughed. 'Probably, yeah, in a fair fight. Good luck getting one out of him, though. Guy's a legendary douchebag, and that was before he started inventing new ways to punch people in the crotch.' 'Yikes.' 'Yeah. Anyway, if you think what goes on around here is fucked up, it's got nothing on the place we work. People around here are just crazy...people out there are fucking monsters.' 'Sounds like a fun place to work.' 'It's not so bad if you keep your head down, and I try to. But fucking Connors, he's just got to go messing with shit. He tried to do the same thing to our big champion, Aimz, that he did to all these people.' 'Jesus.' 'Yeah. Well, it didn't work, because Desade stepped in, and Wyatt is absolutely terrified of her.' 'Of...her?' 'Don't laugh. She's got her own little cult of followers, and they're actually competent at what they do. She's managed to basically take control of the company, no matter who owns it, and she can kick ass besides. She's like some kind of cross between a ninja and Satan. Anyway, because Wyatt pissed her off so bad, all the rest of us have to fight Desade and Aimz.' 'So, wait. You're afraid of a couple of women? A big, strong man like you, with four other guys helping you? You're kidding, right?' The look on his face told her quite clearly that No, He Was Not Kidding At All. 'Five on two? That doesn't sound fair.' 'I know. They should let us have guns. I'm actually not even supposed to be a wrestler--I only signed on as Wyatt's bodyguard. The rest of the guys just aren't very good. And Aimz isn't very big, but she might as well be ten feet tall. Hell, she took out my knee a couple of months ago.' 'Sounds like the kind of people you wouldn't want to meet in a dark alley. 'More like the kind you wouldn't want to meet at all.' 'Well...what about Connors? Won't he help you out at all?' He snorted in obvious disagreement. 'Are you kidding? That son of a bitch is probably thanking God right now that he doesn't have to be out there with us.' # # # 'And dear Lord, I would also like to thank You for my brave flock, who have chosen to stand athwart the great evil so that I may concentrate on the souls of those who might yet be redeemed.' Wyatt Connors kneeled on the floor of the Jessups' guest bedroom. Before the series of events that led him here, he had never been much of a religious man, and could hardly remember the last time he had prayed. After all, when you do the kinds of things that he does, the last thing you want is to draw God's attention. But the touch of the Lord changes a man in many ways. 'I humbly ask that you watch over their souls should they fall in battle. They have been invaluable in Your service, and I hope You do not think it selfish of me to hope that you do not take them from this world just yet.' Encouraging words, to be certain. 'And finally, I ask that You continue to guide me on the path You have chosen.' 'I will, my son,' came the reply. 'I will.' After a whispered 'Amen,' Connors lifted his head to look upon his master, Reverend Ibrahim Seck. Seck's teeth shone in the darkness as he smiled down from his golden throne (which was a ratty old recliner with a pattern that was considered ugly in the 70's.) 'I believe a progress report is in order, my son. How did your holy mission in Vermont go?' His voice, much like his skin, was recovering from years of mistreatment, but they still had a long way to go. His words sounded almost like a croak. 'Not well, and You have my deepest apologies for my failures. It seems that the denizens of the area have irrevocably lost their way in your absence.' 'That is unfortunate, but not wholly unexpected. And what about the other?' For the first time in the conversation, Wyatt looked his master directly in the eyes. 'Very nearly successful, sir. Sister Amelia had accepted the offer, and was about to partake in Your holy refreshment.' 'And then?' 'And then...She intervened. It seems that She has her own designs on Sister Amelia, and prevented her from...accepting the offering. While he did not come right out and beg, the tone in Wyatt's voice made it quite clear he was pleading for mercy. He would find none. 'You...fool.' What he did find was a stinging right hand across his cheek. 'Do you not realize what this could have meant for me? A wretched sinner, turned toward the light? She could have delivered our message to millions that we could have never reached otherwise. She would have been a powerful ally in many senses of the word. But you couldn't even manage the task.' 'I am sorry, Master, but--' He was silenced by another slap. 'Your tongue only shames you further. You have disappointed me greatly, and you deserve to be cast out of my sight forever.' 'Oh, please! I beg of you, don't-- Slap. 'I SAID BE QUIET.' The words fell like an avalanche, and hurt more than the strikes. Connors averted his eyes from his savior, and began staring at the floor. 'I should cast you out...but I will not. You are still of use, my son. I think that a different mission might be in order, however.' Wyatt looked up again, and opened his mouth to reply. But the protests died in his mouth with one sharp look from Seck. 'No, I think it best if we shift our focus away from her for the time being. I know you wish to serve me, but your fear of this other woman will only lead to repeated failures, I think. You must strengthen your faith in the Lord, and then your fear of the Spider will be no match.' Connors winced, being on edge about even a mention of Alex Pierce. 'In the meantime, I think it best to focus on what we have already accomplished, and build upon that. You must increase your sphere of influence within SCCW, both in the ring and out. I know that the riches of earth are nothing compared to those of heaven...but gold can gain respect and authority, which we can then turn into souls. Do you understand?' Slowly, Wyatt nodded. 'Good. You may go then, after you have joined me in one final prayer.' Once more, Wyatt Connors found himself on his knees, with folded hands. While his head was bowed to the floor, Seck's was raised toward the heavens. 'Dear Lord,' he said in his ragged voice, 'Thank You for the Decent People.' Wyatt parroted every word. # # # That night. Seth stared up at bunk above him, unable to sleep. There were times when he wished he was one of the other sheep, and either didn't have to worry about things like fighting Desade, or at least didn't have the sense to worry. But he did, and he did, which was why he was the only one in the whole Compound still awake. Or, so he thought. 'Psst.' Seth sat up slightly, doing his best not to rouse the others. 'Psst. Hey.' Seth jerked his head to the side, and saw that someone was at the door. It was cracked open, and a head was poking in. 'Abby?' he whispered. 'C'mon,' she hissed back. 'I can't sleep either.' Seth grabbed a shirt from the floor and slipped it on, then headed for the door. He closed it quietly, then turned toward the young woman who got him out of bed in the middle of the night. 'Now, what's this all about--' Only, she wasn't there. She was about ten yards away, running toward the gate and trying to keep her giggles quiet. 'Can't catch me!' 'Jesus,' he muttered as he chased after her. He couldn't gain ground on her, though--he had a much longer stride, but he also wasn't wearing shoes, and gravel tends to hurt. Still, he didn't lose sight of her until she scaled the gate and ran off into the darkness. It took a few minutes, but eventually he was able to follow her laughter back to the source. She'd managed to run all the way out to the edge of the wheat fields that used to be the source of the community's income. She stood with her back to him, looking at the pitiful crop. Somewhere along the way, she had removed her shoes. 'Well, it's no corn field, but it'll do,' she said. 'What are you talking about?' Seth asked, as soon as he had caught his breath. Abby tossed a smile over her shoulder, and then grabbed the hem of her shirt. Slowly, she peeled it off her body and over her head. Her hair bounced around her shoulders as it fell out of the shirt, which she then tossed to the ground. She turned around, covering her breasts with one arm, while the other was positioned suggestively on her hip. 'I think I might have come up with a way to take your mind off your troubles.' 'Wow,' he answered, breathlessly. She took a few steps toward him, unbuttoning her shorts as she did so. They fell to the ground with the next step, and her white cotton panties soon followed. 'I may be a good Christian girl,' she said, 'but I still know how to have fun.' Seth's heart was racing as fast as his mind, which unhelpfully reminded him of how long it had been, and also of the Reverend's opinion of penises. 'I don't know if this is such a good idea...' he stammered. Abby silenced him with a finger against his lips, and wrapped her other arm around his waist. 'Why don't you stop talking and find something better to do with your tongue?' Seth did.
