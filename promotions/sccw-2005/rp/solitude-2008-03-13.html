Solitude

13 March 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/13/08 Solitude is the theme of the day. It’s part of Rashaun T. Bishop’s everyday life. At eight o’clock in the morning on weekdays, he exits the hotel he’s been staying in near the outskirts of Las Vegas. The bell boy, a Hispanic named Raul, nods to him as he passes, and Bishop offers a gruff "Morning." It’s the only word he says until the afternoon, when he hits the gym. On this particular Thursday, he says a few more before that time, as a call is routed to his cell phone on his way to a quick lunch. "Yea." "Mr. Bishop, sir, this is Pierre from the Angles Hotel? I wanted to let you know that someone called for you this morning." Bishop snorts. "You had to call me? That’s what answering machines are for. I’ll get it when I come back tonight." He starts to hang up the phone but hears a long "uhhhh" from Pierre, so he brings it back to his ear to hear, "Yes, that certainly is true, sir, but in situations like these I feel it is best to bring it to the tenant’s immediate attention." Sighing, Bishop stops walking and leans against a nearby building. "What is this ‘situation like these’?" There is another pause before Pierre answers, "The sort of situation where an angry African-American man threatened to use my non-existing female genitalia as a snow shoe if I didn’t immediately put you on the phone." Bishop chuckles and puts his hand over his face. "Snow shoe. Classic. Listen, Pierre. You tell him that I’m not available, and he’ll have to find me if he wants to speak with me." Bishop can hear Pierre gulp over the phone, and it makes him smile. "I’m not sure I want to invite him to find my hotel, Mr. Bishop." "He’s harmless, and even if not, I’m assuming you don’t have a vagina, so he can’t make good on his threat. Good luck, Pierre," Bishop says and hangs up, still smiling. ************************** The smile, wiped away by hours of hard work and training, immediately returns as Bishop enters the lobby, his eyes falling on a large man whose feet are currently resting on an expensive-looking sofa. "I didn’t think you’d actually come out to find me," Bishop tells the wide black man with graying hair. "Yes you did," says the older man, winking up at Bishop. "I saw your eyes flicker as soon as you walked in the room. Haven’t seen you in ten years and you’re still predictable as shit." Without responding, Bishop grabs the chair that the older man’s legs are resting on and pulls it a few inches away, causing his legs to drop. "What did you…" Bishop chuckles. "Wasn’t predictable, was it?" "No, it was stupid. Why you gonna go and piss me off, son?" Still smiling, Bishop nods his head to the front desk, where a young man is staring at them anxiously. "Because Pierre over there doesn’t want you ruining the upholstery, Pops." There’s a long pause before the older man laughs and grabs Bishop, pulling him in. "Come here, Ray-Tay! It’s been way too long." "Yeah, it has. Come on up to the room." ************************** Bishop and his father, James Bishop, have a curious relationship. Although he left when Bishop was very young, the SCCW wrestler doesn’t have any animosity for his old man. He believes that if he were in the same situation, he’d leave too. This is why Bishop is smiling and laughing in a way he’s never done at home or on SCCW cameras. "Ya liked that, huh?" Bishop says, opening the door to his hotel. "Oh, yeah! My son is a real monster, powerslams and shit. When you gonna kill somebody?" Bishop laughs."Maybe next show. You should come, I can get you a…" James holds up his hand and shakes his head. "Thanks for the offer, son, but I don’t…I don’t have a lot of time this week." The tone of his voice makes Bishop stop and look at his father. "Pops, you in trouble?" "Just a little bit. Ray-Tay…Tavon. I have to tell you something. Uh…sit down, sit down." Bishop slowly sits down, looking up at his father curiously. After a moment, James Bishop sits down on the side of the bed, looking at his son with a severity that makes him uncomfortable. "I’ve gotten myself in a jam, and it’s mainly because I’ve been trying to hide a secret from the world." He sighs. "When you lie to everyone you know, your reality kinda gets fucked up. You start to forget what’s true anymore." "Pops, what are you talking ‘bout? What’s going on?" "Won’t bother you with details. I been in trouble before, and I’ll be in trouble again again. But I feel like I need to get right with you. You know why I left your mom all those years ago?" Bishop shrugs. "Yeah, because she drove you away. Because Biloxi is quicksand." James Bishop chuckles lightly. "That’s true, but more than that. Tavon, you remember when I went off, right? How me and my pal Dennis just kinda left to make it big together?" "Yeah. You told me he was your business partner, but I never heard anything ‘bout him after that." James sighs. "He wasn’t my business partner. He was…he was my partner partner." Bishop cocks his head to the side, his mouth slowly opening. "What…" "Tavon," James says, heaving in a sigh that takes most of the air out of the room, "I’m…I’m homosexual." There’s a long pause before Bishop laughs loudly and heartily, slapping his knee. "Good one, pops. Damn, you had me GOING. Haha, how you were like ‘I’m a fag’ and I’m all like ‘no way!’ That’s classic. Man, I…" and Bishop stops because he sees his father isn’t laughing. "You’re serious." James Bishop nods, looking down at his feet. "Yeah, I am." Bishop stares at his father for several minutes, not sure what to say. Then, he nods. "Okay. You can be gay. Nothing wrong with that. Still my Pops." James smiles. "I’m glad you feel that way. Now, before I leave to deal with this trouble I’ve gotten myself into, I need a place to stay for the night. Mind if…?" Bishop shakes his head. "Not at all. You can stay here. Just…" he nervously looks at the bed. "You take the bed, I’ll stay on the couch." For the first night in many cold ones, Bishop wasn't going to be alone. ************************** "Cyrus Raynes, I know this ain’t one for originality here, but you made one big ol’ mistake." Bishop recorded this video segment that he sent to SCCW offices for distribution on their website. As can be seen, Bishop has abandoned his usual accent for a bit of colloquial flavor. He isn’t wearing a shirt, just blue jeans as he stares into the camera. "You need to understand what it is I’m doing here in Sin City Championship Wrestling. I’m here for a purpose. I’m here to make sure people who don’t deserve it are not here. Itachi wasted his time. So did Matthew Randall. And you know as well as I do that Malachi doesn’t deserve the time of day. "So why save him?" Bishop rolls his shoulders. "Because you think you’re supposed to. And you’re not. "You have done well for yourself recently, Cyrus. You’ve made waves, gotten notice, and title shots aren’t too far in the future. You’ve worked for your status. The guy you’re defending didn’t. He just kind of floated, and when I tried to give him his just rewards, you intervened. "You’re gonna find out how stupid that move really was soon enough." Bishop pauses for a moment before moving closer to the camera. "See, I told Ethan Peters a few weeks ago, before my debut match, that taking out people who don’t earn their keep is my hobby. But my job is a professional wrestler. And this week my job pits me right across from you, someone who I have a new motive against. So I’m going to do my job and do it well. The Gateway Champion and my partner are not going to be involved much, as I just want to be there to prove something to you, Cyrus. "I do my job just as well as I do my hobby. And because you wanted to get in the way of my hobby, you’re going to feel the full effects of me being good at my job." Bishop pounds his chest, then walks out of the camera’s field of vision. It turns off soon after.
