Part Four: When I Grow Up

26 May 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/26/08 'But be careful what you wish for cause you just might get it…' In many ways, the life she now finds herself leading is the life Alanna Marshall has always wished for. When she was younger Alanna found herself, as girls sometimes do, thinking and dreaming about "The One", that one guy who would just be your perfect counterpart in every way. Your "soul mate", even. Unlike the other girls she knew, who could recite every detail of their dream man from memory, Alanna had never been quite that specific. She’d never imagined how tall he would be, the color of his hair or eyes, the amount of money in his wallet…none of that had ever crossed her mind. Truth be told, on her list of personal priorities, such things ranked just above "must be breathing" and "must have a pulse" on her list of criteria. For Alanna had looked at the girls who had detailed fantasies of the man they were going to marry, paid close attention to the girls who had nurtured elaborate fantasies about their weddings since the age of fourteen. She had seen them turn into vapid, simpering airheads in the presence of boys they like, seen them play down their intelligence and strength for fear of "scaring" or "intimidating" the boys. And it had disgusted her. She was never, she swore, going to be like that. If there was one thing growing up in a house full of brothers had taught her damn fast, it’s that she would have to learn how to take care of herself. Long before Beyonce and the other two had started crowing about being "Independent Women", Alanna had learned that expecting someone else to take care of her or provide for her would be the biggest mistake she could make. So she stayed calm when boys would get weird about the fact that she was an athlete instead of a cheerleader. She didn’t panic when guys would find it "intimidating" or "scary" that she had no problem speaking her mind…at length and at volume. She didn’t even mind all that much when no guy would even approach her for two months after she decked Jenny Morasca for calling her a whore in front of the whole school. Because all of "them" didn’t matter. If she ever found "the one", Alanna thought, it would be someone who understood who she was and respected her for it. Who would treat her as a partner, not an object or a trophy. Who would not force her to compromise herself for the sake of another. And then had come Lance. She hadn’t been looking for anyone that day on the beach. She certainly hadn’t been expecting to fall in love and damn sure hadn’t expected it to be with a mainlander (and a white boy at that). But there had been something about Lance…something that had almost forced her to take another look at the haole that had caught her eye that day. He’d certainly been cute, no doubt about that, but there were plenty of boys on the island who fit that description. And she hadn’t exactly been sending out the most welcoming of vibes. But he had been sweet and kind and persistent. Every thing she threw at him, every thing that normally ended up scaring the boys away…just seemed to draw him closer, turn him on even more. And he had said she was beautiful. Other boys had told her she was pretty or cute or sexy or fine…but he was the first who had used the word beautiful. And that, more than anything else, had stuck with her. In the short time they’d managed to spend together, he managed to make her feel cherished, treasured…valued. She had decided, on that very last day, that there was no way she was going to let that go. The years since had passed in almost a blur. Getting married, getting into the wrestling business, having Zach and everything else that went along with it…all leading up to this moment with her behind the wheel of her Mercedes, Pussycat Dolls blasting out of the stereo, one very cute four year old staring out of the window enraptured at the sights as they made their way down South Main Street. If things could stay like this…if not forever, then just for a while at least…then, Alanna thought, things would probably be as close to perfect as things could get. So, of course, she could never be that lucky. She had run into more than her share of unpleasant people in the business that was for sure. It was a fact that hadn’t overly bothered her at first. Most of them were yappy little things, able to dealt with either by a little trash talking (most of the time, this being a skill both she and her husband shared) or a little ass-whupping (the rest of the time). And then there had come Desade. The fact that Alexandra Pierce didn’t like taking no for an answer didn’t, Alanna found, make her all that special. The fact that she wanted things her way all the time…again, not all that unique. What had struck Alanna, however, were the lengths to which Desade was willing to go. For most of the folks she had dealt with, any animosity ended at the arena door. There may be problems at the show but, once the show was over, those problems were no longer an issue. There was a clear divide between work life and civilian life. With Desade, that divide simply didn’t exist. There was no difference between the Desade SCCW fans saw on the shows and the Alexandra Pierce that existed off-air. Everything was personal, every perceived insult was to be avenged and every perceived slight was to be retaliated against…ideally in the most painful and humiliating manner possible. Lance had been threatened countless times, certainly, but that was just an occupational hazard. She had been threatened as well…but she’d come to accept that. What had scared her, she thought, glancing at her son in the car seat, was that Lauren Fox had been willing to threaten her son…a statement that Alanna knew had to have been at least tacitly approved by Desade. Anything she’d thought to do at that moment had immediately been superseded by Lance’s reaction. Although he’d managed to keep it under control, Alanna had recognized the look in Lance’s eye…the one that said he would easily snap Lauren’s neck given the chance. Lauren had laughed it off, treated the remark with the same contempt she treated everything and everyone who wasn’t part of Desade’s inner circle. That, Alanna knew, was Lauren’s fatal flaw. Desade was whip smart, hard to predict…but Lauren was just another one of those folks who believed that Lance and Alanna would only go so far and no further, no matter what any member of the hand threatened to do. Alanna wondered just what the expression on Lauren’s face would be like when she finally managed to maneuver herself into a fatally compromising position…and found Desade willing to prune her away as casually as a dead tree branch. Pulling into a metered space in front of Dirty Girls Dry Cleaning (a literal miracle at this time of day), Alanna pulled the car to a complete stop and slipped it into park. She then allowed herself another glance at the car seat in back, a longer one this time. "You okay, baby?" Zach turned and smiled at his mother, a smile full of the joy and wonder of life that Alanna prayed he’d managed to hold onto as long as possible. "I’m fine, Mommy." The man in the beige Ford Taurus snapped away, the camera lens pointed at the dry cleaners across the street. He continued to take pictures as the female target removed the child from the backseat and as the two made their way into the building. It was only once they were both inside that he allowed himself a quick swig of coffee from the thermos he kept in the passenger seat. What they were doing was hardly a mystery…and, to be brutally honest, completely irrelevant. All that mattered was knowing when the two of them went in and when the two of them left. He’d been given minimal explanation as to why he was to keep track of the whereabouts of this woman…and, once again being brutally honest, he didn’t really care. He was being paid far too much for it to be an issue. "What’ll you have?" Lance Marshall glanced up from his menu, looking at the waitress in her pastel colored polo and black slacks. The goofier part of his brain wanted to ask where her "flair" was. Instead, he replied "9 ounce strip, medium rare, plain baked potato on the side." The waitress then shifted her attention to Lance’s lunch companion, sitting opposite him in the booth. Whereas Lance’s looks might be described as "rugged", his companions went full tilt in the direction somewhere between "matinee idol" and "utterly ridiculous". His smile, however, is what keeps the rest of his face from being too overwhelming. For it is a smile that, no matter what else you may think of the man behind it, lets you know that its owner is a complete and utter goofball. "I’ll have the same." "No problem. Kitchen’s fairly quiet today so it shouldn’t take too long." Lance’s lunch companion gazed at the waitress’s retreating form, taking in the faux wood paneling and forced joviality almost bleeding into the air. "Wow, man," he replied, his voice dripping with sarcasm, "you really know how to spoil a guy." Lance smirked in response, the words out of his mouth with barely a second’s thought. "Shut up, Mikey." "So, come on, man, what’s up?" Taking a quick swig of water from the glass in front of him, Lance answered "Does something have to be up? Can’t I just invite a friend to lunch?" "Dude, you do the whole wrestling thing. Your life is drama central. Something is pretty much always up." The smirk on Lance’s face had turned into a grimace. "True enough. You been keeping up with my work lately?" "When my schedule allows. You guys are heading to Spike soon, right?" "Uh huh. Even been booked for the main event." "Well, congrats, man! You must be stoked." No response from Lance. "Okay, not so much with the happy. Why?" "How much do you know about the people I work with?" The expression on Mike’s face is halfway between a smile and a puzzled frown. "Try me." "BloodAngel." "Seems cool enough, from what I’ve seen. A bit nuts but that’s dealable. Why, is he who you’re going up against?" "Nope. He’s my partner." "Sounds cool, then. What else?" "Desade." "Redhead, kinda scary in a devil spawn sorta way?" Lance laughed at this. "Not completely inaccurate. Aimz." "Redhead, kinda scary…there’s kind of a theme going on here, isn’t there. Short, short fuse…and I thought Gina had a temper…" "Actually," Lance replied, "Gina’s a great example with the whole MMA thing. What would you say if you saw a match scheduled putting Gina up against, say…Lyoto Machida?" Mike’s eyebrow raised in puzzlement. "I’d wonder what Dana White was smoking. Who would do an intersex match?" Lance simply spread his hands in response, a resigned look on his face. "You’re kidding," Mike replied. "The redheads are your opponents?!" "Yup." "Oh, you’re going to have people hating you." "Didn’t you just describe the two of them as scary?" "Yeah. But even if they were willingly put into the match, you’re still gonna have people riding your ass for daring to hit a woman." "Tell me about it," Lance sighed. "What really chaps my ass, though? They’re the ones trying to convince people that they’re being screwed." "Um, they’re full of shit," Mike answered. "You win and, hey, wow, you beat some girls, big deal. You lose and, dude, you got beat by some girls?!" "Whereas if they win," Lance answered, "they get congratulated for beating the big guys. And if they lose…well, it’s not like anyone realistically expected them to win anyway." "So it’s lose-lose for you." "Pretty much." The goofy smile returned to Mike’s face. "So, fuck it. For the next hour, or however damn long it takes for us to finish up here, we won’t talk about it. You’ll talk about how Lani and Zach are doing. I’ll talk about the time Gina nearly kicked my ass for catching her topless and how Evan really is that damn hyper all the time." Lance smiled at his friend and training partner, raising his glass of water in a toast. "Thanks, man." Mike raised his glass in response. "No problem, man. What are friends for?" And for the next blessed hour, all talk of SCCW was strictly forbidden. And for the briefest of moments, Lance Marshall allowed himself to feel what a normal life might be like.
