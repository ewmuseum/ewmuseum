Blood Red Hands (Finale)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 22nd 2010 "We stand on a precipice, my friend. An ending is near. Fate seems as if She has not yet decided whose end it will be, but rest assured, these matters will be over within the week." Gunnar Arnisson looked up at his friend, who was grown gaunt over the past few weeks. Worry furrowed Lars’s brow, and suddenly it was if the years that he had been holding back were overwhelming him. "It almost looks like you welcome it," said Gunnar. "I do not, for this ending may be mine, and if it is then I cannot know what Magnus Larsson will do with the world. There will be nobody to rein him in, to try to limit his impulses." "What about Suzanne?" "She will be his puppet, or if he is planning what I think he is, then worse." Gunnar raised his bushy silver eyebrows. "What do you mean?" "Never mind. It is merely a hunch. I have been looking over the old books, and there are things written there that worry me. I have studied them, poured myself into them, but..." Lars left the words hanging in the air. "We will overcome him," said Gunnar, the bear of a man rising from his chair to place his broad hands on the shoulders of his oldest friend. "If it were just him, I might be confident, but we have failed with Suzanne. We failed to sway her to our cause." "And Charlie?" asked Gunnar. "Do we not have him on our side?" "Yes," said Lars. "We have manipulated him. He will say exactly what we want him to say, and he will do everything we need him to do. He is a pawn in this, like Suzanne, and hopefully he will be able to destroy Magnus." "And Suzanne?" "If it comes to it, Gunnar. It it comes to it, we must kill Suzanne as well. The future of our country is more important than her life." Gunnar ran his hand through his thinning hair and nodded. "This ending you see, Lars. Does it come with death?" "Yes, my friend. There is death. Blood red hands reach out, soaked in the life of one of the people in that final room. They reach out, and some will die." "Tell me the truth now, Lars," said Gunnar. "Will my life be lost in this battle?" Lars Halldorsson shook his head. "I do not know, my friend," he lied. "I do not know." *** "Focus, Suze!" Charlie’s right hand, encased in a red leather glove, slapped into the ribs of the Valkyrie. She barely felt it, but the knowledge that he could slip her guard was more troubling. "Jesus!" Charlie exclaimed as his daydreaming twin sister let him hit her right in the face. His sparring gloves and the general lack of strength that he possessed meant that no real harm was done, but Charlie’s concern was there for all to see. "Suze, what the hell are you doing?" "I... I don’t know," said the Valkyrie, walking despondently away from her brother and stepping between the ropes of the gym’s practice ring. "Come back. You can’t expect to beat Quinlan if I can hit you in the face!" Suzanne looked back over her shoulder and considered, briefly, returning to the ring, but with a shake of her long, braided hair she turned away and walked back to the locker room. Her brother’s plaintive cries fell on deaf ears as she went through the door and didn’t look back. The locker room was humid; steam coming from the showers invaded the changing area and the cold sweat on the Valkyrie’s skin grew tepid and clammy on her skin. The austere surroundings reflected her mind in those moments; empty, blank, vacant. She’d spent so much time obsessing over her wrestling career, and now that she had a shot at a title that meant something to her, other events were coming to a head at such a pace that she couldn’t focus on anything. Her mind was a wasteland ever since her father had asked her to be his assassin. "I will not do it," she muttered to herself as her grasping fingers ripped the tape from her wrists. Crushing it into a ball and slinging it at a nearby locker, Suzanne noticed the note slide in the the side of her locker. This was not a new thing, nor was it a surprise. Magnus often communicated with her in this manner, and as much as it infuriated her, she knew he had his reasons. The Valkyrie unfolded the note and began to read. ‘Tomorrow. Nine pm. Sheraton Room 241.’ As terse and illuminating as ever, nevertheless Suzanne knew that she would be there. What interested her more, was who else would be attending. *** Thirty-some hours later, Suzanne Larsson was knocking on the door of room 241. As it opened, she prepared herself for more of her father’s cryptic messages, and was startled to see the face of Gunnar Arnisson looking at her. "Lars, it is..." "I know," came the familiar sound of Lars Haldorsson’s voice. "Magnus has chosen here and now as his battleground." "Wait, this does not need to end up how you all want it to," said Suzanne. "It does, my dear," said another voice, as Magnus Larsson walked briskly down the corridor. "We cannot run forever, and if we stop running, then we will be killed." Lars sighed. "You had better all come in, then," he said. Suzanne and Magnus entered the room, and Suzanne was in for another surprise, as her brother stood before her, next to Lars. "Lars," said Magnus. "I haven’t seen you since you tried to have me killed. Strange how the time flies." "Did you?" asked Suzanne. "I cannot deny that we tried to have Magnus eliminated. We thought we had succeeded, but apparently not. Suzanne, we wish no harm to befall you. Your Gifts are of vital importance to us, but your father has crossed us too many times. He uses his Gifts for purely personal profit, and thinks nothing of where they came from or how to pay his country back." "And for that you want to kill him?" "He is too dangerous otherwise." "Listen to them, sis," said Charlie, his voice weak against the strength of the Gifted. "Or don’t," said Magnus, pulling out a small pistol and tossing it quickly to Suzanne. In the very act of catching it, Suzanne saw that Magnus, Lars and Gunnar were all now rooted to the spot, unmoving, locked in a struggle that was taking place on a plane she could neither see nor sense. Feeling the weight of the gun in her hands, the silencer on the front of the gun tilting it downwards slightly, she steadied an aim on Lars Haldorsson. "Don’t shoot, Suze!" "They want to kill our father." "He’s a bad man, Suze. They explained it all to me. He wants to kill them. He’ll probably kill me too. These guys won’t. They’ll let you do what you want to, if you just listen to them." "I don’t believe you." Suzanne looked across from her father to her brother. "You have to trust me, Suze," said Charlie. "You’re on the wrong side." "No... they’ve manipulated you. They can do that, mess with your mind, Charlie. They’re wrong. They killed Kyle. All they want is for us to go back with them to Iceland and pretend to rule the fucking world." "No. They just want to help you." The love for her brother fought with the desire to believe in her father, as Suzanne danced on the knife-edge of indecision. The air crackled with tension as Magnus Larsson stood mere feet away from his enemies, his family, the Icelandic Council. "Charlie," said Magnus, the word clearly requiring effort and coming out almost in slow motion. "I will not harm you." "He lies, Charlie," replied Lars, his voice similarly affected. "Do it, Charlie. Do it now." In an instant, Charlie dropped as if he was a marionette and his strings had been cut. Instinctively Suzanne tensed, and the gun bucked in her hand, forcing her to take a step backwards to steady herself. The suppressor turned the noise into a dull clap, and Suzanne would perhaps have thought that none of it was real were it not for Lars slumped down on the floor, one arm trying to hold on to the sofa, a slowly widening patch of claret on his chest. He looked at her briefly with eyes that showed only confusion, and then fell to the ground with an air of finality. "No!" screamed Gunnar, the noise coming out of him like a bellow. Magnus turned slightly so as only to face the Bear, but instead of getting easier for him, Gunnar seemed to be growing in stature by the second. "No!" he repeated, his eyes full of tears and his voice wracked with bitter emotion as he advanced on Suzanne. The gun sounded a second time, and then a third, dropping Gunnar to the carpet. Suzanne gasped at what she had done, but did not drop the weapon. "Is it over?" she asked her father. "I think that..." "No!" roared Gunnar, raising himself up to one knee. His right arm steadied him and prevented him from falling over, whilst his left was curled against his body, trying to stem the flow of his life’s blood. "Suzanne! You must listen. He has lied to you. He killed your husband. You must believe us. It was him, it was always him." She fired again, but it almost seemed to spur him on. She fired and fifth and sixth time, and just before the bullets found their mark, Gunnar seemed to leap, landing against her and slamming her into the ground. Suzanne reacted almost instantly, professionally, and swung her hips, pivoting and getting out from underneath the dead weight of the Icelandic Council member. Her t-shirt and hands were both soaked from Gunnar’s blood, and she held them out in front of her, seeing them again as she had in her vision. "Now... now is it over?" she asked, her voice catching on each word. Her father nodded, and smoothed back his hair. He seemed to have become older from the confrontation somehow, as if facing down Lars and Gunnar had aged him. "I... oh God," Suzanne said, dropping the pistol at her feet and burying her head in her hands. "I didn’t think I’d be able to do it." "You did well." "Did I?" asked Suzanne. "Yes." The relief flooded through her, and made her think that perhaps it was all going to be fine. "He was right, you know," said Magnus, as a grin slowly appeared on his weathered face. "What?" "Did you know before, and do it anyway?" Suzanne looked at her father, and realisation hit her in the stomach like a lead weight. "No." "You must have suspected though." "Please," begged Suzanne. "Please tell me you don’t mean that." "I’m sorry," said Magnus, his smile growing wider. "I killed your husband. He was holding you back, and I couldn’t risk the Council reaching you when you were mentally stable. I needed you to be off balance, to mistrust them from the very beginning. Or else, well, we wouldn’t be here now." "But... but why?" she asked, trying to take a step towards him, but finding her path blocked by an invisible force. "I need you, Suzanne. They would have taken you back to Iceland and tried to get you to make the country ‘great’ again, for all the good they ever did it. I have bigger plans than that, but I need you." "And you think I’ll come willingly? Now? After all this?" The Valkyrie gritted her teeth, focused her mind and took one step towards her father. "Not willingly. No, Suzanne, I respect you too much for that. But I am stronger than you, and I can control you until the time when you realise that I only ever wanted what was best for you, and for your brother." Suzanne glanced over to Charlie, and felt her resolve slip a fraction. ‘He’s trying to distract me’, she thought. "You killed my husband. Your own family lies dead in this room, and you say I’ll come around to thinking it was okay?" "Not okay, but necessary," said Magnus, steel creeping into his voice. "Do not forget whose finger pulled the trigger, my daughter. Their blood is on your hands. Nobody forced you to do that." "You made me think that they were going to kill us!" shouted Suzanne. "They were!" "No, they were only going to kill you!" The Valkyrie took another step forwards, and she noticed a bead of sweat trickle down the cheek of her father. The mental battle with Lars and Gunnar had taken a toll on him, and he was weak. This was Suzanne’s chance. "They were going to kill you, and you couldn’t stop them, so you turned your only weapon against them. You turned me on my own family. I should have listened to Charlie. I should have listened to Lars, and to Gunnar." "They did not want what was best for you." "Yes they did. They just didn’t want what was best for you!" Suzanne spat out the final word, copying Gunnar by concentrating on her anger. It could fuel her, allow her to break the mental shackles employed by Magnus. Slowly, steadily, like a glacier moving forwards, she advanced on her father. Magnus began to look worried as his defences fell, one by one crushed under the slow, inevitable movements of his daughter. The glacier rarely seems to move at all. It creeps along, but when it reaches an obstacle it does not treat it as such, it just swats them aside as if they were nothing. Some forces really can be unstoppable, and it was that way as the Valkyrie took another step, and one more that left her face to face with Magnus Larsson, close enough to smell the whiskey on his breath. "This is where I should be the better person, isn’t it?" she whispered. "This is where I should leave you a beaten man, vowing to change your ways forever." Slowly, Magnus nodded. Suzanne repeated the gesture, before looking down at her hands. They were still red, covered in the blood of Gunnar Arnisson, the man she had shot and killed just moments before. The man who did all he could to plead with her that she was following the wrong path. The man whose Gift had not been strong enough to overcome her father. She would be stronger. "I am sorry, father. You do not deserve to live." Her hands shot up, like lightning compared to their previous movements, and vice-like fingers tightened around Magnus’s throat. A brief croak escaped his lips before the windpipe was shut off, crushed like paper between Suzanne’s thumbs. She looked directly into her father’s eyes, hoping for some glimmer of regret or sorrow. What she did not expect to see was satisfaction. The pain hit her in the throat and her chest first, and made her head swim. It was as if she was the one being choked; her trachea being destroyed and lungs starved of oxygen. It threw her, but she resolved to double her efforts and dug in harder still, her blood red hands shutting down veins and tightening arteries. Nothing would get through to her father’s head, and the he could play no games. There would be no tricks left. Still, the pain came again, stronger this time, and leaving her so disoriented that she barely knew where she was. Suzanne blinked her eyes and tightened her grip further, willing the last of the life to leave her father’s body. She blinked again, sure that she could see her own face looking down at her, contorted with rage and fury. The Valkyrie fought harder, but now the world was a blur, and she could no longer tell up from down or black from white. All she noticed was that her body felt different, and at that moment, in a flood of unfamiliar memories, she finally understood. And then she died. *** Magnus Larsson kicked the body of Lars away from the sofa and crashed down on it, barely able to keep from passing out from exhaustion. His mind fired in ways it had never done before, and bereft of his usual Gifts, he struggled to parse the information that his new eyes were sending to his brain. It was certainly going to take some time to get used to it all. A twinge of regret passed through his mind as memories came flooding to him. Suzanne as a child, growing up with her brother. The early years of her wrestling career, before GTT7 happened and her life changed forever. The meetings with the Icelandic Council, and with him. Seen through another’s eyes, Magnus could not help but feel empathy towards his daughter. Truth be told, had he been able to achieve this without bloodshed, and without the loss of his own child, he would have taken that route. Yet, he could not see a way to do it, and so his Gifts had brought him here. He looked down at his old body, now a lifeless carcass. He had been suffering from terminal cancer, and whilst his abilities had been able to slow down the progression, he had been living on borrowed time. He had always feared death, always been terrified of oblivion, and known that if there was an afterlife of any sort then for him it would not be comfortable. So he had found his bigger plans, reaching out towards immortality by finding the only person suitable to exercise the fifth and final Gift that he possessed. The Gift that had been forgotten over the ages, and become first a myth and then not even that. The Council’s records had been poor, and Magnus had needed to dig deeper into his country’s history than he had expected, but eventually he had learned the ways, and now it had paid off. "I... I... what happened... shit! Are you okay, Suze?" Charlie Larsson’s confused voice echoed around the hotel room. "I’m fine," said Magnus, the new voice sounding strange to him. "You were right all along. I’m sorry, Charlie. I’m sorry, but he tricked me. The bastard made me kill Lars and Gunnar." "Don’t worry Suze, we both survived, and he’s gone now. He is gone, right?" asked Charlie, coming in to grab hold of Magnus. "Oh, he’s gone. That’s the last you’ll ever see of him," said Magnus Larsson, encased as he was in the body of the Valkyrie. They embraced, and as they did so, Magnus thought about what his next moves could be. He’d had to play a long game to steal his daughter’s body in just this way, and now he was in unfamiliar territory. Her body still knew how to fight, and how to wrestle, but he was without his ability to coerce and make people forget. Luckily, he still had contacts who could clear up the mess that they stood amongst. Magnus Larsson released the man who had no idea as to his identity, and looked down at his hands. The blood was starting to dry on them now, caked in to his pores and the tiny ridges of his skin. He admired them, seeing the way that the lines flowed across his new body, and he came to a decision. Until he got regained his Gifts, if he was to regain them at all, he would have to continue as if nothing had happened. Nobody could suspect that something was up. He still had Suzanne’s memories, tucked away in the synapses of her bullish head, and he would use them to blend in, to be a chameleon until the time was right. Magnus Larsson flexed muscles that he had never worked to achieve, and smiled. This was, perhaps, even going to be fun.
