Conversations

17 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Temptation in Iowa Death is funny. In a not really sort of way, but it definitely is interesting. The permanent end of life; some fear it while others worship it. In some cases people even become deities as a result of it. A lake sat on the edge of Craig's house and it was there on this particular morning that Craig found himself lost in his thoughts as he felt the hollow edges of death on the fringe of his mind. His left shoulder was still sore, stitched together, and his mind was frazzled from his recent near-death experience. Except, he had actually died, and now was being given another chance at righting his wrongs. With a rock in his right hand he skipped it across the water, and watched as it disappeared into the water. The majority of his time lately had been filled with questions that he couldn't possibly begin to answer. Images had filled his mind in death, images he couldn't even be certain were real. Yet, if they were then they signaled that the end was coming, and it would be here before he knew it. Almost as if to hammer home the reality and seriousness of this situation, Alexandra Pierce had visited him. 'Thought I would find you here.' Craig glanced over his right shoulder and found Jack Harmen mere inches away from him. The sullen face of Craig looked back at the lake as Jack took a seat next to him and took a long gaze at the lake as well. 'You've been sitting out here for the past few days, Craig. Alone,' he said to Craig in this cautious tone. 'Are you asking me or telling me, Jack?' Craig responded with a question, his voice filled with venom. Jack immediately knew that Craig was on a completely different level, that something had changed him irrevocably outside of his own death. 'I'm just mentioning it. You went through so much lately. Samantha won't even let me in on what's happening. The problem I guess I'm having is that I thought we were friends. That you trusted me more so than most people around. Instead, you're hiding out here away from everybody, and I'm almost certain that you're planning something,' Jack notified him and Craig didn't illustrate a hint of emotion. Unconsciously, Craig touched his injured shoulder as he stared out at lake. It almost seemed like that Craig hadn't even heard what Jack had said. 'I'm sorry about Mary-Lynn, Jack. I never intended for that to happen. I just... I guess I didn't see it coming,' Craig reacted solemnly. Jack continued to look at his friend and could see the pain on Craig's face. 'Craig...,' Jack began but hesitated as his voice trailed off. Deep down, Jack wanted to be angry, and slap Craig around a bit in the process. Nonetheless, he couldn't find the energy or strength to do so. 'I know that you never intended for Mary-Lynn to get hurt. None of us expected it--,' Jack's voice cut out once again. 'Screw it, I'm pissed.' A bubbling sensation was rising into his chest as he expressed his true feelings. Craig looked back at Jack. 'About time. If I had to sit through you trying to rationalize everything instead of letting me have it, I'm certain I would've decked you,' he enlightened him. The two locked eyes for a moment and in that moment the friendship they shared went straight out the window. 'Well, since we're tossing the niceties out the window and feeding them to the dinosaurs, I'll be straight forward with you. You claim that you're the best around in your field, that you're capable of protecting people, but I all I see is bullshit. Everyone on your team is dead save Samantha. Mary-Lynn almost died. You almost died. So, explain to me how this all worked out to be part of your plan?' Jack attacked Craig, his words pointed, and his body language heated. Craig took a minute to let it all soak in as his eyes glanced towards the lake for a brief second. 'It didn’t. None of it went to plan. I got played, plain and simple Jack, and if I could go back and re-do it then I would. I fucked up, is that what you want to hear from me? Because, believe me, I didn’t want things to play out the way they did.” “So, instead, I have to watch as Mary-Lynn nearly dies on the table?! Or how about the fact that you were actually dead! How about you wake up and smell the roses. You’re not a savior. You’re not a God. At the end of the day you’re human and you keep getting yourself in over your head and have to find a way to bail yourself out of it before it gets too late. In a way, this time, you couldn’t get out in time, but somehow were granted a second chance at life.” Jack was right and Craig knew it. He didn’t want to stay on the subject though. “I gave Samantha some medicine that’ll help the healing process on Mary-Lynn. In a few days, she’ll be perfect, almost brand new, and she won’t even feel the effects of what happened to her. If I could remove her memories of that moment, I would.” Jack sighed as he shifted ever so slightly. “Do you think,” Jack commenced before he interrupted him. Craig gazed over at him and nodded his head. “Fine, do you think that God is giving you a second chance at life?” It was a question that had weighed heavily on Craig’s mind for the past few days. Somewhere along the way he had found an answer that felt suitable. “I think that my work here isn’t done. I think I needed to see things so I didn’t lose my way any longer. I think the images I saw were only capable in death-like state. I don’t think I was dead though. Just, not here any longer.” “That’s some fucked up life you’ve got going on over there, Craig. You think Cozen was behind any of it?” he asked and Craig wasted no time as he shook his head in disagreement. “I think Cozen is many things, but one of those things is not someone who can control life or death. I think those images were real and using Cozen as the messenger worked perfectly. I will always listen to what she has to say, no matter how out of her mind she is,” Craig confided into Jack who just sighed. “What’s her deal?” Craig smiled as he thought about it. “Well, she’s a person no matter what Alexandra or anyone else thinks. She’s just broken and has been for a long time. She’s gifted though, gifted beyond our wildest imagination, but she’s just not capable of dealing with it. One day she will, once Alexandra realizes what she’s done. In the end though, Alexandra holds that key and I can only hope that she’s let Cozen loose from her own personal hell.” “I’ve got to say,” Jack began. “I never knew that this is the kind of life that you led. Something as deep and confusing as this.” Craig glanced at his friend for a moment. “You want out?” “Never.” The two laughed at the sentiment and then gazed out at the water again. So much had changed in Craig’s life and a new landscape had just began to settle in. Deep down, he was happy that he had people around him who would always stick with him no matter what happened. As the images of Cozen and Alexandra lingered in his mind, and he thought about the past, he knew it was coming to the point where there would be no turning back. Secrets that had been kept would have to come out. Conversations would have to happen and the truth would have to finally be looked in the eyes. In a way, Craig was ready for that moment and had been for so long. The reality though was that he knew it would break him to the point of nearly dying. *** His shoulder throbbed with pain as he tossed and turned in the bed. Craig had an entire California King Sized bed to himself, but it never felt quite large enough especially in a world that felt like it was increasingly shrinking on him. A conversation with his buddy Jack had left him in quite the funk and he was certain that sleep would help solve that. It didn't. Unknown to him was that in the threshold of his bedroom door was a woman who wore a pair of blue silk pajama pants and a white spaghetti strap shirt. She fidgeted as she stood there, felt rather uncomfortable, and didn’t know who the real her was in this moment. Her auburn-colored hair flowed naturally and beautifully to her shoulders. The women kept wringing her hands uncomfortably as her green eyes stayed on Craig as he rolled around in the bed. Slowly, he opened his eyes and at first stared at the vacant ceiling above. As he cursed under his breath and proceeded to sit up, Craig saw that there was someone else in his room. Even with the lights off he could tell from the red hair who it was that stood on the edge of his room. “Mary-Lynn, what the hell are you doing awake? Much less, you know, in my bedroom in the middle of the night?” Her left hand trembled slightly as she awkwardly searched for the light switch and came across it unexpectedly. She flipped it on as a bright stream of light bathed the room from top to bottom and both Mary-Lynn and Craig covered their eyes in protest. “Alright, yeah, sure, you can turn on my light and kill my eyes in the process.” “Sorry,” she whispered nervously and inched into the room before she shut the door behind her. Craig stared at Mary-Lynn with this odd look on his face. “I just couldn’t sleep, and I guess neither could you. I think we both know why as well.” Craig took a long look at Mary-Lynn and motioned for her to come sit next to him. Cautiously, she did so, and the two sat there in silence as Mary-Lynn glanced around the room with uneasy eyes. “Mary-Lynn,” Craig began and knew he needed her attention first if they were going to work through this together. “What’s bothering you?” “Shouldn’t we be dead?” The question was straight to the point and took Craig by shock. He knew Mary-Lynn was healing quite well from the medicine that Samantha had been giving her the past couple of days. However, he didn’t even think about the fact that Mary-Lynn could be suffering mentally from what had taken place. “I guess when you think about the circumstances of what occurred Mary-Lynn, we should be dead.” “Is it wrong for me to wish that you were dead, then?” That one hurt and it showed on Craig’s face. Mary-Lynn though didn’t show any emotion with the question whether it be anger or sadness. She was rather indifferent. “Well, I would first have to tell you that you’re not the only one on this planet who wishes I was dead, but no, I don’t think it’s wrong of you. Not considering what happened to you and the part I played in all of that.” Craig stopped as he didn’t have any clue as to what else he could say and Mary-Lynn sighed. She shook her head. “I’m not talking about that.” Craig was lost at this point. “Then what are you talking about, Mary-Lynn?” It took her a second to form the words in her mind. “I just—I don’t want you to suffer any longer. I don’t want you to have to live this life sad with the deaths that have been put on your shoulder and the blood that has stained your hand. I don’t want to see you have to struggle with love or have to deal with having to be human when you’d much rather not. You deserve to be who you are and you don’t deserve to have to go through hell any longer.” Her words took all of the air out of him. Sheer disbelief took over him and he had never thought of his life in those fashions before. “When you put it like that,” Craig began to say as the shock washed over him. “I guess you have a point. Maybe in death is the only place I can find true happiness.” They sat there in silence again as Mary-Lynn glimpsed down at the ground as tears ran down her pale cheek. Craig desperately wanted to wipe them away, but managed to hold back on that urge. As the silence grew and the minutes passed, Craig put his hand on Mary-Lynn’s shoulder. “I’m sorry for what happened to you, Mary-Lynn,” he apologized, but she shook her head in response as she inched closer to Craig and he wrapped his arms around her. “You never wanted to see me get hurt; I understand that more than anything. If you had known, you would’ve been the one there to protect me. I get that and I don’t blame you for that. Alexandra knew how to get at you, how to play you, and you just couldn’t see it. You were dealing with so much on your plate. The sheer reemergence of Alexandra. Alexander Lewis. Your people. It was so much on your plate,” she told him through her persistent tears. He had essentially been absolved by Mary-Lynn and he didn’t know how to respond to that. “I don’t blame you.” The two just sat there as the sun began to peak over the horizon. Craig glanced at the granite clock on his wall and was amazed to find that the two had been there for well over ninety minutes. Mary-Lynn had fallen asleep once or twice though she quickly snapped out of it. With the tears dried on her cheek she began to sit up and gazed at Craig for what felt like an eternity. “What’s next?” she asked him and he was speechless at this question. Many things had pondered his mind on the next step to take, but none of them had sat particularly well with him. He needed a chance to take a break from everything, to see the world once again, and let his mind just clear out. None of the options that had presented themselves had fit though and it left him at a loss. “I don’t know yet,” he honestly told her and Mary-Lynn nodded in agreement. “I’m sure it’ll come to you sooner or later, and when it does, I’ll be there to help you along that path,” she said and Craig saw that the peppiness of Mary-Lynn was starting to return. That was definitely a good sign in his book and she kissed him on the cheek which warmed his soul. As he watched her walk towards the bedroom door, something bothered him greatly. “Mary-Lynn,” he spoke and she stopped in her tracks before she took a quick look at him with this wide smile on her face. “Why? Why would you help me out considering what happened?” She licked her lips before she scratched her head and then pointed at him. “Because, Craig, people like you need people like me.” *** The steam from the bathroom filtered out of the room as Craig opened the door. With only a towel wrapped around his waist he's quite surprised to find someone waiting for him as he walked through the threshold. His red eyes looked at the blonde as she sat on the edge of his bed and he turned his still wet back towards her as he opened his dresser and looked for some clothes. 'Samantha, I know you might be in the mood to get 'some', but honestly I don't think I'm in the mood,' Craig informed her with a sarcastic tone. She scoffed at the suggestion before she laid out on his bed. 'You only wish I was here for that Craig,' she nearly moaned at him and he just shook his head in response. He looked over his shoulder and noticed a manila folder resting on his bedspread. With a black shirt in his left hand, Craig walked over to the bed and glanced at it for a brief minute. Samantha could only look on as it looked as if Craig had zoned out. 'Trust me,' he said, his mind returning to an active playing field. 'I wish I had the strength for a round with you right now. But, I don't. Plane leaves in a few hours.' He then flipped open the folder and saw various documents and pictures waiting for him. Within the flicker of blink, Craig had tossed on a shoulder over his torso before he began sorting through the information waiting for him. 'Anything good?' 'She was speaking the truth,' Samantha revealed to him and a cold look overcame Craig as he stood there, his eyes fixated on nothing in particular. 'She left the Order of St. Julian or at least plans to. Apparently, she's decided to move into the same city as one Amy Campbell.' Craig's lips pursed one another as he nodded his head. 'Something tells me that this wasn't something Amy expected in the least bit. So be it. I just...,' Craig began before his voice trailed off. The idea of Alexandra leaving the Order, even for a short period of time, had deeply affected him. 'You know, she stood there in that hospital room, and she wasn't the same woman any longer, Samantha. Not the same person I had known for the better half of my life. She wanted...,' and once again, his voiced faded out. Samantha looked at Craig long and hard, a sense of sadness sweeping her in as well. 'She wanted peace. Love. Happiness. All of the things that you wanted with her,' Samantha helped him finish his sentence and Craig found himself nodding his head without even knowing it. The blonde felt for him as she grazed her fingers against his arm. It never registered with him though. 'Have I always been an optimist?' His words surprised her and more so because of how sudden it came at her. She swallowed hard as she fished around for an answer. 'Why do you ask?' she responded with a question, desperate to find out the underlying motive. He sat down on the bed next to Samantha and found himself looking into a mirror. 'It's something that Alexandra told me. That I've always been an optimist. Especially in the case of Cozen.' 'In her case,' Samantha began to respond. 'You have been. She's always been two cents short of two pennies. Then again, maybe she's right. It's why you decided to live, isn't it? Because you still see the good in Alexandra.' 'Am I foolish because of that? Because I think that I can still save her?' Samantha shook her head in response, but could tell it wasn't exactly what Craig was looking for. 'You want to be the thing that not just saves her from this world, but from herself. Craig, somehow in the midst of everything, all of the death and destruction that you've been privy to, your heart has remained in love. It's that love that separates you from Alexandra. It is why you're the light to her darkness. Because you care and you have a reason to care. Alexandra, she doesn't. It's her innate flaw, the one thing that eludes her, and the thing that leaves her feeling colder than Hell itself.' Samantha had spent plenty of time watching Alexandra and the way she worked. 'In the end though, without her, none of it is worth it.' Samantha took a long look at Craig and shook her head. 'And that's what will actually get you killed, Craig. She will get you killed because of your inability to let go.' 'Thanks Sam, glad to see that you're taking the necessary effort to make me feel better over here,' Craig responded sarcastically. Samantha in turn flashed him one of her patented smiles. 'So, why don't you tell me what's in Africa?' Craig sighed as Samantha asked him the question before he shrugged his shoulders and moved from the bed. 'Redemption, hopefully,' he honestly told her. Samantha shifted in her seat as Craig walked the length of his room towards a walk-in closet. 'Redemption? Craig, what the hell are you talking about? Why would you need redemption and how are you going to find that in Africa?' Her tone was unsteady much like her thoughts. Craig emerged from his closet with a few pair of jeans that he dropped onto the bed. Samantha's eyes met with his as he looked like a new man almost. 'There's blood on my hands, Samantha. Derek, Tyler, Eliza, and James are all dead. Alexander Lewis as well. Alexandra Pierce had to actually come to me and ask for me to just leave her be so she can try to have a new life, a happy one. Yet, here I am. I can't let go. There comes a point in a man's life when he had to stop pointing the finger at other people and finally look inside to find out what the true root of the problem is.' A moment of silence followed before Samantha nodded her head as if she understood. 'You think that problem is you?' Samantha pondered aloud and her words sent a jolt through Craig. He paused for a second to give her question a chance to truly settle. Craig's eyes closed as he thought about it for a split second. 'I don't know,' he told her as he opened his eyes. 'I might be the problem. I know that I'm not perfect; I'm not a man that can look at himself in the face right now either. For so long I wanted to put the blame on Alexandra's plate, but what if the blame belongs to me? I have to figure out it out.' Craig rounded the bed once again and walked into the closet once again. 'So, what's in Africa then? What there will help you find that redemption?' she asked as he walked back out of the closet, this time fully dressed. Craig dropped to his knees before he began looking under his bed. 'A man I met a few years back is living there. He's doing some honest-to-God good work down there. I talked to him last night and he'd like my help with this village he's been mentoring. Thinks I could help them break the mentality that they have to be involved in war, drugs, and sex, wants to bring some sort of order and semblance to their lives. Think I might be able to help them out.' He then pulled out a large leather bag that he tossed onto the bed right next to Samantha who decided to help him by throwing the clothes he had pulled out into it. 'Does this man actually know you?' she joked. Craig looked at him and rolled his eyes. 'It'll give me a chance to do something good for a change, you know? Actually get a chance to feel like I'm not just killing people, but making an actual difference in someone's life. It'll be good, I think. Just going out there for a week,' he informed her and she nodded her head as he zipped the bag up and tossed it towards the bedroom door. 'So, let me ask you. What do you know about the Order of St. Julian?' The question caught Samantha off guard and it was evident based off the facial expression on her face. 'Uh... I know that Alexandra is, or was now, a cell leader for one of their cells. Other than that, nothing really.' Craig nodded his head in agreement as he took the manila folder into his hands. 'You ever seen Alias?' he questioned her and she looked shocked. 'Since when have you watched television?' Craig chuckled at the suggestion. 'Jack bought me a ton of DVD's for Christmas. Told me I should watch the show, so I did. The Order of St. Julian is sort of like the show. An organization who's grasp is wide-reaching. Each cell has their purpose and they're everywhere. Been around forever to be frank and they're good at what they do. That's why Alexandra is with them,' Craig laid it all out for Samantha who just took it all in. 'If that's the case, then why aren't you part of the Order?' Samantha asked him and his expression became somber. 'They did, I turned them down. Obviously, Alexandra didn't. It's how I came out with the red eyes,' he responded. Samantha immediately wished she hadn't asked him the question. 'Anyways, you need to read up on them and get some information on them for me. I need to know everything, Samantha, and I do mean everything. Because, I have a feeling we're going to run into some issues with them in the near future. Especially now that Alexander Lewis is gone and Alexandra is on leave.' Samantha was confused though. 'Why? What is it that they do anyways?' 'Their mission,' Craig began before he took a deep breath in. 'Is to bring balance to the world, or so they say. Knock down the light when they're getting too big for their britches, and knock back the dark when it spreads too far. Something tells me--' 'Something tells you that they're not living by that message any longer,' Samantha interjected. Craig nodded his head to confirm her thoughts. 'Exactly. I think they've allowed the dark to linger a little too long. I think if Alexandra is serious about wanting a chance at a new life then I owe her that much, even if it's not with me. Unfortunately, the Order isn't going to be too happy with that idea and they will take matters into their own hands,' he told her as she looked on slightly confused. 'Wait, are you trying to tell me you're going to wage war against the Order to protect... Alexandra?' Samantha almost couldn't believe that the question came out of her mouth, much less that Craig didn't seem to be denying it either. Craig sighed as he weighed the options before him. 'Yeah. That's exactly what I'm saying.' 'Now, I've seen everything,' Samantha said in disbelief. 'Good. Plus, Sam, we're the light now and it's shining pretty brightly. Time to see if we can make some changes in this world before it all comes crashing down on top of us,' he told her before Craig kissed Samantha on her forehead. Samantha closed her eyes for the briefest of moments and when she opened them back up she was alone in the room. A smile appeared on her face before she shook his head. He never ceased to amaze.
