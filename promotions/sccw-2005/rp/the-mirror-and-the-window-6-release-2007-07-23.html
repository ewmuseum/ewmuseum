The Mirror and The Window 6: Release

23 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/23/07 6.1 the mirror: release Wrestling is supposed to be your release. You realized at a young age that the normal, everyday stresses of life were too much for you. With your estranged family, your high school bullies, and every other typical facet of life, you were stretched out and feeling like you needed something to keep you from going insane. And wrestling was supposed to be that thing. Your one love that made everything easier to deal with. Until it became too much to deal with itself. It was understood since you dotted the "i" in your last name on that FUSE contract that you were going to have duties besides just coming out to loud rock music and putting your arms around other men’s necks. You were supposed to be at appearances, license and subtly promote your merchandise (you wear the new life shirt every time you’re in public now, much to Katie’s dismay), and be a face for the company. And all that was well and good. But now the everyday hassle of the business is almost as stressful as the everyday hassles of real life. And what was supposed to be a release is something that you need release from as well, and you’re not really sure where to turn. And when you’re unsure of where to go to and who to see, you end up finding the only familiar and friendly face you can think of. 6.2 the window: sarah riley "Any more visits from you and I should get a counselor’s license and start charging you two hundred a visit," Foster Nackedy dryly says as he opens the door and lets Jonathan into his house. "How are those frequent flier miles working out for you? Flying from Indy to Baton Rouge is tough work, man. You sure you don’t need to buy a private jet or something?" Foster is smiling, but Jonathan can see there is a troubled look behind his eyes. Grabbing the beer offered to him, Jonathan looks around nervously. "I heard about your, uh…" Jonathan starts, unsure of how to continue. Jonathan has never had the ability to talk about uncomfortable situations; it’s a side effect he picked up from living in a reclusive family. "My time in jail?" Foster crudely finishes, gulping down his beer perhaps a bit too fast. "It was painless – barely even lasted a day. I got in, got my lawyer to explain the situation, and got out before anybody got any ideas. Well, one guy started eyeing me and was making fun of my last name, but…you know. I handled it." Foster sighs, and when he looks at Jonathan the troubled look he had seen had vanished. "So why are you here?" "Well…" and although Jonathan has been easily able to focus on the thoughts in his own head, it is another problem getting them out. "I know I’ve asked this before, but it’s become a common problem." Foster eyes Jonathan, and he can almost tell what the question is before it’s asked. "How did you do it?" murmurs Jonathan, looking at his feet and shaking his head. "How did you deal with both wrestling and everyday life when the wrestling got to be too difficult to deal with?" Foster nods, then kicks back his beer, finishing it quickly. "I know something that can help you." Jonathan waits for Foster to elaborate, but all he does is stand and walk towards his kitchen. After a moment, Rhine stands and follows him. "Yes?" Foster looks at his protégé with a grim look and shakes his head. "You’re not going to like it." Jonathan sighs, exasperated at not having the answer given to him. "Come on, man. Just tell me." Foster nods after a brief pause, then holds up a piece of paper. Jonathan takes the paper and looks at it strangely. "What is this?" "That’s Sarah Riley’s number. She specializes in…taking wrestler’s minds off of things," Foster says, trying not to smirk at Rhine’s horrified reaction. 6.3 the mirror: mallory You are immediately brought back to your high school days, a memory that is as fresh as it is painful. You’ve only cheated once in your life. You’re not proud of it, and the taste it left in your mouth still hasn’t entirely left you, but that doesn’t mean that it didn’t happen. Her name was Mallory. It’s funny to note that you don’t even remember the name of the girl you were dating, but you’ll never forget Mallory. Even now sometimes you think about her, and her image leaves you with a guilty, yet satisfied pleasure. The girl you were dating (Jane, perhaps?) had just yelled at you about something, and you were angry. You were too immature to realize that relationships are supposed to have moments of yelling, and not everything is supposed to be all easy, all the time. So when it happened, you left school, just started walking. Eventually you made your way to a gas station, and you bought a Pepsi to cool both yourself and your emotions. As you were getting ready to walk back to school, you saw her standing on the corner of the street, looking around lazily, smoking a cigarette. Something about her hit you, something you still haven’t been able to identify after all these years. Something made you go up to her and pretend that you had also skipped school because you were a badass, and not because you were simply mad at your nameless girlfriend. After a few minutes, she asked you if you wanted to kiss her. You were so thrown off by the question that you didn’t reply. She took your silence for consent. She tasted like an ashtray, and surprisingly you didn’t really mind it. You were caught in the moment, high off of what you were doing…and then it was over, and you felt terrible. As you walked back to school, you tried to think of some sort of way to tell your girlfriend what happened. By the time you got back, you had convinced yourself not to tell her, and you never did. It still makes you feel sick inside, so there’s no way you’re going to cheat on anyone again. Especially not Katie. 6.4 the window: bottled "No way," Jonathan says, returning to Foster’s living room to grab his backpack and make a hasty retreat. "I told you that you wouldn’t like it," his trainer says simply, watching slightly bemused as Jonathan tries to leave. But he doesn’t. "Why would I like it? You’re talking about adultery! You’re talking about cheating on the one person who has stuck by me." Foster raises his eyebrows. "And the same person you need a release from?" This stops Jonathan cold, which gives Foster time to keep going. "If everything is going so good, why were you relying so much on your job being an escape? Why do you need something now when your job is getting to be so stressful? Why don’t you go back home and treat her as your release?" After a mocking pause, Foster continues. "Oh, that’s right. You need to break from her. From the normal. And let me tell you something, Sarah Riley isn’t normal. She’s good at what she does, and she helps. Better yet, she’s in Indianapolis, about three blocks from where you just moved. If you don’t want to take the advice I give you, that’s fine. But you’ve got to know that if you keep your personal life bottled up in the ring, and your professional life bottled up at home, eventually those bottles are going to burst, and you along with them." Even with the gravity of the situation, Jonathan cannot suppress a smile. "Good analogy." "I thought so." Jonathan pauses, then shakes his head. "I don’t know, man. I’m not the cheating kind. I can’t do that to Katie." Foster nods, and his face softens. "I understand, Jon. I do. I know you want to be the good guy, and that’s damned honorable of you. But sometimes…" Foster sighs, and again Jonathan sees the troubled nature under his features. "Sometimes being the good guy is too much to take." Jonathan stares at Foster for a second, then nods and slings his backpack over his shoulder, muttering, "See you around," before walking out of the front door. 6.5 the mirror: he envies you You don’t know why you keep going back to him. For the first few visits, you would leave Baton Rouge with a cleansed emotional palette. You voiced your concerns, and while he wasn’t always comforting you would leave his home feeling better for some reason. But now you haven’t gotten anything from the last few visits, and you’re wondering why you keep coming back. You appreciate his honesty, and the way he doesn’t just suck up to you like everyone else from your hometown did. But when he offers you the number of someone who may or may not be a prostitute, that’s where you have to draw the line. You always looked up to Foster as a sign of strength, as the guy who was stable enough to come to whenever you had a problem. But the last few times you’ve visited him, you’ve noticed his troubled face that he sometimes forgets to hide. You’ve heard about the trouble he’s been in recently. And just now, when he was ranting about it being okay to cheat, you saw his eyes, and you realized something for the first time. He envies you. You’ve been sitting at his side for years, thinking about how great he was and how lucky you were to be learning from him, and he is jealous of your success. You knew something was wrong when you went to train with him for the first time after you won your title and he didn’t congratulate you or tell you how well you were doing, something he would always do before. He envies you, and that makes it harder to keep coming to him for advice. How can you constantly seek help from someone who doesn’t want you to succeed? How can you continue to look up to a man who is wrapped up in jealousy? You don’t know the answers to any of these questions. It’s probably better for you to worry about it later. You’ve got your normal life to get back to, after all. 6.6 the window: shutting out The light is starting to dim as Jonathan arrives home, shutting the door behind him. He sits down on the couch and closes his eyes, taking time to rest and relax after a long flight and a busy day… "Jonathan, get in here now!" comes Katie’s angry voice. Jonathan sighs and stands up, walking into the room warily. As he enters, he sees Katie curled up in bed, looking at him suspiciously. "Where were you?" she asks. "I told you where I was, I was in Baton Rouge, with Foster." Katie looks at him for a second and shakes her head before rolling over. Jonathan hates this move, and he lets out an exasperated moan as he sits on the bed and looks over her. "Come on, what?" "I hate that you’re always gone. You just came back from your match and then you left for Louisiana…I thought you said I would get to have you every day that you weren’t wrestling." Jonathan sits there, his mouth slightly open, not sure what to say in response. Finally, he tries. "Uh…I’m sorry. I just have been trying to get my head around some things. I’ve been feeling…" He is surprised with the quick movement of her body rolling over to face him, and even more surprised when he sees her tear-streaked face. "Messed up? Depressed? Not sure of yourself? Dammit, Jonny, did I move in with a pussy?" And if he couldn’t have been more surprised before, he definitely is now. "Uh…I…" "You’re always needing help, lacking direction, not sure of what to do, all the time. You sit there, solid as a rock in front of me, whenever I want to help you refuse it, but you always go everywhere else, you fly halfway across the fucking country to get advice from someone else." Jonathan shakes his head in anger and leaves the room, but Katie is right behind him, shouting. "I’m your girlfriend, Jon! You can talk to me. You can tell me anything. Don’t walk away, don’t make me the only person you’re shutting out." Jonathan turns around in a whirl, taking Katie by surprise. He looks at her with fury in his eyes, and opens his mouth as if to give her a blistering retort. One never comes. Instead he shakes his head and leaves, slamming the door. Katie watches him out of the window and sighs, crying more tears as she moves to the bedroom. 6.7 the mirror: lizard in a jar You wanted to tell her that she wasn’t the only one you were shutting out, but you doubt that would make her feel any better. The truth is, you’re not really offering all of yourself to anyone. Not Katie, not even Foster. You haven’t truthfully been completely open with anybody. And that’s why you need a release. Release from being one-dimensional in the wrestling ring, from being a guy who, while admired and respected, is only seen as a good guy who is good at fighting rough. From being punching bags every week by guys who don’t have the same scruples as you do. Release from coming home and not being able to talk to your girlfriend. Not because you don’t care about her, or because you don’t think she’ll understand you…because you don’t know if she’s going to end up being the wedge between you and the business. Because you’re afraid she might one day cost you your job. Everything you’ve been hiding from FUSE and Katie will eventually consume you. Foster told you that. And now you’re holding things from him as well. You’re starting to feel like you’re a lizard trapped in a jar, and no one left you any holes in the top of the jar so you can breathe. Eventually you’ll be out of air, and you don’t know what will happen after that. God damn, you need a release more than anything. And at this point, you don’t care how you get it. 6.8 the window: mallory part 2 Jonathan sighs, putting his hands in his jacket and staring at a wooden door blankly. He breathes slowly, looking nervous. After a few seconds, the door opens and a woman looks out, wearing a night robe and staring at Jonathan, confused. "Can I help you?" Jonathan timidly looks down at his feet. "Uh, Ms. Riley?" The woman still looks confused, but answers in a confident voice. "Yes, that’s me." "My name’s Jonathan. I’m…I’m a wrestler. Foster Nackedy sent me." The confusion melts away into a smile as she opens her door wider, then steps back to allow Jonathan entry. "Come in, Jonathan, come in. I’ll make you some coffee."
