A Steel Rose Grows Anew

undated

Date: 2 months ago With the back of her head buried into the top of the rear passenger seat padding, Dawn McFarlane had her striking green eyes closed. The Steel Rose was not asleep, they had only come 20 minutes into downtown Fort Lauderdale. The focus, the single objective in mind, was so important to deal with that she ddin't want to be distracted by anything else. The Haitian driver, a knockoff of George Clinton, informed the Mistress of the mat, 'We are here, my friend.' She always hated being called friend by people she didn't know. To her, they didn't mean it, and if they knew her, they'd rescind it anyway. The meter ran at $17, but Dawn wasn't on a one-way trip. She had to do just one thing and then she'd be gone. Leaning forward she handed a worn twenty to the driver. 'Just give me $2 please.' She wasn't watching him as she spoke, instead looking out the window at the polished front of the Cloud Nine wrestling academy. Pocketing her change she slips out of the back door, dragging her backpack behind her. It was intended to be a short trip, so the usual suitcase and luggage assortment had been left at home. She was a long way from home, anyone looking at her could tell it easily, travel backpack aside. Her milk white skin was already starting to tingle under the Florida sun, and sweat darkened the collar of her t-shirt and dampened her red-blonde hair. Sighing she ran a her fingers through the stringy mess atop her head. 'What am I doing here?' The question had been asked before, many times, though phrased differently. Adjusting the strap of her bag she gnawed the bottom of her lip, watching silhouettes move behind the glass of the windows. Her relationship with Ciel, the owner of the building she now stood before, had not been a good one...and somehow also not a bad one. They had been at odds, opponents since the day he stepped through GCW's doors, but there had been a level of civility she'd never encountered in an enemy before. Her attempts to drive Ciel away had met in failure, failure and understanding. He'd been one of the first men she'd ever encountered who both wanted to understand her and did -not- want to climb into her pants. Or if he did, he wasn't obvious about it. It niggled at her, gnawed and pulled at the back of her mind. It had since they'd first met, and only intensified in the intervening two years since they'd parted ways with GCW. Now that her life had stabilized some and she didn't always feel the rug was going to be pulled right out from under her...it was something she could no longer avoid addressing. 'Apologize and leave,' She mumbled, setting her hand on the door. 'I'll be on tomorrow's flight back to BC.' Taking a deep breath she pushes the door open and steps inside. What greeted Dawn's eyes was the most organized, largest training facility she'd ever seen. There were four rings, each with a different trainer. Many students learning techniques, or working out in a rear weight room. Even some mats for gymnastics. She'd heard that this was Ciel's school, but if so, where was he? Wrestlers always started their own small training dojo and then made the whole affair an exercise in beating down newbies whose dream it was to get into the sport. There was none of that here... it was a business, definitely, but everything was orderly and even, judging by the faces of the trainees, fun. There was smiling. Laughing. Nothing like her training experience. 'You. I'm looking for Ciel. Where is the owner?' Dawn demanded, grasping an arriving Chinese girl by the shoulder. Her answer was a flick of the wrist and a sharp elbow jabbing into her side. The petite fighter couldn't be more than 120 pounds, not even 5'7', but she knew what she was doing. A familiar voice chastised the smaller girl for the attack. 'Zha, that wasn't necessary.' It was Ciel, stepping out of his office. 'Your class is waiting in ring three. Ms. McFarlane, please. Come in.' There he stood, all 235 pounds of him wearing white Consadole Sapporo soccer jersey with blue stripe. The jeans and sneakers let her know that he wasn't in the mode to train anybody today. Being the professional that he was, he always wore proper tights and boots. Not like other wrestlers who would walk in off the street and hop into the ring with whatever they'd been wearing that day. Still smarting from the unexpected assault, Dawn turns to face the man she was looking for, off-guard and not liking it one bit. Folding her arms she sizes him up as she always had, one eyebrow raising slightly at his attire. 'Big Japanese soccer fan?' Ciel shrugged, stepping back into his office and taking his seat. 'Well I was born there,' he stated plainly, leaning back in the leather ergonomically-shaped rolling chair, 'but actually, a student asked me to wear it.' The cleanly-arranged desk had an iMac upon it. It was opened to the internet, he had been sending an e-mail to Tegan at Apple One. Rotating the axis to face Dawn directly, the man asked simply, 'So then, what brings you here?' Stepping in after him, Dawn eases the door shut slowly behind her and leans back against the frame, mulling over the answer to the question. The very same question she'd been asking herself since she'd decided to embark on this 'mad' adventure. 'You. Well, duh, obviously. You and I, us. But not us like *us* but like...' She growled, frustrated as she raked her fingers through her hair again. 'Lemme start again.' Taking a deep breath she pushes off the doorframe and begins to pace, arms either folded or gesturing from moment to moment. 'I came to see you. I need to talk to you about GCW, about you...and how you were while we were there. And how I was when I was there.' She frowns, tapping one finger against her chin. 'You're...an anomaly to me, like I'm *this* close to understanding you...without being anywhere close to understanding you. I need to clear things, I need to...apologize I guess.' At that she stops, turning to face him, green eyes earnest. Anybody could see that she was confused, that wasn't genius insight. CL, however, could tell from the woman's body language and her somewhat wavering tone that she was unclear about her feelings. She knew that there was something that she needed, but wasn't understanding what that was. So there she stood, asking this man she'd never spoken to more than a few minutes a week over the course of a month, for an apology for a trangression or wrongdoing. Giving this sight, the man could only say, 'Apologize for what?' After all, was she apologizing for behaving the way she did, but still feeling fully justified in her assessment of him at the time? Was she apologizing for having distrusted him? Was she even apologizing *about* Ciel or was it more an apology to herself and she needed somebody to vocalize it to? 'For how I acted, towards you and about you...it wasn't fair.' She winds and unwinds several strands of hair around one finger, her other arm held tightly against her midsection. 'Being on the opposite side of the ring is one thing, but there wasn't any need for how...ah...judgemental I was. It sort of goes above and beyond you know?' She turns away again, walking to one window to pause for a moment before moving to another. 'I didn't know anything about you, I still don't. And the way you treated me was unnerving, it confused me...it still confuses me.' Making an annoyed noise she ruffles both hands through her hair before smoothing it out again. Quick steps take her to the nearest chair and she seats herself, an obvious effort to control the nervous energy that's been driving her all about the room. 'You were nothing but polite and I was nothing but an ass. And I'm sorry, I wish I hadn't been.' 'I see,' was all she got as the wrestler formerly known as The End took in everything she'd said. It did appear as though the apology was truly genuine. Dawn McFarlane, Miss Untouchable, was opening up to a stranger. Apologizing. The question was, would CL accept? 'Apology accepted.' That answered that. Interlocking his fingers, he leaned forward while setting his forearms to the surface of the desk. As Dawn inhaled deeply, but somehow she wasn't relieved. It was what she wanted to hear, but it felt... empty. She came to apologize, but now that she'd gotten it, it was as though it were too easy. 'Is there anything else you need?' he asked of McFarlane. 'Uh...no I guess not.' She smiled weakly, settling back into her chair. 'Unless you know of anything really fun I can do until ym flight tomorrow.' Hands down, arms folded behind his head, Ciel leaned back at an angle with a thoughtful expression on his face. The ceiling was uncovered, exposing the air ducts and framing to the floor above. It was all very sturdy, more so than it appeared. 'Since you have some time, perhaps we can use this opportunity to talk about why you were the way you were. After all, you apologized, but I'm not convinced that you've really come to terms with why you behaved as you did. You certainly haven't come to me with an explanation of anything, just an admittance that it may have not been the socially-acceptable way of addressing me. That's a good start, but it won't bring any real closure.' The way that he spoke, the way CL could break down the situation... it was uncanny. It was the reason that he always put Dawn at an unease. She prided herself on being unapproachable. She thought herself as the complicated individual, and she was. And yet, whenever they had dealt with each other, Ciel saw right through her smokescreens, broke through the walls she'd put up. He didn't know everything about her, in fact, that was part of the problem she had with him. He would dissect and destroy all of her barriers, come so close to the core of the woman, and then walk away. It was as though, as much as he had no regard for all of her defensive mechanisms and constantly took it as a welcome challenge to get past it, he wouldn't force himself into her mind until she was willing to invite him. 'So tell me,' as his eyes dropped back to Dawn's, 'how you got here. You took a plane, took a taxi, but I want to know as much of your life, and your way of thinking; your goals and pressures and everything in between that eventually led to you coming here to apologize for the rudeness.' She blinks once, off-guard again and remembering why talking to him always set her teeth on edge. Folding both arms around her midsection she cocks her head to one side and regards him curiously for a long moment. 'Where did that come from? No offense but not only are we not the best of the friends, but until a couple seconds ago we'd been nothing but enemies. Why would I tell you that? Hell why do you want to know? Wouldn't you be just as happy if I turned around and marched myself out of here?' And that was the crux of the issue. With one notable exception, most people preferred Dawn McFarlane in small doses. With Ciel she'd always had to force the size the of the dosage, or risk being pinned down long enough to have to explain herself. Now she'd come to his world, on less then her own terms...and was finding herself pinned already. Ninja Toadie: A strong, brown-skinned hand rubbed at the trimmed goatee Ciel sported. The other remained behind his head, sitting atop the braided cornrows that his daughter gave him. 'Why would I be happy? I see a woman who has a lot of issues going on in her head.' Sitting up straight, he continued. 'Granted, we're still little more than acquaintances, but let's be clear here: you came to me. I'm listed in the phone book, you could have found my number online in less than a minute and called me to ask for forgiveness. Even 12-step programs for recovering alcoholics don't demand that they be physically present to make amends. You're here, in my office, and what I don't see is a woman who is satisfied with the forgiveness that I just gave. The forgiveness that you ostensibly flew out here to seek. Suppose I just sent you away. What would that accomplish? I'd think nothing more of it because so many wrestlers have been rude to me over the years that it rolls off of me like water. And you'd still be unfulfilled and unclear how to fix it.' Ciel stood up and walked around the desk. Dawn tensed up, clenching a fist. It wasn't the fact that she was worried about an invasion of her personal space; Ciel knew better than that. She just didn't like it when there was an attempt to empathize too much with her. How could someone empathize if they didn't know her? But she didn't let people in to understand her. It was a cyclical sequence of putting others off, but it was how she functioned and she got into the rut. And for a while she liked the rut, she consigned herself to it. The owner of the Cloud 9 Training Facility did not approach Dawn herself, but she was close to the thin door that separated them from the workout area. The door, and indeed the entire wall of the office facing the rings, was composed of a strong plastic plexiglass. It allowed him to keep an eye on what was happening outside, but the noise didn't penetrate. Usually that wasn't a problem, but now and again there was an important call that required his full attention and no ambient sounds of bodies slamming against a mat. Looking out at the students and other trainers, CL shared that, 'I'm a wrestler. I compete for the sport of it and I'm happy with what I've done in the ring. But my greatest achievement, my calling, is to serve not as a solo competitor but as a trainer and mentor. All of these people here, be they teens or adults, came here because they wanted to train. And I give them that training. Beyond that, though, I give them my ear. I provide a nonjudgmental shoulder to lean on. For all of these people, I'm as much a counselor as I am a wrestling coach. If any of them needs to talk, at any time of day, if they have to vent about their frustrations... if they can't find a job or if they can't afford rent. If they need a carpool or conflict resolution. If they're concerned about the state of their relationship with a spouse or their boyfriend or girlfriend. Whatever that problem is, I take it upon myself to help them because somebody has to and more of them need it than they first realize.' Ciel's arms were folded behind his back. He didn't move from the glass, understanding her need to have a certain shell around herself when not in a wrestling match. Outside, one of his students waved to him, a young man not even 16 years old but with a drive to succeed that outshone any in his class. The owner gave him a smile and a nod. 'I can't force you to do anything, Dawn,' he said, his attention on her now. 'You could walk out of here now and I wouldn't stop you. I would, however, like you to at least take with you the understanding that my intent and offer is not to change you or fix you. That's your responsibility. I'm only offering you the chance to talk to someone who has no preconceptions of you. When I ask what brought you here, you have no esteem to damage in my eyes. You don't have to worry about how I'll take anything you say.' His head turned toward Dawn, slightly, just enough to get her form into eyesight. Once again, CL's eyes were at the level of her own, though she wasn't really looking for that human connection right then. 'Furthermore, I wish for you to understand that I am not perfect. I don't consider myself to know everything, and I certainly don't know you. As much as I am a good listener, it requires practice and patience. Not only that, but it requires an understanding of the speaker. Now, in your case, you know why you are apologizing. I do not, and for my own part that makes the acceptance of your apology a hollow gesture. There's more to it than a mean look one time backstage at a Worldwide event. I'm interested in you as a person, and if I am to be able to understand and help others who may have the same temperament as you, I have to be able to understand where they're coming from. So as much as it seems as though I'm prying, this is as much a request to you, as a learning experience for me.' Breaking eye contact she looks away, brow furrowing as she tries to process everything he's said. It was true and she knew it, him accepting her apology had done nothing for her state of mind, nothing for a sense of resolution. If anything it had made her want that feeling more, to achieve some kind of end she point to, like an piece of an anchor to cling to in a storm. 'You're one of the only people who's tried to get to know me for a reason other then worming into my bed.'' She forced out, cheeks pinkening. 'Even when I threatened you backstage you were always asking why, always...knocking the mask aside and making my stand face-to-face.' Her arms tightened around her midsection, body language closing as she tucked her knees up and shrunk in on herself. 'I need to know why, I need to know why you care....and...I need to know if it works. I'm trying to get better, trying to stabilize and learn, to fix whatever it is I've done to myself.' She looks up again finally, her expression curious and fearful. 'If you can knock my mask aside...maybe you can show me how to do it myself.' His eyebrows raising, he exhaled slowly. 'I will do my best.' There wasn't a motion uncontrolled. Dawn wanted that for herself. She could only imagine what manner of self-confidence it took to be Ciel, even as he was telling her that he was only human. And for Ciel's part, he couldn't fully appreciate the vulnerability that Dawn felt as he was an open person himself. He would have to learn why she was the way she was. It would have to be a team effort. CL would have to get to know Dawn so well, more than he even expected to, in order to give her the help that she needed; but it would have to be a reciprocation, and that meant properly sharing himself with her so that she didn't feel at a disadvantage.. And Dawn would have to give Ciel the benefit of the doubt, not just let him be nice but let him be the counselor that he fancied himself, and trust that he wouldn't use anything against her. Not that he could, they weren't in any company, wrestling against each other. How she could feel that he was always one-upping her, even as she'd beaten him when they faced off in the ring... she didn't like that feeling, but Dawn was willing to entertain the notion that the problem was hers to overcome. She wasn't a lost cause... she just needed some help from somebody whose only job it was to provide it. That was what made Ciel a good man, a good person to come to for her problems. Now, the hard part would begin. 'You know,' Ciel took his seat again, 'This may not be something we can accomplish by tomorrow. In fact, I'd want you to take a day or two to gather your thoughts, at least the ones you're comfortable with sharing.' Exhaling she nodded slowly, relaxing a muscle at a time. 'I can cancel my ticket, get a refund if I'm lucky. I guess I need to find a place too, maybe an apartment or loft somewhere near here.' Looking at her backback ruefully she runs one hand through her hair. 'I'll need to go shopping too, I only brought a day or two of clothes.' That impressed Ciel. He wasn't at all considering her temporarily moving down to Florida. After all, she didn't need any wrestling training like the others at this school. Maybe just another flight out, then talking over the phone or e-mail. And here was Dawn, from one minute thinking to up and leave, the next, talking about apartments. 'That's far more than I was expecting you to say, you surprised me. I don't want to disrupt your life with this, necessarily. But you wouldn't say that if you had many obligations back in British Columbia... if you're serious about staying in the area, I wouldn't want you to break the bank to do so. Rent in the area can be exorbitant, even by South Florida standards. I have a sizable guest house next to my own; if you'd like, you may stay there... at least for the night, until you find another place if that's what you want. There is another tennant, but she's usually out all hours of the day, or at my own house, so you shouldn't run into her if you just take the empty room to sleep.' She mulls it over for a moment, lips pressed together as she rolls the idea around her head for taste. Even if she were to meet the other woman, it was unlikely she'd be stand-offish, or rude...no more so then Dawn herself anyway. And it was a better alternative to looking for an apartment or staying in an expensive hotel or sleazy motel. 'For now I think that sounds like my best bet. You know the area better then I, so we can talk about it more after the first night.' 'Alright then,' Ciel gave her a friendly smile, reaching for the mouse and clicking on his internet phone. A string of 10 numbers quickly entered on the 10-key pad at the side of the white keyboard and the speakers gave a ring. 'Hello?' the female voice floated out from the monitor speakers, sounding somewhat busy. In the background, ambient sounds of hard impacts and voices... he was calling inside the building. Easier to be heard that way, than to shout outside the door. Ciel asked, 'Would you come to the office please? I need to introduce you to someone.' A quick 'Alright,' was the response before the phone hung up. 'Just a moment,' Ciel closed out the program. Dawn nodded, wondering who he was calling for her to meet. That mystery didn't last long when the door opened and in stepped a huge woman. Dawn herself was bigger than the average female wrestler at 6' and 148 pounds, but the sun-kissed ethnic Indian woman she saw walking through the door in mangled pink Hello Kitty shirt cut to make it a sleeveless halter top, with white tearaway warmup pants covering her legs. The woman had to be at least six-foot-four and her muscular frame, though toned to a feminine shape, still struck Dawn with the estimate of close to 200 pounds. 'Hi,' she entreated, passing the threshold and closing the door behind her. CL stood up, greeting her, and waving his left hand in gesture toward his guest. 'Saadra, we're going to have company, for the night at least. You probably know of her already, but this is Dawn McFarlane. She'll need the extra room in the guest house. I trust you'll take care of her.' Saadra smiled and offered a hand to McFarlane to shake. 'Nice to meet you, Dawn. I'm Saadra M--' 'Saadra Mehta,' McFarlane interjected, knowing her own identity already. 'I saw you and Tohya Miho on television when Pinnacle Pro Wrestling was on airplay in BC. You're very good.' The Steel Rose gave a grin, her eyes taking in the woman standing over her. Choice in clothing aside, it was clear from her physique and the strength in her handshake that Saadra wasn't just for show; there was real strength there. If there was one thing Dawn liked seeing, it was strong women... and not just because McFarlane was lesbisexual (in other words, having a heavy preference for attraction to females). 'Oh, Saadra,' Ciel jumped in again. 'Warn Zha that Dawn will be over for dinner tonight.' Dawn blinked, turning her head back to the man she sought for help getting herself back together. 'Dinner?' He nodded matter-of-factly. 'We're having a big dinner tonight, it's one of my daughters' birthday. She's turning 11. Plenty of food to go around and you might as well join in the occasion.' Dawn was moved. Not 10 minutes ago they were this close to never seeing each other again and now he was inviting her to share in a family moment. 'I--' taken aback by this, she searched her mind for the words to say. 'I don't have anything to wear.' 'Nonsense,' Saadra patted her on the shoulder, obviously not having the same hang-ups about personal space with others. 'We're a real casual family.' McFarlane tipped her head. 'We? You two are...?' She hadn't placed it, in fact, she thought Ciel was the least sexual man she'd ever laid eyes on. Aside from being nice to her for little more than the desire to be nice, Ciel also had a long friendship with Tessa Windsor and yet seemed to be the only man who never attempted to date her. Ciel laughed, capable of it even though he always seemed so cool and reserved. Ms. Mehta shook her head, explaining, 'We're friends; just friends.' 'Oh,' Dawn dropped her chin into the top of her chest, unclear why she felt like Ciel's relationship status mattered at all when she didn't think of him in that way, and she herself was in a long-standing open relationship with someone else. 'Saadra and Zha are trainers here, and they live with me, officially as tennants,' the man let her know. Saadra added, 'We've been with him for a few years now, and we're so involved with Sky and his kids that we're just part of the family now. Like Full House; only I'm Uncle Joey and Zha is... well, she's not Jesse exactly.' 'Not if she's dating Tania, she's not.' 'But she is the coolest one of all of us.' 'Cool as measured by 106 and Park.' 'Who would know cool more than them?' Saadra and CL shared a laugh. Dawn observed how casual they were with each other. How comfortable they were, not strangers at all. They addressed each other as equals. Just as Ciel had tried to do with her 2 years ago in GCW. Dawn decided then and there that she wanted the same. To have her own sense of family. To have people whom she could cut loose with and laugh, joke, love, and not have to think about anything else but enjoying herself. She saw that in these two, she saw that she might be able to understand how to get that for herself by being near Ciel for a time. It began tonight, a new awakening for her. But first, she had to say something, the right thing, the only thing that would get her off on the proper start. 'Ciel... Skylar... thank you.'
