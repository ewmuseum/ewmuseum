Live or Die, Part IV 'Fever Pitch'

Date: whatever today is Live or Die, Part IV "Fever Pitch" 1 'The Final Piece" Harold Gabriel, better known by his friends and patrons as simply Harry, poured another beer with another phony smile for the customer and handed it over. He was seventy four years old now, and he sometimes wondered if he wasn't getting a bit old for this business of running a bar. After all, the times had gone and changed on him, and the patrons had changed since the old days. Back in those days, people were happy to come in, shoot some pool, and knock back a few beers without having to have the smut that everybody seemed to want in this new age. They had gathered, instead of the quiet little tavern that Harry's father and grandfather had run, at the strip clubs uptown, or at the bigger bars that offered video lottery terminals, slot machines, and video games. And so Harry had been forced to conform to the times. Harry poured himself a beer, the doc's worry about his blood pressure be damned, and took a sip of that good, cold brew while he looked around. His father would be aghast at the state of things today, but it was either this or close up shop. The big red stage against the far wall was a new addition, built only five years ago, the pole standing up and gleaming in the dim lighting. The peeler wasn't using it just now, she was too busy rubbing those surgically enhanced tits against the face of some young punk in exchange for a twenty dollar bill. Harry hated having that in his bar, but he had to admit it did bring in the coin. Some fellas were against the other wall, playing the slots, while still some others shot a little pool at the big tables. But still, after all these years, Harry felt like he was ready to quit. But two previous generations of this family had died behind this very bar, and he's promised his father he'd run it till the end. He only had another four years to go until he'd caught the age dad had been when he died back here, and nine until he caught up to granddad. The old barkeep sighed and took in another mouthful of cool ale, feeling it wash against his tonsils, bitter yet so damn good that he indulged in another mouthful. He ruffled his graying hair with a calloused hand and turned back to the floor. Quite often, on nights like this, it was just him and his niece, Jasmine, here holding down the fort. Nobody wanted to work on these Saturday nights, though it wasn't such a bad night. But, the oldster sighed to himself, while he served beer and cheap chicken wings, other people had gone out and married, had children, things he'd been too busy with the bar to do... he sometimes wondered if he hadn't wasted his life in here. He saw Jasmine slide over the bar, her black skirt and tank top sticking to her every curve with the heat of the bar, and stand for a long minute in front of the fan before she grabbed another mug of beer and handed it to its owner. With a short glance at the mug in her uncle's hand, a frown stole over her classic features. 'You shouldn't be having that, you know." Yeah, he guessed that he did know. 'You shouldn't be in a dive like this, either, Sassy." His old nickname for her brought a smile to her face. Harry was proud of this girl, she was in fact the closest thing to a daughter he'd ever had. She worked hard and would do well for herself. Not only was she amazingly beautiful, with her waist length blond hair, ocean blue eyes, and the body that drove men wild, but she was smart as a whip. Psychology was her major, and she worked part time in the office of some shrink here in Montreal. She'd be a good one, all right. "Maybe I should look at hiring somebody..." He knew he'd get nowhere with her, but he had to try. She simply ignored him and vaulted back over the bar. One thing he had to give the girl, she was as tough as she was pretty. Harry scanned the bar intently, taking in the usual crowd, but there was one man at the very end of the bar that caught his attention. Fella didn't look right, he looked kind of messed up, like he was sick. His face was real pale, and it kind of looked like he was wearing a mask the way the skin was baggy and pallid in certain spots. He wore a long black trench coat, buttoned all the way up, but the thing that concerned Harry the most was his eyes. They were coal black, like two splotches of black paint on a pure white canvas. And they followed his niece across the room with a predatory sort of look in their depths that Harry didn't like. 'Hey, mister." His gravelly voice assaulted the ears of Harrison O'Dell, though Harry didn't know that was his name, who looked up at him as soon as he heard the sandpaper tone. Harry didn't like the look of O'Dell any less when he looked him in the eyes, it was like looking into something empty. No emotion swam in the depths. "Can I get you another beer?" His answer was a nonchalant shrug as O'Dell pushed his mug forward for a refill, and, with the bar being slow, the old man decided to find out more about this creep. He eased his creaking old frame out from behind the bar and dropped onto a stool beside the man, no easy feat for his old body to do. 'I've never seen you around here before... you new around here?" This time the man looked at him with annoyance, the first hint of expression in those obsidian eyes. "I mean, where are you from?" He didn't really care, he just had a weird vibe off of this character, and wanted to keep his fixation off of Janice. 'The name's Harry, I'm the owner of this place. It ain't much, but I like to know who is in here, just in case I need to be concerned about somebody I don't know." Finally, the serial murderer that he didn't know looked up and opened his mouth, and the barkeep found himself wishing he hadn't. His breath smelled like something had died in his mouth, a foul stench that nearly made the oldster puke. He was halfway tempted to offer the man a mint. 'I'm Dell Harrison. I'm here... well... I'm here on business." The man's voice sounded weak, it had a whiny tone to it like a small child. "Just for a while, then I'll be leaving." Business. The halting way he had said it aroused the suspicions of the old man, but Harry didn't press the issue. He did, however, go back to his office behind the bar and jam the .44 handgun that he kept for protection into his waistband, pulling down the blue and white shirt that he wore to cover it. Normally the gun stayed in its locked cupboard, but he didn't normally have customers as weird and creepy as this guy. He also couldn't help but notice that as soon as Dell had finished talking, his eyes went right back to Jasmine as she delivered some drinks, and those black eyes showed his desire with a sort of malice in them. 'OK, there, Dell Harrison. I got to get back to work," back in his place behind the bar, the old man had to work not to snarl at him. "But I'd like to key you in on a rule around here. That is my niece over there. Her name's Jasmine, and she's one hell of a good kid. I don't like it when drunk bar trash think they can stare at her or grope her, and if I see you doing it, even watching her, you'll be out, understood?" Again, an uninterested shrug, though Dell's eyes did return to the beer in front of him. What was it with people these days? Honestly, Harry couldn't figure out just what had gone wrong with the world that people were becoming so damn strange. But that thought was wiped from his mind along with a lot of others when some regulars dropped in, excited, downright giddy, over the Canadians game they had just watched, and their thirst for cold beer kept his hands busy for quite some time. When he looked up again, Dell Harrison was gone. It took him a few minutes, however, to note that his niece was as well. He leaped over the shiny surface of the bar, fear giving him motion he'd never have been able to manage otherwise. "Have you seen Jasmine?" He asked as he bolted through his bar, searching for a flash of wheat blond hair in the dim lights. Desperation drove him on, and he ducked outside. 'Jasmine?" He knew she didn't smoke, so there was no real reason for her to be out there, yet still, he was worried. 'Sassy? You out here?" He raced, his pounding footfalls panicked and as fast as his old legs could run. She had to be around here somewhere. And as he rounded a corner, she was there. A cry of agony tore from his chest at the sight of the ropes binding her hand and foot, and the blood that now mingled with her wheat blond hair. That blood trickled down her ebony skin, forming a pair of small rivers that mingled and then split apart as they journeyed down her face. 'Jasmine... oh, god, girl. Let me get you loose." He pulled feverishly at the knots holding her, ignoring the pain in his stiff old fingers. "Then we'll get you some help..." Her eyes were open wide with a dual combination of shock and pain, yet she was still conscious enough to whimper and to try her hardest to hold back the tears trickling out of her ocean blue eyes. She was a tough kid, all right, but Harry hated the thought of her being in this state. To scar something so beautiful, he thought feverishly to himself, was like slapping God himself in the face. 'Hold up, baby girl." The knots were resisting him, whoever had done this (and his mind immediately suspected the enigmatic Dell Harrison) was skilled at the art of knot tying. He grunted with pain, arthritic hands bothering him, when Jasmine suddenly let out a strangled cry of warning, her broken jaw unable to articulate the words, but there was no mistaking the despair in her cry. Harry turned, even as he did so seeing that it was too late for him to fight back, too late for him to save her. To save either of them. The blade buried itself to the hilt in his chest and he felt the pain, like a rushing wave of sheer agony throughout his entire upper body. A soft grunt escaped him, and suddenly he was on his back with no idea how he had come to be there. 'I'm sorry about this, man." Dell Harrison pulled the knife free and wiped the blade on the soft fabric of Harry's sweat pants. He didn't look sorry, not one iota, in fact his face was still the blank mask it had been in the bar. "I need to do this... your girl here is lucky, really. She's one of the last puzzle pieces I need. She should be honored." Behind them, Jasmine maid a strangled, horrified sound that said plain and simple that she was anything but honored. 'This has to be." Harry felt a strange heaviness in his arms and legs, indeed he couldn't even open his mouth to reply. Had he been able to, he would have been begging, pleading for their lives... but he just couldn't make the words come. All he could do was watch, helpless, as the bastard grabbed Sassy by the hair and began to drag her towards a black van. All he could do was lay there, feeling his life force draining out and watching the closest thing to a child he had ever had being taken to a certain death. All he could do was nothing. 'Jas... mine..." It could be a long time, precious minutes, before anybody from inside noted the bloody specter now lying helpless on the ground, and it would be even longer before help arrived. Harry had no choice, looking at the ragged, gaping wound the combat knife's serrated edge had made, but to admit that he was dead. Not long left. Jasmine had disappeared from view, behind the big double doors of the van, and now Harrison was climbing into the driver's seat, his face still blank and his eyes still emotionless. The old man reached out one calloused, liver spotted hand, one last vain attempt to help, and then all went black as the van started and pulled out of view... Harry breathed his last, and the exhalation was on single word. 'Please..." He died alone, drowning in his own blood as the only family he had sped away into the Montreal night. 2 'Warning" Joaquin Pierre knew nothing of the murder of the bartender, nor of the kidnapping of the bartender's pretty niece, but if he had, his imagination would have kicked into overdrive. He already suspected that Harrison O'Dell, alias Dell Harrison, was behind every shadow, and every unexplained noise, and his paranoia was only growing by the moment. Anything more, and he may have gone mad from sheer worry, but fortunately for him, he knew nothing. No, Joaquin was alone in his small house, clutching his sword and the .38 revolver that had once been O'Dell's, ready for an attack that he felt was inevitable. He was bare chested, the jagged scar left over from his last encounter with the monster a dim red line in the light of his home, and his hands were white knuckled on the sword. It had, he had previously thought, killed his foe the first time. Oh, if only he had been right. Somehow, as if the devil himself were driving him on, Harrison had arisen from the dead and come back to haunt him. Come back to try and do what he failed to do the first time, and that was to kill Joaquin. Of course, he could not have been dead, only wounded, when he had been knifed. But back he was, and dangerous he was as well, no matter the circumstances behind his return. It had been four days since Pierre's conversation with Montreal Homicide Detective Malveau, and though he had provided the cop with everything he knew about O'Dell, he privately thought to himself that there was no way they would catch him. No way. O'Dell was far too evil, far too sneaky, for that. He would only be caught if he made a mistake. And if Joaquin knew his enemy, a mistake was not likely to be made. No, a confrontation would be forced, and either he or his nemesis would die. That was the only way this chapter would end. And so, while O'Dell was murdering an innocent bartender and stealing his beautiful niece only a few blocks away, the Frenchman sat waiting. Waiting for some sort of sign that the final battle was nigh, that this would soon, one way or another, end. He wore only a pair of his black wrestling trunks, an old pair that he used early in his career in Global. The white fleur de lis was fading and cracking at the edges, but they were comfortable and more importantly, they allowed him to move quickly should the need arise. He ran a hand through his coal black hair, wondering if he had the ability to once more drive his sword into the black heart of Harrison O'Dell. He did... didn't he? On the other side of the room, the phone rang. Once, twice, three times the shrill ring echoed in the darkened room, and after the third time, he could hear his own voice, the recording made in a happier time when his voice didn't sound strained and terrified. "Bonjour, you 'ave reached Joaquin Pierre. Leave me a message, s'il vous plait, and I shall answer as soon as possible. Thank you." It was, as it had been at least a dozen times that day, Sarah, calling from the rented hotel room Joaquin had arranged for her and Sean to hide out in until this was done. He knew she was worried, that she didn't understand just how she had been dragged into this situation. And Joaquin also knew that Sarah wanted vengeance for her late husband Antoine, who had also died at the hands of Harrison O'Dell. But to have her, or Sean, or Joshua Kosidlo, his son Andre, or any of his friends, family, or casual acquaintances near him was to put them in danger. And he would be damned if he allowed anybody else he cared about to die simply because they were with him. 'Joaquin, answer the god damn phone!" Her voice was angry, though he couldn't help but note that the rage served only to enhance the sensuality that seemed to pour off of her soft, velvety tones. "We haven't heard from you in three days... please, at least let us know you are all right, OK? We're worried, and... well... we'll talk once this is over." He hoped so. They had a lot to talk about, a great deal of something strange between them that neither expected nor were looking for when Joaquin walked into her office for his first therapy session. But O'Dell drew them together, chained their lives for the moment into one. Joaquin didn't know how he felt about her, but he wanted them both to survive this to find out more. Again, the shrill tones of the phone cut through the soft sounds of his own breathing, and again he tensed himself, clutching his weapons as he waited for the sounds of the hissing, snake like voice he had come to know so well. The voice that haunted his dreams every night. The voice of a human monster. 'Hello, French." It didn't disappoint. Joaquin expected the sound to chill his blood, and so it did, every inch of his six foot five frame seemed to chill. His knuckles cracked ominously, they were so clenched, and his eyes widened in shock as the voice continued. "It seems like we're both wanting to finish this off... well, at least I am, and I'm sure you are just as ready as I. I think that the time has come." A pause. 'You once told me that, if you were free, you would end me with your bare hands. Of course, that was before I emotionally castrated you by taking out your bitch and your brats. I don't think you've got the balls now, personally. But why don't we see. There's an abandoned warehouse a mile or so from your home. Fifteen minutes." The only sound on the line was the heavy, excited breathing. 'Don't be late." 3 'Insanity" Jasmine felt the pain her physical body felt, and knew she was in bad shape. But it was more sorrow and pain for her uncle that overwhelmed her. She knew that by now he was gone, there was no way he could possibly have survived that massive blood loss, and his passing left a huge void in her own life, which looked like it, too, would soon be over. She lay on the hard flooring, her once beautiful face crushed against the unforgiving cement which rubbed raw her blood and tear stained skin. She had no idea about where she was. Her world had compressed into itself, had collapsed into this small area of bare cement she could see, the feeling of spittle and blood beneath her broken jaw, and the harsh rubbing of the ropes that bound her. That was all she knew, the world outside of the room could have ceased to exist for all she knew. Where the man who had taken her was, she could not say for certain. She sort of felt like he wasn't in the room at the moment, at least she couldn't sense his presence or hear the hissing sound of every breath. Nor could she smell the foulness of his breath as he hovered over her in the darkness, watching her through his snake eyes. But he couldn't be far away, and escape was an impossibility she dared not hope for. 'Help me..." she tried to cry out, hoping somebody somewhere would hear her, but the sound came out distorted, like an animal's sound. "ellp meeee." She had no way of knowing that Joaquin Pierre was on the move, rushing towards the building with murder in his heart and a sword in his hand. She couldn't possibly know that the Frenchman had every intention of slaying her tormentor. Indeed, he was only a hundred yards or so away from the abandoned building, watching as the chain link fence came up faster and faster in his headlights. He vaguely saw the "CAUTION" sign in red and white on the gate, and the words on another sign "TRESPASSERS WILL BE PROSECUTED" before the front bumper of his car mowed down the gate, and the powerful engine ate up the distance between them. Though Jasmine didn't know it, Harrison did. He watched the car approaching, and knew that it was time to make his move, time to put the final sequence of this war into motion. His footsteps pounded on the metal steps as he rushed down the stairs, and in only moments, Jasmine felt his presence. In only another moment, he stood over her, all hideous, pallid face and yellow teeth, looking every bit like one of the monsters she had always feared beneath her bed as a child. 'Time to go, pretty eyes." She felt his hands on the ropes that bound her, and she felt the cold air of the poorly heated room on her bare skin as he tore at her shirt. It ripped neatly in half, and she saw him pin something to it and leave it sitting on the blood stained floor where she had been. She caught only a glimpse of it, a note of some kind... Indeed, when Joaquin Pierre, sword in hand, rushed down the stairs like a knight ready for battle, he found only a bloody swatch of shirt, with a short note pinned to it. Short and sweet, yet typically O'Dellian in that it was sinister and terribly foreboding. French, You know the old phrase, home is where the heart is? I always liked that one, and in this case, its particularly true. Do you remember Paris? This isn't your son's heart, but unfortunately you are a bit short on kids these days. SO I had to improvise. Take Care. PS. Sarah says hello. He had to read the note twice, and then a third time, to get past the awful pounding in his head and the terrible sense that he was about to pass out. Somewhere, in his home, O'Dell had left a calling card, a heart. But worse yet, he had Sarah... Oh, god, if it was her heart... He heard his sword clatter to the floor, the .38 in his other hand slipping as well to clatter down beside it. All he could think of was Sarah, her beautiful face terrified and bloodied, the victim of O'Dell's evil schemes. Her chest cleaved open, the heart missing from the gaping wound. Harrison would think that terrible fun, and a great way to torture Joaquin in the bargain. The mention of his son brought the darkness rushing forward, nearly overcoming him, nearly knocking him over with the force of the bad memories. Marcel, his youngest son, had been cleaved just as the note said, indeed, Joaquin had found his son's heart in the tunnels beneath Paris. New memories flashed, of his child's lifeless face, pale and bloodless, the eyes so like Joaquin's own staring vacantly back at him. For a long moment, Joaquin knew nothing. During that stretch, which lasted only a minute or so but felt as though it would never end, his past rushed by him with an uncanny speed, everything that he had loved and lost hitting him with the force of a bullet in the head. He saw them, all of them, and knew that he could have stopped it had he only been a few minutes faster. He knew that he could have saved the lives of those he loved, had he only been less selfish. He admitted, for the first time, that it was his fault. And then he rose to his feet, once more grasping the gun and sword, a look of steely determination in the blue depths of his eyes. The past was just that, in the past. Those he had lost were just that, lost. There was only the here and the now, and the pursuit of a man who was without a shadow of a doubt insane. A man who, if he wasn't stopped, would only continue to kill until somebody did stop him. Joaquin staggered slightly going down the stairs, his equilibrium still threatening to fail him, yet iron determination drove him forward. He had to do it. He had to stop him. It was his job. He reached his car and threw open the driver's door. The seat caught him as balance failed. The world spun, yet he shook his head to clear it. There could be no room for weakness. Later, perhaps, but for now, he had a job to do. There was blood on the seat. The body of Jasmine Mallory sat on the passenger seat, her ravaged, beautiful face staring right at him through eye sockets that were empty of their former ocean blue eyes. Her chest cavity was split open, as promised, and the heart was missing. Joaquin had no doubt that he would, eventually, find it when he got home, sitting on his kitchen table, or perhaps under his pillow. But Harrison had left her here to show Joaquin that he had the edge. He had the advantage, no matter what the Frenchman did. Point, Harrison. And all he could do was hope to come back and win it, with the odds stacked against him. With Jasmine's dead body sitting limp and floppy in the seat beside him, Joaquin Pierre started the engine and put the car in drive. The roar of that engine carried the car down the gravel driveway, spraying gravel in its wake as he rushed towards the downtown area. O'Dell was going after Sarah next, and he would be damned if he was going to let another innocent die. 4 'The End is Nigh" Sarah hated the feeling of being cooped up here, waiting to hear whether or not there was still danger. She wanted to be out there, wanted to be living her life, killer or no killer. From what little Joaquin and the police had told her, the man that hunted Joaquin would not hesitate to take her out if it meant getting to him, and, in their words, the risk was not worth it. But for a woman who stood only five foot four inches, and didn't even weigh a hundred and fifty pounds, Dr. Sarah La Vega was a scrapper. She wasn't one to sit idly by and wait. Back in the days of the West she could have been a real life gunfighter, or a bounty hunter, or some form of legendary female warrior in ancient times. She was tough, and she would scrap until she had nothing left. She brushed her shoulder length black hair out of her face for what felt like the hundredth time, keeping away from the window as she had been instructed. She didn't watch television, nor did she pick up the book she had brought. She could not sit still, could not move. There was too much energy, too much emotion, too much god damn excitement, for her to sit. Sarah's lithe legs, powerful from running and working out, carried her to her feet, where she once more paced the length of the room. 'This is making me crazy." She paused, wondering if she really was going crazy or not, but self assessment had never been her strong point. 'Maybe I am losing it." She knew that, one way or another, she had to get out of here. This endless waiting was too much for her to take. Pacing back and forth, muttering under her breath, was going to get her nowhere. Nor was endlessly calling Joaquin on the phone and talking to his answering machine, hoping for news. The end of this thing had to be close, and she would be stuck here until it happened. Or so she thought. The knock on the door caught her completely by surprise, as though she had, in her isolation, forgotten that anything existed outside of these four walls. Shocked, she simply stared at the door, wondering if she had imagined it. But no, there it was again. 'Who is it?" Her voice held just the slightest quiver, but she thought she sounded strong, authoritative, and downright tough. "Who's there?" 'Police, ma'am. We need to move you out of this location, it's been compromised." Without a second thought, Sarah threw open the door. In retrospect, it was a stupid thing to do, to blindly trust. But hindsight is twenty twenty, and Sarah didn't have the luxury of hindsight. The first thing in her vision was a sweaty, pallid, ugly face, and a mouthful of yellowed teeth, the man she had met only once. The murderer of her husband, Antoine. Harrison O'Dell smiled at her with those disgusting teeth, and she winced, backing slowly away from him. 'Hello, Dr." He leered at her, still smiling that disgusting smile. "Is it too late to make another appointment?" Sarah had run out of room, her back coming up against the taupe wall on the far side of the room. And still, he advanced, a step at a time, slowly and deliberately. His hands were behind his back, and he strolled as though he were out on a nice evening walk. 'What do you want?" He didn't answer, but his hand did reach out to caress her silken cheek with its stubby tip, and he didn't even notice that she flinched from his touch. His other hand stayed behind his back, and the caressing finger moved slowly down her chin, and to the neckline of her shirt. 'Harrison..." she maintained her best therapist's voice despite her terror. "I think you should sit down, so we can talk." When her eyes followed the hand that touched her, the other quickly came out from behind him, the rag in it pressed against her nose faster than Sarah could hope to react. It smelled sickly sweet, almost nauseating, but Sarah didn't smell it for long. Her head swam, her eyes seemed to fill in with blackness, and in only moments, she was unconscious in the arms of Harrison O'Dell. He smiled and caressed her face again, watching his finger tracing the supple flesh with a smile. 'Don't worry, Dr. We will talk. Sooner than you think." In only a few short moments, she was hidden on the cart, beneath the little curtain that hid the contents from view, and they were in the elevator. It was glass, and Harrison smiled as he watched the floors melt by them, one by one. He could plainly see the other elevator, and it was stationary. Good. That meant the frog hadn't gotten here yet. He was quite sure, however, that he would be seeing Joaquin Pierre again soon, really soon. And the end would be nigh when he did.
