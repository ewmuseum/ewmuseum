Have Yourself A Merry...

28 December 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/28/08 The first surprise had been when he’d allowed her to drive. Actually, let’s rephrase that in the interest of both accuracy and personal safety…it had been force of habit which dictated that, every time the Marshalls travelled to visit Lance’s family, Lance would drive whatever they managed to get out of the rental car agency. New York State was his stomping grounds, after all, and he knew his way around better (and, accordingly, it was Alanna who tended to do the driving every time they visited the Big Island). This time around, however, as the lady at the rental car counter has slid the keys of their rented SUV into Alanna’s hand, Lance had said nothing. He merely began loading up the trunk with the luggage and presents they had brought with them while Alanna helped Zach into the back seat. Alanna had felt a twinge of worry at that. She said nothing, though, convinced she was overreacting, making a big deal out of nothing. With Zach securely buckled in and sleeping comfortably in the back seat, Alanna had pulled out of the rental car lot and the trio had made their way smoothly (if slowly) out of the airport and into the city. Alanna felt a mild twinge of envy as she quickly glanced at her son’s sleeping form in the rear view mirror. He looked happy, peaceful, contented. He could, she marveled, sleep anywhere, in circumstances Alanna wouldn’t have imagined possible. It was a bit of a gift, that, one that she would have loved to have taken advantage of on several occasions. The trip over had been relatively painless. Even with the recent run of lousy weather, the flights had been running more or less on time. Zach had barely noticed, alternately absorbed by either his Nintendo DS or his iPod. Zach, chuckled Alanna to herself, had definitely inherited his father’s "gadget gene". When Lance had asked him if he wanted an iPod, Zach had responded eagerly in the affirmative "but not a Nano please, Daddy, ‘cause those are for babies." So, a Classic it had been with Lance eagerly helping Zach fill it up with stuff (and Alanna offering the occasional suggestion as to what would not be appropriate on a five year old’s iPod). Lance had also been quieter than expected, alternating between nursing a cup of coffee and the latest James Lee Burke paperback. Even now, as Alanna made her way through the city traffic, he seemed content to just sit and stare out the window at the gently falling snow. It wasn’t that he was being surly or cold or uncommunicative. Heck, he wasn’t even all that unresponsive. They’d certainly talked through the flight (about travel arrangements, updates on both their families, what they’d gotten Zach for Christmas) and there had been pockets of conversation during the drive (Lance had seemed especially receptive to the idea of seeing Benjamin Button over the holiday). But they had all been conversations that Alanna had initiated. Had it not been for those brief moments, the trip would have passed in near silence. And that had been the second surprise. Alanna turned to glance at her husband, who was once again staring out the window. "Baby?" Lance turned to his wife, grunting softly. "Hmm? "Are you okay?" The pause was barely perceptible. "Yeah, hon, I’m fine." The conviction in his voice was distinctly underwhelming but Alanna decided it would be best not to push too hard. "You’d tell me if anything was wrong, right?" "Of course." The rest of the trip passed in silence. "Uncle Stevie!" Zach Marshall launched himself at his uncle, enveloping him in a flying hug. Steven Marshall responded with a mock "oof" and a slight stumble, meeting his nephew’s hug with a hug of his own. "Christ, Runt, Zach’s sprouting like a weed! What’re you feeding him?" Alanna could see the red slowly creeping into her husband’s face at the mention of the hated nickname. Lance Marshall could hardly be described as a small man by any definition of the word. But, as both the shortest and youngest of the Marshall brothers, he’d been dubbed "Runt" at a very young age…and it had stuck, much to Lance’s unspoken annoyance. Alanna spoke up quickly, before things escalated. "Hi, Steve." Steven Marshall smiled at his sister-in-law, placing Zach gingerly down on the floor. Zach pelted at full speed towards the clutch of younger Marshalls sitting over in the corner, greeting each with a smile, a hug and a hearty "Hi!" "Hiya, Lani. Looking beautiful as ever." "Stop flirting with my wife, jackass," Lance growled. At any other time, Alanna might be worried. But here, among family, she knew this was one of the only places in the world where jackass could be used as a term of endearment. A family as testosterone laden as the Marshalls could be, she knew, wasn’t exactly prone to soppy displays of affection. Steven Marshall strode over to just in front of his baby brother, making full use of his six inch height advantage. "Gonna make me, runt?" he smiled. "If I have to," Lance smiled back. For a moment, nothing was said. Then Steven broke into a deep, booming laugh and the two brothers hugged in that uniquely male way. Alanna sighed. Men…sometimes… "You haven’t changed a bit, Lance!" Steven laughed as the men separated. "How’re you doing?" "Good, all things considered," Lance answered. "How’s things by you?" "Well, everybody’s taken a hit with the economy being in the shitter…" "Language, Steven!" boomed a voice from the next room. "Sorry, Dad!" answered Steven. Turning back to Lance, he added "But, yeah, we’re doing okay. You gonna be staying long?" "Lani and Zach are gonna head over to her parents for a few days. But I’ll stay here, yeah. Work’s in the area." "Then come on. We’ve got a lot to catch up on." Alanna headed into the kitchen, comforted by the steady chatter of voices coming from inside. Lance’s mother, Anna, stood in command, issuing instructions to the various wives and family members who stood around working on their various courses. Alanna marveled at her mother-in-law’s ability to remain calm, composed, and elegant even with a thin layer of flour coating her clothes. Alanna walked over to Anna, greeting her with a warm smile and hug. "Hi, Mom." Anna returned the hug. "Lani, darling, hello! Please forgive the mess, I’ve been so busy lately…" "Don’t worry about it, Mom. Everything looks great…and it smells divine." "Thank you, darling," smiled Anna, "I do my best. How’re the boys doing?" "Zach’s doing great. Life and soul out there, keeping everybody entertained. And Lance…" Alanna trailed off. Anna stopped, making her daughter-in-law the sole focus of attention. "What’s wrong?" Alanna hesitated to say anything, sure she was overreacting. But she had opened the door… "Everything’s great between us, honest." "But…?" "I’m convinced that Lance has got something on his mind. Something that’s bothering him. I’m not sure what it is and he’s not exactly being forthcoming." "Could it be work related?" Anna asked. "I do know he has a big match coming up." Alanna couldn’t help but chuckle at the thought of this elegant figure before her even being aware of the world of professional wrestling. "There is that, yeah…but I don’t think it’s just that, you know?" Anna nodded. "Believe me, darling, I know. William’s been like that since we first met." "So how did you two deal with it?" Anna smiled. "I learned, eventually, that he would tell me when he was good and ready to tell me. And I know that he would never keep anything important from me. So, I wouldn’t worry, darling. If Lance is anything like his father…" Both women smiled at this. "And they’re a lot more alike than Lance would like to admit…" Alanna added. "…then he’ll tell you soon enough" Anna finished. "Until then, the most you can do is let him know you’re there for him. Now, if you could help Rachel with the mashed potatoes, darling?" Anna pointed at a petite, obviously pregnant brunette. "I told Charles that she should be resting but she absolutely insisted and Charles swears it’s useless to try and stand in her way." "And the guys swear they don’t take after their father…" Alanna smirked. Anna answered with a laugh and a kiss on the cheek. "Thank you for coming, darling. It’s so rare that we get everyone together all at once." "I’m glad to be here, Mom." With an immediate family in the double digits, Anna Marshall had long ago realized that trying to get everything needed for Christmas dinner organized neatly around a dinner table was a battle she was destined to lose. So, for convenience and sanity’s sake, she had some years back switched to a considerably more free form style of dinner. Seating was catch as catch can and dinner was arranged buffet style. It was front of the turkey platter, plate in hand, that Lance Marshall now found himself. He reached out with his fork to grab a slice only to be nearly speared by a fork which quickly darted in and out of vision, grabbing the slice that Lance had intended for himself. "Gotta be quicker than that, Unk…" Michael Marshall was part of the third generation of Marshall’s, being James’s oldest son. A few years out of college, he was working in Manhattan as a personal trainer (and, Michael was happy to add, doing quite well, thank you). Whereas Lance’s brothers tended to venture into the realm of "scary huge", Michael resided comfortably in the realm of "seriously buff". That, combined with his close cut blonde hair and infectious grin, made him look more like someone who should be headlining an action flick at your local multiplex instead of someone whipping yuppies into shape. The fact that Lance had a nephew in his mid-20’s…Lance had gotten used to that. The fact that said nephew was married and Lance was now a grand uncle…that he was still processing. Lance put his plate down, grabbing his nephew around the shoulders with one hand and ruffling his hair with other. "Hey, hey, not the hair," Michael mock-whined in protest. Lance smiled. "How’re ya doing, Mikey?" "I’m good, Unk. Lisa’s great and Maddie…" Lance’s smile grew even wider as he saw his nephew break into a look of blissful reverie, a look he recognized from his own face when Zach was born. Madeline was only a few months old and already the joy of Mikey’s life. "I love her more than anything, Unk, y’know?" "Yeah," said Lance. "I do." "I just hope I do as good a job with Maddie as you and Lani have done with Zach. He’s a great kid." "Thanks," smiled Lance. "We’re really proud of him." "Now, you better grab some of that turkey, Unk. Cause you know we can put it away…" Lance quickly grabbed at his plate in response. The chatter of conversation quickly died away as the sound of spoon hitting glass rang out from the front of the table. William Marshall stood at the front of the table, his iron straight posture and closely cropped grey hair the only immediate signs of his many years spent in the service. "Thank you. I’d like to thank everyone for coming tonight. I know how hard it can be, with everyone being scattered across the country and leading such busy lives. So I treasure moments like these more than you could possibly imagine." A chorus of "Hear, Hear!" rang out from the table. "Now," added William, "let’s eat. Don’t want the food to get cold, do we?" Alanna smiled as she surveyed the scene around her. As filled with drama and craziness as her and Lance’s lives could be…a night like tonight…a night filled with warmth and laughter and love and family…a night like tonight made everything worth it. We now return you to your regularly scheduled lives. And a happy holidays to all of you at home.
