Westmoreland

24 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/24/07 Introduction Within every man is a goal... A purpose...a pre-ordained reason for placement upon this earth... For many, this goal manifests itself through a series of tasks, trials, and tribulations, coupled by years of desperate searching. The most trying part of the journey of such individuals is the search, for when the goal is found, the quest for self greatly aids one's need for accomplishments. For many others, the goal is clear...obvious. The goal hits as soon as inception from the womb. As light floods the pupils for the very first time, such goals are set forth, and the journey is not to discover, but to accomplish. I am of the latter. You see, for me, it is all about rising to the top. No matter what the circumstances, I maintain that my professional and personal life must always be in check. Every win brings me closer to my ultimate goal, to sit atop the mountain, playing the active protagonist in what I consider to be, in essence, my own story. Each obstacle impedes my progress. Last time, it was John Covel. This time -- The Alliance. Gideon Fontaine and Neil Fracas are of a similar brand of wrestling. Trained in Japan, these gentlemen are schooled in the techniques necessary to bring down the biggest of opponents, recognizing skill, and efficiently winning matches quickly and cleanly. For this, I commend them. In the days of NEW, I noticed one thing about the Alliance. They have, as it seems, found a formula so advanced for their own success that it is a matter of simple mathematics. The equation has always the same variables, so the men use their know-how in order to come out victorious throughout each bout. If the variable never changes, and the factors stay constant, then victory is assured. But every once in a while, a wild card is thrown in. The variable changes. Fracas and Fontaine, now that I have come along, the equation changes. The brain becomes accustomed to one way of reasoning and begins to slack. When a spark is thrown in the wheel, the entire system is shocked. The solution is much different because, let's face it, gentlemen...your ideas and your style are old hat. For me, it's a new game. Each new day brings a new challenge. With each challenge overcome, new opportunities are presented. Each new day brings me closer to the ultimate goal -- the reason I was put on this planet... ...to matter. I Darkness slowly spreads over the skies, retiring the sun for the day and beginning the shift of the moon. An old day burns out like a candle from its wick, announcing twilight's spell of resurgence before the day is brought forth anew. Cars are few and far between as Tanaka accelerates the Turcell's small engine to 40...50...60... Gone is the green Astro Van, the last vehicle seen in the White Plains case of 'The White Plains Two.' Of course, Tanaka and Shelley knew the real truth behind the incident. In the eyes of the public, however, such facts were fiction, waiting only to be discovered by an onlooker either incredibly insightful or devilishly lucky. Nevertheless, much like the two young men, the truth of the event is buried deep in the cold ground, never to be unearthed. Tanaka had seen to that. 'I'm hungry,' Shelley mumbles from the back seat, bound by a thin nylon rope around his hands and legs. 'When do we stop for food?' 'Since your little freakout,' I sigh, 'The ability for you to make such decisions has been stripped away. You will eat when I eat. Right now, I feel we have much more important objectives than to fill our bellies. If you say one more word on the subject, I'll be sure that the only thing you can taste is the bitter flavor of your own blood.' With that, I drive. The small, sleepy town of Westmoreland, New York is home at about 14,592 people, adding about a handful of citizens each year. Their high school's mascot is the Fighting Jaybird, their socioeconomic standing is upper middle, and the town hero is one Michael Ross Mitchell - fire chief, avid bowler, recipient of the Good Neighbor Award (three years running), and model citizen. But even in the cleanest, most purest of rooms, there are always skeletons in the closet. And I feel a lot like digging. II 'I said, a double cheeseburger...large fries...and a coke!' There is once more confused static. A voice is heard, as if from light years away, undoubtedly belonging to an adolescent that lives only to bum beers from his father's fridge and lose his virginity at the prom. My frustrations mounts. I lean my head out the window, my mouth almost touching the speaker, and speak again. 'Excuse me? Hello?' 'Hi, welcome to Bunky Burger, may I take your order?' the voice rings. Immediately, rage wells up within my throat. I suppress a scream. Darkness has now completely overtaken the sky, and moths and other assorted insects of the night flutter around the glow of the Bunky Burger sign - a constipated looking beaver with a red shirt and a pennant that reads 'BB'. Underneath, in the clear window, I see two figures at a table. I recognize them immediately, the glow of the table lamp exposing every facet of their being. Mitchell and Kat. I veer the wheel all the way to the right, avoiding the cars in front and behind me in line at the drive thru window, then slam on the gas. The car barrels over the curb, across a small patch of shrubbery, and to the other side of the lot. Several cars in the area honk in protest. I slam on the gas, pulling the car to the back of the restaurant. I pull the car into the shadows. My eyes focus on both the exit of the restaurant, as well as the blue Ford Taurus that belongs to Mitchell, according to the New York Department of Motor Vehicles. Several minutes pass. My mind begins to wander. My muscles become tense from inaction. My being thirsts again for blood, still feeling the fresh sting of Covel's flesh on my body. I imagine again and again his cries of pain, the taste of victory as I take the first step to ensure that immortality is mine. The Alliance will be mere rats, an appetizer fed to me until the main course. War in Washington. Clinton Sage. My day will come. As I begin to-- 'Yoshi,' Shelley whines once more. 'What about the food?' 'Goddamnit!' I snap, taking my eyes off the parking lot and the Taurus for the first time. 'You say one more thing about food and I will--.' As I speak, suddenly, the Taurus pulls out of the parking lot, making a left turn signal near the road. I turn the wheel sharply, peeling out, and approach them. However, I am sure to keep my distance. The car turns, and I turn as well. So begins the chase. III 'Shit,' I mumble, banging the steering wheel in frustration. Nearly twenty minutes later, it is apparent that Mitchell knows he's being followed. He gives the obvious signs: several turns, stopping and going, speeding up. However, the dagger in the heart came when he was able to run through a yellow light. In hot pursuit, I attempted to barrel through the red, only to have to swerve sharply to avoid oncoming traffic. The result was a busted tire occuring when I jumped onto the curb of the soon-to-be deserted cross-streets of downtown Westmoreland. Wanting to waste no time, I jump out of the car. The front tire is demolished. I begin to admonish Shelley for his unwillingness to help, only to realize he is tied up in the back. I open the back door, drag him out by the arm, and cut the ropes. I hold the knife to his throat, peering at him in a threatening manner. 'You change the tire, and do nothing else,' I warn, in a low, deadly voice. 'You try anything, and I'll make sure your last words--' I hold the knife closer to his throat '--are a shrill whistle of your own oxygen whizzing in and out of your neck. Shelley gulps, as if to say, 'I understand.' As Shelley changes the tire, I sit on the curb, admonishing myself once more. Then again, Mitchell was the one who decided not to be cooperative. We could have settled this like men, but he was unwilling. What he doesn't know is that from his personal information, I have seven addresses of his. Including his current. 'Looks like we're doing this on your turf, Mitchell,' I mutter, smiling. I run the edge of my knife against my hand, a prickling sensation of pain running throughout. The skin breaks, and blood begins to ooze. However, as my blood slowly trickles, I smile, giggle, even. The prospect of exposing Mitchell for what he is -- inside and out -- excites me even sexually. The final battle begins. IV With the car rolling smoothly on a donut replacement tire, and Shelley passed out in the back, bound once more, I steadily make my way to 651 Denville Street. The directions are simple -- a left a Sycamore, a right on Holloway, another right on Jefferson, straight until Jefferson becomes Main, then left on Denville. I feel his home creeping closer and closer. The time on the dash now reads 10:51. He is probably ramming his tool into Kat's innocent flesh about now, their bodies moving as one, her screams and moans of pleasure nauseating. To think of another man, using something that belongs to me! My stomach turns, and I taste bile in my throat. I quickly swallow it, feeling an intense burning down my esophagus. My heart races with anticipation, almost a feeling of euphoria. Before the night is over, I know that Kat will once again be in my arms, and Mitchell will...well, his fate is yet to be determined. The car rolls past a 711, a Super-R-Fill Gas Station, and a closed office for auto insurance. The car propels down Main Street, the headlights casting a glow over all, until I see the green sign at last. A sign of destiny, one that will bring me to the fruition of a plan set forth for several months...a plan which, in its infancy, seemed rather extreme, but in the course of events has become right. My heart rate increases. Behind me, Shelley stirs, but I ignore him. I turn the car, driving down the street. 'Shelley,' I call. Shelley stirs again, jerking his head suddenly. 'Y-yes?' 'It's time for you to fulfill your duty. Mitchell is here, in his home, and your place in this saga has arrived. I took you from being an addict to being a part of my very own destiny, Shelley, and I can promise you that after this, you will be allowed to do as you please, free to pursue endeavors of your own free will. But you were selected at the beginning for a purpose, and now, it has arrived.' 'Y-you mean that?' Shelley sobs. 'It'll all be over? Like that?' 'Like that,' I agree. I see the numbers of Mitchell's home in the distance. I pull up to a wayward curb and park the car, quickly shutting off the headlamps. 'Why we parking here?' Shelley asks. 'We don't want them to see us park in front of his house. We need to be stealth, Shelley. I have had a plan in place to retaliate against Mitchell, and tonight...tonight, it will all be over.' I exit the car, letting Shelley out. The two of walk, side by side, partners in destiny. V The long, winding road throughout the secluded neighborhood proves imposing. If not for the fact that we are in the abso-fucking-lute middle of nowhere, I would venture to be scared. We use the light of the moon as our guide, hiding in the shadows of the trees. My grandfather once told me of the great war fought in Japan around the 1940's. Many brave men use their brains, as well as cunning, in order to accomplish a singular goal. Hiding in the cloak of night, many of these men would invade and attack the forces of the Allied Powers. Their actions would forever win them respect among my people, using the darkness as their ally, sneaking and destroying. Mitchell's house was a two story, colonial style home. The yard was perfectly kept, and in the driveway was parked a white Ford Focus -- Kat's car. But Mitchell's car was nowhere to be seen. 'Where's Mitchell?' asks Shelley, a usual expression of awe on his face. From the look in his eyes, I can tell his mounting excitement for freedom and anxiety of knowing his role in this plan. 'Ain't he home?' 'Guess he's afraid,' I scoff. 'Let's wait for him inside, shall we?' We duck and hide in the trees, which line the yard like great sentinels. However, unlike sentinels, the trees protect the owner's enemies, and do not impede them. We slither in the shadows, serpents of the night. Leave rustle in the slight wind. We slide to the side of the house, then to the expansive backyard, where a screen door looms. 'Why we goin' through the back?' Shelley asks. I sigh in frustration, 'If we go through the front, Mitchell will see us. This way, we can sneak attack him inside.' Shelley nods. I make my way to the screen door. I lift the handle -- unlocked. I try my luck with the door beyond the screen -- locked. However, the door has a window contained in the middle. I begin to investigate an object to use to break the window. A large stone in the garden strikes my eye. Within seconds, the stone is inside the house, and the window is in pieces. This done, we enter. VII 'The bedroom, huh?' I look at Shelley in the dim light of my pocket flashlight. We are in Mitchell's bedroom, hunched over on the left side, on the other side of the bed. I can see Shelley sweating from the heat of the flashlight, coupled with his nervousness. I, too, feel a rush, but of a different kind - exhilaration. Tonight, Michael Mitchell's life will be over, and everything will come to a close. Kat will be mine, and this entire incident can be banished from existence. We wait in the darkness for what seems to be an eternity. The moonlight illuminates the room. In the darkness, I can see the traditional decorations of a new age, has-nothing-better-to-do collector. Pretentious art, gaunt paintings, and extravagant furnishings are seen throughout the room. The entire room has a dreadful, almost lingering feeling, as if haunted. I shudder, moving further from the bed, knowing damn well how many times Mitchell and Kat 'expressed their love' for one another. Two lights suddenly fill the room. Mitchell and Kat are back. 'Shelley,' I say suddenly. 'The time has come.' 'Now?' 'Now. I need you to be perched over there, in the darkness, in order to serve as a second attacker. When Mitchell comes in, you tackle him, while I attack. Pin him down the best you can. Surprise enough should be our best weapon. Got it?' 'Got it,' Shelley replies weakly. Shelley walks to the corner of the room, his skin and dark clothing blending into the shadows perfectly. I once again reach for my knife. I run my finger along the blade. This is the knife that will ruin the life is Michael Mitchell, right down to the last point. Shelley perches on one side of the door, while I hide on the other. A flank attack is our best strategy in a situation such as this. However, this is not the strategy I have in mind as I jump across the doorway, plunging the knife deep into Shelley's chest cavity.
