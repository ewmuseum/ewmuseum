Part Seven: I Feel The Earth Move

2 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/2/08 Morning dawned on Southern California as it had so many other times in the past; the jet black skies of night slowly melting away, making way for the oranges, reds and purples of early morning before finally giving way to the crystal clear blue that the skies of California were so famous for. At Starbucks location #725, the morning shift began their regular routine of wiping down tables, mopping the floors and stocking the pastry counters in preparation for the new day and the first rush of morning customers. Jason Minetti smiled as he tied the green apron around his waist, carefully smoothing out the fabric that lay against his body. This was, thought Jason, not exactly what he would have considered to be a celebrity hotspot. When he’d accepted the job, he certainly hadn’t expected to encounter anyone famous (mostly, he’d just been grateful for the steady paycheck and dental). Which, he continued thinking to himself, was being a bit silly. Everyone needed their caffeine, celebrities even more so with the crazy schedules he’d heard of them having. So it was that in the few weeks that Jason had been a barista he’d served Lindsay Lohan (total bitch), Charlize Theron (really sweet and hawt as hell, he thought, a blush creeping its way down his body as he remembered how awestruck he’d been in her presence) and Jude Law (nice but quiet). He’d heard other employees talk about meeting people like Will Smith and Zac Efron (Jimena from afternoons claiming he was "so awesome") and US Weekly swore this was Britney’s favorite coffee place. As the doors opened and the morning rush began, Jason started to recognize several of the regular customers making their way in. He was still having a hard time remembering many of their names but their drinks? That was no problem. Iced Venti Latte. Cinnamon Dolce, extra hot. Cappuccino, soy milk, triple shot. As he began setting aside paper and plastic cups, yanking out milk from the fridge and revving up the blender, Jason began to prepare himself for a day like any other. Most of his days, in fact, tended to be very much the same. And, he thought, today looked to be no different. This, thought Zach Marshall to himself, was a happy day. He’d gotten all the way up to the top of the jungle gym for the very first time. Davey had shown him the new Pokemon game on his Nintendo DS, which had looked so cool. And Mrs. Konover had told him the picture he drew for art period looked really nice. Now, he and the rest of the class were actually getting to watch an episode of Johnny and the Sprites (his favorite show in the whole world) while Mrs. Konover talked to one of the other grownups out in the hall. Zach looked over to his right at Jakey, who stifled a giggle at some of the antics on the screen as he looked back. Zach liked Jakey. Jakey’d been the first kid to talk to him on his first day and, while he’d gone on to make friends with the other kids, Zach never forgot that. He’d already had Jakey over to his house and they had play dates all the time. Zach smiled back at Jakey, glad that his friend was enjoying himself. Yeah, thought Zach, today was a happy day. Alanna Marshall slammed on the brakes as she made her way through the morning traffic, barely avoiding colliding with the rear end of the Mercedes that had just cut her off. Resisting the urge to let off a string of curse words (largely because she couldn’t decide which language to do it in), Alanna forced herself to count to ten, taking deep breaths with each number. It wouldn’t help, she thought, to lose her temper. Too much had to get done today, too much needed to be accomplished before the next show. She laughed at this; a short, humorless laugh. All the people out there in the business, claiming to be criminals or mass murderers or multi-billionaires in their off-time…Alanna found herself wondering, not whether they actually did half the things they claimed to do (which was the direction her mind usually found itself wandering in) but where they found the energy to do them. Just existing, she thought, with all the day to day drama that entailed…sometimes she found it hard enough doing that. She stifled a yawn, forcing herself to keep one eye open as she did so. It had been a lousy night’s sleep last night…not because of anything Lance or Zach had done, she thought, but because of everything that had been rattling around in her head. She’d managed stretches of about three hours at a time…not, thankfully, that Lance had noticed. Alanna had quickly realized that her husband redefined the term "heavy sleeper" by being able to pretty much sleep through anything…burglar alarms, ambulances, Zach blaring the TV at six a.m. on a Saturday morning, you name it. On nights like last night, she thought, she was insanely appreciative of that fact. And as Alanna Marshall inched her way forward in the morning traffic, she tilted her head skywards for a brief second; offering up a brief prayer for the strength to get her through another day. Lance Marshall checked his watch as he walked through the front door of the gym, smiling as he did so. Good, there was still five minutes before he was due to meet Mike for their workout. He’d actually planned for twenty minutes ahead but traffic had been especially bear like and, for a moment, he’d actually been afraid he might be late. He could even hear Pa’s voice in the back of his mind, reminding him (as he had all of his kids) that even on time wasn’t sufficient…it was either early or nothing. Across the room, Mike waved at him, a broad smile crossing his face, bright white teeth fairly gleaming in the fluorescent lighting; with his short blond hair expertly styled Mike looked, thought Lance, like he’d just come from a photo shoot. Which, given his schedule, he probably had. Mike bounded up to Lance, placing his hand on Lance’s shoulder. "You ready to tear some shit up, brother?" Lance smiled. He couldn’t help but get caught up in Mike’s energy, his infectious joie de vivre. "You know it, dude. Let’s do this." And life continued on as normal for millions of Los Angelenos. People loved, fought, ate, slept, played, partied and generally lived as they had always done. The sun was shining, the skies were bright and everything looked as though it would be another picture perfect California day. At least, it did at 11:23 a.m. At 11:24 a.m., however…everything changed. 11:14 a.m. Alanna slid her car into the first available slot she could find in the parking lot, marveling that no matter what time of day or night, Starbucks always seemed to be packed. She was entirely happy with herself for making the stop in the first place, you understand. But she’d been finding it hard to keep her eyes open at the wheel and that last yawn had been so loud, she swore they’d been able to hear it in San Fransisco. Jason looked up as the front door bell tinkled, announcing the arrival of another customer. She wasn’t someone Jason recognized…with that body, he thought, it’d be damn hard to forget someone like her. Tall and sexy, she carried herself with a grace that suggested one of those big jungle cats that Jason had seen in a special once on the Discovery Channel…beautiful as hell to look at but very dangerous. It struck Jason that this woman looked like she was very capable of kicking someone’s ass. Was it wrong, he thought, that he was turned on by that? "Hi, welcome to Starbucks. What can I get for you?" Alanna smiled at the teenage Barista standing before her, short black hair sticking out in spikes from his head. "Hi. Um…grande cappuccino with skim milk and a cinnamon scone, please." Jason rang the order up quickly and the sum flashed on the monitor. "Thanks. That’ll be six ninety five." Alanna handed over a ten and silently took her change. A few moments later, the Barista she’d spoken to earlier handed over a paper bag and a paper coffee cup, cardboard sleeve already on it. "Thanks again, ma’am, and have a good day." "Thanks." Ma’am, thought Alanna as she grabbed a seat…she was aware the Barista was only trying to be polite but, g-d, did that make her feel old. She sighed as she took the scone out of the paper bag and took a bite. Instantly, she felt better as she chewed, imagining the sugar rushing through her body. It was a cheap high, she knew, and she’s be crashing again soon…but for right now, none of that mattered. As she swallowed that first bite, she noticed that her table was shaking slightly. She paused, placing the scone on the table. Not a loose leg, she noted, but somehow the shaking was getting worse… The table was now shaking at a furious rate…and it wasn’t just her table. Oh good g-d, thought Alanna…it couldn’t be… "Everybody get down!" 11:22 a.m. With a grunt and an exhalation of breath, Lance hefted the heavily loaded weight bar back into place. It had been a heavy set and he could already tell that he was going to be feeling the effects tomorrow. "Nice going, man!" said Mike. "You really ripped that one…" Mike’s sentence drifted away. Lance noticed that the bench was starting to shake, something that the bench really should not be doing. All around the room…pieces of equipment were vibrating, mirrors were wobbling, people were swaying unsteadily on their feet… Mike looked stunned. "Earthquake?" Lance just grabbed onto the sides of the bench in response, grabbing hold as if his life depended on it. 11:23 a.m. Zach and Jake both laughed and clapped as, on the screen, Johnny had just finished a song with the Sprites. Zach was having so much fun that he only just noticed that the floor was starting to shake. And that was funny cause floors weren’t supposed to do that… Suddenly the shaking got worse. A lot worse. The other kids looked around themselves in panic, their eyes darting to and fro. The door to the classroom banged open and Mrs. Konover came in, looking like she could barely stand. "Children, listen to me! I want you all to please lie down. This will be over soon, just make sure you stay very still." Zach did as Mrs. Konover said, stretching out on the floor. Besides him, Jakey reached out to him, grabbing onto Zach’s hand. "Zach? I’m scared." Zach turned to Jakey, giving him his best smile to try and make him not so scared. "Don’t worry, Jakey. It’ll be okay." 11:24 a.m. "This is Malik Jackson, reporting live for Channel 9 Action News. We are receiving word that an earthquake registering at 6.1 has just rocked the Los Angeles area. For more on this, we go live now to our reporter on the scene, Brenda Marquez. Brenda?" 11:25 a.m. There were some things that just couldn’t be prepared for. It was a fact of life and one that the dark haired man, this particular strand in the web, accepted. It didn’t make things any easier, he thought, as he picked himself up off of the floor, brushing his pants down as he did so. He looked around him quickly, at the piles of magazines that now littered the floor. The newsstand had been an ideal vantage point, a place where he could easily keep tabs on the female target without appearing to be watching for anyone. Now, however, it was becoming a distraction. He slipped his way through the entrance, fishing his mobile phone out of his pocket as he slid through the door. The press of one fast key later and a familiar voice soon came through the phone’s speaker. "Thank you for calling the Trinity Corporation, this is Magdalene speaking. How may I help you?" "Alpha 675 Delta," he replied. "I need to speak with the Director." "Just a moment." A pause that, the dark haired man noted, lasted exactly sixty seconds. "Yes?" That voice, that ice-cold voice…"Director, there is news. Something has just happened. I’m not certain of its significance at the moment but I felt you should hear it from me before you heard it from other sources." The dark haired man glanced quickly at the entrance to the coffee shop. A number of patrons were streaming from the entrance, some in a noticeable panic, but the female target was not among them. Good. "What has happened?" How to explain this, the dark haired man wondered? "There has been…an incident." 11:30 a.m. Lance Marshall bolted his way into the gym parking lot, offering a hurried "see ya later" to Mike as he sprinted towards his car. He needed to get home, needed to be sure that Alanna and Zach were okay. That, he thought, was some scary shit. He couldn’t remember the last time L.A. had been hit by something that big. There had been tremors before, he remembered, small ones that had barely made an impact (he remembered Alanna teasing him for weeks about the fact that he’d actually managed to sleep through one). As he sped his way through the chaotic tangle of traffic, Lance just kept repeating one thought to himself. "They have to be okay, they have to be okay, they have to be okay…" From the hands-free unit on the dashboard came the sound of ringing. Lance reached over, pressing the button to begin the call. For just that split second, his attention was on something other than the road. This is probably why he failed to notice the SUV bearing down on the passenger side of his car at forty miles per hour. 11:28 a.m. Alanna sprinted across the Starbucks parking lot, reaching her car in just a few quick strides. After making sure no one in the Starbucks had been seriously hurt, she had placed a frantic call to Zach’s preschool. It had taken a few tries but she had finally reached Mrs. Konover, who had assured her that the children were all fine (if a little shaken up). She had breathed a heavy sigh of relief at that. Ending the call as quickly as she could, she then dialed the number of Lance’s mobile, eager to make sure that he was all right as well. It had been on the second ring that Lance had answered. All she had heard was "Hello?" And the noises, those horrible noises that she would remember for the rest of her life. The squeal of tires, the impact of metal upon metal. And then…nothing. "Lance?!" "LANCE?!" 11:35 a.m. It is unknown what the other driver was thinking, primarily because no one actually got out of the other car. Perhaps they were upset over the damage done to their car. Perhaps they were afraid of the imminent rise in their insurance premiums due to the accident. Or perhaps they were afraid of what the much larger, now currently unconscious man would do to them when he woke up. Whatever the reason, the other car quickly reversed, veered sharply over to the right and drove past the SUV carrying the now unconscious Lance Marshall… …and ran, as fast as forty thousand dollars worth of German engineering would take them. 11:40 a.m. "911, what’s your emergency?" "Yes, I’d like to report an accident…"
