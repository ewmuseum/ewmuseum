Soundtrack of your life: Track 8

Date: Today "Surrender" by Cheap Trick (Heaven Tonight, 1978) It was not sudden, but persistent. Although the dark had long since given way to the morning’s light my eyelids and will to stay asleep did not waver so easily. But like all wills of man, the relentless and ever present elements chipped away until the Sphinx eroded and I woke up. I let out a lion’s roar and came to in time to see that I had woken to enjoy the final moments of the morning: 11:46 am on the clock. I grabbed the remote and commanded the television to come to life with electrons dancing across glass. As I pressed ‘up’ they switched rhythms and styles, but nothing pleasuring to look at, or at least not so much that it might outdo my lack of attention. Are all commercials taking on the "annoy the viewer to pay attention, even if it associates a hatred with our product" technique? Although they have figured out my secret attention grabber: whistling. Bastards. But the commercials eventually end and I get some cartoons. What bizarre, poorly animated, poorly told stories were they trying to pass off on the children of today? I mean back in my day we had the greats: X-Men animated, Spider-Man, The Adventures of Batman, Pokemon (before it was watered down with the retelling of stories with ‘new creatures’). I pitied them, and felt old myself. Did I really just say ‘back in my day…’? And then the senses kicked in, particularly taste. Each morning my mouth became death - the destroyer of worlds. Each morning, a new quest to vanquish the taste bud assault of flavours unpleasant. I rose to my feet and like the noble sire, set out to do battle. My sword was a toothbrush and my shield the paste. Victory was delicious, and peppermint-y. The silly games and mindset of waking cleared and the memories of a long night returned. Oh how stupid I saw it now that I would spend the night fretting the possibility of defeat, that shitty little drawing and everything little behaviour that saw me stay within myself, beneath myself. It was just so much easier to fall back upon the old habits, even if it meant another in a string of failures, but the only easy day was yesterday. Yesterday is dead. Today I am a more confident man. A confident man who was eating Cap’n Crunch like it was the breakfast of champions. It wasn’t long before the intense crunch of dry cereal had derailed the motivational train of thought, and I instead sought to find patterns in the wallpaper other than the obvious floral one. Looked at the crucifix and then back to the wallpaper, to the doorframe and my mother standing in it. "Wow, he does make it up before the crack of noon. I was thinking I might have had to head up there with a mirror under your nose." "Heh. . . yeah, good morning." I lifted the spoon back to my mouth for another load of condensed sugar. "Don’t you have that plane to catch today?" she asked. And while I chewed my food I was a still a little taken aback that she would assist in any nature toward the success of the path I walked down. I figured that she might try and see me fail. As I swallowed back the yellow mush I thought that my mother had finally either warmed to the idea of me being a wrestler, or grown indifferent. It was this thinking that led me to say, "Hey mom, I kind of wanted to hear your opinion on something." Her head turned like a dog hearing an ultra high-pitched whistle. I guess we were both surprising each other this morning, now noon. "Sure Mitchell, what is it? You know I am still your mother. Even when you are married and with your own children, I will be here for you for these things." "Well, you see, I have a bit of a situation with head office and," my voice trailed as her blue eyes grew wide. "And I am almost done with the contract they had me sign to start." "And you don’t know what to ask for in the next one?" "No, I am not sure there will be a next one." I tried to remember that confident man who resided in this body moments earlier, but that statement came out a cross between sad and matter-of-fact. "Really?" my mother asked, about as happy as a child on Christmas. And while I was looking at this as an opportunity to work my tail off, leave a mark so that even if they did not recognize the value in me someone else might, but I could see she invisioned this as a different opportunity all together. "Well, what would you do if they didn’t give you another one?" "That’s kind of what I wanted your opinion on." "You know that your father and I have supported this endeavour of yours, but I would not mind see it coming to and end. To have you walk away from this while you still can walk. I still remember my little boy in tears waiting for surgery, and have prayed that this family would be spared any similar grief." Ah damn. I knew I made a mistake when I opened myself up to this. "Do you not see any improvements in me since I started this? You know the kind of things that I would continue to improve upon becoming a better man? Like how I am in much better shape than I ever was sitting at a desk job. I have seemed to found my tongue." "Mitchell, what I have seen is a man hobbled with pain. I’ve seen a growing aggression that I thought you had overcome as a child." These words stung like only the truth told to you by a loved one could. "Do you really still wish I had never done this at all? Settled for a life in accounting and nights filled with television watching, games playing and other such anti-social activities? Have I not grown at all?" "You have grown Mitchell, but not into the man that we had hoped for." "I am still a catholic man; I thought that was what Dad and you wanted to see out of me?" "We are still proud of you; it is just that you could be doing so much more. It seems like you have lost focus on the things that really matter." I sat in silence, searching the database for an argument that might see me make my way out of this battle still respectable if not victorious. "When was the last time you have seen your god daughter?" But there it was. She scored the knock out punch. I could hear Myers’ voice now, "And the winner and still champion Mrs. Quinlan!" "Alright, alright, I think I have gotten your opinion now. That’s two votes to me quitting on my dreams." And with that final swipe, like a bumble bee stinging, I bowed out of the fight and the kitchen, leaving that bowl of now crumbs sitting on the table. I knew the first thing that I had to do, before I caught that plane to Las Vegas. ---- The dial tone repeated itself ten times. A ringing came through the ear piece, too many rings. "Damn it," I expelled. I had gotten the machine, but the anger of that unsuccessful attempt to catch up with my old friend was soothed when I heard that silly voice deliver the message. "Hi, you have reach," Emily started, pausing for mommy and daddy. "Tom," said Tom. "Mary," said Mary. "And Emily. We are not here now. Please leave a message and we will get back to you as soon as we can. Okay, goodbye!" Alright, this day hadn’t gone the way I had planned. Two strikes thus far and it was only a half past noon. I got that sinking feeling that I would be striking out today. As the day wore on I found myself feeling more like my beloved Cleveland Indians, whose season was just two series shy of being mercifully over. I knew I had to pull out of that, out of myself, and so I sought out to the one place that I can’t help but figure my contemporaries (I want to take advantage of calling Aimz, Rhine, and the lot that while I still can) would be quite unfamiliar with. ---- I pulled open the doors that remained unlocked and stepped into the dimly lit space. The candles flickered as the breeze found its way in behind me. I dipped my hand into the bowl of lukewarm water and rose that hand to my forehead, lowered to my sternum, and touched each shoulder. Slowly and with reverence I walked down that centre aisle as I cast gaze to vaulted ceilings and the ivory columns that reach down to the ground. My destination was marked for me, the pew near the front of the church with a multicolour light scheme shining down upon it. I genuflected and entered into it, placing the kneelers down and putting myself on my knees. Next I offered my thoughts and prayers to the Lord, asked for the Mother Mary to represent my case to Him, all while gazing upon the massive and iconic image of the Lord hanging on the cross. I will not tell you these thoughts, but rather just impose to you the reason why I had found myself praying before the altar, before the tabernacle. It is a simple conviction that has been installed within me as I grew up in this house of God. It is the belief that while we are all given crossed to carry, prayer and reflection would catch me whenever I stumbled. To not bail out on the path because it is narrow, and emulate the Saviour in his final days before He would conquer death. To pray to the Father to take away the cup, not by our own intentions, but if it is His will, have these anxieties and insecurities pass by. Desperately I have tried to avoid those hardships not necessary, but equally damaging to the relation that I have with Him. But between my righteous preachings of asking help in the face of undue hardships, the man that had taught me that lesson appeared behind the altar, busy with the work of preparing it for the mass later that afternoon. When saw that I was finished my prayers, he wandered over and put a hand on my shoulder. "Ah Mr. Quinlan, greetings," he said, removing his hand from my shoulder. "Father," I offered in reply. I couldn’t help but think now would have been a great time to be left alone, but alas, maybe something else was in the works for me. "I hope that you are here under no great presence of grief." "No more than usual," I report with a grin. While I may be something of a very minor celebrity on the Southern Ontario indy wrestling scene, and smaller still in the eyes of SCCW fans, this pastor would never remember what it was that I did, or really anything more about me other than that I was Jerry’s son. "Oh, I see. Anything you would care to discuss?" he asked, the good shepard. "Not particularly. Nothing major, just a flight ahead of me, some choices I have to make that are just making me a little nervous. You know: the usual." "I know the type," he said, his voice trailing as he had already departed from my side. He left the church and it was again just me, the statues and Him. But already saying my peace, putting in what I thought would be a sufficient request I had little left to ask for. So I stood up and shuffled down the pine, again genuflecting as I turned my back to leave. I opened the doors to a burst of light and a reminder that the calendar has changed seasons and left summer for some other time. Across the way and really all surrounding the property owned by St. Basil’s Roman Catholic Church appeared to be inhabited by the less than contributing members of society. Drug addicts, those who have forgone any morality - or morality as I recognize it – and unashamed sinners formed a perimeter around God’s house. But keeping my eyes on the horizon can be hazardous around children at play. It took my best stutter-step just to avoid steamrolling the young offender. Before my tongue leapt to curse this retched youth, screaming something to discredit the parenting the child was receiving and show the every growing disconnect to my own youth at the ripe old age of 23, I stopped. Maybe this child was being told the correct thing to do anyhow, and was simply disobeying. Perhaps he turned a blind eye to the sage that his parents possessed and hurried hastily into play and fantasy. Maybe this was not about that child any more, who had hightailed his big wheel now to the other side of the parking lot. ---- Father Don’s Houdini act was the magic of a man driven to that next smoke break. Above his head bellowed a cloud of carcinogens as the end of the loose cigarette combusted in flame. And although I have long considered the habit outdated and the general act of stupidity I could not help but respect the man for the simple message his actions delivered. It echoed a homily from years back. It was enough for me to stand up and take notice, to repeat it on call. Well, paraphrase. "Part of faith is recognising that this life is not all there is. We are not to try to safeguard these bodies from everything. Sometimes it is that healthy disrespect for the body that shows a great sign of faith," was what I can recall from that message. Or maybe he never said that, and my mind just rationalised all this. But damn if it did not justify my reckless use of this vessel in pursuits of title belts and cheering fans. As I walked past him, he raised the hand not occupied with a cancer stick in a wave, and I got about four steps past him before I turned on my heel and walked into the vicinity of second-hand smoke. "Padre," I addressed the man in simple black cloth. A groan of recognition and intrigue his retort. "There was one thing that I wanted to talk to you about," I started and waited for further response. I passed the time by rubbing my chin as he inhaled a puff more of poison. "What," coughing, "what was it that you wanted to talk about?" "Its them ten rules Moses dragged back down the mountain with him." "Ah, the Ten Commandments," he restated decoding my poet and meandering speech. "It is one in particular that seems to be a sticking point these days. I’m good with the first three, all about the Big Guy. Them last six are pretty solid and straightforward. But four, four I am having a little trouble understanding." "Honour thy father and mother," he called out from what I think must be a mental rolodex of Bible quotes. He began to recite to me the view points presented in the Catechism, "It’s placed fourth because God has willed that, after him, we should honour our parents give us life and who have handed on to us the knowledge of God. We are called to honour and respect all those whom God, for our good, has given His authority. It means to honour, give affection, and gratitude toward our elders. Respect the wisdom that they pass down to us. Any clearer?" "Yeah, yeah it is." And his long winded explanation had sent him gasping greedily for air before gasping another helping heap of toxins down. "Thanks, but I think I got a flight to catch so, I’ll be seeing ya. And thanks." ---- So there it was, I was a mile in the air and I had never felt further from God. I know that my parents want what they know to be best for me. They possessed a wisdom that I lacked, the clarity that comes with age. It was commanded of me by faith to yield to this. It was simple. I knew what was the right thing to do, but as the Duke, Ripcord, Snake Eyes, well not Snake Eyes, but the rest of the GI Joe’s who weren’t mute would say, "Knowing is half the battle." I had chosen to disobey and disregard the call. ---- I had laced the boots tight, slid into place both knee pads, and generally gotten myself ready for the Pay Per View performance. My nerves in knots, I went about the prematch routine and caught myself with my hand headed to my forehead. I ran it instead through my hair and thought, ‘No use praying for something He ain’t really going to give. He ain’t really going to give me anything to contradict His Word.’ So that night I didn’t pray before my match and a funny thing happened; I won. Now to just collect that contract.
