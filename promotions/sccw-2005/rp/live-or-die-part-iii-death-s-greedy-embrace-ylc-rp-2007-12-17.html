Live or Die, Part III 'Death's Greedy Embrace' (YLC RP)

17 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Decemer 17/07 Live or Die, Part III 'Death's Greedy Embrace' Prologue 'Fists of Rage, Fists of Sorrow' As usual, Joaquin's problems begin with a dream. His physical body hasn't moved since nearly a full day ago, when he was dragged in here and placed in his current position. Flat on his back, his body nestled into the soft fabric of the layers of blankets curled around his body. He appears totally relaxed, not a muscle moving. In fact, if not for the slow, rhythmic rise and fall of his chest, he would appear to be dead. His mind, however, is not sleeping, nor is it dead. No, his subconscious is working overtime, and it is showing him the things that he will never live down, and he will never forget. He sees it all over again, in his mind, and then a face swims before his vision. A pallid face that appears shrunken, withered. A face that leers at him. All at once, the body that was relaxed comes to life, his eyes opening wide with a fire burning in their cobalt blue depths. His hands, only moments ago resting upon the downy surface of the blankets, clench into tight fists. The knuckles crack from the force of the fists and the skin across them turns white with the strain. They are fists of fury, fists that, at one time or another, have reduced some of the best wrestlers and mixed martial artists in the world to bloody pulp. Fists of rage. He sits bolt upright, sending blankets and pillows flying as his face contorts into a mask of sheer rage and his legs kick. His fury is all encompasing, his entire body buried beneath a mountain of sheer rage and hate. 'L'arrêter! Les partir seul!' As that roar of primal rage echoes in the high ceilings of his home, his teeth slam down on his lower lip, and a trickle of blood joins in with the saliva that sprays out of his lips with each snarling breath. The sound is one of the first that his rescuer has heard in the entire time since they returned here, and he springs to his feet, the book he was reading dropping to the floor with a dull thunk. Sean Moreau hears the words that Pierre shouts, and he hears the panic in the other man's voice. But he has no earthly clue why Pierre would wake from an alcoholic haze screaming 'Stop It' and 'Leave them alone!' His feet hit the hardwood floor running, and he pounds towards the room where the Frenchman previously slept. 'Joaquin? Are you...' He stops on a dime, his own blue eyes widening in shock. Where previously Joaquin had lain in the deepest sleep Sean had ever seen, now stands a snarling, raging demon, mixing in curses and threats with deep, gutteral roars like an angry bear. 'Je terminerai ceci, je jure à Dieu!' Sean understands Pierre's promise to end something, but the man has already turned from him. His rage unleashes upon anything and everything in sight, fists and feet smash cavernous holes in the plaster of the walls. 'Vous le bâtard!' He turns again, this time spotting himself in the full length, gleaming mirror hanging on the wall beside his bed. Pierre hurls himself at it, and the sound of the glass shattering and falling to the floor fills the apartment. Blood immediately begins to flow from his head, his arms, his chest. The shiny shards cut his bare feet as he thunders around, and more blood flows. 'Hey! Joaquin...' Moreau is forced to duck as a small painting flies by his head, a woman holding her baby on her lap. The canvas hits the wall by his head and tears, crumpling forlornly to the floor. 'Calm down!' Sean steps forward, grasping Pierre's arm with both hands and pulling him around to face him. 'What the...' He flies backwards, his brain barely having time to register the incoming fist until the second it slams into his jaw. The sensation of falling turns his world into a confused mess, and the man also known as Sean Molson hits the floor with a thud that knocks the wind from his lungs. Stars swirl before his eyes, twirling and whirling and mixing in with the darkness that threatens to overcome him. 'Pas cette fois, O'Dell.' Desperately, Sean crawls, putting distance between himself and Pierre, who continues to close. His eyes have turned nearly black with the fury burning in them. To lie here is to die, given the rage that drives Pierre on, or at least to be seriously hurt. Sean's often injured head still swims, and he has no desire to take another punch from a man with the well-deserved reputation of one of the hardest hitters out there. 'Joaquin... don't...' He drops to his knees with a soft thud, at the same time swinging a massive hammer fist that sends rivulets of pain all the way down from Sean's shoulder. Another blow, and another. Desperation sets in, but he cannot throw off the crushing weight of the bigger man. Sean raises his arms, deflecting the worst of the blows with forearms that quickly go numb. This flimsy barrier is all that is between him and being battered to death by the hammer like fists. One fist slips by his defense, and the crack of bone on hardwood bring a roar of agony from Joaquin's chest. With every ounce of quickness and flexibility he can muster, Sean slides out from his unexpected opponent's grip and grabs his head beneath his right arm. 'Calm the fuck down!' He can feel his own heart beating fast as he hisses the words and cranks back on the guillotine choke he has sunk in. Pierre's blood slick hand scrabbles at his back as he fights the hold. 'Calm down.' A punch thuds against his ribs and he can feel the air woosh out of his lungs, but Sean only bears down harder. His only hope is to keep Pierre down until he calms, or passes out from the choke. 'I'm your friend, Joaquin. It's Sean... Sean Molson.' The World's Finest Wrestler has to use every ounce of endurance he has, but eventually the lack of oxygen and blood to the brain begins to do its work. The struggles of the larger man weaken, his clawing, clutching, striking hands unclenching, and his body goes limp. Slowly but surely, he gives up his struggle. 'You know me, French. It's Sean, and I'm not here to hurt you or anybody else.' The only reply to his words is a choked gurgle, and Sean eases back on the choke only a little. 'Remember?' Sean can feel the pain in his bruised jaw, and the spots where teeth have been loosened. His mouth is full of blood, and he spits onto the floor. 'If I let you go, please, don't start fighting again. For the love of God, I'm here to help you.' He released Joaquin's head and rolls sideways, both men lying on the floor. Two pairs of lungs expand and contract as they struggle to fill their aching lungs. Sean can feel the effects of the punches still, his head whirling with pain. It's been months since his last match, and even this short battle has killed off his energy. 'I'm... sorry.' Pierre chokes out, his eyes seeking out Sean with sorrow in their depths. 'I thought... you were... him.' The World's Finest Wrestler returns his gaze, wondering just who the enigmatic 'him' is, but elaboration comes before he can question. 'O'Dell.' 1 'Face to Face' Less than an hour into her day, and Sarah knows that she has rushed back to work. The familiar confines of her office feel like they are closing in on her, and she has to resist the urge to grab hold of one of her books or paperweights or... or something, and hurl it across the room. What was she thinking? Hell, poor Antoine hasn't even had a funeral yet, she hasn't even picked out a coffin. But Sarah needs something familiar, something that she knows. Something normal. Some fleeting bit of normalcy is all she wants, some semblance of the way things were just the other day. Towards the end, she and her husband spent most of their time fighting, or locked up in separate rooms trying to ignore the presence of the other. But marital problems happened, even to the best of them, and Sarah held up hope that they would eventually work out their problems. But, of course, the mysterious sniper who took his life with a bullet to the side of the head has killed any chance of that happening. And so this morning she has decided to ignore the advice of the Montreal police homicide unit and go back to work. The fact that they have yet to find the killer, or figured out why he would have wished to target Sarah and Antoine, means nothing to her right now. If she stays home, she will only find herself reliving that terrible night over and over, and slowly crushing herself with the iron weight of her guilt. The assurances from the police that they are 'pursuing all leads in this case' mean nothing when there are no leads to pursue. 'You need to focus.' She tells herself sternly, well aware that talking to herself is not under any circumstances a good sign. 'Get your head in what you are doing.' Though it is early, she feels like she has survived a war. The day's first appointment, a gruelling sixty minute session with one of her most challenging cases, has left her drained and tired. She can feel a splitting headache pounding in her temples and threatening to override her carefully planned bearing of calmness and togetherness. So far, she has found herself trying in vain to keep her attention focused, and spending more time asking her receptionist and patients to repeat things than actually helping. She leans back and closes her emerald green eyes, trying to relax as she listens to the leather of her chair creaking. In fifteen years of working and living in this city, she has earned the reputation of being one of the best in her field. Charities and organizations have given her numerous awards for her dedication to her job, and for her ability to maintain a degree of patience, understanding, and professionalism no matter what the case. So why in the hell can't she seem to get past this and get back to work? 'Excuse me, Dr. La Vega?' the voice of her receptionist, a pretty college student named Janice, comes over the small speaker on her desk. 'Your ten o'clock is here.' 'Thank you... could you have him fill out the questionnaire and give me a few minutes to look it over before you send him in?' This is the last thing she needs, and she wonders for what feels like the hundredth time today if, perhaps, she isn't doing the wrong type of job. Sometimes she wonders. 'Uh... sorry.' Janice's blonde hair flashes in the sunlight as she pokes her head in the door. 'I already had him do the survery, so I figured I'd just bring it in.' Sarah smiles, the girl's nervousness making her remember her own college days for a moment. Janice moves quickly across the room, moving with the fluid grace of an athlete, and drops a folder on the desk in front of her, and then opens her red lipstick covered lips as if she wishes to say something. 'Is there something you need, Janice?' 'Ummm, no. I was just... are you ok?' The last three words burst out of her chest in a rush, as though she didn't quite know how to say them. 'I mean... just... do you want me to reschedule this guy? You look tired.' So such things as kindness, concern, and good old fashioned decency do still exist in this crazy world. 'I think I'll be fine, Janice. But thank you, I appreciate it. Do you think that you could give me about ten minutes?' Sarah says, checking her watch and tugging on a strand of her dark chestnut hair. 'And then send him in?' She opens the folder and sees the writing, scrawled across the page in a shaky, spidery line that looked like a child's. Maxime LaVelle. Sarah could hear Janice through the closed door, assuring Mr. LaVelle that the doctor would see him shortly, that she had recently suffered a loss in her family and needed a few moments. If only she could get over this in only a few moments. 'You'll live, Sarah.' She consulted her expression in the small mirror built into the gleaming mahogany surface of her desk. Nothing out of the ordinary, really, except for the tired circles beneath her eyes. She wears the same type of demure black suit she has always favored when working, designed to hide the sultry curves of her body and not distract the patients, and her long hair is tied back as usual. Even the rounded lenses of the reading glasses she usually wears in the office are the same. But she does look tired. 'Janice, please send Mr. LaVelle in.' The man must have been waiting right beside the swinging doors to her inner sanctum, because the words are no sooner out of her mouth than the brass doorknob is turning with a slight creaking sound. As this happens, Sarah breaths one deep breath, trying to calm her fluttering nerves and unstable emotions. 'Good morning, Doctor.' Maxime LaVelle's voice sounds weak and slurred, as though he has been drinking or suffers from a speech impediment. 'I am sorry to hear about the loss of your husband.' That's odd... she can't remember hearing Janice mention anything about it being her husband. But Sarah supposes that she has been space trekking this morning and could have missed it. She can only manage a polite nod, choking back the sob that threatens to burst forth, and waves him to one of the black overstuffed chairs in front of her desk. 'Thank you, Mr. LaVelle. It has been difficult, but I'm sure I will be fine. Can I offer you something to drink?' Standing up, Sarah can feel her long legs wobbling as she crosses to the small fridge. 'Water? Coffee?' At the shaking of his head, she grabs a bottle of Evian for herself and heads back to her desk. 'So, I have read your answers to the survery I always have new patients fill out, but you've left most of it blank. Was there something in particular you wanted to talk about?' She has an odd feeling that this is going to be a man she will have a tough time getting a straight answer out of. When he first entered the room, he looked eager, excited, for all the world like an exicted schoolboy despite the sagging gut and wrinkling face of an older man. Now he sits in his chair with his dark grey eyes staring sullenly at the floor, little to no expression on his pallid face. 'Excuse me? Sir?' No answer. He only wrings his short fingered hands and rocks his flabby frame back and forth, shifting his weight in the chair. 'Look, if you aren't going to talk, why don't you do both of us a favor and go home?' She is usually not so short with hesitant patients, but this is not a normal day. 'I'm sure we both have better things to do than sit here waiting.' This is the last thing she needs right now. 'I have dreams.' 'That's a very vague answer, Maxime. You are going to need to give me more if I'm going to help you. What kind of dreams?' A tears rolls down his pallid face, over the skin that looks like bread dough. 'Sexual... sexual dreams. I feel ashamed of them. They are violent, and very gruesome.' Oh, joy. A real sicko, though that is not a term she learned at school. 'You say violent... how so? Rape? Sadism? Sodomy? Whips? Chains?' For the first time, he brings his eyes up to meet hers, and she is surprised and disturbed by the way they appear. The eyes are so dark they appear to be no more than a solid black mass surrounded by a small ring of white. It looks like somebody has filled his eye sockets with liquid coal. One of her old profs at school used to tell her 'Sarah, the eyes are the window through which you can read the soul. Nine out of ten times, you can get a good read of a person by their eyes.' Whether or not that is true, it matters not in this case. In Maxime's eyes there is nothing to read. They are blank. Emotionless. They look sick, and devoid of anything and everything. Evil is the only word she can think of. But of course, she is under stress, he deserves a chance to tell his story before she can judge. 'All of that and more, I guess. So much more that I can't even think of it all, ma'am. It makes me feel sick, I throw up every time.' 'Who is committing these acts? You?' 'Sometimes.' She waits for him to continue. 'Sometimes I'm watching. Sometimes I'm the victim. Sometimes I'm laughing, others I'm crying.' For the first time all day, Sarah, repulsed as she is by what he is telling her, finds her interest piqued. There is something here she can't put into words. She sits up straighter and looks right at him. 'When did this start?' The answer is usually the same in cases like these. Childhood. 'And how did you react to them? I'm not going to judge you, but I think your reaction is crucial to your recovery.' He swallows, trying to wet a throat gone dry. 'At first I, well, I guess I liked them, ma'am. I didn't know any better, really. But then I realized that it wasn't how it is supposed to be, and I got scared. Scared to go to sleep.' 'Think carefully, Maxime. Was there anything that set this off? Something that you maybe don't want to remember?' She pauses, letting him think. 'Did somebody do something?' 'NO!' His voice rises, becoming high pitched and whiny as it does, and he sounds for all the world like a child trying to tell a teacher about a 'bad thing.' His eyes close, and another tear leaks out. 'Don't get upset, please. I'm trying to help.' 'I need to go. I need to go, please.' Avoidance. 'Of course, but I would like you to come back. You took a very big step, a brave step, coming in here today.' He nods, and practically runs toward the door, letting it slam behind him. Sarah sighs, her eyes rolling as she does. Another one that will probably end up doing something stupid and horrible because they wouldn't talk about their problems.... just as she starts to think about making a call, to get a second opinion on what she just experienced, she notices it. A gleam of brass on the gloss black surface of the overstuffed chair. The casing of a bullet. Sitting on top of a piece of paper. With her name written in large, capitalized letters on it. She crosses the room with a sense of numb disbelief. Carefully, she picks up the paper, and opens it where it is folded. Sarah, If you are reading this, then you know. I was helping you that night. You are better off without him. And you are better off without the frog. He and I know each other, pretty well actually. Ask him about me sometime, show him my mark, and see what he says. I'll let this message sink in now, but you'd best know that I don't make idle threats. I'm a dangerous man, and an intelligent woman like yourself should know better than to cross me. Lose the frog, and I mean now. Or you can join him in death's greedy embrace. See you soon. HO. Sarah lets out a muted cry and collapses, her eyes rolling back in her head as her legs turn to jelly. The last thing she remembers is Janice's panicky voice, and the girl begging for an ambulance.... 3 'Like An Old Friend' Joaquin's assorted stitches and wounds burn a little, but he has emerged no worse for wear from his encounter with the broken glass and destruction of his bedroom. His mind is currently occupied with the dream, and with why O'Dell has returned to his mind now, of all the times he possibly could. 'You OK, man?' Sean's voice calls to him in his reverie, reminding him that the man is still here. 'If you need to talk, talk.' Sean is a good man, a man he has a lot of respect for. In fact, along with Joshua Kosidlo and Eryc Morgan before Morgan's bizarre transferrance into the entity known as Corvus, Sean is one of the few fellow fighters Joaquin considers a friend. The fact that he would journey here just to make sure he was all right is a fact for which he will be eternally grateful. 'Who is O'Dell? You kept talking about him when I found you in the graveyard, too, but you were too drunk and messed up to tell me who he was.' Joaquin shakes his head. There can be no talking about that beast right now. 'What is going on with you?' 'I don't know anymore, Sean. I think part of me died with them.' Sean knows that part of the story, hell, everyone who knows him sent their condolences after the news of the murders broke. But what Sean doesn't know is the full story. 'I think 'zat 'ze only time I'm really me anymore is when I fight.' Defiance was a prime example of this. He stepped into the ring with a sense of purpose, something he lacked when not in the ring. He wanted to show Corvus that he was not the unstoppable beast he thought he was. But, as has been the case for him the last while, he failed. 'And as you can see, I can't even do 'zat anymore.' 'I understand, man. Believe it or not, I do.' No, he doesn't. Nobody can. 'My wife and daughter were lost to me too. On Christmas Eve, they crashed on a snowy road. I wasn't there, but...' He seemed to choke on the words, and Pierre could hear the pain he still felt. ' I know what it feels like to lose a family. It tears you apart.' The nail is hit right on the head, and Joaquin buries his face in his hands, unable to hold back the tears anymore. Unable to hold back everything he has been fighting so hard to keep held back. 'You never feel quite whole again.' It is as if Sean's words are reaching right into the Frenchman's soul, tearing into his very essence with their truth. 'But you have to find a reason to keep going, to keep on. Think of Marie, Joaquin. What would she want you to do?' Joaquin knows just what Sean is talking about. He remembers hazy bits of what happened at the graveyard, remembers sinking to his knees at the side of her grave so many times that his knees had begun to wear little grooves in the soil beside it. He recalls the flask, in his hand, the liquor burning its way down into his already pickeled stomach. He remembers the shame. 'I doubt she'd want you to be like this.' No, she wouldn't. 'I think she would want you to pull yourself together, no matter what. You have a shot at redemption here, French.' The Young Lions Cup. 'You have a shot at proving yourself to FUSE, to the fans, to Corvus, hell, you have a shot at proving yourself to yourself. And I'm sure Marie would have wanted you to do just that.' He is right, of course. Sean moves out of the room, rummaging about in the rubble of what was once a chest of drawers, and he finally finds what he is looking for. When he emerges around the corner again, he is holding a foot and a half of polished brass, with a large cross on one end. The object is very familiar to Joaquin, as well as to anybody who is familiar with him. 'I think that you have been missing something else, too.' With a smooth motion, Molson tosses the object over to Pierre, who catches it with unerring skill and stares down at it, clenched in his shaking hand. 'You are right, Sean.' He needs to go back to the way he was, to the man who never once backed down from a fight, no matter how bad the odds looked. He needs to go back to the old Joaquin Pierre. For his own sake. For Marie's sake. For his children's sake. ''Zis feels... it feels right.' With a slight twist of his hand, he pulls on the cross and draws a foot and a half of polished steel blade from within the handle, staring at his signature weapon with a reverance he has never showed it before. After all, this is the blade that pierced Harrison O'Dell's black heart. It feels like an old friend in his hand, like the thing that has been missing in this whole puzzle. He always carried with him in Global, more for show that anything, but it has become something of a talisman for him now. He slides it back into its polished handle and smiles, a genuine smile for the first time in months. To Sean, it looks like the man he remembers is emerging from within an old, shrivelled, destroyed husk of a man. It looks like the old Joaquin Pierre has finally returned. 'Remember what Joshua told you? Go out there at Uproar and prove yourself to everyone and anyone. Do it for your loved ones, and do it for yourself.' He opens his mouth to reply, but before he can, the shrill shreek of the telephone cuts him off. Moving across the room, he picks up the receiver and holds it to his ear. 'Bonjour?' The voice on the other end is deep, powerful, and authoritive. It is the voice of somebody who is very used to being in charge. 'Monsieur Pierre, I am Detective Malveau with the homicide unit. We require your assistance in an investigation.' Never a good thing. Joaquin swallows, then opens his mouth to ask a question. The man cuts him off. 'We will send a car for you shortly. Please be ready.' 'Bon, I will be waiting.' The man's tone leaves no room for arguing. Joaquin looks over at Sean, his eyes full of bewilderment as he hangs up the phone. 'What was that about?' Sean seems equally confused. 'Everything OK?' 'I... I don't know.' Outside of the snowy window, a police cruiser pulls up, and Joaquin looks at his friend with a slight shrug. 'I'll be back soon. Thank you.' But Sean's pep talk and its influence on his future in FUSE will have to wait. Something has happened, and whatever it is, it involves him. Why is it always something?
