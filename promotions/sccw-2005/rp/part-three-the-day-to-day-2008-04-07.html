Part Three: The Day To Day

7 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/7/08 #If there’s a camera up in here, then I best not catch this flick on YouTube...# In the pantheon of deeply crap pieces of music, Mariah Carey’s "Touch My Body" rates a mention in, if we’re feeling particularly cruel, maybe the first circle of bad song hell. Sure, the lyrics are fairly inane and Ms. Carey surrenders to the unfortunate habit of "whisper-singing" that has plagued contemporary R&B for years (which, unfortunately, tends to obscure the fact that Mariah does actually have a proper voice). At its core, though, "Touch My Body" tends to be the sort of song that does a nice job of fading into the background. Audio wallpaper at it’s finest. Unfortunately for Lance Marshall, it’s the third time he’s managed to stumble across the song on the radio within the last hour and he’s growing thoroughly sick of it. Hurriedly, he stabs the button for the next preset on the SUV’s radio. #If you want it, come and get it…# Lance allows the pairing of Madonna and Justin to retreat into the corner of his brain marked "not really paying attention" as he maneuvers his way through the maze of L.A. traffic. It hadn’t quite been his intent to be behind the wheel in the first place, especially at this time of day. It had taken a quick glimpse of the fridge at home and the realization that neither of them had been particularly concerned about keeping the house stocked with food that had sent Alanna running to the grocery store…and, consequently, had Lance winding the Cayenne through near parking lot level traffic to make his way to…well, we’ll find that out in just a few moments. A brief surge in the traffic propels Lance towards the corner just before the oncoming traffic light and he hurriedly turns right, ditching the mass of cars to make his way down a side street. Just a few moments later, a quick slide into an empty parking slot sees Lance arrive at his destination. The building itself is fairly non-descript, designed largely for functionality and painted in a not terribly attractive shade of industrial grey. What could only be described as a mass of children have been corralled into a patch of lawn at the front of the building, a clutch of older men and women struggling to maintain some semblance of order. As Lance makes his way towards the front of the building, one particular child makes his way free of the pack. This particular child has only recently turned three years old (although a casual observer would swear that he was at least five). A blur of black hair and Gap Kids jeans, he runs full tilt (well, as full tilt as he can manage) towards Lance. Lance pauses, waiting until the kid gets within a few feet of him before bending down and scooping him up in his arms. The child’s face is beaming, his smile threatening to burst free of the confines of his face. His eyes gleam with the energy of the very young and with a hearty cry of "DADDY!" he wraps his arms as much as he can around Lance’s neck, holding on for dear life. It never fails, thinks Lance. No matter what kind of day it’s been, no matter how shitty things may feel…just one smile from Zach and everything was right with the world again. Balancing Zach easily on his left arm, Lance waves with the right to the teachers gathered on the lawn, a few of whom wave back. He then turns, walking back towards his parked SUV. Zach continues to be balanced on his arm, resting against his father and keeping up a steady stream of babble in the direction of his father’s left ear. He can, Lance thinks, clearly make out about one word in ten. Lance carefully lowers Zach to the ground as they reach the SUV, opening the back passenger side door. He then picks Zach up again so he can carefully strap him into the car seat, Zach wiggling all the while. Once Zach is secured, Lance tickles him briefly causing Zach to giggle loudly. Lance closes the back door and makes his away around to the driver’s side, clambering in behind the wheel. He opens the glove compartment, rifling through a small pile of CDs until he comes across the soundtrack to one of the Disney Channel’s latest offerings, "Johnny and the Sprites". With an ease born of serious repetition, he slides the CD into the appropriate slot on the SUV’s audio system while backing his car out of the parking space. Sliding carefully into traffic, he turns the volume on the stereo just loud enough for Zach to hear while not being nearly loud enough to drive him crazy. Gazing into the rearview mirror (and being greeted by Zach’s beaming smile), Lance barely has the chance to get out "So, how was your day, Zach" before being pelted with… "Gweat, Daddy! We pwayed Hide and Seek and I found evewybody and I twaded my cookies at lunch for a juicebox and we got to pway on the computer and I found this neat anthill on the pwaygwound and…" "Whoa there," answers Lance, "hold on a minute. Daddy can’t think as fast as he used to, okay?" "Sowwy, Daddy." "So, you had a good day, huh?" "The bestest! Where’s Mommy?" "Mommy went to get some groceries so I get to come and pick you up today. That okay?" "Okay." The ride proceeds in silence for a moment before… "Are you okay, Daddy?" "I’m fine, Zach. Why, did someone say something?" If it was Jesse or, worse, that boyfriend of hers…both he and Alanna had given her strict instructions that Zach was never meant to see any of the shows… "Jamie said his older bwother saw you on TV…saw you getting hurt real bad." Great, now it was the other kids he had to worry about… "Hey, Zach, nothin’ to worry about, right? Nothing can hurt me, right? And why is that?" "Cause you’re SuperDaddy!" Zach giggled. "That’s right. And what does SuperDaddy do?" "Beats the bad guys!" "That’s right. And I’m gonna do it again this week." "Yay!" The rest of the ride passes in a blur of playground exploits, silly songs and that really cool snake that Mister Corwin brought to class last week. Zach is still babbling happily away when Lance pulls the SUV into the driveway, noting with some surprise that Alanna’s BMW was nowhere in sight. It had been a few hours since his wife had left for the grocery store, noted Lance, and it normally never took quite so long. But then, there was any one of a number of reasons you could get delayed at the grocery store. Bad traffic. Having to wait for displays to be restocked. Bad line at checkout. Bad line at the deli counter. It wasn’t like anything really major could happen at the grocery store, right? #I’m a new soul, I came to this strange world, hoping I could learn a bit ‘bout how to give and take# Piped in music, thought Alanna Marshall as she watched the cashier slowly scan through the items in her cart, was a tool of the devil. Had to be, she decided. Something designed to slowly suck away your will to live as you… "That’ll be one hundred and twenty dollars, ma’am." Alanna reached into her wallet, grimacing inwardly at a kid who looked no more than sixteen calling her "ma’am". He was either trying to be polite or just parroting his corporate training, she hadn’t decided which…but the end effect was just to make her feel old. She swiped the American Express card through the terminal, pressing the button for "credit", drumming her fingers against the plastic as the receipt paper spooled out from the cash register. She should have been home at least twenty minutes ago but traffic, so far, had been an absolute bear…She signed the paper the cashier handed to her, quickly scribbling her name along the dotted line. The groceries now all packed snugly into eco-friendly paper bags, she pushed the cart along as she made her way outside to the parking lot. Her BMW sat in the afternoon sun, sunlight gleaming off of the paint. Alanna gave the car a quick once over, noting with satisfaction that the car seemed to be no more the worse for wear. No new dents or dings, no new scratches… …she was pretty sure the piece of paper resting in her windshield hadn’t been there before. Alanna grumbled slightly as she popped the trunk, placed the bags inside and closed it again with a solid THUNK. It had become a disturbingly regular source of annoyance, she thought, that people felt free to put things on her windshield. Not just sales circulars, either, no. Notes praising her, notes cursing her out, threats, proposals…not to mention that one letter a week ago containing a request that Alanna would have sworn was illegal, obscene and physically impossible… Alanna pauses, her train of thought interrupted as she unfolds the sheet of paper and reads what is written on it. Frustratingly, we don’t get to see the contents of said sheet of paper…but from the look crossing Alanna Marshall’s face, perhaps that’s for the best. "Oh, hell no…" The next few moments will see Alanna Marshall pull out of the grocery store parking lot with perhaps more force and almost certainly more speed than is completely necessary. #Don’t blame it on the sunshine, don’t blame it on the moonlight# One of the earliest lessons David Robuchon learned on becoming a photographer was that there was no such thing as a typical client. The earliest lesson, of course, is that everyone is going to assume you’re gay, no matter what evidence there may be to the contrary. But shortly after that lesson was the one about needing to be able to handle any type of client…and make them come out looking their best. It was a lesson he put to good use when hired as one of the official photographers for FUSE/SCCW. It had surprised him, at first, to learn that men and women who could be so vibrant and so theatrical in front of a television camera could be so stiff and uncomfortable when posing in front of a still camera. When his first few attempts at taking pictures for Mister Duluth had ended in utter disaster, David realized that action needed to be taken. The simplest thing to do, he quickly realized, was to try and make the atmosphere more relaxed. So on went the old school play list on the iPod, in went the goofy props and out came the drinks. It wasn’t a completely foolproof strategy (G-d, but that shoot with Andre Jensen had been a disaster) but it had been a real morale booster to see what quality shots he could get if he could just get his subjects to loosen up a little. This particular day had seen him working with a couple, one of the inrushes of new folks that had joined SCCW in the past few months. The husband was a nice enough guy…pleasant, friendly, surprisingly quiet and massive in the way that tended to prompt involuntary cries of "please, don’t hurt me". The wife…well, he hadn’t quite gotten a handle on her. When he had jokingly suggested doing a King Kong/Fay Wray theme, she had blown up. The husband had merely politely declined but she…she had certainly had some choice words for him. It had not surprised David terribly when the wife had stormed off nor when her husband had followed her. David had seen the two of them talking in the corner, the husband obviously trying to get his wife to calm down. What had been a surprise occurred five minutes later. David caught a glimpse of the wife heading towards him and grimaced inwardly. G-d, he was getting tired of getting bitched out by wrestlers. It had been a dumb idea, yes, but he’d let go of it almost immediately and… "I’m sorry." Well, this was unexpected. "Pardon?" "I shouldn’t have gone off on you like that. Nothing you said or did deserved a response like the one I gave you. So I just wanted to apologize." "Well…thank you. That’s very kind of you. And yeah, it was kind of a dumb idea." A bright smile, a giggle, and the feeling that the temperature in the room had risen at least five degrees. "To be honest, Mrs. Marshall, I am a little surprised by your apology. I’d heard some stories about you and was, well…kinda prepared for the worst." Alanna raised an eyebrow in mild confusion. "What do you mean, "stories"?" "Oh, the usual kind of thing. She’s demanding, a total diva, a bitch, yadda yadda yadda…" David notes that Alanna’s smile never wavers. It’s obvious that she’s heard this before. "Who told you that?" she asks. "I think her name was Kassidy or something…" Alanna laughs and in that laugh David can see what might have brought her and her husband together. The rest of the day had proceeded uneventfully. David had run through a few rolls of film of both Lance and Alanna in their "work clothes", Lance running through a series of standard bodybuilding poses and Alanna working with a Fatboy that he’d rented for the week. Nice stuff, to be sure, but nothing that really popped. The duo was now standing in front of a green backdrop, having changed outfits. Alanna was wearing a pair of dark jeans (nearly spray painted on) and a black T-shirt which read, in elaborate, flowery script: IRRESISTIBLE FORCE. Lance was dressed similarly, only his T-shirt read in large, chunky, rock-like lettering: IMMOVABLE OBJECT. The two of them stood ready for whatever David had to throw at them. Unfortunately for David, ideas were not being all that quick in coming. "Guys, I am stumped," David admitted. "If you’ve got any suggestions, I am all ears." Lance bent his face to Alanna’s right ear, whispering something that David couldn’t catch. Whatever it was, Alanna’s response was a smile and a "You sure?" "Absolutely." Lance then turned to David, raising his hand as though he was back in school. "Um, David? I think we have an idea." And so it was, just a few short moments later, that the image printed up into thousands of posters bearing the logo of "Team Marshall" was as follows: Lance hitting a front bicep pose, smile as wide as Texas plastered across his face, eyes locked straight ahead. Resting atop his shoulders, Alanna, both legs crossed in front of Lance’s neck, blowing a kiss at the camera. We now return you to your regularly scheduled lives, already in progress.
