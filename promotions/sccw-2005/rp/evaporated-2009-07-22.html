Date: 7/22/2009 "And where the offense is, let the great axe fall" – William Shakespeare * * * Sometimes, the epilogue comes first. In the backseat of a cab, speeding through the outskirts of Minneapolis, Walter Halvorson had a rotten feeling in his stomach. It reminded him of the one time he failed a class in high school, some thirty eight years ago. On the bus ride home, where he knew the report card was waiting, he imagined what his mother would say to him. He imagined the dissapointment, the lecture, the suspended priviledges. Only this was worse. This was a lot worse. It had been raining since he got off the plane, not that he had paid it much attention. Inbetween all the panic stricken thoughts that festered in his head, he remembered something else: the one time he ever heard Alexandra say something good about him. Roderick Ashe had asked her why she kept Walter around, and from outside the room he had overheard her say: "he knows all the players, and he has a way of putting the ball in the right place". She was simplying her analogy for Mush Mouthed one, but the analogy made sense, or used to make sense. How had this gotten so fucked? Ironically, one of the things he was good at, was knowing things were fucked before anyone else. He had developed a serious obsession with chess in his late twenties, and could see the end of the match twenty moves ahead. He had probably won more money than Lane Stevens and Phillip Kennedy combined playing poker (all of it online), although never told anyone about it. He knew when to fold his hand, when a charade had gone too far. "Here is fine," Walter said, and the cab pulled over on the curb. After paying the driver, he stepped out of the vehicle. His walk was not hurried because of the rain, but because he had to know. As you may or may not recall, almost two months ago Walter had scouted out a job from a Mr. Reginald Sharnell, a man Desade had dealt with several times over the years. A man whose bark was a lot worse than his bite. A cocky man who wore bright suits and acted like he owned the place…any place, every place. The payoff was bigger than a man like Mr. Sharnell could have typically financed, but for whatever reason Walter let that slide. The job was easier than the money indicated, and for whatever reason Walter let that slide. All Lane Stevens had to do was keep a feeble old man, whose name didn’t ring any bells, with no security of any kind, who lived in the middle of bumfuck nowhere, from attending small meeting of Chicago businessmen to discuss a new construction project. That was it. Unfortunately, the bells had begun to ring. And Mr. Sharnell was not returning any phone calls. Walter’s thinning gray hair did not look suave when it got wet, but that wasn’t the most pressing issue on his mind. He jogged up the stairs of the main door of the McCullum building, which was eight stories of rented office space for men and woman of various backgrounds in the twin cities area. He pushed through the glass double doors with as much force as he could muster, grunting slightly. There was a brunette receptionist he once had a detailed fantasy about in the lobby. "I can be here," Walter mumbled awkwardly as he passed her, flashing the ID tag that was made for reoccuring guests and special vendors. Her eyes squinted in confusion, but she saw the badge and nodded. No more fantasies, bitch Walter said in his head, and felt silly a moment later. What kind of punishment was that for her, really? Jesus Christ, focus. Halvorson was on the elevator a moment later, riding up to the fourth floor. He listened to two men have small talk about the Minnesota Twins and wondered if he’d ever feel comfortable enough in his own skin to have small talk ever again. A dinging noise snapped him out of this thought, and he got off the elevator. That sinking feeling in his stomach, it got worse as he neared office 407. Before he turned the corner, he took a deep breath. Here we go, he thought. What Walter saw before him as he entered the room (after a few knocks) was for all intensive purposes, an empty office. The abstract art that Mr. Sharnell pretended to understand was off the walls. The tacky paper weights were gone. There were no chewed pens littered about the desk. The leather couch had vanished. The only thing that remained was an envelope sitting in the center of the room. As Walter approached the envelope, all the hairs on the back of his neck stood up as he saw his own name written on it. "Not good," Walter whispered. He paused for a few moments, before suddenly leaning down and eagerly tearing it open. Inside there were two items. 1. A note 2. A picture The note said: "It is worse than you know." It was not signed. The picture was an action shot of Lane Stevens, bashing an old man in the head with a brick, just under two months ago. The mere existance of this picture, of the fact that Lane, and in turn Desade could be connected to this event, would mean the end of Walter’s existance at the very least. Which of course meant this was not something he could take back to Alex. Which in turn meant that for the first time since he had been in her employ, he had a secret. Which made him alone. "Not good," Walter whispered again, even quietier. He briefly wondered why it smelled like cigarettes in this non smoking building, before resuming the business of worrying. * * * A few days earlier. "Two things you’ll never need in New York City…a car, or manners." Miles Cavanaugh puncuated this point by licking a dollar bill, and slapping it on the strippers backside. It stuck. Miles and Jeb Stewart sat directly in front of the stage at Dolls, one of New York’s more famous strip clubs. Despite the complete absense of light in the establishment, it was actually pretty early yet, 4:30 pm eastern time. Miles had a PBR in front of him, while Jeb was drinking a Heineken. "You ever been to a joint like this before?" Miles asked, taking a sip of his Pabst. "Of course I have," Jeb replied instantly, with a bit of irritation in his voice. And just for the record, he was lying. "No need to get bent outta shape, you just don’t seem comfortable." "Just don’t see the point, is all." Miles winced, as if that comment reached out and slapped him. "Tits. Tits is the point. Don’t think about it so hard." They both watched as the blonde dancer came back to their part of the stage. There were only a few customers currently sitting close enough to tip the girls, so these visits were frequent. She got down on her knees, and smiled at Jeb, who awkwardly returned the smile. While maintaining eye contact with him, she played with herself lewdly, which made Jeb feel embarassed for her. She was in her early thirties, it appeared. And like most women in this establishment, she had some work done. A second later the dancer reached out, grabbed him by the back of the head, and rammed his face into her chest and rubbed it around for a few moments. When she got up, Miles noticed a few flecks of glitter on Jeb’s face, and he smiled briefly at this. Seconds later, Miles was getting the same treatment. "So what happened between you and Lane?" Jeb asked, as he often did. "Jesus man," Miles said, his voice muffled by breasts. The stripper moved on to another customer, and Cavanaugh turned to face Jeb for a moment, clearly looking exhausted by the question. But before he could say anything, he felt his phone vibrate in his pocket. "Slam the beer," Miles barked, briefly flashing the identity of the messenger to Jeb. * * * On the other side of the country, Lane Stevens closed his phone. The Hilton Garden Inn, The Warlord’s chosen hotel for the week, was by far one of the nicer places to stay in Laramie. He wasn’t used to this kind of crowd. Even when things were going well and he was running with the Affliction, he was usually shacking up at a Holiday Inn. Places like these, you could depend on people to be predictable. It was the well to do crowd, trying not to make any waves. People here weren’t desperate. And while he had no problem dealing with desperate people, and in fact had gotten quite good at it, it did get a little taxing. A desperate man is capable of anything. Here, he could headbutt the next person he came across, and it probably wouldn’t escelate too much farther than that. And he got off on that. In cards, wrestling, and life, Lane had a way of spotting who had nothing to lose…and who would bend over backwards to to keep even the samllest of scraps. His win/loss record in SCCW, like in FUSE, was not the stuff that legends were made of…but he was doing his job. Men like Dusk, Spacely, etc, they had to deal with him. And if they were dealing with him, they were not dealing with Alexandra Pierce. She appreciated this, even if she did not directly so. And so, here he was, sitting under an umbrella at the hotel’s outdoor pool, sipping on a margarita. This was her thank you. And like his surroundings, Lane’s appearance had changed as well. Instead of an old wrestling or band t-shirt, today he wore a custom tan suit. Beneath his shades, his eyes traced the outlines of most of the women in the pool. Young, old, fat, skinny…it didn’t matter. He was fascinated by the female form. His concentration was broken by a voice. "Looked all over." "Hiya gramps, take a load off," The River Rat said, without even looking up. "And by all over, I’m pretty sure you just mean the bar." Walter Halvorson, who was carrying a small cardboard box, took a seat on the other side of the table. "It is an off day so…yeah" Walter replied, and followed Lane’s gaze. He was not surprised to find what was at the end of it. "This is only my second drink, Mommy will be so proud," Lane mused. "Well, she did say you have been drinking less lately." "Not less, per se, just better timed." "I’m glad." Stevens had not even glanced at Walter once since he arrived, but knew something was off about him. Halvorson didn’t even seem to have time to be repulsed by him. There was a lot on his mind, clearly. "Anyway, just wanted to let you know that I’m going to hit the road. I have some pressing business elsewhere," Walter said. The Director’s WMD finally turned his head slightly, and saw Walter looking straight ahead. He was barely even breathing. It looked to Lane like he was trying to become invisible. "Alright," Lane said, and returned his gaze to pool. "If something should come up, I assume your men can handle it." "They’re on an errand too, I’m afraid," Lane started, and before his babysitter could respond, "you don’t want to know about it." "I imagine you are correct." "I’m covered though, chief. Have fun, get laid. Don’t get the HIV or nothin’ though. I hear that shit sucks." "Thanks," Walter responded, and stood up. Something really is up, Lane thought. "Oh, you get the camera?" Walter looked confused for a few moments, before finally looking down at the box he was carrying. "Yeah…yeah I did." "Good boy," Lane said, and smirked. * * * It was in a back alley somewhere in New York City that Miles finally answered Jeb’s question. "We were both twenty two, right." Jeb Stewart, whose head had been on a swivel the last twenty minutes or so, turned all of his attention to his associate. Cavanaugh waited for a homeless man pushing a shopping cart to pass them by before he started again. "Lane wanted to hit up some college party, which was weird for him…cause he hated that shit. He hated frat boys, keg beer, loud music. It wasn’t his scene. But he insisted. I thought something was up, but I agreed. I didn’t have shit planned. So we get there, and I don’t see the motherfucker pretty much the whole time I’m there. Dissapears into some upstairs bedroom. I just assume this is about a bitch, cause back then, our badass boss would lose his shit whenever a girl would even give him the time of day." Jeb practically giggled at that. "Yeah, no shit," Miles continued, "so I’m talking to these jerkoffs, drinking a few beers, talking to a few girls…then they bring the hard stuff out. I’m doing shots of Jameson with some random douche. Next thing I know I’m three sheets. Maybe four sheets. Lane comes down, suddenly in a big hurry to go. Naturally it happens as soon as I’m having a good time. Just how things work with that guy. I probably pass out like ten minutes into the car ride home. But when I wake up, there’s a flash light shining into the car, and some rather unfriendly conversation. He was stopped for a broken tail light, some dumb shit. But seeing as Lane is chief asshole #1, turns the routine traffic stop into a full blown search cause the cop thinks he’s on something. Which is hilarious, cause he’s stone sober. Not so much as had a Mountain Dew." In Cavanaugh’s mind, he can still see the scene playing out, slightly blurred from years gone by, and the amount of liquor he had consumed on that evening. "As I’m getting out of the car, some baggies fall out of my coat pocket. Lane always had some kind of new scheme to make money. To ‘get out of this town’ and all that cliché shit. This week, apparently, had been selling weed. Whatever he got at that house, he had stuffed into my pockets when he was stopped. Still didn’t have the presense of mind to just say, yes sir…no sir. Fucking cocksucker." Jeb’s eyebrows raised. "I already had a marijuana arrest on my juvi record, did some community service, and so on. But this was enough to sell. And to gift wrap it even further, it was in individual baggies." Miles laughed, Jeb did not. There was a silence between them then. In the distance, they saw a man walking towards them. "Lane told ‘em it was mine, and the kids at the party cut a deal, rolled on me too. One thing our boss could always do…was bluff. Those kids were scared shitless of him, thought he was connected straight to satan. So I went away for a bit. My parents had about enough of me anyway, they freaked out, haven’t talked to them since. Irony was I had been clean from everything but booze for a while before then. Started doing harder shit after I got out of prison though, just passing the time, ya know?" Jeb did not know. "Fuck man," was all Jeb could say. "Fuck, man," Miles repeated with a slightly different delivery, his eyes on the approaching visitor. Even farther off, cars honked and people yelled. The city seemed angry. "So why are you…" "Here?" Miles finished, and shrugged. "What else am I supposed to do? Join a yoga class? He turned me into him. Not giving a shit about anything, including what he done. Ain’t that a bitch?" Before Jeb could comment, the man that was approaching them had gotten within shouting distance. "You Tim?" Miles hollered. "I ain’t no one," the man replied. "Fair enough." It was a young kid, and from what Jeb knew of this errand, was probably just starting his residency and needed the extra cash. The transaction happened so quick Jeb barely had time to take note of any of his features. He handed Miles a manila folder, and Miles handed him a small paper bag that was full of money. Immediantly the youngster turned and walked away. The walk soon turned into a jog. "That was easy," Jeb said. "Don’t be saying a thing like that without a crystal ball, my friend." Stewart didn’t really know what that meant, but he nodded and acted like he did. * * * The following footage was slipped under Dusk’s hotel room door in DVD format. Static. Black. Audio feed. "Well Craig, guess its about time to do this again, eh?" Video feed. The River Rat was pretty much exactly where we left him, a few hours and a few margaritas later. The sky was dark, and the pool was closed. But sometimes, the rules have a way of not applying to this man, inside the ring or out. The camera was sitting on the table, perhaps a bit too close for comfort. His face was barely illuminated, but what you could see of it was scarred. Some of it from the last match with Dusk, some of it from the beating he had taken at the hands of Amy Campbell not long after arriving. "Its time for you to get all amped up, and ride into the battle against the forces of darkness. Because that’s how you need to see the world, isn’t it? It’s the big tough man fighting for all that is good, against the lackey. The soulless asshole on the payroll. If Hollywood wrote this shit, I’d give you the edge, for sure. But unfortunately, you’re going to have to actually come into that ring, and beat me until I can’t get up anymore. Which I’m just not sure is all that likely. But I’m not going to sit here and debate who is going to win our match, because that’s fucking pointless. I want to talk about about something a little more personal." The Warlord took a sip of his margarita "Inside of that ring, you are a tough customer. You can take a beating as good as anyone I’ve come across. But outside of that ring, are you so tough? If you did not have a fantasy to cling to, if you could not make believe that you were a super hero to these fans…how tough would you really be? If you saw the world as I do, for how it truly is, would you have already laid down in the corner and given up? Would you have put a gun to your head and ended your waste of a life? One day maybe we’ll see. Hell, the fans already like Kennedy more than you and that guy is a DICK. An opportunistic self important cocksucker. Its circumstance that made him popular, not his character. Unlike you, he doesn’t even posture to have principles. Hell, if we ever get Rhine back Kensington might relegate you to being his driver." Because of the lack of light, it seemed like it was the shadow of Stevens that was laughing. Regardless, it was a cruel laugh. "I can see how ugly you are, Craig. Underneath it all. Even if no one else can. You lobby to Kensington that Alexandra should be spared, all the while she destroys lives at the same rate you are losing matches, and somehow that’s okay because you presume she wants to change. But all it took was her to give you a wrestling move…A WRESTLING MOVE, for you to flip out, and demand her head on a stick. You are a selfish brat with a crush, buddy. Just another hero with a hard-on for Alex. And that ain’t so original. That ain’t so new. So get over yourself, asshole. I was put here so people like you wouldn’t clog Desade’s schedule. But I got all night long for your dumb fucking ass. And I’m going to have fun too buddy. I’m going to teach you that in your black and white little world, sometimes the villain has more resolve. Sometimes the villain is just…well, tougher. Ever since I returned, you have called me nothing but a stepping stone. Nothing but an errand boy. And that’s fair. But after I take care of this, my net worth will be a little higher. So I got a new nickname for you. " Before turning the camera off, Stevens leaned in so close you could see the redness on his tongue from the margarita. It had a way of making him look savage. "Paycheck." He grinned like a wolf. Fade. * * * In the backseat of a taxi, Jeb and Miles were on their way to the airport. Cavanaugh was holding the contents of the manila folder up to the light. They appeared to be medical records. At the top was a name: Alanna Marshall. "This is kind of fucked," Jeb remarked. "Par for the course. This course, anyway," Miles said flatly. "How do you suppose he got like this in the first place?" Jeb asked. "Well usually people that care about nothing, they used to care about everything. He poured his heart out and…you know…" * * * Seventeen year old Lane Stevens, standing in the hallway of his high school. He watched as a group of kids walked past him, boys and girls having fun, goofing around. Lane’s lips started to move, but there were no words. "…it evaporated." He said nothing. * * * And sometimes, the prologue comes last. Days earlier, on the fourth floor of the McCullum building, Reginald Sharnell was starting to understand his fate. "You know, the first time you was in here…flashin’ that tall paper around, I thought to myself, this man is going to be the end of you Reggie. Just somethin’ about ya." Sharnell smiled sadly to himself, and took a drink of scotch. He was trying to hold it together, but there was a tear forming at the corner of his eye. Behind him the blinds were closed, and in front of him the door was locked. Mr. Hayden sat in the chair across the desk from him. He was dressed as he always was. A brown trench coat covered his black suit. He lit a cigarette, and a few moments later ashed on the desk. Standing over Mr. Hayden’s shoulder was Spencer, a balding middle aged man in a plaid shirt. Spencer’s eyes were wide, and as terrified of people as they were of him. "The fall of the Order will have many chapters, Reginald," Mr. Hayden started, "and rest assured you will be a footnote in the greatest story to never be told." Spencer reached for his pocket. Reginald Sharnell’s last thought was, I don’t know what that means.
