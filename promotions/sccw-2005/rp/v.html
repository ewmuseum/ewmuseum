Any similarities to any living persons or locations within said city are coincidental.* Live or Die : Showdown 1 The darkness provided the perfect cloak for the man monster as he sat on his high backed wooden stool. He liked to be able to hide in the shadows unless he absolutely had to, and usually felt quite comfortable there. The shadows, after all, were as black as his own heart, so it was only natural they would feel like a good spot. Yet on this night, something was not right. Something was off with his head, he could not seem to focus on anything. He couldn't seem to get his bearings, his head spinning rapidly and threatening to hurl him to the floor from his rickety wooden perch. Those black eyes that had terrified so many of his victims were locked on the only spot in the huge room that was lit, and that lit only by a single dim light bulb. Over in that corner was the cage where he kept his prey, a huge metal box, the original use of which was long forgotten. That contraption swan in Harrison O'Dell's watery vision, and he felt himself nearly falling again. It was, perhaps, the smell, which even he had to admit was getting to him. It was the foul odor of decaying flesh, (had he left somebody out there in the darkness? It had been so long since he had turned on the lights that he couldn't remember) blood, and human waste. It permiated his lair, and Harrison stood up on shaky legs, feeling vaguely sick. He needed air... he needed... his brain couldn't quite seem to put it into words. He swayed once more, his weak hands grasping feebly at the metal table beside him even as gravity finally caught up to him. The cement floor rushed up at him, and his body slammed down with bone numbing force on the harsh surface. The lower shelf of the table struck his head, and he felt blood immediately begin to run out of it, complete with a sickening crunch that told him his skull was none the worse for wear. His eyes rolled back in his head but he remained conscious, enough to curl up in a fetal position and begin to cry in long, drawn out wails that sounded much like an infant's cries. Across the room, Sarah La Vega heard the cries from inside of the box, and she smiled at his anguish. He's coming apart she thought to herself. 'Sarah, do you hear that?' Harrison's other prisoner, who had been listening at the locked door of the box intently, hissed. 'I think... I think he fell. Maybe we can figure out some way out of here!' A week or so, even three days, Sarah would have tried. She would have felt elated, but now, all she could do was stare at the rough metal wall in front of her in disinterested silence. She had been here much longer than the girl, and knew from experience that there was no escaping the box unless somebody opened it from the outside. 'Sarah? Did you hear that?' The girl was young, and if Sarah knew the monster's style, probably beautiful. But she was also naive. The girl, whose name was Emily (or Emma, she couldn't remember) was the third woman with whom Sarah had been locked in this box in the last two weeks. With each of the previous two, Sarah had schemed and planned and tried her best to come up with an escape plan. But, with every option and plan obsolete, she was the only one left until this morning when Emily (or Emma) arrived. She had sat in the dark and cried, the tears running down her naked body, while she heard the screams of her two cellmates as O'Dell went to work on them. She heard every moment of torment and pain, and she heard the moments of their deaths. And she had come to a single, inescapable truth. There. Was. No. Hope. Beyond a shadow of a doubt, Sarah knew that soon, both she and Emily would be dead. There could be no escape. 'I think you should forget about that, kid.' 'Forget it? How can you say that? Maybe we can...' 'Emily, I said forget it. Please.' She could hear the girl, in the darkness, moving. The soft scrape of her bare flesh on the metal surface was a sure sign that she was coming towards her, and Sarah groaned. The last thing she wanted was human contact, but there was no way of avoiding a roomate when locked in a ten foot by twelve foot cell. The sound continued, and Emily popped up in the tiny circle of light that came from the light above. The girl's face and body were caked with grime after only a day, and Sarah shuddered to think of what she must have looked like. Small rows of clean flesh were indicative of the tears the girl had shed earlier, where the tears had streamed down through the grime. 'We need to at least try, Sarah, if nothing else.' Sarah had to admire her spirit, the narrow minded way that she tried to figure out how to escape so she could continue on with her life. 'Please, help me.' She imagined Emily as a very beautiful and popular girl ont he outside. She could see it beneath the grime and the dirt and the terror that the girl wore on her heart shaped face. Every inch of her naked hourglass figure shook with chills of cold and fear. But the fact that she was beautiful did nothing to change the fact that she was a. here b. naked and helpless and c. going to die. In the words that Harrison himself had used, they were totally fucked. The girl's hand was on her shoulder now, trying to shake her into being the strong rock the girl needed to lean on. 'Please, Sarah.' 'Get the hell away from me!' The force of her shriek startled even Sarah, and Emily jerked away from her and out of the light. She felt sorry immediately, but her mouth was moving of its own accord, out of her control. 'Get this through your head, for the love of God! Do you see that door?' She stabbed a single ragged fingernail at the metal latch. 'That is a metal door. We have nothing in her we can break it with, or pry it open, and more importantly, it is locked. From the outside.' Emily was crying again, huge tears leaking from her blue eyes and running new paths through the grime. 'I am a doctor, Emily, a psychiatrist. I've made a career out of studying people like him. You know what he's doing right now? He's breaking down again, the little part of him that can control himself is weakening. I've seen and heard that bastard out there do this twice since I've came here, and you know what happened? Somebody died. Somebody that was in this cage with me.' The teen sobbed, a sound of despair even more terrible than the screams coming from Harrison outside. 'We. Cannot. Get out of here.' Her tone had lost its venom, but each word conveyed that she no longer cared. Sarah La Vega had dialed in the last part of her life. She simply wanted it to be over. 'I'm sorry, really, I am. But hope is just a waste of what little time we have left. So make your peace with God. Send a mental vibe to your famile. Pray. Whatever you need to do, Emily, but wrap your mind around the fact that we are both going to die, and one of us is going to die very soon, probably within the next few hours.' The sobs abruptly stopped, out there in the darkness. A scraping sound , as if Harrison was using a piece of furniture to pull himself up, sounded. Then, a grunt. Emily gasped and scrambled over to sit beside Sarah, trying desperately to get as far from the door as possible. For her part, Sarah was quiet and very calm as she looked into the young beauty's face. She said only two words. 'He's coming.' The lock slid back, and the door squeaked ominously as it opened. Neither of them could see him, but Sarah heard his weak, pathetic voice as it echoed in the dim confines of the box. 'Where is he, doc? Do I need to send him a message?' Sarah knew he was referring to Joaquin. 'We can't have any fun together unless he is here to watch. Why hasn't he come?' She didn't know the answer to his query, but in what had previously been the empty space in her heart, hope bloomed. Harrison wanted Joaquin Pierre here to watch her die. And, if he succeeded in getting Pierre to his lair, that would be very, very bad news for the monster. And very good news for Sarah. She had almost started to smile when his gaze shifted to Emily. 'But you, my pretty one, you and I can have some fun while the doc and I wait for the frog.' Emily sobbed, but that didn't stop him from starting towards her. 2 The news had broke, and it only served to make Joaquin's job more and more difficult. After all, how was he to save lives and end a monster when an entire city would be on the lookout for him? A man who, for the second time in his life, had been accused of the crimes of Harrison O'Dell, and though he was innocent until proven guilty, the verdict that already been passed in the minds of many. He needed only to look down at the newspapers in his lap to know the sad truth. Why, Frenchie, Why? Another Wrestling Monster? The Story of Joaquin Pierre. Wrestling Industry Rocked by Yet Another Killer Grappler The press was always so quick to jump on a murder story, especially one that featured a reasonably well known athlete. Joaquin cursed under his breath, and then ducked down on the seat of the stolen car as what looked to be a police car rolled by. No, a taxi. He allowed a short breath to bubble free of his chest, and knew that he would, for the moment at least, be able to continue on. But his movements would be very, very restricted now. And every moment wasted was another moment that Sarah could die in. Joaquin was quite sure that she still lived. There had been no word about her body being found, and he was quite sure that O'Dell would want to have him watch the killing. He knew that the monster's mind had deteriorated to the point that he cared about little more than making Pierre suffer, and the more innocent people he was able to kill along the way, so much the better. He reached out and turned the radio on with a soft click as the dial engaged. No clues would be forthcoming, but music may help to calm him down. 'You are listening to Jacque Marceau at 9 on 99.3 FM, where we talk about anything and everything. Today's top story just might be the news that broke out earlier today about a serial killer supposedly stalking our city.' Marceau's voice held that same annoying tone that Joaquin hated about all radio hosts. It sounded arrogant and slightly patronizing, and enraged him to no end. 'Only hours after announcing the news, Montreal police then said the only suspect they currently had was none other than well known French pro wrestler Joaquin Pierre.' Ah, yes, there it was. 'Now this guy has always played a good guy on TV, he's the guy always fighting the Christian Darkes and the Corvus's of the wrestling world. Just a few weeks ago, he beat perennial bad guy El Diablo to win the SCCW Gateway Title. What I'm getting at with this, is how in the hell did this guy fool us with his goodie two shoes act? We've seen it a million and a half times in the wrestling world. Guys play at being a good guy, play at being the big hero, and then turn around and shock us all when they show their true colors.' Joaquin felt the rage bubble up in his chest at that, though he knew the man's argument was valid. Many of his cohorts in the wrestling business had made plays at being the hero, then turned out to be drug addicts, or drunks, or, in the case of some, killers. But not he. He'd always tried to keep his character on TV as close as possible to himself in real life, and to have this total stranger categorize him like he knew him enraged him. 'I can't even begin to tell you how much it angers me. But what about you, Montreal? What do you think of this piece of...' The radio beeped in the sound of a rooster crowing to cover the word 'shit.' 'The phone lines are open now, and we are waiting for your calls.' Silence on the line for a long moment, before the first caller came on. 'You are on the air with Jacque Marceau, what's on your mind?' The caller was obviously a prepubescent teenager, from the crackling sound in his voice, yet Joaquin's heart swelled with pride at the words the kid spoke in his defence. 'I'm just calling to say that you and the police have this guy all wrong. I've watched him on the internet for years when he fought in Europe, and I've been a huge fan ever since he signed with GLOBAL and SCCW. You can tell just from watching him that this guy cares about others. Look at how he fights the baddest of the bad to try and keep them in line? He got publically fired and beaten down by the Nevermore for being a thorn in their side.' 'Keep in mind this is a fake sport we're talking about here, buddy.' 'I don't care! This is a good man you guys are slandering, and when his name is cleared, I hope you'll issue a full public apology, you son of a...' The sound of a click. 'Well, we're just gonna move on to our next caller, but before we do, who are you gonna believe, Montreal? An obviously homosexual fanboy, or the highly trained detectives of the Montreal police force? Go ahead, you are on the air with Jacque Marceau.' Joaquin wondered how much more of this he could handle listening to, just how much more of this he could tolerate. The kid, fanboy though he might be, had said one thing correctly. There would be an apology for this. When he did clear his name, this particular wrestler would be exacting his apology in person. 'Hello, Jacque.' The voice chilled Joaquin's blood. He sat bolt upright in the car's seat the moment Harrison's sick voice came over the air. He would know that voice anywhere, it was the voice that haunted his dreams. 'First off, I'd like to commend you on your commentary on this issue. Scum like this is a pure example of why...' Scum? Who was the scum? A man who had always prided himself on doing the right thing? Or a murdering beast who preyed on the innocent? 'I have only one thing to say. Joaquin Piere, if you are listening, I hope you have the good sense not to get caught. I hope you have the good sense to make your way as quickly as possible to 943 Landresse Street, because I'd love to be the guy that finds you.' He heard a chuckle from Marceau over the air, as if he thought the man was joking. 'I'm working security at this warehouse, and it'll just be you and me, Pierre. If you've got the balls to show up, I'd love to send you to the morgue.' The sharp click cut off the call, allowing Marceau to open his fat mouth once more. 'We do not advise approaching Pierre if you do see them, ladies and gentlemen. He is, after all, a trained martial artist, and said action may not be safe. If you see him, immediately call police...' The soft sound of the dial shutting off killed his voice, and that sound was in turn cut off by the roar of the car's engine. That roaring sound was quickly followed by the rush of spinning tires, the car shooting out onto the street in a spray of dirt and burned rubber. Joaquin Pierre was on the move, and he was going to war. 3 Harrison smiled, the sickly grin rushing across his face like a wave in a pisspot, as his old grandfather always said. He'd sent the message, and he could only hope that the frog was able to hear it. The warehouse he'd spoken of was actuall a block over from his lair, but that was all right. He could wait quite comfortably there, with the real security guard stripped of his apron in the closet and he sitting at the desk. Pierre had had plenty of time, had he heard the message... so if he was coming, it should be any time. As soon as Pierre arrived, they'd make the trip to his true lair, and then it would all be over. 'Come on, little froggie.' That only made him laugh, finnding himself all too clever for his own good. As soon as he was done wheezing out that hideous laugh, his eyes drifted back to the door. 'Any time you are ready.' Killing the froad had been his obsession for a long time now, nearly a year. It was time to start making some plans about what he would do, where he would go, once this was done. After all, once he had extracted his pound of flesh and put the icing on top of this particular cake, he would be free to go whereever. He'd rather enjoyed his time in Paris, when he'd iced the frogs family... maybe he'd go back there for a while. Or maybe he'd visit the old hideaway in the desert, where some of his victims still lay, buried beneath the sand. Or maybe... A sharp clang echoed from below. The side door. Harrison smiled, coming up to his full height of five foot nine inches. His pallid, toadlike face lit up with malice, like a flame suddenly sprung to life from nothingness. Where once no emotion had existed, his eyes now practically glowed. He watched, hidden, as Pierre inched his way across the room, holding that ever so ridiculous sword in one hand and Harrison's own nickel plated .38 revolver in the other. The frog looked tired, like he hadn't slept in days, judging by the dark circles under his eyes. He looked tentative, and afraid. And, the monster who stalked him liked to think, he looked like he was haunted by the bad memories of the last time they were in this little situation. That was a good thing, seeing as how he'd worked so hard to semi recreate that little party. Let's see... one person strangled with barbed wire, check. Poor little Emily was probably still bleeding out in his lair. He almost felt bad about that, but her life had been given to a good cause. Another person with their chest cavity opened wide... check. Janice Mallory was probably still whereever it was that the frog had put her. One person dead of a gunshot to the head. That would be Sarah, after Harrison has his fun and made his enemy watch. And finally, a nice stab would for the frog to finish it off. Yes, it would all be perfect. All he had to do was get him there. Slowly, as stealthily as he could move his pudgy frame, Harrison crept out around a few wrapped pallets that appeared ready for shipping. He wasn't sure of his next move, but he was driven to move. He had to move, or he'd be caught. The fire extinguisher. That shiny red cylinder looked beautiful to him as he hefted it in his hands. It had weight to it, but not so much that Harrison's pathetic arms couldn't swing it. The perfect way to subdue a man much larger and stronger than he, he thought with a smile. He eased around a corner and spotted the frog with his back turned, and charged. In only a moment, before he could react, Joaquin Pierre felt a blinding pain and white numbness as something metal collided with the back of his head. It dropped him to a knee, seeing stars and hearing a dull ringing in his ears, but still he tried to rise even as Harrison swung the extinguisher again. The second blow knocked him cold. When he came to, Joaquin found himself strapped to a table, thick leather straps holding down his arms and feet. Each strap was pulled so tight he could see purpling in his fingers, the circulation cut off. He was naked, he noted with a start,the sensitive parts of his anatomy covered with a towel. How had he been so stupid as to rush into the building? How had someone as pathetic as Harrison gotten the drop on him? The lights came on, showering the room in a burst of light that burned his eyes. He could see three tables, all gunmetal grey, like the one he lay on and all occupied. The thick leather straps held the wrists and hands of two women who were definately expired. On his left lay a decomposing form of what had once been a woman, her flayed and tortured flesh graying rapidly and smelling as foul as anything he'd ever smelled before. On his right was a young girl, tiny in death, but it was the cruel wire pulled tight into the soft flesh of her neck that caused Joaquin's breath to catch in his throat. The way the razor wire had pulled and ripped as it journeyed through her flesh had nearly decapitated her, and it reminded him of something else... the body of his son flashed before his eyes, dead in the same way. Gabriel... 'Are you having fun yet, French?' The weak, mewling voice called his attention to the foot of his bed, where Harrison O'Dell, a Cheshire cat grin on his joweled countenance, stood. 'I so hope you are. I think somebody's been waiting for you.' The door of the box came open beneath his stubby hand, and in only moments, she filled the tiny doorway. It was Sarah, but as Joaquin had never seen her. Naked, for starters, her supple flesh made less beautiful by the grime and dirt of too long in a disgusting place. Second was the look of despair on her face when she saw him tied down. He could read that look too well, it said that she had been counting on him. That he was the last hope. 'So...' O'Dell snarled as he grabbed a handful of her thick hair and dragged her over by Joaquin. 'This is a nice little reunion.' He slapped a handcuff on her wrist, securing the other end to the table. 'I think that, maybe...' His fist lashed out, rocking her head back and immediately sending blood gushing from her broken nose. 'We need to have some fun here. You guys are looking too...' Another blow, this time a backhand that snapped her head around. 'Serious.' Sarah slumped to the floor, falling on her side and not moving. Joaquin snarled and lunged at O'Dell, managing to strain the straps enough that they crackled and creaked under the pressure. But he would not, he knew, be able to break them, even one of them. Hope was lost, and this war, evidently, was as well. He didn't notice, nor did the beast, that Sarah's eyes were open, and full of hate. 'Let me go.' 'Why would I do that?' The beast's smile widened, forming more of a taught leer than anything. 'We both know you'd destroy me in a fight. So why, I ask you, would I give you that advantage?' From the table beside him, with the moulding cadaver, Harrison picked up a foot and a half stretch of polished steel. His own sword. His weapon. That which he had stabbed O'Dell in Paris. 'This is appropriate, I think.' Harrison ran the flat of the blade across Pierre's chest, the cold steel raising the hairs and raising goose bumps at the same time. 'You stabbed me and left me for dead with this... I think it's only fair that I return the favor.' Something glittered, just beyond her reach. It was a flash of nickel, knocked off the table by Harrison himself when he picked up the sword and forgotten in the haze of murderous desire the monster felt. She stretched, reached out with everything she had, and could only graze it with the tips of her fingers as Harrison raised the blade high. 'Au Revoir, mon ami.' He snarled, mocking Joaquin's quote from his wrestling career. 'I'm going to stab you, but you wont die right away. I'll have time to finish off Dr. La Vega here... and then finish with you.' Joaquin strained again, the force of his effort actually causing the heavy table to bounce, only a little. But it bounced in just the right direction. Her fingers wrapped around the gleaming handle as she pulled back the hammer, not even knowing if the gun was loaded. The sword made its descent just as she squeezed the trigger... Harrison's eyes seemed to widen in shock as the report, their black eyes filling up with, for the first time in a very long time, fear. But it existed only for a moment before the entire front of his head seemed to collapse under the impact of one .38 slug. A second followed, smashing his teeth. The sword slid from lifeless fingers, crashing to the floor only seconds before he did. 'Sarah.' She couldn't move, couldn't think. She'd just killed a man. The blade had nicked Joaquin's ribs, leaving only a shallow scratch to mark its passing. He was reaching out to her as well as he could, the look in his eyes desperate. She heard the gun clatter to the floor as she rushed forward to unstrap his arms. 'I'm sorry...' he started to say, but was cut off as she threw herself into his arms the moment the straps came undone. Not even caring that neither of them had a stitch of clothing on, not even caring that they were surrounded by death, she simply clung to him. She needed something strong to hold until she got her bearings again, and obviously he did too as he wrapped his strong arms around her. They both felt as though they'd just come out of a nightmare.
