A Universal Constant

Date: Various Wednesday, December 14, 1983 "What did you wish for, Jay?" The subject of the question stared intently at his chocolate cupcake and the five thin candles sticking out of it, but he didn’t get a chance to answer. "Now now, Andy." Mary-Ellen Sykes set a vanilla cupcake down in front of her other son. It too had five candles. "You know if you say it out loud then it won’t come true." Andrew Sykes’ pout was a thing of legend, and he wielded it with tremendous skill. Unfortunately it had no effect on his mother. Its effect on his twin brother Jared was a different story entirely. "I’ll tell you later." Jared thought he was whispering, but in reality he’d only made his voice more breathy. "Okay," Andy replied in the same tone. Mary-Ellen could barely contain herself, and when she started to giggle her boys followed suit. The boys had the same laugh. They made the same high-pitched squeak when they got excited. Their smiles even curled the same when they were up to no good. That was a look their mother knew all too well. "Blow out your candles and then you can open presents." She crouched low between them and put a hand to her belly. She’d only been pregnant a few months, but already her baby bump was showing. By next summer her two five-year old boys would have a new baby sister. "And try not to spit on this one, Jared." The chubbier boy tried to do as she asked, yet somehow managed to pepper his treat with spittle. He frowned when he realized what he’d done. "It’s okay," Andy said. "You can have some of mine." "Vanilla stinks." "Nuh unh." "Yeah huh." "Mom, tell Jared that vanilla doesn’t stink." Mary-Ellen smiled, and pushed back to her feet. "I’m sure they’re both delicious. Can you two promise not to make a mess while I go get presents?" The boys nodded enthusiastically and in stereo, and then proceeded to go about destroying their cupcakes. Andy started with the bottom of his so as to save the frosting for last. Jared ended up wearing more of his than he ate. "Mom?" Jared called out through a mouthful of cake. "When’s Daddy coming home?" "Yeah," Andy added, wiping some crumbs away from his mouth. Mary-Ellen sighed as she walked back into the room. She carried with her a large stack of gifts, each one wrapped colorfully and coded for each boy. GI Joe paper for Andrew. Star Wars for Jared. "Your dad had to work late again." She set the stack down in clearing on the coffee table. "He’ll be home soon though." While she’d never say it, Mary-Ellen couldn’t understand what was so damned important that her husband couldn’t leave work to spend the kids’ birthday with them. It wasn’t every day that they turned five. As it were he was spending more and more time at the office lately. She made up her mind to talk to him about that, especially with Christmas less than two weeks away. "Come on." She smiled as she cleared away their empty plates. "Let’s open presents." Mary-Ellen had just stepped into the kitchen when she heard the tiny voices from the living room. This time she was able to stifle her laugh so they wouldn’t overhear her. The boys still thought they were whispering. "What’d you wish for, Jay?" "You first." "I wished to be a wrestler on TV." "I wished to be Luke Skywalker." * * * * * * * * Tuesday, October 4, 1986 "Andy?" Jared stood on his tiptoes and gently tried to shake his brother awake. He was whispering, not wanting to be too loud for fear that his parents would know he was awake. They were yelling enough already. "Andy?" he asked again, this time shaking his brother a little harder. Andrew shifted restlessly beneath his blankets and pulled his red and yellow sheets up over his head. Andrew loved to sleep, but lately he seemed to be getting tired more often. It was becoming fairly common for Jared to find him asleep on the couch, or curled up on a beanbag. Jared pulled himself up higher on the bunk bed’s ladder and leaned over. He was only a few inches away from his brother’s ear when he tried to wake him next. "Andy. Wake up." He raised his voice just a little in the hopes it would get his brother’s attention. It seemed to do the trick. "What is it?" Andy groaned. He poked his head out from under the sheets and rubbed at his eyes with his hand. "They’re yelling again," Jared whispered. "I’m scared." Andy rolled onto his side and opened his eyes. "For how long?" "I don’t know, but they woke me up. I heard a bang, and then they were yelling." "It’ll be okay." Andy closed his eyes and adjusted his pillow, intent on falling back to sleep. Jared would have none of it. "But I’m scared," he whined, lightly shaking his brother again. Reluctantly, Andy pushed back his blankets and sat up. He sighed, and again rubbed at his eyes. Only four minutes separated him and his "younger" brother, but in the eyes of both Sykes children that made Andy the older brother, and as the older brother it was his responsibility to take care of Jared. "Let’s go see," Andy yawned. "But…" "I’ll protect you, Jay." It was a responsibility he took very seriously. "But…," Jared tried to protest again, but Andy was already at the edge of the bed. "But what if they hear us?" "We’ll be really quiet." Andy yawned again, and Jared had to move out of the way as his brother started to descend the ladder. "Like Snake Eyes and Storm Shadow." Jared tugged at his Star Wars pajamas and pouted. "But he’s a bad guy." "Nuh unh, he’s a ninja, and ninjas can’t be bad guys. It’s in the rules." Andy was on the floor now, slowly creeping his way towards the door. Jared took a step to follow. Reluctant as he was, Andy had invoked The Rules, and Jared knew there was no arguing The Rules. He wasn’t sure what they all were, or whether they were written somewhere, but whenever his big brother would bring them up Jared was compelled to agree. After all, Andy wouldn’t lie. Andy never lied. "…but you always do that! Always!" It was the first thing the boys heard when they opened the door and crept into the hall. They knew the voice, but not the tone. Kevin, their father, had never yelled at them like that. "It didn’t hurt anything, did it, Kevin?" their mother shot back. "That’s not the point!" Jared hung back, staying near the door so that he could dart into the bedroom at the first sign that they’d been spotted. Rather defiantly, Andy moved closer to the railing, sliding his feet across the floor so as not to attract unwanted attention. It was only when he reached the railing that he turned and gestured for his brother to follow. "C’mon," he mouthed, waving for Jared to join him. Jared vehemently shook his head no. "C’mon," he mouthed again, and the peer pressure was too much for the younger boy. Disappointing his brother was far worse than any punishment his parents could inflict. Jared slowly raised his left foot and gently set it down on the carpet. When the floorboards beneath started to squeak he quickly slid back to the door. "No." Andy’s whisper was almost inaudible. "Slide." "Okay," Jared whispered back. It took him a minute, but he eventually slid next to his brother. Both boys crouched low and peered through the posts of the railing, trying to get a good look at the drama unfolding downstairs. Jared sat Indian-style. His brother rested on his knees. "Why are they yelling?" Jared asked, his voice quivering. "I don’t know." "They always yell." "Yeah." The boys didn’t know why, but the fight had escalated. Their parents had been fighting a lot lately, but neither boy knew why. They never fought before, and parents weren’t supposed to fight. Surely that was written somewhere in Andrew’s copy of The Rules. "So that’s it?" they could hear their mother yell. "You’re just going to walk out?" They could hear their father grumble something unintelligible. They could see shadows moving across the living room. That’s when Jared started to cry. "Andy, I’m scared," he whimpered. Jared tried to hold back his sobs. He didn’t want to let them know he was listening, convinced that his parents finding them would only serve to upset them further. "Me too, Jay." Andy leaned close and put an arm around his brother. "And what about your children?!" Their mother was yelling louder now. "Are you just going to walk away from them?!" This time there were no grumblings from their father, but the shadows were getting closer. The two boys held their breath as he passed below them, storming to the front door. He said nothing as he pulled it open and slammed it behind him violently. The force of it shook the house. Down the hall they could hear Stefanie, their baby sister, start to cry. Jared pulled himself into a tight little ball, but he could feel his brother’s grip on him grow tighter. In that moment, however fleeting, he felt safe. Below them their mother stepped into view. "Shit," they could hear her say. For a moment they watched as she cradled her face in her hands to cry. "Mom?" Andy asked through the railing. "Oh, God," Mary-Ellen said to herself as she looked up. She dabbed at her eyes in an effort to wipe the tears away. "You didn’t hear that, did you?" Andy nodded an affirmative. Jared’s sobs were strong enough to shake him and his brother. Their mother sprinted up the stairs, dropped to one knee and threw hers arms around both children. "It’ll be okay, guys," she said, pulling them both in close. "You hear me? It’ll be okay. I promise." "I know mom," Andy said, finally allowing himself to cry. "I know." Jared knew it to be true. After all, Andy believed it, and Andy wouldn’t lie. Andy never lied. * * * * * * * * Thursday, October 22, 1992 Meetings between Mary-Ellen and her ex-husband Kevin had become far more frequent than either of them would have liked. Lately it seemed they were seeing as much of each other as they had in the last days of their marriage. Just as it was then there was conflict that needed a resolution, only now their thirteen-year old son Jared was the one causing it. In truth they’d have much preferred to scream at each other than him. "I’m sorry for having to bring you both in here again." Leonard Buckhalter adjusted his glasses as he looked across the desk at three very familiar faces. He’d worked in education for close to thirty years, and had been a principal at this school for ten of them. He’d met with a lot of parents in that time, but none as frequently as the former mister and misses Sykes. "It’s as if we’re getting to know each other so well, I’m thinking about inviting you to my barbeque next weekend." Leonard smiled pleasantly. He often tried to use humor, or what passed for humor in his case, to help try and defuse these situations. Unfortunately neither Kevin nor Mary-Ellen was in the mood to laugh. This was after all their third such meeting in the last month. "What did he do this time?" Kevin asked as he cast a glance over his left shoulder. Behind him, seated on the couch against the wall, Jared fidgeted uneasily. Leonard’s smile faded. He adjusted the cuffs of his suit, a light cream color in contrast to his deep ebony skin. "I’m afraid your son is still in the habit of starting fights with other students," he said, folding his hands and leaning forward on his desk. "We had another incident today. The teachers who witnessed the event tell me that your son was involved in an altercation with a boy named Jeff Murray, and from what I understand it got out of hand quickly." This time it was Mary-Ellen’s turn to cast a stern glance at her son. Jared didn’t return her gaze. Instead he let his eyes drift to the floor as he rubbed at his nose with the back of his hand. Even from where she sat his mother could see the scrapes and scratches that came as a result of fighting. The therapy was supposed to have prevented this. "Now I know it’s not uncommon for boys his age to do this sort of thing from time to time," Leonard continued. "But in the case of your son this is the third time in almost as many weeks, and the school year is only two months old. On our end there is only so much we can do. Detention is fine for a first offense, but at this point we’re looking at suspension at the very least. Anything beyond that and we’re looking at a possible expulsion." "Oh, Jared," his mother whispered to herself, quite ashamed of her son. Kevin said nothing. Stewing silently was something he was quite skilled at. It didn’t matter though; the color of his face and furrow of his brow was enough to clearly convey his frustration. There were times that Jared was convinced his father’s anger could be seen from orbit on a clear day. "At this point it’s important that we start to look for the cause of what is a very concerning issue." Leonard again adjusted his glasses, this time looking between both parents to the young boy on the couch. "It’s quite obvious that your son is very angry, but it’s also quite apparent that he doesn’t know how to deal with it. Is either of you aware of something in your son’s life that might have brought this on? Anything at all?" Both parents exchanged a worried glance. Jared felt the air in the room grow suddenly thicker. "Last year…" Kevin started to speak but finding the right words became almost impossible. "We, umm, we had a pretty tragic loss in the family last year." Mary-Ellen finished where Kevin could not. She had always been the stronger of the two. "Jared’s brother passed away. They were twins." Behind her, on the couch, Jared had pulled his knees to his chest. It didn’t matter where he was or who was around, mentions of his big brother always elicited the same reaction: tears. "I understand," Leonard said solemnly. "And I can see how that would be difficult. I’m sorry – truly sorry – for your loss." Mary-Ellen nodded. "He goes to therapy a few times a week to help try and get through it, and we’d thought it was helping, but…" Again she turned to look at her son. Angry as she might have been, watching him like this, cowering and small, it broke her heart. Kevin moved from his chair to the couch and put an arm around his son. For a moment the room was silent. Experience had taught Leonard Buckhalter that in situations like this it was best to let things settle on their own before trying to move forward. Only when he was reasonably sure that everyone had a chance to ground themselves did he continue. "If I may be so bold, perhaps what your son needs is an outlet. If nothing else it might at least give him a way to channel his anger into something more constructive." "What did you have in mind?" Kevin asked, looking up from his son. "Maybe try out for one of the sports teams? I can have a few words with the coach of whatever team he finds most appealing." Jared shifted uneasily under his father’s arm. He was never the athletic type. If given the choice he would park himself in front of the television with his Super Nintendo and play Mario Brothers until his fingers fell off. "Tell me, Jared, what you think about football?" "Too small," came the reply from the couch. "Okay. Hockey, perhaps?" "Can’t skate." "Hrm." Leonard leaned back in his chair and scratched his chin. He thought for a moment and presented a third option. "What about the wrestling team?" Wrestling. It was never Jared’s dream. It was Andy’s. "Okay." * * * * * * * * Saturday, April 18, 1998 "This is a joke. This has to be a joke." Kevin Sykes paced through his living room. One hand rested on his hip. The other was pressed to his forehead in exasperation. "Tell me you’re kidding, Jared." He pulled his hand away from his face and turned his attention to a nearby loveseat. His only son shrugged. "I’m pretty serious, dad." Jared had begun shifting a small stack of coaster that his father kept nearby on the glass coffee table. There were just enough to build a wall of three houses plus the roof. "This is the craziest thing I’ve ever heard." Kevin pressed both hands over his ears and shook his head. It was the same flair for the dramatic that he’d passed on to his children. Unfortunately, it wasn’t the only trait his kids had inherited. They were equally stubborn, perhaps even a bit more. Lately it seemed that conversations between the two Sykes men devolved into arguments all the quicker as a result of their shared pigheadedness. "It’s not though," Jared protested as he gently created a tiny door in his coaster house. "And you’re acting like I haven’t thought about this at all." "No. No." Kevin waved his arms as he spoke. "There’s no way this is happening. There’s no way I’m letting this happen." "Look, I hate to break it to you, dad, but there’s not really a whole lot you can do about it." Jared’s father cast him a baleful glare. "Seriously, you can’t." Jared looked up from his plaything just in time to see his father abruptly turn away. "I’ve been living on my own for six months now. I’ll be twenty this December. You’re kind of shit out of luck." "Language," Kevin chided. Jared rolled his eyes. "Whatever. There’s still nothing you can do about it." "And your mother? I’m sure she’s okay with this nonsense?" "She’s actually pretty okay with it, yeah." Jared grinned mischievously. He was about to take the gloves off. "They both are." "I don’t care what he thinks about this." "What?" Jared tried to play innocent. "I’m just saying that both mom and Brian are both okay with it. Don’t need to get grumpy." The speed of Kevin’s pacing quickened. Quite pleased with himself, Jared let his eyes fall back to his makeshift art project. He quickly set about trying to see about getting a structure with only two walls stable. "I can’t believe you’re going to waste your potential like this," Kevin said at last, moving into the living room proper and taking a seat near his son. "You’d have almost graduated college by now!" "Sorry for wanting to take my life in a different direction, I guess. Besides, it’s not like there’s some law that says I had to go to college right out of high school. I’ve got my whole life for that." "But you don’t have your whole life for this?" "No. This isn’t exactly something dudes start doing at thirty or forty, dad. I’ve been working two or three jobs at a time over the last year just to save up for this. This is the first day off I’ve had in three months. Plus, it’s already paid for." The coaster house fell, unable to be properly supported by only two walls. "And what’s all this about ‘potential’?" "I always though you were smarter than this." Jared paused briefly. "Thanks, I think?" "No, it’s just that you always did so well in school…" "You mean when I wasn’t getting in fights every day?" Jared interrupted. This also drew a hard glance from his father. "You know what I mean, and don’t give me that attitude." "Sorry, didn’t know you had the exclusive rights." "But you were always so talented. The pictures that you drew? I figured that you’d have been an artist, or an architect." Jared again rolled his eyes as he neatly stacked the coasters back up and pushed them away. "I don’t think you put ‘look at what I drew in therapy’ pictures in your portfolio, dad. I’m pretty sure most places frown on hiring crazy people." Kevin pushed himself up out of his seat and resumed his pacing. The argument was becoming pointless. Invariably, it would continue until one of them gave up and stormed out. That’s the way it always worked. "I can’t support this decision." Kevin had meandered over to the sliding glass doors that overlooked his backyard. "I can’t get behind the idea of you being a professional wrestler." "I didn’t come here looking for your support. I just figured you’d want to know." Jared rose to his feet and stepped closer to the door. He wasn’t giving up, but he was leaving. "Been a real treat as always, dad." "Can I ask you why?" It was Kevin’s last desperate cry for a different resolution. "’Cause Andy would have loved it." * * * * * * * * Friday, December 11, 2009 "I think I fucked up." The ground felt hard underneath him. Recent days had seen a drop in temperature, and while there were still small patches of snow dotting the grass, the area where he sat was dry. Above him the sky was cloudless, though the air held the scent of snow. It was a cold day, but the temperature didn’t bother him. In fact, Jared preferred it that way. If there was a flaw to be found in the weather it was the wind. "I was supposed to watch out for him, you know? Like, I promised him when we were younger that I’d always have his back." A leaf landed in his lap. Jared picked it up between his thumb and forefinger and cast it aside. "We got into an argument, a pretty bad one actually, and it all kinda fell apart." Jared shifted his position and folded his legs Indian-style. Mindlessly he picked at a patch of grass nearby. "It was all my fault, too. He was right, but I didn’t wanna listen. Now he’s gone and I can’t ever apologize for it. He… He hated me when he… Fuck, I can’t even say it." He let his eyes fall to the ground as he threw a few blades of grass across his chest. The wind picked up again, catching a large group of leaves and blowing them against the cold granite stone before him. Jared pushed himself forward and onto one knee. Hurriedly he reached out his arm and brushed the leaves away, taking a moment to let his eyes linger on his big brother’s headstone. Andrew D. Sykes December 1978 – April 1991 His was a man’s courage Content with his work in cleaning the leaves away, Jared fell back to his seat on the ground. "I was supposed to be there that day. We always went together. I mean, yeah, I sorta drove the guys who worked there nuts, but they always let me play, you know? We didn’t get to go last year because I fucked up and let him get hurt over nothing. I’d planned on going this year, but then we had that fight and…" He shook his head. "But if I’d have been there, maybe I could have… Maybe I could have done something, or stopped it. I dunno. Maybe it would have been me instead, and then he’d still be around and I wouldn’t have to deal with this. And I can’t deal with it. Every time I think about it I just wanna curl up and die. It got so bad, I saw it on the news so often… I kinda lost control. I figured all the anxiety meds I had would still work, you know? Just didn’t think to not drink with ‘em. Really fucked me up. Even ended up in the hospital." Instinctively his hand went back to the same patch of grass he’d only moments before had been picking at. "I haven’t told anybody. Only one who knows is Gabe, but that’s just ‘cause he found me. Fuck, I might have been dead if it wasn’t for him." He found the thought of having been discovered by Gabriel Simon, someone else he was fairly sure only tolerated him out of a sense of obligation, to be mildly amusing. He didn’t show it. Instead he mindlessly uprooted the last of the grass in that patch, leaving only a spot of dirt behind. "I didn’t go to the funeral. His dad and I, we, well, he never liked me. He always thought I was a bad influence, and he might have been right. Merv’, he… he said he put his life on hold because of me, so I guess his dad was right. I didn’t think they’d want me there." Jared sighed. He could feel the tears starting to well behind his eyes. It was only a matter of time before they’d try to break free. "I didn’t go because I didn’t want it to be real. I haven’t even visited where he was… Yeah. I need this to be some horrible fuckin’ nightmare, because I can’t handle it. I really can’t. It’s like you leaving all over again, and I’m afraid of what that’s going to do to me. It wasn’t good then, Andy. It really wasn’t. You’d have been so disappointed." His eyes drifted to the tops of some nearby trees and watched as icicles formed by a snowstorm earlier in the week began to melt. Jared cleared his throat. The longer he spoke the more he had to think. The more he thought the harder it was to speak. "And I’m sorry I had to come early this year. Company planned an event and I have to be there, so I won’t be around on Monday. It sucks so bad." He paused, trying to clear the lump that had formed in his throat. "The kid I have to fight – Thad, he kinda reminds me of you, and a few months ago there was this convention, and we met a kid who was sick, and… And I fucked that up, too." Geoff Miller. That was his name. Jared had been able to get him the audience with Spacely that the young boy desired, and the group of them had taken a picture together. A copy of that photo hung in the nerd haven that Jared lovingly referred to as his "office". He lifted a plain white box from the ground next to him and broke the red string that held it shut. From inside he produced a pair of cupcakes, one vanilla and one chocolate. "I brought the usual." Jared slid a thin candle into each cupcake. He lit the vanilla cupcake first and gently set it down in its traditional spot in front of his brother’s headstone. He lit his own candle carefully so as to prevent the wind from blowing it out. He cupped his hand around it and crouched low near his brother’s grave. "Happy birthday, Andy," he said, the tears now flowing freely. "I wish you were still here."
