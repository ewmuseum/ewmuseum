Part Seventeen: The Camera Doesn't Lie

26 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/26/09 Kerry Sandhurst paced in a tight, controlled manner around his suite at the Bellagio, his leather wingtips softly scuffing the carpeting beneath his feet. His fingers drummed tunelessly against the legs of his pants, keeping an irregular tempo. Everything about Kerry had been carefully calculated to give the impression of a young up and comer: carefully styled hair, slightly too flashy clothing, a watch that was meant to subtly impress by virtue of just how bloody expensive it was. It would have been nice, thought Kerry, if he’d actually owned any of it. It was certainly an image he would have liked to claim as legitimate. A hustler, a mover and shaker, someone who got things done. But when you’re not even two weeks out of NYU film school, he thought ruefully, it was a major success just to know where your next meal would come from. His parents had been cool, helping him out as much as possible while he was at film school. But the understanding between Kerry and his parents had been clear: once he graduated, he was on his own. He’d spent most of his time at school not thinking about it. It had been fairly easy to do so as the mechanics and logistics of shooting student films consumed an enormous amount of his time and energy (not to mention his finances). It had been just two days before graduation that the realization had finally hit him: he was soon to be adrift, alone, no safety net to rely on…and he had no idea what to do next. He’d applied at every studio going, launched himself at every internship he could find. But with the economy in dire straits, Kerry had quickly realized that the folks in Hollywood were far more concerned with keeping their own jobs instead of letting anyone new in. If he’d needed any more convincing, he thought wearily as he rubbed the bridge of his nose, the sheer number of doors closed in his face would leave no doubt. So it had been with no small amount of relief that he had taken the call from his mysterious benefactor, the strange voice on the other end of the line that had led him to Vegas at this moment in time. The idea, when it had been pitched to him, had two huge marks in its favor: it was blissfully simple and it was not his. With the recent success of the film "The Wrestler", the voice had told him, there had been an upsurge of interest in the day to day realities of professional wrestling. It just so happened, the voice had mentioned, that she knew some people who worked for one of the…Kerry remembered that the word the voice had used had been "colorful"…wrestling organizations out there, one based out in Vegas. If Kerry would just contact the man in charge, the voice had said, he would be able to get the makings of a fascinating documentary, a real life "Wrestler". Despite his benefactor’s no-nonsense tone, Kerry had still been surprised when he’d received a rather cheerfully written letter from a Doctor Nigel Kensington; informing him that, while he could guarantee Kerry nothing, he’d be happy to pass along his employee’s professional contact information . He’d been outright shocked when he’d gotten letters back from several of the wrestlers informing him that, certainly, they wouldn’t mind participating in his project one bit. The first letter back had been from the gentleman he was now waiting for, in fact. Even with this advanced warning, the solid thump at the door of his suite nearly caused his heart to leap out of his chest and land on the floor. Relax, Kerry thought to himself as he made his way to the door, there’s nothing to worry about. You’re a professional. You can do this. The sight of the gentleman standing before him as he opened the door did little to help Kerry’s nerves. It wasn’t that Lance Marshall was being particularly threatening or unpleasant. Hell, rocking a button down shirt and dress slacks as he was, he could easily have mistaken Lance for someone heading to a job interview. But there was just something about standing in front of someone who resembled a comic book illustrator’s wet dream that was…well, Kerry wasn’t quite sure what the proper terminology was but he was guessing it was somewhere between "intimidating" and "pants wettingly terrifying". The first thing that Lance thought as he studied the kid (and he most certainly was that) standing before him was that he was trying way too hard. Lance had seen his share of documentaries and one thing he’d noticed about most documentary filmmakers (when they’d actually been on camera) is that their personal style tended to veer towards the casual. This kid, with his too new suit and his too flashy watch…yup, trying way too hard. The little voice of suspicion in the back of Lance’s head urged him to turn around, to head in the other direction, to run. That this was a trap, a set-up, that any minute now Desade or Philip Kennedy or Aimz or one of the many other folks who wanted his head on a platter would round the corner and attack. Lance ignored that voice. Instead, he extended his hand towards Kerry’s. "Lance Marshall. Pleasure to meet you." Kerry snapped to attention, as if out of a trance. "Hello! I’m Kerry Sandhurst. Please, have a seat." As Lance made his way across the room and lowered himself into a chair, Kerry paused briefly to turn on the camera he had stationed a short distance away. Once he spotted the familiar red glow, he made his way to a chair about ten feet away from where Lance was sitting. While he feared he might sink into the seat, Kerry seemed to notice that the seat was just about accommodating Lance. "Are you comfortable?" "I’m fine," Lance smiled. "They just don’t tend to make much for guys my size." "I can imagine. How big is that, by the way?" "Six feet two, about three twenty. And yes, it’s all natural." "It’s not as if steroids are unknown in your business. After all, as "The Wrestler" showed…" Lance smiled. "Great movie. I think Mickey Rourke did a fantastic job in it." "Some would argue that he was robbed on Oscar night." A slight shake of the head from Lance. "I wouldn’t say that. I’ve seen both films, I think they were both deserving." "But you’re deflecting. You can’t honestly say that steroids have no presence in professional wrestling, can you?" Lance’s features hardened, just slightly. "I never said the sport was completely drug free. But I can only speak for myself and my organization. SCCW has a very rigorous drug testing policy. I’m tested regularly and I have passed every time." "There are ways to get around that," Kerry tutted. "If we’re working from a starting point of inherent suspicion, so be it. I can only speak for myself and public record will back me up. Hell," Lance smiles, "I’m actually the smallest among my brothers." "You’ve got to be kidding." "Nope. My brothers range from six four to six nine." "How many brothers do you have?" "Four. I’m the youngest." "Five big, strapping kids…I imagine your mom must have had her hands full." "Oh, we knew who was in charge," Lance smiled. "We rough housed, played around…but at the end of the day, what Mom said went. And if any of us were feeling our oats that day…well, let’s just say that Pop didn’t take any guff." "Was he ever physical with you?" "No," Lance said with a firmness that brooked no argument. "Wasn’t his way. Besides, he never had to be. You survive twenty plus years in the service, five boys aren’t going to be much of a problem." "Your father was a military man?" "Army," Lance nodded. "Did any of you follow in his footsteps?" "No. I almost did but…well, this ended up happening." "And he was okay with that." Lance’s eyes locked straight on to Kerry’s, never wavering. "He just wanted us to be good, solid, productive members of society. Didn’t matter to him if we were president or working in a factory, so long as we were doing right." "And by doing right," Kerry asked, "you mean…?" "Taking care of family. Working hard. Being kind to others. " "How very Norman Rockwell." Lance’s left eyebrow arches at this remark. "What can I say?" "Moving on a bit…how did you end up getting into wrestling?" "Pretty standard story, really. I was approached at a show by a talent scout who tried to get me to sign up with his organization. Told me I had the right build for it, would make a great presence inside the ring. Went for the real hard sell, you know?" "Did you bite?" "Not at first," Lance admitted. "It just wasn’t what I pictured myself doing. But I did a little research, looked into the sport…and the more I looked, the more it seemed like it might be an interesting road to take. So, I signed up for a wrestling school. Did pretty well, there. Signed on with my first organization shortly after graduating and worked my way up the ranks." "Was the "superheroic" persona you ended up adapting your decision or one your employers made for you?" Lance lowered his head slightly, letting out a sigh. When he raised his head again, his eyes looked slightly weary. "I have never positioned myself as a superhero. I’ve played around with that kind of imagery, sure, but usually because I knew it would get a reaction out of people. But I have never claimed to be perfect or above reproach." "So, the whole Incredible Hulk angle that your current entrance revolves around…?" Lance smiled. "David Banner is a regular guy. The Hulk is a force of nature. It’s just a shorthanded way of demonstrating that there are two sides of me: the Lance Marshall you see inside the ring and the Lance Marshall you see outside of it." "What would you say is the biggest difference between the two Lance Marshalls?" "I like to think that the Lance Marshall outside of the ring is a good guy. The Lance Marshall inside the ring…well, he can be pretty fucking scary." "Have you ever done serious damage to an opponent?" Lance paused, briefly. "Define "serious damage"." "Beyond what would be considered normal injuries in professional wrestling." Another pause. "There have been moments." "Deliberate moments?" "Yeah." "Why?" "Usually because they threatened my wife or my family," Lance answered slowly. "I have my buttons just like everybody else…and I make no secret of the fact that it’s a very bad idea to push them. What surprises me is how many people think I’m joking around or exaggerating when I say this." "Family is important to you?" "Family is everything to me," answered Lance, placing particular emphasis on the word everything. "This," he added, waving his arm around and gesturing at the suite, "this will all go away one day. When I can’t do this anymore, when I am too old or too hurt for this sport, my family will still be there. Without them, I am nothing." "And you’re married?" "Over ten years now." "Did you meet your wife through the business?" Lance shook his head. "On vacation. We met, she made the first move, the rest is history." "So you like a woman to make the first move?" Lance smiled. "I love it when a woman makes the first move. I need someone strong, someone who’s not afraid to smack me upside the head when I’m being an idiot. My wife does that and I love her for it." "Sounds like she’d have to be pretty strong herself to stand up to someone like you." Lance nodded vigorously. "She is. She’s stronger than me in some ways, if I’m honest." "And how does she feel about your darker side?" "She’s been there for me through some of my darkest moments. Moments where I swear somebody else would have just given up, left me alone to destroy myself. And she’s just stood by me, never wavering. I’m not sure I deserve her sometimes." "Are there any examples you can share with me?" Lance paused again, taking a moment to collect his thoughts. When he resumed speaking, it was the closest his calm demeanor would come to cracking. "I make no secret of the fact that I’m an alcoholic. Some years back, I developed a drinking problem. I turned into someone pretty damned ugly. And I wouldn’t have blamed her if she’d just walked right out the door." "But she didn’t." "No. She got me into rehab, took care of me when I couldn’t take care of myself…stood by me through everything. I owe her more than I can ever repay." "Are you drinking now?" Lance shook his head firmly. "No, I’ve been stone cold sober for about…I think it’s about seven years now." "So you’re recovered." Lance shook his head again. "One of the big ones at AA is that there’s no such thing as a recovered alcoholic. You’re always recovering because you know that one little slip, one moment where you let your focus drop…you could be right back at the bottom again." "I wouldn’t have guessed this of you." "Not many people do." "What else about you do you think people would be surprised to learn?" Lance sighed. "That I’m not some cave-dweller whose knuckles scrape the ground when he walks. That I actually have a college degree. Hell, that I actually have a brain comes as a hell of a shock for some people." "People actually think of you as stupid?" Lance nodded. "Usually people who have no business talking themselves. At worst, they think I’m sort of Neanderthal who needs help putting together a complete sentence. At best, they think I’m Ray Bolger in the Wizard of Oz." Kerry glanced at Lance, a look of confusion on his face. "Hmm?" "You know," Lance smiled, "If I Only Had A Brain…" Despite himself, Kerry couldn’t keep from chuckling. "Rest assured, I won’t be doing that." He glanced quickly at his watch, jumping suddenly out of his chair and almost running towards the camera. Once the familiar red glow had dimmed, he turned back to Lance. "That’s all I’m gonna have time for now, really. I’ve got a few other folks I’m speaking with today and I’m running a bit short on time." Lance stood up, extending his hand to Kerry’s. "That’s fine. You’ll call me if you want to talk some more?" Kerry shook Lance’s hand vigorously. "Definitely. It’s been a real pleasure talking to you." If Lance had lingered behind at the door after leaving Kerry’s suite, he might have heard Kerry exhale a sigh of relief as Lance left. Kerry made his way to a side table where his cell phone sat charging. Unplugging it from the charger quickly, he flipped it open and move quickly through the menus till he reached his contacts list. There, right at the top of the list. The entry marked with a star… "Hello?" "Yes, he was here. He just left, in fact. No, I don’t think he had any idea." "He was actually pretty forthcoming. I don’t how much of this you already know…" "Of course. I’ll get a copy of the footage to you as soon as possible, Ms….sorry, Director." We now return you to your regularly scheduled lives, already in progress.
