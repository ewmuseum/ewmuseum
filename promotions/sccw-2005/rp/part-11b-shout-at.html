Part 11b: Shout At

Date: Today 'I saw a demon stare into my face And an angel touch my breast Each one softly called my name The demon scared me less; The demon scared me less.' Darkness. Ow. Ow. Ow. Oh shit, that's a headache. That is a motherfucker of a headache. Then, voices. 'Is he ready, Brother Pablo?' 'Creo que él ahora está despertando, Amo.' What? Piercing lights made their way through cracked eyelids. 'Aah!' 'Ah. You're awake. Good.' The speaker--whoever it was--was trying to make his voice sound calm and soothing. He failed spectacularly. 'WHAT THE CRAP IS GOING ON?!' 'You must be very thirsty. Here.' 'I'm not thirsty! I'm wanting to get the fugggggblubublublubaabhharrbhgh!' The sudden sensation of a sugary liquid pouring into his mouth sent the young man's eyes open wide. It took a few moments for things to become clear, but once they did...he really, really wished they hadn't. He was strapped to a bed, his arms pinned to his sides. He still wore the black cargo pants and AFI shirt he'd had on when they took him, although the shirt was now soaked with sweat and what tasted like a fruit-flavored drink. There were a few man-shaped figures huddled around him, and slowly they came into focus as well. Two of them were very familiar, as he'd had several dark matches and house show bouts with them in their time in SCCW. The larger one was Mac Deesul, known until recently as 'BIG TRUCK.' The other...he couldn't be sure without the mask, but from the size he could guess it was En Fuego. They were decent enough guys, if not exactly riveting conversation. But that was before they were pressed into service with the Decency Crusade. After that, they got all...weird. They never left the Crusade's dressing area except for matches, or on orders. They never spoke with anyone, apart from their master. And that means... Standing at the foot of the bed was a diminutive man with a demonic smile. White shirt and black tie, mostly covered by a long white robe. Black hair, styled perfectly into a pompadour. Bandages over his nose, courtesy of Edward Lambert at Cataclysm. The eyes...oh God, the eyes. 'The False Prophet,' Wyatt Connors. He's seen Wyatt Connors plenty of times, but only on TV or the backstage monitors. He never really understood what the big deal was--it was just this little guy, smaller than most women in the company. Wasn't even a very good wrestler. There really didn't seem to be anything special at all. Now that he was face to face with the man, he understood. This wasn't like Amy Campbell's destructive rampages, or King Blueberry's shenanigans, or Drusilla's...whatever the hell was going on in her head lately. He'd seen all kinds of crazy in his time with SCCW, but nothing like--no. No, that wasn't true. Savant. He had seen it before, the few times he had met Lauren Fox. A madness so severe that, were it ever projected inward, would destroy the person completely...but somehow, it was always directed toward everyone else. Insanity with an iron will. Belief. 'I hope you've had a good rest,' Connors spoke. His voice was too loud to be a whisper, but it still sounded like one. 'We've got a long day ahead of us, but of course your compliance will be requested. It makes things so much easier when the soul is wiling to be saved.' More liquid was poured into his mouth--yes, that was Kool-Aid. He managed to close his throat before it spilled to his stomach, although a few drops leaked through. Glaring fiercely at Wyatt, he spit it back out at his captor. Unfortunately, he was unable to get the proper projection, and the contents landed on his pants, embarrassingly close to his groin. 'Fuck you, Jesus freak,' he hissed. Connors smiled pleasantly, which was not pleasant at all. 'I had hoped you wouldn't fight. It's so much simpler when you don't fight.' His eyes widened in horror as he watched Wyatt's lips stretch out farther than they should be able to, forming an insane grin. 'But it is so much more gratifying when you do.' Panic squeezed the young man's heart as he futilely struggled with his bonds. He could feel Wyatt's glare, like it was burning through to his very soul. Measuring. Judging. Jimmy 'Misfit' Dawes had never felt so alone. # # # 'If I turn toward the light Its beauty cannot last; For I think that it must be destroyed When I see how we contrast.' Six hours, and no progress. Wyatt was determined not to let the frustration show on his face, just as James was determined not to break. 'I don't understand why you're behaving like this, James,' he said, more like a high school principal than a religious maniac. 'I offer you everlasting life at His side! A relief from the weights and worries of the world--not to mention rescue from an eternity of torment! Why do you spurn these gifts?' Jimmy's eye--the one that hadn't been nearly swollen shut as the result of repeated abuse--sparked with defiance. 'Because you're a fucking whackjob trying to sell me on a God that doesn't exist.' Up until now, comments like that had earned Jimmy an immediate smack in the face. This time, it only got a sigh from the False Prophet. 'James. I'm trying to have a reasonable conversion here--' 'Unstrap me from this bed, and I'll show you 'reasonable!'' '--and I want this to be as quick and painless for all of us as possible. But I can't do that if you insist on poisoning the air around us with your filthy blasphemy.' Wyatt's face started to turn red at the end of his sentence, but he managed to maintain control. Instead, he nodded to his underlings, who approached the bed. 'Oh, no. Not again. You keep your damn hands off muhhhhhfuuuhfuuhfuh...' Jimmy screamed, as Macarthur Deesul pried his mouth open with a big, beefy hand. While Jimmy squirmed, Pablo approached with a plastic cup full of Kool-Aid and poured it into Jimmy's mouth. As soon as the cup was empty, Deesul clasped his hand over Jimmy's mouth, while Pablo pinched the boy's nose. Unable to breathe, Jimmy was forced to swallow the tainted drink. A feeling of peace quivered in his belly. The liquid refreshment was welcome--you could wear yourself out pretty quick in a screaming match with Wyatt Connors. Not only did it cool his parched throat, but it also relaxed him. Maybe all this yelling wasn't necessary. Maybe he could just-- No. That's how he gets you. Fight it! Fight! As Pablo and Macarthur returned to their stations, Wyatt took center stage again. 'I hope you're willing to be more sensible now.' 'Screw you.' 'That's better. I'm trying to give you a better life, James. One where you are not beset on all sides by demons. One where you will be steered away from the temptations and degradations that Satan brought to this world. Free from sin. Free from strife.' 'Free from thinking, too!' Jimmy snapped. 'Look at your pet monkeys here! They never used to be like this!' Wyatt frowned, hoping he could have further expounded on his point before being interrupted. 'Of course not. I...fixed them.' 'Fixed?! They're more fucked up than ever! You can't just take someone's brain out and call it Jesus! That's not how it works!' 'You poor, deluded fool. The brain is just a mushy lump of matter. I repaired their souls. Their broken, twisted little souls. Now, look at them! I have given them bliss, by rescuing their souls.' Jimmy did look, and the fact that he'd been staring at it all day didn't make it any less unnerving. Macarthur and Pablo stood off to the side, smiling vacantly and staring at something that wasn't there. 'No, you didn't! You gave them bliss by making them stupid! I thought Jesus was supposed to be about free will, not no will at all!' Connors was quickly losing control of his anger, and his upper lip curled into a snarl. 'What would you know about Jesus, sinner?' 'I used to be Catholic.' 'Hmmph,' Wyatt muttered. No wonder this was so difficult. Catholics--especially lapsed Catholics--tended to be notoriously stubborn about religion. 'Yeah. Went to church for a while--just long enough to figure out it was a crock of shit. Y'know, like you.' 'Curb your tongue, James.' Jimmy smirked, knowing that he had the upper hand--at least, until Mac Deesul hit him or made him drink again. 'Or what? You're going to cut it out to spite my face? You're a bigger fraud than the priest I had when I was a kid. At least he never tied me down to a bed. What are you going to do next, touch me in the bad place?' Wyatt gritted his teeth and balled his fists. 'Be. Quiet.' 'I bet you want to, you sicko! It'll just be a little secret between you, me, and God, right? What's the matter, am I a little old for you? You goddamn psychopathic--' And that's when Wyatt lost it completely. 'DO NOT BLASPHEME!' he shrieked, before leaping toward his restrained target. Jimmy had enough time to think, He sounds just like a Dalek when he says that, before a stinging right hand caught him across the temple. Connors screamed that same phrase over and over, with each syllable punctuated by another backhand across Jimmy's face. Blood spattered onto the bedsheet beside Jimmy's head, and he felt one of his teeth get knocked loose. The first thing he thought was, Ha. I'm winning. That thought did not last long, as one look into his assailant's face told him what a cold comfort it was to be right. He could almost see a dribble of foam in the corner of Wyatt's mouth. Jimmy's next thought was, Oh shit. He's going to kill me. 'DO!' Smack. 'NOT!' Smack. 'BLAS--' Jimmy winced in anticipation of the blow, but it never came. He opened his eye--as far as it would open, anyway, and saw that Wyatt had frozen in place, with his arm reared back. The rush of anger in his face quickly faded away, replaced with a blank stare. 'Of course,' he whispered. 'Yes, I should have thought of that before.' He clambered down from the bed and walked back to the wall where he had stood before. He spoke in hushed tones, low enough so that Jimmy couldn't make it out even if his ears weren't ringing. There were these strange pauses in Wyatt's speech, though, as if he was having a conversation. Only, nobody was talking back to him...at least, nobody he could see. When Wyatt turned around, he was all smiles. 'They're not coming for you, you know.' 'What?' 'Brother Mayhem. Miss Devonshire. They've abandoned you.' 'They'd never--' '--do that, yes, yes. But they have. James, if they truly cared about your well-being, would they have not burst through the door by now? Brother Mayhem's talent for destruction is legendary. I have no doubt that if he wished, he would burst through the gates of the compound, utterly demolish any man foolish enough to stand against him, tear the door to this room off its hinges, and haul you away and back to your life of sin before you could say 'Jack Robinson.' And yet, here we are. And where is he?' Wyatt looked around, as if he expected Mayhem to crash through the wall at any moment. 'Huh. Must be caught in traffic.' 'That's not fair--' Jimmy started, but Wyatt had no time for argument. 'They're not coming, James. Do you know why?' Jimmy screwed his eyes shut, and wished he could do the same to his ears. 'You can't hide from the truth, my son. They are not here, because they do not care about you.' 'No! No, that's not true! They're my friends!' 'Is that so?' Wyatt's smile became even more sickening. 'Then why aren't they here to take you away? Here's a better question: why didn't they prevent my Crusade from rescuing you at Cataclysm. Surely, if they thought so highly of you, they would have made a misguided attempt at thwarting our holy mission. Yet when we found you, they were nowhere in sight. Why is that?' 'They were busy! Mayhem had that big match to get ready for!' 'Mm-hmm. And Drusilla?' 'She's always in meetings with Desade--' Wyatt's eye twitched at the mention of her name. '--but she's looking out for Mayhem and me.' 'I'm sure she is. After all, your own career has taken off like a shot since she assumed control, hasn't it?' 'Well, no, but--' 'No? But she was looking out for you? Well, surely Mayhem himself has reached the top of the mountain? It's been nothing but title shots and whores lately, I imagine.' 'Not really--' 'Oh. So what exactly is it that Miss Devonshire does for your little band, then? Because as far as I've heard, she's spent her time getting closer to...Her...for her own sake.' Jimmy's face went flush with anger. 'Don't you talk about her like that!' 'Struck a nerve, have I?' Wyatt asked, blatantly mocking his captive. 'You think she loves you, just because you desire her body? Love does not come from the penis, James. The only things that come from the penis are urine and wickedness.' 'YOU SHUT UP ABOUT--' 'No, James. I will not. I am giving you the Lord's truth right now. And that truth is that none of these people would even notice whether you lived or died.' Jimmy's bottom lip quavered as he desperately tried to control his emotions. 'Yes they would! You're a liar! A goddamn liar!' 'Oh? Well, if I'm a liar, then why didn't they halt our rescue? Why haven't they come here to get you back. Why haven't they talked to the police?' 'You don't know that--' 'I do know, James. I know that they haven't done anything about your disappearance, because they have not noticed. They have not noticed, because they do not care. For the love of God, do you remember what you were doing when we found you? You were getting their coffee! You went from being a valued member of the team to a worthless burden, suited only for menial tasks! They found a better, more powerful group of allies in the Dead Man's Hand...and when you've got people who can actually do things for you, who has time for Jimmy Dawes?' A sniffle. Connors' words were finally sinking in. He took a few steps toward the bed, his focus never moving. 'Face it, my son. Nobody. Cares. About. You.' Tears trickled out of the corner of Jimmy's swollen eyes. He couldn't tell if he was angry, sad, frustrated, or confused. He just knew that he wanted the horrible man to stop talking. 'Rose. My sister. She cares.' Wyatt Connors smiled broadly. Teeth glistening like Satan's promise; eyes burning like his guarantee. 'Then why didn't she take you with her when she left?' 'Wha--?' Wyatt continued his approach. With every step, he lowered his voice...but what it lost in volume, it gained in sting. 'She abandoned you as well, James. She left you behind.' 'But...I thought she'd come back! I mean, she always came back before...' 'No. She knew, James. Your sister knew that she was done with Mayhem forever.' Finally, Wyatt leaned down to James' ear, speaking in a voice that was whisper-soft...and razor-sharp. 'And she didn't take you with her.' Jimmy's head became light, and the room seemed to spin around him. Was this the result of Connors' tainted beverage? Or was this what the truth felt like? 'Can you still tell me she cares?' Jimmy Dawes did not answer, except for a few light sobs. He had held on for a long time--longer than anyone else that Wyatt Connors 'converted.' But he ended like all the others--a pitiful, broken man, brought low by the holy word. Wyatt nodded in satisfaction. Pride had fallen, and the young man had fallen with it. Now, all that remained was to build him back up. 'Macarthur? Pablo? I think our friend is...thirsty.' # # # 'This wickedness in my reflection Is familiar to my eyes. It's the image of this love before me That I do not recognize.' Three hours later. They had given Jimmy some time to rest...after all, the knowledge that you are a worthless human being is a lot to take in all at once. But the process was only half-completed. The hard part was over, but the important part remained. Wyatt asked, 'How are you feeling, James?' 'I want to die,' came the whimpered response. 'I understand how you feel. Despair is only natural at a time like this. But you must resist the temptation to give in completely, for surely there must still be hope.' Jimmy said nothing, only turning his head away from Connors. 'Yes, James, there is still hope. Your faith was strong, but misguided. You put your trust in the wrong places before. You valued worldly treasures, and you wasted your belief on other people. Terrible, flawed people, who would only betray your trust and crush your heart. You loved those who did not love you back. You know that, don't you?' Sniffles. Tears rolled down his puffed cheeks. 'Yes.' 'There is someone who loves you, James. He loves you very, very much. He wants you to live forever at His side, forever safe from the forces of evil.' 'I don't...I don't deserve...' 'None of us do, James. We are all sinners, and we all fall short of His perfection. But that doesn't matter, James! His love for us is endless and unconditional! No matter what we have done in the past, as long as we honestly and truthfully devote ourselves to Him, all our sins are forgiven!' Jimmy looked up into Wyatt's face, looking a lot like a neglected puppy. He'd heard this whole spiel before, of course; years ago when his mom made him go to church. But somehow, this time it sounded different. It sounded...real. 'Really?' 'Absolutely! I myself used to be a horrible wretch of a man, worse than you ever were. But He saw the goodness in me, and He brought out so that I might show others the light. He wants to do the same for you, James. He would like to entrust you with a holy mission, if you would only accept Him into your heart.' Jimmy Dawes' head reeled, a combination of a rekindled belief and prodigious amounts of tainted Kool-Aid. He lifted his and to his face...and only now noticed that Wyatt had undone the straps. If he wanted to, he was free to leave. But he didn't want to leave, not anymore. 'He is the flesh of God's flesh, and the blood of God's blood. He is the one man that can save you, like he has saved all of us. Would you like that, James? Would you like to be saved from yourself?' 'Y-yes...' 'Would you like to meet Him, right now?' James wiped the tears out of his eyes and nodded. 'Open the door, Pablo.' The small Mexican man nodded, and opened the door. On the other side, there was a figure in a wheelchair. Jimmy craned his neck to get a better look, but the man's face was obscured in shadow cast by Macarthur, who stood behind this other man. Macarthur pushed on the chair, bringing the man into the room. Soon, his face was in the light, so that James could look upon him and know salvation. Although, to an objective eye, the man didn't even look like he could save himself. James didn't see the frailty, though. He didn't see the cracked skin or the sunken eyes. He only saw the belief. The religious fervor that put even Wyatt Connors to shame. 'James, I would like to introduce you to your Lord and personal savior. Look upon Him, and know His name.' The man in the wheelchair smiled, showing his gleaming-white, capped teeth. 'Hello, Brother James,' said the Reverend Ibrahim Seck. 'Welcome to the Crusade.' # # # The congregation had gathered in the main hall for supper, like they did every evening. Abigail Proctor sat in the back of the hall, at the back table, at the back corner. Naturally, multiple people had offered her closer seats so that she wouldn't have to go as far on those unwieldy crutches, but Abby would have none of it. A nice middle-aged man had carried her tray for her, and set it down in front of her. Beef and vegetable stew tonight, with cornbread and beans. Not exactly South Beach Diet, but it did just fine after a long day. And of course, there was a cup filled to the brim with red Kool-Aid...but that would remain curiously untouched throughout the meal. Next to her, as usual, was Seth. The two of them had spent most of their time together recently, and a few people were starting to think that the good Reverend Connors might have to conduct another wedding before long. They were wrong--not that Seth wasn't interested in her, of course. She was definitely attractive--maybe not a knockout like Alanna Marshall or anything, but definitely easy on the eyes. She was smart, too, but not so smart that she made Seth feel like an idiot. But the main reason why he spent so much time with her was that she didn't act like a robot, like everyone else in the Compound. He had managed to stop her before she drank the Kool-Aid...and again, he thought about how that joke just wasn't funny anymore. The meals weren't as stressful as they used to be, as the congregation chattered a little amongst themselves. Sure, it was the same conversations they had every night--lovely weather, good food, isn't Reverend Connors just wonderful--but it was better than nothing. The big farmhand took a look toward the front of the hall, and his heart sank. There was an uncovered table, with a few empty chairs. He knew what that meant--tonight would be another one of Wyatt Connors' 'presentations.' No sooner had the thought run through his mind, when a group of four men entered the hall, single-file. First was Pablo, known better to wrestling fans as Illuminado. Next was the hulking form of Macarthur Deesul, a.k.a. 'Caravan.' After that came Reverend Connors himself, followed by a fourth man in a white robe, with a hood draped over his head. This newcomer was skinny, and had bad posture, but that was all that could be determined. 'Who is that?' Abby asked. Seth knew, but didn't want to say. 'BROTHERS AND SISTERS!' came the booming voice of the shepherd to this pack of mindless sheep. 'Good evening and God bless!' 'HALLELUJAH!' they shouted in response, with Seth and Abigail parroting right along with them. She'd caught onto this aspect pretty quick. 'I have wonderful news, my friends!' Wyatt shouted. 'I have gone once more into the breech, and I have returned with another lost soul, delivered unto salvation!' 'HALLELUJAH!' 'This confused young lad was taken into hell, and abandoned there by his own flesh and blood! He was surrounded by pleasures of the flesh and false idols! He gave his faith, his trust, his entire heart to the very beast that we fight against every day! But through His divine grace, I have shown this boy the true path to glory! And now, a troubled young boy has become a good, strong man in the service of our Lord!' 'HALLELUJAH!' they thundered, with a unified voice loud enough to rattle the windows. 'Fellow believers, I give you...Brother James!' Wyatt lowered the hood of the new man's robe, showing his face to an appreciative audience. They clapped and rejoiced and yelled 'Hallelujah!' like they did at all of Wyatt's unveilings. Seth reacted the way he always did, too. With horror. He never got used to it...and this one was the worst yet. 'Oh my God,' Abby quailed. 'This is the most horrible thing I've ever seen.' Seth wanted to comfort her, but couldn't think of anything to say that wasn't a total agreement. The bruises were still fresh on the boy's face, but he still had that same dull look on his face, the same red stains at the corners of his mouth. And there was just enough room in the swelling around the eyes to let a few tears leak through. Tears of pure, unadulterated joy. James Dawes had spent most of his life playing the outcast, the misfit. He had never really fit in anywhere--not with his family, or school, or even in the world of wrestling. Now, for the first time in his life...Jimmy belonged.
