Date: The past two weeks. It was like ghosts performing the ballet, each step a perfectly choreographed whisper outside the walls. They lingered near the ivy, the tall one watching through a night-vision monocular as the single security camera that might be able to see them swept lazily back and forth. The mans lips worked silently under his ski mask, counting out the time – fourteen seconds for the camera to pass in each direction. Plenty of time. There was a click at the end of each pass – so quiet it was barely noticeable unless specifically listened for. When the sound came, the tall man dipped his head – it wasn't much, but his partner moved anyway, running as fast as she could right at him. Without breaking stride, she stepped onto his linked hands; his boost sent her flying, arms outstretched as she soared up over the wall. At the last second, the woman tucked her chin, rolling across the well-manicured lawn to slow her momentum. Backing up swiftly, she dipped gloved hands into the leather pouch at her left hip, trusty tools coming easily to her hands. The alarm box popped open just as easily, and a slit and rerouted wire killed the security system. It wouldn't last long, but, if they were lucky, it would keep until they were done. The woman's partner hauled himself over the fence in far less spectacular a fashion, and they didn't say anything to each other, moving in unison towards the imposing building that loomed ahead. The lawn was a wide-open space, well lit by the fat gibbous moon. The house's last guardian was its front door, but even it couldn't stand up to the pair; the man's deft touch with a pair of old-fashioned lockpicks was all they needed to gain entry, though they'd both be lying if they said they hadn't been nervous. Once inside, they moved quietly; the only light in the place spilled blue-white from the PDA in the woman's hands, displaying the building's blueprints. In the end, their guidance proved unnecessary, as only one door on the main floor was made from reinforced steel. The door had a six-digit keypad combination lock, and the intruders came prepared, since simply dusting it for prints was unlikely to work – until just a few months ago, the house's owner wore gloves all the time. The man affixed a small black device to the pad. The thing cycled through numbers at a remarkable pace, and he watched intently as it narrowed down the proper choice. He felt beads of nervous sweat leap to his forehead as the count froze briefly; part of him expected her to show up at any moment, and then there'd be hell to pay. So focused was the masked man on the tiny red numbers on the device, he missed the lanky redhead's approach until he felt the barrel of a tiny pistol press into the small of his back. His eyes snapped up to the distorted mirror of the steel door; the house's would-be defender looked even more like a character from Muppet Harry Potter in its surface. ''Don't move!'' The quiver in Cambrian Cambridge's voice told the man a host of things – most notably, that he hadn't actually experienced a violent confrontation, even given his employer's... predilections. ''Don't you move!'' ''Easy now.'' The mask muffled the man's voice, and he rasped it out a little more; he liked to think he sounded like Sean Connery, but the truth was a little further off. ''Let's not do anything hasty.'' For too many years, the thin Irishman had worked for the owner of this house – and maybe his work wasn't as glamorous, but it was just as necessary. But Cambrian knew the types of people his employer did business with, so his voice may have been unsteady, but the gun wasn't. ''You're trying to break into the one place she said to never go into. I'm not being hasty; I'm earning myself a raise.'' The intruder looked down at the keypad; just one more number left. ''You've never shot that outside of a practice range, have you? Do you really want to start now?'' Cambrian tried to shrug indifferently, but it came off more of a twitch. ''I know what's behind that door. Do you?'' ''Do you think we would be here if not?'' '''We'?'' Cambrian looked over his shoulder. That was the opening the man needed. He whipped around, the point of his elbow catching the gangly techie square in the mouth. The blow sent Cambridge backwards, and his assailant pressed his advantage, lifting his knee into his victim's breadbasket. Cambrian doubled over as the larger man stepped in, grabbing his wrist and twisting it to wrench the firearm free. Unused to hand-to-hand combat, the other man could only flail about, long, spindly fingers digging into the eyeholes of the ski mask – but even then, he just pulled the thing askew. An awkward right cross sent Cambridge skittering backwards into the foyer, just long enough for the other man to strip off the offending face covering. He saw his partner before Cambrian did, a lance of moonlight glimmering across the tapered point of the knife in her hand. ''Don't!'' the man barked. Cambridge straightened a little at first, cocky – and then straightened all the way as the black-clad woman's punch-dagger pushed between his vertebrae and severed his spinal cord. The light in Cambrian's eyes went out like someone flipped a switch, and he slumped to the floor. ''You didn't have to do that,'' the man groused, a frown creasing his lips as he replaced that mask. His partner did not respond, stepping over Cambrian's corpse to key in the code that the descrambler chose. The door opened with a hiss of disused hydraulics, but still required both of them to pull it open. Within the small room, there was only one piece of furniture. Despite its name and foreboding reputation, the Shadow Cabinet was just a small, ordinary-looking thing, just two deep drawers full of the kinds of deep, dark secrets no one wants to get out. It wasn't even black. Beneath her mask, the woman raised an eyebrow. Her partner couldn't help but grin. ''I still haven't forgiven you.'' ''I know,'' the woman whispered. ''But you will.'' The two of them lifted the cabinet together; it was gone by the time the live-in chef arrived the next morning. Anyone who saw them would probably call them ''girlfriends out for dinner.'' They would not mean it the way they should. ''You know, I don't usually like pizza.'' Alexandra Pierce had manners ingrained in her for years – which fork to use, what order to eat in, and her napkin always in the lap, that sort of thing. So it was little surprise that her elbows didn't touch the wrought-iron tabletop, head up, a smile curling across her lips. ''Take that back.'' It was a horrified gasp, incredulous at the very concept. ''No, I'm serious.'' Pierce hooked a stray lock of rust-red hair behind her ears (one simple diamond stud in each lobe), ducking her head into her smile. ''It has always has that tendency to be cardboard with cheese on it.'' The woman across from Alex looked rather skeptically at the Spider's plate. ''So your solution was to put... what the fuck is that?'' Alex dabbed the corners of her lips, and it wasn't really that she smiled again as much as it was that she couldn't quite stop. ''Black bean salsa, grilled chicken, and some kind of chipotle sauce that, I have to say, is making my lips tingle in a not-altogether-unpleasant manner.'' ''Pizza should be meat and sauce and crust, Al.'' Amy Campbell wore a hoodie, protecting the (now former) Universal Champion against the chill ''October in the Bay Area'' air, and also against the prying eyes of any onlookers, and she waved her own slice to emphasize her point. ''Not gonna lie, seeing you eating pizza with some lame, vegan emo douche on it is... it's kind of a betrayal of everything I stand for, and I feel like we should fight.'' It was the first time the two of them had chanced an outing alone since their brief time apart and failed attempt to be just roommates, sitting outside on the patio at the Walnut Creek California Pizza Kitchen. Though both redheads were smiling, their insides were a quail of nervousness. ''I really don't understand why you didn't just get plain old pizza. We went out for pizza.'' Amy reiterated, ''And you're eating Mexican food on a breaded plate.'' ''I live with a fifteen-year-old girl. Two, if you count.'' Alex's smile softened anew. ''I've had plenty of pizza. And it's good.'' Again the napkin dabbed the corners of her lips. ''I have to say, I'm kind of...'' Her eyebrows creased over those spectacularly grey eyes, a brief flicker of unease. ''I have done far worse at a dinner engagement than this, but either I've the worst case of indigestion from this pizza, or the butterflies in my stomach are threatening to recreate that horrible Alien movie.'' ''Am I making you nervous?'' Amy queried, ''Like, is my nervousness rubbing off on you? I promise I'm okay.'' ''You're nervous too?'' Alex picked up her fork, more to have something to do with her hands than to actually make use of it. ''I just... I don't want this to go badly. I've faced... I faced Lance Marshall alone. I have faced an entire federation of bloodthirsty jackals. I regularly face people who literally want me dead, in the ring and... and outside it.'' Her eyes flickered down, spreading some salsa over her pizza. ''And I'm terrified that I'm going to do something that you... that I will mess this up.'' ''Hey.'' Amy's voice was soft; her hand reached across the tabletop to touch Pierce's wrist. ''I just... I don't get to be happy. It's never been in cards for me, and I've always been okay with that.'' Campbell's head tilted to one side. ''So you're not letting yourself be happy now? I... I mean, no offense, but if either of us is supposed to be absolutely miserable right now, I've kind of got the monopoly on terrible luck.'' Alex lifted the pizza slice, but it didn't reach her lips. ''I'm... I'm scared you'll decide this was all a mistake, that you'll have some tearful reunion with Darcy. I want this to work. More than anything else in the world, I want this to work and for us to be happy. Together. But... every word I say, I'm scared you'll decide I'm... I'm just her.'' ''And every word I say, I'm scared that you or somebody else is looking at me and seeing the serial failure, cheater, addict.'' ''We're...'' She smiled quietly, eyes still down. ''We're pretty much a mess, aren't we? Is this... is this the way it's supposed to be?'' ''We're a bit of a unique situation,'' Amy spared Pierce a similar smile, though her eyes had drifted to a nearby table where she could have sworn a couple was giggling about her. ''In about a hundred ways.'' She brought her gaze back to Alex, and the smile grew. ''But nothing we can't handle. I think we should probably skip the movie, though – maybe get this to go and watch a DVD? Quinn would kill us for seeing Zombieland, anyway.'' ''I... you're probably right.'' She lifted her eyes, but not her chin. ''It was too early for us to try... this. How many times do you think they've looked at us?'' ''C'mon, Alex. You're smarter than I am in most respects,'' Campbell said, ''We'll always get looked at because of who we are – we're not exactly a nice blend with the crowds. We're just getting a little extra right now because of... well, things got a little public and people got a little angry. Fuck 'em. We can wait out the heat at home, right?'' Pierce nodded, lifting a hand for a passing waiter. ''Two boxes, please.'' The man nodded, drifting away. ''Patience is...'' She swallowed. ''Patience is good, yeah. Thank you. For... understanding that I'm a little clueless about... about some things.'' ''You're clueless?'' Amy chuckled, ''At least you've had more than one relationship with—well, I guess the crazies don't really count.''  Alex tried to smile, but ended up dropping her eyes again. That drove Amy to continue ''You know we're gonna be fine, right? No tearful reuniting with anybody. I'm not interested even a little in somebody who turns so fast to try making my life miserable as some childish revenge. Try not to get bored of me.'' That drew a laugh, and then a smaller smile from the unusually timid Spider. ''That is remarkably unlikely. You are a lot of things, but none of them are boring.'' Alex didn't even look at the bill, sliding a platinum American Express into the receipt holder. ''What kind of things am I?'' Amy's brows lifted. Alex's smile flickered again, her lower lip trapped between her teeth. ''I meant you're funny, and pretty. And you make me smile, when I'm not sure I put my foot in my mouth, which I hope I didn't just do there.'' Of all the nervous habits and strange behaviors, and with all of her bravado, Amy Campbell was used to yes-men. Genuine compliments, she still had trouble with. ''You look beyond uncomfortable saying that.'' She laughed. ''Let's get home before you whip out another corny adjective that makes me blush.'' The signature across the receipt was a formality and the redheads stood swiftly. ''It's not corny if it's true,'' Alex mumbled. She grabbed the shopping bag with the women's leftovers, and risked an arm around Amy's waist as they left the patio. ''You realize you have to be in charge of what movie to watch, because I have no idea what is good.'' Amy would have sworn she wasn't thinking when she broke the contact to spin around in front of Pierce as they left. ''I never go to the theatre without you or Quinn, so we'll have a pretty decent selection. We could watch those X-Men DVDs I bought the other day, though I dunno if I wanna open the collector's edition packaging and...'' She would have sworn it wasn't because of the extra eyes on them, sworn it was just her excitement over the alone time. Really. ''Well, we wouldn't want that.'' The conversation continued like that as the two of them made their way to Alexandra's convertible and most of the way home. If you looked closely, however, you might notice the smile on Pierce's lips wasn't quite as bright as it could have been, and you'd say, ''She noticed.'' But then, she notices everything. Alexandra Pierce didn't sleep well, or often. She was plagued by dreams and memories, by the ghosts of the people she'd been and the people she'd killed. In truth, she didn't think about it any longer, just took her four or five hours and rolled with it. Recently, though the dreams hadn't quieted, the Spider slept more often and a good deal easier, which was how her partner found her on her back, one leg out from under the sheets, head turned to the side. The Director's already wild hair was even more out of control after a hard night sleeping – the nightmares that danced in her mind were often quite vivid, and Amy was only awake because her partner's thrashing jostled her. Campbell didn't know what to do in a situation like this – she didn't have anything funny to say, and, if she were being honest, she didn't really want to know what Desade had done that played in Alexandra's mind. In the end, the Raver awoke, got dressed, and busied herself with something she would never admit Darcy had done for her on the afternoons she slept in. Amy crawled onto the edge of the bed, dredging up her smile as another dream caught Pierce unaware and the Spider curled nearly fetal. ''Alex?'' Amy prodded, trying not to sound as worried as she was – anyone who slept like that should be more tired when they wake up, not less. ''Alex, is every—'' Pierce sat bolt upright, swinging across her body with her left hand (she always led with the left when she wasn't thinking about it), but Amy's reflexes were part of the reason she'd been champion for over a year, and she managed to dodge the lazy punch with little effort. ''Whoa!'' Amy shouted. ''It's okay... it's just me.'' ''Amy?'' The Spider's stony eyes took a moment to focus; she rubbed at them to speed the process. ''What... what time is it?'' ''A quarter past eleven.'' She crawled up and quickly flopped onto her stomach, letting fingers dance along the blankets that covered Pierce's hip. ''Early for me, late for you. I'm eventually just going to wear hockey gear to bed.'' The redhead's wink and small laugh were strangled by the little memories that came in every breakup – namely the fact that her husband had been a hockey coach in his spare time. Recovering with a grin that wouldn't quit, she slid up further and tucked a stray bunch of hair back behind the Director's ear. ''You think it's cute that I talk in my sleep, I'm just finding it hard to keep excusing getting elbowed in the ribs and kneed in the ass as an adorable quirk.'' ''Sorry.'' Pierce's head dropped. ''I'm... not used to sleeping in the same bed as someone else. I've spent more nights awake all night rather than blowing a... nevermind.'' She tried to smile; for the most part, she succeeded. ''Is everything all right? Quinn's not—'' ''Quinn's at school, and perfectly fine. I thought we'd... well, not blow things, for one.'' Amy's brows quirked. ''And I think you should get up!'' She might have been bleary-eyed, but her instincts had gotten her just as far as Amy's reflexes. ''Uh, why should I get up?'' ''Because you trust that I don't have some secret plan that involves getting you out of bed to do you harm?'' There was something inherently kind about Amy's eyes. As fiery as she got, that impossible to describe 'something' was ever-present, especially in the calm of moments like that. ''Remember who you're with, Al. This is me.'' ''All right, all right.'' The covers were tossed off a moment later. ''If there's a group of people out there to yell 'Surprise!' and I look this horrible, I'll... well, I'll probably pout some and then forgive you.'' Though she hopped back to her feet and smiled, Amy snapped her fingers. ''Gah, I forgot you were dressed! That's unfortunate, but I'll deal.'' Just as Alex reached to the nightstand for the same thing she spent almost every waking moment with, Campbell tossed the Director's iPhone onto the bed. ''Your phone was buzzing and I was scared it'd wake you up before I was ready, so I took it. I didn't look through it or anything, though - was too busy breaking the toaster.'' ''Which reminds me.'' She continued from the door. ''I've got Eggos to thaw, and you've got ten minutes to do your big, mean Bosslady Alex stuff before I come drag you out.'' With that, Campbell flounced out of the room, leaving Pierce to her business, which (as usual) meant a heavy-duty scan of email, Twitter, and the Gainsay message boards. She did all of this while she brushed her teeth and her hair and washed her face. Alexandra returned the call that Amy answered; troublingly, it came from Rosa, her live-in chef in New Mexico. Another call found her options limited, and she was just hanging up, toothbrush hanging from her mouth, as she staggered out from the master bedroom. And stopped. ''I'll call you back,'' she said. Her eyes were dark, her brows knitted; you'd have to have a PhD to read Desade sometimes, but it was clear that she was fighting something in her mind – the news was not good. Alexandra Pierce was meticulous in her neatness (''A place for everything, and everything in its place,'' Ms. Winters often said). But with a teenager and... well, a not-as-fanatical roommate, the place got rather messy rather quickly, and it didn't help that the Director rarely had time to actually cook anything. Everything was changed this evening, everything was organized, everything was clean – the place had even been vacuumed, and the Spider would never know how she slept through that. The loveseat and Alex's chair were closer; the former had a new couch cover, a deep red that set off the rest of the room remarkably well. Amy was in the kitchen; the smaller redhead looked up from the toaster oven, where she was using what appeared to be a pair of barbecue tongs to fish the remnants of a frozen waffle out of one of the slots. ''Good lord. How long have you been up?'' ''Couldn't sleep.'' She answered. ''Well, didn't sleep. Do you think Eggos can be fried? Quinn and I got the ones with Optimus Prime stamped on them and... well, I didn't think you could melt his face until now. Why are you looking at me like I have six heads? I... still have eyebrows, right? The toaster gave me a run for my money.'' ''As it is wont to do,'' Pierce mumbled, stepping past the frustrated firecrotch with a small smile. She spat her toothpaste into the sink, setting the brush down on the counter. She stepped up behind the Red Raver, smiling just a little; no matter what, Amy brought the best out in her. ''So you decided to redecorate my condo and clean up the place? Or is it some anniversary that I forgot?'' ''Is it weird that I think it's really hot when people are brushing their teeth? It's kind of weird, I'm sure. Like, so casual to have a toothbrush hanging out of your mouth. It takes me like twenty minutes to do my teeth, and I chew on the brush after.'' Amelia Campbell, the master of deflection, had shut the stovetop off upon realizing the breadth of her Eggo failure. ''There's that 'Bitch grew six heads' look again. Let's sit down, I'll scrape Optimus off of the pan later.'' Alexandra nodded softly, slowly. ''All right. Then you will tell me what is wrong?'' When Pierce was calm, the contractions came, relaxation taking away her grammar, so when she dropped them, Amy knew she'd tensed up. ''You're always afraid I'm going to suddenly turn around and say 'Hey, Alex is boring, it's back to dick for me!''' Campbell chuckled, sinking down into the armchair she'd moved. ''Or that I'll leave you because I hate what you... well, what you have to do sometimes. I'm not condoning what you used to do, but I know you can't fully escape that life and I'd be a moron to demand it of you. So...'' It was Amy's turn to swallow nervously. ''I'm working on accepting everything, I just need one hell of a favor from you to start doing it.''  ''Anyth—''  When the smaller redhead interrupted, it was with arched brows and a look that spoke her expectations – slim to none. ''Absolute transparency. Honesty. I've tried to learn as much as I possibly can about you, and I have questions, so it's kind of a two-part favor. You're completely open with me from now on, and you tell me the truth about whatever I ask of your past.'' ''I—'' ''Please don't say no.'' Campbell's leg bounced a little, a nervous habit. ''I don't want to have that conversation.'' ''Amy,'' Alex put both hands atop her partner's. ''I have done some reprehensible things, and I don't want to put you in the position to know things that may put you at risk with the authorities or... or worse.'' ''You've killed people. You've...'' In that moment, she wouldn't look up. ''It's fucked up that I can say that so casually now, but I like to think they were guilty people, at least. And that you've never consented to anything with... with kids and families or rape or...''  Amy shook her head suddenly, violently. ''I know you haven't gone that far. That part of you is a lot of things, but not... not quite that bad. I can live not knowing about the truly awful things, I think. It's the things I've heard about, the manipulation, the 'relationships'... things that make me worry.'' She sighed. ''Things that make me worry about me. About how genuine you are with me.'' Ask, and I will answer, as best as I know how.'' ''You've slept with men. I mean, that's obvious because of Quinn, but I've got a feeling - and heard - that it wasn't exactly all forced. You're worried about me with guys,'' Nervous as Pierce was, it was Amy who couldn't quite meet her eyes. ''And supposedly most of your significant relationships pre... ugh, her... were with them.'' ''There... there haven't been many. I'm not Kathryn.'' ''But there have been some.'' ''Yes. There have.'' Pierce drew her leg up underneath her, her hands folded in her lap, not as neatly as she might have preferred, but enough. ''Do you want names? Addresses? Favorite positions?'' Amy flinched. I, uhm... I'm really an idiot, aren't I. I didn't even think you were attracted to... and you didn't date or 'fall in love' or...'' She shook her head, repeating ''I'm such an idiot.'' I gave it up for this, and I... Al, I don't want to be a good memory. I want to be the only one, ever, and I've gotta be honest that I want you to tell me that women – men, especially – before me were just... absolutely nothing. It, uh... it's gonna take a lot out of me, emotionally and financially. He's jilted and I'm... I'm going to end up emptied of everything that doesn't live in this condo. ''It's not a distraction. Not a lie. I've said so a hundred times before and I'll say it as many times as you need. I don't want a husband; I don't want a child. I told you about... about him because he was the most recent. I could have told you about what I did for business, but what I do for business isn't me, so I would have been lying by omission.'' She slipped off the loveseat, crouching in front of her armchair. ''There is no Damon in my life, no one else. There will never be anyone else, I swear to you. That...'' Long fingers brushed the stone. ''It's you, here. You asked me to be one hundred percent honest with you... please understand that I am. You asked me to tell you the truth, and the truth is that Alexandra Pierce, the woman here, with you... she has not shared her heart with anyone since 2000, not really, not honestly. And she never wants to.'' ''I can't get past that you shared your heart with anyone at all, ever.'' Amy said, ''I hate being the one that came after the others. I don't... I hate that you thought of anybody else even a little like you think about me.'' Campbell wiped tears with her sleeve, but scowled. ''It's really bad that I think I could kill anybody else you did this with, isn't it? I just... it feels like you lied to my face whenever you told me I was the most important, the only one you really shared yourself with. I was... I mean, I expected you to fess up to random sex, even repeat performances. I thought this was me accepting your job, it wasn't supposed to be me finding out about the other versions of me, the ones with dicks from a decade ago. He knew Desade, the strong, heartless girl who wanted to make it in the wrestling business, who wanted revenge against her brothers. He didn't know me, not like you do. Please understand that.
