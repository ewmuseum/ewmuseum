Part 11a: Speak Of

undated

Date: Today It had been a long trip home. They always were, of course, although the time had been cut down dramatically since the beginning of the east coast swing--chartering a plane was far more cost- and time-effective than a twenty-six hour ride in a pickup truck with a half-dozen sweaty men. But now, the small Army of God (who has recently issued a statement disavowing all knowledge and endorsement of the group) had returned to their home, the Decency Compound. The sun was just cresting over the hills as Seth drove through the front gate. The truck's engine rumbled to a halt, and the Crusade made their exit--Seth, Connors, and Illuminado from the cab, Angel Verde and Caravan from the bed. Caravan had a large bundle over his shoulder, which occasionally wriggled and made muffled noises until Angel Verde hit it with a wrench. 'Get yourself some rest, boys,' Wyatt said in a voice that came from somewhere far away--partially because of the bandages that covered his face. 'Very important event coming up.' The others nodded, and shuffled off in the direction of the barracks. 'Ah,' Connors said, poking Caravan in the ribs. 'Not you. I need you to come with me.' He then turned and walked away, in the direction of the Jessups' home--the place where Ibrahim Seck, the true leader of the Decency Crusade, took residence. Caravan shambled after him, still carrying his burden. Seth shook his head as he watched them go. He couldn't believe that they'd done it again. Once again, they had abducted someone from an SCCW event and brought them to this godforsaken place. Once again, nobody stopped them. In fact, nobody noticed. Not the cameras, not the fans...not even this person's supposed 'friends.' They probably didn't realize what happened until they got to their car and found out that their bags were still in the locker room. There was something deeply concerning about that. As soon as Macarthur and the Reverend were out of sight, Seth's mind turned to another pressing matter, and he headed straight for the infirmary. There was someone there--not someone special; he wouldn't go that far. After all, he only met her a few days ago. But she was different, and at this point that was close enough to special that it might as well have been. He darted through the door--as well as a six-foot-ten man can 'dart,' anyway--and looked around the room. There was Buford McCoy, who hit his head on a crossbeam and got a concussion last week. One of the more recent members, Henry Wiggins from Albuquerque, was in another bed; he'd put a nail through his foot. And there, down at the end of the row of cots was someone else, with their nose buried in what looked like a newsletter of some kind. Behind that paper was a head of dirty blonde curls. 'Abby?' he whispered, hoping not to rouse anyone. There was anxiety in his voice. If they got to her while he was gone... The paper lowered, and soon Seth found himself looking at a pair of bright brown eyes. 'Oh, it's you! Welcome back.' Abigail Proctor smiled, showing a vibrance that had been in rare supply in the Compound. Seth let out a heavy sigh of relief. She was all right. For now. 'How are you holding up?' he asked, with a friendly smile. No reason to panic. Well, that wasn't really true. But there was reason not to show that panic. 'Not bad,' she replied, setting her reading material off to the side. 'They want me to stay off the ankle for a few more days, but after that I'll be ready to pitch in around here.' 'All right. So...you're sticking around, then?' 'Sure. Might as well see what the place is about, right?' Seth nodded, although he hoped she never found out. 'Well, I suppose you need your rest. You don't need me bothering you.' 'Oh, go ahead and bother me. Have a seat,' she said, and pointed to the neighboring bed. As the big man sat down, he asked, 'Are you sure?' 'Yeah. It'll be nice to talk to someone who isn't all weird.' So, she had noticed something, then. 'How do you mean?' 'Well...I don't want to be mean or anything, but some of these people are just really...weird. Like, the way they stare at nothing half the time. You ever notice that?' His response was a noncommittal shrug. Of course he'd noticed it, but he wasn't about to say. 'They've got some funny ways, but they're good people.' 'I guess. I should probably get to know them if I'm going to be staying here. Anyway, they're still better than the folks back home.' 'Oh? And where's that?' 'Nebraska, just outside of Kearney. You ever been there?' 'No.' 'Figured. There's no reason to go unless you really, really love corn.' 'And yet you willingly came here.' Abby laughed--she had a nice laugh, not like you'd expect from someone with the typical flat Plains accent. 'Yep. Traded one middle of nowhere for another.' 'Heh. So what brought you to the Compound, then?' 'Well...' she started, and then paused for a moment, like she was making sure she picked all the right words. 'We'd always been a big church family, right? Every week we'd put on our fancy clothes, drive into town--my dad used to be a farmer--and attend the service. I mean, we were there every week. Even through high school, where hardly any of the kids went to church anymore, 'cause it wasn't 'cool,' I still never missed it.' 'Okay.' 'So then I went off to college--five years at NU, to get my degree in Agribusiness. And while I was there, I got involved with some of the campus church groups; Campus Crusade and that kind of thing. And they talked about a lot of stuff that made sense, and it really changed the way I thought about God. You know, like how people get caught up with the little things in religion and end up missing the whole point. I mean, it really opened my eyes, you know?' 'Right.' 'Anyway, I go back home after graduation, and I start going to my old church again, and I hang out at a few of my mom's 'social lunches,' where she and some other church ladies drink wine all afternoon. I couldn't believe what I heard there. It wasn't just the gossip, although that was bad enough. It was the bigotry. I couldn't believe the garbage that came from their mouths...and one of them, my own mother! But there they were, talking about how worried they were that 'the blacks' had bought a house in town, and how they weren't going to support this candidate for mayor because she was Catholic. The next day, I couldn't even look her in the eye. After that, I just couldn't stay in that house anymore. And then they all got eaten by dinosaurs.' 'What?' 'Sorry. Didn't think you were listening anymore. It's not really much of a story.' 'No, it's fine. Keep going.' 'Oh. Well...after that, I packed up, got some cash, and hit the road. I've been at it for almost a year now...wandering around, looking for God. I know, that sounds kinda hippy, but...well, my faith is important to me, and I don't think I could stay in a place where I couldn't feel His presense, you know? And I sure as hell couldn't feel Him back in Kearney.' 'Wow. So, you've been a lot of other places before this?' 'Sure. Not too many 'organized churches,' because most of them have the same problems as the one back home. But I've found a few communes and things. Some of them were all right, and the people were nice enough, but..I dunno. It just wasn't quite what I was looking for. Then, about two weeks ago, I was in Okie City, and saw a pamphlet for this place. Thought I'd check it out.' 'What do you think of it?' 'I haven't seen much so far. And yeah, there's that whole weirdness thing I mentioned earlier. But...I don't know. There's definitely something about this place.' Seth chuckled without humor. 'Yeah...there really is.' 'Everyone's really nice, though. They've even been good about bringing meals in for us.' 'Oh? Do they bring you anything to drink?' 'Yeah, a little cup of Kool-Aid. I don't like drinking sweet stuff, though. I've just been drinking out of my Camelbak.' 'Good call,' Seth muttered, as if he was trying to just think it, and failed. 'Huh?' Seth looked at Abby again, now with trepidation. 'I've got to tell you something. This is going to sound crazy. I know that, but...well, just hear me out.' 'Not exactly inspiring confidence there.' 'I know, but...just listen. They're going to be bringing the Kool-Aid around all the time. Do whatever you can...pour it on the floor, dump it out the window, spill it all over your shirt. Just don't drink it. Whatever you do, don't drink it.' Abby looked directly into his eyes, and blinked. 'You're telling me not to drink the Kool-Aid.' 'Yes.' 'Could you be any more cliché?' 'I'm serious, Abby. You talked earlier about how sometimes they have these vacant stares? That's what does it. They put something in it, I don't know what, but it...it's like it sucks the souls right out of people.' 'Yet you seem to be fine. Are you telling me you've never drank it?' 'It hasn't been easy.' Once more, their eyes met. She saw just how tired he was...and not all of that was from the travel. 'Oh my God. You are serious.' He nodded, sadly, and laid back on the cot. 'Why don't you leave?' she asked. 'That's...actually, that's even more complicated. I can't really say, not right now.' Something crept over Abigail's face. It wasn't quite fear...more like concern. It was at that moment that she really started to understand how wrong this place was. Seth recognized the look immediately. 'If you wanted to run screaming from this place right now, I wouldn't blame you one bit.' Abby turned the corners of her mouth into a defiant smirk. 'I could never do that. For one thing, I've got to wait for this ankle to heal.' That got a smile, and then a laugh from Seth. It was a well-needed tension breaker. 'Besides,' she continued. 'I couldn't leave you here alone with all these weirdos.' Seth didn't say anything, but his smile grew wider. It was like a weight was lifted off of his shoulders, and it seemed like his physical as well as his mental self was able to relax because of it. Within moments, Seth had fallen asleep on the infirmary cot. Had it been any closer, Abby would have stroked his hair while he slept.
