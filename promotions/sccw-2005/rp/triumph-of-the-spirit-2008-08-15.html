Triumph of the Spirit

15 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 15, 2008 Too often in life, we simply live. It sometimes takes times of deep hardship or traumatic experiences to move the human spirit. Looking at the world in a narrow view, the human-being is just another animal that eats, sleeps, and breathes. As I run through parts of this world that appear to be blemishes in society, such thoughts enter my mind. When it is dark—when our bodies can’t help but stay more alert out of the fear of what our eyes cannot see—this is when I like to run. Along with me, I take my friend, Jibril, who is a pit bull mixed with a mastiff. He is quite a powerful creature, but yet so civilized. However, Jibril is much different from me. Jibril simply lives. I question whether or not there is a spirit in him that can be moved in the same way mine can. I possess the powerful human spirit in which God has blessed us all. Does he simply act on instinct? Does he lack the ability to reason and restrain himself? I know that on August 18th, my human spirit will be put to the test. Watching my opponent, he poses the question, "Do we live but for survival? Or do we survive so we can live?" As my run brings me to a street that sees a seemingly constant flow of cars, I decide to take this road, despite having to breathe the fumes that at times can seem suffocating. Although the air is thick, I enjoyed the atmosphere on the whole. The noises from the vehicles cause me to appreciate human progress. What if one of the early descendants of Adam and Eve witnessed this scene? We have come so far. If the so-called poor of western society lived in the same fashion hundreds of years ago, they would not have been considered so poor. The human spirit has triumphed over and over and with it, our standards get higher and higher. As time progresses, humanity will triumph and our standards will rise. I looked down to my friend, Jibril. From the time that his species set foot on this earth until now, have they progressed? Perhaps they have adapted in several ways, but for the most part—he eats, sleeps, and breathes just as his deep ancestral roots had. Progress is being made right now. When I watch the Summer Games in Beijing, I sometimes shed tears of joy at the spectacle. What an amazing test of the human spirit. These athletes have devoted their lives to pushing their physical limits. One man, an American athlete has set a new world record in every event he has competed in. One man? One spirit. When I ponder over how one would answer Dualton Sheridan’s question, I begin to think that the premise is too simple. Life is complex. There is more to life than what our earthly beings can grasp. We know this. Some people try to kid themselves by professing that science is the answer to life’s questions. Jibril and I have found our way back to our humble home. We have finished our run. My spirit feels good. I know that we all answer to a higher power. God has blessed us with a human spirit. August 18th is quickly approaching. As I push my limits, I know that there will be a triumph of the spirit in Cleveland, Ohio. One man—one spirit.
