Date: Previously One Month Ago: After you have spent enough time in enough hotel rooms across the country, they have a tendency to all start looking the same. Even when that hotel room is located in Las Vegas, and the hotel in question is Bellagio. And Oswald Remington Smythe had spent plenty of time in hotel rooms over his career. He took a long look at his evening's dinner, and felt a small pang of guilt come over him. Despite the fact he had ordered a Caesar salad and shrimp cocktail from room service instead of the lobster or duck he was originally inclined to, he always felt a little guilty charging something to the room when he wasn't the one paying for it. He checked his Blackberry for what must have been the twentieth time since he had returned from the blackjack tables an hour earlier. With a deep sigh, he thumbed the remote for the room's television and quickly tuned it to Showtime before muting it, leaving it to silent roll the credits on some movie. He was putting the dishes from dinner in the hallway for housekeeping to pick up when his Blackberry chirped, signaling he had a message. Expectantly, he checked the message, and a slow grin spread over his goateed face. He unmated the television and began to watch. 'Making his way to the ring, from Kentwood, Michigan.....' 'I'll say this about Matthew Randall. The title of his entrance music is very fitting. Plus, he wants a piece of Prince, who's going to be going against the new Universal Champion, Jonathan Rhine later this evening.' 'The only reason Rhine is the champ is because Sage felt bad for him. Sage didn't want to embarrass Rhine at Defiance.' 'Sage is a coward, just like Prince Pride.' 'Matthew Randall.' Oswald watched for a while longer, occasionally jotting down a note in his Moleskine notebook. Eventually, he muted the television again, picked up his Blackberry and dialed a number. 'Yes, hello. May I speak with Vincent Matthews, please?' 'Excellent! My name is Oswald Remington Smythe, and I used to manage wrestlers under the name of-' Ah, so you are familiar with me, Vincent, good, good. How would you-' 'Excuse me?' 'I see, my apologies, Mayhem. And yes, Oz will be fine. As a matter of fact, that has a certain ring to it that I rather like. The reason I am calling-' Oz closed his eyes and pinched the bridge of his nose. Normally, he was the one doing the interrupting, not the one being interrupted. Forcing a smile onto his face and, more importantly, into his voice, he continued. 'I was owed a favor by Drusilla, so I called her on it. I think we both know where she got it.' He tensed, waiting for the conversation to sour. He was taking a calculated risk bringing up Mayhem's failed relationship with the female wrestler Rosa Dawes, better known as Black Rose. He exhaled softly, relaxing as Mayhem didn't immediately hang up on him. 'I am calling because I want you to go back into wrestling, with me as your manager. I would ask if you are familiar with FUSE, but from the sound in the background, I'd say you're watching it as we speak.' 'You don't say. Well, I would say you've found a keeper in that one then. But back to the business at hand.' 'Well, we both know it's not the paycheck, or the long days on the road, the nights in hotel rooms. In fact, Drusilla told me I was wasting my time, there was only one thing that could possibly bring you back to this business…' 'Exactly. That is precisely what, that is precisely who I am offering you.' 'No, not yet. But they will be. Trust me on that one.' 'I have certain things, my dear Mayhem, that I wish to do. One final run, and my, won't it be fun, before my time in this sport is through. And since our goals have so much in common, with my wit and panache, and your sizable physical talents, we shall be part of a movement that shakes FUSE to its very core. And besides, you'll be getting paid to do what you love most. Beat people up.' 'Actually, I've already spoken to Mr. Duluth, and shown him some footage of your career. And since he doesn't have a nephew in this sport that I am aware of, he agreed to bring you in with the condition that I be your manger. And by that, he means 'babysitter' so there is no repeat of the Phoenix incident. So, what do you say, Mayhem old boy? Do you want to be infamous again?' Oz waits, sitting expectantly through a long pause on the other end of the conversation. Eventually, he gets the answer he was looking for. 'Excellent. I believe my number should be on your caller ID. I'm in Vegas for the next week under the name Raphael Onion at Bellagio. How soon can you make it up here? 'Excellent again! I'll have a ticket for you on US Airways, the 2:55 flight tomorrow. First class, of course, I'm only six feet tall myself, and coach always leaves me cramped. We'll go over the particulars when you get here. Oh, I have Cirque tickets for tomorrow night, if you're interested in that sort of thing. It would be such a waste for me to go alone, or even worse, have to hire myself a date.' 'Then Cirque it is. I'll have a car waiting for you at the airport, should I put it under the name Mayhem?' 'Then I will see you tomorrow. Looking forward to doing business.' Oz hung up and went back to watching UPROAR, continuing to make notes in his notebook. As the show came to an end, he picked up his Blackberry, and at the exact moment it went off the air, the phone rang. 'He's in.' 'Surprisingly little, actually. I'm going to need a first class ticket from Phoenix to Vegas, US Airways, tomorrow, the 2:55 flight. Put it under Mayhem, that's his legal name now, apparently. I'll also need a limo to pick him up at the airport under the same name to bring him to Bellagio. Oh, and Cirque tickets for tomorrow night.' 'Yes, I'm aware of that, but I figure if anyone can make that happen, it would be you. Nothing as ostentatious as front and center, but certainly not nosebleeds either. I have an image to uphold, you know.' Oz grinned at the phone, grinning like a hyena as he was hung up on. He stood, grabbing his jacket from a chair and decided to head back to the casino again. Perhaps he would play poker this time, something where his skills at manipulating people would give him an edge. After grabbing his cane, he paused to look at himself in the room's mirror. 'You've still got it, old chap. They'll never know what hit them.' One Week Ago: Most people know Smitty T. Duluth's office from all the times they've seen wrestlers called into it to be chewed out in arena after arena across the country. Many of them have no idea that in the FUSE Headquarters, Smitty also has an office where he conducts business that doesn't happen to occur on the nights a show is being broadcast. And if they did know about this office, they would assume it would be more conducive to business and less kitschy then the one shown to the wrestling fans. And they would be totally wrong. If anything, his 'permanent' office is even more over the top then the ones set up in small backrooms on a semi-weekly basis. The desk is much larger, the stripper poles are shinier and more numerous, the couches covered in zebra print velvet, and if you were to look up? Yes, that's a fully functioning disco ball hanging from the ceiling. The rumors about him having a fog machine installed in his desk cannot be confirmed or denied at this time. It is in this office that Smitty T. Duluth sits, staring in confused consternation at the rather plain, perhaps even severe looking woman in the sensible brown dress sitting in front of him. 'So, wait, who are you again?' 'My name is Dr. Virginia Rhodes, Mr. Duluth. I was hired for the psychological evaluation on one of your new employees, a 'Mayhem,' I believe?' Dr. Rhodes opened a manila folder with the name MAYHEM written on it neatly and flipped through several of the pages within. Smitty continued to look confused, tapping his chin. 'Just call me Smitty, babe. Yeah, I'm completely not remembering hiring you. I interview every shrink personally, and I'm pretty sure you weren't one of them.' If Dr. Rhodes took offense at Smitty calling her 'babe,' she certainly didn't show it. 'The original psychologist you hired was Tawnya Dees, and-' Smitty cut her off, snapping his fingers. 'Yeah yeah, pretty blonde girl, had 'Wild Thang' tattooed right above her ass crack! What happened to her?' 'She was suspended by our firm for unprofessional conduct. Seems she had a habit of getting a little too personal in her sessions with clients,' Dr. Rhodes answered while straightening her glasses. Smitty sighed, his thoughts elsewhere. 'So, tell it to me straight, doc. Is this Mayhem really as loose a cannon as he seems?' Dr. Rhodes closed the folder and fixed Smitty with a stare. 'May I ask you something, Mr....Smitty?' 'Sure, go ahead,' Smitty replied with a shrug. 'Why do you have your employees undergo a psychological evaluation before you let them, in essence, attempt to beat each other to death?' Smitty laughed deeply. 'The kinds of work I have paid people to do attracts a certain mindset. They're all fucking nutso.' When she started to speak, Smitty cut her off. 'Yeah, yeah, I know, you prefer the term 'mentally challenged' or 'reality impaired,' or whatever polite bullshit euphemism is popular right now. But seriously, let's cut through all that, and tell me if Mayhem is a fucking whackjob.' Dr. Rhodes opened the folder again, looking over what was written inside. 'Very well. No, Mr. Duluth, he is not 'a fucking whackjob.' As a matter of fact, compared to some of the behavior and beliefs many of your talent displays, Mayhem is practically normal.' Smitty's eyebrows arched in surprise. 'Normal? He had his name legally changed to Mayhem! How's that 'normal?'' 'Mayhem is a nickname he acquired at a very young age and he identifies with it very strongly. He says he could never see himself fighting under any other name, and so he had it changed to legally protect his rights to that name.' 'Ok, that makes sense. What about his family, there's got to be issues there, huh?' Smitty leaned forward, rubbing his hands together.' Dr. Rhodes flipped a page and scanned it. 'He grew up without a father, which is what led to him joining gangs at an early age. Soon after, he discovered he could make far more money fighting, and the discipline that came with training kept him out of trouble.' 'And his mother?' 'Violet Matthews died of a drug overdose in 1999. He took two weeks off to bury her and settle up any business she had, and then returned to fighting,' she explained. 'He hadn't been close to her in years, so there was no real mourning involved.' Smitty leaned back in his chair and crossed his arms. 'So you're telling me he doesn't believe he is a vampire, has never undergone extensive torture or long-term brainwashing, no post traumatic distress, no daddy issues, nothing?' The psychologist closed the folder and smiled. 'His only quirk is a marked lack of respect for authority, Mr. Duluth. But he told me that wouldn't be an issue since Mr. Smythe would be the one doing most of the talking to you.' She stood up, straightening her skirt. 'In short, yes, Mayhem is psychologically fit enough to beat people up for other's enjoyment. Or, he's not fucking nutso, as you put it. I will now leave you to the business of running a wrestling company.' With that, she stood and walked out of the room, into the hallway where Oz was waiting for her. 'So, do you think he bought it?' Oz asks, as they began to walk down the hall together. 'Hook, line, and sinker, old chap. Hook, line, and fucking sinker.'
