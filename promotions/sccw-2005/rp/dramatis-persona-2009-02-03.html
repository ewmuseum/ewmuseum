Dramatis Persona

3 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/3/09 In a small room bedecked with crushed blue velvet, two people were engaged in a flurry of activity. One was a woman of medium-height, platinum hair concealed beneath a cap. She moved around the room like a hummingbird, making sure everything seemed to be in order. 'Master,' she asked, an inquisitive look on his face, 'why are we so hurried today?' The other denizen of the room was bald, with a long, curved nose. He sat before a table, shuffling a deck of tarot cards with an amused expression on his face. 'We are getting a guest shortly,' he said, 'and everything must be in order.' ------------------------ January 3, 2009 8:50 PM 'Thanks man! Have a good one!' With an appreciative nod to the GameStop employee, Seymour Almasy left the gaming store, clutching a bag containing a brand new copy of Kingdom Hearts: Re Chain of Memories. With his new game safely in hand, the remainder of the weekend looked to be one full of RPG goodness for the one time king of Primetime Central. 'Now for something to eat,' Seymour told himself, and fortunately, just the eatery happened to be located between the GameStop and his Manhattan apartment. The walk to the Chipotle Mexican Grill wasn't a long one, but it gave Seymour plenty of time to think about what was, quite honestly, a paucity of things to do. He'd travelled the world several times over, and been booked tighter than some presidents for long chunks of time. With so much free time now that he was about of the sport, it was almost an embarrassment of riches for a man with so many time-consuming hobbies. Walking the streets of New York never ceased to amaze him, in spite of being as well-travelled as he was. There were so many different types of people, all hurrying this way and that, off to any of dozens of possible locations. Usually, he himself had been too busy to step back and look at it all. Now, though, he had all the time in the world. He wasn't working, though he did make time for a variety of charities, personal appearances, and gym time. After all, considering his diet consisted of fun things like Mountain Dew and Chipotle, without working out, he would balloon to sizeable proportions. There was no line at the Chipotle, and he breezed through the service line, ordering his usual beef burrito with medium salsa and guacamole. Seymour paid for his meal, added the bag to his GameStop bag in his right hand, and continued walking along towards his apartment. It was late, by Almasy's recent standards, about 9:30 or so. When he thought about it, so many days had been like this one as of late. He got up at about 9 AM. He got breakfast, went to the gym, and then played some video games. Then came lunch, and more video games. Next would be dinner, and, you guessed it, video games. Seymour was quite happy with the life. There was nothing like being on the road. Nothing like competing in front of a capacity crowd that was either cheering you or booing you with everything they had, At the same time, though, that life had a price. It was a price that Seymour Almasy knew all too well. Professional triumphs were tempered by personal hardship. It seemed, to him, at least, that Seymour Almasy the wrestler and Seymour Almasy the person could never be happy at the same time. Retirement, at least, had solved that problem. Seymour Almasy the wrestler was dead. With his spoils for the day in hand, Seymour unlocked the door to his apartment building, and stepped inside. A brief elevator ride later, and he was inside apartment 3C, taking in the ambiance of his small yet cozy apartment. Memorbilia from his career hung everywhere, as if he could ever forget the years spent waging war inside the squared circle. Of all of the pictures, though, it was the final one, hanging just above the 52' plasma screen, that confirmed to him that Seymour Almasy had died, R.I.P., months ago. It was his final match, an action shot expertly taken by one of Global Championship Wrestling's crack photographers. The picture captured the final conscious instant of Seymour Almasy in a professional wrestling ring, just as Lia Ambrosi's knee drove itself into his skull courtesy of her Canto VII springboard Buisaiku knee kick. Sure, he'd thought about going back a few times since then. Generally, though, looking at the Ebon Magdalen caving his skull in was enough to stop such foolishness. ----------------------- January 3, 2009 11:59 PM A couple of hours of Kingdom Hearts had been played, and the burrito was long eaten by the time Seymour got up for his bathroom trip. He figured that he had about three more hours of gaming left in him before he'd crash for six hours of sleep and get up to start the routine again. As he stepped inside and closed the door, his gaze fell upon himself in the full-length mirror on the door. Seymour smiled. In his own mind at least, he looked good. Less stressed than he'd been in forever, for certain. A bit heavier than in his prime, but he wore the weight reasonably well thanks to his time at the gym. Whether it was his imagination or not, he'd never know, but he could have *sworn* that his reflection's mouth curled in a smirk at that very moment, and the words 'it's your fault, you know' echoed in his ears. 'What is,' he asked, out loud, even as his mind realized that there were a great multitude of things that could be his fault. 'Oh, pretty much everything, Mr. Wrestling Hero,' the voice in his head continued. 'Your wife's death, and the fact that you've basically hopped from bed to bed since then trying to find someone who gives a fuck about you for starters.' 'It's my fault Rich Rollins slept with her and handed her a gun,' Almasy demanded of his reflection, which whistled innocently back at him. 'Nope,' the voice said, 'but all you had to do was quit wrestling and that wouldn't have happened, now would it?' Seymour's jaw clenched tightly in anger, looking for the words to refute the voice, but they would not come. 'And ever since,' the voice continued, 'you've been a miserable excuse of a person chasing every skirt you can find in this business. Let's see, there's Tessa, Dawn, and Lia at the very least, right? Makes you wish you'd quit back when Laura asked you to, doesn't it?' Every muscle in Almasy's body tightened to match his jaw, the once Final Fantasy's form rigid and inflexible as he confronted his accuser...an accuser that seemed to be...himself? 'Who the fuck *are* you,' Almasy finally sneered, looking at his smirking reflection in the mirror. The voice inside his head didn't relent, replying with a slightly too-pleased tone. 'I'm you, of course,' the voice said. 'Who else would I be? Look at me, for Yevon's sake!' Seymour did. And of course it was him, it was his damned *reflection*! 'Why are you *TELLING* me all of this?' Seymour demanded. 'What's the point?' 'I just want some recognition,' the voice said. 'I've been so lonely. You're so busy remembering your past glories that you forget what an asshole you've been. You're self-centered, arrogant, and one of the biggest egotists walking in wrestling. And you always have been, no matter how many people love you...' Solemnly, Almasy gazed at his reflection in the mirror. After several long moments of contemplation, Seymour nodded. 'I am,' he admitted, shutting his eyes to the pain that the statement brought. 'That's why I'm alone in this apartment playing video games all day.' A dazed feeling overtook Seymour, his hands going to the mirror to brace himself. His final view of his reflection was of a smiling Seymour, who nodded once as Seymour fell to his knees. Almasy's hair hung down over his face as his eyes closed, the blackness of unconsciousness claiming him. As Seymour slumped to the floor, the voice in his head returned, different this time. Powerful and booming, it echoed in his mind, and would continue to do so for days on end. 'Thou art I, and I am thou...' ----------------------- January 4, 2009 9:00 AM Go now if you want it! An Otherworld awaits you! 'Ugh...' The sound of Seymour's I-Pod waking him up with 'Otherworld' from the Final Fantasy X OST brought Almasy back sharply to the conscious world. Rolling over to his stomach, Seymour opened his eyes, groaning piteously. What the Hell had happened last night? It all seemed like a dream, looking at himself in the mirror and having his reflection lash back out at him. Force him to admit his considerable crimes and faults. He could have denied everything, of course...but that wasn't reality. Seymour Almasy had done many laudable things, as well as many things he'd have to answer for in the next world. A lot of wrestlers had to channel other aspects of themselves depending on whether they were being loved or hated. For Seymour, he always just ended up being himself, and people would react to him how they would. Seymour's left hand clicked the I-Pod off, and he pushed himself up to hands and knees. He was out of bed a moment later, headed to the kitchen to get himself some Cheerios, a banana, and a nice glass of milk. His silly dream, or whatever it was, would be forgotten in a matter of moments. It was on his way to get breakfast that he noticed the envelope slid underneath the door to his apartment. Seymour stooped and picked it up, turning it over in his hands. The letter was addressed to him, with a return address of Las Vegas, Nevada. 'Odd. Who the Hell even has this address besides bill collectors,' he asked, out loud, eyebrow quirked. 'Moreover, who would have it delivered personally and not in the mailbox downstairs?' Definitely strange, Seymour thought. As he tore the envelope open, and read the contents, he was sure of that fact. Dear Mr. Almasy, Greetings. My name is Jack Kensington. I am the owner and operator of Sin City Championship Wrestling. You spent a brief period of time under contract to this company during its tenure as FUSE Wrestling, and beyond that wrestled occassional matches as per your PTC contract. Our company is undergoing a time of great change and turmoil. We are seeking to bring in talented wrestlers from around the world, but the existence of a faction of wrestlers in our fold, the Dead Man's Hand, is making this difficult. Jonathan Rhine, our most marketable and well-known talent, was driven from the company by this group. I am told that he was a friend of yours. The Hand counts amongst its roster Alexandria Pierce, better known as Desade, the leader of the group. She is largely responsible for the domination of the Hand, as well as SCCW's crushing victory over PRIME at the Cataclysm PPV against PRIME. Athletes such as Jadian Bridden and Phillip Kennedy support her, as does the SCCW Universal Champion, Amy Campbell. I have been told that you like a challenge. That you like battling the most difficult opponents wrestling has to offer. Sin City Championship Wrestling has the most difficult competition in professional wrestling today. I would very much like to speak with you. The Dead Man's Hand is a significant threat to the survival of my company. I will pay you handsomely to join us. Sin City Championship Wrestling needs a hero. Be that hero, Seymour. Dr. Jack Kensington Owner, Sin City Championship Wrestling The man known around the world as the Final Fantasy placed the letter down on his dining room table. He had seen a few SCCW shows, and knew most of the relevant players. If nothing else, Kensington knew how to push Seymour's buttons. Bringing up Jonathan Rhine was evident of that. To many, Rhine was the heir to Almasy's tradition of being a clean cut good guy in Primetime Central, one that was probably better at it than Almasy himself. And what had it gotten him, in the end? Constant trouble and a forced retirement from the sport at the hands of the Dead Man's Hand. Seymour's dander was up. This letter was, in essence, a challenge. The implication was clear. Yeah, Seymour, you were a great wrestler, but you've never had to deal with a challenge like this before. 'Dammit,' he groaned, shaking his head. 'I'm not a hero anymore.' Almasy looked at the letter again. On the SCCW letterhead was contact information for Dr. Kensington. It seemed apparent that Sin City's owner had an offer to make. Would it be so bad to hear that offer? He could always say no after the fact. He'd heard offers from smaller American promotions as well as Japan, and been able to say no to them perfectly capably. He could do the same here. Seymour plucked his cell phone off the table, dialed a number, and placed it to his ear. Somewhere, though, in the pit of his soul, he was hoping to God, Yevon, or any other eligible deity that Nigel Kensington's offer wouldn't be anything that made him think. ------------------------ January 4, 2009 11:30 AM Two hours later, and Seymour's jaw was somewhere in the vicinity of the A/C/E line train headed north to the Bronx. 'He can't be serious,' Almasy groaned, looking down at the sheet of paper on which he'd written Kensington's proferred terms. 'That's more money than I've made...ever.' The terms of the possible contract Kensington had offered were rich. There were performance incentives seemingly everywhere, merchandising clauses sprinkled liberally throughout. At the end of the day, the contract would be the most lucrative of his career. And if half of what Kensington had related to him about the Dead Man's Hand was true, he would earn every damned penny if he went to war with Desade's band of lackeys. The ancien regime that signed the Hand had fucked up well and truly, it seemed. Signing them to contracts that virtually negated typical forms of retaliation within company means, such as handicap matches, suspensions, and fines. Seymour had flippantly suggested that he be given similar protection. Kensington's reply? Fine, sign with us and you've got it. SCCW was offering him everything, and then some. Even more dangerously, the company was offering him time. Promising him that they wouldn't run him ragged as Steven Caldera and GCW had. At the end of the call, Kensington had been gracious as can be. Told Seymour that he didn't have to decide now. If Seymour wanted to jump on board, all he'd have to do was show up backstage at Defiance II from the Nassau Coliseum, a stone's throw from his Manhattan apartment. Seymour's other point of contention had been that he didn't want to be a lackey for Kensington against the Hand. The doctor had replied casually and told him that he didn't have to be. So long as Seymour didn't aid the group, Kensington had said, he'd be perfectly happy. After that, Seymour'd hung up the phone, with a promise to think about the offer and show up if he was interested. It went without saying that Dr. Kensington had given Almasy quite the offer to think about. Once more, Almasy looked up at the picture above his television. The image of Lia kneeing him in the skull was a powerful one. One that gave him considerable pause. No, Seymour Almasy was not a hero anymore. He was human, mortal, just like anyone else. He knew that signing with SCCW could only result in more pain. More glory, perhaps, but certainly more pain and misery in his life. Playing video games was a nice existence. Nice, safe -- and ultimately boring. No, it was time for Seymour Almasy to return to the ring. That meant that he needed to sharpen his skills, perhaps come up with some new moves. Get ready for the challenge of his lifetime. Yet again, Seymour Almasy would make an ill-advised comeback to the sport of kings.
