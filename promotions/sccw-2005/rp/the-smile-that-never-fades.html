The smile that never fades.

2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/8/09 Through everything he’d been through, he never lost his smile… Chase Harper once had it all. The money, clothes, cars, and girls. He was on top of the world and for that moment felt that nothing could hold him back. Being born into a family of great wealth, and living the lifestyle of the rich and famous, who could really blame him? He was a golden child. Teachers loved him, and every boy and girl dreamed of being him. The parents around his neighborhood even compared their own children to him. It seemed like the sun miraculously illuminated his path because along with his fortune he was very intelligent and had a very bright future. However, he never had any real goals or aspirations. Money was at his fingertips and it seemed like everything he touched turned to gold. He never once would have thought that his whole world would come crashing down upon him. But it did. He remembered it like it was yesterday, and still the words continued to come back and haunt him. "Chase, I‘m sorry but your father is dead." "Dead," he asked as his eyebrows furrowed in concern. "Yes," his mother replied, "he is dead." Chase was eighteen at the time and had just finished high school. He’d graduated with honors, and had planned to attend Princeton University, just like his father. "Oh," he said, lost for words. "How did it happen?" His mother breathed heavily and adjusted herself upright in the chair. She ran her tiny pale hands through his unruly mop of blonde hair and gently caressed his ear. "Never mind that now Chase," she said. "I’m afraid I have even more bad news." Chase hung his head in sorrow and could feel a tear swelling up in the pits of his eyes. But he held it back. He knew he had to be strong. His father would expect that from him. But still, he could not figure out what news could have been worse than what he’d just been presented with. And when he heard ’it’, the words hit him like a ton of bricks. "I’m not your mother." His mouth dropped open and his heart sunk into his queasy gut. He could suddenly feel his whole world falling apart around him, and ending slowly. Tears came rolling down his cheek like rain; and there was nothing he could do to hold them back now. "Chase, I need you to be strong," she pleaded. "We had planned to tell you but were just waiting for the right time. I‘m sorry it all had to come to this." He looked up from the ground, eyes aligned, and replied in confidence. "I understand ma…" he paused. "I understand but where do we go from here?" She flashed a faint smile and stood from her chair. Chase wiped the tears from both of his eyes and watched her as she walked over to the window and drew back the curtains. She looked out into the distance as the sunlight bathed the two in a golden glow. However, Chase didn’t feel so golden anymore. "You’re cut off," she said. "Huh," he asked, his tone rebellious. "What do you mean I’m cut off?" "Your father put me through some kind of hell throughout our entire marriage," she told him, her voice growing slightly. "I never wanted to take you in." She continued to look through the window. "But what does that have to do with me?" She turned and pointed a finger at him. "YOU," she said as she walked closer. "You represent everything I HATE about him!" She jabbed her finger it into his chest repeatedly. Chase jumped from the chair and noticed that she was crying now. He began to back towards the door hoping to get out of harms way. "And now that he is dead I can finally get even with him. I want you out of here. Now!" "But what about school," Chase asked. "Who needs school," she said. "Rose, please don’t do this," Chase pleaded. "I’ll do anything. Please. I don’t have anywhere to go." Rose laughed hysterically with a mix of fear and excitement. "Your father should of thought of that when he was out humping other women," she told him. "Oh yeah and don’t think you’re the only one either. There are a whole generation of Chester Harpers running along out there. You‘re not the only one. Just the only one I agreed to take in." Chase placed his hand over his mouth as he vision blurred with tears. He couldn’t believe it. He’d never seen this side of Rosa before but he knew that there was a reason for all the alcohol. He knew that he looked nothing like Rosa, but the thought of her not being his mother never did cross his mind. "What about Sarah," Chase asked. "What are you going to do with Sarah?" Sarah was Chase’s younger sister. She loved her brother and on Rosa’s drunkest days Chase was usually the one taking care of her. It wasn’t that Chase didn’t know that Sarah was Rosa’s blood daughter; the two were splitting images of each other. They both had red hair, freckles, and sharp cheek bones; just like Rosa‘s eldest son Eduardo. Eduardo hated Chase, maybe he’d knew all along. Chase didn’t want anything bad to happen to Sarah; she was only twelve years old. "Don’t worry about her," she told him. "Sarah will be well taken care of." "Rose, I can’t believe you are doing this to me. My father worked thirty hard years on those hotels. He’d never do this if the tables were turned." Chase was right. Chester loved Eduardo like his own. He had put him through college and medical school. The "Harper Hotel" industry had brought the family a lot of money. More than anyone person could spend in a lifetime. Which is why Chase still didn’t understand. Rose laughed again, "well Chase, the shoes aren’t on the other foot now are they?" She pulled a cigarette from her gown and lighted it almost immediately. "Now if you’d just get your things now and leave I’d really appreciate it. You wouldn’t want me to call the cops to escort you out now would you?" Chase shook his head and headed for the stairs. There was nothing he could do. Not now anyway. "No, no, no," she says as she shook her head sharply. "Your bags are already packed and waiting for you in the cab." It took everything in Chase to keep him quiet and from walking over and beating the hell out of Rosa. How could she do this? It still hadn’t hit him yet. "Can I at least say goodbye to Sarah," he asked. "She is my little sister whether you like that or not." Rosa blew a ring of smoke before she decided to answer, "write her a letter. You know the address." She pointed for the door. "Now out, seriously before I call the police." The door shutting in his face was the last thing Chase could remember about that night. He tried hard to block it from his memory but it was impossible. He never did go to Princeton, or any college for that matter; he couldn’t afford it. And he never did see Sarah, Rosa, or Eduardo ever again. However, He did write Sarah everyday that first month but she never returned any of his letters. Maybe they got lost in the mail, or maybe Rosa intercepted them from her. Maybe Sarah just didn’t want anything to do with him anymore. Chase went on to work on boats for the next three years; and when he saved up enough money he brought himself a sail boat. He remembered the days he and his father would go sailing in the hot summer nights. For that reason, he loved to sail, it took him away from the harsh realities of the real world. He enjoyed life now for what it was and adored the little things it had to offer. Like long bike rides on a hot summers afternoon, or the smiling faces of families he sees as he’s walking by. He even sometimes stopped to watch the butterflies dance across the green grass on a sunny spring day. Chase sometimes thought that he was a caterpillar, waiting to morph into a beautiful butterfly and fly away. Years later, Chase learned to wrestle; another passion of his father. An avid fan from the start himself, he grew to adore it more and more over time. However, he had another setback. He broke both of his legs in a motorcycle accident three January's ago and was told that he’d never wrestle; or walk again. As you can see he’s made a ‘REMARKABLE’ recovery. If there is one thing Chase Harper learned from all his experiences, it’s that anything is possible and miracles happen everyday. And above all, "Never lose your smile because life is a long journey." For Chase Harper, his journey has just begun.
