Hoss Richards Biography Real Name Jay-John Justin Richards Nicknames The Corpus Christi Colossus, The Texan Titan, H-Train, Jolly Green Giant Hometown Corpus Christi, Texas Birthday August 16h, 1973 Height 7'2' Weight 425 Fed Titles Soon-to-be savior of FUSE!!!!11!!!12 ... But he'll nab some shiny belts in the process. Fed Awards See above. Biography August 16th, 1973 was a very proud time in the life of Boomer and Missy Richards in the town of Corpus Christi, TX. Richards Industries – a highly successful oil company – was reaching an economic boom in a world where fuel and oil were in high demand. Missy herself was the stay-at-home mom who didn’t need to work a day in her life, but valued the little work around the house that helped to make a growing boy named Jay-John into a hard-working person. However, as the years went on, Jay-John was a little bit slow on the uptake. While most kids learned their three R’s, Jay-John was an outcast. Hated on by rich kids because he was massive at an early age and hated on by the poor kids because he came from such a wealthy upbringing. Jay-John worked as best he could in school and scraped by on the bare minimum. His heart wasn’t into much of his schooling, but by the time he dropped out of school, Boomer found his son a steady job in order to teach him a thing or two about hard work and sacrifice by snagging him a job on one of his father’s off-shore oil rigs. In this place, Jay-John found what he considers to this day to be his second family. The employess on this oilrig were quick to give him the nickname "Hoss" for his gargantuan size and muscular frame. Hoss proved to be a great asset to the company with a Herculean frame that most people would kill for. On their off-duty, the employees would go out and party and would take Jay-John with them to have nights on the town. It was safe to say that for a man of his size, Hoss did pretty well for himself with the ladies. Over time, the partying lifestyle caught up to the young Richards boy. He would put off work, blow off his friends and live a life that would send him on a downward spiral. Eventually, his own parents caught wind of the things that Jay-John had done, then thrust the boy with two choices. One: He comes back home to them and gives up this life of partying where they could find him a new outlet in which to make use of him. Two: He went back to his life of partying and boozing. Disappointed in their son who’d chosen the latter, they disavowed most knowledge of their son and left the mammoth man to fend for himself out in the cold. Boomer even went so far as to blackball his son personally and took it upon himself to smear his name through the mud so most other businesses would have nothing to do with Jay-John. As fate would have it, Jay-John seemed to be destined for more. Professional wrestling had been a side hobby that Hoss liked, but didn’t really pay that much attention to. At a local indy show in the New Mexico area that some friends took him to, a talent scout by the name of Preston Baxter took immediate notice of this seven-footer and offered him a tryout on the spot. Jay-John was sloppier than sin, but Preston would teach the newcomer to mask his weaknesses and accentuate the positives… Size. Strength. Intensity. Jay-John would be a mainstay for a couple years in the TTC (The Texan Company) federation, a small group dedicated to the aspect of old school. During a talent exchange with another promotion in Washington, Jay-John met the famous Silver Brothers, Sonny and Timothy Silver, who ran their own gym, The Silver Lining, out of Seattle. It was a chance for the massive man to go out and see another part of the country he’d never been. He was more than happy to accompany the brothers. They took Jay-John under their wing and to say that it was a chore was an understatement. Jay-John’s size made him somewhat awkward in the ring, but the brothers were determined to take this kid under their wing and show him the ropes. Jay trained alongside another giant – Sonny’s own brother, Rex, who was just getting into the business himself after a former career as a law enforcement officer in the King County area ended on a rather sour note. The two stuck by through thick and thin until a federation finally snatched up Jay-John after several audition tapes. Hoss Richards thanked the brothers for everything they’d done for him and sent him on his way. Today, the giant now finds himself a part of one of the fastest-rising companies on TV today by the name of FUSE. But what of Jay-John’s party-going ways… had he truly learned any sort of discipline from his new chosen profession? Nah, he’s a heel, people! A HEEL, I say! =P See, Hoss remembers all those people that bothered him because of his size. Well, if people had such a problem with it in the past, he's DAMN sure going to make it a problem for whoever crosses his path now. Other Feds FUSE is Hoss' first professional wrestling endeavor. Other Fed Awards ^ Wrestling Style Green-as-Grass Powerhouse Finisher Move The Hoss Toss/Hosstile Takeover Finisher Description Two-Handed Chokebomb/Front Powerslam Finisher Setup Move The Texas Two-Step Setup Description Stands on grounded opponent's back or chest Other Moves PERSONALITY: Hoss Richards is a MASSIVE mountain of a man. Seriously. He’s also incredibly slow on the uptake. He's lost in a sea of his own ignorant bliss and believes that he can 'save' FUSE from what he views as 'boring little people.' He’s most likely going to take up roster space wherever he goes. He can’t wrestle very well aside from doing various actions involving hitting things into paste with his gigantic frame, yelling a lot, and putting on a generic "I’m gonna git you, sucka!" mean face. Like this. >=( See? ENTRANCE: Not much to this, my friends. 'The World Needs A Hero' blasts over the PA and Hoss Richards ducks underneath the entryway to make his apperance. He gets BOOHISSDIEYOUSUCK heat and walks to the ring. You can dice this up any way you want. Give him pyro, give him strobe lights and the like. He’s not picky since he really just wants to hit people and earn a paycheck. APPEARANCE/RING ATTIRE: Appearance: Muscular body akin to that of Paul Wight/The Giant/The Big Show circa WCW, with a few extra inches, height-wise. Black straight shoulder-length hair a la Bradshaw during his earlier APA phase. Caucasian male. Green Hazel eyes. Tattoo of a closed fist on his bare chest is present. Ring attire: Generic black wrestling trunks like something Austin/The Rock would wear with the words "HOSS" on the front and back in gold lettering. Black kneepads and black knee brace wrapped around his left knee. No upper body wear whatsoever (ex. Elbow pads, wrist tape, the like.) ADVANTAGES: -His monstrous size proves difficult for most people to overcome. -His seemingly low IQ belies how dangerous he can be in the ring. -His innate obliviousness to his fellow man allows him to harm without remorse. DETRIMENTS: -Easily manipulated by others in the case of Sonny Silver -Easily distracted. -Slow, SLOW, *SLOW* -Left knee is encased in a knee brace, making it an easy target. IN-RING NOTATIONS: --The ultimate goal of Hoss Richards setting foot in any wrestling organization is to crush all in his path as quickly and as effectively as possible under the guise of 'saving' it from anything he views as bad. --As a wrestler, Hoss Richards is a little better than people of the "big man" ilk (ex. Snitsky, Tomko, Great Khali, Matt Morgan) but not much better. He prefers to use his body as a weapon, barraging people with elbows, headbutts, knees, and punches while wrestling moves appear far and few in between. --As stated earlier in the MWA, Hoss is basically devoid of any knowledge of the people he’s going up against. He assumes that simply because he’s as big as he is, he’ll always win, making his pride his major weakness. While most veterans are considered to be "famous" to some degree, Hoss TRULY has no idea who these people are since he’s actually a two-year veteran of the sport. --As a result of the aforementioned obliviousness, he’ll never get anyone’s name right, referring to them through some sort of bizarre or completely obscure epithet. --He’s a lone wolf, as it were. Face, heel, tweener, child, woman, adult, monkey, whatever. He treats EVERYONE the same. --While he’s large, he’ll sell fairly well for a big man. It was one of the few things during training that he got RIGHT. MOVESET: Hoss Richards prefers to hit things until they become nothing more than a sack of jelly. As a result, nearly all of his matches will simply be him beating the opponent around with only the rare wrestling maneuver thrown in. He makes use of a barrage of nasty headbutts, Open-handed chops, knees, and Clubbing Forearms with the occasional show of strength just to prove that he means business. Basically, you probably aren’t going to see a Hoss Richards match that goes above two stars unless the opponent happens to be either a miracle worker or Jesus himself. The only real wrestling moves that Hoss makes use of -- signatures aside -- are as follows: Vertical Suplex, Back Suplex, Biel Throw, Polish Hammer, Side Slam, and Military Press. Signature Moves: -The Texas Two-Step (Two steps over grounded opponent’s back.) -The Branding (Running Big Boot as done by most WWE giants.) -Texas Tornado (Giant Swing) Weapon of Choice: Branding Iron, his bare hands moreso. Finisher: -The Hoss Toss (Chokebomb) -Hosstile Takeover (Front Powerslam aka The World's Strongest Slam) Entrance Music 'The World Needs A Hero' by Megadeth
