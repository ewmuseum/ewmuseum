Wyatt Connors Biography Real Name Classified Nicknames Wise Guy Hometown Manhattan, NY Birthday Classified Height 5'7' Weight 170 Fed Titles LiveWire Championship (5/25/08-7/20/08); PTC Elite Champion (11/13/08-Current) Biography There are several rumors about Wyatt Connors' life before entering the world of wrestling. They are almost certainly not true. What can be verified is that at the beginning of his career in wrestling, he wasn't even a wrestler. He started out as an agent to up-and-coming stars, and was later hired as a color commentator. He also served as a manager for a while, before getting into the sport itself. While people always remembered who he was, he never acquired much in the way of fame or titles. This began to change when his most promising clients, the tag team of Crash & Burn, made their way to the National Wrestling Council. They quickly became stars in MSW (one of the NWC's regions, based in Minnesota), and Wyatt gained more notoriety along with them. As their fame grew, he thought he'd try his own hand at the NWC. None of the regions would take him, however, which left him only one option--joining Hell's Kitchen Wrestling, one of the charter members of the fledgling Affiliated Leagues. He blazed through the competition during a tournament to determine the inaugural champion...but at the time of his first defense, it was announced that the region was moving up to the NWC...and moving out to Phoenix, Arizona. Wyatt, a native New Yorker, was obviously less than pleased, and took out his displeasure on the management. This would mark the beginning of a trend. PUW was also where Wyatt Connors would achieve his greatest success--not only was he their first champion, and only two-time champion...but on the NWC World Title's first stop in Phoenix, Connors defeated 'Peerless' Hunter Sabuani to become the World Champion. He defeated two more NWC legends, Lance Marshall and Scorn, in his lone successful defense, before dropping the belt to Jeremy Thomas. It would be the last gold Wyatt Connors would hold for six years. This didn't bother him, as he had bigger fish to fry. First, there was his attempt to deep six PUW by berating the company at every turn. After the NWC went through a restructuring, Wyatt Connors was shipped off to NWC: South, and didn't like it there any better. This made it all the sweeter when Ben Van Iten announced his intention to shut down the National Wrestling Council. Wyatt Connors was the most vocal member of the opposition, and nobody suspected he was in on the con...at least, not until the Return to Purgatory. The main event was the final of a 64-man tournament: Devin Fox vs. Wyatt Connors, with the winner deciding who received the controlling stock in the NWC. Connors, of course, backed Alex 'Desade' Pierce, who pledged to keep the company open. Fox was part of the army of Chris Schukar, the NWC's first Executive Director, and had sworn to finish what he had started. To add another twist, the guest referee was Hunter Sabuani, who now found himself on the same side as Connors. Devin Fox ended up winning the match, but in a shocking turn of events, he chose Desade as the recipient of the stock. Not to be outdone, Desade then closed the NWC, with Connors and Sabuani grinning ear to ear beside her. For the next few months, the group (known then as the Illuminati) made their presence felt in the regions that formed after the NWC's closure--Connors in Seventh Circle Wrestling; the others in the Diamond Wrestling Syndicate. While these were the most successful out of the four erstwhile NWC regions, they closed in less than a year, leaving Wyatt Connors outside the wrestling world once again. He resurfaced with little fanfare in less warning in late 2006, after securing a roster spot at Old Line Wrestling, one of the few surviving territories from the now-defunct CAL. He was teamed with his partners and lackeys, Deacon Dale and T.J. Ratigan--according to Connors, OLW would not sign him to a singles contract. Together, the group known as The Conspiracy took the tag division by storm, capturing the Trios Tag Team Titles. Around the same time, OLW joined the World Wrestling Alliance...and it wasn't long before Connors wormed his way to the top there as well. The Conspiracy received the World Tag Team Title shot at the WWA pay-per-view International Wars. Before the match, though, Connors was brutally attacked, leaving him unable to participate. Without their leader, Deacon Dale and T.J. Ratigan--two career losers up to this point--still managed to win the belts from the Texas Outlaws, a pair of WWA Hall of Famers. Connors swore he would find and punish those responsible for the attack, but nothing ever came of it. It later came out that Connors had orchestrated the assault himself, and had been faking injury ever since. Connors made his last appearance in WWA in late January 2008. He had spread word that he had a major event planned for the Crown of War event, but WWA owner Victor Mandrake came to him with an offer instead: take a lot of money, and in return, disappear forever from the company. Connors took the offer, only minutes before he was scheduled to manage Dale and Ratigan in yet another title defense, which they would go on to lose. Once more, Connors fell off the face of the earth. And once more, he has reappeared...but not as the double-dealing triple-bastard we all know and hate. No, it would seem that during his last trip into the unknown, he has found God. This might be a good thing. Considering the number of people in SCCW who would love nothing more than to break his neck, he's going to need all the help he can get.
