Trendkiller Biography Real Name Joshua Chris Charles Barton Nicknames The Great Southern Killer of Trends; The Big, Bald Badass; The Beast Hometown Dallas, TX Birthday April 20th, 1976 Height 7'1' Weight 365 Fed Titles N/A Fed Awards N/A Other Fed Awards Several Wrestling Style [ see moves list ] Finisher Move The Trendkill Finisher Description [ see moves list ] Finisher Setup Move [ see moves list ] Setup Description [ see moves list ] Other Moves WRESTLING STYLE Powerhouse & semi-technical. Using his size to his advantage, Trendkiller uses a powerful combination of slams and suplexes, strikes and excruciatingly painful holds to wear down his victim until either of his three finishers can be performed. Not being much of an aerial performer, Trendkiller prefers to keep his feet on the ground, although he is known to deliver a couple of sick bumps from high altitude to further take advantage of an injured or incapacitated opponent. Most commonly though, these moves does not involve the big man jumping, but rather his victim falling. Although his size does not give him any obvious advantage in the ground game, Trendkiller’s height helps him get elevation when using submission holds on an opponent. Especially his finisher, SOUTHERN COMFORT, is a devastating hold due to Trendkiller’s sheer size. His immense strength also helps in the submission department, although his technique is not the all-time best. Brawling, now that is Trendkiller’s back yard. You do not want to go toe-to-toe with the great southern killer of trends, because if you do, his reach and power is enough to knock anyone in the world of wrestling out. His right, gloved fist is the size of a saucepan and once that baby strikes home, there are star spangled banners for whoever receives it. THE COMPLETE MOVES LIST Simply put: if the move is not on the list, Trendkiller won’t do it. With a few exceptions, as I haven’t written out everything that can be done with a chair, with cage netting, irish whipping the opponent into the steel post or the guard rail instead of into the turnbuckle or ropes, etc. But this is as "complete" as a moves list can get without spelling everything out… FINISHER #1: THE TRENDKILL; A chokeslam, but instead of grabbing the opponent’s throat, Trendkiller grabs the face of the victim, like Fritz Von Erich’s "Iron Claw", and then performs the chokeslam. He often pauses a bit with the hand around the victim’s face, squeezing and signalling the move by doing a "devil" sign with his left hand over his head (pinky and index finger extended, the hand otherwise balled in a fist) however, the move can also be performed lightning fast – without the posing - should the need arise. FINISHER #2: SET UP MOVE: SUICIDE RIDE; Hammerlock body slam. SOUTHERN COMFORT; Wakigatame arm bar, after a Side arm takedown (or Suicide Ride into the Wakigatame arm bar). FINISHER #3 (rather uncommon): FACE IT; Double underhook jumping spinning facebuster (a.k.a. "Angel’s Wings", performed by Christopher Daniels in TNA). TRADEMARK MOVES: INSIDE OUT; Spinning short arm clothesline. Trendkiller grabs the victim’s right wrist with his left hand, then spins around 360 degrees while holding on to the victim, using centrifugal forces to add momentum. As the 360 is completed, TK takes a step forward and inwards, smashing his lower left arm across the chest/neck of the victim. EAT DIRT; Double underhook face breaker. Trendkiller bends the victim down and hooks both the victim's arms. Then TK jumps the victim up and then drops the victim face first into his knee. THE EQUALIZER; Guillotine face driver. The victim is bent over. Trendkiller places one leg over the back of the victim's neck and jumps up, using his weight to drive the victim face first into the mat. Sometimes the TK holds the victim's arm as he throws his leg over the victim’s neck. TOUCHDOWN; Shoulder tackle. Goldberg and Edge calls it a Spear. HUNG OUT; An elevated guillotine choke. Can be modified into… LAID OUT; Grounded guillotine choke. FATAL FIGURE FIVE; First, a Crippler crossface-esque takedown (Fujiwara arm bar style), then the victim’s arm is pinned between TK’s knees, then TK traps the opponent’s face in an underhook, effectively pinning the victim’s face into his armpit. TK sits up on his knees and at the same time bends the victim’s head and neck backwards, in effect attempting to break the victim’s neck, just like the Crippler Crossface, but using the "underside" of the arm. (This move can be used as a finisher on a very worn down opponent, and the move is very rare even then.) THE CRUSH CRASH; An outright brutal Vertebreaker. BASIC STRATEGY: WHEN WINNING: When Trendkiller can feel the whiff of victory, he usually goes all out to try and take advantage of any weakness displayed by the opponent. If the opponent is showing signs of fatigue, he tries to up the tempo; if the opponent is clearly hurting in a particular body part, TK will focus on that body part with holds, slams and strikes, trying anything to hurt the opponent even more, until applying a submission hold on the injured body part. Alternatively, he might well go for his #1 finisher The Trendkill after pummelling his opponent senseless, to prevent a counter-attack. When he spots an opportunity, he goes for it and it either makes or breaks the match for him. WHEN LOSING: When Trendkiller is on the receiving end of the loser’s alley, he tries to evade his opponent as much as possible to catch his wind and then try to strike whenever the opponent shows a sign of weakness or is not 100% focused on Trendkiller and the match. If he is taking a bad beating, he might well try to get out of his predicament by cheating – if the referee’s back is turned – or by delivering a finisher move "out of nowhere". He is extremely strong headed and if caught with a hurting body part, he will try to keep it out of the way of his opponent, but still wrestle on as usual, trying not to show the pain or fatigue. IF HURT: If Trendkiller gets hurt, he tries to protect that body part at all costs. If it’s a leg that’s hurt, he might well try to take his opponent into a ground game; if it’s his arm, then he might well try to take the fight to the outside, where his long limbs give him an advantage. Basically, he will try to ignore the pain, but protect himself more vigorously, trying to evade his opponent. BACK UP STRATEGY: Should everything else fail, Trendkiller will try to cheat his way to winning. If he is a champion and his opponent might beat him, he will try to get himself DQ’ed, so that at least he won’t lose the title. He will try any dirty trick in the book to get himself out on top. Also, he will try to damage one of his opponent’s body part beyond functionality if it can make his own position better. He is ruthless and will prove it time and time again.
