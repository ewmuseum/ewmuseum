Reyna Preciosa Biography Real Name Julissa Minorez Nicknames Jules Hometown San Antonio, Tx(currently residing in Austin, Tx) Birthday February 2, 1987 Height 5'6' Weight 124 Fed Titles Former Livewire champion Biography Born to Benito and Maria Minorez, Julissa is a second generation wrestler. While his name is hardly recognized nowadays, Benito "Benny Minor" Minorez once terrorized regional territories throughout the United States. Eventually finding his niche in the National Wrestling Council’s Pacific Region alongside his brother Mike, before his body finally started giving him enough aches and pains that he had to retire. Which, he told himself, was good in some ways, because it allowed him to dedicate himself to spending time with his family, and also to work in the wrestling school he had established during the latter years of his career. Benito was a little upset when he found out his wife had given birth to a daughter, though he had tried his best to keep it from showing. All of the dreams he had of training a son to follow in his footsteps had quickly vanished, a feeling that vanished as well once he held his new daughter Julissa in his arms. While he was hardly around during the early part of her childhood, he did everything he needed to support his family and provide a good life for them. Julissa took to her father’s craft at an early age, staying up late at night on the weekends to watch the independent promotions he worked for on the television. Of course, being on the road meant he missed that sight, he never saw her eyes sparkling as he and her uncle lifted their first major promotion’s tag team titles. Her mother did her best to draw her attention from her father’s chosen career, hoping ballet lessons and gymnastics would occupy her time enough to lessen the effects that wrestling were having on her. All it did was help her become more flexible and agile…a trait her father would learn years later. In 2003, Benito finally hung up his boots. The strain on his limbs, as well as the constant nagging from his wife to give it up before he injured himself to the point where he would not be able to walk, had become unbearable. And you know what they say, "Those who cannot do…teach." For a while, Jules' life was fairly stable. Everything changed, though, when she turned sixteen. Benito forgot what scheduling conflict had occurred that day, but he had been forced to take her along to one of his training sessions. He hoped the TV in his office would be enough to keep her occupied and away from the gym, where half naked men would be working out and sparring in the ring. At the end of the day, however, she had not even touched the remote. She was fascinated with the basic moves her father was teaching his students, following along as the workout became more advanced. He still remembered the ride home that night, when she, warily, asked him to 'teach her how to fly like that.' He had resisted, at first. Having spent so much time in the business, he knew the way people looked at women in the sport, how they wewre treated as nothing more than eye candy. They were there to show off their breasts, flash a little skin and give a few skirt shots to the guys in the first few rows. He didn't want to expose her to that, but he also couldn't keep her away from the ring. It was in her blood, he assumed. That same drive that motivated him was now coursing through her veins. And like a junkie with her first fix, Julissa couldn't get enough. Over the past three years, Benito taught his daughter everything he knew about wrestling. After all his regular classes, he and Juli would run through move after move, until he began to feel like he was back in active competition again, with how much she wore him out. Eventually, a few of his students heard about his 'side project' and took time out of their schedule to work with her as well. Benito's breath caught in his throat the first time he saw his baby girl spring off the ropes and land a springboard hurracanrana on Diego Juarez, one of his most promising students. Something about the whole enterprise, however, made him melancholy. No matter how good his daughter became, there would be no way he could allow her to travel down the same road that had taken up so much of his life. He had enjoyed spending time with his daughter, but deep inside, he felt it was in service of nothing. He could not allow it to become anything more.
