Logic Biography Real Name David Walter Smith Nicknames the Pensive Punisher, the Immense Intellectual, the Thinking-Man's Grappler Hometown Orlando, FL Birthday April 18, 1978 Height 6'9' Weight 300 Fed Titles None. Fed Awards Agent of SCCW superstar Phillip Kennedy Biography The agent of SCCW superstar Phillip Kennedy, David Walter Smith is nonetheless best known to a legion of pro wrestling fans as Logic, the 6'9' intellectual man mountain who appeared in the fWo Survivor IV contest, and eventually got a spot on the roster, earning shots at the fWo World, Internet, and Tag Team Championships, though coming up unsuccessful in all. Injured in PRIME in 2008, the torn ligaments in his leg took slightly over a year to heal and rehabilitate. At the request of his client, Phillip Kennedy, he will be stepping into the ring for the first time since his injury. Will it be a one time event? Or will Logic's love of competition return to him, and inspire a full time return for perhaps the most cerebral man in the sport of kings? Other Feds fWo, OCW, XUW, PRIME Other Fed Awards XUW Television Champion (as Professor Wisdom), third-place finisher in fWo Survivor IV Wrestling Style Technical powerhouse, emphasis on high impact slams Finisher Move Proof Positive Finisher Description Steve Williams-style backdrop driver Finisher Setup Move Inescapable Logic Setup Description Swinging full nelson. Usually applied to weaken a foe, before Logic throws them forward into the corner. He will almost always follow this with Proof Positive. Other Moves Wrestling Style: Technical powerhouse. Logic is well-schooled in the technical elements of wrestling. This doesn't mean that he uses lots of headlocks, but rather, he knows leverage points and trains strength considerably so that he knows the best ways to throw around opponents. Moveset: Anytime Moves: HARD Straight Right, Palm Strikes, Delayed Vertical Suplex, Kneelifts, Powerslam, Big Boot, Bearhug, Sidewalk Slam, Corner Splash, Standing Splash Aerial Moves: Top Rope Splash, Top Rope Diving Clothesline, Vader-style Pump Splash Power Moves: Crucifix Powerbomb, Multiple Backbreaker, German Suplex, Sit-Down Spinebuster, Delayed Brainbuster, Death Valley Driver, Screwdriver, Press Slam Variants (to Samoan Drop, to Over-the-Shoulder Powerslam, to Gutbuster, to Backbreaker, to Death Valley Driver, etc) Submission Moves: Standing Anklelock, Crossface Chickenwing. Rare Moves (PPV or big match only): Elbow Suicida, Top Rope Plancha/Splash to the floor Signature Moves: The Socratic Method: Two-handed sitout chokebomb. In a lot of ways, this is Logic's 'test' move. If he's able to hit this and get a close two count with it, he begins shifting gears and moving towards going towards his finishing sequence. Flash of Brilliance: Inverted Rydeen Bomb. Logic grabs an opponent facing away from him under the armpits, elevates them, and then sits out to pancake them face first to the canvas. Sometimes this will directly setup Proof Positive, particularly against dangerous or persistent opponents. Strengths: 1) Intellect. Logic's smart. SMART, smart. Genius IQ, member of Mensa, the whole nine yards. When it comes to gameplanning, there are few better than Logic. He's not going to make cardinal mistakes. No, he will make you make those mistakes instead. 2) Well-rounded. Not many 6'9' guys who have an anklelock, a crossface chickenwing, and a splash off the top rope in their arsenal. Logic does. His size makes even simple submission moves harder to escape, and a simple splash off the top potential death. He uses his power for more than just power moves, and his arsenal of moves reflects this. 3) Knock-Out Finisher. Proof Positive is a nuclear bomb in Logic's arsenal. He can pull it out quickly, from a lot of situations, and if he hits you with it you are most likely NOT getting up. Weaknesses: 1. Submissions. He doesn't tap out EASY, per se, but where someone like Lindsay Troy would fight tapping like Hell in a throwaway match on ReVolution, Logic will tap if he can't find a way out and fears the risk of lasting/permanent damage. Better to live to fight another day. 2. Rigid. As smart as he is, he is equally stubborn. He develops strategies based on probabilities and analysis. Opponents who take a lot of chances give him fits, as he will expect them to crash and burn more often than they likely will. Once he determines a plan, he will stick to it with almost slavish dedication, out of fear of improvising causing a loss. 3. Frustration. He is prone to being frustrated against particularly tenacious opponents, who can survive a lot of his big bombs. On the uber-rare occassion Proof Positive is kicked out of, he will lose his calm vaneer and become VERY vulnerable to quick flash pins. Flash pins in general are a weakness of his, especially once his head's starting to go in bad places. In-Ring Tendencies: 1. Logic will try to keep matches in the ring at all costs. He has very limited hardcore experience (mostly involving Lindsay Troy and Chinese throwing stars) and tries to avoid it if at all possible. Fewer variables that he has to keep track of within the squared circle. 2. Logic, not being an idiot, is pretty much immune to distractions by managers, people appearing on the tron, etc. If a referee is distracted, he will generally physically turn the referee around and make the official count falls. 3. His general gameplan is to start off slow, testing out an opponent with basic moves and strikes, building to his more potent offense. Once he gets a few close twos, he will move to try to finish with Proof Positive. Entrance Music 'Mars, the Bringer of War' by Gustav Holst
