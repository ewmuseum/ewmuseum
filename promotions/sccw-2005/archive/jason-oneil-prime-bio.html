Jason O&#039;Neil Biography Real Name Jason O'Neil Nicknames 'The Scarecrow', 'Absolutely', Jace, JO, The Jackass. Hometown Cleveland, Ohio Birthday February 11, 1971 Height 6'2' Weight 253 Fed Titles None. Fed Awards None Other Feds GCW, FSW, RPW, OSW. Wrestling Style Technical/Power Finisher Move 'The Absolution' Finisher Description Crossface Chicken Wing, with full body grapevine. Finisher Setup Move 'The Redemption' Setup Description A Shoulderbreaker. Other Moves During the feeling out stage of the match, O'Neil tends to get very straight forward, almost like Bret Hart used to be. You knew you were getting the same ten moves, you just didn't know what order. Same holds true for O'Neil. Early in the match, he'll use a variety of feeling out moves, aside from the traditional collar and elbow tie ups. -- Armbar -- Spinning Armbar -- Hammerlock -- Irish Whip -- Clothesline -- Standing Elbow -- Elbow drop off the ropes -- Leg drop off the ropes -- Headlock -- Reverse chinlock -- Vertical Suplex Most of O'Neil's mid-match moves will revolve around incapacitating both the legs and left shoulder of his opponent. Shoulderbreakers, spinning arm locks with kicks to the shoulder blade, hammerlocks, arm bars, etc., will be his favored attacks then. -- Shoulder Slicer (A foot in the pit, a lift of the arm, and then a forceful kick to the armpit. Kayfabed not to be a move to do actual damage) -- Kneebar -- The Flying Leg Submission (You may remember Brock Lesnar's leg behind the head submission. Same thing. If not, don't use it.) -- Shoulder DDT - Like a DDT, but, with a straight arm, O'Neil drops his off elbow into the shoulder, and hits the mat. This will always be followed up with an armbar, but, never a submission. He's toying with his opponent. -- Pressure (Comin Down on You) (Think Pedigree. Except, instead of dropping down, a la HHH, O'Neil simply pulls backwards into the corner, hanging his opponent upside down, with lots of pressure on his shoulders. Obviously, this takesa 5 count to break, and he always will.) Special Moves - These aren't all used in one match. Usually, he'll toss one or two in there, and that's that. Use at your own discretion, please. Hammer On DDT - After kicking an opponent in the stomach, O'Neil will come off the ropes, and use the foreward momentum to DDT his opponent, with a sliding motion. This is usually used with more athletic opponents. Alternate Finisher FACE FIRST! - A Chris Jericho style bulldog that almost always ends with O'Neil going outside the ring, like Jericho nearly did everytime he used it. The Quadfecta - O'Neil will go after his opponent with Irish Whips, sending them off the ropes, and directly into his waiting knee. This is a smooth, fluid motion of irish whip to the ropes, holding the arm, knee to the gut. He does this four times in a row before letting go for the final one, and dropping a knee to the top of the head, HHH style. Tilt-a-Whirl Spinebuster - Like a Tilt-a-whirl slam, but, with a spinebuster. Usually a pin follows, but, not always. It's great as a momentum shifter. ---------- Submissions- O'Neil is a submissionist. While any idiot can finish with a pinfall on an opponent that doesn't know he's being beaten, it takes talent to make a man realize that he's awake, he can't move, and he'd better submit to fight another day, or spend some time rehabilitating an awful injury. Here are his most common submissions - Compression to the 5th Degree. - legs, back Remember the Stump Puller? Yeah. Remember the one Konnan does, where he puts the dude's legs in the figure four, and pull up? Yeah, again. Figure Four - If you don't know, you need to be shot. And killed. Quickly. The Legend - mouth - A tribute to Mick Foley, occasionally, O'Neil will bust out with a mandible claw. He will not, however, pull off a boot, and yank off a sock, nor will he pull one out of his crotch. He doesn't use this often, and uses it more as a reversal, momentum shifter. Pretzelated - legs, abdomen It's a grapevine with body ride. Think of it as a horizontal abdominal stretch. Can also be shifted into a tease pin Burning River - lower back Canadian Back Breaker. Simple, eh? The Falsetto - the ankle and balls Picture the Kurt Angle Leglock, the one he does when he wraps his entire body around the opponent's leg. Now, take one of those legs, and use it to squash the opponent's nuts. Like 'The Soprano', except this one would actually work. >=) ---------- Suplex School - A direct retaliation to Chris Storm's claim of being the master of the Suplex, O'Neil came up with these suplex chains to taunt Storm. They never wrestled, but, the chains still exist. Pick one, if you'd like, and use that, once. Suplex 101 - Vertical Variations Vertical Suplex => Hanging Vertical Suplex => Snap Suplex => Fisherman's Suplex => Pin Suplex 201 - Remedial Release Belly to Belly => Belly to Back (German) => Gutwrench Overhead Release Suplex => Spinning Sidewinder Suplex (This is a vertical suplex, hold for a second, and then turn and release, face first, in a horizontal position. Suplex 306 - Themes of Attack Butterfly suplex (back) => Pancake Suplex (chest) => Hanging Vertical Brainbuster (head and neck) => Fisherman Suplex (back) Suplex 120 - Repetition • 3 vertical suplexes • 3 release belly to belly suplexes • Triple Salto Suplex • Double Belly to back, followed by a Belly to Back, Cradle Suplex ---------- Alternate Finishers - Sometimes, the chickenwing's not going to cut it. Sometimes, we need something crazier. Here then, is the crazy: The Nightmare Driver - This is the Rikishi style piledriver that O'Neil used as 'The Dark Messiah'. This is pretty much a hit and quit move. If it's used, it should end the match. It's a legacy move, and it's just a simple request to do that. The Fall From Grace - This is a top rope full nelson bulldog. This is usually used to go through a table, or something similar. O'Neil will always hurt himself after this move because, well, it hurts. Flip Flop, Hip Hop, Tip Top Drip Drop and STOP! - This is a 360 corkscrew legdrop. This is a move that if O'Neil goes there and uses it, will ALWAYS result in the W. Always. No matter what. Don't tease it, and don't use it unless you're going to deliver the move. In fact, ask me if you're even going to consider it. Please. ---------- The Psychology of Jason O'Neil - O'Neil believes in Honor over everything. He's not one that will take advantage of an opponent, and will rely on their mistakes to turn against them. Regardless, O'Neil will not allow a match to get out of control. If he feels things are getting too fierce, or an opponent is shooting, he'll lock on a painful submission hold for a bit, not to get someone to submit, but to remind them that he's not one an opponent wants to take liberties with. He also doesn't do the punch kick type stuff that others do. He's methodical in his approach. If he's going to try and finish a match with one finisher, he'll work over that part of the body until such time that he feels it's sufficiently worn down. Then, he'll lock in a submission hold, exploiting that body part. Against smaller foes (-30lbs or more) - O'Neil will wrestle a very grounded game. He'll try and take away the speed of his opponent, and equalize it with his power game. He will always work on the legs first, then the shoulder. Against same-sized foes (+/- 30lbs) - He'll wrestle a very technical game, mixing power and speed. He favors much more of a logical process of moves, meaning he'll almost always stick to the shoulder attacks. Against larger foes - (+30lbs or more) - He will try and rely on speed, but, he's not particularly fast. O'Neil does not wrestle a bigger wrestler well, unless he gets him down. After that, he will do whatever he can to keep him on the mat. There will be no attempts at anything other than pure ground wrestling. Against women - O'Neil will always make the mistake of taking them lightly, using lighter than normal moves, etc. Take advantage of this with a well timed faked injury, or, a series of ass-kicking moves. O'Neil is 0-5 against women, lifetime, because he doesn't match up to the speed potential. Again, exploit that and his tentativeness. In tag matches - Outside of kayfabe, O'Neil usually prefers the face in peril role in tag matches, being the guy taking the beatings. Since he's mostly a submission wrestler, his style doesn't lend well to tag matches, because you really can't use submissions in tag matches. That said, with a good partner, he'll develop a really good chemistry, and O'Neil tends to be the 'power man' in the tag team. Think of him as the Herculese in Power and Glory, the Hawk in LOD, or the Niedhart in the Hart Foundation. In cluster matches - O'Neil will take the shortest route to victory. He doesn't see them as wrestling matches, and so, he'll just kick ass and chew bubblegum, and... well, shit. He's all outta bubble gum. In three/four-ways - He hates these matches, because as a submissionist, he's prone to losing. To counter that, He will almost always attempt to take the stronger of the two opponents out, and then concentrate on getting the weaker one to tap out without the submission attempt being broken by his second/third opponent.
