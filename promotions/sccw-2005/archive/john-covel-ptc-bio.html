John Covel Biography Real Name Jonathan David Covel Nicknames 'The American Pastime'; 'The Insane Extremist'; 'The Paragon of Piledrivers' Hometown Mazon, IL Birthday February 15th, 1980 Height 6'4' Weight 240 Fed Titles Former 2 Time FUSE Wrestling Universal Champion; Former LiveWire Champion, Former CUSTOM C.H.A.O.S Champion Fed Awards Triple Crown Winner, Longest reigning LiveWire Champion in FUSE History; FUSE Wrestler of the Year 2006; FUSE Face of the Year 2006 Biography John Covel was born February 15th, 1980 in the small town of Morris, Illinois. His parents are Thomas Covel and Liz Covel. He led an average life as the middle of three children. Older brother Josh, born 1978, and younger sister, Lauren, born 1986. He was brought up in a slightly above average life style. Thomas worked as a plant manager at the nearby Caterpillar plant, bringing home modest income. Elizabeth had small part time jobs until 1990, when she took a full time job at the Grundy County Sheriff's Department as a secretary. John was brought up to love sports. The entire family were HUGE NASCAR fans. They also lived on baseball and football. Like every little six year old, John entered Tee Ball. He had fun just being a kid, but took a liking to the game as well. Growing up just an hour and a half from downtown Chicago, John was caught up in the South Side/North Side rivalry. He and his dad took their oath as Cubs fan early on, and that was that. They've always been those 'Wait 'til next year.' people. As John continued to grow, so did his interest in baseball. He kept good grades in school and always made sure to catch two things on TV: any Cubs game he could and WWF wrestling. Wrestling was cool, but John buried himself in baseball. He had advanced from tee ball to Little League, playing right field. He grew nearer to the end of his fourth grade year, playing in the final game of the 'season.' A strong throw to the plate to put the tying run down after a fly out turned some heads. His coach suggested he might want to start working on pitching. So he did. When he hit grade school, he came on as a pitcher. The small sanction of Little League teams had never seen anything like him. A ten year old throwing consistent 50 - 60 MPH fastballs. He stepped out of Little League his sixth grade year and joined the school team. He worked on his skills day in and day out. When he entered his seventh grade year, John had three great pitches in his arsenal. His fastball had improved to 65 - 75 MPH and he added a wicked curveball that dropped 1 - 7 like you wouldn't believe. On top of that was a mean change-up, fooling hitters left and right. He added a good slider near the end of his eighth grade year, and took all four pitches to high school. He wanted to try out for the varsity team, but the school didn't allow Freshman on the Varsity team. They, instead, played their own Freshman team games. John made sure he turned some heads. In his first high school start, John struck out thirteen men and gave up only three hits. He went on to lead the team with thirteen wins, 98 strikeouts and an ERA of 1.89, a school record for freshman. It was no surprise that he made the Varsity team his Sophomore year. He remained the Varsity team's number one starter the final three years of his high school days. His sophomore year was a lot like his first, fourteen wins, ERA of 2.01, 206 strikeouts. But his Junior year is what really got everyone's attention if they weren't looking already. John became the first player in school history to win twenty games as a starter. He was a machine. He finished the season with an ERA of 1.98 and 215 strikeouts. John led his team to the state semi-finals but fell just one game shy of the schools first State Championship berth in twenty-five years. Not only did the college scouts gather to check John out as he entered his Senior season, but MLB scouts became more frequent as well. John was in reach of three state records that year as well. And he got them. He struck out 237 men, giving him the strikeout record. His career ERA ended at 1.87 with a senior year of 1.32, lowest in state history, and his final season's win total was sixteen, giving him a state high 63 career wins. His four pitches had only gotten better and were very appealing to three big league ball clubs. The White Sox, Yankees, Cardinals and Cubs all showed interest in the high school sensation, but he passed all four offers up, including his life-long dream of playing for his beloved Chicago Cubs. He, instead, chose a full scholarship to the University of Illinois and pitched as an Illini. His impressive performance continued in college, setting a new school record for ERA with a low four year total of 1.21. Though he didn't break records in college like he did in high school, the major league scouts still kept their eye on him. After graduating with a Bachelors Degree in Meteorology/Broadcasting. It seemed stupid to him to not enter the First Year Player's draft. He did and it was a good choice. He was taken as the Chicago Cubs first round pick in 2002. They saw the promise he held, having amazing control and command at 22 and signed him to a four year contract worth a grand total of $950,000. That's rich for a draftee. He was placed at Double A West Tennesee for a whopping....three starts before beign promoted ot the Triple A Iowa team. Things went great his first season in the organization. He hit 23 and looked forward to his first complete season pitching for Triple A Iowa. But on June 3rd, he recieved a call from Cub's General Manager Jim Hendry himself. John had been activated to the Cub's big league roster, effective the next morning. He was ecstatic, buit kept his emotions in check, he still had a game to start that day for Iowa. John had always shown lots of emotion on the diamond. He was intense, just like Cubs ace Carlos Zambrano. John let his nerves get to him that day. He hit a total of three batters, only the second, third and fourth of his career. Things got a little out of hand, and his emotions went wild. For now, we'll just sum it up like this: fight + ejection + pissed off + bat + umpire's back and face = lifetime ban from baseball in USA. That story is going to be explained later. John needed a new sport to occupy his tiem while he sorted things out with baseball. So he took the money he had left and entered a wrestlign school. For the last two years, he's been bouncing around the mid-west, looking for a home. Appearance: Not ripped but not flabby. He has the build of Mark Prior, Dontrelle Willis. His arms are larger than normal. He has deep blue eyes. His hair, always dyed blonde can vary in length. Sometimes he'll have it buzzed all over, buzzed on the sides and about an inch or so long on top, or shaggy. He normally keeps it groomed to the buzzed and short combo. He can be seen outside the ring wearing somewhat preppy clothes. Aeropastle, American Eagle, Hollister Khaki cargo pants or shorts. He wears dressy shirts, but also likes to wear t-shirts with smart ass sayings or baseball jerseys. Anywhere from an hour before his match to the hour following his match he will be seen in his wrestling attire. He comes to the ring in a pair of special made shorts. They are based on mesh workout shorts, two-toned, black and dark blue. The majority is black, but down the leg of each side is a wide, dark blue stripe with silver lining containing it. They hang just below his knees, exposing his black ace bandage knee support on the right knee. He wrestles shirtless, but comes to the ring with the ACTUAL Cubs jersey he was made for his Major League debut, name on the back and everything. He could wear his home version, his grey away version or his blue alternate. He wears simple black Vans as shoes. He does have a tatoo on his right bicep. It's a sun-shaped figure with a small baseball inside it.
