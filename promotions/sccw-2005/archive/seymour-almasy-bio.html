Seymour Almasy Biography Real Name Jason Seymour Wilson Nicknames 'The Wild Card', 'The Protagonist' Hometown The Velvet Room (actually residing in Montreal, Quebec, Canada) Birthday June 12, 1981 Height 5'8' Weight 192 Fed Titles None. Fed Awards None. Biography Seymour Almasy debuted in the Universal Wrestling Federation in 2002. The man who would go on to be arguably Primetime Central's best known and most loved (or hated, depending on the day) character certainly had his quirks. Among these quirks was a steadfast belief that he was, in fact, living the life of an RPG protagonist, a hero aware of the fourth wall and able to use that knowledge to protect those he loved and achieve success beyond his wildest dreams. This fantasy world ended when the love of his life, Laura Marie Winters, explained to him what the world really was. After this epiphany, Seymour rose to even greater heights, only to have those heights interspersed with catastrophic losses. By the end of 2008, Seymour Almasy was out of wrestling yet again, this time, by most accounts, his absence would be permanent. For the few that knew him, life for Seymour was simple. He worked thirty hours a week at a GameStop a few blocks from his New York City apartment, not because he needed the money, but because he enjoyed being around games and needed something to do with his excess time. It was thus to the astonishment of practically everyone, the man known as the Final Fantasy appeared at SCCW's flagship Defiance II PPV, signing a contract proffered to him by Dr. Nigel Kensington. In doing so, Almasy became perhaps the biggest name signing in the young history of Sin City Championship Wrestling. Why would a man considered by most to be over the hill return to perhaps the most inhospitable environment in wrestling? Some say the desire for the spotlight overtook him. Some cite his competitive drive. Almasy himself has not given an answer. Considering his recent lack of will to talk much, it isn't likely that he will give an answer. All he will say when pressed is that January 3, 2009 changed his life, irrevocably. Maybe it's all in his head...but ever since then, the world seems somehow different. ------------------------------------------------------------ Sin City Championship Wrestling Match History (0-0-0) Temptation: Temple February 10, 2009 Seymour Almasy vs. Roderick Ashe Other Feds Almost everywhere by now, really. Winner of the first-ever PTC Cruiserweight Tournament. GTT5 Semi-Finalist. UWF Grand Slam Champion. Dozens of other Wrestling Style Counter-oriented all-rounder, with an emphasis on high flying and precision striking. Finisher Move The Evoker Finisher Description Step-up knee strike to the temple, with theatrics. See moveset for more specifics. Finisher Setup Move No definite, always used setup. He does like to use the Truth-Seeker to set it up, though Setup Description See above. The Truth-Seeker is a springboard dropkick, thrown to any part of the body. Again, see the moveset for details. Other Moves Finishers: Known for having an excess of these, Almasy advances on SCCW in 2009 with but two. The Evoker, however, is easily his most powerful finisher to date. Should it be needed, Orgia Mode provides Almasy with the sort of firepower needed to take on the most hateful of foes. It is rumored that he possesses one final move, to be revealed when the truth is finally discovered... 1. The Evoker: Step-up knee strike to the temple. Similar to a Shining Wizard in concept, but the knee is thrown to the temple, instead of the face. Usually set up a la the Wizard, though Almasy will throw the strike stepping up to the apron, the ring ropes, or anything he can reach. Seymour's agility and balance mean he can pull this off from a surprising amount of positions. The specific placement of the strike to the temple makes it far more damaging then most such moves, and will often knock an opponent clean out. 2. Trademark Moves: These are Almasy's 'named' moves. With the exception of the Galactic Punt, he pulls these out mid to late match with an eye on winning, or wearing down the opponent to set up the Evoker. 1.) Truth-Seeker: Springboard missile-style dropkick. Generally, Almasy runs to the corner and then springs back at an opponent with it, but he can use the middle of the ropes as well. If he's looking to set up the Evoker, he'll aim for the kneecap, but he can hit high, middle, or low if he so chooses. 2.) Galactic Punt: Jump-spinning roundhouse kick, aka, Trouble in Paradise. Unique among Almasy's signatures in that he goes for this early in matches. He'll use it like a dropkick at that stage of a contest. 3.) 4.) Operation Babe Hunt!: Somersault tope con hilo to an opponent on the floor. Almasy lands on his feet out of the move. It gets its name from the fact that after landing it, Almasy will search out a female fan wearing apparel of his in the first few rows nearby, hop into the crowd if need be, kiss the woman on the cheek, and then roll back into the squared circle. 5.) Club Escapade: John Morrison's Moonlight Drive, aka a flip over corkscrew neckbreaker. A flashy move that Seymour generally doesn't execute unless he's in control or needs to turn the tide. Signature Moves: These are Almasy's non-named moves that he nonetheless pulls out during most matches. 1.) Superkick, usually thrown to counter a charging opponent or after ducking under a strike. 2.) Wrist-Clutch Inside Cradle. Essentially a small package with the extra wrist clutch. This can finish matches, and if he's going for a win by rollup, he'll go for this. Sometimes he will use it early matches to disorient opponents, force a kickout, and then force angry retaliation that he can take advantage of. 3.) Double-Arm DDT. He prefers this to the standard variant, and generally uses the double-arm variation when he has a chance. This includes Tornado DDTs as well. 4.) Anklelock Submission. Seymour will not submit many people with his version of the move due to his size. Opponents can easily counter or move to the ropes. He uses the hold to force opponents to make quick decisions to break, and also to inflict damage to the leg quickly. Almasy doesn't truly work body parts, so he uses the hold to give opponents something to worry about while he utilizes his aerial/striking offense. Also helps immobilize opponents to make such moves easier to hit. 5.) Cannonball bodyblock (off the top rope, apron, etc.) Combines Almasy's flair with a bit of practicality. The somersault bodyblock works well when it connects with an opponent. If it misses, it leaves Almasy with time to break his fall, as well as be facing upwards to spot any onrushing opponents. Normal Moves: Almasy's regular moveset. Most everything in here he'll pull out whenever, moves that are used sparingly are noted. Entrance Music 'P3 FES' by Yumi Kawamura f. Lotus Juice
