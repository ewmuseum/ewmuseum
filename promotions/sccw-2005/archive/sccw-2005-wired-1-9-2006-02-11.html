He had just finished a warm-up set of sit-ups, and realized that his match was only a few minutes away. A small radio played in the corner of the training room. His footsteps had carried him from the depths of this business, this lifestyle, and the ranks of the world's finest. Men had come and gone in and out of that ring, and only the blood that stained the canvas remained when they were pulled away by limitations of failed motivation. Damien Cruz pulled on the black boots that had brought him so far. The laces pulled tight against the leather, the boot holding tight against the leg. Too tight and he ran of the risk of doing damage to the tendons, the muscle, and the bones. Too loose he could jump out of them, or break an ankle and end his career with a missed blow. It was a balance. As he laced up the boot with the practiced movements that no longer required thought, he remembered the first time he had done so. A world away, but still close enough to touch. Damien Cruz pulled tight his boots, thinking about the long road he had been on to get to that night. Two weeks, and more enemies than he could keep up with. He was accustomed to it. He learned to understand that nothing is handed to a person, and no amount of success comes easy. Tying the laces hard, he rose, and walked to where a small sink was filled with cold water. * The cold water rained over his head, and he hated it at that moment. Every nerve in his body revolted against the assault, but it was necessary. His muscled flexed and relaxed under his skin, and with the release went the tightness. The muscle that roped around his body were pliable, relaxed, and loaded with ions that would make them explosive. There were fewer more dangerous weapons than a body not limited by inability. Years of training had made the machinery perfect, and he was grateful for every strand. * Damien Cruz stood, and pulled on his white tank top, and then the elbow pads that would serve as his only armor in the coming battle. He tied his shoulder-length hair back, and stared at himself in the mirror. He was getting older. For the first time, he realized that he was the veteran facing the upstart. He was the elder statesman looking to fend off the predatory young wolf. Somehow, in the course of his career, he had proven himself as the man that the others justified themselves against. He had, without realizing it, become an icon. The questions of when Damien would be a threat were replaced by the understanding that Damien was one of the greatest threats on the roster. It was a sobering revelation, and one that Damien Cruz shrugged away as futile. It is a simple equation; once the acceptance of achievement sets in, the desire to achieve more fades. Damien refused to entertain the thought, even as he noticed the three strands of white hair growing along his temple. Damien looked down at his hands. He looked at the perfect white linen and rubberized plastic*that wrapped them. He looked at the scarred knuckles. He looked at the deep-set lines that creased when they were balled into fists. The Latin Assassin looked at the word that he had written across the back of his fist without remembering when he had done so. In simple, perfect print he had written "PRIDE". That most devastating Force. The Sin and the Virtue. The five-letter word that burned like a curse and shone like a blessing. He looked at the word he squeezed shut his eyes once more, this time to wash away the stinging pain of what he was about to do.
