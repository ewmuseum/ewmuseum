An October 2000 interfed project staging fantasy sub-federations under the WWF, WCW, ECW and UWF banners, its WOW Daily bulletins predicting and recapping matches among real and invented wrestlers. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

World of Wrestling billed itself, from its first bulletin on 21 October 2000, as “the new Interfed Frontier” — a project staging fantasy sub-federations under the banners of the WWF, WCW, ECW and UWF. Its own reporter, Buzz Stone, opened what he called the first of many WOW Dailies to come, reporting WCW's first four signings (Rikishi, Kurt Angle, Buff Bagwell and “Road Dogg” Jesse James) and previewing the UWF's opening card, a ten-man tournament for its own World Title at Pro Player Stadium in Miami between Sean Simmons, “Stone Cold” Steve Austin, The Rock, Tazz, Ice, The X, Sting, Raven, Tha Punisher and the Suicidial Maniac.

The following day's bulletin reported ECW finally finding promoters, who signed the tag team of Justin Credible and Lance Storm as the Impact Players and set a single weekly show, ECW Hardcore TV; the WWF found its own promoter the same day. Buzz Stone closed the bulletin with his own round-by-round predictions for the UWF tournament to come.

The 23 October bulletin recapped that tournament, Monday Night Maul: Steve Austin beat Sean Simmons; Tazz upset The Rock with a Tazzplex over the top rope and then assaulted him on the stretcher; Ice beat The X; three no-shows from Raven, Tha Punisher and Sting collapsed half the bracket and gave the Suicidial Maniac a bye. Ice went on to beat the Suicidial Maniac and then upset Tazz by roll-up to become, in Buzz Stone's own words, “the first ever Uncensored World Champion” — Tazz beat him down after the loss.

The same bulletin previewed the UWF's second card, Thursday Night Thrasher, reported Sting released outright for his no-show, and had the UWF answer its two absent stars by signing the Sandman and Balls Mahoney; the Impact Players, in their own voice, issued an open challenge to ECW's roster to come test them.

No bulletin later than 23 October 2000 survives, no result of Thursday Night Thrasher is held, and nothing states that World of Wrestling ever closed — its own record simply stops there.
