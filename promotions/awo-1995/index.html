A UK-run blended-roster federation founded in May 1995 whose founder played its own on-screen President; it paused after a card that July, relaunched that December on real-wrestler-only rules, and remained active into early 1996 with a new title announced. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Amalgamated Wrestling Organisation opened on 4 May 1995 with an open door on its own roster policy: “I don't care whether you create wrestlers or nick them from WWF, WCW, ECW or Uncle Ted's Wrestling.” Its founder ran the whole operation alone, offering four men's singles championships (English, Australian, United States and AWO) alongside tag and women's titles, built entirely from character-sheet submissions worth seventy-five points apiece.

That first run lasted less than three months. “I can't be bothered any more,” its founder wrote that July, giving members forty-eight hours to claim their characters back before the records were scrubbed for good.

It returned that December rebuilt around a stricter policy — WWF and WCW wrestlers only, with a handful of ECW names allowed by exception — and a promise this run would last, since its founder had, in his own words, “sod all better to do” for the foreseeable future. Its January 1996 debut broadcast, AWO Raw, ran live from a San Antonio arena and introduced a house-original gimmick alongside the returning championships: Wildcard Tag Team Titles, in which a tag team's partner for each round was drawn at random and revealed only at ringside, so that no team knew who they would be fighting alongside until the bell.

The promotion paused again the following month while its founder looked for co-writers to share the load, framed at the time as temporary. The last dated show in its own record is a broadcast from 27 January 1996; no later document confirms whether that recruiting drive ever succeeded, or whether the AWO ran again after it.
