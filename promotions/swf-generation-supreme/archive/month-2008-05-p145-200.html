TEW.com Monthly Report: April 2008

Monday, Week 1, May 2008

To help wrestling fans keep up with around the globe keep up with happenings around the rest of the globe, TEW.com offers a monthly report on all the happenings in wrestling.

United States

Quote:

After an interesting March in the American wrestling industry, the month of April had a lot to live up to. It did just that and more. The rivalry between Total Championship Wrestling and the Supreme Wrestling Federation is typically called the "Tuesday Night War", and that rivalry shifted into overdrive this month.

TCW actually beat SWF in the ratings war for the first time in a long time in the 2nd week of April. It was a short-lived victory, as Supreme put on a series of very solid shows through the month and took back the ratings lead. TCW's Monday night Wrestle Grand Prix continued to pull very impressive ratings, though the caliber of the shows themselves seemed to have dropped a bit.

Richard Eisen did manage to extract some revenge on his rivals, as he signed away one of Total's biggest stars in Rocky Golden. The 24-year old looks like he could become a global star for Supreme, much as Jack Bruce has since making the same move four years ago. Tana also made the same move, but comes with less star quality and less expectations. Insiders believe that TCW will struggle to replace Golden with someone of equal popularity, and that the worlds number two promotion is desperate to ensure that no other stars jump ship to the SWF when their contracts come due.

In April, SWF saw two title changes as well as added a new title, dubbed the SWF Generations championship, for the younger wrestlers. TCW saw the unassuming Harry Allen win the All-Action title from Sammy Bach. In bigger surprise, Charlie Thatcher defeated Wolf Hawkins for the International championship at the start of the month, only to lose it to Rick Law at the end of the month on his first defense. It was a busy month for Hawkins, as he also turned face after losing his belt and looks set to rival his mentor Tommy Cornell.

Though United States Pro Wrestling continues to fly under the radar for many wrestling fans in America, Sam Strong's promotion continues to build a strong fanbase. The shows continue to grow in quality, as well. The USPW Television title changed hands, as Nicky Champion was pinned by veteran Freddie Datsun. And Strong's daughter, Alicia, finally won the Women's Championship from Cherry Bomb.

Amongst the smaller promotions, things continued much as they have. CZCW put on a very good April show at the Spring Break Bash 2008. The Coastal Zone Championship was won by Erik Strong at the show. Rhode Island Pro Wrestling also put on a quality show. Steve Flash defeated Nevada Nuclear for the RIPW Championship at the School's Out show. Also of note was NYCW legend The Stomper finally retiring from active competition.

The American wrestling fan continues to have plenty of choices, and some great quality wrestling from the top promotions.

Canada

Quote:

The DeColt family and their Canadian Golden Combat enjoyed a pretty good month in April. Unfortunately for them, the Stone family's North of the Border Pro Wrestling had a very good month. The mid-month pay per view event Super Slam was a classic. It was as good a display of pure wrestling as you will see in North America.

If NOTBPW continues on its solid run, many industry insiders are predicting that the promotion will hit National status before the end of the year. As the Stone's offer a product that is a bit different from what any American promotions offer, its possible that the Canadians could break into the American market to some degree. Of course, its the same prediction that many industry insiders have been making for a long time. In 2007, losing Duane Stone and firing Edd Stone put NOTBPW back a step. Given their quality, its unlikely that the likes of Johnny Bloodstone, Dark Angel, and Sean McFly are being overlooked by SWF, TCW, and Japan's biggest players. Losing some of their top talent has to be a major concern for Dan Stone's promotion, but its a reality that they face.

Mexico

Quote:

It was an interesting month in Mexico.

South of the Border Pro Wrestling, of course, maintained their spot at the top of the lucha libre world. The promotion has not quite fully recovered from the loss of top star Champagne Lover, but no single man makes any promotion. The month of April did see Hijo Del Mephisto win the Campeón de Menor from Blood Raven.

In OLLIE, the only real news of note was the Swarm II and Swarm III winning the Campeón de Parejas.

MPWF had a very good month. The promotions Epidemia Del Odio event was easily the best in Mexico through all of April. The Campeón de Herencia de Mundo match between Ultimate Phoenix and Soul Taker was as good a wrestling match as lucha libre presents.

The upstart hardcore promotion, MHW, held its second show which was rather forgettable. The unique promotion faces an uphill battle to challenge any of Mexico's three bigger promotions.

United Kingdom

Quote:

21st Century Wrestling maintained its top spot in the United Kingdom but was unable to halt its backward slide. Jeff Nova's promotion continued to lose popularity. 21CW is in a bad spot after losing two of its biggest stars - Joss Thompson and Adam Matravers - in the preceding months.

Of course, things are still worse for 21CW's competition. Men of Steel Combat gained a small degree of popularity but Ring of Fire did nothing but maintain.

Japan

Quote:

It was a huge month for Pride Glory Honor Wrestling. The ownership and booking of Eiasku Hoshino lead the promotion to finally overtake Burning Hammer as the number one promotion in Japan. PGHW moved fully into the number three spot in the world.

The news only got worse for Burning Hammer of the Wrestling Gods. They started the month by losing their television slot for Lords of the Ring. BHOTWG is still capable of putting on top-caliber shows, but they are inconsistent, while consistency is PGHW's greatest attribute. The month of April did see White Samurai winning the Burning Openweight Championship after Reijiro Hiraki won and lost the belt within a week. As well, the combo of VENOM and Yoshi Shiomi won the Burning Junior Tag Team belts.

BHOTWG not only has to look up at PGHW, but has to keep a sharp on those promotoins below it. World Level Wrestling had a consistently successful month, putting on solid show after solid show on its Ragged Glory Tour. The new World Level Intercontinetal belt was claimed by Tsurayuki Kamachi at the start of April. As well, Silver Shark won the Show Stealer title and the Tag Team belt was won by The Incredible KOYAMA and KONI Ishibashi.

Wrestling insiders around the globe are watching Japan closely. The rest of 2008 could see anything happen - PGHW hitting the heights that Burning Hammer once enjoyed, WLW continuing its climb, even BHOTWG reclaiming its top spot. In this crazy sports, anything is possible.

Australia

Quote:

To say it was a quiet month on the Australian wrestling scene would be an understatement. After the excitement of March which saw a drug overdose, all that April featured was a rather forgettable monthly show from Australian Pro Wrestling. It seems like more Aussie wrestling fans are trying to find out how Swoop McCarthy is going in the SWF than actually watching wrestling at home.

Random thought of the day... the truth hurts, the truth can't be handled, and the truth sets you free. But if the truth can do all of those things, how do you know what its going to do when you choose the truth?

I had what was probably my most interesting night as the pseudo-booker of SWF recently. It was the night that we debuted Rocky golden. The show as fantastic overall. We made the booking decision to stick almost entirely with guys rated as upper midcard or above, and the results speak for themselves. You can't do shows like that very often, other than on pay per views, but sometimes its necessary. It absolutely worked in this case. I particularly enjoyed the Bruce-Eisen showdown segment. Mainly because I wrote the whole thing. The "writer" Spengler was supposed to, but what he wrote basically came across as cartoon dialogue of the superhero confronting the villain. While that has worked many times for Supreme in the past, its not the direction we're going at the moment. I'm not so sure that Spengler is necessary or "working out". But that's not my call. Yet.

After show, Richard Eisen approached me. "I thought the segment there came off pretty well," he told me.

"With you and Bruce? Yeah, it came off really well," I agreed.

"Some good writing there," Eisen said. "I'll have to let Spengler know."

"Um, about that..." I began.

"Yeah?"

Honesty? Do I tell the boss that Spengler is a creepy self-absorbed jerk who spends most of his time making the female talent uncomfortable by staring at them? That he hasn't been doing anything of late to earn his paycheck? Instead of honesty, I muttered, "I'll pass it on."

Eisen didn't depart, though. He continued to stare at me for a long, uncomfortable moment before he asked, "So what about the new guy?"

"Golden," I stated, thinking back to when Eisen asked me about his last signing from TCW, Thom Barrowman. This time I got to be honest. "He's going to be very big very quickly. We won't have to give him an overt push. There are many different things we'll be able to do with this guy. The fans will love him and love to hate him."

"Great!" my boss laughed with genuine enthusiasm. "I promised him the moon when I signed him. If the fans buy him, I don't have be a liar. Or much of one."

It wasn't like Richard Eisen to be self-deprecating, and I had a genuine laugh with him.

I had to frantically wave him off as he started to say, "About those other guys we have coming in..."

"That we'll talk about in private," I told him with a knowing. He realized what I was getting at right away.

We were backstage of the V. Thompson Arena. There were plenty of people about, both roster staff and stadium staff. Much like we had kept the signing of Rocky Golden quiet until we introduced, we wanted to do the same with any other new signings we were bringing in. It the wrong person overheard a name mentioned, it could end up all over the place as a rumor. There was a good chance the signings would get out before we actually introduced the workers, but we wanted to at least try to keep thing quiet.

Eisen had one other question, asking, "Are we still going with the direction we discussed for the main event at Puppets?"

I shrugged, responding, "I think so. It's the best we have. I'm willing to listen other ideas, but no one is coming up with anything notable."

Eisen nodded, told me to keep up the good work, and disappeared.

I went wandering through the back, looking for Robbie or someone else to grab some food with. I was wondering through a big concrete mezzanine where there were a ton of backstage staff getting all the gear out when I saw it. I saw what I had been expecting for two weeks.... Scott Sinclair charging at me.

I saw Sinclair from fifty feet away and was temped to make a bee-line for an exit. What the hell good will avoiding it do? Laremee was trailing behind Sinclair, with a "oh no, here we go" look on his face. He started yelling my name - my assumed name, anyway - from halfway across the mezzanine. I stopped and waited. And I tried not to laugh at Sinclair's scrunched up, angry-red face.

"Avatar! Don't you run away from me!" Sinclair hollered at me.

"I'm not going anywhere, Scott," I answered casually as he finally reached me. The stupid roid-head was actually breathing heavy from his fifty-foot near-run.

"I know you've been avoiding me, but d*mmit, I want to talk to you," Sinclar told me. His voice was louder than necessary, though he wasn't shouting. I realized right away that he was putting on a show. This was a performance for him, which is why he was doing it someplace public rather than a normal meeting at my office. He wanted as much of the roster as possible to see him "giving it to the kid". I wasn't about to let him have his fun.

"Avoid you, Scott? I've been at every show for the past month. I'm available at my office three days a week. You can reach twenty-four hours a day on my cell phone. You have the number, just like everyone else on the roster. But I sure didn't see any messages from you. So I'm guessing you haven't been trying real hard."

The steroid freak didn't quite know what to do when I called him on his bluff immediately. He looked confused for a moment, then pressed on in a loud voice, "I know you've been spreading this bullsh*t about me. These... these... lies! I won't stand for it, kid."

That made him turn a bit deeper shade of red. "Unprofessional?" he blustered at me. "You know how long I've been in this business, kid? What the hell do you know about being professional? You're barely out of your teens!"

I couldn't resist egging him on, saying, "Apparently age has little to do with maturity or professionalism, Scott."

Even Laramee, standing behind Sinclair, had a smirk at that one. There was a bit of a crowd gathering around us now. Some wrestlers, some backstage workers, and some arena stuff. No one was moving to intervene but it didn't really look like the situation would break into violence. I hoped, at least.

Sinclair didn't find my verbal jab funny. Not at all. If he had been smart enough to let it drop right there, I probably would have to. But of all the many things Scott Sinclair is, smart really is not one of them. So when he kept going, I decided I was going to make this hurt for him. Just by being honest.

"Oh, you think jokes mean something, kid? You're nothing in this company. You're a wrestler who doesn't get to wrestle. Talk sh*t about me all you want, but at least I'm out there on TV. I'm a star. I'm going to be carrying this company in a few years. And where will you be? Nowhere. Maybe still backstage here trying to be a ring rat."

"Carry this company? Are you serious, Sinclair?" I asked incredulously. A few of the by-standers snickered. "You couldn't even carry that little company in Pittsburgh, and everyone but you knows it. Do you really think this company missed you while you were on "sabbatical"?"

"You're just jealous, kid," he sneered at me. "You wish you were where I am. You're pulling all this backroom sh*t to try to screw me."

I actually laughed. I told the self-absorbed meathead, "Trust me, I have much bigger things to deal with than you. If I was jealous and wanted you gone, you'd be gone. Simple as. No one here is trying to screw you. You can't get a title run here because management can't trust you to stay clean. They dropped you out of the Grand Prix for the North American title because of that. You screwed yourself and Laramee out of title run with the tag belts. We had that one booked and everything. They had to pull the plug because of you. So the only person screwing Scott Sinclair is Scott Sinclair."

There was a harsh silence. I did notice Laramee staring hard at the back of Sinclair's head after what I said about the tag belts. I had tried to keep that one quiet, but I was past the point of caring. I also did notice Robbie standing near the of the crowd, trying to make eye contact. I gave him a quick head shake that no, I didn't need any help with this. I didn't think Sinclair was about to get violent and I figured I could handle myself if he did. It wouldn't last long anyway, as the crowd had plenty of SWF employees who I knew were friendly towards me.

It didn't come to that. Sinclair had run out of steam. He just stared at me with nothing to say, but I could almost feel him deflating. Was it his ego? His anger? His inflated sense of self-worth? Maybe even his time left in this promotion?

"I really would have preferred to do this in private," I told him. "But if you're done yelling and stuff, I'd like to grab some food. So excuse me." And I walked away.

Suffice to say that I had plenty of company for that meal and it was quite enjoyable. A lot of people kept trying to talk about Sinclair, but I simply wasn't discussing the situation any further.

Unfortunately, that little showdown turned out to be a popular topic and I couldn't keep it from getting around. Everyone knew about it, probably within hours. And it remained a popular topic for discussion for awhile. There was no concern about conflicting versions floating around this time, as there were plenty of witnesses.

Suffice to say that Sinclair managed to keep a low profile following his blowup. I just went on my life. I did have a fun little discussion with Kurt Laramee, though. He approached by just a few days later and asked if we could talk.

"Alright," I said. "What's up?"

"Was that true?" he asked bluntly. "What you said about Scott juicing costing us a shot at the tag titles?"

Honesty time. "Yeah. It is. I was trying to keep that from getting out, but when Sinclair got in my face, I got a little unprofessional and let it slip," I admitted.

"So was it a possibility or a sure thing we would get them?"

"Sure thing. Before the incident in January with Sinclair, we were going to start pushing you two towards the February pay per view, and then have you take them from the brothers there. We had to go with Gilmore and Sexy instead."

Laramee nodded and was silent for a moment, contemplating. In a way, I felt sorry for him. Laramee doesn't have the best reputation around these parts, but he's not that bad a guy. He's not a real popular guy in the locker room, but its not really his personality. From what I understand, a few of the long-term SWF guys didn't like the hype he got when he signed from DaVE. A few other guys didn't like his heavy style. Plus all the other ex-DaVE guys dislike him because of how he left that promotion and "betrayed" Vibert. I can't imagine having Vibert here makes that any easier - I've never seen the two of them even in the same room together since Phil joined SWF. I think there are even a few guys who just don't like Laramee because he's friends with and tags with Sinclair.

Laramee looked hard at me, and I honestly wasn't sure what his response was going to be. He said, "Look, I've kept pretty quiet here for the past few months. I honestly prefer to keep a bit of a low-profile now that Vibert's here. But I would like to ask one favor, Avatar."

"Sure." I wasn't agreeing to anything until I heard it.

"Don't team me with Sinclair anymore," he said. I wasn't sure what he was going to ask, but it wasn't that. I can't say it surprised me, after the title revelation I let slip. But I did not expect it. "Look, I've had it with the guy. Even before I heard about the titles thing, I was ready to be finished with him. That just made me more sure."

"Um, well..."

"I'm just tired of his crap," Laramee told me with utter honesty. "I'm tired of him bitching about everything - his push, other workers, his reputation, the fans booing him. I'm tired of him flipping out over the weakest stuff when we're on the road. I'm tired of him finding a way to make me pay more than my portion of everything. I've put with it awhile now and I'm just done."

"Okay Kurt, I'll see what I can do," I told. I meant it, as I genuine felt bad for him having to put up with the inhumanity known as Scott Sinclair.

But Laramee wasn't quite done. He surprised me as he continued, "Look, I know this probably puts you in a tough spot. Push-wise, and all that. If it means I'm pushed to the side for a bit until you can come up with someone new for me, I'm fine with that. I'd rather job for a bit than deal with him more."

I laughed, saying, "I can understand that. Look, I won't make promises. But I'll definitely see what I can do. And this might actually help me come up with something." Which was entirely true, as I already had a nice little idea brewing.

So not only did that little public showdown contrived by Sinclair not help him in any way, it actually helped me quite a bit. Laramee seems to be genuinely appreciative that I'm willing to help him. Bigger than that, I seemed to have gained in more respect from a lot of the roster by not backing down from Sinclair at all.

The day after my discussion with Laramee, I got a call from Richard Eisen about it. "Look kid, I heard about what Sinclair pulled. I'm sorry about that. If you want him gone, he's gone."

"No, its fine," I assured my boss. "I handled it."

"I heard that," Eisen laughed. "Did the *ss threaten you?"

"Not really. Just a lot of blustering. It was kinda like one of his promos - a lot of words and not much meaning."

Eisen laughed, "That's good, kid. Look, if he gives you any more problems, you let me know."

My answer was, "Well, honestly, I'd rather deal with it myself. I'd rather wait until until he screws up again, and then I'll get rid of him myself."

"I like your style. You got it! Now, about that main event idea of yours..."

Who knew that one little meeting would lead to so much. That choosing to be honest in this case could have some rather profound and positive effects. I'd like to think that's always the case when choosing honesty, but its not. Sometimes it works and sometimes it doesn't. And its tough to get right. This time I did... I think...

Beyond making no friends and making everyone in the locker room hate you, I mean. I've been here six months and I've had a far bigger impact on the fortunes and direction of Supreme that you could hope to. The fans might not remember. But the works all will. And they'll remember what I did for them. That's far more than you can say about your time here and you know it."

There was a heavy silence.

"F*ck you."

That was all he could muster. He glared at me, grabbed the paperwork and stormed out. I tried not to laugh as he stalked down the hallway... in the wrong direction... I did laugh when he stalked back the other direction to get to the elevator.

There were so many more professional ways I could have handled that. But I was absolutely satisfied with the way it went. That was a memory I would cherish forever. And any time I happen to find clips from somewhere nowhere little sh*t promotion which was desperate enough to hire Big Smack Scott, I'll watch those clips as BSS makes a mess of another match and I'll think back to this day and these moments. It will bring a smile to my face every time. That's the kind of memory that you can just can't put a price on.

Afterward, I found myself in the mood to work. Which was good, because I had a great deal to do. It was also good, because I wouldn't want to find myself feeling introspective in a moment like that.

If I were feeling introspective, I might find myself musing about how perhaps Sinclair and I aren't so different in some ways. I might think that Sinclair's cage is abusing a certain substance. That by choice or by addiction he simply cannot stop, thereby making him powerless to keep from going in whatever direction that cage takes him. My cage is not so different, built out of my own ideas and creativity. It too is something I cannot escape now that I've created it, and I am equally powerless to make it take me where I want to be. Yeah, I definitely wouldn't want to musing over thoughts like those instead of papering the walls of my cage and making them ever thicker....

Quote:

Random thought of the day... is the idea of being "problem free" just a myth in this business, with no more basis in reality than leprechauns, Sasquatches, and the planet Alderaan?

I didn't really believe my problems would be over after I fired Sinclair. I hoped, but it was a baseless and shallow hope. There are too many personalities in this place. That is both a good and bad thing at the same time. I got to enjoy the first few days of the post-Big Smack Scott era as I worried about nothing more than my work. I knew it couldn't last. I just didn't expect the problems to come from the bookends of the age spectrum...

The first new problem started out subtly enough. I was backstage at the Generation Supreme show the other night and enjoying myself. I always enjoy myself at the Generation shows. For one, we now hold them all at the Supreme Arena at the Eisen Center and its just a beautiful facility. But moreso, I'm treated like a rock star. All the younger workers love the show and love me for creating it. I should say having the idea to create it, but I usually don't bother to correct them any more. Most of the young guys still job on the show, but they are at least on TV regularly. And they know that exposure on TV is the best way for them to move up the card and start getting wins.

Midway through the show, Jay Chord approached me. Stormed up to me would be a better phrase, I guess.

"Hey booker man," he said. "I want in on this second generation thing."

I tried to hide my distaste at the statement. I'll just keep it civil by saying that Chord has not left the best impression on me so far. The kid is still just 17 years old - he should still be in school! Instead, he's a professional wrestler with a sense of entitlement and ego that outweigh his considerable skills. If the kid already has a bit of an attitude problem, what's he going to be like when he actually gets a push? I honestly was not a fan of signing him this early, but Eisen was adamant about it. I think Jay would be best served spending some time with his dad's promotion, but we have him here instead.

"What second generation thing, Jay?" I replied dumbly. It wasn't a huge secret, but it also wasn't a plan we were trying to make sure everyone on the roster knew about. That's how things got out to the marks way before you could put it on screen.

"The Kings thing," he told me coldly. He definitely wasn't taking the touchy feely approach here.

"Its not a second generation thing."

With the dripping sarcasm that only a 17-year old American male can generate, Jay told me, "They're all second generation guys. So its a second generation thing."

"No, three of them are second generation," I correct him. "Newton isn't. So its not a second generation thing. That's not how we're presenting or pushing."

"Well I don't care," was Chord's response. "I want in on it."

"That won't work."

"Why not?"

The guy was like petulant child. Had Rip even tried to teach him any discipline growing up? Probably not too. Too busy getting hammered between shows.

"Because its being called the Four Kings because there are four guys. If its five guys, the name doesn't exactly work. We've spent three months setting this up and we'd have to throw it all out the window."

This just pissed the kid off, which I didn't really understand. "Fine, whatever. What the hell am I supposed to do then?" he demanded.

"What? We have something set up for you. We told you all about it," I told him. Was he drunk? Did he take after dad that much?

"Yeah," he nodded. "But that's not going to start for like a month."

"Maybe two," I said slowly. "You have to be patient, Jay."

He scowled at me and then turned and stomped away, muttering something about it all being a waste of time.

The kid can't wait a month to get involved in a storyline. Really? If he's that anxious over this, what's going to happen when he doesn't get his first world title reign by the age of 19?

Of course, trouble rarely comes alone. The next day I took the first of several calls from people on the roster letting me in on a little something. I would hear the same story nearly a dozen times from a dozen different workers over the next week. The story was the same every - a respected wrestler was continually bad mouthing the whole Legends show and roster split to other workers.

That worker? None other than Eric Tyler. The oldest active wrestler on the SWF roster. The most experienced guy. The guy is supposed to add leadership and help out with the younger workers. What the hell is wrong with him? If he has an issue with the direction and the booking, why wouldn't he pursue the proper channels and talk to someone in management? Like me.

I can understand Jay Chord acting like a jackass. He's a kid. That's the danger of hiring youngsters. But what excuse can Eric Tyler make?

I haven't had a chance yet to talk to Tyler about it. I have zero doubt that its true. I might doubt the validity of it had it only come from one or two other workers? But close to a dozen doesn't leave a lot of room for reasonable doubt.

Suffice to say being "problem free" didn't last for long. I don't know yet if either of these issues will blow up to be anything near to the problem that Sinclair became. And if they do, if they will have an outcome I will enjoy as much. With all the changes that SWF is going through right now, I get the feeling that these problems are just the start of many that I'm going to end up dealing with.
