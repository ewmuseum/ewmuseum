SWF Legends

Wednesday, Week 2, August 2008

Isaak Road Arena (Mid Atlantic) - 14,433

Announcers

Duane Fry - Phil Vibert - Melanie Florence

________________________________________

Duane Fry: I would like to welcome all our viewers to SWF Legends! We have two and half hours of action for you tonight, leading into Welcome to the Jungle tomorrow night! I have Melanie Florence and Phil Vibert with me.

Melanie Florence: A pleasure to be here, love.

Phil Vibert: I'll be happy to be here once this first segment is out of the way.

Quote:

Eric Tyler, BJ O'Neill, Kid Toma, Akima Brave, Bart Biggz + Brett Biggz

Mr. Tyler Says Segment

The show once again starts with a Mr. Tyler Says segment. This one takes place on the stage rather than in the ring. The Legends brand GM is joined by The Samoan Wildboyz and their manager, as well as both Biggz brothers. Tyler stands between the two groups.

Eric Tyler: I haven't had this job a month yet and I'm already sick and tired of all the whining and complaining. Some of you need to learn to man up and get things done yourself instead of complaining to the boss. Because sometimes the boss doesn't want to hear it. Sometimes... the boss doesn't care...

This draws a round of boos for the General Manager.

Eric Tyler: There was a lot of crap brought to my attention this past week that I could have addressed here tonight on Mr. Tyler Says. I decided to address this crap. Complainant number one, take it away...

Tyler points at BJ O'Neill, who is handed a microphone. She steps forward like a game show contestant.

BJ O'Neill: Well, Mr. Boss Tyler, its like I told you this week... I don't think its fair that Toma has to face Biggz at the pay per view for his Shooting Star belt. The deal was Biggz had to beat Akima Brave. He didn't beat him.

Eric Tyler: He won. Via disqualification.

BJ O'Neill: Yeah, bra.

Eric Tyler: Because you interfered.

BJ has nothing to say to that. She avoids the hard gaze of the brand GM. He takes the second microphone from her.

Eric Tyler: And now we get to hear from Mr. Biggz...

Tyler makes to hand the microphone to Bart, and then pulls it away just before he can grab it.

Eric Tyler: ...but no one really cares what you have to say, Bart. No offense, but everyone here knows what you're going to say, so why waste everyone's time? Now, while Mr. Biggz here did fail to actually pin Akima Brave, no such qualification was put on the challenge. Therefore, the win stands as a win. And Ms. O'Neill... it may have helped your argument if you hadn't been directly involved in Akima getting disqualified. So the Shooting Star championship will be defended at Welcome to the Jungle.

There is some weak approval from the crowd. The prospect doesn't excite them to any great degree. Tyler slowly looks out over the crowd, a musing look on his face.

Eric Tyler: You know, I feel the same way. I'm not entirely convinced that Bart and Toma will put on a great match at the pay per view. They might. But I'm not willing to risk the reputation of the Legends brand on "might". So to make sure the match is worthy of the Sky Club Division and the Shooting Star championship, I made the decision to "spice things up" a bit...

At this, a series of pyrotechnics go off in a circle around the arena as the lights drop. A series of spotlights and lasers move about the arena, finally converging to focus on a spot at the opposite side of the arena. Partway up the balcony is a suspended clock. Music begins to play. Familiar music...

The man in the spotlight is diminutive, short but stocky. He has on a colorful mask... The lights focus a bit and it becomes that the man is...

Acid

Acid drops from his perch atop the clock, dropping about a dozen feet to the concrete floor below. He lands in a crouch, hesitating for a just a second before he breaks into a dead sprint down the narrow walkway towards the ring. He leaps over the barrier and makes an athletic leap into the ring from the floor, grabbing the ropes and launching himself to the other side of the ring. He balances himself on the far ropes, those closest to the ramp and poses. He points at Biggz and Toma, pointing at them.

Eric Tyler: That's right... Acid is in the Supreme Wrestling Federation. And he'll be facing Bart Biggz and Kid Toma in a Threefold Threat match for the SWF Shooting Star championship.

This gets a pretty notable pop from the crowd.

Grade: B-

Duane Fry: Acid... Here in the SWF! Another former DaVE star!

Phil Vibert: And this had nothing to do with me!

Quote:

Shady K vs Brett Biggz vs Troy Tornado

The match is an interesting contrast of styles – the lightweight flier Biggz, the heavyweight brawler Shady K, and the all-around skills of Tornado – but it all somehow works. The match is essentially a showcase for Tornado, as he has the most offense and he impresses as he seamlessly transitions between styles. The match flows pretty well and the crowd is into it. Throughout the match, it seems as though everyone – the participants and the crowd – are waiting to see if The Golden Faction appears, but they do not. Tornado is left alone to earn the win, after he hammers both Biggz and K with his Star Maker, then pins Shady K.

Troy Tornado Wins at 11:04 via Pinfall

Grade: B

Troy Tornado, Rocky Golden, Bloodstone + Texas Pete

Post-Match Attack

As soon as the bell rings to signal the Tornado victory, Rocky Golden and Bloodstone step through the Supreme Gate and slowly make their way down to the ring. Biggz and Shady K clear out, while Tornado stands his ground in the ring. There is a short standoff, with Tornado inviting his former stable-mates into the ring. One member is missing, however, and Texas Pete makes his appearace as he hits the ring through the crowd and waylays his former tag team partner. At that point, Golden and Bloodstone jump into the ring and the three men begin to pummel Tornado.

There is a roar from the crowd.

Christian Faith + Chris Caufield

Faith and Caufield appear through the Supreme Gate and they make their way down to the ring in a hurry. The three members of the Golden Faction fight back against the duo for a moment, then bail. The trio are all smiles as they slowly back up the ramp. While Caufield tends to Tornado, Faith grabs a microphone.

Christian Faith: Not so brave when the numbers aren't in your favor?

At the top of the ramp now, Golden is handed a microphone.

Rocky Golden: Faith, one has to realize when its time to fight and when its time to walk away in glory. And the Golden Boy is blessed with a perfect sense of timing. Its too bad you don't have the same, or you would realize you time is well passed, old man.

Christian Faith: If you feel like get embarassed by an old man tonight, Golden, I'm ready.

Rocky Golden: Remember, Faith, perfect sense of timing. I'll take you down and take your belt when the time is right.

Christian Faith: How about a tag match then? You and Bloodstone.

Rocky Golden: Against who? You and Caufield? Please, Bloodstone here already showed that Caufield isn't so hardcore.

Christian Faith: While I have faith that Chris here would be willing... I had someone else in mind...

There is a roar from the crowd. Golden, Bloodstone, and Pete all turn to see someone has slowly emerged from the Supreme Gate and is standing behind them.

Chris Morrisette

Morrisette gives the trio an intense stare. They try to act un-intimidated, but only Golden really manages it.

Rocky Golden: You and Lobster Boy against me and Bloodstone? Sure, why not.

Golden drops the microphone and the trio stalks off the stage, though the gate. Morrisette gives them a hard stare as they walk past him. When they are gone, he poses for the crowd to incite further reaction.

Grade: A

Duane Fry: Christian Faith... Chris Morrisette... Rocky Golden... Bloodstone... We told you tonight was going to be big!

Melanie Florence: Big is good, dear.

Quote:

High Concept vs The Upper Class

SWF Sky Club Division Tag Team Championship Qualifying Match

A mediocre at best match that the crowd is simply indifferent to. There is a bit of reaction for a couple of the big spots that Benson and Bling hit, but its otherwise pretty flatline. The match is won on the superior teamwork and the impressive quickness of the experienced duo. Greed and Huntingdon make an honest effort of cheating their way to victory but they can't manage it. After hitting their Game Over on Greed, Benson gets the pin.

Elmo Benson Wins at 7:31 via Pinfall

Grade: D+

Quote:

Eric Tyler + Ana Garcia

Backroom Segment

Eric Tyler is seated in his office, looking somewhere between bored and annoyed. Sitting across from him, near to tears, is Ana Garcia. The GM clearly care much about what she's talking about, but that isn't stopping her.

Ana Garcia: Every time he comes near me now, I just start shaking. Its horrible... just horrible...

Eric Tyler: Yes, that's awful.

Ana Garcia: So you're going to do something?

Eric Tyler: About what?

Ana Garcia: About what? About Vengeance! About him scaring everyone backstage and making everyone nervous. The man is a menace!

Eric Tyler: Yes. And the man is also a d*mned fine wrestler. Which means I'm not going to do anything.

Ana Garcia: Well, then I... I... I...

Eric Tyler: You'll what? Quit? Move to the other brand? Be my guest, Miss Garcia. Great wrestlers are plenty valuable. Pretty interviewers are not so hard to come by.

Ana Garcia: Well, I never!

Eric Tyler: I'm sure you have. Just stay out of his way and he'll leave you alone.

Ana gets up and storms out of the office. Tyler shakes his head and rubs his temples. Listening to everyone's complaints is obviously getting to the General Manager. There is another knock at the door and he doesn't attempt to hide his frustration.

Eric Tyler: What? What now?

Kristen Pearce

[I}The manager of The Golden Faction pokes her head into the office. [/i]

Kristen Pearce: Can we talk about that title shot for Rocky now, Mr. Tyler.

Eric Tyler: Sure, Kristen. Come in. And please close the door.

The door to the office is closed, shutting out the camera.

Grade: A

Duane Fry: That can't be good.

Quote:

Brandon James vs American Elemental

Another easy win for James as he overpowers and overwhelms his overmatched opponent. The match starts out with a bit of comedy as the lightweight tries a few arerial moves in hopes of taking Brandon off his feet, but he doesn't budge Big Money. James lets it go for a few minutes, swatting Elemental away when necessary but doing little. When Emma Chase gives him the command, he grabs the masked little man and slams him around. A short batting, a Big Cat Pounce, and its an easy three-count. James doesn't even break a sweat.

Brandon James Wins at 6:54 via Pinfall

Grade: B-

Duane Fry: Another dominant win for James!

Phil Vibert: Against the stiffest competition!

Quote:

Pure Energy vs The Tokyo Underground

SWF Sky Club Division Tag Team Championship Qualifying Match

The match is actually probably better in quality than the earlier Sky Club Division Tag Team Championship Qualifying match, but the crowd is even less into it. They would clearly prefer to see more non-competitive squash matches than a decent bit of tag team interplay. The Japanese duo get some help from Mr. Miwa as the overcome the Canadian duo, who still have yet to notch a win in the SWF.

Ota Wins at 7:14 via Pinfall

Grade: D-

Quote:

Grandmaster Bling, Enygma + Ana Garcia

Backstage Interview

Still looking a bit rattled and flustered, Ana Garcia is on an interview set with Grandmaster Bling and his client, Enygma.

Ana Garcia: I'm here with Grandmaster Bling... Wait, weren't you Grandmaster Phunk before? Or Mr. Bling? Or something?

Grandmaster Bling: That's right, Ana. But you got to be changing with the times. The Grandmaster is back at the top of the class to show the whole SWF how he's mastered his craft. No one is safe when the Grandmaster shows his skills to the masses.

Ana Garcia: Alright... Now, you're managing Enygma now, is that correct?

Grandmaster Bling: That is correct as can be, Ana. This man has been ignored. It is unconscionable, unbelievable, unethical, and possibly even unconstitutional to treat this man the way he has been treated here in this federation. He is a former SWF World Heavyweight champion. The belt worn by the greatest of the great, the biggest of the big, and the richest of the rich. And it was worn by this very man here. Now they have him wrestling kids for belts that aren't worth the paper they're printed on. Now you tell me how that's right!

Ana Garcia: I, uh...

Grandmaster Bling: You can't because it ain't. You know and I know it and they know it and he knows it. And you better believe the Grandmaster is here to do something about it.

Ana Garcia: What exactly are you going to do about, Grandmaster?

Grandmaster Bling: You'll have to wait and see like everyone else, Ana.

Chris Caufield

Chris Caufield strolls onto the set, staring down Grandmaster Bling and Enygma.

Chris Caufield: Talk is cheap. You wanna Enygma to prove something, do it in the ring.

Grandmaster Bling: Why, my hardcore friend, that does sound ever so much like a challenge.

Chris Caufield: That's because it is.

Grandmaster Bling: Well then, in the name of my masked and silent client here, we accept.

Chris Caufield: Then see you in the ring.

Caufield walks away. Grandmaster Bling looks pretty pleased with himself. Just before the camera cuts away, Ana is looking around nervously and jumps when there is a slight noise from off camera.

Grade: B-

Quote:

Texas Pete vs Pistol Pete Hall

The two big cowboys have themselves a “barn-stormer” of a brawl. The two big men hammer away at each other with gusto, with the battle moving out of the ring and even nearly into the crowd. The fans enjoy the opening, but things fall apart a bit in the middle when Pete takes control. The crowd never gets back into it as the veteran Hall battles back. He nails the younger wrestlers with a series of heavy blows and nasty lariats, finally nailing his opponent with his Pistol Whip Lariat and getting the pinfall.

Pistol Pete Hall Wins at 8:44 via Pinfall

Grade: C-

Phil Vibert: Don't you want to say something about that being a “barn-stormer”, Duane?

Quote:

Engyma vs Chris Caufield

The two veterans put on a solid and entertaining match that the crowd seems to enjoy. Its a back-and-forth match where both men spend some time in control. Caufield starts to take over the match, but Grandmaster Bling does his job – not by interfering but simply by providing a quick distraction. Its enough for Enygma to hit his opponent with a unseen flying shoulder block, followed up by several suplexes. After an Enygmagiri Kick, the former world champion locks Caufield in his Enygma Variation submission hold. Caufield refuses to submit, though clearly in pain. He works his way to the ropes to break the hold. Caufield gets to his feet slowly, but he begins to batter away his masked opponent with a series of bunches and elbows. He nails Enygma with his Hardcore American Clothesline, and follows it up with the Danger Drop DDT, which gives him the win.

Chris Caufield Wins at 9:13 via Pinfall

Grade: B

Quote:

Jett Stream vs The Supreme Nightmare vs Black Label Fight Club

A mediocre three-way tag match this offers a clash of styles again, but this one doesn't work quite as well. The duo of Jett and Hernandez don't manage to mount much offense against the bigger men, for the most part, outside of an impressive suicide dive by Jett. Its the former world champion Runaway Train doing most of the battling for his team, with Eisen tagging in at the most opportune times. There is an interesting sequence near the end the match where Train brawls with both Black Label guys and gets the better of it. After Train hits his Locomotion Backbreakers on Jett, Hernandez tries to make the save, only to get hit with a Train Wreck powerbomb himself. It gives Train the pin as Eisen keeps Bonhan and Elderberry from interfering.

Runaway Train Wins at 9:32 via Pinfall

Grade: C+

Quote:

Knuckles vs Steve Frehley

The match starts slow, with both men trying a few power moves. It seems like Knuckles is slowly growing in confidence as he manages to hand with Frehley through the first few minutes of the match. Coming off the ropes, Knuckles kicks his opponent in the stomach and goes for a double-underhook facebuster, only to find he can't lift Frehley. Instead, the former champion simply powers out of the move and grabs Knuckles in a pumphandle backbreaker. Wearing a small smile, its clear the Frehley was simply toying with his opponent. A Frehley Suplex Throw is particularly impressive against his big opponent. The match turns back in Knuckles favor for just a moment when he tries his Stay of Execution – a low blow followed by a roll-up pin attempt. Frehley kicks out and he looks rather angry. A nasty Dark Destroyer Spear is followed up by a second one before he pins his opponent.

Steve Frehley Wins at 7:54 via Pinfall

Grade: B

Quote:

Emma Chase + Brandon James

Emma Chase leads her client out onto the stage. They don't bother coming down to the ring. Emma has a microphone and she does not look impressed.

Emma Chase: We're out here to voice a complaint. Because having my client, Brandon James, face opponents like American Elemental isn't just a waste of time... Its an insult. Big Money isn't just the best wrestler in this promotion, but in the entire world. Having him face clearly inferior competition is an absolute waste. Part of the reason we moved to this brand for the level of competition. And beating up lightweights proves nothing.

Emma stops for a moment and lets this sink in.

Emma Chase: Tomorrow, at Welcome to the Jungle, Brandon is going to beat Steve Frehely and earn a title shot against Christian Faith. Then he's going to take the Supreme Championship from the old man. In the meantime, Brandon needs to face some worthwhile competition. Guys like Frehley and Faith might be happy taking easy wins, but that's not what Big Money is about. So please, Mr. Tyler, give my client some competition worth our time and effort.

Grade: A*

Quote:

Ray Diaz vs Vengeance

The match is everything you would expect – a hard-hitting brawl. The match is slow moving but intense, and the crowd enjoys it. Both men work stiff and there is some genuine power behind every blow. Diaz gets busted open after a series of headbutts from Vengeance, the chrome mask lacerating Diaz's forehead. It doesn't phase the big man and he keeps battling. The blood actually seems to motivate Diaz and he takes Vengeance down with a Monster Mash clothesline, following it up several Savage Smashes. He goes for the pin, but The Harbinger has plenty of fight left and kicks out. Diaz goes for his Ray Gun piledriver, but Vengeance simply sits up. Diaz batters away but Vengeance shows no effect from the blows. He's pretty much unstoppable at this point. A Reaper Leaper and a Bones Shaker is followed up by a pin attempt but Diaz isn't done yet. Diaz battles back, but his comeback is stopped short after another Reaper Leaper clothesline. Vengeance's Skull Krusher piledriver puts Diaz down for the three count.

Vengeance Wins at 12:43 via Pinfall

Grade: B

Vengeance + Ray Diaz

Post-Match Attack

After the win, Vengeance climbs out the ring, but only to grab the heavy chain he always brings to ringside. The Harbinger then climbs back into the ring and attacks Diaz with. He whips the defeated big man with it several times, then wraps it around Diaz's head and squeezes, making the wound in Diaz's forehead bleed again. The referee intervenes, trying to pull Vengeance away but he's shoved out of the ring violently for his efforts. Then The Harbinger goes back to trying to completely destroy Ray Diaz.

There is a roar from the crowd as Steve Frehley appears through the Supreme Gate.

Steve Frehley

Vengeance clearly sees Frehley racing down the ramp towards the ring, but he continues to choke Diaz with his chain. Frehley slides into the ring and drives himself into Vengeance with a nasty Dark Destroyer Spear. Vengeance pops right back, though, and whips Frehley with the chain. It doesn't phase The Dark Destroyer, who takes it to close in on Vengeance to drive a couple of forearms into his chest. He kicks The Harbinger in the stomach, then pushes the big man out of the ring. Vengeance makes to climb back into the ring to go after Frehley again, but several SWF officials get in his way. He roars in anger as he is surrounded by about ten officials, all of them trying to keep him away from the ring.

When Vengeance finally gives up and stomps away, Frehley tends to his battered friend.

Grade: A*

Phil Vibert: Something needs to be done about DeBones...

Quote:

Warrior Faith vs Golden & Bloodstone

A d*mned good tag team match between four great workers.... Well, three great workers and Rocky Golden. The match is open and entertaining with plenty of crowd interaction. Referee Jay Fair struggles to keep control of the match as all four men get involved time and again. There is a great sequence of technical grappling in the middle of the match between Morrisette and Bloodstone, with the Legends champion getting the better of it. It allows Bloodstone and Golden to wear down and isolate Morrisette. Faith jumps in a couple of times, but Golden and Bloodstone keep their opponents from making the tag. When Faith finally tags in, he cleans house. The crowd goes insane for the champion. Golden and Bloodstone don't go down easy, however, as they slow up Faith after a bit as well. Recovered by now, Morrisette gets in there and battles Bloodstone. When Bloodstone gets Morrisette in the Bloodstone Variation, it looks like the face duo might be in trouble but Faith interferes to save the win. Morrisette quickly hits his Fisherman's Suplex and gets the pin on a surprised Bloodstone.

Chris Morrisette Wins at 15:32 via Pinfall

Grade: B+

Christian Faith, Chris Morrisette + Vengeance

Staredown

After the match, Golden and Bloodstone don't clear out immediately, as they jaw at the victors. The Golden Faction duo finally clear out of the ring, just as Vengeance appears at the top of the ramp. Realizing what's about to happen, Golden and Bloodstone clear out of dodge.

The Harbinger makes his way down to the ring, stalking with serious intent. He wants at the champion, Christian Faith. Morrisette stands in the ring beside his friend. There is quickly an entire army of SWF officials at ringside, as security is determined to keep the situation from escalating. Vengeance attempts to get in the ring, but a wall of secuirty officials keep him away. He roars in anger, bellowing at Christian Faith. The champion stands stoically beside Morrisette, both of them ready for The Harbinger to get into the ring. It doesn't happen.

The showdown between Christian Faith and Vengeance will have to wait for the pay per view.

Grade: A*

SWF Welcome to the Jungle 2008 Preview

Thursday, Week 2, August 2008

The Nevada State Armoury is playing host to the Supreme Wrestling Federation tonight, with more than 25,000 tickets having been sold for Welcome to the Jungle 2008. Once again, SWF will offer up the best professional wrestling action in the world. Don't miss out.

A full month after he claimed it, Rich Money will finally defend the SWF World Heavyweight championship. He faces not one but two challengers, taking on Randy Bumfhole and former champion Jack Bruce in a SWF Threefold Threat™ match. The first title defense for "Hard Cash" will not be an easy one.

Christian Faith is taking on the monster Vengeance in the main event, with the SWF Supreme Championship on the line. The two had their issues in the past week. The Harbinger may have more to worry about as well, as Steve Frehley may be looking for some payback over what Vengeance did his close friend Ray Diaz after their match last night on SWF Legends.

Speaking of Diaz, he is apparently still going to wrestle tonight, facing "The Golden Boy" Rocky Golden. Diaz is reportedly suffered some serious injuries at the hands of Vengeance, but he is determined to still take on Golden. This will have to hand a serious advantage to Golden.

Steve Frehley and Brandon James will face each other in a Number One Contenders match. The winner will likely face the winner of Christian Faith and Vengeance for the SWF Supreme Championship, a match which would most likely take place at the next Supreme Wrestling Federation pay per view, SWF Under Control 2008 in September.

Few champions in SWF history have coveted their championships more than Marc DuBois covets his SWF North American championship. The Canadian youngster has a tough challenge on his hands, as he takes on Mexican star Antonio Marquez and the loner Darryl Devine in a Threefold Threat™ match. With the talent of these three youngsters, this match is one of that could steal the show.

Another match with great potential is another Threefold Threat™. This one is for the SWF Shooting Star championship and champion Kid Toma will face nemesis Bart Biggz as well as newcomer Acid. One of the most exciting and talented high-fliers in the world, Acid is a major addition to the SWF's Sky Club Division and he will be looking to make an immediately impact.

The SWF World Tag Team titles will be on the line, with the current champions Valiant & Giedroyc taking on Sin Inc. Joe Sexy of Sin Inc is a two-time World Tag Team champion, but with former partner Angry Gilmore. He's keen to land the belts with new partner John McClean, which would make Sexy one of the few men to hold the tag team belts three teams in the SWF.

Also on the card is a match to determine the new number one contenders for the SWF World Tag Team Championships, with Badd Kompany facing The Supreme Nightmare and The New Wave.

The SWF Sky Club Division Tag Team championship belts will also be on the line, and won for the very first time. It will either be the High Concept duo of Elmo Benson and Groucho Bling or newcomers Ota and Narto - The Tokyo Underground. This one should be an entertaining match.

In a moment that many fans are eager to see, the heavily-hyped "Payne" is set to make their debut. Just who or what this is, no one seems quite sure. Even Richard Eisen appears to be in the dark. This one has the wrestling world talking, and you cannot miss out.

SWF Welcome to the Jungle 2008 is available in North America exclusively on North America Prime Select pay per view.

Random thought of the day… sometimes, success is a subjective thing. One man's failure is another man's success. It isn't always that easy to tell the difference.

The mood after the pay per view ended was jovial. Everyone was thrilled with how the show had gone down. Maybe not quite as good as The Supreme Challenge last month, but still another really show. The crowd was hot and the matches were very good. It would be a shock if the reviews weren't fantastic and the pay per view buys numbers weren't strong as well.

Richard Eisen was in a great mood. More than great - he was downright jovial. He moves around the room, talking to everyone - from the superstars to the jobbers. He glad-handed his way around the room like a campaigning politician.

The room was huge. It was one of the many convention rooms in the Nevada State Armoury. The building was once the biggest armoury in the Unites States, but had been sold to a private investors in the mid 1990s and they converted it into a massive arena and convention center. Its the best arena that Vegas has at this point. Eisen had booked the room for the show's post-party. He had spared no expense, with full catering. Shortly after the show ended, pretty much everyone was there. That was a bit surprising, given then "no alcohol" rule of Eisen. I was sure the party wouldn't last, as people who disperse to smaller parties of their own - we were in Vegas, after all.

It wasn't hard to understand why Eisen was in such a good mood. Once again, he had won. With back-to-back pay views that were great, and consistently good ratings on TV across the board, the Supreme Wrestling Federation was sitting exactly where Eisen felt it should be. TCW still hadn't recovered from their fall yet, and might not for awhile. Though the casual fan might not realize it, Eisen's choice of venues for the pay per view was made to send a message. The Armoury is where TCW had held some of its biggest events over the years, but they hadn't been there yet in 2008 but they quite simply couldn't fill the arena at this point. The SWF just drew close to twenty-five thousand fans there - that was a message Eisen was sending to Cornell, and I doubt the TCW World Heavyweight champion would fail to understand that.

Eisen had even brought out his wife and daughter with him to Vegas. They were both at the after-party, which made things a touch interesting for me. I talked to Amanda a bit, but we kept out distance for the most part. She gave me the occasional smile, but she kept things discreet. It could have been a lot more awkward for me if this happened a few weeks before, when Kristen still seemed to think there was something between her and I. She seemed to have figured it out and moved on. The rumor backstage was that she had something going with Rocky Golden... which I'm sure Rocky's pretty young wife wouldn't approve of.

As I munched on my plate of food, I gazed about the room and found myself considering whether the pay per view had been a success for me. In a basic sense, it definitely had been. The matches were good and the pushes our stories ahead. The debuts of both Payne and Acid had gone over well. Yet some things didn't sit right with me. A lot of guys had been left off the card. So what is success? Success is probably whatever you want it to be. Its a strange thought, but its just too subjective to say that what equals success in one person's life will mean the same in another. We all have different expectations, dreams, and goals. So what would equal out to success for me? That was the question I really needed to answer...

Random thought of the day... one has to take responsibility for one's own actions, but is that something you can force someone to do?

I took the seat across from Richard Eisen's desk that was becoming quite familiar to me. The owner and CEO of Supreme Enterprises assessed me with his hardest Chessmaster stare, saying nothing. I sat patiently until the boss told me whatever it is he had to tell me.

Was it really only a few months ago that this hotseat would have had me quivering internally? When that hard stare would have felt as it was probing the very depths of my soul? No, it did nothing. I was perfectly comfortable taking the seat across for my boss, at his request and unknowing what he wanted from me. I had moved beyond the point of fearing Richard Eisen, and I wasn't certain if that was a positive development or not.

Of course, this situation could get uncomfortable quite quickly if Eisen posed to me a question about me having been seeing Amanda. I was actually less concerned about than ever. In part because I felt I was becoming indispensible to the Supreme Wrestling Federation. Moreso, it was because the new semester at Brown was about to begin and Amanda had moved into the city in anticipation of that.

Richard finally broke the silence, demanding, "Okay, kid, tell me about your idea again."

I immediately knew which idea he was referring to. I could see a magazine sitting facedown on the credenza behind his desk, and I would have wagered a significant sum that I knew which magazine and which issue that was. I mused on this for a second before I said anything in response to Richard.

The magazine was the latest issue of "In Our Time", the popular and influential bi-weekly news publication. That particular issue was causing a bit of a stir through the professional wrestling industry due to a little two-page article buried near the back of the magazine. The article was the story of a former wrestler named Keith Irvine and while it was not overtly negative, it did let loose a few dirty little secrets of the business.

Irvine had been a "star" during the late 1980s and early 1990s, at least according to the magazine. Really, he had been a traveling jobber who kept in steady work due to a couple of interesting gimmicks and his willingness to take a bump. He had wrestled as "Testy Irvine", "Mad Dog Ralphus", one of the many "Masked Patriot" incarnations, even a few masked heel gimmicks. He was apparently big enough to be menacing with a mask on, and that helped him find work, too. So far as I could learn, he had never found real regular work with any promotion. A lot of his stuff was done with the tiny local promotions that popped up and disappeared, sometimes almost at random, during that period. He had done a bit of work in the Texas Wrestling League, Ed Henson's Canadian Wrestling Federation, and with a couple of the promotions that were involved in the East Coast War. He didn't say anywhere for long, whether that by choice or situation. The article did state that for most of that period, he was wrestling full-time without a job on the side, which is pretty tough for a guy in that situation. The article also mentioned Irvine as a "former champion", though I don't think he ever held a belt in any of the promotions of note, so its safe to say I wasn't taking the writer's research as gospel.

The article touched on Irvine's career, but that wasn't the focus. The focus was split between Irvine's post-career health problems and the prevelence of such concerns throughout the business.

Tragically, Irvine is confined to a wheelchair. The immediate cause of that is a car accident he was involved in about six years ago, but the accident simply exacerbated a severe back problem he already had (at least according Irvine and his doctor). The back problem was a product of his career as a professional wrestler. It wasn't the only long-term consequence of his chosen career, as he had battled addiction post-career to both alcohol and painkillers. Irvine's story was that the hard life on the road as a wrestler had let him using those as methods to cope, and he had a quit those addictions without the aid of rehab as he had no health coverage and couldn't affort it himself. The lack of health insurance while wrestling was apparently the primary reason that the back injuries had gone largely untreated while he was wrestling. That part of the article also detailed the financial situation that Irvine had suffered through after his career as he didn't make a great deal of money and had saved none of it, had no pension, and the bad back and addictions left him in no condition to find real work even before the car accident.

The second half of the article talked about how Irvine's story was not unique in the business of professional wrestling. It mentioned how many former wrestlers found themselves facing major health concerns due to the damage done to their bodies and the addictions that many of them managed to acquire. The article revealed a fact that was not quite a secret but was also something the industry didn't like having openly proclaimed - wrestlers under contract are not actually employees of a given promotion. They are almost always considered sub-contractors. Which means they receive no benefits, no pension, usually no health insurance, and the promotion is not responsible for their welfare the way they would be for an employee. There is no union to represent these wrestlers - yet - so they are little on their own. Though the Supreme Wrestling Federation was not mentioned by name, it was noted that even the superstars of the business were on their own as far as health insurance and long-term financial security went. Though the article was not heavy-handed about it, it was criticizing those who ran the industry for allowing these men to abuse their bodies for so long and then not taking care of them once their career was done.

Was the article damaging to Richard Eisen and the SWF? In a basic sense, no. It didn't even mention the SWF. And the article didn't mention that for the past decade, all SWF contracts had included mandatory health insurance. The cost of the premiums was split between the company and the worker, but the inclusion was not optional. This was a result of a couple of incidents in the mid 1990s. However, the insurance coverage ceased when the contract either ended or was terminated. The SWF retained to responsibility to an injured worker who was no longer under contract. However, despite this, Eisen was smart enough to recognize that there could and likely would be some media backlash towards the SWF. As the biggest and most visible wrestling promotion in the world, the SWF represented the entire industry to many in the media and even the general public.

Even if this article didn't generate any backlash towards the SWF, it was going to be the first of many such articles. I had to assume that Eisen had done the same research I had. Or perhaps he had known before the article came out, as the man was far better informed than I was. The "In Our Time" article wasn't a random writer finding a story. It was the first shot in what was sure to be a full-out assault on the media by a popular publishing company that was publishing a book about Irvine's story. Expected to be released early in the fall, the book had been written by a well-known non-fiction writer. It was going to be the inspirational story behind Irvine overcoming the obstacles through his life. The early reviews for the book were strong. Even if it didn't become a best-seller, it was not going to be your typical pro wrestler biography aimed purely at wrestling fans. There was even talk of a movie deal.

So Eisen was looking at doing some preemptive damage control. Which is brought him back to my idea.

I finally spoke, saying, "Well, its actually a couple of ideas. The first would be to have every contracted wrestler go through a short seminar about long-term financial planning and preparing for retirement. The second was to set up an optional pension plan that workers could contribute to if they chose. And third, look at having a policy of extending the health insurance coverage for any injured workers who are out of contract."

There was a long moment of dead air between as Eisen mused on this. I didn't get nervous and add more, not did I try to justify my ideas. They were what they were and he could take them or leave them. I first made the three suggestions about a month ago in a meeting with Richard and Jerry. The three thoughts were quickly dismissed by both father and son. I didn't feel offended by it at that point - I'm an ideas guy and I come up with a lot of stuff. Obviously not everything I pop out with becomes reality. But at least I know that my ideas are listened to and weighed, not simply rejected outright for no reason.

This is an idea I had a few weeks ago. I asked Robbie if he wanted to go out for some fun one evening and he told me he couldn't afford to. That statement stuck in my head for some reason. Robbie isn't so great with his money, something I've known as long as we've been friends. His contract with the SWF isn't exactly making him rich, but he's making pretty respectable coin for a 22-year old with no post-secondary education. While Robbie is a very smart guy, he's still a 22 year old and not saving anything he makes. That's very typical of the younger guys in Supreme, just as it is anywhere.

After my idea was initially rejected, Jerry Eisen gave me a bit of an explanation a few days later. It was because the SWF - well, really the Eisen clan - held forth the ideal of personally responsibility. The contracted workers were grown men and women, and they had to take responsibility for their own actions. A good theory, but someone else failing to take responsibility for their own actions could affect the promotion.

Richard sighed heavily, telling me, "Well, I talked it over with Jerry and we figure we gotta do something. You heard about this "In Our Times" article?" I nodded slowly. "Well, that's part of the problem. We're gonna be the biggest target if the media decides to turn on the industry to any extent. It really won't matter to them that we never employed the guy at all."

"No, it probably won't," I agreed.

"Its not going to get better, either. Someone like Chord is a walking time bomb. After the damage he did to himself in the ring and out of the ring, its only a matter of time. There will be some serious fallout from that."

"Or Sam Strong."

Eisen just nodded slowly. He told me, "So if we get something in place now, it looks like we're being proactive."

I didn't fail to notice that Richard Eisen wasn't worried so much about the actual long-term welfare of the workers as he did about what effect such things could have on his promotion. That did not surprise me at all. It probably should have concerned me, at least.

"So what are you thinking?"

"To go with your ideas," was the owner's answer. "I'll have the Business Office look into setting up the pension thing. You can do the presentation, so you can start putting that together." Eisen went on for a couple of minutes, outlining the plans. I struggled not to grimace when Richard put the seminars on my plate - I hardly needed something else to do at this point, with everything else I was dealing with.

As I left Richard's office, I found myself wondering whether trying to force someone take responsibility for their own decisions for the wrong reasons was really any better leaving them to wherever their own decisions took them?

The four hour wrestling spectacular was awarded TEW.com's coveted A* show grade. The promotion also saw a couple of debuts, with Tyson Baine playing the monster heel under a leather strap mask as Payne and Japanese legend Pistol Pete Hall. While the impact of Baine/Payne is not surprising, the way that fans have taken to Hall has given pause to many in the business. Former DaVE stars Bryan Holmes and JD Morgan also joined the promotion.

Total Championship Wrestling continued to display what many consider questionable decision-making. The lone show in the month of August for TCW was the Hotter Than Hell pay per view. A solid show, it earned a B grade. However, it was considered disappointing to many as the long-awaited Tommy Cornell and Wolf Hawkins match took place in the main event. One of the most anticipated match ups in the entire wrestling world, the result was solid but hardly classic match. A B+ grade match is not usually a disappointment, but with many fans and insiders expecting the long-awaited match to be a certain A* affair, its hard to not see the match as disappointing. To make matters worse, Cornell pinned his former protege clean to retain his TCW World Championship, leading many fans to grow louder in their concerns that the star of TCW is putting his own ego ahead what is best for the promotion. The match also officially ended The Syndicate, a stable storyline that was the hottest angle in America in late 2007 but that many fans feel was wasted with questionable booking through 2008. There was good news in August for the promotion, as NCTV will put TCW Presents Total Wrestling back on the air. This good news could be countered with more problems, as there is talk that the legend Sam Keith is moving very close to retiring from active competition. There are also rumors of locker room problems, with some of TCW's top talent getting envious of former TCW stars like Rocky Golden, Troy Tornado, and Tyson Baine being made global stars in the SWF.

The slow climb of the United States Pro Wrestling promotion of Sam Strong continued its gradual ascent. An ascent so slow that some argue no progress is being made at all. Beloved by a relatively small group of hardcore fans, USPW is finding it difficult to find a mainstream audience, even with some familiar faces on the roster. Many industry insiders are beginning to question how far USPW can climb before those familiar faces on the roster are lured away. Through the month of August, USPW put on shows that graded out in the C- to C+ range, including the Apple Pie and Wrestling pay per view grabbing a C+ rating. The main event saw James Justice retain his belt over Big Smack Scott in a surprisingly watchable match. Will the much-hated Big Smack Scott bring enough heel heat to make fans tune in, or will his often-awful skills in the ring drive them away? Strong is taking a risk in featuring the ever-confident and usually-entertaining muscle man, but its one that could pay off.

Big Smack Scott was an unlikely hot topic amongst America's cult and regional promotions, as well. The highly-regarded Coastal Zone Championship Wrestling surprised many when the SWF washout Big Smack Scott snagged the Coastal Zone Championship at their Wave of Fury 2008 show, beating fan favorite Fox Mask. The C- show was better than the fare offered by FCW It Tolls for Thee (D+), PSW Revenge (D), and RIPW Lights Out (C-). Those were all ahead of BSC's Wanted Dead or Alive, which grabbed an impressive E+. Then again, if a serious wrestling promotion can't put on a better wrestling show a pure TNA federation, it might be time to pack it in. The mighty TEW.com reviews gave C grades AAA Girls Night Out, NYCW Test of Wills, and MAW The Battle of the Atlantic. The MAW show as probably the best of the bunch, featuring a pair of C matches.

Will the return of TCW to the TV sets of wrestling fans change the trends of 2008? Insiders are doubtful, but TCW remain the only promotion in America - and, really, the world - in a position to give the Supreme Wrestling Federation any degree of competition.

Canada

Quote:

Canada's stalemate amongst its promotions continued. With the Supreme Wrestling Federation pulling respectable ratings on CBN and North of the Border Pro Wrestling not on TV through August, the Eisen's can make a legitimate claim to being the biggest promotion in Canada. That can't sit well with the Stone's or the DeColt's.

The DeColt's Canadian Golden Combat had a solid month, putting on a series of decent shows. It didn't really allow the DeColt's to close the gap on NOTBPW, however.

The Stone's had a quiet month, putting on their Summer Smash show mid-month. It was hardly the most spectacular of wrestling displays that the Stone's have ever put on, earning a B grade. The main event was another clash between the Stone brothers, with Jeremy and Dan putting on a match that hardly lived up to the classics they have put on in years past. The promotion seems to desperately miss the talents of Johnny Bloodstone and will have to try to find something quickly, as the Stone's have resurrected NOTBPW Championship Wrestling which will now be seen on Canada's Pop! Network.

4C remains a long distance between the big two. The One Night Only show pulled a decent D+ grade. Troy Winner's promotion has a long way to go.

Mexico

Quote:

The world of lucha libre saw a quiet month in August. It was relatively status quo for Big Three. None managed to offer outstanding fare and none had shows bomb. The TV shows that each promotion now has pulled respectable ratings. However, with SWF showing its ultra-popular SWF Supreme TV on the sizeable TV-Mex beginning in September, things could get interesting.

To the surprise of many, the upstart Mexican Hardcore Wrestling promotion signed a deal with El Canal Hombre to debut a show in September. The show is to be called Arte De Luchar and represents a serious step towards making the Big Three a Big Four in Mexico.

United Kingdom

Quote:

The big story in the United Kingdom was the apparently drop in quality from 21st Century Wrestling. Clearly the class of professional wrestling promotions on the British Isles, 21CW now finds itself struggling. Like many promotions around the globe, 21st Century lost some of its top talent to the Supreme Wrestling Federation. That does not entirely account for the lackluster shows that Jeff Nova's promotion has put on of late. The Best of British Wrestling show in Week 1 was given just an E+ grade. To put that in perspective, the Las Vegas TNA promotion Babes of Sin City put on a show that earned the same grade. The big monthly event for 21CW earned just a D. Its going to be difficult for the promotion to move back up from its current Regional size with such shows.

To make matters worse for 21CW, Ring of Fire put on a very solid show with their Attitude Adjustment event. Though barely 200 fans showed up, it earned a C+ grade and featured a B main event of Merle O'Curle defeating British Samurai. ROF could push past the bigger promotion if things continue.

Japan

Quote:

Japan's biggest story and moment happened on the final day of August, as falling giant Burning Hammer of the Wrestling Gods chose to take the book away from Haruki Kudo. The story broke late in the day yesterday and details are still sketchy at best. Better known as Hooded Kudo, Haruki Kudo is one of the most respected men in the business. A long-serving star for BHOTWG, he has held the prestigious Burning World Championship three times and is recognized by fans around the world. His decisions out of the ring have been questionable, however, with Burning Hammer losing their coveted National status in recent months due to a high degree of inconsistency in their shows. The promotion has clearly fallen behind rivals Pride Glory Honor Wrestling. Kudo's decision to put the Burning World Championship on Yasuhiko Taira, who has had a lackluster run thus far, is rumored to have sealed his fate. Without the Head Booker position empty, BHOTWG is in a tough position heading into a new tour. It also remains to be seen if the decision will sour the relationship between owner Kaneie Komine and Kudo.

Of the promotions on tour through the month of August - Pride Glory Honor Wrestling, 5 Star Supreme Wrestling, World Level Wrestling, and Warrior Engine XXV - the order of the month was inconsistently. All of the promotions, even WEXXV, offered some very shows through their tours but also some less than exciting fare.

Pride Glory Honor Wrestling was particular guilty of this. They set a disturbing trend on their Hell Hole tour of having secondary main events outshine the main events. This was painfully obvious in the main events featuring current Glory Crown champion Mito Miwa. The "Pride Warrior's" reign as champion thus far has been more disappointing than his first run in 2005 and 2006. PGHW did see two belts change hands, as Team Toronto lost the Glory Tag crowns to Masaru Ugaki + Ryoma Muruyama and Kozue Kawashima claimed the Historical Japan title. Though the promotion is hardly in danger of falling far at this point, they will likely look to make some changes as they prepare for their next tour in November.

One of the more interesting moves in August was Munemitsu Senmatsu leaving Warrior Engine and signing with World Level Wrestling. He made an impact with WLW, as he won the World Level Streetfighting championship. Insane Machine won the promotions Show Stealer belt. It was a solid month for the growing promotion, which continued to win over new fans with its Powerslave tour.

August was something of a breakout month for 5 Star Supreme Wrestling. The Joshi promotion is often overlooked, even in Japan. The month saw several A-caliber matches between Sensational Ogiwara and Kit Hatoyama on 5SSW's Wrathchild tour.

Inspire's Golden Tour saw Tadiyuki Kikkawa's promotion continue its slow move towards nation with a series of solid if unspectacular shows. Inspire has shown a bit more consistency than most of Japan's promotions through the month, but they were not completely even either.

The month of September will be one to watch in Japan. With PGHW, World Level, and Inspire all powering down and Burning Hammer of the Wrestling Gods on tour again, it will be a chance for the fallen giant of Japan to regain some ground. Golden Combat Grappling will be out on the road again as well.

Australia

Quote:

While it might not be fair to say that Australia is the most boring region in the world when it comes to professional wrestling... well, let's just say life isn't fair. APW did have themselves a good month as they earned a D+ grade for their Championship Carnival 2008 show.

Retirements + Events

Quote:

No big names walked away in August, after July saw the legendary Dread walk away. Thunder Hike, little known outside of her native Japan, decided to retire from active competition at the age of 38. Murderous Mikey also left active competition, though few fans are likely to notice. Mexican legend Mephisto left active competition nearly thirty years ago, but he quietly left the business this month. As did Preston Holt, the former wrestler and owner who is far more likely to be remembered for his role in the East Coast Wars.

One of the biggest women in the world of women's wrestling - literally - had herself a bit too much fun while in Japan. Gorgon was arrested for an incident outside a nightclub, though no word yet on whether charges will be pressed over the affair.

SWF.com: Top Ten

Monday, Week 1, September 2008

SWF.com introduces a brand new monthly feature, a Top Ten of the Supreme Wrestling Federation roster! The list will appear on the website at the end of each month. The rankings are based off of fan voting on the website and the opinions of several SWF.com staffers.

The rankings are based on won-loss records, opponents, match quality, and those intangible "style points". And remember fans - this all just for fun!

Quote:

1. Rich Money

SWF World Heavyweight Champion

The SWF World Heavyweight champion only put his belt on the line twice in the month of August, but he successfully defended in both. The defense against Guide was a very good match, but it was the Threefold Threat™ match against Randy Bumfhole and former champion Jack Bruce at Welcome to the Jungle 2008 was a show stealer. The man who calls himself "Hard Cash" won every match this month, including teaming with close friend Remo to defeat highly-regarded tag team The New Wave. Rich Money's month is about as it gets for any wrestler in the Supreme Wrestling Federation.

Quote:

2. Christian Faith

SWF Supreme Champion

This one is sure to have many of the legend's fans howling about the injustice of the SWF Supreme Champion only being #2. The 42 year old put his Supreme belt on the line four times through the month. The highlight was an absolute war against Vengeance that was the main event at the Welcome to the Jungle pay per view. So why did Faith lose out on the top spot to Rich Money? Well, he found himself destroyed by The Harbinger after his win at the pay per view, plus he only won by disqualification last week on SWF Legends. Despite the two champions being on separate brands, many fans already see a classic rivalry forming - will we ever see the two meet in the ring, though?

Quote:

3. Remo

The man that many feel could become the face of the Supreme Wrestling Federation, if he ever makes it out his friend Rich Money's shadow. Remo had a great month, winning every match. His peak was beating Angry Gilmore in a match that many believed he would lose at the Welcome to the Jungle pay per view. "The Alpha Dog" is proving to be just that as he tears through opponent after opponent.

Quote:

4. Jack Bruce

It took an un-winnable challenge of Supreme Double Jeopardy at The Supreme Challenge 28 for The Long Island Angel to lose the SWF World Heavyweight championship he held for nearly a year. The former champion had a good month, despite losing matches to Rich Money and Remo, and drawing against Randy Bumfhole. Bruce always puts up an epic fight, even when he falls to defeat, and the fans love him for it.

Quote:

5. Steve Frehley

The Dark Destroyer continued his solid 2008. He only managed a draw against Brandon James at the monthly pay per view event in August, and fell short in a Threefold Threat™ against Rocky Golden and Christian Faith, with the Supreme Championship on the line. Frehley has often found himself on the outside of the world title scene, which is something that hurts his ranking here a touch. His burgeoning tag team with friend Ray Diaz helps, but the duo cannot pursue the SWF World Tag Team championships while on the Legends brand.

Quote:

6. Brandon James

"Big Money" gets this spot mostly on merit, rather than performance. The big man scored three wins this month, but they were only over opponents such as Remmy Skye, American Elemental, and Elmo Benson. James did have a big match at Welcome to the Jungle against Steve Frehley but it ended in a draw. Even with continued wins, James will have to take on some stronger competition to maintain this spot or move up.

Quote:

7. Angry Gilmore

Consistency is something to be admired, and few wrestlers in the Supreme Wrestling Federation perform as consistently at a high level as Angry Gilmore. The Canadian won three of four matches in August, losing to Remo at the pay per view event. A loss that many insiders feel should have been a win. Gilmore's win over youngster Sean Deeley was a particularly good match.

Quote:

8. Vengeance

No one here at SWF.com is quite sure of what to make of The Harbinger. The masked monster scored a pair of wins, over Ray Diaz and Steve Flash. He lost once, to Supreme Champion Christian Faith. Much of his impact in the month of August was felt outside the ring, as Vengeance made the Legends brand fear the very sight of him. The month ended with him being taken away, and no one is certain of Vengeance's status at the moment.

Quote:

9. Bloodstone

SWF Legends Champion

Though many questioned how Bloodstone would do in the Supreme Wrestling Federation, the technician has excelled. Since winning the SWF Legends championship belt from Chris Caufield at The Supreme Challenge 28, Bloodstone has put on a series of very good matches. His title defenses against Pistol Pete Hall and Robbie Retro were immense matches. Many feel he has already outshone stable leader Rocky Golden.

Quote:

10. Marc DuBois

SWF North American Champion

The 21-year old champion has been on a roll since he claimed the SWF North American championship several months ago. DuBois did lose in the month of August, but it was in a great match against The Long Island Angel Jack Bruce. The youngster successful defending his North American championship belt twice.

Honorable Mention: Chris Morrisette, Pistol Pete Hall, Rocky Golden, Randy Bumfhole, Acid, Antonio, Marquez, Guide, Scout, Ray Diaz, Valiant + Giedroyc
