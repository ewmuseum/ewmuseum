TEW.com Monthly Report: March 2008

Monday, Week 1, April 2008

To help wrestling fans keep up with around the globe keep up with happenings around the rest of the globe, TEW.com offers a monthly report on all the happenings in wrestling.

United States

Quote:

Things got interesting on the American wrestling scene in the month of March.

Things stayed steady at the top with the Supreme Wrestling Federation. The top promotion in the world continued to churn out world class Sports Entertainment. The promotions new "B" show, Generation Supreme, had been well received as a showcase for young talent. There were no title changes to note, though the promotion did introduce a new title, the Generations championship, for the youngsters but it has not been won by anyone as of yet.

What made things interesting is the continued growth of Total Championship Wrestling. Under the guidance of Tommy Cornell and Joel Bryant, the number two promotion in the wrestling world hit "International" status on TEW's ranking system. It marks the first time that TCW has reached that status and marks the Hollywood promotion as the clear number two in the wrestling world. Many insiders feel that TCW may indeed be closing the gap on SWF.

Part of what fueled the jump in stature for TCW was the success of the new show, TCW Wrestle Grand Prix. The new show has pulled strong ratings on Monday nights for GNN Total Sports. On the back of the move to International status, TCW has also been actively recruiting new workers. Some of new wrestlers added to the TCW roster include Puerto Rican Power, Shawn Gonzalez, Jack Griffith, James Prudence, Jack Marlowe, and Black Eagle.

Not all the news in March was good for TCW, however. Star brawler Eddie Peak suffered a shattered elbow at the start of the month and faces a year on the sideline. One of TCW's top tag teams, The New Wave, was torn apart as Thom Barrowman - aka Guide - chose to jump ship to SWF.

Not a great deal of note happened for rest of America's wrestling promotions. USPW put on a solid Liberty and Justice! pay per view, but offered up fairly bland stuff on TV. The CZCW show at the start of the month, Uprising, was one of the better offerings from the smaller promotions. As was AAA's Girl on Girl Action show (hey, we don't name 'em!). Nobody folded, or even looked close to it, and no one made a jump of significance.

Canada

Quote:

The status quo continued in Canada, with North of the Border Pro Wrestling continuing to churn out some top quality pure wrestling. The entire Stone family had to cringe when 41-year Jeremy Stone injured ligaments in his knee, but the resilient veteran continued to work through the pain.

Canada's other two promotions did little to make headway against NOTBPW.

Mexico

Quote:

The talk of Mexico was the launch of Mexican Hardcore Wrestling, a unique promotion launched by El Jefe Militar. With Carlos Moreno on board as booker, MHW spent the month of March populating the roster, then ended the month on a surprisingly strong debut event, El Vivir Para Hoy.

United Kingdom

Quote:

The news out of the United Kingdom was not good. The flagship promotion, 21st Century Wrestling, took a hit when it dropped from Cult to Regional status. The status of 21CW was not helped when main event star Adam Matraves chose to join GCG on a touring contract, leaving the struggling London promotion entirely.

Japan

Quote:

It was a very interesting month in Japan, and not all in a good way.

The new era for Pride Glory Honor Wrestling started with a band, as the Golden Tour debuted with two A-rated shows. One featured an A* match between Yoshimi Mushashibo and Eisaku Kunomasu, the other another A* match between the same duo. The PGHW Glory Tag Crown was captured by Team Toronto, comprised of Alexander Robinson and Lee Bennett. PGHW did see injuries to PRIDE Koiso and Eien Miyamoto, though both are working through them.

The combination of a strong tour and weakening competition saw PGHW take over as the de facto number one promotion in Japan. Though many predicted the slide suffered by Burning Hammer of the Wrestling Gods, it was still a bit startling for insiders to see.

The BHOTWG Impact Tour has featured a series of inconsistent shows, and the resulting drop in popularity saw the giant promotion drop from National to Cult status. Though a still-strong importance ratings has kept Burning Hammer as #3 in the world, PGHW seems certain to surpass them. In fact, the falling giant is in danger of losing its Lords of the Ring TV show, as network Japan Sports Vision had publicly admitted they are unhappy with ratings.

Things may not get easier on Burning Hammer, as the end of the month saw them lose two wrestlers to injuries. Veteran Sessue Kawate will miss time with a bicep injury, while Hyosuke Kokan will mess several months with a spinal injury.

INSPIRE's Day Zero tour has been solid, if unspectacular. Warrior Engine XXV made some new fans during its Burn it Up tour. Both World Level Wrestling and Golden Canvas Grappling were idle through March.

Australia

Quote:

With only one promotion - Australian Pro Wrestling - one wouldn't expect too much to happen Down Under. And they would be correct. Perhaps the biggest moment was Aussie youngster Swoop McCarthy making his debut for SWF. But actually on the island continent, there were two off-setting newsworthy items. APW got a boost when young star Nathan McKenzie resigned. Then the promotion took a notable hit just weeks later when diva Stephanie Drucker publicly overdosed on an unnamed hard drug.

Quote:

Random thought of the day... when you wear a mask for long enough, does that mask eventually become your true face?

It was barely a year ago that I was just another average professional wrestling fan. Every Tuesday, I would get together with some friends to watch Supreme TV... and Total Wrestling, to be honest. I went on Internet forums to read about and discuss. I played the wrestling video games. Somehow, I've gone from that to... this...? To being part of it. Part of the process.

The most disconcerting thing to me about going from fan to participant is realizing the differentiation between fantasy and reality. You go in knowing that the characters you see on TV every week are just characters, yet it still throws you somewhat when you realize that the actual individual is much different from the character.

Not everyone is different. Jack Bruce is definitely Jack Bruce. He really does talk like that and act like that all the time. I'm pretty sure I heard him shouting "show time!" before heading into the bathroom the other week. Brandon James is just a big dumb hulk, kinda like the talented meathead football player every high school has. Steve Frehley is indeed just a quiet, intense guy who loves to fight. Robbie Retro is what Robbie really is in real life. And Bowen really is a jerk.

A bit on Barry Bowen - I've never been a fan of Runaway Train. At all. I know that Richard Eisen loves his monster heels and they will always have a place in Supreme. When he was a bit younger, you could buy Train as that unstoppable force, just like you could with Bruce the Giant or Dread. When they had him take the belt back from Steve Frehley, though... I didn't see the point of it then. That my opinion of him as a fan. As a fellow wrestler and a booker, my opinion is much worse. After I came through my initial training with the DeColt family, they told me that, as much as they liked me, they didn't have space at the moment as new guys had been added to the roster recently. So Alex DeColt told me he'd make some calls. DaVE was already done at that point, but he checked with his contacts in most of the promotions in Canada and the US. He called both Chris Morrisette and Barry Bowen in Supreme. Bowen flat out said no. He had no interest in trying to help out a young kid, even as a favor to the DeColt family that helped him out at one point. Morrisette was more than willing and he got me the initial tryout. I've wrestled Bowen on more than one occasion, and I've had to book him for months now. As a wrestler, he is simply limited. He needs his opponent to carry the match or its going to be brutal to watch. Train has a few offensive power moves and that's it. To make matters worse, he doesn't feel he's limited. He seems to think he's on the same level as Jack Bruce, Angry Gilmore, or Rich Money. Delusional much? Suffice to say that Bowen has never given me any reason to like him, or even really respect him. Anyway, enough of my rant on Bowen...

A few of the guys did surprise me when I got to know them, though. Rich Money and Remo both turned out to be really nice guys. Very different from their SWF persona's, though both are guys you would not want angry at you. Eric Eisen is actually a pretty nice guy, too. Tom and Jessie Gilmore are the sweetest couple. A few guys - like Gimore and Enygma - are "normal" enough outside the ring in every way that you probably wouldn't realize they do something as crazy as wrestle professionally.

With the guys who have personalities that match their TV characters, I've started to wonder if that's really who they are or if that's who they became? Did they make the character out of their personality, or did their character eventually alter their personality?

This thought worries me. Because I've come to realize that I've been wearing a mask. My role in SWF doesn't allow me to be me without reservation. I've started to have to play the political games that I said I never would. I'm saying the things that people want to hear, instead of always saying what I truly think or feel. I've talked it over a bit with Robbie, and he seems to understand. He also told me that its no different that you end up having to do at almost any job. Compromising your beliefs a bit and becoming part of the "system" is just part of growing up. He's right. It just doesn't make me feel much better about it all.

If I have to pretend to be this person that I'm not for long enough, will I become this person? Will I forget who I really am? Or would this person I'm pretending to be who I was supposed to be all along? Holy hell, this is making my head hurt. Isn't wrestling just supposed to be entertainment? Just fun stuff to enjoy. Why does this feel so much like work?
