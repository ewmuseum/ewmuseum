TEW.com Monthly Report: May 2008

Monday, Week 1, June 2008

To help wrestling fans keep up with around the globe keep up with happenings around the rest of the globe, TEW.com offers a monthly report on all the happenings in wrestling.

United States

Quote:

The Tuesday Night War continued through the month of May. The kind of battles are always tough to score until the final blow is thrown, but this month past looks like another one for Supreme.

After losing Rocky Golden and Tana the Mighty to rivals SWF in previous months, TCW lost Troy Tornado and Texas Pete. The versatile Tornado was just returning from a long injury lay off and is seen by many as a major loss for Tommy Cornell's company. To cope with the losses, Total signed some workers with previous TCW ties, including Stevie Grayson and Fumihiro Ota. Several industry insiders have pegged this as "negative progress".

It was not all bad news for Total. They did manage to resign young star Wolf Hawkins. After having several stars poached by Supreme, the expiring contract of Cornell's protege was apparently seen as a major issue within the promotion. Every possible effort was made to conceal that fact, and Hawkins was offered a very sweet deal. In the end, the former TCW International champion chose to stay loyal despite a considerable offer from Richard Eisen.

The roster losses have taken a toll on Total Championship Wrestling. Despite managing to stay relatively strong in the ratings department, many observers feel the quality of the shows from TCW has dropped slightly. With Eisen constantly on the prowl for ways to hurt his competition, any other contacts coming due in the near future has to be a major concern for the #2 promotion in the world.

As for Supreme, the Eisen clan attempted to keep a new TV deal quiet. But word hit the streets about a new show before the promotion could announce it on Supreme TV last week. Showing Thursday nights on the CBA Network, the new shown is being called SWF Legends. It is a new full two-hour live show. No official word yet on what fate will befall the B-show Generation Supreme.

Perhaps the biggest news out of the Supreme camp this month was that of a departure. The Big Smack Scott experiment was finally terminated, with the reason being given as violations of the promotions Personal Conduct Policy. Sources indicate it may have been related to steroid use.

The often-overlooked number three promotion in American enjoyed a good month. USPW put on a series of solid shows. Leper Messiah joined Sam Strong's promotion, replacing The Amazing Winkie (aka Roger Dodger) who signed with the SWF.

The smaller promotions enjoyed a productive month, as several put on very solid shows. The post-Stomper era started out well for New York City Wrestling. MAW and RIPW put on shows of similar quality. CZCW was only a step behind. In regards to personnel, MAW saw some turnover as several of their roster joined TCW on written contracts. NYCW signed Sgt. Bubba Lee West, while CZCW made the surprise move of signing SWF castoff Big Smack Scott. Whether the big man can have an impact on the Coastal Zone will be interesting to watch in the coming months.

The coming summer will be interesting. The SWF will be stretching its resources to cover three shows. TCW will be trying to recover from the roster attrition of the previous months. USPW is trying to sneak up on both. It is definitely an interesting time to be a wrestling fan in America.

Canada

Quote:

Can the same be said of being a wrestling fan in Canada? For the most part, yes.

It was a relatively quiet month for Canadian Golden Combat. The DeColt household stayed together as star Alex DeColt chose to extend his contract with his father's promotion. Though nothing official was noted, one has to expect that landing a DeColt - especially the star of his generation - had to be appealing to the American promotions and event to NOTBPW.

After several months of growth and consistently great shows, North of the Border Pro Wrestling took some hits in May. It started at the beginning of the month when reign Women's champion Melody Cuthill retired at just age 27. Grace Harper won the vacated title belt, and the promotion hired Wanda Fish to replace Cuthill.

Mid-month, Jeremy Stone and Johnny Bloodstone engaged in a classic match that may go down as the best of 2008. Stone followed that up by winning Bloodstone's Canadian title at the end of month. A pair of persistent rumors could take away any positives from those, however. The more notable of the two rumors is that the Stone family is having trouble negotiating a new TV deal for Championship Wrestling, which could leave the promotion off the air in Canada. The second rumor is that one of the promotions stars is out of contract soon and could be looking to leave the great white North.

The only news from 4C was a bit of a surprise, as Hell Monkey signed a pay per appearance contract with the tiny Canadian promotion. A prodigious talent who has been making waves in Japan, signing with Canadian Charisma Championship Combat hardly seems like the best way a mark in North America.

Unless the fortunes of NOTBPW are indeed falling, things look set to continue in Canada as they have for a long time now.

Mexico

Quote:

It was a quiet month in the world of lucha libre, for the most part. The big news was both MPWF and OLLIE landing weekly television shows. MPWF Televisión and OLLIE Acción Viva will both be showon on Los Deporte Hoy starting in June.

The show of the month belonged to MPWF, as their Una Noche Salvaje show as the best Mexico had to offer. SOTBPW continued to produce solid shows. Upstart MHW is still trying to find itself a market, a problem that many insiders predicted for the unique Mexican promotion.

United Kingdom

Quote:

May is hardly a prime time for professional wrestling in the United Kingdom. The footie seasons are coming to an end, with all the championship and relegation races gaining all the attention.

The sole event of note in UK wrestling through the month was DJ Reason winning the 21st Century United Kingdom championship from Daniel Black Francis. The promotions in mainland Europe provided no moments of note either.

Japan

Quote:

Japan offered up some moments of note for the wrestling fans.

Pride Glory Honor Wrestling maintained its enviable position atop the heap in Japan. The promotions Golden Tour continued to be a success as it ran through the end of the month. True blood PRIDE Koiso won the PGHW Historical Japan belt. However, it was not all good. Superstar and PGHW Glory Crown champion Yoshimi Mushashibo suffered a broken neck late in May that is expected to keep him out of action for more than a year. Its a vicious blow to the promotion to lose one of its key stars. PGHW didn't slow down, however, as Mito Miwa defeated Eisaku Hoshino for the vacant Glory Crown belt just days later. The injury will definitely affect the promotion, but the quality remains to maintain.

Now a step below PGHW, it appears that Burning Hammer of the Wrestling Gods may have finally stopped the slide. The Super Impact tour continues through into June, and has offered varied fare. The fallen giant promotion is simply inconsistent with its shows at this point. However, a major step forward for Burning Hammer was reaching a deal with Prime Japan TV to get BHOTWG Lords of the Ring back on the air.

In other good news for Burning Hammer, legends Haruki Kudo (aka Hooded Kudo) and Elemental both extended their deals with the promotion. Rising star Yasuhiko Taira won the Burning World Championship from Kinnoji Horri. Gaijin Red Panther claimed the Burning Openweight Championship at the end of the month.

World Level Wrestling continued its Ragged Glory tour through the end of the month. It was a success, as WLW put on some great shows. If it continues, the promotion stands a genuine chance of surpassing Burning Hammer. The notable news for the promotion was Kazuma Narato winning the World Level Intercontinental belt.

Widely overlooked, Japan's lone female-only promotion has been making great strides forward. The Civil War tour put on by 5 Star Supreme Wrestling has been consistently solid, offering wrestling on par with all but the best of Japan's other promotions.

In GCG, Toshiharu Hybanshi unseated Pistol Pete Hall as the GCG World Heavyweight champion at the start of May. The only news from Inspire, not on tour for the month, was the resigning of Tornado Nagai.

Australia

Quote:

To say it was a quiet month on the Australian wrestling scene would be like saying the outback is sizable. Massive understatement. Australian Pro Wrestling held a show. Um, that happened and that's about it.

Quote:

Richard Eisen

In-Ring Promo

Richard Eisen comes out to a chorus of boos. He revels them for a few moments, before addressing the crowd while still at the top of the ramp. He tells the fans that they should enjoy the spectacle they are about to see, as its going to a great one.

More boos escort the CEO down to the ring. He climbs into the ring and addresses the crowd again. Eisen says that the whole SWF Legends show starting this week is making him feel a bit nostalgic. So name of nostalgia, the Times of Trouble pay per view will feature the Supreme Warfare double-cage matches!

Eisen explains for the younger fans that from the mid 1980s through the mid 1990s, the Supreme Warfare matches were the centerpiece of Times of Trouble. Four versus four in two SWF rings, both surrounded by a stainless steel cell.

The first Supreme Warfare match at the pay per view will pit Chris Caufield, Eric Tyler, and Thom Barrowman against Texas Pete, Troy Tornado, and Rocky Golden.

The main event match will feature Vengeance, Brandon James, Remo, and Rich Money facing Christian Faith, Chris Morrisette, Steve Frehley and Jack Bruce.

Oh, and Jack Bruce will also be defending his SWF World Heavyweight championship against one of the most exciting young stars in the SWF today.... Eric Eisen!

Phil Vibert

Video

A video plays on the Supreme Screen, with a camera following Phil Vibert through a series of hallways. A title states that this occurred "Earlier Tonight". Vibert stops at an unmarked office door and knocks. He enters.

Richard Eisen

In the office, Richard Eisen is seated behind a desk. Vibert says that he was told that Eisen needed to see him. The CEO questions the camera and Vibert states that its a lesson he learned from Eisen.

Eisen tells Vibert that he knows what he's been up to. That he knows Vibert was behind Tyler and Caufield trying to influence young wrestlers like Marc DuBois and the Bumfholes. Eisen says nothing for a moment and seems stunned at the suggestion. He then says it will never happen. Vibert just shrugs, saying that its the only way they can co-exist and its the only way to give the fans the best of everything that the Supreme Wrestling Federation has to offer.

Eric Eisen + Runaway Train

In-Ring Promo

The duo come in wearing suits and scowls, getting some notable heat from the crowd. As always, Eisen does all the talking while Runaway Train just stands around and glares.

"The Chosen One" tells the crowd that being part of something as important as the Supreme Protectorate takes loyalty and dedication that most men simply don't have to offer. Enforcer Roberts didn't. Runaway Train proved he does, by getting rid of dead weight that was holding him down.

Eisen tells the crowd that he has been scouring the Supreme Wrestling Federation roster to find more men - or even women - who fit the profile that being part of the Protectorate demands. He's found precious few. Most are part of the problem, which means they cannot be part of the solution. Most of the roster are self-centered and self-serving, unable to see the big picture and understand what is truly important. But Eisen believes he can find those who have the qualities soon enough.

He says that a strong Protectorate is important, as he will need help once he wins the SWF World Heavyweight championship from Jack Bruce at Times of Trouble 2008. Eisen states that he intends to be a long-reigning champion, and he will bring the pride, honor, and dignity back to the belt that Jack Bruce has managed to strip away from it.

The ref then struggles to keep the Samoans out of the ring while Devine grabs his belt and clears out through the crowd.
