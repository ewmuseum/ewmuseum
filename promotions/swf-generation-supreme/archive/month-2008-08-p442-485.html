Zimmy Bumfhole vs Flex Garcia

A solidly entertaining match to start the night. Z-Bum shows off some of his athleticism and skills while Flex tries to do whatever it takes for him to win. He fails, despite his best underhanded efforts. A very impressive Bumfhole in One allows the youngster to get the pin.

Richard Eisen

Backstage Segment

The camera follows Richard Eisen as he stomps through some hallways in the backstage area. He has a cell phone to his ear and seems to be looking for something.

Richard Eisen: Yeah, no, I can't right now... What? No, I'm busy at the moment... I don't to be honest. Some of the young guys asked me to attend something... No, I really don't know what. They just said a presentation...

He stops suddenly, staring to his left. He seems speechless.

Richard Eisen: Yeah, I'll have to call you back.

The CEO of the Supreme Wrestling Federation hangs up his phone. He can't stop staring at the wall. The camera finally moves around to show what he's staring at.

The long decorative wall is lined with a series of posters of major events that have been held at The Dust Bowl. There is a central collage of posters of SWF events that have been held there through the years. Its this collage that Richard Eisen is staring at.

He's staring at it because someone has sprayed graffiti all over it. In big red and greed spray paint is the phrase "Vive la Revolución" across the middle of the posters. Other phrases - "Mexi-Power", "Mexi-Can", and "Los Diablos" - are painted on the wall. Small devil horns have been put on every face of every poster.

Eisen stares at the graffiti for a long moment, clearly not happy but not really upset either. He turns around suddenly.

Antonio Marquez + Gino Montero

The Mexican duo stare back at the owner of the Supreme Wrestling Federation for down the hallway. The staredown lasts a long moment, like an old west staredown. Then the Los Diablos duo give their two-arm Mexi-Power salute and disappear.

Eisen wanders away, muttering under his breath.

Quote:

Giedroyc vs Ash Campbell

A solid if predictable win for Giedroyc over the youngster. Campbell is far from living up to his father's standards yet. Its not quite a squash match for one half of the tag team champions, but its not far off either. Even a bit of cheating from Playboy Jake Sawyer doesn't slow Giedroyc down much.

Darryl Devine + Dani Nevada

Backstage Interivew

Ace Newton, Ash Campbell, Cameron Vessey, Casey Valentine, Playboy Jake Sawyer + Richard Eisen

Backstage Segment

Valentine and Vessey reach under the bench and drag out a dirty-looking gym bag. They reach inside together and pulled out a shiny new championship belt.

Ash Campbell: This is yours, Ace.

Newton looks shocked. He takes the proffered belt from Valentine and Vessey. He cradles it, staring at it for a long moment. Then he looks up at his friends. He says nothing, just embraces all of them. Watching this, Eisen looks slightly bemused.

Richard Eisen: And that is the new official SWF Generations championship. Congrats, kid. But I have to be going.

The CEO departs while the Four Kings and their manager huddle up. Newton begins psyching them, encouraging them, at the same time as thanking them for the new belt.

The Heartbreakers vs Black Label Fight Club vs Sin Inc

Quote:

Random thought of the day... grudges are a waste of time and energy.

It seems like an odd, thought give that grudges and feuds are pretty much the basis of pro wrestling. In a Sports Entertainment promotion, at least, they're the lifeblood. A pure wrestling promotion like those in Japan seem to be able to get away with portraying professional wrestling as a sporting competition, but they even play up the occasional feud. Cornell likes to think of Total Championship Wrestling as similar, apparently, but he knows that he can't sell the shows without the feuds and grudges and some basic storylines. For Supreme, they are indispensable.

In real life, though? I'm not such a fan. Honestly, I've never been the type of guy to hold a grudge. And at this point in time, I'm just too busy with everything Supreme to really give a d*mn. Its sometimes entertaining that others don't feel the same, though.

There was a ton of stuff on my plate, but much of my morning was spent working on two new projects. Two new ideas I'd had that I had mistake to vocalize. The stories were entertaining, but I soon found myself yawning as I sipped on my coffee.

I was exhausted. I had worked yesterday, then stopped by Vibert's condo. He wanted to show me a couple of his favorite matches from the early days of DaVE. It ended up being a marathon viewing session that lasted until well past 2am. Somehow Vibert still looked fresh and rested, yet I was a wreck. The man worked long hours, had a wife and two kids, traveled, and yet he seemed to handle it far better than I did, despite being a decade and a half older. I was jealous. Well, not about the wife and kids part, though it was a really nice family.

I found myself musing on Pete's situation. Not that Golden Canvas Grappling were not a very big company anymore, but they were still a respected and important one. He had left a great position with them for a woman. Perhaps she would be the love of Pete's life, or maybe she would be just another doomed relationship. Yet he had taken the risk. It wasn't just the money from GCG he had left on the table, but the sponsorships that working in Japan brings, which can be very lucrative. Would he regret that choice? I got the feeling that Pete Hall wasn't the kind of man to worry about burned bridges. Life keeps on trucking, whether you make the right decisions or the wrong ones.

Could I do that? Its not like I'm living my dream with what I'm doing here at Supreme, but I do love it. Would I sacrifice this all for the love of a good woman? No, I don't' think I would. Is it because I'm too selfish? Or maybe because I haven't met the "right" woman yet, if there is even such a thing? I've really enjoyed the time I've spent with Amanda Eisen so far, but I would walk away from that without hesitation if it came down to it. Does that make me cold-hearted? Does it mean that its really not meant to work out? Do I care at this point?

Its been more than a week now since I saw Amanda, and while we have traded a few emails and instant messages, its been sporadic. I'd like to be able to spend some time with her, but its not exactly burning a hole in my soul that I can't. If I was smart about things, I should be trying to hook the girl and marry her - I doubt there's any better way to ensure that you will always have a role in this business than marrying into the Eisen clan.

Does Pistol Pete know something I don't? Likely. He probably knows a lot of things that I don't know. But I guess I have to work through things and make own mistakes. We all do.

As for Pete's specific role on SWF Legends, we have yet to determine that. If the fans continue to take to him, it might become a prominent role. Or he might stay as the occasional guy that we figured when decided to put him on the show against Bloodstone. The interesting thing about Hall is that he's more of a legend within the business than to the fans. He got hot in the 1980s as a big man with rare athleticism and all around skill, but he retired in 1992 at just 33 years old with a knee injury. For more than a decade, he continued to work in the business as a road agent and trainer, and there were few better. Hall was a great teacher. He help nurture so much talent. So while Hall doesn't have a long list of accolades like some legends, but he's done as much in his own way as someone like Christian Faith or Eric Tyler. The typical fan might not realize how much Hall has done, but much of the roster does. And maybe that's as important the more obvious kind of legend.

Random thought of the day… why does it sometimes seem like the things I do for myself impress others the most?

Richard Eisen gave me a hard stare from across his desk. The silence in his office felt tight. "Is this for real?" he demanded.

I nodded slowly.

My boss didn't say anything for another long moment. He looked at the document again, flipping through its myriad of pages slowly. "I'm going to need some time to really digest this," he told me slowly. "But this might be your most impressive work, kid."

I shrugged. I nodded. I shrugged again.

"Seriously, this is really impressive," Eisen said. "This is probably the best long-term booking I've ever seen in my entire time in the business."

"Its just something I put together," was my reply.

Which was pretty much true. I didn't assemble the document to impress anyone. It was for my own purposes and I didn't even intend to show it to anyone. So why exactly was my boss looking through it?

In booking terms, we are now at the point where I'm functioning fully as the head booker. Still don't have the title, of course. But I'm the one putting everything together and just using the Creative Committee for ideas and suggestions. There's been some stuff I've had to figure out on my own, as I've never had anyone like Michaels take me aside and show me how they did the long-term booking.

My system I have been using is that I only do detailed scripting of the shows about two weeks in advance. I do plan much further ahead than that, obviously. I was using the high-tech method of keeping notebooks. About a week ago, I decided that was not only messy, it was also not very secure. Losing one of those notebooks could drive the Internet fans wild and mess us up for months. So I started putting things together in some electronic documents. I ended up doing a full year. It obviously wasn't heavily detailed far into the future, just with major events, storylines moments, and title changes. Certain things were assumed, of course, such as new signings and debuts. I realized you had to be flexible and account for things like injuries. But this provided plenty of direction.

The only reason that Richard Eisen was flipping through the document right now was because Phil Vibert happened to walk into my office while I was working on it. He thought it was great, enough so that he mentioned it to Jerry, who suggested strongly that I should show it to his father. So here we were...

Eisen flipped through the pages slowly. He stopped somewhere and read a couple of the notes. He smiled at me, saying, "Now that would be a debut." Not sure which one he was referring to, I simply nodded again.

There was a lot in that document. A lot I didn't want to anyone outside of the Creative Committee members and key SWF staffers to see the document. Password-protected electronic documents felt a lot more secure than a couple of notebooks.

"Since I have you here, there was something I wanted talk to you about," Eisen told me. He gave me a hard look.

My heart started beating a bit faster, and I prayed it didn't show. Did Daddy Eisen know that I had been seeing Amanda? Would he approve? Was I about to get the "don't break my baby's heart" speech... or perhaps something worse?

Eisen slowly stood up and walked over to an old credenza that looked a bit out of place in the modern office. He opened and withdrew a box. A couple of quick steps and he was standing beside me. "Here," he said, shoving the box into my chest.

I looked at the white box. It was an Apple MacBook Pro laptop. "What? For me?" I questioned.

"Yes, but open it."

I opened the box and quickly realized it was not your standard MacBook Pro. The standard silver was instead SWF-blue and the backlit Apple logo on the top was actually an SWF logo.

"Whoa," was all I could manage.

"Yeah, I custom ordered a batch of them. That's the first," Eisen told me. "Cost a ton, but I think they're pretty nice looking."

"Pretty nice looking," I echoed. "That's one way to put it."

"This is the first one. I was going to give it to Jerry or Peter, but I figured after seeing this, you deserved it."

Whoa, that was a loaded statement. How were Jerry and Peter - and even Eric - going to react when they heard that I got the first one? Would they care? There was nothing I could do so I couldn't worry about it now.

"Thanks, Richard," I said sincerely.

"No, thank you, kid. You have no idea how much smoother things have been since you got here. Its starting to feel like the old days again. D*mn, but I miss the 80s and 90s sometimes. It seemed like everything we did worked. Every angle just got the crowd hotter. Every heel turn was monstrous. You shoulda seen the way Chord or Strong could work a crowd. The fans are just different now. Things have changed," Eisen finished wistfully.

"Yeah, they have," I agreed. I didn't know quite what else to say.

"But everything you've done, it feels a bit like its back in those good old days," my boss told me. "This laptop is just a small token of my appreciation. The first of many, kid."

"Thanks," I repeated.

"You best get going if you wanna make that flight," Eisen laughed as he looked at the clock. I nodded as he made the call down for his car to take me to the airport.

With a shiny blue new toy to play with, the flight was suddenly looking a bit more appealing. So I had once again managed to impress the boss when not trying to impress anyone. What could I pull out my *ss next to top this one?
