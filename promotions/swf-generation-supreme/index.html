Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

SWF: Generation Supreme is one author's diary of the Supreme Wrestling Federation, the biggest promotion in the CornellVerse, kept in the Total Extreme Wrestling 2008 game from March 2008 and posted to a Grey Dog Software forum thread from 16 September 2008. Its set-up post puts Richard Eisen's company at the top of American wrestling after three decades, challenged by Total Championship Wrestling, and asks whether the Sports Entertainment approach can stay relevant.

The author promises two voices: show reports and news told in a third-party manner, as if by a website, and first-person posts that give backstory and backstage storylines. The first-person voice belongs to Avatar, a young contracted wrestler who has been given a seat on the Creative Committee and whose idea the whole Generation Supreme movement is, with younger workers booked into their own show and, in time, a hand in booking the company.

The first two months run Supreme TV on Tuesdays and the new B-show Generation Supreme on Fridays, with Awesome Impact in March and The World is Watching in April. Jack Bruce defends the World Heavyweight Championship against Brandon James and then Remo while the masked Vengeance hunts him; Phil Vibert reveals that Richard Eisen is working with Emma Chase's Big Money Incorporated; and at The World is Watching Darryl Devine wins the Shooting Star belt, Steve Frehley wins the North American belt and walks away from it, and Ace Newton becomes the first Generations champion.

The diary's set-up post, its posts for March and April 2008 and the two pay-per-views are served here whole, with the roster, titles and reigns they establish. The rest of the saved copy, 1,123 pages in all, is collected and not yet served.
