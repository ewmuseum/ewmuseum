Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Burning Japan Pro Wrestling introduced itself in the early summer of 2000 as an e-fed built specifically around the puroresu tradition — the style of professional wrestling associated with promotions like All Japan and New Japan — promising clean, decisive finishes and heavy tag-team competition over screwjob endings and crash-TV angles. Its weekly broadcast, GOUKA (Japanese for "hell fire"), aired under a fictional TV-Tokyo banner, with the promotion's own president, Masahiro Tamura, presenting championship belts and leather jackets to its titleholders in a house tradition the promotion called its own.

The promotion staged its debut GOUKA on 21 May 2000, its own commentary team calling the show the start of the promotion's legacy, and followed with a second broadcast on 5 June building a World Heavyweight Title tournament through a roster of quarterfinal and semifinal matches. Three championships anchored the promotion from the start — the World Heavyweight, World Tag Team, and World Junior Heavyweight titles — the first of them at stake in a tournament final held over the following weeks.

The tournament reached its final on 21 June 2000 at a supercard called The Coronation, where the masked competitor Zebra Mask defeated Ed Dean to become the promotion's first World Heavyweight Champion. In the same broadcast, the new champion pulled off his mask, gave the crowd his own name — Mark Cutter — and announced his retirement from wrestling on the spot, handing the championship's future to a reign the surviving record never continues.

A roster update three days later trimmed several inactive names from the card and promised a growing mailing-list archive of future shows. No later broadcast or closing notice survives among the held documents, and how — or whether — Burning Japan Pro Wrestling continued past the summer of 2000 isn't in the record.
