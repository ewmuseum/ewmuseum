Silent Harris (Part 1): Dream Another Day

17 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10 / 17 / 02 It’s another humid, muggy, tepid morning in Florida and Marvin Harris is there to greet it on the balcony of his hotel room just outside the cramped metropolis of Tampa. He watches mist as it rises from damp grass and off the rows of spruce pines that line either side of the highway. He listens to the soft music of the birds fluttering overhead, collecting pine needles and bits of trash for their nests. He feels the encompassing embrace of the south Florida heat all around him causing him to sweat just a little, but still comfortable to a native. This had potential to be the most peaceful, tranquil moment of Marvin’s life, but instead he is more miserable than ever. He lets a long sigh roll out from the filter of his new gasmask, taking back in a deep whiff of its ‘new’ smell. His eyes drifted downward and his head bowed as he pondered how long he’d been wearing such masks, and it frightened him that he could barely remember a time when he didn’t. He’d just spent five months of his life motionless in a coma because of his gasmask, because of whom the gasmask made him. He wasn’t Marvin Harris the nobody factory worker with 2.3 kids, a decent looking wife, and a pinto. He was Marvin Harris the deranged gasmask-clad sycophant that took men’s careers, tortured, punished, and crippled people all in the name of a pseudo-sport. He had nothing else now. He realized he would have to spend the rest of his life bringing pain to others just to sooth his own even for a little while. He was not proud of what he had become, oftentimes the very thought of it disgusted him, but he was resigned to the truth of the matter. What else is there for me, he’d ask himself, and where else could I go? The answer was just as simple as it was damning: nowhere. As he stood from the balcony and walked back into his hotel room, he pondered on what exact moment had led him down the road that he currently walked. Naturally, there were plenty of people to blame for it: Fraud, his family, Fraud, no friends, Fraud, his own fear. No, mostly it was Fraud’s fault. That much he silently agreed was concrete. But there had to be more to it than just one ‘man’, right? Surely one, albeit torturously cruel and insane, man alone cannot change the very fabric of another’s destiny, can he? It was something to keep his mind occupied, something to eat up all that free time. He certainly had both of these since he got out (read as: escaped) of the hospital and back on the RUSH tour. His days would be hectic for the next few weeks, having to juggle his RUSH appearances with the PURE Deathmatch Tournament, and to top it all there was that student writing a thesis on him. He really enjoyed talking with her, more than he did most people. She was young, beautiful, and intelligent. A rare combination these days. He had to stop himself before he thought about her too much; because he knew he was only torturing himself more to obsess over someone that’s going to be in and out of his life in the blink of an eye. She’d write her paper, maybe get praise and a psychiatry degree for it, but no doubt her relationship with Marvin Harris would end there. Such was the way things had to be, the way they always had been for Marvin. He held no illusions about finding lasting happiness with a girl he’d just met and talked too for less than an hour, but he even wouldn’t deny himself the joy of remembering how it felt to have a woman listening to what he was saying, intently, intelligently, and not end with her running away screaming, literally. 'The gasmask,' he said to himself, 'It must be the mask that turns them off. Because it sure as hell can’t be the rest of me.' He chuckled and patted his stomach, letting the sarcasm drift off into nothingness along with all thoughts of her. He lies down on top of the bedspread and put his arms behind his head, staring up at the lazily turning ceiling fan. How many more days did he have of this left for him? He didn’t want to know, the number was surely gigantic. He drifted then, slowly falling towards a deep sleep, when all of a sudden he was awoken by a knock at the door. He quickly hoisted himself off the bed and walked over to look through the peephole. 'Monty? How in the hell do they always find me?' He opened the door for his older brother, and a decrepit man only in his mid-thirties made so due to his involvement in Marvin’s life on previous occasions walked into the room. He wore a suit, as usual, and had all the look and manner of a gentleman, everything Marvin was not. He seemed neither pleased nor displeased to see one of his younger brothers, but nonetheless his expression puzzled Marvin. 'Marvin,' he said, 'While you were in your coma this letter came for you. I just had to make sure you got it. That’s all. That’s all...' He pulls a black envelope from his pocket and hands it to Marvin, then without another word promptly turns around and walks back out of the room and down the hall. Marvin leans out of the doorway, watching with an even more puzzled look. 'Monty! What’s wrong?' he called after his brother, 'Whatever it is I’ll take care of it, man. Don’t shut me out again, come on! DAMNIT!' He slammed the door closed and began pacing around the room, staring at the unopened envelope on the bed. He knew every time something dramatic like this happened in his life it always led to something bad, and he was determined that this time, he just wouldn’t get involved, he just wouldn’t play the game. Of course, that lasted about another five or so minutes before he ripped the envelopes cover and pulled out the note. It was a rather small correspondence. Do you seek peace? Do you want to find your place in this world? Do you crave happiness? I can offer you all these things, Marvin, and I’ll be waiting here to give them to you. Here, in a place called Silent Hill. Your dreams await you. Come and see me, one last time. Signed,Prof. Sickman Fraud
