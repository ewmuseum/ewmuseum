Date: In time... [The normal hustle and bustle associated with the inner workings of an airport have understandably died off at 3 a.m. on Friday. A few scattered red-eye patrons tread slowly and methodically to the baggage claim. A few others read papers in the coffee shops, waiting for their air travels to begin. A low hum, the combination of voices, Muzak, plane engines and hundreds of yawns, fills the concourse.] [To the side of our view, a massive scrolling electric sign reads 'TERMINAL THREE -- LAX -- ON TIME' over and over. A handful of attendants stand idly by, preparing for what appears to be an unloading process. In the seats that accompany the terminal, a paltry number of friends and family wait quietly, half-asleep, for their loved ones to step onto solid ground once again.] [Two figures, however, break the mold of trance-like patience. They stand together, visibly attentive in the gray-black haze of early-morning darkness and bluish flourescent lighting.] [One man paces violently, the sombrero in his hand swinging with each step. The other, jittering violently, makes a fleeting attempt to read the magazine in front of him. From the wide shot, their movement stands apart from the lull that envelops the area around them.] [As their rest-defying activity draws attention from its contrast with the rest of the universe at this time of day, their voices - thanks to the magic of remote microphones - begin a limited explanation to their presence at Terminal 3.] 'What the hell is taking so long?' 'The plane is here.' 'I know the plane is here. I watched it ever since it came into view out of those goddamned clouds.' 'So what's your problem?' 'I'm wondering why the hell we're waiting three hours for them to get off the damn plane!' 'It's not three hours.' 'It feels like it.' 'It's only been five minutes.' 'Well it feels like three hours.' 'He'll be out eventually.' 'Eventually is a word we've relied on for entirely too long. He's on deadline. If we don't get something in withing the nest two days, he'll be fired.' 'Five minutes won't affect that.' 'It will if it takes three f*cking hours.' 'We can still get something in by deadline.' 'We shouldn't have even worried about deadline. We've been holding down the workrate pretty well since we got here. The no-show threat shouldn't have even figured into the equation.' 'Maybe, but-' 'But nothing.' '...' [They're uncomfortable silence is broken by the opening of the gate, and the subsequent filing out of the passengers from flight 1438 from Los Angeles.] [A few weary tavelers stumble out and prepare to join the line to the baggage claim. After the first influx of patrons, a lone man walks out into the terminal.] [Dressed in heavy clothing despite the summer weather, he keep a baseball cap pulled down over his face. As he ambles slowly out the door, his eyes catch the two figures, prompting a pause. Then, slowly, but surely, he makes his way over to them. His voice is picked up as well.] 'How did you guys find me?' 'A few phone calls.' 'Why are you here?' 'Because you're about to get fired by RUSH.' 'I've got special permission. No match, no need to take TV time from the guys who do.' 'Bullsh*t.' 'I swear to God.' 'So what do we do at the PPV?' 'We just show up, and see what happens.' 'Napoleon's Battle Plan?' 'The very same.' 'Why are you in L.A.?' 'Business.' 'What kind of business?' 'The kind that is necessary to only be referred to as 'business.'' 'You've been gone all week.' 'Business was good.' 'So are we making any waves at Ignition?' 'We'll see.' 'We need to start planning.' 'Nada. I fly back to Cali tomorrow.' 'What?' 'You heard me. I'll be back in time to meet you at the show on Sunday night.' 'You're killing me.' 'Sorry. Business.' 'Stop using that word.' 'Everything will be fine.' 'You're lucky you don't have a match.' 'That's the way it panned out.' 'You could overwork yourself.' 'Doubtful.' 'I think you're going to screw this over for all of us because of your greed.' 'I'm not greedy. I prefer the term 'entrepreneurial.'' 'Don't f*ck this up for us.' 'I'm not planning on it.' [With a huff, the three men turn from their conversation and towards the camera. The shot fades as they grow closer, just enough to cloud their features as they pass by...]
