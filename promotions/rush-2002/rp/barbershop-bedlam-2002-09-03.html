Barbershop Bedlam

3 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3rd September 2002 The floor was covered in faded black and white tiles; cracks, stains and mounds of hair were all the prominent features. The walls covered in photographs of various black figures throughout history; kings, queens, politicians and activists all held their place inside the humble frames along the barber shop walls. Underneath the spinning ceiling fan were four middle-aged men, staring at the man who had been introduced by the chiming bell and the opening door. He wore a bright Bob Marley t-shirt and baggy blue jeans. His clothes looked like they wouldn’t fit him in a million years, hanging from his defined frame like bats in a cave. He didn’t pay any attention to his reflection in the mirrors and simply stood there with his arms limp at either side. A man, stood next to the centre barber’s chair, was obviously the barber here. He held a white cloth in one hand a pair of shining scissors in the other. “How’s it going? Here for a haircut?” Asked Gil the barber. Joe nodded slowly, looking at the three seated men on the side through the corner of his eye. As they saw him looking they ruffled their newspapers and pretended to look thoroughly engrossed. Gil wiped the seat with the cloth and wafted his hand in a display ‘here it is’ type motion. “Take a seat, son.” Gil offered, a smile on his aged face. Joe stepped forward and sat down on the leather chair, resting his arms on the rest on each side. Gil began ruffling with Joe’s hair, wondering what he could possibly do with it. “What ya want doin then?” Gil asked, rubbing his chin and squinting his eyes. “Ah wan mah head shave bald mon, and could ya cut mah beard too?” Joe requested, scratching the overgrown black bush of bristles on his face. “You want all this hair cuttin off?” Gil asked, a little bewildered. “Yeah.” Joe replied, a feint smile beginning to appear on his lips at the thought of the drastic haircut. One of the three men put down his paper and stared at the reflected image of Joe’s face as Gil began work. “You a Jamaican boy then?” Isaac asked, his crackling voice cutting through the barber shop like a knife. “Yeah, Ah was born dere an moved over here a few year ago.” Joe responded, trying to see the trio behind him in the mirror as Gil shuffled around. “A Jamaican boy eh? You raised on all that Bob Marley stuff then?” Asked James, his soft and weak throat only just audible to Joe from across the shop. Before Joe could answer the third of the triad, Cliff, sidled over in his chair and turned the switch on a small black radio, the station wanted already tuned in. The low volume made the blaring James Brown song, already half way through, no more than background music. “When y’all talk bout music, you just need to have some on.” Cliff explained, folding his newspaper and placing it onto his lap gently. “You just have that radio on in case they put some of that modern music with the beats and young ladies!” James wailed in disapproval of the music-type. Isaac and Gil laughed, leaving Cliff with a disgruntled look on his face. He scrolled his newspaper up and looked around for flies to swat as he waited for the laughter to die down and the subject to change. “It true though, Cliff. You love all that modern music, you hate all the classic.” Isaac said from two seats away, leaning forward to see Cliff’s face. “That aint true, ask me anything and I promise I’ll get the right answer.” Cliff said, trying to boast and get himself out of the mockery. Gil turned round to face the trio, working on Joe’s hair without looking. A grin on his face as he thought of a question fit for Cliff. “Ok, name the pips!” Gil said in a chuckle, knowing inside he would not know the answer. “He wont know that, he more likely to know the code to some nasa rocket sh*t than that!” Isaac squealed in laughter. “Shut up! Shut up all o’ ya! I’ll name em all. Well, there was Gladys Knight.” Cliff began, desperately scrambling inside his mind for the lost knowledge. “Hell, we all know Gladys Knight! Nobodies asking for Gladys Knight! We want the backing singers names!” James exclaimed, holding back the amusement now. “Hell! Who knows the backing singers? Everybody remembers Gladys Knight, nobody remember the backing singers though.” Cliff replied, weaselling out of the question at hand. Joe scratched at his beard for a second, deciding to join in the conversation. “One of dem was call Bubba.” Joe said, interjecting himself. “See, the new kid know more than you Cliff!” James said, holding onto his stomach unable to control the laughter. “What kinda music you listen to boy, you just into that reggae business?” Isaac asked Joe intuitively. “Pretty much, Ah don have no problem wit any kinda music.” Joe explained plainly. James stopped laughing and looked into the mirror in front of Joe, studying his face carefully. “Hey, do I know you from somewhere kid?” James asked, trying to picture where he had seen the face before, “What do you do?” Joe smiled, his eyes brightening up as he had found somebody who recognised him. He shook of the stupid grin and looked at the reflected image of the man behind him. “I’m a pro-wrestler, I work for RUSH.” Joe revealed, readying himself for swarms of praise and general approval. “Aint wrestlin fake?” Cliff asked, frowning his brow and looking to the other two seated men. “Well…” Joe started, unable now to cut in. “What about that Junkyard dog? You sayin he was fake?” James asked. “Take it back, y’all better not jus forget Bobo Brazil. Now that man is a legend.” Isaac said, opening up his newspaper once again. “Wha bout me?” Joe asks, disheartened that his name had not been mentioned. “Who are you? Don’t even know your name.” Gil asked, still furiously working on Joe’s head. “I’m Joe Bananas, Ah wrestle in RUSH.” Joe stated, pride beginning to swell up as he awaited his kudos. “Joe… Bananas? What the hell kind of name is that?” Asked James, his face screwed up in disapproval of the name. Joe thought back to the days when he first received the nickname. The pointing fingers, the laughter that spun through his head. Joe Bananas! Joe Bananas! The name was so strange at the time; it didn’t even make much sense to him. “Now, Reverend Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. would never have been called some dumb name like that.” James said, his voice dry and coarse in the hot air. “Malcolm X neither, aint nothing wrong with Shabazz. Whole lot better than a fruit.” Isaac added. “Why? Is that Joe guy gay?” Cliff whispered to the other two. “Probably, he don’t look the most rugged of the rough ya know.” James speculated. “Careful, he look like he’d pimpslap ya in a second.” Isaac said, backing away inside his chair a little. “Ya think so?” Cliff asked, sitting on edge as the work on Joe’s head nears completion. Now down to a bare minimum of hair, Gil begins to shave at the head with a razor. Each movement revealed more and more of the ever-lessening hair on Joe’s head. He was thinking deeply, staring at the reflection of himself in the mirror. The light shining off his baldhead, he smiled and nodded in Gil’s direction. Cliff didn’t want to take any chances and had retracted himself from his seat, and walked slowly over to Joe and stood beside the chair as the finishing touches were done to the bare skin of Joe’s head. “Errr… Joe?” Cliff asked, a little nervous and on edge. “Wha ya wan?” Joe asked, not looking away from his reflection. “I just wanted to know…” Cliff started, his voice frail and the words seemingly trapped. Joe pulled his attention away from his own image and span round on the pivoted chair he sat in. He looked straight at Cliff, who had a hat his held to his chest. “Wa gwaan?” Joe asked, wondering what this could be about. “Well… you aren’t going to… pimpslap us, are you?” Cliff continued. “Pimpslap ya, why would Ah do dat?” Joe asked, confused after the odd question. “Errr… no reason.” Cliff said, slightly relieved and backing away. Joe shrugged his shoulders and reached into one of the pockets in his jeans, pulling out a piece of fruit known as a nectarine. Joe holds the round, orange fruit up to his ear. “What should we do Nigel? It seems like we’ve outstayed our welcome.” Joe said down to Nigel the Nectarine. Joe bobs the fruit up and down a little beside his ear, looking very interested in what it has to say. Cliff looks on nervously, biting his nails and his knees clattering together. “I think I pissed him off.” Cliff said, his teeth chattering. “He aint that big, we could take him down easy. Sure he’s young and we’re not, but there’s three of us!” James rationalised. “Nothing like a bit of cheating.” Isaac chimed in. Joe nods to the nectarine and shoves him back into his pocket, squashing him tightly against other pieces of fruit. He looks at the trio, the light reflected from his head. “Ah spoke to Nigel and…” Joe began. “Nigel?” Gil asked, not believing a single word of the entire conversation. “Dat nectarine, his name is Nigel. Anyway, as I was saying…” Joe began again. “Are you drunk or something?” Gil asked, stern looking. “Wha? No! Well, I had a few drinks before I came here.” Joe said confidently. “Ok buddy, out you go.” Gil says as he begins forcing Joe out of the barbershop. Gil gets Joe back out onto the street and slams the door hard behind him as he retreats back inside. Joe, with a shocked and puzzled look on his face pulled Nigel the Nectarine back out of his pocket and the two began walking along the path.
