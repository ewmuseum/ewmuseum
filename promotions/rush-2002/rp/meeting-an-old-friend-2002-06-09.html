Meeting an old friend

9 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-09-02 (Voice of Livewire) What do you get when you cross an eccentric Mexican, with a witty Brit and a fruit-loving Jamaican? You get the most dysfunctional stable in RUSH history. Yes folks, the Shack-Pack is dead, over, finished!!! And to be perfectly honest not a moment too soon. Ok, yes we had fun, yes we enjoyed each others company, but towards the end of our tenure I couldn’t wait to get shot of the two of them. Having to put up with another “Que?”, from El Spiffy or another “That’s ma fruit ya damn pickney” from Joe was becoming beyond a joke. So the Shack-Pack is no more, I wonder if this would have happened if Christopher Shackleton was still around. Chris was a genius, a man who had unbelievable plans and ideas for us. But he walked out months ago to sort out personal problems and the Shack-Pack hasn’t been the same since. Myself and El Spiffy actually thought that by recruiting Joe Bananas that we could fill Chris’ void. How wrong could we be? Joe made things worse and now I have to make things better by inflicting pain on the man who ruined the Shack-Pack. But Heck, at least the Shack-Pack lasted longer than Anti-Trust, the Solution and Playaz Inc. London is usually a cold and damp city, but during the early Autumn the weather seems to get even worse….if that’s possible!!! The late morning drizzle continues to shower down on the derelict graveyard that Livewire is standing in, causing the personification of cool to occasionally shake the rainwater from his damp head. Livewire continues to stand very still in the middle of the graveyard, his brown fur coat tightly fastened to his muscular body, his wet hair, draped over his face. Livewire seems to be staring at one particular grave that is directly in front of him. He takes a few steps forward and uses his right hand to brush his long black hair from his face, revealing his emotionless eyes. Livewire mumbles something that cannot be heard under his breath and kneels down next to the bouquet of flowers that he lay on the grave a few minutes earlier. Livewire bows his head, almost in a sign of respect and then gently touches the graves tombstone, the soft pieces of the tombstones brick and granite crumble in his large hands. The name on the tombstone reads Thomas Ian Smith : March 17th 1983- August 26th 1994. . A small tear runs down Livewire’s cheek as he speaks again, this time the volume of his speech is slightly higher making him more audible. “Well here I am Tommy, I’m eight years too late. But I’m still here buddy.” Livewire wipes another tear from his eye, his bottom lip quivers as he continues to speak, his voice now cracking as he blurts out every word. “Tommy, you were my best friend, the one person who I could trust when we were kids. I was an only child and sometimes I thought of you as my little brother, sheesh we were so close that people thought we actually were brothers. But you were better than me weren’t you Tommy? You weren’t stupid like me were you? You were sensible, you did things by the book. You were the perfect son, pure, almost. I detested that in you. I wanted to be like you, I wanted to be strong and deter the temptations of alcohol and drugs.” Livewire’s voice trails off as he says this. He takes a tissue from out of his jacket pocket and blows his nose before continuing, his voice now with more base in it. “Do you remember that fateful day Tommy? The day I accidentally took your life, I can remember it as if it was yesterday. March the seventeenth, nineteen ninety-three. A warm spring morning Tommy, a morning full of such delight and fun. We all went out didn’t we Tommy? Down to the local beach, it was me, you, that tubby blonde girl called Sarah and your elder brother Stevie. All of us away, the whole gang together. Stevie was seventeen and said he would look after us, he told both our parents and Sarah’s parents that there was no need to for them to come. Stevie was great like that wasn’t he Tommy? So dependable, so cool, so self-assured. Just a nice spring day on the beach huh, except it never turned out that way unfortunately did it Tommy?”. Livewire suddenly starts to struggle to breathe, every word uttered is followed by a deep breath. “Everybody was having a great time on the beach. It was fun, Sarah made the biggest sand castle imaginable didn’t she? So what went wrong Tommy? How could such a perfect day end up so sad? It was ME wasn’t it? It was all my fault Tommy, I just didn’t want to do normal things that people of our age should do.” Livewire stops talking momentarily and winces re-calling all the venom and hatred that he had kept hidden all these years at his young rebellious attitude. He clears his throat and begins to talk, keeping his eyes firmly focused on the Tommy’s tombstone that is beaming back at him. “Later that night when Stevie and Sarah went back to the hotel we sneaked out didn’t we Tommy? You said Don’t do it, we have to wake up early tomorrow to catch the coach home . So What I said, let’s have some fun. Was it fun though Tommy? Did you enjoy seeing me tip-toeing into your brothers hotel room while he was sleeping, to take his half-empty bottle of whiskey and two rolled up joints? I think not, you called me mischievous and stupid. I mocked you and said that you were a mummy’s boy, a pussy with no backbone. You hated that didn’t you Tommy? My mindless bullying of your innocent ways.” Livewire stands up and rubs the side of his head that he bruised the previous week in his title match against G.O.D. His eyes are now red and bloodshot from all the crying that he had been doing and his face looked pale and gaunt from stress and worry. To relax himself Livewire took a pack of Marlboro cigarettes from out his jeans pocket and lit a cigarette as it rested in his mouth. It may not seem respectful to smoke and flick cigarette ash on somebody’s grave but Livewire needed a cigarette to continue this apology to Tommy, to continue to recall the events that led to his untimely death. “So we went back to the beach didn’t we Tommy? It was pitch black in the morning and very cold. I reckon it was something like minus 39 degrees out there that night. What the fuck are we doing out here Rob, it’s two o’clock in the morning? You asked in all sincerity. I ignored you though didn’t I, I just lit that joint and started to get high. The fumes seeped into my young lungs and even though I was only thirteen I could handle my drugs. I then started to drink the whiskey and then the bullying started again didn’t it Tommy? I always acted like an idiot when I was drunk, I still do to this day. I poked fun at you because you wouldn’t touch drugs and you wouldn’t drink like I would. Shit, how the hell could I have been such an ass-hole? You were only eleven years old for Christ’s sake, why should you have to indulge in toxins that fucked up people twice your age?” The tears started to roll down Livewire’s cheek again. His eyes closed as he remembered the most disturbing part of that night. “I forced you to take that first drink of whiskey, I literally shoved it down your throat, the same way I forced you to inhale that joint, your first ever joint. Your small, little angelic face curled up….for I moment I thought you were seriously ill and then you just started laughing. You had the giggles, the first reaction when a newbie is starting to get high off marijuana. I was loving this, seeing my best friend get high off drugs and drunk on alcohol. I kept on saying that you were a real man now, but you sure as hell were more of a man than me.” Livewire takes a crumpled up tissue from out of his pocket to yet again blow his nose. He spits out some phlegm that was blocking his throat and takes another puff of his cigarette, the cold London rain causing the cigarette to wither and his hand to shake slightly. “Then I had the stupidest idea of my life. I said that we should go for a swim in the sea. I can remember you giggling uncontrollably at the idea before nodding your head and agreeing. You were in no state to disagree with me, you could barely walk in a straight direction as I took off my jumper and shorts and ran into the cold water in only my underwear. You did the same and we both swam about five hundred metres into the sea. And then it happened, you suddenly stopped swimming. Your face was white as a sheet and your arms suddenly stopped moving. I thought you were kidding at first, I even nudged you in the arm and told you to quit fooling around. But you weren’t fooling were you? By the way your eyes were rolling back into your head and your incoherent speech, I knew immediately what was happening…you were having as the druggies and junkies would say a BAD TRIP.” Livewire puts his right hand to his heart as images of Tommy’s limb body run through his head. “Damn it Tommy, I didn’t know you wouldn’t be able to handle it. I thought everybody could handle drugs and alcohol. How dumb could I have been to expect your little eleven-year-old body to be able to handle such toxins? I started to panic, I cried…Tommy wake up, I shouted…for the love of god please wake up. But you didn’t wake up did you Tommy? You just lay there in my arms, helpless. Your face was now turning a shocking shade of blue and knew I had to think fast. The tide was cold and wild that night wasn’t it Tommy? It dragged me sideways for a few minutes as I thought about how to help you. Tommy wake up I shouted again trying to get some sort of response out of you. But I didn’t get a response did I? All I got was a chill down my spine as your eyelids closed, the salty sea water splashing in your face”. Vivid flashbacks seem to run through Livewire’s mind as he holds his head in his hands, unsure of the emotions he is now feeling. Guilt? Fear? Delusion? Or a combination of the three. Livewire didn’t know, but one thing was for certain, recalling the events that led up to Tommy’s death was just as painful as witnessing it those eight years ago. “There was a big wave coming towards us Tommy, I had to make a decision soon. Swim, I shouted, you’re going to have to swim Tommy. The wave was mounting onto us Tommy I had to do something didn’t I? I tried to move your arms, but to no avail. Your body was freezing, just trying to you move made me shiver. You were an excellent swimmer weren’t you buddy, the best in our junior school. Fuck...you won two inner-city school swimming championships the previous year. This wave was easy for you Tommy, you could easily out-swim it and the tide. Well you could if you were conscious Tommy, but on this night you were far from conscious weren’t you Tommy? Trying to wake you was like trying to get blood from a stone. “ Livewire kneels down again to touch the tombstone, this time however he refrains himself from touching it and instead starts to sob uncontrollably. Livewire covers his face with his arm, almost ashamed for anyone to see him in this state…ashamed, crestfallen….WEAK. “I admit it, I panicked. I let you go and swam back to shore. My arms swinging frantically as the current of the tide came close to pulling me back. Finally I felt the soft sand of the beach as I reached the shore. I turned back Tommy and…and..and….I left you. I left your innocent body to get carried away by the waves. I was scared Tommy, frightened and scared, I let you down and boy did I know it. Like a thief in the night I picked my clothes up from the sandy beach and ran, ran as fast as my little legs could carry me, all the way back to the hotel and all the way to my hotel room where I fell into bed. My hair was wet from the sea water and my body lay still. I closed my eyes and just hoped, hoped that you would be found Tommy and that everything would be alright.” Livewire takes once last drag of his cigarette and throws the butt to the patchy grass that covers most of the graveyard. He maintains his pose and manages to control his breathing and sanity enough to finish his elongation of words. “But you never came back to us did you Tommy? I can remember coming home from the beach the next day with Sarah and Stevie. Sarah was in tears, holding a photograph of you to her chest. Stevie could barely speak, worried of what your parents would say to him about their missing son and his little brother. I will never forget the words of your mother the minute we got back to London to speak to her. Oh my god, no, not Tommy, where’s my Tommy? she gasped clutching Stevie’s leather jacket. During this whole time of worrying I could have told the truth. I could have told your parents what had happened, why you had gone missing. But I couldn’t, I was the very thing that I ribbed you about all those years ago Tommy, for not being a risk-taker like me…Tommy…I was a coward. I allowed things to get out of control because I wasn’t strong enough to do otherwise and I don’t mean strong in a physical sense either, mentally I wasn’t to be the positive influence that you needed in your young life. I was wicked and took your pure soul”. Livewire runs his hand across Tommy’s grave, the mud and silt and earth engulfing his hand the further he plunges into it. In a strange way this touching of the grave gives Livewire a stronger connection to the best friend that he has tragically lost. “A few weeks went by Tommy with no sign of you. The police in the area were working over-tine in a bid to find you. During this enforced period I still didn’t have the guts, the testicular fortitude to own up and admit my mistakes. Every night that you were missing Tommy I slept guilt-ridden, my blanket covering my head as I lay in bed feeling sick for what I had done, turning my back on my friend in his hour of need. Finally I was put out of my misery, your body was found in the exact same sea by the beach we had visited. The police tried to be as comforting as they could but the pain was evident on their faces. You were dead, you had drowned. I can still remember the uneasy, queasy feeling in the pit of my stomach when my dad told me the news. No, I cried, please don’t tell me it’s true. How could I lie like that, keep up a false pretense. Of course it was true, I knew you were dead weeks before, it was all my fault.”. Livewire pulls his hand from Tommy’s grave and stands up. He buttons up his fur coat and says a few final words before walking away and leaving the graveyard. “Eight years on and now I’m a famous professional wrestler. Amazing huh, considering that I was barely five foot tall when we were kids. Well Tommy let me dedicate everything I do now in a wrestling ring to you, every headlock, kick, slam will be done with you in mind. I miss you so much buddy particularly now at a time when I’m facing my demons, cleaning out my closet so to speak. Well Tommy, before I go I just wanted to say that one five letter word that I was too gutless and afraid to say to you all those eight years ago…..SORRY”. The final shot is of Livewire seating behind the wheel of his jet black alfa romeo spider. He bows his head and starts to cry again, he tries to wipe the tears away as he slips his keys into the ignition and drives. He repeats that five letter word every few seconds accompanied by another flood of tears each time. Strong words about a forgotten and lost friend. The Lively one is re-building his life, praying that his past misdemeanors and mishaps can be forgiven. But will this new found direction lead him to wrestling glory? Only time and his devotion will tell. Take care Livewire-ites.
