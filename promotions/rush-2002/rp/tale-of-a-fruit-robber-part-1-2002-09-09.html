Tale of a Fruit Robber (part 1)

9 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9th September 2002 “But how can this be so?” Asked the podgy sales assistant. The small grocery store, which had been a respected family business embedded within the hearts of many within the community, was entrapped within complete and utter mayhem. At first sight it was to be believe that a robbery had taken place, but the register had not been touched. In fact, one of the only things in the entire store that had hardly even moved was the register. “I don’t know John, who breaks into a grocery store and doesn’t take any money?” Tony replied with a rhetorical question of his own. Carrots lay scattered across the white and black tiled floor, orange stains smeared along under the heel of someone’s boot. Packets of various snacks; thrown around and splattered onto every wall. The two staff members, opening up the store early that morning, walked slowly around the store. The only sound heard by their pricked ears being the clicking of their brown leather shoes against the cold floor below brought the two into a heightened state of anxiety. Never knowing what could happen as they turned each corner into another aisle and searching with their darting eyes for any place someone could be hiding. Kicking boxes and squashed food underfoot, as if clearing a path with a machete deep in a thick jungle, John and Tony found it a hard time to inspect the store completely. “What the hell happened here?” John asked himself, shaking his head in disbelief and licking his dry, pink lips. Tony’s quivering eyes bulged with tears as a realisation that if nothing had been stolen, then this must have been a crime of hate. He thought deep inside is ticking mind, trying to work out who on earth could hate the family and the store enough to do such a horrendous thing. Who could be so cruel as to commit such a misdeed against them? “Do you think… it’s gang related?” Tony asked his older brother, his voice shaking with fear. John turned and looked down at his co-worker and sibling, he was barely into his later teenage years and he knew that this ordeal would probably be a little too much for him. John placed his comforting hand onto Tony’s shoulder, trying to reassure him. “I’m sure that it is just some mindless accident, drink probably involved… you know how it is.” John said soothingly, trying his best to comfort. “Yeah, you’re probably right. Who’d break into a store and not take any money if it was planned.” Tony stated, no confidence in what he was saying. “You know drunk drivers these days, only the Lord knows what those crazy kids get up to.” John said back, looking away from his younger brother to try and hide his face. The brothers continued walking around, still in astonishment at what was happening to them. But the shock sank in deeper, like a parasite eating into flesh, as they reached the fruit section. They had kept it in a row of refrigerators for years and found them very popular as they had them delivered fresh from nearby orchards. But this part of the store was unlike the rest; it hadn’t just been ransacked and mutilated. As they searched through, checking every place they could, they found every single piece of fruit that they had kept in over night was gone and nowhere else around the store. “Where’s all the fruit?” John asked, completely bewildered. No grapes, strawberries or even oranges. Everything had been taken, with nothing left at all. The only thing they found were black marks streaming across the floors, almost like tyre marks but very small ones. These black lines lead out of the store and down the street, but faded away after a while. This would not be the end for the search, the culprit would be found; or so John and Tony believed. Fliers began to appear first. Nobody could get to their car without seeing one of the brightly coloured sheets of paper speaking of the terrible crime. This was a community ordeal, as the family that owned the grocery had been hit hard and was seeking help to get back up on their feet. Telephone calls, group meetings and bake sales were all involved in the fight against anything like this happening again. But, all these efforts amounted to nil. A second grocery store, not all that far from the first, was attacked in a very similar way. Everything tossed around and destroyed, but with every piece of fruit that they had was taken away. The small tyre marks no longer the only clue, as the second store boasted new evidence. A red spray can had been used, near the drained fruit section, to write the words ‘we shall fight for freedom’ sloppily. No matter how much thought was put into this, nobody could figure out what it meant. What relevance had it to the fruit robbery? What relevance had it to anything at all? Nobody knew the answer. Despite the theories, such as social outcasts who had been banished from the grocery system many years ago, no sense could be made of it. * The sticky night air was swarming around, thick and near tangible in the dark hotel room. A feint light blue glow was the only light seen, illuminating from a small television set as a figure lay sprawled across the bed. With remote in hand, the man lounged back against the mound of cushions as the local news came to the story he had been waiting for. ‘And in other news, the search for the grocery store fruit robber has been heating up in recent days. With a total of five stores attacked the investigation has deepened greatly. With no visible fingerprints or any loose fabrics found, it is being run on very thin grounds.’ Informed the young female news reporter. The door was rapped loudly three times before a wailed ‘room service’ was received by the pricked ears of one Joe Bananas. Joe pressed the spongy red button and waited for the screen to implode into a blank black. He then bounced from his bed and wobbled across the light grey carpet and slowly opened the door with a creak. Covering his head and squinting his eyes at the sudden burst of light, Joe found it difficult to focus on the young man before him. “I have the tuna sandwich you ordered, sir.” Piped the young boy in the red uniform. Joe rubbed his eyes with his tightened fists. He clumsily motioned behind himself as he tries to accustom his eyes. “Jus set it on da table mon.” Joe said, his voice cracking like a ripe seedpod. He stepped back a few paces to allow the boy in, beckoning his hand continuously towards the small table beside the bed. Joe stumbled backwards a little further, jolted by the sound of metal meeting wood as the plate sat on the table. “Oh!” Joe exclaimed out loud, “Ya need a tip.” The boy smiled, not arguing at all with the decision. He brushed his shoulders and stepped forward a little as Joe searched around for his wallet. But a look of dismay overtook the smile, as the young lad’s eyes became accustomed to the light level. He could see that there were dozens and dozens of different pieces of fruit all around the room. Baskets, hampers, piles of the things all around this small hotel room. He then wad forced to produce the smile once more as Joe returned with a meagre offering of money. “Here ya go kid.” Said Joe with a smile, “Don’t spend it all at once, ya hear?” “Erm, ok… sir.” Replied the boy meekly. Scuffling away, closing the door behind him, Joe was left in the darkness of the room once more. He eagerly leapt back onto his bed and brought the television back to life. Seeing that the store on the fruit robber had vanished Joe leaned over and sunk his teeth into the sandwich that had been left for him. The fruit all around him watching with invisible eyes. The fight for freedom had begun.
