About a Book (part 1)

7 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/7/02 “The moment business ethics became an oxymoron was when the majority started to realize that some really smart folks made a weekly income equivalent to their retirement savings” Sun Tzu didn’t die in time, nor did he even fade away. It simply underwent metamorphosis. Any person smart and patient enough to fathom and learn whatever the old man wrote. He really did deserve better than a life of teaching freshmen with a majority who couldn’t understand half a line of his brilliance if their lives depended on it. Those who did comprehend and understand paid him credit by moving up far and high But how they ended up, well, that’s another story. If Sun Tzu knew how much individuals and organizations are making out by putting his methods into practice by now… There would be no doubts about who’s stirring in who’s grave. Of course nothing lasts forever, the Art of War was never meant to last through the ages on a real battlefield, especially when the Western world bought in firearms and most certainly not on the fields of Flanders nor the beach of Gallipoli, which are sad narratives of their own. All Quiet on the Western Front would tell you one of the narratives. However, Sun Tzu was to be re-incarnated not just as a textbook of war principals, which is another appalling oxymoron by the way. It became the businessman’s bible. The real dilemma is that there is no textbook for business. Business textbooks are as bad an oxymoron as business ethics. In fact, to be successful in business, rule number one is never, ever follow the textbook. If business school was any useful to a prospective businessperson, it would be to show how NOT to go about doing things in the business world. Predictability and routine are two very dangerous words in the business, leading to many disgruntled individuals in the business world who just couldn’t “get it” why did they fail. If one were to delve deeply into the business world, it would take a lifetime to completely and properly cover it all. Not just does it encompass what many in three-piece suits see at Wall Street on a daily basis nor does it even stop at individually own community-based businesses known colloquially as “mom and pop’ stores”. There are also the unseen businesses What Sun Tzu made for king and country Others applied for self-interest. On the battlefields of a turbulent Middle Kingdom, a Celestial emperor, or at times, emperors warred against factions and rarely was there a clear-cut winner. Sun Tzu’s tactics were basically made to minimize loss and make the best out of victories. Emperors weren’t the brightest knives from the celestial drawer. Some generals who actually paid heed won victory after victory before receiving a knife in the back for their pains. The age of the Republic was soon ushered in with the people sick of constant conflict headed by a charismatic Sun Yat Sen. However, how it all ended up is another story. Actually, the real followers of the great text were not the patriotic generals and definitely not the self-serving warlords who followed afterwards… The real followers started off with a cause to uphold the mandate of heaven dictated to man… A religious following? It did came from that A cult? Used to be… A Sacred Trinity Christians? No. They were the one and only whose legacy still carried until the age of microchips. Societies of Secret Or better known as… The Triads
