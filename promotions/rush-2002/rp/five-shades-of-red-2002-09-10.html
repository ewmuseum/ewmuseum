Five Shades of Red

10 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/10/02 Caleb McGlocklan sat on his brown leather chair in his boxers, eyeing a Rolex wristwatch carefully on his hand. His sock covered feet were propped up on a table in front of him, cans and bottles of alcohol scattered on the floor underneath. Pizza boxes were stacked in a corner (some with half eaten slices still inside) with a television standing atop them. Still eyeing the wristwatch, he counted down the time… ‘3…2…1’ and with a sudden flick of the wrist, pointed the remote to his tube and hit the power button. With a sigh of relief, he slammed on the armrest of the chair rather hardly. With a swoosh of air, the backrest of the chair tilted back and the bottom propped up, providing a nice reclined feeling. It was time for his favorite show to come on – ‘The Jetsons’ – every night at 7:00. The news was winding down “And in other news,” began the reporter on channel fourteen, “peace talks between Israel and Palestine continue to—” It happened. A large explosion blasted on the speakers of the television, the image of the reported bobbling back and forth. Caleb’s eyes narrowed in shock and sat up in his chair, the back popping back into place. The camera stopped juggling and came to a complete stop, the reporters holding onto the edges of their table. “What the hell was that…?” Five Shades of Red. The Red of an Explosion.McGlocklan gripped the handles of his recliner tightly, his knuckles burning white lacking circulation. The camera in the studio panned to a window where thick smoke was rising into view. The female reporter, Susan Richter, climbed from her bench and ran over to the window. Several people were crowded around it, looking down below. Parting the mob, the cameraman reached the window and aimed his recorder down below. The images of deep red flames engulf the screen, chunks of sidewalk, shrapnel, and building scattered across the soot stained street. “My God… what happened?” asked Susan, he breathing hampered by the poison rising into the air. Ted Henson, her partner, shook his head in an unknowing fashion. “The hell if I know… sounded like a bomb, though,” he responded, his voice shaking and cracking like a teen going through puberty. The group, still crowded around the small window, their noses and faces slammed up against the glass, contorting them into pig shapes. Susan left the group and reached into her purse some ten feet off the set and grabbed hold of a cellular phone. Punching in three numbers, she lifted the small plastic piece up to her mouth, her body shaking in fright. “Emergency Response Team. What’s your urgency?” The Red of Flashing Sirens.“Hang a right here, Randy!” shouted Miguel Fernandez, his hands gripping the steering wheel sensationally tight. In the back of the truck, Randy Montalba pulled ferociously on the steering wheel controlling the back tires. With a screech of burning rubber, the truck rumbled down a dark alley, leaky water spraying up on the windshield, making it completely opaque. With a swear, Miguel slammed something down on the dashboard. With a ‘vrrrrmm,’ the blades of the wipers cut through the grime. “Left!” Again, Randy turned the wheel of the truck and hung a sharp turn, the wheels rumbling on the loose pavement below. “What the hell happened, anyway?” asked Ricky White, a rookie to the force. “Some kinda explosion. Those second and third trucks behind us, Randy?!” Randy lifted his left hand with his middle and index fingers stretched – ‘yes.’ Miguel nodded and pulled at the wheel, taking another turn. The trucks arrived in front of the flaming building and came to a screeching stop. Immediately, the back doors of each were kicked open and men in suits of black and yellow poured out into the streets with ropes, ladders, and hoses gathered in their hands. The Red of the Fire Hydrants.White roared while dragging the hose behind him. Five men behind him caught up and grabbed hold of the material, helping him with the load. White dropped the hose closest to the nearest hose, reached into his belt, and pulled out a wrench. Dropping it on the metallic nub of the hydrant, he unscrewed the side and hooked on the hose. Peeling the tool off the block, he dropped it atop the hydrant and swung. A gush of water exploded through the hose, shooting it up like a cobra. The firemen maintained control and dashed over to the flaming building. White grabbed hold of the handle and pulled it back, releasing the liquid over the flames, blasting them back. Miguel was busy reeling out another hose attached to the truck. A squad joined him and helped him steady the hose towards the flames. Pulling back the handle, soapy foam blasted from nozzle and doused the flames to the best of their ability. Randy ran towards the revolving glass doors of the building and with a swing of his axe, shattered them to pieces. With a group of ten, he leapt through the shards and rushed up the flights of stairs to the people above. The Red of Their Blood.The ambulances behind the trucks pulled up at last, the backs opening up. Groups of paramedics dashed out the back with kits ready under their arms and dashed toward the scene. Jane Watson, first day on the job, ducked under a hose and slid to her knees, grabbing hold of a civilian holding his chest. He gasped, sweat rolling off his forehead, and looked up at her with tears in his eyes. “Don’t worry,” she said softly, “you’re in good hands.” Pulling out a dressing, she applied pressure to a gaping hole in the man’s shoulder. Louis Finnegan sat next to an elderly lady, wrapping her bleeding forehead in bandages. Standing, he led her away from the site and to a bench across the street. She tripped over her own feet, woozy and tired, needing rest. Helping her down, he wiped sweat off his brow and returned to the site, helping Missy Vandyke with a dying patient. The Red of His Damned Lips.Caleb McGlocklan sat in his chair, a fresh beer on one hand, his socked feet propped up on a table, his eyes carefully watching the carnage that lay in front of him. The sweat on the fireman’s faces as they battled the flames, the blood dripping to the floor, the flames crawling up the side of the building. He narrowed his eyes, released his eyebrows, and for the first time… in a long time… smiled. Smiled at their pain. Smiled at their agony. Smiled at their sorrow. His lips curled into a smile, enjoying the hell before him. The son of a bitch smiled. He lifted the remote out of a slot on his chair, pointed it at the television, and with a click, turned it off – utterly satisfied. With one last swig of alcohol, he dropped the bottle onto the carpet beneath him, and slid back in his recliner… drifting off to an undisturbed sleep. End.
