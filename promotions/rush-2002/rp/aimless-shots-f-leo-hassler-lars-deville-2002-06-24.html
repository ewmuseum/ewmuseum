Aimless Shots (f/Leo Hassler & Lars DeVille)

24 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 24, 2002 Tell me, have you ever sat there, watching a match burn? Watching a lighter spout it's flame, or watching a bonfire go, listening to each little crackle that the burning of the wood makes. With the match, watching it slowly burn down the little cardboard stick, giving you ample time and notice to blow it out. With the lighter, watching the base of the flame, the blue part, the hottest part as it leads everything else up in such a beautiful shape. With the bonfire, holding your hands out over it, keeping them warm and then falling into the undeniable urge to, yes you guessed it, prod the ashes with a skinny little stick. You may be thinking what does this have to do with wrestling? What the hell does this have to do with anything, and if you are asking that...then you are an imbecile. There are two reasons for this little appetizer, and I will tell you why… These are all things, all things that people, whether they know it or not, all stare at. These things all have something in common, with a wrestler who seems to be immortalized under the name of Scott Cage. People are amazed, dazzled even by the ability, the technique, the skill, the charisma and the staggering ability to make others look so... so...amateurish. There stare themselves blind, watching as he does things that people wouldn't dream of trying, or even dream of dreaming. They stare quite frankly, because they cannot look away. Is it chemical? Possibly. Is it Mechanical? Most likely not, but in some cases you never know. Is it Admiration? Most likely. Livewire, are you staring now? Most definitely. The second reason, is, while staring over the burning enigma, the spots just above, become cloudy, wavy even as the heat from the fire burns away into the air. You can't help but look through that, wondering why it does that, wondering why it leaves the fire so peacefully and quietly into the cold air, when it was perfectly safe, and warm in the raging fire. Wondering why it would leave everything it has been with for it's entirity, wondering how it lives surrounding the main focus, the thing that everyone came to see. You watch it slowly burn away and then evaporate into nothing, into the exact same thing that everyone else sees... everyone else sees nothing. In case you haven't noticed, this is a perfect metaphor in itself. The Raging Fire, unstoppable in all aspects of itself... get that Livewire? IN ALL FUCKING ASPECTS OF ITSELF! That means that I await your idiotic response of, 'Well I will throw a bucket of water on you and put you out.' What it means is that the Fire, the long burning one that no one person can keep there eyes off of…is me. And the lowly little exhaust coming from it, the little haze parts surrounding the fire, living off what it makes, what it creates, encompassing the main focus of everyone's eyes... is you, Livewire. Trying to prove yourself and get everyone's attention like I have. Trying not to evaporate into thin air…yes into thin air like so many others do. You will evaporate Livewire, into thin air… First of all, before I go on, I am sick and tired of you Ricky Starr. I have already proven that you are only there, because I paved the way for it, I am an original in a sea of unoriginality. I am the man who broke the world open with my God-like enigma, and your blatant thievery of my style makes me sick. Why does it make me sick? Because not only have I proven it, not only have all the fans in the world realized the truth, not only have you realized the truth, but you have attempted to stay silent with it. You have attempted to pretend that I never said that, proved that, and you have attempted to pretend that you don't know the truth... I am not sure who you think I am, whether you believe I am a charity auction, that you can take what you want from me for a measly price, however if that is your thought, you couldn't be more incorrect. Because I am Scott FUCKING Cage, the NETWORK CHAMPION. Yes, and what that entitles me, other then not being able to protect my own style, my own technique, to being the best, being the smartest, being unequaled by everyone else. You hear me? Even though you mimic my every move, even though you attempt to be excatly like me, you are not like me. You are nowhere near me, because I have something, that even little 3 year olds have, that even a simple chimpanzee has... a brain. Find one, use it, and watch your stupidity, cause now you are pissing me off. Ricky Starr, you are finished. Your chances of winning this war are worse then they have ever been, and what Sirius said was all true. He has never called you a nothing, however I am not he. Ricky Starr, you are nothing and will never be anything. You are not smart enough to be something, or anything for that matter... you are a nobody facing a somebody... you don't have a chance in Hell. (As we fade in, we see Scott Cage's newly hired bodyguards Leo Hassler and Lars DeVille sitting at a bar, with a bucket of peanuts in front of them, peanut shells carpet the bar top and the floor. Leo reaching out, searching for the perfect peanut, the mystical shell that contains three nuts, as opposed to two. His search however, goes unfulfilled as he settles on a normal one with two nuts. The stools they sit on are tall boys, without backs, and Lars leans forward onto the edge of the bar, elbows down, fingers cracking. The bar setting is all wood, the floor is hard oak being glossed a dark color to cover the usual one. The bar is the same wood, and behind the bar, stands one bartender, who seems to be cleaning out glasses right now. The place isn't packed, yet it isn't near empty either. The waiter arrives and takes their order, and briskly walks back to the kitchen to report before he forgets the order.) Leo Hassler: After watching the boss's last confrontation with Livewire, I have realized something else about the man. Lars DeVille: What's that? Leo Hassler: That everything that he says, every single word, is ALL one giant sentence. That is how unintelligent he is. He cannot even figure out where to stop talking and end the sentence, so he just doesn't. Even my little nephew has enough smarts to know that you need to STOP talking at one point. Lars DeVille: Leo, are we going to be talking about Livewire the entire night? Leo Hassler: I wish I could say no, But unfortuantely something of this kid needs to be exploited. So the answer most definately, yet still unfortunately, will be Yes. Lars DeVille: Hell, at least you aren't lying about it. Leo Hassler: I mean, when you don't have honesty, what do you have? Lars DeVille: Money? Leo Hassler: Only in our case, Lars. (Leo smiles again and winks once at a passing waitress, who returns the wink and then giggles softly, trying to cover it with her right hand. Leo leans back into his chair, placing both elbows on the arm rest, and he begins to think. Lars looks on, wondering exactly what he is thinking about, knowing the basic subject being Ricky Starr. The wheels are churning...) Lars DeVille: So…what are you thinking about? Leo Hassler: Thursday. That punk kid, Livewire. The thing that I don't understand with this man, that I just can't seem to grasp, is how he seems to only be fighting to get respect. I mean seriously, respect? Man, in this business, in this world, in the RING, when Livewire steps into that ring…he's fighting only for respect? Why? Lars DeVille: Well, the only reason I can think of, the only thing that I can come up with is that he is quite sure already that he can't beat the boss, and if he says that he will win the match, he would be lying. With those words, while muttering what he thought was something that would make him look more clever, more mature, more confident, he actually made yourself look more childish and afraid. It shown through his 'tough guy' act, and he has now let everyone know, that without his efforts to cheat…he is nothing, and he is AWARE of it. Leo Hassler: While I have said that he will be no challenge for the boss, and while he is going to be a walk in the park as he is out of his element, I don't think Scott never uttered those words. See, Livewire was the one that told the world he was nothing, he was the one that said the words that equaled out to that, and he is the one that doesn't believe in himself. I have one last question for a second, one little question that if he can answer might possibly save his career...Why? If you don't believe in yourself, why be in this business, in this life, in this world. If you don't believe you can win, why do you fight? (Leo Hassler shakes his head slowly as if he doesn't understand, and then looks across at Lars, who this entire time was smiling at his partner…) Lars DeVille: Let's face it Leo, we are the future of Scott and Sirius Cage's survival, and while young people expect us to not be experienced, not be knowledgeable very much in the ring and not know how to work strategies, how to regroup from unexpected things like you said. But while people expect those things, and while they think those things, they unfortuantely end up finding out that all of there hunches and assumed guesses, were wrong. However luckily Livewire is not thinking the same as others, he has done his little research, watched a few of Scott's matches and he realizes exactly what we are. Leo Hassler: Now, how do you suppose this Livewire character, plans on defeating someone like Scott Cage who is the best Pure Wrestler in the world? A man who knows everything there is to know about everything, who knows every single move and how to apply it best, who knows every single way to hurt a man legally, and win while doing it. That is Scott Cage, and Livewire better keep that in mind. (Right after Leo says that, the waiter returns with the food. For Lars a large salad, and a large cheese pizza, for Leo a large plate of chicken and spaghetti. They begin to eat slowly, yet that doesn't simmer down the conversation, not at all.) Leo Hassler: How is it? Lars DeVille: So far so good. Leo Hassler: Same here. Lars DeVille: Still thinking of Livewire? Leo Hassler: Nah. Thinking about Ricky Starr. Lars DeVille: Two of the same kind of wrestlers. Jobbers. Leo Hassler: But a few weeks away. I know Scott will crush that Ricky Starr, I can feel it in my bones. He's a better wrestler than Starr, he is by far smarter then Starr, and he is so scared of him that he keeps recalling back to a victory over him that he doesn't have. Lars DeVille: I have only one more thing to say to the poor sap Livewire, and I hope that he can take himself away from the gay-porn for long enough to listen. In a fight this Thursday, it is evident from his actions that he is fighting mainly to get respect, so that we will respect him as a person, as a wrestler, and as an opponent. What the hell is he talking about? As a person, I respect him all ready, because he entered in a world that maybe one in a million actually can get into. As a wrestler... he may not be lucky enough to get that on Thursday, because I do not think Scott plans on taking lightly to him at all. Whether it be stretched, flown, powered or whatever, he will be coming from all directions towards him. Leo Hassler: But respect as an opponent I can also promise him, he will not receive from me, and I will tell you why. As an opponent of my boss, the interviews count, every word he says about him and toward us count, every time his face is shown on television, it counts. Now listening to the words that he has said, listening to his words where he doesn't believe he can beat Scott, he is losing the respect that I already don't have for him. He was sitting in a hole of the world's respect, and instead of climbing out and getting it, he is digging yourself deeper into that hole. Let's face it, if Livewire wants to gain respect, he is going to have to think more, and talk less. (Leo and Lars go back to eating their meals, and do not notice the stare they are getting from the other customers. The camera begins to fade backwards, making them both look smaller, and more and more people are being shown in the restaurant just staring at the babbling duo. The interview is done, and not only has Leo and Lars's point been made, but it has been sealed and delivered.)
