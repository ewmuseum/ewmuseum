The Last Slatkovic (part 2) {PPV}

6 July 2002

Date: 7/6/02 “No!” Click Boom Not really satisfaction guaranteed but it came in close It went back right up to the time he felt something small and metallic at the palm of his hand The rest just fell into place. In three sets of split seconds, it happened just like that. He was right when he said the kid was a natural He was fast He didn’t even blink And when he finally did He was around 500 meters away from the damn joint In the eyes of some he was nothing more than just ordinary looking riff-raff, to be avoided at all costs. In the eyes of some he was their icon, a voice of a generation, a suppressed clique. In the eyes of some, he was just a pair of hands, skilled hands, looking for the highest bidder to get through the day with a fat wallet. Scum? A Hero? A Freelancer? The day Steffan Slatkovic ran out from that orphanage with the number 3 in his casualty list and a shiny automatic in hand… He became the dark, sordid manifestation of American life that would linger around him forever Steffan Slatkovic Was, Is and will always be ‘till the grave A Gangster What exactly makes a gangster tick? What makes a person WANT to become a gangster? What motivates a gangster in life? A wide variety of reasons actually One of them is the money. From the first taste of ill-gotten cash, it’s done hook, line and sinker. Be it from the first pocket picked, the first gram sold or even right down to a snatch and grab job, and it’s the weight and feel of the first easy dollar that counts. The rest is up to history to decide. Another one is the fast lane. Believe it or not, there are actually those who have made it to the top of the ladder with the fat wallet and all but still, STILL have an urge to satisfy. Some call it living on the edge Steffan called it Life on the Bullet It’s a sordid sort of thrill actually, one that quite a number find worth living every second for. It’s a reason why there are a good deal of gangsters out there who die young and bloody but still wear a smile on their face to the grave. Psychologists all over the world try to fathom it In essence, it is actually quite simple It’s pretty much knowing that you’re going to die The real question is: How soon? The rest is left to the adrenaline and neural impulses to satisfy Although it may be tempting to think so, Steffan was not really that type of guy He belonged to another group. Steffan did not really intend to double his bank account in a matter of hours Nor was he even a killer He was, in essence A Survivor From day one, Steffan wanted one thing and one thing only… To just get through the day And it never mattered to him how. Through that he became one of the fastest guns in the underworld, if not THE fastest. The secret to that was through time, in a cold, harsh world like the streets of New York One becomes apathetic Everybody thinks Thinking is not necessarily a bad thing, in fact, it’s good at most times, allowing a person to seek more light on a subject, to see situations more clearly. Steffan did think…He really did but the thing was, no matter how long or how hard he thought, there was only one possible route every time he sat down to think At the end of the day Steffan taught it best not too think too hard, not too think too far ahead Not to care at all Unlike the Celts, the cared more about living than winning. Unlike Statuz Quo, whatever pride he had disappeared when he was treated as a rug in this foreign land. He neither craved excessive amounts of money Nor did he care for power The lusts of the flesh were the last of his concerns He cared for one thing, the one thing that set him apart from many in his league Living When Steffan was a soldier for Alexis DeVore’s gang, DeVore had surprisingly quite a lot to say about his enigmatic henchman “Steffan in essence, was the perfect soldier. He didn’t really want the cash and just took his keep from me, which pretty much means he was not as much of a greedy fuck than the rest of the cutthroat bunch. I could trust him not too die on me because he cared too much about the state of his living carcass rather than blowing the brains out of the guy on the other side trying to cap him. Steffan didn’t give a flying fuck about anything else around him except for one thing: His own throat. All that shit about loyalty and blood brotherhood just went out the window for Steffan. If it meant he was going to die by my hand, he’d take me out of the picture quicker than you can say “Shit!” It didn’t mean jack to him if the punk on the other side of barrel had two kids and a wife. All that mattered to him was to shoot first and breath later and anybody who shoots faster than he breathes has the right to be called the fastest gun in my book” Such was the opinion on Steffan Slatkovic, the survivor branded as a gangster. There was, of course, a footnote on that “If I ever wanted to gut a tough as nails place like Harlem for instance, I’d just drop Steffan off in the middle of the street with a quarter and his automatic and tell him to make his way home by dawn. After that, it’s just sitting back and watching him just trying to get by” Ricky Starr walks alongside his brother in the middle of a recreational park. The pathway is lined with well-manicured trees. He looks at Steffan, who seems to be sipping on a cup of hot coffee. “Your time on the street. How were they?” Steffan throws the cup into a bin as he gulps the last mouthful down “Well, you know, it’s just me trying to get by. Just little ol’ me”
