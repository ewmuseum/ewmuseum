Date: August 28th, 2002 A.D. (Handlers Note: I apologize for the shortness of this roleplay, got a little caught up in some school work.) MCI Center- August 1912:08 AM “You’re the champion” I said to myself, my eyes glistening in the shadowy mirror that stood in front of me. I smirked, a smirk of a superior man standing triumphant over fallen enemies. I felt like I was the leader of a great army standing on top of a hill with my national flag waving behind me, a defeated army of enemies at my feet. This is what I felt like. “You’re not the champion, you’re THE champion.” I smirked again, before brushing some of my loose, now black hair out of my face. “Fuck!” I yelled out when I went to move, after the match with Deamont I felt like hell – especially my chest. That damned steel staircase, I wasn’t expecting that. I peered down at my ribs and my chest; the blue bruises from the staircase crashing on me were still glowing radiantly on my tanned skin. I surveyed the damages of the match, bruises on my chest and my legs were more apparent then anything else, blood stained my hairline from when I bladed myself – nothing new. Shifting the belt off my shoulder, I threw it toward my opened up Gym Bag and missed poorly. For some odd reason, it’s my right arm that’s giving me more trouble than any other ache in my body. “Goddamnit” I said, squinting my eyes in annoyance at the bruises on my aching body. “It’s just getting more and more painful, after 6 years of this, it’s just getting harder to do.” 6 years? What am I talking about when I mention 6 years? I’ve been wrestling for the past six years of my life in Japan, Germany, and Britain, and here in the North American Indy Circuit. Countless injuries, bet you didn’t know I have a metal plate in my left arm, did you? Nah, of course you didn’t. By now I would of thought I’d be use to the aching muscles, sore backs and piercing headaches but I guess I’m not quite use to it since the tame days of CHZ. “Ahhh” I said as I rubbed my shoulder and winced at the pain from the time when Deamont slammed me into the guardrail a few times. I started a slow circular motion with my right hand, trying to massage myself. Wincing my eyes I continued to massage my shoulder, trying not to scream when my crusted fingers ran over a red laceration just below my hair. “Fucking gimmick matches, why can’t we just go back to conventional wrestling!?” Minutes went by as my absent-minded massaging continued, I thought I was about to drift off to sleep, that was until I felt an incredibly cold hand trickle onto my shoulder. Spinning around, I clenched my other fist, a stern ‘ready to fight’ look gleaming through my emerald green eyes. “What the hell!?” I said, turning around fully and eyeing this person up. I didn’t recognize her at first, since this was the locker room area for a him. After a few quick seconds I recognized her immediately, she was my soon to be sister in law, my....my...my beloved Leah’s sister, Jessica. “What in the HELL are you doing here!?” I demanded from her. She backs off a little bit, scared that I would hit her, my fist is still clenched, though. “Jess, how exactly did you get back here!?” “Chris” she said, solemnly. I immediately noticed the envelope in her hands, my eyes dart to where it quietly sits, curious as to why she’s got this with her – is it for me? “I...I have s...something for y..you....I..I’ve been m...meaning to give this to you e...ever since L....Leah died.” “What are you talking about?” I said, emotions building up inside of me as I remember the death of my fiancée, trying to hold back some tears. “Jess....?” “Just take it.” she said, sternly to me. Jessica shoved the envelope into my chest. She sniffled a little, the memories of her sister, my girlfriend – fiancée, travelled back to her once more. She spun on her heel’s and headed for the door, as I looked down at the golden envelope. By the time I had ripped it open and pulled the white paper out, my ‘could have been’ sister in law had long gone – I assume already leaving Washington and back to Toronto right now. I pulled the letter out, and read the first few words silently to myself, but started to read it out loud – I didn’t care if anyone else heard it. “Dear Chris” I began the oh so traditional way of starting off a letter in today’s modern world. “I would like to invite you come to the family home so you could just a pay a little respects to Leah, since you decided not to attend her funeral. Bye.” I folded the letter back up and stuffed it back into the envelope. I quickly changed into my normal street clothes, discarding my battered and blood stained white wrestling pants. Heading for the doors, I grabbed my gym bag, stuffing my newly won RUSH World Championship inside of it. I tired to open the door with my weaker arm, but decided against when I felt a jolt of pain race up my arm. Using my strong arm I headed for the exit of the MCI Center, I could feel a small tear on my cheek.... the memory of Leah snaking it’s way back into my sore head....
