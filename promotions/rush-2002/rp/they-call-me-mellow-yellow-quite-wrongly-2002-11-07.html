They Call Me Mellow Yellow, Quite Wrongly

7 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7th November 2002 The rain was getting heavy, not that he cared. A long black overcoat was draped across the back of the little wooden stool, his leg crossed over the other as he read a crumpled newspaper. His eyes would scroll across the page, and his hand would reach for the steaming hot cup of coffee on the round table, but other than that he was still as stone. The coffee shop was small and hidden enough that the average ‘starbucks-goer’ would never even think about such a small and dingy dive. This was the charm, peace and quiet, that Joe found the most alluring. “Everything ok?” Asked a pale skinned man in an apron, looking at his watch to indicate he was taking a lot of time. “Leave me be!” Joe snapped sharply, never taking his eyes from the newspaper. The man backed away, getting to his other duties. Joe took the last sip of his cold coffee, the bitter flavour swirling around his taste buds as he swallowed it slowly. He then looked down to his own watch in shock; it was much later than he thought. He quickly folded up the paper and dropped a handful of money next to his empty cup, leaving a few shiny pennies for a tip. After what he considered generosity, Joe began wrapping himself back into his coat. The outside didn’t look terribly inviting as he looked through the steamed up window. He shook his head, grunting in dejection. “Rahtid America.” Joe grumbled to himself. He lowered his head and strode out through the door, feeling the rain instantly splatter sharply against his skin. He shivered in shock of the cold as the door closed behind him. The pattering drops of precipitation were more like thousands of knives falling from the heavens. He pulled his coat around himself more tightly, his knuckles numb as they bent around the material. He began jogging as quickly as he could, he couldn’t stand this a moment longer. The streets seemed much more grey than they would normally and more intertwined and complicated. He wound himself through the maze of streets and alleys. Joe looked up at the black clouds above, his face totally taken by sheer sourness. He closed his eyes and imaged the warm beaches of Jamaica, the wafting breeze caressing his skin. A smile emerged on his face; he couldn’t help but add in the extra luxuries of weird and wonderful cocktails with scantily clad beauty queens to serve them. He suddenly realised he wasn’t moving, his legs and feet weren’t working. He opened his eyes and shook himself back into reality. He saw four dark blobs somewhere in the distance, he couldn’t quite make them out bu they were definitely there. He began walking closer, intrigued for some reason. They were people, he could tell that now, but with something strange about their legs. Joe knew curiosity might be leading him into something he should stay out of. But he couldn’t help himself, he needed to know why their legs were jolting up and down so. He could make them out more clearly now as he crossed the large, empty market square. The mist between them was lessened as the distance decreased, and he could see them now. They all had long hair and beards, straggly as a bramble bush to match their outfits. There was something moving on their legs as they jolted, but he still couldn’t figure it out yet. “Hey man, you come to join us for a game of hacky sack?” Asked a smooth voice from one of the undistracted hippies. “Hack sack?” Joe asked, bewilderment overtaking him. “Yeah man, give the sack a kick.” Said a second voice. The hippies relayed the colourful sack a few times before passing it to Joe, who raised his leg up high with power to send the sack flying into the distance. “Hey, don’t sweat it man, accident happen.” Said one of the hippies. “Dat wa’n’t an accident, pickney!” Joe exclaimed, a look of satisfaction embedded below the scowl. “Don’t be so hard on yourself man, just chill out and enjoy mother natures beautiful rain.” Said another one of the hippies. “Enjoy da rain? Ya’ll jus foo foo pickneys wit long hair.” Joe said, his forehead wrinkled underneath the drenching rain. “Hey, without the rain we couldn’t grow fruit to eat.” Said the first hippy with a smile, pulling a fresh apple from his pocket. Joe pushed his fists together, cracking his knuckles as a small grin appeared on his lips.
