Common Ground

7 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 7th, 2002 “I hope this is a good idea…” I thought to myself, as I stood in front of the doors of a small, rather gothic little two-story nightclub in metropolitan Los Angeles. I certainly wasn’t dressed for the occasion, so to speak; but then again I didn’t need to be. I looked down at my attire – a plain white T-shirt, and a pair of raggedy old blue jeans – and contrasted it with the other patrons that stood behind me in line – leather black miniskirts, white face paint, you name it. I didn’t care, because my business here wasn’t to pick up Goth chicks or even to have a good time and dance about like a fool. My mission was to find some common ground. Third in line. People were looking strangely at me and my rather disheveled appearance as I stood in front of twenty-six others (and behind two). I just shook my head and let my blonde locks fall into my face and cover my eyes, as I was so accustomed to doing as of late, when I didn’t want someone to see how I felt. I felt that my hair concealed me from the rest of the world, from those who wanted to see my true feelings and from those I didn’t want to share them with. It was my shell, so to speak. Second in line. I took a small step forward as a soon-to-be partygoer stepped through the double wooden doors and entered the club. My hair swished a bit, but soon fell right back into place in front of my cerulean eyes. I didn’t want to come out of my shell. Not yet, at least. I gulped, the lump in my throat rising quickly before following down just as fast. I didn’t want to have to be in a social situation. Too bad – the sole person in front of me pushed on the doors, systematically filing in the doorway like all the others did before him, and all the others behind me would. First in line. I turned slightly, facing the bouncer. I didn’t feel like opening my mouth; I just stared down up at the six-foot-six man dressed in a black muscle shirt. He took one look at me, and then his clipboard, before strangely opening his mouth to speak first. “Well, yeah, bro…you’re on the list,” he said to me. “Go on in. You’ll find him upstairs.” I simply walked through the double doors, paying no heed to him. “Fucking socially inept wrestler,” I heard him mutter under his breath as I entered the nightclub. “Fuck you,” I thought to myself as I wandered through the mess of strobe lights and tables filled with just as many glasses of alcohol as laughing, jovial people. “If your memory had been lost just a month ago, you wouldn’t want to talk to a nightclub bouncer on minimum wage either.” “Prick,” I added for good measure, even though nobody could hear me, seeing as I wasn’t, well, TALKING. I had my words to save for more important matters. I spotted a staircase just ten feet to the left of me, which was ten times as illuminated as the rest of the bar slash nightclub slash brothel, whatever you’d like to call it. I shrugged, and simply made my way up the wooden steps, stopping at a single chiseled mahogany door, complemented by a contrastingly cheap metal doorknob. I knocked. Twice. “Hold on,” shouted a familiar male voice inside, followed by a few whispers between that voice two females, apparently. A few moments later, a chain was pulled off the door and it swung open. Cassius Deamont. He looked at me for a moment, my head lowered slightly, and began to close the door after saying nary a word. The door met only my left foot. “We need to talk,” I said quietly, finally making eye contact with the silver-haired ex-champion. “Oh, do we?” asked Deamont coldly. He was dressed in a black silk shirt and a pair of black jeans. I’m sure Erica and Kathryn were lurking somewhere in the sea of sheets and furniture behind him, hiding out of sight so as not to be seen by me, the unexpected visitor if you will. “Yeah,” I responded. “Y’know, I spent a lot of time hating you. But I look back on it, and honestly, I don’t see why.” “Heh…” Shit. I guess I wasn’t off to a very good start. Then again, maybe that’s just how he was. “Look, man,” I stated, sensing the edginess in his voice. “Plain and simply put, I came here to find a common ground between us. This is a business full of assholes, backstabbers, and liars, and you can use all the friends you can get.' “Kathryn and Erica are good enough for me,” he replied. No nonsense with this guy. “Yeah, but…” I started. Then it hit me. “But, what good is a life full of chicks if you’ve got no good friends, anyway? We both had that sad excuse for a friend Matthews, and then…” I dug my fingernails into my palms as I bent the truth slightly. “…He turned on both of us!” I finished. With that, a slight smirk came across his face. He opened the door fully and backed away a bit. “Come in.” I obliged. I walked into the messy apartment, stepping over a stack of, oddly enough – old PCW magazines. I knelt down and examined the top one – and nearly jumped back at the face staring at me – Chris Sloboda. “Been doing your reading,” I inquired, “have you?” “Oh yeah…” he replied. “I’ve been meaning to ask somebody about him. Maybe you can tell me something about that has-been.” “I worked for the guy for months, and watched him for years,” said I. “What do you need to know?” “Anything!” he replied, excitedly – a trait I’d never seen in him before. “I mean, I ‘worked for’ the guy for a month and a half too, but hey; there’s only so much you can learn from a guy that you’re using like the tool he is.” We both shared a laugh – an awkward laugh, but a laugh nonetheless. “Well, the guy’s big. Just use your speed to take him down.” Hey, it’d probably work. I rubbed the back of my head as I thought about what a Boda Bomb felt like after charging straight into his chest. “Don’t get me started on his intelligence,” responded Cassius. “It honestly doesn’t matter if I can’t match him strength-wise, because he’s as dumb as a sack of bricks. Why can’t people just figure out when I’m using them like a lackey? Who else is stupid enough to run not one, but TWO promotions into the ground?” Another shared laugh. A little less awkward, this time. “No shit,” I replied. “The big dumb Czech couldn’t even hold on to his top talent!” “Except you, of course,” Cassius added in, most kindly. “And to think that Boda was at the top of PCW for so long,” added the first RUSH Champion quickly. “But then, look at RUSH, and Matthews; talentless nobodies somehow getting to the top isn't exactly uncommon.” I thought about a response for a moment, until the obvious hit me. “What made you choose Matthews as an ally, anyway?” Deamont’s face turned sour. “It was all Walden’s idea.” “So, it was kind of a lackey assigning you a lackey?” Deamont smirked. “...Walden seemed like a semi-intelligent guy at the time. I suppose I chose my allies badly there.” “Maybe you did,” I replied. Of course he did. He chose his allies badly, I chose my allies badly. We both had been screwed over by Boda in some way, shape or form – he had only been a bit more intelligent than me in his dealing with him. We had both been friends with Chris Matthews at one point, and both of those friendships, however loose you’d wish to use that term, fell apart. 'Allies, heh...Matthews...Walden...Brent...Boda...D-Kay...Cannon...Flare...I'd say I win the prize for 'worst choice of allies'. And to think, I'm supposed to be intelligent...' 'I'd say you're intelligent enough, Cassius. You may have poorly chosen them all as allies...but you had all seven of them in the palm of your hand for months. Besides, look at where they are now. Flare and Cannon are off in Shitman's harem. I ended D-Kay. Brent's gone. Walden is stinking it up over in FSW. You're handling Boda just fine, and Matthews sucks just as much as ever.' I grinned slightly, looking towards the ceiling. 'Meanwhile, we're on top of our game.' Deamont seemed to agree whole-heartedly, and indicated so with a completely comfortable laugh. 'I guess all has turned out for the best. Speaking of top of the game, how'd it feel to beat Matthews for that number one contendership?' I smiled. 'I was more than happy. Not only did I beat that whiny excuse for an ex-Champion fair and square, but now that no-talent hack is going to fight for a belt that's more at his...how shall I say it - 'skill' level.' We shared another laugh, and I relaxed in the black leather recliner that I was seated in. Come to think of it, we never had anything against each other personally. Maybe I have found some common ground.
