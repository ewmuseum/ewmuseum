Megabytes & Mayhem (AD #1)

14 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 14th July 2002 (The scene opens in a somewhat run down room. The dirty walls and floor have roaming cockroaches scuttling along them on their hurried, thin legs. The very weak lightbulb, which hangs from the ceiling near the middle of the room, illuminates the only furniture, a small chair standing still before a table. On the table is a white computer, whirring with noise as the screen struggles to stay alive. The keyboard, with keys sticking and faded characters, produces a repeating clicking noise as eight fingers and two thumbs work as quickly as they can. The typing hands, although determined, make repeated mistakes and typos. The owner of these hands is none other than Joe Bananas, who is typing an e-mail to send to his family back in Jamaica. His struggling mind forces Joe to speak out loud as he taps the keys in front of him.) Joe: How is uncle Hugh? Still got dat bad cough or he feelin’ better? Ya should tell him ta take walks outside more, get da fresh air in his lungs. (Joe was not new to the World Wide Web; he had been sending e-mails and surfing for a few months now and was beginning to get the hang of it all. The move to electronic mail had come about from Joe’s poor hand writing, his mother could never read them and would not be able to pass the messages on as clearly as wanted.) Joe: Da flight Ah jus took yesterday was god-awful. Dey had facilities ta make coffee, but dere wasn’t nah hot water. Dey expects meh ta drink cold coffee, dat don’t even work right. Oh an’ get dis, dere was nah Jamaican white rum on the trolley. Wha dey say bout airplane food i’ right. (As he takes a moment to rest his tired fingers, Joe gulps down a long sip of his tall glass of pineapple juice. He uses his left forearm to wipe the juice from his lips and beard as he places the glass back down on the table.) Joe: Well, Ah better bring dis ta an end. Ah got trainin’ tomorrow, before dis big match against dis guy named Ali Khadafi. Ah swear, RUSH is pushin’ us hard… Ah guess it’ll pay off in da end. Well, Ah hope ta sees ya soon. Sen’ mah love ta gran’pops and make sure he tek his med’cine. Love, Joseph. (Joe stops typing as the e-mail is brought to an end. He leans back in the hard, wooden chair and grins with satisfaction. He lifts his glass once more, to finish off his cold drink.) Joe: Ah gettin’ good at dis internet t’ing. (As he pictures himself surfing the web and doing all those things in internet scare and hacking movies, Joe drifts off into a daydream. His arms relax and his fingers lose their grip on his glass of pineapple juice. The thick yellow liquid flies in all directions as the glass shatters against the table. Joe snaps fully awake and guards his face from the thousands of air born shards. The sticky fruit juice slides into every crevice it can find, in between the spaces between the keys and every other part of the unfortunate computer. Joe’s jaw drops down, allowing his pink tongue to loll out of his mouth, and his eyes widen as the screen, which once showed his long and not yet sent e-mail, is overtaken by pure black and darkness.) Joe: Wha da…? (Joe claws at the blackened computer monitor, trying to capture his lost e-mail. It was useless though, this realisation did not come to the slow minded Joe for a few moments, and when it did he was still in wonderment as to why it did. He slapped the side of the monitor with the palm of his right hand sharply; hitting things usually make them work.) Joe: Chupid computer! Why da hell di’ it do dat? (Joe pushes himself up out of his wooden chair and pulls the monitor towards him a little, looking behind it at the dusty unknown. He ventures through the seemingly endless amount of tangled wires and plugs, looking for some kind of answer and a quick fix to whatever the problem was.) Joe: Why da hell are dere so many dyamn wires back here? (Joe’s hands swim through the colour coded cables, confusing himself more and more with time. He figures he won’t fix it without trying, so he pulls a bright pink wire from its socket and tosses the lead to one side. He pulls himself back into his seat and looks again at the blank screen.) Joe: How da hell did dis break so bad? (Joe looks down at the keyboard, the sticky juice seeping in and staining everything possible. Small sparks can be seen emerging from the wires protruding from the back.) Joe: Ah gotta fix dis sheg up t’ing nah! (Joe sighs and shakes his head before standing up out of the uncomfortable chair and heading towards one of the doors in the small room. The stiff, brass handle is difficult to turn. The sticky door bursts open violently as it finally opens and Joe stumbles through into the kitchen, which is equally dirty and revolting in appearance to the previous ‘computer room.’ The mud stained, black and white tiles are cold underneath Joe’s bare feet and a tingling sensation goes up both of his legs. He takes a few steps over to the filthy and obviously unhygienic refrigerator and pulls the door open with his left hand, releasing a swift breeze of cool air. Joe rummages through the vast quantities of food, searching for a few things in particular.) *A Few Moments Later* (Joe gets to work on the broken computer, his equipment set down on the table nearby. He now has a large white apron, with a cartoon red crab dancing on the front, draped on him to protect his scruffy clothes from getting dirty.) Joe: Time ta fix dis piece o’ crap. Nah, where mah screwdriver? (Joe searches through the mass of tools until he finds a yellow, plastic handled screwdriver underneath everything else. He then uses this tool to get to work on the computer’s tower, prising open half the side and revealing the inner part of the PC. He drops the tool to the table and grabs at his next item without looking. He has a handful of grapes clutched between his grasping fingers.) Joe: Nah guys, ya know dis computer is brucked up. Ya small enough ta fit in dere and see wa gwaan. Have a look aroun’ an’ see why i’ aint workin’ no more. (Joe nods to the dark purple coloured fruit and moves them closer, still grasped against his palm, to the exposed inner compartment. He takes the first one in between finger and thumb of his free hand and pushes it gently through a hole in the side, getting the whole fruit inside and stuck in the computer. He then continues to do this with the rest of the adventurous fruit until they are all in.) Joe: Good luck guys! (Joe then turns back to his equipment and picks up a very bright red cardboard box with an image of a woman on the front, overly smiling in front of a bright yellow sun whilst carrying a large basket of grapes. The words ‘raisins’ is printed above her dark hair in bold white letters. Joe’s clumsy fingers peel at the sealed flaps on top, inelegantly scratching and tugging at the ripping cardboard. The explosion of dark and wrinkled dried fruit fly in all directions as the box finally opens, with great force and power. Joe is then forced to scramble around on the floor, scooping up as many of the of the dried grapes as he can.) Joe: Dyamn raisins, getting’ all over da place! (Joe then sits back in his chair and begins to push the raisins into the computer in the same fashion as the grapes. Due to smaller size, he is able to get a lot more inside.) Joe: Nah, make sure ya fix dis t’ing aight? (A fizzling noise grows in volume slowly, deep from inside the computer. As this sound becomes louder and louder bright white sparks can be seen. The sparks become larger, and loud popping noises can be heard accompanying them now. Joe decides that he will have none of this and takes a pineapple in his hands and begins to hit the top of the tower and monitor as hard as he can, both fruit and computer breaking apart gradually.) *The Next Day* (Joe drops the large cardboard onto the counter before him with a thunderous clatter. Inside the box are the remains of what was once his computer. He doesn’t pay attention to the starring shoppers, who do nothing more than bustling through the store aimlessly, and looks coldly into the eyes of the man in front of him. This person, who from his nametag is apparently named Harry, wipes the sweat from his brow. It was the middle of a hot and busy day, and he was not sure he was ready for what was about to come.) Joe: Ya dyamn computer dat ya sol’ me don’t work nah more! Harry: Well sir, what seems to be the problem? Joe: Da screen went black when Ah was tryna sen’ a e-mail, wouldn’t work at all after dat. Harry: Well, I’ll see what I can do. (Harry begins to rummage through Joe’s box, sifting through the endless amount of disconnected wires and chunks of broken plastic. He mumbles to himself as he finds more and more vital components dis-attached from the main body. Joe can think of anything to do more than smiling stupidly.) Harry: What the hell did you do to this thing? Joe: Wha? Harry: This computer is mangled up, broken into so many pieces. You must have broken this yourself! Joe (in Gary Coleman impression): Wha chu talkin’ bout Willis? Ah mean, Harry! Harry: This thing is in pieces, there are connection cords all over the place and even your mouse has a huge split in the back. Joe: So? Harry: So! So?! So, you must’ve mangled this up yourself. You cannot tell me that this just… happened, by itself. Joe: Umm it jus’ happened, all by itself. Ah wan’ even in da room. Harry: You weren’t even in the room? Joe: Nah! Ah was fixin’ mahself suttin ta eat. Ah t’ink Ah had chicken, rice an’ peas. Had some pepper sauce too, some hardough bread on da side. Harry: I don’t care what you had to eat! Unless you put it in the damn computer! Joe: Well, to be honest… Harry: Oh for the love of… (Joe can do nothing but weakly smile, Harry’s face fuming red. He has no choice but to comply and wear a smile as he does so. One of the many tortures that come with the world of retail. He smiles, very meekly, back at Joe.) Harry: This would take a very long time to fix sir; we can get you a replacement if everything checks out. Joe: Ah wan dis one fixin’ nah a new one! Harry: Well, we’d have to check this one out first sir. We’d have to find out what is actually wrong with it before we can fix it. Joe: Do ya know a lot about computers? Harry: Of course sir, your computer would be in perfect hands. Joe: Well, if ya know a lot bout computers… why don’ ya look at it now an’ figure out wha’s wrong wid it? Harry: Sir? Joe: Jus have a look, see if ya can figure it out. Harry: I… I can’t do that sir. Joe: Why not? Harry: Well, I guess I could have a quick look. (Harry begins to rummage through the box further, pulling pieces out and placing them onto the counter beside him. He nods his head as he begins to find cohesion, putting certain parts together. He then begins to find sticky parts, covered in some kind of purple pulp. He then finds what seem to be raisins, scattered around.) Harry: Sir? Joe: Wha? Harry: Are these raisins here? (Harry holds a few of the dried grapes in his hand and holds them closer to the watchful Jamaican.) Joe: Yeah, dey are. Harry: Do you mind if I ask why there are raisins in your computer? And what this purple stuff is? Joe: Well, Ah sen’ a load of grapes an’ raisins inside ta try an’ fix dis t’ing. Dey obviously got lazy, dumb grapes. Harry: You sent grapes and raisins into the computer to fix your computer? Joe: Yeah, load a help dey was though. Should a sen’ some strawberries in prolly. Harry: Sir, are you ok? Joe: Of course Ah ok! Hush ya mout’ an’ fix dis computer. Harry: Sir, could you calm down please. Joe: Hush up, or I’ll get mah pineapple! Ah won’ hesitate to bruck ya up foo! Harry: Yes sir, of course. Let me just get something first. (Harry then reaches underneath the counter and pushes upwards, the tip of his index pressing against the button below.) Joe: Hurry up den! Ah don’ wanna be waitin’ all day fa dis! (Harry becomes worried as Joe’s face shows his growing anger, gritting his teeth and clenching his fists. As two overweight men in blue inform stomp into the store his worry turns into satisfaction and relief. Harry reaches up and points to Joe.) Joe: Wha da bumbaclot ya doin? Harry: Nothing sir, now these people will look after you. They will take you where you can talk to all the raisins and strawberries you want. (Joe pivots on his feet and spots the security guards. He turns back round to Harry.) Joe: Ya lil rahtid white bwoy! (Joe piles the components of his computer back into the cardboard box and cradles it in his arms. He frowns and kisses his teeth at Harry before turning back round to face the music. He ducks down and begins to wade through the masses of feet, knocking over compact discs and other computer accessories as he does so.) Security Guard 1: Come here! Joe: No! (Joe turns as many sharp corners as he can until he sees the opening of the store. He begins to stand back up straight, the security guards not knowing where he is and scratching their heads and Harry looking angrier than ever before. Unfortunately, the pimply teenager spots the crouching Jamaican and points him out.) Harry: There he is! Get him! (The guards turn round and see Joe with their own eyes. They lunge forward as Joe makes his exit, back into the main body of the bustling mall. Joe, who is still walking with bent knees, moves deep into the swamp-like crowd in an attempt to lose the two fat guards. He waits a few moments, ignoring the passers-by who look down at him with curiosity and confusion. As soon as he believes the coast is clear, he stands back up and wipes the worry from his forehead. The guards point and yell as they see him, they had obviously not gone away and were still on their hunt. Joe clutches the box to his chest more tightly and begins to scurry through the crowd as quickly as he can, looking back every few seconds at the chasing security. He clumsily tries to navigate himself and swerve through the many people. The guards are gaining speed, pushing everyone in their way down to the ground.) Guards: Stop that man! (Joe speeds to his left, knowing he cannot carry on where he is. He doesn’t look at where he is headed, but dives into the nearest shop. He runs along the wooden flooring past rails of clothes, headed towards the back. His hefty legs, dragging him along, knock over rails and racks of clothes as he continues to move with very little grace. He slows down a little and turns his head to one of the female workers.) Joe: Is dere a back door to dis place? Assistant: Yes, there is, but why… Joe: T’anks! (Joe hurries away from the blonde woman before the guards can catch up with him. He skids on his black sandals as he reaches the door ahead of him, nearly crashing into it. His sweaty hands fumble at the handle before it awkwardly opens. He then stumbles through into the back area of the store, closing the door tightly behind him. He looks around frantically, finding nothing but boxes all around him. He leans down and slowly moves as many of the large, cardboard cubes in front of the door so as to block the guards and stop them following him. He rests for a few moments before beginning his search for the way out. He walks around in the dim light, finding nothing but storage areas and more boxes. That is until he sees a stairway, and like a ray of light beaming down, Joe scrambles up the flight to freedom. Freedom being, in this case, the opening door before him and the outside world. The blinding white light, pouring from the skies directly into his eyes, showed Joe the way the way to the nearby bus stop.) Joe: Yeah! Time ta ge’ back ta da yard nah! (The scene fades out as Joe waits for his bus, clutching his shattered box of computer parts to his chest.)
