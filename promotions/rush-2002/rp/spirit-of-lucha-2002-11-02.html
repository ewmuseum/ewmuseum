'Spirit of Lucha'

2 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Nov 2nd The lights of the warehouse burned bright, even though they weren't needed with the natural glow that fell from the skylights. Even in the month of November the Arizona sun was fierce and bright, casting an aura on the murals that covered the walls of the 'Spirit of Lucha' Wrestling School. Rendered in bold gigantic strokes, bright and vibrant reliefs of El Santo, Rayo de Jalisco, Blue Demon, Dr. Wagner, Huricon Ramirez and others stood large over the 20 or so wrestlers currently going through their paces. Of the students, more than 15 wore masks. It was tradition and the rule at 'Spirit'. If you aspire to be a luchadore, the mask will be on at all times when participating in anything wrestling related. The moment you left the lockeroom, if the mask wasn't on, it was to be confiscated. You were required to earn it back, and the road to redemption was long and arduous. In fact, in the history of 'Spirit of Lucha', only one man had ever regained his mask. Only one man had ever had heart enough to finish the grueling progressions that Jose Gonzales; the owner, teacher, mentor, and master of the Spirit of Lucha school demanded before honor could be returned. That man was Kevin Sandusky... Years ago in a fit of teen angst, Kevin had ripped his mask off in the heat of argument with his stepfather. It broke Jose's heart to take his stepson's mask, but it was tradition. The bonds of the art could not be broken, even for family. 12 months. 12 months of backbreaking workouts, torturous exercise routines, lung busting and leg burning runs to the 10,000 ft summit of Mt. Lemmon. Marathon hikes in the summer desert heat, and nearly suicidal in ring training. 12 months. In the beginning, a contrary and spiteful Kevin Sandusky set out just to prove his stepfather wrong. To rub it in his face that he could take all the punishment and keep going. To prove that he would not be broken. But somewhere in the the midst of it all, a change happened. Spite became ambition, angst become pride, and the goal was no longer to prove his father wrong. It was to prove to himself that he could not, would not be bowed or dishonored. In that year, an angry youth was cast into the fiery forge of tradition. His desire and focus honed to a gleaming razor's edge. His body broken down and rebuilt as the masters of legend looked on from the walls in silent approval. And from this crucible emerged not a youth, but a man who's soul was stoked with the fires of tradition. And one year to the very day, his family and friends gathered in the halls where the Spirit of Lucha resides, and watched as a young man regained his honor and family name. And on the walls there is a place for him, next to the image of the man he calls 'Dad'. One day in the future, alongside the legends, the face of a young man destined to take his tradition, his art, and his family name further that it has ever gone before will appear. Honor. Tradition. Destiny. Ebola III...
