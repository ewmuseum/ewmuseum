OJ Is Innocent

31 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 31st July 2002 (The scene opens to the sound of far off Spanish guitars. The gentle notes drifting along the gentle breeze, accompanied by the sweet singing the guitar’s player. The light grey, paved path made a clomping noise underfoot as Joe walked over and sat down at a table, waiting to be served. Around him was the gentle hum of conversation, accepted by the open landscape around them and the clear blue sky above. The red umbrella, high atop a white pole at the centre of his table, provided adequate shade from the warm sun above. Even the birds seemed happy, the squawking that Joe had been used to replaced by peaceful song. Such was he wonder of the small Spanish café, Luz del sol.Dressed in a plain white t-shirt and short blue pants, Joe was free to be warmed by the rays of the sun above as the missing clouds were left only to imagination. A well-tanned waiter, dressed in the usual attire and adorning a smart head of jet-black hair, made his way casually over to Joe’s table. The almost Zapata style of moustache he grew below his nose had a sheen to it, obviously taken care of.) Pedro: Ola señor, my name is Pedro and I will be your waiter today. May I take your order? (Joe’s eyes skim quickly across the list of drinks, his fingers clench as tightly as possible around the glossy edges.) Joe: Ah gon have a slice of watermelon, a grapefruit an’ a glass of orange juice. Pedro: Ok señor, it will just be a few moments. (The waiter steps away, trying not to turn his back on the customer until far away. He screeches a long string of spanish in a strong and defined accent. He strides through a set of double doors into the main body of the café and leaves Joe sitting as still as he could be. He twiddles his thumbs; he whistles a tune he even tries to count the sombreros that are scattered around atop other dining customers, laughing and smiling as the lunch found its way down to their stomachs.) Voice: Hey! (Joe spins his neck around, the voice seeming to be aimed in his direction. He is met by the sight of a small boy, a messy mop of black hair on his tanned head. His sparkling eyes stare up at the wrestler before him in sheer awe.) Kid: Are… are you Joe Bananas? (Joe fills up with pride, rising up into his chest and forcing a smile on his face. He has a pretty good idea what was wanted here, so he reached into his pocket and revealed a black ink pen, clasping it hidden in his hand.) Joe: Yeah, I’m Joe Bananas. Wha ya wan? (Excitement swelling underneath the skin of his face, his lips stretch outwards even further. He is so happy to be signing autographs, finally being recognised by the youth. Joe knew that the youth were the future; they were the ones who had the hands, which our fates were to be in. Children always knew what was right, their innocence and purity being their driving force and fuel for the natural truth buried inside them.) Kid: Hey! Wake up man, this isn’t time for sleep! Joe: Wha? Kid: All I wanted to know if whether or not you can get me El Spiffy!’s autograph, he is my favourite luchadore. Joe: Wha bout Mil Mascaras? Kid: My father liked him, when he was a kid. So, can you get me his autograph or not? Joe: Wouldn’t ya much prefer mine? (The child stares blankly into Joe’s eyes, watching the disappointment rising like bread in an oven. He chuckles to himself and taps Joe on the arm.) Kid: Be serious, you joined his team last week so you could get it easy. Joe: Well… actually Ah do have his autograph right here wit meh. (Joe turns around, making sure his back is totally facing the child. He snatches a napkin from the table and scribbles roughly onto it in black ink. The words “Del Speeffie?” jotted down, Joe finds it hard to contain his giggles as he hands the ‘autograph’ over to the small child. The boy doesn’t seem in the least bit unsatisfied and carefully holds the napkin as if it were a slab of gold with the directions towards the holy grail inscribed on to it.) Kid: Gracias! (Joe watches as the child’s legs scurry away and sit him back down with his amigos, a frown coming across his face as he sees them all wearing ‘El Spiffy!’ t-shirts and crowding around the kid’s new autograph. He tries to wave at the few turned head of the group, but the children try as hard as they can to dismiss him from their vision.Joe turns back around, slumping his face into his crossed arms on the table and beginning a long and melodramatic sulk.) Latin Thug #1: Hey, let’s go in here! (The voice, which belonged to a short young man, with a bandana wrapped around his head and a black t-shirt with the words ‘muchos locos’ printed on, guided five of his friends through to the small café. One carrying a small radio on his shoulder, the Latin hip-hop beat bursting out and tearing into the ears of every customer at the Luz del sol, and the others bearing nothing more than menacing glances and gold chains. They guide themselves down to a small table and gather round, overcrowding and practically sitting on, the table.) Latin Thug #1: We’ll sit here vatos! (Pedro, Joe’s waiter, strolls along quickly to deliver the fruit and juice. He doesn’t look directly at Joe, always with his eyes set on the group, which had assembled rather clumsily.) Joe: Wa gwaan? Pedro: Que? Joe: Wha’s happenin’ wit dese guys over dere? Pedro: They come here every week to cause trouble, like a pest really. (Pedro heads back inside as Joe gets stuck into his food, putting the locos vatos out of his mind for the moment. His teeth sinking in like a vampire to a neck, the sloppy pulp flying all over his chin and the juice dripping, he continues to eat like a pig with a large mouth and an even bigger stomach.) Latin Thug #2: Hey! Latin Thug #1: What the? (As the five Latinos turn to see Joe, slurping away greedily at his meal, they scowl. The one who lead the group to the Luz del sol wipes a large chunk of grapefruit from the shoulder of his shirt. They shift away from their seats and make their crowd form around the Jamaican wrestler, clicking their knuckles and crossing their arms to create an aura of threat.) Latin Thug #1: Excuse me! (Joe looks up from his fruit at the five men around him, the sun blocked out and the warmth dying down.) Joe: Wha… sorry, Que? Latin Thug #3: Is he mocking us? Latin Thug #4: I think he’s mocking us. Latin Thug #1: Nobody mocks us! Joe: Que? Latin Thug #5: Shut up! Joe: Oh, I get it… well ya guys could have jus’ asked. (Joe lifts the knife from his plate and slices into the grapefruit, making sure each of the five pieces is the same size as the rest.) Latin Thug #2: We don’t want any of your damn fruit! Joe: Wha’s dat supposed ta mean? Latin Thug #1: It means that we don’t want any of your damn fruit, got it? Latin Thug #4: Actually, I’d quite like some fruit sir. Latin Thug #3: We haven’t eaten all day, and we don’t really have enough money to actually pay this place for food. Latin Thug #1: Ok, ok. Although we DO want some of your fruit, we don’t you to give us and. (The Latin man reaches down and grabs the sliced fruit in his hand, handing them out to the rest.) Latin Thug #1: We’re stealin’ them! How d’ya like them apples? Joe: How could ya steal mah fruit, dis is rahtid beyon’ belief nah! (Joe snatches the glass on his table and dashes it upwards, the yellow liquid flying into the faces of three of the Lain Thugs, most spilling on the leader.) Latin Thug #1: Orange juice!?! It’s time for the beatdown de la muetre now! (Joe falls backwards off his chair and begins to crawl away, the advantage that the stinging citric juice blinds the ones chasing him. He scrambles under the tables and chairs of many customers, brushing past legs and knocking over old ladies. Before he can reach freedom, the collar of his t-shirt is pulled and forcefully dragged. In a flash, a yellow stained Latino is pushing Joe against a small wooden fence at his shoulders and starring directly at him with an angry scowl.) Latin Thug #1: Nobody throws orange juice at us! Nobody!! Joe: It aint da orange juice’s fault, ya know! Latin Thug #1: Orange juice is the worst, it is guilty along with you. Joe: Ya can beat me, ya can torture me… but never diss da juice o’ da orange!! Latin Thug #1: Que? Joe: Look! Crazed mariachi maniacs!!! (Joe lifts the last slice of grapefruit from his pocket and pushes it into the face of his capture and falls backwards over the fence behind him, he scrambles back to his feet and begins to run from the café at top speed as the Latinos care for the eyes of their leaders stinging eyes and realising that the crazed mariachis are actually not there.)
