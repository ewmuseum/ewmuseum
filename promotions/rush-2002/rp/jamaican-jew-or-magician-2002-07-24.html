Jamaican, Jew or Magician?

24 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 24th July 2002 (The scene opens in a darkened room, long purple and red cloths have been hung over the windows with pins and duct tape. Sat on the thick maroon carpet are dozens of small children, around the ages of 4 to 7. They are all sat in silence and awe as they await the show start. At the back of the room are a group of parents, huddled together in a large clump. They whisper and murmur to each other, also anticipating the show. There is a general good feeling filled in the air, incense sticks have been set around the room and a few candles lit. At the head of the room is a fold-up table with a purple cloth spread across. On it are various odd looking items, such as crystal balls and various packs of brightly coloured cards. Above the table is a sign printed on cardboard, hanging from the ceiling from the support of two pieces of string. On the sign is a bright blue star of David and in black block letters the words ‘Levinski the amazing.’ The young Jewish children stare in wonder as the curtain begins to wobble and the figure of a man can be seen. The figure begins to come into view; he is tall with a bright purple hat on top of his dreadlocks. He is dark skinned and a broad grin spread across his face.) Joe: Hi kids! Kids in unison: Hi! (The parents in the back begin to mumble to each other as they see Joe. They are a little confused as they see his dark skin; they figure he must have converted. They encourage each other to clap for the young magician and get the children to join in. Joe’s face goes a little red and his smile grows larger as he soaks up the applause. Joe walks behind the table and begins to set up the equipment, waiting for the ovation to die down.) Joe: Thank you, thank you. Fa mah firs’ trick Ah gon’ take dis perfectly normal top hat. (Joe bends down and lifts a dark top hat from below the table. He holds it up high and spins it round slowly, before brining it back down to stomach level. He then reaches his hand deep inside and searches around.) Joe: Arrgh! (The audience look in shock as Joe pulls his bleeding hand back out from the hat, bite marks covering his fingers and wrists.) Joe: Da t’ing bit meh! (Joe’s face is overtaken by a vicious scowl as he tips the hat upside down, the white rabbit from inside falling out and hitting the table hard. A few of the children begin to cry, and the parents begin to talk with each other. Joe doesn’t seem to notice and pushes the animal from behind, forcing him to scurry off the table and into the crowd of kids.) Joe: Ok, well… dis pack of cards is jus’ like any other. (Joe raises a normal looking pack of playing cards, spreading them out with his fingers for all to see. Nobody seemed to be interested as they watched the rabbit running around frantically. Joe carried on regardless and began to wave his hands above the cards.) Joe: Ah will use dis iron spike ta skewer right through dis pack, and den Ah will fix dem all and dey will as good as new. (Joe waves a silk, pink cloth around before encasing the deck inside it and clutching it tightly at either side. He lifts the sharp iron rod with his free hand and aims it directly at the cards. He looks at the audience one more time, building up as much tension and mystery as he can. His eyes set back again; he thrusts the spike straight through the centre of the deck. The point sticks out at the other side, a few sprinkles of white paper stuck to the end. After a few moments Joe pulls it back, the hole now visible and plain to see. Joe looks through the hole, able to see the audience through the spike’s work.) Joe: As ya can see, da pack has been shot straight through. (Joe releases his fingers gently from the deck and clings to the edges of the pink cloth, he then wafts his hand up in the air and reaches up to try and grab the cards. The cards, all with holes in the centre, fly across the room into the rows of children. Joe manages to grasp the cloth as it floats down to the ground. He holds it up above his head, before noticing the audience members covered in cards.) Joe: Well, sorry about dat. Fa mah nex’ trick Ah gon use dis big melon. (Joe holds up a melon, just as he had promised, and began to slowly turn it so that all sides could be soon. After he had proved that it was a real fruit, and nothing had been to it, he placed it down on the table below. He clenched a purple cloth in his hand and waved it around above his head wildly. He then spread this across the fruit and hovered his hands above for a few moments. None of the children were paying attention, all screeching at the biting rabbit at their feet and the fallen cards all around them; some wiping them from their heads onto the floor.) Joe: Now, Ah gotta sprinkle some magic dust above and say a few incantations. (Joe opens his hand and allows the coloured dust to fall all over the table, getting as much of it on the melon as possible. He danced around a little in a feeble attempt to create an air of mysticism, before kneeling down to get the next vital part of the trick. As he stood back up he revealed a large sledgehammer, heavy and difficult to handle in his hands. He smiled triumphantly as he prepared to lift it.) Joe: Da magic dust’ll protect it from dis mighty hammer. (The hammer, now raised high above Joe’s head, looked old and rusty. This did not affect its heavy weight and destructive power. Joe wobbled around a little, trying to get a bit more dancing in, before brining the sledgehammer down into the melon with great force. Of course the dust did not protect anything, the hammer squashed the melon completely and sent large chunks of it flying in all directions. The children’s faces were covered in sticky, orange pulp. The parents in the back, who had evaded the flying fruit, were now swearing at Joe in Hebrew. Joe is kneeling on the ground, the force pulling him down as the table had shattered and split in the middle. Splinters, broken pieces of the table and splattered fruit covered the room along with the deathly silence of pure shock and a building anger. Joe’s trick has not gone down as well as he had hoped.) Jewish Mother 1: Who the hell are you? (Joe clambers back up and stares at the on-looking audience, all with an angry scowl or teary eyes. He smiles meekly, unsure what to do next.) Jewish Mother 2: Well? You aren’t a real magician are you? Joe: Why ya say dat, jus’ cause ONE trick went wrong… well two. Wha’ kinda position ya in to question me so? Jewish Father 1: What have you done with the real magician? Joe: Ah am da real magician, I’m Levinski da amazin’ an’ ya better recognise! Jewish Mother 2: Are you even Jewish? Joe: Excuse me? So it comes down ta racism nah does it? Jewish Mother 2: This is a Jewish community centre. (The curtain, through which Joe had entered, shakes a little before falling to the ground after a ripping noise. Now revealed is a man, wearing nothing but a pair of boxer shorts and a white vest, who has a thick layer of tape across his mouth. He looks angry with Joe and stamps over, tearing the tape from his mouth and throwing it to the ground.) Levinski: You! Joe: Hey mon! Levinski: You hit me with that frying pan and stole my clothes and magic kit, what are you a psycho? Joe: Ah was tryna save ya when dem rahtid Jewish thugs brought it to ya an’ took ya clothes. Dey were brutish Ah tell ya. Levinski: Why are you wearing them then? (Joe pushes his dreadlocks back over his head and straightens out his beard as he thinks of an explanation.) Joe: Well… they forced… me to wear ya clothes. Dey threatened ta kick mah rass if Ah didn’t put dis magic show on fa da kids. (Joe grins, satisfied with his quickly created explanation. He crosses his arms on his chest; defiant and standing like stone. Levinski The Amazing crosses his arms also and turns to the angry parents and children, a wicked grin on his face.) Levinski (shouting): Get him! (Joe’s eyes widen and he gasps in shock as the entire audience before him rise to their feet. They clench their fists, grit their teeth in evil grins, scowl their faces up and roar as they charge head-on at Joe the fake magician. Joe screeches loudly and darts backwards, pushing the real magician out of the way, and back through his entranceway and now exit. He sprinted away from the on-coming crowd with ease, all being children and careful parents after all. He runs through the well-lit corridors until he finds the fire escape through which he entered the community centre. He pushes down the handle and tumbles out into the open air. He lay, exhausted, on the ground for a few moments before he heard the door behind him slam shut. Joe had only wanted to understand the world of magic a little better, teaming with Andi The Magnificent in a tag match at the next adrenaline. Joe stood up and dusted himself off, disgruntled and a little annoyed.) Joe: Dat’s da last time Ah try ta get into da mind of a tag partner.
