Tale of a Fruit Robber (part 2)

17 September 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 17th September 2002 The sun brightening the soft, springy grass to a radiant shade of green and the sky a perfect, cloudless blue, the day was happy. Everyone who walked the streets did so with a smile on their face and a skip in their step. Young children with water fights and old ladies nattering with one another, this was a very social explosion of vibrancy and enjoyment. Even the most depressive, angst ridden teenagers were out sulking in the sun. Lying on the grass, surrounded by flowers and the delicate falling petals was Joe Bananas. His hands held behind his baldhead as if a pillow substitution and his eyes staring up at the still sky above and drifting his mind into directions of fantasy and fancy. As images of castles in Spain floated like juice in his mind an interruption abruptly snapped him back into reality. A piece of paper, floating in the barely substantial breeze, fell atop his face and his dreams came to an end. With his hand he blindingly prised the intrusion from his sleeping eyes and was ready to toss it back into the wind that carried it here. But he felt a need, for some reason, to look at it. Maybe some psychological explanation can be given, or even some tarot card medium with supernatural powers could have given a point of view, but whatever had compelled him to look was certainly something with a right frame of mind. ‘Any information that can be given to the police on the illusive ‘fruit robber’ would be very much appreciated and rewards may be considered if the case develops well.’ Joe’s upside down frown made a u-turn as the displeasure towards this yellow flier became apparent. Bending his knees and pulling his legs into action, he got back up and stared around, his neck turning in the most awkward angles that were physically possible. The sun seemed to have gone behind a cloud for a moment, a much more dim and eerie grey light now dominant. Joe looked down once more at the flier in his hand, closing his fist and crumpling the paper into a rough ball and tossing it to the ground by his feet. This was a call to arms, the trumpet wailing at the top of the bloodied hill. * The streets at night, dark and menacing, were no place for an elderly woman. But late on that eventful post twilight day, Betty had suddenly realised that she was in desperate need of milk. She knew that cookies simply couldn’t be made properly without it, and the small grandchildren who were arranged to come to visit the following day would certainly but disappointed and disheartened and may question the true value of so called humanity. As she walked through these seemingly empty midnight streets, in full knowledge that gang members and thugs roamed and circled round her like wolves, she simply tried to keep her thoughts focused fully on the goal. The image of possible distraught children crying their eyes out up at their failure of a grandparent, probably internally scarred for the rest of their lives. This helped her to trundle along, working her stiff legs into overdrive as she hobbled closer and closer to the store for which she was headed. “Hey old lady, got the time?” Asked an almost ethereal voice from the black distance. Betty looked down to her wrinkled wrist, her small beady eyes squinting at the small hands that ticked in circles. “Eh… eh… 12… eh… 0… 5.” Betty wheezed, still unsure whether or not the time she had given was actually correct. “After midnight? Should an old lady like yourself be out at such a time?” Asked the voice, getting stronger gradually as the echoing footsteps drew closer. “Well… eh… I need to get… ummm… milk, I need… milk.” Stuttered Betty; looking around for any signs of the other end of this conversation. “There might be trouble around these parts, some rough boys might want to take advantage of such a vulnerable person as yourself and steal your purse.” Explained the voice, progressively becoming rougher and more grounded. From out of the deep indigo shadows he appeared, as did the rest of his gang members. They all looked as if they would tear each other’s eyes out and spit them at small children if it would get them money. Each with spiky stubble on their chins and messy hair, it didn’t look good Betty. “If you give us your purse nice and slow, we promise we want hurt you.” Snorted the know visible owner of the voice. Betty knew this was a lie; there was no way in heck that they would take the money and prance away daintily. As the quick shine of a knife being drawn shone past her thick spectacles she began to scream. The screeched her lungs out, wailing for all around to hear. The men circled in closer, trying to shut the old bag up with their hands and feet. Her cardigan being clawed at by gnarled fingers she let out one last almighty howl. One man heard this sound, diving quickly from the shadows as he dropped his array of objects in order to save this old lady. He instantly saw what was happening and attacked the gang members, punching, hitting, kicking and clawing at them all in a blinding rage of fury. He didn’t know how anybody could even think about beating up an old lady for money. Dropping them with as much speed as possible, with nose bleeds and bruises everywhere, this saviour of the day stood proud after the hard fought battle. The old lady clinging to his arm, her entire body shaking in terror. “Thank you young man, how on earth can I repay you?” Betty asked, her eyes glassed over and her glasses steamed up. “Ya gratitude is enough fa…” Joe began as he turned to face the victim. Joe gasped in terror as he saw a small, vulnerably tangerine trapped in the clutches of the monstrosity named Betty before him. She began to sink her claw deep inside the skin, peeling away with viciousness as the bare flesh beneath was revealed. She smiled as she prepared to tear one of the segments hideously away from the rest of the orange with complete contempt and wicked joy. Joe Bananas, in a less than clear-headed action, lunged head first at this butcher and drove her down into the ground. Snatching the injured tangerine from her grasp and sighing in relief as he began to walk away from the scene, nursing the fruit back to health as best he could. He turned his neck round one last time, looking at the fallen gang. He simply shrugged his shoulders and left Betty the butcher to be robbed violently. Joe still had something he had been planning ever since earlier that day, when he found a flier caught in the wind. He had many objects ready for this plan of his, including a long roll of paper and small tins of paint. With a grin on his face all the way to the grocery store that had been the first to start the campaign against the notorious fruit robber. He was ready to take it to the next step. And this new level would heat things up greatly. * The sun rose with ominous power. Setting of a firework display of oranges, reds and everything between. The cloudless sky seemed happy to provide yet another joyful day of warmth and sunlight. The grocery store was the last to be illuminated, hidden in dark shadows behind its fellow buildings. But when the glorious light finally hit, the terrible truth was revealed. It was a dark day for the owners as the stared gormless at the anarchy symbols painted onto their store. The fliers floating all around, preaching the truth about the way fruit are treated. And in big, bold red letters, the paint still dripping to the pavement, were the words ‘freedom for fruit.’ The fruit robber had struck again, but his motives for doing what he did now very clear.
