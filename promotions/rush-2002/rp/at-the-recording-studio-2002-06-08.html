At the recording studio

8 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-08-02 (Voice of Livewire) There is a tinge of disappointment from myself and among my Livewire-ites looking back on last weeks tag-team match against Playaz Inc on Adrenaline. Myself and El Spiffy! lost the match, handing the two RUSH hood rats yet another win other the stable that has been a thorn in their side from day one. I emphasize the fact that it’s a tinge of disappointment, because the following two weeks offer great opportunity for the stable formerly known as Shack-Pack to make amends for recent past failings. First of all on Adrenaline 13 live from the Fleet centre in the Boston. El Spiffy, my friend and everybody’s favourite luchadore gets the opportunity to go into Crossroads, the second big pay-per-view RUSH production as the RUSH world champion. He also has a chance to win the Syndicate title from the thuggish Statuz Quo. Now if that wasn’t great enough, then there is the awesome tag-team match on adrenaline 13 which will see the team of myself and Joe Bananas or Biohazard and Synhazard earn the right as victors to go into a triple threat match for the Network title on the Adrenaline straight after Crossroads, now, how cool is that? Throw into all of that the six-man Network title match which includes me and my stable buddy Joe. And the Crossroads brawl for all, with the participants being all the members of the active RUSH roster vying for the opportunity to become the world title number one contender and Syndicate title number one contender respectively and you have yourselves two amazing weeks for myself and my Livewire-ites. Now you understand why I only feel a tinge of disappointment about last weeks defeat .Wow, so much to think about and so little time to think about it. The time is five thirty in the afternoon and it is a hot day in Beverly Hills, California. The camera pans past a few of the cities glamorous clothes stores and conglomerates of restaurants. As the camera continues to pan we come across a tall building that is obscured by the limousines and press journalists gathered outside it. The camera continues to pan towards this building and across the front door reads “Sony records recording studio”. It is now that we can see two figures standing in the entrance of the studio. The height of the two men and the physique of one in particular gives away to the naked eye exactly who the duo are. The camera stops moving at the feet of one of the tall men and slowly rises up past his torso and massive chest up to his face to reveal the cheeky smile of none other than RUSH superstar and honorary American Livewire. By his side is the not quite as famous but equally recognizable Ted Wanstead, Livewire’s scheming agent. Ted is talking on his cell phone as Livewire waits patiently for him to finish, crossing his arms and staring into space. Yes honey, I’m fine, how are you? asks Ted as he whispers into the cell phone, paranoid about prying ears, in particular Livewire’s. The conversation finishes with the usual “I love you” and “see you soon”. Before Ted hangs up with a disgruntled look on his face. “What’s up?” asks Livewire his broad London accent sticking out like a sore thumb in the razzmatazz of the recording studio where many people are happily chatting away drinking their cappuccino’s. Ted just shakes his head in response to Livewire’s question as he tucks his cell phone into his back pocket. “The wife, Wire, she fusses way too much. Ok the sex is great, especially when she does that thing when she raises her legs and groans, but she still fusses way too much”. Ted cuts Livewire a strange look as Livewire covers his mouth and pretends to vomit in a mocking fashion. “Ttoo much information there Ted, that was too much information. The next time I ask you what’s up, just say nothing” splutters Livewire in between mock vomiting noises. Ted just shrugs his arms and looks at the recording room that is closest to them with a number six written above its door. There is a large transparent window to the recording room, which allows the general public to see which musician, or musicians are singing in the room at the time. Livewire takes a pink slip from out of his pocket, not a Boda pink slip thank god, and reads the printed text that is on it. “Hey Ted....starts Livewire…I was given this slip by that spotty kid when we had to give our names at the entrance. It says here that number six is the recording room that we are going to use this afternoon.” Ted ignores Livewire and starts pointing excitedly at the recording room like a child in a toy shop and mumbles some words frantically trying to show Livewire the window of the recording room in the process. “Look..look..mumbles Ted..its….its..Tori Amos!!! Oh my god…Tori Amos!!!” Livewire smirk and looks at the window to see a red headed women singing on stool with a headset on and it indeed looks like the nineteen eighties pop diva Tori Amos. Livewire turns his head to look back at Ted and notices that his agent is now jumping up and down on the spot like happy dog that is about to be fed it’s dinner. “Did you see here? Did you see her?” shouts Ted now tugging Livewire’s leather jacket as he speaks, Ted indeed seems to have a soft spot for Tori Amos. “Yes I…did..replies Livewire..so what?” Ted lets go of Livewire’s jacket and looks at him with great surprise. “What, what…..stutters Ted....don’t you think she’s hot?”. Livewire looks back at the room’s window where Tori Amos is singing and turns back to Ted who seems to have finally calmed down. “Urm…no…Livewire chortles…she’s not attractive. Only a middle-aged sap, who still holds onto his childhood fantasies would find Tori Amos attractive. Actually, I can see exactly why you like her” laughs Livewire. Ted rolls his eyes and just stands staring at the room window in a trance like state. “I hope she comes this way when she leaves” says Ted, his mouth covered in drool at the thought of Tori Amos actually being in the same building as him. “Yeah so do I..answers Livewire as he looks at his watch…she’s taking longer than she’s supposed to. She should have finished about fifteen damn minutes ago”. Just as Livewire says this Tori Amos takes off her headset and smiles at the two technicians in the room before leaving the recording room and walking towards Livewire and Ted who are still standing by the entrance area. Ted nudges Livewire in the arm and whispers, his voice getting very breathless. “Wire…Wire..she’s coming this way. Oh my go…oh my god..oh my god..what do I say????” Livewire slaps his forehead in frustration, a form of action this is becoming far too common in Ted’s presence and slowly whispers back. “How about…..Livewire starts grinning….asking her why the hell she took so fu*king long. I could have been at the gym training for my tag-match with Joe this Thursday. Or I could have spent the afternoon with Fiona. But no, I’m standing here like an idiot with you, waiting for some faded pop star to finish her damn recording”. “Ssshh…whispers Ted, putting his finger to his mouth…she’s about to walk past”. Livewire sighs and stops his rambling as Tori Amos walks past, her white see-through dress exposing to both men the lingerie that she is wearing underneath. Ted tries to give her the puppy dog eyes and is about to say something to her but his voice trails off with no sound. Tori Amos brushes past the two of them and leaves the recording studio. Ted looks to the heavens and starts smiling before nudging Livewire repeatedly in the arm annoying him somewhat. “Wire, bro..please tell me that you saw those panties that she was wearing….she is ssooo sexy!!!” Livewire shakes his head and takes off his dark sunglasses. “Meh, I have seen better Ted, Fiona wears leather thongs for me sometimes when she’s in the mood”. Ted nods his head and closes his eyes, a perverted look comes across his face. “Really?” Ted asks with great trepidation. “Yeah...replies Livewire…and sometimes she wears this nurses uniform that…..hey are you fantasizing about my girlfriend Ted? Ted opens his eyes and look up at Livewire, there is now an alarmingly large bulge in his trousers. “No of course not Wire, I would never do such a thing” answers Ted with a smirk on his face. “Good…replies Livewire with a stern look on his face…come on lets go into that recording room and get this over and done with. As the two walk towards the recording room Ted rubs his hands together “Wow, I need to get a picture of Fiona in that nurses uniform” he says under his breath. Livewire stops walking and turns around “Huh, did you say something? Livewire asks innocently. Ted just shrugs his shoulders with a butter wouldn’t melt in his mouth look and the two tall men continue walking and slowly enter the recording room. The recording room of a major music entertainment industry is truly a majestic place to be in. Livewire and Ted quickly marvel at the cleanliness of the recording room. The floor of the room is wooden and vanished and the walls have two large speakers hung in two corners. Ted and Livewire continue looking around until they hear a tapping sound in the room. They both turn around at the same time to notice that the tapping noise is coming from behind a large perspex screen that divides the room. Ted and Livewire both walk through a door that’s positioned next to the screen and realise that there is another part of the recording room, the production room. Sitting in the production room are two elderly middle-aged, overweight men. The slightly older looking of the two men is wearing a white T-shirt that is obviously two sizes to small for him. The T-shirt has the words “Sony music crew” written across it in bold writing. The other man has a chubbier face and is wearing a green Bob Dylan hooded top. The chubbier man is twisting and turning some knobs on a music frequency machine. Each knobs adjusts and affects some sort of circuitry in the control room and Ted looks mesmerized at all the hi-tech machinery that is in the room. Both men turn to Ted and Livewire and other to shake hands, the elder of the two men first. “Hello….he says, his free hand adjusting some other computer wizardry in the recording room….my name is Steve Larson, I will be your sound engineer for the afternoon”. Livewire and Ted nod their heads in unison and shake his hand they both then turn to shake the large hand of the other seated man. “Hi, my name is Bobby West, I’ll be your vocal mixer for the afternoon.” he says in a deep voice. Livewire and Ted both nod their heads and smile, having no idea of exactly what the job of a sound engineer or vocal mixer is. Ted steps forward and introduces himself and Livewire. “Hello gentlemen, I am Ted Wanstead, agent to the sports stars and this is my number one client, wrestler Robert Amanze also known as the RUSH superstar Livewire.” Ted grins smugly as Livewire sheepishly waves to both seated men, it’s not often that he gets an introduction as well delivered as that, sheesh not even Sheila Tyronne does an intro as good as that. Bobby West scratches his head and looks at the hulking figure of Livewire from head to toe. “You the English guy right?” he asks curiously. Livewire nods his head as Ted takes a seat by the two men, without invitation of course. West takes a further look at the bone structure of Livewire’s face and turns to Steve Larson. “Yep Steve, he looks the part. Frank was right about him, he does seem to have pop star potential” he grins. The Frank that he is talking about is of course Frank Baron, multi-millionaire music mogul and president of Sony records. Steve Larson also looks at Livewire from head to toe and strokes his chin and turns to reply to Bobby West. “Hmm…he will definitely appeal to the eleven to sixteen year old market, big muscles seem to be what all the teenage girls want to see these days.” answers Larson. The two men continue to look at Livewire, analysing every framework of his body, causing him to feel a little uncomfortable at their scrutinizing eyes. After a few seconds of an uncomfortable silence, Bobby West places a firm hand on Ted’s shoulder and clears his throat. “Mr Wanstead, your client certainly does seem to have mass potential. He should make Sony music and yourself a lot of money in the near future”. After hearing these words Ted’s eyes light up and imaginary dollar signs seem to flash across his eyes. Like all scheming agents Ted breaks into a wicked smile and cackle. “Muwahahahaha * cough * I mean excellent, when can we start recording and productions?” asks Ted impatiently. “As soon as you like Mr Wanstead, here is a song sheet of the song we would like Livewire to sing for us today” replies Steve Larson handing Ted a sheet of paper with lyrics for a song etched on it. “I hope you and your client understand that we wanted to make Livewire’s first single a cover song. Myself and Steve both agree that it would be far more beneficial to all parties if Livewire’s first single was a familiar song with the general public that would endear him more to the mainstream audience that we are targeting. Of course once a foothold is made in the market there is then room to experiment and try and diversify Livewire’s sound” chimes in Bobby East, a thoughtful look on his face. Livewire and Ted both look rather confused at what the rather large man has just said. Despite Livewire having an astute marketing and business brain from his years at college, the complexities and jargon of the music business is still very hard to fathom for the most studios of minds. Ted hands the song sheet to Livewire and the personification of cool reads the song title and mouths the words “What the fu*k” before looking at Bobby East, Steve Larson and his agent Ted Wanstead each in turn with a horrified look on his face. “I can’t sing this” exclaims Livewire his bottom lip curling up under extreme worry. “Of course you can....replies Ted…..the lyrics aren’t that difficult to remember” barks back Ted, trying to re-assure his money making client, surely Livewire cant back out now. Livewire waves the song sheet angrily in his hand seemingly puzzled by the choice of song to sing as a cover. “It’s not the lyrics…moans Livewire…..it’s the song. Bye, bye bye by N Sync. Gees, could you guys have chosen an even worse song” whines Livewire with a hint of sarcasm. “What’s wrong with it?” asks Ted worryingly, he takes a handkerchief from out of his trouser pocket and dabs his sweating brow. The pressure of trying to appease his number client is starting to get the devious manipulator. “It’s a crap pop song Ted, the guys in the locker room are going to give me hell for this” replies Livewire. Livewire then smiles and proposes an idea. “Why don’t I do something like a Creed song or even a Marilyn Manson one, I think there is still a niche for the solo rock star to exploit, don’t you think Ted?” asks Livewire desperately trying to convince Ted to change the song and save him any embarrassment. “No…answers Bobby West sternly….kid, I have been in the music business for over twenty years. Do you think people in music actually sing music that they like? Hell no, the music they sing sells, that is all. Now go into that recording room and sing. Time is of the essence.” Ted seems somewhat shocked by the force of Bobby West’s voice, but this man like Steve Larson beside him knew what they were talking about. It seems that if a music artist had the choice to sing a song that brought them respect or brought them money then many an artist would choose the latter, unfortunately. Despite his size and intimidating presence, Livewire knew that he was in a no win situation seeing as he is under contract and a decision by him to veto the choice of single would be seen as a breach of contract and subsequently lead to a lengthy court battle. Something that he cannot afford, especially now that he has been given a chance to possibly win three single titles in the space of a couple of weeks in RUSH. What use would the Lively one be to Boda and his legion of Livewire-ites stuck in a county court jail awaiting bail? Yep, this one of those rare moments where Livewire would have to bite his tongue, keep his mouth shut and agree to the decisions being made. Livewire mumbles something under his breath, probably a few swear words and leaves the production area to enter the recording room. Ted breathes a sigh of relief and manages a wry smile as the production team of Bobby West and Steve Larson begin the process of adjusting the graphic equalizer and all sorts of wave balance frequencies as the backing tune of the song Bye, bye, bye by N Sync starts to kick in. As this happens Livewire takes a seat on the metal stool that is placed in the centre of the recording room and starts to read the lyrics of the song in his head. It is now that he finally realises that he has to sing. Heck, in the wave of euphoria of the signing of the record deal and the finalization of the Crossroads pay-per-view card, Livewire had completely forgotten the fundamental ethics of the music business “singing”. “Sh*t, I’m not sure if I can do this…says the usually unfazed Brit to himself…I haven’t attempted to sing in years, sheesh not since ninth grade at the high school recital”. (Livewire’s thoughts) Oh fu*k, holy sh*t…the damn ninth grade recital. The choir, I was kicked out. Oh the shame, my music teacher said I should never sing again until my voice had broken. I can’t fu*king sing, I’m to music what Hessian is to the Catholic Church. Panic attack!! Panic attack!!! Ok, breath slowly, relax Livewire, ok I’m calm. The first verse is about to start, just sing, just sing, don’t panic, just sing. Livewire opens his mouth, closes his eyes and attempts to sing the first verse of the N Sync hit Bye, bye, bye. The words attempt to sing is used loosely as Livewire delivers a crescendo of noise that can only be likened to a screeching cat on fire. After only two minutes and thirty-three seconds, Bobby West and Steve Larson stop proceedings, obviously not pleased with what they had heard so far. Livewire cuts both men a confused look from the recording room as Ted pathetically tries to prevent them from pulling the plug on the highly lucrative deal. “Hey, guys, he’s got a sore throat….laughs Ted sheepishly….give me two weeks, two weeks, he’ll have the voice of an opera singer by then. Come on two weeks, just two weeks damn it!!” Steve Larson shakes his head and folds his arms while Bobby East looks at his watch. “Well Mr Wanstead…starts Steve Larson….it seems that your client has the looks of a pop star, but sadly not the voice. Bobby, I think Livewire needs the Britney Spears treatment”. “Huh?” replies Ted, now getting more confused by the minute. “The Britney Spears treatment Mr Wanstead, the looping of one person’s voice onto another in order to produce the illusion of one person singing” winks Bobby East. Ted just shrugs his shoulders unsure at what the hell either man is talking about. Bobby East slaps his head in frustration and raises his voice “For the love of god…we’re going to dub Livewire’s voice with somebody else’s”. Ted understands and nods his head now feeling a little more secure about the success of this music project. Bobby East takes out a large red book, which looks like a telephone directory and flicks through it, checking various names. “Steve..he says smiling…let’s use that Terry Tamos kid, he sounds English when he sings”. Steve Larson runs a hand through his long grey hair and nods his head approvingly. “Yes I agree, he may have awful acne and a face purely for radio. But he does have a voice like an angel which strangely does say rather, British so to speak”. The two continue talking leaving Ted and the still waiting in the recording room Livewire totally in the dark about further developments. “Er gentlemen, what happens now, do we have a lot of work to do? asks Ted nervously. Steve Larson uses his cell phone to ring the number of the person he wants to use to dub Livewire’s voice with as Bobby East answers Ted’s question. “No, quite the contrary Mr Wanstead….he says grinning arrogantly….we will take over with things from here. Just make sure you and Livewire are in the area during the next fortnight, the video to accompany the single may need to be completed by then”. Ted smiles and thanks both men for their help and is about to stand up to leave when he suddenly stops and asks them one final question. “Um…guys, what’s that big red button on the control panel for? It has been bugging me all afternoon.” he asks inquisitively. “Steve Larson and Bobby East both roll their eyes and pass it off as nothing. “It’s just a security message” says Larson, “Yeah, nothing important” adds East. “Oh, so you wouldn’t mind if I pressed it then would you gentlemen?” Ted laughs like a little child and presses the button repeatedly like the fool that he is. “No that’s the sprinkler sys…” before Steve Larson can finish his sentence and stop Ted, all the lights in the recording room flash on and off before the sprinkler system turns on totally soaking Livewire and all the circuitry in the recording room. Livewire bangs angrily on the perspex screen as Bobby East and Steve Larson both give Ted a menacing glare. Ted covers his mouth and snickers both simply saying “oops”. Will Ted Wanstead ever tell Tori Amos his undying love for her? Will Livewire’s music career start off with a bang? Will somebody ever turn of the recording room’s sprinkler system? Who gives a damn? Take care Livewire-ites.
