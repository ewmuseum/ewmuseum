Date: just because “I KEEEEEEEEL YOU!” “Please…ull thro….sir.” “IIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIII” We fade in to a cloudless suburban night. Amidst the hustle and bustle of Pittsburgh on a Thursday night, coupled with the extra crowd leaving the Mellon Arena after AD15, we find ourselves outside a local Taco Bell. Normally, we’d be at Bill Gates’ Burrito Shack, but we’re not in Idaho, or Iowa, or wherever the hell that stupid thing is. Idling in the drive-thru lane of the “south of the border” fast food joint, we find our heroes packed inside their familiar gold El Camino. Juan Carlos Smith and Julio Diaz Perez O’Neil, the Spanish announce team, sit in the front seat, that familiar mix of stress and apathy masking their faces. RoboGates, the Bill Gates android, sits in the back, emitting a high-pitched siren that sounds whenever he comes within 100ft of one of his namesake’s rival food chains. “IIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOO” And of course, also in the back, leaning halfway out the back window to scream into the order box thingy, is El Spiffy!. He shouts as loud as he possibly can to be heard over the RoboGates siren. “Do you hear the Spiffy!? No guacamole or I keeeeeeeeeeeeel you!” After a faint burst of static, the box responds. “Yo…tota….five doll…eighty…ents. Pull…ough.” “What the hell did he say?” Juan Carlos demands, not even bothering to turn his head. “He said pull through,” Julio responds, rubbing his temples against the loud screeching emitting from their robot. “Fine, I’m pulling through. Watch yourself, Spiff.” Of course, El Spiffy! doesn’t hear him. No, it wouldn’t be fun if El Spiffy! had heard him. So instead, he’s still hanging out of the car, as close to the box as he can get, screaming at the top of his lungs. “The Spiffy! says no guacamole! If the Spiffy! finds guacamole…I KEEEEEEEL-” *THUNK* The El Camino lurches into gear and jumps forward. El Spiffy!’s head bounces off the metal box, knocking him silly. He slumps out of the car and to the asphalt below. The El Camino rolls for a few feet before stopping. “We’ve got a luchadore down.” “Jesus Damn.” “We’d better go get him before some fan picks him up and takes him home.” The cartruck shifts to park and the announcers climb out. Lucky for them, no one else is in the drive-thru. “That’d be their loss anyway. He’s like a genie. We’re stuck with him until someone else is stupid enough to come along and rub the lamp.” “Nah. Genies are either funny like Robin Williams or hot like Barbara Eden.” “Fair enough. But can’t we at least tie his stupid ass to the hood or something?” “I don’t think there’d be room.” Both men look back at the car, where Stimpo is lashed to the roof. The budding pop star shivers against the night air, but clutches the Syndicate title closely to his chest. “You doing alright there, pal?” Stimpo gives them a thumbs up. They exchange a shrug and turn back to El Spiffy! on the ground. “I keeeeeee…” “Yeah, you’ve pretty much covered that, man. How long have you been saying that anyway? Weirdo…” They haul the Syndicate champ to his feet, look at each other, then chuck him into the bed of the El Camino. He lands with a thud, and groans a little bit. Stimpo’s tied-down arm reaches down with a pen and a pad of paper. Despite being semiconscious, El Spiffy! signs an autograph and hands the pad back to the strapped-down protégé. The Spanish announcers look at each other, then slowly get back in the car. It’s 2am in Pittsburgh. They’re in a Taco Bell drive-thru lane. Stimpo is tied to the hood of the car. El Spiffy! is out on his feet in the back. And a robotic double of Bill Gates is beeping wildly to advertise his owner’s Burrito Shack. . . . Just another day in the life.
