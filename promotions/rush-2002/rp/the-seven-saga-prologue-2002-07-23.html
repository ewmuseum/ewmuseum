The Seven Saga: Prologue

23 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/23/02 There will be interference. That one line echoed through Caleb’s mind as he sat in front of his fireplace, a polishing rag in hand. There will be interference. He knew there would… judging upon the stipulations made by the man in charge… No disqualification. No count out. There was going to be interference. It would be a run-in, obviously, Dante Jackson and Ali Khadafi being the most obvious perpetrators. Caleb shrugged. He’d slay them in cold blood if they were stupid enough to bother him. Not that it would be any trouble, anyway… his blade had returned to him, after all. He had no problem doing it to Arcayin – that moronic barbarian – vanquished him in front of a crowd of 20,000 on one addition of Mayhem. Those were the days… Enough reminiscing. He lifted the rag and brought it across the blade of that marvelous katana – only to split the fibers clean in half. That was tall tell sign that it was clean and sharp enough. McGlocklan stood from his spot on the floor and sheathed his blade. When suddenly there was a knock on his wall – a desperate knock at that. Caleb swung around and gaped at the mirror that hung there… it wasn’t his reflection that he saw. He bolted over, his heavy trench gliding noiselessly across the stained oak floor. McGlocklan: Now now, Michael, you always taught me patience was a virtue. Michael: Yes, well, in times of trouble, patience can go to hell. McGlocklan: Hah! Quite the sour one, aren’t we? McGlocklan stepped away from the mirror and wandered over to his kitchen. He lived in one of those “studio apartments” where most of the rooms weren’t separated. Lifting a small metallic pot, he stepped over towards the sink and filled the top with water. Michael was looking on, a grimace showing through his teeth. Caleb put the pot on a coffeemaker, hit a few buttons, and took a seat in front of the mirror. McGlocklan: Care for a mug? Michael: Clever. Clever indeed. McGlocklan: Very well… I do, however, know you’re not “here” just for a chat. Michael: Oh? Is that so? McGlocklan: So spare me the formalities and great straight to it, will you? Michael rolled his eyes, annoyed, and took a deep breath. A sudden burst of nausea came over Caleb… quite strange. It was a gut instinct, no doubt. Something horrible had happen once again. It was a continuous cycle. Caleb went to Earth, saved humanity, only to return to heaven and save the dead, only to return to Earth and save everyone once again. Caleb tilted back in his chair and folded his arms – he had only returned weeks before hand. Michael sighed… this would be hard for McGlocklan, he knew. Michael: Lucifer again, I’m afraid. McGlocklan: Obviously. Fill me in. Caleb could see that Michael looked uncomfortable. Some kind of serious blunder, probably. Caleb sighed. If Lucifer was behind it, it was serious. Roman demons were clever, true, and could meddle with the best of them. But they had nowhere NEAR the power of Lucifer… that bastard. Michael unraveled a piece of parchment and held it against the mirror for Caleb to see. After reading a few lines, he made a quick assumption. McGlocklan: Augustus? Michael: Aye. McGlocklan: I’ve always had a problem with him guarding the swords. Why leave a highly dangerous job like that in his hands? He was blatantly irresponsible. Why didn’t you see this happening? The council will have him for this. Michael: No need. McGlocklan: What? Why not? Michael: Slain. Caleb turned his head with his hand across his mouth. Augustus was, indeed, a fool… but a wise fool. He wasn’t the greatest fighter… or even the greatest eye, but he was full of pearls… and he was a friend. Caleb’s eyes narrowed and he placed a hand on the hilt of his blade. Gripping the finger grooves tightly, he turned back toward the mirror, his eyes burning with plots of revenge. Michael rolled the parchment and slid it up the sleeve of his cloak. McGlocklan: Transport me immediately. I have nothing of importance in this dwelling anyway. I will consult the council and deliver my plan upon them. Michael: That’s not necessary. McGlocklan: Oh? Feel me in, noble one. Michael: Lucifer is no troll. He knows the cards he’s dealt, and he’s going to play them. He’s scattered them across earth – his finest warriors guard each. We’ve located the first three. The all lie in the United States. One sword on the eastern boarder; the second on the west; and the third central, naturally. McGlocklan: And the others? Michael: Estimated at two in Asia and one somewhere in Europe. McGlocklan: Interesting… which three in the United States? Michael: Antioch, Tetra, and Blithe. I trust you’ll retrieve them? McGlocklan: Have I a choice? Michael: Well… no. Not really. McGlocklan: They will be in your possession by the end of the week, then. Michael: Very well. Michael made a motion as if he was to leave, but Caleb held up his hand. Michael stopped, looked at McGlocklan in a puzzled sort of fashion, and waited impatiently for him to say something. A grin appeared across Caleb’s face. McGlocklan: Bring ambrosia next time? The mead here is thin and takes three glasses before it even begins to hit the spot. Michael made an unmentionable motion with his arm and hand and vanished from the glass on the wall. McGlocklan chuckled to himself and buffed the collar of his coat up. Demons… silly gits. Taking the pot off the coffeemaker, he dumped the liquid into the sink. There was no time for coffee now, sadly. He stretched his back, cracked his neck, and flexed his arms. It had been a month since his last real battle. He made his way to the front door, grasped the brass handle, and pushed open. Out into the hall, he took heavy strides to the staircase. With a swish of the coat, he was down the stairs and toward his destiny.
