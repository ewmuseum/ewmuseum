From Mexico to Woodstock

29 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 29th October 2002 The rope was suddenly cut, sliced by the edge of the shimmering blade with lightning quick speed. The sword held between his teeth, he grabbed onto the cord and let his feet loose, swinging through the air. As the velocity built up further, the gravity seemed to disperse and the sensation of flying overtook. Dr. Velasquez looked up in horror and disgust, his black top hat and curled moustache shaking in the draft. The hero let go of the rope, falling down hard on the Mexican earth. He stood back up to face his nemesis, holding the tied up hostages to the explosives. “So, ju have come to foil my plot?” Velasquez snapped, his throat spewing with venom. The hero stood up, his bulging muscles emitting a wave of power and good. His face was encased in some kind of black mask, eyeholes sill revealing none of his identity. “Ju cannot stop me, it is only a matter of time before the mayor must give me all those lovely pesos, and ju shall be defeated by my sword!” Exclaimed Velasquez, dropping down from the platform that held the hostages. Velasquez whipped his rapier from its hilt, stepping into stance and twirling the pointed end of the blade at his enemy. A blurred dazzle of a sword fight then ensued, sparks flittering in the air as metal meets metal. Before time could even pass, Velasquez was down, a trickle of blood on his cheek, his top hat fallen from his shimmering baldhead and his long black cloak torn. The hero stepped forward, the point of the sword pressing against Velasquez’s throat. “End it, bring this horrific life of mine to an end.” Velasquez muttered, his eyes filled with fear. “No Velasquez, it doesn’t end this way. You shall have a chance at life and to change your wicked wicked ways.” The hero said heroically. The hero then sprang up the steps onto the platform and began freeing the prisoners from the bomb. Oranges, apples, pineapples and bananas all began fleeing away, freed and fearful. The hero swiped away his mask, dabbing at his dark forehead as he began starring at the entanglement of various coloured wires. He had no idea which ones to cut. The blue wire? The red one? “Use the force!” Said an ethereal voice from nowhere. “The force?” Asked the hero. “Yes, use the force mon!” Repeated the ghost of Bob Marley. The hero swiped at dozens of wires, stopping the bomb and saving the town in the blink of an eye. He had glory and the girl; it was a happy ending before the meteor came. He fell face first onto the ground, landing in some sort of brown goo. * His eyes slowly opened, awoken by the stuck record playing ‘Incense and Peppermints’ by Strawberry Alarm Clock over and over. He lifted his face up, stuck onto a plate covered in messy tofu. The room was covered in heavy green wallpaper and other bodies, all in a lethargic state of unknowing as the smoke hung heavy above them. Joe rubbed his head, no memory coming to him at all. He looked around, believing himself to have stumbled upon a hippy graveyard. One of the indolent tree-huggers began blowing white powder out of his nose and groaning slowly. Joe’s throat was dry and red roar, making his speech thorny and horrid. “Where am Ah?” Joe asked, his voice brining pain to him. “Hey man, don’t you remember anything?” Asked the hippy. “Ah wouldn’t be askin’ if Ah did ya stupid foo!” Joe exclaimed. “Hey man, don’t get aggravated, it’s all about love here. Stay mellow, man.” Replied the hippy, his long hair floating along his face. Joe began tiptoeing around the room, being careful not to step on anybody as he searched for his belongings. His shoes came back to his ownership, as he found two people hugging one each. He tried to block any memory from coming back to him, he didn’t want to know. All he knew was the dream he had before waking. As he found his wallet, pushing it back into his pocket, he turned again to the hippy he was also awake. “Where’s the door?” Joe asked in a very commanding voice. “Just out there.” The hippy replied, pointing to an open door leading to the hall. Joe stomped through, less than careful as he stepped on a few of the laying hippies. He stamped out and opened the door to the outside, looking around for any indication of his location. He saw a bus stop nearby and shrugged his shoulders.
