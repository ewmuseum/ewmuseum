The Seven Saga: Blithe

7 August 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/7/02 I stood there, hesitant, and wondered what I was doing. The gates laid out before, opened like a gallon of milk in a cookie-eating contest. My sword was already drawn and gripped tightly in my hand. This was dangerous… a little too dangerous. An abandoned prison just south of Vermont, front entrance unlocked, lights on in several windows. It was inviting. Inviting me out of the cold to the horrors within. I gulped, a dry budge sticking to my throat. It was dry—parched. A tall tell sign to get the hell out of there… but I couldn’t. The sword. Blithe: those that bind the undead. The third of the seven was just ahead of me. I proceeded forth, pushing the right gate aside and walking up the path. “Warden,” I spat to myself. The Warden was a mean little bastard. Rumor had it that he wandered around that prison at night, slamming his food tray against the cold, unforgiving metallic bars of those cells. He had died by electrocution – the chair. Capital punishment. He had killed seven children and thirteen adults – twenty in all. Killed being an understatement, naturally. It was hard to put the pieces of the bodies back together seeing as how he carved them in articulate designs, placing their flesh on pieces of canvas—matching the work of his favorite artists. He was a sick son of a bitch… no denying. I stepped forward, past the field now, and at the front doors. They were rusted open – easy access… almost too easy. I narrowed me eyes, got a good grip on the hilt of my blade, and continued on, my feet barely lifting off the floor. I wasn’t prepared for this at all. All I knew was that the Warden was somewhere in the giant building, and that he was guarding Blithe. Checking to see that the coast was clear, I sprinted across the hall and turned the corner – only to come face to face with a screaming corpse. Swear out loud, I lifted my sword above my head and swung it down rather ferociously, splitting the demon in half and sending it from where it originated. “Little bugger,” I exclaimed, breathing rather heavily now. My heart was racing… that thing gave me quite a stir. I now knew that he has minions… probably hundreds. Shrugging, I kicked over the split carcass and continued on down the hall, checking the cells as I went, my eyes peeled for any strange activity or for the sword. Suddenly, a roar thundered behind me. I turned… saw nothing. Another turn. Nothing. My heart was racing again, like a rookie doctor making his first incision. And then it happened. A giant beast dropped from the ceiling. His yellow eyes were the size of beach balls, his breath like poison, and his teeth like a shark’s—rows upon rows of them. I stepped back, my sword out stretched to block any projectiles. “Vile angel—” I didn’t hesitate to kill the beast. As it opened it’s mouth for a word, I lunged forward with lightning quickness, spearing it’s throat from tip to hilt. Wasting not a second longer, I leapt up, the sword splitting through vital chords and tubes, until it exited the top of his head. Swearing, I swished my sword off to the side, the acidic blood sliding to the ground with a sizzle. It let out one more groan before exploding in a heap of dust. Wiping my brow clean of sweat, I slew another three undead before continuing on down the hall. Entering a large room, I gasped at the horror that lay before me. Several hundred—if not thousand—undead prisoners, each with a knife and fork in their hands, seated at several long tables. In front of each, a metallic plate filled with nothing but dust. Shivering, I thought better of it not to interfere and took off, avoiding eye contact with any of them. Up a flight of stairs, I sensed that I was getting closer. The air was becoming more and more cool. The sweat was freezing and clinging to my face. The dust was getting in my gloves and in my mouth. Finally, he showed his face. “Caleb McGlocklan…” he stated, a serpentine grin spread across his face, “a pleasure to meet you at last.” I scoffed at him, brandishing my sword in his face. He sneered and ran his long fingers through his hair. I shivered at the thought… those hands that slaughtered those innocent people. Behind him was blithe, naturally. He smirked and picked it up with those knotted meat hooks, holding it above his head. “If you want it…” he called at me, his eyes flashing in between green and yellow, “go and get it…” With a toss, he send it down another stairway. Swearing at him in a mysterious language only certain men can understand, he stepped back, guarding the entrance to the flight of stairs. “You’ll die for that, filth,” I shouted at him, my katana gripped rather tightly. “Twenty people’s lives rest in hilt of my sword… and I will grant them their vengeance.” He smiles and kicked up his foot. The ball that hung from his ankle immediately flung forth. He grabbed hold of it and swung it above his head like a ball and chain. Smirking, I approached, sword readied. He took a long, swooping swing at me, which I easily avoided. Ducking inward, I was inside his range. Smiling face to face with him, I slashed my sword upwards; meeting with his arm and slicing it clean off. He groaned in what might have been pain and stumbled backwards down the stairs, his ball following closely behind. “Have a nice trip,” I said, smiling. He swore up at me as he rolled down, breaking his neck if he hadn’t already been dead. I followed him down. It was bitterly cold now, things chapping when a brisk wind was up. I looked around, fortunate enough to see nothing. That was my mistake. I took off to gather up Blithe when suddenly, I was smashed in the side of the face with that bastardly weight of his. Hitting the ground, I groaned, holding my face with my free arm. “Stupid little beastie,” he laughed, “always keep your… head up! Muahahaha!” If you had heard that laugh, your blood would have been boiling. I roared as loud as my lungs would allow me and leapt to my feet. I rampaged at him, slashing the chain where his weight dangled away from his hand. He whimpered as it sailed away. “Filth!” I roared again, boiling with anger. Sheathing my sword, I pounded my hand into his dry, dusty face, sending him backward. A kick sent him back a bit more. I looked around, although not taking my eyes off the demon, and knew where I was. I saw behind him. I laughed. Another punch sent him back further. Another left and he stumbled a few more feet. He was there… I could see it. One last kick sent him back, back, back and into a chair. I smirked; he looked at where he was. I grabbed hold of the handle… I pulled… and he fried. Thousands of volts of electricity ran through his dead hands and skin. I smirked. He whimpered. He wasn’t dead—yet. Only one thing could do it. I unleashed my sword. He gazed at it and trembled. Twenty ice-cold eyes peered at him through the metallic blade. I aimed it at his head and swung and full force forward. Clean through his spine. His head toppled off his shoulders and to the ground, where I lifted my foot and brought it down. Smashed under the heel of my boot. Spitting at the ground, I sheathed the law enforcer. I would no longer fuck around with these vile buckets of vomit. It was serious. Antioch restored. Imps bound.Tetra restored. Carver’s bound.Blithe restored. Undead bound. The End. To Be Continued.
