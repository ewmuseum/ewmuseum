I could have gone home.

29 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 29, 2002 I could have gone home. Yeah, I was done wrestling. I had beaten D-Kay Calyx, even with his little buddy Deamont in his corner. Sure, I got a chair shot from Brock for my efforts – but that’s something we’re all used to in the wrestling business. No promos to cut, no more matches to wrestle. I could have gone home. But I didn’t. I don’t know what made me stay. Matthews came out to keep an eye on me in my match – hell, I guess I’d return the favor. So I sat there, in my locker room, just in time to catch the last half of the main event after a quick shower. “Damn, the crowd is going nuts for this,” I said to myself out loud. They were hotter than the midday sun in the heart of Texas for this match, and with good reason, too. Both of these guys put on a great show. Down came Boda. Lead pipe shot, Deamont got the cocky pin. Okay, all was going according to plan. Right, right…I decided I could leave then. I knew what was going to be said, just like everyone else in the locker room did. I could have gone home. But something just didn’t feel right. Something about that seemed a liiiiiiiiiiiiiiittle bit too…real. The show had been over for about three minutes by this point. Most of the people in the stands were gone, back to continue on their normal, everyday lives after the two hour break from reality we give them every Thursday. Little did I know that the next fifteen minutes would be all too real for myself, as well. I packed up my belongings after getting dressed. I was too lazy – heh, seems like I’m too lazy for a lot of things these days – to put on any normal clothes, so I just slipped on my wrestling attire and walked out of my – somewhat musty – locker room’s door. I could have gone home. Something bothered me, though. I paced around backstage for awhile, passing by the few people that stuck around. I nodded at Mick, who was just walking out the door – sword sheathed on his back as always. I could have followed him; I could have gone home. Ugh, fuck it. I began walking toward Chris’ locker room. Was he there? Was him lying out there just a work to make it believable for the fans that decided to stick around a bit later? I had hoped so. I was wrong. I saw him, ten feet or so in front of me at the bottom of the cold, steel steps that led down backstage from the middle of the arena. He was lying face down, a monsoon of blood pouring out from a huge gash on his forehead, and his mouth. “Fucking sick…” I thought to myself, staring at the bloodied man that was my best friend. I gasped as he started to squint, trying not to look at himself. I knew he had a lot of pride. He couldn’t stand to see himself like this. “Damn,” I shouted, shaking my head. I looked down at the poor guy, spitting blood out of his mouth; his teeth and gums were lined with crimson syrup. I’d never seen anyone that bloody before in my entire career, much less my entire life. Yeah, guys blade all the time…but this…this was all too real. Matthews just barely pulled himself up to one knee, but I knew it was no use. I looked down at him, but he refused to make eye contact with me, obviously not wanting to look at me in this situation. I didn’t care. I just hoped the guy was alright. I put out my right arm for him. May as well help him walk. His hands were as cold as ice, having to use both of his arms to muster the strength to pull himself up. After what seemed like an eternity, he finally did, leaning on my right shoulder as he struggled to walk. I heard the spattering of his blood and saliva smack the concrete floor every ten seconds or so as he cleared the blood out of his mouth. “Ugh…I could have gone home,” I thought to myself as we limped along, searching for his locker room. Hell, it was only the second locker room from the backstage entrance, but it seemed like forever as we struggled to make our way there. Finally, I found it. “Chris Matthews” on the nameplate of a not-too-fancy oaken door. I literally kicked the door down. Fuck the doorknob. “Jesus,” I said to myself as I pulled him into the locker room. “Remind me…never cross Cassius fucking Deamont.” I looked outside at the tens of workers backstage, all minding their own business even after seeing a trail of blood decorating the floor and hearing the door being kicked down. “Fucking workers,” I cursed as I let go of Chris for a moment. Five seconds later, I turned around and saw him lying there, nearly drowning in a pool of his own blood. “FUCK!” I screamed at the top of my lungs, pissed off beyond belief. I looked down my friend, and began to shake his arms violently, trying to stir him. No dice. He was fastly becoming unconscious, and there was nothing I could do about it. I was going nuts. I hated to see anyone like this, especially a friend. Fucking business. It’s supposed to be our goddamned jobs, not a personal vendetta. This shit between Deamont and Matthews has gone too far. I kicked the fallen door, stubbing my toe before running back outside. “SOMEBODY CALL 911, YOU LAZY FUCKS!” I shouted, catching the attention of one “Good Samaritan” that decided to get off his ass and pull out his cell phone. I walked back into the locker room. He was lying there, in a pool of his own blood, eyes shut, breathing soft. Yep, unconscious. I could have gone home. But I didn’t. I stuck around to watch all this, to see my best friend be brutally assaulted – for real – at the hands of some sick fuck and his cronies. Thoughts began to race through my head at a blistering pace. A clusterfuck of random worries from the recesses of my cerebrum. What if I was next? I hope Matthews is going to be okay. I’ll fucking kill Deamont. Goddamn EMTs…they better hurry the hell up. D-Kay…I want that goddamned folder. I wonder who I used to be. Look at all that blood… Fuck, did I tip the bellhop at the hotel? I wonder how Coach is doing… I…wait a minute. Coach? Who’s Coach? Suddenly, something hit me like a pile of bricks. I thought harder and harder, trying to remember a “Coach”…but to no avail. Heh, they said this would be a slow process after all… I looked at Matthews, peacefully sleeping, unconscious, his head cocked to the side with the blood flow finally beginning to let up. I waited patiently for the EMTs and police to arrive, thinking to myself… I could have gone home. I should have gone home. But I’m glad I didn’t.
