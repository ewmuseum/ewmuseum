The Texas Chain Store Massacre (Part 2)

23 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 23rd July 2002 (It was late in the evening, the sticky air quite overpowering in its heavy, wet and clinging clutches. The sun was setting, still just peeping over the horizon line before it brought darkness down and allowed the shining moon to rise. The sky was a watercolour wash of reds and oranges, spilling across the clouds like dripping paint. The dark and spiny branches of the trees surrounded the small park, casting claw-like shadows across the grass. These outstretching spines of darkness barely reached the lone park bench, where a notice had been stapled on. At the bottom of this white sheet was the signature and smiling face of Joe Bananas. Above was the information, which he had so carefully typed into his now fixed computer; he was out on a protest. After learning of the cover-up that was the local grocery store, and the entire chain of stores just like it, Joe had began his quest to put a stop the evil that lay in waiting.) Joe: Dere’s another one, dis is goin well. (Joe had been getting these printed pages to as many people and places as he could since the crack of dawn. Shoving them onto cars as a direct obstacle to the driver, posting them in dozens onto the sides of walls of any kind, posting them through people’s doors, he even gave one to a dog to take to his owner. He had been working hard, and he had finally gotten to the last of them. He now was to move on to the next part of his ever so devious plan. His destination was the very grocery store he was fighting against. He had to make solid proof to the people of what he was telling them. He would post up more of his ‘fresh from the Xerox’ goldenrod series, tell them of the evil plan the bad guys behind the chain store had all set out and then prove to them that it was all truth right before their eyes.) *Half an hour later* (Joe was stood outside the local store, the same place where he had been locked up in a prison cell before making his escape and beginning his battle against them. He was stood atop a small, light brown soapbox and was waving his arms wildly as he made his opinions vocal. Signs were all around him, the paint still wet and dripping down with such slogans as ‘Gross Grocery’ and ‘Behin’ Da Store Is Da Gore’ and many more just the same.) Joe: Da head behin’ all dis business is a brutish, rahtid lookin’ sheg up nazarite. He jus’ lookin’ ta get ya dollars. He supply ya wit old food, gone off fruit and is jus’ a general rass-raider. Kid 1: Shut up man! Get a real job! Joe: Hush ya noise pickney! Kid 1: What? Talk properly. Joe: Ahhh it’s on nah, nahbody diss da patois wit’out a batty kickin’. (Joe dived off his soapbox at the young child, his gnashing teeth headed straight for his face. The child screeched in a high pitch and ran as fast as his little legs would take him away from the crazed Jamaican.) Joe: Ya rahtid pickney! Don’ bruck anymore of dis or Ah bruck mah fist upside ya head! (Joe shook his fist in a threatening stance before deciding to leave the almost out of sight child alone. This part of his protest was not going as well as he had hoped, so he decided to move on and get to the most important part. He stood back up on his soapbox and raised his hands above his head, drawing people closer to listen.) Joe: People, Ah can prove ta ya all dat wha’ Ah been tellin’ ya is all true. Man 1: What? How? Joe: Simple, but ya all have ta follow me firs’ and it will all become clear as water ta ya all. (Joe climbed back down, off his box, and strode towards the entrance of the store. He pushed the double doors open with both hands, giving him a strange feeling of being in Dynasty. The customers and workers inside all gasped as Joe lead the talkative crowd into the store and towards the health food section in a very proud and triumphant walk. This was to be the proof, the proof that he wasn’t insane and what he had been preaching was the truth. He beckoned the crowd to take a step back as he placed his fingers firmly on the refrigerator section which he believed lead to the secret corridors behind. He pulled and pulled as hard as he could, but to no avail. It was tight and sealed; he could not prise it open as hard as he tried. The crowd waited for a few moments as Joe continued to tug at the side of the refrigerator, but as they saw nothing was happening began to gradually disperse. They were all now convinced that it was all lies, leaving him to do as he wants and live out the rest of his life. Most of them had wanted Joe to have been right, wanting a little excitement and controversy in their lives. The veins on the side of his neck, pulsating from pumping extra blood, stuck out and became very visible. His knuckles becoming white under the pressure and his teeth gritted together, he would not stop until this door opened. An old lady, with curly white hair and a blue cardigan, shuffled over and tapped Joe on his waist as she couldn’t reach his shoulders.) Old Lady: Excuse me. (Joe span round, taken aback by these sudden spoken words. He looked down at the elderly woman, his eyes widened and his raw and aching hands held up in defensive fists by his head.) Joe: Wha? Old Lady: You’re in the way; I just want to get something out of here. (The old lady pointed at the health foods section, which had been trying to prove, was a door for some time. Joe held his hands against his hips and grinned.) Joe: There’s no point, it won’t open because it’s a cover up for a secre… (Before Joe could finish the old lady had taken it upon herself to barge past and pull open the clear plastic door, opening the small world of healthy food stuffs to herself. Joe stared in awe; he didn’t believe it possible. He scratched the back of his head in amazement, he thought it was just a makeshift door filled with painted on food. The old lady decided against purchasing any of the items inside and decided to head for the cookies and ice cream section of the store instead, slamming the door behind her as she did so. Joe tested the door, opening and closing it a few times, as a kind of affirmation to himself that it was true.) Joe: Dat aint possible, is it? (Joe shrugged his shoulders, he was very sure that it was a secret entranceway to the back as well. He continued pulling on the side, tugging harder and harder to try and prise it open. He even tried using his teeth to unlock the secret passageway.) Joe: Dis t’ing is solid, bu’ Ah need ta get in dere. (Joe began punching the cabinet, clawing at it and even tried giving it a dropkick… but none of it worked. He was becoming desperate now, knowing that there must be some way of opening the damn door. He needed to chill his worn out hands, so he opened up the cabinet and cooled himself off. He used packets of 99% fat free meals under his armpits and on his head. He began searching for something bigger, to cover up his stomach. He stuck his head inside a little, to try and get a better look. He searched the bottom row and saw a can at the very back, completely out of place with the rest of the row. On it was printed the words ‘weight gain formula’ in block red letters. Joe tried to lift the can but could only tilt it on the base of the row, as he did so he heard a loud whirring noise. He let go and allowed the can to snap back into place, the secret door slowly opening. Joe jumped up and door, celebrating his victory.) Joe: Yes! Dyamn all ya ta hell who din’t believe meh! (Without thinking or even drawing up some sort of plan, Joe charged through and darted down the darkened corridor. This indeed it, he had gotten inside at long last. He turned back around, to throw his triumph in the faces of everyone he could find. The light was fading, the store disappearing, as the door closed back shut. Joe dived towards it, but it was too late. He tried to open it once more, but this time it was shut solid. He thrust his fists as hard as he could into the wall, as it was now. He had to find another way out now, but he was surrounded by the unknown and definite danger now. He staggered, blinded by the dark, along the corridor. Feeling against the walls for any signs of a doorway, or even a light switch if he was lucky.) Joe (whispering): Come one! Ah can’t see in dis t’ing! (Joe felt something hit his shin, it was some kind of wire that was spread from one wall to the next. Joe gulped as he realised what he had walked over and began to run as the red lights swirled around him and the siren blared out at top volume. The corridor was a little illuminated now, the bright red beams showing him the way. He could hear doors snapping open behind him and the heavy footsteps of what could only be guards. He had no time to look back, he had to press forward to try and find some kind of escape. The pursuit was extremely tiring and Joe’s breath became heavier with every step he took, the guards getting closer and closer. The corridor ahead took a very sharp left turn, which Joe decided to turn as difficultly as he could to throw the guards. He turned on his heels in as sharp an angle as possible, turning to his left and skidding a little before getting back to the same pace as before. The darkness was beginning to lift as the hot white lights above buzzed into life, Joe could see as clear as day now. But the guards were still chasing him, and everybody else here would wan to capture him. He was searching for a ray of light and salvation, and it came in the form of a green ‘men’s room’ sign. Joe dived through the door and fumbled with the latch, locking the door shut behind him. He knew it wouldn’t be long before the sentinels of grocery broke down the very thin door. Joe climbed up onto the toilet seat, shielding his eyes from the overly bright light above. The cramped room left very room to move and Joe held his arms as close to him as he could. He looked up at the grill above; an air vent is always needed in such a small toilet room. He pushed it up and slid it to the side, pulling himself up with ease up into the vent system. As soon as his whole body was up, including his dangling legs, he moved the grill back into place. He knew these guards weren’t the type to ask ‘where’d he go?’ and would go for air vent straight away, so Joe began to crawl for his life. He was shrouded in darkness and charged forward like a possessed rhino. He stopped dead still at each new grill, peeping down to see if it was a suitable place to escape. At ever disappointment he swivelled his head back around to see that the guards were far behind him.) Joe: At least Ah got suttin. Ah need ta get outta here though. (Joe continued his desperate crawl, his knees becoming raw and painful now. The skin on the palms of his hands were beginning to receive the bad side of friction burn and were ready to stop in defiance against Joe’s wishes. He stopped at the next grill, trying to catch his breath back in a well-earned rest. He couldn’t believe at all that he was here, doing this and just wanted to get out and forget all about it. If it hadn’t been for the intruder alarm then he could have gotten away quietly without being noticed. Joe looked down slowly through the slits, seeing the interior of a well-furnished room with a burgundy carpet. Two men were sat at either side of a mahogany desk, seemingly signing some kind of paper.) Man: Well, Mr. Carlos, I think you’ll find everything in order. I hope we both find great benefit from this agreement. Carlos: Si, I also hope that this bitter feud can come to an end. Man: me too, we can put the past behind us can’t we? Carlos: I certainly hope so, people may think this a little crazy… but that’s why they call me ‘Loco’ Carlos. This risks will pay off I believe. (The man, who Joe now recognises as the person who had spoken to him in the holding cell before, offers Carlos a drink of brandy. The overweight Latino accepts and raises his glass.) Man: A toast to new beginnings for you Mr. Carlos. (‘Loco’ Carlos nods his head and takes a long slurp, the glass and heavy brandy blocking his vision as the man pulls a gun from inside his jacket. Joe winces as the gun is fired, and the bullet slices through the heart of Carlos. Clutching his hands to his bleeding chest, Carlos falls to the ground in a heap leaving the man chuckling to himself before he leaves the room. As soon as he believes the coast is clear, Joe shuffles the grating aside and stealthily falls into the room. He moves away from the fallen body and looks over the recently signed papers. He sees that it is documents and order forms, all to do with the plan the man had explained whilst Joe was locked away. The drugged fruit was being imported from Columbia, Carlos had signed it and not known of the filling in the food and the consequences this would have on him, or the memory of him as was the case now. Joe grabbed a pen from the other side of the desk and decided to alter a few parts of the writing ‘Loco’ Carlos had missed. He replaced many of the words on the forms, ‘drug’ was replaced with ‘love’ and ‘bad fruit’ was replaced with ‘fresh and ripe fruit.’ Joe wiped a few of the bloodstains before carefully placing the documents back as they were. He then moved over to the door, through which the man had exited, and slowly pushed it open. He peeked out of it, checking for any signs of people he didn’t want to see. To his surprise the door lead out to the store, this door was labelled as the manager’s office. He looked around, being as careful as possible not to run into anybody at all. He then dashed forward towards the main doors, driving his body through them and getting away from the store.) *Many days later* (Joe sat down comfortably in the park bench, the sparkling sun illuminating his face with its warm and welcoming golden glow. He had revisited the store just a few minutes ago, and had bought a carton of fresh fruit. They all had stickers of red lovehearts on them, which Joe thought was absolutely perfect as a symbol of his triumph. If they had any more plans, Joe was not going to play the hero every again and would stay as far away as possible. He had saved the honour of fruit (and some dead guy named Carlos) and that was a great thing. He was the secret saviour, behind the scenes and unknown hero that won the battle. He bit into the ripe, green kiwi as he stared at the scenery around him calmly.)
