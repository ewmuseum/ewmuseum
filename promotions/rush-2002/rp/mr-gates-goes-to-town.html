Mr. Gates Goes to Town

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: very well, thank you. From the blank screen, the godawful song “I Love NYC” by Andrew W.K. explodess, destroying all senses. The pitch black transitions to a heavily tinted car window, speeding by on a busy road. The camera zooms out and pans as the car makes a turn, and we get a glimpse of our location, the bright lights and big buildings of New York City’s Times Square. Billboards and Marquees flash against the night sky as the sportscar, and thankfully, its music as well, disappear into the city. The camera swings back to where the car used to be, and now captures two familiar faces, Juan Carlos Smith and Julio Diaz Perez O’Neil, as they stand impatiently on the sidewalk. Both men are dressed in street clothes, and are without their usual Spanish-commentator gear. The camera approaches the two to catch them in mid-conversation. “...and then he and Gates just disappeared in the software guy’s helicopter!” Juan Carlos screams. “It can’t be that bad. I mean, at least he’s in New York already. All we have to do is catch him and tie him down until Adrenaline. No big deal.” “Yeah, but first we actually have to FIND him. This is a big-ass city. And with Gates’ money, they can get wherever they want to go.” “But how many places would a guy in a luchadore mask and a timid computer giant go in NYC?” “I don’t have a clue.” Juan Carlos starts up one side of the street, the comes back into frame headed in the other direction. As he passes by, Julio grabs him by his shirt and pulls him back, pointing across the street. He points across the street, and Juan Carlos nods in approval. Without a word, both men step off the curb and head towards whatever it is they’re looking at, prompting the camera to swing around and follow. It’s your average NYC nightclub, if there is such a thing as an average NYC nightclub. But the intriguing part is the scrolling marquee underneath, which reads “The Original Players Club” in bright letters. That’s why the commentators bothered to come over. “He’s gotta be here. He probably thinks he’s being clever by going to a ‘players club’ to prepare for his match this week.” “I can’t believe he thinks this will actually help he and Shack.” “I can’t believe you let him loose in the first place.” “He’s a grown man.” “A very juvenile grown man.” “Point taken. But anyway, I figured Gates could watch him. They said they were doing promotional work.” “And you believe them?” “Well damn, I sure wasn’t going with them! If I hear one more thing about stupid tacos and Mexican pizzas and gorditas, I swear to God...” “But Jesus, man! You left El Spiffy! alone in the city with no one other than Bill Gates to keep him in line?” “Ok, in retrospect-“ “El Spiffy! and Bill Gates!” “I see your-“ “Alone in the city!” “I under-“ “Alone!” “OK, OK! I get it! So what if El Spiffy!’s leading some stupid escapade? I mean, it’s just two men! What kinds of damage could they do?” On cue, a large group of lavishly dressed clubgoers come piling out of the building, yelling and shouting obscenities. Not long behind them, a body flies out of the door and hits the pavement. The wife beater...the do rag....the leather pants...all look extraordinarily out of place on the pale white man lying on the sidewalk. The commentators stroll over to his side, the face instantly recognizable. “Mr. Gates, I presume?” The media mogul blinks painfully against the bright lights. A black eye is starting to form on his left side. He winces as the two men stare down at him. “...mommy....” The two men exchange a look, then reach down and hoist Gates to his feet. As he regains himself, a yell from inside the club catches their attention. “AAAAIIIIIIIYYYEEEEEEEEE!!!!!!!!!” Gates looks up and his eyes grow wide. Both commentators follow his gaze, then hastily step out of the way. Seconds later, El Spiffy!, clad only in his tights and mask, flies out of the club and collides with Gates, sending both down on the pavement. Once again, Julio and Juan Carlos step back in to survey the damage. Gates is in more pain than ever, now crushed under the weight of El Spiffy!. The luchadore, although more than slightly inebriated, appears to be just, well, spiffy. The two men attempt to lift El Spiffy! off of the injured Microsoft CEO. As they get him halfway up, a bouncer from in the club approaches, holding what looks like the clothes El Spiffy! used to be wearing over his ring gear. “Do these two belong to you?” the bouncer asks. The announcers look at each other, then drop El Spiffy!. An audible cry of pain can be heard from Gates offscreen. “Maybe. What’d they do?” Julio counters. The bouncer shoves the pile of clothing into Julio’s hands before he continues. “I caught the little white bitch making trouble with a couple of our regulars. From the looks of it, they took care of the problem, though.” Gates groans in pain and acknowledgement offscreen. “But the cat with the mask,” the bouncer goes on, “he’s had quite the night. First he claimed that it was ‘hot in there’ and he started taking his clothes off. Then he put my DJ through a Spanish announce table.” Juan Carlos tries to stifle a laugh. Julio looks confused. “You guys have a Spanish announce table?” “No. That’s the thing.” “Well, how-“ “He took a table from the bar, wrote ‘Spanish announce team’ on it, and made some of our Latino customers sit at it while he proceeded to DDT our house jockey through it.” Juan Carlos is nearly in hysterics. El Spiffy! gives a little chuckle at his own antics. Gates tries to chuckle but then moans and grabs his side. Julio and the bouncer look at them, then turn back to each other. “I took the liberty of footing the bill on the credit card that fell out of Manilla Ice’s pocket while he was getting jacked.” He fishes through his pockets before pulling out a silver card and presenting it to Julio. “Everything’s cool for now. But needless to say we do not expect to see these two around these parts again.” “Neither do we,” Julio replies. He thanks the bouncer as Juan Carlos tries to pick up the troublemakers. But between Gates’ near-paralyzation and El Spiffy!’s drunken stupor, it’s not an easy task. Julio takes a look, then gazes down the street thoughtfully. “How long is the walk to the hotel?” “About ten blocks.” “Eh, not too bad.” With that, each commentator takes one of the charges and slings him over their shoulder in a fireman’s carry. They get their balance, and then head down the street into the night, as El Spiffy! breaks out into a horrible rendition of “New York, New York” to bring us home...
