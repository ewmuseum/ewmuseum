Tale of a Fruit Robber (part 4)

9 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9th October 2002 The soil had deep, trench like, grooves embedded in them. They were far from organised, they covered in a rough and wiggly stream of chaos. The trees had been uprooted, the remnants left behind a strange mixture of broken branches and roots all caked in the damper soil from below. Even the warm Florida sun couldn’t make this scene any more pleasant than it was, confusion and mystification was all it had to offer. It was only a small grove, but the effort it would take to get every single one of the trees dug up and out through the gates, in one night, does not seem to be held by common mischief-makers and bad doers. As Randy and George, the owners of this independent company, found no clues but the large piece of graffiti, spray-painted in yellow, on the brick wall beside the main entranceway. It was a curved line, fatter in the middle than at the edges with blunted ends. “What d’ya think that is, Randy?” George asked, peering over his round spectacles. This was never the real question, and it never got answered. As they saw their empty grove, and their empty wallets, they couldn’t do anything more than stare at the sky until late in the afternoon. Randy and George’s orange business had hit a brand new low, and they still didn’t know why. * The tall, dark skinned man clumsily stumbled into the lobby. He was wearing baggy jeans and thick layers of sweaters and coats. His hand held tightly at altering points of his body, round lumps seemed to cover him entirely. This alone was more than enough reason to stop and stare, but as Joe Bananas dragged the large, yellow stained sack along the perfectly polished floor there was the opportunity for a few jaws to drop on their hinges too. He didn’t seem to care, a quality many find out of reach, and walked by without a second thought. One middle-aged woman, a caring and charitable lady, even tried to help what she believed to be a disabled person. Joe flung her from his arm, however, and continued on his trek up the staircase. Nobody seemed to suspect the name printed on the sack, ‘Randy & George’s Orange Grove.’ After fumbling with his keys, unlocking the door and practically falling into the room with exhaustion, Joe lay down his fourth load of squashed and stolen oranges. He helped them out of his clothes and sack with gentleness and care. He stepped over to a small table, using the minimal light to search around with hovering hands. He finally grasped onto a folded piece of paper, pointing his finger as form of conformation to himself. “Y’all all be free tonight, bwoys. Don’ ya worry bout dat.” Joe said, his neck tilting around to air his voice to the entire audience. Joe smiled, his eyes seeing nothing more than the happiness and celebration of his companions. Smiling releases a great amount of stress relieving chemicals in your body, relaxing you and setting off a chain reaction of mild pleasure. Well, that’s what they said on the TV. Joe knew by now that the box of lies was not something he should pay attention to. He had greater things to worry about, thus ending the ‘chain reaction’ his smile may have caused. Moving to a neatly folded pile of clothes, he picked out a predominantly black ensemble and began to change. As began to unbutton his loud Hawaiian shirt, he turned his neck around to face the orange crowd. “Do ya mind?” Joe asked. As soon as he was satisfied they were no longer looking, Joe continued preparing himself for the night ahead. * Joe hauled the massive wooden box from the rental car, the assistance of two other men making the work no less difficult. He couldn’t help but shed a tear as it was taken away towards the airplane. “Wait!” Joe wailed, running forward to catch up. Joe peered through the cracks in the wood, looking down at the many orange faces inside. “Ah jus wanna wish ya good luck, an be safe on dis trip.” Joe said through his best effort at a sugarcoated voice. “Is there an animal in that?” Asked the man given the task to push the metal contraption that allowed it to be moved. “No!” Joe retorted condescendingly, “It’s candy… candy for the starving pickneys of Canada.” “Fair enough.” Replied the man, “We’ll have it there in no time.” “Good, they shall find a new freedom in a new country.” Joe said, half to himself. “Huh?” The man squeezed out of his mouth, his forehead wrinkled. “Jus push da dyamn t’ing!” Joe screeched. He complied, and as it went off into the distance towards its’ flight to freedom up north Joe could not help but wave his hand furiously. He had not so emotional since his encounter with the Raspberry refugees. A single tear, rolling slowly like syrup down his cheek, was highlighted in the silvery glow of the moon.
