Deprivation, Failure, Defeat

12 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 12th, 2002 A.D. The curtains open up just behind the stage of RUSH Wrestling’s Ignition Pay-Per-View. Electrical cords and wiring snake their way up and down the grey cement. People rush past, yelling orders into their headsets queuing for the arrival of another wrestler; ready to make his appearance on the first ever pay-per-view in the RUSH Company. Technical Technicians hurry past, running toward their position for lighting and Pyro techniques. Security guards keep the fans back away from the backstage area where the men and women work. The black curtain – a barrier between the backstage and the arena itself – ruffles a few times as another technician scurries past. Then we see.... Chris Matthews. His head hung low toward the ground, his face cupped over by his face in what appears to be disgrace and shame. A water bottle sits on a steel trundle box next to him; three quarters of its contents seemed to evaporate. He sighs long and loudly, his hands still buried over his face. “I lost.” His voice is hoarse and raspy, he continues to breath hard and heavy through his sweaty hands from this previous and incredibly physical match. “I lost.” Matthews speaks again, his voice dry and in need of hydration. Blonde bangs hang down from his head, draping down over his forehead. Matthews swallows some saliva down this throat, the adam’s apple that sits in his throat bobs up and down as the saliva trickles down his throat and into his empty stomach. A technician walks past Chris; shouting orders toward one of his many handy and well trained handy men backstage to move a large crate. “I’m worthless...” His hands glisten with sweat as he slides off the trundle box next to him, Matthews picks up the towel that was next to him. He slowly starts to walk down the short steel ramp that’s seats the place known as the Gorilla Position, his boots clicking on the steel. A technician walks up to Matthews, as if he’s asking a question to the young Quebecan. “Mr. Matthews” he starts to say to the solemn Matthews. “What seems to be the problem?” Matthews continues walking, his eyes barely even open. The technician tries to keep up with the fast and furious strides of Matthews, but to no avail. Suddenly Matthews stops and turns around; his eyes filled with sorrow for his own failure. The technician begins to grow a little uneasy at Matthews’s stares, until the words leave his mouth. “Like you even f^cking care..” Matthews spits out turning his back. “Go back and do your job and leave me the f^ck alone.” The technician goes bright red in the face, embarrassed at being told what to do by someone without a name tag labelling him as a superior. “Oh..” he replies. “Okay.” Matthews turns his back fully and slowly walks down a flight of stairs, his black leather wrestling boots clicking down on the steel steps. He walks down the empty and echo filled hallways, his walk all screwed like a drunken man’s. Matthews head’s down a corner before stopping and looking up at the ceiling, as if he was looking for god. “A lot of help you were..” spits out Matthews at the ceiling. He starts walking down the hallway again, boots echoing down the hallways.
