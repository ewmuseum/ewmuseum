Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

RUSH Wrestling was a website federation of the WrestlingTattler and Primetime Central family, staged as weekly arena television and billed on its first night as Thursday Night Adrenaline. Its own newsdesk called the run its second era: an earlier RUSH left only roleplays bylined as far back as 8 February 2002 and talk of “old RUSH days”, and its surviving cards open with Adrenaline 1 from the United Center in Chicago on 2 May 2002.

Twenty-two cards survive: twenty numbered Adrenaline shows, from Chicago to Miami by way of Madison Square Garden, the Fleet Center, the Air Canada Centre and the Georgia Dome, and two pay-per-views, Ignition on 16 June and Crossroads on 18 August. Each is staged as television, with a two-man announce team shouting through every match, a ring announcer calling the entrants, songs named by title and artist, recaps of the week before and backstage scenes running between the bouts.

Its three championships, the World, Syndicate and Network, each began vacant and carry nine reigns between them. Crossroads moved all three belts in one night, and the World Championship was vacated on 18 October 2002, at an Adrenaline 21 that its own title history records and no card of the surviving run reaches.

Those twenty-two cards stop on 11 October, but the run did not. Its newsdesk, which runs to 24 November, names five more, and three of them survive from web-archive captures of its own site: Rising From the Ashes, a third pay-per-view, whole as a script from the Ocean Center in Daytona Beach on 13 October; the six-month anniversary show built around the first Golden Boob Awards, announced for 1 November, as the script of its awards ceremony; and Adrenaline 23, billed for 8 November at the Rupp Arena in Lexington, as a preview card with no result. Adrenaline 21 and 22 survive only as news recaps, and the result of Adrenaline 23 not at all. Two roster cards carry results from those awards, a first winner of the Platinum Sports Bra Award and a runner-up for Best Heel of the Semester; the script confirms the first and does not name the second.

On 24 November 2002 its newsdesk announced the federation sold to the president of a rival promotion, as an early Christmas present, after RUSH’s wrestlers helped him to a tainted win at that promotion’s Alliances card. The sale was not an ending: the buyer’s own cards go on billing RUSH, and one claims a RUSH World Championship reign from 22 December 2002. What survives is its own: the twenty-five cards, 252 roleplays, sixteen news posts, its biography cards and the reigns of its three championships.

Affiliations

Primetime Championship Wrestling (PCW, Primetime, 2000–2004)

Acquired RUSH (2002)
Matt Elia’s PCW bio quotes PCW President Xavier Kannon outright: he “bought RUSH outright... his goal isn’t to show that he has it to win anything, but he has it to help regain RUSH’S freedom as it was stolen right from underneath everyone’s nose.” RUSH is the single most-mentioned outside promotion in PCW’s own corpus, with its CEO named as “Day Johnson.” Whether this reflects a storyline buyout or a real cross-site business arrangement isn’t clear from the text alone.

Primetime Central (PTC)

Member of RUSH (2002) (2002)
Primetime Central's own wiki article on RUSH opens “RUSH Wrestling was a federation that was part of the former PTC”, and its navigation box lists RUSH among Primetime Central's past federations (captures of 7 May 2008 and 19 August 2010); RUSH's own Rules page states the same family in its own words, “RUSH Wrestling is a member of the WrestlingTattler/Primetime Central family”. Against that, PTC's own FAQ of 3 June 2002 answers whether PCW, OSW, NTICW, RUSH, XWA or PWA will be part of PTC with “for the most part the answer is NO”, keeping them out of its Channels and rankings, so the membership is the looser one the wiki and RUSH's own page describe, not a place in the network's formal tier.

Shared ownership

Pro Wrestling Circuit (PWC, Pro Wrestling Circuit, 2003) (2003)
A genuine out-of-fiction copyright notice on PWC's own Rules page, separate from any in-fiction alliance storyline, names one real-world operator group behind PWC and a dozen other federations and sites, among them RUSH. These were sister sites under one owner; the operator isn't named here.
