Ricky Starr Biography Real Name [name withheld] Nicknames 'Natural Born Talent' Hometown Bosnia, currently residing in Cameron, NC Birthday 12/1/79 Height 6'0' Weight 201 Biography Remus Slatkovic was born in a derelict concrete hovel right at the dead centre of destabilised Bosnia. He grew up in an intact family, a rarity in Bosnia with his former labourer father, a mother, three elder sisters and a younger brother who came two years later. If one could sum up his early life in a catchphrase, it would be “anything but roses and candy”. His world was far from perfection then but what can one expect in a nation seething in racial and ethnic hatred that has its roots way back in the First World War. However, as broken and imperfect as the everything around Remus seemed, his family still believed in a proper and thorough upbringing for Remus and his siblings. They sent him to an underground school run by a former Hungarian University professor. They paid in currency, food, livestock, or whatever of value they could earn, all in the name of a halfway decent education. As much as the professor proved himself an adept teacher, Remus proved himself a more than adept student, throwing himself with an academic fervour into the subjects of Mathematics, History, World History, Geography, the Arts, Literature, World Literature and last but not least, the all-important global language of English, one that the professor places heavy emphasis on. His efforts and quickness pleased the professor. As the years went by, Remus proved himself brighter than the professor’s estimation, up to the point where the professor literally begged his parents to discontinue their payments for his education, insisting that he was now on a “scholarship”. It was when he was in the middle of his secondary level education when he learned about the nation halfway across the world known as the United States of America. Everytime when the professor talked or taught about America, he always spoke in a fervour never reserved for any other subject, topic, or field. He would quote the constitution on many occasions, the tone of his voice-excited everytime he talked about such rights as freedom of speech, unheard of in a place like Bosnia. “But most importantly” as the professor always used to say “everybody is given a chance”. Remus would always listen, wide-eyed and amazed at the wonders of such a nation. One where everybody could voice their opinion without fear of being taken away by the “red-caps”, a country where everybody is given a fair chance to make use of their life, a Utopia compared to where he was now. All of these planted a seed in the mind of Remus Slatkovic in the form of a dream, a dream that one day he would step foot in the land of “the brave and the free”, and to live out his life there. His parents had lost hope a long time ago, his elder sisters were too cynical to care and his younger brother, too young to understand or comprehend the magnitude of that dream. His peers laughed at him for that dream, for either they were too ignorant to believe in such a Utopia, or they were far too realistic to believe it was possible for the likes of them to reach such a paradise. Despite these factors against him, Remus held on to it stronger than ever, thinking with each passing day “I will make it”. On a cloudy day, just two months after his 15th birthday, the red-capped Serbian troops marched into town. The Bosnian War erupted three years ago and this was the pinnacle of it. The Serbians had already more than their fair share of massacres on their hands. NATO had already hit them hard with airborne bombings but the mass killings were far from stopped. Remus and his family tried to flee but seeing many attempting that being mowed down by sub-machine guns and automatic rifles, they decided it was best to stay, although many years later, Remus doubted the validity of that decision. First, they rounded up the men, placing upon them accusations of collaborating with partisans, many tried to explain that they were innocent but the troops would have none of it. They were gunned down in lines just like that, in a cold, swift, noisy movement, they were reduced to dead flesh, Remus’ father among them. His elder sisters were taken from the household screaming, Remus never saw them again, nor did he ever hear from them again. Remus, his mother, and his brother were the only ones left in the wake of the military occupation. One night, when Remus and whatever remained of his family were sleeping in their shelter, there was a massive gunfight skirmish right outside their shelter. Remus, being the most alert among them, woke up to the blasts of sputtering bullets, the blood curdling screams of falling men and the deafening explosions of thrown handheld grenades. Remus ran out to find a retreating redcap army. Much to his horror, he found some retreating troops torching civilian houses as they ran, his house being one of them. Remus ran back in to get his sleeping brother, which he carried out and set down a fair distance from the shelter. As Remus ran frantically back to get his mother, a sudden explosion sent him flying back, crumpling on the ground with an ugly thud. He looked up in horror to see his shelter and the adjacent ones decimated by the explosion. It seemed that a redcap had blindly discharged a rocket-launcher with the hope of hitting their enemy as they retreated. Remus watched the burning ruins, feeling helpless and powerless over the horrid turn of events. As he kneeled on the ground watching, the town turn into a funeral pyre… For the firs time in what seemed to be an eternity, he broke down and cried. At the next dawn break, Remus found himself walking alongside partisan troops, their shabby uniforms hanging from their body like rags, rifles slung across their shoulders with a rich array of cartridges visible through the straps. By noontime, Remus and his brother found themselves in a relief shelter of some sort. Remus was sat down underneath a tent with his brother. He saw partisan soldiers talking among themselves and taking supplies off trucks. He saw Red Cross nurses running around, checking on bed-ridden individuals, some with conditions so gruesome they could not be put into words. At one point, a shadow was cast over Remus, a tall shadow bearing the outline of a well-built individual. Remus looked up instinctively and what he saw would re-inflame, re-spark and re-invigorate the dream that Remus had clung to…. It was a smiling American soldier. Remus stared open-mouthed in awe at the stature of the soldier, his weapon slung on his back, gleaming with a polished-like perfection, his smile wide and his teeth as white as the finest of pearls, his uniform was set in place right down to the boots with all their straps fastened and done up. He wore the American insignia proud on a shoulder along with his denim nametag sewn and stitched onto his uniform. His dog tag gleamed in the sun along with his smile. The soldier kneeled down to face Remus at an eye level. The soldier extended a firm hand out. “Hello there, nice to meet you” Remus, recognising this custom, extended out a smaller hand to meet his. He found the soldier’s grip to be firm, strong but not too overbearing. “Nice to meet you too” The soldier looked at Remus in a gesture of surprise and relief thrown into one “Ah, so you speak English too” Remus seemed proud of himself now but caught himself before he went too far “Yes, I learned it” The soldier looked at Remus with a smile, a smile that captured the very essence of America, the land of the brave and free Remus thought. “You hungry there?” Remus replied, ”I have not eaten for two days” It showed in his weakened stature. The soldier dug into his pockets and pulled out a Hershey chocolate bar. He gave it to Remus who stared at like and held it as if it were gold. The soldier stood back up onto his feet, his smile as pleasant as ever. “Enjoy it kiddo” The soldier walked off as Remus saw him off until he disappeared into a tent, as if he were in a good dream and did not want it end. Remus then remembered his hunger and tore open the chocolate packet. He broke the chocolate bar in half and handed the other half to his brother. He bit hungrily into his chocolate and woofed it down like a hungry animal. He looked hard at the packet as if imagining something would emerge out of it. He read the fine print with the words “Made in America” printed in small yet legible bold print. “America” He softly whispered from his lips Ricky Starr was standing underneath the Statue of Liberty, the aged green monument that became a symbol of America. He puts a hand to his chest and looking up, he whispered. “God Bless America” He never been prouder to be an American, one could say there was a tear in his eye at the moment.
