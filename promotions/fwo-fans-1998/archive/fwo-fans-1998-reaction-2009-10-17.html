unstable infrastructure Huddled somewhere in a darkened booth of a dive bar in downtown Charlotte, Krow sat idly staring at his beer and then the clock on the wall. It’d been seven days since Krow shocked the fWo universe with his violent expulsion of Josh Listerin, better known as Paladin. One Common Goal was fully disbanded, but Krow’s one common goal burned brighter in his mind than ever. It’d been several months since a mysterious man with important ties to the fWo had approached him outside a hotel, while on the Legends tour. Krow didn’t particularly care for the man’s personality, but he’d proven valuable with information and constructive feedback in regards to Krow’s desire to destroy the fWo. Krow learned that this man’s vision was closer to his own, more so than Shadows or Listerin. The man was exiled, excommunicated and in a fitting finality – brutally maimed by his own friends on live television... As Krow pondered his next steps, a man with a UNC hooded sweatshirt, matching cap and Ray-Ban sunglasses sat his in booth with a Bud Light Lime in his hand. His beard was growing, the hood over his head hid the tousled and unkempt locks of hair. “Took you long enough, nice hat.” Krow grumbled. “Shutup fucknutter, if anyone in this state recognizes me right now, my whole cover is blown.” The man spoke emphatically in a whisper as Krow sneered, the man smirked in response to Krow’s anger. “Sorry, I forgot you hate the word blown.” Krow waved the fleeting insult off with a shake of his head. “You asked to see me, then show up a half-hour late. What do you want?” Krow spoke quietly and quite to the point. “You finally took my advice on Listerin…” the man was still smirking. “No, I weighed the usefulness of someone as weak as him and realized that your connections could be stronger…” Krow’s eyes rose to meet the sunglasses of the man, “…and even though you don’t show it too well, you’re far more intelligent than they were. I’ve done my research on you and while I almost gave up complete hope on humanity and evolution, for whatever reason...you’re good at this.” Krow leaned forward, pushing his beer that he may or may not have sipped to the side of the table. His eyes glared down hard at the mysterious man, “Why did you call? I’m not your friend, I’m not your partner. I don’t want to be seen with you unless it’s for good reason. Profitable reason.” The mysterious man didn’t move for a moment, his smirk widening as his head nodded. Finally, he leaned back as his arms stretched out on the backseat of the booth, the bottled beer still in his right hand. “When you see a loose thread on a sweater, do you just cut it off? Or do you like to pull slowly on it, watching to see exactly how it unravels?” Krow leaned back with a grunt, “I’m not in the mood for you're inane blabbering, tonight…” “Maybe that’s why you’re clueless to this place’s history and infrastructure. Maybe that’s why…you don’t want to put up with me, you want to treat me like shit and yet…” The mysterious man took slow swig on his beer, “…you’re sitting her and putting up with my shit. I’m the one man that after you’ve done your research, well you’re not surprised that I know that the best fight that you and Steven Shadows ever had was before the fWo came back.” Krow’s eyes widened for a moment, then squinted in curiosity. The mysterious man in the shadows of the booth simply took another swig and then leaned casually forward, his free hand’s index finger twirling around in a circle. “You’ve entered this universe, this promotion…this place where horrible things are simply swept under a rug or by the years of time itself. The fWo’s foundation was built on simple things,” the man smiled, “Greed. Betrayal. Murder.” Krow nodded, “I know that. The entire world knows that. They've all picked up a newspaper and read it.” The man laughed, “The news? The news only reported what was caught on camera. Men like Fallen or Neo are not the foundation, they’re just a lit match on the gasoline trying to fool the insurance companies. That’s the point of pulling on the string slowly and watching where it goes…knowing the foundation built by Dudley and Beaven spawned worse, worse intentioned millionaires. But the fWo’s walls are different… as we see them at least. Ironically, the walls are held up by guys like Strangler, Frontier, Alias…” “Chronos.” Krow said bluntly. The man’s smile faded quickly, his nose twitched in anger. “No. Chronos is a parasite. Just like Zimmerman, Frenchie and Jacobs. The walls are held up by fools that never checked the foundation…that protect it blindly.” Krow soaked in the words, seeing the bigger picture of a house that was their intention to destroy. He grabbed his beer, turning it slightly and finally taking a deep chug. After finishing, his expressionless face found itself staring back at him in the man’s sunglasses. “What is it?” Krow asked curiously. The man’s smile finally returned, “Gas the parasites, finish the walls you’ve been tearing down already, grab the title to the house, light the match and watch it blow up from the inside.” “Light the match?” Krow’s head cocked. The man chuckled, “The freak in the mask came after me, which means I look at my laws of 3. Those I fucked over, those I fucked up and those I just fucked. Naturally, I’ve narrowed it down to fucked over and fucked up.” Krow retorted, “It could just be the gasoline, which we can use to our advantage.” “Maybe, but not likely.” the mysterious man nodded in disapproval, “The gasoline dried up on the walls as far as I’m concerned. I think a parasite is playing with fire near the tank…and since we’re dealing with the 230 pound versions, I’ve brought us something to use tonight. It’s far more efficient than gas.” The mysterious man stood up and lifted up his sweatshirt, revealing a maple handle sticking out from his jeans. Krow, for the first time all evening, smiled and shook his head in a laugh. “What?” “For a moment, I thought that was a sledgehammer and I think I’ll just stop there,” Krow laughed again, taking a sip out of his beer. “Yeah, maybe on Halloween. For now, for reasons I’ll explain later…we’re going to poke at the parasite’s nest and see if it brings up anything personal.” “Personal?” “Well, if this isn’t personal...then maybe there is more gasoline.” Krow smiled. "If that's the case...I'll light the match."
