Introduction This is the way the world ends... this is the way the world ends... Not with a bang... But with a whimper. 14:02:30... Silver Fox walked into the conference room and blinked. He had expected to see the board here, as they always were, seated around the long table and reviewing the latest numbers and forms... like they always did. But instead the room was empty, save a long envelope on the table and... "Mr. Tort. How are you?" Tort nodded curtly to Silver Fox and gestured toward a chair opposite him. Silver Fox sat, and a feeling of foreboding washed over him. Whatever caused this change in procedure could not be a good sign. Had Tort bought out the rest of the board? If so, how long before he drove the entire promotion into the ground? As if reading Fox's thoughts, Tort smiled his old and nasty smile. "Confused as to where the rest of the group is are you? Well, no matter. Gerald... bring Mr. Fox some coffee, won't you?" A large black man came into the room and glared down at Mr. Tort. "For the last time you old fool, it's Raymond. Not Gerald. Raymond." Mr. Tort sighed and waved the man away. "Good help, as I'm sure you don't understand, is so hard to find. Mr. Fox, I have many important business matters to attend to, and thus I shall make this quick. In front of you is an envelope containing the appropriate legal documents which shall place yourself in full control of the wrestling promotion in question. These documents have been properly notarized and are ready to go, but you will need to have several copies signed and delivered to the offices of Disney Entertainment." Silver Fox's mouth dropped open, but Mr. Tort continued as though Fox's reaction was of no importance to him. "The authority of the Board of Directors has been dissolved pending the final sale of our holdings to the Walt Disney Entertainment Corporation. At close of market tomorrow this entity shall be the majority shareholder in your promotion and as a publically traded company will work with you to authorize a new Board and whatever trustee authority they deem necessary. However, as part of our sale we have insisted... or rather, Mr. Humbold insisted as I could care less about the runnings of your little wrestling show... that final authority into the promotion will be held by yourself and yourself alone. If, at some point, you determine that you wish to retire from the wretched business, you and you alone will be allowed to name your successor. In the event of your death, your successor will be apointed based on the recommendation you place on the forms you must sign between now and end of market trading tomorrow. Are you following all of this?" Silver Fox's eyes were still wide, and he was looking at the envelope on the table with nothing short of shock. "I... the promotion is mine?" "No, idiot," said Tort, sighing, "the promotion is the property of the Walt Disney Company. You my dim-witted fairy, are simply in charge of it." "But," Silver Fox said, looking up from the envelope for the first time and into Mr. Tort's dark eyes, "why have you all sold? The promotion is doing wonderfully... our stock has never been higher." Mr. Tort sighed, and stood up with no small amount of effort. He glared at Raymond, and then back at Silver Fox. "Young man, what you may not understand is that in business the correct response is to attempt to make money. We invested in the Fans Wrestling Organization because we believed that the tools were in place to grow our investment. This occured, thanks to a number of factors coming into place. The Fans Wrestling Organization outdraws every other national wrestling promotion in the United States, and thanks to our partnership with the Disney Corporation, it runs slimmer and more efficent than Vince McMahon's little empire. While the WWE brings in more total funds, the fWo currently has a better profit margin, which has been reflected in the earnings and the stock. When the stock rises, you sell. We, young man, have sold. Disney has bought, beliving that their investment can grow further. This is largely insanity, as wrestling is built on a popular culture bubble that is nearly ready to burst. Thus, rather than ride our investment down into the gutter, we have sold. The result is that I will no longer have the plesant task of interacting with you. Now, I say to you, for what I dearly hope will be the final time, good day." Mr. Tort walked from the room, leaving Silver Fox sitting alone and once again looking down at the envelope in front of him. A small smile crept onto his face. They had done it. All of their hard work had finally paid off. They were in control of their own destiny, and thanks to Humbold's generosity, no longer would they be subjected to the whims of a board of directors.
