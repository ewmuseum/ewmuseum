VII Years Bad Luck Calamity hopped on one foot into the offices of Jim Dudley, his view obstructed by the white t-shirt he was hurriedly pulling over his head. He was still just getting dressed; after a wild night of celebrating, Pedro Zavala had slept all day and had woke up not too long before arriving at the arena. His head finally popping through the hole at the top, his hair was messed about and his scattered and numerous eye veins were redder than cherries. Dudley cleared his throat, looking at his watch, but Calamity remained resolute and collapsed in a leather-upholstered chair, rubbing the eyes beneath his thick-rimmed glasses. Jim Dudley didn't say a word, just slid the documents across the desk to fWo's newest superstar. Calamity took one glance at the paperwork and immediately nursed his temples with his fingers. He had a pounding headache already; the 10-point font War in Peace contract did not help matters. "Basically it says that you are signing a seven year contract." Dudley slowly stated, saving the gradually-sobering wrestler. "We don't usually have such expansive contracts. You should feel lucky." After destroying a mirror a few days earlier, this was not a good sign for Calamity. "Usually we'll just have a girl fill out your W2's and other tax documents." A low groan escaped Calamity's lips. Just the thought of looking at a typed page, in his condition, nonplussed Calamity greatly. "...But she's sick. So here's a pen. There's only about 10 sheets. If you have any questions, keep them to yourself. I sure can't make anything out of it. Do you want some coffee?" "Not real--" "Sure you do," Dudley said as he stood up and poured a few cups. "You're going to be wrestling soon." "That sounds great, I can't wait to get started." "Good. Your career begins tonight." "What?! You know I'm in no shape to--" he said before trailing. He wasn't trying to make waves so early, so he consoled his emotional Mother Nature for a minute and continued, "Well who am I paired with?" "Eddie Cheno." PPPBBBFFFFT! "You know him?" Dudley asked his wide-eyed employee. He didn't appreciate the coffee spray on his desktop, so he insinuated a clean up by tossing a few napkins Calamity's way. "Yeah, I tried out for Asylum a few years ago. Him and I smoked a few bowls." Dudley cleared his throat and sat back down. "Well then you two will probably have a friendly reunion later," Dudley replied with a sneer. "Not really," Calamity said while taking hold of the pen, "Asshole only let me have a few tokes........DAMNIT!" "What is it?" Dudley asked, cocking an eyebrow at the sudden outburst. "Sorry, your pen is out of ink," Calamity shot back plainly, though he appeared to be unraveling, "I'm probably just overreacting, but I seem to be having the WORST luck lately." "Probably just a coincidence," Dudley remarked with a brief chortle, sliding another pen his way. "Probably," Calamity mocked softly. The parroted word didn't catch Dudley's ear. Calamity placed pen to paper, but just as he did the lights in the room flickered and turned off. The black room enveloped the two, and one could hear Calamity mutter: "Can I fill these out later?" "Sure," Dudley replied, his voice weakening in disbelief. "Is it just me, or is it raining in here?!" Laughter, and some surprising cheers from that comment. Ivy did not appear to notice. "Boo him because it's the cool thing to do is the same as booing him because everyone else is doing it. It's herd behavior at its worst, it's one of the reasons that professional wrestling is considered the scum of the entertainment industry," she said as she stopped in the middle of the ring, "I know better. I've met thousands of wrestling fans in the past decade and nearly all of you have been intelligent, rationally-minded people. So what the ****'s up with the current trend of booing a man for stickin' to his principles?" She laughed. "Or is it because of those icky little morals that he sticks to? You shallow, shallow, moth*******rs. Today, this man is being persecuted for publicly speaking about his beliefs while forcing them on nobody, tomorrow YOU'RE gonna be persecuted for privately talking about your beliefs in your home or your friends' home. Why? Because in both situations, the beliefs were not considered 'popular'. Let one go, and the floodgates're free t'open." Ivy stepped out of the ring, still holding the microphone. Several security guards were waiting at the entranceway to the arena. These were real Arena security, not the ones that typically run out to break up brawls in the ring. "You guys are better than that," she implored the fans, "you always have been... But if y'can't see what's in front of your faces, than the hell with all of you." Step toward the back, but she stopped as another thought apparently hit her. "AND," she said, "if you simply refuse t'see what's in front of your faces... then again I say... **** you." The credits for the show had already rolled, so the feed was simply cut. But before Seinfeld returned to America's television screens, the beginning of something in the arena could be heard. For just a fraction of a second... ...Applause.
