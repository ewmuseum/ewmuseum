Introduction At the fWo Pay Per View event, Meltdown, great plans and plots were revealed... as Frank Smythe, Brian Cage and Stephen Roberts formed on live television the offshoot organization OCW from the ashes of the Survivor IV contest. This new promotion, determined to upstage the fWo and show the wrestling world what professional wrestling really meant, took the first stab at the fWo's credibility and honor... wounding it and humiliating the fWo's corporate owners, the Disney Corporation. As bad, the prophetic leader Silver Fox sold his interest in the promotion to the Disney Corporation, ensuring that the fWo would remain in Disney clutches for some time to come. But was it actually Silver Fox who made the sale... or a long-lost enemy of the promotion seeking some unknown revenge? Also at Meltdown, Craig Miles returned with an agenda that threatens to turn the promotion on its ear and forever change the careful balance of power. Now, tonight, Thomas Murphy is set to address his promotion for the first time as the undisputed owner, and perhaps lay the groundwork for the revenge to come... The way things are "I want to be clear, so I encourage all of you to listen carefully... and understand that I will not be answering any questions. Questions breed misunderstanding, and this is one topic that I want clearly understood." Thomas Murphy looked at the ring where the fWo roster stood. Most of them anyway, and those that were not in attendance were being carefully noted by Simon and his PDA. Next to Simon was Ted Tallison, his face dark and expression unrevealing. The arena was, as yet, empty, but in a few hours it would be filled with fans. More fans, in fact, than usual, and Murphy was all-to-aware as to why that was. Thomas Murphy was an older man, but still younger than fifty and still set to climb higher in the corporate ladder. He had no illusions... he was an honorary member of the Disney Corporation Board of Directors, and he was on close terms with Roy Disney. But he would never be where Eisner was, and this fact caused only the slightest amount of bitterness within him. But he was a master of his domain, and he was able to move mountains if he wished them moved. Under his care was the entertainment and sports branches of the corporation. On most days this meant a calm, albeit empire building position. The Fans Wrestling Organization was meant to expand his empire, not make it a joke. Ten days ago, three men had done just that. Brian Cage, Frank Smythe and Stephen Roberts had conspired against him, had taken advantage of his quiet generosity, and had spat in the face of what he had brought to them all. Corporate power, financial and professional security... forever. Under the Disney umbrella you were forever free from rain... as long as you appreciated the hand that held the handle. Cage, Smythe, and Roberts had not appreciated the hand, and now changes were in order. Through his thin eyes, Murphy surveyed the men before him. Small men, with small dreams. None would run a company. None were capable of the responsibility he wielded on a daily... no, hourly basis. These men were tools, tools to make money for the corporation, and in light of the recent deception... tools for revenge. "Frank Smythe, Brian Cage, Stephen Roberts, Kellen Kinkade and Kristen Jacobs have been fired from the organization. They are not allowed on the premises, and if they are caught on grounds they will be arrested for trespassing. Anyone assisting these men in attempts to gain access to fWo grounds or fWo intellectual property will be fired and served with legal action. Anyone mentioning this event, or other events which paint this company in a poor light as it relates to any 'OCW', will be fined and suspended. Any attempt at using company resources for the gain of an outside organization will be served with legal action. Any contact with members of an 'OCW' while on company time will result in a fine and suspension from the organization." Murphy stopped, ignoring the frowns, dark glares, and muttering from the roster. They would understand the new world order, or they would suffer the consequences of disobedience. "Further, while there are penalties for disruptive action, so too shall there be benefits of positive action toward this company. We are doubling the budget of the promotion, and will resume active touring of the country. I have also determined that the new commissioner of this organization should be someone more familiar with wrestling, and not another corporate agent without a working knowledge of the industry. We are in final negotiations with this individual, and they will be joining us next week in our final show held in the Orlando sports complex." Murphy set his face into what he assumed was a fatherly expression of kindness, but looked far more like a leer. "Help us build a strong, successful product and you will go far. Work against this company, and you will live to regret it. For while other, shoddy organizations may make idle threats of violence, I pledge that you will not be physically harmed... but you will be buried under legal and financial debt for the remainder of your pathetic, hopeless life. Such as it will be." Murphy cleared his throat and gestured toward Simon. "I trust I have made myself clear... Simon will be later distributing paperwork which will entail your new non-disclosure agreements and non-compete clauses. Sign them if you wish to appear on television." Thomas Murphy turned, ignoring the rumbling behind him, and headed back up the ramp, out of the building, and into the pleasant confines of his Audi. But it would take some time, weeks or months most likely, to erase this particular blemish from his life. Cage, Smythe and the others had taken him by surprise... well, one for them. But there would not be another. Not without severe reprisals... and if OCW believed they would be successful... well... Thomas Murphy smiled. Goliath was long overdue to smash David. Turned Away Hours before Ruahh took to the air, Mitch Wilson stood in the will call area outside of the arena, standing in the "W" line. A light rain fell from the gloomy sky, counterpointed every little while by the grumble of thunder. The rain fell on Wilson's head and soaked his shirt to his skin. It felt cold at first, and Wilson didn't mind the shock that came each time it hit his skin - in fact, he welcomed it. Before long, however, the feeling would fade away in the Florida warmth. Even when it rained here, it was warm. Wilson was making no effort to disguise himself, but despite that fact, no fans approached him. Some pointed, some stared, but none came close. It would be hard for any of them to say exactly why they stayed away instead of asking for an autograph, or shooting the breeze, but it all came down to the same thing. They knew that Wilson was a dangerous man, and pain and frustration were pulsing out of him in almost tangible waves. And after all, pain plus frustration plus danger equals aggression. It was strange enough that he was out here at all - shouldn't he be backstage with the rest of the wrestlers? Only those hardcore "smart" fans on the internet would know that his contract had expired at the end of April, and that his presence at the end of Meltdown wasn't part of an elaborate angle. These "smart" fans would also know that he had recently undergone a risky, dangerous new kind of neck surgery as a result of the backdrop driver Primetime had given him at CyberSlam, one that had been improperly executed and had almost broken his neck. What they didn't know that Wilson did, was that it had been properly executed. No doubt about it - it was one hundred and twelve percent intentional. Slugger, Death, whoever had been trying to have him taken out permanently. He had failed. Now that he was back on his feet, he intended to make Slugger pay as steep a price as possible for as long as he possibly could. Not only had Slugger attempted to take him out, he had taken out Wilson's father. The wrestling legend Slade Wilson still lay comatose in a hospital bed, a vegetable shot through with tubes to keep him alive. If Mitch Wilson had anything to say about, Slugger would experience every inch of the hell his father was experiencing - and more. He stepped up to the ticket counter. The woman behind the glass looked at him for a moment, eyes wide, unable to help herself. The man standing before her was an apparition, with closely cropped dark hair, a beard of black stubble covering his face, and the sunken eye sockets of a skull. She gathered herself with an effort. "Name, please?" "Wilson. Mitch Wilson." She pressed a few buttons on a keyboard, then frowned at something that came up on the screen. "Sir, if you could just wait here a moment..." He waited. Less than a minute later, three extremely large men in blue uniforms had surrounded him. "Mr. Wilson?" The burliest of the group, a huge man with a shaved head and goatee, addressed him. "Yeah?" "We've been told that we are not to allow you to enter the arena tonight. If you do, you *will* be arrested, and you *will* be prosecuted to the fullest capabilities of the Walt Disney Corporation. In other words, you'll be ass-deep in lawyers before you can wink, and before you can blink, you'll find yourself locked up and crossing off days on a calendar in between bouts of trying not to drop the soap. They will find a way to make that so, are you understanding me?" Wilson looked the man in the eyes. The man returned the look for a moment, then dropped his head. "Look at me," Wilson said. The man didn't. "LOOK AT ME!" he shouted. One of the other security men took a step forward, raising his arm. "Try it if you want," Wilson said to him. "If you're really that curious to find out what it feels like when your arm's not connected to your shoulder anymore." The man stepped back. The first guard was looking up at him, and Wilson addressed him. "You think I give a shit about what Disney can do to me? Not a chance in hell, my friend. But I've spent the last three months waiting. Lying in a hospital bed, waiting, thinking of the day when my hands would be around Scott Slugger's throat. One more day, one more week, one more month, one more year, it's nothing to me. Nothing at all. So get your hand off the top of your gun, you goddamn pussy." The guard looked down and saw that his hand was right where Wilson had said it was. With a grimace of shame, he yanked it up. Wilson continued. "I'll go. Peacefully. But I'll keep coming back again... and again... and again... and one of these times, I'll get through. It already happened once. It'll happen again. And when I do..." He trailed off, letting the cold hard blankness in his eyes speak for itself. Then he turned and walked away, disappearing into the Florida rain, which was first cold, and then warm.
