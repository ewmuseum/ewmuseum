But thirty minutes wasn't long, and she could call Miles afterward and have a few shots of something... she'll play it out. So she sat, arms and legs both crossed. "If you'll forgive me for asking," said Cambridge, "How did you get involved in the wrestling business? You look almost normal, compared to the rest of the people I spoke with today." "Those non-normal people you're talkin' about are some of the best people in the world," countered Ivy, "but to answer your question, I got into this business because I had to help my brother in law. And I'm still in this business because I have a lot of experience and knowledge that I think could be beneficial towards the younger generation." "What kind of knowledge?" "Too complex," replied Ivy, "you'd have to understand the business to understand what I'd tell you." "Try me. It's all fake, isn't it?" Ummm... Ivy just stood up again. "Fake?" "I mean--" "I know what you mean," interrupted Ivy, "What's fake about a wooden stake going through a man's knee? What's fake about a branding iron burning the letters 'TCB' onto another man's back for life?" Ivy put her right foot on Dr. Cambridge's armrest and pulled her pants leg up past her knee, revealing a seven inch scar. "What's fake about a knee joint collapsing on itself, essentially making all the cartilige in the joint explode, in front of twenty thousand live and twenty million via television?" "I think you--" "Shut. Up." interrupted Ivy, as she grabbed the notebook out of the intern's hands, "Is that what you're writing here? Why are these people so broken up over a fake sport? What's fake about six men burning to death in a fire?" A tear ran down Ivy's cheek. "Well?" Dr. Cambridge looked at her in stunned silence. "I asked you a question," repeated Ivy, "What. Is. So. Fake. About. Six. Men. Burning. To. Death. In. A. Fire." She reached into her pocket for something. "The wrestling business has given me both the best and worst moments of my life," said Ivy, "Real people trying to live real lives... real people who mean so much to me... and you condescend to me? Did you condescend to them, too?" As Ivy thumbed through the notebook, Dr. Cambridge noticed the object she'd taken from her pocket. A zippo. "I've spoken to most of your organization today," continued Dr. Cambridge, "and I assure you, I condescended to none of them, as I'm not condescending to you. I've been working towards my doctorate and I truly--" She stopped talking as Ivy held the notebook up in her face, with the page of notes she'd taken on Deacon. "Are you fuckin' kidding me?" asked Ivy, "Who the hell do you think you are?" Cambridge went to grab at the book, but Ivy was too quick for her. "Those notes are not for others to read," she insisted. Ivy shoved her back, and with two quick motions, flicked her lighter across her waistband once to open the top, and a second time, to light the zippo. She held the flame to the notebook long enough for the pages to start burning. "Hey!" said Cambridge, "You can't do that!" As she attempted another grab at the burning book, Ivy sidestepped her and managed to grab the recorder off the endtable as well. She dropped the notebook - now engulfed - on the marble floor and dropped the minicassette on top of it. Dr. Cambridge attempted to put the fire out, but it was too late - her notes were destroyed. She looked back at Ivy, with restrained anger flashing in her eyes. "Don't worry about it," said Ivy, collecting her things, "It's a bunch of notes on a bunch of professional wrestlers that's burning there." Cambridge looked back at her, confused. "It's fake, remember?" As the Psycho Bitch left the smoldering pile of ash and melted tape, Dr. Cambridge reached for the phone. "Hello, receptionist? Connect me with that man who was in charge of the wrestling company, please. I think there's a situation he should be aware of."
