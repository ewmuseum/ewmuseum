Boost for the Troops The transition from professional wrestler to promoter had not been an easy one for William “Kid Cool” Beaven, but it was an endeavor he was quickly educating himself on. Weeks have passed since the re-opening of this legendary promotion, and Beaven was proud to say that most of the operation had gone swimmingly. Wrestling dirt sheets had, aside from the anticipated ‘rumors’, been quite positive in their reviews of the FWO, tickets for events had begun selling out, the tribute to the troops show was finalized and would result in great press, and everything seemed to be going in the right direction; everything except the two individuals that were ordered to his office… On one side, engulfed in a circle of security, stood the 6’8”, 280 pound Hardcore Champion known as Graphic Violence. GV stood angrily with a smug look on his face as the FWO Hardcore Championship hung over his extremely broad and tattooed shoulder. His smug look displayed his detest for authority, but also hatred towards the very man that stood a mere few feet away from him. On the other side, also circled by his own crack team of FWO Security, sat the mysterious wrestler from the P.I.W, whose penchant for chaos was matched only by his ability to evoke it. His name was Krow. Krow sat on one of the wooden chairs placed before Beaven’s desk and apathetically stared at the floor as he cracked his knuckles calmly. Beaven briefly glared at both men for a moment and ruffled through a few papers on his desk. He organized a small stack of them and held them up towards the two behemoths. “Do you see this?” he asked rhetorically as he presented them to both GV and Krow. “Do you know what this is?” “Paper?” Krow arrogantly interjected. “Ha-Ha, very funny smart a—,” Shadows shouted, before being cut off by Beaven. “Shadows!” Beaven interjected, cutting the cuss word off in mid-flow. Beaven slapped the papers down on his desk and stuck a finger on the top of the stack. “What this is is a result of your negligence for this company. All of these papers right here are the headaches you’ve caused me and every staff member back here to deal with. They are bills, fines, and minor law suits we now have to deal with because of your reckless endeavors. I’m overdosing on Tylenol thanks to you two.” “Maybe you should switch to Advil, I heard it’s much more effective,” interrupted Krow with his oh-so-famous trouble making arrogance. “Beaven, if you don’t tell this piece of s---,” GV continued, but again, was cut off in mid-cuss by a raise of Beaven’s hand. “Look! I don’t know why you two want to get at each other’s throats,” protested Beaven as his hands gestured tensely towards GV and Krow. “I don’t know if Shadows slept with Krow’s sister, or if Krow burned down Shadows’ home. I don’t know if you got in a hit-and-run, or if you two have some deep-rooted heat from another company. All I know, or care, about is that this company and its operations run smoothly…and you two haven’t been allowing me to do that.” Krow clicked his teeth and shook his head in disappointment. “Aww, poor FWO,” Krow said with a Cheshire cat smirk. GV bit his lip and paced impatiently in his security-induced circle. “Now, I’ve looked at all the options here. I could fire you two for insubordination, but I’m not going to do that. I could suspend you two, but I’m also not going to do that. I tried putting you in the ring together in tag team action, and that failed miserably. I mean, not even an FWO Legend like Chronos can keep you two from killing each other, so…” Beaven momentarily paused as he internally questioned his own decision. “So, what?” Shadows shouted impatiently. “Oh, here’s an idea Kid. Why don’t you take these two guys that have been beating each the hell out of each other since we opened and…oh, I don’t know…put us against each other?” Shadows said sarcastically; Shadows never expected Beaven’s response. “My sentiments exactly,” Beaven said sternly as he looked up at the intrigued eyes of Shadows and Krow. “If you two want to kill each other, then fine, I’m going to let you do just that, but on my terms.” “Next week, we’re going overseas to participate in a Tribute to the Troops show. It’s probably one of the biggest shows we’re having since we reopened our doors. It’s something I’m very excited about and look forward to presenting. It’s also something I want to pull off flawlessly with as much excitement and success as I possibly can,” Beaven said as he pulled a piece of paper out and presented a BETA promotional flyer that had an image of Krow VS GV. “That’s why it’s only right to put a former military soldier, such as Shadows, in the main event,” Beaven excitedly said. “At the Tribute of the Troops show, it will be Graphic Violence, a former military soldier, a man who fought for our country, defending his Hardcore Championship against a man of equal power and strength, a man who has attempted to take down a military soldier, Krow. It’s the classic face versus heel encounter we need to headline the show. How does that sound?” GV smiled and cracked his knuckles roughly. ”Beaven, you don’t need to ask me twice. Book it.” And while Graphic Violence expressed his gratitude through tough-guy mannerisms, Krow sat silently in his seat. His eyes stared blankly into the distance, not particularly focused on anything. His silence caused a lull to awkwardly encompass Beaven’s office. Excitement was the only emotion Beaven expected from the two men, and he found Krow’s silence to be peculiar. “Is there a problem, Krow?” Beaven asked. “Getting cold feet?” GV interjected. Krow continued sit silently for a moment, but lifted his eyes softly towards Beaven and cracked a sinister grin. ”Tell me, William, is this show mandatory for talent?” Krow asked calmly. “What?!” GV astoundingly questioned. “I…I don’t understand,” Beaven confusedly replied. “It isn’t a hard question to answer, boss,” Krow arrogantly chimed. “Is.This.Show.Mandatory.For.Everyone?” “Well,” Beaven cautiously replied as he looked over at the wide-eyed and enraged GV, “Technically, no. It’s a tribute show and not apart of your contractual obligations. However, it’s an experience unlike any other and would really put over the company, as well as yourself.” ”Well, if it isn’t part of my contractual obligation, then I’ll pass.” GV looked down at the ground with his arms folded over his chest and began to amusingly laugh. "I knew it, I knew it! You are scared to be in that Hardcore Title match with me. You know I'd rip you limb from limb as I have done before. Mandatory. HA! Just so you know Krow, only the dark match bums don't attend this event. Maybe that's where you belong." Krow closed his eyes, breathed deeply, and ignored the banters of GV. ”I don’t understand this, Krow. Are you turning down an opportunity to face Shadows?” Beaven shook his head in disbelief. “You do realize that this basically legalizes all destruction you two cause. You can do whatever you want to each other in that ring without any punishment. You can tear each other limb from limb in that ring. And if you win, you get the reward of being Hardcore Champion for your efforts. And you don’t want that?” Krow sighed disapprovingly. ”Call it…a fear of long plane rides,” Krow answered passively. Krow's response to why he couldn't attend sent GV into a Joker like state of laughter. "HAHAHA! That is too priceless! Krow, the man who is named after a bird has a fear of flying! That is gold my friend, pure gold. HAHAHA! Oh my stomach! HAHA!" Krow ignored the banters, not so much as peering towards GV, and lifted his eyes back to Beaven. ”However, I’m not one to simply cast aside an idea without a back-up plan,” Krow tranquilly said, garnering the attention of both Beaven and a cackling GV, whose laughter stopped intriguingly. “You’re correct, William, having this upcoming Tribute to the Troops show headlined by a former military personnel himself is quite the main event. However, I feel that such an event should be headlined by something even bigger.” ”I’m listening…,” interrupted Beaven. “Why not have Graphic Violence enter a contest he’s never entered before. You have tons and tons of military personnel watching that dream of achieving victory every single day. They dream of overcoming the odds and being the victorious underdog. They dream to be successful in their most ultimate endeavor. So why not give them a boost in morale?” Krow asked. “How so?” asked Beaven, as GV watched intently. “By giving a solider his chance at ultimate victory…by giving Shadows a chance at the World Championship.” The lull recreated itself in Beaven’s office. GV, though angered by the cowardice of Krow, could not fend of a chance at a championship that eluded him his entire career. GV shifted his eyes towards Beaven, who sat quietly and rubbed his chin. “I like it,” Beaven calmly retorted. “Graphic Violence versus High Flyer for the World Championship at the Tribute of the Troops show; I like it a lot.” ”Wait, this isn’t adding up,” GV interjected as he turned towards Krow, looking over the sea of security. “What the hell is with this disparity? You wussing out on a match and think I’ll forgive you by pitching a World Title match for me? Is that it? You afraid of what I might do to you tonight for bailing out on a match with me? Is that it?” Krow didn’t answer, even as GV cackled insanely to provoke him. Instead, Krow closed his eyes and joined GV in his laughter. GV cocked a bewildered eyebrow up as he heard Krow’s low chuckle of amusement. Krow sat up from his chair and looked towards Beaven. ”I’m glad I could help, I apologize for any stress I may have caused,” Krow eerily told Beaven, who cautiously backed up into his chair a bit. The Satanic God maneuvered away from the desk and made his way towards the door. As his hand clutched the door knob, he stopped and finally peered over at Shadows; their first eye contact all night. ”Our battle can wait,” he said. “Until than, good luck on your match; I’ll be watching.” Krow smiled wickedly towards Shadows and calmly exited the office. “Something doesn’t smell right here Beaven. This feud you have going on with Krow…it’s entertaining but also expensive. The two of you already have me buying new equipment and we just got out of the first month”. Shadows thought about it for a moment. “I’ll only take the night off if it’s with pay. Or better yet, maybe you can take my pay tonight and put it towards a shrink so that big oaf can get over his fear of flying. You really need to stop hiring douche bags Beaven.” “Uh, yeah. Anyways…” said William Beaven. “As you know Steve, we have that big tribute to the troops show next week. I have been doing tons of planning and promotion. This event is going to be huge and will really help rebuild the FWO’s image by giving back to the community”. Shadows interjected. “I already see where this is headed…” “Now, since you’re the only Army combat veteran the FWO has…I think it would be really great if you did a mini state side USO tour. Visit some wounded troops, help raises their spirits and help show that the FWO is here to help out in the community.” “Sure. Everyone stood as 'Juke Joint Jezebel' filled the arena and the Flying Frenchie stepped through the curtain. He stopped just at the halfway point, put both hands on his hips, and flashed a blinding smile to the camera. Behind him, Brawn walked slowly, holding his arm close to his body to keep his shoulder from moving. He wore a 'Flying Frenchie: Around the World' T-shirt, but the bulge of some kind of wrapping could be seen beneath the fabric. A thousand flashbulbs went off around the two men as the former four - time FWO World Heavyweight Champion reached the ring and vaulted himself over the top. Brawn did not follow him: instead, he took a seat with the announce team and put on a pair of headphones. Frenchie rolled his shoulders as he waited. I won't be told anymore... that I've been brought down in this storm... and left so far out from the shore that I can't find my way back, my way anymore... He didn't wait long. Poison Ivy was the first one through the curtain, but Eli Flair was half a step behind her. They stopped right at the entrance for a moment, allowing more pictures to be taken. Eli's face, as per usual, was a stony, stoic mask, devoid of emotion. Ivy, on the contrast, slapped as many hands as she could. For some reason, she had emerged without her Singapore Cane. Of course, after more than four matches against the Flying Frenchie over the course of Eli Flair's career, she knew exactly what to expect when they faced off, and knew that an extra set of attentive eyes were worth more than a weapon. They circled the ring on opposite sides, stopping just in front of the announce table. A brief stare towards Brawn, and Eli was on top of the corner, fist raised. As the music died, he asked Stevie Roberts for a microphone. "Pierre," said Eli, as he took off his black leather trenchcoat and held it at arm's length, "If you really need to steal it, please dry clean it first." The fans erupted in cheers and laughter, and Frenchie regarded them all with a smirk while he asked Eli for the microphone. "I assure you, Eli, zat your coat is safe. After all, I, as a French man, posess a quality known as 'style'." With that, he handed the microphone to the referee, who handed it off to Stevie Roberts, and the bell sounded! They locked up in the center of the ring, and Eli almost immediately picked Frenchie up and slammed him into the corner! The referee was on the spot to back him up, but he hadn't followed up: he allowed Frenchie to step back to the center of the ring. Of course, Pierre took a few cautious steps. They went to lock up again, but the Frenchie ducked under Eli's outstretched arms. He turned around, confused, and it didn't abate when the Frenchie held up his hands and gestured that he wanted a test of strength. Eli looked around the arena as the fans erupted. There was only one reason why a man who gave up five inches and sixty pounds would go for a test of strength: he had something up his sleeve. Frenchie insisted. Eli hesitated. The fans were split between encouraging the lockup and warning Eli that the Flying Frenchie always had a plan. But, what can you do? Eli locked up with him, and immediately, Pierre fell to his knees, a look of abject pain on his face. Eli, on the other hand, looked around, confused. He locked eyes with Poison Ivy and shrugged his shoulders; a closeup of the two wrestlers showed Eli's hands completely relaxed. Ivy yelled at him, but too late. In half a second, Pierre had let go of one of his hands, slid his body on the mat, dropped Eli Flair with a DTH~! and leaned back on his still - trapped arm, putting pressure on the shoulder. "What the hell?" shouted Ivy, "You actually fell for that?" "A trifle rusty, non?" asked Pierre, loud enough for the first row to hear, "Zis is child's play!" The Flying Frenchie, with the sudden advantage, pressed it as much as he could. He kept Eli Flair off balance for the next several minutes with a stunning display of mat wrestling. In the three years that the FWO had been off the air, and even with his recent loss to High Flyer in the FWO World Title match, it was obvious that Pierre had been doing much more in the way of training than Eli Flair. That's not to say Flair was totally helpless. He proved his skills had not fallen off with a reversal of nearly every hold the Frenchie locked him in. He reversed the shoulderlock by backing up to his knees and slowly rising from the mat with the Frenchie still holding on for dear life. He broke out of a sleeper hold by flipping him over his shoulder and locking on a rear chin/armlock of his own, one that Pierre needed to stre-e-e-tch for the bottom rope to break. After about eight minutes of back and forth wrestling, the Flying Frenchie slid to the outside and backed up, just out of reach of the fans in the front row. He gestured to the referee for a time-out, and of course, the count began. Eli broke it up twice by trying to go to the outside, but of course, he was stopped. So, he went out the other end. Frenchie saw him coming and headed in the other direction, but stopped short, six feet in front of Ivy. Just enough time for Eli to catch him and pound a pair of forearms in between his shoulderblades. "Talk about style, French man?" asked Eli, "I'm not the one wrestling in a beret, am I?" Eli whipped Frenchie into the ring steps, but Pierre planted his feet and pulled with all his might, and reversed the whip. He stumbled to the floor, but Eli crashed into the ring steps and knocked them to the floor with a metallic CLANG. "Ze difference, Eli," replied the Frenchie, "is zat you cannot make ze beret work." Eli rose to his knees, but he appeared to have hurt himself. Frenchie grabbed him by the hair, ignoring the referee's warning to take it back to the ring, and bounced it off the announcer's table, hard enough to make Brawn flinch. "Now Brawn, zat's how you take care of bus--OOOF!" Frenchie was cut off when Eli returned the favor. He shot an elbow into Pierre's gut, and bounced his skull off one of the monitors. Frenchie collapsed to the floor, wobbily trying to get back to his feet. Eli stared at Brawn, with a large bruise forming on his cheek. "Never take your eyes off of your opponent, kid." He moved back to Pierre and pulled him to his feet - and was immediately doubled over by a thumb to the eye. Frenchie shoved Eli back into the ring and slid under the bottom rope himself, just in time to beat the count. "Hey, kiddo," said Poison Ivy, as she leaned against the announce table, "Do as they say, not as they do." Frenchie took over from that point, hammering Eli with shots from all sides. Finally, after an Irish Whip reversal and a high knee to the chin, Eli was toppled. Frenchie took the opportunity to climb to the top rope and signal that he was about to land the Guillotine Legdrop--- ---but Eli rolled out of the way as soon as the Frenchie was in the air, giving him the entire trip down to think about it. They managed to get to their feet at the same time, but when Pierre fired a punch, Eli sidestepped him and shoved him chest first into the turnbuckle, as hard as he possibly could. Frenchie staggered back a few steps, and the Original Nobody rolled him up - - much to the chagrin of the Frenchie himself, of Brawn at the announce table, and the fans at ringside - - for the three count. Frenchie popped up a second after the bell, and stared at the referee with disbelief in his eyes. His gaze drifted to Eli, who was on his knees halfway across the ring with a smirk on his face. Confidently, Eli held up three fingers. Frenchie grinned, and gave him a slow clap. It was the oldest remaining rule in the FWO: Expect anything and everything when you wrestle the Flying Frenchie, because that's what he'll do to win. Many of them whispered their excitement for the Tribute of the Troops show, especially William Beaven, who couldn’t stop talking about it the entire night. It would be the first time, since FWO’s reincarnation, that the company would receive such favorable press. It was exactly what the company needed to reemerge from the shadows of negativity that shrouded them since the infamous inferno that set the company ablaze in 2005. And while many would anxiously wait for their flight overseas to pay tribute to the men and women that sacrificed so much for our freedom, one man anxiously waited for another reason. Krow leaned against the wall, outside of the Key Arena, as he whispered ominously into his black cellular phone. He spoke a language that was not of American descent. In fact, the dialect was not one familiar with American culture at all. The vernacular he spoke was of Mid-Eastern society. He spoke with a fluency that was comparable with natives of the land. And while his words were not recognizable to an American ear, his intentions were obvious through his facial mannerisms. Narrowed eyes, a menacing smirk, and a calm disposition foretold a portentous message. With a click of the phone, the conversation ended. Krow nonchalantly lobbed the phone into the air and caught it with ease. He blew a chilly smoke into the frosty air of Seattle and chuckled lowly. He sighed pleasingly to himself and closed his eyes. “Good luck, Shadows,” he replied to himself with a whimsical tone. “I’ll be watching.” While many flaunt their muscles to show dominance and strength, they neglect the most powerful asset that a human being can have: information. And when it’s in the wrong hands…bad things can happen. He’ll be watching, Steven Shadows. He’ll be watching.
