fWo Survivor Q&A

14 July 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
fWo Survivor NEWSWIRE fWo SURVIVOR Q&A July 14, 2000 fWo offices have been flooded with requests to be in the new fWo Survivor contest. At last count, over six hundred applications had been sent in, and with a full three days left before the deadline for entry, many people speculate that the Survivor contest will eclipse the century mark before it is over. Said Stevie Roberts, Survivor Program Coordinator, "We expected a strong response, but certainly not this strong. I think it's a testament to the strong fans of the fWo and the great people out there that make up the eWrestling community that we have such strong entries so far." In an fWoWrestling.com exclusive interview with Mr. Roberts, the once fWo World Heavyweight Champion answered several questions that had been on the minds of viewers from interviewer Marc Burnet. Marc Burnet: "Stevie, with so many entrants into the Survivor contest how are you ever going to select only twelve people?" Stevie Roberts: "Marc, it's not going to be easy. One of the really amazing things so far is the high quality of applications we have seen. Unlike what we have seen in the past with applicants, fWo Survivor seems to be bringing in the top notch people. It's not going to be easy getting it down to only twelve people... because no matter what happens some really great people are going to be left out. However, the opportunities for future Survivor contests are always here, and given the popularity of this one and the high critical acclaim it is receiving I'm certain there will be more." Marc: "Survivor 2?" Stevie: "If this one works well, sure. We are really doing something unknown here... nobody has ever attempted anything like this before so we don't know what is going to happen." Marc: "A few people have expressed concern over the ideas of alliances forming. Do you see this as a realistic problem?" Stevie: "I think there is no doubt that we will see some alliances form. We are trying to get a good mix of people in the applicant process. You're not going to see four or five people from another eFed all be accepted... we want a good mix of people, representing all forms of the eWrestling world. I think in that way we will cut down on some alliances forming, although over four weeks friendships are bound to develop. We have a few tricks up our sleeve to keep it interesting, though." Marc: "Such as?" Stevie: "Immunity contests will be a part of fWo Survivor. We have three wrestling shows a week, and the winners of things like Battle Royals or small tournaments will be granted immunity from the next voting round. Also, the fate of who will be the final survivor will be partly determined by past entrants in the contest... meaning that people who go out of their way to screw people over to get ahead are going to be hurt in the end." Marc: "How are the other members of the fWo roster viewing this contest?" Stevie: "There is a lot of curiosity there. Obviously they know that one of the twelve is going to be coming into the fed... so they will be watching with keen interest." Marc: "How much fan activities do you have planned for fWo Survivor?" Stevie: "Obviously we want people to discuss things in our public forum, but we have plans for polls and other fan activities. Marc Burnet: Stevie, a lot of people have questions about how this whole contest is going to work. Can you clear some of the details up for us? Stevie Roberts: Well, remember that this is a brand new venture we are dealing with. Nobody has tried anything like this before, so there is a lot of making up as we go along. Obviously our goal is to provide an exciting contest for the fans, which has always been the number one priority of the fWo. But we also want to showcase some of the talents of some people not in the fWo. Marc, there are hundreds of people out there with the skills and ability to make it big in the fWo or any other big wrestling promotion. The problem is that there just isn't the time or resources available to put them all on center stage where they deserve to be. The waiting list to get into the fWo is huge, and the Survivor contest is one attempt at curbing this problem. Obviously we are just dealing with twelve people at a time... but twelve is better than none, right? Marc: Are the twelve applicants actually going to wrestle? Stevie: Of course! This is a wrestling promotion after all. Wrestling will happen three times a week on three shows. Now, since this is a startup contest the shows are going to be primarily wrestling... and few angles. Wrestlers will have their hands full working to showcase their skills through interviews and endear themselves to the other wrestlers and fans watching at home. That's why cards will be designed mainly to move wrestlers from one voting period to another, and not to progress storylines. Marc: Are there going to be any storylines at all? Stevie: Yes, but on a limited scale. Wrestlers can elect to provide interviews or segments they do with each other that run on the card. But unlike the fWo and most other federations, the wrestlers won't know who they are facing until a day before the event. This is designed to keep things shaken up and showcase as many wrestlers against one another as possible. Marc: It sounds tough. Stevie: It is tough. It's called Survivor for a reason. This isn't like a tryout... it's an actual event and it is going to really bring out the best in people. Of course, once someone goes through Survivor it benefits them in several ways. Several other promotions are watching the Survivor contest closely, and if someone shines during the course of the contest you can expect a host of offers to come in to that wrestler. Regular wrestling isn't going to be near as stressful as the Survivor contest... so someone who does well in Survivor is going to excel just about anywhere else. Marc: What about the rumors that a current member of the fWo roster will be tossed into the mix of twelve in disguise? Stevie: No. This contest is strictly forbidden to current fWo wrestlers. We want to showcase new talent here, and that will be done with twelve unknowns to the fWo. Marc: Let's be honest now... a lot of people have applied. Stevie: Yes, currently we are just under the century mark. Marc: That means people have one in ten odds... if the entry was closed today... of getting in. How are you going to break the news to the disappointed entrants? Stevie: It's the most unpleasant part of the job. I'm not going to kid anyone... a lot of great people aren't going to get in. Frankly I was hoping the Survivor contest would not be as popular as it was, but we are at that bridge now. I think the best thing we can do is make the experience the best we can for the twelve entrants... and look toward a sequel. Marc: Has anyone been rejected already? Stevie: We aren't looking at the entrants seriously yet, and won't start until tomorrow. We have tossed out a few entrants... but only because we aren't sure why they sent to us in the first place. One person was asking how to join the Survivor contest and said he always wanted to go to Australia. He must have had us confused with the CBS series. Another person wrote in to say that, and I quote, "If you don't let me in I'll rip your balls off". That wasn't his interview either... that was his subject line. A third person wrote in telling us that he was the Flying Frenchie and that he wanted back into the fWo, and that Survivor was his only shot. Well, the Frenchie still has contact with the fWo head office, so I'm not sure what this person was trying to pull. So yes, those entrants were discarded. But only those. Marc: Many people have wondered about their photo shoots... Stevie: Everyone entered in the fWo Survivor contest has a photo shoot. Not as much time will be spent as the members of the fWo roster, but I doubt anyone will be complaining. Marc: Voting. This plays a critical part of fWo Survivor. Tell us how. Stevie: Well, following the conclusion of the events the voting form will be open, and members will then have 24 hours to vote which of their number has to leave. It's really that simple. There are only two real rules, and those are that you cannot vote for someone who has immunity and if you don't vote in the 24 hours then you automatically vote... against yourself. That is why we set up the 24 hour window. It's important to hit the deadline and get your vote in if you wish to survive to the end. Marc: And how does it work if there is a tie? Stevie: We don't expect this to happen often, if at all, but if it does occur the winner is determined through a match. Marc: What happens when only two people remain? Stevie: Well, the final vote is done by the previous ten members. That means even if you get voted out you still have a say in who the ultimate winner will be. That is why it is so important for people not to offend others even if they get voted out. One of those people voted out can pay you back if you aren't careful. There is a lot of strategy involved in fWo Survivor, but most importantly it simply gives twelve people to show the world what they are made of. Marc: And in the end one person survives. Stevie: Only one. Let me tell you, whoever that person is enters the fWo with one hell of a reputation right off the bat. But what happens to them after they enter the fWo is another matter completely out of my hands... but I look forward to watching it. Marc: Any final words for our readers? Stevie: Good luck, and thanks for watching! We are here because of you, and we hope we provide you with one hell of a show.
