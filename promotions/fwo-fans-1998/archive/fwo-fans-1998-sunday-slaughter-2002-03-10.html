Sunday Slaughter, 10 March 2002

10 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Introduction Mufanga Kagos wandered restlessly around the arena's backstage, treading silently barefoot in the concrete hallways. He felt trapped... caged. His leopard-print loincloth moved like a second skin as he marched through the arena, looking for... something. A familiar sound snapped him out of his brooding silence. Music from the past. Queen? Or something more recent? The music getting louder, drawing closer, he turned the corner as the florescent lights danced along the spiralling white bodypaint etched over his dark skin. The music was coming from a garish, overly large silver stereo, with the words" Boom Box" stamped into the speaker covers. It was surrounded by a group of people, roadies for the most part, some writers and tech people. But this music wasn't the spectacle that had these passerbys standing, stopped in their tracks. The spectacle was Ice. Vanilla Ice... breakdancing. As Mufanga watched, Ice began to clumsily do the "robot" as the crowd clapped to the beat of "Ice Ice Baby". He then dropped to the floor in what looked quite a painful transition, and began to spin on his back, his parachute pants puffing up around him. Arms crossed, Mufanga watched this go on for a few minutes as Ice began "The Running Man"; jogging in place, horribly out of sync with the music... even for a white guy. All came to a crashing halt as Mufanga reached out and pushed the stop button on the Boom Box. The silence was deafening as Vanilla Ice stopped dancing, a questioning look on his face as he caught his breath. It was quickly broken by the young Kagos. "This is the legendary Ice?" Mufanga stated abruptly. "This is the former three-time fWo Champion? Breakdancing out here with your name shaved into the back of your head? Vanilla Ice? That's gotta be the lamest gimmick ever." "Yo G, why you illin'?" Ice asked, fixing the cuff of his 'Word to Your Mother" jacket. "I be here stylin' and profilin' with my homies, and here you bust in all gangsta-like? You know you shouldn't be freakin' wit me, man. I'm Vanilla Ice. I'm the biggest star in the wiz-orld. I'm straight from da hood, from da mean streets of Piz-ortland. You step to me, you best get to steppin'" Mufanga laughed. "A Vanilla Ice gimmick? Jeez, man... didn't Vanilla Ice go out of fashion with MC Hammer and the Mr. T cartoon?" "Watchu talkin' bout, gimmicks? This ain't no gimmick G, this is me. Vanilla Ice. And what you think ya doin', schoolin' me on gimmicks, ma Nigga? You the one runnin' round here barefoot in a furry nut-rug. Whatcha doin' with that at on yo' sack, G?" Mufanga looked at his loincloth awkwardly... but then returned his attention back to Ice. "My Nigga? I'm nobody's nigga, whitey." They were toe to toe, eye to eye... all muscles tensed and ready on both athletes. And that is how they stay as Slaughter heads to the ring... Donaven Winters vs. Eli Flair Slaughter heads to the ring for a big hardcore matchup, in which a Hardcore Title shot at CyberSlam is indeed on the line. "Tainted Love" plays, and the fans cheer as Eli Flair heads to the ring, dragging a cart filled no doubt with weapons of various kinds behind him. Flair digs around in the cart and comes up with a long 2 by 4. He hefts the weapon, nods approvingly, and stands by the ring to wait for the entrance of his opponent. "Piggy" plays and the crowd boos, but Donaven Winters is not apparently present in the entry way. Flair stares down in that direction, ready for the Hardcore Champion to make his appearance. As a result, he doesn't see Winters coming out of the crowd on the opposite side of the ring. Winters slides into the ring, then runs toward the ropes and baseball slides out, nailing Flair in te back of the head. The 2 by 4 goes flying, and Flair hits the deck. Winters stands up and digs around in Flair's convenient cart, coming out with a fire extinguisher. As Flair gets up to his feet, Winters clocks him with the fire extinguisher, knocking Flair down to the floor, where he lands on his knees. Winters then lets Flair have a blast with the fire extinguisher, hitting him full in the face and thus blinding him. Flair stumbles off, heading down the entrance ramp, and Winters follows him. He grabs Flair from behind and shoves him off the ramp. Flair takes the five-foot drop and lands chin-first on the guardrail, knocking out of his mouth what is either a tooth or a very distinguishable loogie. Winters hops down and slams Flair's face into the guardrail, then leans down into his face to talk trash. Flair takes advantage of the mistake to kick back, nailing Winters below the belt with a mule kick and causing him to utter a few words that are not suitable for air on any non-pay television network except apparently ESPN. Winters stumbles back, and Flair hooks him up and drives him backwards into the side of the entrance ramp with a russian legsweep. Flair then grabs Winters and whips him back towards the ring, where Winters slams hard into the apron, jarring his back against it. Seconds later, the knee of a running Eli Flair slams into his midsection. Flair then grabs Winters and executes a front faceslam, driving him into the floor. Flair makes the cover, but Winters kicks out at two. Flair brings Winters to his feet and goes for a suplex, but Winters grabs the guardrail to keep from going over, then knees Flair in the gut. He then hooks Flair's head and drives him into the floor with a DDT. Winters backs up and digs around in the cart, coming out with a medical forearm brace. He puts it on and waits for Flair to get back up to his feet. When Flair does, Winters charges at him. He spins and blasts Flair full-on with a rolling elbowsmash, driving his reinforced forearm into the mouth of Eli Flair. Flair crumples to the floor, and Winters removes the brace and makes the cover. Somehow, it only gets a count of two, Flair just getting a shoulder up in time to stay alive. Winters brings Flair right back up and hooks him, trying to hiptoss him into the cart. Flair, however, reverses it and hiptosses Winters into the cart. Winters screams, presumably having landed on something sharp. Flair takes the cart and begins to run forward with it, then shoves it off the side of the entrance ramp. Winters and the cart slam into the floor, and Winters is buried in weapons of various types and covered by the cart. Flair looks down, perhaps says a little prayer, then runs and leaps off the ramp, crashing into the cart and by extension the weapons and Flair with a flying, Mick Foley-style elbowdrop. The fans start an "fWo!' chant in appreciation, which surely means a lot to the competitors as they lay semi-conscious and in incredible pain on the floor. Right. Slowly, Flair drags himself from the wreckage, followed just behind by Winters. Flair digs around in the debris and comes out with an aluminum baseball bat. As he digs, Winters reaches into the scattered goodies as well like some twisted arcade prize-digging game and comes out with what very well may be the fender of a car. Each man swings their weapon. Each man connects. The two fall to the floor, and neither man stirs. This goes on for much beyond what would be a traditional 10-count, and it is clear that neither man is getting up any time soon. The ref shrugs his shoulders and has no choice but to call the match a draw. Slaughter heads to commercial with Frankton and Armstrong wondering what will now become of Eli Flair's possible CyberSlam title shot... "No." Lance Knight vs. Grendel As the Deftone's "Elite" filled the arena, Lance Knight made his way down to the ring, receiving generous support from the fans, many of whom were still amazed that Knight had picked up the victory over the veteran Jon Crisp on last week's Slaughter. Knight strolled confidently to the ring, barely favoring the knee that had been injured in his contest two weeks ago against the man he would be facing this week, Grendel. Knight rolled into the ring and stretched himself on the ropes as Pop Will Eat Itself's "Everything's Cool" began to play. As the song started, Lance looked alertly around the arena, attempting to make sure that Grendel wouldn't be able to gain the early advantage by sneak attack like he had two weeks prior. However, Knight's concerns were all for naught, as Grendel appeared at the top of the ramp and slowly made his way to the ring, his mask hiding any emotion that might've been present on his face. Grendel rolled into the ring and stared at Knight, his reddish eyes burning a hole through the young man from Pittsburgh. The official called for the bell, and the match was begun! Grendel moved in for a tie-up, and as he did so, he picked his head up and appeared to sniff the air around Knight. Smiling slightly, Grendel turned the collar-and-elbow tie-up into a boxer's clutch of sorts, firing right hands repeatedly into Lance Knight's side. Although the blows looked powerful, they wouldn't normally have affected Knight so seriously; however, he staggered backwards, groaning, before rushing forward with a lariat that sent both him and Grendel to the outside. As both men slowly got to their feet, Grendel smiled again, in spite of the impact of the move and the tumble over the ropes. Though most of his face was concealed by the mask that he wore, it almost seemed as if he were gloating over Knight, who seemed to be having an extremely rough go of it as he staggered unsteadily to his feet. Grendel advanced, forcing Knight to take a step backwards as he tried to account for the fire that flowed freely in his side. However, he quickly backed himself up against the ringpost, and before he could move, Grendel had snatched him up, delivering an elementary body slam to the floor. With Knight again down, Grendel rolled into the ring momentarily to break the count. Back on the outside, Grendel pulled Knight to his feet and shrugged off the weak blows that Knight aimed at his chest. Could this really be the same opponent who had provided such a challenge a mere 14 days ago? This meatsack? Grendel smiled again before sending a hard chop into Knight's chest that shut down Knights' offense completely. Knight staggered backwards, nearly tripping over the ringsteps, and that gave Grendel all the opening that he needed. Grendel lunged forward and scooped Knight overhead into a gorilla press position. He backed away from the ringsteps slight and then proceeded to drop Knight onto the steps, twisting the man in his arms as if Knight weighed no more than a child. As he connected with the cold steel of the steps, Knight gasped as his side caught the edge of the uppermost step. The pain that he had experienced to this point, which had felt like molten agony, could not compare to the torture that exploded into his side upon impact. With a groan, he collapsed to the floor, one hand pressed to his side, where liquid fire jolted up and down in rapid waves. Grendel approached, leaning his shaggy head in close, and for the third time, he appeared to smell Lance Knight. After several seconds, Grendel's head popped back up and he turned and confidently rolled into the ring, demanding that the referee begin his twenty-count. Knight heard this and did his best to get to his feet, but he quickly collapsed under the stress that the pain in his side was causing. This act in itself caused him to land painfully on the ring steps again, and Grendel uttered a guttural laugh that seemed to frighten the referee, who continued to count Lance Knight out of the match. With a snort, Grendel rolled out of the ring and kicked Knight in the side, eliciting a shriek of pain from the rookie. He rolled into the ring once more, and this forced the official to begin his count again. This time, however, Knight's jaw clenched and he fought to his feet, using the ring steps to steady himself. He crawled into the ring, taking care to stay in the ropes so as to prevent Grendel from stomping on his exposed side. Pulling himself to a seated position in the corner, Knight eventually rose to his feet, only to see the three-hundred-plus pounds of Grendel come literally roaring forward at him. At the last possible moment, Knight threw himself out of the way, wincing in pain as he hit the canvas, but his ploy had been effective, as Grendel had collided chest-first with the turnbuckle and was now staggering out of the corner backwards. Knight crawled forward and grabbed Grendel from behind, pulling him into a roll-up that forced the referee to drop into position for the first time during the contest. He counted: 1... 2... No! Grendel managed to kick out, and Knight groaned, as his best hope for victory had been thwarted by his completely fresh opponent. However, he still managed to get to his feet, fighting through the pain, and as Grendel approached again, Knight managed to heave him into a belly-to-belly suplex. Grendel bounced along the mat and back to his feet, still smiling as he could see the pain that so basic a move from Knight's arsenal had inflicted. How could he manage to execute his infinitely more complex Knightfall suplex when a normal belly-to-belly had left him nearly motionless? There was only one way to find out, Grendel supposed. He smiled as Knight got to his feet, and approached with a deliberate lack of speed that allowed Knight to pull him into an Irish whip. As he rebounded off of the ropes, Grendel smiled as Knight ducked his head the meatsack had done exactly as Grendel had expected, and he was looking for the quickest route to victory. Grendel decided to continue to toy with Knight, and rather than countering the move, as he easily could've, he allowed Knight to try to take him over in the watermill suplex that had beaten Jon Crisp seven days earlier. The one thing that Grendel counted on was what eventually surprised him. Unbeknownst to the furry behemoth, Knight allowed his opponent's momentum to do most of the work in his deadly Knightfall finishing maneuver, and he easily hoisted Grendel up and dropped the big man onto the mat. With another subdued groan, Knight rolled over and made the cover, and three seconds later, it became apparent that Grendel's plan had backfired. Somehow, Knight had overcome the pain that Grendel had dealt out and managed to pick up the win. As his bestial opponent stirred on the canvas, Knight rolled to the outside and requested some help to the back from one of the nearby ringside security crew. He'd have to see Universal later in the night and let him know what was going on. Result: Black Quicksilver by pinfall History (I) The man sat in front of the camera, the room darkened so it was not quite clear who he was. He sat there for a moment, head down, not saying or doing much of anything. A single spotlight then shone on his face, revealing in its backglow the fWo logo painted on the wall. It was a logo that had not been seen in a long time, the original, spray paint style fWo logo. The man was Scott Slugger. "There are days in a man's life when he wakes up and just knows that something is different. Not in a bad way, I mean. I mean that there are days in a man's life when he wakes up and knows, somehow just knows, that the events that take place on that day will affect the course of his very life, right up until the day where he's dead and buried, six feet under the ground. One of those days was April 24, 1998. The date of the first CyberSlam. At the time, I had a nodding acquaintance with one James Dudley, employed in the fWo as Jim 'Don't Call Me Daddy' Dudley. I remember vividly what happened before the show. Jim walked up to me I can still see the details. He had a cup of coffee in his hand. His blond hair was tied back into a ponytail, and he had one of those shit-eating grins on his face. You know the type. He said to me, 'Scott, baby, I got a feeling. I got a feeling about that battle royal, man.' I looked at him. 'The one for the World Title?' He says, 'Yeah, that's the one, baby. I can feel it, there's no way anybody's gonna be able to stop me tonight. Come the end of tonight, I'm gonna on top of the world.' At the time, I thought it was just idle bragging. Boasting, like everybody does in the locker room. I'm gonna kick your ass, I'm gonna win this, I'm gonna win that. I thought it was just talk. Then I looked in his eyes. I saw something in Jim Dudley's eyes that night, and the amount of times I've seen it since I can count on one hand. You see, even though the fWo hadn't so much as held a show yet, he knew that what happened tonight would go down in the annals of history. And he knew that he would make as big a name for himself as one can possibly make. He wasn't bragging... he was just stating a fact." Slugger paused as if in reflection, then continued. "That night, Jim Dudley outlasted 39 other participants to win the first ever fWo CyberSlam battle royal... and as a result, became the first ever fWo World Champion. The first ever. Now, right now I couldn't tell you where in the hell Jim Dudley is. I don't know if he's even still in the business. But I still remember every single thing he did that show. And nobody will ever forget what he did that show, because he went out there and seized the moment. He knew what it meant, to be at CyberSlam. He knew that there was no better place to make a name for one's self. What am I telling all you this for? Simple. It's CyberSlam. Let me repeat. It's CyberSlam. There's no bigger stage in the professional wrestling world than CyberSlam. I first said this before CyberSlam III, to Scott Collins, and I'll say it again now. At CyberSlam, men become heroes... heroes become legends... legends become immortal. You step up at CyberSlam, and your name will echo through the ages. You won't just be winning a match, or a title, or doing something special... you'll be becoming a part of the very history of this business itself. There are 48 days until CyberSlam. Who'll be this year's Jim Dudley? Guess we'll just have to wait and find out." With his piece said, Slugger bowed his head again and the lights slowly dimmed to black. Austin Davis vs. Erik Kelly Slaughter headed to the ring for the main event, a huge one-on-one matchup, which is also Erik Kelly's singles debut since returning. The buzz is coming off the crowd in waves, as they await this true clash of the titans. "Defy You" suddenly plays, and the crowd erupts, coming to their feet with a thunderous ovation for the fWo World Champion, Ruben Ross, who makes his way out with the fWo World Title belt around his waist. Ross rolls into the ring and unstraps the belt, then lights it high in the air as he poses on each turnbuckle. Finally, Ross re-straps the belt and rolls from the ring, joining Jeremiah Frankton and James Armstrong on commentary. "Down With The Sickness" plays, and the Rose Garden rocks with boos as Austin Davis heads out from backstage, looking as evil as ever. Davis sneers at the crowd as he walks to the ring, then rolls in and stretches himself somewhat using the ropes. Davis leans over the ropes to trade a few words with Ross, a man it would be kind to say he's less than fond of. "Let the bodies hit the... FLOOR! POWERBOMB" The cheers are incredible as Erik Kelly makes his way to the ring, clearly more than ready for this huge matchup. Kelly slowly makes his way to the ring, keeping his eyes trained on Davis the whole way as he goes. Kelly walks up the ringsteps onto the apron, then steps over the top rope and into the ring. He still keeps his eyes locked on Davis as he goes, and Davis returns the stare right back in kind. The ref issues a few last-minute instructions to the participants, and then he signals. The bell rings. Kelly and Davis slowly circle each other, each man looking for an opening. Finally Kelly lunges forward and catches Davis in a lock-up, which is bad news for Davis, as is evident to anybody with two eyes. Kelly violently shoves Davis back, and Davis flies into the corner. Kelly charges in at him, but Davis rolls away and Kelly slams into the turnbuckles. Kelly stumbles out, still facing into the corner, and Davis kicks him in the back of the knee to bend him over, then drives him to the mat with a reverse DDT. Davis runs into the ropes, then drives the point of his elbow into the throat of Kelly. He gets up and repeats this, this time finishing with a kneedrop. Davis then kneels over Kelly and lifts his legs into the air and lets them drop down, hitting a series of kneedrop slams to the throat, head, and side of Kelly. Finally, he stops and covers, but Kelly kicks out with authority at two. As Kelly gets up, Davis runs into the ropes and leaps high, hitting him with a huge clothesline and knocking him back down to the mat. Davis lands on top of Kelly and moves into a mounted position, then begins to unload with a series of punches to the face. Finally, Davis tires of this, brings Kelly up, and hurls him through the middle rope to the outside. Davis slides out and goes after Kelly. Davis grabs Kelly and sets to slam him into the guardrail, but Kelly puts a stop to this easily, blocking Davis' try by grabbing the guardrail. He then shoots an elbow into the gut of Davis, then slams Davis face-first into the guardrail instead. Davis stumbles back, and Kelly grabs him, then whips him hard into the ringpost. Davis clocks his head on the post and falls off it, crashing to the ground. Kelly stalks over to him and brings him up, then sets him up to feel some serious pain. Kelly lifts Davis up over his shoulder, then throws him down to the floor in a variation of the Defcon 1. Davis hits hard and practically bounces off the floor. Kelly brings Davis right back up and tosses him back in the ring, then brings him up again and throws him into the corner. Kelly stalks into the corner, then lifts his boot and applies it to Davis' throat, using his incredible size and power to choke the life out of Davis. Kelly breaks it at the count of four, then begins to lay into Davis with a series of big, wind-up elbows to the upper chest and head. Finally, Kelly brings Davis out and uses all of his considerable strength to whip Davis across the ring and into the opposite corner, where Davis hits with incredible force. Davis' momentum carries him back out of the corner, and he runs right into a monstrous lariat from Erik Kelly, knocking him straight down to the mat. Knowing that he's in trouble, Davis rolls from the ring to the outside before Kelly can make the cover. Kelly rolls outside as well and begins stalking Davis, who's walking across the ring outside to clear the cobwebs. As Kelly comes up behind Davis, Davis reveals that he's suckered the big man in, by kicking backwards and catching Kelly with a mule kick below the belt. Kelly is doubled over, and Davis grabs his head and slams him into the floor with the DDD. Kelly is down, and Davis heaves him back up and rolls him into the ring, then follows him in. With Kelly still down on the mat, Davis makes the cover. 1... 2... Kelly gets a shoulder up. Davis curses, but brings Kelly back up anyway. He kicks away at Kelly's right knee and leg, finally dropping Kelly to one knee. With his head in position, Davis locks on The Truth not the annoying commercials, but rather the devastating mandible claw submission hold. Davis cranks the hold, really having it locked in, and Kelly looks like he's beginning to fade. Kelly goes down to both knees now, and Davis keeps the move locked in. The ref raises Kelly's arm once. It drops. Raises his arm a second time. It drops. Raises his arm a third time. It stays up. The crowd goes nuts as Erik Kelly gets the adrenaline flowing, powering his way back up to his feet. He reaches out one huge right hand and wraps it around the throat of Davis, choking him. The pressure is so intense that Davis has no choice but to release the hold. Kelly still has his arm around Davis' throat and takes full advantage of the situation. He lifts Davis up, then hurls him down to the mat with a chokeslam. Then he stands, looks around, and decides it's time. "POWERBOOOOOOOMB"! The crowd goes nuts again as Kelly brings Davis up and puts him between his legs. Only seconds stand between Davis and annihilation more specifically, Eradication. Kelly reaches down to hook Davis. Davis drops to one knee and low blows Kelly. Just in case that didn't have full effect, he punches Kelly directly in the testicles. Kelly staggers back, the hold broken, doubled over. Davis gets to his feet and walks over to Kelly, then double-underhooks his head. He prepares Kelly, then drives him into the mat. Deadly Game. With Kelly down, Davis makes the cover. 1. 2. Kickout. Davis slams the mat in disbelief as the crowd cheers, and barks a few words at the ref. He brings Kelly back up, trying to figure out what to do next. Davis grabs Kelly by the head, going for one of his old finishers, the Baad Ass Bottom Line DDT. Kelly, however, uses his sheer power to lift Davis up and throw him backwards. Davis lands on his feet and charges at Kelly, who blasts him full in the face with a big boot. "POWERBOOOOOOOOMB! AGAIN!" Kelly wastes no time in bringing Davis right back up, preparing to end it. He sets Davis up, lifts him, and hurls him down to the mat. Eradication. Kelly makes the cover. 1. 2. 3. The crowd explodes, and Kelly's arm is raised as he is declared the victor. Kelly celebrates a little, walking around and playing to the crowd, then sees Ruben Ross at the announce table and leans over the ropes, pointing at him and by extension his belt. Ross just gestures for Kelly to bring it on, and Kelly takes the invitation by stepping over the top to the apron, hopping to the floor, and trading words on a bit more up-close-and-personal level with Ross. Inside the ring, Davis is slowly getting to his feet... Then, he is hammered from behind by a wooden baseball bat wielded by none other than Scott Slugger. The crowd goes nuts as Slugger, who came from the crowd, stands over Davis. Slugger is apparently back from his concussion much sooner than expected, and he rolls from the ring and grabs a microphone, steering well clear of the continuing Ross/Kelly argument as he does so. Slugger rolls back into the ring and stands to the side of Davis. "Hey, Davis! Betcha didn't expect to see me back so soon, did you? Well you should've. You should know by now a nothing, a nobody like you can't keep me down for long. But I'm not here to remind you of your stupidity, I'm here to issue a challenge. You. Me. Next week." By now, Davis has regained consciousness and crawled to the other side of the ring, where he, still feeling the effects of the bat, uses the ropes to pull himself up and gestures for a mic, which is handed to him. "Slugger... you son of a bitch... I'll accept your challenge, and I'll go you one better if you've got the balls. Our match next week... let's make it... a career match." The crowd gasps. "I've had enough of your shit. You ruined the FTfWo, you've cost me countless matches, titles, you've done more to me than anybody else in the fWo right now. And I'm gonna take this into my own hands and do something about. You know, my birthday's next Saturday. I can think of no better birthday present than to be rid of you... once and for ALL!" On the last word, Davis drops the mic and charges at Slugger, who side-steps him. Davis' momentum carries him into the ropes, and Slugger swings the bat baseball-style into Davis' gut as Davis comes off. He then executes the Curveball, driving Davis into the mat to huge cheers. Slugger picks up the mic again. "You're on. The WGA will be glad to go down in history as the man who finally rid the fWo of you... permanently. Until next time... it has been your pleasure!" Slugger tosses the mic aside to cheer as "Forever" plays, and as Slaughter goes off the air, Slaughter gets one last shot of Ross and Kelly, still in each other's faces and trading words, then back to the ring, where Slugger leans down into Davis' ear and wishes him a happy birthday... Result: Erik Kelly by pinfall
