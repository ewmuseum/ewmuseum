Introduction As the teams merge into one, immunity is now an individual contest. Where the Survivor wrestlers once worked as one... now it is every man for himself... Change of Plans The audio-visual room of the Walt Disney Sports Complex served as the base from which all sound and video came for the fWo Survivor IV shows. Technicians worked hard during the productions to give the wrestlers the flash and pizzazz that Disney sought. For now, however, the least flashy man in the contest had taken the room over. Three hours before the latest rendition of the Survivor show was set to go on the air, Logic had taken up residence in the room, with permission, of course. Videos and binders lay scattered all over the room, evidence that Logic had compiled over the course of the contest. It was time to figure out the contest's secret. ... Fast forward to two hours later. "This is RIDICULOUS!" Logic screamed, emotion showing through. "How in the name of heaven are we supposed to figure this out? This is a promotion that's had a mirror universe, terrorist bombings, several on-air deaths, drug charges, and SPHERE! How am I supposed to have any CLUE what these three nutcases have planned?!" The three nutcases, of course, were Brian Cage, Stevie Roberts, and Frank Smyth. Former members of the Dark Riders, attuned to every aspect of the fWo. Men who had been involved since earlier in the year. So early, in fact, that it seemed that this plot of theirs could stretch until Countdown. Countdown always WAS the event where long term plans reached a conclusion... Logic just hoped he'd be around to see it. He didn't doubt that he would too much, even if he was turned upon by the competitors, the three co-conspirators still needed to keep the banished Survivors within close range. Whatever they had planned though, Logic planned on winning the game. "I've been going about this all wrong," he said, watching a tape of the March 15th show. Ted Tallison, Silver Fox, the three Survivor guys, Fallen, the masked not-quite former champion...it's all tied in somehow. But I don't know how...and to an outsider like me, it's not even close to evident." Whoever was leaving the trail of clues was making a cardinal error that many mystery writers made. The clues were too obscure. To someone who knew the whole story, say, Brian Cage, it was blatantly obvious. But to eleven athletes completely in the dark as to the greater plan, the greater story...there was little hope of unlocking the secrets of Survivor IV. At least, not yet anyway. Logic was losing it. His current guess for the identity of the mystery man was, of all people, former fWo and WWE superstar Cactus Jack. The secrets of the competition were bigger than Logic. Bigger than Team Gold, Silver, or Black. Bigger than Survivor IV. Hell, probably bigger than the fWo. That was what made it so infuriating not to know. But worrying about such things was driving Logic to the depths of frustration. It was time for a change in tactics. "Trying to guarantee myself a spot in the final two by discovering the secret is currently unfeasible. Perhaps it is a route I can pursue at a later date. But for now..." Logic reached for one of the dozens of remote controls in the room, and raised it, pointing the remote directly at one of the television monitors, which featured an image of Cage, Smyth, and Roberts. Men who were acting on emotion, not reason...which made them difficult to figure out, difficult to logically understand. The type of people that annoyed Logic the most. Click. "For now, I must focus on the contest itself. It is called Survivor for a reason. The winner will have survived nineteen other men. Up until now it's been a team game, but now, it is every man for himself." Logic rubbed his head where Steven Windsor had given him a painful reminder of that fact before his elimination. Cage and Smyth had babbled that Windsor was the key to something or other, but Logic was quite willing to bet that War and Famine were trying more psychological warfare. "Good thing too, because my team would have sucked," Logic said ruefully. It would have been down to him, Doctor Curiosity, Bantam Diablo, and Nick Santiago. Of the other three, Santiago could wrestle. Doc had given him a solid fight, and Bantam was too busy with 'team leader duties' to do much else. He had been Silver's workhorse, and he knew it. But he wasn't the type to brag about it like Osyrus. Logic wouldn't ruffle any feathers. The men on Team Silver, outside of the ring, were potential allies....and even close acquaintances. Silver had separated the wheat from the chaff in their votes, except for the elimination of Reno Davis... A damn shame, Logic thought, despite being one of the men to pull the trigger. Nonetheless, most of the weak links were gone now. And even though Bantam and Curiosity survived...all they had to do was find an alliance to keep themselves alive and give them power their wrestling skills could not. After the final Team Silver vote, there would be ten men remaining. The vote presented problems in and of itself, but Logic believed himself able to weather the storm. Regardless of the result, there was one undeniable fact. The fall of one of the better competitors was due. Logic didn't want that competitor to be him, but the other part of his strategy required him not to make blatantly obvious alliances so as not to alienate anyone. This was where things got messy. Making the final two was tough enough, but doing it in a way that you could get people to vote for you to win the thing was even more difficult. No backstabbing. No lying. Or, at least as little as possible. First things first, though. He was pretty sure he would survive tonight's vote, but next week's could get difficult. Allies had to be found, if only temporary ones...but maybe that was the paranoia speaking. Logic, in all honesty, had no clue where he stood with the rest of Silver. His standings with Gold and Black likely weren't very good though. Of the ten men left in the competition, he had beaten four of them. With ten men left, and just two more on that bandwagon... Logic would be done. It was a sobering thought. For a while, elimination had been like the tooth fairy, or Santa Claus. Mere legend. But now, it was real. It stared Logic in the face and made him shiver. An alliance against him, and he was gone. Hell, ANY alliance, and he was gone. Maybe a strategy of pleasing everyone wasn't the best gambit at this point in the game. Or maybe it was. Logic wasn't a mind reader. He couldn't see into the brains of Rana Venenosa or Neko Case. But over the next few weeks, he'd have to at least try to get to know them, try to figure out what those men he had not interacted with were thinking. The man with a genius-level IQ and a million dollar body was, for the first time in his life, almost entirely in the dark. In this case, what Logic didn't know COULD hurt him.
