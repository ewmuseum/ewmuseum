Introduction The road to Cyberslam moves on... 319:48:00... Jim Dudley looked out onto the bay and didn't feel the cold. He was the only person out on the deck, and in the cold February weather this was hardly surprising. Inside the ferry the rest of the passengers drank coffee, made idle conversation, and in rare cases took a look at the man standing alone out in the cold. Dudley saw his breath in his hands, but his mind was miles elsewhere. He had been the first fWo World Heavyweight Champion, but that had been years ago. Years ago when the promotion was much smaller and played on a much smaller stage... he had been the big fish in a small pond. But when the pond grew it no longer had room for him. Jim Dudley didn't feel the cold and if truth be told hadn't felt it in some time. The water was dark and black and inside of it Dudley saw nothing that resembled his future. If he jumped... perhaps someone would remember him once more. Because when you have felt the stage and the light shining upon you there is no retreating into the darkness. Not for him. Of course, if he jumped someone would likely save him and then rather than be the tragic star he would be the pathetic washed up former star who couldn't take it anymore. Dudley doubted if many understood the full nature of his situation, to be stuck in a life you didn't want anymore. Death is impossible and yet the only thing that could save him. He was startled out of his thoughts by a buzzing in his pocket and withdrew a cellular phone. The number was not one that he recognized, and after a moment's consideration he flipped it open. "Hello?" He recognized the voice at once, and he closed his eyes. He listened. He had let the phone hang in his hand long after his caller had stopped speaking. When it clattered to the floor, he didn't notice it. He sighed, and with his eyes closed he saw darkness... darkness and green. Nothing but green. "I suppose," said Dudley, "that the bishop must return to the board." Perhaps death would not be so bad... if only he had the option. But there were no options. Not anymore. Nameless It was hot under the black mask, his dark skin sweated under the cloth that made up his uniform. Once upon a time he arrived to the arena in plain clothes with just the mask to conceal his face... it wasn't essential, as many had seen what he looked like before without it. But tradition was tradition, and the mask was worn. Those days, however, were over. Nameless walked in the shadows toward the Walt Disney sports complex and found it strange that his path to revenge would take place in an odd, multi-colored building with his adversary very likely thousands of miles away. Well... the path to the completion of one's goals was rarely a straight line. Nameless walked past ring hands and members of the studio who said little as he passed. Someone had said something, they knew he was coming and perhaps some of them had their own opinions and their own judgment that they kept under their breath as he passed. Perhaps. When you walk on the path of revenge all else becomes white noise. Few understood this. With the bravado of angry, in-ring beatings and general shouting there is little that is serious about it. As angry as people get, as much as promotions enjoy their little plot twists and shadowy sadistic owners... it was all just words on a script. Some scripts, of course, were better than others. And then there was revenge. Few would understand it when it was all over. He accepted this with the same level of dismissal that he had given to doctors that warned him to take more time off before returning to the ring. What they didn't understand was that the ring was bait. He was no longer interested in collecting titles or winning matches. Every time he stepped foot inside the ring it was to be bait. To draw the enemy out. To make the enemy comfortable and slack of mind. To lull them into a false sense of security. Nameless smiled under the mask. The events of Countdown had changed his direction. Glory later, perhaps, if he still fancied it... but now, back at the arena, back in the ring he would allow himself to be bait. And wait. But, and here the luchador who now called himself Nameless smiled wider under the mask, to be effective bait you must show the prey you mean business. Nameless had walked into the main staging room of the arena and picked up a clipboard with the week's programming schedule upon it. There, near the end of Uprising, was his name and the name of James Renshaw. Nameless nodded to nobody. Renshaw it was. Let the hunt begin. Someday Flyer laced his boots.
