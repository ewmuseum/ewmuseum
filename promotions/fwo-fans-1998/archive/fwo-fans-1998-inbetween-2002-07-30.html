In which we learn of the fates of long-lost friends, and a new g The smell of the gym is a mix of sweat and leather that no amount of cleaning seems to do much about. In the evenings, a cleaning staff will assemble and go over every corner of the room, rags in one hand and buckets of solvents in the other. But no matter how hard hands scrub; dipping calloused hands into soupy water and rubbing dirty rags against dirtier floors and equipment, the smell never does seem to go away. In the small environment of the gym, the smell is part of the ecosystem and if it were to ever change it would no longer feel like home. Even now, dust hung in the still air. The day was hot, and the leather bags and weight belts were thick and wet to the touch. It was not a day for the usual training that would be heard in the gym in the early afternoon. The heat would have been reason enough for the inactivity, but in the gym that played host to the Southern California Professional Wrestling development league there were more pressing issues on the minds of the men who were present. "We're looking at two options here, and I'll be honest with you," and the man who once wrestled by the name Silver Fox took the grubby metal chair with a sigh. His days in the ring, in a national ring, seemed like light years behind him. Hired as a favor to the fWo management three years ago, his run had been short and ultimately to his liking. The days of wrestling a regular schedule bore on him through his back, his knees, and the scars that laced his face and arms like dirty spider webs. When he pulled himself out of bed this morning he faced facts: he was old, tired, and wasn't in the mood to do what he was being asked to do. But the faces that looked back at him dammed him more certainly than anything else could have. It was a sea of hope that looked back at him. Most of these people were kids, not aware of what the business was really like and years from seeing wrestling as he did. They always did though... but who was he to stand in the way of their dreams? Let them discover that all dreams turn into nightmares over time. A better man might have told them all certain facts of what their futures held for them, but the man who once headlined an fWo show (but only one, as the nagging voice in his head told him, only one) wasn't that man. "I'll lay this out for you, and try understand what I'm getting to. You came here to train, to learn the craft of being a wrestler. Many of you are far along, and a couple of you were weeks away from being called up before...," and here Silver Fox drifted off with a wave of the hand. Over the last couple weeks they had talked about it ad naseum, and he was sick of answering their questions as someone who was supposed to know something. But he knew little more than they did, and when the reports came in on the internet he was as surprised as anyone else. "Before the big superstars got too big for this business. Your chances of moving up into their spot has been put in jeopardy because of the insecurities and jealousies of some over-paid talent that haven't lived up to their over inflated reputation for years. "Silver Fox turned and sighed. Samuel Smooth leaned against a wall and smoked a stubby cigarette, his brow heavy with sweat and creased with irritation. This, like so much else, was nothing he hadn't heard before. "The wrestlers of the fWo forgot where they came from. So fine, they walk. The question is, what does that mean for all of you?" Samuel Smooth paused and looked around the room, looking at every face that looked back at him. It was a group of people he had worked with over the last year, certainly not as much as Silver Fox had, but he had shaped them in his own way. He smiled as he saw heads nodding with agreement to his words, and he pushed forward with what had become a well-rehearsed speech. "This business has been built on the hard work of many people who have come before you but were once like you. Young, untested, but with a passion for this industry that told them that nothing would stand in their way to get to the top. To get to the so-called big time. Well, for many the fWo was that big time, and when you were in the ring with thousands of fans around you, cameras pointed at you, lights shining down on you and everyone hanging on your every word... well I guess that was the big time, wasn't it? When thousands of people chant your time, you remember it don't you? Bet your ass you do. "Silver Fox sat silently and let Smooth talk. He had come to understand the differences between himself and Smooth over the course of the year Smooth had been assigned to the developmental leagues as a trainer like himself. Smooth wasn't ready to be done yet, and when you go to the developmental leagues after being in the big time, chances are you're as done as it gets. Nobody comes back, and to Silver Fox that was a relief. To Smooth, it vexed him and tortured him... and he wanted back so badly he actually bought into what he said. "To some of us..." and here Smooth paused and motioned to himself, an action that brought laughter from the men that sat before him. Silver Fox groaned inwardly, rolling his eyes at the way Smooth never did seem to stop working the audience in front of him. "To some of us being in the spotlight is enough. We do it for the fans... we do it for the pleasure of doing it. Yes we get paid, and we are happy to get it. Making fWo money is a lot different from making developmental money, something all you boys know well. But to some people it isn't enough.""I heard," came a voice in the back, "that the reason the wrestlers walked was because they organized. That they walked because they wanted the same benefits that every other worker in this country gets... every worker that isn't in wrestling anyway. I heard they walked because they wanted to be treated like people and not like cattle. "Smooth turned and glared at the young man who had spoke. Joseph Eliot had a bag over one shoulder and unlike the other men in the gym was not in workout attire. Eliot looked back at Smooth with an expression of stillness that always unnerved him. Eliot wasn't like the others... Smooth didn't understand why he was in the development camp at all. "You heard wrong. The roster of the fWo wanted more than this business gives. Nobody is above this business, and no single person, or single roster, is this business. You know what you get when you start here, and you don't go trying to change the rules after you've signed on the dotted line. There is no health care in wrestling. There is no union, and there never will be. Understand? There never will be. That isn't what wrestling is about. "There were nods of agreement in the crowd, and Eliot only shrugged and walked out of the door. Silver Fox watched him go with a frown and winced at the pain in his back. Tonight would be a bad night... the heat and the stress would make it a four pill kind of evening. He wondered if Smooth would be so self-assured in his opinions when he had to be helped out of bed at night and found it a guilty pleasure to ponder. But the day was growing long, and Silver Fox didn't have much energy left. Standing, he cut Smooth off before he could sermonize any farther. "Regardless of the reasons, the fact remains that the fWo is without a roster, and a wrestling organization without a roster is not a wrestling organization. Now the board of directors has given all of you an opportunity, but in the end the decision is yours. Remember that the decision will always be in your hands, and that you do not have to take their offer if you aren't ready, or you don't agree with their policies. The board will not negotiate with members of the roster... if nothing else, that point has been made very clear over the last few weeks. So you have a choice, you can sign up as I call your name, or you can wait until you are ready. You always have a choice. "Silver Fox looked at their faces, the smiles and the bright eyes, and knew that calling their names was a waste of time. Not a man in the room would turn down this opportunity. "Kincaid." A nod of approval from the long haired man with the bandana wrapped tightly around his forehead. "Slider." The boy with the shaven head slapped his hands together and laughed. His joy was obvious and he grinned with wide, white teeth. "James Renshaw." A huge man, his muscles rippling against grease and his hair tightly cropped against his square head. "Spyder." A look of uncertainty from this one, but he nodded all the same and wiped at his mask. He had been on the indy circuit for years, and Silver Fox knew that he saw this as his last chance at making it big... he couldn't turn it down. "Marmot." The small boy grinned and whooped to the laughter of the others. A true cruiserweight, he couldn't have weighed more than a hundred and fifty pounds. "Rat... Thomas Francis, and Dog... Michael Dixon." The pair grinned and slapped hands. There were fewer and fewer tag teams in wrestling today, although Silver Fox had heard that the Orlando developmental league had more of them. "Captain Red." Older than some of the others in the room, he was still in his twenties and smiled beneath a mop of red dreadlocked hair. "Reborn." Fists pumped the air, and he sneered a smile that would have made Spencer proud. Smooth's star student, he was never going to last in the industry with his attitude. "Stuart Calm." A huge man, his frame would never make him a cruiserweight in the ring, but his agility was surprising and always a crowd pleaser. "Paladin." Another veteran of the indy leagues, the tips of gray were starting to inch into the closely cropped hair and he nodded with something that looked like exhaustion and relief. "Jordan Cable." The boy slapped him a salute, and sat down on the ground with a sudden rush of breath and excitement. He was going to get frozen alive when he stepped out of the curtains and saw the crowds for the first time. "Cali." The small man hopped onto his feet in a quick kip, and nodded his assent. A full house after all."... And Joseph, who made his intentions clear earlier." Silver Fox looked up from the list at their faces; a wash of smiles. Well, they wanted the big time, and through fortune or misfortune they were going to get it. They were all too raw of course... perhaps Paladin, Oktober and Spyder could have gone into the fWo right away (and truth to tell, they were the ones who were ready to be called up) but the others were raw... green... and Silver Fox didn't see any way it could not be a disaster. "And of course, your humble trainers Silver Fox and myself will be joining you in the ring to make sure everything goes oh... so... smoothly..." and with that Samuel Smooth took a deep bow and the laughter rolled across the room. The tension had broke, and all of them were heading up to the fWo. Silver Fox wished them well, and thought again about how he could get out of it and still sleep at night. It was going to be a disaster, but it was he who had trained them. To many, he was their father away from home, and he could not abandon them through this. Not when they were so close to their dreams, and not when they didn't know the true measure of what was coming. Silver Fox walked to the door, not wanting to take part in the celebration that would likely go into the night and absorb a local Denny's before it was all over. But outside it was no cooler than it was inside, and he slumped against a wall and wished for the thousandth time in the last two years that the fWo had a developmental promotion in Alaska. "They all went for it, didn't they?"Silver Fox turned and nodded at Joseph. He was sitting against the brick outside wall of the gym, and looked silently ahead of him. Silver Fox nodded, and grunted. "Of course they did. This is their dream... and of course they did. "A lone car drove by, loud music thumping out of the windows. The moment passed, and Joseph looked up at Silver Fox. "You're going too. "Silver Fox nodded. "I have to. I can't leave them alone with this. "More silence, and Silver Fox was nearly done with it then, ready to call it a day and go home to his pills when Joseph spoke again. "I'm going with you then. I can't leave you to carry that burden by yourself. "Silver Fox turned back to Joseph and regarded him with tired eyes. "Be sure. This is going to take more out of you than you realize. They were right you know... the old roster. This industry needs organization... the people are treated like cattle. "Joseph nodded. "I know. But I can't leave you to it. So I go, and Oktober get a chance to see the world." He stood, and regarded Silver Fox with the same tired smile the man more than thirty years his elder wore. "But I've got one question for you... why is this going down like this? Surely the board could hire established wrestlers... these people aren't ready.""I don't know," Silver Fox shook his head and clapped Joseph on the back, "but I'm glad to have you with me on this. Maybe we can find the answer to that one together. "They walked, or rather, Joseph walked and Silver Fox hobbled, to the parking lot and the old Camero that was what served Joseph in the glorious world of Southern California. Inside the gym the celebration was just getting started, but Samuel Smooth watched his fellow trainer with an expression of silent humor. "Silly old fool... he doesn't realize what a gift this is. I've been waiting for years to get back up there... and down in this hellhole there wasn't ever a chance. Their arrogance is our gift old man... take it. It won't come around again for either of us. And who knows? fWo World Champion Samuel Smooth sounds pretty good by me.""Mr. Smooth? Hey... phone over here for you." Samuel Smooth turned and thanked James as he walked to the phone. He couldn't quite get the grin off his face... and it was wonderful. Picking up the phone, he beamed at his students and leaned into the earpiece, one hand automatically coming up to cup his other ear so he could hear. "Smooth..." He waited, then his expression changed to one of more interest. Signaling to those around him to be quiet, the expression of good humor was replaced with concentration. "Yes sir, they've all been informed." A pause, then, "Yes... everyone will be there. Orlando gets the news tomorrow? Well, I'm sure they will be excited, although the trainers down there are... well... odd... but I'm sure it will be fine. "The rest of the room had grown quiet, and watched Smooth with expectant eyes. For many of them, this was a dream come true and the dream hadn't quite settled down into reality just yet. The worry was there on their faces... was this the call that was to tell them it was all just a joke?"Sounds good... and sir? Thanks for the opportunity. It's going to be great working with you... again I mean... great working with you again. It's been... what... three years? Four? Too long." Samuel Smooth smiled, and the room relaxed. Then, with a nod, "Of course... I'll see you Friday. All of us will see you Friday. "Smooth hung up the phone and turned back to the crowd of wrestlers behind him, wrestlers who were now officially a part of the fWo roster. "Now, now my students... baby... we celebrate!"From nearly a block away the roar of approval could be heard. But it would be nothing like what they would hear in four days. In which we remember two old faces "So what do you think about all of it?"Ryan Moore tossed a glance to the door of the diner, shrugged and sighed. "I don't know... I got some papers from the person handling the inquiry, but I've been out of the loop for months. I'm not even sure why I'm being involved with any of it... I don't have anything to gain from all of this really, I'm only listed as part of the active roster because paperwork hasn't cleared through yet. At least the leg is getting better... but other than that... Things had improved over the summer, but it still hurt and he was starting to suspect that he would always walk with a limp. One bad injury... at a house show no less, and here he was. He didn't blame the others, a freak accident could happen to anyone, but his scheduled storylines with Knight and Chronos had burnt out and fizzled before they had a chance to go anywhere. He hated leaving things undone, and in the last couple of years it had seemed like that was all he had to look back on. Storylines with Douglas, storylines with most of the locker room... and of course, scheduled storylines with Neo. "I don't buy it, none of it," said Neo, and worked on demolishing what was left of the bread in front of him. Ryan smiled, of all the people he had known in wrestling, Neo was most like his character. He supposed he should find this disturbing, but the thought was oddly comforting. It was nice to see that some things were consistent inside and outside of the ring, even if the consistency boiled down to an often sick and pathetically insecure man. "I mean... come on," Neo said, continuing despite the food in his mouth that was falling down his chin in wet, bready crumbs, "People like Flair and Ross are a bunch of pussies at heart, but do you really see them striking over heath benefits? What would they want with an organized union in wrestling anyway? The whole thing stinks Moore... stinks worse than what the whole wrestling world has come down to. I mean, you look around at these promotions today. Action? When in the hell did wrestling promotions stop having the word wrestling in them anyway? And don't get me started about the Asylum... that whole idea was stolen from me anyway. Who was the first person to try and introduce fighting, underground fighting with a hard attitude to wrestling? Me. I punked out that little Rottdogg bitch something good, and if the fWo would have had the stones to let me just do what I was hired to do then you would see all of those little Asylum punks on the ground licking my... hey, more beer over here! Jesus... jack offs everywhere. Christ on a god damn popsicle stick... "Ryan shrugged. Pure fighting wasn't his game... he had spent years in Orlando training to get where he was, and it seemed a shame to give up the art in favor of the brutality. But it wasn't his to argue with, none of it was, and tonight wouldn't be the night that he pointed out to Neo that his gimmick had been taken from a Brat Pitt film in the 90's. These things, as they say, happen. Life goes on. "I don't know, I'm not in the loop. But you're right, it doesn't seem as simple as that. My thoughts were that with the push to get the new television deal there just wasn't the money available to pay the talent. So Kelly goes and injures himself and somebody mentions something about a union. Okay, so the people holding the purse strings of the promotion get rid of the talent, using the differences and the straw man of a union to not negotiate or give us any warning. They bring up people from the developmental territories, which saves them a lot of money in salaries and allows them to close down the developmental camps at the same time. The country is in a recession Neo... so it all comes down to money. "Neo shook his head, grabbing the beer that had been placed in front of him with a dirty, scabbed hand. "See, but that has one big hole in it Moore... most of the fWo roster would have done it for free. If money was such an issue, most of them would have taken the pay cuts. And from what I heard the TV deal was done for a song. That was bringing cash into the promotion, not out. Aw, fuck it anyway..." Neo drank the beer and looked back at Moore with unsteady eyes. "One punch thrown at the network guys and I'm out on my ass without so much as severance. All before I got to bag that foreign bitch on TV. The last memory people have of me is of some happy ass pussy. I left being your old character. Fucking Neo Joy... I can't believe my career ends like that.""There are worse things," said Ryan, and his eyes looked distant, "so just be glad it ended how it did. There are worse ways to leave this business. Look... it's getting late. I've got to go, catch a bus and get this leg into some water. A torn quad isn't exactly the way I was hoping to spend this year, but you deal with the cards you're given. I'll let you know if I hear anything, okay?"Neo waved him off, and watched Ryan hobble down the aisle and out of the door. He sighed, and it was a deep sigh that spoke volumes. He was miserable... and somewhere deep down he understood that his misery was brought about by himself. He didn't know why he did the things that he did... when he was in front of people he just acted. Just reacted. And here he was. If it was all true, if the old roster was standing up to management and forcing them to give them the benefits that they deserved, well, he should be there. They wouldn't have him, but he would want to be there for them. They never liked him in the end, never really cared and why should they? It wasn't like he was exactly opening up the door for their kindness and respect. His attitude was all he had. Why didn't people just realize that... but then again, why should they?Neo tossed crumpled bills on the table and stood up with a wobble and a weariness that reminded him he was getting older. He walked unsteadily toward the door and out into the night air, stopping once he was in the parking lot to lean up against a car that wasn't his and light a cigarette. The smoke filled his lungs and he coughed, the air and smoke combining with an acrid thumping in his chest. God, he was getting too old. Too old for all of this. He dropped the cigarette and it rolled into a puddle, the thin flame going out in a wet rush. Cursing, he reached into his pocket for another. His hand found a piece of paper instead, and he pulled it out and tossed it on the ground as he continued his search. He had lit the second cigarette and had taken several drags before his eyes wandered down to the paper he had discarded. A stock statement, Neo sighed and bent over to pick it up. The market wasn't exactly doing rage business these days, and his modest collection of FWOE shares weren't going to put him on easy street any time soon. Glancing at the numbers with a sigh, his brown wrinkled as he noticed a line about controlling interest and a recent buy back of shares. Strange... but who would invest so heavily now with business so down? The thought brought memory, memories from last winter and someone talking about it, someone talking about what enough money could do... "Hey... got an extra smoke?"Neo turned and looked with bleary eyes at the boy in the flannel shirt and cap standing next to him, slumped in position and with a tired expression on his face. Christ but he was getting old... he didn't hear him coming. Neo shoved the stock summary in his pocket and sniffed. "Piss off boy, I'm busy out... I... oh, screw it. Here, take the pack kid. "Neo tossed the half-empty pack of cigarettes at the boy who caught it with one hand and nodded his thanks. Then he took the other hand out of his pocket and brought up the gun. "Now your wallet old man. Hand it over. "Neo looked at the gun with dawning realization that slowly ebbed into anger. This, of course, was why he acted the way he did. The people around him were assholes... you don't make friends with them. The whole god damn world was like this, you give and you give and you give... and they take. Every chance they get, they take. "Listen kid, you better just back the fuck up right now before you enter into a whole world of pain. Don't you know who the fuck I am? Don't you know?"Neo took a staggering step toward the boy, and the trigger was pulled. Neo stopped, stumbled, and looked down at himself in surprise. He stumbled, his hands clutching at the charred and smoking hole in his chest. Blood ran over his hands, and he remembered the feel, remembered the touch of his own life. He fell, his face in the water but it did not feel cold. The boy reached down and pulled Neo's wallet from his pants. "I don't give a shit who you are old man... nobody does." Tucking the gun into his belt, he fled into the night as patrons of the dinner slowly came out to investigate the noise. Neo's body was warm all over, and he smiled. They had lied to him after all... he always was told that death felt cold. He'd waited a long time for the moment that would soon be upon him, a moment that for years, he'd told himself he wouldn't see... and the closer it grew, the thinner his patience became.
