This was new to Temerario, he had not experienced this particular brand of pain before. His eyes wouldn't open, or at least would not see. White light penetrated everything, revealing nothing. Slowly he began to take stock of his surroundings. He was in a bed, and his arms, and the sharp, metal ache in his joints told him that he was hooked to an IV. His skin felt harsh and dry, painful to the air around him. He heard the low hum of noise around him and identified it at once, although his mind was slow and sluggish. He was in a hospital of some kind. He thought back, remembering what he could and trying to determine what the cut off point was. He remembered white light, he remembered Neo's face. He remembered cold, a cold that was all consuming. He remembered steel under his knees. He remembered his prayers, and he remembered her still, photographic face. Temerario tried again to open his eyes and found he could not. He made a noise, deep in his throat and heard the sounds of people coming near him. "In or under?" "Under. For now. The grafts are still in process, we don't want him moving to disturb them." "Dosage?" "40CC's, drip of five ounces over the next five hours, then an evaluation." "Done. Sign." There was more noise, and he felt movement very near him. He tried to speak, but then he felt cotton slide over his mind and heard nothing more. Above Temerario the doctor looked down at his scarred form with dismay. "This sport is disgusting. Look at what it's done to this man... and for what? No amount of money is worth this." "Yeah, well, people have their reasons." Yukio stood and glanced down at Temerario with an unreadable expression. "So what's the final word... I need to get back to the arena." "I know this is a second chance. I just had to do something, don't worry about it. I'll be there soon." She flipped the phone shut and walked to her car, looking back once at the hospital with an expression that the doctor wouldn't have recognized if he had seen it from her... but might have softened his opinion of the industry a great deal. Great Dragon vs. Tim Walden As Matt Panzer announced that the following contest would be a third-round Wrestler of the Year tournament bout, Korn's "Reclaim My Place" began to play over the PA, and the Great Dragon walked through an eruption of green pyrotechnics. There was little question who the fan favorite was in this match, and it certainly wasn't Dragon, for, while he was one of the most technically gifted wrestlers on the roster and a former Cruiserweight champion, he was also prone to brutality, and his single-minded, oft relentless attacks on a single appendage during a match had earned him a reputation that was less than positive. Ignoring the booing as he made his way towards the ring, Great Dragon instead focused on advancing in the WOTY, as his opponent for the night surely would be as well. As Wide Mouth Mason's "Midnight Rain" filled the arena, Tim Walden walked out from behind the curtain, and conspicuous by her absence was his tag team partner, Jane Matheson. Unlike his prior match against Thomas Harper, Walden didn't feel that he needed the added pair of eyes in this match, for as ruthless as Great Dragon was, he was still a man of honor, and Walden felt that he could trust Dragon to keep the match relatively clean. Sliding into the ring, Walden walked to the center of the ring and looked down at Dragon, his two inches of additional height and fifteen or so pounds of extra weight not something he was especially used to in matches, especially in the fWo. And as the bell rang, much to Walden's surprise, Dragon motioned for a test of strength. Fearing the worst, Walden refused, not about to be set up so early in the match. Shrugging his shoulders, Dragon applied a collar-and-elbow tie-up, quickly pulling Walden around into a side headlock. Walden shoved Dragon forward, both freeing his head and sending the Great Japanese Inferno running to the ropes, and on the rebound, Dragon caught Walden with a high cross body, earning a one-count for his efforts. Quickly getting back to his feet, Walden looked for a spinning belly-to-belly suplex, but Dragon blocked his initial attempt by putting his full weight down on his right leg which ended up between both of Walden's. The bigger competitor struggled for the suplex again, but Dragon broke free, countering with an STO that left Walden dazed on the mat. Working quickly, Great Dragon began to apply a cross armbreaker, but Walden rolled away from the move and back up to his feet before Dragon could even get a firm grip on his arm. Unfortunately for the Tax Man, Dragon was ready for him, and the former Cruiserweight champion blasted him in the head with a spinning roundhouse kick. And as Walden hit the mat again, Dragon stomped hard on his left hand, following it up with another pair of stomps before the referee backed him away. Walden made his way to his feet, shaking out his hand as he got to his feet. No sooner had the referee stepped out from between the two competitors than Dragon rushed forward, intent on connecting with a dropkick to keep Walden grounded. However, the Survivor 3 alumnus managed to catch Dragon's dropkick attempt and, arching himself backwards, dropped Dragon face-first onto the mat with a variation of a wheelbarrow suplex. Wasting no time, Walden rolled to a crouch and pounced on Dragon, applying a Buffalo sleeper. Dragon struggled against the move, trying first to reach the ropes. When he had no luck in that regard, he attempted to get his feet beneath him and push upwards, in hopes of reaching a vertical base; however, Walden's slight size advantage still proved to be enough to keep Dragon on the mat. As a last ditch, Dragon reached up and grabbed Walden's left hand, bending the fingers backwards in what was clearly a very painful manner. Walden shouted to the referee in protest, and the official warned Dragon to break the hold at the risk of disqualification, but he waited out the ensuing five-count before releasing his hold on Walden's fingers, and by that time, the damage had been done, and Dragon used a overhead wristlock reversal to end up behind Walden with the Tax Man in a hammerlock. With Walden's body between him and the referee, Dragon took advantage of his momentary opening, driving a hard knee into Walden's trapped left hand. He managed to connect with two more knees before the official became aware of what was happening, and when he was forced to release the hold, Dragon instead opted to turn it into a hammerlock suplex, releasing at the height of the move to ensure that Walden would land squarely on his hand. Upon hitting the mat, Walden rolled to the relative safety of the ropes, pulling himself up into a defensive crouch and waiting for what Dragon would throw at him next. To his surprise, Dragon instead began to circle around to Walden's side, forcing the Tax Man into a circling pattern as well. After feigning in twice, Dragon instead fired a kick at the left side of Walden's head, which, while blocked, still served its purpose, as Walden used his injured left hand to block the kick. Seeing an opening, Dragon moved in, looking for a takedown, but Walden caught him squarely in the chin with a hard knee strike, rocking Dragon's head backwards. Walden immediately followed it up with a hard right-handed lariat that further damaged Dragon's neck. Pulling Dragon back to his feet, Walden whipped him to the ropes, and on the return the Tax Man pulled the former cruiserweight champion into a tilt-a-whirl suplex, a move that CW Anderson had called the Ferris Wheel in ECW. Pulling Dragon back up once more, Walden executed a spinning neckbreaker before hitting the ropes and coming back with an elbow drop to Dragon's neck. Pulling the smaller man up a third time, Walden went for an Irish whip again, but this time, Great Dragon had enough left that he was able to reverse it. He charged after Walden, connecting with a high cross body block that sent both men tumbling over the top rope. Dragon was up to his feet first, and he managed to get in another pair of stomps on Walden's hand before drawing the ire of the referee. And while Dragon realized that there was little chance he'd get disqualified while outside of the ring for such a minor cheating tactic, he opted instead to pull Walden up to a vertical base and attempt an Irish whip of his own. Instead, Walden took his turn to reverse, sending Dragon into the ring post, but Dragon ran up the corner between the ring apron and the post, Jackie Chan-style, and backflipped to his feet. Unfortunately for Dragon, he landed in perfect position for a Walden rear waistlock. Walden attempted a German suplex, but Dragon blocked, hooking his leg inside of Walden's to effectively block the move. Walden hesitated a moment, preparing himself for an even greater amount of resistance when he tried a second time, but this proved to be all the opening Dragon needed, as the Great Japanese Inferno peeled back the fingers on Walden's left hand and, holding it secure with both of his own, drove it downward even as he brought his knee up. The effect was simple, yet effective shooting pain through Walden's hand that allowed Dragon to break free of the waistlock. Wasting no time, Dragon maintained his hold on Walden's hand and spun around behind him, cinching in a half nelson on the Walden's left side before driving him into the thin protective matting with a half nelson suplex. Not only was Walden folded up like an accordion upon impact, but he also landed on his left hand again, and he didn't immediately stir, leaving the fans at ringside who had been privileged to see the move up close to wonder if he had been knocked unconscious from the impact of the move or from the pain in his hand, which had surely reached an intolerable level. Regardless of the cause, the effect was a defenseless Walden, and Great Dragon returned to the offensive immediately, applying a wakigatame and twisting Walden's arm and fingers, which revived the Tax Man enough that he could let out a piercing scream of pain. When Walden refused to submit, the official slid out and checked on him, forcing Dragon to break the hold in order to better judge Walden's condition. But rather than stopping the match, the official, at Walden's request, called for EMTs, who first applied a layer of gauze to Walden's hand, and then a thin plaster meant to serve as a temporary cast. While this would limit the mobility in his hand slightly, it would also prevent Walden from sustaining any further serious damage to his hand. While Walden was receiving medical attention, though, Great Dragon slid back into the ring and began to taunt the fans, who responded to his jeers with boos of their own, as well as a shower of garbage from the fans close enough to reach the ring. As the EMTs returned backstage, Walden struggled to his feet, the slight tremble in his arms and legs a sign that his adrenaline was kicking in to try to block out the pain, one of the body's most natural reactions. Sliding into the ring, Walden rolled immediately to the right to avoid Dragon's stomp attempt, then shot out his right arm and tripped up Dragon. Regaining his feet, Walden waited for Dragon to get to a vertical base as well before swooping in and catching him with an exploder suplex. With Dragon stunned on the mat, Walden rolled him to his stomach and applied the stepover toehold full nelson that he liked to call "No Evasion"; however, interlacing his fingers was extremely difficult due to the gauze, and Dragon was able to break out of the hold and catch Walden in the temple with a hard elbow strike. Regaining his feet, Dragon drug Walden to the edge of ring and placed his left hand on the bottom rope. Placing one of his boots on it, Dragon grabbed the top rope and bounced up and down twice before launching himself into the air, his foot never leaving Walden's hand. Dragon backed away, only because the referee physically interjected himself. And while the official shouted at Dragon, Walden somehow improbably made his way back to his feet, adjusting the gauze on his hands as he did so. Coming forward, Walden took a shot at Dragon around the official, which prompted the ref to jump out of the way, leaving Dragon surprised enough that Walden was easily able to catch him in the midsection with a knee. Locking in a double underhook, Walden looked for the Due Date, but Dragon was able to twist his body on the way down, again due to Walden's inability to maintain a firm grip, and the Japanese superstar took most of the impact on his shoulder instead of his head. Both men were slow to get back to their feet, however, and when they did so, Walden managed to dodge an elbow smash attempt by Dragon, ducking behind the Survivor 2 competitor and hitting a belly-to-back half-nelson suplex. Penalty Plex. And with it came Walden's first real advantage of the match, which he parlayed into another attempt at the No Evasion stepover toehold full nelson. When he couldn't get his hands locked again, Walden ground his left forearm down into the back of Dragon's head, driving it into the mat. With his opponent thus indisposed, Walden used his free right hand to tear the gauze off of his wounded hand. Throwing it out of the way, he once again applied the full nelson, and this time, he managed to lock his fingers, trapping Great Dragon in the hold for the first time in the match. With nowhere to go and no escape, Dragon once more reverted to more devious tactics, fighting against the pain and ramming his head backwards into Walden's interlocked fingers, which, because they were locked so tightly, nearly dislocated his already injured left digits. Releasing the hold in pain, Walden rolled to his back on the mat, his eyes closed tightly as the throbbing sensation once more dominated the adrenaline in his body. Before he could even realize what was happening, the Tax Man fell prey to Great Dragon's dreaded cross arm breaker, and rather than risking further injury, Walden opted to simply tap out, giving Dragon the win in the contest and a berth in the next round of the Wrestler of the Year tournament.
