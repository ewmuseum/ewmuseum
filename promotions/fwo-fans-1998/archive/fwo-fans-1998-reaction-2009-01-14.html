Introduction William felt uncomfortable in the suit, but felt it was important to make the right first impression. He cleared his throat, and wondered if they would all still see him as he was; the boy who was stoned more often than not. If they would see him as the kid who once wrestled comedy matches against Ultra Violet. He stepped out of the small, makeshift office and walked to the elevator, riding it down to the main staging area just outside the arena proper. The arena was neither new or old; it was at the timeless stage where, in a couple years, people would start to discuss a complete remodel. With the basketball team now moved to Oklahoma, it would perhaps buy it a few more years. Key Arena was a good place to start again; it feel comfortable, if not elegant. But then, there would be time for elegance later. As the door opened, the noise within died. Everyone looked at him, and it felt real. For the first time, it felt completely and frighteningly real. It was his show now… it was showtime. Around the room faces stared at him. High Flyer, alone and yet dominating the room, a few steps away from Brawn, a new face who looked slightly ill. Mike Bear, returned from so long ago and strapped down with handlers on either side of him. Keith Scott Zimmerman. One Eye. Lusus. Katsuro. Eli Flair, towering over most of them. Deacon, a few steps away. The Codemaster, his huge mask under one arm. Tallison and Ivy, standing close together much as they had the night it ended. Ted Tallison turned to Ivy. “You were right,” he said quietly, “it isn’t over.” In spite of herself, she smiled. For the brief moment, she could just enjoy the moment of rebirth. Clearing his throat, William “Kid Cool” Beaven found the words easier than he would have expected. “Well… it’s good to see all of you. Old friends and new friends. It has been a very long three years… but in less than two weeks we are going to walk out onto that stage and be what we are again. I think all of you know I’m not any good at speeches; but hopefully I can do us proud. I don’t think that eight years ago any of you would have seen me as the guy pulling all of this together. Years ago the FWO started something... my father and a group of individuals started a promotion that, over the years, entertained millions around the world. Most of the people here were not present on that day…” William looked at the Flying Frenchie and nodded, the only original member of that first roster who was with them today. If Pierre had more to say he decided now wasn’t the time, but even despite his bravado a smile played at the corner of his mouth. His place was one of honor… one that nobody could take away from him. “…now, it’s been three long years since the world has heard of us in a way that wasn’t associated with violence, crime, and destruction. The FWO’s name has been burned, but we are not done, not out, and we’ve been absent from the world for too long. Just a few years ago nobody talked about professional wrestling without recognizing the biggest and best in the world. It wasn’t just bragging… it was a fact. We were the best, and we will be the best again. Not next year, not tomorrow, but today. Right now. In about…” William glanced at his watch. As if this was a cue, music could be heard from outside, and through it cheers. William looked back at the room. Every face read the same… excitement because it was real now. Cameras were rolling, the packaged theme was playing, and the FWO was very firmly back. “We’re back.” The FWO World Heavyweight Championship William Buckley walked to the ring, a microphone in one hand and a briefcase in the other, and looked at the crowd that were here to see it all begin again. He pointed, smiled, and knew that this was just the edge of the sword; thousands more were watching at home. But those that were here were the faithful, and the curious. They wore FWO shirts, old and new, and even an Erik Kelly shirt could be seen from the third row. It was best to go without the pyro. It could come later, but given the fire and general destruction it was easier to clear with the building officials and the media would have nothing to complain about. The lights were good; blazing a path as smoke drifted around his feet. On the speakers the show theme was winding down; Saliva’s “Ladies and Gentlemen” was fitting. He climbed into the ring, and raised his fists to the crowd surrounding him. “Welcome to the FWO!” And then the moment where the cheers drowned out the music he knew it was real. All completely real, and he allowed himself the huge goofy smile that had entertained so many years ago. “You’re here… you’ve come to see the best, the very best, wrestling right here in this very ring. Tonight you will not leave disappointed, because tonight you will see not only the best wrestling in the world, not only the most exciting superstars the world has to offer, but you will see the best of the best, the top of the ladder, the single most coveted prize in the world… this prize right here.” He let the briefcase latch fall open, and pulled from it the object of some of the most amazing and physically brutal contests the world of wrestling had ever seen. The FWO World Heavyweight Championship. He held it up, raising it above his head as around him flashbulbs popped as a thousand blinding white suns. He slowly turned, giving everyone in the arena a look at it and then lowered it to his shoulder. He felt good there, and suddenly Kid Cool wished he wasn’t standing here in this suit, but instead lacing up his boots and getting ready to fight to keep it. The belt had that effect on people; Jim Dudley had let it cost him everything before he had died, and this moment, in front of the crowd with the belt over his shoulder he started to understand why. The title meant something; it meant everything. But it was not his, not this night, and Kid Cool raised the microphone once more. “Tonight, in this very ring, you will see the FWO World Heavyweight Championship defended by the last… and current… FWO World Heavyweight Champion. Ladies and gentlemen, your World Heavyweight Champion High Flyer!” Once more music blared over the speakers, and the spotlights found a man standing in the smoke that filled the entrance. High Flyer, bathed in green and white light, ran a hand through wet hair and looked at the ring with the intensity that only he possessed. Time had done nothing but transcend High Flyer into a legend; the man had climbed to myth as he walked slowly to the ring. He glanced from side to side, taking his time as he walked to his rightly earned championship. Climbing the steps he paused and looked around him once more, the moment bigger than the man. He approached William, who pulled the belt from his shoulder and placed it in High Flyer’s hands, the title once more back where it belonged. Once more they were obscured in lights from cameras; for in hours there would be hundreds of blogs with photos from the event. Photos from fans that were there. It was history. As High Flyer secured the belt around his waist, William once more spoke to the crowd. “Tonight, in this ring, the FWO World Heavyweight Champion, will not only wrestle but will defend his World Heavyweight Championship against a former champion, a man who in 2003 took the FWO to new levels and was honored with the title of Wrestler of the Year from PWI and the Torch, the masked luchador… Spyder!” Rammstien played over the speakers as a beam of red hit the entrance. In the ring High Flyer’s eyes narrowed; he would not be beaten on this night but he wanted to look his opponent in the eyes. Smoke poured over the makeshift ramp, but the light shone only on darkness. The moment came and went, and then finally a form was seen moving through the smoke, but it was quickly clear that it was not Spyder, but an unmasked man; Ted Tallison. Once the former tag team wrestler Thumper, Ted had worked as a backstage hand for the last several years. He stopped just outside the entrance and pointed to Flyer. He was also holding a microphone, and for the moment but William and Flyer looked back with the same suspicious expression. Without removing his eyes from Tallison, Flyer whispered to William, inaudibly mouthing "Is he lost?" “Spyder happily accepts the opportunity to once again win the championship from, let’s face it, an undeserving champion who would be better placed inside of the Women’s division if the FWO had one. But to give Flyer an opportunity to scout his physique? To get an unfair advantage of what he’s facing? Spyder doesn’t think so… so High Flyer, you enjoy the next hour and a half you’re World Champion, because you’re just a transitional champ. The man who lays flat on the mat and is the footnote that reads, ‘High Flyer was defeated soundly by the greatest champion in FWO history.’ Believe it.” Tallison dropped the microphone as Flyer slowly smirked and brushed the hair from his eyes with a casual hand. He leaned into the ropes and as his music began to play again the crowd roared them into their first commercial break, the screens advertising the main event… the fight for the FWO World Heavyweight Championship.
