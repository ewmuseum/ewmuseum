Silver Fox smiled a tired smile, "Ah old friend, I'll tell you a story..." I had been at the company for just two years when a peer of mine held a retirement party. This, in and of itself, was not unusual. Back in the nineties it seemed as though every week an employee, one who had been with the company for some time mind you, was cashing in the stock options and leaving the company exceedingly rich. Remember, this is a time when the technology industries (and stock options in particular) were making people who previously knew the concept of wealth only by collecting gold coins on Super Mario Bros. multi-millionaires. We all know what happened a few years later, but in that period of time smiles were wide, faces bright and fully-vested employees were dropping off like flies. This man had been with the company for fifteen years. He had a photo in his office of a small group of people smiling around a conference table. In the photo were the very well-known company legends, many of whom had long since left for greener (no pun intended) pastures. He was a nice enough guy, personable and amazingly bright, but like many employees who had seen the company nearly from birth he had a slightly dazed air about him. It was similar to the state of shock you might see from someone who has just had a car accident where nobody was hurt. You might describe it as feeling that there had been a near miss somewhere along the line, and that in a few hours you would be aware of how amazingly lucky you were. This guy was like that, but on the day of his retirement wasn't going through the same dog and pony show that others had. There were no slide show presentations or big posters of the employee's face, beaming, hanging from the walls. This was a low key event; outside of the building in one of the groomed park areas with buckets of beer and chips collected on small tables that had been taken outside and set on the grass. There were no speeches, and people were simply milling around and talking casually to one another. The man of the hour sat against the wall and chatted amicably with people who wandered up to greet him, shaking hands and accepting their well wishes in good grace. After an hour the party began to unwind as people grabbed 'another beer for the road' and headed home. It was an unusually mild day in Redmond, there was no rain and the late afternoon weather was only slightly breezy and comfortable. Thinking it would be a good idea to go over and express my own goodwill sentiments, I headed to the man of honor, who was unattended and looked lost in thought. At this point in my career... my corporate career mind, which was a lot different from the wrestling schools I can tell you... I had settled from the early days of being mystified by the gross amount of wealth that was tossed around seemingly indiscriminately. I had been on a few business trips and become comfortable with the impact the company had on the global corporate culture. I had started low in the company, and was working my way up. I was never the personality type to look at the org chart and plot my next advancement by any means, but I wanted to succeed and to prove to others what I could do. This is often the attitude from those just out of college... it isn't like having a chip on your shoulder, but like wanting to take the chip off your shoulder and show it to anyone who might wander nearby. This is bright, eager-eyed nature of humanity that I suspect everyone has had at some point in their lives before it is replaced with a wiser and perhaps more accurate view of the world around them. I sat down next to the man of the hour and smiled at him. I remember mentioning something about how he would be missed, and that how strange it was to have so much knowledge and experience leaving the company. The company was big on what it called 'transferal of knowledge' in those days, which from a corporate perspective meant ensuring that the loss of an employee would not mean the loss of critical information. At the time several employees joked about it being the company's way of ensuring that everyone was ultimately replaceable... a joke that was always told with bright eyes and slightly nervous laughter. One ... Two ... Three ... Four ... Five The fans counted. Brand Frontier counted. Everybody counted except for the referee, who was still arguing with the senior official at ringside. Exasperated, Brand got to his feet and pulled on the referee's shoulder. "Call the match!" he yelled. "I'm doing the best I can!" Rusty yelled back. "You do your job, I'll do mine!" The senior official climbed up on the ring apron. Things looked like they were breaking down quickly. He started to step into the ring, but the Edwards restrained him and shouted for him to get out of the ring. The senior official, not wanting to disrupt the match any further, complied. The referee watched him leave. Frustrated, Brand turned around - - low blow by Creed. Vic lifted Brand into the air as he stood beside the World Title belt that Rusty was good enough to leave in the ring. Power Trip. On the belt. The referee turned just after Creed kicked the belt out of the ring. Vic hooked Brand for the pin. ... One, Vic put both feet on the ropes. ... Two, Vic grabbed a handful of tights. ... Three. Garbage began to fly. The referee was stuck in the side of the head with a large paper cup of beer. Or at least, it looked like beer. Paper, shredded programs, popcorn, shoes, everything not nailed down started flying through the air. Neither Creed nor the referee wasted any time in getting out of the ring. The ref raised Creed's hand, but the big Alaskan yanked it away and snatched his belt. He quickly began hopping up the ramp, trying his best not to get hit with any heavy flying objects. Extra security flooded out of the back as a chair flew from the side and clipped KVC's injured leg. The crowd was angry and hostile. Brand Frontier had been robbed. One match was hoped to settle the score between the two men. But all it did was add salt to the wound. Unleashed was over. Result: Kodiak Vic Creed via Pinfall
