Introduction It is the start of a new era, as the fWo moves their traditional Wednesday Night show to Thursday... and a new show in the form of Uprising. But show name changes are not the only things that the fWo must get adjusted to... with the new assistance from their network partners and parent company the promotion finds itself adjusting to a new and uncomfortable working style... New Show Order "Mr. Fox... Mr. Fox..." Silver Fox turned and spotted the young man from the networks jogging over to him. He smiled and nodded. The whole thing was strange and would take some getting used to. He showed up at the arena early to go over permits and forms from the city. Many people who attend the wrestling shows don't realize the kind of wrangling that goes on behind the scenes with the town government. Over the years efforts had been made to minimize the kinds of chaos that would go on before and after the shows... either with stricter drinking permits or staggered show ends, but city governments had been used to getting the big payoff from the larger feds to make things run smoothly. But today Silver Fox had shown up to discover that everything had been done for him. Officials had met with the town, cleared the permits quickly and from what he gathered used their political clout to bore down hard on the city council to avoid any kind of bribe. He was grateful, but now he had circled the arena nearly a dozen times on foot and was growing restless and uneasy. There just wasn't anything to do anymore... everything was so smooth, so calculated. "What's up? Got something for me to do?" The man, who Silver Fox recognized as Frankie, nodded. "Sign this paperwork sir, we've got a couple slots open on the morning talk shows next week and want some fWo representation." Silver Fox raised his eyebrows. "Yeah? Who's on tap?" "Not sure...," Frankie shook his head, "But Annette knows. Annette! The list!" Annette turned from where she was going over some pre-prep work with Geoff Green and walked over to join them. "Gentlemen, good to see you. Frankie, we've got the stage notes from the lighting department... the new beams from MGM came over and we're getting them set up for the broadcast tonight." "Great news! Fox, we've asked our production studios to help with some of the equipment... right down to the digital feeds we're going to use to broadcast tonight. Geoff's going to work with some of our trainers next week to go over the new process, but I think you should start to see a noticeable change in production quality right away. Now, Annette, who do we have on tap for interviews next week?" "Well," Annette said, flipping through notes, "We've got Kodiak Vic Creed, the champion, Brand Frontier, this Spyder person..." "Cancel that one. Replace it with Flair." Silver Fox grinned the grin of a man who feels he just dodged a bullet. "Noted...," Annette made a note and continued. "We also want to get Aimz and Jade on some shows next week. You know, really push the woman succeeding in a traditionally male dominated workplace aspect. We've had a lot of interest and there was a callback on the possibility of doing a couple specials regarding their career paths. We've contacted old members like Briana, VL and Stone to come on and do some commentary, and we're really close to signing Rachel Leigh Cook to do the special as Aimz." Silver Fox wrinkled his nose. "Cook? Not really much of an actor and certainly not much to look at... I'm sure we can do better than that. Hold on... what am I saying... television specials? You're kidding me, right? I mean..." "No joke Mr. Fox," said Frankie. "This is all part of the overall strategy. You're in competition in the big time now, and getting brand proliferation is what it's all about. Speaking of which, the numbers came in for Monday and while we are trailing it isn't as big of a gap as your number guys predicted. In fact, I'd say that with a strong media push we can have things in good stride before football season." "How is that going to work, anyway?" Silver Fox wondered aloud. "I mean, ABC has the whole Monday Night Football programming right... are we talking pre-emption?" "Not exactly," said Annette. "We're looking at moving the program fore or aft depending on the football slot. It will still go on, but we will use the program as a lead-in or follow-through, depending. Don't you worry about that. But we have been contacted by Titan Sports regarding the 'Ruahh' branding. They have filed a preliminary injunction regarding brand similarity to Raw. But don't worry, we're on it." "Well," said Silver Fox, "Ruahh isn't a name we're married to, right? I mean, it can always just be changed." "Changed?" Samuel Smooth's voice came from behind them and the trio turned. Smooth was dressed in a slick black suit with a flaming green plume feather hat. He had a gold cane and was beaming. "Screw that noise, yo... that name is off the hook bro... right up in this. Vinny can't handle a little shizzle up in his nizzle? Word yo, we be smacking that ass all over this." Silver Fox, Frankie and Annette looked at Samuel Smooth for a moment, then turned back and continued talking. "Is this something we need to worry about?" "No," Frankie said, "It's an expected move. Just hang in there and keep doing what you're doing." "Right. Speaking of which...," Silver Fox said, broaching the subject that had been bothering him, "What is that exactly?" Annette clapped him on the shoulder. "Running the ship! You're the captain!" "The captain. Right." Silver Fox sighed and turned to glance at Samuel Smooth. Smooth was adjusting to the changes a lot easier than he was by the look of things, but he still couldn't help feeling slightly useless. Well, perhaps he would track down Twitch and Thumper... or whatever he was calling himself now... and poke over ideas for the Development Communities. They had been talking about it on the road, and it would be something to occupy his time. Silver Fox grabbed a glass of wine that had been set up on the catering table and wandered off, looking for something to do. The Undeniable One....Returns "FIGHT! FIGHT! FIGHT!" All good things must come to an end....and it seemed Voran's absence from fWo television was ending after two straight missed shows. Indeed, 'Undeniable' Voran Xias came out from the back, his trademark scowl looking even angrier than usual as he stepped out onto the stage. The fans, of course, were on their feet and booing him as loud as ever. Voran raised his arms in the air, and then power-walked down the rampway, giving the "evil eye" to the fans booing him on each side of the rampway, before climbing onto the apron. He quickly turned around, looking out at the jeering fans, and then entered through the middle rope. He yelled for a microphone, not even bothering to do his usual top turnbuckle pose, and then reached out and roughly grabbed the microphone from the ringside attendant. After taking one last angry look out at the fans expressing their hatred for him, he pulled the microphone to his mouth and began to speak. "Since I know less than 5 percent of the fWo's general fanbase is actually smart enough to use a computer, I'll run-down just why I missed not only the last-ever edition of Wednesday Warfare, but the first-ever edition of Monday Night Ruahh~!! as well." Voran said. The crowd, of course, just booed louder. This certainly wasn't the way they wanted to start off the first Uprising. "The fWo management has had a conspiracy against me from the start. First, they refused to force Brand Frontier to give me a re-match for the United States Title- after I made Brand tap-out, cleanly, in the second fall of that match, and Brand needed an outside distraction to beat me in the third fall! Then, they cut off my video feed while I was ranting to that pathetic pile of crap Chris Finn. Next, they give me a different plane ticket for Wednesday Warfare, and surprise surprise, I never make it there in time for the show. And finally, they use a technicality to keep me off of Monday Night Ruahh~!!, forcing me to take a pay cut in order to get rid of the clause they used to keep me off the show. Now do you see where I'm coming from? This promotion doesn't want to see me succeed, just like all of you ungrateful bastards, but all great men have faced roadblocks before. Because, you see, that's all the fWo management- and the fWo fans- are, but a bunch of roadblocks. And roadblocks won't last long in the face of a giant fucking steamroller. Am I right, or am I-" But Voran was cut off, by that now-infamous chant. "FUCK YOU VORAN!" clap clap clapclapclap "FUCK YOU VORAN!" clap clap clapclapclap "FUCK YOU VORAN!" clap clap clapclap- "SHUT THE HELL UP!" Voran screamed over them, drawing another chorus of boos. "You people are fucking IDIOTS! That chant was clever and funny when a bunch of IOW fans in the Manhattan Center turned around my mocking of their "Let's Go Rangers!" chant into that one. But all of you guys doing it now is just sad and pathetic, at the same damn time. It's like when fans of the wee-wee used to chant "EC-Dub" for former ECW wrestlers in the fed- despite never attending an ECW show in their lives! Like I just said, it's sad and pathetic, and proves once again that the mean intelligence level of the fWo fanbase is somewhere between '-5' and 'Man, That's Really Fucking Dumb'." More booing, of course. Voran only paused to laugh, before he started to talk over them again. "But I've already wasted enough promo time on you idiots, so let's move.....backward. Let's head back to Monday Night Ruahh~!! for a moment- namely, the Brand Frontier vs. Lance Knight United States Title match. Now, I've been in the ring with Lance Knight before, and while I don't like the guy personally he's a hell of a competitor. And he proved it on that show, when, in his hometown, he kicked Brand Frontier's ass, winning a fall and going to a double count-out in the third one. Now, I know the already-established less-than-four-percent of you who aren't complete idiots must be thinking something like- 'Gee golly, Voran, that's a lot like what happened in your match with him!' And give those fans a prize, because they're exactly right! I'd also like to point out that, like myself, I don't see Lance Knight getting his well-deserved re-match anytime soon. How do I know that, you ask? Just call it a hunch." Voran turned to the camera and did a corny wink, drawing more annoyed boos from the fans. "Seems to me like Brand 'Mighty Big Coward' Frontier is ducking two men who have his number. And Frontier, you can't duck both of us forever. We'll get your ass eventually. But anyway.....now that I've revealed the REAL Brand Frontier, let's get back to you morons! You see, talking about the Manhattan Center has me longing for one of my favorite games ever! It's called 'Make fun of the losers sitting in the front row!' Let's start!" Voran climbed out of the ring, heading to the outside. He walked up to the barricade, and saw a man with a cowboy hat and a Ring of Honor t-shirt straight in front of him. "I didn't know there were cowboys in Rhode Island!" said the smirking Xias. "And an ROH-loving cowboy at that! All I have to say about your choice of shirt is that anytime a promotion can become cult favorites for ripping-off Japanese wrestling, the American wrestling landscape must be really, really shitty. Next?" He looked over at the woman next to him. "Bleh, ugly whore. Next?" Voran walked a little to the left.....and then, he recognized the person he was staring directly in the face at. "You're.....Scott Powell." Voran said, in partial disbelief. "The little internet peon who tried to make me look like a fool." Powell put his hands up, as if to plead his innocence. "Oh, don't worry Powell." Voran said, his anger replaced by a cocky smirk. "I'm not going to dirty my hands by beating up an overweight primate. No hard feelings, right?" Voran extended his hand to Powell, and Powell looked down at it. With a bit of hesitation, Powell extended his hand....and Voran suddenly elbowed him in the side of the head. Security began to rush forward as Voran began to punch Powell in the face quite a few times. Now in a full-on rage, he pulled Powell over the barricade, causing his overweight body to land roughly on the floor of the ringside area, and then began to kick him. Over and over again. He kicked at his stomach, chest, neck, and head as Powell laid against the barricade, until security pulled him away. As Powell got up, however, Voran managed to knock security away. Voran quickly pulled the mats off of the floor, exposing the steel, and then kicked Powell in the stomach, putting him between his legs. Before security could stop him, Voran crashed Powell's neck into the steel floor with the Cradle Piledriver. "Fight Song" cued up again as Voran stood back up, and raised his arms in the air. The crowd only booed, but Voran didn't care. He left the ringside area and walked back up the rampway with a smirk on his face. Voran Xias had returned.....with a bang. Tallywhackers vs. Ragnarok Uprising headed to the ring for a tag team match that could best be described as unconventional, or perhaps unusual, or some other word that begins with "un". In any case, it was time for what most observers believed would be a classic squash match. "If I Only Had A Brain" started playing, and the crowd cheered as "Crocodile" Davy Dingleberry and James Cagle appeared from backstage. To the amusement of the crowd, the duo was dressed in pimp suits. Dingleberry sported a furry, zebra-striped bodysuit, while Cagle was clothed in a garish combination of neon orange, purple, red, yellow, and various other colors. Each had what appeared to be pounds of gold chains around their necks, and wore large hats with feathers sticking out of them. The crowd's amusement was quickly cut short as the Tallywhackers' "Ho Train" came into view. It was a rolling, roiling tide of flesh - a half-dozen or more women, all of John Tenta size or greater, wearing clothing with an unfortunate tendency to disappear into body cavities in which it was not meant to disappear. The women danced around the Tallywhackers, their rolls of fat jiggling hypnotically. Cagle and Dingleberry danced with them, making their way to the ring. Finally the Tallywhackers rolled into the ring and shed their chains and hat, making ready for the upcoming matchup. Finally (thankfully), the lights went out. After a moment, fireworks exploded and "Back In Black" hit. The crowd erupted in cheers as Scott Slugger stepped out from backstage, smirking at the prospect of actually having to face the Tallywhackers. The cheers became a more mixed reaction as Mitch Wilson stepped out a second later, looking as serious as always. Slugger made his way to the ring first, deftly dodging the members of the Ho Train. Wilson was not as lucky, and was caught by a paticularly large woman, who wrapped her arms around his neck invitingly before he could react. Wilson shuddered and backed away violently, holding his arms out in front of him in a warding-off gesture. Moving with his back to the women all the while, Wilson slowly backed towards the steps and up into the safety of the ring. Wilson and Slugger conferred and settled on Wilson to start as the referee checked to make sure the Tallywhackers really had gotten rid of all their chains. As this happened, Wilson charged into the Tallywhackers' corner and hammered first Cagle, then Dingleberry from behind. The referee backed up, out of the way, and signalled for the bell to ring. It did. Wilson grabbed Cagle and whipped him hard into the ropes. As Cagle came off, Wilson went for a standing rolling elbowsmash, but Cagle ducked and continued off the ropes. Wilson turned to face him, but he had underestimated the speed of the big man Cagle. Cagle collided with Wilson, hitting a shoulderblock, and at the same time, Dingleberry hit Wilson with a shoulderblock from behind, effectively sandwiching Mr. Main Event, who fell to the ground. Wilson got to his feet, and Cagle met him immediately with a series of jabs from either hand. He followed that with a left and right hook, staggering Wilson. With Wilson stunned, Cagle went into an exaggerated windup, then prepared to unload on Wilson with a haymaker. He swung, and Wilson blocked it and, in one fluid motion, spun, grabbed Cagle by the head, and drove him to the mat with a bulldog. With Cagle down, Wilson went down on top of him and applied a headlock, then quickly transitioned down the body into a hammerlock, wrenching up on the arm. Cagle tried to swing backwards and knock Wilson off, but failed. Wilson decided to switch up again, then time smoothly transitioning to the legs. He set to apply a boston crab, and did so - but before he could lock the move in, Davy Dingleberry hit the ring and clotheslined him down to the mat to cheers. Cagle got to his feet and brought Wilson up with him, then set him up for a suplex. He lifted Wilson up, then drove him to the mat. Cagle then tagged out to Dingleberry. Cagle brought Wilson back up again as the ref counted to five, and lifted him up in the style of another suplex. Instead, he brought Wilson back forward, setting him down in an almost seated position on Dingleberry's knee in a sort of assisted atomic drop. In his corner, Scott Slugger waited impatiently for a chance to tag in, looking on as Dingleberry set to working over Wilson. Dingleberry grabbed Wilson and set to whip him, then turned it around right into a short, stiff lariat that dropped Wilson. Dingleberry covered, but Wilson kicked out at two. Dingleberry brought Wilson back to his feet and shoved him into the Tallywhackers corner, then went to work with a series of bearpaw strikes. He then tagged out to Cagle, who hit the ring and kneed Wilson, still in the corner, in the gut. He went for another, but Wilson lifted his leg and blocked it, then raked the eyes of Cagle.
