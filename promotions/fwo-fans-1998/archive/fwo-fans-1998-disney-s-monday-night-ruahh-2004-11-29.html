Introduction Previously... In years past the fWo has presented Mob Rules, a tag-team elimination style pay per view event. Tonight, in the spirit of tradition, the fWo returns to their once popular format on Ruahh... and what comes out of these matches could change the face of the fWo going into the always cataclysmic Countdown events... Something like a War "Why do arenas have conference rooms?" asked Eli Flair, "Seems like most'a the business should be held away from the venue." Ivy laughed as she sat at one end of the table, her feet up on another chair. Her left boot was unzipped and bulging from the ACE bandage wrapped around it, and she had one leg of her jeans rolled up just a bit to give her better access to the minor injury. "D'you realize how much space there is in this place?" she asked, "It makes sense. Besides, this is so 'corporate' of Dudley. Pull a meeting before the event, try to get inside my head or yours. Just let it roll off y'back, Wilson's got Halo ready t'go, and our mystery fourth might or might not be here. Let it be, E... let it be." Eli continued to pace. "I really flippin' hate this, Ivy." Their conversation was cut short by the opening of the double doors, and Jim Dudley walked in, dressed in an old-school DUDLEY T-shirt and black jeans. "Can we get this over with?" asked Ivy with impatience in her voice, "I realize this has more to do with us than you, but there's a pretty big event going on right now that we have t'get prepared for." "What?" asked Dudley, "What're you talkin' about? What's this about?" Ivy and Eli traded glances. "Didn't you want to see us?" asked Eli. Dudley smirked, his control returned. "Why would I want to see you? I see too much of you two as is. I got a message from one of the higher - ups' secretaries to be here for a 'creative' meeting. How hilarious." He took a seat at the head of the conference table. "Between the Disney suits and you two, there ain't enough creativity in the room to write a half - assed Christian Rock album, let alone brainstorm for the number one wrestling promotion in the world." "Is that so?" asked another voice from behind. Dudley turned around with annoyance in his face, which slowly drained away. Along with all the color. "Mr. Murphy. I---I..." "What you can do, Mr. Dudley," said Tom Murphy, Executive Vice President of Disney's Professional Wrestling project, "is get out of my chair. I called this meeting with Ms. McGinnis and Mr. Flair, and as commissioner of the Fans Wrestling Organization, you have a professional duty to be here for it." "Of course," said Dudley, with condescention barely masked by politeness as he stood. His eyes caught Ivy's as he headed to the other end of the table, and he shot her amused glance a look of death. "Mr. Flair, Ms. McGinnis," started Murphy, as he sat down at the head of the table and took a folder from one of his hangers- on, "You're probably not aware of this, but I've been dabbling in some minor information - gathering at the past few shows. Nothing major, just trying to gauge the fans' reactions to the things we've been doing, trying to get a feel for where they'd like us to go next, and the like. It's been a fairly productive beginning to what I hope will be a long-standing series of polls and reactions for this project, but at the moment I have some good news for you." Eli and Ivy looked at each other, and both looked - first at Dudley, then Murphy himself. "The corporation has decided," continued Murphy, "to put you, Eli Flair, against the fWo World Champion, Jon Crisp, at the next pay event." The room appeared to be sucked away from Eli Flair, as he blinked back the disbelief. "What was that?" "The corporation," repeated Murphy, "has put you into a World Title match." "What?" asked Ivy, Eli, and Dudley alike. Murphy looked at the three, one at a time. Ivy, however, was the first to continue. "Where'd this come from?" asked Ivy, "What about Wilson, who just lost the belt? What about Death, or Quicksilver, for that matter?" Murphy turned his attention to Eli. "I've gotta agree," said Eli, "What about the men who've earned their return match?" Murphy sent the folder of figures sliding down the table to the King of Extreme. "The way we see it, Crisp defeated Wilson, and last week, you defeated Death. To me, that puts both of them out of the running. And forgive me if I'm mistaken, but Quicksilver is nowhere to be found to ask for a return match at the belt. Besides, that isn't any of your concern. This opportunity is in front of you, Mr. Flair. What do you say?" Eli looked at the folder of figures that had been sent across the table, as well as the contract for the match that simply awaited his signature. "There are other men who've earned a shot at this belt. Brand Frontier is just the most prevalent amongst 'em. Why me? Why do I get this shot?" "Because we're being nice enough to offer this to you," answered Murphy, "The fact that you're insisting that others deserve it more is admirable but irrelevant. Either you sign the contract now, or we offer it to the next name on our list." "Who's that?" asked Dudley, expectantly. Murphy looked at him in slight annoyance, then down at his paperwork. "That would be the international superstar of You All Are On a Need-To-Know Basis, and none of you need to know." "We'll take it." Eli looked at Ivy. "We'll take it," repeated Ivy, "As Eli's manager, I'm authorized to sign his matches, and we'll take it." Murphy nodded his assent, as he slid the contract across the table to Ivy. She skimmed it quickly, and, sufficiently convinced that there was no hidden agenda inside, signed her name to it. "Anything else?" she asked. "Nope," replied Murphy, "just, do the best you can and bring in the people." Eli nodded, but kept his disbelief focused on Ivy as the two stood up and left the room. "Why'd you do that?" asked Dudley, after a moment of silence, "What's the corporation thinking?" "You don't need to understand our business strategy," advised Murphy, "all you have to do is your job." Outside the door, Eli tapped Ivy on the shoulder to stop her forward momentum. "What was that about?" "What?" "That 'we'll take it' stuff," explained Eli, "I don't get a say no more?" Ivy stopped, and exhaled sharply. "You heard him. This shot was here or it was gone. You say no, and there's no telling A, who gets the shot; B when you'll ever get another; or C, if you'd ever get another." Eli looked away, and nodded slightly. "You were looking for that moment of immortality," continued Ivy, "it's here. You were looking for another shot at Crisp... barring the improbably on next week's Ruahh, it's also here. So decide, kid... what's it gonna be?" Exactly. On The Brink Samhain took a deep breath and let it out. This was the first opportunity he had for a moments rest away from Sphere. For the first time in seemingly ages his mind was his own again. No schemes or master plans being forced upon him. He was a product of creation not by his own will, but the creation of a monster that controlled his minion perfectly, never letting him forget who was in charge. Yet, even at this instance of pure darkness and silence, he heard the voice calling out to him. "Samhain." It came like a shock of lighting running down his spine, but Samhain pushed it to the back of his mind. He was growing tired of Sphere always being in control. Samhain didn't want him to keep calling out to him, because it was slowly destroying his resolve. "Samhain." Again, it ravaged his mind and forced him to push it away. Samhain couldn't even be sure if Sphere was calling out to him, or if it was a product of his own imagination and mental instability that Sphere had placed on him. "Samhain." Night and day it was there. Always it was there putting Samhain closer and closer to the edge. The voice didn't even have a face, it was just a call. One that the shell of a man didn't feel like answering right now, but he wasn't going to be given much of a choice...when the master calls the minion follows. "Samhain...come it's time." He wanted to clean his tormented soul, but he didn't have an option. Samhain rose to his feet and let a crooked and sinister smile crossed his lips. He hated it, but Sphere had a job for him, a reason for him to continue and he couldn't deny that. "Coming master," Samhain whispered as he walked out of his solitude. Another day would pass as he walked down the path of losing all self-control, but he needed it. He lived for it...he lived to do Sphere's bidding. Faith in Craig Miles Chris Shepherd sat, sipping his coffee, wishing he was in sunny Florida and not New York City on a book tour. The "on again-off again" manager of Deacon, was on a sabbatical from the 'biz', but if this meeting went as he expected, that would be changing real soon. The unbelievable part to him was who he was here to see. He was meeting with Craig Miles, the man that had whipped him with 40 lashes on national television. The scars, not only surface wounds but to the muscles beneath, still kept him up on some nights. He had tried to forget them, but every night they had a way of reminding him just how sick the world could be. Well, I guess that just means they're like me... in need of a Savior, Chris thought. "Mind if I have a seat?" Chris froze as the voice reached over his shoulder as some demon sneaking carefully into your conciousness. "You can sit." Chris said, the frost on his breath made colder by the emotion in his voice. Craig sat and smiled at Chris. He didn't smile back, but Chris knew that Miles would've expected no less. "You said this was important, that it dealt with Deacon, so let's go." "You need to stay away," Craig said. His voice was even, controlled. "Well, since Craig Miles tells me to stay away, I'll see you at Mob Rules." "You don't understand, Chris." Craig interrupted. "I know this is odd for Deacon, the fans have always been with him, but I don't know how I know ... but what is happening ... should happen." "And after what you did... to him... to me. You expect me to simply take your word for it. Listen Craig, I brought Deacon into this country. I brought him into this business. I will not abandon him to a few fans turning the tide. He was never the talker, and I know that half of this is over his accent, his heritage, his differences." "You're probably right, Chris. But that doesn't change what I know." Craig said. Chris laughed. "You're a piece of work, Craig. After all you did to me, to us. Do you even believe yourself? Tell you what, you prepare for your match and I'll see you from Deacon's corner." Chris would cancel the rest of his dates, rework the tour to coincide with the fWo's travels. It would work out, it would have to. "Don't Chris... just trust me." Craig said. Chris looked him in the eyes, and somehow he seen an honesty. No, he thought, I'm just being selfish. I don't want back in that business. I want out, a chance to do something that leaves my past in the sea of forgetfulness. But the Deacon needed him. "Craig," Chris said, almost not believing the words as they came out of his mouth. "I'll stay out of it for now, but if you so much as do anything to hurt that good man, I want you to be thinking of one thing. The assault charges for that 'shoot' beating you gave me would still be good. I let you and Deacon 'work' it last time. You screw him over, I'll be 'shoot' dealing with it. I will own your part of New Frontier." Chris said. He stood up to leave. "The coffee's on me, so you'd better enjoy your coffee, cause if you 'shoot' with Deacon, I assure you that the jailhouse brew won't be nearly as good."
