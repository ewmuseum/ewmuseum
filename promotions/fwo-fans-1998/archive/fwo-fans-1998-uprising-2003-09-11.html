Phoenix vs. Emo Kids As Unleashed came back from commercial, just a trace of Saves the Day could be heard, Carter and Carraba were headed to the ring. The fans gave them a mixed reaction, not knowing what to think of the two would-be rock stars. The two approached the apron slowly, pensively looking into the ring. They were about to face two fWo veterans, albeit ones that were part of the new wave. Then again, the two weren't exactly top drawer competitors. Martyr and Rahl, collectively called Pheonix, had recently made a resurgance in the tag division, but it fizzled quickly. The popularity of the Emo Kids was steadily on the rise, and this match would give them an important step up on the fWo ladder. For Pheonix, the victory would be just as important, but in the opposite way. With careers that steadily waned into nothingness, another tick in the 'W' column would position them back in the right direction. Result: High Flyer & Koji Tamura Two-Wheeled Terror It was trickier than he thought, but David Cross persevered until he found someone who would rent him a bicycle. Not just any bike either we're talking all-terrain mountain bike with more gears than David had fingers and toes, small shock absorbers, and big knobby tires. The ride to the arena would be a great workout, and save himself a few bucks on a car rental in the process. Plus, it was good for the environment. Cross would not be emitting any noxious gasses into the atmosphere... other than those produced from that triple-bean burrito he had for breakfast. Crucifix was determined to be a positive force in the universe. But it wasn't very easy going at all. People behind the wheel of a thousand pounds of metal seem to think that they owned the road, and they would crush anyone smaller and more fragile than them. David avoided getting run over outright at least a dozen times by sheer luck, and the odd swerve into a telephone pole or two. The cars and trucks seem to have a life of their own, responding to his cries of "go around, for the love of mercy go around" with angry blaring of their horns and bumpers brushing against big knobby tires. Crucifix was positive all right. Positive he was going to become a bloody skid mark. After several blocks, David rolled up behind a dusty brown pickup truck. Even the gun rack mounted across the back window didn't deter the Hardcore Luchador from ducking down beside the truck and grabbing the side of the box. It might have been a tad illegal, but a little tailgating beat getting splattered across the grill of some monster truck with tires and small shock absorbers sticking out of various parts of his body. It was a little tricky, but David managed to keep the handlebars straight with one hand as the truck sped down the street. He was making great time and was soon only a few block away from the Alamodome. "Sometimes I even amaze myself," Cross said with pride as another bug smacked into his forehead. It was like he was surviving a personal struggle, the small mountain bike versus the tons of mobilized steel around him. He didn't have the size, but he made up for it with speed and cunning. The hot Texas sun beat down on his bare back, adding some color to his scarred flesh. To most people, he looked like just another suicidal health nut (much like people saw joggers in the middle of winter). But as a small car pulled up beside him, David knew his cover had been blown. Wearing only a pair of shorts and running shoes, it was hard for anyone not to notice the huge cross-shaped scar on the side of his face. The driver of the small car rolled down his window. "Crucifix!" he yelled as he drove alongside. "Man you're crazy!" He'd gone for months without people even knowing who he was. Now his "celebrity status" was going to get him killed. Fate, Karma, he'd curse them all if he lived long enough. Cross held a finger to his lips and hunkered down lower, his head resting near the handlebars. The car drove away with the driver giving Crucifix a final wave. David looked up into the truck's rearview mirror where could see the truck driver's expression. The man was obviously curious as to why someone would be waving at him, or at least to his truck. He began checking his mirrors. David took that as his cue to leave. Too late. The driver's angry glare met David's horrified one. The truck swerved into the far right-hand lane. David struggled to keep the bike upright and finally let go of the truck. The bike jumped the curb (thank goodness for the shock absorbers), tore across the sidewalk (quick blessing for the traction on the knobby tires), and barreled headlong into a large blue and red mailbox (a thousand curses to gravity, and one or two for the post office). The bike crumpled underneath him. He wouldn't be getting his damage deposit back. Finally the two grapplers did just that, grappled, hooking in with a tight collar and elbow tie up. Scotty pushed back as best he could but he had already lost the momentum and found himself being back into the corner. Not wanting to be in such a predicament he released quickly and ducked under Frontier's arm. Brand spun around and was met with a series of five hard knife-edge chops, the audience 'whooed' with each one. Brand shrugged the stinging blows off though and took hold of Bowman's arm with both his hands, ducking under SJB's arm and twisting it behind his back. Bowman winced in pain but threw a stiff elbow over his shoulder that connected with Frontier's chin. Brand released the hold and SJB twisted himself in the air and landed a spinning heel kick that sent Brand to the side of the ring. Bowman took Frontier's wrist and shoved him against the ropes, but Brand countered the maneuver by bouncing off, countering the maneuver, and pulling SJB against him with a straight-arm clothesline. Bowman hit the mat hard. Frontier looked into the audience but the wily Bowman took advantage of his placement and took Brand down by hitting a leg sweep. The two rose to their feet quickly and Bowman rocked the former United States Champion with a sure-to-be-perfect dropkick to the midsection. Frontier, however, used his strength advantage to block the move and take hold of Bowman's legs. Brand Frontier held Bowman's legs under his arms, lifting his legs up Frontier began to swing Scotty around. The Sacramento native tried to reach a rope, the ref's leg, anything to stop the ride, but he couldn't. Frontier tightened his grasp as the momentum picked up. With Bowman a good three feet off the mat now Brand suddenly let go. The look on SJB's face was the same as that of Rusty the referee as Rusty hopped over the flying cruiserweight as he went skidding out of the ring. Bowman's head swam and he couldn't get his baring, suddenly the audience were screaming in unison and as Scotty J looked back to the ring he say the 279 pound Brand Frontier flying straight at him. Too late to react, Bowman's eyes nearly popped out of his head as the Paragon landed squarely on him thanks to an out of nowhere suicide dive. Brand reached down and grabbed the long blond mane of Bowman and tossed him back in the ring. Scotty came slowly to his feet and began to spin around in an attempt to meet Frontier head on. He was too late however and Frontier pushed him into the ropes. He fell back against the arms of a waiting Brand Frontier, who took him by the back of the waist and hoisted him on his shoulder and down to the mat. With a stiff bridge, the Paragon went for the pin. 1... 2... A strong kick out by Bowman. Bowman flipped his legs over his shoulders in a backward somersault and got to his feet. As Brand Frontier stood and moved towards his opponent, SJB took to the air with another perfect dropkick. This one hit the nail on the head, and hard, Brand went down. Bowman ran to the side and jumped to the middle of the top rope, and as Frontier rose to his feet he was hit with a twisting moonsault. Capitalizing on the speedy attack, Bowman hooked both legs for a pin. 1... 2... The Paragon kicked out and the crowd cheered, if Frontier lost here tonight then Creed would get away Scott-free. As Bowman fell off of him, Brand grasped the Bowman's foot and applied an ankle lock. Once on his feet, Brand pulled SJB's ankle high in the air for leverage, inciting a moan from Bowman. Rusty, the referee, crouched on the mat and looked for Bowman to tap out. SJB refused to do so. Brand continued to work the ankle of Bowman as SJB raised himself by his arms and struggled to move towards the ropes. Bowman's arms lifted his body off the canvas as he pushed closer and closer. Finally, he got within reach, and held his left arm to the bottom rope as his right arm began to shake. Frontier raised Bowman's leg higher and yanked backwards. SJB's arm buckled and he fell to the mat. The crowd erupted as Brand dragged Bowman back to the center of the ring. SJB lifted his body again and fell forward with a somersault that caused Brand to lose his grip of the ankle lock. Frontier released his opponent's foot but instantly dropped an elbow straight on SJB's chest, very close to his throat. Frontier hooked his arm over Bowman's neck and lifted him with an inverted neck vise. Frontier then hoisted Scotty J up in the air and held him there. The fan's cheered as Frontier dropped him with a face first slam, following up with another elbow drop, this time to the back of the head. The Paragon flipped him over quickly, hooking a leg again for leverage. Rusty, of course, was in position and smacked the mat, perhaps a bit quickly. 1.. 2.. 3... Bowman's shoulder shot up at the last second. Brand pounded the mat in frustration but grabbed Bowman by the hair again, pulling him to his feet. Scotty pulled back with his own hair, distancing himself from the massive hero just enough to land an open hand knife edge thrust to Brand's throat. Frontier reeled back from the blow, gasping. Rusty pushed Bowman back, admonishing him for his actions. Bowman simply shrugged the warning away and rushed over suddenly to Frontier, sending him over the top rope and sprawling to the concrete with a clothesline. With a single bound Bowman leaped over the ropes, hitting a leg drop. Bowman quickly got to his feet retreating to the middle of the ring. Brand, on his feet, watched as Bowman ricocheted from the far ropes, intent on landing another high risk maneuver. Frontier waited until the last minute and surprisingly hopped into the air himself meeting the missile like Bowman coming over the top rope again with a standing drop kick. The fan's began another obscenity laden holy chant as Brand grabbed Bowman by the shoulder and pushed him back into the ring. Once in the ring Scotty quickly regained his footing and attempted a lightning quick sidekick to Frontier lowered head. But Frontier ducked and took hold of Bowman's other leg, lifting him up. Bowman regained his balance and hopped on one leg before jumping up and connecting with the side of Frontier's face with a snap kick. Frontier fell against the ropes, allowing Scotty J. to hit a Fireman's carry and take Frontier on his shoulders. The fan's jeered, The Bowmanator wasn't too far off. The jeers turned to cheers though as Frontier rolled quickly off Bowman's shoulders to his feet. The Paragon then took SJB's neck, placing his head between his legs. With a roar Brand lifted Bowman up, aiming for the corner. Suddenly out of the corner of his eye, Brand saw him. The fWo World Heavyweight Champion. The crowd erupted in hatred. Vic Creed stood at the top of the ramp, his Creedzilla muscle shirt stretched across his massive frame and the Golden Fleece draped over his shoulder. Scotty took the opening for what it was worth and wriggled his way free from Frontier's grasp. As he landed on his feet he spun around, ready for Brand's counter. He was surprised however that Brand simply stood there, in the center of the ring, staring at his polar opposite. Bowman grinned slightly as he hurled himself against the far rope, fully intending on capitalizing on the situation. Leaping into the air Bowman struck the back of Frontier with a hard flying axe-handle. Brand staggered and turned and roared again. His face twisted into a mask of fury. Scotty J. Bowman's pulse skipped. The mighty hero's fist thundered into his chest. Dizziness overcame the cruiserweight as he slumped to the mat, thanks to the Hammer of God. Brand kneeled over Bowman's body placing an open palm against his chest as Rusty slid in close and began the count. 1... 2... 3... It was over. Brand Frontier had won the first round of the Kodiak Survivor Challenge. You couldn't prove it by him however as he simply stared. Stared at the being responsible for his every heartache these last six years. The bell sounded by Rusty's instructions and Result: Brand Frontier via Pinfall
