Introduction Previously... The fWo was saved from the brink of destruction due to a jealous and biter former champion's attempts to assert his power over all he once was a part of. The promotion was saved, but not without the generosity of high level investors... a generosity that will now come back to haunt the fWo forever. Fix Travis Beaven sat in the large chair and felt the presence of the room. Across from him and behind the desk the owner of Tort Enterprises stared back at him. Mr. Tort was an imposing man by sheer history and little else. Old, dark circled eyes stared back at him more intense than the body language might suggest. "What is this all about?" "It's about closure Mr. Beaven. You see, several years ago I purchased a wrestling organization as part of my media empire. As Gerald points out to me, I was distracted. Distant from the purchase. I allowed my own private interests to get in the way of good business. Well, needless to say things did not work out as I planned. My purchase was snatched out from under me and whisked away across the ocean. Championship Wrestling League... well, such is life. Full of ups and full of downs... wouldn't you say?" Travis held his mouth in a thin line. "I wouldn't say. But part of the agreement of you being an investor... and do remember that you are not the only one... I wasn't about to put the promotion in the position of being owned and operated by a single entity... part of that agreement was that you were hands off. The promotion is doing well, and you're getting a healthy return on your investment. So I ask you again, what is this all about?" In the corner of the room Gerald watched with mild interest. Men in business suits trading bravado was nothing new in these parts. Next to him stood Bridget Summers, who fidgeted slightly. Wrestling was of no particular interest to her since her mild relationship with Mortimer had fizzled. Completing the trio observing this conversation was Richard Moore, father to the late Ryan Moore, who watched the proceedings with predinatural interest. Mr. Tort frowned. "You have other investors to be sure, but none with the control that I do. Do you honestly believe that I got where I did by playing nicely with others? Do you believe that I would let you just walk all over my gift... let you just take the money and be on your way?" "Your money is being returned to you. With interest." Mr. Tort smiled. "Indeed... but part of that interest has just begun to be collected in the last couple days Mr. Beaven. Please, familiarize yourself with this." Mr. Tort pushed a small pile of papers across the desk toward Travis. He glanced at it, then eyed Mr. Tort suspiciously. "If this is an ownership bid..." Mr. Tort laughed. "Please... such ploys are so overdone. An ownership bid? Hardly. The spector of the evil wrestling owner is so cliche, wouldn't you say? No, this is about getting a measure of revenge. I know your ties to the Championship Wrestling League. I know you remain friends with them, and since I cannot strike at them I shall strike at you. But I shall not do so at the cost of my own business... no, such would be bad business, and I would never take such a move that would threaten the return of my investment. The papers before you, Mr. Beaven, are signed and before the president. In less than a week they will be law. Oregon has such laws on the books already... creating a precident that made all of this so very possible." Travis flipped through the papers. "These are drug abuse laws you're citing... these prevent us from running in states without testing... you've got to be kidding. We don't employ addicts. This would have no effect on us." Mr. Tort's eyebrows raised. "Oh no? Well, then you have nothing to worry about, do you? But dear sir, I hope for your sake that you are correct because if not you won't find an arena in this country that will carry you. And it would be a shame if you had to run away like Terry did with his pathetic little CWL organization. Run away and I'm sure there will be people that will come to your shows... but how long do you think you will be able to have televised shows in this country without a US presence, hmm? How long will you be able to cover your costs if you're banished from your home territory? Long enough for my investment to get returned, I'm certain. But much longer?" Travis narrowed his eyes. "We don't hire addicts. We have nothing to worry about." "We shall see." Mr. Tort smiled wide. "We shall see." "Triple XXXStasy" Sean Stevens vs. Lance Knight Just as they had one night before, technicians hurriedly set the bases up at the top of the ramp, and moments after they scurried backstage, an eruption of green pyrotechnics shot forth into the sky, accompanied by the bass line of Eminem's "Until I Collapse". Even with Crusader on the shelf, the fans picked up the stomp-stomp-clap cadence of this song, and a letter appeared at the top of the screen. The single X flashed, followed by the silhouette of a figure. A second X joined the first, followed by a shadowed shot of the figure's face. The third X joined the previous two, and then all three disappeared, replaced with a shot of a very familiar pair of blue eyes. The fans roared as "Triple XXXStasy" Sean Stevens appeared at the top of the ramp, and coming off a victory at Countdown 3, Stevens raised his arms, eliciting an even larger cheer from the crowd. Even coming off of a tough loss last night, he was ready for this match, and the fans didn't seem to have lost any of their vigor in spite of Knight's victory. Stevens walked slowly to the ring, favoring his right leg, the knee of which was clearly heavily wrapped under his tights. The video screen went dark once more, as did the house lights, and a laser display projected the now familiar "K" on the ramp. The Survivor logo flashed on the big screen, and the laser lights spread out over the arena before converging again on the ramp as Lance Knight appeared on the display. The shot zoomed in on his right arm before cutting to a clip of Knight suplexing an opponent. The Roots' "Adrenaline" was in full swing by this point, and the shot panned to Knight's left arm, before showing Lance hitting Knightfall on Grendel from last year's CyberSlam. Knight himself appeared on the top of the ramp, and he too received a good cheer, very nearly equal to the one Stevens had received moments earlier. Knight raised his arms above his head, much as his opponent had done, but before the crowd even had a chance to cheer, he was forced to drop them back to his sides, before moving them in small circles in hopes of loosening up his shoulders, which remained tight from the match at Countdown 2. In place of the reckless charge to the ring he had favored last night, a more deliberate approach was taken, as Knight climbed the ring steps and stepped between the middle and top ropes. In the ring, he pulled on the ropes in his corner to loosen his shoulders up as much as possible, but in the back of his mind, he knew that he'd still be hard-pressed to deliver as much of his normal offense as he would've been just 24 hours earlier. Instead of patting the combatants down, the official merely explained the rules to the contestants. Because there were no disqualifications, even if the men had any foreign objects stashed in their ring attire, the referee could do nothing about it. Instead, he resignedly signaled for the bell and awaited the inevitable carnage that this match was sure to bring about. But instead of coming out with chairs swinging, both men moved forward slowly before cautiously coming together with a collar-and-elbow tie-up. Stevens struck first, twisting Knight's arm out into an armwringer, an intelligent strategy in light of the abuse Knight's arm had sustained the night before. Still not satisfied, Stevens pulled up on the arm, and then jerked it back down, before driving his shoulder into Knight's. Knight howled in pain, which only prompted Stevens to repeat the combination once more. However, the second time he attempted to ram his shoulder into Knight's trapped appendage, the former Survivor competitor was ready, catching Triple X in the face with a stiff forearm shot that sent him reeling backwards. Knight charged with a clothesline... ...But Stevens was ready. He caught Knight's arm and drove him to the mat with an armbar takedown, keeping the hold applied once their bodies had hit the mat. Knight's entire left side exploded in pain, but he gritted his teeth and inched his way towards the ropes, finally stretching out his other arm, which was only slightly less sore, and grabbing the bottom rope. Stevens broke cleanly, in spite of the absence of rope breaks in the contest, but he was quick to get to his feet and stomp on Knight's shoulder, his strategy for the match clearly illustrated. Wasting no time, he dropped an elbow onto Knight's shoulder, then kipped up to his feet and hit the far ropes, looking for a running version. However, all he found was the empty canvas, as Knight had managed to roll clear of the move and get back to his feet. Dropping his full weight onto Stevens' chest, he slammed Triple X's head against the mat repeatedly, until finally, Stevens was able to halt the assault with an awkward uppercut. Pulling himself to his feet, his jaw smarting from the shot Stevens had just landed, Knight backed up to the corner and waited for his foe to regain his feet before throwing himself forward with a severe lariat that nearly decapitated Stevens. In a surprising move, Knight made no follow-up attempt at offense, instead opting to roll out of the ring and grab the live microphone. "You had enough, Sean? You ready to say it yet?" Stevens' response was not a verbal one, as he simply sat up, wiped the blood from beneath his nose, which had started to leak when Knight delivered the forearm shot minutes earlier.... ...And he laughed. Enraged, Knight slid back into the ring, microphone still in hand. He lunged at Stevens, driving a knee squarely into Triple X's nose and sending a spray of blood in all directions. Tossing the microphone away, the former jOlt wrestler pulled Stevens to his feet and sent him to the ropes with an Irish whip. On the return, Stevens floated over and looked for a DDT, but Knight used all of his strength to counter the move into a high-angle Northern Lights suplex that dropped Stevens directly onto his head. Knowing the bridge would be futile, Knight released the hold and slid out of the ring once more. Grabbing the first empty chair he could find, he turned and slid back into the ring, only to be caught in the face with a flying forearm shot from Stevens that sent the chair flying from his grasp and Knight himself very nearly back over the top rope and to the floor. Somehow managing to catch himself, Knight rolled back in under the bottom rope and turned to face his opponent, who had bent over the grab the chair. Before Stevens could fully stand with the weapon, Knight lashed out, sending a hard kick into Stevens' afflicted right knee and causing him to collapse to the mat. Though his face had hit the chair, the impact was minimal, and as a result, Stevens was able to roll over and throw the chair directly into Knight's face when Knight approached for the follow-up. Due to his lack of leverage though, the chair shot had only served to anger Knight, in spite of breaking open a cut above his left eye. Knight retrieved the fallen chair and connected with a thunderous shot to the head. He followed it up with a shot to the knee that caused Stevens to cry out in pain, and then followed that one up with a move that drew a resounding chorus of boos from the fans: he opened the chair halfway up and slid it up Stevens' right leg until Triple X's knee was sandwiched between the backrest and the seat of the chair. However, rather than destroying his opponent's knee, Knight picked up the microphone and again asked Stevens to say the words before thrusting it into Triple X's face. Stevens' response was verbal this time, and in spite of the career-ending nature of what was about to transpire, his voice was unwavering and determined. "Do what you gotta do, kid," he spat around a mouthful of blood. Knight, more shocked than angered, halted, unsure of what to do, and that allowed Stevens to do the impossible: in spite of the pain it caused his knee, he rolled towards Knight and kicked the superstar's legs out from beneath him. Unfortunately for Triple X, Knight fell forward, causing the chair to constrict painfully around his leg, but the fact that he crawled from beneath Knight and pulled the chair from his leg was a true testament to his inner fortitude and heart. And when he made his way unsteadily to his feet, the fans gave him a roar that was befitting so courageous a man. Stevens smiled in spite of the pain before turning his attention back to his foe, who was slowly climbing back to his feet. Knowing he had perhaps one shot at winning the match, Stevens backed up and waited for Knight to get to his feet. Throwing himself forward as best he could with an injured wheel, Stevens brought his left leg up and connected squarely with the point of Knight's chin with a superkick, the Factor X... ...Only to see himself collapse. His right leg gave out moments after he had planted it on the canvas, and as a result, he found himself crawling on the mat in search of the microphone. He found it mere inches from Knight's body, and, pulling it to his mouth, he demanded that Knight concede the match to him. "Say the words, kid. No one's going to respect you any less, because you're clearly outclassed." Even with his ability to walk severely in question, Stevens remained confident that he was the better superstar in the match, a sentiment he would soon find out that his opponent shared. "You'll have to kill me first, Sean." Stevens, surprised at the resiliency of his foe, merely shrugged his shoulders. "Have it your way, then." Slowly pulling himself to his feet with great help from the ropes, Stevens turned around to face his opponent, who had only just begun to roll to his stomach, the first step in getting back to his feet. It was then that an idea began to form in Stevens' head, and with a quick grab, it was his turn to utilize the chair. After sending four shots into Knight's head to keep him on the mat, Stevens rammed him once more, this time in the ribs, and then began the slow climb to the top turnbuckle. Choosing to climb the inside so as not to have to deal with crawling through the ropes, Stevens ascended the bottom turnbuckle, and then the second, and with each tremulous step, the ovation from the crowd grew. Finally, he had reached the apex of his climb, and the crowd let out a dull roar at the prospect of what they were about to see. Much to Stevens's surprise, however, they quickly fell silent... ...As Knight delivered a crushing blow with the chair to Stevens' knee. Stevens collapsed immediately, crotching himself on the top turnbuckle, and he would have certainly fallen to the floor on the outside had Knight not steadied him. Knight himself then climbed to the bottom turnbuckle, paused, and decided to ascend once more, before picking Triple X and balancing him precariously on his shoulder. Knight paused momentarily before arching gracefully backwards, sending Stevens careening into the mat headfirst with one of the most dangerous moves in professional wrestling: A super backdrop driver. The referee could only look on as both men lay on their backs, neither moving nor so much as stirring. More out of habit than anything else, as he knew it wouldn't matter if he counted to fifty, the official began a standing ten-count, hoping to keep the fans in this match during this relative lull. Although he had been in the business for a few years, the referee still had mistaken the crowd's silence for a wane in interest, rather than what it was: a concerned hush over the safety of the two warriors who still lay unmoving on the canvas. At 7, Knight was able to stagger to his feet, with Stevens only seconds behind him in reaching a vertical base. Charging at Stevens with a clothesline, Knight was surprised to have his foe duck, sending him passing harmlessly by and into the ropes. Too late to turn and hit the ropes normally, Knight found himself crashing chest-first into them. Staggering backwards, he collided with Stevens, and once again, both men collapsed to the mat. Neither man stayed down for long, however, as Knight rolled off of his opponent and pulled himself up, using the ropes for support. He turned, surprised, to find Stevens already on his feet, smiling and beckoning for him to charge, just as Knight himself had done two nights before at Countdown 3. Determined not to let Stevens inside his head again, Knight approached cautiously, halting Stevens' tie-up attempt with a boot to the stomach. Seeing his opportunity, Knight grabbed Triple X by the wrist and pulled him back to a vertical base. Sending a weary Stevens to the ropes, Knight waited until the last possible minute, lowered his shoulder, and then dropped Stevens squarely on the chair with his most recognizable move: the watermill suplex.
