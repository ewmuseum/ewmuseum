Introduction Previously... Several months ago the fWo manager and owner, Silver Fox, was attacked by Ray Chavez and put into a coma. The fWo fell into the hands of the Disney Corporation, who over the months have attempted to enforce their will on the product. Many in the fWo longed for the return of Silver Fox, alive and well, to take the promotion back into the hands of those who loved wrestling... but mysteriously Silver Fox checked out of the hospital without anyone in the fWo knowing, and has not been seen since. Meanwhile, Brian Cage and Frank Smyth have worked together to put the biggest hoax the industry has ever known into effect through the national contest "fWo Survivor". With millions watching, Cage and Smyth have built national names for a small roster of virtual unknowns... and hold the clout of a newborn promotion in their hands. Across the sea, the CWL continues to run shows in Europe, but the owner, Terry Nodar, has been restless of late, his stress brought on by the condition of his wife, Penny. Searching for answers that are not there... Terry struggles through the days wishing that his friends the Lone Gunmen were once more by his side. Finally, somewhere in the lost highways of America the man known as Fallen sits and waits for his chance to get revenge on the final object of his insane rage... Ric Chronos... Hooking his leg around Eegor's Rana Venenosa executed the Livid DDT, driving the back of Eegor's head into the unforgiving concrete. Looking around ringside, Rana could find no sign of the Doctor. Result: No Contest They say it only takes a spark, but will the fire get going? Through tired and squinted eyes, Taylor Rogers observed his surroundings. The lights perched above him shined an obscenely bright yellow. Even in his disheveled state, they allowed everything around him to be seen perfectly. He saw the faces of passersby as he hobbled through the hallway. He saw their eyes glance down at his taped up ankle, which rested limply a few inches above the ground. He knew what they were thinking, for it was the same exact thing that he, Taylor, was thinking. He was a loser. A shell of the competitor he had once been. A week ago, when he was in her arms, knowing that wouldn't have mattered to him. Now it was a completely different ballgame. He didn't enjoy being embarrassed, especially when doing something that he'd done at least a hundred times. And that was exactly what'd happened. His own ring rust, a rust that probably wouldn't have developed had he showed up to the lot when supposed to, had been his downfall. Sauntering next to him, completely silent, Benjamin Davis wore a blank expression. Taylor knew how badly he wanted to smile. How badly he wanted to chuckle and say "I told you so". It would be any moment before his wants overcame his common sense. And then... "FUCK!" His body crashed against the concrete floor. Lost in thought, he had tripped. For the second time in an hour, he had made a fool of himself. At least the crowd hadn't been there this time to see it. Taylor would've never expected that though, since the sound of applause still existed. Was he hearing things? No, he couldn't have been. A loser, maybe, but a psycho, never. He hadn't sunk that low. Not yet, anyway. But what could it have been? Picking his head up, he found his answer beneath the same yellow light that he had seen the disappointed and mocking faces under minutes earlier. Continuing with the theme of d Taylor Rodgers vs. Logic Strength. Technique. For the most part, such things were lost in heavyweight professional wrestling as of late, with big men opting instead to use big boots and power bombs nearly exclusively. That was about to change. "Song 2" by Blur hit the speakers of the Walt Disney Sports Complex, and from the back came perhaps the best technical competitor in Survivor IV, Taylor Rodgers. A former champion amateur wrestler who seemed to invent suplexes as he went along. Taylor the Terrible rolled into the ring, his manager Benjamin Davis cheering him on from ringside as Rodgers went through a pre-match stretching routine, and tugged on the ropes several times. Suplexing the smaller members of the Gold Team was relatively easy. Even average sized men weren't much of a problem. But considering Taylor's opponent, the former amateur star wanted to be nice and limber. "Mars, the Bringer of War" by Gustav Holst was next to play, and its ominous tune brought out the 6'9", 290 pound Logic, the largest man in the Survivor IV competition. The Thinking-Man's Grappler looked at Taylor as an interesting challenge. The two men were on a nearly even keel in terms of power, while the advantage in technique belonged to Taylor. Logic could only hope that his perceived intelligence advantage over Taylor would carry the day, but then again, Taylor had Benjamin Davis to help with such things. Perhaps Rodgers was smarter than he looked? The bell sounded as Logic entered the ring, and immediately Rodgers shot in for the takedown. Before Logic quite knew what was happening, Rodgers had taken the larger man down to the canvas, and had a front facelock applied. Two points, were this an amateur competition. Davis shouted encouragement to his man as Taylor cranked on his front facelock. Slowly, Logic began to rise from beneath Rodgers' grip, getting up to his knees, and finally using his shoulder to drive Taylor back to the corner to break the hold. Logic followed with several driving shoulders to the abdomen, then whipped Taylor across the ring to the opposite turnbuckle. Rodgers hit hard, but still had enough of his wits about him to plant a foot in Logic's face when he charged in. As Logic staggered back to the center of the ring, Taylor stepped forward quickly. It was time to see if the technical master could suplex Logic out of his shoes, as Taylor had done to so many others in the competition. Rodgers wrapped his arms around Logic, and dropped his center of gravity. Taking a deep breath, he popped his hips, and lifted. Belly to belly suplex. With an overhead release. On a man nearly 300 pounds. Of course it was done perfectly, this is a former amateur champion we're dealing with! Logic SAILED across the ring from the suplex, landing flat on his back in the corner where his ill-fated charge had gotten him in this mess to begin with. Benjamin screamed at his charge to follow up, and Taylor did not disappoint. With Logic still stunned, Rodgers simply applied a waistlock, and lowered his center of gravity once again. Taylor dead-lifted Logic up from the ground, and while the crowd was surprised enough at THAT feat of strength, Rodgers popped his hips for a second time and SNAPPED Logic over with a vicious German Suplex. As Logic laid on the mat, back and neck aching, he thought to himself sadly that this really wasn't how he wanted to start a match, any match. Taylor, of course, would take this beginning any day of the week, and twice on Sundays. On the advice of his manager, Taylor dropped down for the cover, hooking Logic's far leg. One! Two! The kickout didn't surprise Taylor one bit. It was still early in the match, and he hadn't expected to get the win so soon. The cover he had made forced Logic to expend energy to kick out. Soon, Logic would be even more worn down, allowing Taylor to execute more spectacular suplexes, and eventually, finish him off with the End All, Be All. Logic didn't appear to be looking forward to any of this, however, and he finally stopped the early suplex barrage with a quick palm strike to Taylor's well-muscled abdomen. As Taylor regained his breath, Logic hooked Rodgers for a suplex of his own. Using his right hand to grab Taylor's traditional singlet for leverage, the Pensive Punisher hoisted his adversary in the air, and took him up and over. Now Taylor felt the pain that Logic had earlier, but to his credit, he managed to get up relatively quickly. Unfortunately, he stood up directly into a Logic clothesline that sent him right back down again. Logic attempted to press his advantage, but Taylor caught him with a knee to the midsection, allowing Rodgers to go for another of his deadly arsenal of suplexes. Using his deadly mixture of power and technique, Taylor Rodgers muscled Logic into a perfect gut wrench suplex. Taylor followed by delivering a hard elbow to the sternum of his nemesis, and covered his foe again. One! Two! Another kickout, but again, Taylor wasn't worried. The gameplan that Benjamin and himself had put together for Logic was working to perfection. Logic was frazzled. He hadn't taken this much punishment to start a contest, well, ever. Taylor knew that Logic was used to getting a fast start, and by preventing that, he had the Thinking-Man's Grappler on his heels. Taylor pulled Logic back up to a vertical base, but the big man lashed out with a wild roundhouse shot. Rodgers easily ducked the swing, and applied a full nelson. Instantly, Logic knew that his attempt to hit Taylor hadn't been the brightest thing he had ever done, and began a focused rush to the ropes, trying to strain against Taylor's grip to stop the impending suplex. Rodgers was strong, but he couldn't hold Logic back from getting his right hand on the top rope. Time for a change of tactics. Rodgers released half of the full nelson, using his right hand to fire hard shots into the hand keeping Logic attached to the ropes. Finally, Logic's grip faltered, and Taylor yanked the bigger man away from the ropes, wrapping his right arm around Logic's waist. HALF NELSON SUPLEX! Davis applauded on the outside, while on the inside, Logic clutched his neck in pain. Seeing Taylor moving in for the kill, Logic rolled to the outside, hoping to recover. Taylor had no desire to let this happen, and promptly followed his opponent to the outside. As Logic struggled back up to his feet, Rodgers applied a waistlock. Thankfully for Logic, a stomp to the foot of Taylor blocked the attempt at death by German Suplex. Taylor's head bounced off the steel post with a sickening thud, as the referee began to lay the count down on both men. By the count of six, Logic had managed to get enough of his wits together to roll his opponent back into the ring. Logic followed up by getting back in the squared circle himself. He had to get back on the offensive if he was to have any hope of victory in a match that had not started well at all. Lifting Taylor Rodgers vertical, Logic sat down with a piledriver, compressing the neck and giving Benjamin Davis cause for his first concerned look of the evening. Northern Lights Suplex. Bridged. One! Two! ... ... THR--NO! A power move on a man of Rodgers' size was no easy feat. Taylor's conditioning made throwing Logic around much less difficult than it would be for a man with lesser endurance. Logic wasn't out of shape by any stretch, but he didn't train maniacally like Rodgers was known to do. Combine that with the fact that Logic had already taken a great deal of abuse, and the Pensive Punisher was sucking some serious wind. Both men were down, trying to will themselves back up. It had been a grueling contest early on, just as most had expected. The crowd began to come alive, clapping for both men. Neither was what would be traditionally termed a fan favorite, but over the course of the Survivor contest, the fans that filled the Walt Disney Sports Complex had come to understand what the Survivors were about. Wrestling. Taylor Rodgers, despite being the man to take the power bomb, was the first man to his feet. Logic looked like hell, and Taylor the Terrible intended to capitalize on the situation. He pump handled a rising Logic, lifted him in the air, and turned the move into a backbreaker. Logic was viciously driven across Taylor's knee, and bounced to the mat. He looked like a beaten man. Benjamin Davis was beyond ecstatic. Logic had been a world-beater in the competition, losing only one of his five matches, and that was to a Reno Davis who was wrestling beyond anything that a man broken down by alcohol should have been able to do. But now, Logic was lying on the mat, body racked with pain after a half dozen of Taylor's most deadly suplexes. Benjamin doubted that he needed to coach Taylor from here, but he decided to make sure that Rodgers was going for the kill. "Come on," he exhorted, "finish him off, TR!" Rodgers nodded to his manager, the man that had saved him from addiction and sloth. When it came to finishing opponents off, Taylor Rodgers had one technique that was head and shoulders above the rest. The End All, Be All was a perfect weapon. It combined all of Taylor's formidable technique and power, and gave him a perch from which to execute his favorite suplex, the belly to belly. Logic began to pour forearms into Rodgers' temples, trying to break the grip.
