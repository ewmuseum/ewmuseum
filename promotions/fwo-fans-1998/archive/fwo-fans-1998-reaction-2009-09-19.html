the Calm before the Storm. Paladin had been sitting in the dining area of the arena for hours, twiddling his thumbs and nervously patting his feet. His eyes stared blankly at the table between his elbows as his mind wandered in deep thought. He hadn’t seen nor heard from his deranged comrade Krow ever since last week’s reAction. Normally, such avoidance from a loner would seem characteristic of his nature, but the association he and Krow shared in the last few months made the lack of communication suspicious. The Krow he knew at the start was a man of questionable mental state, but of a calmer, more calculated demeanor. A little known fact to many; Paladin had known Krow for years, but as no more than an acquaintance. He knew Krow has the upstart psychopath of the rookie division of his time. His actions were sporadic, his motives were mind boggling, and his demeanor was consistently daunting. And while the third of the three remained constant throughout his career, the others faded away with maturity. The Krow he encountered at the start of the One Common Goal was a Krow who was mature and deadly. He was a cerebral mastermind, who could destroy one’s mind and manipulate it to his liking at the cold snap of a finger. However, the Krow of recent events was different. The Krow he knew now was almost a throwback to the rookie Krow of old; sporadic, mind boggling, dangerous, and lacking strategy. This Krow was treacherous to their unified mission; and this Krow…was a Krow that Paladin…feared. In most cases, a man with no strategy was a man Paladin could easily annihilate. But in the case of Krow; a man of his caliber with no strategy is like a Rabid Lion with no cage; it could eventually die, but it would take a lot of effort – more effort than one man could supply. Joshua Listerin lifted his head for a moment and noticed the beaming glares of the FWO personnel, all of them staring a hole through him. His body remained still, but deep within him, every nerve ending was standing on end. He cursed the name of Krow beneath his breath, scorning him for his blatant neglect of calculated strategy and incessant need for unmitigated violence – a neglect that would, no doubt, cause a rebellious riot – even from the weakest of FWO alumni. Frustrations grew heavily in Listerin, as he allowed his angry emotions to brew silently while he sat in his chair. With the beaming eyes of an angry FWO locker room looming over him slowly, he tightened his grip around a mysterious piece of paper in his hand. It was a letter left to him this morning at his hotel room. Though hours had passed since its delivery, its message still burned a hole deep within him; it was a daunting message – one that he wasn’t sure he would like. He opened it up one more time, briefly reading it to himself, before crinkling sharply in his monstrous grasp. In cold scripture, it read; “Our message will be as clear as day, Joshua.” It was signed, “Your friend, Krow.” Joshua’s eyes sunk.
