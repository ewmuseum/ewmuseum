Introduction Previously... The fWo began many years ago with an event called Cyberslam. A champion was crowned, and a legacy was born. Over the years hundreds of wrestlers and personalities came... and left... the fWo. But through the trials and tribulations, one thing remained: the spirit of the promotion was always alive and well. Well, until the summer of 2002. Through a mix of allegations and rumors, the fWo vanished from the scene, with many believing that it was never to return. But time is the bringer of many things, and now, on the night of Halloween, the fWo prepares to return from the proverbial grave. Opening Fans had been milling around the inside of the Key Arena for some time. The expressions were varied. Some looked excited, pleased to be the first ones to see the return of what was once a cornerstone promotion in the industry. Others seemed skeptical... perhaps they had gotten a look at the merchandise stands and quickly surmised that many of their favorites were not present, replaced instead with t-shirts of Calm, the "Real" Alaskan Juggernaut or James Renshaw. Many fans looked pleased that the usual requirements of beer limits on orders was waved for the evening, and even though the event had not yet begun, interest was high into the sheer volume of liquor available. Outside it was cold, rain fell and the temperature was low enough that several fans were worried about the roads freezing for the trip home. But it was the electric sign outside the Key Arena that directed fans in from Denny Avenue that the fWo was appearing. Live. On Pay Per View. For many, it had been far too long. Finally, the wait was over. No matter if the fans were at the arena in person, or settling down in front of their television screens, it was a night to remember. The fWo was back in business. But even though excitement was high, as fans looked down at their tickets and realized that the event was now more than fifty minutes late in starting they begin to grow restless and annoyed. Viewers at home wondered when the show was going to start, as the exact same promo promising the "new generation" had cycled for almost an hour. Fans began stomping on the floor with their feet, with the restless attitude turning into an angry one. All the while, the beer continued to flow. Parents began to look nervous as drunken fans began to start small fights in the stands. Women were whistled at, yelled at, and leered at. In a small section to the right of the arena, a man with redish hair and an old Flying Frenchie t-shirt looked down in disgust at the program which promised no such wrestler. The ring remained empty. The fans waited, but slowly a dull roar began to build in the crowd. Impatient fans began to consider hopping the metal barrier and run into the ring. Something, anything to break the monotony. But just as one large man in his early twenties began to amble over to the barrier and start to heft himself over it, the lights dimmed. The effect was near instantaneous; the crowd began to loudly applaud, then break into an "it's about time" chant. The fWo logo appeared on the large screen over the entrance ramp, and the fans cheered again. But as the fWo logo dissolved into black, and an old theme began to play through the arena, fans looked at one another in confusion. Nothing about this was what they knew, and the man striding onto the entrance ramp with a smile on his face and a microphone in his hand was not someone they had seen in some time. Jim Dudley, the first fWo World Heavyweight champion walked casually down the entrance ramp, beaming and obviously comfortable in his three-piece suit. He pointed to fans, grinned, and waved to people in the audience. There was a small pop for his appearance; he was somebody they recognized, but his appearance confused the crowd and made them wary of what was about to come. Dudley reached the ring, and with a move that resembled the days of old he swung himself up and over the ropes and into the ring. Still smiling, he turned a complete circle and stared out at the audience around him. Inside the arena and at home, fans looked back. "Ladies and gentlemen, nothing gives me more pleasure than to welcome you... back... to the fWo!" Cheers followed, although they were tempered by confusion from the crowd. Fans waited to see what was coming, and after a minute Jim Dudley continued. "Let me be clear about one thing. This is not the fWo you remember... no... no it isn't." The fans had begun to boo, and Jim Dudley nodded his understanding. "This is not the fWo you last saw... the fWo that was filled with the greedy, self-important 'sports entertainers' that took your money and made themselves rich." The booing grew louder, and somewhere in the back a "bullshit" chant broke out. Jim Dudley waved it down. "Yes, yes... I know. That was bullshit... but luckily we're here to steer things back on track. Back the way they should be. Because let's face it. The fWo has been in a slump. We care about what you had to say, and we made the corrections necessary to get things back on track. Get things back... to where they belong." The fans were still booing, but had begun to look at one another in confusion. Were the stories true? Had the wrestlers really walked out on the promotion? On the fans? Title matches, debut matches... and much, much more. Tonight, in this very ring, you will see the fWo Hardcore championship decided and a worthy hardcore champion crowned. Unlike the old days, this match won't be between two washed up has-beens looking to extend their own glory at the expense of the fans. "In addition, I am proud to announce that tonight we will see the fWo debut of the most dangerous man in wrestling! Oktober vs. Spyder The crowd booed loudly. Oktober had walked out onto the entrance ramp and looked with some apprehension at the fans in attendance. Their faces were a wash of anger, confusion, and dislike. Large areas of the arena were empty, and that wasn't a good sign so early in the crowd. Some fans sat still on their chairs, choosing not to recognize Oktober's presence at all. Other fans looked sheepishly down at their fWo shirts they had bought at the event and wished they had spent the money on liquor instead. Oktober saw all of this through worried eyes, and thought for the thousandth time that he was better off staying in California and not getting caught up in this mess. Yes, it was important to Silver Fox, and yes... he stood by his friends. This though... this was a mess. Moving slowly he paced to the ring, his body a mess of tension and nerves. He held his body stiffly as he neared the ring, then slid under the ropes and lay against the turnbuckle, his large body slumped against the ropes. Regardless of why he was here, it didn't matter now. He was in the ring and it was time. The ringside announcer was clearing his throat and preparing to announce Spyder's, but Oktober heard none of it. Spyder had been in the wrestling business for a long time, and it was an honor to work with him. Spyder had run through the Mexico leagues and the California leagues for years... he was a known name down there. Here he was just another masked luchador, but perhaps they would be able to change that as the match progressed. It wasn't exactly his style of work, but between the two of them perhaps they could come up with something that could settle the crowd into a decent match. That was the best case scenario anyway. Spyder's music played, and a small and fairly shameful display of pyrotechnics exploded from the top of the entrance ramp. Spyder walked out into sight, his mouth a thin line beneath his mask. Oktober watched him come, concern growing visibly on his face as he noticed that Spyder was limping slightly. Spyder came slow, stopping to slap hands with the fans as he came, although the majority of fans had already sat back down or appeared uninterested in any kind of contract with the wrestler. Spyder looked at Oktober, shaking his head slowly. It was what it was. Inside the ring, they slapped hands and waited for the bell to ring. Inside the arena the crowd murmured with small interest, but Oktober couldn't help but notice the small fights breaking out in the crowd, the lines of people waiting to get beer from one of the vendors, or the masses of empty, empty seats. They looked at each other and shrugged. They were friends... and friends do the best they can with uncomfortable situations. Oktober and Spyder locked up, and to the bigger man's credit he was able to push the masked man into the ropes. Turning him around and flipping Spyder as he went, Oktober took him to the mat with a quick arm drag and then stepped forward and lifted him up into a smart side suplex. The move wasn't complicated, but looked unique enough that Oktober was expecting more of a response from the crowd to the hot opening. Disinterest was all that greeted him, and Oktober mumbled under his breath. This was going to be harder than he thought. Then Spyder was moving, grabbing him by the legs and using his weight against him. Oktober tumbled to the mat, and Spyder drove a knee in, then lifted him just enough to hook a leg and do his sitting huricarana that drove Oktober face-first into the mat. Another unusual move, and the crowd greeted it with complete apathy. Oktober saw the frustration in Spyder's eyes and nodded for him to go up to the top of the turnbuckle. Spyder climbed, preparing for the 450 splash, which had to get some kind of reaction from the fans. Spyder extended both arms and prepared to flip off, but just then a bottle flew from one of the members of the crowd and struck Spyder in the side of the head. The mask muted the blow, but it was enough to cause him to lose his balance and he topped over, down and crumbled into the mat. It was too quick, and Oktober didn't have a chance to move. Spyder crunched into his knees with his head, and from the thick slapping sound it was obvious that he was hurt. Spyder lay still as Oktober slowly climbed to his feet, although tenderly. The impact into his knees wasn't what he expected, and now his leg was killing him. He glanced down at his opponent with concern. Spyder didn't rise, but instead only looked up at Oktober with an expression Oktober didn't like. It wasn't frustration... it was anger. But why? Spyder was a professional... Oktober just hadn't had time to do better with the move to protect him. Now alarmed as he saw the hatred forming in Spyder's eyes but unable to do anything about it other than continue the match, he moved around as if circling his opponent. The crowd still didn't care, and at this point Oktober had bigger concerns. Oktober approached the masked man, urging him to rise. Spyder didn't, but instead shot out a fist and connected solidly with Oktober's groin. He groaned, and stumbled back. His vision was blurry, the dull ache from his groin was spreading. What in the hell was going on? He was a professional here... but if one mistake was going to cause this kind of reaction... if Spyder who had been in the business a hell of a lot longer than he had couldn't be a professional... Reborn vs. James Renshaw Lights flashed and James Renshaw swept back the curtain and peered out at the audience. He blinked. There were a lot of them there, and most of them looked pissed. He really didn't have any interest in the belt, and until earlier that day wasn't really sure what a "hardcore" match was. A production assistant had explained it to him and the whole concept sounded pretty barbaric. But, here he was and he had a job to do. Reborn's music played and the fans at first cheered his arrival, but then got a good look at who he was... or rather, who he wasn't... and began booing him loudly. He threw his head back to get the straggling hair out of his eyes. Paladin raised himself to one knee and looked toward the younger man. With a smile and a nod, he rose to his feet and went for a short tackle. Downfall fell backward, causing his head to ricochet against the corner of the steps. The fans quickly went back into their shells and continued the discussions amongst themselves. The veteran didn't care, as he knew that they would be drawn back in later. A quick suplex onto the hardened ground caused Downfall to arch his back. Paladin looked toward the referee. Seven fingers in the air, he realized it was time to return to the ring. He rolled the dazed body of Downfall onto the canvas and stood on the apron. As he prepared to get back in, Downfall stood up and hooked him for a suplex of his own. Northern Lights Suplex, but Downfall broke the pinfall up before the referee even had a chance to count. Ideas rushed through Downfall's mind, but then one thing came. No hatred overcame Downfall, just utter frustration. He picked himself up and walked up the isle. The referee counted, but Downfall didn't care. Fans screamed, but the rookie didn't care. Nothing mattered right now; he just wanted to get away from this place. The bell rang. Paladin was declared the winner, but the fans weren't going to have it. They all chanted against Downfall and his cowardice. A fan picked up his chair and tossed it into the ring. This seemed to be the go-ahead for everyone else. The fans rose from their seats and rushed the ring. Security was not ready, and the barricades were ripped down from their place in the ground. Fans entered the ring and cut down the ring ropes. Banners were torn from the side of the rings. Announcers and ring crew members were trampled. The announcer's table was taken apart completely. Beer cans flew. Everything was being destroyed, yet they were somehow kept from the ring. Wrestlers poured from the backstage area, trying to settle the fans down. They were only mildly attacked and berated by the hate-filled and angry fans. The cause, Downfall, paced backstage. A monitor displayed all the carnage. The young man quickly sprinted out toward the chaos and kept his place at the head of the ramp. All of this was his fault. Slowly, he walked toward the ring. He ignored the threats, the bumps, and the objects flying past his head. Again, he was an utter failure. The overwhelming depression took a further grasp on him, even if for that one moment. Security were stationed at the guard rail and looking furious. They hadn't signed up for this, and Matt was starting to think that the line that separated them from the unruly fans was painfully small at this point. A call had been made to the local police, but after dealing with the Flair incident and the Creed incident the overall impression they were getting was that the fWo was shit out of luck and would be fined for any damage that ultimately occurred.
