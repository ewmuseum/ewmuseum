fWo — Card

Sunday Slaughter, 27 August 2000

27 August 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Introduction The day after an fWo pay-per-view usually has some interesting developments. In May, the day after Redemption, we saw the formation of the Future Foundation. We also found out why Scott Slugger turned on Dead. And The Big Blonde Machine - Ice - returned after a three month layoff. In June, Scott Slugger & Eron each won number one contender spots to the fWo World Title. Eron defeated the owner of the fans Wrestling organization - Chris Finn - to gain his. Scott Slugger defeated Ice to earn his. That same night, four matches of the Next Level tournament were also held. In July, the day after SummerSlaughter saw Chris Finn announce the date and location and the huge main event of the Ryan Moore Memorial Show, and Primetime won a "Choose Your Fate" match that gained he and his partner the right to enter the Meltdown main event last. Meltdown was last night. Primetime didn't win. Primetime might've ended the career of Fallen, however, as towards the end of the event we saw Primetime toss Fallen from the ring through a table with a backdrop driver. But Primetime did get what was coming to him. "D" was revealed. The Flying Frenchie is back, and Primetime has hell to pay. Over the last few months, Primetime has claimed to injure several people. It began with Havok, and then Matt Douglas. Ric Chronos fell prone to the dangerous backdrop driver on the cement floor at the Ryan Moore Memorial show. And last night, Fallen fell victim to the same move, this time over the top rope through a table. The disabled list of the fWo continues to grow each day. Primetime must be stopped. Tonight, on Sunday Slaughter, he faces Mike Bear, who was the partner of Fallen and essentially could not do a thing about what Primetime had done. Until now. And how will the Frenchie factor into it? Those aren't the only things people are talking about the day after Meltdown. They're talking about how the impossible may have happened. Wretch - formerly known as Chris Kidman, Jr. - a man who's been in the fWo since the day it opened, and never reached the level of success he's achieved until recently, almost two years after his debut, won Meltdown last night. He has now earned the right to a full sweep of title shots, and tonight he will receive at least one of them. Everyone is also talking about the alliance forged between The Boston Strangler & Austin Davis. At Meltdown, they seemingly worked as a cohesive unit. While The Boston Strangler wasn't scheduled to compete tonight, he is still 'in the house'. So is Austin Davis. And so is Rain, who didn't fare too well in the Meltdown battle royal. He was eliminated due to his own partner, essentially, who didn't understand the concept of the match that well. But that's okay. It wasn't Rain' fault that he ended up on The Mutha Fuckin' Abusah Of Yo Momma's team. He couldn't do anything about it. Tonight, he would, however. As he entered the Staples Center in Los Angeles, California, a look of anger resided upon his face. But not only was anger evident - confidence could be seen if you looked deeped into his mysterious eyes. The camera's zoomed in for a close-up, and Rain muttered something under his breath. It was loud enough to be picked up by the audio however. "Armstrong better be here tonight." And with all that has happened the past few days - Chris Finn being injured by The Boston Strangler, Austin Davis returning to the fWo, Primetime injuring Fallen, six new wrestlers joining the fWo roster, "D" being revealed as the Flying Frenchie, and Wretch winning the Meltdown battle royal - Sunday Night Slaughter began. The Meltdown Winner Has Something To Say... The arena was already rocking. Fans were already on their feet. The cameras went live. The people went live. The announcers went live. "Paint it Black" hits, and the fans exploded. The Staples Center was so loud it shook. Big Wretchy The Ratings Saver walked out onto the ramp, and as the announcers welcomed everyone at home to the show, he walked down the ramp with a microphone in hand, certainly with something to say on his mind. Wretchy walked up the ring stairs into the ring, before waiting for his music to die out. He posed for the fans as his pyro's exploded high above the ring, and the music finally died out. Wretchy addressed the crowd first. "Last night, history was made. Last night, the Wretchster walked into that ring, and he tossed out thirty nine of the fWo's biggest superstars. Last night, The Wretchster taught everyone in the entire business what the power of Wretch-A-Mania is all about, dude." The crowd cheered as he paused to contemplate his next sentence. "When the smoke cleared... it was The Wretchster standing tall in the ring with his hands raised. And now he has the fortunate opportunity of a full sweep of fWo Title Shots. Well, brah, The Wretchster only wants two, and you see, he can't get the one he truly wants right now because it's customary to receive the fWo World Title shot last. That's fine, brah... because tonight, I'm going to get one of those shots, brah. And dude... the power of Wretch-A-Mania is going to run wild throughout the fWo, brother!" The fans cheered loudly, even though it was apparent that The Wretchster was babbling like a madman. "Now, dude... The Wretchster has one request. And that is that the acting owner of the fans Wrestling organization - James Armstrong - get his bald ass out here, because The Wretchster has a question, dude!" The fans again cheered at this, before "Pretty Fly For A White Guy" by Offspring resounded throughout the arena. The fans reaction was mixed; with boos & cheers spread throughout. Armstrong walked out onto the stage wearing a blue jacket and black dress slacks, completed with some nice looking dress shoes and an outrageous colorful tie. Armstrong acknowledged the crowd before entering the ring, and shaking the hands of Wretch. Wretch then spoke about the reason he wanted Armstrong out there. "James brah, The Wretchster has a question. First, let me jog the memories of everyone here tonight. Now, a few weeks ago, The Wretchster challenged Mitch Wilson to a two out of three falls match at the Memorial Show for the fWo Internet Title. The Wretchster said if he won, dude... he would never ask Mitch for a shot at that fWo Internet Title again. Well brah, tonight... I'm owed a shot at that title, but I can't get it until I receive my other title shots... well dude, the Wretchster has no interest in those shots. So, what I'm askin', dude... is if I could let my lackeys, brah... have those shots, and in return, I get my Internet Title shot right here tonight in the City of Angels!" The crowd popped loudly at the mention of their city' name, before chanting "Wretchy" repeatedly. Armstrong pondered the suggestion, before asking Wretchster how he planned to give the title shots to his lackeys. Wretchster already had a plan. "Well, tonight, dude... Scot Hartt & The Abusah, they would face Damnation for the fWo World Tag Team Titles... and then next week, Hartt would face the Cruiserweight Champion... and Abusah would face the Hardcore Champion, dude. And as far as the International Title shot goes... well, I guess Hartt & Abusah will face each other in the near future and whoever wins will get that shot. So, dude... with all of that aside, The Wretchster wants to know if he can face Mitch Wilson tonight for the fWo Internet Title, dude!" Armstrong nodded slowly at first, before telling Wretch that yes, tonight, he would face Mitch Wilson for the fWo Internet Title. The Wretchster thanked Armstrong, before reminding all of his Wretch-A-Maniacs to train, eat their vitamins, and say their prayers... and then told Wilson that once again - tonight - Wretch-A-Mania would run wild on him. Slaughter went to a brief highlight package of Meltdown, and then it's first obligatory commercial break... Ruben Ross vs. Red Dragon Slaughter returns to the ring for it's first match of the night, featuring fWo "Survivor" winner Ruben Ross, and newcomer Red Dragon. Both men had wrestled their first fWo matches last night at Meltdown. Ruben Ross was quite successful, as he won a four-way dance to earn the right to face Mitch Wilson for the fWo Cruiserweight Title. Ross didn't win that match however, but he fared just as well as anybody had as of late against Mitch Wilson, who seemingly hadn't lost a straight up singles match in quite some time. Red Dragon didn't win his first two matches, but did quite well in those, and several fans had obviously taken note of it as they had suggested he would do quite well in the fWo. The cameras took to the stage, and the sounds of "Battle of the Mound" by Basil Poledouris bellowed throughout the Staples Center. Red Dragon walked out onto the stage, below all of the NBA Championship Banners of the Los Angeles Lakers. Dragon looked up into the balcony and saw the man he teamed with last night at Meltdown, Black Quicksilver. BQ was sitting with Stump, and looked to be enjoying themselves. Dragon was cheered as he entered the ring, and did some light exercises to warm up for his third match in the fWo. As he did, his music faded, and was replaced by the theme music of "Black Plague" Ruben Ross, which was "Firestarter" by The Prodigy. Ross entered the ring and removed his vest, before stretching out, and looked ready to kick things off as the bell rang. Ross & Dragon locked up, and Dragon took the advantage with a headlock. Ross countered into a go behind into a hammerlock, and Dragon countered that with a snapmare. Surprising even himself, Dragon charged to the ropes and returned with a cartwheel into a backflip - apparently going for an elbow, but Ross hooked him from behind with a waistlock into a German Suplex with a bridge. Dragon kicked out after a close two count. Both men sprung to their feet, and exchange chops, which resulted in the crowd chanting "whoo!" at the sound of hands smacking across bare flesh. Dragon sent Ross to the ropes, but Ross reveresed, and then took him down with a short arm clothesline. He then brought Dragon up and hit a reverse Russian legsweep, before going for a pin. Dragon got a foot on the ropes at two. Ross continued on the offensive by shooting him into the ropes, and as he returned Ross took him down with a belly-to-belly suplex. Ross then took to the skies, and went up to the top ropes. He flew off with a diving headbutt, of which Dragon moved out of. Dragon then went on the offensive and put Ross out on the floor, and then took a high risk with a tope con hilo. He hit it, and then sent Ross into the ring. He came in and hit a Michinoku Driver, and got a count of two for it. Taking another high risk, he flew off with a senton bomb, only this time he hit it. Went for another cover, but only got two. Dragon decided to finish things off - or attempt to. He went upstairs once again, this time flying off with the 'Dragonsault', a reverse shooting star press. However, as he stood on the ropes, he became distracted with something in the crowd. The aforementioned Black Quicksilver & Stump mysteriously fell from where they were sitting to the area below them, where internet reporters sat talking about how the finish of Meltdown was originally supposed to have The Boston Strangler win, but was changed when word leaked out on the 'net. BQ & Stump didn't appear too hurt however, as the internet reporters were rather bulky, and their added weight protected the fall of the light former Cruiserweight champion and his constant companion. While all of this went on, it allowed Ruben Ross to recover, and with Dragon focused on the crowd still, Ross climbed to the top rope and reached up and grabbed his opponent, before hitting a Michinoku Driver from the top rope. He hit it, and then covered Dragon for the pin. The fans applauded at the short but excellent match the two had, and Ross celebrated with his arms raised as Slaughter went backstage. Result: Ross by pinfall Mystery Revealed? A shot of newly crowned three-time fWo Hardcore Champion "The Eradicator" Erik Kelly entering the Staples Center is what we saw next. The first thing everyone noticed was the still present look of anger, before realizing Pamela Dee was not at his side. He acknowledged some backstage workers, before stopping to sign autographs for some "fans" who were lucky enough to have backstage passes for tonight's Slaughter. Kelly walked further into the arena, before a door opened, and Pamela Dee was pushed out of it. Kelly stopped and grabbed her, before watching the door shut. He didn't pay attention to that, but instead asked Dee if she was alright. She didn't appear harmed, but her skin was somewhat red from being tied up and held captive for so long. In her hands contained a piece of paper however, with a note. As Kelly opened to read it, the camera zoomed in. It read "Vengence... is mine." Kelly' jaw dropped, before he helped Dee take a nearby seat. He then walked over to the room she came out of, and tried fiercely to enter the room, which was locked. Slaughter went elsewhere as Kelly let out a violent, angry scream. A Deal Is Struck "Now, you've got to realize - we've got stroke tonight, being in California and all. I'm sure we can get you a title shot next week on Slaughter IF you do something for us in return." Rain raised an eyebrow at the two gentlemen, who were up until twenty four hours ago the fWo World Tag Team Champions. "Yeah? What makes you think you can accomplish that? And what do I have to do in return for you?" Courage and Star conferred in private for a moment, before resuming the conversation with Rain. Courage spoke on their behalf. "You see, Rain baby... we're owed a rematch at the fWo World Tag Team Titles. And we want that match tonight. But James Armstrong already gave that title shot to two men you happen to hate. Scot Hartt..." Star picked up where Courage left off. "And The Mutha Fuckin' Abusah Of Yo Momma, biotch!" and then we went back to Courage. "So, if you take care of those two men, we'll talk to Armstrong and see if you can get a shot against Slugger next week on Slaughter." Rain pondered it, before answering with "Fortunately for you two, the God's say yes. But if we don't get that shot - and I end up doing this work for nothing - you two are dead." Rain gave the duo a stern look, before leaving. Behind his back, Courage & Star mocked him, by making noises that didn't resemble lightning no matter how much they tried. Star smirked, before saying "God's of the storm... my ass! Everyone knows that if God were a wrestler, he'd be Lance Star." Courage nodded, as Slaughter rolled on. Never Again... "Tables... ladders... and chairs... oh my! Tables... ladders... and chairs... oh my! Tables... ladders... and chairs... oh my!" Suddenly, from out of nowhere came "The Legend" Jon Spratt. Spratt had a baseball bat wrapped in barbed wire, and as he got Foley' attention, he smacked him as he turned around with the baseball bat. He then grabbed the bag that Foley was carrying and dumped the contents of it on Foley, before kicking at his former idol relentless. Spratt then reached inside the 'fannypack' of Foley and found the rental car keys, and unlocked the trunk. He battered Foley some more, before tossing the commissioner in the car, and slamming the trunk shut. Out of nowhere arrived wwf announcer Howard Finkel, whom looked pretty startled. "Howard... you take this piece of trash back to Connecticut... and you tell him to never come back. You hear me? And tell him that everything I once thought of him... I don't think of him the same way anymore. He can rot in hell." Spratt handed Finkel the car keys, before leaving. Finkel got in the car, and drove away... Mike Bear vs. Primetime Back inside the arena, Primetime's theme "Blind" by Korn roared throughout the arena. Primetime walked out onto the SlaughterStage with a smug look on his face, and the leader of the Future Foundation raised his arms in the air. This irked the crowd who immediately began booing him. He walked down the the bright, shiny entrance ramp and from behind came Mike Bear with a double-axehandle that sent Primetime rolling down the ramp. The response from the crowd turned to an ovation, as Bear was surely a fan favorite. Bear brought Primetime up and slammed him head first into the guardrail, and the referee joined them on the floor to try and get things to the ring. Bear didn't care what the referee wanted - Primetime was going to pay for injuring Fallen and ruining Bear' chances of winning Meltdown. Bear took Primetime up on the stage and hit a snap suplex on the floor, before bringing him up again and hitting another suplex. He then took him down with a german suplex, and then another. Bear elbowed out of the headlock, and the two exchanged punches and forearms on the ramp. The referee got in between them to break it up, and they pushed him aside. They continued to fight on the stage, and they got near the end of a stage. Wilson grabbed Wretch with a headlock, and the two began to brawl up into the crowd, who were at a fever pitch. Wilson hooked Wretch for a piledriver, but those never work. Wretch countered by kicking out of it, and Wilson' back hit the pavement. Wretch then grabbed a headlock and brought Wilson back near the guardrail. Wretch winded up for a running bulldog, but at the last moment Wilson sent him flying in the air over the guardrail. Wretch smacked hard, and the crowd booed Wilson. Wilson gave them a middle finger gesture, before climbing over the guardrail, looking as confident as ever. Wilson grabbed Wretch and sent him into the ring, before covering him. One. Two. Wretch kicked out, and the fans popped loudly. Wilson brought Wretch and sent him into the turnbuckles, before peppering him with some forearms, and then thrusting his shoulder into the rib area repeatedly. Wilson then propped up Wretch on the top turnbuckle, and taunted him, telling him it was over. He also said this to the crowd, before joining Wretch up top. Wretch however, had a different plan. He fought back. Lefts. Rights. Wretch pushed Wilson off, and Wilson hit the canvas. Wilson quickly got back up however, and Wretch flew off with a hurrancanrana. He hooked the legs. One. Two. Wilson reversed it. One. Two. Wretch escaped. Wilson went back on the offensive, stomping at Wretch' rib area, before sending him to the ropes. Wretch returned, and Wilson took him down with a tiltawhirl backbreaker. He covered Wretch again, but got only a two count. Wilson then brought Wretch up, and signalled to the crowd it was now over. He hooked him for a suplex, but not just any suplex. The self-dubbed "SuperStar Suplex". Completing the move resulted in only a two count. Wilson knew he had to come up with something big to pull this one out. Sending Wretch into the corner, he followed him in, before going out onto the ring apron. He climbed behind Wretch in the same corner, and got his attention with a push to the back of the head. The two began to climb up to the top rope, but Wilson had the advantaged. He got on the side, and hooked him for the top rope headliner. The referee told Wilson not to do it, and stood trying to wave him off. Wilson did it anyways. The two went crashing down, knocking the referee out with them. The collision took life out of everyone. A minute passed with nobody in the ring moving at all. Wilson finally crawled over for a cover, but the referee was still out. Enter Ice. He came down to the ring and entered, before reviving the referee and pointing at the pinfall attempt. The referee crawled over, and counted. One. Two. Two and a half. Two and three quarters. Wretch kicked out. The fans pumped their fist in the air and cheered loudly, before Ice entered the ring again and kicked the referee to the outside. Ice helped Wilson up, and the two made a plan. Ice went to the outside, and grabbed a table from underneath the ring. As Ice & Wilson set it up, Wretch climbed up to his feet from behind, and rolled Wilson up for a pin. Again, there was no referee. Ice saw his opportunity, and took it. He grabbed Wretch off Wilson, and grasped his throat inside his massive hand. Ice taunted the crowd, telling them it was over. But his pause allowed Wretch to escape. He nailed Ice with a lowblow, and then walked up from behind him and hit the Depression, before pushing him out to the floor. Wretch then waited behind Wilson as he got up near the table, and put him through it with Wilson' own finisher. The Headliner. The table burst into pieces, as Wretch covered him. There was no referee. Wretch got up and looked around, and saw the other referee on the floor. Wretch had the match won, clearly, to everyone in the arena. But how could he win if the referee was out cold? Wretch put his hands on his head and sighed in frustration, a few seconds away from wearing his blonde hair out. But he never got to that point - another referee rushed to the ring. Wretch went over and covered Wilson again. One. Two. The Big Blonde Machine wouldn't have it. He pulled the new referee onto the floor, and they began to argue. Wretch got up to see what the commotion was, and to give Ice a piece of his mind. That gave Wilson a moment to recover. He did. Sneaking up from behind, he grabbed Wretch, and hit a reverse Russian Legsweep. Sensing it was over, he prepared to cover him, before he heard a loud ovation of cheers from the crowd. Knowing that it wasn't for him - not that he wanted any - he turned around. The Abusah was inside the ring, via entering through the crowd. With his head bandaged up, Abusah charged at Wilson who walked towards him. Spear. Unlike last night at Meltdown when he tried to hit Greg Davis & instead he Wretch, this time he didn't. Abusah planted Wilson in the ring, before grabbing Wretch and draping his arm over Wilson. Abusah went to the outside, and informed the referee of what had occured in the ring. The referee went in, and Abusah & Ice watched as the referee counted. One. Two. This time, however, Ice pulled Wretch to the floor. Abusah couldn't take anymore. He charged. Spear. He hit it, before unloading with punches on Ice on the floor. Wretch shrugged it off, and went to the top rope. He flew off with a frogsplash, but Wilson moved. Wretch hit the mat, and now it appeared the tide had turned to Wilson. Bringing him up, he again went for the Headliner, but Wretch elbowed out of it, went behind him, and hit the Depression. The crowd went nuts. Wretch got up and staggered around the ring, seemingly in shock. Deciding that it was over, he stood over Wilson, and kicked his arm. He then tore off his bandana, as the everyone watched on in confusion. Sending the bandana into the first five rows of fans, Wilson pointed back and forth, before charging and bouncing off the ropes, returning and hopping over Wilson' prone body, and bouncing off that set of ropes. He approached Wilson, and hit the most dangerous move in the history of our sport. The legdrop. It's over. One. Two. Three. Wretch had finally done the impossible, he had beaten Mitch Wilson for the fWo Internet Title. Abusah & Ice fought to the back, and Wretch stood in the ring in celebration, as the referee handed him his newly won title. Wretch held it up as the announcers praised his most recent accomplishments - tonight, and Meltdown. Wretch stood on the ropes as "Paint It Black" echoed throughout the arena. Wilson exited the ring, and swallowed his pride. He looked at Wretch who stared back at him, before Wilson got his fWo Cruiserweight Title and held it almost as an afterthought in the air. The crowd booed. Wretch smiled. Wilson walked up the ramp, and bumped into someone nobody expected to see. The Boston Strangler. The Strangler and Wilson exchanged a glare, before Wilson sucked it up and just went backstage. Wretch got off the turnbuckles and he looked at The Strangler suspciously. The Strangler entered the ring, and he looked at Wretch. Not knowing why he was out there, Wretch backed up a little bit, as the crowd silently watched in anticipation. In Strangler' mind, everything was fine. Perhaps he forgot what had happened last night at Meltdown, about how he had aligned himself with the most evil, vile, disgusting man to ever appear on the fWo roster, Austin Davis. Perhaps that was erased from memory. In the mind of Paul Revere Adams, he was just out there to congratulate Wretch, a man who had been in the fWo for nearly three years, but was just winning the second most important title in the organization. Strangler knew how hard the young man had worked, and wanted to congratulate him. But nobody else knew that. Especially Wretch. Wretch backed away from him some more, but Strangler stuck out his hand. Wretch shook his head no. Not knowing why Wretch did that, the Strangler appeared confused; which wasn't a good thing judging by recent examples. Strangler reached forward and grabbed Wretch, who couldn't escape. Seconds later, Wretch was in pain. The Boston Massacre. Strangler tightened the hold as the fans booed. The referee tried to get him off, but it was of no use. He was much too small to even try to get the much larger & stronger Strangler off of the new fWo Internet Champion. But, there was hope. Mike Bear staggered out with a chair in hand and bandages around his head, and some around his waist. Bear wasn't in good condition. He had suffered a grueling match with Primetime some twenty five minutes earlier, and could barely even walk. Strangler saw him coming, and that just made him tighten the hold on Wretch. Wretch writhed in pain. And then, out of nowhere, as Bear got near the ring, someone came in through the crowd. Austin Davis. Davis hopped over the guardrail, and Bear saw him. He charged, and swung with the chair - weakly - and Davis countered with a simple boot to the midsection, before hooking him for a sitdown tombstone piledriver. The Crucifixion. Davis stood up, and looked at the Strangler. Davis held out his arms crucifixion style. Strangler smiled. The fans threw beverages and other trash into the ring, nearly hitting the Strangler as he released the hold and stood up. Strangler shrugged it off, but then his attention as was everyone else's on the stage. "Back In Black" hit, and Scott Slugger appeared on the stage. As he walked out, the fans ovated with a sickening amount of cheers, but the cameras stopped rolling. Slaughter ended just as other recent Slaughter's had the day after a pay-per-view - this time, with some more than just "interesting" developments. Result: Wretch by pinfall
