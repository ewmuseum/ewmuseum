The Deep End I was here last week, just like Miss Ivy suggested. I went around and tried to introduce myself to some people, but it was a bad time. I don't blame anyone, it was the first show after a pay per view and there was fallout to consider, and from what I gathered, everyone was on edge about the main event. Krow and Steven Shadows tore each other up in a pretty ugly fight, and the thing that concerns me is that, because of a time limit draw, nothing was settled. But it wasn't a total loss. The Flying Frenchie was fairly gracious, but for some reason, kept insisting that he wasn't going to mentor me. Jeff Garvin was in a bad mood all night: I caught most of his in ring rant on the monitor in the back, but after I told him I trained with Eli Flair he seemed to look at me like something a step or two above pond scum. High Flyer worried me, because of our history elsewhere, but we agreed to leave Baltimore in Baltimore. This is Jack Harmen's house, and he should get the proper deference in cases like this. That new kid that Vince Jacobs beat up wouldn't even look at me, let alone stop to say hello. The only people I didn't get to meet that I wish I had were Karina Wolfenden and Ruben Ross. The K-Wolf has been one of my favorite wrestlers to watch in the past few years, the insane intensity she wrestles with makes her one of the few people I'm actually anxious to someday (hopefully soon) face off against. And of course, Ruben Ross is one of my idols. He made it possible for guys of our size to be seriously considered as World Title contenders. This week, I managed to convince Rose to take two days off from work and come out with me. But I didn't count on the production area being such a madhouse. "Outta the way, kid," said someone I didn't recognize, "Are you s'posed to even be here?" Yeah, I said. I'm Impulse, I was here last week, was hoping to talk to Miss Ivy or Mr. Beaven regarding my debut. "And she is?" asked the guy. My girlfriend, Rose. "Interesting. Look, if you're supposed to be here, go talk to McGinnis," said the guy. "Phil, we need to rearrange the second hour," said someone else over by the big whiteboard. She started to walk away, but I grabbed her shoulder. Qualifying match for what, I asked her. "There's a match in a few weeks," said Miss Ivy, "Winner gets a shot at the Cruiserweight Championship. You're gonna need this, I said, so nobody kicks you out. I need to do this match up, then I'll be back. "Okay," said Rose, and she hugged me, "You'll do great." She walked away, following Miss Ivy. I took a deep breath. I was hoping I'd be able to ease into this company, but it looks like I'm diving in the deep end on my first night. And I can't wait. Oh well. Either I hit it or I don't. I took another deep breath, and I shoved off. In his grip was a black and silver mask. It was far different than his trademark Kato mask. His eyes examined it closely with images of past horrors racing through his head. He knew the pain and suffering it brought. To him, it was a shroud of darkness… a darkness that he constantly fought to keep under control.
