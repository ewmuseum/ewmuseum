Introduction First light. This misted field is the world, that man slipping the greased bolt back and forth, that man tunneled with blood the dark smudges of whose eyes call for sleep, calls for quiet, and the woman down your line, the woman who screamed the loudest, will be quiet. The rushes, the grassless shale, the dust, whiten like droppings. One blue grape hyacinth whistles in the thin and birdless air without breath. Ten minutes later a lost dog poked for rabbits, the stones slipped, a single blade of grass stiffened in sun; where the wall broke a twisted fig thrust its arms ahead like a man in full light blinded. "I'll be there." A pause. Then... "Of course. I'll be careful. You'll be safe." He clicked off the phone, and let the silence drag out for what felt like hours. "You did the right thing, you know." Silver Fox turned and stared John Smith in the eyes. But even the sea of green couldn't hold his attention. It was the bag, and it was what Silver Fox knew was inside of it. John Smith could sense it was well, and smiled. He ran a loving hand through the young girl's hair beside him, and nodded his approval. Silver Fox went for the bag but as he passed Smith, a hand extended and caught him by the arm. "This is for today. Lead Jim to him tomorrow, and you get the rest... but neglect your duty, and you will find the entire world as dry as the Sahara desert. You follow me?" Fox nodded, and scratched at his arm. His eyes were hollow masks within his skull. His hair thin and whisp-like on his scalp. His lips were parched, dead skin more than live, and he jittered like grease on a stove. Smith released him, not watching to touch the shell of a man any longer. Fox grabbed for the bag and retreated into the darkness. Smith watched his handywork fade away, and smiled. He turned to his young charge, and stroked her hair. She smiled up at him, and Smith was pleased that her twelve-year-old grin still held innocence after all of the things she had seen. And done. "Come my dear... let's get you somewhere warm and into something more... comfortable. Tomorrow, Daddy is going to scratch a much-needed itch." Graphic Violence vs. "The Mecca" Alex Creed In a tournament like the Wrestler of the Year tourney, every match had an extra element of importance to it. To win the tournament was to be recognized as the Best. Scouting and preparation was key, because any edge a wrestler could gain could only benefit them. But for Alex Creed, the reigning fWo Internet Champion, he had even more on his plate. At first, he wasn't sure who he would be facing this round. When he found out he'd have to step into the ring with the man who not only defeated his father, Kodiak Vic Creed, but sent him to the hospital... well you could say the Mecca's enthusiasm dropped somewhat. No dancing, no posing from Alex Creed as his music hit. He stepped through his pyro with the Internet Title draped over his shoulder. Alex mustered a wink and a brash smirk for the nearby camera as he passed, but the young man was very concerned. Would the Mecca see the next round of the tournament? Or could this match be his last? Fancy light shows and music were meaningless to the fWo Hardcore Champion. Graphic Violence kicked the curtains aside and stared at the crowd. He'd destroyed the Main Man and took the Hardcore Title from him. That meant he was double tough. He'd also seen War and Death, some at his own hand. Could anyone else around him say the same? The fWo's very own War Machine was going to win this tournament. Tonight, he'd treat the son to the same pain and destruction he showed his father. The elder Creed ended up in the hospital. The younger Creed wouldn't be so lucky. The Hardcore Champion had just stepped into the ring when he charged and drove the belt into the back of Alex's head. The Internet Champion was handing his own title down to the ringside attendant, and now he was taking the brunt of another guy's belt. The referee shouted something, but Graphic Violence was too busy stomping the young Champion into the mat to hear him. Once GV had tossed the Hardcore Title out of the ring, he forearmed the Mecca down to the mat. Alex tried to lift himself to his feet, only to have Graphic Violence kick him in the ribs. The referee signaled for the bell, officially starting the Wrestler of the Year match. "Geez," Alex gasped. "Who piddled in your Corn Flakes?" Graphic Violence stomped Alex in the face, dropped to one knee, and punched the fresh footprint until it disappeared. Pressing his shoulder into Alex's face, GV repeated the elbows until the referee finally had to step in and threaten him with disqualification. The Hardcore Champion wasn't about to give the young man a second to breathe. They had come from Florida, from Colorado, from Mexico. They were, in their own way, legion. "Around you are your tasks." They looked. "There are buildings. Break into them." Number 816 clutched what he hoped would serve well as a home-made firebomb. "There are cars. Destroy them." Number 126 tightened his grip around a long, oiled wrench. "There are people on the streets that need to be woken up from this slumber of hypocracy and delusion. They will work with us, or suffer behind us. I don't care which, and neither do you." They smiled. It was bigger tonight. More free than it had been before. They shuffled in their black t-shirts, the scrawled white 'f' encircled and nearly glowing in the street light. "This is the start. In two nights, we will spill into and out of this arena, and shut it all down. Shut everything down." He smiled, and it was a smile that many might remember. It had hardened since the days when he had been on television, and hardened still further since the days he had left the ring. The gleam in his eyes... that was still there, but brighter. Brighter than the flash of the gun that had pretended to end his life. "Go. Raze destruction on this city... and we will leave a trail of fire and destruction behind us everywhere that we go." The men and women who were numbers moved as one... but that numerical designation had been given to the man who guided them, to the man who told them what to do, and to the man who despite all of his instructions refused to be called their leader. He was the first, he and the man in green, and was One. For Neo was just another way of spelling One.
