Survivor IV: Episode Fourteen, 28 July 2004

28 July 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Introduction There is freedom within, there is freedom without Try to catch the deluge in a paper cup There's a battle ahead, many battles are lost But you'll never see the end of the road While you're traveling with me Hey now, hey now Don't dream it's over Hey now, hey now When the world comes in They come, they come To build a wall between us We know they won't win Now I'm towing my car, there's a hole in the roof My possessions are causing me suspicion but there's no proof In the paper today tales of war and of waste But you turn right over to the T.V. page Now I'm walking again to the beat of a drum And I'm counting the steps to the door of your heart Only shadows ahead barely clearing the roof Get to know the feeling of liberation and relief Hey now, hey now Don't dream it's over Hey now, hey now When the world comes in They come, they come To build a wall between us Don't ever let them win Getting some air And then there were three. Seated in metal folding chairs at the sound stage, the remaining survivors felt the emptiness of the room previously bursting with activity and drama. Brian Cage, Frank Smyth, and Stevie Roberts were in a heated discussion. Apparently nervousness about the contest coming to an end wasn't the exclusive property of the contestants. While they argued (more accurately Frank and Brian argued while Stevie tried to keep up), Rana Venenosa began to undo the laces on the back of his mask. With a sigh, he removed his actual face from his assumed one, stood up, and walked towards the double doors in the back of the building towards the loading dock. Everything was being played for high stakes now. Cage and Smyth had their decisions to make, and so did Matthew. Some space would be a blessing, but sadly for him, he was being followed. Doctor Curiosity was agitated. He needed to prove himself before Rana Venenosa could make his decision. He could do this by defeating them all in a match! It was simple, and it would show not just Rana, but the entire world that Doctor Curiosity was someone to be taken seriously. Very seriously. The doctor approached the man with the mask in his hand, and coughed. Matthew Wakeman turned around and just about managed to avoid rolling his eyes. He had wanted some peace, but instead, this. "Jah, Rana Venemenosta , I vish to challenge both you and Logic to a fight, to prove myself. For too long I have..." It was as far as the doctor got before Matthew interrupted him. "We've all fought before. I've fought you, you have fought Logic, and myself and Logic have fought. There's nothing more to be done here." One eyebrow arched across the doctor's face. "You have made your decision zen?" "Jah," replied Matthew, unable to resist putting on a German accent for a few seconds. The Curious One looked flustered, and a million thoughts assailed his brain at once. Since even two simultaneous thoughts could sometimes cause the Doc problems, he became utterly dumbstruck at this news. Sadly for him, Matthew didn't notice that to be a change from Curiosity's normal behaviour, and so he walked on towards the loading deck. Eventually the feeble mind of the (allegedly) German madman caught up to current events and he ran to keep up with the man who would decide his fate. By the time he found the man he knew only as Rana Vene-thingy, there was another man there too. Logic. The doctor cursed under his breath, and the two other men turned to look at him. They both looked fairly tired, and Logic's eyes flicked to the window. It seemed pretty nice beyond the glass. "Might I suggest we leave here? Fresh air can work wonders," remarked the big man. Matthew seemed to agree with him, and before Doctor Curiosity could protest that it was a trap, or that Logic was trying to win Rana's affection, the men were moving. Soon they passed through the large doors and were out in to the parking lot. "Nice night," commented Matthew. Logic remained impassive, and Doctor Curiosity just shrugged. This was small talk, and none of them were particularly adept at it. "So we have decided not to fight," said Logic. It wasn't a question though, merely a statement of fact. "Looks like it. There's nothing to be gained. The game is up, as they say," replied Matthew. Doctor Curiosity fidgeted, and hopped from one foot to the other. He was uncomfortable. He wanted to prove himself, like he had promised, but now the other two were so set on taking the night off that he couldn't possibly go against it. There was a slim chance that Rana had picked him to progress to the final, and he didn't want to alter the decision by trying to start a conflict. Decisions, decisions. "Jah, zere is no need to fight. Zose of us in ze final need to save our energy!" Curiosity looked at Rana, trying to see even a flicker of a reaction. None was forthcoming, though Logic just laughed and turned away from the other pair. Tonight was the final elimination decided by the people left in the contest. It all came down to one choice, and that choice lay out of the hands of Logic and Doctor Curiosity. The doctor looked around the parking lot. In the distance he could see the far off spot where Eegor had parked their appalling rental car on the night of the first Survivor show. It seemed decades ago, back in the day when Kenji Lee was still a name associated with fWo. Half a world away. Curiosity turned back and faced Logic and Rana. The big man had been lost in his own thoughts, and Rana... well, Curiosity had never been able to tell much about him. They stood there for a while longer. Nothing more was said, because nothing more needed to be said. Apart from what Rana Venenosa was going to say. Apart from who he was going to choose. And he was going to do it sooner than anticipated. Spilling Sitting down on the edge of the loading dock, Matthew did everything he could think of in order to keep his cool. In every elimination he'd felt a measure of guilt. But now, when it was obvious that he was completely one hundred percent responsible for deciding who would remain in the competition he ... So, keeping his eyes away from the two of them, Matthew tried to light a cigarette... *click, click, click, click* He couldn't stop himself from thinking of Brock Tyler, the genuine smile of welcome on his face when Matthew had walked into that diner. The absolutely authentic way he spoke to everyone openly and honestly, his genuine enthusiasm for the contest. He was there just because he loved the fWo so much. *click, click, click, click* With no success, he threw both the cigarette and lighter out into the night. Standing up and turning around to face the rest of the team, Matthew said, "I chose Doc." Logic lowered his head, swallowing. Doctor Curiosity blinked and started sniffling. Eegor, finally fed up with this nonsense, put a hand on the Doctor's shoulder and said, "He meant he's taking you to the finals. He's eliminated, Logic." Whirling around on Eegor, Doctor Curiosity shouted, "I know that! Occasionally, I really do know what people are talking about, all right?!" "So there, I said it," said Matthew, "I fucking hate this. I've hated this the entire competition. And I hate this now. I hated doing this, I hated being scared it was going to happen to me. And now I hate that everybody is gone." "You know who I miss? Brookes. That motherfucker scared the living crap out of me, and never had a damn nice thing to say to anybody. But I got used to having him around. Now he's gone." Looking directly at Logic, Matt said, "And now you're gone, because of my vote. So I figured it'd be better to hear it from me than go through the next few hours to wait for them to tell you." Rising From the Ashes Logic blinked. Once. Twice. To say that he hadn't seen it coming would be an out and out lie. It was the logical decision. Facing Logic or Doctor Curiosity? An easy decision for Rana. That didn't mean it didn't hurt Logic like hell though. With a somber expression, David Walter Smith turned to Rana, and spoke. "Thanks for giving it to me straight, Doc." Silence. "Err, sorry. I had this planned for if Chris won immunity, doesn't work as well now, does it?" Matthew rubbed the back of his head, "I guess not. I don't know. They don't tell us how to handle these things. They don't tell us how to coexist with each other, or how to handle departing." "That's because we aren't supposed to," Logic spat. "We're supposed to be at each other's throats. And then, like sheep, we forget it all to follow those idiots on their damn fool idealistic crusade." The Pensive Punisher rose, looking at his comrades, his partners in crime. "I don't care where we end up, be it fWo, OCW, WWE--whatever. We are going to raise the hell that we should have raised in this competition. This is not up for discussion. This is a fact. Matthew Wakeman. David Walter Smith. Brookes Davidson. Chris...whatever Doc's last name is. We're in it together, from now on..." Logic looked around the room once more, offering simply, "Are you with me?" The first person to signify agreement was Eegor, who said, "Yeah." Doc nodded, his attention focused on an imaginary spot on his broken glasses. Matthew Wakeman approached David (Logic), and much to Dave's surprise, embraced his fellow competitor with a hug. Caught entirely off guard, Logic could do nothing but return the embrace. He had to question the smile on Wakeman's face, but still was sure the gesture was mostly out of true feelings. "And I'm sure Brookes'll be up for it, he always likes the chance to cause carnage." Matthew smirked, "Besides, what would he do without having us to pick on and abuse?" "My thoughts exactly," Logic nodded. "You know what guys? I don't think OCW stands for Orlando Championship Wrestling, or even Obviously Curiosity Wrestling." It was Logic's turn to smirk. "It's Our Chance Wrestling. Our chance to show everyone what we're truly capable of." "I'm still fond of Obviously Curiosity Wrestling myself," said Doc, producing laughter from the friends that had seemingly found each other a little late. But better late than never, right?" It's Lonely Out Here Waving goodbye to Logic from the dock left things a little quiet. Mostly the survivors were lost in their own thoughts. Turning to Eegor, Matt asked, "What's your first name anyway?" Eegor said, "Paul." Rana nodded sitting down on the loading docks, "Brock and I used to hang out here before the shows until Brookes found us. Then he'd start fucking with us, so instead we went back to hang out with everybody else. There were times when Brookes would shut up. When Osyrus was around, Brookes was pretty quiet. Shawn Arrows didn't really take any crap from Brookes either. Brookes would pick on Hailowe but Hailowe would start shouting nonsense no one understood. Rogers would never say anything to him." "It was pretty strange, he's the biggest asshole I ever met but it kind of held us together as a group, to have to tolerate him." Eegor sat down next to Matt, unsure why he was being told this, but deciding that the kid wanted to vent a little, he saw no harm in it. "Rogers had this obnoxious manager, Benjamin Davis. The guy was really smarmy, irritating in fact. Thought he was better than the rest of us, and would boss Rogers around a lot. The satisfying thing though, as the contest progressed, Rogers got more and more tired of Davis. I think that maybe, had Rogers not been eliminated so soon he might've told his manager to go to hell. I hope he did anyway. I gotta say this though, Paul, you're a much better manager than Davis was." Paul blinked and said, "Thanks... I guess." "This has been so weird. I'm thinking of the Disney match, where they forced the nonsense onto us, and we didn't know better at the time to refuse. The chess game match or whatever it was. It was stupid. But I thought it was all in fun up until the point where they made Brookes and Arrows run all over the place. That's when it really occurred to me how dangerously stupid it was. Brookes' ankle was giving him trouble. Arrows' only wore a knee brace every moment that he wasn't showering. They shouldn't have had to do that. I felt bad about having to sing `Beauty and the Beast' but that wasn't nearly as bad as what they had to do." "Why are you telling me all this?" asked Eegor. "Because no one knows these things," said Matthew, "And I have to tell somebody about them. It's either you or ... Doc, and well, I think you're probably a little easier to communicate with. I don't know, I guess it's not important ... but it is important. This is the end of it. The last chance to say anything." Paul nodded once. There were certainly difficulties in trying to have conversation with an emotional teenager but he'd give it his best, "What is it that you want to say?" "I wish things could've worked out differently. I wish we could've done this without the fear, and the paranoia. I wish I was stronger in the beginning. Things might've worked out better in the end that way." Nodding despite Matthew not making any sense, Paul said, "Osyrus got signed to tSC. There is life after Survivor, I guess. Heh heh heh." "Oh yeah, of course," said Matthew, "Life goes on. But, is that what he wanted to do? Why didn't he wait to see what those goons had planned for us at the end?" Shrugging, Paul said, "Maybe he doesn't trust them." Leaning back and staring up at the Orlando sky Matthew said, "Maybe he was right." Misty water-coloured memories... The three survivors stood around in the loading bay, thinking of the time past, and the time ahead. Logic was clearly the least happy of the three, but he knew the decision wasn't a knife in the back. Curiosity imagined that being told up front would be better than finding out from an anonymous vote... but really his mind was just turning cartwheels. He was in to the final two. He still had a chance of winning Survivor. They all turned away from one another, and thoughts flowed like a river of... well... thoughts, I suppose. Doctor Curiosity was thinking about the man who had just picked him; Rana Venenosa. What was it about the good doctor that had made Rana choose him? As men they seemed very different. Doctor Curiosity had not been like many men in the competition. In fact, only one sprung to mind as being anything like him. Bantam Diablo. Sure, he wore a mask and tried to wrestle properly, but the Doc could sense something different within him. He wasn't trying to do things the proper way, he was just doing them in a way to make himself a success. Bantam Diablo had been set on world domination, a subject close to the doctor's heart, and he'd just gone right out there and tried to take a hold of the team. He had failed, much like Curiosity had failed to make as much of an impression on the others as he had hoped. Yet here he was, in the final of Survivor Four. Maybe there was hope for his sort after all. Maybe, in the end, you could be successful without being the best wrestler in the world. Many better wrestlers than Curiosity were gone. Christian Elijah had been his first opponent, and the win had gone to 'The Regular'. A man who spent most of his waking life on the toilet had beaten the great Doctor Curiosity. He had not been the first to get the 'W' in a match with The Curious One, and he would not be the last. Curiosity was still around, and the others were not. There would be hope for Bantam Diablo yet. QED, as Logic might say. The doctor stifled a laugh as he turned around and looked at the others once again. Rana had started talking to Eegor, but this was all immaterial now. Perhaps the assistant could even get some dirt on him for... No. Curiosity grew... well... curious with his own pattern of thoughts. It was going to come down to Rana Venenosa and himself, and he was turning down a chance to get some dirt on the masked wrestler? Surely that was the right thing to do, the most devious course of action to take? If he could tarnish the name of Rana Venenosa, then victory would be his. Yet he didn't want to. The survivors had been going for so long now, fighting as much against the system as against one another, that Rana didn't seem like the enemy. He seemed like a... He seemed like a friend. Curiosity almost regretted getting Eegor to make the posters saying that Rana was a known devil worshipper who has sex with animals before killing them. He actuallydid regret mailing a copy of the poster to each member of the three teams. The good doctor shrugged. What was done was done. The names ran through Curiosity's head as he strolled back over to join Eegor and Rana. Nick Santiago... there had always been something unusual about him. Now the doctor felt slightly less guilty for staring at his... at her ass in the locker room. Chris Moliano... the doctor's first win had been against that man. He'd gladly work with him again, as it had been a fun time. He'd helped Curiosity a lot at the time. The Mystery Man... who had clearly been La Parka in disguise, hence the lack of meaningful speech from him, and the appalling lack of skill in the ring. He could stay in England for the moment... and the doctor prayed he wouldn't have to go follow him. Logic... Logic. The man who had helped him through the group. They had nurtured a sort of team together, as a pair of 'acqvaintances'. Logic is someone he would miss, even if the big lump thought he was far more intelligent than he actually was. Which left Doctor Curiosity and Rana Venenosa... and then just one of them. Curiosity saw Rana lean backwards and speak to the heavens. Endings? Beginnings? When you can have this much fun, who cares? Epilogue At long last, David Walter Smith was free. The chains of the most oppressive physical and mental experience of his life had been undone. As much as he would have liked to keep them on for one more day, it was still a great relief to be virtually done with the contest. This meant that he could finally move out of the hotel room he had occupied for the duration of the contest. Room 319. "It is finally time for me to vacate these premises," he told himself, a suitcase of his belongings on the bed next to the duffel bag he transported his wrestling gear in. But for how long? If Orlando Championship Wrestling really was his destiny, he would be back in the city before too long. Although, if he was to be a permanent resident, he'd make sure that he at least had an apartment as opposed to a hotel room. He looked around the room one more time, almost as if awed that he could finally leave it. Logic looked down at the bed, upon which he had come up with plan after plan. The television set which had entertained him. The bathroom which he had showered in every night after every match. As much of a pain in the ass as it was to live out of a suitcase, Logic knew he would never be able to forget Room 319. For it was here, and in the Milkhouse Arena, that a man who had been wrestling for six months managed to parlay his skills and intelligence into coming third in the Survivor contest. Third was good. But wherever Logic ended up, he resolved to make it all the way to the top. And with that thought firmly in mind, the big man scooped his baggage over his shoulder, and exited both the hotel and Survivor IV, stage left. Meltdown would be tomorrow. And with any luck, it would be a better day than today.
