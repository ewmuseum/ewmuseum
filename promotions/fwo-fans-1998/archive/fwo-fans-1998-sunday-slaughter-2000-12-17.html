Sunday Slaughter, 17 December 2000

17 December 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A Different Primetime? Fresh off of a solid pay-per-view just twenty four hours earlier, the fans Wrestling organization braced itself for the night after fWo Twilight. A pay-per-view that saw three titles change hands, Ric Chronos get screwed (again) by Primetime, and the fWo career of the Flying Frenchie. A pay-per-view that was truly unforgettable in every sense of the word. A pay-per-view that set up the final live Sunday Night Slaughter of 2000, of which fans inside the Phillips Arena in Atlanta, Georgia prepared for. fWo ring announcer Matt Panzer counted down from ten and as he reached one, fans began screaming wildly, while the typical fireworks shot off from strategically placed locations inside the arena. The cheers soon turned to boos, however, as the reigning fWo World Heavyweight Champion Primetime came out to the sounds of 'Snap', by Slipknot. Primetime didn't look as confident and arrogant as he usually does, instead he wore a different expression, one we certainly hadn't seen him use before. It seemed odd that Primetime would appear 'down' when he was twenty four hours removed from undoubtedly the biggest win of his career, not to mention his career and job with the fWo still intact. Primetime reached the ring and didn't pose or mock the crowd as he we were accustomed to him doing. No, he entered the ring and asked for a microphone. It appeared as if he had something to get off his chest. Finally, after the fans stopped booing him loudly, and calling him an 'asshole', the champion commenced speaking. "Last night, at Twilight, everyone on the fWo roster went out in front of forty thousand people, and put on the best show they possibly could. They put their hearts, their souls, their livelyhood on the line to make sure that Twilight would go down as one of the best pay-per-views anyone has ever witnessed. The standard was set last night, and may never again be surpassed. I can't possibly express what it was like to be part of such a show. Guys who you hate and despise, all working towards one common goal. To give you the best show we possibly could. To put on the best matches we possibly could. Everyone did their part. Everyone showed up with their working boots on, so to speak. The effort we put into last night's show was the epitome of what every wrestling organization should try to live up to each and every time you guys spend your hard earned money to watch us do what we do best - entertain. And hopefully, all of you were entertained last night. But despite all of that, I couldn't help but notice during my match with Frenchie - who I will refer to by his name from here on out. I couldn't help but notice that during my match with Pierre, that the respect I once had for him, started to come back. I no longer looked at him as my number one enemy - I looked at him as a man who slowly, but surely, was bringing out the best of me. He was making me do all that it could. Nobody has pushed me to that limit before. Nobody. Not Mitch Wilson, not Wolf Fang, not Wretch, not Mike Bear... not Havok, not Austin Davis, not Lance Star, not Courage... no one. I have never been so into a match like I was last night. We took it to each other like no two other wrestlers have before. One of us would raise the bar, and the other would raise the bar this much higher the next time. I've never been so motivated to win a match in all my life. The confidence I showed to you this week during my promos - was not there. It was a smokescreen to how I truly felt this past week. I was scared. But as the match progressed, I gained confidence. And I gained respect - respect for the man that I was wrestling against. Respect for not only Pierre the wrestler, but Pierre the person. And now that he is gone, it's a shame. There's not much more I can say about him here tonight, but Pierre, I have two words for you. Two words that are truly how I feel right now. Two words that will sum up how I feel about you, your career, and our match last night at Twilight." Primetime rubbed his chin before taking the fWo World Title off his waist and putting it up on his shoulder. He then looked at the crowd, and with a very serious expression, he asked for the nearest camera to zoom in for a close-up - but not too close. The cameras captured his entire body. It captured him... grabbing his crotch. "Respect THIS!" The fans began booing loudly. The Phillips Arena was rocked by the amount of jeers Primetime received. The arrogant youngster then looked at the camera, and acted as if he was addressing The Frenchie directly. "Pierre, thank God your career in the fWo is over. Thank God that you won't be in the fWo again. Why? Because quite frankly, I did the fWo a favor. I got rid of you, for good. And I know of at least one man around here that's happy about that. He and I may not be on the best of terms, but Mitch and I can agree on one thing - good riddance, Pierre. Good riddance. You did take me to the limit last night - but your best wasn't better than my *worst*. I only have one thing to say to you... don't let the door hit you on your ass on the way out!" Primetime grinned deviously, as the ovation of boos grew louder. Primetime then changed the subject. "Mike Bear, you have the unfortunate task of facing me here tonight. You call your finisher the whiplash. Well, as most people know, whiplashes tend to happen in car accidents. And what is the one sound whiplash makes? That one sound... is *snap*. And that is the sound you're going to be hearing later tonight. Snap, crackle and pop, Bear. And just like your former Underground mate, your career will be over, too." Primetime raised his arms and basked in the detest of the crowd. He thrived making the fans hate him, and tonight he succeeded admirably. Just when you thought he couldn't sink any lower, he did. But tonight, he would be defending the fWo World Title... he would walk in as champion tonight, but would he walk out as champion? With that question in mind, Slaughter was well underway... and now, so it seemed, backstage... Important Meeting A limosuine arrived at the Phillips Arena, and out stepped the acting owner of the fWo, James Armstrong. He held the door open as about five men also stepped out of the vehicle, all dressed sharply in nice suits. Armstrong retrieved a briefcase from the limosuine, and the six men began walking into the arena. Dick Venom walked up to Armstrong to tell him something, but Armstrong would have none of it. "Dick, I have a very important meeting tonight with some investors here at the arena tonight in Atlanta. Please, do not disturb me for any issue." Armstrong began to push Venom out of his way slightly, but Venom asked him a question. "Is it about the sale of the fWo?" Armstrong sighed. "Since you let the cat out of the bag, yes, it is about the sale of the fWo. And that's why it's a VERY IMPORTANT MEETING. Please, tell people I'll be in my office tonight, but not to disturb me for ANY issue. Okay?" Venom nodded, and moved out of Armstrong' way. The cameras followed Armstrong and the men long enough to hear Armstrong ask them if they liked bun cake, before Slaughter went to a commercial break. Carnage (c) vs. Sphere vs. Vengence Sunday Slaughter returns to the sounds of "Wicker Man" by Iron Maiden. One half of the Guano Apes, Sphere, comes out and he's ready for his hardcore title match. As he prepares inside of the ring, "Fire on High" by ELO, whoever the hell they are, starts up, and Vengence comes out to a bad reaction from the crowd. He ignores the jeering, and simply waits for his other opponent. "Guilty Conscience" by Eminem starts up, and Carnage, the reigning fWo Hardcore Champion comes out. He immediately runs at Vengence and the two begin brawling on the aisleway. Sphere takes this all in, until he decides to join the action with a suicide plancha that takes down both of his opponents. He begins pounding away with rights and lefts to the head of the champion, until Vengence grabs Sphere from behind. He sends him head first into the ringpost, and Sphere falls to the ground. Vengence goes to continue his attack on Sphere, but from behind, Carnage comes with a low blow that keels Vengence over. He turns Vengence around and then delivers a crushing DDT to the outside. Carnage rolls Vengence into the ring and then covers, but as soon as you know it, Sphere is in to break the count up with a top rope elbow drop. Sphere then covers Carnage himself, but the Hardcore Champ is able to manage a kick out. Sphere then sends Carnage into the ropes, and nails a tilt a whirl backbreaker. Vengence then comes from behind and spins Sphere around. He plants him with a death valley driver, and then covers, but Sphere kicks out at two. Vengence brings Sphere up and hooks him for a chokeslam, but Carnage nails an axhandle to the back of Vengence, and that causes him to drop Sphere to the mat without damage. He spins Vengence around, and then nails a snap suplex. He covers, and Vengence easily kicks out at two. Carnage brings him up, and looks for an irish whip, but on the way back, Vengence is met with a superkick from Sphere. Sphere then quickly jumps onto cover Vengence, but Carnage quickly breaks that up. Carnage hooks Sphere, and then brings him over with a fisherman's suplex. He hooks, but Sphere breaks free at two. Vengence then comes over, and sends Carnage into the ropes, and nails a big boot to the face, followed by a quick legdrop. He covers, but since Vengence isn't Hulk Hogan, Carnage kicks out. Sphere then jumps on Vengence's back, but Vengence turns that into a big samoan drop. He covers, but Carnage breaks that up. Carnage looks for a vertical suplex, but Vengence gets out of it, and from behind, Vengence throws him over the top rope and to the outside. Vengence then goes back over to Sphere, hooks him, and then delivers a big chokeslam. With Sphere and Carnage both down, it would seem like a great time to get the cover, but, mysteriously, Vengence leaves the ring, and heads to the back. Perplexed, Carnage looks at the situation, and then stumbles to the top rope, where he flies off with the big top rope elbow drop onto Sphere. He covers, and at three, retains the Hardcore Title. As Carnage celebrates inside of the ring, and the fans wonder why Vengence simply left, Slaughter rolls on. Primetime began walking down the ramp, and Chronos couldn't take the mere sight of him anymore.
