Extra Effort The Haven Diner New Haven, CT 2:15 PM local time "Chicken Ceasar Salad, dressing on the side, and water, please. And a baked potato with sour cream." The waitress took down Silver Fox's order, turning toward Poison Ivy. "And for you, hon?" Ivy turned toward her and gestured with her hands. "A bacon cheeseburger, with a fried egg on top. And a side of onion rings. And a coffee." She wrote the order down, and turned away before Ivy could see the disgusted look on her face. However, the Psycho Bitch saw the disgusted look on Fox's face. "What?" asked Ivy. "You're gonna have a heart attack before you're thirty," replied Fox, sipping his water, "the fWo is going to be indicted in wanton recklessness as it relates to your arteries, and we're all going to watch you have twelve consecutive heart attacks." Ivy smiled at him. "Ain't happened yet. I've been eating diner food my entire life." Fox laughed and nodded at her. "How've you been feeling?" "Watch this," replied Ivy, as she stretched both her arms straight above her head, "I can stretch without pain in my shoulder, without worrying about tearing any of my stitches since they're all out... I'm finally out of pain and it's bliss. Can't work out yet, but one thing at a time, right?" "Exactly." Her coffee arrived, and she added liberal amounts of cream and sugar to it. "So, what's on your mind?" "Why should something be on my mind?" asked Fox, "We haven't spoken in a few weeks, and I was wondering how you were doing." Ivy shook her head. "You don't call me at home, ask me to pack for a night out, and tell me that there's a ticket waiting for me at the counter at Grand Central, just to find out how I am. What's the deal?" Fox sighed. She could always get a killer read on him. He reached into his coat pocket and pulled out a white envelope, and slid it across the table toward her. "What's this?" "Open it." "... Tokyo." "Yep." "Listen, Fox--" "Hear me out, Ivy." She gestured to him to continue. "Things have been different since you asked for your release--" "Things look good," interrupted Ivy, "Everyone's stepped it up a notch for the end of the year push toward Countdown." "Which is true," continued Fox, "but things could always be better. When Donaven left, when you and Elijah left, we lost a good set of locker room leaders." "Bull," replied Ivy, "There's Brandon. There's Knight. There's Creed, such as he is. There's Big D. There's Crisp, whether he wants to admit it or not. You guys, again, have been doing just fine." Gonna turn me in?" "Why? You're part of the family. We finally realized that while we've been looking for a way to push things into overdrive... we had the perfect catalyst sitting at home doing nothing." Fox was about to answer her but the food showed up. They ate in silence for a few minutes, before he wiped his mouth on his napkin. "So tell me, Ivy... where in your busy life did you have time to take a train for two and a half hours from Manhatten to New Haven for an afternoon lunch?" She glared at him, but her mouth was full. You miss everyone in the locker room but you don't want to be there if you're not contributing." "I--" "And you don't feel like yourself being in the lockerroom without Eli or Sean there." Will you grant us that much?" She took a bite of her heart attack on a bun, and thought about it. She thought about it. "Sure." "Hey, my pleasure, Fox." Hunted Carrying nothing with him, Jack Lundin entered the arena nonchalantly. He was not booked for a match this evening, and that was fine with him. Where there had once been an abundance of TV time and only a handful of active competitors, there were now so many wrestlers wanting to try to shine, it was hard to fit everyone on the shows. And being just the lowly hardcore champion, Lundin found that getting him on every show was simply not a priority management ranked highly. That suited Lundin just fine. His purpose since returning to the fWo so many months before as Remnant had been singular, and his wanted ends were not TV time. Hell, they weren't even the Hardcore Title. Occasionally he stumbled upon such things, and this did not bother him, but he did not seek them out. No, that wasn't quite right either. The Hardcore Title HAD become important to him, but only insofar as too keep it off the Dynamo. The Dynamo had loved that title, and that he couldn't have it bothered him. That Lundin had it was all the merrier. It was just another weight stacked atop young Masters' shoulders. He was cracking. Lundin knew it. Silver Fox knew it. Sam Smooth knew it. The fans knew it. The only one who didn't know it, it seemed, was the Dynamo. As Lundin turned a corner he heard a shuffling. Before he could turn towards the direction of the noise, which was somewhere behind him and to his right, he felt the serrations of a thick blade pressed against his Adam's apple. A feeling struck Lundin that was simultaneously distant and familiar. It was something he had not felt for some time...years. Lundin had become the hunted. Emo Kids (c) vs. Walden & Matheson Note: Not only is this an fWo Tag Team Championship Match, but it is also part of the Round Robin. The Emo Kids wouldn't have to wait long as Wide Mouth Mason played through the speakers and the tandem of Tim Walden and Jane Matheson walked down to the ring. The referee lifted the titles into the air as the brothers took off their t-shirts and Atkins went to the apron. Walden stayed outside for the challengers and Jane and Chris started to circle each other and the match was on. Carraba got the better of the opening tieup and got Matheson in a front headlock. Jane blocked an attempted bulldog and shoved "The Heart of the Kids" into the ropes. He rebounded back and got a nice flying elbow for his efforts. Carraba stumbled back, and this time comfortably went behind for a wrist lock. He wrenched the wrist a little bit, until Jane struggled out and started to hit some knife edge chops. Jane chopped him straight into a corner and whipped him across to another corner. Too bad for Chris Carter, it was the challenger's corner. Jane tagged in Tim, who promptly started to land forearms on the younger Carter. The referee pulled the two away, and Tim grabbed the arm of Carraba and whipped him into the ropes. Carraba came back and got taken down to the mat with a fierce waist takedown. He goes for the pin, however gets halfway to the win before the kick out. The two popped up and this time Carraba ran at him and got a dropkick for his efforts. With Carraba on the mat, The "Tax Man" studied his opponent carefully. He had seen what Carraba could do with his athletic ability and Walden wasn't about to let the "Rock Star" get the best of him. He pulled Carraba up to his feet, kicked him in the midsection, and sent him back down to the mat with a "Due Date." Walden went for the in. One, two, but Carraba somehow got a shoulder up which got a nice pop from the crowd. Walden looked around and shook his head at the referee. Walden rose back up to his feet and tagged in Matheson. Matheson threw a punch at Carraba who was rising back to his feet and sent him bouncing off the ropes. Carraba came back and dove at Matheson. Problem: Matheson also dove at Carraba. Result: Both men, or should I say, the man and woman were sent flying down to the mat. The crowd stood and "ooohed" as the collision took place. Now, referee Daniel Adams began counting the downtime for both competitors. One, no movement at all. Two, still, no movement. Three, Carraba shook his head and started to regain awareness. Four, Carraba slowly started pushing himself back up to his feet. Five, Carraba continued and Jane started regaining her awareness as well. Six, Carraba got back to a knee as Matheson started pushing herself up. Seven, the crowd started cheering Carraba spotted Atkins, whom was reaching anxiously for the tag. Matheson got back to a knee also. Carraba reached over towards Jim "Atkins" Carter. Tim Walden knew that this was very important. He was getting a shot at the tag team titles AND competing in the round robin at the same time. He might not get this opportunity again, so he did what all smart wrestlers should do. Walden entered the ring causing referee Daniel Adams to turn his attention to him. Carraba dove out and tagged in "Atkins" which got the crowd roaring like crazy. As Matheson regained her balance, Atkins muttered "forgive me, mom" and clotheslined Matheson to hell. Walden obliged with the referee's orders and got out of the ring. "HEY! WHAT THE HELL DO YOU THINK YOU'RE DOING!" The referee asked Atkins. Of course, Walden had outsmarted both of the Emo Kids and got the referee's attention. Therefore, no tag was seen, and Carraba was STILL the legal man. The crowd booed Tim Walden, but he didn't care. Matheson was already in the corner after the clothesline from Atkins so Walden simply made the blind tag. Carraba was still down, out of breath, and Walden enjoyed it. Walden pulled Carraba's "rock star" hair and kicked him in the midsection. He attempted another due date, but Carraba pushed out and sent Walden flying into the ropes. Carraba attempted a clothesline, but Walden ducked under. Instead, he caught him in position for. No Evasion. The crowd gasped, as Matheson ran at "Atkins" to knock him off the ring apron. Walden hooked Carraba's leg as the referee began count. One. Two. Three. And the hearts of millions of emo fans were broken. Their gods, the Emo Kids, had just lost their tag team titles in their first title defense. Tim Walden and Jane Matheson were the new tag team champions of the fWo. "Midnight Rain" played as Daniel Adams handed Tim Walden and Jane Matheson the tag titles and raise their hands in the air.
