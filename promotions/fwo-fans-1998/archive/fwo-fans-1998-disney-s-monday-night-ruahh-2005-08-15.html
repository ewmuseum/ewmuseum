Introduction Turmoil has engulfed the Fans Wrestling Organization. The high ticket prices of events is in dispute. Hoping to resolve the matter through brute force, the greedy Ticketmaster has stopped all online sales of wrestling tickets. While the promoters and Walt Disney Corporation parent company endlessly debate this alarming chain of events, the backstage agents have secretly dispatched bookers, the organizers of wrestling events and angles, to settle the conflict... Punishment "You wanted to see me?" Ted Tallison sat behind his desk, looking at some papers on a clipboard. "Yes, Ivy... come in." Ivy McGinnis closed the door behind her and sat down across the desk from the Commissioner. She checked her watch - this meeting had better be over quickly, her staff should be arriving any minute and they had a lot of work to do. The Convention and Trade Center was a fairly new building, one that nobody had any familiarity with. "Remind me, Ivy, what your job is." Eh? "I do a little bit of everything," replied Ivy, "mostly with the techs, but--" "You're a tech," interrupted Tallison, "as you seem to constantly forget. You didn't want to be on television anymore, but somehow you just keep popping up." "No, no, no," countered Ivy, crossing her arms, "I was on TV once because Deacon needed some support. I was on once more for the same reason - and I was worried about him. He's my fr--" "This is business, not friendship," interrupted Tallison, again, "and you're setting a bad example for the rest of your staff. We go back a long ways, Ivy, and I hoped we could have a professional relationship without all this acting out, but apparently I misjudged you." This time, Ivy stood up, her hands on the desk. "Acting out? What the hell are you talking about?" "Come on, Ivy," replied Tallison, "It's obvious you wanted my job when Dudley turned up missing, and I can tell it burned you when I got it instead. But that's no reason to cross the line and ignore the boundaries between the wrestlers and the staff." He put the papers in his hands aside and leaned back in his chair, while Ivy slapped her hand against her forehead. "This is a show, you fucking moron," she spat out, which changed his mood drastically, "and everyone... everyone back there has an equal share in putting it on. There's no 'lines,' no matter how much you'd like there to be. And if I, or any of my boys, can help the show by being in front of the camera, then that's what's gonna happen." This time, Tallison stood up, and Ivy was reminded just how much larger he was than she. "Go home, Ivy," said Tallison, "and stay there until I call you and tell you to come back to work." "What?" she asked, in disbelief, "Are you suspending me?" "Call it what you want," he answered, "but you continually show up on television after being specifically told to stay away, you don't listen to your managers orders, and you're obviously intent on being a disturbance, and I can't have that here tonight. We're two weeks away from Meltdown and the last thing I need is one back here." He sat back down and returned his attention to his papers. Ivy paced back and forth angrily for a moment, but every time she started to talk, she looked at him and realized he was fit to simply ignore her. She had no choice, but to leave the office, slamming the door on her way. Tallison looked up at the sound of the slam, and reached for his Nextel. "Jones?" he asked, trying to raise the head of security, "If McGinnis isn't outside in the next five minutes, discreetly remove her, please." Outside the office, Ivy kicked the wall, hard. "You okay, Ms. Mac?" asked Travis, one of her techs. "Can you guys run things tonight?" she asked back, "Tallison's just suspended me, and you guys are gonna have to fly solo tonight. Are you sure you can get the system figured?" He looked at the other four members of the team, then back to Ivy. "He's sending you home?" "Yep," replied Ivy, icily, as she walked around them and headed for the back exit. The five techs followed her closely, "so again... are you guys gonna be okay?" "Definitely," replied Joe, "We know what we're doing." "Good," she said, "I've got a lotta faith in you." She continued walking while the rest of them stopped. "So what are we gonna do?" asked Travis. "I've got an idea," replied Joe, "but I'm gonna need all of you to be with me on it. Are we in this together?" Yes. Yes they were. Venting The two huge men in black suits barely acknowledged Cabbot Wilson as he walked between them and opened the door. "Are all of those security guards necessary?" he asked as he stepped into the room and closed the door behind him. "You think Graphic Violence is going to come after you now?" Kodiak Vic Creed slowly taped his fists, covering the recent cuts on his hands. Cabbot tried to ignore the slight tremble in his fingers, but he didn't know if they were caused by nerves or the increase in drugs the Main Man had started taking. Punching holes in furniture tended to do damage to the puncher as well. Apparently the standard painkillers weren't enough for Kodiak Vic Creed. But then, Vic always thought he was a special case. Cabbot walked up to his client, who sat on the edge of his chair wearing only a pair of underwear. "I have the security to keep people out for their own good." As if Vic knew what was good for anyone, including himself Cabbot thought as he watched him clench his fists. If he was going to be a success in the fWo, or anywhere for that matter, the problem needed to be addressed. "Vic, I don't mean any disrespect here. You know I respect you. But it can't be healthy to keep mourning your daughter like you have been." The tape was set down on the floor, and Vic held up his disfigured foot. The skin was still black and discolored in places, and he wiggled his remaining toes in his revolted agent's direction. "I lost these two toes standing outside my cabin in the middle of winter, watching the police and my sister take my daughter out of my custody. Funny, I never thought about my toes before then. Now I can't ever forget them." Creed's voice had as much passion as one would have discussing what they had for breakfast. It was this suppression, this dwelling on the past, that was keeping Creed from moving on. His self-destructiveness was getting worse, and Cabbot hadn't signed on to a sinking ship. Which was what this had turned into. Vic set his foot down and Wilson rested his hand on the big man's shoulder. "You've got to let go Vic. This isn't healthy. It's been affecting your work." Vic looked at the fingers touching his skin. "Take your hand off me Cabbot." So much for reaching out. Cabbot crammed his hands into the pockets of his jacket and began to pace the length of the room. He couldn't hide his frustration any longer. "You blew a chance at the World Title. "You better start remembering your place," he said as he stood up. "I heard all the shit you were talking about me all week, and you're lucky I didn't beat your head in for it." Cabbot saw the Juggernaut's jaw clench and thought he was about to be bludgeoned to death when Creed's fist flew. Luckily for the agent, Vic only punched a hole in the wall. Twice. Okay, three times. Cabbot smoothed the front of his suit and brushed the plaster dust off his shoulder. "I'm surprised you could hear anything at all after crawling into whatever pill bottle you had with you." Vic had started to walk away from his agent, but stopped after Cabbot's last comment. He pointed a finger at Wilson. "See, now that's the kind of bullshit I was talking about. Don't sass up on me. You're not irreplaceable, and you're certainly not untouchable." Cabbot was suddenly glad he had a contingency plan to get to the top in the fWo, because it was becoming clear that Kodiak Vic Creed would not be able to do it. He thought about all the abuse he'd taken from the Main Man, all the illegal things he'd done for him, and for what? For Creed to drop to his knees and weep on national television over his kid. What a waste. Wilson spoke bitterly as KVC pulled a pair of socks out of his small suitcase. "Fine. Forgive me for being disappointed in your poor decision making abilities. I'm doing everything on my end to make you a success. You just aren't listening to me. If you don't want to be serious about this anymore, I don't know what I'm doing here." The Main Man was stunned. "What is this? Did your balls drop or something?" The shock wore off and was replaced with a slow-burning anger. "You don't talk to me like you're running the show. I'm calling the shots. Remember that. It might save your life one day." Not if I set fire to one of Sarah's dolls, crybaby. Cabbot's comments were brash, almost flippant. "All the drugs you're taking. The booze. Now every big goon thought they could bully him. They'd all pay. For now, Cabbot Wilson put on a brave face and a happy smile. "Whatever you want Vic. Good luck in your match tonight." Creed walked into the bathroom and slammed the door. "Tonight, I'm not the one who's going to need luck," he shouted from the other side of the door.
