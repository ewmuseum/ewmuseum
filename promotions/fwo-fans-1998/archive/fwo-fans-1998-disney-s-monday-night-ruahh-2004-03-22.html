The Flying Frenchie stepped out onto the entrance ramp, just below his twenty-foot tall image. Fireworks exploded on the stage around him, and blue, white and red lasers flashed along the ramp in rhythm to the music. In a minute this crowd would hate him, but he stood on the stage soaking in the cheers of fans who had not seen him in action since Countdown 1. In wrestling, even the villains get heartfelt applause from time to time. Frenchie paced towards the ring, his eyes fixated on it, getting back into his zone. He slid under the bottom rope, and stood up in the middle of the ring. He turned in a slow circle, flashing his trademarked and copyrighted beam to the audience, as the very ring posts let out streams of Frenchie-centric pyro. This was his moment. And then it was gone, as "The Hate in Me" brought out the number one contender for the FWO World Title, the man who had earned the right to challenge for that title at Cyberslam by winning Body Count, and had been defending that right for the last month. Black Quicksilver made a beeline for the ring, ignoring the crowd reaction. In the ring stood a man he'd never lost to, a man who many thought of as an FWO legend, while they dismissed his own vaunted history. BQ knew that this would be a grueling encounter, but believed it would end much like every other match he'd had with Frenchie. Black Quicksilver climbed up the ring steps, stepped onto the apron, then flew off into the guardrail, caught by an unexpected Frenchie superkick. Ding ding ding. Frenchie ran the ropes, and then dove out onto Quicksilver as he stood up, driving his back against the guardrail again. Now positioned on top of BQ, Frenchie flailed away at him, punching him repeatedly in the face. Tiring of that, Frenchie got up, picking up BQ as well, and whipped him into the apron. Quicksilver grimaced, stumbled forward, and was scooped up, then slammed down onto the pretty ringside mats. Frenchie slid back into the ring, and stood in the far corner as the official started a ten count. A win was a win, and a count-out would give him the Cyberslam title match, just the same as actually beating Quicksilver. The referee had only hit five by the time Quicksilver had regained his senses and his footing. BQ rubbed his back a bit as he paced outside the ring, giving himself the most time allowable to recover from Frenchie's cheap shots. At nine, he slid back into ring, making sure that the official was between him and Frenchie. BQ and TFF circled each other, waiting for an opening. They lunged at each other at the same time, but BQ went low and swept the legs, bringing Frenchie to his back. Still holding Frenchie's legs, Quicksilver kicked his thigh a few times, and then flipped over him, bringing his feet down to his ears. Frenchie flopped on the mat, and rolled to the nearest ropes, grasping them while clutching his own back. He glowered at Quicksilver, who smirked in return, then knocked him out of the ring with a baseball slide. Black Quicksilver wasted no time, grabbing the top rope and sailing out of the ring and on top of Frenchie with a slingshot senton. Black Quicksilver rolled along with the move and back to his feet, before dropping an elbow on Frenchie. BQ got up, waiting for Frenchie to do the same, then grabbed the back of his head and rammed him into the ring post. BQ leapt back up to the apron, then vaulted off with an Asai moonsault to the wobbly Frenchman. Getting back to his feet, Quicksilver popped his head under the bottom rope to break the count, and then retrieved The Flying Frenchie, opting to roll him back into the ring. Black Quicksilver climbed to the top rope, thinking to hit his patented Silver Splash, but noticed that Frenchie was already back to his knees. He staid on the top, perched until Frenchie turned towards him, then jumped onto Frenchie's shoulders, falling backwards into a hurricanrana. He hooked the legs, getting a two count. Both men got back to their feet, and Frenchie struck first, with a series of chops and European uppercuts, driving Black Quicksilver backwards to the corner. There, he delivered a spinning back elbow to stun BQ, and lifted him to the top turnbuckle. He followed up, getting a front face lock, and looking for a superplex. BQ stopped that with several closed hands to the ribs, getting Frenchie to release the face lock. He shoved Frenchie off the turnbuckle, and then applied his own face lock, looking for a spinning DDT. Frenchie shrugged him off in mid move, reversing it into a spinebuster that left BQ folded in half on the mat. Frenchie covered, his upper body pressing BQ's legs onto BQ's chest, his own legs on the second ropes. That nearly got the three count, but Black Quicksilver got the shoulder up in time. Pierre stood up, argued with the ref for a bit, then stalked off to a far corner. Meanwhile, Quicksilver pulled himself back to his feet, and marched off towards Frenchie. BQ grabbed his arm, looking to whip him into the ropes, but that was reversed. Quicksilver went off the ropes and back towards TFF, extending his arm for a clothesline. Frenchie ducked under it, (no mean feat, given the height difference), and caught BQ in a sleeper hold, a move which had won precisely one match for him. It quickly became evident that he was not looking for a submission, as he subtly twisted BQ's mask, obscuring his vision. Taking that as a fait accompli, he released the hold and spun BQ around, slapping him around the face for fun. Realizing that it wouldn't take long for BQ to correct the problem, he placed him in a standing head scissors, and then lifted him into the air for a powerbomb. "Trois, deux, un," he muttered before bringing BQ crashing to the mat with the Timebomb. He grabbed Black Quicksilver's legs, thinking for a second to sprawl on him for a cover again, then thought better of it, and locked in the Cannes Cloverleaf. Black Quicksilver grunted, but had his priorities straight. First, fix the mask. Check. Second, look for a way out. He had been planted pretty much in the center of the ring, so there'd be no quick grabbing of the ropes. He was slightly closer to the ropes on his left, so he crawled right. His gambit worked, as Frenchie pulled back to the left, and Quicksilver simply used his momentum to get to the ropes. Frenchie released almost immediately. He spun around, kicking at Black Quicksilver, trying to knock him out of the ring, but to no avail. Even under the barrage of knee strikes, BQ got up to one knee, then both, and finally to his feet. Frenchie didn't relent with the kicking, until Black Quicksilver ducked under a high kick, and whipped Frenchie into a turnbuckle. BQ followed in with a knee smash, then whipped him to the opposite corner, and followed that with a handspring elbow. He grabbed a face lock, then cradled the leg, and placed Frenchie firmly into the mat with the Silver Lining. He covered. One. Two. KICKOUT. Quicksilver didn't take that too nicely, but also didn't waste any time arguing with the official, or anything else as counterproductive. Still in the corner, he grabbed the top rope with both hands, pulled himself to the top, and came back down with a split legged moonsault. One. Two. Foot on the ropes. BQ looked at the top rope, but Frenchie was already stirring. He pulled Frenchie up to his feet, and was met with the ultimate devilry: a thumb to the eye. Black Quicksilver staggered away from Frenchie, his hands to his face, and was left completely open to being scooped up in a fireman's carry, which he was. One DVD later, and Frenchie was climbing the top rope. Up top, he noticed immediately that he'd erred, and that BQ was too close to the ropes, to go for the Guillotine, so he opted for the 450 splash instead. He'd won many a match with that move, and a title or two to boot. Frenchie hooked the leg. One. Two. Shoulder up. Muttering various French vulgarities, Frenchie picked up Black Quicksilver, and then delivered a picture perfect brain buster, perhaps not the best in the business, but not too shabby. He saw Black Quicksilver's positioning, and smiled. Nearly mid ring, the distance was just right for Frenchie's plan. He climbed the turnbuckle once more, measured the distance, and Frenchie flew. His right leg extended, he came down exactly where BQ's throat had been. Yes, had. He wasn't there anymore, to Frenchie's chagrin, but you wouldn't hear Black Quicksilver complaining about it. BQ went for the jackknife pin. One. Two. Three. Black Quicksilver let the official raise his hand in victory. It had been as tough as any of his previous matches with the Flying Frenchie, but once again, he walked away with the win. Only this time, he was one step closer to assuring that he would meet Jon Crisp at Cyberslam, with the World Title on the line. Also much like Uprising...someone's entrance theme hit. "Not again.", BQ thought to himself. "Gonna Fly Now". Jon Crisp, the fWo World Champion, stepped out onto the ramp. Although many names were considered for this match, due to one reason or another, most of them were unable to be signed on to face you. There was one name that made it through, and as result you'll be facing Kellen Kinkade next Wednesday." This announcement was met with a chorus of boos, since, even though about half the crowd didn't like BQ, the *entire* crowd hated Kinkade even more. "Hey, hey now, don't waste all your boos just yet, I haven't told you the BAD news.", Crisp said to the crowd. The crowd began to boo once more, thinking they were being robbed of seeing Jon Crisp vs. Scott Slugger for the World Title. "Yes, that's right...that match is now a TRIANGLE match. The third man? Well, it's someone who you should be familiar with, Sean. Next Wednesday at Unleashed, I will be defending the World Heavyweight Championship against not only Scott Slugger... ...but Ric Chronos as well." BQ couldn't believe it. He had JUST defeated Chronos four days ago to keep his shot, and Chronos ends up getting a shot at the title anyway...a whole month before *his* shot. "I know you're a bit ticked off, Sean...seeing how two guys you've beaten in the past month to hold onto that shot are getting an opportunity at MY title, but hey...shouldn't matter to you. You're used to getting screwed over by now, right? Well, I've wasted enough of my time out here. See you at CyberSlam, Sean. Maybe."
