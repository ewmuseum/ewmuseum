Wednesday Warfare, 5 January 2000

5 January 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Introduction Chris Finn stood outside the arena and paced back and forth. There was no sign of the owner of the fWo, and had not been since David Spencer drug him off almost a week ago. Chris Finn idly wondered if Spencer had gone to far and perhaps killed the young promoter. He doubted it, but all the same the new year was here, the fWo was in one of the largest venues in the world, and no sign of the owner. There was also no sign of James Armstrong. After it was certain that the owner wasn't going to show up, Finn had tried calling Armstrong at his winter home in Florida. There wasn't any answer, and Finn wondered if he wasn't home, or perhaps on a drunken binge that in nine months would earn him several new child support payments. Finn had been busy, and many were turning to him for advice. He wasn't comfortable giving it. For the last four days he had been talking to reporters, arranging arena venues, dealing with contract negotiations, and working to clean up some of the chaos Armstrong had managed to cause during his short tenure as acting commissioner. Finn didn't see Chris Universal until he bumped into him, and spun around angrily. Universal sneered at Finn, and something inside the young announcer snapped. "Dammit, Chris... can't you watch where you're going? Yeah, I know you're rich and the 'common man' means nothing to you, but perhaps just a tiny little bit of compassion is in order? I mean, I've worked my butt off to try and keep this promotion afloat, but does it mean anything to you? Do my efforts mean a damn thing to Mr. High-and-Mighty rich boy? Does the fact that I helped promote your World Title match tonight into the early hours of the morning mean anything to you? Does it? Well?" Chris Finn fumed, panting slightly. Universal was taken aback, and blinked at the young announcer. He cleared his throat and offered Finn a hand. "Well gosh, Chris... I never thought about it that way. I guess I should say thanks." Finn looked at Universal's hand in confusion, and then shook it. "Really?," Finn asked, timidly. Universal laughed. "No, dumbass... you're a stupid announcer who is the product of inbreeding." Universal held fast onto Finn's hand, and swung him around until he slammed into a pile of crates against the wall. Universal dusted off his hands and laughed as he walked back into the depths of the arena, and from even deeper inside fireworks sounded as the first Warfare of the millennium took to the air. Underground vs. Canadian Connection vs. Ultra Cool 2000 vs. Chaos The Canadian Connection already stood in the ring as Warfare began the show. As Cactus Jon, their corner man, gave Tom and the Ninja advice on their upcoming bout, the Dark Riders made their way to the ring and immediately attacked the tag team of the foreigners. While the two groups brawled, both the Underground and Ultra Violet came running from the back. Upon hitting the ring Mike Bear charged Cactus Jon and the two quickly took it to the outside. Meanwhile, Plague and War executed a spiked powerbomb on Tom the Bomb and went for the pin, but their effort was quickly broken up by the Ninja and Wretch. Wretch dragged Plague towards the ropes and threw him to the outside, where Fear proceeded to batter the lone Dark Rider with a steel folding chair. Inside the ring, Ultra Violet worked over an already dazed Tom the Bomb in the corner while War crept behind Wretch and planted the Underground member with a low German suplex. War quickly went for the pin, but Mike Bear, having disposed of Jon with the help of Fear, slid into the ring in the nick of time and saved his partner. Plague was about to reenter the ring when he noticed Kid Cool slowly making his way down the ramp, guided by Violet. Plague grinned and began walking towards the Kid, but Wretch sent Plague sprawling into the barricades with a tope con hilo. Ultra Violet turned to see his partner, but soon paid for the price of this distraction by being tossed over the top rope by both members of the Canadian Connection. The referee, seeing how all control of this match was being lost, began screaming at the wrestlers to get on the apron or he would disqualify each and every one of them. Wretch and Plague each slid into the ring while this was going on, and were both declared the legal men. Wretch quickly rolled up Plague with an inside cradle when he attempted to tag in War, but Plague kicked out at 2. Wretch rebounded off the ropes and charged at Plague, but the Dark Rider leapt over him and Wretch rammed into Kid Cool, who had just gotten onto the apron. Cool was sent flying into the guardrail while a stunned Wretch was rolled up in Plague's own inside cradle. 1... 2..., but Mike Bear made the save for his partner. War stepped into the ring to challenger Bear, but the ref quickly got in his face for it. As this was occurring, the Dark Ninja hopped onto the top rope and sprang onto Plague and Wretch with a double clothesline. Both men went down, and the Ninja followed up on Wretch by executing a sliding dropkick, knocking him to the outside. When the ref finally turned around, he has no choice but to declare the Dark Ninja and Plague the legal men. This didn't last long, though, as Plague made a dive and tagged in Mike Bear. Bear entered, but was sent stumbling by a dropkick from the Ninja. Bear recovered, and this time when Ninja jumped Bear pulled back, letting his opponent land on his back. The Dark Ninja kipped up to his feet almost immediately, but ran right into a Bear lariat that sent him flipping end over end. Bear then tagged back in Wretch, and then lifted the Ninja onto his shoulders. Wretch scaled to the top rope, but Cactus Jon jumped onto the apron and pulled down on the top rope, crotching Wretch. While the referee admonished Jon, Bear made the best with the situation and ran towards the ropes, flinging the Ninja off of him and onto the arena floor surrounding the ring. But, before Bear could turn around, Tom the Bomb clocked him squarely in the face with a chairshot, laying him out. Tom covered, but by the time the ref had turned around to see the action Ultra Violet had already broken up the pin. Bear rolled to the outside, obviously trying to get his wits back, as Ultra Violet scooped up Tom and slammed him in the center of the ring. Ultra Violet then moved to tag out to Kid Cool who had finally returned to the ring apron, but Cool had other thoughts. Eventually, Ultra Violet slapped Cool on the chest and stepped onto the apron. Kid Cool, reluctantly, climbed into the ring, but turned back around and once again began arguing with Ultra Violet. As the two squabbled, Tom drug himself to the Underground corner and tagged in Wretch, who ran towards Cool and caught him up in a surprise rolling reverse cradle off the ropes. 1... 2... 3, and Plague made the save one second to late. War stormed the ring, looking for blood, and found it in the Canadian Connection, whom both were on the receiving ends of choke slams. Meanwhile, the Underground walked to the back, elated, while Ultra Violet looked angrily at Kid Cool and suspiciously at Violet. Result: Underground by Pinfall The arrival Backstage Chris Finn wiped a small ribbon of blood from his nose and looked angrily in the direction of the main backstage doors. "Stupid rich man and his rich clothes with their rich little..." An official approached Finn, and talked into a walkie talkie as he came. "Roger... roger... yes... roger." He turned to Finn. "He's arrived." Finn nodded and straightened out his suit, brushing the bits of dust and plaster from it that had come from his earlier spill. "Is he angry?" The security official shrugged, evidentially not in possession of that information. Finn fretted as the gates to the back parking of the arena opened and the limousine slowly pulled toward him. It stopped a couple feet from Finn, and the driver got silently out and walked to the back door. Finn strained to look into the limousine but could see nothing through the dark glass. The security official leaned over and whispered, "Hey, wouldn't be funny if Terry Funk was in there?" Finn shrugged. "I don't get it." The security official pointed to the limo and explained, "You know... because of that other promotion's show a few days ago... you know, Nitro." Finn looked puzzled. "Nitro? Never heard of it." The security official shrugged. "Well, that's not that surprising I suppose... I mean, it does..." But he was cut off as the door to the limousine was held open and the Flying Frenchie stepped out. He looked around himself, and sniffed the air in disgust. "Zut. New York." Havok (c) vs. Courage The music of the French Foundation begins to play, and out to a reaction of boos walks Courage, gesturing that he's about to win the belt. When Havok's music begins to blast from the loudspeakers, the fans erupt in cheers as the Cruiserweight champion makes his way to the ring. Havok slides in, and raises the belt high in the air for the crowd. He hands the belt to the ring attendant, and the bell rings as he and Courage circle each other. They lock up, and Courage takes the advantage with a knee to the gut. He grabs the arm of Havok and sends him into the ropes, catching him coming off with a big dropkick. Havok quickly gets back up, however, but is caught by an armdrag by Courage, then another. As Havok gets up from this, Courage again whips him into the ropes and goes for a dropkick. However, Havok counters by grabbing the ropes, causing Courage to hit the mat. Havok quickly hits a running senton, and covers Courage. This only gets a one count before Courage kicks out. Havok is up first, and waits for Courage to get up. When he does, Havok hooks his head and plants him with a hard DDT. He signals that he's going to end it, and heads to the top. Once up top, he measures Courage, and then leaps off with a huge flying elbow that hits its mark. Confident he's won, Havok covers without hooking the leg. This gets two, but Courage puts his foot on the ropes before three. Havok brings Courage up, and blasts him back down with a superkick. Havok then springs off the ropes with an Asai moonsault onto Courage, and quickly hooks the leg. Courage barely manages to kick out after being hit with his own finishing move. Havok brings Courage back up, and lifts him in the air in a suplex position, before bringing him down with a brainbuster. Havok goes for a sharpshooter, but Courage grabs the head and brings him over, but only gets two. Havok quickly gets up and regains the advantage with a running back elbow to the jaw as Courage gets up. Courage is staggered, and Havok charges at him. However, Courage back body drops him over the top rope. Havok manages to grab the top rope going over, however, and manages to use it and his momentum to land on the apron. He quickly grabs Courage's long hair from behind, and jerks him down. Courage gets right back up, however, only to be met by a springboard hurricanrana from Havok. Courage just manages to kick out, and now Havok is slightly frustrated. He brings Courage up, and whips him into the corner. He charges in, but Courage flips over him with a sunset flip, getting two. Havok is up quickly, and charges at Courage with a lariat. Courage ducks, and when Havok turns around to face him, he kicks him in the stomach and delivers a rocker dropper, planting Havok. However, due to the beating he has taken, Courage can't make the cover, and the ref begins to count both men down. At this point, Wretch begins to make his way to the ring, looking to scout these possible upcoming opponents. The ref continues to lay the count down, and both men begin to get to their feet around seven. By nine, both men are up, and Courage swings wildly at Havok. Havok ducks, and pastes Courage coming around with a hard clothesline, knocking him down. When Havok walks over to Courage, looking to make the cover, Courage reaches up and dumps him through the ropes with the aid of Havok's tights. Havok takes a nasty spill to the outside near Wretch, and Courage begins to shake off the cobwebs inside the ring, before seeing an opportunity. He runs off the opposite ropes, before leaping onto the side ropes and sending himself up and over the top onto the just-getting-to-his-feet Havok with an incredible moonsault. Both men are down, but Courage quickly gets up and brings Havok up as well. He swings at Havok, but Havok blocks it and fires back. He backs Courage up with repeated hammer punches, but Courage manages to duck one and Havok hits Wretch instead. Meanwhile, Courage quickly grabs a nearby chair, and starts to swing at Havok. However, Havok has just been dropped by a right hand from Wretch, and the chair hits Wretch instead. Seeing that he's lost all control of the match, the referee inside the ring calls for the bell, declaring the match a no-contest. Courage and Havok continue to brawl on the outside, and when Wretch slowly begins to get up, he leaps onto the ring apron, runs, and takes out both men with a somersault bodyblock. At this point, a great deal of refs make their way to the ring, and separate the three cruiserweight combatants. However, each man breaks free from the refs' grips, and begin to go at each other once again. Warfare continues on with the brawl showing no signs of stopping... Result: No Contest Triangle of terror Backstage Kid Cool attempted to put an arm around Violet and was denied. He looked confused by this, and attempted again. Once more, Violet pushed the arm off her shoulder and Kid Cool scratched his head in confusion. "Hey baby... what gives? After New Years I thought that..." Suddenly Ultra Violet burst into the room, and Violet quickly scooted up next to Kid Cool and put her arm around him. Still twirled her finger around in his hair and blinked her eyes innocently at Ultra Violet. Ultra Violet stomped his foot. "Oh, I thought I'd find you two here! In our locker room! With your... hair and... stuff! Well this locker room isn't big enough for the C of us!" Kid Cool stood, and shoved Ultra Violet in the chest. "Well you're right about that 'pal'... and you know what? I don't care if you are the most highly technical wrestler in the world, Violet and I are in love and there's nothing you can do about it!" Ultra Violet dug his heels into the ground and fixed his gaze upon Kid Cool. Violet looked at the two men staring at each other and gulped. "Uh oh..." Rain (c) vs. Primetime "Bawitdaba" starts playing, and former fWo World Champion Primetime makes his way to the ring, being soundly booed by the live crowd. As he mocks them in the ring, "Symphony of Destruction" begins to play, and Rain walks out, looking as if his mind is elsewhere in some ways. He unstraps the Internet title as he gets in the ring, and stretches in the corner as the bell ring. Primetime charges in, but Rain moves and PT hits the turnbuckles. As he staggers out backwards, Rain nails him with a clothesline to the back of the head. Primetime goes down, and Rain begins viciously stomping on him. Finally he rolls Primetime over and goes for a cover, getting two. Rain waits from Primetime to get up, and catches him with a running knee, sending Primetime for a loop. Rain drops a big elbow on PT, before covering again for two. Rain brings PT up, and whips him into the ropes, and catches him coming off with a powerslam. He hooks the leg, but again only gets two. Rain brings Primetime, and sends him into the corner. He charges in with a lariat, and then begins to go to work on Primetime, hitting him with a series of lariats while trapped in the corner. Primetime finally slumps out of the corner, and Rain decides to permanently ground the much smaller and more agile man by working on the leg. He lifts it up, and slams it into the canvas repeatedly before bringing Primetime to his feet. Rain grabs his leg, and brings him down with a dragon screw. He then repeatedly stomps on the leg, before bringing Primetime up again. Rain whips PT into the ropes, and catches him with a chop block, sending PT flipping up and over. Rain quickly walks over to Primetime, and flips him over. He sets up the legs and applies an indian deathlock, a painful hold that works especially on the legs. Rain falls back on the legs repeatedly, and Primetime is screaming in pain. Primetime is close to tapping out, but reaches back inside to stretch for the ropes...and barely manages to grab them. Rain complies by releasing the hold, but quickly brings PT up again. He whips him into the ropes, and readies himself for another chop block. However, Primetime leapfrogs over, but this takes a toll on his leg and stops him cold for a moment. Rain takes this opportunity to get back up and attempt a clothesline, but PT manages to duck and catches Rain turning around with a series of jabs, followed by a European uppercut which sends Rain back against the ropes, and then catches Rain coming off the ropes with a clothesline of his own. Primetime leaps onto Rain with a splash, and covers for one. Primetime is up first, though limping, and whips Rain into the ropes, catching him with a Samoan drop. Primetime reaches back to cover, but only gets two due to the arrogant cover. He waits for Rain to get up, and brings him down with a beautiful dropkick, but again hurts his own leg in the process. After a moment to recover, he covers Rain, but again only gets two. Primetime brings Rain up again, and takes him down with a snap suplex. He holds on, and executes another. Again he holds on, and brings Rain down for a third time, before going to the head and applying a dragon sleeper. Rain has nowhere to go, and cannot reach the ropes. Primetime is confident that he's about to become Internet champion, and loosens his hold briefly to make a particular gesture to the booing fans. Rain takes this opportunity and quickly gathers his strength, slowly standing up with Primetime still on him, unable to let go as Rain grabs him and drapes him over his shoulder. Rain then drops Primetime with a emerald fusion maneuver, taking out Primetime. However, Rain has taken a beating too, and by the time he has crawled over to cover Primetime, Primetime has regained enough energy to kick out at two. Rain slowly gets up, and lifts Primetime up as well. He strikes with a series of side kicks to the leg, knocking Primetime down to a seated position. He then nearly takes Primetime's head off with a seated dropkick, knocking him on his back. Rain drags Primetime out to the middle of the ring, and looks to apply a figure four leglock. However, Primetime boots him off and into the ropes with his good leg, before rolling over as Rain comes off the ropes backwards to trip him down on his back with a dth variation. Primetime staggers to his feet as Rain gets up, and Rain kicks for PT's leg. However, PT catches his leg, spins him around, and catches him turning with a fist to the midsection, bending him over. Primetime quickly puts his head between his legs, and signals to the crowd that he's about to go for the Primetime Bomb. He attempts to lift Rain up, but cannot do so due to Rain's larger size and the work Rain has done on his leg. Rain grabs the legs of Primetime and brings him down with a double leg trip, before flipping over Primetime holding both legs. With more damage done, Rain goes for a boston crab submission, but PT pokes him in the eye as he grabs his legs. Primetime struggles to his feet, as Rain, blinded, stumbles about the ring. Primetime heads gingerly to a top rope, looking for a high-risk move- which is especially high-risk due to the condition of his knee. However, in Rain's stumblings, he bounces off the ropes that Primetime is on, and Primetime falls and crotches himself. Rain stumbles around for a few more moments, before his vision begins to clear up, and he sees Primetime on the top. Thinking quickly, Rain climbs up top, looking for a top-rope Rainmaker. As he reaches the top, Primetime begins to fight back, but Rain diverts his attention by hammering on his leg, and quickly hooks Primetime in a powerbomb position facing the crowd. He leaps off backwards, and hits a super sitdown Rainmaker, leaving him in a pinning position. The ref counts to three, and Primetime kicks out immediately after the count, due to his weakened leg strength. As soon as the ref counts three, Courage storms the ring to attack Rain, and Courage and Primetime brutally beat down the Internet champion. PT rolls outside the ring to grab a chair as Courage continues attacking Rain. He rolls back in with it, and Courage heads to the top rope as PT blasts Rain with the chair. He brings Rain back up, and holds him in position from behind, with the chair held in front of Rain's face. Courage on top leaps off with a missile dropkick, and Rain is busted open from the impact. Laughing at the carnage they have left behind, the French Foundation members walk to the back together, until the lights suddenly dimmed at familiar music began to play... Death vs. Scott Collins vs. Ice vs. Scott Slugger vs. Boston Strangler vs. Flying Frenchie The first Warfare of 2000 rolled on as it was now time for the number one contender match. Inside the arena, fans stood to their feet in excitement as the lights dimmed. On the screen appeared the number "4", and out came Scott Collins. Collins raised his arms in the air as he looked at the crowd, who gave him sort of a mixed reaction to his appearance. Collins reached the ring and awaited the other five participants. "Crazy Train" by Ozzy Osbourne began to play, and that could only mean one thing: Ice. The Big Blonde Machine came out with a look of determination etched across his face, and upon entering the ring he and Collins engaged in a staredown. It looked as if the two men were going to get started on the match early when "O Fortuna" began to play, and out came the ominous form of the Boston Strangler. The Strangler gazed at two of his opponents in an eerie fashion as he methodically walked to the ring. He reached the ring steps and stood on them steadfast. He climbed in just as "Back in Black" began to play. Out came the only four-time fWo World Champion Scott Slugger. Slugger appeared somewhat different, and it seemed as if he had ulterior motives in the works. Slugger made his way into the ring and the referee kept him from reaching Ice, as the two most certainly would attempt to kill each other if they were given the chance. Before they could, "Juke Joint Jezebel" by KMFDM began to blare throughout the arena, and out came The Flying Frenchie. The Frenchie looked at the crowd in amusement, and carried with him a French flag. Frenchie set the flag near the timekeeper at ringside and rolled into the ring, staring at his most hated enemies. Just as all hell was about to break loose, the lights dimmed once again, and out came Death. Death looked at the ring with the look of evil written on his face, and a gleam in his eye hinted that dangerous and bad things were going to happen tonight. Death began to stroll down to the ring silently looking at his opponents in the ring and the fans at ringside. As he neared the ring, his opponents all looked at one another. Death stared at them wondering what they were plotting, and before he could react it became apparent: his five opponents were going after him. As he realized this, Frenchie and Slugger bounced off the ring ropes on the other slide and both men executed baseball slides out onto the floor. Four feet hit Death square in the face, and he fell back near the entrance ramp. Frenchie and Slugger helped him to his feet in a violent fashion and brought him near the ring. They stepped aside as Strangler risked life and limb by leaping over the top rope with a plancha. Death' body hit off the floor with a sickening thud and Strangler grinned as if he enjoyed causing Death pain and punishment. Collins & Ice made their way out onto the floor, and Ice hit a grueling running powerslam on Death. Death continued to get his ass kicked from all five of his opponents, each one of them hating him as much as the other. Collins rolled Death into the ring, and hit a "Death Penalty" out of nowhere, before Slugger brought Death up and executed 'The Curveball' out of the corner. Frenchie leaped up to the top rope and rather than going for the Guilotine Legdrop or the 450, he went for the 810. Hitting it, he passed Death along to Strangler, who locked in the "Boston Massacre", as Death yelped in pain. The five men literally brought one of the most evil men to his knees as he winced, feeling the effects of the Crossface Chickenwing. With Death rendered incapicitated, Strangler rolled him onto his back. Inviting the others to partake in a group pin, each of them stood above him and put a single foot on various areas of his body. The referee counted to three, before Strangler and Frenchie worked as a team to throw Death over the top rope onto the floor. With Death out of the way, the others looked to eliminate each other. Slugger & Ice re-ignited their feud, while Collins and Frenchie engaged in a war. Strangler sat back and watched, resting his body. A wise move on his part, as Collins hip tossed the Frenchie over the top rope onto the floor near Death, who was reaching his feet. Death began to stomp on the Frenchie before Collins came flying off the top rope with a clothesline, taking Death out. Collins and Death began to brawl, despite Death having been eliminated. Frenchie struggled back into the ring, and as he did he was met with a chokeslam from the Strangler. Strangler went for a cover, but Frenchie kicked out. Slugger backed Ice into a corner and chopped him repeatedly, making his chest a crimson red. Strangler brought Frenchie to his feet and hit a gutwrench powerbomb. Another cover, and another kickout out two. Collins finally made his way back into the ring as the officials and other wrestlers hauled Death off to the back. Slugger then turned on Ice, dissolving their temporary moment of teamwork. Slugger hit a piledriver, which Ice kicked out of, and then his finisher the Curveball. Strangler pounded Frenchie across the head with forearms, before he noticed Slugger making his move. Slugger climbed up to the top rope and prepared to fly off with his old finisher the Grand Slam, but Strangler grabbed him by the arm and drove him into the mat. He locked him in for the Boston Massacre, and Slugger winced in pain. Frenchie got up and rather than seek revenge for the tombstone and forearms subsequently given to him from the Strangler, he decided to help him eliminate Slugger. Frenchie executed sitting dropkick after sitting dropkick into the face of Slugger, and Slugger blacked out from the massive amount of blood loss. The referee deemed Slugger eliminated from the match, and finally it was down to both the Strangler and the Frenchie. Several ringside attendants began to help Slugger from the ring, but as they did their help was turned down as Heaven, Hell and Living instead helped him from the ring. Slugger looked back into the ring at Frenchie and Strangler who stared down at him, and he gave them a double-finger gesture. As Heaven, Hell and Living helped him backstage, Frenchie charged at Strangler. Strangler caught him off guard with the Boston Massacre, but Frenchie reached the ropes quickly to escape. Frenchie went out onto the floor and baited the Boston Strangler into a game of hide and seek. Frenchie ran and Strangler followed... methodically. As Frenchie turned a corner, Primetime & Courage began to make their way down. Courage leaped up onto the ring apron and distracted the referee, and as Frenchie rounded another corner, he ducked the ensuing Primetime clothesline which instead took the Strangler off his feet. Primetime and Frenchie high fived each other, before Primetime brought Strangler up for a Primetime Bomb. With the Frenchie's help, Primetime and Frenchie powerbombed him onto the concrete floor. The crowd gasped as Strangler' head bounced off the floor with such a velocity it is a surprise that the Strangler' head remained intact with the rest of his body. Primetime rolled Strangler into the ring before he and Courage left the ringside area. Frenchie reached the top rope quickly and hit the 450. He followed with a hook of the leg for the pin, and with the Strangler nearly unconscious from the Primetime Bomb on the cement floor, it was all academic from there. The Frenchie became the number one contender to the title, and beamed with overwhelming pride as he would face whomever the champion would be on January 22nd in his homeland. He celebrated in the ring as a team of medical officials rushed to the ring to help Strangler from the ring via a stretcher. The officials hauled him away from ringside as Frenchie continued to celebrate, while Warfare took it's final commercial break. Result: Flying Frenchie by Pinfall Another return Backstage Havok was talking with the Black Panther about his upcoming match with Chris Universal. They talked between themselves quietly, and the Panther looked from side to side nervously. He saw the figure that passed down the hallway only out of the corner of his eye, and it passed so quickly that he told himself that it couldn't have been who he thought it was. They went back to talking between each other, and at some point down the arena hallway Mike Bear, Wretch, and Fear collected their belongings so they could leave the arena. They were so absorbed with their task that the man in the black jeans had passed them by before they took note of him. Wretch turned to Mike Bear. "Was that...?" Bear shrugged. "Couldn't have been... he wasn't wearing any..." Further down the hallway Chris Finn ran from one end of the room to the other attempting to talk on two phones at once. "I don't care what Armstrong said... we aren't paying for any hookers. No! No! I don't care... send them to the Mayor... I... hang on a second..." The man walked into the room and faced Chris Finn. Finn spotted him, and nodded saying, "Hey... Wol..." The man held up a hand, cutting Finn off. "No... not that. Douglas. Matt Douglas. And I start on Sunday." Finn shrugged and said, "Well.... Sunday is awfully soon, I'm not sure the card..." The man who was once Wolf Fang shook his head. "No. I'm starting on Sunday." Black Panther (c) vs. Chris Universal Returning from it's final commercial break of the evening, the fans in the arena stood to their feet as "Do you buy what The Sellout is selling?" blared throughout the arena. Chris Universal walked out to a nice ovation from the crowd, and the crowd's roar grew louder as Megan Dawn stood at his side. Universal then pointed at the entrance underneath the Warfare screen, and "The Future" Lance Star made his way out in a wheelchair. The crowd exploded as the fans were unquestionably looking forward to his return to the fWo. Dawn pushed Star down the ramp for him as Universal walked into the ring, looking at the sold out crowd of the legendary Madison Square Garden. Universal stared into the sea of fans and remembered what history the arena had, before he removed his metallic silk shirt. Universal shook hands with Star and hugged Dawn as a sign of good luck from the two. As Universal' music died down, pyrotechnics exploded and the word "champion" appeared on the Warfare screen. "Killed by the Cat" began to play and out came the newly crowned fWo World Champion and Wrestler of the Year for 1999, The Black Panther. The crowd erupted, showing their appreciation to Panther for defeating Death and saving the fWo from grave circumstances. The Panther walked to the ring, slapping the fans hands along the way. The Panther stepped into the ring and held the fWo World Title high above the crowd, in full view for everyone to see. The salute to the crowd drew a most favorable reaction from the fans, and Universal, surprisingly, clapped in Panther's general direction. The Panther handed the fWo World Title over to the referee, and the referee showed it to Universal. Universal looked at it and made a gesture as if to get the match started, and a few moments later the match was underway. Universal and Panther stood in the middle of the ring, both of them "talking trash", each man trying to get a psychological advantage over the other. Maybe another attempt at psychology, or maybe a genuine way of saying "may the best man win", Universal stuck out his hand for a handshake. The fans tried to convince Panther not to complete the handshake, but Panther looked at Universal in the eyes, and shook his hand. Surprisingly, Universal didn't try to take advantage of Panther, and the men circled each other, before engaging in a collar and elbow tie-up. Panther got the advantage with ease as he took Universal into his clutches with a side headlock. Panther wrenched away on his opponents head for several moments, before Universal sent him off into the ropes. Panther returned and Universal went to the mat on his stomach, and Panther hopped over. Panther returned as Universal reached a vertical base. Universal went for a hiptoss, but Panther reversed it and instead it was the wealthy, arrogant man from Miami who went crashing to the mat. Fans would note that the last time these two met inside a ring it was in Universal' home of Miami back in July, the night Lance Star debuted. Panther got the victory in that one, but this one was just getting started. Universal got back to his feet and charged at the Panther, ducking his clothesline and instead going for a crucifix. The Panther kicked out at two, in a close call. Both men rushed to their feet and Universal charged at Panther with a high crossbody. Panther caught him and executed a fallaway slam that sent Universal over the top rope onto the floor, just missing Dawn and Star. Panther went out onto the floor and made sure Star and Dawn were okay, before grabbing Universal by the hair and rolling him back into the ring. Panther went for a cover, but Universal kicked out at two. Panther brought Universal to his feet and whipped him to the ropes. He ducked for a backdrop, but Universal stopped and went for a ddt. Universal covered, and Panther kicked out again. Both men quickly jumped up to a vertical base, before Universal charged towards Panther. Panther took him down with an armdrag, and repeated it again when Universal charged towards him again. Panther stood to his feet as Universal looked up at him with concern, and Panther motioned for Universal to "come get some more". Universal hesitatingly restarted the match, and both men came to the center of the ring for another lock-up. Universal got the advantage this time with a side headlock of his own. Panther sent him off towards the ropes and as Universal came back Panther went to the mat on his stomach. Universal leaped over him and returned. Panther went for a hip toss, but Universal counter into a hip toss of his own. Panther blocked it, but Universal nailed him in the midsection with a knee. As the Panther bent over clutching his rib area, Universal hit a rocker dropper. Universal rolled Panther over for the cover, of which Panther kicked out of at two. Undaunted, Universal rushed to the outside and went up to the top rope. Panther got up, and Universal flew through the air, both feet nailing him in the jaw. Panther sprawled back into the turnbuckles rather than go to the mat, and Universal charged in with a clothesline. As Panther staggered out, Universal jumped off from behind for a bulldog. Another cover, and Panther kicked out just a millisecond before the referee's hand hit the mat for a third time. Universal was clearly frustrated... not knowing what to do next. Universal got some momentum by bouncing off the opposite ropes as Panther recovered to a standing position. Universal went for a spinning heel kick, but Panther caught him in mid-air and planted him into the mat with a sitting powerbomb. Universal' shoulders were pinned to the mat, but somehow he managed to break free with a pair of boots to the side of the Panther's head. Both men shrugged off the effects of the previous damage and sprung to their feet. The World Champion and the challenger charged at one another and went for lariats. Both men went down as a result of the lariats, and a ten count from the referee ensued. He reached six before Universal crawled over and laid one arm onto the Panther. The referee counted the pin, but Panther kicked out at two and three-quarters. Universal brought the champion to his feet and whipped him into the turnbuckles. He ran towards him but Panther moved out of the way, and instead Universal hit the turnbuckles sternum first. The Black Panther caught him from behind with a waist lock, and appeared to be going for a belly-to-back suplex, but instead lifted Universal up onto the top turnbuckles. From behind, he hit a reverse neckbreaker from the top rope, and went for a cover. At the count of two, Universal got his bottom foot draped over the second rope. Exhausted in his own right, the Panther continued. He brought Universal up to his feet and whipped him to the ropes. Universal returned and Panther kicked him in the stomach, and then hit a swinging neckbreaker. Thinking Universal was softened up enough, he took him into the corner, and propped him on the turnbuckle again. The Panther climbed up to the second rope and hooked Universal in for the superplex. Moments later, both men crashed onto the mat as the Panther hit 'the Pounce'. Panther was clearly exhausted but climbed onto Universal for the pin in the center of the ring. Universal got his shoulder up just in the nick of time, somehow, and Panther plotted his next move. With the crowd on the edge of their seats, the Panther stood to his feet and pointed at the top turnbuckle. He went out onto the ring apron and climbed to the top. Waiting for Universal to get to his feet, he went for a lariat. Universal moved out of the way and Panther crashed into the referee. Panther wasn't too hurt from the collision and checked on the referee. As he did, Universal spun him around and hit the Cha-Ching. Universal covered him, but the referee was out cold. Universal began to wake him up, and the Panther snuck up from behind for a roll-up. With the referee still dazed, Panther brought Universal to his feet and nailed him with five to six forearms in a row. He whipped Universal into the turnbuckles and charged in, but Universal moved and Panther crashed into the turnbuckles. Universal rolled him up from behind, but the referee was still out. Universal released Panther and then pointed at Star, telling him "this one is for you". He brought Panther to his feet, and hit the Shooting Star/Rock Bottom. Universal went for the cover, and finally the referee crawled over to count the pin. After a slow count which reached only two, the lights went out inside the arena. Moments later, the lights came back on and Heaven & Hell were in the ring. They pulled Universal off the Panther and whipped him to the ropes. Universal returned and ducked the double-clothesline before waiting for Heaven & Hell to turn around. As soon as they did, each were taken down with a simultaneous Cha-Ching. The lights went out once again. The fans began to boo, and moments later the lights came back on and Living was standing inside the ring. He laughed at Universal before charging at him, but as he did he was hooked in for a Shooting Star. The lights went out again, but as they did a red light shadowed the stage. Above the stage, a casket began to lower from the ceiling. The casket was lowered by four chains, and as it was lowering, a bell began to toll. Universal helped Panther to his feet as they both stared at the stage in confusion. Heaven, Hell, Living and the referee were all out of it as the casket reached the stage. After the bell reached the sixth toll, "Beautiful People" by Marilyn Manson began to play throughout the arena. The casket opened up, and a figure sat up inside. He climbed out, but nobody knew who the person was as they wore a black trench coat, a black shirt, black pants and black boots. The person began to walk to the ring methodically. As Universal and Panther were about to go meet the person in the aisle way, Scott Slugger came into the ring behind them through the crowd. He whacked Universal and Panther each over the head with a steel chair, before he took out the Panther with a elevated reverse DDT. As the masked man reached ringside, Dawn moved towards him and he pushed her aside via a hand to the face. The masked man grabbed Lance Star and pushed the him into the guardrail while he was in his wheel chair. In the ring Heaven, Living and Hell pounded on Universal on the mat, while Slugger stomped Panther repeatedly. The dark figure stepped up onto the ring steps and outstretched his arms in a crucifixion pose, before raising his arms into the air. A strobe light began to flicker throughout the arena while his music continued to place. He entered the ring as Heaven/Living/Hell brought Universal to his feet. The crowd booed loudly as he did a throat slash gesture, before kicking Universal in the stomach, followed by a sitdown tombstone piledriver. Slugger and the masked man dragged Panther on top of Universal, before they woke up the referee and had him count the pinfall. Three seconds later, and the Panther remained the fWo World Champion. Hell went to the outside and retrieved the World Title, before he brought it into the ring. Slugger grabbed the referee and threw him over the top rope out onto the floor. The arena darkened as Hell went to his knees, kneeling in front of the masked man with the title in a grandiose salute to Him. He held up the title to him while a spotlight shined on the masked man. The masked man took off his mask... and moments later... the world saw who it was. Austin Davis had returned. The second coming had arrived. Davis snatched the fWo World Title from Hell, and he looked at it. He spit on the title, before holding it in one hand above the fallen bodies of Universal and Panther. Slugger held the other part of the title with a single hand as well, as their friends knelt before them. Davis and Slugger dropped the title onto the Panther, while "DEAD" and "PERFECT" burst onto the video screen. As Davis and Slugger stood in the ring over the two men, the two words transformed into three. "Fuck the fWo." With the arena in utter shock, chaos and confusion; Warfare faded to black. Result: Black Panther by Pinfall
