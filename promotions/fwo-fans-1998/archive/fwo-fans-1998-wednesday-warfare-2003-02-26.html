He looked across the table at Mr. Tort, billionaire and president of Tort Industries. Mr. Tort had made his impact on the entertainment world over the years; financing large-scale sporting projects in order to get them onto cable television, investing in media ventures on the internet and creating the cellular boom into the wireless web and text messaging. Mr. Tort was an old man, an often senile man, but none would dispute that he was a powerful man. In this dark and now empty building, Tort still held power. Several months ago, Mr. Tort was approached by Travis Beaven, the owner of the fWo, and asked to help invest in the company. It was part of a larger plan to get the fWo out of being influenced by one particular entity. Investors were selected and kept secret from one anothter so that it would be impossible for a group of them to work together to impact the promotion in a substantial way. Rather than looking at the typical figures for investing, Travis looked for people outside of the sphere of wrestling influence. People would wouldn't be likely to care about much of anything aside from a return on their investment. The plan had worked... but Mr. Tort was the failing part of an otherwise successful plan. Mr. Tort now looked across the table at Richard Moore, an employee of Tort Industries and lobbiest for the company on behalf of the United States Government. It was Moore who had worked so hard to enact the existing laws that would force the promotion out of the country once it was discovered that one of the wrestlers was using heroin inside the arena. Moore had his own reasons for doing what he did... his son Ryan was a wrestler in the promotion, but his estrangement from his wife and son had prevented Richard from ever knowing him.
