Wednesday Warfare, 8 March 2000

8 March 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Introduction "I want ze tape. Now." Mike Bear and Wretch of the Underground had been heading into the arena along with several other fWo superstars when they were confronted by the Frenchie. Mike Bear exchanged a glance with Wretch and rolled his eyes. "Listen Frenchie, you're not going to get it, so just get lost. I don't have time to waste with you." The Frenchie didn't move and Mike Bear dug into his bag. Several fans were outside the fence and looking in at the wrestlers arriving before the show, cheering loudly. Wretch turned and waved to them with a smile on his face. Bear had retrieved a video tape and held it up in front of the Frenchie. "Listen Frenchman... you're not ever going to get this, so just go back to your locker room and beat it... or whatever it is you frogs do when you're alone." The Frenchie gave Bear an angry stare, and then nodded. "Very well. You 'ave clearly gotten me beat zen." The Frenchie turned, and Mike Bear started to put the tape back in his bag. The Frenchie suddenly gave a high laugh and pulled a shock stick from behind his back. He zapped Mike Bear and Wretch before they had a chance to defend themselves and snatched the tape from Bear's hand. "Ha ha! Ze Frenchie strikes again!" He quickly ran away as Bear and Wretch struggled to get to their feet. Bear wore an expression of intense rage across his face. "Get me Finn... NOW!" Inside the arena fireworks exploded as Wednesday Warfare took to the air... Hellrazor vs. Robert Jensen Robert Jensen, a man who came up empty on Slaughter in an attempt to win gold, appears first, the boo birds out in full force. He is soon after followed by a former hardcore champion himself in Hellrazor, as always graced by the presence of Lamont. The audience shifts to a loud applause and only settles down once the bell has rung. Hellrazor strikes first with a lunging kick to the gut, then whips Jensen to the ropes. Jensen evades Hellrazor, darting to the side and setting his feet so that once Hellrazor turns he uses a basic football tackle to floor him. Yet Hellrazor works his feet into Jensen's gut and extending them, sends Jensen flipping off and over him and to the mat. Hellrazor is quick to slap on a headlock before working it into a side headlock and marching Jensen to the turnbuckle where he pounds Jensen's noggin into it repeatedly. Now as Jensen reaches for his forehead, in a moment of vulnerability, he falls victim to Hellrazor who grabs his head and barbarously slams it to the mat with a mat slam and rolling over Jensen, looks for the victory. At two, the match appears nearly over, when Jensen is able to stretch out a leg just enough to catch the rope and break the pin. Hellrazor, irritated, drags Jensen to the center of the ring and mounting a now kneeling Jensen, grinds his face mercilessly into the canvas with a reverse face driver. Unfortunately for Hellrazor, Jensen has other ideas, and as he gathers up enough strength to lift all of Hellrazor onto his shoulders, and inch by inch slowly rise to his feet, the tide begins to shift. Jensen unsteadily shuffles to the corner, yet once there heaves a susceptible Hellrazor up and off and brings his head careening dangerously down and smacking as well as snapping back off of the top turnbuckle. Instead of pinning, Jensen slides out to the aisle and finding a table beneath the ring, sets it up outside, obviously up to no good. He slips back into the ring and lifts Hellrazor, using an Irish whip Maneuver. However Hellrazor reverses the weak whip into one of his own. Jensen socks the turnbuckle and stumbles out into one more Irish whip, clapping the opposing turnbuckle. As Jensen exhaustedly collapses onto the ropes, Hellrazor dashes towards him and has Jensen twisting and flipping over the top rope. Yet his momentum causes him as well to take the tumble, and much to his alarm, follows Jensen through the table, splintering it with a resounding crackling. Fortunately, he lands upon Jensen and cements him to the floor, thus covering him and allowing the referee to register the three count, handing the match to Hellrazor. The crowd stands and shows their appreciation for the effort put forth by Hellrazor, just when VL looks to send a message. As both men are helped from the table they have reduced to sawdust, VL catches them off guard, steel chair in hand, and wrapped many times over with barbed wire no less. She begins to drive the maniacal weapon she carries into the defenseless figures before her. Warfare is quick to cut to commercial, yet the damage has been done as the images of barbed wire holding onto flesh flash before viewers, and a message has truly been sent. Result: Hellrazor by pinfall Flying Frenchie vs. Fear As the Underground theme broadcasts over the arena Fear makes his way down to the ring. Fear steps through the ropes and steps onto the second turnbuckle, playing to the crowd as he awaits his opponent. A few moments of silence pass, followed by the Flying Frenchie's music. The former multi-time world champion, the Flying Frenchie, steps out onto the entrance ramp bearing his trademark smirk. The crowd gives him a huge reaction, though mixed. TFF walks down to the ring, slides in, and awaits the the bell. The referee signals the timekepper, and the match is officially underway. The two men dance around for a bit before locking up in a collar-n-elbow tieup in the center of the ring. The Flying Frenchie is shoved into the corner by Fear, who puts his hands on his hips and gives Frenchie a "What now?" look. TFF dusts himself up and reapproaches his opponent. The two men briefly lockup, but Frenchie comes out of the blocks with a series of quick punches to the face. Fear is not dazed, however, and responds by shoving Frenchie in the face to the canvas. TFF hops to his feet quickly, but as he does Fear pushes him into the corner. Fear whips the Frenchie across the ring and attempts to follow up with a running clothesline, but the Foundation leader dives out of the way. As Fear stumbles backwards out of the corner the Frenchie bounces off the ropes and bulldogs him into the mat. TFF slingshots himself onto the apron and dashes up the turnbuckle post as Fear recovers, and manages to catch the Undergrounder in the face with a flying dropkick. Fear goes down once again as TFF makes the cover. Two count. TFF picks up Fear and tries to whip him to the ropes, but Fear uses his weight advantage to counter it with a short arm into a mammoth of a sidewalk slam. The Frenchie rolls on the mat, holding his head in pain, as Fear shakes the cobwebs out of his head. Fear lifts up TFF and pushes him into the corner, then drives a couple of knees into Frenchie's midsection. Fear backs off for a second before using his boot to choke out the former world champion. At the count of five Fear relinquishes the choke, allowing TFF to slip to the mat. Fear picks up Frenchie once again and places him laterally across the top turnbuckle, then proceeds to drive an elbow into the chest of TFF, causing him to jackknife in the corner of the ring. Fear takes a momentary breather then, as if a lightbulb just went off in his head, signalled to the crowd that he was going to demask the Frenchie. The crowd popped in anticipation at this as Fear grabbed TFF by the beret. But, as Fear started to yank it off TFF low blowed him. Fear doubled over and teetered out of the corner, allowing the Flying Frenchie to prop himslef up on the second turnbuckle. Fear slowly turned around, and found himself subjected to a flying DDT from the Frenchie. TFF rolled Fear over, made the cover, and got the one, two, three count and the win. After the bell rang and Fear rolled out of the ring, Mike Bear appeared on the entrance ramp. In one hand was a French flag, in the other a microphone. "Sacre blue cheese!", Bear yelled, "It appearz az tho ze Frenchie iz getting ah bit dirty! Maybe he needs zu towel 'imself off!". With this Mike Bear dropped the mic, and began to run the French flag back and forth between his legs. An enraged Flying Frenchie ran out of the ring, charging Mike Bear. Bear, however, simply dropped the flag and went off to the back with a smile on his face. Result: Flying Frenchie by pinfall The arrival The rain patters down continuously on the alleyway, playing echoing stacatto notes on the metal trash cans and plastic bags in a repetitive, thunderous hum. The alley is dark on this moonless night, with long shadowsstretching their fingers across the wet concrete. The camera pans backwards, exposing more of the dark alley to view. The street the alley leads to is empty, cold and dark, while the rain runs rivulets and tiny waterfalls off the sides of the brick-lined buildings. A tiny sound, bleeding in and out of the rain in the background, begins to make itself apparent. It continues, growing louder and louder, and as it grows more audible, so too does its pace quicken. It sounds like feet slapping wet pavement. The rhythm falters,as the sound nears the mouth of the alley, and from around the corner appears a gasping, rain-soaked man in sodden jeans, and a ruined leather jacket. He slumps against the brick wall, and sinks back onto his haunches, clutching his knees and desperately trying to regain his breath. He pants and coughs, then begins to come back up to his feet, his chest still rising and falling spasmodically. He looks quickly around the corner of the alley into the street as though looking for some pursuer, his hair whipping ribbons of rain away from his head. He brings his head back into the alley, and straightens leaning against the wall. He paces his breathing for a moment, then makes a break out of the alley. He exits the mouth of the alley, and runs into the middle of the street.There is a muffled bang, and a bright, white spotlight suddenly illuminates him from above. He turns around, facing its source, and shields his eyes from the piercing light. "Oh, Christ!" He sobs. "Please, God no!!" He turns away from the spotlight, and continues to run down the street. The spotlight stays in its position for a moment, then winks out. The man runs down the street, through another alley, leaping over a rickety wooden fence. He trips, and bangs his knee on a wall fixture, and begins to limp, continuing his desperate flight through the streets, limping and hopping awkwardly. He runs, and sees a payphone on the street corner. He stumbles inside, and fumbles around in his pockets for a quarter. "Gotta tell someone, gotta warn someone." he mutters to himself. He slides the quarter into the slot, dials a number, then waits. A single sigh escapes his lips, and he begins to breathe regularly. Suddenly, a hand wraps itself around his face, and pulls him out of view. The phone dangles in the inside of the phone booth, still ringing on the line. A young female voice answers. "Hello, this is the Fan's Wrestling Organization Information Hotline, how can I help you? Hello? Hello?" A delicate, cufflinked hand, adorned with a gold wristwatch gently replaces the phone on the hook with absolute care. There is a single muffled scream outside the phone booth, then once again the rain is all that can be heard." Ric Chronos & Fallen vs. Chaos Warfare returned from the standard plethora of advertisements and subliminal messages enticing you to purchase items you don't need or want. In the ring already stood War & Plague, the two men who team to form Chaos and two-fourths of the Four Riders. They awaited their opponents of the night, Ric Chronos and Fallen. One would think given the disention between Chronos & Fallen due to what occured at Trapped, Chaos were the favorites. 'Time' by Anthrax began to play, and a gold spotlight covered the stage as fog and smoke floated about. Ric Chronos came out to a moderate pop from the fans, and from behind Fallen followed. The attitudes between the two men was literally like night and day. A look of energy and determination was on the face of Chronos, but the expression that was on the face of the man who did not wish to be known as John Skyler anymore was confusion and depression. The two men neared the ring, before Chronos entered first, while Fallen & War took to their respective corners. Chronos and Plague locked up, before Chronos took Plague to the mat with a headlock takeover. Plague countered into a headscissors, before Chronos rolled through into a pin. At the count of two, Plague bridged out into a backslide for another pin attempt. Chronos struggled out just before three. The two men jumped to their feet and charged at one another. Chronos took Plague down with a knee to the midsection, before bringing him up by the hair and taking him down with a side russian legsweep. He was going to attempt a cover, but deciding not to and instead taking him down with a single arm DDT. Finally, he went for a cover, but in came War. War pulled him off and then was going to go for a chokeslam, but remembered that his friend Scott Collins didn't like chokeslams, so he tossed him to the mat with a throat toss. Fallen saw this and stepped off the ring apron, before slowly... walking up to the backstage area. War & Plague began to dominate the match, with War & Plague hitting a rocket launcher as War launched Plague off the top rope into a frog splash on Chronos. Fallen turned his attention to the ring and saw his partner was in trouble, and turned back towards the ring. After Plague hit a inverted DDT, he stood to celebrate. From behind however, Fallen took him down with his finisher, 'The Lowfall'. War entered the ring and Fallen caught him offguard with a boot, then a piledriver, before pushing him to the outside. Fallen put his partner atop of Plague for the pinfall. Fallen & Chronos were victorious, but as Chronos recovered inside the ring Fallen left him again... with everyone wondering what exactly was going on between the two men. Result: Ric Chronos & Fallen by Pinfall Machina vs. Mad Trucker As the crowd stood on their feet a determined Machina made his way to the ring. Over one shoulder was slung a plastic bag, and as he made his way to the ring the announcers reminded the crowd that this was a paper vs. plastic match. Machina slid under the ropes and awaited the Trucker, passing the bag from one hand to the other. He didn't have to wait long. A loud TOOOOOOOOOOOOOOT! came as the Trucker came out onto the ramp and pointed down toward the ring. He held a paper bag in one meaty hand and climbed over the top rope to face Machina. The masked superstar growled, "Tree-cutting bastard." The Trucker spat back, "Non-biodegradable landfill-filling creep." The referee had to pull them apart before they began trading blows right there, and the two sulked back into their respective corners. Two referees entered the ring, and took the bags from the two wrestlers. Working swiftly the bags were placed over the heads of the wrestlers and drawn tight so they could not see. Confidant in their work, the referees left the ring and the wrestlers moved shakily to face each other. The match was designed so that both wrestlers would have to battle without the aid of their sight, but several fans wondered aloud if Machina's clear plastic bag was really accomplishing that goal. The Trucker felt his way forward, vision obscured by the brown paper of the sack he wore over his head. Machina on the other hand was standing still, and appeared to be smiling. He coughed, and the Trucker lunged toward the noise. Machina deftly sprung out of the way of the Trucker, and gave him a kick in the rump as he passed. Machina was moving too well to be really blinded from the bag, and laughed as he continued to taunt the Trucker. The Trucker turned and moved slowly in the general direction of Machina, who began running around the Trucker in small circles and easily avoiding his blind punches. Making noises like Curly of the Three Stooges, Machina jabbed at the Trucker from time to time, but seemed to be slowing down. His face appeared very red, and he looked as though he was having trouble catching his breath. With a sudden realization Machina became aware that he had cut off his oxygen supply by tying the plastic bag too tightly around his neck and passed out, falling backward onto the mat. The Trucker meanwhile stumbled backward over the prone body of the masked Lone Gunmen and fell hard, knocking his head squarely on the mat. Both men lay still, not moving. One of the Trucker's feet was resting on Machina's leg, and the referee fell for the count. Neither man moved as the three count was made and the Trucker was declared the victor. As officials worked to revive the two, Warfare went to a commercial. Result: Mad Trucker by pinfall Eradication Clips of various sporting events are shown. Larry Bird of the Boston Celtics makes a clutch three to win Game 1 in the 1986 NBA Finals. The scene cuts to Carlton Fisk's dramatic home run in Game 6 of the 1975 World Series. A voiceover takes place; a loud booming voice: "Many years have passed since the city of Boston has witnessed greatness." As the voice continues, footage of 'The Captain' Ray Bourque making a big goal on Bill Ranford in the 1990 Stanley Cup Finals. "Heroes and legends have mesmerized this city for generations." Footage of Bobby Orr scoring the game winning goal of the 1970 Stanley Cup Finals while vertical is shown. "But as the town enters the 21st Century, a darkness has shadowed over it for years. No hero has emerged from the fray..." The scene now shows one faint spotlight barely silhouetting the shadow of a man who appears rather large next to a woman that is clearly a foot shorter. Only their figure can be made out, as the lighting prevents revealing any more of their features. "Until now..." A HUGE explosion engulfs the background behind the two individuals. The graphic forms into a nuclear mushroom cloud explosion behind the duo. "The New Millenium dawns to reveal one man who will emerge as the next Boston legend...one individual striving to overachieve in an ever competitive world...one word to describe his mission..." In big bold letters appears: ERADICATION The fWo logo appears, and a red O with a red + in the middle covers the logo...it appears to be crosshairs from a targeting system. "fWo...You've been targeted..." The scene segways to a blue backdrop with a headshot of an attractive blonde who appeared in Sunday Slaughter. This is Pamela Dee. She smiles as she winks her crystal blue eyes at the camera and speaks "And boys? 'There ain't a damn thing you can do about it!'". Her face dissolves into an orange and red Lance Star & Chris Universal vs. STUMP Squad "We're Not Gonna Take It" begins to blare, and out to a good reaction walk the STUMP Squad, Black Quicksilver and Mike Heftel. They roll into the ring and await their opponents, and the crowd pops as we hear "Do You Buy What The Sellout Is Selling?", and the Hollywood Blondes make their way to the ring. They quickly roll in, and the referee signals for the bell as Universal and Quicksilver circle each other. Universal charges, but Quicksilver leapfrogs him. Universal turns, and Quicksilver attempts a whip into the ropes, but Universal reverses it and BQ heads into the ropes. However, he leaps onto the top rope and moonsaults over Universal, before quickly wrapping him up from behind and attempting a rolling reverse cradle. Universal holds onto the ropes, but BQ rolls unharmed to his feet and charges Universal. Universal back body drops him up and over the top, but BQ uses his agility to land on the apron and strikes with a forearm to the head of Universal. He leaps onto the top rope and attempts a springboard dropkick, but Universal moves and Quicksilver strikes the mat. He gets up quickly, but Universal takes him down with a russian legsweep before tagging in partner Star. Universal whips Quicksilver into the ropes, and lifts him up high into the with a version of a flapjack. Star bounces off the ropes as he does so, and leaps up, taking Quicksilver down and adding a rocker dropper to the flapjack. Star quickly covers, but Quicksilver kicks out at the last moment. Undaunted by his kicking out of that great tag team move, Star brings Quicksilver up again. He lifts him up in a bodyslam position, but holds on midway through the move. Star then throws Quicksilver over his shoulders with a fallaway slam. He attempts a cover, and the ref counts to two before Quicksilver kicks out. The crowd's attention is briefly turned to the entrance ramp as tag team champions the Guano Apes, Sphere and Jackhammer, appear, presumably to scout the team they're facing at Body Count. Star brings Quicksilver up again, and attempts to erase his face with an inverted swinging neckbreaker. However, BQ breaks free in mid-move and goes behind Star, quickly taking him down with a reverse layout DDT. BQ and Star both quickly reach their feet, and Star attempts a clothesline, but BQ catches his arm and takes him down with a cubic zirconium cutter. Both men crawl for their corners, and both men tag in their partners at approximately the same time. Mike Heftel comes charging in and blocks a quick punch from Universal, before firing back with a series of rights himself. He backs Universal into the ropes and whips him across, before catching him coming off with a leaping back elbow. Heftel quickly brings Universal up, and kicks him in the gut, doubling him over. Heftel then lifts Universal up, and spikes him into the mat with a cradle version of Heftel's piledriver. Heftel quickly covers and hooks the leg, but Star breaks it up at the last moment. This brings in Heftel's partner Black Quicksilver, who begins brawling with Star. Heftel meanwhile lifts Universal to his feet again, and whips him into the ropes. Universal bounces off and spots Heftel with his head down, and nails a floatover version of the Cha-Ching!. Universal quickly covers, but BQ manages to break free from Star in time to break it up at two. Star quickly clears BQ out of the ring, throwing him through the ropes. He signals to Universal, who brings Heftel up. Star then takes Heftel in a powerbomb position, and lifts him onto his shoulders before turning to face the turnbuckle that Universal is climbing up. Universal reaches the top, and leaps off as Star falls backward, taking Heftel down with a super variation of the Cha-Ching!. Universal quickly covers, and Star kicks BQ out of the ring as he attempts to make the save as the ref counts three. The Blondes have their arms raised in victory, and the Guano Apes make their way backstage as Warfare continues... Result: Star & Universal by pinfall Trouble Machina rubbed his neck. A red line had formed there, and he was wincing under the pain. The locker room was mostly deserted, with most of the wrestlers out in the arena to see what was going to happen next. The Trucker entered, and the two exchanged angry expressions before softening. They both started to speak at once, and then Machina coughed. "Listen... Trucker... you know, we are wrestlers, and like all wrestlers you and I are just big balls of emotions just waiting to explode." The Trucker nodded. "Yeah, I know pal. We shouldn't get so upset over this stuff. Hell, both types of bags are bad for the environment... it's all just a matter of personal choice as to which one you like better. Personal freedoms are what make this country great." Machina extended a hand and the Mad Trucker shook it. "Friends?", said Machina. "Friends.", said the Mad Trucker. The two smiled and then went to opposite ends of the locker room to get changed so they could get on the road. "Hey Trucker, you still ride that big rig to the arena? I'll bet with the gas prices so high thats..." But Machina suddenly broke off. He was looking at the Trucker, who was changing into a fresh pair of jeans. Machina looked aghast. "Fruit of the Loom? Briefs? Oh Trucker... you can't be serious!" The Trucker looked down at himself with a frown. "What? I like the snug comfort that a good set of briefs provides. Don't tell me you're one of those ho-has that wears boxer shorts..." Machina gritted his teeth. "You're damn right I wear boxer shorts. When I'm fifty I still want a sperm count... something you blessedly won't have!" The Trucker clinched his fists. "Those," he said, "are fighting words..." French Foundation vs. Perfect & Mitch Wilson Cameras head down to the ring for Warfare's Main Event, and the fans begin booing as "Blind" starts playing and out walk the French Foundation, Courage and Primetime. They soak in the boos, and arrogantly walk to the ring. They roll in, and await the entrance of their opponents. "Oh My God" by Guns N' Roses plays, and the booing intensifies as FTfWo, Perfect and the man who recently dubbed himself "Mr. Main Event", fWo Internet Champion Mitch Wilson, appear at the top of the entrance ramp, bathed in a red light, Mitch holding his belt high. Fireworks follow their path to the ring, and both step onto the apron simultaneously before stepping in. The two groups stare each other down from opposite corners of the ring and seem to be about to come to blows when the ref steps in to intervene, and the two teams determine that Perfect and Courage will start the match. They lock up and Perfect gains the advantage, armdragging Courage over. He catches Courage getting up with another armdrag, and tries a third but Courage lands on his feet. Perfect charges for a clothesline, but winds up in the Foundation corner, and Primetime nails him with a right to head, causing him to stumble back towards Courage, who grabs him for a back suplex. Perfect flips over and lands on his feet, and quickly grabs Courage's head from behind and charges into the corner for a swinging bulldog, but only he flies to the mat as Primetime grabs Courage in mid-move to prevent it. Courage quickly leaps onto the second rope as he gets up, and climbs up top. He leaps off with a hurricanrana attempt as Perfect gets up, but Perfect holds on and powerbombs him down. He holds on and drops him down once more, and then drops him down a third time with a Liger bomb variation. The referee counts, but Courage kicks out at two. Perfect brings him up and whips him into the ropes, and catches him coming off with a perfectly executed samoan drop. Perfect covers, but Primetime breaks it up at two. This brings in Mitch Wilson, but the referee prevents the two rivals from fighting. As he does, Perfect brings Courage up again, but Courage strikes back with a low blow. He follows this up with a rocker dropper, and Primetime leaves the ring, followed by Mitch. Courage takes a moment to recover from the moves he's taken, then covers. The ref counts, but Perfect kicks out at two. Courage brings him up, and takes him over with a snap suplex. He mounts the second rope and dives off, nailing a flying legdrop. Courage covers, but Perfect kicks out at two. Courage brings Perfect up, and takes him down with a hard implant DDT. He makes the cover with a leg hook, but Perfect again kicks out. Courage backs up against the ropes, and makes a tag to partner Primetime, who enters the ring. As Perfect gets up, Courage bounces off the ropes and takes him down with an ultra-stiff lariat, which is immediately followed up by a big splash off the ropes by Primetime. Primetime covers, but Perfect kicks out at two. Primetime brings Perfect up, and attempts to whip him into the ropes, but Perfect reverses and attempts to bring Primetime back into a short lariat, but that is ducked by Primetime, who holds on to the short whip and attempts to bring Perfect into a short lariat of his own. Perfect ducks it and goes behind Primetime, before taking him over with a german suplex, no bridge all impact. Perfect takes a moment to clear the cobwebs, and brings Primetime up. He hooks him up, and takes him over with a thunderous exploder suplex, which throws Primetime all the way across the ring into his own corner, where Courage tags in. He charges at Perfect, who takes him down with a drop toe hold, sending him throat-first onto the bottom rope. Perfect tags in Mitch, and they bring Courage up and double whip him into the ropes. As Courage comes off, FTfWo catches him with a double hiptoss, but catch him in mid-move, before driving him forward into the mat. The ref admonishes Perfect to leave the ring as Mitch covers, and the resulting cover only gets a two count. Mitch brings Courage up again, and goes behind him, double-underhooking his arms from the back and driving him face-first into the mat. Mitch covers, but Courage again kicks out. Mitch brings Courage up and whips him into the ropes, before leapfrogging him as he comes off. Courage stops behind Mitch, unbeknownst to him, and schoolboys him from behind. The ref's count reaches two before Perfect rushes in to break it up, and he is met by Primetime, and the brawl is on. The referee is having trouble keeping the two separated, and fails to see Scott Collins marching to the ring, chair in hand. Courage sees him coming and brings Wilson up, holding him in position. Collins quickly rolls into the ring and bashes Wilson on the head with the chair, and Courage takes advantage of the situation by immediately taking Wilson over with a tiger suplex. Collins, confident his job is done, leaves the scene. Courage appears to have the match won, and the referee finally manages to separate the feuding ex-World Champions and sees the pinfall. He counts one... two... and Wilson kicks out at the last moment. Courage is shocked, but brings Wilson up anyway. He goes behind Wilson and attempts a falling reverse DDT on him, but Wilson counters with a questionable reverse kick. Both men are down, and the referee begins to count as they race to tag. Courage reaches his corner first, diving to tag in Primetime, and Wilson is within tagging range of Perfect... but turns back towards the ring and decides to go face-to-face with his archenemy. Pleasantries and greetings are duly exchanged, and Wilson strikes first, slapping his former partner across the face. Primetime turns away for a moment, checking his mouth for blood... then turns and fires a punch at Wilson, who blocks it and attempts a punch of his own, which is blocked by Primetime. The two begin brawling wildly, and Primetime attempts a hiptoss in the chaos, which is blocked by Wilson into a hiptoss of his own that sends both men up and over the top rope. Perfect enters the ring and leans over the top rope to get a better view of the fight, and never notices Courage coming from behind and rolling him up for the three-count and the victory. Perfect curses himself for being so stupid as he gets up, and quickly rolls out of the ring to assist his partner. A low blow from behind to Primetime turns the tide, and Wilson immediately takes advantage and hits his inverted russian legsweep, now known in accordance with his nickname as the Headliner. Courage sees this, and vaults himself over the top rope and onto FTfWo to break it up. The two groups continue fighting, and it shows no sign of stopping as Warfare heads off the air... Result: French Foundation by pinfall
