Jim Dudley ran one hand through wet hair and glanced back at the cab driver. "No, you don't. Thanks for the ride." But the cab driver didn't look away. He looked at Dudley with an expression he recognized from all of his days on the road. It was the expression of a man who was trying to put it together... the expression of a man who knew exactly who he was, but out of the normal enviornment of ring and tights it was having trouble coming to him. Through the years Dudley had come to hate that expression, a far cry from the old days when starved for attention he would walk through airports or shopping malls for no particular reason other than to be recognized. This was the path they were all on, most of them just didn't know it yet. Wrestlers were bound to become recluses sooner or later, the question was more just a matter of how fast and how deep. After tonight, Dudley suspected it would be deep. He reached into his traveling pack and shifted the contents over to the other side. The cab driver had been looking at that too, and with a moment of surprise and horror Dudley noticed the clear outline of the gun that showed through the canvas. He was here on dark business, but few would understand it. "Yeah, I've seen you before... never forget a face..." Dudley ignored him as he fished for wet bills inside of his pocket. He was miles from where they were expecting him, but when he had purchased his plane tickets here it was in motion and too late to turn back. He would be noticed, and Dudley hoped that using traveler's checks through a booking agent had slowed down notification that he had decided to come here rather than watch over the promotion he was the General Manager of on the biggest night of the year. Word would get to Smith soon... but Dudley hoped he was still ahead of the news. Seattle was dark, even thought it was only three in the afternoon. The rain came down in a slow sheet, soaking everything it hit but giving the sidewalks a shiny glaze. Steam rose from gutters and underground vents, and it hit Dudley that it had all begun for him just a few miles away from where he stood eight years ago. Even on that night, he was involved in the game. He was just too young to know it then. Dudley handed the bills to the driver and turned, one moment too late. "Yeah, I know you! Wrestling champion, right? James! James Dudley!" "Jim actually, but yes," Dudley turned and offered a weak smile that conveyed no warmth. "I'd give you an autograph, but it's raining here and I'm already late for the..." "Dudley, yeah... say, do you know Austin Davis? He was always my favorite. Real rebel, did what he pleased..." "Davis, sure, great guy. Listen, I'm going to go in now, but you have a good night." "Wait a second," the cab driver said, his brow furrowed, "isn't there a show tonight? In Chicago? Shouldn't you be there?" Dudley closed his eyes, felt the rain mat his hair to his head, and sighed. Then the smile returned and he waved goodbye. "Night off. Have a good afternoon sir, drive carefully with the rain." Dudley moved into the awning and then inside the building. Inside the cab, the driver watched him go with the same troubled expression. Funny, a wrestler going into Benaroya Hall where the symphony played. Funnier still that he wouldn't be watching, or at, the biggest wrestling show of the year. But what was most strange to the cab driver was why he would be carrying a gun he obviously didn't want the driver to see. Waiting until Dudley was gone from sight, the driver picked up his mobile phone and dialed the dispatch office. Better he tell someone about this... just in case. Fallen Friends, One Ric Chronos looked around at the spartanly decorated dressing room. So far, it seemed like he was one of the first in the building - just the way he liked it. Plenty of time to think, to reflect, and mentally prepare oneself for whatever the night held in store. None of the bustle and bluster that normally would be involved in the production of a major wrestling event would be evident yet, and it was moments like these that seemed the most pure to Ric, the most serene. Leaving his luggage by one of the corner lockers next to Pierre's, Ric fumbled in the pockets of his worn, faded jeans to scrounge up some change. Finding a few dollars, he extracted them and walked out into the hallway. A few moments of searching brought his attention to a vending machine, and striding up to it, he deposited his money and received a Gatorade. Ric paused for a second to open and swallow just a little bit, enough to take the edge off his throat, then turned back to the locker room. Upon entering, Ric noted that Pierre had appeared, and was already beginning the transition from Delacroix to the affable Flying Frenchie. Ric inwardly winced as his frequent partner and close friend disrobed, revealing a jagged array of scars along his knee. "That's what happened the last time Fallen took an interest in the fWo," Ric thought to himself. "I'd do anything not to see it happen again." But outwardly, he forced a smile, and greeted his closest friend. "Hey, brother, you ready for this?" Pierre likewise forced a grin, but his was warmer, less saddled by the weight that Ric seemed to be carrying. "Always, mon ami. You?" "Of course, of course," Ric took another swig from his drink. "It's only a tag match." "You believe zat Oktober can pull his own at the big dance?" Pierre asked, quizzically raising his eyebrows. "He'd better. He's too heavy for me to carry him AND the Animals." The two friends laughed, and sincerity finally came to Ric's face. Maybe tonight wouldn't be so bad after all. Ric sat down, slipped off his sneakers, and reached for his luggage to begin his transformation from man into wrestler as well. A manila envelope impeded his reach, and looking at the item with some bemusement, Ric picked it up. It was addressed to him by first name only, in very non-descript writing. "What's this?" Ric pondered aloud. "You giving me another 11x7 glossy photograph, Pierre?" The Frenchie turned his head from lacing his boots to regard Ric. "No. Zat was here when I got back here." Curiousity flooded Ric, and he reached for the edge of the envelope. Opening it, he pulled out a single sheet of poster size paper. Cracked and yellowed with age, it was an advertisement for the Chronos Professional Wrestling Training Academy that had been run by Ric's father, Mike. Ric glanced over the item once, twice... and that's when it sank in. "I'm a fucking idiot." Ric thought to himself. Pierre knew every one of Ric's quirks - the two had spent so much time together ont he road and in the ring that there was an almost instinctual bond between the two. Pierre felt Ric bristle almost as if it were a physical thing, and turned to study his friend closer. "Ric... are you.." "Yes, I'm fine," Ric interjected, "But, something's come up. And, uh, take care of yourself. Kick Spyder's ass." Ric grasped Pierre's hand for a second, and then pulled him into an embrace. Breaking apart after a moment, Ric turned smartly on his heels, and exited the locker rooms as quickly as he could manage. Pierre watched him go, but part of him wanted to run after Ric, to harangue Ric until Chronos gave up whatever was eating at him. And he didn't know why, but part of him felt an ominous dread, as if this might be the last time he saw his friend, and he couldn't shake this odd feeling as if Ric were... ...as if Ric were trying to say goodbye.
