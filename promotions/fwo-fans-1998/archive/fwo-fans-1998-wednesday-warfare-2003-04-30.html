Introduction Back in the United States the fWo learns that their extended abscence and following exile from the country has left their business struggling. With networks pulling out of contracts nearing expiration rather than renewing, the fWo finds itself looking for a new television home. To further complicate issues, the group of investors turned Board of Directors feel the best defense against sagging ratings and falling interest is a good offense... and has instructed road agents to prepare the promotion to compete on a more national level against the biggest wrestling organization in the industry. But as if this distraction wasn't confusing enough, the divide of trust between the wrestling roster and the road agents and management continues to grow ever deeper as tensions increase... Still, we forge ahead. A small penance to the taxi driver. Trent Harrison was not one for worldly possessions, more out of a lacking need than a cultic belief. He grabbed two small gym bags and walked around the back of the cab. Inside, Allyra sighed. This would be her first time in a social social setting since the accident. "My lady?" Trent said in a faux British accent. He extended his arm out to her which she grabbed with a slight of a smirk across her face. Everything was better this way, the two destroyed remains together. Both pale-skinned, injured, and paranoid. The love in the air was laced with worry, but that wasn't the current concern. The US Air Arena looked quiet from the outside, but was brimming with a roaring populous on the inside. A faint sound of cheers and boos could be heard from the inside as Trent escorted Allyra to the back entrance. A man in a blue uniform stood up straight from his leaning position. He placed a hand on Trent's chest and pushed him back slightly. "No fans. Go on through the front if you have a ticket," explained the guard as he looked toward Allyra and Trent. "Please speak with Silver Fox. I am Trent Harrison, and I work here. I do not know if he's expecting me or not," Trent replied. All he could think about how much harder things were for him in the brief past--when he didn't speak. The man nodded and walked into a small building nearby. He picked up a radio and spoke into it for a few moments. Trent watched as he placed it back on the table and walked back outside. "Sorry about that, Mr. Harrison. Just doing my job. Down the hall and to the left." "It's no trouble, really," Trent replied in a reciprocated apologetic tone. The guard nodded and the two walked through the gateway together. The scene was a sea of gray. Metallic boxes to be filled with unknown objects, smooth concrete walls, pillars, and a matching floor; everything was so bland. Trent and Allyra looked into every open door they passed, catching a quick glance at coworkers and actors alike. Finally, a door with a black label across it. Evidence "Are you sure you want to do this?" Samuel Smooth had a slightly worried look on his face as he questioned his partner, Silver Fox. "As certain as I've been of anything since we took these positions," Fox replied, though he didn't look as confident as his words would've suggested. "Then that's good enough for me," Smooth returned, "But why show the footage? Why not just make an announcement on the show?" "We've already made an announcement on the site, Sam," Fox countered, "But the fact of the matter is that these guys have been here for over a year now, and both have enough of a following that we need to show people why they're on probation, and not simply tell them again. Hell, that's why they were only put on probation and not fired outright." "Fair enough." Smooth still didn't seem convinced, but he trusted Silver Fox's judgment in these matters. If Lance Knight and Mufanga Kagos were to become reprobates, then he supposed the fans did deserve to see why. And why exactly had the two men fallen out of the good graces of the company? It had started with a "warm-up" match that happened between them at Slaughter. Basically, the road agents had sent the two out to warm up the fans and to make sure that all the cameras were working. They had gotten a lot more than they had bargained for, though, and now both men had been on the verge of suspension or termination, but because both had clean records in the fWo (for the most part), they were given a lighter punishment and given 90 days on probation. So long as another incident like this happened, neither would be further punished, and the slate would be wiped clean. And in order to explain the punishment, the footage from the match would be shown on Warfare, in spite of there being no original intention for it to ever see the light of day. That alone should've suggested that something major had happened. "We haven't had anything this potential disastrous since that Spyder/Oktober match from Halloween Hell, have we?" Smooth questioned. "No," Silver Fox answered, "And if I have my way, this will be the last time." Mufanga Kagos vs. Lance Knight The following match was pre-taped for this program As the drumbeats of Nas' "Warrior's Song" cued up throughout the arena, Mufanga Kagos came out to the ring, walking with confidence but aware that his actions would only be seen by the fans in attendance and the production assistants in the back. He was slated to be in a "warm-up" match with Lance Knight tonight, and since it wouldn't be televised, they wanted to put on a good match, but save their best stuff for the fans on television. Plus, if there was something overly good in the course of the match, it would be on tape, so that the camera crew could test that all the cameras were working. Nas' "Get Down" played next, and Lance Knight came out, also without the TravisTron video and pyrotechnics that he was known for. Sliding into the ring, he and Mufanga faced off in the center of the squared circle. They received instructions from the referee and immediately went into a collar-and-elbow tie-up. While Kagos enjoyed a slight height advantage, Knight was the stockier of the two competitors, and he used his superior strength to force Kagos into a side headlock. Kagos pushed Knight off and into the ropes, and on the rebound; he caught the Pittsburgh native with a dropkick. Knight was quickly back to his feet, only to be met with another dropkick. On his next attempt at gaining a vertical base, Kagos again went for a dropkick, but Knight caught him and executed a spinebuster, and then, holding onto Kagos' legs, Knight suplexed him with an inverted wheelbarrow suplex. It was a move he had been hoping to try out for quite a while, and this match had proven to be just the opportunity, for if the live crowd didn't like it, at least they were the only ones to see it. And while it was relatively well received by the live crowd, Lance still wanted to talk to Ben and see how he felt about the move before he decided to work it into his repertoire. Finding himself in control, for the moment at least, Knight pulled the Wildman of Lagos back to his feet and whipped him to the ropes. As Kagos returned, Knight looked for a release overhead belly-to-belly suplex, but Kagos countered, showing tremendous agility as he twisted through the air to land on his feet. Catching Knight from behind, Kagos hoisted him onto his shoulders so that the former tag champion was on his back across the Survivor competitor's shoulders. Spinning in a half-circle to pump the crowd up a bit, Kagos then fell backwards, hitting his trademark inverted Samoan drop. As he dropped Knight to the mat, Kagos was happy with the concept of this match, as he and Knight could just go out and worry about entertaining the fans in attendance, and not have to worry about the evils of television and the internet. Just two guys who enjoyed their craft doing what they loved to do. Allowing Knight to get back to his feet, Kagos charged, and when Knight ducked, he rolled over Knight's back and waited for his former Survivor teammate to turn around. And when Lance did turn around, he was met with a superkick to the point of the jaw, a move he had been more than happy to simply watch his now-former tag partner do to other unwilling saps since January. In fact, he'd almost forgotten how much a good superkick could actually hurt, and he went down like a ton of bricks, much to Kagos' delight. Charging to the nearest corner, Kagos leapt to the top rope and turned around. There, two-thirds of the way across the ring, Lance Knight lay. Kagos pounded his chest momentarily, again drawing a nice cheer from the fans, before diving off of the top rope with the Wildive. As he flew through the air, however, Kagos realized that Knight was moving out of the way, something he hadn't prepared for, and as a result, he crashed facefirst into the canvas. And while Ben Jacobson had been the man to crash into the mat, it was the Wildman who finally rolled to a crouch moments later. Baring his teeth at Knight, Kagos snarled, and loped across the ring in a kind of half-shamble. Taking down the straps of his singlet, Knight prepared for the worst, all while hoping for the best. He softly pleaded for Ben to let him know what was going on, but it seemed to be to no avail. Mufanga tackled Knight to the mat and began to beat on his chest again, which got the fans into what they believed to be a routine even more. But in the ring, Lance Knight was actually beginning to wonder what was going on. He hadn't intended to roll out of the way of the headbutt, but a last-second case of nerves had forced him to take the coward's way out. And while he knew that his whispers for Ben were seemingly falling on deafened ears, he also hoped that he hadn't pissed off a guy he considered to be okay backstage. When continued pleas for information as to what was going on were met with clawing slashes at his chest, Knight roughly pushed Kagos off of him. Trying to circle around the Wildman in hopes of locking on a sleeper hold or something equally restraining, Knight was instead met with a knee sweep, as Kagos lashed out with his arm and sent Knight to the mat. Kagos ran to the ropes and came back, landing hard on Knight's chest with a double stomp. With the air rushing out of his lungs as fast as the patience was draining out of his body, Knight found himself grabbing Kagos by the leg and executing a dragon screw leg whip. Holding onto Kagos' leg in a standing leg lock, Knight tried once more to question his opponent and associate. When he received nothing but grunts in response, Knight tightened his grip and kneeled down, putting an even greater amount of pressure on Kagos' leg and hoping for the submission. What he received instead was a bare foot to the face, as Kagos somehow used his free leg to lash out at Knight. Releasing the hold, Knight staggered back, surprised to find a trickle of blood coming from his nose. Further angered, Knight charged at Kagos, tackling him to the mat just as he himself had been tackled, save for the fact that Kagos was able to get a knee between their bodies, and he used that as a lever to send Knight back to the mat. Knight quickly rolled to his stomach en route to getting back to his feet, but Kagos caught him in the back with a hard elbow drop, sending him back to the mat. And that was when things started to go downhill. Kagos mounted Knight's back and began to claw away furiously, which was the final straw that broke Knight's restraints. Bucking like a rodeo bronco, Knight threw Kagos off and turned towards his tormentor. Seeing no look of recognition in his eyes, Knight delivered a stiff lariat to Kagos' neck, sending the Wildman to the mat with a roar of pain. Continuing his assault, Knight jumped onto Kagos, pinning the Wildman's arms down with his knees and just pummeling the African-American superstar with lefts and rights. Kagos began to bleed from a split eyebrow and from his nose, and even a thin trickle of blood began to seep from his mouth. Knight's back was also bleeding, and that was enough for the official to call for the bell. The match had started out okay, but now both men were bleeding hardway and neither seemed to be on the verge of relenting, as Kagos had finally managed to get his arms free and throw Knight off of him. Both men got to their feet, oblivious to the repeated ringing of the bell that signaled that the match was over. They circled briefly, each man trying to outflank the other without opening up their guard. And when Mufanga finally barreled forward, looking to tackle Knight to the mat once more, he was met with a knee squarely to the jaw that sent his head rocking back with a sickening snap. Knight pounced, hoisting up what was essentially 240 pounds of dead weight, and then driving Kagos back to the mat with a piledriver. Believing his opponent suitably stunned, Knight mounted the Wildman's chest once more and resumed pummeling him, oblivious to the referee's efforts to get him to stop, and furthermore, also oblivious to the roar of the crowd at the man who was coming to the ring unannounced. Brand Frontier.
