fWo — Card

Wednesday Warfare, 2 August 2000

2 August 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Introduction A year ago, they were the worst of enemies. Bitter rivals in a feud that saw Fallen attack and maim Cactus Jon by any means necessary. Jon always put up a valiant fight, but in the end, it was always Fallen who attacked with fierce passion and unrelenting hatred. Unbeknownst to everyone else at the time, Fallen was being controlled... and perhaps was attacking unknowingly. Perhaps things have changed. Fallen has endured a lot over the last year... and it was months before he could trust a man who only wanted the best for him. Eventually the duo became close friends and tag partners... but a look into the past revealed that the two men were friends at one time. It took awhile for Fallen to trust someone he was good friends with... and trust isn't given to someone overnight. If you came up to me on the street, I likely wouldn't trust you for months, likely years. Trust is built over time... and tonight, the fWo have put trust that this time "The Legend" Jon Spratt is serious about making a full-time comeback. For this is his last chance... and only time will tell if Jon is ready to give it one last run. Tonight, the comeback begins. Jon Spratt vs. John Skyler. One year later. Jon has a new moniker... and perhaps John has a new outlook on life. Those who once controlled him are now long gone... and tonight we will see if John has buried any old feelings of hate that he once had for Jon. The rivalry continues? The cameras walk into a dressing room, where a familiar near 7 foot figure paces around as he gets ready for his matchup in just a few short moments. He wears a t-shirt that on the front has a nuclear mushroom cloud explosion, with the words "TOTAL ERADICATION" below it. On the back, the familiar fWo logo with the words "and there ain't a daaaaamn thing you can do about it" written. His facial expression appears focused, intense. As he turns back towards the camera, a door in the room opens to reveal the entrance of his lovely partner, Pamela Dee. She is dressed in what can only be described as a comfortable business suit. She smiles upon seeing Erik, then notices the camera and speaks out. "You know, recently the Big Blonde Crybaby started making complaints about his match against Erik at SummerSlaughter. Of all things, to whine at little old me, and then to give my good friend Kristen Jacobs a hard time! The nerve!" Erik comforts Pamela. "I know, Pammy. I hate to see a grown man cry myself. And what was that garbage he was spitting out, anyways! He's the heel, and he's supposed to break the rules!? Who says he's the only one allowed?" "Let me put it to you in a way you might understand in a few years or so with the lack of grey matter upstairs, Ice. If Dwayne Johnson can sneak up on Paul Levesque, hit him with a chair, put him between a gorgeous Barbie doll's legs in front of 25,000 screaming fans...I think Pammy can call her friend down to give your ice a good blow...in other words, I play by MY rules...no one else's. And my rules are why I am the Hardcore Champion. And tonight will be all about who's the real machine around here. No saying hello to block tonight. It's all about old school Kelly and anything short of a street fight. Ice, prepare to face a sub-zero greeting tonight..and expect your ass to be handed to you plenty. And if you don't think that's gonna happen..." Erik and Pamela start to walk out the door as the camera turns to watch them leave. "Read the back of the shirt for my thoughts on that..." The camera closes in on the '...and there ain't a daaaaaaamn thing you can do about it' catchphrase as the scene fades. The opening Warfare montage begins to play... Erik Kelly (c) vs. Ice And inside the arena, fireworks go off. Sirens blare. Welcome to fWo Wednesday Warfare for the first time in August 2000. The opening montage fades to a shot of a live, sold-out crowd inside of the Pepsi Arena in Denver, Colorado. Suddenly, "Bastard" by Motley Crue starts up, and the Big Blonde Machine himself, Ice steps out on the entranceway, with a mean, cold look in his eyes, and a block of dry ice in his hands. Ice enters the ring, and begins preparing himself for the action in the ring. Ice may have lost this past Saturday at SummerSlaughter, but tonight, he wasn't about to lose. He'd been waiting since Saturday night to get some type of revenge, and tonight was his chance. "Break Stuff" by the Napster endorsing Limp Bizkit starts up, and the fWo's Hardcore Champion, Erik Kelly, and his vivacious sidekick Pamela Dee step onto the entrance ramp. Kelly, with the Hardcore Title draped over his shoulder, and his cinder block in hands, walks to the ring. Dee takes to the outside, and Kelly steps into the ring. He throws the Hardcore Title to the side, and immediately goes nose to nose with Ice. The men begin brawling, and Ice gains the advantage in this Hardcore title matchup. Ice sends Kelly into the ropes, where Kelly ducks a clothesline. Kelly comes back, and Ice is able to plant him with a powerslam. Seizing the opportunity, Ice picks Kelly up, and plants him with a vertical suplex. Immediately, Ice slides to the outside, and begins looking under the ring, when he finds himself a pretty well-placed garbage can full of plunder. Ice slides the plunder filled can into the ring, and he pulls out a singapore cane. Ice looks to take some batting practice on Kelly's head, but Kelly ducks the swing. Kelly nails a boot to the gut of Ice, that causes the cane to fall out of Ice's hands. Immediately, Kelly nails a facebuster onto the singapore cane. He covers, but Ice kicks out at two. With Ice stunned, for the time being, Kelly decides to go back into the garbage can, and he finds the lid of it inside. Ice gets to his feet, and is taken back down with a shot from the lid. Kelly then dumps out the contents of the garbage can, so he can use the can itself as a weapon. he swings the can at Ice, but Ice ducks. Ice kicks the can back into Kelly's face, sending it, and Kelly, to the floor. Ice brings Kelly up to his feet, and takes him down with a shot to the head via that singapore cane. With Kelly out, Ice goes to the outside once more, and throws the timekeeper out of his chair, so he can use it as a weapon. Ice sets the chair up in the middle of the ring, and picks Kelly up. He grabs him by the throat, looking for the Attitude Adjuster on the chair, but Kelly counters with a mule kick. Kelly hits the ropes, looking for a move, but Ice turns it into a swinging neckbreaker, where Kelly's neck hits the chair. Ice jumps on with a cover, but somehow, Kelly kicks out. Ice brings Kelly up, and grabs him by the throat. He slams Kelly into the mat with the Attitude Adjuster. One. Two. Then, a miracle happens. Kelly kicks out of Ice's finisher. Ice, stunned, brings Kelly to his feet. He looks for a second Attitude Adjuster. Then, another miracle happens. Kelly nails Ice with a kick to the balls, and with Ice keeled over, he hooks him. Kelly lets out a mighty cry of "POWERBOMB!" before planting Ice in the mat. It must be over. One. Two. A miracle happens in favor of Ice. He becomes the first man to kick out of the Eradaction. This time, it's Kelly with the astonished look on his face. Kelly goes over to the plunder, and begins digging in, while Ice inches towards the corner, for his dry ice. Kelly grabs a baseball bat, and he goes over to Ice, in the corner. He swings at Ice, but Ice kicks Kelly low, knocking the bat out of Kelly's hands. Ice gets up, and, bam, dry ice smashed over Kelly's head. Ice covers, but miraculosly, Kelly kicks out at two. Ice goes over to the other corner, and grabs the cinder block, as Kelly struggles to his feet. Bam, cinder block to the head. This time, it is enough, as Ice covers, and picks up the win and the title. However, as Ice is celebrating, Xavier comes from the crowd with a hockey stick. He comes from behind and crotches him with the blade, before cracking the stick over his head. Ice falls in a heap, and Xavier grabs the Hardcore title, and scurries to the back with it, as Warfare goes to commercial. Result: Ice by pinfall to win the fWo Hardcore Championship Chosen Warfare returns backstage to a shot of The Future Foundation entering the arena. We suddenly see Matt Loco and Geoff Green - the fWo's notorious interviewer and camera man duo - charge them and bombard them with questions. Primetime looks a little miffed, Courage looks dismayed, and Star looks... like he's having a helluva time carrying around his fWo World Tag Team Title. Loco begins to question the Kings first, even though everyone knows the reason he is there is to find out if Primetime has "chosen his fate" yet. "Courage & Lance, how does it feel to be the fWo World Tag Team Champions?" Courage - removes his glasses - and pulls Loco towards him. "Let me tell you something you lowlife interviewer boy, you want to know how we feel? Well, Courage'll tell you how we feel. We feel like crap. We wish we never would've won the fWo World Tag Team Titles. We wish Damnation would've kicked our asses. We are so mad about winning the titles that we're seriously thinking about giving them to Finn and having him start a tag team tournament, baby. We're so pissed about this!" Loco - taken aback by it - has only one question. "Really?" Courage scoffed, before pushing Loco backwards with one hand slightly. "No, you're just a moron. Obviously, someone realizes that the California Kings equal rock and roll, they equal ratings, and they equal asses in the seats, and that's the reason we're the champs and Damnation isn't... well, that and Damnation got their asses handed to them on a silver platter by the most rockingest tag team of all time, baby!" Courage then gave the camera a devious grin, before he and Lance exited the area. Loco kept Primetime around for one question, however. "Primetime, this past Sunday night you won the Meltdown 'Choose Your Fate' match and quite frankly you enhanced your chances of winning Meltdown for the second year in a row. Certainly, the question on everyone's mind is have you decided your fate? On one hand, you can pick a partner of your choice like say Courage or Lance Star... they are two men you obviously trust and being experienced in the tag team division like you are and like they are, that could perhaps increase your chances of winning the event. On the other hand, you can choose to enter last... and have your partner assigned to you at random. You can take the chance that somehow Courage or Star might end up your partner randomly and still end up the last entrants in the match... or you can risk the chance that someone who doesn't like you ends up on your team." Primetime scoffed at this suggestion. "Someone not like ME?" Loco kinda looked around for an answer. "Well, let's face it, Primetime. You're not the most popular guy in the world. You injured Havok, you injured Matt Douglas, you and Mitch Wilson are not on the best of terms, you've created a lot of enemies for yourself as of late... Mike Bear & Scott Slugger probably want to take your ass and..." Primetime interrupted the 'lowlife interviewer'. "And what, huh? What? I don't care what they want to do. If they want a spot in some hospital next to Havok & Matt Douglas, that can be arranged. One word: Snap. And as far as what I've decided with the gift that I bestowed upon myself, well, it's really simple. I don't care who's on my team, because last year I carried Matt Douglas to the final match, and I won. It's as simple as that. So if I can carry Douglas, I can carry anyone. So, my answer is very simple. You're looking at the last entrant into Meltdown... and if I have my way, the winner of Meltdown for the second year in a row. Does that answer your question? Good. I've got a job to do. Get out of my way, or else I'll snap your neck in half too." Primetime pushed Loco to the floor, and his statement about having a 'job left to do' made viewers wonder what exactly the leader of the Future Foundation had up his proverbial sleeve... Fallen vs. "The Legend" Jon Spratt Warfare returns to the ring for one of the premiere matches of tonight's event. The sounds of "Bombtrack" and the Zach De La Rocha led Rage Against The Machine echo off the arena walls, and the fans stand to their feet to see the first match from the wrestler formerly known as Cactus Jon. "The Legend" Jon Spratt walks out onto the ramp, and as his music continues to play he walks into the ring. It looks as if he's dropped some weight, and looks to be in the same physical condition he was in his prime two years ago as the fWo World Champion. Jon acknowledges the crowd, who turn their attention to the ramp. "One" begins to play, and out walks the man Spratt was nearly killed by last year, Fallen. John Skyler walks out to the ring and is cheered by the thousands of Denver natives in attendance. He enters the ring and he and Spratt engage in a brief staredown, before Fallen takes to the ropes for a rare pose. Looking as cheerful as he ever has, Fallen turns back around and the referee signals for the bell. Fallen and Jon meet in the center of the ring. Appearing motionless, the duo simply stare at each other, perhaps speaking without words to one another. They circle each other, feel each other out, and then lock up. Fallen takes the advantage into a side chinlock, before into a hammerlock. Jon attempts to go-behind into a hammerlock of his own, but Fallen holds his ground, and simply won't allow it. Jon tries to counter the hold in the same manner Triple H tried to show Trish, er, Stephanie this past Monday on RAW. Jon bends over to go for it, instead he hurries and go behinds Fallen into a hammerlock, pulling off the move successful. Fallen counters out into a standing drop-toe-hold. Fallen then backs up and charges Jon, who stands to his feet. Jon counters by ducking for a backdrop, but Fallen tries to go for a piledriver. Jon backdrops out of the move, but Fallen holds on for a sunset flip attempt. Fallen reaches down and makes Jon release the grasp with several open fists, before charging towards the ropes again. He bounces and returns and Jon ducks his lariat attempt, they both turn back around to face one another, and Fallen walks towards Jon who grabs him and hooks him for a Michinoku Driver. Fallen however slides out behind and grabs Jon for the Lowfall DDT, but Jon reaches back and grabs Fallen and hooks him for a belly-to-back suplex. He hits it, then goes for the first pinfall attempt of the match, which garners just a one and a half count. Jon brings Fallen up and sends him to the ropes. Fallen returns and Jon hooks him for a tiltawhirl backbreaker. He completes the move, and goes for another cover. Kickout at two, and this leaves Spratt frustrated, yet not willing to give into his frustration just yet. Spratt brings Fallen up and sends him hard into the turnbuckles. Spratt follows him in and hooks up for a suplex, instead he sets him on the top turnbuckle. He climbs up and peppers Fallen with some forearms, before hooking him for a belly to belly suplex. They both go crashing on the mat, Jon follows up with a cover, this time Fallen gets his foot on the bottom rope. Spratt brings Fallen up once more, and sends him into the ropes. Fallen returns and Spratt goes for a hiptoss... Fallen blocks it, then counters into one of his own, but turns it into a face buster, slamming his face against his knee. Fallen now shakes off the cobwebs, before going to work on Spratt. A few stomps here and there to soften him up, before bringing him up and hitting a beautiful snap suplex. He covers him, but Spratt kicks out. Fallen brings him up and goes for another piledriver, and unlike earlier, this time he hits it. Another cover, but Jon kicks out. Fallen brings him up and sends him to the ropes, and as Spratt returns he's taken down with a nicely done Samoan Drop. Sensing it's over, he goes for the Lowfall. A second later he goes for a cover, but somehow, the Legend kicks out. Undaunted, Fallen decides to reach deep inside his bag of tricks... he goes to the top rope. He took too much time, and this allowed Spratt to recover. Spratt walks over and presses Fallen off the top rope towards the middle of the ring. Fallen hits and springs back up, and Spratt hooks him for his new finisher, The Legend Bomb. Seemingly hitting it out of nowhere, he gets the pin, and first win in his return to the fWo. Jon celebrates briefly, before Fallen gets up. The two don't exchange a handshake... a stare of perhaps newfound respect for each other. Nevertheless, the fans cheer for the two men who put on a nifty little match, as Spratt walks to the back, celebrating his first win in quite some time. Result: Spratt by pinfall Phone Calls In a lockerroom sat Eron The Relentless. Eron sported a black trenchcoat, which was hardly becoming of him for the weather we're currently enduring in the West. The hot temperatures, and smog from all the fires throughout the area hasn't helped... and one would believe that Eron was a certifiable lunatic from his choice of clothing for the night. Nevertheless, Eron looked at his cell phone, and dialed some numbers. Nobody knew who he was contacting, but it was quite clear who he could be. And certainly later tonight... something would go down. The guys who had been crucified were nervous... and perhaps rightfully so. Nobody knew who was behind it. Nobody knew the motivation. But everyone understood one thing, and one thing that was perfectly clear. Whoever was behind it... wasn't going to be living much longer. California Kings (c) vs. Rateings R' Us Warfare EXPLODED~! back into the arena, to a shot of everyone's favorite lackeys WALKING~! down the ramp as their theme music echoed throughout the Pepsi Center. Scot Hartt & The Mutha Fuckin' Abusah Of Yo Momma' walked down the ramp, looking eager to compete in their tag team title match that was forthcoming. They entered the ring and were greeted with an ovation of cheers because certainly, the fans loved these two goofy guys. The cheers turned to boos as "Last Resort" by Papa Roach started up, and out walked the reigning fWo World Tag Team Champions, the California Kings. The formiddable experienced duo (remember, they teamed before this in April when Star was the 'Future Frenchie') paid no attention to the jeers, as they joined their counterparts in the ring. They handed the straps over to the referee, who held them above the ring for everyone to view. The referee signalled for the bell, and the match was on. Lance and Hartt locked up at first, with Lance sending Hartt to the ropes. Hartt reversed it and as Lance returned he was met with a hiptoss, and then a Japanese armdrag. The hot crowd applauded these moves, as Hartt backed Lance into the ropes and sent him to the other side once again. Lance returned and was nailed with a backdrop, and then a dropkick which propelled him backwards over the top rope onto the floor. A groggy Lance was shortly helped to his feet by Courage, and the crowd cheered wildly at Hartt in the ring who got the crowd more involved. Hartt saw Courage & Lance and decided to take a high risk. A pescado over the top to the floor was in order, and he hit it. From there, he hit a snap suplex on Courage on the floor, before slamming Lance head first into the nearby ringpost. He then rolled him back into the ring, before going to the top rope. As Lance staggered on spaghetti legs in the ring, Hartt flew off with a beautiful high crossbody. Lance however mustered enough up to catch him, and then turn it into a running powerslam. He draped Hartt over his right shoulder Davey Boy Smith style, then charged towards the middle of the ring to complete the move. However, Hartt fell behind him and then captured him with a waistlock, before hitting a German Suplex with a bridge, which surprised everyone, including Hartt. The referee counted, but Courage came in to break up the pin. Enter Abusah. Abusah stood in the corner and pounded his chest Tarzan style, and as Courage turned towards him... he felt it. The spear. Courage rolled out onto the floor clutching his stomach, and then the referee had to turn his attention away from the belly-to-belly suplex Hartt just hit in the ring and had a cover on Star. The referee backed Abusah into his corner, and Courage reentered the ring, although still suffering the affects of the spear. Courage pulled Hartt off of Star, and hit a reverse neckbreaker on him, before going outside the ring and waiting for the tag. Slowly, but eventually, Star crawled over and made the tag. Courage came in and began to take control. He sent Hartt to the ropes, and as Hartt returned he was nailed in the stomach with a knee to the gut, and then hitting a bulldog. Courage followed it up by going to the top rope and hitting a frogsplash, before going for a cover. Hartt kicked out at two, and then Courage tagged out to Star. They sent Hartt to the ropes and when he returned Courage backdropped him into a sitting powerbomb by Star. He hooked the legs for another cover, but the referee urging Courage out of the ring stopped this momentarily. Abusah saw an opening and took advantage of it, coming in and kicking Star in the head two or three times to break up the pin. This 'caused the referee to back Abusah out of the ring, giving Courage and Star another opening. Abusah wouldn't listen though, and continued to argue with the referee. "I WANNA B TAG TEEM CHAMPEEYON!" Courage came in and Star signalled to proceed quickly with their finisher. Courage prepared for it, as Star brought Hartt up for the Shooting Star. He hit it, then the referee saw their treachery and wanted Courage to get out. Courage finally did, and the referee returned to count the pin. One. Two. Kickout by Hartt. Scott friggin' Hartt just kicked out of the Shooting Star. Star brought Hartt up and sent him to the ropes. Hartt reversed it. Star returned and Hartt ducked for a backdrop; but Lance countered with one of his favorite moves, the inverted swinging neckbreaker. Thinking it was undoubtedly over, he went for a cover. Somehow, someway, Hartt kicked out. Lance decided to take a high risk maneuver. He went up to the top rope, and prepared for a shooting star splash. Instead, Abusah shook the ropes and Lance ended up crotched on it. This bought Hartt some time, and he crawled into his corner. The crowd was on their feet hoping for the tag. And then, it happened. Abusah made the tag. The crowd exploded. Courage charged to buy Star some time to get off the ropes, but Abusah took him down with a simple clothesline. Abusah then saw Star in the corner, and decided to follow him up there. He did, and hit a belly-to-back suplex off the top rope. He went for a cover, but Courage broke it up with a kick. The referee then tried to get Courage out of the ring. Standing to his side, Abusah decided to get Courage out of the ring himself. Instead, Abusah charged... it was again time for Courage to "FEER DA SPEER!" The crafty Courage had different thoughts, and put the referee in front of him instead. All three men crashed into the turnbuckles, with the referee looking worse for wear. Courage fell to the mat. The only one who appeared to go unscathed was Abusah. He turned his attention to Star, who was just getting up. He charged... Hartt went to the outside and grabbed one of the tag team titles. Star saw Abusah coming, and hooked him as he was about to execute the spear for the Shooting Star. Not knowing the referee was practically unconscious, Star went for the cover. One. Two. Three. Four. He had him pinned, yet nobody could count it. Enter Hartt. Hartt came in, beckoned Star to get up, he turned... and smack. Hi, I'm Lance Star. Hi, I'm your World Tag Team Title. Lance fell to the mat, and Hartt rolled Abusah over onto him. Again, the referee was out however, and couldn't count the pin. The crowd booed, but they stopped as another referee entered the picture. He saw the pin, and began to count. One. Two. Three. Rateings R' Us had done it. They had won the fWo World Tag Team Titles. The Pepsi Center exploded, the roof last seen floating over the Atlantic Ocean somewhere. The fans cheered wildly, and the referee awarded the tag team titles to Scot Hartt & Abusah, who couldn't contain their celebration. Courage & Star tried to sort it out in another corner of the ring. The announcer got on the microphone as Hartt & Abusah began to walk up the ramp... as they did, down came Primetime. Primetime scowled at them and told them not to go anywhere so soon. He came in and began waking up the other referee. Primetime told the scheduled official for the match what had happened. The referee didn't believe him at first... the announcer began to announce that we had new fWo World Tag Team Champions; until the original referee halted him, and told him not to get ahead of himself. Primetime pointed at the Warfare screen, where a video now played of Hartt hitting Star with the tag title. This evidence was more than enough. The referee made a quick decision. He told the announcer that Rateings R' Us had won, but by disqualification as a result of Courage using him as a shield. He then awarded the fWo World Tag Team Titles back to the California Kings, which resulted in the ring becoming a proverbial landfill, what with all the trash that was now in the ring. The crowd was now booing full force, and the referee had the announcer demand that Team Ratings bring the tag titles back to the rightful owners. They did, but in their own little way. They charged the ring and laid out everyone in sight with the belts. Down went Courage. Down went the original referee. Star stood above Courage looking down at him, and Abusah nailed him from behind. He fell, and ended up in a compromising position atop of Courage. The crowd exploded into cheers, but that didn't make up for the fact that Rateings R' Us had just been screwed. One way or the other though... they would get a rematch, and they were comfortable with the fact and confident enough to beat the Kings cleanly next time around. Warfare headed for a commercial while security and fWo backstage officials escorted Team Rateings backstage... Result: Rateings R' Us by disqualification Differences Warfare returned backstage from commercials to find Fallen slumped up against a locker, fresh off of taking off his ring attire. The fWo International Champion Ric Chronos entered the picture, with his title hanging over his shoulder. He appeared ready to go for his rematch with Crusader, but before he did he engaged in some conversation with his good friend Fallen. "John... you win some you lose some. You know that. Don't be discouraged over a loss like this. He was just ready for a couple of weeks, that's all. C'mon, Jon's a legend." Fallen looked back at Ric emotionless, but nodded. Chronos turned to exit, before turning back towards Fallen. "And another thing... don't get between me and Panther anymore. We need to settle this, and it's unfair of us to try and make you choose a side. We're BOTH your friend, and we're going to have tosettle OUR differences one way or another..." Fallen again nodded, before wishing Chronos good luck as he exited the room... Ric Chronos (c) vs. Crusader Biohazard's "Switchback" hits the PA system, and the fans rise to their feet as Crusader walks down the aisle for this fWo International Title shot. He slaps the hands of a few fans, then hops up on the apron, hits the turnbuckle, and backflips into the ring. He warms up while "Christian Says" jams the house, bringing out the International Champion, Ric Chronos. The fans continue to cheer as Chronos walks down toward the ring. He slides in, and looks at Crusader, before handing the belt to the ref. The ref holds it high in the air, and the bell sounds. Chronos and Crusader, before locking up, shake hands, much to the delight of the crowd. Yes, the crowd ALWAYS enjoys the shaking of hands before a match. The beginnings of this wonderful gesture was discovered in...oh wait, the match has started, thus ending this quick history lesson. The two stars lock up, with Chronos using his power to give him the edge. As soon as the tide starts turning away from Crusader's advantage, he quickly drops to the mat and uses his feet to push Chronos into the ropes. Chronos hits the ropes and stares at Crusader as he gets back to his feet. Chronos charges Crusader; Crusader ducks, and follows Chronos toward the ropes. He locks his arms and tries to roll him up upon Chronos hitting the ropes. However, Chronos locks his arms on the ropes, and Crusader flies off, hitting the ground. Crusader rolls backwards and lands on his feet, just in time for Chronos to charge him. Crusader leap frogs him. However, Chronos catches Crusader, and drives him to the mat. Chronos goes for a pin, hooking both legs. The ref counts to two before Crusader gets his a shoulder up. Chronos uses this momentum of the shoulder rising to completely turn Crusader over for a Boston Crab. Crusader refuses to give up; however, this isn't the reason for the move. Chronos knows that this won't put him out of submission. If Crusader taps out at this point, well, he'd just be a sissy. Chronos holds him in for about a minute and a half before letting go. The REAL purpose of this move is to slow Crusader down. Once Chronos releases the move, he picks up Crusader and slams him to the mat. He picks him up again, locks him in for a suplex, and nails it. He goes for a cover, but only gets a two. He picks up Crusader and tosses him into the turnbuckle. As he is doing this, the Black Panther makes his way down toward ringside, obviously to watch the match, and look out for Ric. Chronos sees this, and immediately becomes a little distracted. He grabs Crusader and begins to throw him for an Irish Whip. However, he reverses in mid-course and throws him back into the corner. He climbs to the second rope and begins to land some mounted punches. After two, he glances at TBP, who waves at him. After three, he looks at him again. After four, he's staring at him. Crusader, who has a strong cranium, uses the punches as a wake-up call. After thirty seconds of Chronos staring at TBP, Crusader gently wraps his arms around Chronos' legs. Then, with one swift motion, Crusader takes a leap, and the legs of Chronos are swept out from under him. His head hits the mat, and he snaps back akwardly. He is obviously out of it. Crusader looks at him, unsure of whether or not to continue. He knows that it's his job to perform, and his duty to win belts. So he picks up Chronos and whips him into the ropes. Chronos flies off, and Crusader nails him with a spinning heel kick. Crusader then takes off for a corner. He leaps for the top rope, and flies off for a moonsault. He nails the moonsault, and the crowd goes wild. He signals to the corner once more, and the crowd goes continues to go nuts. He climbs up, and then takes off. He nails the Gravity 101, and goes for a cover. Three hits of the mat later, and Crusader has won the fWo International Championship. The referee retrieved his newly acquired championship, while Chronos sat in the ring with his head down. He looked incensed; and then stood up and turned towards Panther. Panther stared at him devoid of any emotion, before walking over to the timekeeper's table and receiving a microphone. Crusader and Chronos shook hands, and Chronos congratulated Crusader on winning the title. Meanwhile on the outside, Panther began to walk up the ring steps. Crusader made his way backstage to an ovation of applause. Panther proceeded with caution towards the now former fWo International Champion... Chronos asked Panther if he had something to say. Panther nodded sternly, before speaking. "You screwed Fallen out of a title in the WOW, and then do the same thing here - some 'friend' you are." Chronos was now a little more than incensed. He walked over and pushed Panther and asked him what his problem was. Panther pushed back, before finally - finally - the two came to blows. They went tooth and nail with each other, each of them getting in their fair share of punches. Referees stormed the ring to split up the melee. Everyone turned their attention to the ramp waiting for Fallen to make his scheduled appearance, but just as he was told... he didn't. He stayed backstage. Panther & Chronos continued to sort throughout their differences... as Warfare went to a commercial. Result: Crusader by pinfall Meeting Backstage, six wrestlers gathered near the Warfare entrance. Five of them had all shared the unfortunate pleasure of being rendered unconscious with ether and then tied to a cross. The sixth hadn't, but knew perhaps his time would come soon. Eron, Ice, Mitch Wilson & The Future Foundation stood backstage... and anyone backstage who had a camera ready was certainly taking pictures, as Primetime & Mitch Wilson were actually civil towards one another for the first time since last October. "We need to find who did this..." spoke Eron. Everyone nodded. "I don't care who it is. Mitch has a match right now, but you can be rest assured if one of us finds that prick... we're all going to tie him to his own cross and light that son of a bitch on fire." spoke Ice. Everyone nodded again. Primetime seemed weary of cooperating with his long-time rival Mitch. "Alright... I haven't seen any signs of him tonight but let's start looking. When Mitch' match is done, we'll meet back here. If anyone finds him, you bring him back here." said one of the four men who had competed in 'The Hour' at SummerSlaughter, Primetime. Mitch nodded. "Start looking... my match is up next." The Kings went off in another direction to begin searching for "D". Primetime walked off on his own, while Ice & Eron did the same. Ice turned back and wished Mitch good luck... who nodded, and then prepared for his match which was coming up next... 'Mr. Main Event' Mitch Wilson vs. Wretch Wednesday Warfare comes back from commercials to the sounds of "Paint it Black" by Gob. The song brings out Big Wretchy, the ratings saver himself, for his Internet Title rematch. Wretchy enters the ring, and "Last Cup of Sorrow" by Faith No More starts up, and the reigning fWo Internet Champion, and the winner of 'The Hour', Mitch Wilson steps onto the entrance ramp. The no-nonsense champion continues to the ring, and steps onto the ring apron. Immediately, Wretch nails Mitch with a flying forearm that sends him to the outside, and sends the Internet Title flying. The bell rings, and Wretch flies onto Mitch who had gotten up with a plancha. Wretchy, a man out of control now, begins planting Mitch with lefts and rights, before the referee orders him to return to the ring. Wretch rolls Mitch in, and follows after him. Wretch brings Mitch up, and looks for a vertical suplex. Mitch gets out from behind, and hooks him for the Headliner, but Wretch nails a few elbows to the side of Wilson, which breaks the hold. Wretch hooks Wilson for an acid drop attempt, but as he takes to the air, Wilson sends him flying across the ring. As Wretch gets up, he attempts a clothesline, but Mitch ducks, and takes him down with a reverse DDT. Mitch covers, but Wretch gets a shoulder up at two. Wilson brings Wretch up for a death valley driver, but Wretch escapes. He puts Wilson in a headlock, and then slams his head into the mat with a bulldog. One, two, and Wilson gets a shoulder up. Wretch brings Wilson up, and attemps an enziguri kick, but Wretch ducks. Wilson hooks him from behind, planting him with a release german suplex. As Wretch's head violently hits the mat, Wilson covers, but once again, to no avail as Wretch kicks out. Wilson brings Wretch up, and hooks him for the Superstar Suplex, but Wretch counters. Wretch then hooks Wilson for the Ratings Saver Suplex, and hits. Somehow, however, Wilson kicks out of the bridge. Both men to their feet, and Wretch runs at Wilson. However, Wilson is able to press him, and seemingly throw him to the outside. However, Wretch lands on the ring apron, and grabs Wilson from behind, throwing him to the mat. Wretch uses the ring ropes to balance himself, and comes down with a legdrop across the throat of Wilson. Quick cover by Wretch, but Wilson gets his foot on the bottom rope. Wretch complains of a slow count to no avail, as he brings Wilson up to his feet. He hooks Wilson for a Northern Lights suplex, but Wilson counters, and turns it into a slingshot. Wretch hits the turnbuckles, and comes back unknowingly into a belly to belly suplex. Wilson covers, but again, it's going to take more than that to defeat Wretch, as he kicks out. Wilson brings Wretch up, and hooks him for a gutwrench suplex, and connects. One, two, no, at two and a half, Wretch kicks out. Wilson brings Wretch up again, and looks for a Headliner again. However, this time Wretch turns it into the Depression. He has hit his finisher. One. Two. No, we came a third of a second away from having a new champion, but somehow, Mitch kicks out. Wretch brings Wilson up, and plants him with a quick brainbuster, before standing him up in the corner, to perform a move. Wretch places himself on the second turnbuckle, and grabs Wilson by the neck. Wretch plants Wilson with a tornado DDT. As Wilson stumbles to his feet, Wretch places himself on the second turnbuckle once more. Wilson blindly stumbles right into Wretch, who looks for a Wretchinoku Driver from the second turnbuckle. He lifts Wilson up, but somehow Wilson lands behind and onto the top turnbuckle. Wilson hooks Wretch, and then plants him with a top rope Headliner. Wilson covers, and a three count later, Wretchy comes up *this* short to winning the Internet Title, once again. Mitch stands up and celebrates... and then the lights go out. Everyone begins to wonder what the hell is going on... and then the lights come back on. Mitch is in the ring bound and gagged to a cross, and appears unconscious. Wretch stands up and looks at the scene groggily, before shaking his head and walking backstage. The lights dim, and the letter "D" appears on the screen once again... They're all empty handed. None of them found "D"... but with good reason. "D" wasn't backstage... yet none of them are aware of this. The Kings enter the picture, and look disappointed when they see everyone without the tall, wiry man dressed in black in their possession. Eron looks around. Ice slammed his fist into a wall, before grabbing a nearby chalkboard and shattering it to pieces. Scott Slugger vs. Mike Bear Rob Zombie's music begins to hit the PA system and home entertainment systems whereever a television is set to their local NBC station. The music is then overshadowed by the fans' applause, as Mike Bear makes himself visable to the millions of fWo fans around the world. He walks down toward the entrance ramp with a rather cool look upon his face. He slides in the ring and begins to warm up as AC/DC hits the speakers, bringing up four-time fWo champion, Scott Slugger. The man who the fans have cheered and booed since Day One of the company's existence garners cheers from the fans on this night. He stops on the entrance ramp, acknowledge the fans' reaction, and takes a look at Bear. Bear, too, acknowledges the reaction. Slugger walks into the ring, and plays to the crowd a little bit. The bell rings, and the two lockup (but, just for those keeping track, YES, they DO shake hands first.) Slugger gets the early advantage, but Bear brings him to a corner. The ref breaks them up, and the fans look to see if either of them will take a shot at each other. However, as a sign of respect, Slugger walks away and allows Bear to reset himself. They lock up again, with Bear gaining the advantage this time. Slugger, like Bear before, does manage to bring Bear to a corner. The ref again breaks them up, and again, as a sign of respect, Bear walks away and allows for Slugger to reset. Ah, the wonder that is sportsmanship these days. Makes you wonder if these guys are doing it for nothing. They begin to lock up once more, when Bear ducks it and grabs the waist of Slugger. He quickly hits a belly-to-back suplex. He leaves Slugger be, and gets up. He gets some momentum off the ropes, and hits an elbow. Bear then puts Slugger in a one-legged Boston Crab. Bear knows that, in order to slow down Slugger, you work from the ground up. He holds the move for about a minute, and then releases it. He picks up Slugger, and whips him to the ropes. Slugger bounces off, and comes toward Bear. Bear nails Slugger in the gut with a fist. Slugger continues past Bear, who rushes toward the ropes. He gets his momentum, and takes off. Flying in mid-air, he grabs the neck of Slugger. He lands a flying bulldog, and immediate goes for a cover. One. Tw....not so fast. Slugger kicks out. Bear picks up Slugger and whips him toward a corner. He charges Slugger, only to see Slugger move out of the way. Bear nails the turnbuckle full-speed. Slugger waits for Bear to wobble out of the corner, and then nails an inverted Russian Leg-sweep. He goes for a cover. One. Tw...er, make that one and three-quarters. Bear kicks out, obviously not-that-fazed from the move. Slugger picks up Bear, and then hoists him on his shoulder. He measures up Bear, and then hits a running powerslam. Slugger goes to the ropes. He begins to climb, all the way to the top. He waits for Bear to rise to his feet. Once he does, Slugger takes off. He nails a flying lariat off the top rope. He nailed the Grand Slam. Slugger makes the cover. The ref counts. One. Two. Th...nope. Bear gets a shoulder up. Slugger glances around at the crowd, and picks up Bear once more. Slugger whips Bear into the ropes. Slugger gets ready for a back-body drop. However, Bear, while making his way toward Slugger, sees this. Thinking quickly, he grabs the neck of Slugger and hits a swinging neckbreaker. The two lay motionless in the ring, although for only a second or two. Bear begins to rise to his feet. He is followed by Slugger. The two begin to trade punches, with Bear winning the battle. He sends Slugger to the ropes. Slugger bounces off, and Bear nails him in the stomach. He begins to pick him up for the Whiplash, when Slugger counters. They move toward the ropes, when Bear counters again. This time, he nails the Whiplash, almost out of nowhere. Bear has the match won. He has him pinned. The ref is counting. One. Two. Three. Bear has won. Or so he thought. The ref looked up as his hand went down for the third time. Slugger has his foot on the ropes. The count, for all intensive purposes, is over. The match continues. Bear, dismayed by this misfortune, picks up the dazed Slugger. He begins to go for another Whiplash. And then, a third entrant into this match makes his appearance. Primetime, with his best friend at the current moment, a steel chair, begin to make their way down toward ringside. The fans boo harder with every step he takes. Meanwhile, Bear, who has drawn Slugger into the middle of the ring, hits yet another Whiplash. As he hits it, Primetime slides into the ring. As Bear stands up to make the pin, Primetime nails him with the chair. Primetime then picks up the head of Slugger, and takes a shot there. He points to his own head, as if to tell the crowd that he's got something that they should consider looking into. Then, the fans cheer. Huh, PT thinks to himself. These fans actually are thinking. Nah, they're still idiots. Someone's probably sneaking up on me. Many a fan who witnessed this match wouldn't exactly phrase it after the night was over as "sneaking up." Rather, the Boston Strangler runs toward the ring and slides inside. Primetime takes a swing at him with the chair, which Strangler ducks. Strangler kicks PT in the gut, and nails a gutwrench powerbomb. PT falls to the mat. The match, having already been thrown out, has become a massacre. A Boston Massacre. The Strangler's finisher. He begins to place the manuever onto Primetime. And then, darkness. Silence. And then, a thud. The lights come back on. Bear is still out from the chair shot. Slugger too. Primetime is out from the gutwrench powerbomb. The Boston Strangler is laid out too. This is due to the man known as Rain, who stands over him. Rain looks down upon the Strangler with fury. And, with the scene in the ring looking like a massacre of a different sort, Warfare's cameras fade to black, ending the show. Result: No Contest
