Introduction There are days that are simple, and days that are easy. Days that seem to go on forever and days that have turned to night in the blink of an eye. There are memorable and forgetable days, and then there are days that test the very soul itself... 10 Days Later Fallen stumbled and grabbed at the wall, his fingers slipping over rough, wet concrete. Above him, high above him, he could hear the distant roar of the crowd at Uprising. Glancing at his watch, he found it had stopped three hours ago, the date July 17th flashing mutely back at him. He frowned, and tossed the watch to one side. It was cold down here, and he shook his head to clear his head from the mute pain in his temples that the chill was causing. How in the hell had it gotten like this? The last several days were a blur, a confused hazy blur of images and events. He owed Chronos an ass kicking. "Comfortable?" Fallen turned around and looked at the face in the shadows. Blonde hair, tussled. Turn the clock back five years and he would be looking at himself... nearly. The eyes, dark and bloodshot gave the mockery away. Despite the yellow tights, the painfully stretched smile... the eyes were haunted and deep. Fallen coughed against the cold and denied the man before him. "You're not Moore... you're sick. You're pretending to be someone you're not." The man, William Bailey, laughed, a deep, coughing laugh. "From the mouths of babes... you're not John Skyler either my dirty friend, you're just a shadow." Fallen slumped against the wall. There was another shadowy figure behind Bailey... but Fallen couldn't make out who it was. His mind reeled... how in the hell had things gotten like this? It seemed like only days ago he was in Fox's office. Fox had just gotten the news that the show was going to be canceled... pre-empted... and it was bright like an inferno all around them... Pre-empted "What? You've got to be kidding me..." Silver Fox groaned into the phone and collapsed on a padded chair at the desk. All around him was the activity of the ring crew, the television agents, the production staff... but Fallen saw none of it. He had gotten out of the hospital a week ago, and at the moment was enjoying with all of himself the bright sunlight that was streaming in through the overhead skylights. It was like a bright inferno of heat, but on cold and stiff muscles, and still-healing skin, it felt like heaven. He never wanted to be cold again... after days of hospital treatments, procedures... "So it's just canceled? Just like that?" Fallen glanced at Silver Fox with a wry smile. It wasn't a job he wanted, that was for sure. Silver Fox sighed loudly and put the phone down, clicking the off button with a disgusted snap. "Pre-empted. They tell us this not three hours before air-time. Well, that's just great... so what are we supposed to tell the idiots down there who came just so they can get their fat ugly mugs on television, huh? This job... this job..." Silver Fox stood and began to pace, glancing from side to side as workers passed him by on either side. "It's just for a week, something about additional coverage for the Tour De France. You've got to be kidding me... if there's a more dull sport than golf, bicycle racing is it. So we air next week, four days in a row... ending with the life Uprising show on the 17th. But enough about me... look, how are you feeling John?" Fallen smiled a smile with no humor. "It's Ryan. And I'm stiff, sore... but otherwise I'll live." Silver Fox nodded, a serious expression on his face. "Ryan. Right. Listen, that was a hell of a stupid stunt you two pulled. I don't want to see any more 'worst of five' series... you understand?" Fallen nodded. "Yeah, but you better put the message out. Temerario's been watching the footage non-stop." "We've talked with him," said Silver Fox, nodding. "But listen... why the finish with the stage dive thing? Seemed in bad taste, you know? Whose idea was that?" Fallen shook his head. "It wasn't mine." Silver Fox's eyebrows raised, but Fallen continued. "Maybe Neo had something to do with it... but I doubt it. Isn't his style. I was as surprised as you were... I thought Bailey was still out of the business." "He quit didn't he? After the whole accident with Ry... I mean with John?" Fallen nodded. "Yeah. William Bailey... Speed Demon. Never was much of anything, sort of an indy wrestler that never quite understood the big leagues. But he was finished with the business after the accident... or at least, that's what I heard. Why he's dressing up like me from four years ago is a mystery... but I think it would be best if you informed security to keep him out of the building. The guy wasn't the most level-headed individual on his best days." Silver Fox nodded. "Agreed... we don't need any problems." "No," said Fallen, a dark expression on his face. "we don't." Unbeknownst to Creed, Slugger managed to flip over fully instead of impacting with the move, and landed on his feet. As Creed got up, Slugger leapt onto his back, hooked his arms with his legs, and rolled forward into a victory roll. One. Two. Kickout by Creed. Both men got up, and before Slugger could go on the offensive, Creed blasted the crap out of him with a stiff, muscular forearm. Playtime was over - Creed was pissed, and for the second time in less than a week, Slugger was bleeding from the nose thanks to a direct forearm shot. Creed grabbed Slugger before he could go down and hurled him through the ropes, then stepped out after him. He grabbed Slugger and slammed his head onto the guardrail, once, twice, three times, and on the third time, Slugger's forehead joined his nose as being busted open. Creed grabbed Slugger and lifted him up in a gorilla press, then dropped Slugger throat-first onto the guardrail, causing Slugger to fall to the ground and clutch his neck. Creed brought Slugger up and shoved him against a ringpost, then backed up enough to get a good running start. The World Champion then exploded forward with full force, going for a spear, trying to squash Slugger against the ringpost. Slugger moved. Creed flew into the ringpost, driving himself against it. He turned his head a moment before impact, and instead of taking it directly on the top of the head, Creed caught a glancing but still high-impact blow to the side of the head. He crumpled to the floor, and Slugger staggered off, trying to regain his balance as the crowd cheered this sudden turn in the match. Slugger finally cleared the cobwebs, and stepped over the guardrail into the front row, where he was assaulted with pats on the back from various fans. Creed slowly got to his feet, and Slugger cleared the fans from his area, then slingshotted onto the top of the guardrail. He then flew off with a version of the Grand Slam, connecting squarely with a flying lariat of sorts on the Main Man. Beginning to get his second wind (or, more accurately, his first wind), Slugger brought Creed quickly up and tossed him back into the ring, then hopped onto the ring apron. Slugger quickly climbed to the top, and Creed slowly got to his feet. When Creed was vertical, Slugger took to the air, sailing across the ring and catching Creed flush on the face with a missile dropkick. Creed dropped to the mat, and Slugger scrambled over to cover, hooking the leg. One. Two. Creed kicked out with a vengeance, throwing Slugger over to the side. Slugger frowned, not pleased by this turn of events, but continued on the offensive nonetheless. As Creed tried to get up, Slugger ran into the ropes, then came off with a half-seated-dropkick, half-baseball-slide that caught Creed's jaw, knocking him back down. With Creed down, Slugger ran into the ropes, hopped onto the second strand, then leapt off with an asai moonsault. Slugger reached across and hooked the leg, and the referee made the count. Creed again kicked out at two with plenty to spare, although at least this time he did not send Slugger flying. Slugger brought Creed up to his feet and whipped him into the ropes, impressive enough considering Creed's strength advantage. Even more impressive, as Creed came off, Slugger lifted him up and drove him throat-first across the top rope, perfectly executing the Line Drive. Creed was down, and once more Slugger hooked the leg. One, two, and another kickout. Slugger sighed and brought Creed up again, then whipped him into the corner. Creed's massive bulk hitting the corner practically seemed to make the ring move, but Slugger took no notice as he backed across the ring into the opposite corner. Slugger charged across the ring and jumped at Creed, going for a leaping bodysplash. Instead, Creed moved, and Slugger crashed into the corner, his head slamming down into the steel ringpost. Slugger staggered out backwards, and Creed grabbed him and, this time, successfully executed the release german suplex he went for earlier. Slugger slammed to the mat and flipped fully over, ending up on his stomach. Creed walked over and stomped on Slugger, then mounted him and applied a camel clutch. Slugger grimaced in the painful grip of the submission hold, made famous by the Iron Shiek among others. Creed yanked back with all of his force on Slugger's chin, and Slugger gritted his teeth to keep in a scream. The referee stepped in front of Slugger and asked him if he wanted to quit. Slugger tried to shake his head, found he could not in Creed's iron grip, then gutted out a negative response. Creed continued to wrench in the hold, then let go of Slugger's head with one hand and began to slam into him with a forearm, hitting Slugger repeatedly. Creed's forearm came up stained with the blood of Slugger, but Creed took no notice, slamming Slugger's face over, and over, and over. The referee asked Slugger if he wanted to quit, and again the answer was no. Finally, Slugger appeared to slump over - as much as one can in a camel clutch - and the referee grabbed an arm, lifting it (again, as much as an arm can be lifted in a camel clutch). It fell. He lifted it a second time. It fell a second time. The ref lifted it once more. If it fell this time, Creed would win and retain his title. The arm fell - then shot up, drawing an eruption of cheers from the crowd. Slugger's hands balled into fists, and he summoned up all the adrenaline he could, drawing on the cheers of the crowd for more strength. He slowly struggled onto his knees... then, as Creed shook his head in negation and shock, stood up. Creed clung to his back like some weird, overgrown species of monkey, and Slugger took care of that problem by falling straight backward, driving Creed into the mat. Both men were down, and the referee began to make his count. One... two... three... four... five... six... seven... Creed slowly began to get up... Eight... Slugger was up again. Slugger swung wildly at Creed, who was able to see it coming and dodge it. Slugger's momentum carried him into a spin, and as he turned back around, Creed lifted him into a gorilla press. He then dropped Slugger in a powerslam, planting him with the Powerdriver. Creed stayed on top of Slugger for the cover, and the ref made the count. One. Two. Kickout. Creed cursed, but saw a chance to do more damage. Leaving Slugger to lay on the ground, Creed stepped onto the ring apron and began the climb to a place he rarely visted - the very top rope. Slugger was still down as Creed reached the top. He measured the World's Greatest Athlete, then prepared to leap with the Creedzillinator that would surely end it all - and then, quicker than a frightened cat, Scott Slugger was up on his feet again. He had been playing possum, and Slugger smirked his famous smirk as he swept Creed's legs out from under him, crotching him on the top rope. Slugger climbed up after him. He hooked Creed up, then drove him off the top rope. As Stephen King once wrote, "so fell Lord Perth, and the countryside did shake with that thunder." Superplex. Slugger bounced to his feet, and he signaled to the crowd that it was time to bring this match to an end - it was Curveball time. Slugger brought Creed to his feet and, with all the force he could muster, whipped him into the corner. Creed slammed in, but not with the impact he normally would have made. The referee, you see, was caught between Creed and the corner, and as Creed staggered out, the referee crumpled to the ground. Slugger didn't see this, however, as he was focused on Creed. As Creed approached him on wobbly legs, Slugger leapt into the air and double-underhooked his head, then spun and drove Creed into the mat. Curveball. The crowd erupted with cheers, and Slugger made the cover and hooked the leg. For obvious reasons, no count was made, and Slugger took it upon himself to make a count, slapping the mat. One... two... three! The referee was still down, however, and finally Slugger saw, from the corner of his eye, the ref in the corner. Before Slugger could go to the corner, a second referee ran to the ring, to thunderous cheers from the crowd. Slugger's eyes went wide, and he resumed his cover on Creed, hooking the leg deeply. The second referee dropped down to count. One. Two. Three. Scott Slugger was a six-time fans Wrestling organization World Heavyweight Champion. The crowd cheered deafeningly, and Slugger got to his feet, elated as he had not been in years. The second referee raised his arm, and the timekeeper brought the fWo World Championship belt to the ring. He handed it to the referee, who passed it along to Slugger. Slugger held it high in the air, and the Portland crowd went nuts as their (near-)native son celebrated. Slugger leaned over a rope with the World Title belt in hand, playing to the crowd even as the blood, symbol of his hard-earned victory... dripped down his face. But something was happening in the corner... Result: Scott Slugger by pinfall... ? Vic Creed (c) vs. Scott Slugger As this happened, the first referee slowly began to wake. He took in the scene before him, dumbfounded, wondering what could have happened. He got slowly to his feet, and saw the second referee. The first ref grabbed the second ref and pulled him over, then began to have an animated conversation. The crowd, ignoring them in favor of Slugger, continued to cheer unabated. Meanwhile, the second ref nodded, and the first ref called over Matt Panzer, the ring announcer, and whispered in his ear. Panzer nodded, and raised the mic. "Ladies and gentlemen, your attention please... I have been informed by the referee, that due to the three-count being registered by a referee who was not the official referee for the match, the match between Scott Slugger and Vic Creed WILL CONTINUE!" On the ropes, Slugger's eyes bugged out in shock, but only for a moment, because the man who had been forgotten in all the chaos, Vic Creed himself, was up on his feet and slamming into Slugger, knocking him over the top rope to the outside, where he landed with a splat. The World Title belt, not Slugger's after all, was sent flying. The announcers explained to the fans that, as much as they may not like it, the official referee made the right call. Creed quickly stepped to the outside and grabbed Slugger, then, before Slugger could counter, Creed lifted Slugger up in a suplex position, then drove him into the ground with the Kodiak Krippler, both stunning and silencing the previously raucous crowd. Creed grinned an evil grin, then rolled Slugger back into the ring. He stood over Slugger, looking down in disgust, then dropped down to make the cover - and was rolled up in a small package by Slugger. The referee (the *first* referee, the second having vacated the area) dropped down and made the count. One. Two. Kickout. Creed kicked, and each man got to their feet, Creed quicker than Slugger, who was still feeling the effects of the Kodiak Krippler. Creed pasted Slugger with a forearm, staggering him, then kicked him below the belt, doubling him over. Creed put Slugger between his legs and, with absolutely no ceremony, executed perhaps the most feared finisher in all of wrestling. The Hangman's DDT. Slugger was driven head-first into the mat, and Creed, still feeling the effects of a hard-fought match, dropped down on top of him. One. Two. Three. This time, it was the official referee making the count - this time, it was official. Vic Creed was still the fans Wrestling organization World Heavyweight Champion. The crowd booed wildly as Creed's title belt was retrieved and handed to him. Creed lifted it high in the air, mocking the crowd, and stood up on the second rope as the crowd rained down their boos on him. Creed had overcome yet another challenger, and he would live another day as champion. On that note, RUAHH~!'s cameras faded to black. Result: Vic Creed by pinfall
