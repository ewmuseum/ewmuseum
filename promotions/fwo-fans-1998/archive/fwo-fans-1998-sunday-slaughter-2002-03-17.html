Introduction "Come in," said Chris Universal, responding to the knocks on his office door. Eli Flair and Poison Ivy enter, both in streetclothes as neither are required to play an active role in the evening's card. "We need to talk," began Ivy, in a firm tone of voice, non threatening but with an edge of seriousness to it, "May we sit?" Universal motions toward the two folding chairs in front of his makeshift office this week, and he puts down his ledger, giving them his full attention. "Eli and I are concerned about the Hardcore Title," said Ivy, "and I know that he didn't defeat Winters last week, but he didn't lose either." "The two of them made a deal--" began Universal, but Ivy cut him off. "I know what their deal was," interrupted Ivy, "But we both want to know what the status of the Hardcore title is going to be at Cyberslam, now. Who's getting a shot?" Universal exhaled sharply, looking over his guests. "Cyberslam is more than a month away, Ivy," he started, "We're still putting things together, we're going over a multitude of scenarios, and we have to account for the unexpected. It certainly wouldn't make sense, for example, for us to put Austin Davis into the biggest show of the year until tonight is passed, yes?" "I know," replied Eli, "But this is important to me. If you have any ideas, it would mean a lot if you'd share them." Universal turned around in his swivel chair, looking toward the calender on his wall. "Eli, Ivy... While I agree that if Donaven Winters is still the Hardcore Champion by the time Cyberslam rolls around, the money match opponent is a no- brainer, I'm also aware of the deal you two made, as well as the number of fans who would love to see Lint in there, too. So, here's the deal. If you can work out a deal with Winters, lovely. That's all the less work I'll have to do. But if not, I'll see what I can do along the lines of including you, Eli, in whatever gets planned. That's the best I can do tonight." "Fair enough," replied Eli, shaking his hand, "C'mon, Ivy - we've got ourselves a mental case to find." Standing a mere inch from the mirror, his broad nose almost touching the polished glass, his steady breath billowing a frosted plume onto the glass before it melted away under the bright lights. He did this before every match, in every arena, in more small towns than he could remember. He stared at his reflection. He searched the reflection. Before, he would look for the fine line between the gimmick and the man, the character and the wrestler. Nowadays, he just looked for himself. He stared into his own eyes. Not the way a lover would gaze, or a friend would casually glance... but the way a scientist would study a freak. He then drives him to the mat with an elevated reverse DDT. Perfect Game. Slugger makes the cover, sure that after all this time, Austin Davis' career is finally coming to an end. One. Two. Kickout. Davis somehow gathers the strength to stay alive, and Slugger is too spent to curse in frustration. He lays back on the mat, laying parallel to Davis, who is also down and out. Davis lays in place, his eyes blinking, as does Slugger, blood still oozing down his face and covering him in perhaps the prototypical crimson mask. The referee begins to count. One. Two. Three. Four. Five. Six. Seven. Eight. Slugger gets to his feet. Nine. Davis gets to his feet. The two men stand face to face, and in that moment even the crowd is silent. The two legends stand face to face. Eye to eye. In that moment, it is clear that this has been brewing, first below the surface, then bubbling violently into sight, since the very first moment the fWo roster convened for the first time. In this moment, it is clear that these two men are feeling a depth of hatred for each other that cannot be fully comprehended except to be a part of it. For the final time, Austin Davis and Scott Slugger look each other in the eyes. Their eyes burn like the fires of hell itself, burning into each others' souls, burning a hole that each man knows will remind long beyond the ring, long beyond the careers, until the very day that they die. Then, the moment is gone, blowing away like dust in the wind. The emotion is gone. All that remains are two very tired and hurt men, two men fighting for their careers, two men fighting for their very lives. Austin Davis throws the first punch, connecting with the head of Scott Slugger and snapping it back, sending sweat and blood flying into the air. Slugger takes the blow and absorbs it, then fires one of his own back, then another, then another, knocking Davis into the ropes. Slugger whips Davis across, but in his tiredness, making the fatal mistake of putting his head down. Davis stops short in front of him and double-underhooks his head, going for the Deadly Game. Slugger, however, double-leg-trips his way out of it, dropping Davis to the mat. He leans over and begins hammering at the head of Davis. Finally he stops, only after Davis as well is bleeding, his blood spilling as Slugger's does. Slugger brings Davis to his feet and lifts Davis up onto his shoulders, trying to put an end to Davis' career in a bitterly ironic way, with a Crucifixion of his own. Davis, however, flips backward, landing on his feet, with Slugger in position. He drops down. Crucifixion. Slugger is driven into the mat, and Austin Davis knows that he is on the verge of ending his enemy's career it is so close he can taste it. He brings Slugger up, looking to put the finishing touches on the career of the man he despises so. He catches Slugger below the belt with a low blow, adding insult to injury, doubling him over. He then positions Slugger and prepares to lift him up for the Dead End. He lifts Slugger up and shrugs up him further, extending him. Austin Davis is only seconds away from executing the move that will end the career of Scott Slugger. Scott Slugger senses that his career hangs in the balance. Out of desperation and a drive to survive, he slips out of Davis' grip, landing on his feet behind Davis. Davis turns. Slugger leaps and double-underhooks his head in one move. He then spins and drives Davis to the mat. Curveball. Cover. One. Two. Three. After three years and eleven months, the career of Austin Davis has come to an end. The career of Scott Slugger will still go on. Slugger has his arm raised by the referee, and he stumbles back into the corner, bloody, semi-conscious, and absolutely spent. He can hear the cheers of the crowd, and knows that he will hear them once again. Austin Davis, also bloody, also semi-conscious, also absolutely spent, simply uses the last of his meager supply of remaining energy to roll from the ring and stagger to the back. Though it would be easier on his body to crawl, he permits himself this last indulgence of pride. They may have ended his career, but they never brought him to his knees. In the ring, Scott Slugger stands triumphant. He lives to fight again. Austin Davis does not. fWo cameras capture for the final time the face of Austin Davis, who goes out in his trademark style by flipping the camera the finger. Slaughter, then, goes off the air on the image of Scott Slugger... bloody, but victorious. His career continues. Result: Scott Slugger by pinfall
