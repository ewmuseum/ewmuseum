Welcome to Afghanistan As William surveyed the bleak desert from the bumpy confines of the noisy humvee, he thought back on how it had come to pass that they were here, halfway around the world in war-torn Afghanistan. He was tired but optimistic and even miles upon miles of uncharted territory did not remove the smile, however thin, from his face. A little over a month ago he had received a call from the marketing agency he had contracted to look for PR opportunities. The FWO had struggled in the wake of their reputation. The events of 2005 were still too close to them all, and they needed to show the industry their good will, good intentions, and thoughtful planning. He was not his father, but he knew a good idea when he heard it. A Tribute To The Troops show, just the kind of thing the WWE used to such success... except better. A live broadcast, filmed early in the morning to meet the timeslot back in the United States. Broadcast from a location that was secretive... certainly more dangerous. Yet he had been assured that the safety of the wrestlers would be assured, that in fact, their hosts were overjoyed that they were coming. That the very symbol of them being there would be huge to their enemies. William smiled and shook free the jet lag. Despite it all, he was excited. It had been a long plane ride; from Seattle they had crossed the country to Washington D.C., and from the states had flown to UAI and finally arrived very late in the day at Kabul International Airport in Kabul. They spent the night in the capital, the boarded a regional plane to what amounted to a dirt runway outside the district of Shah Vali Kowt. Their hosts were local; war weary and quiet. The region was dangerous and there was an air of nervous energy as they boarded the small set of humvees, old vehicles with patches of rust flaked paint in route to the base where they would be performing. William looked at the patched seats and jolting accommodations. He sighed. It was ridiculous the way the US Government poorly funded the equipment for the men and women serving the country. Perhaps Obama would do better here; this was a disgrace. He looked over at Ted Tallison, who sat in silent contemplation looking out at the desert. "I respect these guys, but man... there is nothing that would get me to live here." Ted nodded and shuffled between Geoff and Kristen. Brian Cage, Olson, Kagos, Chris Finn, Armstrong, Collins, Brandi McCloud, Chronos and Roberts were all stuffed into this humvee with the wrestlers in the caravan of cars following. "It's harsh terrain. Makes you respect what we have," said Roberts, also looking out of the bumpy windows at the stark land around them. "Makes you respect these soldiers even more." Kristen nodded. "We're fortunate... these men are fighting a terrible enemy. People who don't care about innocent women and children for their... their jihad. It's just awful." "You ask me," said Ken Armstrong, "they should just nuke this whole place. Not like anyone would notice." Geoff shook his head. "Not that simple. Besides..." Geoff paused as they all looked out the windows. The humvee was slowing down; after thousands of miles and endless hours in transport they were here. But rather than look exhausted, they were energized. "We're here," said William. "I can't wait to get out there and shake the hands of the troops." Brandi grinned wide. "I know it isn't recommended, but if I get the chance I'm going to give one of those brave men a little extra deployment... if you know what I mean!" "Wow," said Jacobs, ignoring the conversation, "The conditions look absolutely terrible. I mean, I know the military isn't funded like it should be, but it looks like these guys are living out of caves." William shook his head. "Rumsfeld... Guantanamo's too good for him. Come on, let's get out there. I can hear the troops from in here... can you hear them?" "Yeah," said Green, "They know we're coming. They're chanting Obama. Good to know the international community is behind our new President." The humvee came to a stop and they all looked out together. It was a bleak camp; little more than battered tents, sand, dirt and rocks. Men walked around dressed in desert garb; robes and dirty packs with rifles slung over their backs. They looked tired, but also excited and expectant. They were all staring at the vehicles and the chant grew louder. "Wow," said Olson, "I know it's not politically correct to say so... but there are like... no white people anywhere. The military must really just focus their recruitment on minorities." A goat walked by, led by a man who scratched his beard with one hand and carried a strap of explosive dynamite with the other. Behind him a woman in full burka led a boy over to a group of children who were throwing rocks at a burning van. Roberts frowned. "These conditions are really awful for our military." Ted exchanged a quick glance with Geoff. Outside the humvee, the chanting grew louder still and was punctuated with occasional gunshots into the air. "Yeah... I don't think they're chanting Obama." "Will...," Kristen was looking at William, fear bordering on complete panic in her eyes. "Will... which troops are we doing a live tribute for?" William looked out the window as his driver opened the door and gestured them outside. The chant broke down into a huge cheer with even more random gunshots into the air. Behind them the other humvees had stopped and wrestlers were emerging, looks of confused panic on their faces. William caught sight of the Flying Frenchie, who held a hand to his forehead and was shaking his head. "American sports wrestling people! Welcome to El Katan Mosaad, camp of the freedom fighters of Afghanistan!" A dark skinned man with a thick beard and desert camouflage was walking toward them smiling broadly. "I see you are alarmed, but do not be worried, I know the English. We are so glad that you came to here for showings the Great Satan America of your allegiance to our cause... you are very brave to stand out against your government, and perhaps will be spared in the fire of the afterlife that will consume your heathenistic kind for that is the will of Muhammad. The man bowed quickly, then shifted his rifle on his back and extended his hand. William shook it slowly, not completely in control of himself. "But," said Brandi, "we're here to do a Tribute To The Troops." "We are," said Ted. "only problem... wrong troops." They looked around at the stark Taliban controlled region, the dirty camp and cliff wall that was spotted with caves, the thousands of men with rifles standing in front of and around the makeshift ring and in the background the tattered American flag that was draped over the burning car. Kagos folded his arms and shook his head. "Aw, shit." Screwed William paced in the sand, kicking up small dust clouds, his brow furrowed. The tent was small and the roster was packed inside.
