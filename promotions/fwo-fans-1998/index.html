Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Fans Wrestling Organization, styled fWo with a lower-case f and o, opened at CyberSlam on 24 April 1998, where a forty-man over-the-top battle royal crowned Jim Dudley its first World Champion. Wednesday Warfare was its weekly from that first summer and Sunday Slaughter joined it that autumn; around them it staged its pay-per-views — Extreme Measures, Numbered Days, Meltdown, Mob Rules, Apocolypse Now, Battle Bowl and Twilight in its first year — with CyberSlam back every spring and, from 1999, a three-night Countdown closing every year.

It kept its own history as it went: a list of every event, year by year, and title histories for eight championships — the World, International, Internet, United States, Tag Team, Cruiserweight, Hardcore and Women’s. Its write-ups changed with it, from the announcers’ transcripts and short reports of its first years to chaptered show pages, and 646 of them survive, from its first card to its last.

On 6 May 2001 it set its Sunday show aside for a memorial to a longtime member of the federation who had died that week. The night opened with the federation’s own tribute, a ten-bell salute and ten seconds of silence, gave the rest of the evening to the words of his friends among its handlers, and ran four matches in his honour; in the last of them both men wrestled in Lone Gunmen T-shirts.

From 2003 its weeklies were Monday Night Ruahh~! and Uprising, the next year under Disney’s name, beside Survivor IV, a fourteen-episode season of 2004. Its first run ended with the third night of Countdown on 31 December 2005. It came back on 7 January 2009 at thefwo.com, with reAction as its weekly, a CyberSlam on 29 March and Legends IV in September, and its record stops with the third night of Countdown 2009, on 31 December 2009.

Crossover booking with fWo (1998) (2001-03)
The two leagues staged a crossover in March 2001: two fWo wrestlers on the CWL's Thursday Night Titans, which the fWo's own commentary calls the first CWL/fWo crossover, and CWL wrestlers on the fWo's Sunday Slaughter of 18 and 25 March 2001.
