The Taking of Team Extreme - 10/10

15 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The Taking of Team Extreme Chapter Ten January 27th, 2004 Somewhere on the Road 4:22 PM local time 'You know that it would be untrue... you know that I would be a liar... if I was to say to you... girl we couldn't get much higher... c'mon baby, light my fire...' The Doors were on the system, and the Road stretched out for miles. It was exactly the same as it had been five months ago, the last time Eli Flair was involved in the professional wrestling business. Only, everything was different. The Heroes of old had become complacent and jaded. The Heroes of old had let their rage overpower their responsibility to the fans, and the Heroes of old had allowed their destiny to become corroded. Brand Frontier had allowed his rage to overpower his sense of duty as he dispatched with Kodiak Vic Creed and currently dealt with Kellen Kinkade's assault on his person and Crusader's assault on his girl. Lance Knight had been injured again, and an arrogant, overconfident, overblown, second-rate Mark Windham had taken his place. Deacon had become distracted once again by Faith Hannum. Fool him once, shame on you. Fool him twice, shame on Deac. He had also formed a tag team with Craig Miles, something a Hero simply does not do. It was the way it had always been - the Heroes fight the good fight while Eli Flair, the Anti-Hero, got his hands dirty. Maybe it was unfair of him to expect more, ethically, out of the Heroes than he was willing to give, but the tradeoff was always the same - the Heroes were the ones to wear the belt. You want a hero to be your Champion, not an anti-hero. That's why Eli allowed himself to be the sacrificial lamb at Meltdown - to allow the spotlight to shift from himself to Brand, as the man to chase down Creed. He thought for a moment, about a year ago, and where things stood in the fWo. The 'Rat Pack' was quickly on its way to becoming a locker room force to be reckoned with, based on a complete lack of patience for politics. Brand was on his way to the United States Championship, Lance and Trip were rewriting the rules for a successful tag team feud, Aimz had begun a war with Martyr that went nowhere due to Martyr's apathy, and Eli himself was embroiled in a backstage war with Erik Kelly. It was the best of times. The actual in-ring product was the highest it had ever been at the time for those five, and the best part was that none of them cared about in-ring glory, as long as they had a good time on the road. One by one, it seemed they all fell apart at one time or another. Eli's hands tensed on his steering wheel. He thought about Ivy McGinnis, and the way he could tell from the sound of her voice that she was inching ever closer to a nervous breakdown from the rules and restrictions Disney had placed her under. Should he have told her what he'd planned? No. She'd want to be a part of it, but he couldn't accept the responsibility for what might happen to her career. Disney was a large, powerful corporation that didn't care a whit for the business, as their current stench on the shows has proven. Also, Eli had absolutely no idea what it was he was going to do - he simply knew that desperate times called for desperate measures. Whatever it was he was going to do at Body Count, he knew it would have to make a very significant impact, he knew that it would make the actual FANS realize there was still some life left in this company. And he knew that it would be an ugly ride. Some fans would understand - most wouldn't. But it was walking that line that defined who he was and how he lived. Some of the boys would understand as well - but most wouldn't. He'd have to live with that. He'd also have to live without his family for the forseeable future. Angel was about to embark on a tour that would test the limits of her patience, her sanity, and her ability to work with the rest of the band. Eli remembered how frayed everyone's nerves were in the fWo between January and April of 2003 and their forced exodus from the United States. Valerian's Garden was about to spend ten months abroad, from Australia to the Far East to Asia to Africa, to Europe in time for all the summer festivals, and would not be back on American soil until December 1st. Team Extreme was more than a clever marketing ploy or a team name that he and Randy Harders came up with for the Multinational Wrestling Corporation. Team Extreme was both Eli Flair himself and his extended family - from Poison Ivy to the Garden to the kids back home. And the Taking of Team Extreme was a hard pill for a proud man to swallow. Things began for him at the front door of his Hollywood condo, home for two and a half years, but somehow it wasn't the same. When he first left New York, it was in the wake of the Fall of the Towers, and the heartbeat of the city seemed dead. Los Angeles - specifically, Hollywood - was as far away from New York as he could get. And he seemed to enjoy the feel of moral decay around him, as if the world was outwardly projecting how he felt inside his soul. And then he met Angel. And he knew he had come to California for a reason. But something tugged at him - something that kept bringing him back to New York. Be it the way April and Johnny had hit it off and kept his old building alive and filled with noise at all hours, be it the new generation of kids going through Coops-- be it Coop himself, who was more full of life at 83 than most were at 21... the City refused to die. Ivy had refused to let it die. That night at Coop's... even after Luty had tried to murder everything in the world that meant anything to him, the way the kids kept going was a sign that New York was alive and well, and far from danger. That was home. That was safe. That was where he knew, he would always have someone watching his back. There would be no backup on this tour, there would be nobody reassuring him that he was doing the right thing. Team Extreme was taken, and the Soul Survivor was in a fighting mood. After ten years, the Road treated him like any old friend would. It left him alone.
