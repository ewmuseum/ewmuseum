I went to Rock n Roll Heaven, and I wasn't on the Guest List...

18 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Wires get crossed. Tempers flare. And a world away, a band on the verge of making it big is about to embark on the tour of a lifetime. A cell phone connects those scattered across the globe in an intricate web of networks, signal towers, and unique combinations of digits. Luckily, some don't worry about semantics. The first message was very simple. "Hey Lindz, it's Ivy. I heard through the grapevine you're gonna be in Australia on business in a few days. If you have any spare time, c'mon and say hello!" The second was just as simple. "Hey Ivy, it's Lindsay. Word travels fast, doesn't it?! I'll give you all the juicy details later, but I'd love to meet up and take in the show. Give me details." And thus, two friends connect. "Yeah, she's with the band," said Ivy to the disinterested woman sitting in the box office, "Lindsay Troy. Mark'er off the list and give'er a sticker." As Ivy handed the Queen of the Ring her all-access laminate, she gave her a quick hug. "Glad you could make it, the guys and girl're just runnin' a check right now. What's been goin on?" Lindsay Troy slid the lanyard down around her neck and fixed her long curly hair so it hung down her back once again. "To make a long story short, my sister got attacked at the A1E PPV while Marcus was fighting in an IronMan match. She's fine though; my aunt and uncle are staying with her for awhile. I've got no real proof as to who did it, but I know Jack and the Empire were behind it. One of the other crazy fools running around A1E was suspect, but the style wasn't his M.O. Besides, he was the one fighting Marcus when Alaina got attacked. Then I found out while I was at the hospital with Alaina that one of the Empire guys, Max, is my blood cousin. Needless to say, I wasn't a happy camper and I went to Houston and let him know that...and then he let me know that I was suspended for a week. She slides her hands into her pocket and sighs. "Damnit, Ive... no one screws with my family. People can beat the crap out of me and I can get back up and keep going. Alaina's got no training, no way of defending herself. She gets attacked and everyone's up in arms, and it all comes down on my head. Sometimes I don't know how to deal with things, I'm only one person and only one person can do so much." "It's never easy," replied Ivy, "but Elijah and I have followed one simple rule for the past decade now...keep family away from the business." As they walked through the main entranceway, the sound of a squealing guitar cut through the air, as well as a loud cry of "Goddamnitt!" brought their attention up. Ivy pushed the door open for Lindz, as the Garden put together their set for the evening's show. "And," continued Ivy, "you need to know when to push the stress outta your head. We've got a food and drink setup in the back, you need anything?" "Water, please. I'm parched." Ivy tosses her a water. Troy unscrews the cap and takes a long swig before swallowing. "Funny thing is, Lanni wasn't even supposed to be there. She came to tell me about Max and got blindsided. I didn't even know she was in the building." Ivy shook her head. "See, that's just wrong. We're in the business, we take the risks. Someone who isn't in the business shouldn't have to deal with it. There's a difference between occupational hazards and assault." She smiled. "Troy!" shouted a female voice from the stage, "This cord is fried!" "Troy's the bus driver-slash-vox roadie," explained Ivy, as Lindsay Troy looked toward the band. Troy laughed, "I was going to say that I'm not very handy with electrical malfunctions. All I know is that if you can't duct it, fuck it." The female voice suddenly had a face, as a pale, black-haired woman hopped to the floor and headed toward Ivy and Lindsay. "Ivy," she said, "Nine needs a hand with the media setup... something keeps shorting out and he can't find it." "K, Angel," replied Ivy, "Be right back, Lindz. Talk amongst yourselves." As Ivy headed towards the stage, Angel looked intently at Lindsay. "I know you," she said, "but I can't think of where." "Eli and Ivy were at the GWE show a month or so ago," Lindsay started, "that's where I met them for the first time. I don't know if you caught the show or not, but I was there." She extends her hand. "Lindsay Troy. Pleasure to meet you, Ivy speaks the world of you and the band." "I was watching that show," replied Angel, "Call me Angel, please." She rubbed the dark circles under her eyes. "You look tired... you've got enough energy to stay for the show, right?" "Yeah, I'll be fine," Troy said with a grin, "I usually run on about 3-5 hours sleep with all the travelling I do. Besides, you're the one who looks tired. How's your baby? I hear she's going to be making a lot of boys cry once she grows up. And probably making them cry too with Eli for a father." She gives Angel a wink. Angel's face lit up. "Beautiful. She just turned thirteen months." Her eyes drifted away for a moment. "C'mon, will show you around. Need anything?" "I'll grab a sandwich in a bit, thank you for offering. The execs I met with took me out for lunch after the meeting. They weren't so bad outside the conference room, but I think my Board of Directors got ahold of them before I touched down and told them not to make me irritated given the family trouble I've had this week." Angel shook her head. "I hate the corporate side of things... Ivy's a livesaver. Seriously, anyone who goes into a boardroom or something like that, and deals with paperwork and procedure and all that shit... might not always respect the goals but I respect the work. Just gimmie a mic and my boys and I'm good to go." She looked down at herself - dressed in a sleeveless tank top and cutoff cargo pants. "Some Gothic Diva, hmmm?" "You pull it off better than I ever could. I'm too tall and too tan. It's the Mediterranian blood, I tell you. Although I could do the black eyeliner with no problem." She laughs. "To tell you the truth, corporate isn't my thing either. If I had it my way, I'd stay completely hands-off for the rest of my life. I'd let my sister run the family business and take care of the day-to-day affairs. I'm not happy holed up in an office, I need to travel and see things and be active. I spend more time with the road than I do with my family, but Alaina knows that I can't sit still for more than two seconds. Of the two of us, I was always the more free-spirited. But I know that once I've decided to hang my boots up for good I've got a responsibility." Pausing to take another drink, she continues, "And so it goes. We take the roads less travelled by, and that makes all the difference, hm?" "Y'know," beamed Angel, "I've got nothin' but respect for professional wrestlers... but even with that, I can still count, on one hand, the number of you all that've quoted poetry that you don't usually hear in a bar. Bless you!" She gestured to the stage. "C'mon... will introduce you to the boys, and MJ. When do you need to go be corporate again?" "I'm all done, just had that one meeting and it went shorter than I expected it to. Just got to put the call in back to Tampa about the figures and the proposals, but I'll do that later. I'd love to meet everyone, and Robert Frost is my favorite poet. Well, him and John Donne." "Well then," replied Angel, "we have lots to speak of. And I think I may just have a few books on the bus that you might be interested in. Shall we?" Troy nodded, and the two women saundered over to the stage, laughing as if they had been friends forever. The dark clouds rolling across the horizon have begun to part. Soon, the sunshine will burst through. And everything will be alright.
