12/29/2001 6:00AM The first faint rays of morning light crept softly across the California sky, silently signaling the arrival of a new day. Below, a few trucks moved along the streets, most of them making deliveries to the yet-to-open shops that were scattered around the city. On a street corner, a man in his forties passed out newspapers to the few people who were out walking, and every now and then a jogger hustled past and nodded or waved to another running in the opposite direction. The Boston Strangler stood on his small balcony, which barely fit one person standing with no room for a chair, and sipped at a cup of bad coffee. The little coffee maker in the room could brew up to 4 cups, but the small bags of ground beans that were provided were hardly of high quality. He contemplated heading over to the small coffee shop down below and across the street, but decided to suffer through what he had for now. Still, his mood wasn't very good at the moment. He was a multiple former champion, an accomplished wrestler, a judge of fine wines, an avid chess player, a student of eastern philosophy... yet all of that couldn't hide one fact: He wasn't a morning person. His grumpy mood, however sour, was dampened even more by the recent events of the night before. As he glanced down on the street again, he caught sight of the phone booth... which reminded him of the meeting at the bar... which reminded him of what he had learned when he returned to the hotel. While he had no proof of anything, the Strangler wanted some answers... and soon. Perhaps what had happened to Darryl Bailey was a chance occurrence, as the police seemed to suspect. Maybe there wasn't much of a connection... but he did know that certain people didn't like anyone poking around in their affairs, and could react violently when provoked. "Nowadays, people are too easy to provoke, though" he thought to himself. "It could have been anyone... anyone at all." His suspicions continued to grow, however, and the nagging feeling (Anxiety? Guilt?) accompanied them. There had to be a sure-fire way to ease his mind on the matter. "Maybe we should go to see him," he said to no one in particular as he swirled the last of the murky looking coffee around in the cup. "Someone here must know where he's at... maybe he could let us know something...anything..." "It's worth a try at least."
