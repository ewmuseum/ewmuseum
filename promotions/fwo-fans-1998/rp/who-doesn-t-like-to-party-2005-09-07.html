Who Doesn't Like To Party

7 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Dakota wasn't a stranger to being slapped around. As a feature dancer, she'd had her share of grabby customers, guys who would follow her out to her car after the show and try to cop a feel. Some wouldn't even wait until she got off the stage and would just stand up and grab whatever she had closest to them. Rarely did things get very rough, but it did happen on occasion. She was not prepared for, well, whatever it was that had happened to her. Dakota struggled against the ropes that tied her to her chair. The bindings bit into her wrists, the friction burning away at her skin. Her head was throbbing and she found it very painful to swallow. Probably from when the guy, whoever he was, grabbed her by the throat as she left the club. He attacked her from behind, reached around, and choked her unconscious before she could even struggle. That took a special kind of skill, but Dakota wasn't going to try and wrap her head around who had done this. All she wanted to do was get out of the dark, scary room she was stuck in. A single light hung over Dakota's head, shining down on her and preventing her from seeing too far in the darkness. She twisted from side to side, causing the chair's wooden legs to drag across the concrete floor. The screech didn't really echo, so she had to be in a smaller room. With the light overhead, she couldn't see if there were any walls nearby. She couldn't make out anything. The boot slamming into her chest caught her completely by surprise. Her chest was on fire, and Dakota immediately knew one of her breast implants had exploded. The remnants of her D-cup spilled to the side, unsupported. The chair fell backward and the back of her head smacked against the hard concrete floor. It hurt worse than the time she had a beer bottle bust over her head in a bar down in New Mexico. If it wasn't for the light that now shone into her face, she'd probably be seeing stars. She was in pain. She was scared. But all that went away when she saw her ex-husband step into the light. "God damn it Vic! Untie me!" Kodiak Vic Creed took off his sunglasses and looked down at his ex-wife. "Where's Sarah?" Dakota was still having a little trouble focusing. That made it easier for her to hide her shocked surprise. "I don't... I don't know what you're talking about. She's dead." Vic knelt down beside his ex, resting his hand on hers while he brushed red locks of hair out of her face. His fingers felt calloused and warm against Dakota's forehead.
