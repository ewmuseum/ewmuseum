One Last Chance.

24 February 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
fWo: Fans Wrestling Organization One Last Chance. February 24, 2000 The camera captured it all from afar. Doctors walked to and fro throughout the area. Several receptionists took down information from incoming patients; some of who were suffering from life threatening injuries, while a few here and there were suffering from minor ones that required merely a brace or several stitches. One of the victims looked as if he had been through a war, with a gunshot wound to the face and the crimson colored plasma oozing all over. Another of the victims looked as if she had taken a fall, and a simple cast would make it all better. Another victim resembled a schizophrenic, as he held onto his wrist with traces of blood spurting out every so often as he mumbled to himself about nothing in particular. In the center of the room sat Dead, who looked at everyone and everything and seemingly photographed every major or minor detail inside his head for future reference. After doing so, Dead stood up and walked towards the camera, before exiting the emergency room for the peaceful atmosphere outside of the large hospital. A flag stood high above the ground, and Dead strolled along the green grass up near it, before sitting at it's base which was about a foot and a half off the ground. The camera faded in with a shot of the sadistical madman, who had a calm expression drenched across his face. Soon he spoke, for he had a lot to talk about. Let me give you something to chew on, Chris. I'm going to give you one last chance. One last chance to take it all back. One last chance to back out of our match this Saturday. You saw all of those people. You saw the man with the gunshot wound, and the blood covering his body as he lay still on the gurney. You saw the young teenage girl, who walked with a slight limp and looked like she required crutches. You saw the other people in there, the elderly gentlemen gasping for air because he simply forgot to take a pill this morning. You saw the police officer with a riddle of bullet holes throughout his body because the stupid son of a bitch risked his life in a high speed chase. You saw the fireman who just earlier today was trying to put out a fire when a backdraft occured and he nearly became a walking fireball. Don't tell me none of that affected you. Don't tell me that what you saw didn't make you think twice about stepping into that ring with me on Saturday. You know, and I know, that I'm capable of inflicting a lot more damage than what occured to those people in there. I won't need a gun to do it. I won't need a flamethrower. We both know that I am the most violent, sadistic & just pure evil man not only the the fWo has ever witnessed, but the entire wrestling industry as well. When I took Jim Dudley out in 1998, believe me, he hurt. When I took David Spencer out that same year, believe me, he was in pain. When I nearly crippled you at the beginning of 1999, you can't tell me that you didn't have second thoughts about ever returning to wrestling. When I maimed nearly everyone in this entire organization, you knew just like everyone else that I would go a long way to make someone feel pain. Even if it meant hurting someone else in the process. Even if it meant hurting someone I actually gave a damn about in the process. Even if it meant hurting myself just to get from point A to point B, I would do it. And I did. If you thought it would be any different this Saturday, you thought wrong. Because whether I end up hurting a referee this Saturday; whether I end up hurting my own flesh and blood this Saturday, whether I end up hurting myself this Saturday, one way or another, I will hurt you as well. Nothing pleases me more than to witness another human being in pain. Nothing. This Saturday I will bring you pain. I will bring you mayhem. I will bring you violence. I will use every weapon imagineable to hurt you. If a cup of water can inflict some harm on you, I'm gonna use it. If a Crayola Magic Marker can make you bleed, by God, I'll take the cap off and stab you with it. If a feather from a bird will make you scream in pain, I will go to the nearest farm in St. Louis and pluck a feather from a bird and bring it back and shove it up your nose and pull it out your ear. But being the kind, well-educated gentleman that I am, I know sometimes not everyone gets a chance to go back and take back what they did and start anew. That's why I'm giving you the opportunity now. If you want to, go ahead, back out of the match. I wouldn't blame you, the fans wouldn't blame you, the wrestlers wouldn't blame you, and I'm sure your family and friends wouldn't blame you. Heck, if they actually give a piss about you, they might even feel glad that you backed out. It would be a smart decision on your part. You'd be a hero. A cult figure. I'd even admit defeat. I'll forfeit if you want me to. You can win the match by default. Just think about all the pain you're going to go through. Think about the long-term effects. If you don't wrestle me on Saturday, you'll have another five-to-ten years left in your career. If you do wrestle me on Saturday, it could be the last time you ever step foot in a wrestling ring. If *I'm* lucky, it might be the last time you ever walk. What I want you to do right now, Chris, is think about it very carefully. Talk it over with your family and friends. Think about what it might be like never to walk again. Think about what it might be like to be taken to an emergency room because of all the pain you went through. Think about what it might be like to have to have a nurse go to the restroom with you because you can't even lift your own [censored] to take a piss. Think about what it might be like to spend the next five years of your life in and out of hospitals going through operation after operation, rehabilitation session after rehabilitation session, check-up after check-up... rather than what it might be like if you back out of the match. If that's not enough to get you to back up, well... this might be. I'm sure you and Lance still speak to one another regularly. And I'm sure he's told you all about what it's like to sit in the ring for thirty minutes after getting injured, not knowing if he'll ever wrestle again let alone walk. I'm sure he's told you about the month and a half he couldn't move an inch because of his injury. I'm sure he's made you aware of the fact that he's spent well over three hundred hours of rehabilitation. I'm sure he's made you aware that a good portion of the last four months of his life has been going from hospital to hospital. I'm positive he's spoke about how fortunate he feels to be able to breathe without the assistance of a machine. I'm sure he's spoke about how lucky he feels to be able to move around on his own accord rather than to be pushed around in a wheelchair. Ask him, Chris. Ask him what it feels like to endure what he's endured. Ask him what he feels every morning when he wakes up. Ask him if he still feels the pain, if his back feels weak before he goes to sleep or if his neck is too tight when he rises on any given day. If that's still not enough, well... you're dumber than I thought. But I'll give you some more food for thought. Ask Lance what it felt like that fateful night last November. Ask him what went through his mind after Collins fucked up and dropped him on his head. Ask him if he thought if he would ever move from that ring. Go ahead, Chris. Have him tell you everything. Every detail. And if you're still willing to wrestle me on Saturday, Chris, you have more guts than brains. So, I want you to picture this. I want you to imagine what I'm about to describe for you like it's already happened. I want you to picture it as if it were a movie of you, and you were far away, watching it. It's more than a dream because you can feel it. You're there. There's no turning back now, Chris. It's Saturday night, and our match ended just hours earlier. You're in a hospital bed, and you can't talk. Why? YOU HAVE NO TEETH! The doctor is trying to tell you something, but you cannot hear him very clearly. Why? You lost so much damn blood that some of it trickled into your ears and they've been clogged ever since. You can't think as clearly as you once did. Why? I took a chair and slammed it so many damn times against your head that thousands upon thousands of your brain cells died an untimely death. You can't move very well. Why? Because unlike Collins, I didn't fuck up on a move and leave you temporarily paralyzed, I tried to leave you paralyzed. If you still want to wrestle me on Saturday after all that, so be it. Because there are two hundred and six bones in the human body, and if I have to break every single damn one of yours twice to make sure you never wrestle again, I will do it. You have two eyeballs, and if I have to rip them from your eye sockets and hold them in my hand so you can watch me while I kick your ass around the Kiel Center, I will do it. You have one tongue, but if I have to cut it and remove it from the very throat that it rests in and use it to slap some sense into you, I will do it. Ask yourself if risking your life is worth it to wrestle me Saturday in hopes of ending the war between you and I. Ask yourself if ending up in a hospital permanently is worth it. Who will be there for you? The fans couldn't give a piss about you. They won't be there to push you around in a wheelchair. They won't be there to take you down to the hospital cafeteria and watch you suck down some food through a straw. They won't be there to help you into the bathroom so you don't make a mess out of yourself. Nobody will. And if that's the life you want, that's the life you will get. And after Saturday, I don't ever want to hear you say I never gave you anything. Dead stood up from underneath the flag and simply walked away as the scene ended abruptly with a preview of Trapped.
