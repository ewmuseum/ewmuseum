Reacting to reAction - Ground Zero in T-Minus Four

3 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It was raining, so I grabbed my jacket before I left the apartment. Technically it was Sunday, but it was three thirty in the morning so it didn't count yet. I just needed a drink. Five stops uptown and I walked the half block to TC's. Fenix was on the door and he started to tell me that last call was already called, and that I needed to go somewhere else, but of course, three seconds later he let me in. The place was packed. Rosie and the two new bartenders were behind the bar, doing their best to fill everyone's last orders for the night. I slid over to the side, farthest away from the register and liquor bottles. Nobody ever ordered from here so I was able to get right up on the bar and wait. I really just needed some human contact. Lou and Sally were spending the weekend at her place, and Rosie was working a double, so I'd spent most of the day living and breathing Killjoy. Try it for yourself and see if you start hearing things. "Hey," said Rosie, as she finally had a second to come say hello, and kissed me on the lips, "Get you something?" No, I said, I'm good. Just wanted to see you. She smiled, the smile that could melt your heart, and went back to work. I sat in silence for a while, watching the rush of people gradually dwindle. There was a good mix tonight of regulars from the neighborhood, newcomers who passed by and looked to give a new place a chance, and newcomers who stopped in because they're wrestling fans. I could always tell the third group from the rest: they were always trying to engage the bartenders in conversation during the busiest times, and even when it was a group of guys talking to one of the girls, they never seemed to think to flirt with her. Takes all kinds. "I need a vacation," said Rose. She leaned against the bar opposite me and took a long drink of water from the pint glass against the wall, "How's your day?" Draining, I said. "You're really gettin' ready for this one, aren't you?" asked Rose. This one is different, I said. I really can't explain it that well, he's just... "Different," said Rose, finishing my thought. Yeah, I said. "Different like Sars?" asked Rose, with a chuckle. I joined her, since she was right - I had faced off against a supposedly unstoppable 'monster' once before. No, I said, SARS was a clown and turned out to be self - propelled hype and attention withdrawal syndrome masking limited ring skills and big floppy clown shoes. Killjoy is different. Rose offered me some of her drink and I took it. Killjoy is on a mission from god, I said, so to speak. Just, his mannerisms, his words, his presence all speak about someone who doesn't care about professional wrestling in the little world sense, and that's pretty unique right now. "Rosie," said one of the other bartenders. I didn't know her name yet. "That guy with the Mets cap says he has a running tab, but Ivy doesn't allow that. What should I do?" Rose looked in his direction. No worries, it was Mr. Skoloff. He's a regular, and Miss Ivy takes care of him. "He's cool," said Rose, "I'll handle him." Newbies, I said, laughing. "Exactly," said Rose, "What did you mean, small world?" Small world? What did I mean? I mean he doesn't care about win/loss records or title shots or standings or where on the card he's working, I said, That's the little world of wrestling. I've been looking at as much as I can find on him, and he cares more - you could say solely - about the big world issues. Where the company is going, who's in charge, and what sort of prep they're doing to make sure what happened before won't happen again. We are free because of him, the saying goes. "Maybe he just believes so much in what he's fighting for that he doesn't want who he is to cloud it," suggested Rose. The Messenger Is Not Important. I can get behind that, I said, but I can't support his means. There's ways to get a message across without trying to bloody everyone in your path. "Case in point?" asked Rose. Exactly, I said. About half the crowd that was here when I showed up had left, and the jukebox was off. Rose pulled her iPod out of her back pocket and flipped through the catalog. "Here," said Rose, "this is just for you." She plugged it into the loose wire hidden behind the autographed picture of Black Label Society, and The Offspring filled the bar. Thanks, I said. I'll wait for you.
