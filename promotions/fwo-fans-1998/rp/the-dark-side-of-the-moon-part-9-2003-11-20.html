The Dark Side of the Moon - part 9

20 November 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
THE DARK SIDE OF THE MOON Part 9 - Eclipse All you create and all you destroy and all that you do and all that you say and all that you eat and everyone you meet and all that you slight and everyone you fight and all that is now and all that is gone and all that's to come and everything under the sun is in tune but the sun is eclipsed by the moon There's a thunderstorm raging outside, all the better for a foul mood. And of anyone in New York City right now, Poison Ivy has the best case for a foul mood. Her mother died tonight. Ivy is in her own apartment for the first time in over a week - she's spent her time either at the hospital or at her mother's place with her brother Brian. They finally fixed their strained relationship.... just in time to say goodbye to Mom. It's like a wreath of pretty flowers that smell really bad. She's wearing a white terrycloth bathrobe, drying her hair with a blue print towel - it's a bit darker than usual and is full of curls. Ivy sits down at her computer, mulling over her schedule this week. She needs to call the guys from Real Wrestling Video tomorrow and reschedule their planned shoot interview. She needs to call the CSWA offices and let them know she's good to go to write her column for the new TRIBUNE format. And Eli. She needs to call Eli. And.... Sean. What's she going to say to Sean? Her eyes wander to her desk - wallet, keys, cell phone.... and an envelope. She's confused for a moment, but her mind drifts back to the hospital, and her brother handing her an envelope. From who? She opens it up and flips to the last page - and she puts her hand to her mouth and simultaneously smiles and starts to cry. Turning back to the first page.... she begins to read. My Dearest Ivy, If you're reading this, I guess I'm dead. Sorry. But I did have a pretty good run, huh? In spite of herself, Ivy can't surpress a slight laugh at this. I knew that would get a laugh out of you - it always did. Ivy, I'm worried. I know that this little fight between you and Brian really kicked up last year when your father died, but it has to stop. It won't end on its own, I know that much. Brian has Kim to tell him that he is in the right, and you have Eli to tell you that you are in the right. That's not going to make it easier on either one of you. But the difference is that at the end of the night, Brian and Kim go home together, and you go home alone. I can't advise you on what to do with Brian other than to tell you that it's worth it to just tell him he's right. You know how he holds grudges, but he lets things go as soon as he thinks he's right. My doctors are telling me that it is a waste of time for me to use this small window of consciousness to write about this, but it's the only area of your life that I can tell you are completely lost in. Find Sean, Ivy. Find him and tell him how you feel about him. Your father and I went through horrible periods of uncertainty before we finally figured out where we wanted to be, and I can see a lot of that in you. I realize how hard it will be, how hard the past few years have been on your personal life, but I can see how you look at Sean, and how he looks at you. Makes my heart all- a- flutter. Or maybe that's just the medication. I can see you laughing again. I'm glad. I don't want you or your brother feeling bad about this, you hear me? I might have only had fifty- some- odd years, but they were full. I had three wonderful children and a husband that I loved with all my heart. We had our own business. My son gave me four wonderful grandchildren, my oldest daughter married the most integrity- filled, most loyal man she could have found, and my youngest child, from what I hear, has inspired women the world over to be stronger and more sure of themselves. My only regret is that that same youngest child goes home alone every night instead of going home with the one she loves. Please make this old lady's last wish come to fruition - and at least tell the one you love how you feel. You have so much to offer, Ivy, and it would be a shame if you didn't tell him. Of the three of you, being Brian, Alicia, and yourself, I can't choose between my own children, but the fact that you do all that you do without help from anybody fills me with so much pride that I feel I'll burst. The least you could do is share this with someone. All my love, Mom Ivy put the letter down, still crying but with a big smile on her face. "I love you, Mom," she whispered to nobody in particular, "and I'll be happy, for you." Ivy walked to the floor- to- ceiling windows in her apartment, and stares out into the storm. There is no dark side of the moon, really. As a matter of fact, it's all dark. ~FIN~
