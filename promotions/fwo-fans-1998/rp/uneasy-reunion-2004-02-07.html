Uneasy Reunion

7 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Everything seemed to be shades of white and gray. A frosty fog dulled the color of the trees, blending the overcast sky with the snow-covered ground. Ashen tombstones poked out of the snow like a golem's fingers reaching for daylight. A slight breeze carried the cold and cut the exposed flesh of anyone foolish enough to be outside. Kodiak Vic Creed didn't budge. The cold didn't bother him. It didn't bother him years ago as he stood in the snow on an evening colder than today. He watched his daughter being taken away by authorities while he stood barefoot in the snow. Hours after she was gone, he still remained in the same spot. He lost his daughter and three toes to frostbite. A little redness on his face wasn't even a remote concern to him. "Hey pumpkin," Vic said as he stared at his daughter's tombstone. A picture of Sarah Creid was embedded in the granite. Her blue eyes seemed to look back at him, which made it easier to talk. Maybe, somewhere, she was watching him. Listening to him. Most likely hating him. "I never wanted it to end like this." Vic clenched his gloved hands into a tight fist. "Children bury their father, not the other way around. I wasn't about to let Darryl pull my strings. I never thought he'd actually go through with it." "You lying son of a bitch." Her words cut him worse than the cold wind did. Because they were true. "What are you doing here Dakota?" Vic asked as he closed his eyes. His sunglasses masked his action, but the redhead who now stood beside the Main Man didn't have to see his face. For a time, they'd shared a life. They created a life. The one snuffed out at their feet. Dakota jammed her hands into her pocket, not nearly accustomed to the cold as the burly Alaskan standing beside her. "I wasn't here for the funeral. I'm paying my respects to my daughter." "After thirteen years, you decide to see our little girl?" Vic said dryly. "That's mighty motherly of you." "I saw her plenty of times, you sanctimonious asshole. I was your sister's friend too, remember? You played uncle, I played auntie." Dakota stared at the picture of her teenaged daughter. "She was so beautiful." Vic nodded. "She was the most beautiful thing I'd ever seen." Dakota looked at her husband. "How could you put a title belt before her?" Vic turned to face his wife. "Dakota look at me. Do I look like a father to you? I'm not even a good husband, and I'm not sure how that little legality was overlooked by my lawyer, so why would I be good with any family structure?" Dakota turned back to their daughter. "You forgot to sign the divorce papers in one place, dummy. You should have double-checked the papers before you went and slept with your lawyer's wife to celebrate our separation." "Whatever." Vic grabbed her by the shoulders and spun her towards him. "I'm a professional wrestler. I'm a very good professional wrestler. I'm a very shitty father, and an even worse husband. If I have to choose between the two, guess which gets put in the back seat?" Slender hands came out of the pockets and gently pulled the sunglasses off Vic's face. Dakota stared into Vic's eyes. "I know you never really meant to hurt her." Vic flipped a lock of her hair with his finger. "You got me hooked on redheads," he said with a smile. "So I've noticed." Dakota smiled back. They both turned toward their daughter's tombstone. Dakota knelt down and kissed the top of the cold granite. "We love you baby." Vic touched the front of the tombstone and caressed his daughter's cheek. "I'm gonna make them pay, pumpkin." Dakota stood beside Vic until the big man finally turned to leave. She walked beside him, neither one saying a word until they neared the parking lot. "Wanna go get a cup of coffee?" Dakota asked. Vic opened the passenger door of his car. "Let's go back to my hotel room instead. We can get coffee when you leave tomorrow morning." With a smile reflecting the fact that she was just as shallow as her husband, Dakota stepped into the car. Vic unzipped his pants and slipped behind the wheel. "Ah, road head," he mumbled as he closed the door and slipped the car into reverse.
