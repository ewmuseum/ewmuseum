The Dark Side of the Moon - part 7

23 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
THE DARK SIDE OF THE MOON Part 7 - Any Colour You Like The door opened suddenly, surprising Poison Ivy, who was in the midst of reaching for the knob. She flinched momentarily as she stood face to face with her brother - a box of knicknacks in his hands. "Leaving so soon?" she asked, gesturing to the box, "And with everything you want?" Brian got out of her way, giving her unhindered access to the apartment. She simply stares at him. "Brian, get inside please." "What?" "I asked you to get inside." "You didn't ask me, you told me. You don't tell me to do anything, Ivy. Fuck you." Ivy shoved her brother through the doorway - she's smaller than he is and as a former wrestler he's in shape, but she surprised him. Once inside, she slams the door and faces him. "What the fuck, Ivy?" he asked, angrily, "What makes you think you have the right to order me around?" Ivy takes her glasses off and drops them on the floor. "What makes me think.... I'll tell you why, Brian. It's because something's happened to us in the last few months that's driven a massive wedge between us! I don't know what it is, and I don't know what I did to piss you off so completely, but I want to know!" Brian rolls his eyes and turns around, but Ivy grabs him by the shoulder and spins him back around. "Don't roll your eyes at me, Brian." He shoves her. She shoves him back. "Goddamnitt! Mom is dying, Brian! DYING! Sooner than later, you and I will be all we have left. Do you understand that? We're it!" "You mean you're it, Ivy.... I'm just along for the ride." Ivy stops short, looking back at her brother. "What's that supposed to mean?" "What do you think, Ivy? You've been doing it all your life." Brian turns around again and paces the living room. Ivy follows him and touches his arm, but he pulls away. "I don't understand, Brian." "What's to understand? This is just another example of how you and I were treated growing up. I get accepted to NYU, you get a full ride at MIT. I have to skip college because I got my girlfriend pregnant and get bitched out by Mom and Dad, you're screwing a professor and nothing is said about it!" "Don't bring that up," warned Ivy, "You know what he was doing to me---" "I don't care," replied Brian, "I don't fucking care. The upshot is that my life is under a constant magnifying glass while you do whatever the hell you want." "I don't think--" "Do you ever? You give up on college to be a wrestling manager. You flit between men like ice cream flavors while I stay behind and help Mom and Dad with the diner! Your whole fucking life has been one long stream of mistakes, while I pay.... OVER AND OVER.... for one mistake I made in highschool. HIGHSCHOOL!" "Are you---" "When Dad died, you took off to go back on tour and left Mom and me to pick up the slack. Now that Mom is.... I'm the one who's been here the whole time, but you're the fucking hero for showing up at the last minute? Fuck that." Ivy's face runs a gamut of emotion.... Anger.... Confusion.... Sadness. She sits down in her father's old chair, putting her hand to her mouth. "I never wanted to make you feel bad," said Ivy, "I've never tried to do anything to hurt you...." "I know that," replied Brian, "but it doesn't make it okay. How am I supposed to feel when you don't even call on Dad's birthday because you're out with Hornet or Sean or God knows who else? How am I supposed to feel when you don't even remember my anniversary because you're whoring yourself out on national television to sell a few tickets?" He kicks the side of the couch angrily. "How do you think it feels to know that your kid sister is better than you at.... fuckin' everything? That everyone knows it? That there's nothing.... NOTHING you can do that'll match her?" Ivy gets up, tears forming at the corners of her eyes. "You're not being fair on yourself, Brian.... you've got something I know I'll never have." "Yeah? What's that?" "What do you think?" Ivy's voice begins to waver, "You've single- handedly carried on the McGinnis name. You're in love with the same girl you were a decade ago. Look at me, Brian.... without exception, every single thing that you're jealous of me over has fucked up my personal life. You were able to stick with Mom and Dad when I was on the road.... that allowed you to have a normal life! Jesus fucking Christ, I've got four beautiful children, Brian! I love them to death, and they're yours and Kim's!" Brian turns back toward his sister. "You need to tell me when things are bothering you," continued Ivy, "Because if you don't.... we're not going to make it." Brian hesitates for a moment, but he hugs his sister tightly. "I can't promise a smooth ride, Ivy.... but we can figure out how we're gonna survive.... together." Ivy had been trying not to cry.... but it's not working anymore. "We lost Alicia.... we lost Dad.... we're going to lose Mom.... I don't think I'd take it if we lost each other...." They stood in silence for a minute, crying on each other's shoulder. For all the feuding this brother and sister have done, for all the fighting and all the harsh words and all the anger that's been spewed back and forth.... at this moment everything seems just about back to normal. So it's no wonder both jump at the sound of the answering machine. "Hi, thanks for calling. You've reached the McGinnis house but nobody's able to take your call right now. Please leave your name and message and we'll get back to you soon." Even that, however.... doesn't get to them the way the next voice does. "Hello, this message is for Ivy or Brian.... this is Dr. Colby at Mercy Hospital.... you two had better get down here quickly. Your mother has experienced some complications and her condition is no longer stable." Brian and Ivy pulled things together on this day. Ten seconds of speaking just pulled things back apart. Brian's heart is beating a mile a minute, Ivy's tears are free- flowing. They don't even bother to pick up the phone - they dash out of the apartment. It's not far to the hospital, which is good.... they can't afford to be late. Not this time.
