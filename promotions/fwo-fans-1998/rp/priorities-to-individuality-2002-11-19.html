Priorities to Individuality

19 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
If someone works their life away toward one ultimate goal, the want of being the best at something or just the will to compete within their field, what happens to those who follow...those who take what their predecessor did so specially and use or build on it? They run out of options. Sit down for a second and listen. Someone's always ripping off of someone else, nothing these days is original. Look at the bands you listen to: You've got Audioslave, just like 70s rock...Linkin Park, just like every generic rap, techno or industrial metal song...Britney Spears, trying to be like anything and everything. Nothing's been original in the music world since a guy picked up the first full-fledged guitar. Any artform can no longer be coined as entirely original, there's no sport that could say it's completely set apart from another. The world and the cultures within it have evolved to the same point the microscopic universe has. EVERYTHING is derived from something else. We're just not that creative anymore. But what can be entirely original, unique and specific to one field, plateau or person? The question is, what's the one thing we have left when you set aside the material characteristics that we tend to prejudge people on? It's called a personality. Here's a good one...what should be done to a person who takes that one unique trait of another and tries to twist it's visage to one shared by millions. Basically, what would you do if someone ran around trying to convince everybody that no, you weren't yourself, you weren't anything special or unique...you were just a simple whore like so many others? ...better yet, want to see? Amy Campbell drove her already bruised knuckles against the wooden door of the rather bland little Motel 6 suite. "THE LITTLE CUNT!" She screamed the expletive while spinning on her heel with surprising grace for someone so enraged, and normally so clumsy. When the redhead turned around, her narrowed glare was met by the usual calm, collected gaze of Darcy Markson. Of course, that look was always mistaken by Amy for uncaring when she needed something or someone to vent at, but at the time she was too far gone in her upset to notice...he could've strut out dressed like Tina Turner and Aimz probably wouldn't have even processed the fact. "It's okay, Amy...I had money on the Bears too. You can't win 'em all." Aimz seemed inclined to smirk a little, but anger had taken over. "I'm talking about THIS," She growled and stuffed an open tabloid magazine towards Darcy's face, fingering a picture of she and Brand Frontier sitting in his car, which was convienently parked in front of Best Western she'd stayed alone at the previous week after Warfare. Darcy wasn't jovial this time, instead he looked up at her from his vigil in front of the TV with a rather frank expression. "Amy, if you lose your mind every time a tabloid accuses you of sleeping around, you're not gonna be able to ever find it again." "If someone was spreading lies about you for every person in the world to interpret personally, you'd react the same. How many people are gonna believe me over this piece of shit? I'm a girl with tattoos...the root of all evil, remember?" Markson rose to his feet, dropping the small weight he had been lifting. "That's odd....since when are you one to care what the world thinks about you?" "I don't! Well...I do! But......no, I don't......." Amy paused, already finding herself mixed up and not wanting to let the vunerable, penetrable side of herself show through. "I just...FUCK!" Yet again, her frusteration boiled over. Fist met door, foot met floor and once she turned back around to an even more disappointed boyfriend. "I don't care what they think...but...shit, I don't ...I just don't know. I fucking hate someone being able to control my image, though! That's what it is...I want to be able to be what I want and look like what I want, fuck what everybody things, but I'll be damned if I let someone manipulate me to make an impression I don't want made." She continued, "This bullshit's depriving me of who I am...I make my own statements and act how I see fit, people don't make those decisions for me." Darcy was silent for a moment, motioning toward himself. "Amy....c'mere." "I don't want a damn hug, Darcy." She huffed, "I want a rifle and the address to a so-called 'journalist'." "You wouldn't believe some of the things they used to say about me back when I used to hang with Jessica and Christi back in the days of the stable we had. You just gotta accept it...it's the price of being a public figure, they can think whatever the hell they want about you, and the lies are gonna circulate. What you CAN control is whether or not you let it affect you." "I can't help but let it affect me...this bitch is going too far into my life. Next thing you know, she's gonna be picking through the trash and making some big eating disorder scandal because I only eat Mr. Noodles and McDonald's." Enter: Amy Campbell, the overly-paranoid. "It can get worse from there...do you honestly want to worry about people peeking in the windows at night?" "You're right!" He chuckled a little, stepping into a 'Eureka!'-esque pose. "I've got a few more pounds to shed before I'm ready let anybody snoop around at home!" Amy's reply summed her thoughts up rather ambiguously. "That's a little...........creepy." Darcy laughed, giving her a wink. "Think of it this way Amy. First of all, it's a tabloid, meaning no one with half a brain is going to really truly believe this lady. Secondly, as long as you go out, kick ass and take names, does it really matter if she's trying to destroy your image? After all, the only image really worth worrying about is whether you're a killer or not.....am I right or am I right?" "But I'm not a 'killer', pitbull or any of that...more like a Chihuaha. She shouldn't tell people what to think of me...that's my job." "She's gonna try Amy, just like the whole world is gonna try. That's why you have to go out and present the image you want the world to see first and foremost." "I do that every day." She pouted, falling back onto a little sofa. "And now look at me: I'm sitting here on a creaky, stained couch compliments of the 'wonderful' people at the classiest Motel 6 in town, whining as usual." "Cause you let them get to you....that's what this Peake chick wants, Amy. She wants you to lash out and lose your mind....keeps giving her ideas for stories. Trust me, I used to be a journalist, I know how their tiny little minds work." Amy giggled a little. If he was going to support her and try to perk the day up, she'd make the best of it. "You know how any tiny mind works...first-hand experience never steers ya' wrong." "Hey now, Miss Amy....." Darcy said, with a fake scowl. "It may be tiny, but it still moves a hell of a lot faster than yours." "Okay..." She laughed again, louder than before just to mock Markson with a confident little grin. "Now that was one of the worst comebacks I've ever heard. But that's not saying much, considering the rest all came from you too." Darcy raised a brow. "This used to be the part where words no longer worked and I'd just beat you into a bloody pulp. Doesn't work on girlfriends though, so I'm lost at this point." "Oh, it works...but you'd be calling me 'Daddy' for the rest of your life if you tried." Aimz returned his wink from before, though a puzzled expression overcame her when she finally fit the rather nonsensical words together. "Daddy?" She looked from side to side for a moment before responding. "Uhh..............yeah!" "That's my Amy," Darcy patted her shoulder, shaking his head with a smile. "Weird as ever." 'Weird' was the perfect word. Not whore, not heathen...just weird. Just Amy. Life had definitely thrown her a bad pitch, but there had to be a way to make the best of it...not just surrounding herself with Darcy, who always managed to show her the light at the end of a bleak tunnel, but working through the situation handed to her, whether or not it meant making her dream of taking a baseball bat to a 'reporter' who called herself Sandra Peake a reality. Brandon Roche might help her...but would it be wise to go so far as to trust and make a friend for the first time in so long? She couldn't forget that the man they called Brand was having the same problems. He may have been 'flawless', but wouldn't he be going through these same motions? Was it time for another conversation, maybe to reach out a hand?
