Disillusionment: Book Two - Chapter III

27 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I watched a woman die last night. No, I'm not some kind of sick fuck who gets off on that kind of thing, but, as usual, I just happened to be in the wrong place at the wrong time. She was a friend of mine, and she knew too much. Well, I don't know if she was really a friend...more of an acquaintance, I guess. Her name was Stephanie. We both knew too much and lived in the same kind of situation, stuck in the now monotonous pattern of eating, running, eating, running, running, running, sleeping, running, etc. We stood by each other because...well...no one else would. I never felt my life would have been any worse off without knowing her, because we just weren't that close and an emotional attachment never existed between us. I wasn't looking for love. Love is an emotion, and remember what I said about those? They hold you down. No, I haven't loved since I was a teenager, back before the hiding and the running began. I don't miss it...I mean, sure, I do miss it sometimes. I remember, when I was eighteen, just lying next to a fire I had built with my girlfriend in my arms...not doing anything...just lying there. I was so...warm...and comfortable. It was the greatest feeling. Sometimes, when I'm lying on a cold, hard concrete floor, after a day of car chases and being shot at, I wish for that warmth: the comfort of a woman's body against mine, but I just can't take the liberty to do that...not with what's at stake. I was never remotely close enough to Stephanie to love her, or to even let myself be attracted to her. But that doesn't mean I wanted to watch her die. It started early this morning...around 5 a.m. We had met up yesterday, and we found a good cellar behind a bar to hide in, but then the Disciplic officers decided to show up. We could hear them above us, and Stephanie panicked. I just stayed hidden, because...well, I've been doing this longer and I know that if I just keep myself out of site, they rarely find me. Anyway, Stephanie decided that it was too dangerous to leave through the bar, because that's where all the officers were. Instead, she decided to take the back stairway, the one that led directly outside. She never even had a chance. It was a trap. The few officers that were in the bar were merely trying to flush us out the back exit, into the waiting hands of what sounded like dozens of soldiers.
