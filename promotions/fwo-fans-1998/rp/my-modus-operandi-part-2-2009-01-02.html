My Modus Operandi - Part 2

2 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Sensation returned to my body. I could feel straps holding me bound by my arms and legs. I could feel hands clenching onto my flesh. The sensations were all strong and returned with great exuberance. I tried to open my eyes, but the blinding lights made me see only blurry outlines of alien figures. These alien figures all wore white and sky blue; and each wore protective white masks and latex gloves that were cold to the touch. They were yelling anxiously at one another and frantically pulling steel utensils up and pressing them against my flesh. I saw myself move violently and heard myself grunting like a wild beast and they aliens all began screaming. I couldn’t make out their words, but their nonverbal communication was enough for me to understand their message. Don’t move. But I couldn’t acknowledge their request. I tried to stop, but I simply couldn’t. I was fighting them, even though I knew they meant no harm. Why was I fighting these saints of salvation? The alien figures could no longer hold me bound, as I saw one of my arms break through the leather bounds. I could see my hand grab one of the figures by her neck and squeeze tightly. I could feel her pulse in my grasp, as if her life rested in my hands. The pulse began beating harder and faster with every bit of pressure I placed around her neck. I told myself to let go, but I couldn’t. It was as if I was simply a spectator through my own eyes; I could control nothing, not even myself. Suddenly, a larger figure grabbed my arm and pulled my hand from her throat. I could see him holding my wrist tightly as a blur of crimson fluid spat from the open wound. I realized then why I was fighting them off. It was simple miscommunication. They thought they were saving me, but in fact that they were persecuting me to a life of hellacious mental torment. I could hear myself scream demands at them, but couldn’t make out my own words. But I could read my own nonverbal communication. I wanted to die, and they wouldn’t let me. I wanted that release, and they simply said no. The bastards. … … “The fuckin’ bastards! The mother fucking bastards!” There is nothing more unpleasant than waking up to the sound of an angry drunk, especially that of an ignorant angry drunk. I shook my head softly as I lifted it from the little nook I created in the sleeve of my arm; it’s become my home away from home. My eyes danced around the bar for a few moments, struggling to refocus and seeing only blurred outlines of moving and station images. Clinton Coldren’s bar was packed to the brim with patrons, but only 3 were rambling angrily towards Clinton. I rubbed my eyes to clear the “lenses” and looked towards the annoying sound of a pissed off New Yorker. He was the typical scumbag of New York, the kind that make foreign tourists return home and tell tales of how horrible and ignorant Americans are. His spiky black hair, soaked in a mixture of natural and manufactured grease, shined brightly against the dull bar lights of Clinton Coldren’s hole-in-the-wall. His skin reeked of cheap lotion as it released an orange hue that made it look as if he suffered from Jaundice, which may have been the case, because all that was coming from his mouth was shit. He also had two men standing behind him; I nicknamed them the twins because they both looked exactly alike with their “New York Guido” fashion style. It was as if Staten Island vomited in this bar. “That’s the way the game is, boys. I’m sorry, but you lost,” Clinton was always so calm in matters of heated discussion. His attitude was well trained and calculated. He was not too close, but not too far. His voice was not too high, but not too low. He wasn’t tense, but he wasn’t at ease. He was at the perfect physical, emotional, and mental medium; he asserted himself, without asserting himself. He smiled towards the brash young men that came out of a GQ magazine, even as one of them, riddled with steroids, slammed his hands on the bar in a fit of ‘roid induced rage. “Do you know who the fuck I am?” what a typical scumbag way to enter a conversation. “I’m someone that can make someone like you disappear. Do you understand me? Do you understand the consequences of stealin’ money from me?” ”Now, son, I didn’t steal anything from you. You and your pals made a damn bet; now in a bet, nothing is guaranteed. You win some, and you lose some. You don’t go to Vegas and win every damn game, do ya?” Clinton asked with a broad and comforting smile on his face. “Now tonight, you boys lost. I can’t help that.” I ran my hand through my hair and slightly massaged the remnants of alcoholic Veisalgia from my mind. The argument grew interesting. This concept of betting intrigued me. It seemed that Clinton’s “hidden” fighting league was growing popular over the last few months. I had heard the battles in the bar’s basement, but through all of my conversations with Clinton, we never exchanged words about it. He knew that I knew what was going on, how could you hide violence from a man such as myself? But we both assumed, through our lack of dialogue on the subject, that we created an unspoken pact of secrecy. I always found it comical that when violence was brought about, that only 20% of the individuals would recognize me from the days of professional wrestling. The Pinnacle of Insane Wrestling had a strong cult following, but apparently that cult dissipated faster than most Las Vegas weddings. The name Krow was now nothing more than a title of a Brandon Lee movie to the ears of the common New Yorker. “Bullshit, that fight was fixed and you know it!” another of the meatheads shouted as he pointed his finger towards Clinton. “Fixed?” Clinton chuckled amusingly. “How the hell am I gonna’ fix a guy breaking another’s guys nose? You think somebody would voluntarily let that happen to ‘em?” ”Why else would those two mother fuckers have a drink together at the bar for two fucking hours?! That’s bullshit! Whenever I fuck up someone, I don’t buy them a drink; I just make sure that their drinkin’ through a straw!” The masculinity of these three was overflowing to the point of nausea. They pounded chests, waved fingers and pursed their lips in that angry Guido fashion. You don’t want a mess with a guy that has a haircut that could stab you, do you? Well, obviously, Clinton didn’t fear the “doo”. “Look boys, you lost. You ain’t gettin’ any money back unless you want to come back next week and throw some money down.” Clinton’s words of calm consolation were proven to be ineffective towards the three young “geniuses” he was speaking with. Every word he spoke only made more steam fly from their ears and nose. Testosterone was flowing through their veins and the repeated slams on the bar top were becoming more frequent to the point of self-annoyance. I decided to break the tension-filled conversation with a raise of my hand, indicating the assistance of a bartender. It went unnoticed. Clinton was too firm in his stare at the three young men, and who could blame him? Their belligerent attitudes and drunken state proved to be quite mesmerizing. I cleared my throat loudly, and drew the attention of the three men and Clinton. Clinton smiled towards me. “What’s up, pal?” he asked in an amused manner. I raised my empty glass to the side of my head and looked at Clinton with an annoyed expression on my face. ”I just woke up. I need my coffee,” I replied coldly. As Clinton chuckled and reached below the bar to retrieve the sacred water which I requested from him. Slam! A fist crashed onto the bar. “Yo, grease monkey, do you not see that we are conversing over here?” shouted the bulkiest of the three men, as he pressed his bear claw-like hand on the bar and stared a hole at me through his $300 sunglasses. He continued his ranting and raving, fingers flying, and his ‘boys’ pounding their chests and exchanging high fives; but I muted their vocal chords out. I watched them intently and felt as if I was watching three monkeys dressed in expensive clothes. I could hear the almost Looney-Tune-like music in the background as they pounded on their chests and jumped around excitedly. Before I could control it, I found myself chuckling and grinning as they angrily spat words of fire my way. “Did I just say somethin’ funny, faggot?” he shouted towards me. I continued chuckling for a moment and ran my hand across my rough beard. “Nothing funny at all,” I dangled my empty glass playfully in my hand and extended it towards the bar. “I just thought this was a bar for drinking…” I smiled. “I didn’t know this was a zoo for dumb apes.” “What did you just say, mother fucker?” I saw him take the violent steps forward. I even saw him clenching his fists during the first few seconds of our short dialogue. I saw the large vein that was bulging from above his right eye. I even saw his friend patting him on the shoulder, the classic ‘I got your back’ signal. I saw his fist lunging forward. I saw it all, like it was slow motion. I always had the ability to sense impending violence; it was an ability I became cultured in throughout my years in professional wrestling. But despite those years of blood-soaked matches and sweat-filled moments of despair, it had been awhile since I took a fist to the face. I had ample time to dodge the attack, he wasn’t the most calculated of fighters, but I wanted to feel that painful sensation once again. So I did. They all laughed as I body flung violently to the ground. I felt the hard unforgiving floor as my body crashed through bar tables and stools and smacked the stiff wooden ground. I heard the cracks and crumbles of the furniture as it either shattered beneath my 240 pound frame or viciously flew afar. I heard the pitter patter of frightened patrons escaping the scene or simply taking a few steps back to get a good look at the mayhem. I also tasted something that brought back many memories. It was a salty and distinct taste that I hadn’t had slip across my tongue in years. The taste of blood; it brought back so many fond memories. It’s amazing how a simple taste of something nostalgic can bring back all of those warm feelings again. They all gloated about their triumph, poking fun at my “weakness”. Comments laced with profanity came my way, and only Clinton was the one to rush to my aid. I smelled his hot alcoholic breath against my face, and I heard his questions of compassion. “Are you okay? Can ya get up?” But as I opened my eyes and looked up at the concerned face of Clinton, I started to hysterically laugh and shine a broad smile at him. Clinton cocked an eyebrow up in confusion and asked if I was okay. ”How do you feel, son? Can ya move?” he asked with concern, possibly questioning my mental stability as well as my physical. ”Yeah, I feel…alive,” I said between short and amused chuckles. The expression his face was priceless; an expression filled with a mixture of different reactive emotions; confusion, concern (for my well being and his own) and a smidgen of amusement. I pulled myself up and saw the man who struck me loosening the top two buttons on his shirt. He continued throwing insults towards me, telling me to simply lie down and he’d spare my life. His comments sent blood racing through my body, but it wasn’t out of anger…it was out of excitement. The feelings of a battle all returned to me, and they were all feelings that I gladly invited. They were quite familiar and embraced with open arms. I lifted my head only slightly, peering at him and his friends through laces of my long black hair. My smile must’ve been wide, because I remember the sides of my face hurting horribly afterwards. It certainly wasn’t from the impending brawl…because they never hit me. He attempted to continue his banter of insults, but he was suddenly silenced…by my tight-grip across his large neck. I smiled widely as I pulled him in towards my face. As he struggled for air, I noticed that the veins in his head grew wider and juicier as they pulsated rapidly. I noticed his skin tone began changing from the bright spray-on tanned orange to a beautiful dark blue. His eyes began transitioning to a more glassy texture. It was quite the picture. So, in this cinematic beauty, I felt it only right to add a catchy one liner, something that could send a message of dominance and superiority. ”Speak up,” I said just before I threw the back of his head into the edge of the bar. I never released my grip and continued to throw his skull into the bar until crimson stains were splattered across the edge. It was then that I finally released my victim and allowed him to fall gently to the ground, resting his head on his new liquid red pillow of blood. The altercation was only a few seconds, but through my eyes it felt like a sonata that lasted an hour. I tilted my head a bit and looked over at this man of many words. He looked peaceful when quiet. Like a baby. His body only twitched momentarily, but when his nerves finally rested from the initial shock and his eyes finally closed, I almost felt like putting a soft blanket over him. However, my artistic adoration was cut off quickly by a sudden stinging in the back of my head. I didn’t recognize immediately what had hit me until I saw pieces of wood flying from behind me. It was the sting of a bar stool. It was struck over the back of my head. The stinging was quite maddening, but not unbearable. I stumbled into the bar, but pushed off it with an elbow extended…and I connected. I felt the warmth of fresh blood literally exploding from the broken nasal cavity of my second victim. His body attempted to fly backwards, but I grabbed hold of his shirt, pulled his near-limp body towards mine and sent the toughest part of my skull careening into what was left of his nose. He flew backwards, leaving an air trail of crimson, and crashed to the floor. I looked for the third member of their party, but the swinging door indicated his escape. I sighed in disappointment; such a loud mouth simply hid a lack of courage. I released all the tension in my body and snapped my neck a few times to the side. “Hey buddy…,” Clinton’s voice was no longer calm and collected, it was cautious and timid. He spoke softly, feeling that if he spoke louder it would evoke the angry beast he just saw before him. I turned calmly towards Clinton and cracked a passive grin his way. Behind Clinton was a sea of astonished patrons, mouth agape. So, this is how 50 jaws dropping looks. Clinton pointed to my head, “You’re bleedin’.” I tilted my head to the side and ran my hand across the back of my head and brought it to my face. It was drenched in thick, dark red. I looked around for something to wipe the blood off, and figured my first victim wouldn’t mind me tearing his $300 dollar shirt off and using it as a terry cloth to wrap around my head. I nodded to the sea of patrons and gave a wave to Clinton. As I walked out of the bar, I spoke over my shoulder towards Clinton with a calm and docile tone. “Same time tomorrow”
