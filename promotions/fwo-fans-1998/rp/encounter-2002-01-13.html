January 7, 2002 Tokyo, Japan 1:04 PM Tokyo Time (January 6, 11:04 PM US Eastern Time) Scott Wasner could still hear the cheers of the fans as he staggered backstage, but that was the last thing on his mind. He had forgotten exactly how much punishment any given match could put on a competitor. Of course, that was any given match - this was... well, let's be honest. We were trying to put together an MOTYC, me and Ruben. Yeah, they had been. Wasner had wanted to prove that his rust was nonexistent, and he supposed he had given that illusion - but it wasn't true. Ross was a fine worker, but a little stiff, and Wasner had taken some serious shots during the match, both from Ross and in general. Plus, Creed had gotten in some potato shots in the post-match brawl too, which was highly unnecessary. Creid, Wasner had decided, seemed to be every bit the jackass Creed was. His neck was sending little jolts of pain up into his head, his legs were rubbery, and his back? My back feels like I jumped out of a plane and landed spread-eagle. Ah, God. Wasner looked for the nearest wall. Oh, right. The one right next to me. He reached out an arm and braced himself against the wall, leaning on it. The backstage area, at least this front section of it, was pretty much empty, for which Wasner thanked God - he wasn't vain and cocky, at least not to the degree of his character, but he still had his pride, and for the boys to see this "legend" grimacing in pain and struggling to catch his breath after a tough match was not high on his list of wants. Fucking hell, this is bad... He had had plenty of concerns about a return to wrestling - could he still cut a promo (yes), could he still work a match (yes), had the fans forgotten him and his accomplishments (no), how would the boys, most of whom were not around for most of his run, welcome him back (well enough), could he resist the temptations of the wrestling life (so far, so good) - but the one thing that hadn't crossed his mind, the one thing he seemed to have forgotten, was the sheer incredible physical toll working took out of one. "Excuse me... Mr., uh, Slugga?" Wasner lifted his head. A short, pudgy, bearded man stood before him. He was clad in sweatpants and an fWo crew t-shirt. His oily black hair hung to his shoulders. "Hi. Listen, pal," he said, and paused as a fresh jolt of pain hit him, he couldn't figure out from where, "you really shouldn't come up to the boys after they work matches... usually you'll either get brushed off or shoved against the wall. I'll give you an autograph, though, but just this one time." "No, no, I don't want no autograph." He doesn't want no autograph. OK. "Then what do you want?" "Well, I usually work in the truck, as you mighta guessed on accounta my shirt... but I, uh, I run a little side business in my spare time, a side business that's very popular in the wrestling business, you know what I mean?" Wasner scowled. "Yeah, I think I do. Why're you telling me this?" "Well, you know, word gets around 'n all that... I heard about, ya know, your, uh... appetites... you know, you and the white stuff..." The scowl deepened, and Wasner pushed himself off the wall to look down at the squat man. He spoke slowly, clearly, and coldly. "I think you had better leave. I don't do that anymore." The little man held up his hands and took a few steps back. "OK, OK, no problem... I can dig that. You don't want it, you don't want it. Of course, you ever change your mind... you know where I am. I'm here all the time." "I said, you had better leave." "Alright, alright, don't freak." He turned and took a few steps elsewhere, then turned his head and looked back at Wasner. "You know, you look like shit. You don't want the white stuff, I got uppers, I got downers, tranks - " "Get the fuck out!" The short man held up his hands again, in mock surrender, then reached into a pocket and came out with a card. He flipped it to Wasner, then turned to go. "And hey... call me Roach, OK?" With that, he left. Reflexively, Wasner caught it. He looked at it. Thomas Preston fWo Cameraman Scrawled below that, what was presumably a beeper number and the phrase "Personal Pharmacist." Wasner rolled his eyes. Drug dealers were a common enough phenomenon in the wrestling business - he would have bet that wrestlers, as a profession, had one of the highest rates of substance abuse - but one with a business card? That was new on him. Oh well. Let it never be said drug dealers didn't keep up with the times. They were probably on the internet now too. There was little point in turning Thomas "Roach" Preston in. As soon as he was taken away, another dealer would pop up to replace him. That much Wasner had learned, and learned well. Wasner stood up a bit straighter. The pain had started to pass, though it was definitely still there. And when he woke up the next morning... whoo, let's not think about that. "Scott, if you could just come over here... we need you for some promos for our syndies." Wasner rolled his eyes. Some vacuous production assistant was babbling at him, and he absent-mindedly shoved the card into the waistband of his pants and started off in that direction. Yep... I'm back in the life, alright.
