16 August 2004

Mojo Risin' Chapter Ten: Rejoin the Human Race "I'm serious, Simon, neither of us care about getting stiffed for the night. Yes, seriously. Wrestling pays by the match, not by the hour. We're not there, we don't get a payoff. Simple." Eli Flair leaned back in the phone booth and closed his eyes. Simon Cowl had been trying every avenue of persuasion, threat, and blackmail to get both himself and Ivy to Orlando for the final Milk Arena show that the fWo would have for a while. It wasn't a matter of a stubborn wrestler and bitchy consultant - it was a matter of seven hours to showtime and the two players in question being in a ridiculously small town several thousand miles away. "Besides," said Eli, as he looked out of the booth toward the small table they had taken over in the diner, "I need to get my leg cleaned and looked at, Ivy needs to get her head patched up, that ain't gonna happen here." He held the phone away from his ear as Cowl got louder. "Okay," said Eli, "I'm hanging up now. Yeah, will get there when we get there. Bye." Click. Cowl continued to ramble up to the point of the receiver slamming down. Eli paid it no mind, and headed back out to the table. The food had arrived, and Ivy was busily chomping down on her burger. "I can hear the Hallelujah chorus," said Ivy as she pushed Eli's club sandwich closer to him, "And I need another shake." They ate in silence for a moment, the first official food that either of them had eaten - not counting verious bits of beef jerky that Eli had in his car - in the past two days. The waitress was good - she had a second shake for Ivy in less than two minutes. "So what did Simon Says?" asked Ivy. "He--" Eli stopped for a second and laughed. "He said we're not getting paid for tonight." "Duh," replied Ivy, "Even with a concussion I knew that." Eli looked at her forehead. She had cleaned and closed the wound herself upon first arriving in town, with rubbing alcohol and super glue. "So we're missing tonight's show," said Eli, "so they're gonna rebook me and Flyer for next week I think and give him a different opponent tonight." "Kay," replied Ivy, "Talk to Angel?" "Message," said Eli, "I tried to figure out the time difference between here and France but the upshot is that she's either pre, post, or onstage. Called the kids, told Momma Cally that we were alive and would be in tomorrow afternoon." "Sure about that?" asked Ivy. "Definitely," replied Eli, "We've got an hour and a half until the bus leaves for Flagstaff, then we've got a flight to O'Hare where we catch a connection to LaGuardia. Mrs. Callasantos said that me and Angel's crap has been moved in - happy as shit that April still had a key - so everything is finally looking up." Ivy sighed. "So there's no time to go shopping, huh?" "What d'you think?" She laughed. "Ain't my fault I like Chicago... think Double G is still living there, actually. If he's not on the road." "Big Kev?" asked Eli, "He's still o-fish-ally living in Chicago, but he and Gabe are always out 'n about, pickin' up dates wherever they can." "Well, they're worth it," remarked Ivy as she finished off her burger. Not a drop of yolk from the fried egg landed on the plate, "Kevin Powers and Apocalypse are one of the best teams to come along in the past decade." "So see, there's nuttin' to do in Chi-town today. Or tomorrow, as the case may be." Ivy reached for her waist, then exhaled solemnly. "What?" "What time is it?" she asked. Eli turned around. "There's a clock on the wall, Ivy." "I know," she replied, "but I miss my phone. Who knows who's tried to call me." "My guess would be, everyone," replied Eli, "At least, everyone that's accustomed t'talkin' to you six times a day. Last summer the whole reason, I think, that'cha ended up with so many missed calls and messages is because of Geoff's story. This year nobody's expectin' us to be in touch until today anyways." "I wonder if anyone's likely to find your car," thought Ivy out loud, "they'd run a plate check and contact the appropriate authorities, and then the jig'd be up." "What jig?" asked Eli, laughing, "I've managed to check in with just about everyone that'd be lookin' for either of us. They know we're alive, they know we're safe, and they know my car is in a junk pile somewhere on the side'a the road." He stopped for a moment and drank down half his root beer in one gulp. "Call me crazy, Ivy, dear heart, but I think we might've successfully rejoined civilization." "And," continued Ivy, "we'll both be home tomorrow." There was a bit of regret in her voice that Eli picked up on. "Something wrong?" "Naaah," replied Ivy, "Not really." "C'mon, kiddo," cautioned Eli, "Out with it."
