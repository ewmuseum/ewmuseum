A Clue! (...excerpts from The Chronicles of Fairview Reed)

17 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I’ve been studying and searching forever, it seems, as the days have begun to seem as just one long day, filled with books and words and messages from men dead and dusty for long centuries. But (finally!) I truly believe I have found something of value! It occurred to me that I had been going in circles of the same reasoning and beliefs – that because the epiphany of The 9 Books had been discovered in the tome of the madman Barastius I would find the answers I desired by scouring and perusing more Barastius and any other mad prophets I could find. The results were of course nonexistent, but I have read so many hideous and fantastical visions of the end of life on this planet! Ignorance is bliss, they say, and if the people making up the populous of this world knew what these seers saw they would tear their own eyes from their heads and wail in terror; shite and urine and blood would run in rivers…ah, but I forget myself. My search had revealed nothing but madness and I began to feel a bit deranged myself. My Assistant convinced me to take leave of my library refuge and walk about a bit in the out-of-doors. The fresh air, she gestured, will clear your mind. And, of course, she was right – we had only just stepped into the crisp wintry afternoon when I looked up and saw the crow overhead. The force of this ebon portent hit me so strongly that I knew that I was exactly WHERE I was supposed to be exactly WHEN I was supposed to be there. We hadn’t been outside but a half-minute and I was refreshed with excitement and flushed with the thrill of finding the next piece of inspiration – I spun round intending to renew my search at once but my Assistant had other ideas, and one look into her face I knew she could not be swayed. (I must admit that the walk was invigorating and was a reminder to me that I still am a living, breathing being on this plane and that I should probably try to remember that more often.) Once our brisk constitutional was finished I hurried to my precious books and threw myself wholehearted into their revered and wrinkled pages. I started with all references of The Crow, which, as you know, are numerous, and of various importances. However there was one particular passage in Fromwell’s Accounts of Mysticism (the Fourth Volume) that held fast my attention: there was a time when those able to could converse freely with all animals, furred and feathered…From there my search led me to a reference of Sir Luther Barnard Tinsley, who was the last man to know the language of birds. He related the most remarkable tale – the tale that the birds told to him. This legend was handed down from hatched generation to hatched generation – and not just one species! It seems as though ALL feathered creatures had their own versions of the story but all and whole it was the same story! A being of great beauty and great wisdom (a birdsound that Tinsley wrote could not be translated as a proper English word but gave his best attempt nonetheless) would set into motion events that would restore to mortals the Skill of Language and men and birds would converse and sing and laugh together as they once did. This is, I admit, a tale I had not heard before and as you know the finding of such gems as this gives me a heady pleasure… The word Tinsley could not translate properly he interpreted as ‘Phoenix’, and I happen to know one…Mrs. Siobahn Phoenix McPhineas… who has informed me that we shall under no circumstances be attending this week’s wrestling festivities. She said she had a ‘very bad feeling’ about some ruckus that occurred previously and that we shall view from the sidelines for now. I have learned before to trust implicitly the feelings and moods of Mrs. McPhineas and shall continue to be guided by her judgment. I shall also be keeping an eye on her…
