The Business - Reality

4 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
So here I am, driving to the next show in my rental car, speeding on the highway to connect San Diego and Los Angeles together. I got to spend some time at my Winter home this weekend, but without my family, it's just not the same. Whatever the case, here I sit, inside this beaten up and rusted piece of junk I'm paying twenty five buck for the privilege to drive. And you know two people probably had sex in this car. You just know it. But I'm trying my best not to think about that. Blasting Do the Evolution, and rocking out to the beautiful guitar pattern on repeat. It's a song that never fails to pump you up, and a song that I felt was grossly forgotten when it was released years ago. Whatever the case, it's helping to fill my mind with the senseless sound, stop it from awandering. Which is what it does. I'm a wandering nomad. It's another reason I'm a professional wrestler. With my mind wandering to different lands, I figured I might as well bring my body alongside me. And I've seen most of the world. It's been one hell of a ride throughout these past ten years. And there aren't a lot of guys who can say they've been here for ten years and still can go. Fission's knees are shot to hell, Malone's retired, and both of them were greener than I was. You'd think with my style way back when, my death day's calendar was set a bit short. But yet, I've outlasted some of the greatest pure talents in this sport. It's amazing to think that me, a six foot, two hundred pound wash rag has been able to make it in this business. Since you know, the wrestler world is built for people a foot taller and two hundred pounds larger than me. Ten years ago, I wouldn't have had a chance to wrestle around the world. I would have had three options. Wear stilts, gain forty pounds of muscle, or quit. And the first two options never really guaranteed you anything. Thank God the world has changed. But not everything has. Guys are so messed up they can't see when they head out into the bright lights for a match. The pain shooting up their sides doesn't have time to heal, it only has pills. That's how some wrestlers tell time anyway. They schedule their days around the amount they pop into their mouths. One pill is breakfast, two pills is lunch. Four is dinner. Five is when the lights turn on and their music hits. It's amazing to see. I'm just glad I don't have an addictive personality. Or maybe I enjoy pain. I'm not sure which caused me to steer clear of the capsule solution, but whatever it was, I'm thankful. I've been able to live in this crazy world relatively safe. Maybe it's because I have something to live for. Maybe it's because I have a wife and a son. But then again, most times when I'm traveling on the road, it feels like they don't exist, because they never get to be there. Really, their presence helps me stay on the road, helps me keep myself clean. And yet, being on the road drives them away. I'd say it's ironic, but I never got a clear definition on what ironic is. Something happens that you didn't expect? I call that a surprise. And think about this. Wrestling is the only sport without a union. Without a governing body to make sure it's performers don't get mistreated. The fWo, in my short stay here, has seemed to be pro-wrestlers though. Maybe it's because of the guys running this place. They used to step into the ring of uncertainty all the time. They know what it's like to head in those four corners, sore as a tenderized pork chop, and work a show that you're not sure if you'll be paid for. Work a show that nearly kills you. And when it does, have nowhere else to go. Wrestling is the one sport in this world with a death toll that can be calculated yearly. When was the last time you heard someone die in a Baseball game? The best of my knowledge was 1922; a man was beaned in the head before the invention of helmets. Same thing with Hockey, same thing with football. And here we are, fighting for our lives every day. If that's fake, then tell me what reality is.
