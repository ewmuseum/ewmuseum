The American Dream

9 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Father..." Yalenchka said as she stood outside the training ring, gazing at her father Borislav getting down to some training inside. "Father, I know he treated us bad. I know he has played us for fools. But it does not matter now, no?" Borislav grunted something from inside the ring as a trainer was on his knees tying the old fella's bootlaces. One gets the impression Borislav's frail frame had difficulty bending over to do it himself. "I was upset. I thought he loved me. But it not matter now. He has gone. These nice people have terminated his contract..." Yalenchka's head dropped, thinking back to her torrid few months. "... much like he did to me... BUT," she said, getting more upbeat, "It has worked out nice, no?" Borislav nodded, as the trainer moved onto the second boot. "The nice people here, they like us." She continued, smiling at the trainer. "We had a horrible time in Russia. I never wanted to go back and was scared we would have to after that horrible man left me. He tried to split us up. But we are now stronger... and these nice people like you so much they want to make you a proper wrestler!" The trainer completed the second boot and nodded at Borislav, signalling for him to prepare to be slammed. He made the attempt to slam the wily old crazy Russian, but instead was greet with a rake of the eyes and a bite on his arm. "Look, you can't learn anything if you're not gonna learn to..." the trainer said, rubbing his arm. Yalenchka was quick to throw her two pennies in, shouting, "Father!" It proved to distract him long enough to be scooped up and gently slammed to the mat. "That wasn't so bad was it?" the trainer asked. Borislav lay flat on his back and groaned. "Oh isn't it great. The American dream!" Yalenchka said. "We get to live out the American dream with these nice people. No more fighting bears. No more living in rags with no money. We are now in America and we are in wrestling! A new fresh start!" Borislav turned his head as he lay on the mat and nodded slightly, a small smile appearing beneath his long bushy beard. "Do not worry about him. He is as tough as, how you say, old boots. He will learn and will be fine." Yalenchka reassured. "We will do you people proud. Being used, spat out and joked at may have been the best thing ever to happen to the Smertin's. We are now strong, and we will make you proud of us!"
