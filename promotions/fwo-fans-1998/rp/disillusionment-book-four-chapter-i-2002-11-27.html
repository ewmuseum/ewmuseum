Disillusionment: Book Four - Chapter I

27 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I have no sanctuary. In the past week, my life went from being wearing and stressful to being completely hell-like. I used to have to live on my toes and, every week or so, I could expect that there would be at least one instance in which I might have to physically outrun some Disciplic predator or brandish my gun. Those instances are becoming much more frequent now. Foolishly, I thought that I was safe in the arenas. I figured that if I was backstage, the Disciples wouldn't risk exposing themselves by going through security checks to get into the locker room, and I thought that, since most of the wrestlers were well paid, they wouldn't turn me in for the cash. I forgot a little law of life, though, that I had discovered as a kid. Those who have the most want the most. It seems ridiculous, but it's true. Professional baseball players didn't, as a general rule, make more than ten million dollars a year until just a couple of years ago. Now we have greedy sons of bitches like Alex Rodriguez signing contracts for nearly three times that much money. Does Alex Rodriguez need two-hundred-sixty million dollars? No, he absolutely doesn't. Did he want it? Yes. Why? Because the human soul is greedy. Anyway, I'm getting off the subject with all of this. What I am really trying to say is that the Disciples have manipulated the greed of the human race to make my life much more volitile. On Saturday night, after my match with Jason Hawk, which I won, by the way, I was sitting on the bench in the corner of the locker room. That's always where I go. I'm not a social guy when I'm getting ready for a match, or when I'm winding down from one, either. The other guys, well, they think of me like you probably do. They think I'm some sort of demented freak with a mind so paranoid that I always believe I have to be running for my life. Sigh......if they only knew. So I was sitting in the corner of the locker room after the match, when two guys, who I had heard behind me, complaining about biased referees in their matches, started whispering to each other. I could hear the footsteps of one as he walked away. I didn't want to draw any attention by turning around to survey the situation, but I didn't hear the other leave. I'm assuming he just stood there, staring at me...turning his own fear over in his mind as he realized he could never hope to understand me or what I've been through. There was silence in the room as I removed the tape from around my hands. My movements were slow and methodical. I didn't want to startle the guy into attacking me, as I could feel his growing fear. The silence was broken by the return of the other wrestler opening the door to the hallway. The few instants that the door stood open I thought about the commotion in the arena and how it was ironic that I was in a building that was so alive, but yet was so alone and vulnerable in this room with only these two other wrestlers. I don't know what I expected of them. I guess I just thought they'd enjoy the freak show I provided them with and then they'd leave. They stood there for several minutes, whispering to each other. I had just zipped up my gear bag and was about to get up to leave when I heard a shuffle in my direction. I tried to quickly jump to my left, but I was just a tad too slow, as I could feel my flesh tearing along my left side. Out of instinct, I grabbed the wrist of my attacker and saw his weapon of choice: a long serrated knife, now stained with my blood. Had I not tried to dodge the blow, I would have been dead. Someone attacked me like this ten years ago, I would not have had the intuition to move. That's one thing I can thank the Disciples for: my increase ability to guard my own ass. I looked back at my assailant's face. The blonde grappler could not have been more than twenty and I could see the fear on his face as sweat, still left from his match, ran into his eyes. Out of the corner of my eye, I could see the other wrestler, about the same age, but larger, move in my direction. I ducked and missed a roundhouse to the face. I maintained my grip on the wrist of Blondie and squeezed, using my strength to make him drop his weapon. I don't know what happened next. I went into a rage. When your instincts take over, they don't leave room for logical thought, and with logical thought, goes memory. The next thing I remember is turning off the light as I left the room, so that the other wrestlers in the hall would not see the unconscious bodies of the men who had hoped I would make them three million dollars richer. I calmly walked out of the arena, and I ran to my motorcycle. Well, I shouldn't say it's mine. I stole it from a Disciple manufacturing center, but I've had it for about a month. I hopped on and I got the hell out of town. The arenas had always felt like safe havens to me. I enjoyed my time there before an after my matches. I didn't need it, but the arenas were the only places I felt safe among large groups of people. Thinking back now, I don't know what that was the case. Anywhere I go I'm going where there's people, there are going to be people who want the three million dollars my capture can bring them. The arena was the only place that I let myself rest. I would get there several hours before my match was scheduled and just sit. I didn't tell you about this, of course, because I knew that if people found out about it, it wouldn't be safe anymore. Well, so much for safety. The first two weeks of this tournament, I even slept in the very arenas I wrestled in. Why not? A wooden locker room bench was more comfortable than a concrete warehouse floor. There will be no more sleeping in arenas, though. I just can't risk it. When we started, I had never wrestled a match in my life, and people had never heard of me, so I was the underdog. The only wrestling training I had was that which my life presented me with. As time has gone on, people have started to see what I can do. They have heard me tell them of the life that I have been forced to live. They have heard how I fight to survive. They have seen me in my matches and suddenly... I am the favorite. I don't like it, this change. When the expectations were low, I could concentrate on my opponent. Now, though, there is the pressure of the expectations. When people expect you to win, you start to expect the same. It's not like I lacked confidence before. I knew that I could win, but I didn't expect it of myself. Expectations are like several of the girls I dated in my teens...they're a bitch. Unpredictability......insanity......whatever it is, it's malicious and violent.
