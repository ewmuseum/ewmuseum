Contest of Champions

3 February 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
fWo: Fans Wrestling Organization Contest of Champions February 03, 2000 Machina sat and looked at the table before him out of the corner of his eye. His teeth gritted. Somewhere in the back of the room the music began to play. Eye Of The Tiger. Machina turned, and walked back and forth in a straight line. He picked up a towel and slung it over his head, beads of sweat flying through the air. A hand went down and picked up a bag, reaching in and pulling a mouth guard. He bit and turned. His enemy was sitting silently on the other side of the table. Mocking him. Glowing at him. If I am to marry Penny... I must defeat you once and for all. Rod... it's time. Machina sat down in front of the table and looked at the game in front of him. Few contests were so apt at testing the mettle of men as this one. This was it. One would walk away the winner. One would be taunted in front of his peers relentlessly until he broke down into tears and begged for the sweet release of death. Rod was a stiff competitor. Rigid in his beliefs. Once he had been his running mate in his unsuccessful bid to become the president of the United States of America. Today he stands between the masked man that is all that is good and pure, the loyal legend Machina... and certain victory. This game... this contest... this epic battle. Machina sat and grasped the plastic handles with sweaty hands. It was time. Time for... Hungry Hungry Hippos. It's tons of fun as you earn a ton. You're the green hippo, and I'm the yellow hippo. Machina's eyes narrowed. Now Rod. Now let's get it on. The game began, and Machina was lost in the thrill of the contest. But even as Machina pounded fist after fist down to get the hippos to devour their white marble feast Machina's heart was heavy. For in the morning he would pack Rod away, a remnant of his old life. The game of Hungry Hungry Hippos would be placed back in the box and go not to the place of honor in his apartment, but back in the depths of his closet. He would put away the toys and symbols of his youth, and walk the walk of adults to Penny. He would ask her to marry him in the morning, and he would do so not as a boy... but as a man. Ha! It's over! Machina won the game, his hippo swallowing far more marbles than Rod's had. He was happy for his victory... of course he was. But why was it that a single tear now fell from his cheek? Fin. "I noticed that in commercials for children's games, even though we had been taught at Sugarloaf that kids did not really want to be competitive, it was still essential that the winner say, 'I win!' The advertisers must have done some elaborate psychological testing, and it must have worked, because Hungry Hungry Hippos has stayed on the market for over ten years, the object of the game being to press your handle down again and again as fast as you can, with no rhythm, no timing, just slam-slam-slam as your hippo surges out to grab marble after marble from the game surface encircled by other players aged four to twelve, one of whom will soon shout, 'I win!' " -Edward Allen The New Yorker
