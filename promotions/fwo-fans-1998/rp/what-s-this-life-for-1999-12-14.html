What's this life for?

14 December 1999
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The video begins with a black screen. As the first few seconds of "Guerrilla Radio" by Rage Against The Machine begins to play, the video turns from complete black into a black and white picture of a young Chris Universal. A very young Chris Universal. As other pictures of Universal ranging in age from eleven months to his current age of twenty four flash onto the screen, the words "Mr. $ellout" appear on the screen. We go back to more shots of Universal, this time stills of him with his girlfriend Megan Dawn, his best friend Lance Star, his mentor Cactus Jack, and a still from last year at Mob Rules '98 of he, Stevie Roberts & Ultra-Cool (Scotty Cool and Ultra-Violet). Before Zach De La Rocha of RATM can begin screaming away, the song changes to "What's This Life For" by Creed and video images begin to play rather than stills. "Hurray for a child that makes it through..." [Shot of Universal making his fans Wrestling organization debut on Wednesday Warfare on July 15, 1998.] "If there's any way because the answer lies in you..." [Shot of Universal cutting a promo days before Hardcore Hell 1998 about "The Rock" Rocky Maivia.] "They're laid to rest before they know just what to do..." [Shot of several former fWo wrestlers, who really never got their chance or made an effort to succeed in the fWo.] "Their souls are lost because... they could never find..." [More shots of former fWo wrestlers...] "What's this life for?" [The fWo World Title splashes onto the screen in blazing fashion. As the lead vocalist of Creed - Scott Stapp - repeats the song title several times, shots of every fWo World Champion from the first to the most recent [Jim Dudley, Cactus Jon, Stevie Roberts, Austin Davis, Ice, David Spencer, The Flying Frenchie, Scott Collins, Scott Slugger, Ultra-Violet and Primetime] appear on the screen in a rapid yet smooth succession. Another shot of Ice in the ring from this past Sunday is shown, with him holding the title high overhead.] "I see your soul, it's kind of gray..." [A still of Lance Star in a hospital bed is shown.] "I see your heart you look away..." [The shot of Star fades off of the screen.] "You see my wrist, I know your pain..." [A shot of Lance Star' career ending injury on November 17, 1999 is seen. Star is injured in a 'Death Penalty' from the top rope by Scotty Cool/Death/Collins.] "I know your purpose on your plane..." [We fade from the shot where Star was injured right into several quick shots of Lance Star doing what he loves: *wrestling*.] "Don't say a last prayer because... you could never find... what's this life for..." [The shot of Lance Star in a hospital bed is shown again as Stapp repeats the title of the song several times.] "But they aren't here anymore..." [A montage of the same shots seen earlier of former fWo wrestlers are shown.] "Don't have to settle the score..." [A sequence of Universal and his friends Scott Slugger & Ice taking their vengeance out on Death appears.] "Cause we all live under the reign... of one king..." Another shot of the fWo World Title appears, only this time next to money. The song by Creed slowly fades; as a question mark flashes onto the screen in front of the World Title and the money. We fade to black... to be continued.
