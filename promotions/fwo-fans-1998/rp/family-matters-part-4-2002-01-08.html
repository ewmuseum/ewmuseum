Family Matters (Part 4)

8 January 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Seated alone in his dressing room, Monte Burns leaned down to finish tying up his boots. The accommodations, however, are not quite as nice as we see today in his days with the fWo. As with most other independent promotions, the NEWA did not hold events in large arenas. Even though Monte was owner at this point, he didn't feel like spending much of his money on the promotion his father and other family members had built. Instead, he was intent on disgracing his father and all that he had made. That is why on this night...June 21st, 1998...Father's Day...Monte Burns would facing his father Daniel Burns in an NEWA Heavyweight Title match. The rumors of the match had spread quickly throughout the backstage area. Many were upset, but most kept their mouths shut. However, Monte's brother Nathan was not one of them. As Monte stood up from the bench, the door to his dressing room swung open and Nathan stepped inside. In his left hand he was holding a piece of paper. "What the hell is this?!" exclaimed Nathan. "Well, Nathan," Monte said with a smile, "it looks like a piece of paper to me." Nathan was not pleased with the sarcasm in Monte's voice and charged towards his younger brother, pinning him back against a locker. Despite Nathan's forearm pressed against his throat, Monte managed to get out something. "That's not...the way..." said Monte through coughs and gasps of breath, "to talk...to your...boss, Nathan." Monte smirked up at his brother, knowing full well that Nathan would think better of what he was doing. Sure enough, Nathan released the choke hold on Monte, not wanting to risk losing his job. Leaning back against the locker, Monte tried to catch his breath, feeling the glare of Nathan. "What are you trying to prove, huh?" asked Nathan rhetorically. "Dad hasn't been in the ring in a long time. Hell, he might not even be able to physically make it through the match. He could have a heart attack!" Despite the strong words from his brother, Monte simply looked up and smiled. He wasn't going to relent on this one. Nathan, on the other hand, grew even more upset. "I don't care if you hate the man," Nathan said. "He's your father, Monte. He's the man who brought you up in this business. He's the man who gave you your start in this business. He's the man who gave you your chance to succeed in this business!" "Whoa, whoa, whoa," said Monte, raising his hand in the air. "Daniel Burns wasn't the one who gave me the chance to succeed. I gave myself the chance to succeed. I'm the one who put in all that time in the ring. I'm the one who pulled off some of the hottest spots this promotion has ever seen. This place wouldn't even be in business still if it wasn't for me." Hearing that statement from his brother, Nathan laughed out loud, obviously struck off-guard by the statement. Here was the man who was trying to destroy his father's company and legacy, talking about how he was the reason the promotion was still in business. The irony dripped from the ceiling. "You're unbelieveable, Monte," quipped Nathan sarcastically. "You are simply unbelieveable. Here you are, trying to ruin your father's career and his company, talking about how great you are. Well, let me tell you something. You may be a great athlete and a great wrestler..." Seeing Monte turn his head and roll his eyes, Nathan stepped directly in front of his brother and looked into his eyes. Monte looked back, and the two men stared silently at each other. Sweat slowly rolled down the sides of their faces, but neither man was willing to step back. Finally, Nathan leaned in closer. "You may be great," whispered Nathan, "but you're no Daniel Burns." Monte only smiled, clearly showing that he did not care about his brother's statement. He had heard it all before, from Nathan, John, Daniel, Sarah, and many other family members and friends. On the outside, he said he didn't care about the comparisons to his father. On the inside, Monte wanted to make sure he made a bigger impression than Daniel ever had. As Monte looked off to the side, Nathan shook his head and walked towards the door. Just before leaving, however, Nathan stopped and turned around to face Monte one more time. "One of these days, Monte," Nathan said softly, "you're going to regret all of this that you're doing." With that final statement, Nathan left the room and shut the door. Monte stood in the middle of the room for a moment, contemplating what Nathan had said. The thought had crossed his mind before, he wouldn't deny that. But this Monte Burns did not give much thoughts to regrets. He only gave thought to fame, glory, and triumph. To be continued...
