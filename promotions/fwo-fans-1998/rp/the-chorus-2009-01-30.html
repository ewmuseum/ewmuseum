The Chorus

30 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
There were no buses; No massive roadie caravans ferrying ring parts and signage into 18-wheeler trucks. Staff walked to their cars and drove out of the garage quietly, two hours earlier than they would if the FWO was touring. The organization ran a very tight ship, and with the economy what it was, the men and women departed from the arena filling their lungs with cold air and pride as they relished their employment, which, as it turned out, was easier than usual. An increased amount of cars rumbled to life in the distance, music to Brawn's ears as he stepped outside of the Key Arena. "Brawn!" He pulled his parka zipper to his chin as he walked off path towards a small crowd of people vibrating in the cold. His white smile pushed through the shadows as he met them at the fence, puffs of smoke coming and going. A boy reached through the slot with a program. Brawn pulled out a sharpie and began signing items randomly. "So what's the deal with The Flying Frenchie?" a voice asked. "Frenchie's a shoo-in Hall of Famer," Brawn replied as he reached for an FWO hat, grinning at a teenage girl. "I want to join him there someday." "You were doing fine without him!" a voice called out. Brawn looked sharply in a collection of large dark smudges to find the source. "After all, you beat him! You held your own against Garvin and Zimmerman. Hell, You made Flyer think twice about bailing in the main event. You made the Champion think twice." Brawn started grabbing the programs more aggressively and his wrist relaxed, not so concerned with rendering a pristine signature. He disguised his frown with difficulty. "I think it's a big mistake," a female voice chirped. Brawn remained silent. "I don't know whether to cheer ya or boo ya anymore," another interjected. A brief murmur of agreement droned from their discolored lips. Brawn slouched, visibly hurt. He looked around and noticed there weren't any other superstars to distract them. The jury was in. His nod and smile was weak. His stomach turned, his throat tightened. "I gotta go folks," Brawn choked out, capping his pen and pushing it in his pocket. "Drive safely." Most of them dispersed; no whining moans for him to stay. Brawn shook his head and walked back towards the lot. He heard a small voice behind him. "Heeey Braawn," the sing-song voice called. Brawn turned, and saw a little boy look up at his father for approval. When he got the nod, he perched on his toes but immediately fell back on his heels. He pressed his lips through a hole in the fencing, sighing deeply before his adenoidal voice rang out: "Beeee carefullll," he sung, laughing hysterically as if he had gotten away with something. Brawn threw his bag in the car trunk and shuddered. Maybe cold it was the cold. Maybe nerves. He couldn't decide.
