Sweet Child O' Mine - Five of Seven

23 January 2003

SWEET CHILD O’ MINE Part Five – TJ ’For others, such as Angela Davison of Valerian’s Garden, the road to musical success is a simple one. Become a fucktoy for a professional wrestler who earned his stripes.’ TJ threw the smarmy ‘zine back down on the chair he got it from. Good thing Angel hasn’t seen it… she’d go nuts. To say nothing of Eli. It’s like Eli and Ivy have always said: you can get noticed through personal connections, but you can only get respect through your actions. Speaking of respect… Mickey hasn’t said a word since they came in. “Penny for ya thoughts, Mick?” TJ didn’t expect an answer, but he got one anyway. The look Micky shot him spoke volumes. Since this is a usual running joke between the two – a long- standing insult contest – he was certainly surprised to see Mickey turn away again. “Go away, Teej.” “I would,” replied TJ, “but a buddy of mine looks like he’s not havin’ a good time. Know anything about it?” Mickey offered him a cigarette, forgetting that TJ quit a few weeks ago. Still, it’s the thought that counts. “Just dwellin’ on shit, TJ… you know how it is.” TJ sighed, sitting down in the chair next to the window. “Yeah, I know how it is. And I know you still blame a lotta shit on yourself… dude, ya need to stop that.” Mickey gave a fake- sounding laugh, obviously not convinced that TJ meant what he said. “I really did wrong by her, TJ,” confessed Mickey, “I don’t deserve to be here.” “Isn’t that her call? And didn’t she welcome you back last year?” “Only because she knew y’all needed those dates with the Misfits.” “Partly,” admitted TJ, “but don’t you think she kept ya around because she knew you weren’t the same guy that used to beat her up?” “But I am.” “Look… you were wrong to hit her. No denying that much. But you took getting fired and dumped like a man. You came back for the right reasons, and you haven’t drank in a year and a half. You don’t think she knows that?” “Maybe.” “Fuck maybe, Mick… you might’ve lost her personally but as a friend and integral member of this band, your spot’s not in question.” “Shit, man… you think I don’t know that?” Mickey had listened patiently but it was rapidly running out, “But with all this happening, I dunno. Just feeling sorry for myself or something.” TJ laughed. “Well, knock it off, ya bastard. I’m getting’ some coffee. You want?” “Heh, naah. I’m good.” TJ nodded, nothing else to say. He headed toward the cafeteria, dejected. Mickey might’ve been wrong so many times when it came to Angel, but he’d certainly done his best to make up for it. A lot of people could’ve been confused over why he’d go so far to defend a man that spent a lot of time drinking and treating his woman – TJ’s best friend – like shit. “TJ!” He turned around, to see Mickey following him toward the cafeteria. “Still feel like buyin’ me that cup of coffee?” Travis Johnson smiled. This is exactly why he defends Mickey. Because while the past is important, it’s also the past… and Mickey’s learned from it. People change.
