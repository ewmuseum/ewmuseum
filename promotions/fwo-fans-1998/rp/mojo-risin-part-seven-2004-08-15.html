Mojo Risin': Part Seven

15 August 2004

Mojo Risin' Chapter Seven - Never Said I Was Perfect "I don't care what time it is that we get to Williams," said Ivy, as she finished off her bottle of Red Gatorade, "I'm going to walk into the first greasy spoon I see and order a double bacon cheeseburger with a fried egg on top, onion rings with gobs and gobs of ketchup, and a bottomless - I do mean bottomless - cup of real coffee." "Sounds serious," replied Eli, as he took the bottle from her and put the empty in his bag, "And a hell of a lot better than the liquid lunch we're currently suckin' on." They'd been walking for two hours, talking about things that don't matter, talking about what they were looking forward to doing once they rejoined the human race, talking about the fWo and whether or not they had a hope in hell of getting to Orlando in time for the next show. Ivy was also relieved to hear that Eli did not, in fact, sign the exclusivity agreement that Simon Cowl told her he did. She had discussed with him the problems she had with the form, and he had the same ones. Most notably, the CSWA. Beyond that, there were a dozen other things that could potentially come up for the former Team Extreme. Doing nothing to help out OCW was a no- brainer, but they'd both recognized the need to not sign until the language in the contract itself was more clear. "Maybe not coffee," continued Ivy's daydream, "maybe a coffee milkshake." "Stop it," laughed Eli, "now you're just gettin' ridiculous." "Hey!" replied Ivy, as she touched her hand to her forehead and showed it to Eli, "Still bleeding, definitely concussed. Cut me some frickin' slack, boy." "Far be it for me to argue with my business advisor." "You'd have to be a psychopath to pick now as the first time to say that this year. So what're you gonna do when we hit town?" "First thing's first," replied Eli, "call Angel. Then I might have to steal an onion ring or two." "No way," said Ivy, "you get'cha'own plate. What do you crave right now?" He thought about it. "Corned beef. Man, a corned beef and pastrami club from Murray's down the street. Hot mustard on one side, russian dressing on the other. Kosher pickle, bag'a jalepeno chips, and a root beer." He looked at Ivy. "And maybe, just maybe, a slice'a cheesecake." "Damn you," replied Ivy, "now I don't care about my burger, I care about where I'll be eatin' it. Ghah, just think... right now I guarantee at least one'a the kids is eating a Staten Island bagel." "An everything bagel with cream cheese and OJ." "You're mean. I'm gonna tell Angel on you." "Yeah? Well I'm gonna tell...." Eli held up. "Damn." "Hah," taunted Ivy, "Can't tell on me when I'm bitter and single and alone." "Why is that, anyway?" asked Eli. "Cause there's nobody to tell, simple as that." Ivy danced a bit as she walked, and dipped her hands into her bag to feel the cold water and sparse handfuls of ice that remained. "That's not what I meant, Ivy," replied Eli, "I meant, why are you single? You certainly don't have to be." She stopped walking. "Don't ask me that, please." He turned and looked at her. "I asked, Ivy. Between yesterday and last night I thought we'd reached an understanding on disclosure." "It's... just hard," stammered Ivy, "If you really want to hear it I'll talk, but reluctantly." "Take your time," said Eli. "In a nutshell?" replied Ivy, "I'm in love with someone who doesn't feel the same." As if that was news to either of them. "Sean," said Eli. It was a statement of fact, not a question, "Why is that, anyways? Not to down him, he's always gonna be my boy, but you two separated a full year ago." Ivy gestured to him to start walking again, and she stayed at his side. He kept an eye on her, and noticed her trying to figure out exactly how to say it. "Like I said, take your time." "Thanks," replied Ivy, "Well, anyways, you pretty much know about every relationship I've had for the past dozen years or so, right? John Anthony cheated on me during my second two years at MIT, made it all the easier to leave school when I did, right? Then meeting and hooking up with Hornet about a year after jumpin' into the CSWA." "Right. You guys redefined 'on and off.' like nobody's business." "Imagine, if you will, going from someone like Paul who couldn't figure out a damn thing that ran through his head, to someone who used to be at constant odds in the locker room with ya." "Trip." "Remember, just after the four of us made our peace, when there was that horrible fire at Sean's building?" "Mmmmhmmmm." "He called me up and asked if he could crash with me for a few days in my Orlando studio." "That was a while ago... you were workin' fast, I take it?" "Sure," replied Ivy, "he slept on the couch. But we were up talking for hours at a time, about everything under the sun. That was about when the CWL was running shows in the States still, and you guys were gettin' pretty tight, too." "But I managed to never hook up with him. Must be my iron will." Ivy laughed at him and shoved him slightly into the middle of the still - deserted highway. "That was really where everything started, I'm serious." She laughed again. "It's funny now that I think about it. I made a pact with myself, that I'd never fall in love again, that I'd think before I acted or spoke. But Sean was so genuinely nice and he was so easy to talk to and get along with, and my head fit so perfectly on his shoulder when we were watching a movie or just hanging out, it was impossible to not start to feel something." "That's what happened with Angel," agreed Eli, "Complete and total love just kinda snuck up on both'a us." "So yeah, we did hook up a few times before we officially got together," continued Ivy, "but it was never anything more than just kissing. And then we lost touch that summer and fall, he and Linda Johns had their fling, and I didn't even talk to him again until that night in the fWo locker room when we met up after the Frenchie match." "You two had quite a lot t'talk about, though," remarked Eli, "left me in the dust at about three in the AM." "It was a sign of things to come," said Ivy, a goofy grin spread across her face, "we would do that over and over again. Just talk, y'know? Hang out in the locker room and BS about whatever was on my mind, on his mind, or what was going on at the time. But we didn't really spend time alone until that night in February." "I remember that," said Eli, "We were in Germany I think." "Mmmmhmmmm," said Ivy, "I could actually speak to the fans but Sean was lost. We shared a cab back to the hotel and ended up looking at old pictures in his room. It was just one of those things, y'know?" "I know how things can happen," agreed Eli, "you guys seemed perfect for each other. Finally had a dude who understood the business." "Well, it helped that he was in the business too," admitted Ivy, " but yeah, Sean understood what it meant to me to be Poison Ivy. And when we hooked up it was like that fairytale romance, the one everyone wants to work. You know, what Ben-Lo tried to be but were too fucking pretentious to ever have a hope in hell at." "True."
