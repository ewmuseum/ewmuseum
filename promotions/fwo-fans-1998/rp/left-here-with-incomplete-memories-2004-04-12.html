Left here with incomplete memories

12 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Brian Wisniewski stood with his bulky body weighted against the inside of the doorframe. He remained expressionless and stoic, except for the light glaze that covered his eyes. Every now and then a tear would escape and run down his unshaven, stubbled face. Brian's glazed eyes were fixed out in the distance, watching the woman who meant everything in the world to him walk away. Thousands of thoughts ran through his head going full speed. There were a million questions he wanted to be answered. What happened? Is it my fault? Is it your fault? What could I have done? What could we have done? Brian didn't understand. He couldn't possibly understand what had happened in the short span of the weekend. Truly, now everything was gone. She was hesitating as she walked away. Her steps were slow, heavy, weighted down by emotion. Brian used all the energy he had left to stand pat. Things had gone too far for them to ever be the same again. All he wanted to do was call out to her and say that everything could be okay again. Maybe if she did, she would turn around and come back to him. He also knew that if he did that he would have to see the look on her face. If she looked at all like he did, he wouldn't be able to bear the sight. It had been nearly three years ago when they first met. She was the most beautiful person he had ever come across. There was something inside both of them that they shared. It was unexplainable, no matter what would happen to either of them, they would find a way to each other and talk. All of Brian's problems were solved through her. Most of her issues were solved through him. Together they leaned on each other for support, it had always worked that way. Neither of them could have ever seen it coming to this. Brian watched his support, his life walking slowly away from him and Brian was powerless. For the first time in so long, Brian felt weak. Brian would always remember what he was told when she had finally grown the courage to talk to him on a regular basis. She told him that she had seen him working one summer night and couldn't help but watch him. There was something about him that had her mesmerized and he never knew it. It wasn't until two or three months later that they met for the first time. Brian was awestruck by how attractive she was. He remembered how incredibly smitten he was and how he wanted to get closer to her before he even knew her name. However, in time it would just be a passing thought. Another tear rolled down the side of Brian's face. All the strength he had once had through her had crumbled and disappeared. She told him that she couldn't have him ruining her happiness. That's when she stood up and walked out the door. Brian wanted to fight back, argue until he was blue in the face. She called him difficult, he never shared any of his problems. He knew things had deteriorated over the past few months, but he hadn't known how much. If only she had said something. Brian knew it wasn't his fault that he couldn't get his emotions across to her. There was too much lurking beneath the surface. All the words he wanted to say to her, it would be impossible to choke them all out before she was gone forever. He wanted to apologize for everything and anything he ever did to make her unhappy, anything that made her hate him. Even if it wasn't hate, that's what it felt like to him now. It was cold and lonely standing against the doorframe. There were some words that Brian wanted to say that he hoped could turn it all around. Sorry had become standard form and it lost all meaning in any trite conversation the two of them shared. There were words he always felt strongly about and he thought she had maybe felt them too, but he couldn't bring himself to say them. Brian always had wanted to tell her that he loved her. Though he had always said those words were overused and should have never needed to be said between two people, he had strongly had the urge to say it to her. As much as Brian hated those three words, he wanted to get them out to her one day before she ultimately left. The one night he got them out, everything had lost all meaning and they were on opposite ends of frayed rope. He was heavily intoxicated when he said them, there was no feeling from her end and he left. He had nothing left to say after that. "We can still save the friendship," her voice was soft and it wavered. Brian watched her as she said this. He knew in his heart they could, but it would take months, maybe years of work to repair the egg they had cracked. Brian sat across from her and shook his head. Not now, it was nearly impossible to save it right now. Besides, what's the point of saving a friendship? Brian looked at her with his glazed eyes, unknowing that she would walk away forever in just a few short seconds. "Not now," he breathed. "It's almost impossible to save it right now. Besides, what's the point in saving it? It's so far gone right now, we'll never be the same. We'll never be as close as we once were. There's nothing either of us can do to salvage what we once had. I for one don't want a broken model, it just doesn't compare to the real thing. At least not now, there's too many feelings I have that I need to let go of. Maybe then, maybe someday we can be what we once were." She didn't say anything. She stood up and walked out the door. That's where Brian now stood, watching her leave. He put his head down into his chest, fighting back the tears that would ultimately come. Brian wished he knew where it all went wrong. The things he had just said made her walk away. Brian knew she would be gone unless he did something to bring her back. He snapped his head up and closed his eyes. He ran out onto the porch and screamed. "HEATHER!" It was too late. She was gone. And so was he.
