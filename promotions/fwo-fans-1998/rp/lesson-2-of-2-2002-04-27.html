Lesson. (2 of 2)

27 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"What the... where am I?" A slightly familiar voice answered, something Wasner did not expect. "Why, Mr. Wasner, you're the same place you were last time you looked." "What?" "Open your eyes." He did. He was standing on the cliff road. The Viper was dangling precariously off the edge, but somehow, he was here. Well, thank God for small favors. Although I suppose that's not really a small favor. Wasner looked at the man standing beside him. Oddly enough, he recognized the man right away. "John Glover?" The man looked just like the famous character actor, late of Brimstone. He was dressed in a business suit and tie, his hair was slicked back, and a grin was on his face. "Well... yes and no." "What?" "I suppose you would say John Glover's just what you're seeing." "So who or what are you?" "That's not really important. Maybe I'm an angel, maybe I'm a demon, maybe you're floating towards the bottom of the Pacific Ocean right now and I'm your mind creating an elaborate illusion to desperately stave off death. Could be any of those. But that's not important. What is important, is that you have a lesson you need to learn, Mr. Wasner. Consider me your teacher." "What if I don't want to learn your lesson? I've got better things to do, dammit." Wasner sneered. Glover - for lack of a better identifier - laughed heartily. "Oh, but you don't. You don't at all. You see, that's the thing. All your life you've been avoiding this, but you don't have a choice anymore. I'm here to make you take a good look at yourself. A real good look." Wasner shook his head. "I've looked at myself. What's your point?" "Oh, but have you really?" "Yeah. I have. It ain't much." "Ah ah ah, and there's your problem. Your self-respect, my friend, is in the dumper. Buck up, little trooper!" The grin remained on Glover's face. Wasner rolled his eyes. "Let me get this straight. Here I am, getting a vision or whatever the hell this is, and all I'm getting out of it is 'self-respect'? Crap, if I wanted to hear that I'd go to a damn shrink. And... 'little trooper'?" "Ah, but you haven't, have you? Gone to a shrink, that is. You've got what we in the biz refer to as issues, my friend." "So what if I do? Everybody has issues. You live with them." "Ah, you say that, but you don't. It's really a fascinating little double life you lead, Mr. Wasner. In the ring, you're Scott Slugger, conquering hero, afraid of nothing, facing all your problems head-on and beating them at that! Then, outside the ring." Glover shook his head. "Tsk tsk tsk. You can't handle anything, anything at all. It's pathetic, really." Wasner went to take a step forward to swing at Glover, but found he could not. He was stuck in place. Glover wagged a finger at him. "Uh-uh-uh. No hitting the spiritual guide, says so in the rulebook." Glover paused a moment, resting his chin in his hands in a thinking position. "But you know... before one can deal with a problem, one has to identify the source of the problem. Tell me, Mr. Wasner... what's your problem? The source, that is." "You?" Glover threw back his head in a hearty laugh, as this appeared to truly amuse him. He shook his head kindly. "Oh, Mr. Wasner... you're a real firebrand, you know that? But, you know what the source is. You just don't want to admit it. I'll admit it for you. It's your business, Scott. It's wrestling. Think about it. You go out there every night and you abuse your body for fifteen, twenty, thirty minutes, then you go backstage to a dangerous atmosphere... and you can't resist the temptations. If you remove the temptations... I think you'll find yourself on the first steps of the path to recovery. But then, that's just my theory, and what do I know?" "Yeah. Really." The two stood in silence for a few moments. "So... what are you saying, I should quit wrestling?" "I don't think I'm really in a position to say what you should or should not do, Mr. Wasner. All I'm doing is appraising you of your situation. You already knew some of it - your constant bitching, for example, that you'd been given, what, two, three opportunities, something along those lines, and you'd blown them, for example - but my job is to fill in the blanks. The things you missed." "I see. So if your job is to fill in my blanks, fill in this one - who are you? What are you?" Glover's grin grew even wider. "Oh, no no no no no. Fill in your own blank there, Mr. Wasner." "So... so far you've given me a bunch of random, sorta-kinda-but-not-really-sensical babbling and a few vague recommendations. I'm just gonna ask this once. Why... the fuck... are you here?" Nothing from Glover. Then: "If you continue to wrestle, Mr. Wasner, you will be dead within six months. You're not invincible, no matter what you may think. This is not a prediction, or a warning, but a simple statement of fact. Do with it as you will. My work here is done. See ya around - well, hopefully not, considering." There was a flash of light, causing Wasner to close his eyes. ----- Saturday, April 27, 2002 Laguna Cliffs, California 12:01 AM PST When he opened them again, he was greeted by a sight that, all things considered, he certainly did not wish to see again. He was looking straight down the steep edge of a cliff into the Pacific Ocean below. The water lapped at the side of the cliff, churning into white foam and then returning back to whence it came. I don't know how long that drop is, and I don't care to find out. He moved his arms, checking them out. Everything seemed to be intact. He felt around for the rest of his body. Everything was still there. He heard a loud noise to his right and turned his head. The Viper had been balanced on the edge of the cliff, and now it pitched forward and fell off. It turned end-over-end on the way down, a truly bizarre vision. It seemed to fall forever. Finally, it plunged head-first into the Pacific, disappearing into the blue with a splash that Wasner could barely hear. It kicked up water for a good distance on all sides. Wasner continued to stare down for a moment, then shook his head and used his palms to push himself up, pushup style. He stood and looked over the edge of the cliff. He had been there himself, surely on the verge of going over. Now, he could feel, he was beginning to take steps back. Wasner turned himself in the direction he had been driving. This is gonna be one motherfucker of a walk. He imagined he would not sleep very much that night.
