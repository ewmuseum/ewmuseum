Don't blow it, go above it.

5 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
There's so much negative energy in this life...every single word spoken by every politician, every "gangsta" rapper, every sad, snobby little emo kid clutching his Saves The Day CD. Everybody's got an opinion, and nobody wants to make it a good one, but they all want theirs to count. Among all of these negative energies, there are a few people who can, on occasion, think clearly enough to walk above and beyond it. Whatever happens to those people? The last person who saw the world for the good in it was literally crucified, and the few who look for what's best today get the same thing, only it becomes tenfold if they succeed. Some escape that fate and go farther above it, though... There're a lot of false images in this life...a lot of men and women trying to act "tough" and "hardcore" when really, they're the same person you step past on the sidewalk daily, some just find more of a drive to stick out in the crowd. So they become an individual, they develope an actual opinion and a good one at that. Whatever happens to those people? The last individual you saw got his ass kicked in high school, he's probably laying on the same sidewalk you passed him on, this time with a broken nose thanks to a few close-minded "thugs". But still, some escape that fate... That's when there comes a time in the world when each and every individual, negative, crucified or otherwise needs a big change, when the track they're on becomes too much to handle and it has to do a 180. During this time, the negative people and the false images do a 360, ending up right back where they were, only a hundred times as frusterated...but a few, the positive people, the true individuals, they have enough drive and strength to stay when they hit that turnaround. Whatever happens to those people? Find out, look at Amy Campbell... At twenty years old, she rose above her false image and the negative energy, no longer afraid to be ridiculed. That's when she "jumped" to fWo...it was time to finally do something for herself, as opposed to keeping up an image to sell and cause as little trouble as possible. The controversy was there, but Amy harbored no ill feelings. Unfortunately, the name-calling was there, too...the "Look at Aimz, I wonder who she had to screw to get this gig", the people who wanted to denounce her work, those who chose not to accept that the girl who once had absolutely nothing and was respected less than all the other wrestling 'eye candy', was finally going places and earning that respect. Was it jealousy? Could be. Feeling of betrayal for leaving others 'behind'? Possibly. What was it, really? Too much thought being put into something so simple; The decision wasn't made for any elaborate plot, no complex reasoning...simple logic: If you can do what you love and get respect for it, go any place that'll let you. Regardless, Amy just looked right past those people now...listening to criticism was one thing, but why listen when the people have no basis for it, other than their own personal opinions soley based upon assumptions? If someone could have given her a reason to not make the change that wasn't "It's selling out" or "You'll be eaten alive", she might have listened. Instead, the opportunity of her lifetime had come along and she'd taken it...as much as it might end up costing her in reputation, there were changes being made and there was absolutely no way she'd fall victim to negativity or fake people all over again. The days of surrounding herself with 'Yes'-men and liars were through, as were the times when she'd sit around all day and mope because the world didn't think she was exactly what she wanted them to...it was all over, time to finally be herself in the public eye, whether every little critic liked it or not. Let the world think she's not ready to work among legends, let the legends themselves think she's not ready...what the hell is that supposed to matter if she think, no, KNOWS she's ready? The old guys didn't like the new guys, the new guys wanted to be like the old guys...Amy just wanted to wrestle and earn her salary, not get drawn into the trivial garbage that littered the wrestling business. The backstage trials and tribulations were over for her, it was time to live life however she enjoyed it, instead of tip-toeing around certain people because they had a bad reputation, or associating with so and so to snake into the main event of whatever. Wrestling was about wrestling, not reputation. fWo had a reputation, but it was about wrestling...and the outside world was what it had always been: all about controversy. Was it all that controversial to want to be yourself for once? Sure, because nobody was ever allowed to be themselves in the world...especially not if they were in the public eye. You always had to conform to one genre, or have some kind of 'gimmick' so you'd appeal to a specific demographic. You were either Britney Spears or Avril Lavigne, East coast or West coat, a 'gangster' or a 'pimp', hard or soft...none of that was for Amy, because she's what she wants to be, whether it's a mixture or something off-the-wall, the only thing her life's 'gimmick' is ever specific to is her mood. What else is she? Sitting alone in her car, listening to the usual brand of generic radio rock 'n roll, more specifically an ancient Depeche Mode song she's whistling along to while waiting for someone, God knows who, in the parking lot of an Atlantic Superstore. A moment or so later, a tall, blonde girl stepped into the passenger's seat, shivering from the early winter air. She didn't look a thing like Amy, so it was plain to see the girl wasn't related, but something in her eyes said they were close, possibly closer than family. She stared intently at the blank expression on Aimz' face as the song ended, and 'Dave Chaulk's Sport's Talk' came on. "Something wrong, Ames?" "Shh.", Amy growled. Her ears were perked to the radio as the snarky personality gave his opinion on everything in the sport world ranging from baseball trades to...wrestling. That's when her eyes narrowed, she found herself listening to yet another opinion on her decision...just before the other woman in the car reached for the stereo knob, swiftly tuning it to some bland French channel. "Why torture yourself with this crap?" Amy just stared forward, almost snarling. "I know..." The girl said, "They're all idiots, forget 'em or you'll find yourself preaching to the wrong choir." It was Renee, her best friend and, as always, she was right. Somehow, she always managed to convince Aimz away from the negative, being an entirely positive person herself, and a huge influence on the little redhead. So, for what seemed like the thousandth time in the past week, Aimz smiled, shrugged and simply said "You're right." Things got better every day, no matter how bad they seemed.
