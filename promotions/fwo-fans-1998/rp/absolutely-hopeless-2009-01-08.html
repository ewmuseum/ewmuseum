Absolutely Hopeless

8 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"The WGA? Dude, I was on the FWO website the other day, in this computer café-like place, just perusing the Interwebz, when I spotted this quote by this Scott Slugger guy. Apparently he's the WGA. At least, he claimed to be the WGA. I think I'm going to go and hang out at FWO shows for a while. Keep a low-pro, make a few bucks if possible, maybe get to know the owner's dirty secrets and then blackmail him like "GOTCHA, NIGGA! PAY ME BIG MONEY OR ELSE!" I've got plans, aspirations, dreams." Lowell had shown up at Corey Jepson's apartment complex between the hours of five and six A.M. He entered (the door was unlocked), unannounced, and made himself a cup of tea. He sat down and watched a little television. Then, a few hours later, he surprised Corey as he made his way to the bathroom to take a shit. Corey shit, all right -- earlier than he would've preferred. Corey stared at Lowell, wondering whether or not he was actually legally retarded, now. It'd been a number of years since they'd met face to face, or spoken at all for that matter, and he'd underestimated what chronic drug abuse can do to a person's brain over a such a long period of time. Lowell's brand of idiocy was astonishing, to say the least. "Uh, actually, I was talking about the Writer's Guild of America. I registered my screenplay a little while back." Lowell looked dumbfounded. "What the hell are you talking about? What the fuck is a screenplay?" "Is that a real question?" Corey replied, bluntly. Lowell crossed his arms, as he waited for an answer. "Well, if you recall, I would bring a typewriter with me to our meetings in the park." Corey instantly recognized the need to paint an even clearer picture in Lowell's drug ravaged mind. "The park across the street from the deli where I bought you lunch every day." "Ah, yes… I like sandwiches. We should go get a couple right now." Lowell's stomach grumbled, tormenting him with hunger pains. "Shut up, stomach, I'm working on getting you fed! … So, sandwiches -- how `bout it?" "Sure. I mean, we can -- just not right this instant, okay? I'd really like to tell you the good news, first." "Good news?" Lowell was intrigued. He stretched back on the shabby, thrift store couch. "Yeah. It's sort of old news now, but then again I had no way of getting a hold of you. You were homeless, after all. You're… still… homeless, aren't you?" His eyes scanned the ragamuffin seated before him: tattered, dirty clothes; unkempt facial hair; sickly thin. He nodded. "Uh, anyway, I didn't think I'd ever see you again. I figured you were, like, dead or something. I mean, you were strung-out pretty severely the last time I saw you. You were mashing up sugar cubes, thinking they were crack rocks, and snorting them through freshly shed snake skin. It was a trip, man. Weirder than anything I've ever seen, and I used to do a hell of a lot of LSD when I was in high school. Everyone I try and tell about you is convinced I'm making you up." "Dude, you should make me up. Seriously. I carry the aroma of pee around with me wherever I go, and two month's worth of dried sweat and strange, unidentifiable grime has turned my hair into a helmet. It's hard -- go ahead, feel!" he said, before rapping on his `do with his knuckles. "That's okay. I can see my reflection in your quaff. Very helmet-like, indeed." Corey didn't want to have to try and explain to Lowell that he was, in fact, wearing a helmet. Lowell had stolen it from a kid a while back, shortly after fleeing the scene of the accident that allowed him to escape the Establishment and the horrible, unthinkable tortures that awaited him. Using Lowell Logic, he figured that if someone were to sneak up behind him and whack him in the back of the head with a sock full of quarters, he'd fall to the ground, wait for them to get close, then flip himself over, kick them in the groin, perform a backwards roll to a crouch position, and finish them off with a Shoryuken that would propel them six or seven feet in the air, allowing him to "juggle" them Street Fighter-style with a plethora of kicks, punches, and special attacks -- maybe even a Hadouken. Fireballs rule. "Yeah, I sweat a lot when I can't have my nose-yummies," Lowell said, with an uneasy chuckle. "I can see that," responded Corey. "So, the screenplay --" "The words." "Yes." "The words that tell my story?" "That's right." "What about 'em?" Corey smiled. He was twenty-six years old, single, living in a dingy apartment. His 110-page screenplay, chronicling the misadventures of Lowell and his partners-in-crime as they attempt to intercept a drug shipment in South California, was a labor of love. Having it read by a film studio exec was a dream come true. Having it potentially made into a movie? Mindblowingly surreal. "Lowell," Corey began, crouching down on the floor to meet Lowell at eyelevel, "we may have a buyer." Risen eyebrows, conveying bewilderment, produced a crease in his forehead. "Are we selling drugs? `Cause if we're selling drugs, I'll require a gat. Not a semi-automatic gat, just a plain ol' do-dirt gat. Nothing that'll get me put away for life. Oh, and I'm going to have to sample the goods." Lowell pulled his pant leg up to reveal a length of snake skin half-tucked in his sock. "Line me up, daddy!" Corey's head went back, as he let out a heavy sigh. "Dammit, Lowell! This isn't about drugs! It's about you and I potentially making a shitload of money off my hard work! You should be grateful I'm even including you in all of this! It's not like I have to! People don't even believe me when I say you're an actual person, for Christ's sake!" He took a deep breath to calm himself. "I'm trying to be nice, here. I know you don't have a lot of money. I know your life's shit. I also know you're to blame for it. I'm not saying any of this is going to happen. The studio hasn't made me an offer as of yet, and who knows if they ever will? They seem interested, though, so we'll see where that takes us. Do you understand?" Lowell stared off into space, as his brain struggled to compute the torrent of information. "So, what you're saying is… I could get a lot of money out of this…" Evil, Self-Destructive Part of Brain: "Money can be used to purchase drugs!" Sensible Part of Brain: "Please, no! No more! We can't take it, dammit! So many causalities! So many perfectly good brain cells lost! Don't you understand? This is suicide!" Evil, Self-Destructive Part of Brain: "You'll shut up, Sensible, if you know what's good for ya!" Evil, Self-Destructive Part of Brain's Goons: *Shakes fists* Sensible Part of Brain: "I'm sorry. I'll go back to disposing of Lowell's knowledge of sewing… Oh, how he used to love sewing…" Corey began to look restless as he waited for Lowell to finish his thought. "…And money can buy drugs! Sweet!" Corey groaned, and ran his hand over his face. "You're absolutely hopeless."
