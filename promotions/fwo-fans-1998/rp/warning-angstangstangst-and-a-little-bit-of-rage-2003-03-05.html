5 March 2003

Amy was rarely ever noticed by 'fans' on the street, probably because she dressed like almost anyone else like her: Usually chains and any comfortable pants, band t-shirts, tanktops or hoodies. Around home, though, EVERYONE recognized her...just because she was one of the three or four girls - the rest being close friends of hers - who actually looked like that. They never approached for 'autographs' or any of that celebrity crap, though; The usually energetic but angsty and melancholy little redhead was a regular sight and had been for twenty years. But recently she'd surprised them. Whenever Amy was home in recent memory, she'd been walking around with a smile. Of course, in a relatively small area news travelled fast, so her being content for the first time with Brandon Roche was really no secret, and the fact that she'd rid herself of a few 'bad habits' to boot was news to most she'd gone to high school with. That smile always seemed to fade when she was near her parents, always had too...they were the usual brand of anal retentive, rather rich 'suburban royalty'. One of her brothers was employed by Microsoft, another had a high-paying career in computers with Michelin, the other co-owned his own car outlet. One sister managed a small pub and had a longtime boyfriend with - again - a high-paying job with a company that distributed to restaraunts, the other was married to a man with rank within the military who came from a rich family etc. etc. etc... In short, Amy - the pro wrestling middle child - was doomed to always be under par. She dated people like her friends normally: 'Scruffy' artistic types, musicians and a wrestler who turned out to be yet another in a series of men into that whole macho overcompensation trend. All disasters and Amy could somewhat understand why her parents had issues there, but she was twenty years old...she finally seemed to have found someone GOOD for her, and she had a pretty well-paying job, albeit not what her parents wanted her to do... ...And that was the running problem. She was home for a few days, and had been discovered at a Wendy's restaraunt by her little brother who was unfortunately with her parents. They invited themselves to sit with her and as always an argument ensued - her fourteen year-old brother had brought up how 'awesome' it was that she was with a wrestling legend in his eyes. Sarcastic commentary by her dad followed and the gloves were off... "Wow, sorry that I didn't want to be in university until I was like thirty just so I could sit at some fucking desk for the rest of my life." Three minutes in. Ten minutes: "Hey, here's an idea: Just because I didn't run off to date and marry some stock broker so we could have our two point five kids and I could be at home where I'm apparently supposed to be, it's no reason for me not to be allowed to have some kind of a relationship with my motherfucking father. You can't just close me out because I'm doing what makes me happy. Just because you don't see it as 'successful', or some portrait of the average North American girl doesn't mean it's no good. JUST because I can't be like you - oh Bill Gates of the courier world - doesn't mean I'm some fucking useless whore." Naturally, until he could think of a comeback, all Amy got was "Don't talk to me like that.". Her brother and mom had already changed tables. Before her father had the chance to open his mouth with whatever he'd thought up to shoot her down again, Amy stood up. "And if you think for one fucking SECOND that I'm going to just lean over and quit doing what I'm doing just to kiss your ass and be something I'm not, you might as well just stop even acknowledging that I'm related to you if you haven't already done that, because you stand a snowball's chance in hell of changing me." Again, before he could even speak..."That's all that needs to be said, stop trying to break my neck to fit what you consider being productive and happy. If you love me or whatever you're always trying to apologize with, then you'll learn to hold your ego down and let me live without your stupid control issues." Corny? Yep. Truth? Nothing but. Amy just had no idea how many people had the exact same problems, and probably much kinder ways of dealing with them... But, as usual, Amy wasn't one to deal with things in the nicest or most logical ways - she prefered saying things straight from the heart as the words came no matter what those words were or how they could be interpreted...she was one of the only entirely honest people she knew.
