Previous Parts: downfall archive In small towns like New Iberia, the dark blanket of night was always accompanied with the pinhole visions of stars. Towering skyscrapers, cars, and other unnatural forms did not interfere with nature. Townspeople evolved to fit their surroundings, and that's what made the place so beautiful. The smell of mist and soggy wood overcame the night air. Bare trees surrounded a natural clearing. Fallen leaves and patches of grass covered the wet and lovely grass. In the midst of all this natural beauty, all creatures paled in comparison. Oaks and cottonwoods dwarfed the red car. Corners of a homemade quilt dangled from the roof. Trent's hand wandered blindly and found Diana. He touched her leg and stared silently into the stars. The sound of a nearby stream was alone in its audible state. It was 1993. "Everything about this place is beautiful. I remember the first time I came here with my family when I was six. Even at that age, it gave me a romantic feeling and I knew that the next time I came here, it would have to be with someone else I truly cared about." A small pause filled the air. "Diana, I never want you to be alone. I never want things to go back to the way they were for you, and I never want things to go back to the way they were for me. I know you'll never leave me, but the potential for the pain and disappointment is there." "I'll never leave you," Diana whispered. Trent pushed himself up to his side and placed his lips against hers. The environment made this kiss seem all the more special. After one year together, they still had the same passion and worry that fill those first fiery months of ever relationship. Trent rolled off the top of the car and tugged on the quilt quickly. His overpowering strength dragged Diana off the roof of the car and into his arms. A proud smile crossed his face as he walked around the edge of the car and placed her inside. He wanted to protect her. He wanted to keep her away from all of the hurt and pain in the world. The car ride home was short. Most of the time was spent slowly trudging across broken branches and moist soil. The automobile turned and entered the driveway of Diana Moorefield. Trent turned the off the engine and walked around the front of the car. He opened the door and dramatically outstretched his hand, smiling. Diana smiled and stepped out of the car. The house was completely dark, devoid of any signs of life. An eerie feeling overcame Diana as she turned the brass knob. The faint sounds of a background television became apparent. Speeches and lectures accompanied her slow walk toward the luminescent bedroom. Color changing light emptied through the crack in the under the door. With much reluctance, she threw the door open. The light spilled into the dining room and the visions flooded the corners of Diana's mind. Drained beer cans and empty pizza boxes covered the once white carpet. A sole recliner sat in the middle of the room, Scott Moorefield perched on top like a vulture watching pray. On the ground was a fallen angel in the body of her mother, unconscious but breathing. Trent followed into the room and placed his hand on Diana's shoulder. For some unbeknownst reason, he wasn't surprised. The glazed and dreary eyes of her father slid upward toward the vision of the gleeful teenage couple. "I think you should go home, Trent," Diana said with a mournful tone in her voice. With a newfound sense of apathy, Trent pecked her on the cheek and left with a smile. The assurance in her voice made everything seem safe no matter how damaging the oncoming train wreck would be.
