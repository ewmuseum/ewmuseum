falling together

18 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A small black box blinked ten thirty-one. Always a prime number. Trent reached over and threw his jacket on with little care. January daylight had become a cold and unwelcome gray no one wanted. Nevertheless, he walked out of the meager apartment and down the corridor. Neighbors had been surprised by his activity; shut-ins were usually condemned to households and other private areas. The elevator chimed as a red luminescent arrow pointed down and the metallic doors parted. Empty, as usual. An entire complex filled with unsociables and unnecessaries. It was almost a heirarchy--the least important and the most aware were on the bottom. Of course, there were exceptions. Trent'd be sporting a basement apartment if not. Twenty floors cycled downward before he was able to exit the damp and pale inside. There was no lobby; the elevator exited to another door leading outside. Salt staines covered the concrete pathway. Black spikes of metal and glass shot into the gray skies and broke the barrier of clouds. In the distance, a muffled sun struggled to break through. Slaves to stocks, businesses, and white-collared America walked the streets in uniform black. Inmobile mouths made for a loud scenery. The voices were unaccounted for, but Trent's eyes were fixed on the silent ones. He watched with a sort of fascination as everyone tip-toed on cracked ice. No one dare say a word, as if it were taboo to speak in such conditions. After a few intersections, the amount of cars and the apperance of the general populous began to die down. Trent stopped for a moment and straightened his slouch. His pale skin matched the pale sky. He turned his eyes to the nearby building. A reflection stared back at him--one with sad gray eyes. Nothing had changed. His movements were almost mechanic as he had made this trip many times in the past weeks. "Chicago Public Library" The inside was nothing extravagant. Walls of gray and shelves of worn books. No one read anymore and few people cared to learn about anything. The few people that did come there were too apathetic to their surroundings to try and change a thing. It was a place of silence and free literature. Trent walked down carpeted isles and eyed famous novels. He passed them by and grabbed a French book that looked as if it hadn't been touched. He placed the plain brown book on a table and sat in an empty corner. A black match walked into the place. Five foot nine and decked out in skin-tight clothes. Her attire was louder than she was. The woman caught the eye of a few shy and older men. None of them were any of her interest, but she did have a thing for the shy and quiet kind of guy. The kind that read French romance novels and sat in the corner just so he wouldn't be bothered. Of course, Trent's plan had failed. A hand as pale as his face touched the table. He did not look up from the text. "Hello," spoke a quiet whisper. Kali kept her voice quiet as to not taint the beautiful silence. She enjoyed the sounds of turning pages and the occasional dry cough. Trent looked up from his novel and saw the familiar face. He nodded in acknowledgement and went back to his reading. Kali sat down and crossed her wrists in her lap. She folded her lips and felt the smooth black lipstick cause them to slide. She never became impatient--she felt perfectly content watching the eyes of the man adjacent scoure pages she knew she wouldn't understand. For a moment, she felt like asking him to read to her, but then she remembered who he was. Trent looked up from his book and placed it on the table for a moment. He stared back at the quiet Kali. Her eyes remained fixed on his. "Those sad eyes must have been witness to a lot." Quiet became even more quiet. The silent man picked up the book and continued to read, but then put the book down for a moment and walked away from the table. Kali sat there with her confusion and pondered what she had done wrong. Later, Trent returned with a pencil and a small piece of paper used to write down call numbers for books people were looking for. The piece of paper remained next to Kali as Trent read once more. "902.63," she said aloud. After walking through the corridors, searching call number after call number, she found a small red book that was also in French. She sat on the floor and crossed her legs, placing the open book in her lap. "Vous pouvez venir avec moi," was scratched into the front cover in jagged pencil markings. Kali had no idea what it meant and when she returned to the table to ask for a translation. Trent had departed, leaving his romance novel at page thirteen. Always prime.
