The Martyr

10 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The clicking of the label gun was familiar, but not at all welcome. Then again, the routine never did much interest Eugene Marder. The clock on the wall read 2:47, but Eugene could've sworn it'd been stuck there for hours. "Thirteen minutes to freedom," he thought. "And then what?" The question was a valid one. What awaited in his apartment? Maybe he could spend a couple hours on his screenplay before calling it a night. "The Ark," it was called. He'd been working for months. It was really very interesting. The tale of a modern-day Noah's Ark- who would be taken, who wouldn't. He had a couple friends in the business who promised to shop it around to a few studios, and maybe get him a deal. That was certainly something to look forward to, especially since his job as an engineer was going nowhere. "Oh, well," he thought, "suits me right for going into such a specialized field." The clock loomed over him. 2:51. Eleven more and he'd be out of there. He yawned. The lack of sleep in the past few weeks was starting to catch up. "Must be all that work on the movie," he thought to himself. Was it really worth it? Would the movie get him out of the cramped studio apartment on the bad side of town? He couldn't rightly carry on his life in that place. Not much to show the ladies, and he always felt constricted when writing. He figured he had to live within his means, though. Not for much longer, with the way "The Ark," was going, anyway. It wouldn't end up like the book. Vision was a book Eugene had started. It was nothing more than a hobby, really. A silly tale about a town where, because of a genetic bottleneck, the entire population was blind. He lost the drive to write it about halfway through, but it wasn't going anywhere, to begin with. Just an experiment. 2:55. It was almost time. Eugene took off his apron blue apron with the "Lucky 7 Mini-Mart" logo that was slowly peeling away. Underneath the logo, it said "Your Last Stop." He felt a pang of hunger as his stomach rumbled, prompting him to remember he hadn't eaten anything since the breakfast burrito that morning. It wasn't his favorite, but it was all he could afford. He got up from his squatting position, and ungracefully twirled the gun around his finger, dropping it in the process. It looked intact under the fluorescent glow of the lights. He walked over to the door and flipped the switch by it, not even having to look. After a few months of working there, everything was automatic. The cold illumination was gone, but he'd always thought the room looked more inviting in the moonlight, anyway. With his hand on the doorknob, he looked at the clock again. 2:57. Ray never liked it when Eugene left early, so he decided to sit down, leaning his back against the metal door. Where was he going? To the dirty, tiny apartment? To his laptop, a present from his father when he went away to the university? To the bed that folded out of the wall; the bed devoid of any warmth? To the pillow which constantly smelled like Jessica's hair? For years, he couldn't get the smell away. "Have I ever even tried?" He looked at his name-tag in the soft light of the moon, shining through the sole window in the room. He hadn't noticed how hot it was prior to that moment. The tag said "Gene." Ray had always called him "Gene," though Eugene hated the name. Truthfully, Marder didn't care much. The job was only part of a transitional period. He'd get some money, finish his movie, and become a success. "I'm deluding myself," he said aloud. "Again." Nothing would ever change. "The Ark," would never be finished. It'd be filed away with his pile of shit book. He chuckled. With his stint in professional wrestling. Eugene as a professional wrestler. A bitter smile adorned his face, light reflecting off of his teeth. "Martyr." All over a girl. Another transitional phase. He kicked his leg out in front of him, hitting a shelf and sending glass jars of pickles toppling to the floor. Shards of glass flew everywhere. Maybe some were stuck in his leg. He didn't care. His mind was moving faster than his environment. He was perpetually stuck in a transitional phase. His clock was stuck somewhere between adolescent ignorance and middle-aged exasperation. But the Lucky 7 would not be his Last Stop, as it was for so many others. He was tired, but he was also hungry and burning up. This would not be how his book was written. As he stood and placed his hand on the doorknob for the second time, the door opened without the use of his force. "What the shit is going- oh, fuck, what happened to you?" Ray, toothpick hanging out of his gluttonous mouth, was staring at the miniscule pieces of glass and trickling blood throughout Eugene's entire body. Eugene surveyed the damage but shrugged it off. "I quit," he said and shoved his apron into his startled boss' unsuspecting arms. He waved the nametag around in front of Ray's eyes, saying, "For the last time, it's Eugene. Not Gene. You can have this back, too," he said, as he put the tag on top of the clothes. Just as quickly, he walked out of the store. As Ray gathered his senses and yelled after him about the broken pickle jars, Eugene had already shut the door behind him, oblivious to the perturbed shouts. Here he was, his birth. A child in the form of a man. He was wrapped in a towel and handed over to an inadvertant mother. Not fate, not circumstance, but will. He would be a hero or a martyr, but never a victim.
