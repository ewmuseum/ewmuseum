One Room, One Conversation

14 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The Serenity Mental Institute was a quaint place, an oddity considering the confines of Los Angeles that surrounded it. The stone sidewalks that led up to the building were fresh with paintings done by the local residences and the stones that were the foundation for the building were stronger than ever. Inside of the building, David looked at his dear old mom and noticed the age-induced wrinkles on her face had grown to be more noticeable over the past few weeks. "How've you been doin', ma?" David asked her, tepidness creeping into his voice. She shrugged her shoulders, the bottom of her auburn hair caught in the white robe provided by the facility. "You know me, David, can't complain," Rebecca responded to him before reaching out and touching his hand. She could tell that something was bothering him, something more than her being in this facility though it could have a direct correlation to this. David sighed as he inched closer to the table, his head low, and his heart clearly on his sleeve as it always is. "I talked to Samantha earlier today," he said to her and instantly her face lit up at the mere mention of her daughter's name. Her right hand brushed the lock of hair in front of her face. "How is she doing? Oh God, how I miss her," she told him and David only nodded his head while his eyes never quite made it to her's. Telling from the lack of excitement in his voice though, she deduces what it is that is bothering him. "They want me to come back, don't they?" David nodded. "What'd you tell them?" she asked him as she pulled her hand away and looked at her son with such empathy. Never did she intend to put him in this position, to make him look like the bad guy, but she knew deep down that it was inevitable. Still, she needed him to be strong, and if David was anything, he was strong. "That I would come see you, pass on the message, but also told her that it wasn't time yet," he responded. Judging from his response, Rebecca could tell that this was literally tearing his heart into pieces. Slowly, she left her seat and walked around the table before she sat on the edge of it and looked at her only son. She ran the palm of her hand against his cheek and looked upon him with such pity that it nearly broke her heart. "You always cared, David. Always. You feel for your sisters so much and I'm not making this easy for you, am I? Dear God, I'm so sorry. If you want--," she continued to tell him, but David cut her off before she had a chance to finish her sentence. "No." Rebecca looked at David with confusion on her face. "David, but--," she tried to tell him once again, but he cut her off quickly. The look in his eyes said it all as he pleaded with her to not make that kind of decision. "You're here for you, not for anyone else," he told her and she nodded her head in agreement. Three months in this facility though had proven to be a long time, and maybe her heart had mended to the point that she could move on with her life. Yet, as she looked at the lines on her son's face it brought back so much grief and heartache. "I know, I just can't get over it," she told him and David wrapped his arms around his mother as the tears started to stream out of her eyes so calmly. Within a few seconds she brushed the tears away from her soft cheeks and looked up at David. "Can--Can you tell me what happened that morning?" David took a deep breath in as he looked at his mother. "Are you sure?" She only nodded her head and David could feel the pain settle into his chest as he took another deep breath in and felt the pressure in the center of his chest. "Alright." *** The chirping of the birds in the air didn't make David more affable to waking up. Instead, he wanted to throw something out the window as the birds and the invading rays of sun that accompanied them was invading in the only place that he could find solace and peace, his sleep. However, once he heard those noises and then his sense of smell felt the aroma of pancakes assaulting him, he knew there was no chance that he would be able to sleep any longer. David rolled out of his bed and somehow avoided a painful scenario which saw him falling flat on his face on the hard wood floor below him. His head pounded as he walked over to the mirror screwed into the back of his door and saw a man who had lived in the shadow of his father for far too long. One deep breath in later he felt like he had the confidence of ten strong men and was prepared for the eventual showdown he was going to have before the first bite of pancakes was even in his mouth. The moment he stepped through the threshold of his door he was tackled from behind as his sister nearly took him down to the ground. Originally thinking that it might be Samantha he was surprised to find his youngest sister MacKenzie the culprit behind this attack. "What're you doing silly girl?" he questioned her as David wrapped his arms around her and lifted her high into the air, and she immediately started to giggle as he began tickling her. Gasping for air, MacKenzie slapped David in the arm as he finally let her down. "Samantha woke me up and told me you were home. I almost couldn't believe it. Are you home for good?" she asked him and he simply shrugged his shoulders as the duo bolted down the stairs. "I hope so," he said as honestly as he possibly could, not wanting to get the hopes of his sister up too soon. The two turned the corner and walked into the kitchen which saw his mother eagerly cooking while Samantha and Sarah were already at the table going through a bowl of freshly picked grapes. David inched to the table before reaching his hand into the bowl, but Sarah smacked him on the hand before giving him a set of the most evil eyes he had seen come from her. "Don't think just because you came back last night after disappearing for three years that you're forgiven for the display you put on last night," she told him sternly and David looked on in shock before he looked over at Samantha. "Yeah, she grew an attitude in the time that you left," Samantha informed him in this joking manner though he could tell that Samantha wasn't joking in the least bit. He immediately looked back at Sarah who rolled her eyes at him and David just shook his head. "Oh, okay miss thing. You think just because I've been gone that you can start having attitudes with me and can roll your eyes. You picked a jerk. An idiot. And your shirt was coming off. Three strikes in my book and I would do it again, and again," he informed her in this tough tone. Sarah looked on, a little shocked at how David had come at her, but she instantly calmed down as he came behind her and wrapped his arms around her petite frame. "I love you," he said before kissing her on the top of the head. "When I left, you had no interest in boys, and I'm always going to protect you. Stop trying to grow up so fast though, alright? Be a kid. Keep your clothes on." She shook her head before slapping him on the arm. "I'll try." David walked over to the counter and kissed his mom on the cheek before he started piling pancakes on a vacant plate. Before he had a chance to even turn around though, a loud, boisterous voice came out of nowhere. "Glad to see you're making yourself feel right at home. Eating everyone's food and invading our lives like you have," the man said and anger ultimately took over David's body as he spun around and glared at his father. "You think just because you raised me for all of my life that you think you can say whatever you want to me?" David instantly tore into him; refusing to give an inch to the man he respected most in life. His father, an intimidating man, moved closer to David and his eyes burned a hole through David's skin. "Yes, it does mean that, David. Because I'm not going to allow you to just walk back into my house and destroy your sister's like you did the last time. You weren't the one that had to console them or hold them when they woke up in the middle of the night with nightmares about how you died. You did it to them once, David, and I'm not letting you do it again," Shawn firmly replied. By this point, David was shaking as his intensity level went up another level. "I went to make something out of my life, Dad! You know that thing that you always told me to do!" David screamed back at his father and Shawn took a step back after hearing the force coming out of his son's mouth. "Outside, now," David told him before he pointed towards the backyard and pool area. David though stood his ground as he glared at his father and felt his chest heaving. "No, Dad, you want to do this then let's do it! Might as well let Mom and Sarah and Samantha see it all play out before their very eyes," David lashed back and Shawn pointed out the door again. "Out. Now." David looked at his sisters and then back at his Mom, all frozen from the terrifying argument the two were having right in front of them. David took one last look at his father before he began to take large steps toward the door. He then pushed it open and looked out at the never-ending sky, wishing that it would fall on top of his head at this very minute. *** "I-- I... I just can't," David told his mother and got up, ready to leave the room. Yet, he didn't move as he stood there frozen in time. Rebecca looked at his son and her eyes pleaded with him not to leave. "David...," she said to him in a soft whisper, but he turned away from her and looked at the door that represented his exit. David's arm folded over each other and against his chest as he could feel those moments of that fateful day hanging on the very edge of his mind. "David, I need you to tell me what happened out there. What did you two talk about?" David turned and looked at his mother in the eyes. "Don't you understand, I can’t? I just can't," he told her and she clearly refused to accept that as an answer as she stood up and looked at her son in the eyes. "David, I'm in a mental institute. I asked you to put me in here because I couldn't take all of the pressure of having to deal with the reality of the situation. I've tried to put all of the pieces together, but that's the biggest piece, David, and I need it to move on," she said to him but he continued to defy her. "I'm just not ready, Mom!" he yelled at her, trying to get her to see his side of the story, but she wasn't having it. "For what, David? You and I both need to come to terms with this. Your Dad died! He's dead. The man I love is no longer here with us. I need to realize that and be fine with that. Neither of us though is facing that moment face on. Instead, we're trying to ignore the problem at hand, and that's that neither of us can wake up in the morning and think to ourselves that Shawn Noble no longer lives in this world with us." Her words were pointed and painful, but as she sat down she felt a large weight come off of her chest. David stood there and looked out the nearby window as the tears pounded on the edges of his eyes with a threat of coming out. He solemnly sat down in the chair and looked as pale as he could possibly be. Rebecca looked at his son and felt for him, but needed him to tell her everything. "David, what happened?"
