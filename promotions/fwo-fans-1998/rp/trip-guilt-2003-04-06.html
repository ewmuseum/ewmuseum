Trip. Guilt.

6 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
5:49 p.m. April 3rd, 2003 Ballyskelligs Bay, Ireland. The smile spread across Brandon's face as he walked up the cobblestone drive. Wanting to surprise Sean and Jarod he had not bothered to call for a ride. Instead he risked his life at the hands of a local taxi driver. The oft-used jokes about nationality and speech restrictions used by the Americans about their taxi professionals were not the same in Ireland. The joke here was a real life horror as getting in one of those off-yellow death boxes was a sign of bravery indeed. The seventy-five mile per hour speed limit was not the biggest part of the threat although if you coupled it with that also being the speed limit on the most narrow of roads it certainly added to it. No, the worst part of the taxi ride was the homicidal maniac that resided behind the wheel and his lack of regard for other living creatures, even those in the backseat of his own vehicle. A breed apart they were. Now though he was safely home. It seemed like eternity since he walked this narrow road. He thought back to the time he presented the new house to his mother and father. They remarked how the road reminded them of their old house before they moved to the States. Neither knew Brandon had so painstakingly rebuilt everything he had even had the driveway fashioned from old photos. When they had passed through the entrance gate and saw the house you could see the tears well up in both pairs of eyes. After long minutes of stammering and questions it finally sunk in what Brandon had done for them. He had recreated their first home together, the one they abandoned so long ago in the hopes of protecting their family. The memory had taken just long enough that when Brandon blinked his way back to current time he stood outside the front door. He could hear Jarod and Sean in the backyard playing, both giving their opinion on something at the top of their lungs. He decided to go in first and drop off his bags before venturing outside to his little ones. Sliding his card through the scanner the door beeped open and Brandon stepped in. The strong aroma of his mother's favorite coffee filled his nostrils. The cinnamon stung a bit, she always did like it strong. Hearing the door beep open a familiar face popped from around the corner down the hall, the one connected to the kitchen. "Brandon Lelan Roche! You scared the willies out of me." Along with the scolding though his mother stepped lively to him and her small arms hugged him big. "Hi Momma, I've missed you So much..." He of course returned her hug, enveloping her with his massive frame. They both walked back towards the kitchen as the obvious set question was asked. "What on Earth are you doing here? We didn't expect you back until next week for a day. How long are you here for? "I just ended up with a few extra days because of some scheduling problems. There wasn't a show on Wednesday so Amy and I spent the day together and then I figured I'd go ahead and head here. I think she's hanging out with Angel and the band and I really needed to see all of you for a few days." Looking around the kitchen at the baking wonders his mother had performed Brandon thanked himself for coming home. He'd be a super-heavyweight by the time Sunday rolled around.
