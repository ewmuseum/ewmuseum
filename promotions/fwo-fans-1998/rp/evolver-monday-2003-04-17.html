EVOLVER_monday

17 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
His eyes opened to the hospital lights. He couldn't move his left arm to an itch in his back. still paralyzed? fuck. i'm a cripple. my eyes still move. Eugene Marder blinked, still squinting at the bright lights that stood in contrast to the darkness he'd beein in for- how long? shit. well, at least i can- "Don't worry, Mr. Marder. It's just the painkillers. You'll be able to move normally in a few hours." british accent. is that where i am? It took him a lifetime just to turn his head towards the doctor sitting in the chair by the wall. By the time his eyes focused on the physician's grin, his thoughts were elsewhere. was she hurt? no, she hurt me. no, he hurt me. still itches... "You're what we call catatonic right now. You'll notice that all your motions take longer, if they happen at all. This also happens with those who are depressed. Sometime's they'll take two minutes to walk ten feet. They go through manic-catatonic cycles in which-" his nametag? Atlee. what about cyberslam? can i wrestle? "-fascinating, really." The doctor suddenly stood up and removed a handkerchief from his pocket, catching some drool that was rapidly escaping Eugene's mouth. At that moment it occured to Eugene that he had asked the question in his head, not loud. Summoning his best efforts, he asked it of the doctor, slurring and stammering. For a few moments, Dr. Atlee simply stared with a smirk of mild bemusement. "Oh, dear heavens, no. There's a bit of hope that you'll be able to wrestle at all, but certainly not for quite some time." Eugene's eyes were open wide now, adjusted to the light but awed at the statement. He made his best effort to sit up dramatically, but found the attempt both faithful and fruitless. After raising his torso an inch or two from the bed, he fell back down, spirits routed. no, i've gotta be there. i'll be there. The doctor chuckled a bit at this, standing from his seat and walking over to the door. He paused in the frame, and turned around, looking his patient up and down. "Sir, you'll want to refrain from doing that. Don't want to open up one of those stitches or pull another one of those muscles, do we?" leave. think. leave. who? i'll be there. RICKY. i'll be there. One phone call, two days, and seven thousand miles later, Eugene was sitting in front of his best friend and doctor, Patrick Nanson. Sitting by his own free will, ready to pleade for Ricky to give him a chance. It would be his only one. The diplomas and accreditations walls said Patrick Nanson, M.D. - Internal Medicine, and Eugene knew it was his fault that it didn't say Optometrist. He looked up slowly, rubbing the back of his neck with one weary hand, the other on his lap. "Hey, Rick...I need a favor." Ricky shook his head, a telling glance permeating Eugene's skull. Ricky knew, and Ricky would take a great deal of convincing. "No, Eugene. Absolutely not." But Eugene knew his friend, and this wouldn't be the first time he'd changed his mind.
