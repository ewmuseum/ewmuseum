It Isn't Easy Coming Home

10 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
He felt a little silly for leaving the fWo behind over one phone call, but Alex Creed couldn't help it. He knew there was something wrong when he spoke with his mother on the phone. Try as he might, he couldn't get her to tell him what the problem was. That afternoon, Alex booked a flight back to Cali. His mother might be able to be evasive on the phone, but it'd be tougher for her to do in person. Alex wasn't booked for any appearances yet, so he had some time in his busy schedule to take a couple of days to head back home and make sure things were okay. Alex stood in front of the luggage belt, waiting patiently for his suitcase to arrive. He missed the sunshine, the warmth, and the fact that there was no snow. Snow. So very, very wrong. Alex felt a shiver of a different kind when he watched a woman wearing a curve-hugging t-shirt and track pants pull her luggage off the conveyor belt. No parka to hide all that hot womanhood. Hot, hot womanhood. Also, hot. A voice behind him broke Alex out of his hot-trance. "Hey Alex, got my homework done yet?" "I'll finish it as soon as I'm finished with your Mom." Alex turned around to see the grinning face of his long time friend, Terry Derkson. Dressed to the nines, as usual, Terry's father headed one of the largest legal firms in California. He'd always been in money, and liked to show it, but Terry was a good guy at heart. He'd go camping with Alex and his mother when they were kids, and Alex would travel with Terry and his mother to ski at their chalet in Denver. He once offered to get Alex a job with his father's firm after High School, but Terry Senior squashed that in a hurry. Much to Alex's relief, since he would have felt bad turning down his friend's offer. "Alex Apostle," Terry said with a smile and a firm handshake. "How's life in the big circus?" Terry always referred to professional wrestling as a circus, a fact that used to annoy Alex. Now he just wrote it off as Terry being Terry and let it slide. "The fWo has been great for the most part. A few bumps and bruises, a concussion or two, but I'm surviving." He looked at the two large men who eyed the crowd of people waiting for their luggage. They appeared to be part of some kind of security detail. Bodyguards most likely. "How are you doing Terry?" "With Gerald and Bernard acting as my not-so-invisible shadows, I'm as good as I can be." Terry looked at the blonde man with pock-marked skin. "Dad has been getting threats again, so I've got these geniuses making sure no one tries to abduct me or something. Not that they could do anything even if someone did try something. They couldn't catch the clap in a whorehouse." Gerald ignored the comment and continued to watch the crowd. Terry threw his hands up. "Oh for the love of Pete! This is an airport! You think anyone's going to try something in an international airport?" "You never know Sir," said Bernard. "There's a lot of crazies out there." "What about you Alex?" Terry asked. "You have a lot of crazies in the fWo?" "Oh, one or two. Dozen," Alex added. "I hear you buddy. C'mon, let's get your stuff and get out of here. I'll give you a lift to your Mom's place." Alex picked up his luggage and followed Terry out the front. A limo quickly approached the entrance and stopped just in front of the loading area. The chauffeur quickly hopped out of the car and opened the door for them. "Ben will take your luggage Alex," Terry said as he climbed into the car. The chauffeur picked up Alex's suitcase and put it in the trunk while Alex entered the limo, followed by Gerald. Bernard occupied the passenger's seat up front. "Wow," Alex said with an appreciative whistle. "Look at all this stuff." "Television, full bar, refrigerator, and a Playstation 2." Terry leaned back in his seat. "Now does the fWo gear you up like that?" Alex picked up a PS2 controller. "Well, actually they do. Except for the bar. I don't really drink." "Well la-ti-da," Terry mocked. "What are you, Mister Straight Edge now? You wear those stupid black X's on the back of your hands when you wrestle?" If there was one thing Terry didn't like, it was being shown up when he was showing off. "No. But I don't think anybody has a stocked bar when the fWo supplies them with a limo. Usually they're only used for business, and they don't want the wrestlers to show up for a public appearance half-cut." "They don't know what they're missing." Terry reached over and pulled out a miniature bottle of Jack Daniels. He unscrewed the cap and gulped the contents down. Terry didn't get along with his father, and often acted out to spite him. Last year, Terry Junior wrote off a Porshe on his birthday after a night of heavy drinking. Nobody was hurt, but TJ had to enter an AA program. Apparently it didn't help. Alex turned and looked out the window. So many familiar places. "How's the old crew doing? Seen any of them lately?" Terry leaned forward and slapped his friend on the knee. "If it wasn't for you and Jane, old buddy, I wouldn't have even known they existed." "How is Jane? Have you talked to her lately?" The one name Alex hoped he wouldn't hear. Still, he couldn't keep himself from asking about her. Or thinking of her for that matter. "I think she's taking some English course at UCLA." Terry shrugged and tossed the empty bottle into the small trash bin. "I can't keep track with what she's doing from semester to semester." Alex tried not to sound too anxious. "Did you two break up or something?" "No, we're still dating. She's still going to be the mother of my children. I'm just not interested in what she's taking in school." Terry leaned back and folded his arms across his chest. "Once we're married, she'll be rich enough never to have to worry about earning a living. All she'll have to do is stay home and take care of my two point three children. I'll be the breadwinner of the family." "I'm sure she'll appreciate your sense of traditional values." Every word Alex spoke dripped of sarcasm. "I can't help it if I'm irresistible. Right Gerald?" The bodyguard remained silent. "See that Alex? The man's speechless. Now just sit back and relax, good buddy. We'll have you home in no time." Looking back out the window, Alex already felt a pang of regret in coming back. There were things, and people, that he was hoping his new life with the fWo would help him forget. But some feelings, no matter how futile they were, would always linger. He only hoped that the trip would get better from this point, and not worse. Somehow, Alex doubted that very much.
