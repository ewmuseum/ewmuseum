Second Heartbeat: An Epic of Time Wasted

11 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
All around me are familiar faces Worn out places, worn out faces. The dock was smooth at its surface, rubbed soft by the air over the Atlantic. Still, a stray splinter could pluck anyone who dared run their hands along the ocean-stained wood. She was born here, at the ocean. Across that ocean. She was terrified of open spaces, too. A place where she was vulerable. Yet here, for comfort, she ran to the manifestation of her fears - the wide-open space she could as easily be attacked on as fall and drown into, the sum of all fears that caressed her at birth. To most, it would be seen as highly unusual to run to what should scare you when you're empty. But that's what you're supposed to do. Nobody escapes instantly to something good for them until they've jogged a path of ill-health a few times...simply because we're animals. Like a pup that takes a few swats on the nose to learn that they shouldn't bite when they're threatened, mistakes are essential to our learning. Bright and early for their daily races Going nowhere, going nowhere. When your pup doesn't learn fast enough not to protect itself for your predatory convenience, you get to rectify things by having a needle in the hand of a sterile professional piss away the life in them. Their tears are filling up their glasses No expression, no expression. She'd been beaten dead by a dozen men before him. You don't protect yourself against someone. You need to trust them. Without blind trust, where's the relationship for them to destroy? How are they supposed to kill something that's not been birthed yet? Break them if they don't drop their defenses. Who cares if they've been hurt before? The pup shouldn't bite you. Why are you hurting something that's only trying to keep itself from pain? Do you really need that much control? Hide my head, I want to drown my sorrow No tomorrow, no tomorrow. The flanel blanket replaced what would've been a warm husband wrapping his arms around her. Like the men who came before him, it didn't change state in the least when her first tear fell. Brandon would have. But he wasn't here. He was back at the house, having a family while Amy was left contemplating the mistakes of her past. Misplaced trust. Believing so many false "I love you"s. Finding out they were fake when it was far too late. Was marrying a man with too much history another one of those mistakes? Falling into so many bad relationships - not learning from so many swats on the snout - everyone knew she was a sucker for punishment. But should it hurt so much when the man she knows is the first who truthfully loved her is back there with what should be her family? And I find it kind of funny, I find it kind of sad The dreams in which I'm dying are the best I've ever had. She didn't want to resent his ex-wife, despite Ana's obvious despise of her. But that was Amy's family! It's not hers anymore, is it? But she's up there with Amy's husband, Amy's boys, Amy's mother-in-law. They were all hers...once. Why couldn't she give them up? Why does she have to sit in the center of what should be Amy's world? Why couldn't she just go away or die or something?! I find it hard to tell you, because I find it hard to take When people run in circles, it's a very, very mad world. The first breath was labored and she watched it linger for a moment in the Irish air, wrapping the blanket closer. The second came with a broken sob. A lot of the time, life didn't make sense. Her mind didn't make sense. She was living among an absolute circus, groping for some semblance of control wherever it could be caught. That control was a rare breath in her hiccupping trains of thought. If there was ever the possibility of organizing thought for her, life would be so much easier. It wouldn't have to make sense then. It could just...be. That is, if she could be. Children waiting for the day they feel good Happy Birthday, Happy Birthday. Made to feel the way that every child should Sit and listen, sit and listen. But that was impossible, at least for now. She was so much more aware than everybody else. Very in-tune. Maybe people living with this kind of insanity were the truly strong ones. Maybe everybody else was fucked, because they couldn't see or feel the same things. Nobody wants to be sad, right? Nobody wants to feel pain. What if those who experience emotions to those depths and extremes - the pain and sadness - were actually the lucky ones, because everyday happiness was an absolute miracle vacation to them as opposed to 'the usual'? When you're accustomed to pain, even the slightest ray of light can be an incredible experience. Went to school and I was very nervous No one knew me, no one knew me Hello teacher, tell me, what's my lesson? Look right through me, look right through me. Amy grinned, red-eyed. Yeah, that was it...she was priveleged to have felt all of that pain, because this true happiness was made even better. The complexity within her only made for a broader experience in life and her own mind. Why was she letting her head wander like this? No good ever came of it. Her so-called career was an example of what happens to her without restraint. But forget work. That was to be kept behind this week.The problem at this moment was her own creation. She just didn't feel at home back there at Brandon's mother's home. Not with his ex-wife there, acting like everything with the situation was okay. Everything wasn't okay, and Amy refused to act like it was. But for the sake of what should be her family, the little redhead simply snuck away. She was much less destructive to everyone else when alone. Her own well-being, on the other hand, wasn't top priority. Which was why she was laying here, alone on this wharf in the winter - open to the world, the ocean, her own mind and all their perils. "Dreamer?" Brandon's deep voice echoed a bit, even in the open air. It startled Amy from her soul-searching. The little redhead took a moment to collect herself, and quickly tried to blink away her obvious upset. She cleared her throat and finally spoke in what was barely a whisper... "Why aren't you with your family?" "I was missing a part of it. I looked around the estate and you were nowhere to be found." Brandon sat down and pulled her to him, she shivered reflexively. "I got tired of competing with history, so I came somewhere more like home." "You wasn't competing with history sweetness, you were helping make new memories. That's what families do." Brandon stared in her eyes, trying to make her understand how sincere the words truly were. "I can only stand her in very tiny doses. I have no idea why we even had to come here while she was around." Amy sighed, breaking the locked stare to gaze back out on the water. "Especially when your mom likes her more. I think you're the only person in your entire life who actually tries understanding me." "I'm not trying to understand you. I already do...more than you know. Now come here." The hug that followed washed over the both of them, filling them with reassurance where she thought there was none. It felt good, almost good enough to have put the last hours throughts from her mind.
