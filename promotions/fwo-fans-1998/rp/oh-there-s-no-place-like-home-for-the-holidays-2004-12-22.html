Oh There's No Place Like Home For The Holidays

22 December 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Attention passengers: Southwest Flight Number 529 to Cincinatti is now boarding at Gate Number 5. "So Vic, what exactly is a Bangkok Battle Royal. The details you gave over the phone were pretty vague." Lindsay Troy took a sip from her coffee mug and looked inquisitively at Vic Creed. The Main Man stared down at the top of the red metal table, his large hand curled around his own mug, while Alex tried to eat his way through dry scrambled eggs, burned bacon and luke-warm homefries. It was 9:00 AM and the three sat in the corner of the Food Court in the Raleigh-Durham International Airport, waiting for their flights to be called. "Pops hasn't said much about it," said Alex through a mouthful of eggs. "If anything, all he's muttered was how he's gonna tear Nick's balls from between his legs and stir-fry them with Asian noodles." "He'd probably be better off as a eunuch." Troy set her cup down and leaned back in her chair. "It doesn't sound as if Nick is planning on letting you live to see the New Year." "If I had it my way he'd have been dead a long time ago." Vic gripped his mug tighter and glared up to meet Troy's eyes. Alex wiped the corners of his mouth with the edge of his thumb. "So what's the deal with this Battle Royal match?" Vic eased his grip slightly on his mug and turned his attention to his son. "It's a match fought under Battle Royal rules - you can only be eliminated by throwing your opponent over the top rope and both feet touch the floor outside the ring." Vic sipped his coffee and set the mug back down. "The difference is that the ring ropes are replaced with barbed wire." "Sounds something like A1E's Pier Six Brawl, except they just put barbed wire around the outside of the ring," Troy said, and grimaced. "Getting thrown into that isn't fun...I've still got marks going back two years." "I've only been in one battle royal and it sucked." Alex added before crunching on a charred strip of bacon. Attention passengers: United Airlines Flight Number 847 to Detroit is now boarding at Gate Number 12. "Think about it. When you're fighting to stay in the ring, you usually grab onto the ropes and wrap your legs around them. You hold on for dear life," Vic said over the flight announcement. "In a Bangkok Battle Royal, fighting to stay in the ring is guaranteed to rip the flesh from your body." The ink-colored fluid swirled around inside his cup, and he watched his reflection twist and distort. "This thing is all about suffering. The more you fight to continue, the more you hurt yourself." "I know you're not averse to getting hurt," Troy said as she watched Vic lose himself in his coffee cup. "But something about this match has you, I don't know... concerned?" Vic didn't raise his head. "I saw Nick wrestle a match like this in a club in Thailand. It was a fifteen man Bangkok Battle Royal. By the end of it, there were two guys left - Nick and a big German guy named Otto Vandenhaus. There were small strips of either skin or clothing hanging off the barbed ropes. Blood was literally dripping off the ring apron. Every time they took a step, you could see them splashing through bloody puddles. A masked guy from Mexico lost an eye when Nick blew a hotshot across the top rope and dropped the poor bastard face-first on the wire." "Well there goes my breakfast," Alex sighed and pushed his plate away from him. "Not that it was any good to begin with." "Do I dare ask what happened with Brandish and the German?" Troy asked. "Both guys were bloody messes. Nick had managed to keep most of the puncture wounds on his back, but this Otto guy was like a pin cushion. Having lost all that blood, he didn't have alot of strength left. Nick grabbed that poor bastard by the neck and threw him right into the ropes. Instinctively he tried to wrap his legs around the ropes. Nick kicked him in the stomach and knocked him out of the ring. But when he hit him, the barbs ripped open the meat around Otto's inner thighs and hit an artery. The referee ruled the elimination didn't count, so not only was Otto bleeding to death but he had to get back in the ring. Nick shredded that poor bastard for another ten minutes before tossing him." "Jesus," Alex whispered. Vic picked up five packets of sugar and poured them into his cup. "They couldn't even give Otto an open casket funeral. He left half his face on those wires." "There's no way he'd get away with that in America. Now with the way rules and regulations are now." Troy commented. "We're not putting on a show in some underground fight club. Dudley may be a dickhead but he's not going to tarnish the Disney image by allowing Nick to make you into Otto Number Two. This company's reputation, however questionable it may be under the Disney helm, is more important to him and his paycheck than his hatred for you." "Nick looks about as bright as a bag of doorknobs. But the man knows how to hurt people. He can accidentally drop you across the wire throat-first and watch you bleed out before the paramedics can get you out of the ring," Vic shook his head. "That's why he didn't request a normal barbed wire match - in those matches, you can avoid the ropes for the most part. In a Battle Royal setting, you're always either near or on the ropes. Always plenty of opportunity for accidents." "There's no question in my mind the lengths Nick will go to. I remember what he did to Kenyon just to get me to put up the Hardcore Title against him." Lindsay gripped her mug tighter. "You're just going to have to get to him before he gets to you." "That's a thinly-veiled suggestion if I ever heard one," Alex smirked and took a swig of orange juice. Passengers are reminded not to leave bags or luggage unattended. Any unattended parcels will be confiscated and removed from the premises by airport police. "Got any suggestions then?" "Other than 'turn Nick's flesh into something resembling the consistency of warm Cool-Whip', no. But give me a few days. Lord knows I'm going to need to keep my mind on something other than this impending holiday." "What's wrong with Christmas?" The Mecca's eyebrow raised in question. "Things just haven't been the same for me for a few years now, Alex. That's all. Maybe one day I'll explain, but not here. It's not really that important anyway," Troy forced a smile in an effort to change the subject. Attention passengers: United Airlines Flight Number 356 to Los Angeles is now boarding at Gate Number 20. Vic's face hardened. He knew about hard times over the holidays. Last year, he was burying his daughter over Christmas. This year, he was trying to take advantage of the second chance he'd been given with Alex. He wanted this to work. He'd fought to hold back those thoughts and impulses that got him into trouble in the past. Sure he wanted to commit grievous acts of harm on Nick Brandish, but for one day would he be able to allow himself to feel something other than anger and hatred? Could he feel happiness? " - our flight. Pops? Uh, Dad?" Lindsay poked Vic's shoulder. "Earth to Vic. You with us? They're calling your flight." Vic shook his head and blinked. "Yeah. Sorry." The wrestling trio stood up. Alex leaned over and gave Troy a friendly hug. "You have a Merry Christmas," he said with a genuine smile. "Or at least, as good as it can be." "You too," she said back and gave him a squeeze. Alex stepped back and Lindsay noticed Vic looking at her with a strange look on his face. At least, a look that was strange for him. "I'll meet you at the Gate," Vic told Alex. "I'll be right there." As the boarding announcement repeated over the PA system, Alex snatched his carry-on bag off the floor and, after giving Lindsay Troy a quick wave, quickly hustled down the corridor. She turned her attention from son to father. "He's a good boy, Vic. You should be proud." "I am. But it was pride that cost me a child last year." He took a step closer to Troy. "You've done alot for him. You've done alot for us." "I'm glad I could help," she said with a friendly smile. "Look," Vic moved closer and his voice dropped to a low murmur. "I have no right to ask anything else of you -" "What is it?" She looked at the dark expression on Vic's face and couldn't imagine what he could possibly want from her. "If anything happens to me, try and keep an eye on Alex. Don't let him walk the road I took." "Vic," Lindsay was speechless. The defeated tone in Vic's voice sounded so foreign it didn't even seem to come from him at all. "Please," he asked softly. "You're a good person. Don't let Alex become his Father's Son." "Vic, I'm really not - " Attention passengers: This is the final boarding call for United Airlines Flight 356 to Los Angeles. All passengers should be at Gate Number 20. We repeat - Vic picked up his bag from beside the table and gave Lindsay a pleading look. "I'll do it," she said finally. There was almost a sound of relief in his voice when Vic said, "Thanks." He stepped away from her, gave her a slight nod, and quickly started walking through the crowd to reach his flight. "Hey Vic!" Vic stopped and turned his head, people pouring around him like a river flowing around a huge stone. "Yeah?" She gave him a wave. "Merry Christmas." The side of his mouth twitched, and for a second Troy was sure the big man was going to smile. "You too," was all his said before jogging toward his boarding gate. Lindsay Troy sighed and dropped her head to the side. "I'm really not that good of a person, Vic," she softly finished her statement. Attention passengers: Delta Airlines Flight number 829 to Tampa is now boarding at Gate Number 32. The holidays weren't starting on a very Merry note.
