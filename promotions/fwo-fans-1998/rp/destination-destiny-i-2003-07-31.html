Destination, Destiny: I

31 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Beautiful, isn't it?" He stared at the landscape that rolled through Rockford, Illinois. Rain slashed across it, warm rain that made the lush green even deeper. He took her here, but he didn't know why. "You're pathetic." The voice carried genuine hatred. It came from a girl bound to a chair in the corner of the room. The room itself stood in stark contrast to the enviornment outside. Its taupe walls were peeling, and the tan carpet was stained beyond repair. A ceiling fan whirred loudly from the ceiling, hanging above a ped with threadbare covers and week-old sheets. The only other accessories were a television standing on top of a dresser, and a chair which now held a struggling young woman. In the car ride, she was much more pacified. When he'd grabbed her and put her into the seat, binding her with a pair of tights from his gym bag, she had kept her composure. They drove across the country, and she would lapse in and out of consciousness. Only when they had arrived did Eugene begin to contemplate how scared she must have been. Why did he bring her at all? The thought had crossed his mind more than once, and he couldn't answer it. Wholistically, she was someone who appealed to him, but it was much more than that. He barely ever met her gaze, lest it be by necessity. When he did, he felt as though she could see through them, straight into the back of his head. He supposed that he was confused, bewildered, and overwhelmed. Nothing in his life was going the way he'd planned, and it vexed him. This person, with her stoney gaze, somehow seemed to offer all the answers. She swore to him that she would never help him in any self-realization, nor could she. Eugene knew that she would. Looking at her, slightly above and to the right, he asked, "Can I tell you a story?" She didn't answer, but her gestures gave away her interest. With that, he launched into a four-day narrative that ended with the two exchanging three precious words.
