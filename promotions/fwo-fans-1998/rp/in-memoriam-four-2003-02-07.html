In Memoriam Four

7 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
As he awoke, the fatigue that hit his senses surprised him. He didn't know how long he had been unconscious, but the state had obviously not been restful. He could tell he was lying down on a cold, hard surface with his arms above his head and his legs spread apart considerably. He tried to open his eyes, but could not. Something was holding them shut. Panic overtook him as he tried to get up. His arms and legs were able to move slightly, but were restrained by cuffs around his wrists and ankles. "You're not going anywhere, Kyle." Kyle Dillon knew this voice. It was the one man who he had ever felt hate for. It was the only man he feared, because no one knew what he was capable of. It was Jack Lundin. "What are you doing, Lundin?" Kyle's voice was horse from hours without use. Hours? He didn't even know how long it had been. Days? Weeks? His head still hurt from the blow Lundin had given him while on his motorcycle, so it couldn't have been too long. "That, my young friend, is the eternal question. Soon enough, you will realize what I am doing, but by then, it will be too late for you." Kyle could tell Lundin was close by...too close. Suddenly, sharp pain shot through his face, as whatever was covering his eyes was removed. Kyle opened his eyes, and found himself staring into a blindingly bright light. His vision was blurred, and Kyle blinked several times to clear his eyes. As he did, and his eyes adjusted to the light above him, he was able to gain some limited vision. The light directly above him combined with the darkness of the rest of the room kept him from seeing more than a few feet in any direction, but he could see Lundin's head right beside his own. "You can kill me, Jack, but there will always be someone who fights you. There will always be someone who sees what you're trying to do and will want to stop it." "Oh, shut the fuck up, Kyle. I'm not going to kill you. That'd be very cowardly of me. Too often in history figures have simply killed those who opposed them, merely because they didn't want to face them. I will not take that route." "What day is it?" "That is not important. We have not passed our date with destiny, and I will make sure you will be there. I will be there as well. I wouldn't miss that for the world." Lundin stood and peered down at his captive. Dillon realized his helplessness as he saw a heavy shovel in Lundin's hand. He was completely at Lundin's bidding, and he knew it. There was no escaping this situation. Lundin did not strike. Instead, he merely held something in front of Kyle's face. As Kyle's eyes focused, he realized it was a photograph. It depicted a young woman with a blindfold around her eyes, a gag in her mouth, her hands tied behind her back and her ankles bound together. She was lying on her side and was even more helpless than Kyle was now. Kyle stared at the picture when the realization came to him. The woman in the picture was Katie. Kyle fought the chains, futilely. "You son of a bitch! Where did you get that?! She never did anything." Lundin offered Kyle no response. Instead, his maniacal laughter was all Kyle could hear. The laughter was maddening. No man could truly have so little regard for the human condition, could he? There was a sharp pain in Kyle's neck. His body went tense before he began to tire.
