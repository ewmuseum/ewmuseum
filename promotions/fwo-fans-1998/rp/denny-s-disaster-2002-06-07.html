Denny's Disaster

7 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
David Cross sat in the corner booth at Denny's, sipping his coffee. He set the cup down, picked up his fork, and pushed a cold piece of his pancake around the lake of syrup covering the bottom of his plate. A waitress passed by and stopped beside his table. Waitress: Were you finished with your breakfast? Cross: ... Waitress: Sir? Cross: No... no I'm not finished. He wasn't hungry. He didn't have much of an appetite when he first came in. But Cross was slowly becoming more and more solitary, more isolated from the others on the fWo roster. He tried to fit in with the others, but he never really felt like he belonged there. Between that and his hallucinations, much like the reflection that had been taunting him for the past half hour, he was rapidly reaching the end of his rope. With few people in the restaurant at such a late hour, the waitress returned to the kitchen. As soon as she was gone, Cross turned his attention from his plate to the window. Specifically, the reflection of himself in the window. The one that had been flipping him the bird the entire time the waitress was at the table. Cross: You're being a real dick, you know that? Go pester someone else. Reflection: Screw that. It's more fun to bug you. Besides, I'm not real, remember? It's not like a figment of your messed up imagination can bother you, right? Cross: Right. Reflection: So when I say that your matches are really beginning to stink up the arenas, you're not offended. Cross: Correct. Reflection: And if I say that you could walk outside and step in front of a speeding car, and that no one would miss you, it wouldn't faze you right? Cross: You've got it, imaginary person who doesn't exist. Reflection: If you don't mind me saying, you're certainly giving a lot of attention to something that doesn't really exist. The waitress, a motherly looking woman with specks of gray in her hazel hair, returned to the table. Cross turned to her, but the reflection wasn't finished talking. Waitress: Can I get you a refill on your coffee? Reflection: Like you give a damn. That's right, you minimum wage trollop! You plaster that fake smile on your face and pretend to care. You probably spit in the coffee before you serve it. Cross: I'd love to have another cup. Sleep is highly overrated. Waitress: You look tired. Not sleeping well? Cross: Oh sure. I get a few hours in between nightmares. Waitress: You poor thing. Reflection: You hear that, Davey? All you want is respect. All you want is for people to give a damn. Now you're the object of pity. You've got a Denny's waitress feeling sorry for you. A Denny's waitress for criminy sakes! David watches the woman pour the coffee, steam rising from the cup. She looked at the young man's weary face. Waitress: Are you okay? You've got quite a few nasty looking cuts on your face. Were you in an accident? Reflection: Yeah Davey, tell her why you've got those cuts. Tell her that you hate your face so much that you try to slice it off a piece at a time. Guilt's a bitch, ain't it. The strain of maintaining control is obvious in his voice, but David struggles on. Cross: I'm a professional wrestler. It's a rough line of work. Waitress: Well you take care. I'll be back if you need anything else. The blood rushed to David's face as the waitress gave him one last concerned look. He didn't want her pity. He didn't NEED her pity. She took her pot of coffee back into the kitchen, leaving Cross alone in the restaurant. He turned back to the window and gritted his teeth. Cross: What do you want from me?! I can't change the past! I can't change the present! All I can do is bust my ass in the ring. His reflection smirked back at him as he pressed the hot cup of coffee between his palms. Reflection: Unleashed is in a couple of days. Internet Title tourney's taking place. Where are you on the card? Cross: Uh, I haven't heard. Reflection: That's because you're not on the card. But Kagos? He's taking on Tempest in the first round. You're getting screwed again, and you're letting it happen. You're a whore to their abuse. Man, you're sure great at destroying lives. First you destroy Tyler's, now yours. Good J- The reflection disappeared behind the spider web of glass, caused by David's fist smacking into it. His anger quickly subsided as the bolts of pain shot through his hand and up his arm. The Denny's staff, waitresses and a rotund man who appeared to be the manager, hustled out of the kitchen. Manager: Get out, psycho! Now! David stood up. He thought about apologizing, but saw that they didn't want to hear anything from him. He reached into his wallet, pulled out a few bills, and set them on the table. The manager started walking towards him, but Cross walked around him and trudged out the door.
