Trust (In the Lord)

20 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The past two days have essentially been a blur, ever since the end of our tag title shot. I've been completely awash in a sea of emotions, running the gamut from fear to anger to guilt to even hope. And I place entire credit on that last, and probably most significant, feeling, on a pair of men: Deacon and Chris Shepard. I had needed to sit down and write that column to clear my head, so I borrowed Ivy's computer for the half-hour or so it took to pour my thoughts out, and then I actually went down the hallway and sought out Deacon. I had been raised Catholic, but I'd never been too strong in my faith. I'd heard that people only turn to God in times of need, and perhaps I was one of those people. However, I needed something to put my mind at ease if I was to perform on Warfare. Smooth and Twitch and Thumper had enough problems trying to find Silver Fox, and no matter how angry their attitudes might've made me, I wasn't about to add to their problems. And as bad as I'd always felt about burdening someone with my own problems when they surely had ones of their own, but in my limited encounters with Deacon through Eli, if there was anyone who had seemed so solid, so secure, in their faith, it was Deac. And as large as he was in physical stature, when I walked out of his hotel room nearly 40 minutes later, I was certain that Deacon's faith most assuredly made him the larger-than-life figure we all believed ourselves to be. The humble assurance that he carried himself with, the underlying confidence in his beliefs, and, most of all, the selflessness and complete willingness to help out his fellow man. If ever there was a living testament to faith, Deacon was most certainly it. And though Chris had had other engagements that took him from our company shortly after I arrived, I also received the same vibe from him. There was just something about the way the big man had sat there, looking at me and nodding his head as I poured my very soul out to him, that made me realize that he was listening. And, although he hadn't been in the main arena concourse when the explosion had happened, Deacon had an air of empathy about him that made me feel that he understood what I was feeling. And finally, when I was done speaking and we had sat in silence for a few moments, he nodded his head a final time before deliberately forming his response. "Everyt'ing happen for a reason. You just need to trust t'at all t'ings are in His hands." And at the time, it really wasn't the answer that I had expected, or even the answer that I thought I wanted. But as I shook hands with the big Mediterranean, the look in his eyes told me that, like all those sermons from years gone by, there was more of a meaning to his words, and that I'd have to find it on my own. I straggled down the hallway back to my hotel room, head still filled with doubts, when inspiration hit me. I fumbled for my key card, and slowly pushed the door open. Stumbling through the half-light that managed to penetrate the curtain that I had kept drawn since checking in, I switched on the small bedside lamp and did something I hadn't done in years. I prayed.
