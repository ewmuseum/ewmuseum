Beautiful Things

13 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
March 7, 2004. Post-Ruahh~! Birth is a beautiful thing. The bringing of Life into the World is a thing to be celebrated. However, when it is celebrated by giving the birthday boy a shot for every year of his birth, joviality can quickly be replaced by worship - at the porcelean altar. Sally Apostle knocked on the bathroom door as the last few fWo superstars waved and left the hotel room. "How's it going in there Alex?" she asked through the door. It wasn't quite English, but when Alex Creed replied with a stifled "Mmaug...", his mother took it as a positive sign. She pressed her hand against the door and smiled before walking across the suite. Lindsay Troy finished stacking the plastic glasses and held them up while Sally picked up a garbage bag. "How's he doing?" Troy asked as she dumped them in the bag. "Surviving, considering he finished off nineteen shots of Jack Daniels." Sally looked at the door as the familiar sound of dry-heaving came from the other side. "This birthday party was a nice thing to do, but I don't think Alex is ready to drink with the veterans yet." Lindsay brushed a stack of paper plates filled with half-eaten food into the bag. "He's young. He's got time to learn to handle his liquor. Next year he'll be able to down the 'one for good luck' before running to the bathroom." Sally straightend out the cushions on the couch and stopped as she reached the cushion David Cross was sitting on. Crucifix rested his arm on the edge of the couch, looking clear and sober. "I'm surprised how well you're doing," Sally said to him. "You were putting them back as fast as Lindsay was pouring them. And she is a relentless bartender." "Comes with the territory," Troy said with a sly grin, and gathered beer bottles in her arms. Crucifix looked at each woman. "I'm fine. Really. My years of martial arts training and conditioning help my body to process toxins like alcohol." He gave each of them a friendly smile and a nod, then slowly tilted to the side and rested himself face-first on the cushion beside him. Lindsay looked at him and bowed. "Black Belt in Mixology, Grasshopper. I needed to have some fun in college, after all." Sally smiled as watched Lindsay pick up the rest of the trash from the evening's festivities and deposit them in the proper trash bags. The beer bottles clinked against the empty bottles of liquor, and on more than one occassion the sound of something breaking inside the bag and Troy's subsequent curse was heard. "I meant to ask you before how your job hunt was going. Alex told me the other day that you were laid off." Lindsay walked into the small kitchen area and began cleaning up the countertops. "Well, it wasn't going very well. But Alex wants to bring me on as his manager." Troy's head appeared around the partition in the wall, her eyebrows lifted into the air. "Sally, I don't think that's..." "In the traditional sense, I mean. Handling his travel itinerary, his bookings, autograph signings. I could work out of our apartment and Disney would pay me. I can separate Mom duties from Agent duties and get a better paycheck than what I was bringing home from waitressing." Sally stood over the bed and admired Alex's birthday presents from Lindsay and her family. "These are really beautiful," she said, running her hand over the silk of the customized warmup jacket and pants from Brent DiPetrillo's dojo. "Do you think the shorts will fit him?" Troy walked over and picked up the satin red and black muay-thai shorts. "I think so. If they don't, Brent'll just get a different size made for him." She held the shorts against the front of her pants. "I think I could fit two of me into these." They shared a laugh before Sally asked, "What's that on the front of the shorts?" "That's a Stupa, or a Chinese pagoda. It's supposed to help with memory, or so Tiffany's told me. She drew the design and the lettering for the shorts, jacket, and pants. I've always told her that she's missed her calling as an artist." Lindsay handed the shorts back to Sally. "I'll let you pack them for him. I'm afraid he may have put some things into his suitcase that he may not want me to see." "And you think he'll want me to see them?" "Probably not," Troy replied with a grin. "But then again, you're not the kind of person who would run and tell the boys as part of a rib." A loud snore escaped from Crucifix's mouth, and both women turned to look at Alex's passed-out travel companion. Lindsay picked up an extra blanket and threw it over David's sleeping body. "The two of them make quite a pair." "David seems to be a nice enough person," Sally finished folding the clothes and slipped them into Alex's suitcase. "I'm just glad my son has friends in this business that have good heads on their shoulders and will keep him somewhat on the right path." The two women sat down on the bed and surveyed the room. A toilet flushing and running water could be heard from the bathroom. "Guess the boy's alive," Troy cracked a smirk, and Sally nodded in approval. A moment of silence passed before Lindsay spoke again. "Sally, I know you have your own opinions of Vic, and valid or not I'm not going to ask what they are because they're none of my business. But Vic did ask me right before Christmas to do something for him...to watch out for Alex in case anything happened to him at Countdown. At the time, Vic was gearing up for a match that could very well have ended his career, let alone his life." "The last match with Nick," Sally remarked softly. "Alex told me about it." "I know what Vic meant when he asked me to do that favor for him, and he probably only meant for me to keep my eye on Alex that one time. But I know that one time just isn't going to be enough. Vic's changed ever since Disney brought in the consultants and have really started to put their backing behind Alex. I didn't know back in December that Vic's request had a double meaning, and even now I don't think he knows. But if I'm to live up to my promise, then I may have watch out for Alex when it comes to Vic himself." Sally stared at Lindsay, looking into the younger woman's eyes and seeing a sort of inner-fire behind hazel orbs. Troy, while the same height as Kodiak Vic Creed, was a far cry from being in any sort of competitive weight class as the Main Man. If Sally needed any more reason to respect Troy, the Queen had just given it to her. "I'm not saying this," Troy continued, "to scare you or anything. I'm sure as a mother you're scared enough having to see your son put his body and life on the line night in and night out. I just want you to know that Alex has people watching his back. As much as power corrupts those who are willing and weak enough to let it happen, there are people willing to stand up to the corrupt." "I don't think I could ever thank you enough for everything that you've done for my son." A tear began to well up in Sally's eye, and Troy pulled her in for a hug. "Your son is a special kid, Sally. I can't be everywhere at once, but I'll do my best to make sure his future stays bright." Another loud snore from Crucifix crackled into the air. "And I'm sure Sleeping Beauty will do the same thing," Troy finished, and they laughed once more. The water stopped running, and a pale Mecca opened the bathroom door. "Never again," he mumbled as he crossed the room and flopped back-first onto the bed beside Lindsay. "Famous last words," Sally said to Troy with a knowing smile. Lindsay ruffled Alex's hair and chuckled. "You've got a long way to go, kid. But I think you're gonna be just fine."
