Blade Job - Chapter 1 - Dreams of the Past

30 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The dream came again, just before the rain. He did not hear the rain. In his sleep the dream possessed him. There was the clearing again, in the African breakpoint between jungle and desert, miles from the city. He emerged from the forest and walked slowly toward the center of the sandy clearing. A bone knife was in his left hand, a bloody knuckle of meat and bone in his right. His dark features were stained red, contrasting like a bright red starburst against the black night. It had taken six weeks for the fWo to find him out here. For six weeks, Mufanga Kagos had run free with the wind-blown sands, directed here and there by the scents of meat and flesh on the breeze. He had hunted well. From the other side of the clearing a man emerged- unshaved, rough-looking, and almost impossibly old. A white shock of hair cascaded around his shoulders like a silver mane. He walked towards Mufanga, and the Wildman could see the man's piercing blue eyes even at this distance, flickering around him, and then across the forest behind him. Mufanga tensed at this new presence in his kingdom. Danger, his senses screamed at him. Run! Mufanga turned to flee into the forest, and ran directly into the hired guides that had been creeping up on him from the treeline. Nets flew, and Mufanga was engulfed in a blanket of twine and rope. He thrashed, as any caged animal would, and managed to bite at a hand that sought to hold him down. "Don't hurt him, dammit," the Wildman heard Silver Hair shout, just an instant before a rifle butt slammed into his broad forehead. And that was always the point where Ben Jacobson woke up, twisted and tied up in sweat-soaked sheets. Where was he? Detroit? No... He hadn't been there in years. Toronto? Seattle? He couldn't remember. Hotel rooms looked the same after years of sleeping in the same type of windowless boxes, with the same come-stains on the carpet and the same bible in the nightstand. No wait, he wasn't on the road anymore. He wasn't allowed. He was home. He was working at the fWo offices now, not even an active wrestler anymore, due to his own stupidity and an untimely accident. Sighing, Ben looked over at the digital alarm clock, brazenly blinking it's red neon numbers into the room as if invited. Seven o'clock. Fuck... time for work. Climbing out of bed, Ben began to get dressed.
