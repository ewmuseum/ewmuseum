Inner Turmoil, Part III

1 January 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Sitting alone in his hotel room, Donaven Winters continued to add ice to his left knee. The knee that had made hard contact with a stray television monitor. The same knee that had been skated over twelve years previous. His entire body was exhausted, he just wanted to fall into a deep sleep and wake up in Tokyo in a week. But the insomnia was coming back, he didn't know why, but he had a feeling of what was causing it. Why won't you let me sleep? Because you're not tired. Yes I am. I've been awake since Saturday night... that's nearly three days. Christ it's Monday morning, we're in a Los Angeles hotel room... a new year... just let me sleep for a while. You can't sleep. I'm not tired. I don't care if you're tired or not. I'm tired, I need to sleep. These injuries won't heal without some rest. And you still fail to miss the point... What point? The point that I control you. No, I'm in control my own life. What's the matter? Don't believe in fate? I like to believe I have some control over my own life. Well, time to face the fact that you don't. You never did and you never will. He stood up and walked over to the bathroom and faced the small mirror in front of his. Giant bags hung underneath his eyes. Blood had dried into his hair, making it rough and tangled. It was dried onto his face, around his mouth, over his arms and chest. He looked at his own body in complete disgust. Disgust of what he had turned into over the weekend. A monster that wanted to see blood. A monster that wanted to create bloodshed and havoc. He was turning into what he used to be, depressed, suicidal. Almost feeling like a child again. A helpless, useless, worthless child from a homeless shelter in Detroit, Michigan. Turning the faucet on, he brought his face down to the sink, splashing the cool water over him with his hands. He looked back up at the mirror and himself, the water had beaded over his face, not looking like water anymore, but looking like tears. While they weren't real tears, the pain was still the same. His body had never been put through as much hell as Eli Flair had put him through just over an hour and a half ago. It would feel much worse if he ever got to sleep, and it would feel worse when he got to Tokyo. The only thing he knew for sure is that the battle with Eli had just started and things were going to get harder, more physical and a lot more blood would be lost. "Just make this go away," he whispered to nobody. His eyes began to water and the real tears followed, he didn't know what to do, or where to go from here. If Monica saw him like this, she would make him take a leave of absence from the fWo and he couldn't do that. It was all he really had left in the world. He was out of touch with his aunt, uncle and cousin. They were the only family he knew of. The rent on his apartment was nearly two months overdue, but the landlord refused to evict him because he was a professional wrestler. Monica had just shown up out of obscurity to try and destroy him... then to help him. There was still a lot of confusion in his mind about that. Something wrong? Nothing you should be concerned with. You're not going to learn to work with me, are you? I don't want to. I can make you go away. I just want everything to go away. You want to be alone, don't you. Yes. In time you'll get what you ask for. I know. Everything comes 'round full circle...
