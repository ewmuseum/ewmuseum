Maybe

22 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Sean 'Triple X' Stevens sighed as he pressed a series of numbers on his cell phone, and waited for the person on the other end to answer. Great he thought, as the voice mail of William “Kid Cool” Beaven played in his ear. Beep. “William, ahh ... it's Sean. Listen, I want to start by apologizing for not finding you so that we could finally hash out this whole contract situation, and get everything finalized. Don't think for a second that I'm playing games with you, because I don't do business like that. I meant everything I said about family, and I'm here for Ivy,” realizing the entire condo was pin drop silent, which, in turn, magnified his normal speaking tone ten volumes higher, Sean nervously lowered his voice and continued, a bit flustered. “Look, I was all over the place this Wednesday, and by the time I had a second to think, it was already well past midnight, reAction had long gone off the air, and you were probably in your hotel room somewhere. Give me a call, when you get this message,” smirking, he offered up one last statement. “I'm sure you have the number in your stack of files.” Stevens hung the phone up, and flopped backwards onto his California king sized bed. It was 7am ... he wasn't sure when Ivy had slipped out of bed, but he did know that it felt like it had been hours since he felt the warmth of her skin rubbing against his, which was part of the reason he was up so early. A routine had been broken. Since Sean had decided to scale back on public appearances, photoshoots, and house show duties for some of the other companies he's obligated to, he had grown accustomed to a certain type of lifestyle. One that saw Poison Ivy in his presence more than she had ever been at anytime in the past. And, it spoiled him. Now, more than ever, he could relate to what Angel went through when Eli Flair decided to return to the fWo for one more quote-unquote last run, which in layman's term simply meant to protect Ivy. The other reason was because he missed his son, terribly. “That's funny,” Trip mumbled to himself, which led to a chuckle. Almost every morning at 7am, some days earlier, Shannon would run into Sean and Ivy's room, because he was hungry, or because he wanted to lay between mommy and daddy, or Trip's personal favorite, because Kodiak Vic Creed was hiding in his closet, and wanted to eat him. Normally, Sean would squeeze a pillow over his head, pretend he was still sleeping, or worse ... ignore his little man until his responsible better half got up to make him oatmeal, and a toaster strudel. Today was the first morning off in close to a year, yet Sean was tossing and turning wishing Shannon would come into the room and brighten up his day. Shannon was his escape. He was the most innocent and pure person that Sean knew, and being his dad taught the man known to the wrestling world as the “blue-eyed badass” more about himself than any book or conversation could. Triple X felt honored to call him Shannon Joseph Stevens his son. And, every single time he looked into that little boy's eyes, it confirmed that he was making the right decision by leaving the business behind. But, now that he wasn't here to occupy Trip's thoughts, his mind wondered. He thought about the reaction he received last night from the fans in the Key Arena. His pop was one that certainly rivaled any other pop received by all of the other performers tonight, and that wasn't ego, it was fact. His mind then drifted to his match-up with Matt. It was a classic good versus evil encounter, that had the audience eating out of their hands, as both wrestlers got to execute some of their best maneuvers, and look strong. It wasn't his best, but it wasn't his worse, and considering how hard it was for him to get over in the FWO – because there's nine million different wrestlers, past and present, who were golden haired pretty boys, with an image similar to his – the fans often took it out on Sean, because he fell in a category that got old fast, not realizing that he wasn't playing “follow the leader”, considering you can't follow a wannabe when you're the originator. He certainly had a lot left to prove. Wait, what are you saying? He thought, slapping himself on the forehead. Go to sleep. But, he couldn't. His mind shifted to his promo, and how comfortable he felt delivering his message. It often amused Sean how some wrestlers would sweat bullets before cutting a promo, nervously biting their nails, all the while, he'd typically finish his in one take. I was born to be a wrestler. When you're born to wrestle, you don't retire in your prime, you keep going. “Shut the f'k up!” Trip yelled ... at himself. It's in your blood, Sean. Wrestling is your life. “No! Shannon's my life! Ivy's my life! Wrestling's gonna lead me to my grave!” he mumbled to no one in particular, sounding as if he were trying to convince someone. Himself, maybe? Suddenly, his mind raced through most of the match's high spots. He rubbed his knees, then his back, then gently massaged his own neck. No pain whatsoever. Maybe he wasn't ready. Maybe there was an underlying reason as to why Sean never signed the part time contract that Kid Cool offered him last week, and why he keeps staring at the original full time one, sitting on the computer desk. Maybe. But, as Sean Stevens shut his eyes, one thing was for certain. He couldn't wait to get on the East coast later today to see his child. Maybe then, all of these ridiculously crazy thoughts would go away for good. Maybe.
