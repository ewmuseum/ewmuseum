Time Out of Mind

10 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Eli Flair declined to comment on the rumors of his all- but- iminent matchup against Ruben Ross at Twilight," read Terry Cooper, his eyes focused on one of any wrestling "dirtsheet" newsletter, "The elusive and self- proclaimed King of Extreme remains one of the most recluse superstars in the professional wrestling business today." He looked across the table at the self- proclaimed King of Extreme and raised his pint of beer toward him. "Welcome home, kiddo." Eli Flair leaned his pint toward Coop's and clinked glasses. The two men drank, all the while taking in the ambiance of the out- of- the- way New York City bar they happen to be sitting in. "How's Ivy doing?" asked Coop, leaning back slightly. "Not bad, not bad," replied Eli, "Her surgery went well so now it's just a matter of fighting off her hatred of sitting still." "Hah! She's just like her father. Always has to be doing something either mental or physical or she's a complete pain in the ass to be around." Eli rolled his eyes. "Don't gotta remind me, old man. You've never sat next to her for eighteen hours in a plane." "Thank God for small favors," mused Coop, toasting the sky. "These dirtsheet writers crack me up," said Eli suddenly, "How much more could anyone know about my life? Between Alicia, Angel, the baby, the band, and every injury I've ever had.... what the shit else is there?" "Ah, but if you share all that so willingly," reminded Coop, "human nature wants to know what kind'a shit you're HIDING." "That a fact?" "EJ, I ran shows outta my gym for almost fifty- five years and never paid one of my boys less than what I promised, and never short- changed anyone on what they were worth," reminisced Coop, as he leaned back, cracking his knuckles, "and all I heard on the day I ran my last card was 'If you knew how to run a promotion you'd still be up and runnin'. Like nobody can really get the fact that an old man wants to stop the hassles of runnin' an indy fed, right?" "Exactly right." They sat in silence for a moment. "First match in that gym, Coop," said Eli finally, "I wrestled my first match in there.... hard to believe you retired this summer." "Wasn't about to let you retire before me, kid." "No chance of that, old man. Before Ivy went under the knife we went over the numbers.... I've still got a lotta time I need to put into the biz." "I thought you were made in the shade." "So'd I, but in a month, give or take.... I'll have a kid. That's not really something I was looking towards a year ago. Hell, up until Angel and I were standing face to face I didn't think it was real." He sighed as he drank more of his beer. "Am I ready for this one, old man?" Coop laughed. "If 'that damn McGinnis girl' can settle down with a nice guy like Sean, then 'that damn Flair kid' can have a family and not have hell freeze over." As Eli pondered this, he pulled a rubber band from around his wrist and tied his hair back with it. He glanced haphazardly at the sheet while doing so, tapping his finger on the "fWo" logo on the cover. "Still predicting doom for the show before Twilight, huh?" he asked, "I don't get how these people can claim to love this business but refuse to think positively about it." "Aw, c'mon... being negative is how the stupid pretend to be smart," replied Coop, "you know that." "I do," replied Eli, "but the shit's been coming out about Angel, too... and that's crossing the line." "Like what?" "Like.... fuck. I don't even wanna get into it right now." Coop motioned to the waitress - two more pints. "Don't worry about it, kiddo... she's a big girl, she can take care of herself. And if she can't, she's got you and Ivy, and the guys in her band to help out." He drained the last drop of beer from his first pint just in time for the second to arrive. "But that's neither here nor there," continued Coop, "You're on a mini vacation right now.... so fuckin' relax." As the bar began to fill up with the Tuesday Night Regulars, Eli seemed to ponder this. "I'm relaxing, old man," agreed Eli, finally, "cuz I needed to stop and take a breather. I have a feeling it's my last chance for quite awhile." Tomorrow night's Warfare, said Eli to himself. Fitting.
