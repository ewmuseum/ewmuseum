Darkness Falls

17 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
David looked down at his hands and saw the many scratches and scars etched across his palms, gifts from barbed wire and shards of glass. Each one reflected pain. Blood. Suffering. Each one seemed trivial. Like a man who hadn't slept in days, David struggled to lift his head. It was all piling on top of him, burying him alive. He opened his mouth to scream, to breathe, but he only ended up choking on the overwhelming burden on top of him. The guilt of Silver Fox's coma. Not stopping Ray Chavez when he had the chance. Nothing David did now would bring Fox back. It was all so futile. There seemed to be no point. He could put up a token effort, but to what end? Chavez was surrounded by an army. Cross was alone. So very alone. The burden of his job, doing the best he could for little or no recognition. He'd been a part of the fWo for quite some time, yet nobody would give him the time of day. The only way anyone gained recognition was either by knowing the right people, or by being a Champion. Cross fit into neither category. Now that Disney had taken over, it was much worse. His best efforts went overlooked as the company implemented new measures to promote more "fan friendly" and less "disfigured" entertainers. He'd seen more death in Tel Aviv than he'd every wanted to see, thanks to the fWo's poor booking. Words couldn't begin to describe the damage done as David sat amidst charred bodies, holding the limb of a small child in his hand. People he had hoped to entertain were torn apart and spread all around him. The woman he loved, who had become a part of him in such a short time, didn't want him anymore. He brought her into his world. He gave her everything she needed to make like comfortable for her as she struggled with the transition between cultures. With her, he did the one thing he swore he'd never do - open his heart. Now she was gone. Nothing seemed real anymore. When Tyler died, the guilt of it almost drove him insane. He almost wished for the insanity to return and release him from his shackles of depression and dread. Every day seemed like an effort in futility. Every hour the pain ached inside him. Every minute dragged him deeper into the Abyss. The light was fading for David Cross. The greyness of his world became darker with every moment. Twilight was lingering and the blackness of Night was fast approaching. David doubted the sun would ever rise again. And still, he carried on. Why? He didn't know. Drugs didn't kill the pain eating away at his insides. Liquor couldn't fill the void. Continuing on seemed the only way to keep himself afloat, and at least a small part of him still wanted that. David didn't even hear the knock at his hotel room door. Sounds all seemed muffled to him. "David? Crucifix?" "Yes?" The word barely registered as audible. He took a breath. "Yes?" One of the fWo's Publicists, Ann somebody-or-other, replied. "It's time for the autograph signing session. Are you ready?" An automation was always ready. He thought that maybe, just maybe, by bringing fans some happiness it might tear through the shroud that covered his still-warm corpse. Hope flickered for a moment before being swallowed by the emptiness. Cross stood up and walked to the door of his hotel room. He pulled a pair of sunglasses out of his pocket and slid them onto his face. Ann's smile brought David little comfort as he opened the door. "Ready," he said softly. "Let's go." It was too late. David Cross was already gone.
