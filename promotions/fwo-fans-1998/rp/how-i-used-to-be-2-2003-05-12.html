How I Used To Be (2/?)

12 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Thursday, May 8 5:32 AM Scott Wasner had been in his hotel room for nearly two and a half hours, but despite his best efforts, sleep was evading him. Perhaps some of it was the Florida air - even with the windows open, the room felt muggy and hot - but that wasn't all. Not by a longshot. He had come to Florida for... what? He had hired Larry Zito to find his ex-girlfriend and his son, and that had gone relatively smoothly. A short wait, a phone call, a meeting, a folder. Simple. The rest was not looking as simple. What did he intend to do - knock on Stephanie Griffith's door and give her a big "Hi!"? Hardly. When last he had seen her she had been storming away from him after that hellish December night with Donaven Winters, a night that had been undoubtedly his fault. She had said then that if she saw him again, he would go to jail, and on that point he believed her fully. Skulking in the bushes was not an option either - it was illegal, for one, and wrong for two. Though he seemed to have no problem hiring somebody to do it, Wasner reflected with a frown as he sat up in the bed and leaned back against the headboard, opening his eyes fully. The first rays of morning were peeking in through the window, turning the room the same light blue as the Miami sky. Usually, during the few times he had been awake at this hour, he felt alive, charged with the energy of the new day. Today he was just exhausted, physically and mentally, and wanted nothing more to sleep. "Dammit," he said aloud, pointlessly. He closed his eyes again and tried to think of nothing. For a while, it worked, then he began to fall into a doze as he remembered what, in the end, all of this could be traced back to. ----- It was February of 1998, and a new era in Scott Slugger's life was beginning. Two weeks earlier, he had been contacted by a new wrestling promotion that would be joining the national scene, a wrestling promotion owned by a man named Travis Beaven. He had first been discovered by Iron Mike Sharpe in October of 1996, and after training for a half-year, he had wrestled his first professional match on Sunday, April 20, 1997. In a rarity for a first-timer, "Pacific Punisher" Waz Wasner went over, cleanly defeating a man by the name of the Paranormal Investigator. The Northwest Wrestling League would soon fold, but Wasner would catch on in the legendary Portland territory, blazing onto the scene and becoming a huge fan favorite. On November 2, a day after turning 25, Wasner won the Portland Heavyweight Championship. This catapulted him to bigger things, as he did a job to Kurrgan at a Seattle house show for the WWF later that month. It was that match, called by those in attendance the best match Kurrgan had ever been a part of (however much of a compliment that was), that caught the eyes of nationwide talent scouts. The newcoming fWo would finally make Slugger his first official offer, which he took. He promptly went home and called his friends, setting up a party. Wasner was living in an apartment complex in Vancouver, not far down the road from where he grew up and only a short drive away from "work". They celebrated long into the night, and on into the next morning. Early on in the party, one of his friends pulled him aside and pulled a plastic bag out of his pocket. "You gotta try this stuff, man," he had said. "This shit'll have you up for days." He pulled a mirror out of Wasner's bathroom and went about setting it up. Wasner held up his hands. "Dude, that's fucking cocaine." The guy nodded at him. "Congratulations, your vision checks out." "No, I'm saying, you gotta put that stuff away. What the hell are you thinking?" The guy set the mirror down. "Hey... lighten up, OK? I'm just trying to have a little fun. Come on, Mister Big-Time Contract - you go up to this fWa or whatever the hell it is you're probably gonna run into worse than this anyway. It's not anything like what they say it's like on TV, it's not gonna kill you or anything. Come on." He had resisted, but only for a short while. Before the night was over he had truly joined the party, making a decision that would shape the next four years of his life. ----- It had led Wasner here, to this hotel room. The demon was gone from his life, but its hand still shaped things. He finally realized why he had come here, and opened his eyes again as he lay in the bed. It wasn't her... that was beyond repair, or close enough, by his own hand. But he had a son. Did he comprehend what that meant, really understand it? No. That was why he was here. Scott Wasner closed his eyes, and as he finally drifted to sleep, three words filled his vision, scrawled on a piece of paper in Larry Zito's sloppy handwriting, as big as the world and twice as bright. Daniel Seth Griffith.
