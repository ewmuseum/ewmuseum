Real? Fake?

6 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The clothesline took Kodiak Vic Creed down, flat on his back. Before he knew it, he was being pinned by the one person he could never defeat. One... two... "... Three!" Sarah Creed cheered. The eleven-year-old jumped into the air, quite pleased with herself. "I beat you again!" Vic sat up and feigned disappointment, conceding his defeat. "You sure did pumpkin. You sure did. Now go get ready for supper." Sarah rushed out of the living room, running past her aunt. "I beat him again Auntie Mar!" Marilyn Creed smiled as he niece disappeared into the bathroom. "Good for you Sarah. You showed him who the boss is." She poked her head into the living room and saw Vic sitting on the carpet. "She got the better of you again, old man?" she asked with a wry grin. "Bite me you hag," Creed said as he got to his feet, a hint of a smile upturning the corner of his mouth. "Girl's got skills." "Yeah, with her patented Wrap-Daddy-Around Her-Finger finisher, you don't have a chance." Vic paused as his sister turned around with the aid of her cane and made her way into the dining room. He was amazed at the progress she'd made in the past year. The doctors had told her she'd be confined to a wheelchair. Now she was actually walking, albeit with a cane. Vic and his sister had a similar philosophy when it came to doctors: they don't know shit when it comes to a Creed. The spread on the table was impressive, a grand feast. For Vic, it was more of a Last Supper. The food looked great and smelled even better, but he knew he probably wouldn't eat much. He never did when it was time to go back to the nomadic lifestyle of the professional wrestler. He'd be leaving his daughter behind again, and that was always hard for the Main Man. But it was something they both knew he had to do. Understanding it was one thing. Accepting it was another. "Penny for your thoughts Vic. You look troubled." Marilyn looked at her brother with an understanding gaze. She knew it was hard for him, for all of them. She also knew Vic would never openly admit it. The man had a tendency to believe his own press clippings and always put on his 24/7 Badass face. "I think your gimpy eye is acting up again. Maybe you need your drops or something," muttered Creed as he dumped a spoonful of mashed potatoes onto his plate. Denial via assholism. Textbook Creedzilla. "She'll be okay Vic. You just get back on the road and take care of business. We'll take care of things here." Vic began sculpting his potatoes with the end of his fork. "Are you planning on watching me this Sunday? I'm going after the U.S. Title." "I'm gonna watch!" Sarah said in no uncertain terms as she plunked herself down in her chair. "I can watch, can't I Auntie Mar?" "We'll both be watching," Marilyn said, speaking to both Sarah and Vic. Both seemed satisfied with the response. "Are you gonna win the United States Champeenship?" Sarah asked her father. "Probably," Vic said as he dropped another scoop of potatoes onto his plate. "Not a whole lot of competition for me there.
