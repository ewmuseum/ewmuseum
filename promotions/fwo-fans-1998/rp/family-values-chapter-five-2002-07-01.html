Family Values - Chapter Five

1 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Between December 26th, 1991 and June 5, 1993 Spencer, Nebraska Angela's parents didn't believe her. More to the point, she never had the opportunity to tell them what happened to her. They called her selfish and hard to manage when, instead of going to the family party, she crawled back into bed and pulled her blankets around herself. While her parents and uncle were at her grandparents' that afternoon, the first of a dozen scars was put on her left arm. She wasn't trying to kill herself... she doesn't think so, at least. But cutting herself was a way of making pain hers again, not something someone else was causing. At the insistence of her parents, she said goodbye to her uncle when he left the next day, and she endured the humiliation of him patting her on the ass as he walked out the door. Her grades suffered almost immediately when she returned to school in January - it was as if she had been lost in a dark place and could not find her way out. Using makeup to cover her bruises, she began to craft the look that would eventually become her own. Black was the main color, white was the main skin tone, helped by the fact that her ethnic background leaned more toward pale. It kept people away - that's what she wanted. She buried herself into books about ethics and morality for the first six months, trying to find reason or rhyme to what had happened to her - and depsite her teen angst she could not accept that it was something she deserved. Many pagan charms found their way around her neck, as the ancient Egyptian and the modern Wiccan spirituality became the most appealing. Taking power back into yourself. That's what she wanted. But her uncle had managed to destroy her sense of self- worth along with her sense of security - power was no longer hers. Until the day she heard The Voice. "It took so long to remember just what happened. I was so young, vestal then, you know it hurt me. But I'm breathing so I guess I'm still alive. Even the signs seemed to tell me otherwise. Got my hands down, and my head down, And my eyes closed, my throat's wide open. I do unto others what has been done to me." Sitting at the bus stop in July, waiting to go home from the summer sessions that are helping to bring her school grades back to where they belong, she heard The Voice singing about the very things she had nightmares about. "Excuse me," she said, cautiously, to the person sitting on the stone ledge with the battery operated CD player and cheap speakers, "What are you listening to?" The person turned around - to her surprise it was a man - and looked at her with a friendly gaze. "They're called Tool. Just came out a little while ago." He was wearing plethor pants, a furry red coat despite the heat - and silver glittery sunglasses. Except for his short hair and his whiskers, Angela could swear he was a woman, from hehind. And from the front, she thought he was at least bisexual, if not outright gay. "I'm sorry," he continued, "I'm being rude. My name is Travis, Travis Johnson." He held out his hand for her to shake. After a moment's hesitation, she did. "I'm new in town," he said, "been looking around for the club scene and all while I catch up on some classes before the school year starts. What's there to do in and around Spencer?" "Not much," said Angela with a laugh - a laugh that surprised even her, "This place is pretty dead. There's a club that plays local bands, but nothing decent has ever come through there." "That sucks," replied Travis, "Brought my guitar with me, will put up the high sign for any musicians in town. You sing or play anything? Or write at all?" "No," said Angela, a bit too quickly - thinking about the notebook full of half- crazy poetry hidden inside her mattress at home, "Nothing like that." "Bummer," said Travis, turning off the radio and hopping down from the wall, "Well, be seein' ya." Angela watched him walk away for a moment before calling out a half- assed "Bye." She returned to her seat on the bench, thinking about the person she just met - her new friend, she considered him. He didn't look at her crazy, he didn't think she was a freak at all. More to the point, he was just as much of a "freak" as she was. Tool. Something about that singer's words told her to seek out the album, herself. And maybe.... just maybe.... she could find some answers for herself. What she found, instead, was a source of trust that formed almost instantly between herself and Travis - TJ, as she soon began to call him. Three weeks after this conversation, amidst a pounding heart and a near- panic attack, Angela showed him some scraps of poetry she had written since Christmas called "Dark Embrace" and "Deepest Desire." To his credit, he did not ask her where they came from, but he offered her suggestion on what she could do to the structure and form to turn it into a song. She liked singing - and to do so with just TJ and his brother Rob watching wasn't a big deal. Her first concert was to the photos on his basement wall, with TJ playing guitar and Rob playing drums. Therapy. Anyone who sings in a band will call it the same thing. For Angela, it was more than therapy. It was salvation. She slowly crawled out of her shell, singing with her eyes closed at the Johnson house and listening to every word of Maynard James Keenan religiously - if there was a religion dedicated to Tool worship she would have joined it instantly. The things that TJ taught her about the structure and flow of music were just as important as the things Tool taught her about how much you can put into your words - music gave a person permission to bare their soul. Quickly becoming a regular attraction at the only club in town, TJ taught Angela how to play guitar, and their first band was a three- piece set with no name, but they had immense presence. No longer alone, Angela was the constant companion of both Travis and Rob, the three constantly talking music. When Angela and Travis graduated from highschool and Travis moved to California with his brother to try and get something going there, Angela did not leave her room for three weeks - she was on a writing binge that shattered the remnants of her relationship with her parents. She didnt' care. For once she felt she had something in her life she was good at. As soon as she could afford to, she intended to move out with them. Until then, White As A Corpse was on hold. She tried to play some shows with new musicians - their music wasn't hard to master - but invariably the question would always arise as to where the lyrics came from. She had been able to convince herself that it was the past - that she was not going to let one event, no matter how traumatic, ruin her life. Until November 22, 1994.... when her past came back to haunt her, in what would be a violent reminder of how much scared little girl remained inside her.
