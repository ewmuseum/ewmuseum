Sins Of The Father - Part 1

18 December 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Click HERE for the Prologue. Mike stepped off the platform into the cold evening. Although snow hadn't visited Hong Kong, the air held a damp, pervasive chill that sunk in well beneath the skin. He had much on his mind, and he still wasn't certain where the solutions were. He turned up his collar to hold off the wind, and brushed back his black hair, which had grown just long enough to fall into his eyes. He rubbed his face for just a moment, trying to shake off the lack of sleep he had experienced throughout his journey. He briskly walked through the milling passengers, only partially hearing the toot of the train as it headed for its next destination. He was looking for a particular person, and after a few moments of activity, he spotted him. The Chinese man was bent, looking haggard and worn, lines crossing his face and hands to give it a haggard expression. Regardless, the eyes beamed with an internal light, a fierce brilliance lying behind those orbs that caught Mike off guard. "Mike: You are... Fen Wei?" The old man laughed, his breath wheezing out of him at odd intervals. Mike would later discover that Fen was just in his early thirties, despite his ancient appearance. When he spoke, it was obvious that English was a difficult language for him to master, but his speech was still remarkably clear. Fen: "Yes, that I would be. You are the American, that is here to see Papa?" "Mike: "Yes, I'm Mike Chronos." Mike pulled himself up straight, trying to emphasize his 6'3" frame, filled with 250 pounds of muscle. He was a giant here, and he knew he needed to impress his new potential employer, even if his servant looked to be little more than a decrepit old man. This left him completely open for the clubbing blow from behind, that caused him to slump forward into Fen's grasp. Already exhausted from the long trip, Mike couldn't have defended himself without exerting strenuous effort. He tried to fight off the impending darkness, but it was no use, and he faded quickly into unconsciousness. Mike awoke some scant minutes later, a throbbing sensation pounding from the back of his head to his temples. He attempted to reach up and massage the pain away, but found that he could not. Summary investigation proved that he had been bound to a chair, and was being held in a small room, with only one visible door. After what seemed to be an eternity, the bolt on the door slid open, and Fen Wei materialized in the now open archway. Behind him stood a Chinese man in his mid-forties, muscular looking, and with the tense air of one who has lived in fear for his life for quite some time. Mike correctly assumed that this man was his assailant. Fen: "So sorry, Mee-stir Chronos. We must take precautions - we knew you not, and there is always the chance that you might have been sent by rival Arrows to cause great harm." The other gentleman bristled at the word "Arrow", something that Mike alertly noticed. Fen: "Do not have anger with Wu Chin here, Mee-stir Chronos. He did only as he was instructed. We will release you now, and Papa will follow soon." Fen and Wu made quick work of the untying, and Mike stood, stretching his now cramped muscles. Suddenly, he threw a powerful left hook at Wu, striking him squarely in the forehead. Mike: "That's for hitting me from behind. Now, we're square." Mike offered his hand to Wu, and was relieved when Wu extended his hand as well. They shook, and Mike was again surprised when, after the momentary contact, Wu did not let go. Mike: "Hey, what give- oof!" Wu deftly fell back, kicking his leg into Mike's stomach, and propelling Mike over Wu's head. Mike took the fall like a regular bump, allowing himself to recover far more quickly than Wu had guessed. It was Chin's turn to be surprised, as Mike clubbed Wu on his way to his feet. Wu snarled, and prepared to leap at Mike upon his recovery, yet the entrance of another figure caused all activity in the room to cease. "ENOUGH!" The newest occupant of the tiny room had an aura of power emanating from him. The very tone of his voice implied that this was a man who was accustomed to being obeyed, without question. His thin mustache seemed an odd anachronism in comparison to the very modern business suit that he wore. This, then, was Papa Liu, the man whom Mike Chronos had come to see. Liu: "I apologize, Mr. Chronos. The behavior of my servants is a poor reflection on myself. I do not wish for my guests to be treated so roughly." A stare from Liu caused Wu to react almost as if he had been slapped. Wu quickly straightened himself, and took a position directly behind Liu. Fen busied himself gathering cups of tea, and setting the steaming liquid in front of both Mike and Papa Liu. Liu: "I understand that you have been directed this way because you are a professional fighter, neh?" Liu spoke with hardly the trace of an accent. "Why would you not be able to find employment in the States, good sir, or in Japan? It seems that your skills are not entirely deficient." If this was an insult or a compliment, Mike wasn't quite certain. He determined that honesty would be the best policy, and replied quickly. Mike: "Well the promotion I was with went bankrupt - the owner wasn't good at handling money. I probably could have gone back to the CWL, but, well, there's still some personal issues that would make that - uncomfortable. And in Japan, they only seem to be interested in jumping around from corner to corner - it seems that the art of wrestling is lost on their crowds." Liu smiled, but the light and warmth of the expression did not touch his eyes. Liu: "Yes, the Japanese have long been little more than bloodthirsty savages. You will find that we appreciate the Wu Shu in China much more than those on that infernal island. But perhaps a demonstration is in order, neh?" The smile remained on Liu's face, and he placed the cup of tea he had been sipping down in a ginger fashion. With a sweeping motion, he appears the door again, and strides confidently into the hall, Wu one step behind the entire way. Mike follows suit, with Fen bringing up the rear. They traverse a few hallways, and Mike can clearly hear muffled cries of excitement - a crowd of some sort. Liu opens a door, and the noise explodes. Stepping in, Mike finds that he is in a posh balcony, much like one would find in an opera or theatre house. But this was far from an establishment for enjoyment of the arts. In a filthy pit, some 20 feet below, a man and a woman circled each other. Both wore harness-like equipment, and for the woman, that was the full extent of her clothing. The man was unremarkable, but the female was extraordinary looking, except for the blood running freely from her nose. The man attempted a few jabs, but the woman deftly blocked them. As Mike watched on, near horror filling his senses, the woman struck back, hitting her opponent several times in the face, stomach, and groin, felling him. The crowd that surrounded the pit on all sides roared with delight, and money was freely tossed about. Liu: "This is our art of fighting, Mr. Chronos. It is very beautiful, is it not?" Mike was at a loss for words - the fact that women fought men was nearly enough to confound his senses, but the conditions in which they fought struck him ever more. Mike: "I... uh... well, this is a bit... different... from what I expected." Liu: "I thought as much. But look at the advantages, Mr. Chronos. You dwarf even my largest fighter, and the pay is beyond even what your top... promotions... in the United States can offer. You have little chance of losing, and I would only have you work very short tours, so that I could keep the novelty of an American wrestler fighting in my pits fresh and new. What would you say, Mr. Chronos?" Mike looked at the scene in front of him, and then thought of his wife, Eleni, back in the States. They had fallen on hard times, and with their second child on the way, the money would be needed. Perhaps he could just do one tour, make a decent sum, and then head back to the U.S. - if the pay really was as much as Liu claimed, that would be more than enough to keep them out of debt for the next few years, long enough for Mike to get his feet under him and debut with some other promotion. But one thought struck him, one question that he had to ask. Mike: "No offense, Liu, but I'll have to think this over. A question, though - how can you afford to pay me as much as you claim? It doesn't seem to be a very, um, high profile venue." Liu: "i thought you would have guessed, Mr. Chronos, that the British would frown upon the discovery of my activities. That is the reason you were brought here unconscious - if you choose not to accept my most generous offer, I could not risk you telling the authorities - or worse yet, my enemies - of my whereabouts." Mike: "So... this isn't exactly - legal, is it?" Liu: "In case you hadn't noticed, there is little in Hong Kong that you can not buy, Mr. Chronos. Regardless of whether the British pass a law against it or not." Mike: "Ah... um... OK. Well, how do I get to a hotel? I'd like to call my wife, and get some sleep. Is that fine?" Liu: "Mr. Chronos, I would not dream of any of my guests spending the night in a hotel. Hotel's are very dangerous places in Hong Kong, and your safety is a top priority of mine. I insist that you spend the evening here, in a guest suite. I can make arrangements for you to have a phone... so long as you give the word that you will not reveal, even to your wife, where you are located." Mike: "Well, I'm not too sure about all this..." Liu: "Mr. Chronos, please do not mistake me. This is not a request." With that, Liu spun out of the balcony room, Wu the ever vigilant shadow. Mike looked back down at the pit, where the blood from the last fight was being mopped up by a small girl. The crowd had dissipated quickly, and Mike knew that one mis-step and this could possibly become the worst mistake of his life.
