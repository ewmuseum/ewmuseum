The Beginning Of... (1 of 2)

Friday, February 22, 2002 9:32 Pacific Seattle, Washington All things considered, Scott Wasner was doing a lot better now than when we had last heard from him. The time in between had passed more or less without incident. The fWo brass was taking special care to make sure that he had as little contact as possible with Vic Creed, which was more than fine with him. He had maintained regular telephone contact with Stephanie - which was also good. God knew they were in a much better place emotionally now than they had been. Life was, well... life was good. For once. And it's about damn time too. And now, back home. Seattle had always been Scott Wasner's city. In his youth, it was where he wanted to live. He had achieved that dream early in 1998. After signing with the upstart fans Wrestling organization, the first thing he had done was to buy a house in Seattle. Four years later, he still lived there. Even when he was billed as being from Cooperstown, New York - a fine gimmick hometown, to be fair - it was always Seattle that was his home. There was no city in the world in which he'd rather spend his time. The atmosphere was indescribable - the city was vital, invigorating, and live - and not just because of all the coffee. Even the weather made Wasner feel alive. Often brisk, but never cold - often drizzling rain, but rarely a full-fledged storm. Let the rest of the world extoll the virtues of Los Angeles, Miami, New York... for Slugger, Seattle was it. He had arrived in town from Florida earlier in the day, and as he sat on the couch now, his recollections brought a smile to his face. Stephanie had been there to greet him, and they had spent the day together - doing everything and nothing, just enjoying each other's company. They had eaten dinner at the Market Grill, a top-notch seafood restaraunt in the famed Pike Place Market, and had arrived home about an hour earlier. Now they were on the couch, watching whatever was on television - Wasner wasn't really sure. He wasn't paying attention to the television. Stephanie's head was on his shoulder, and his arm was around hers. In the months to come, even through all he would endure, this moment would remain in Scott Wasner's memory, a sharply focused photograph of a moment that meant everything to him. A thought would be packaged with the memory. This is how it's supposed to be. It would be his last real memory of a time he was truly happy. ----- Saturday, February 23, 2002 1:26 AM Pacific Scott Wasner carefully rolled out of bed, taking care as to not wake Stephanie up. He checked his watch, pressing the light-up button in the darkness. Four minutes. This had been a decision that had been on his mind for weeks now. By now, it was too late to turn back. He had made the call four days ago, calling up an old Seattle acquaintance of his known as the Snake. They had set up their meeting for 1:30 today, and it was almost time. Wasner would bring the money, Snake would bring the merchandise - the cocaine. Just like old times. Wasner stopped in the doorway of the bedroom. As he stood, he realized the importance of this moment. What he decided here - to go out or to stay here - would affect the rest of his life. There was no real reason to suspect that this decision alone could determine the path of his future... but still, he knew it was true. What I've built for myself these past five months... my life. My career. My girl. I've finally got it all together... well, as close to together as it's gonna get. Is this really worth it? Taking even the slightest chance of going back to the way I was for most of last year... blowing probably my last chance to really be anything? To be anyone? Wasner shook his head. Paranoid, Scott. You're being paranoid. You're in a good place right now, and this isn't gonna change that. You've had those pills for a month now and you're still fine. You're finally where you want to be - think of this as a reward. One bag, a few snorts... and then that'll be it. Nobody'll even find out! Yeah. I'll be fine. I'll be fine. Wasner turned the doorknob and left the room with one final thought on his mind. You stupid bastard. Twenty minutes later, he was back inside. The cocaine was safely hidden, and he slid noiselessly back into bed.
