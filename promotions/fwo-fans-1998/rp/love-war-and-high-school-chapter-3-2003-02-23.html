Love, War, and High School- Chapter 3

23 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Another night. It was February 17th, 1995. Matthew Vorankias was pushed roughly out of the backseat of a red Pontiac, and he looked behind him as the car drove away. He looked down to see sidewalk, and up above the full moon was out, shining its light across the San Fransisco suberb. He looked out at where he had been dropped off- it was a large, blue house. The distinct sounds of a party could be heard from it- from the hard-rocking heavy metal rock music that was played so loud, he could hear it standing out on the sidewalk, to the loud drunken laughter. Matthew looked down at his clothing- he was still dressed in his school clothing- and couldn't believe he had let his friend Andrew talk him into coming here. He had no idea what to do at a party- he had never touched alchool in his life and didn't know how to deal with it. "What if they try and make me smoke marijuana?" Matthew muttered. "I could die with one puff!" "You can't die with one puff, Matthew." came the famailar female voice from behind him. Matthew turned around, and standing there was the girl who was simultonausly his love.....and his enemy. "What do you want, Amy?" Matthew asked her. Amy gave a sheepish smile and held out her arm. "Well....we're both here alone, and so I figured maybe we could go in together?" "Sure, I guess." Matthew responded. He hooked his right arm around her left arm, and together they walked down the pathway into the house of the apparent party. Entering through the door, they both quickly realized that they were out of their element. The air smelt of alchool, puke, and marijuana smoke. Teenagers of all ages- from Freshmen to Seniors- danced around the various rooms of the house, knocking over lamps and furniture. Others sat on the couches, boys and girls either whispering things into each other ears or making out, some even sharing a marijuana joint. The carpet they stood on was damp with a number of different substances. To be rich and bored. The two stepped into the domain, finding a spot in the living room where there was relativley nothing on the floor and no drunken teenagers dancing. They leaned against the wall, seated there and listening to the blaring heavy metal music. They did their best to look "cool", but instead ended up just looking uncomfortable. Neither of them knew what to say to each other. They ended up turning their heads and looking in opposite directions, watching the party rage on. Fifteen minutes later, one of them finally spoke. "I'm sorry." Amy said. Matthew turned to face her. "Sorry for what?" he asked her. "I don't know, but I must have done something." she said with a sigh. "I can tell you've been avoiding me over the past few weeks." "Yeah...." Matthew said, his voice trailing off. "What did I do wrong, Matthew?" she asked him. ".....I don't know." he responded after an uncomfortable few seconds. "Look, I've gotta get going." Matthew stood up and started walking towards the door, but stopped when he realized Amy's hand was on his shoulder. "Please don't." she said. "I want to know what's going on." "It's nothing!" Matthew finally exclaimed. "Look, I just have to get home, okay?" Matthew took her hand with his and moved it off his shoulder, and then continued walking. Amy watched him leave that house and the party. Why won't he talk to me? she thought. Why won't he tell me what's wrong? As Matthew exited the house, he took one last time to look back, and saw Amy still standing there, watching him. She's as beautiful as ever. Matthew thought. But....I can't do it. Things are too confusing right now. I need to be alone. "Matthew Vorankias?" Matthew turned back around from looking at Amy, to see a police officer standing there. "That's me." he answered. "Wha-What is it?" "You didn't tell your foster parents where you were going." the officer said. "Well, I'm sorry I left without telling them where I was going, but why would that be a matter for a police officer?" Matthew wondered. "Son.....there's some news at home for you." the officer said. "Some bad news. Please, come with me." A confused Matthew simply nodded, and got into the passanger seat of the police car. The car drove off into the night. Alone.....
