Waking up and getting up has never been easy...

22 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"When the fuck did I start drinking scotch?" Ivy McGinnis sat in the cool basement of her three - level living quarters on the outskirts of Seattle, watching the replay of the FWO's Free Wrestling Day. She had a half - filled bottle of Guinness on the table in front of her, as well as the crust of a slice of pizza on a plate. There were no windows so she couldn't tell what time of day it was or what the weather looked like, but by the look of her she was unshowered for at least today, and dressed in cutoff jean shorts and a New York Yankees jersey with #42 on the back, looked like she was probably not intending to go outside. And she was on the phone. "Don't ask me," said her older brother, Brian, on the other end, "every time we've hung out to drink it's been beer, or it's been shots." There was concern in his voice; though she didn't catch it. Of course he was worried. She was essentially doing the same job as when the FWO last closed down, only now, she was supposed to have a boss who had the final say - so. Only now, she was a senior member of the team by far more than ever before, and the youngsters, perhaps remembering the business predator she originated in Eli Flair's corner for fourteen years naturally went to her to make sure what they wanted to do got done. "It's just a sign of the times," continued Brian, "We're all getting older." Ivy muted the television, cutting off High Flyer's closing quip. "What the shit is that s'posed to mean, Brian?" she asked, "I didn't start drinking Scotch, other than when we were talking about Daddy, until---" Realization. "Until I started going to Beav's office for some peace and quiet." Ivy stood up and drank a large sip from the bottle. "This job changed me, man. And I really, really don't like it. I used to be feared and respected, now I'm just the crazy lady that's slowly going gray. I'm not supposed to be gray at thirty - four, Brian!" Brian chuckled. At thirty - seven, married with four kids (three daughters), he was plenty gray and with good reason. "You've been in charge before, either of a company or of a specific portion of it. Did anything in Greensboro ever stress you out this much?" "No." "Why do you think that is?" "I don't know." "Yes, you do," he insisted. Ivy paced. She heard movement upstairs and pulled the phone from her ear. "No beer, Lowell! Go to sleep!" The movement stopped, then started again in the opposite direction. "Sorry," she said to her brother, "You were about to fix me?" "Want to know why you're going crazy?" "Besides the obvious, right?" "I wanted to wait for you to say it." "Fuck you," said Ivy, laughing. "You know I can't say it back or Kim will smack me." It was true. Ivy's sister in law had a very strict no-cursing policy in their house. "So get to it," continued Ivy, "What's the problem?" "You're not setting the tone anymore," replied Brian. There was silence. "What are you talking about?" "Go watch some of your stuff from 2005 and points forward," continued Brian, "or before then, every time you were in a management and someone came to talk to you, you did the talking. You made the decisions. With this new group, they're dropping the situation in your lap and forcing you to decide, but I know you, Ivy. You're not officially the boss so you're not calling them on their bullshit." "It's not my company, I can't be that hardassed," countered Ivy. "That's who Cool hired, though," replied Brian, "that's who you're meant to be." "A psychotic bitch?" "Or shorten it to a Psycho Bitch," reminded Brian. "Mommy!" "Shit. Brian, I gotta go, Shannon's calling me." "Okay," replied Brian, "Love ya. Why don't you come home for a few days and clear your head?" She thought about it. "You know something, you're on." The phone clicked off, and Ivy headed for the stairs. Maybe a few days in New York would work. The Yankees were on a nine game winning streak and she could hit the Philly series this weekend. The FWO had sucked up so much of her life, she hasn't even been to the new stadium yet despite holding season tickets. And the Singapore Cane that had accompanied her for forty percent of her life was still mounted on the wall at the bar. Maybe? No. Fuck maybe.
