The One In South California - Part One

30 December 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Hello, my name is JP Doutreaux. I'm an executive at Visually Appealing Studios. The film studio, not the graphic design firm. I know, I know, I should've sued those name-stealing bastards a long time ago, but lawyers in this state are expensive, and VAS has very little operating capital at the moment to begin with, due to a series of bad investments that crippled us financially. Bottom line: Another flop, and that's it--we're done, finished, caput. Writers these days only seem to want to pen high concept, expensive tripe. Style over substance is the mantra of the film industry in the 21st century. The films of yesteryear, the good ol' days, could be made for cheap, and still generate big bucks at the box office. Directors nowadays will scoff at a fifty million dollar budget. Fifty million--I kid you not! They all want A-list money, but very few do A-list work. Earlier today a screenplay entitled "The One In South California" was placed on my desk. I'd asked my assistant to bring me a script with a simple premise that would widely appeal to the coveted 18-to-35 demographic, and that could be made for next to nothing. (Once again, VAS can't afford to lose money on this one.) Anyway, as fickle as I've been in the past when presented with something written by an unknown, I was intrigued enough in my present state of desperation to cancel my lunch date, and read the damn thing from start to finish. To my surprise, the writer had attached a cover letter--a nice touch, I thought, and something you don't ordinarily get from an established name. In it, he stated that the plot of the film was based on true events that were told to him by a strange man he'd met while living on the street, shortly after moving to Los Angeles from Vancouver. Apparently the man's conviction was enough to convince the writer that the story was real. Upon reading it, however, I'm not so sure. Then again, sometimes fact is stranger than fiction. And sometimes the insane ramblings of a crack addicted nut are just what moviegoers will pay to see… JP Doutreaux, MBA Visually Appealing Studios *** THE ONE IN SOUTH CALIFORNIA Screenplay by COREY JEPSON Story by LOWELL FADE IN: EXT. SOUTHERN CALIFORNIA-SECLUDED AREA-LATE NIGHT LOWELL and NUñEZ, recent escapees from the federal penitentiary of Victorville, RUN at full-speed through an open field somewhere in San Bernardino County.
