Waste, Revisited

8 January 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A friend? He laughed at the mere thought of what had taken one-third of a year ago. Four months, it seemed so long ago. The hotel room, over-the-counter sleeping pills, a phone call, looking in the mirror at the self-destruction. And, a friend. London was rather foggy, he watched the setting sun from his small room. His eyes kept wandering from the window, back to the phone, hoping it would ring. He didn't care if it was Monica, the bellhop, room service, a wake up call at 5:56 p.m., he just wanted the phone to ring. But it didn't. And it wouldn't. "Dammit," he grumbled under his breath. Thoughts had been creeping back into his mind from the recent past. The most recent being one night, September the first, to be exact. He had started to end his life, slowly and painfully. Swallowing each pill, no water, if he was going to go, it wouldn't be easy. But, what he desperately wanted right now, was what saved him in the hotel. A phone, that rang and had a voice on the other end that wanted to help. A call from the person he had gone through hell with and even gained a little respect for. "Jon," he thought aloud. The last thing he remembered was that he disappeared shortly after that night. Gone for what seemed like two, maybe three months. There was virtually no contact between the two since that night. Except for Twilight. They had fought of Flair and Wretch, together. Together. He laughed at that. They fought together. Later on that night, he and Monica were back together. Funny how things happen that way. They happen in pairs, one meaningful, like Monica. The other, seemingly meaningless, but yet, there is a lot left unscathed under the surface. His eyes were focused on the thick fog that blanketed the royal city. It was nearly as if he were in the clouds. Normally they were so high, floating slowly through the sky, his imagination making out shapes from their different forms. Again a laugh escaped him. It all seemed childish to him. Waiting in the room, just hoping, nearly praying that the phone would sound. Hopeless even. Monica was in the next room over, probably gone down to eat in the extravagant dining room the hotel had. "They spend their lives in a box The cardboard of society All carbon copies of each other Falling faster into decay Their minds as thin as paper All are thinking alike No mind to call their own They're quickly melting into nothing They're the mindless pen Making it all work Toiling into nothingness Their lives, completely meaningless Society has worn them down They feel nothing now As they watch each day end They realize that they're the wasted" That exact feeling ran through his body again. His shoulders slumped, his head fell. For some reason, feelings from the past came back, he felt like nothing again. So close to the person that he cared about, but he was still so far away from her. Nobody would understand his plight. No one ever did, or tried to understand what he went through. Even if he tried to feel for other people. He still felt like nothing. Raising his right arm, he looked at the bar code tattoo on his wrist, Social Security Number underneath. It was how the government knew him. Why not everyone else? If the government only cared about taxes, figures, and no names, no faces, just a number. Not caring about feelings or any other single thing, why should anybody else? The government dictated the way society is run, sticking mindless drones inside of cubicles. They had no true feelings, they are part of one single collective. Feelings mean nothing to them, therefore nobody else in the world should feel anything for themselves, or one another. Empathy no longer meant anything in this world. "Fuck empathy."
