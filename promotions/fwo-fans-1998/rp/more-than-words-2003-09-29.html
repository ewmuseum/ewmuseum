More Than Words

29 September 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Last call was 20 minutes ago, man," states the bartender, "It's time to leave." The man he was peaking to was Wippit Guud. But not Wippit Guud. Dressed in a white t-shirt and faded jeans, his hair a tangled mess, no 'trademark smile' or catchy phrases. Just a man, at a bar, bottle of Polar Ice 4/5th empty in front of him, and a well-used shot glass. Wippit raised his eyes from the bottle he'd been staring at, eyes now bloodshot from too many shots in too little time. A twisted mockery of a smile split his lips as he focused his attention on the man behind the bar. 'And juss where da hell am I sposed to go? Home? I don't have a home anymore. My home up and lef me." "Listen man, I don't care about your personal troubles, but I have to close up." the bartender noted, quietly signaling for a bouncer to apprach. 'That's the problem," said Wippit, "no one cares. All anyone cares about is themselves. It's not sposed to be like that. I cared... I loved! it wasn't for me, it wad for her! And how did she repay me? Lef me. Took my son and lef. 'Can't handle it anymore, Chris.' she said. 'Can't live wif you anymore.' she said. And now I gotta live with myself?" Wippit felt the hand of the boucher come down on his shoulder, "A'right, I get the picture, I'll leave." He stood up and look at the burly 6'3", 240 lb man who now stood next to him, " I don't wanna cause trouble. Never have, never do." He started to walk, off balance towards the door. His drunken voice flotaed over the now-emtpy bar, "But maybe you should start to care. If everybody cared, juss a little..." his words were lost in incoherant mumbling as he walked out. Alone.
