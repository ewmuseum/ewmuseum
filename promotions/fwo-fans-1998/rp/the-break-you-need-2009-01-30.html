The Break You Need

30 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
William Magnum Decker sat on the barstool, a half bottle of cheap whiskey and a dirty shot glass in front of him. The bar was dirty and almost empty, the sort of dive you could only find if you actively looked for it or had an extreme stroke of luck. Decker wasn’t smart or ambitious enough to have actually looked for the place but happened to stumble upon it during his exploratory ‘tours’ of ALL the neighborhood bars. He found it, liked it, and stayed. No rocket science for Decker, just action and reaction. The bartender stood behind the bar like just one more of the dingy fixtures of the place. Decker had been coming here for years and never bothered to learn his name. One other barstool was occupied but after glancing over once a couple of hours ago Decker hadn’t given the other hunched figure another thought. An old television glowed overhead, bathing everything in its eerie electronic light. The pitiful scene had a sad, odd beauty to it which, of course, was totally lost on Decker. Decker poured a shot and downed it with a grace and ease that comes only with years of practice. He wasn’t a smart man – that had been established long ago and had been proven on a daily basis – but he was a determined man, especially when he was on a case. He knew how to get results and take care of business; he’d been a piss-poor cop, he was a deplorable human being, but he was a damned good private investigator. Another shot. He had to admit that he had taken this current case as far as his capable talents could go, but he had run into a brick wall. He’d questioned, bullied, and threatened every last person he’d previously relied on and got absolutely nowhere. Decker and failure were old friends, but this was a first as a P.I… “……hundred and twenty two pounds, making his FWO Debut, from Upstart over Downington's Cross, FAIRVIEW REED!" The name pierced Decker’s alcohol-impaired attention like a harpoon. “Holy… Hey! What is this?” Decker addressed the bartender for the first time in their long acquaintance. Decker pointed to the TV and watched intently. A Plain Jane of a man instinctively put one foot in front of the other and had his nose buried in a huge book. Following the man protectively was the biggest woman Decker had ever seen (and he’d seen and done plenty of big women). “It’s wrestling, genius.” “I mean, what channel is this? What wrestling? Where are they?” As he watched the bookworm and the big girl made it to ringside. The man marked his place in the book and took off his glasses. Decker stared at the screen, focusing on the man. Here suddenly, miraculously was the man he’d been looking for, dropped in his lap like manna from heaven. The match itself was over almost before it started. It was apparent that his man was no fighter. “FWO,” said the bartender. “Seattle, I think.” Seattle. Decker took one more shot and took out a small notebook and pen. It looked like travel was in his immediate future.
