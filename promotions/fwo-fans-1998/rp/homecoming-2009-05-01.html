Homecoming

1 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Miami Beach. September, 2004. “Yes sir. I understand,” I say as that stupid lump in my throat comes back from the pit of my soul. “Thank you, Mister Beaven. I appreciate the opportunity.” I hang up the cellular phone and look back to the road. Tossing it in the empty passenger seat, I take a deep breath as I notice my driveway isn’t empty today. So much for subtle, I joke internally. I step onto the dirt and gravel, closing my door as loud as possible (apparently). With my limp making that off-beat sound that annoys the Hell out of me, I stagger toward my house. Gripping the front doorknob, I take another deep, soothing breath and enter. “Daddy!” The booming thuds of little feet near the corner and then make my eyes explode when I spot hers. My baby girl jumps into my arms and I spin her around like all fathers would do if their daughters stayed this small. “How’s my big girl? Been good today?” She smiles and nods, not making a peep. That always gets me. But at this point my back and leg are killing me, so she’s got to go. Placing her on my shoulder, I carry her into the living room and bounce her off of the sofa. I can hear the air behind me grow short and the door open. It must be her. My Unicorn. My Maiden. My Celeste, I say inside my skull. “Jesus,” she blurts out at first sight. “Honey, look at your neck!” She scampers to my side and places her soft fingers on the bruises. It doesn’t hurt. It feels better instantly. If only she were there when it happened. I wouldn’t feel so worthless. She then finds more to look at underneath my shirt and removes it at will. She gasps at the sight of my bare body. “It’s nothin’, babe,” I say clasping my hand over hers. “Just a paycheck, remember?” She lets out a snort and rolls her eyes. “Yeah, and strippers are dancers, too, right?” Well, yeah. I think for a few unfiltered seconds. “This has to stop,” she says, again lightly running her fingers over my scars and shoulders. “I’ve seen you go from rough to worse and down a hole I’ve never seen anybody go down before. It’s... frightening.” Her aqua eyes make me feel as if I have a say in the matter. So serene and colorful, like a painting you would find in a beach house. My body and thoughts are overcome with complete relaxation. “Well...” I say with extreme caution. The type of caution that, when you live with someone for a long time, others will clue in on. Like a long pause before a lie. Sort of like what I’m doing right now. Shit, I think immediately after. “Well... what?” She waits and stares into my soul. The waiting comes from her knowing the answer isn’t something I want to tell her and really isn’t something she wants to hear. Or is it? I wonder. I smile at the mother of my children. “I won’t have to anymore.” “You...” Her furrowed brow and scorn look are quickly washed away by an expression of clarity. “Won’t have to? What do you mean?” We’re caught, thinking softly to myself. Play it cool. Play. It. Cool. “It’s just like you said,” I say walking to the liquor cabinet. Pulling out the finest Brandy in the house, as such an occasion requires, I hold it up to offer a glass. Celeste declines with a wave of her hand. I nod and pour my own. Damn that’s sharp, I thought as the fiery quench rests in the back of my throat. “This is getting out of hand. I’m working too late, never here with my family, and constantly have been under public scrutiny... and for what? Being the best at what I do? Not being afraid to tell the world about it? Being one of the elite athletes around? No way.” I take another pull off the ice cold Brandy. “I’m done.” Downing the rest of the glass, I can feel her eyes on me like a GPS tracker. My every move, every breath is under scrutiny inside my own home. Slowly looking to her, I see what her next line is before she even opens her mouth. “You quit again, didn’t you?” Arms crossed, Celeste waits for a good few seconds as I stare at her caught completely off guard. She throws her arms in the air and storms off into the deep cave-like halls of the Miami Beach mansion. Damn she’s good. “Wha- QUIT? No!” She walks beyond my sight but I know that the acoustics in this house are ripe as a fresh picked peach. They work well. “Think of it more as... a relief of my duties, babe! ... To take on more duties... here.” I pause in effort to muster up a better line. "WE quit them!" She stomps back into the doorway for the final word. “You don’t think I want to quit teaching?! Quit selling Girl Scout cookies? Quit... everything!?” “Well, no. You love that stuff,” I remind her of how I remember her telling me that when we first met in college. “Look, Celeste. It’s okay. We're gonna be-“ “No! You look,” She stops and looks me square in the eye before taking off to do whatever she does after we argue about money and life and planting things in the Spring time. “YOU need to figure out what we’re going to do for food and money and a life after all this bullshit. You keep doing this! On and off, then on again. How are we supposed to live, eat, or sleep in peace with you constantly throwing a wrench in the engine? Tell me. How?” Local VFW and house shows!? Hell no! I scream to myself. Not my family! “And I’m not talking five, six hundred dollar gigs at the VFW on Miller Ave, darlin’,” she says prying into my head. “I want to eat right, live in this house until I’m old, and drive that damned blue Acura every day until you buy me a new, silver one!” Celeste stamps out with a harsh tone echoing in my ears. I notice Dani is nowhere in the room. She usually runs off before we finish the dramatics. Poor kid. I let out a deep sigh and look to the window, cursing myself for being me. Way to go, stud. I look into the once filled glass of Brandy and ice and knock the cubes around a few times before setting it down. I reach up, tightly gripping the neck of the bottle, and pull it to eye level. Taking a strong pull won’t help me feel any better but at least it’ll numb the pain. I hope. There’s no time to stop and think. I suppose ‘Quit’ is an easier excuse than ‘Couldn’t cut it anymore,’ right Greg?
