Girls' Night Out - Introductions

8 July 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Siobhan McPhineas stood outside the quaint brick building. Lush, full trees lined the street, couples strolled the sidewalks, and the soft breeze carried just a hint of the ocean. The scene was truly picturesque and, unfortunately, unseen by the auburn haired beauty. Truth be told, Siobhan was petrified. The quaint brick building was McP’s Irish Pub & Grill, and would soon be visited – by Siobhan’s own invitation – with the people she desperately wanted to get to know better; the women of the Fans’ Wrestling Organization. With William Beaven’s recent ultimatum Siobhan had to learn how to manage a professional wrestler, and actually she could use some female friends as well. The Assistant was in some ways closer than a sister to her, but sometimes Siobhan really, truly needed real conversation, not the non-verbal communication she had to carry on with the massive blonde beauty. Speaking of which… “Are y’ready?” Siobhan glanced over at The Assistant, who smiled broadly and nodded once. And then she winked, mischievously. Siobhan laughed in response, feeling a bit more relaxed and ready. The two entered the bar. Five minutes later they were seated at their large, reserved table near the back; far enough away from the tiny stage (even now someone was up there, checking wires and moving amps) that conversation wouldn’t be impossible but close enough to enjoy the show. The Assistant was sipping her iced tea and Siobhan, in an uncharacteristic moment, had a Smithwick’s in front of her. Whether it was nerves or just feeling the freedom of the night to come Siobhan was having a beer and ENJOYING it! Responsibility and fear had rested on her shoulders for far too long and she had a feeling – a true feeling – that it would be a long while before she’d have a chance like this again. The door opened and someone familiar walked in. Game on. Ivy McGinnis scanned the room and saw Siobhan and the Assistant at their table. She nodded toward the women, but took a sharp right turn toward the bar. A brief conversation with the bartender yielded two full shotglasses. She picked the filled glasses up and carefully walked to the table, and put one down in front of Siobhan. "This'll be easier with just us here," said Ivy, "Drink up." Siobhan looked at the shotglass with a discerning eye. "What's in it?" "Bushmill's single malt sixteen year," replied Ivy, "from one Irish lass to another, cheers." She tapped her shotglass against Siobhan's, and downed the whiskey in one fluid motion. Siobhan hesitated, but took the shot as well, and as she finished, one of the barbacks had arrived with a bottle of Dos Equis for Ivy, and he removed the glasses. "We won't be alone for long," assured Ivy, "I saw a few more familiar faces looking for a place to park." And Ivy's theory was correct. "I can't believe they got thrown out of a bowling alley." Brittany Chambers was saying to Michelle Masters as Mrs. Bear and the fWo Cruiserweight champion wandered in. "At least I wasn't there for that one." "I can't believe they actually managed to bowl a whole game before being thrown out." Michelle countered with a grin. "Though I'm not sure if what Mike and Pierre did technically counted as bowling." "Love em, but I think it'll be good to be away from all the craziness." Chambers opined. "Amen to that." Michelle agreed as she collected a Heineken for herself and Chambers grabbed a (Bear fill this in!). Ivy waved and called out Michelle's name, and the two wandered over to join Ivy, the Assistant, and Siobhan at the table. @%@%@%@%@ Siobhan looked around to make sure everyone had a full glass or bottle. Satisfied, she started things off. “Well, ladies, sure’n I’m wanting to thank ye all for making it here. It really means a lot t’me.” Siobhan raised her beer. “Here’s to Girl’s Night Out!” Laughter, hooting, and the sound of many glasses lightly tapping each other. “I was wondering if any of ye noticed the cheeky bastard across the way? Long hair and the smary look on his mug? He’s looking familiar, t’be sure…” “Argent”, someone offered. More hooting and laughs, and a boo or two. They all looked directly at him which made him smile lecherously back at them. Siobahn raised her beer to him but addressed the girls out of the side of her mouth. “Well, I’m thinking if we were t’send a drink to him that might keep the wee lad occupied…” She took a long swig. “…An if we keep ‘em coming I’m sure his ego’ll overcome his common sense and he’ll be under the table soon enough.” She waved a waitress over and set the devious scheme into motion, though Ivy McGinnis took immediate control. "Here," she said to the waitress, as she handed off a credit card, "put everything on this tonight. Also, there's a man at the bar - blonde hair, dim look in his eyes, you see him?" The waitress looked in the general direction of Ivy's gaze. "Yeah, I see him." "Put his drinks on my card too, with our compliments," said Ivy, "and make them as strong as you possibly can." The waitress nodded, as if she understood exactly what the ladies were looking for, and she immediately returned to the bar. “So, now that he’s taken care of I’m supposing it’s down to business. If’n ye don’t know I’m sure ye’ve guessed by now that I’m Siobhan. And this is my close, dear friend The Assistant. And before ye ask, that IS indeed the only name she’s ever known. She’s been with Mr. Reed far longer than I have and that’s about all I know.” Siobhan shrugged her shoulders. “As for my own self, I’m from County Kilarney, Ireland. I’m the only daughter in a long line of brothers, and I know nothing about bein’ Mr. Reed’s manager! Sure’n there’s scheduling and paperwork and settin’ matches and all that, but really – what can and should I be expectin’ in this business? “What am I getting’ meself into?”
