Oversight...?

15 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The door to Simon Cowl's office swung open with a bang. He looked up momentarily, then returned to the stack of papers currently occupying his attention. This intruder didn't really warrant a lot of his notice. This intruder was beneath him. "You stupid, stupid, stupid son of a flea-bitten bitch." Apparently, the intruder, fWo Commissioner Jim Dudley, didn't think Cowl was above the interruption. "What's twistin' your tit, Dudley?" "You're supposed to be keeping an eye on the contracts, Cowl... it's one of the things you're paid to do." "I am keeping an eye," retorted Cowl coolly, "I'm doing what Smith wants - keeping the ones we should be and letting the others expire." Dudley rolled his eyes and slammed the door hard enough to cause a small bit of plaster to fall off the wall. "You didn't renew Flair's contract?" "Both are more trouble than they're worth, and I'm not the only one who thinks that way." Dudley stared holes of flame into the top of Cowl's head. "HE'S THE CHAMPION YOU ASSHOLE!" "Champions can change," reminded Cowl, "as you, yourself, know." Dudley rolled his eyes and withdrew a piece of paper from his back pocket, and slammed it on the desk. Cowl stared at him with exasperation for a moment, before he unfolded the paper - looked at it for a second - and tossed it. "I've got this, it's the last page of Flair's contract." "Did you do the math, and figure out when it runs out?" Cowl exhaled, annoyed, and read it for a few more moments. "So?" "When does it run out?" "...April 27th. Again... so what?" Dudley didn't respond right away. He reached over the desk, grabbed Cowl by his collar, and pulled him up face to face. "So, you've let the World Champion's contract lapse, two days before the biggest show of the fWo year. Despite how you or I might feel about Flair and Ivy personally... that is the single worst business decision that could ever be made." Cowl sneered. "So he'll show up. He's got no choice." "Wrong," replied Dudley, "he's got all the power here. Contractually, he doesn't have to be there." "We'll make him--" "Have you seen what he does to himself and everyone else?" interrupted Dudley, "There's nothing we can physically do to him if he won't let it happen." The commissioner dropped the lawyer back into his seat. "You got the company into this, Cowl," said Dudley as he headed for the door, "so you'd better start tap-dancing... because you've gotta get us outta it."
