Tryptich - Part 3

15 February 2003

Tryptich Part Three - Eden Eye "2001 was certainly an odyssy for me. I'd been in the business for seven years and had seen more backstabbing, blackballing, and destructive politics than I'd ever thought possible, and I was sick of it. The CWL, the only promotion I'd ever come across where the politics seemed to have missed a bet, was forced to uproot and head overseas exclusively. Thanks, in part, to myself and Steven Shadows. How's that for an inferiority complex? Then the towers went down and I could feel the city die. And I thought that if I was gonna be a shiftless waste, I might as well be somewhere that encourages it." "Angel, at the time, was dating her guitarist... a guy who gets nasty and violent when he drinks. She'd seen enough violence from people who were supposed to love her to last a few lifetimes... but she still did her thing. Worked shit jobs for little money and no respect by day... singing her shit to crowds that ranged from impassioned to indifferent at night. She reminded me why I had stuck with wrestling after Alicia died... because if you love what you're doing, you don't just stop when things are hard. She waited tables, she danced on top of them, she worked in video stores... she refused to let the world beat her even when her boyfriend would." "I know I'm hard on Mickey, and I think he deserves it at times, but he also hasn't had a drink in over a year and has proved to everyone in and around the band that he's changed. Good for him, he's the best guitar player I've ever heard." "It's funny how two people can be so similar, and change so drastically as a result of being together. Angel used to be a quiet, shy girl... extroverted solely when she was on stage singing. I went from this bitter, cynical little bastard, too full of my own self- righteous fervor to see that if I truly loved my profession I wouldn't be pointing out all the things wrong with it, but trying to highlight what's right about it. I'd like to think it was some of my influence that turned Angel into the most outspoken musician you've never heard of - YET - and I know it was her influence that helped turn the King of Extreme into someone a little more human." "Like that was my problem, right? I needed to be less human to survive in this business. At least, that was what I thought based on my definition of a human. But now I know... I can't save everyone. I can't protect everyone. I can't solve everyone's problems. But I also have to accept that and not beat myself up over it. The world - and the people I love - will survive if I'm just there to lend an ear. Angel told me that the night we met... she's right, y'know." "I had two choices. I could either go even farther toward an early grave, thus fulfilling the destiny that both my parents and all my grandparents left for me, or I could become more human. For the first time in my life, there was someone that didn't try and label me, for good or bad, and tell me what they thought I should be about. Angel simply said 'You do your thing, and we'll be fine.' I know that it's only Angel I would've ever traveled halfway across the continent for, simply to tell her that I loved her. I know it's only Angel that I could've ever imagined myself actually settling down, such as it is, and raising a family with. She's made me think of a life beyond the wrestling business... something that would've been a foreign concept before." "How pathetic is that? If it wasn't for the wrestling business, I wouldn't even've had a life. And wrestling is still one of the most important things in my life but it shouldn't be the most important. I should be willing to give the whole thing up for something I believe in... for something I think is worth it." "Angel isn't Alicia. I know that and I never tried to make us into the latter. What Alicia and I had was special in that it kept me going during a hard phase of my life. But now, with Angel, I know who I am. I know what I want out of life. And I know what I actually knew all along... that all I ever needed to do to be happy was be myself." "It's a little unsettling, most of my public life has seen my private life go up in flames. I'd reacted to this by shutting my personal life down, much to the growth of my wrestling career in ways I've never even imagined. And now, the legend of Eli Flair seems to have eclipsed the truth of my career direction, and has overshadowed what I'm able, or even willing to do with it nowadays." "I know I won't be wrestling for much longer. I'm thirty one years old, and logistically, there are things I could do at twenty- two that I simply can't recover from easily anymore. There are things that I won't do anymore because the risks are too high and the expectations have risen through the roof. But there are things that I need to do, both in and out of this sport... that are too important to pass up." "And as time goes on... there's more that I want to do out of it than in it."
