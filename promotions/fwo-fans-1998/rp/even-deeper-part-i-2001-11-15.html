(even) Deeper, Part I

15 November 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
School had just ended another day inside of Detroit's inner city. The cold November day was crisp, the air harsh and chilly, the light snowfall only added to the blustery conditions. His short eight year old legs pumped up and down and skidded around a short corner. Emphatically sucking the air into his lungs he thrusted his body against the cold, hard adobe brick building. Hesitating, he inched his body towards the corner of the building to look down either side of the street. They weren't chasing him from what he could see, but he knew his young eyes failed him quite often. It had been nearly a week since they last caught him and beat him down in the middle of the street. He could never figure out why nobody ever helped him, some of the passing people would just shake their heads repeating the same sentence over and over again; "Boys will be boys," before continuing their ways down the sidewalk. This time it seemed as if the coast was clear. He placed his left foot around the corner and back into the traffic of the people. Then his right foot and the rest of his body came across and he began to walk down the sidewalk, looking behind his back every few steps, wary of the danger that he knew surrounded him. In his peripheral view his eye caught the site of one of the boys. His little heart began to race again. The fight-or-flight syndrome rushing through his body. Instinctively, he began to sprint towards "home" again. In his mind he wasn't very far from the shelter and if he could beat them there, he'd be protected by the staff of the shelter. One small problem, he missed a step and his foot got stuck where the sidewalk elevated a bit. He lost his footing on the slippery brick sidewalk and fell to his hands and knees. His teeth instantly gritted together and tears formed in the corner of his eyes. Quickly he pushed himself against one of the shop walls that lined the street and assessed the damage. His new jeans were ripped open at each knee, small red blots forming from where the skin had ripped away. Looking at his hands he saw the blood begin to start rising underneath the scraped skin. Everything became blurry in his eyesight as he started to cry. He knew that his father would beat him because the jeans were ripped. And he was about to get a beating when the older boys caught up with him. A dilemma of what to do and which beating would be more severe. The broom, or immature twelve year old fists, pounding away at his weak body. Before he realized it, three preteen boys were standing in front of him. Their fists were balled up underneath gloves, ready to attack. He quickly covered his face with his bleeding hands. "Please don't hurt me," he sobbed from behind his hands. "Just leave me alone." The red head, who was seemingly the leader of the group chuckled before kicking a rock in his direction. "We'll do whatever we want with you, boy," he snorted. The red head nodded and the three boys jumped down and began to attack. Beating the small child relentlessly. His hands dropped from his face and blood began to form in the corners of his mouth. Tears were flowing from his eyes, but he sobbed silently. His mind raced, asking questions over and over again. When will they stop this time? Why won't someone make them stop? Please, someone make them stop. Help me. Why doesn't anyone do anything? I wish someone cared. His thoughts and the three boys were interrupted when the voice of a girl around his age yelled out. "STOP," she screamed at the top of her lungs. Behind the girl who two police officers. The boys tried to run away from what they had done, but they were no match for the officers. They caught the boys quickly and held on to them tightly. The small girl raced over and helped lift up the battered boy from the ground. His mouth was crimson red, tears mixed with blood were stained onto his coat and the white shirt underneath. He looked at her through his tear-filled eyes and released a small smile. She smiled back. "Hey, are you okay," she asked, already knowing the answer. "No, they got me pretty good this time," he replied as he wiped the tears from his eyes. "Yeah, I've seen them do this to you nearly every day," she remorsefully remarked. "I just finally told someone. Sorry I never did before." "It's okay," he said. "It won't stop them." "Yeah it will," she smiled. "Trust me. I'm Monica, who are you?" M-my name's Donaven," he said. "Donaven Winters..." "Well Donaven, it's really nice to meet you," she smiled again. Two more officers arrived on the scene and got Monica and Donaven into the cruiser. The three boys were hauled off into a second cruiser. They would have to face the tame consequences of their parents, while Donaven would go home to the shelter to find another beating. But it didn't matter. Someone had finally tried to stop what he had wanted to stop for nearly three years. Half of the beatings he took, from the boys and from his father. Now if only Monica could stop his father. He had needed a friend badly, maybe she would be one, maybe not. Only the test of time would tell us if this were a one time thing or not.
