Chips 'n Dip

3 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
As soon as Randall Knox and Rosalyn Callasantos entered the condo, they knew something was up. "I've tried everything I can think of, Beav," said Ivy, "He's still locked in the room." Ivy McGinnis, their friend and mentor, was sitting at the top of the steps with her phone to her ear and her free hand on her forehead. They quietly went into the kitchen with their shopping bags and left her to it. "No, all I hear is the TV," continued Ivy, as William Beaven's voice was inaudible, "I knew I shouldn't've given him Eli's old room with the private bathroom." Her eyes went to the closed door, which had a handwritten sign taped to it that read "L.O.T.S. Express Inc: No Admittance!" in big block letters. "He's still in there?" asked Rose, emerging. Ivy nodded, listening to Beaven on the other end. Rose kicked her shoes off and silently tiptoed up the stairs past Ivy, and knocked on the door. "Lowell?" she asked. "Go away!" replied Lowell, "Tony and Angela are on!" Rose laughed. From the bottom of the stairs, Knox - professionally known as Impulse - laughed. "I know," replied Rose, "I was just thinking, Who's the Boss? is a lot better with some chips and dip, right?" "Hold on," said Ivy into the phone, as she turned toward Rose, "What are you doing?" Rose held up a finger. "We just got back from the store with a huge bag of potato chips and some french onion dip. It's really too much for us to eat ourselves. If we had someone else to share 'em with..." Lowell heard the words "potato" and "chips," and promptly responded, "...Are they Ruffles?" "They sure are," replied Rose. Silence, followed by the sound of the door unlocking. "I'm just going to quickly run downstairs and grab the dip," Rose said. "I'll be back in a sec." As Rose walked away, Ivy took her cue, and entered Lowell's room. Inside, she found him sitting on the bed, knees to his chest, eyes fixed on the fourteen-inch television set. Tissues lay strewn about all over the floor, and Ivy wondered whether they were "sad" tissues or "happy" tissues. She navigated around them as if they were landmines, and sat down on the edge of the bed. "So, do you think you might come to the arena with me Wednesday night?" Ivy asked. Lowell shrugged without looking at her. "I `unno..." "Because I think you showed definite promise, and --" Lowell glanced over at her, and replied, in a whiny, child-like tone, "I saaaid, 'I `unno.' Fact of the matter is, I don't want to go within fifteen feet of wrestling ring ever again. Wrestling's a young man's game." Ivy's brow furrowed. "Uh, Lowell? You're twenty-seven." "In human years, sure, but I figure all the drugs I did multiplied that number exponentially. So, really, I'm like... a thousand. I can feel it my bones." Lowell made the pouty face, and rubbed his shoulder, before adding, "I should be looking into life insurance, Costco memberships, and some nagging bitch of a wife. One with a huge ass, that wears floral pattern dresses, and gets her hair permed at a local salon. Like that shrill cunt from All In The Family. I haaate her. But I know that's who I am destined to be with. Well, her... or some buleimic model that wants her own Pete Doherty to look after to make her feel better about herself." "There's time for that when you really are old, kiddo," replied Ivy, "But the fact remains, you're clean now - or off the drugs, at least, and I really think this company is where you belong." She took a deep breath. "Besides, I've got a surprise for you." "A surprise? ...Are we going to have sex!?" Lowell blurted out. The look on Ivy's face gave him his answer, and his head hung forward shamefully. "I'm sorry... I just don't have that thing that censors what I say. My mouth is like a filterless cigarette. Both in the metaphorical sense, and the fact that it permanently smells like filterless cigarettes." Ivy nodded. "So, are you gonna come?" Lowell fingered his chin stubble for a moment. He loved surprises, but he wondered if this was all a ploy to get him back on the proverbial horse. On the horse... off the wagon. Another crushing defeat could result in such an occurance. "Well... OKAY!" announced Lowell. "But don't even think about asking me to wrestle! I'm done! Through! Finished! My skills are like a scarce, unrenewable resource -- when ya got 'em, everyone wants a piece, but when it's all gone, peeps gotta look elsewhere for an inferior alternative." Wonderful, said Ivy to herself. But at least he was going to come to the arena this week. "We'll see, Lowell. Now, do you want to watch TV downstairs on the big screen so you don't spill dip all over your room?" "Yeah, that's probably a good idea. I'm kind of disoriented, having just woken up from a nap, and I'd hate to mistake..." He glanced over at wad of tissues laying on the floor by Ivy's foot. "...not-dip... for dip. Could be weird if I develop a taste for the stuff, y'know?" Ivy smiled at him and gestured to the door. Lowell got up slowly and walked - nay, staggered - down the stairs. Once out of earshot, Ivy picked her phone up and hit a speed dial. "Beav? Ivy. Listen, do you know any really good carpet cleaners? No, this is definitely an emergency."
