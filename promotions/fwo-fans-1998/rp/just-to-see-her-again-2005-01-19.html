Just To See Her Again

19 January 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Naturally my agent Cabbot was a little apprehensive when I told him I was going to sit on the roof of a twenty five story building. "Feeling okay, big guy?" he asked with what looked to be genuine concern on his face. "I just need to clear my head," I told him. He looked at his watch. It was well past midnight, and we'd just gotten into town. Damn weather had my flights all turned around but I still got to town in time. The rental was ready for me to pick up, thank God. It's nice having someone else to look after this kind of shit for me. It gives me time to take care of my own business without having to remember things like car rentals and hotel reservations. Cabbot wanted to take me out on the town to take my mind of things. I wasn't up for it, and I certainly didn't feel like going for a drive. Not tonight. "I'm fine," I told him. "I just need some time for myself." "But the roof?" "I'm not jumping, prick. But I am going. I'll meet you in the lobby at five to go to the gym." Cabbot was already heading to the front desk. "I'll set up a wake-up call for you at four." Like an idiot who'd forgotten that he's not a young buck anymore, I decided to take the stairs. Twenty-five flights of stairs. By the tenth floor, my knee started throbbing. On the seventeenth floor, my leg started to protest the stupidity of this line of action. When I finally got to the twenty-fifth floor, jumping off the roof seemed like a better alternative than setting foot on the stairs again. Deciding that an elevator would be just as effective, I stepped out onto the roof of the hotel. There was still plenty of snow up there, almost a foot of the frosty white stuff, but that didn't stop me from walking the length of the building to the ledge surrounding the rooftop. I brushed some snow over the side and watched it drift down to the street, scattering into the night. The cold and wet stone felt good against my palm as I leaned on the ledge. I felt grounded, and that's what I need right now. "Did you lose the World Title and stop seeking it because it stinks of your daughter's raped and pillaged feminine sanctity!?!?" Craig Miles didn't just step over the line, he wrapped it around his neck and jumped over the side. He's not playing with fire, he's playing Russian roulette with a rocket launcher.
