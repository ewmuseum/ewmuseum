Awakenings

4 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"It's stupid to blame yourself, Ivy. How were you supposed to know?" "That's the thing that kills me, E... two years ago, five years ago - I would've known. I mean, I love where I am now and I love the way my life is, but there's things I feel like I've lost that were such a part of who I was... am I making any sense whatsoever?" Eli nodded. He and Ivy sat in the enclosed porch off the back of the condo. It was glassed in for the time being, but the heat didn't reach very far, so both of them were slightly bundled up. Eli wore an old, faded 'Staking a Claim' T-shirt, cargo shorts, and his black leather trenchcoat. Ivy was dressed in one of Sean's T-shirts, a pair of his boxers, and heavy wool socks. A flannel shirt was wrapped around her upper body, and a knit hat was on her head. "I don't think you give yourself enough credit," replied Eli, "You've had to put up with a lot of stuff since you started out in the biz, but it's pretty much been in the vein of 'You're a girl, you can't possibly know how this stuff works,' not 'You're a girl, you must be here solely to be something to look at.' Again, how were you s'posed to know?" Ivy sipped the coffee she held between her hands, "You've got a point, but I'm also not stupid. I should've seen it coming." Eli leaned his head back in the chair he was sitting in. "Again, why? Because a new member of the roster was nice to you without cause? What about Knight, or Brand? Or the multitude of guys and girls that extended their hands in friendship to us when we got here? Ross wasn't looking for anything other than friendship, neither was Pierre or Tempest or Strangler, or anyone else who welcomed us those first few weeks. The past few weeks have been isolated incidents by one specific scumbag, nothing more and nothing less." She seemed to consider this for a few seconds. "You've got a point, but I didn't get to where I am by being oblivious to my surroundings. It just really worries me." "Think about it," said Eli, as he took the coffee cup from her and made a grimace while he sipped it, "the last time you were in the FWO you were the boss. Stuff went down with the fans, and everything went dark for years." He ignored her uncomfortable shift, but handed the cup back to her. "That's awful, by the way." "There's a fresh pot brewing." "Anyways, what've you been doing since January 2006? You worked for the CSWA where you're an OG, and you worked for NFW, where you're essentially grandfathered in as an OG. Then, you and I went on tour for nine months and six months with the Garden, where they're finally earning respect on their own terms instead of beating respect out of them with the spectre of Ivy McGinnis behind them. And you became a mother. AND you and Trip are finally planning your wedding. Take all that, and add an FWO roster that's half new faces and it's not surprising that you're not on top of your game." "I should be." "You can't do it all on your own, kiddo. For all your insistence on being just a manager now, I know what you've been doing. You got Slugger for the first main event, you fixed the Fantron for the second, and you did a pretty good job of stretching last week when Russa turned up injured." "The fan poll never did materialize." "See, that's not your job to get working. You did your job by telling the fans about it, it's not your fault if the webgeeks are still fixing holes in the site." "Still, it comes back on me." "That's because you're a leader, not a follower. You take a personal stake in everything you do." Ivy laughed. "You're the second person in the past week to tell me that." Eli rubbed his face, and pulled his hair back tightly before he let it fall again. "The thing is, you've gotten used to regular life. Being home, working out of your apartment or from the bar, and being able to conduct most of your business from your cell." He took a deep breath. "Every other woman in this sport would kill to be you, y'know. For all the work you've put in over the past fourteen years this is still a boys club, and you know it, and you probably deal with less sexist BS than any of the rest of 'em. But that's because your reputation has mainly preceded you. These new guys who don't know you from the next woman don't know that." "Are you saying I should've expected this?" "No," assured Eli, "None of you girls should have to put up with what you do. I'm just saying you've had it comparatively easy over the past few years. But give it time, you built your rep up once before, you'll do it again." "I'm not getting involved again." "Sure you will. It's your nature." She didn't say anything for several seconds. "Don't tell Sean, okay?" "Tell him what?" "That we're having this conversation. He's got enough on his plate right now, he doesn't have to worry about me, too." Eli laughed. "First of all, you're my lil' sis and anything you tell me stays with me. Second, you and Trip are a team... what happens to one'a you happens to both. Isn't that why you skipped out on the bar last week?" "True." "I can appreciate your not wanting to dump a lot of this on him until you understand it yourself, but that's what he's there for. That's what you're there for for him. That's what you're there for for everyone, it's the least we can do to return the favor." She smiled weakly and drank more coffee. "Not tonight, though. I just want to get to the arena, hide out in Haggan's space until your match against Pierre, and go home." Eli nodded. "Sounds like a plan. I might join you." "I need a refill," said Ivy as she stood up and opened the sliding door, "You want?" "No," he replied, "but if you could get me something less harsh like a cup'a gasoline..." Ivy laughed, finally. "Wiseass." She closed the door behind her, and Eli remained on the porch, looking into the Seattle morning. "Until the end, my friend."
