Family Matters (Part 2)

1 January 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"I told you I would get you, Dad." The voice came from Monte Burns as he was standing in the corner of the room, while his father Daniel sat at a desk. This had been the culmination of months of devious work from Monte, where he finally forced his father into a position near bankruptcy. From the time Monte began to have trouble backstage, his relationship with Daniel and his brothers had rapidly deteriorated. A loving family of wrestlers had been splintered by the very business that they loved. What was once a typical father-son relationship had become a rivalry that could not be easily settled. But now, on the afternoon of January 16th, 1998, the war was about to end. The papers that would finalize the sale of Burns, Inc. and the NEWA lay in front of Daniel. Monte simply looked down at his father and smirked. "What do you have to say for yourself, Dad?" Monte asked. "Huh? You always talked about never giving up, never quitting. What do you call this?" Burns laughed heartily, obviously taking pleasure in the humiliation that Daniel was experiencing. Daniel could barely even take a look at his son. What kind of man would betray his father, his entire family, simply out of spite? "Monte," Daniel began, "as much as I hate to sign away my companies to you, I owe it to the workers to keep the company alive." "Awww, how nice," said Monte with a mocking tone. "You know, Dad, that sounds like something right out of a greeting card. I wish I could be a right and just man like you." After pounding his fist on the table, Daniel shot up from his chair and walked over to Monte in the corner. Despite the age difference, Daniel was still in fine physical condition and could more than hold his own against his young son. The two men stared into each other's eyes, neither man saying a word. For the longest time, words of hate and venom had spewed from both sides. Monte calling his father and brothers jealous; Daniel, John, and Nathan replying that Monte was being a naive young man who had no idea what he was doing. But now, as Daniel and Monte stood nose to nose, the expressions on their faces spoke loud enough. The veins bulged in Daniel's neck, as it appeared he was eager to vent some frustration on Monte. On the other hand, Monte's face bore an entirely different expression, a sly grin teasing Daniel in much the same way Monte always teased his opponents. Finally, after what seemed like hours of silence, Daniel spoke. "I want you to know something, Monte," he said. "No matter what you do to me, no matter what you do to my company, no matter what you do in your life, nothing is going to change the fact that you are my son." For a moment it seemed that the cockiness of "The Golden Child" would fade, that Monte Burns the son would realize that his father was standing in front of him, not an enemy. However, that was to be the case on that day. "Oh really?" remarked Monte sarcastically. "Well, that's very interesting, Daniel. That's very interesting, because since the first moment I started in the NEWA I haven't been treated that way. You, John, Nathan, and everyone else treated me like crap. Hell, Nathan spent half the time dumping my bag or pouring bleach all over my clothes. Is that how everyone else treats their sons?" "Monte, we only wanted to treat you the same as we treated everyone else," said Daniel. "Nathan did those things, because he didn't want people thinking you were getting special treatment." Monte rolled his eyes at Daniel's comment, obviously not believing a word of what his father had said. Instead, Burns stepped around his father and walked over to the desk. He picked up the papers and dangled them in front of Daniel's face. Daniel looked at the papers and sighed. Monte smiled. "Sign the papers," Monte demanded. "Sign the fucking papers." Feeling utterly defeated, Daniel snatched the papers out of Monte's hand and turned back to the desk. As Daniel leaned over and scribbled his name on the remaining pages, Monte nodded his head and smiled. The day had finally come when he would have his revenge on his father for taking away the title that he felt was his. Now, Monte had taken away Daniel's company, the company that had been in the family for generations. On that day, he had the greatest feeling in the world. A few years later, Monte would regret ever forcing his father into that position. To be continued...
