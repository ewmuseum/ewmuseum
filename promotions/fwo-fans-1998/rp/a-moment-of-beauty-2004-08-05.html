A Moment of Beauty

5 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I can still see her face as she sat in the bonsai garden, surrounded by beautifully sculpted trees. We’d raced up the path, the crushed stone crunching under our feet like packed snow. I followed behind her because she knew the winding path better than I did and would ensure I didn’t trip over any of the tree roots that occasionally poked through the stones, tumbled through the long grass and thin trees, and fell off the side of the mountain we’d spent all morning working our way up. I also liked running behind her because she had a very nice butt. Once we reached the stone archway, I saw the twinkle in her eye as she looked over her shoulder. “You still back there?” she said with a sly grin. Of course. Also, I would like to wear your ass as a hat, I thought to myself. “Of course. Also, I would like to wear –“ I was clearly exhausted from the run, despite not having the urge to throw up all over my shoes. Michelle stepped through the archway and waved for me to follow. We were the only two up here in the garden. Nobody else for miles and miles. I was sure we were going to bump uglies. I wondered if she was a screamer, because I knew I sure was. The white stones cut a stark contrast with the deep green of the grass on either side of the path. The bonsai trees, bent and twisted trunks with beautifully trimmed shrubbery, stood vigil over the entire park. The green grass ended abruptly at the edges where the imported soil met the edge of the cold mountain rock. The amount of effort taken to haul the soil up here, the care taken in grooming the grass and trees, was amazing. “David,” Michelle called my name. Surrounded by this pocket of natural wonder, her voice was like music. I expected to hear birds chirping and a Disney soundtrack to cue up. I walked over to where she was sitting, flanked by two of the biggest bonsai trees I’d ever seen. I could really relate to those things – bent and twisted at the base, yet with a certain beauty on the outside. Only I wasn’t all that beautiful. Michelle seemed to think so, by the way she smiled at me so coyly. Maybe it was the beauty of the nature that surrounded us. Maybe it was the way the sunlight bathed her face. At that moment, she’d never looked more breathtakingly gorgeous. I sat down beside her. I wanted to say Those Three Words, but was afraid my voice would disrupt the serenity of the moment. Michelle held a finger to my lips and pulled me close. We kissed. We made love on top of that mountain, and it was the best experience of my life. That’s how I wanted to remember her. Remember us. Locked forever in that one perfect moment. A few months later, I ruined that by having her join me at work. She got hurt because she was in the wrong place at the wrong time. A couple months after that, the whole world watched her and Cody Williams make animal sex. Still, I held on to our moment. I guess that was the hardest part, knowing that a moment like that would never happen again. Or could it? Damn it, women have corrupted the minds of men since the Dawn of time. There’d be more time to reflect on that later. Right now, I was going to enjoy That Moment just a little longer. Of course, hearing Sam moan as he played with himself just three feet away made it quite difficult.
