Previous Posts: prologue The poor daylight crawled up the ivy-clad wall. At the same time, a young boy sped down the tar-covered street with a knap sack thrown over his shoulder. He reached behind his back and grabbed a newspaper from the bag. The paper flew through the cold morning air and landed on the porch of a nearly empty white house. It was 1992. Alarm clock squeals attempted to awake Trent from his slumberous state. He hugged the thin sheets over his head in a futile attempt to muffle the sounds. Time passed and the young teenager decided to give in to the daily routine. The warm drops of water covered his toned body. Thoughts of the coming events and school in general filled his mind. Trent longed to remain in his bed and forget about the day to come, but that was common. A black cotton shirt and a pair of loose fit blue jeans would suffice for the daily attire. A patchwork of smells filled the empty air as Trent stepped from his room. The kitchen was warmer than his room, but still cold. The heater had to be given time to warm up. At the present time, this thought passed with little consideration. The bus at the end of the street was the current problem. A piece of toast and a kiss goodbye. Trent felt he had grown too old for such acts of affection, but Mother thought the way his face controted when she pecked him on the forehead was almost cute. Still, the teenager picked up his bookbag and ran out the door where the yellow bus awaited his presence. The doors opened and Trent paced through the seats, looking for an open one. Balls of crumpled paper and unwanted lunches flew through the air as he looked for a seat. Only one remained open, and for good reason. A young woman the same age as the boy sat there with her legs crossed gently and tilted toward the window. Her flowing brown hair covered the white spaghetti straps of her lace shirt. Outside the window was nothing of interest, but she had become used to this position over the years of siting alone. Reluctantly, Trent placed his backpack on the ground, beneath the seat in front of the girl. She turned her head for a moment and stared up at the fine adolescent. He smiled sheepishly before he sat at the very end of the chair. The odd silence between them was uncomfortable. Trent reached over his body and extended his right hand toward the girl. He introduced himself, but she ignored him. This young girl was so beautiful, yet so unwelcoming. The young boy sighed and continued to stare foreward. The school day waved a rough hand goodbye as Trent stepped out the door. Nothing extreme had happened, but nothing ever did. The boring days called for exciting afterschool activities. Trent unlocked his bike and stared at the silent girl as she stepped out of the building, as well. With a sense of curiosity, he peddled off. The silence was so deep that the ruffling autumn leaves was the only audible whisper. "Why are you always so quiet?" asked Trent. The girl knew his motives before he had even finished the last word, and because of that, she remained in her quiet state. Curiosity fumed, and Trent stepped off of his perch and down to her Earth. A quick glance, but she then continued to watch the concrete pass. "I suppose you don't want me following you all the way home now, do you?" She nodded. Any reply was a progressive step. "There's quite an easy way to get rid of me, actually. All you have to do is talk to me, and once you've lost interest, I'll go away. No long term commitments, just engage in some passive conversation and it will all be over before you know it. Besides, you can't enjoy staying to yourself all the time." "My name is Diana." Two times around heaven. They walked the holy blocks for hours before realizing that day had fogged to night and their time was spent. Neither slept that night.
