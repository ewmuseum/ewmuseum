Destination, Destiny: II

31 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
They call it Stockholm syndrome. It's when a kidnapped victim ends up being brainwashed. The victim is delusional, usually from lack of nourishment and poor treatment, thus making him or her more succeptible and receptive to thought modification. The result yields a victim who is sympathetic to his or her captor(s). The most famous case occured in the kidnapping of Patty Hearst. She fought off SWAT teams in Los Angeles as they attempted her rescue. In this case, they would call it unintentional Stockholm syndrome. That means that the effects were unforseen and not deliberate, on the kidnapper's part. It happened in a bed sitting in a dingy motel room, where Allyra was not unbound, lying freely in the same bed as Eugene Marder. Marder was the man who had shoved her into a car, strapped her in, and driven her across a country, away from the only man she ever loved. All that seemed even further away than Trent Harrison as she stared into her kidnapper's eyes. Only she wouldn't call him that anymore. Now, he was just Eugene. For four days, she'd endured a story about his life. The story was moving to the point of tears...To the point of forgetting that the man that told it was a felon. He'd allowed her to call Trent on the third day, if only for a bit. She found that they didn't have much to talk about. Eugene didn't seem offended when she played the victim, telling her later, "He is more worthy of you than I am." "No." She said it staring into his eyes, her conviction and honesty speaking volumes. Trent told her that he had not phoned the police because he wanted to catch Martyr himself. That the police would only get involved and would put him out of a job by letting fWo management know that he had failed to control Marder's erratic behavior. Confiding in Allyra that he was on the verge of getting authorities involved, she told him not to, saying she'd be more satisfied when Trent killed him personally. Harrison wondered why Marder had let her call, but she assured him that it was only because he had let the room to get food, and she had managed to dial the phone with her nose. When asked where she was, she pretended Eugene had come back from his trip and hung up the phone. Allyra could count all the lies she ever told on one hand. It didn't bother her that more than half belonged to one exchange. "You should've told him." Nothing about Marder's voice hinted at mockery. "Too late now." The shrug gave away her exchaustion. Mental, physical, emotional. "You may yet want to be rescued." The moonlight crept through the blinds, cutting the room. To Allyra, they could've been bars on a jail door. Instead, they were columns of light. Sitting in the chair next to the bed, her hands long since untied and her knees to her chest, she savored the silence. The lines were cast on his face as he stood across from her. She was not sure where he was looking, but it hardly seemed to matter. She stood, but didn't feel the stained, tan carpet. She walked, but didn't feel her own movement. It was instantaneous- one instant spent sitting in a chair, the next with her lips on Eugene's, making his breath hers. Was it empowerment or emotion that propelled her, ever so gracefully, to him?? That was just another inconsequential variable. To her, the moment meant nothing more than an exchange, the peak of rapture. ********** She felt him on the back of her neck, tiny hairs swaying to his escaped oxygen. Breathing was just another physiological process of the body, one of the many that keeps the human machine running. So was a hangover, so was sex. She almost laughed at equating the two in her mind. Though, she thought, one does often accompany the other. More often than most hope it would. This philosophy helped her rationalize what had just happened between her and the man whose skin was touching her naked back, the man whose arms now encircled her naked waist, the man who slept in this bed with her. The man who was not Trent Harrison. The man whose breath envigorated her, much to her dismay. She wished to God she could think of him otherwise. She didn't know what she would tell Trent- or Eugene, for that matter. She was having trouble thinking of what she would tell herself. But maybe it wasn't important for her to think anything other than what had occured and how things would change. Change was not something she dealt with well. Nor was passion or spontenaity. She'd always been one to hold grudges and slow to forgive. What change had occured in her then, to allow, no, to entice, this man to take not only her physical self, but her emotional being as well? Change seemed to be a recurring theme in the thoughts of Allyra as she stood from the bed, gingerly easing herself from Eugene's arms. She headed to the bathroom to get a drink, taking a passing glance at the door out to the hall. Leaving hadn't even crossed her mind. On the other side of the bed, a storm of equal proportions but greater severity was brewing. He felt her slip from his arms, her weight gone from the bed. He was never quite asleep, but now he was fully awake. Her wake lapped gently at his mind, stirring its contest. There seemed imminent danger of a bit spilling over the top, but that was never an area of concern for Eugene. Especially these days, his mind was always full, dangerously close to overflowing. He was suddenly overcome by a stream of emotion. He'd thought, for a fleeting instant, that she might attempt escape, but the notion quickly left his mind. When she entered the bathroom, Eugene turned over, facing the window, and closed his eyes. It suddenly became apparent that he couldn't remember the last time he'd slept. Before this whole ordeal, it must have been. Fatigue slowly won the battle against a pensive mind already worn from an emotional night. What, then, did they call it when the victim won her kidnapper over? When, through his or her actions, the weak and defenseless broke the will of the captor? In this case, they would call it love. That means that Eugene Marder would have a hard time letting go of the woman in the bathroom.
