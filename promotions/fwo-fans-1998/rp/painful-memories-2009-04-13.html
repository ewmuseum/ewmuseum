Painful Memories

13 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
For David Noble, his mom, Rebecca, represented the most important person in his world. As Rebecca stood there in the threshold of the door, her hair tied up in a neat bun and obviously dressed for bed, her face showed the sheer surprise of seeing her son out on the lawn beating up the boy his sister, Sarah, was just kissing. With sweat running down his face, a little worked up from the situation beforehand, David glanced back over his head to look at the odd boy before he looked back at his mom. "David, what're you doing here?!" she yelled out before Rebecca walked out of the house and hugged her only son. David hugged her back before he buried his face into her shoulder, happy to be there with his family once again. As he looked up and kissed her on the cheek, the two broke their embrace as she looked over him briefly. "Dear Lord, you need to eat more often. Come inside and I'll make you some food!" David could only smile at how quickly his mother had forgotten about the scuffle he had been involved in just moments ago. Behind him though, the boy who just had his face punched a couple of times, wasn't too happy. "Is that the adopted asshole?" he asked, directed at no one in particular. As he heard those words come out of his mouth, David spun around and began storming back towards the teenage boy with the profane mouth. "Let's see what this adopted ass--," he began to get out of his mouth before his older sister, Samantha managed to wrap her arm around his stomach and stop his progress. The other sister in this sort-of ridiculous situation, Sarah, sprinted up to the guy who had been kissing her before and slapped him across the face. "How dare you?!" she said as his face recoiled and turned to another gear of anger. However, out of the corner of his eye he caught the glare coming from David's eyes and thought twice about it as Sarah caught his attention again. "Go home, Brad. Just go home." Her words were finite, even coming from such a tiny girl, but she turned away from him as her dark blonde hair flailed around in a loosely wrapped ponytail. As she reached David though, she gave him a look of her own. "Don't you even THINK about talking to me," she informed him and David could tell that she wasn’t happy about interfering in her business. David looked at Samantha before he wrapped his arms around her. "I missed you, sis," he whispered in her ear as the two just held onto each other for a brief moment. Growing up, the two were inseparable, doing any and everything together when it came to activities. Some even thought they were twins even though David was the younger of the two, but one thing was for certain about both of them, they had the same kind of soul. As the two broke away from each other and started to head into the house, with their mother in front of them, Samantha shot David a quick look before she shook her head and offered him a smile. "You never did like being called adopted," she told him coyly as he raised his eyebrows at her and then closed the door behind him before he looked around the house and just let it all sink in. The last time he had been home, it hadn't been a great experience for many of the people in the house. Fights had been breaking out every day, mainly because of David's temper. "I honestly never thought we'd see you again, David." The low rumbling voice that came from above was very familiar to his ears, enough so that he didn't even have to look up to know who it was. Out of a sign of respect though, something deeply engrained in him from a young age, he looked up and saw his father who stood there with a menacing look. Even though he was adopted, he had never looked at his parents as anything else other than his parents. He had never sought his biological parents or ever questioned who his parents were. As he looked up and saw his father standing at the top of the stairs, David nodded his head towards him. "Evening, father," he said, his voice and tone very soft and hushed. "Caused quite a ruckus out there, don't you think?" his father responded in a rather stern and strict tone. Before David had a chance to respond though, Samantha walked out from the kitchen and gave her father this daring look. "Dad, do you really have to give David the third degree thirty seconds after walking through the front door? How about you come down and enjoy a bite with us. Maybe you can try this thing weird new thing. I think it's called being happy," she told him sarcastically before she grabbed David by the hand and started to drag him towards the kitchen while their father started to walk down the stairs. As David walked into the kitchen and was guided towards a chair, his mother stood in front of the refrigerator and looked for something to feed her only son. "Are you in the mood for chicken or steak?" she asked him, her eyes never looked over at him instead they continued to scan the fridge for what else they had to eat. Samantha sat next to David while Sarah stood over the sink and washed her hands. Then, in walked their father who just stood in the threshold, not amused in the least bit with the return of his son. His arms were crossed over his chest as he glared at his son with disappointment etched on his face. "What're you doing in your life, now, David? Still doing that wrestling crap? Trying to break your neck?" he asked him, each question more pointed then the last with his tone growing sterner with each spoken word. David looked at his father, his hands clasped on the table, while Samantha looked at her father with utter disbelief. "Father--," she began to say, but was immediately cut off with a sudden look from him. "Samantha, this conversation doesn't pertain to you," he told her before his attention turned back to David. As he did so, the door to the fridge slammed shut as his wife looked at him, and tried to figure out what he was doing. "Shawn, let the boy come home and enjoy a minute to himself, okay?" she pleaded with her husband to no avail. "Rebecca, this boy just can't come and go as he pleases. I won't stand for it. He's twenty-two years old with no college degree to speak of. He left this house three years ago with this fantasy in his head that he could be a wrestler, and I want to know what he has accomplished with his life that he has decided to just come back home!" he yelled at the top of his lungs, frightening Sarah who turned around, and looked at the scene as if it were a horror show, which it slowly was. David looked up from the table at his father and just shook his head. Shawn began to walk towards the table before he put the palms of his hands onto the wooden table. "Well?" David at first just sat there, trying to find the strength to respond, before he swallowed and felt the razor blades as they slid down his throat. "I quit a few months back," he said, ashamed and just flat out embarrassed. As his eyes began to look down at the table, unable to meet his own father's eyes, his attention was grabbed again as his father slammed his hands onto the table as hard as possible and created an echo in the room. David looked at his father, no longer filled with the fear and insecurities of a scared little boy, but with fire in his eyes. "I've worked too hard in my life, David, to just let you piss your life away. I've given you and your sisters everything in this world. In return, what have you shown me? That you can't follow through with the things that you feel strongest about. Instead, you quit, and now have decided to just return home. I'm not bailing you out of your failures, David! You're a better person then that, and I'm a better father then that!" he roared at David before he turned away and began to walk out of the kitchen. David just shook his head as he looked at his father. "Dad," he says, but he never got a chance to ask his question as it father immediately cut him off. "I want you out of this house, first thing, tomorrow morning," his father said in a low growl before he disappeared out of sight. David just sat there, stunned, as Samantha put her hand on top of David's hands, and tried to give him some sort of support. From behind came Sarah who kissed David on the forehead. Yet, even with the sentiments and caring from two of his sisters, David still felt like the biggest failure in the world. *** Then, there was a clicking noise and a woman’s voice answered on the other end. "Hello?" At first, David couldn't find the words to respond. He just sat behind his piano and waited there as the seconds lasted for an eternity. The silence filled the air while the cackle of the two phones lingered heavily between the two. The woman on the other end of the phone knew who it was that had called her and refused to hang up the phone until he talked to her. David's eyes scanned the ground below until they closed and he took a deep breath in. "Hey Samantha," he said, his voice dry, and his heart heavy. She laughed into the phone, unable to control herself, until David could hear those laughs turn into tears. "David, my goodness, it's so good to hear your voice," Samantha responded between her shallow breaths as her tears sounded much more like happiness then sadness. David sort of smiled as he heard his sister's voice on the phone, her warm tone filling a coldness and emptiness that had been in his heart for quite some time now. "Same here," he managed to respond as his body leaned against the piano in an awkward pose. Samantha continued to try and stifle her tears, desperate not to lose her brother during this difficult period of time. She had been trying to get in touch with him for weeks and she wasn't going to blow this opportunity to get inside of his mind. "How's life?" she asked him and David sighed, unwilling to go through the mundane and trivial avenues of typical conversation. This wasn't meant to be a typical conversation between the two and he knew it. "Samantha, I know you care about me and all, but I also know that you didn't just call me so you could see how I'm doing," he replied bluntly and could tell just from her stony silence that he had hurt her pretty badly with his words. David desperately wanted to apologize to her, but couldn't find it in himself to actually do so. He listened as David could tell she was wiping her eyes again. A few more moments pass as the silence fills the air again, an uncomfortable and painful silence that seemingly won't go away. "It's time to come home, David. Enough is enough. The two of you need to come back," she said and David could feel the anger in his chest. "I'm doing this for everyone, Samantha! I'm trying to protect you, Andrea, and everyone else. We just can't come back. Not now. It's just not time yet," he said solemnly, but knew that Samantha wouldn't appreciate the sentiments in the least bit. David could hear the heavy breathing on the other end and glanced up at the clock to see that it had to be early morning over there and realized that Samantha hadn't slept yet. "It's time to put this behind us, David. Can't we do that? Mistakes were made and feelings were hurt, but this is no way to go on living our lives. We all love you and we need you in our lives. You know, we've been watching television the past few weeks and saw that you've been winning a few matches. Heard you even signed with another major company. We're so proud of you, David," she told him, hoping to break down the icy walls that protected his heart. David smiled at the fact that his family had taken the time to keep tabs on him even if he couldn't do the same to them. David played with the words in his mind for a brief moment before he shook his head. "I know you're tired, Samantha. I've gotten the phone calls, but I just don't know if it's a good idea. Not yet at least, but give me some time to think about it. Maybe I can fly you out and the three of us can talk about it. How does that sound?" he asked her and could feel his chest tighten from the anticipation of hearing her thoughts on the idea. She paused for a moment and he could almost hear the gears in her mind working to figure out if this was a good idea or not. "I'll call you in the afternoon?" she asked him and David could tell she was reluctantly agreeing to the idea. "Sounds good, sis," David told her. "I love you, David. We all do. Even Dad," she said to him and David looked down at the ground again as he could feel the heaviness in his chest start to grow to the point of being unbearable. The silence lingered there for a moment before David removed the phone from his ear and ended the phone call as he was unable to find the words to say goodbye to his sister. His right hand inched over to his piano and wrapped his fingers around the cold glass before David lifted it to his lips. He finished off the alcohol before he stood up. David's eyes looked around the room and found nothing that made him happy here. He sighed before he violently threw the glass in his hands into the nearby wall and watched as it shattered into shards. As it fell to the ground, David fell back onto his piano bench before he covered his face with his hands. On the piano, where he had placed his phone, it vibrated and as David looked over he saw a text message had arrived on his phone. It read, "We don't blame you." *** When you first looked at the golden oak building, the fancy trimmings around the threshold of the door, and the luxurious setting of the trees and peacefulness, you don't expect it to be a mental health center. As David stood on the sidewalk outside of the building and looked at it he thought about walking away, but decided against it as he walked into the building located in Pasadena. His eyes wandered around the facility as his eyes caught upon the impressionable receptionist. "Good Morning, Mr. Noble. Are you here for your monthly charity visits?" she asked him referring to the time he volunteered at the place in an effort to increase the happiness of the people there. "Not today, Susan. Not today," he said without leaving any other thought. Today, he needed something to make sense to him, and he needed answers. Oddly enough, he expected to find those answers here from one of the people who had always offered him perfect advice. David walked to his left where he saw a few of the members playing various games such as checkers or Connect Four. He smiled at them as they waved at him while he walked by a woman he was fighting her orderly who wanted to give her a dosage of medicine. David walked over to the older woman and placed his hand on her shoulder. Slowly, she looked up at him and smiled as he returned the gesture before taking the medicine from the orderly's hand and handing it to the kind woman. "Here, it's going to be okay," David told her and she only nodded her head. The woman had lost the ability to speak in a nasty accident three years prior and slowly took her medicine as the pain subsided just from David being there. David then kissed her on the forehead as the female orderly flashed him a smile to thank him for helping her out. He didn't think anything about it though as he turned around and walked into the next room where he found a group of people sitting together while they drew paintings of whatever came to their mind. At a table adjacent to them though was a woman who drew by herself. David shook his head as he walked over to her and sat down across from her. She glanced up and looked at David, before finding the biggest smile on her face. David looked at her with loving eyes and slowly rubbed the palm of his hand across her cheek. "Hey Mom."
