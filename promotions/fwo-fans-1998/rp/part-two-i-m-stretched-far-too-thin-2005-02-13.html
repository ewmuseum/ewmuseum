Part Two: I'm Stretched Far Too Thin

13 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Dolores Wakeman had apparently been well versed in the art of putting a witness at ease. Ivy didn't know what she'd done before now - she was back in school - but whatever it was, she had learned well, the art of conversation and relaxation. When Dolores picked her up, Ivy was struggling to hold back a well of tears, and Dolores wisely stayed away from any kind of questioning that would spark her mood. She asked Ivy about her flight, her opinions on the state of various airports around the country, and the kids in New York. She asked Ivy about her family and growing up in the city. She asked Ivy about how she splits her time between the day to day grind of the professional wrestling business, working with the kids in the city, and managing the family's diner. Slowly, steadily, Ivy calmed down. As they reached the familiar Wakeman home, Ivy was pleasantly surprised to see that Dolores had started brewing a pot of coffee before she'd left. "So, are you doing anything special for Valentine's Day?" asked Dolores. "Work, of course," replied Ivy, "and then back to the hotel. Or back to a car to get to the next show." "That's no good, a pretty woman like you," replied Dolores, shaking her head, "Why isn't my son taking you out somewhere?" The guilt racked Ivy's brain. "We're not... actually together," she confessed, "Matt was afraid of what you'd think if he came home by himself, which is why he invited me and Craig and Nixon." "I'm not as naive as you might think," laughed Dolores, "I know you and Matthew aren't together - I do know my son." Ivy stopped, mid - sip, and looked at her. "You knew? But why--" "You were all having such a good time making him squirm," replied Dolores, "and telling me stories about him that I wouldn't have dared break it up. It told me a lot about him, and I'm really glad he has such good people around him." She suddenly got that worried look in her eyes, the one that only a mother can pull off. "He's okay, right?" "He's doing fine," assured Ivy. Dolores nodded. "So tell me why you're here." Ah, the point. "There's a man. It's... complicated." "It's always complicated with men involved," assured Dolores, "Why don't you start from the beginning?" Ivy looked around the kitchen. "I think I need something stronger to drink, if I'm gonna start from the beginning." A mischievous look filled Dolores' eyes. "Craig Miles left half the bottle of Jack Daniels here at Christmas, and I don't drink very much. Do you partake?" "Oh, I partake," replied Ivy, "and I think you should know, Craig didn't leave half the bottle. He left half of the third bottle." Dolores looked impressed at the combined drinking ability of Craig, Nixon, Ivy, and Matthew. "You don't have much to do on that tour, do you?" "Trust me, we all don't drink with Craig all that much," assured Ivy, "After a night at the bar with him, you wake up the next day with your liver sitting on the side of the bed, smoking a cigarette saying 'You're such an asshole.' as it dry-heaves." It took a moment for Dolores to process that, but when she did she started to laugh. "I can see that. Okay, so if you don't want to start at the beginning, start at the end. Who's the guy that's got you all worked up?" Ivy hesitated, and downed her first shot. "His name is Sean, and we have a history that goes back a few years." "Was he the one?" "I don't know," replied Ivy, "I mean, all the stuff we went through when we first got together... I wouldn't've done that if I just had lukewarm feelings, y'know?" "I think I know." "And once we got together, that was it. Everything was perfect. Have you ever had those perfect moments with someone, where you knew in retrospect that it couldn't last and that the bad times would inevitably be even worse when you knew what it was you had?" She stopped. "I'm rambling. I'm sorry." "No, you're making sense," assured Dolores, "Keep going." Ivy finished the second half of her shotglass and refilled it. "He was there for me for my worst times - we first started to really become friends around the time that Daddy died, and then we got together just before Mom. That screwed things up even more, since I kinda... I left him hanging afterwards because of Mom. But he was strong enough to get through it all and see that I was just a little crazy at the time, and all that stuff. And yeah, I overreacted to a few things around then, too... but I eventually came to my senses. And we were just a good couple. We went out and did things, we stayed in and cuddled, we were both up for anything at any time but we didn't have to be together all the time, if that makes sense." Dolores nodded. "And then it just all collapsed in on itself." Another shot. "It was the summer of 2003, everyone in the company was friendly, Sean had just won the Internet title, Eli was in the main event at Meltdown... and everything broke. Sean broke up with me for reasons I still don't know, I got shot in the Bronx--" Dolores gasped. "--Eli left the business, I left the business, then I came back to do something completely different." "Different?" "The family was essentially shredded," explained Ivy, "I had no real support structure left in the company, which is why I was gone. I worked a bit with the kids until Silver Fox asked me to come back to the fWo as a consultant, someone to help coordinate the creative and business ends of things. It was okay, too... Fox really put a lot of trust into me and I took that job seriously." "What happened?" "They died." Dolores nodded. She had heard the story of the events of Countdown to Oblivion 2003. "All that time, from then until now it was like I'm doing this all on my own. Yeah, we're doing okay with Disney running the show, we're all making enough money to live and we're putting on a good show... but I feel like something is missing from it all and I have to stay on guard." Neither woman noticed it as they took down another shot, but Ivy's eyes had started to tear again. "I can't let my guard down at all," continued Ivy, "because this is nothing but business now, there's no family loyalty toward anyone... and I have to make sure that the boys all still have a livelihood. But it was like, I kept suffocating. Fox was gone, Eli was against me, Miles started a war... I had to learn to do a hell of a lot on my own. I feel like I'm standing in front of everyone, like I've gotta be the first line'a defense in case something happens." Dolores didn't answer immediately, she nodded and poured herself another shot. "What do you think would happen?" "I don't know!" replied Ivy, forcefully, as a tear dropped to the table, "But I just have this overwhelming sense of doom, that things have been okay for so long, now me and Eli are on the same page again and Sean is back in my life... if I let any of that distract me I'm gonna miss the warning signs." Ivy took her glasses off and covered her eyes. Dolores reached forward and embraced the Psycho Bitch, holding her tightly. "Shhhhh," she cooed, "It's okay, honey." They stayed in that position for a few seconds, before Ivy backed up suddenly and put her glasses back on. "I'm sorry, I'm okay." "No, I don't think you are," replied Dolores, unconvinced, "You've got a lot of good people around you, do you ever talk to them about how you feel?" "I don't," admitted Ivy, "but they've got their own problems to deal with, they don't need to listen to me whine about nothing at all." Dolores shook her head. "May I play psychiatrist for a few minutes? It sounds to me like you feel personally responsible for how everything in that company has run, is running, and will run in the future. It sounds like you're the first person to push compliments onto the people who did well, and the first person to pull every criticism towards herself when things go wrong. And it sounds like you carry around all of your own burdens plus those of the people you're closest to. Am I right?" A rueful smile crossed Ivy's face. "I s'pose." "Don't suppose," said Dolores, "I'm right. You can't do everything on your own, or you'll end up with more of this--" she touched her hand to the streak of gray hair on the side of Ivy's head, "--before you're thirty one." Dolores put her hand under Ivy's chin and removed her glasses again. "So listen to me, Missy... you can't do this all by yourself. Nobody could." Ivy started to cry again, as Dolores pulled her back in toward her shoulder. This time, Ivy stayed put.
