In a Flash

17 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Soot and dust from the pulverized concrete mixed with the blood flowing from David Cross's face, forming a muddy mask of gore over his features. A strip of his scalp had been torn back from a piece of brick which had grazed his head. The hairy flesh dangled behind his ear like a bloody lock of hair, his meaty skull glistening in the fading daylight. The side of his face throbbed badly, and he had no vision in his right eye. He wasn't even sure if his eye was still in his head, but he didn't want to touch his face to find out. Cross sat up and let the bloody bubbles form on his lips freely. He suspected his ribs were broken, most likely when that charred severed leg slammed into his side like a fleshy battering ram. Each breath was agony in it's purest form, a cruel joke of sorts. The pain was killing him, but to keep breathing he had to endure more of it. Luckily he'd managed to pop his shoulder back into the socket after the shockwave threw him through the segment of wall he'd been watching the show behind. The show... he wasn't even booked to wrestle today. He was only here to fill in in case someone didn't show up. Just to be a nice guy, a company man. After the show, he was going to be heading back to the States for an acting role in a real deal Hollywood movie. Life had been so good up until today, despite some of the pitfalls he'd overcome in the past. But now... now there would be no movie. The fact that he was alive was incredible enough. How long he would stay in that condition was debateable. David felt emotionally cold and oddly detached from everything. It was like he wasn't really here, really feeling the pain from having his body crushed, burned, and nearly torn apart. Maybe that was his martial arts training, his inner spirit, steeling itself to the living nightmare unfolding in front of him. Or maybe it was just his psyche retreating to escape the horrors around him. Had the pressure wave not ruptured his sinuses, leaving blood and mucus trickling from his nostrils, Cross would have gagged on the scent of the burned building, charred and bloating pieces of what were once innocent men, women and children, and the faint scent of sulphur. A potpourri of horrors, a scent that he would never be free from. The explosion had also deafened David, leaving only a high-pitched whine ringing in his ears. He was unable to hear the tortured screams of the wounded survivors like himself, the cries of people calling out for missing loved ones. He couldn't hear the solders standing near him, asking in broken English if he was American. An honest question, considering his bloody and dirty appearance. He couldn't hear their footsteps as they walked away, dismissing his motionless bloody body as one of the fatalities instead of one of the survivors. He couldn't even hear the medics calling out and asking if anyone needed medical assistance. No, all he had left was the sight in his one good eye. With that eye, he saw the remains of the building he had been in mere hours ago. A structure, beautiful in its architecture, gone in a flash. He could see what he could have been smelling: wisps of smoke rising from the coals of the building. Bubbling and cracked pieces of flesh which had been wrestling fans. The piece a few feet away from his leg looked like a woman's torso. Cloth fragments hung off her charred form, her flesh black and peeled back from the intense heat of the explosion, and the shockwave which had torn her limbs from her body. Her head was still attached, and the eye that survived the explosion stared blankly at Cross through a gaping hole in the orbital socket which once cradled her eye. The eye which had been watching Sunday Slaughter. The show he wasn't even supposed to be on. Everything changed, gone in a flash. As he had pulled himself to a seated position, ignoring the large patch of bloody flesh which festered from his pant leg, he dislodged a piece of what could have been a door once upon a short time ago. Like a dog finding a hidden toy, Cross for some reason couldn't wait to see what he'd found. It was almost hard to make out exactly what it was as he turned it around in his hands. It took two minutes of one-eyed examination before he finally realized what he was holding in his hand. It was the little fingers that tipped him off, you see. Despite not being able to hear, one scream - a long, wild, soul-wrenching scream - did catch David's attention. His own.
