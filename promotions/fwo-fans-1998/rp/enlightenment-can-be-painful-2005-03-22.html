Enlightenment can be Painful

22 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
David Cross sat quietly on the roof of Chateau Troy, looking up at the night sky wearing only his Snoopy housecoat and a pair of black sweatpants. He folded his legs and adjusted his posture to something more comfortable, then closed his eyes. The evening air warmly embraced him like a mother holding her child. Despite being perched on the roof about three stories up, David felt safe and let his mind drift. Thoughts began to unravel in his mind like a ball of twine. David let the images flow, not looking for anything but allowing them to pass through unhindered. He found it very soothing to do this as often as he could. It kept his perception clear and objective, and helped him to focus. For a guy whose life had been filled with chaos, focus was as precious as gold to David Cross. It wasn't as if he hadn't had his share of reflection time in the past. The weeks David had spent in the Psych Ward gave him the opportunity to dig deep past the horrors of his life, all the anarchy and confusion, and find something within him that was close to the truth. He'd disfigured himself, and drove himself insane trying to discover his own truth. Between seeing women and children blown up around him in Tel Aviv, having his best (and only) friend kill himself, discovering his adopted mother dead on the floor, dealing with various cross-shaped marks on his face (which he tried to hack off with a shard of glass), there was plenty of thoughts to clutter his mind with. Through it all, David kept searching to find out who he really was. He didn't know where he came from, since he took a family name that wasn't his own. Even his wrestling name came from somewhere else. Truth be told, for the longest time David didn't feel like he belonged anywhere. Wearing the mask was almost a comfort for him, since it let him hide himself away from the rest of the World. But deep down, he'd really only hidden himself away from himself. The feeling of not knowing who he was literally drove Crucifix insane. Some noted that was a short trip. David didn't disagree. That was then. Now, after much introspection and soul-searching, David had come to terms with himself and soon found it easier to deal with others. He opened his eyes to marvel at his progress. He'd gone from being nothing with no one to being a guy with friends and a future in a business he loved. Somewhere, somehow, things turned around. The breeze carried with it an odor, a mix of fresh flowers with a hint of car exhaust. David opened his eyes as he heard the sound of a vehicle driving away from the house. Enough reflection for one night, he decided as he stood up and bent forward, grabbing the backs of his calves and pulled himself down where his head touched his knees. Relaxed and refreshed, David crossed the small rooftop patio and pulled on the small door that led back down into the house. Locked. "Well this is certainly going to put a damper on my evening of enlightenment," David said as he looked around the rest of the roof. The patio was the only of its kind on top of the house, and there didn't appear to be any other way back down. He walked to the edge closest to the front of the house and looked over the side. A window, and it appeared to be open. Perfect. He'd just make his way down the front of the house and slip in through the window. The perfect crime. David tightened his robe and walked to the edge. He lowered himself off the roof and probed for the ledge below with the tips of his toes. No luck. It was there, just a few feet underneath him. He let go, catching the window ledge as he fell past it. The popping sound from his back and shoulders were a little disturbing, as was the burning sensation in his neck. Still, that discomfort was a whole lot better than the twenty foot drop down to the ground below. As he pulled himself up to peek through the window, he noticed two things. One, the window opened up into a rather large, rather comfortable looking bedroom. Two, Lindsay Troy didn't react very well to being startled. Her foot managed to catch David right in the forehead, hard. In the brief moment when David seemed to pause in mid-air before plummetting down to the ground, Lindsay realized what she'd done. With a surprised look on her face, she poked her head out the window and yelled down to Crucfix who lay in a heap amidst the small hedge below. "David! I'm sorry... I thought you were breaking in or something. Are you okay?" From somewhere in the dark below came a voice that didn't quite sound okay. "I'm fine. Luckily this clump of catuses broke my fall." "Cacti," Troy corrected him. "Are you sure you're okay." "Yeah. A few cuts and bruises, but I'll live." A pause. "Uh, is that dogs I hear?" The night wasn't silent anymore, as growling Dobermans mixed with a shrieking wrestler who sprinted for the front door. Crucifix grabbed the handle. "Locked." He turned around. "I used to like dogs. Used to." Lindsay yelled something unintelligible out the window and ran down the stairs. Quickly, she opened the locks and threw the door open. Troy saw the dogs at the edge of the porch, one of them still with a scrap of Snoopy hanging from its mouth. She knelt down. "Are you okay?" Dave gave her a bloody thumbs up. "Nothing a hundred yards of gauze and some iodine won't fix." Lindsay brushed a branch out of Crucifix's hair. "I'll see what I can find." She stood up and disappeared into the house while David stared at the dogs. "Not big Snoopy lovers I take it," he mumbled before lying down on the porch.
