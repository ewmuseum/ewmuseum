The Road: 2009 Edition

2 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Hey, c'mon guy. Last call was twenty minutes ago." The guy didn't look like he wanted to hear that. He had been sitting at the bar for most of the night, ordering round after round. Someone probably should've cut him off, but it was a busy night and they were shorthanded. "Naaaah, c'mon shhhhhweetie... one more f'r thhhhroad?" The bartender smiled at him, but shook her head. "Sorry, big fella, but time's tickin' and I need to get things cleaned up so I can get home. Now do me a favor and finish that, okay?" She gestured to the mug in front of him that had one or two mouthfuls of beer still floating in the bottom, and she returned to her previous task of clearing empty bottles from the surrounding tables. The bartender was a young woman who looked to be in her early twenties. She had shoulder length brunette hair carelessly pulled into a tight ponytail behind her head and was dressed in a black tank top with the logo "TC'S PUB: The Soul of the Bronx" imprinted on it, and black jeans with a 'church key' sticking out of her back pocket. The man at the bar watched her a little too intensely as she carried two overflowing handfuls of empty mugs back to the bar. "Awwwwh, one more round'n'I'll help ya clean up thish messh." "You're sweet," she replied again, with infinite patience, "but I just need you to hit the road so I can do my job. Need me to call you a cab?" "Naawh, I live jush' around the block. Wanna sheee?" "Rock and roll is dead! Rock and roll is dead! Rock and roll is dead, let's do somethin' else instead!" This time, she ignored him. The bartender walked to the jukebox and keyed in a code. Immediately, the song that was playing, 'Rock is Dead' by The Doors was silenced, and with a few more taps of the keys, the machine whirred back to life with a new sound. She could tell he didn't like it. "What the fuck'sh thish?" "It's time to go home," she continued, "now c'mon and do me a favor, okay?" "Dead light, holiday. Killing time to make us stay. Hollow as the promises of yesterday... On and on the music plays. Memories in paraphrase, falling past the window like the morning rain..." As she approached the bar with another handful of mugs and bottles, she felt his arm slip around her waist. Just as suddenly, he was pulled out of his seat by his shirt and dragged backwards three steps. "Wha'the fuck--" he started, before he turned around. Even with his blurred vision and hazy brain, he could tell he was looking up at an enormous man, "Who th'fuck're you?" "I'm the owner," replied Eli Flair, "and you need to leave." Eli helped the man walk to the front door, and locked it behind him. He turned around and looked to the left to see the bartender, completely nonchalantly continuing her closing routine, while to the right, Ivy McGinnis sat at a table near the middle of the floor with an almost - empty glass of Chivas Regal in front of her. "Cally, you need to stop scheduling yourself for solo closing shifts," said Eli, as he walked toward the bar, "He was drunk, but a few less drinks, he could've been dangerous." "Naah," replied Cally, "Mr. Nurbak gets a little touchy feely when he's that far gone but he's never done anything more than a slightly inappropriate hug. I can handle it." "Rosalyn Callasantos." The sound of her full name stopped her in her tracks. "Yes, Miss Ivy?" Eli laughed. "How come I'm the oldest one here, but I get 'Eli' while she gets Miss Ivy?" "It's how I say it," replied Ivy, as she picked up her glass and walked towards the bar. Eli followed from the door, and the three converged, "Rose, what is this music?" Cally looked at the jukebox and back at Ivy with disbelief. "It's the Birthday Massacre, of course." "You turned off The Doors for this?" "Well, you just said it, Mr. Eli," replied Cally with a wink, "you are the oldest one in the room." "Don't call him that again," laughed Ivy, "it just sounds wrong." This time, all three of them laughed. Cally continued to wipe down the bar. "Leave it," said Eli, "c'mon around and have a drink with us." "I'll have a drink," said Cally, "but I ain't leaving this. I've tried cleaning up when I've opened and it's never a pretty sight. Plus, I wouldn't want to leave a mess like this for Julian, it's not right." "I'll clean up in the morning," replied Eli, while Ivy walked behind the bar and pulled out a bottle, "I'm gonna crash in the loft, I think." Cally stopped wiping, and turned around to face him. "Are you shittin' me?" Eli's eyes drifted between her and Ivy. Ivy simply shrugged. "'Scuse me?" "You're leaving for Seattle in what, a week? And you're not gonna go home to Angel? Dude, isn't she still pissed at you for this whole setup?" "She says she isn't," answered Ivy, "and she might actually mean it. But she's got a point, E." "You're just pissed because I made the deal this time," replied Eli, as he took his new glass from Ivy. She hesitated before she spoke. "No... not exactly. I get why they called you and why you accepted, but Angel never will." "I just spent two years on the road with her," explained Eli, "and you, in case you forgot. Angel said it herself as soon as we bought the house, she wants to take some time off to get the studio in order and then start writing and recording again. She has to be home for that, so it made sense to me that it was time to get you back up on the horse." Cally and Ivy glanced at each other as Cally took the second glass from her. "Metaphorically speaking," replied Eli, rolling his eyes. "Still," said Cally, "She thought she had you back for good, man. I guarantee you the last thing she ever expected was t'sit there waiting for you to call her with a clean bill'a health after a match." "She won't have to." "Says you," replied Ivy, as she held up her glass, "Cheers." They all drank. "All I mean is that I know you," she continued, "You're gonna get out there and wrestle, and the fans'll pop for it because you're that fuckin' good. But you'll want to give 'em something extra every night, and that's what adds up. I know you've been gettin' ready for this for two months now, but you also haven't wrestled a full schedule in a year and a half and that's where a lotta Angel's concerns are. Shit, it's where mine are, too." "This, coming from the person who told Huph, 'thanks but no thanks' when she got the call." "Dick," replied Ivy, "Yeah, I needed some convincing, and I think we're doing the right thing. All I'm saying is that I understand how Angel feels." "No you don't." "'Scuse me, Rose," replied Ivy, "but you're s'posed to be on my side." Cally laughed, and took another drink. "Isn't Sean gonna be there?" "Yeah." "So, you really won't know exactly how Angel feels, will you?" "She's gotcha there," replied Eli, as he drained his glass. "You two are makin' me dizzy," replied Ivy, as she took another sip, "How'd we get on this, anyways?" "Eli didn't want to go home." "See? That's what I meant!" Eli threw his hands in the air and spun around on the barstool. "I can't win with you two. Okay, Cally... you wanna clean up tonight, that's your call. Just don't try to take the subway home, okay? Call a cab - or better, take the loft upstairs or go to my old apartment. You have your key, right?" "In my bag, Eli. Thanks." "And you," continued Eli, looking at Ivy, "Take a cab home, 'kay? What time are Trip and Shannon getting in?" "Two." "I'll be in around six." "Wonderful." "You okay to drive?" asked Cally. "Never better," replied Eli, as he picked his black leather trenchcoat from the coat hook and wrapped himself up in it, "Goodnight, ladies." A cold wind blew inside the bar for a second as he opened the door, but it abruptly shut. "Tell me the truth," said Cally, "Angel's still pissed, isn't she?" "She was pissed when he first got the call," admitted Ivy, "but it's behind her. She understands why we need to do this, even if she doesn't like it. And she'll support him in whatever he thinks he has to do." Cally smiled. "I like that." Ivy clinked her glass against Cally's. "Drink up, kiddo... we've got some cleaning to do before we go home. Where's that boyfriend of yours, anyways? He hasn't gone back to grad school yet, has he? Why isn't he here?" "Not sure," replied Cally, "s'posed to see him tomorrow, though." "Nice," said Ivy, "You should take a few days off if you want to next week and come on out to Seattle for the first show. We've leased a condo in the area, you can stay with me and Sean and Eli." "Condo?" "I thought it'd be easier to have a place to call home in the Northwest instead of living hotel to hotel, y'know? Plus it's a good place for any of the rookies who didn't plan ahead to crash out if need be." "That's what we like about you, Miss Ivy," replied Cally, "you're always thinkin'." "Yeah," replied Ivy, "Always thinking. Let's hope the streak continues."
