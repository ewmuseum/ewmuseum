Too Much that Time Cannot Erase

6 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
June 8th, 2003 Washington DC 4:21 AM local time "mmmmm...." It's the night before one of the biggest events of their lives. In about fifteen hours, Eli Flair will go up against one of the greatest pure wrestlers the world has ever seen in former CSWA World Champion Evan Aho, for the rights to the next shot at Mark Windham's belt. More importantly to him, his wife Angel has written a 'Battle of the Belts Anthem' in an attempt to sway CSWA Commissioner Chad Merritt to sign the band to a recording contract. She attacked the challenge with eager ferocity, obsessed with proving her worth as a singer apart from the fame her husband has earned. Everything should be perfect. Beyond perfect. Filled with nerves... but beyond perfect. "NOOOO!!!!!" Eli woke up with a start in his hotel room, feeling his wife's trembling body next to him. They were asleep, so he thought. "Angel?" "Lights." "What's wrong?" "LIGHTS!" repeated Angel, her voice unsteady, "Turn on a light. Please?" Eli leaned backwards and hit the small light on the nightstand. When he looked back at his wife, however... "C'mere," he said, opening his arms. She nearly bowled him over as she hid inside his hug, "What's wrong?" "Hold me," she cried, her voice nearly a whisper, "Just hold me, please..." Eli held her tightly, giving a quick look at MJ - blissfully asleep. He eased the two back down into bed, where he could cover her bare body with the blankets. "It's okay," he whispered, "I'm here... we can just be if you need." The minutes passed, and Angel slowly stopped shaking. She leaned up and kissed Eli on the chin before she buried her head in his shoulder. "Promise me something?" she asked. "Anything." "No matter what happens tomorrow, we're gonna be okay, right?" Eli looked confused, out of Angel's line of vision. "Course," he answered, "Nothing's gonna change between us. After everything we had to get through to be this happy, you think I'm gonna let you off the hook that easily?" She laughed, but it was half- forced. "And we're gonna be there for Mariella, right?" "Right." "And you're gonna be right there, right?" "Angel?" "What?" "Where's all this coming from?" "..." "What were you dreaming about?" "... My father." "Ahhh." Angel's father was a strict man from the midwest - unable to show his daughter that he loved her. And eventually his frustration with her musical aspirations, combined with severe antisocial tenancies based on abuse translated into violence. "I just feel like," she struggled with the worlds, "like if this doesn't work I'll prove him right. Like if I can't make this work, he'll be laughing at me, calling me names--" "Angel." He interrupted her gently, and she stopped her self- destructive thoughts for him. "You need to stop worrying about things that happened in the past. And as far as tomorrow night goes, believe it to be so and it'll be so. Simple as that." "I guess." "And as far as the rest of things... I'm here for you. I'm not here for you if - anything. I don't want a thing from you, I don't expect a thing from you, except for your love and support." He felt her hug him tighter. "And even if you don't get signed - even if you never record another song or play another show... there is nothing in the world that would make me love you less." He felt her cheek move on his chest - she was smiling. "I love you," she whispered. "And I, you," replied Eli, "And I don't regret a moment of it." Eventually, the two fell asleep.
