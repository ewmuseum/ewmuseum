Coming Home / Leaving Home

9 October 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
David Cross stared at the cardboard sign taped to the front of the elevator doors. "Out of Service. Use Stairs." The door leading to the stairwell was wedged open and a single bulb flickered inside. "Nice," David thought to himself. "Four floors up a darkened stairwell. They should hand out prizes to people who survive the climb." He stepped into the stairwell and started his ascent, the soles of his sneakers brushing against the concrete steps as he jogged in the darkness. Up on the third floor, another fluorescent bulb flashed like an inconsistent strobe light. In between, there were large blankets of darkness which could have hid anything or anyone. Even a trained martial artist like David didn't waste time in the dark. He wasn't afraid of the unknown, not anymore. But there was no reason to take an unnecessary risk. Once he'd sped to the fourth floor, David finally stopped to breathe. The young man was ready to celebrate. He'd done what was thought to be impossible by advancing to the second round of the Wrestler of the Year tournament. He'd taken the first step towards being considered a serious competitor in the fWo. He was fast, he was fearless, and he was ready to go as far as he could. The police tape in the hallway first caught David's eye. The two officers standing in front of his mother's apartment door didn't seem overly strange at first. His mother was a phone sex operator; maybe she'd been arrested for some reason. But the tape, that was bad news. "Hey gents," Cross said to the first cop. "What's going on in there?" "Move along," the officer said in a dismissive tone. "Nothing to see here." "That's good news, since I live here." The second cop pulled his note pad out of his jacket pocket. "You're David Cross?" "That's right." David tried to look into the apartment. "How's my Mom doing? Is she okay?" The first officer stepped towards David. "What do you know about your Mother's business affiliations?" "She's some kind of phone operator or something. I don't really pay much attention to her business. I just sleep on the couch." The second cop gave Cross a long look. "You're that guy from the fWo right?" A fan. Wow. "Yeah, that's me. Now about my Mom?" The first cop spoke up again. "So you didn't know she worked for a prostitution ring?" "She's a hooker?" David wrinkled his nose. "Different strokes for different folks, I guess." "Let's talk inside." David followed the two police officers into the apartment. He had an idea what they were going to tell him. When the officer told him, "Try not to touch anything until forensics get here," he knew his Mother was dead. Two steps into the apartment told David all he needed to know. There was blood everywhere - long looping streaks along the wall behind the couch, a red spatter pattern across the television screen There was a large white blanket draped along the floor. There was something underneath it, and blood had soaked through so heavily that puddles were forming on top of the sheet. The large, round figure under the sheet didn't have to be seen for David to know the truth. "That's my Mother," he said matter-of-factly. "I'm sorry," the second officer said. David continued to stare at the lump. "She wasn't my real mother. I was adopted. It looks better when you're getting checks from the government if you have two kids instead of one." "I'm sure she cared about you." All the beatings. All the abuse. The fact that when he burned his face, she spent the insurance money on herself and forced her adopted son to wear a mask to hide his hideousness. David reached into his wallet and pulled out an fWo business card. "If you need to get in touch with me, give that number a call. They'll be able to reach me." The officers stepped apart and let David pass by. He didn't look back as he walked out of the apartment. "We can let you gather up some of your things if you'd like." Cross looked over his shoulder. "There's nothing in there for me. I guess there never was."
