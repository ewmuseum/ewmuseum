My Modus Operandi - Part 3

4 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“For the last time Clinton, I’m not interested.” ”I just don’t understand how a man as good as you could pass up an opportunity to make some money.” ”But, I don’t need money.” “You don’t need money? Bullshit! I can’t believe you can say that with a fuckin’ straight face! You come in here wearing the same damn thing everyday, your face looks like it hasn’t seen a damn razor in 3 years, and you look homelier than any sum’bitch that walks in this place,” Clinton looked around at his surroundings with his arms spread out and his eyes wide. “And that’s sayin’ something!” ”So much for that secrecy pact,” I muttered to myself as I brought my glass of Tequila to my lips. The secrecy pact between Clinton and I had been broken ever since I brutalized the three young ‘gentlemen’ in his ‘fine establishment’. That secrecy pact was an unspoken promise created out of mutual silence between me and Clinton. We’ve had many drunken conversations throughout my inebriated endeavors at “Clint’s Pub”, but not one conversation strayed towards the hell he created below our very feet. I heard the violent outrages from the depths of his bar’s basement on a consistent basis for the past half-year. With my experience, I could even make out what attack was thrown and summate where it made contact. My senses grew sharp to brutality and bloodshed over the years; I thank my experience in torturous battles for that. And for months, Clinton hid these explosive and violent resonances with loud music, thinking it would shield the brutality below. And for months, it worked on the common patron; not one person that walked into his bar took notice to the violent outbreaks. However, Clinton never realized that the ‘bar bum’ he spoke with on a consistent basis was a veteran of violence…at least, until 3 months ago. Ever since he discovered the beast behind the man, he couldn’t help but crave more. “Look, all I’m sayin’ is that this is a great opportunity for you. Why not exploit the hell outta’ it?” Clinton asked frustratingly as he slapped his hands softly on the bar and leaned forward towards me. I stared at Clinton for a moment; he was no longer that collective person I had known for years. His eyes shook slightly in excitement and his yellow smile was wide and enthusiastic, like a dog staring a meaty steak across the room; he was ready to pounce on the opportunity. I sighed depressingly and took another sip of my tequila. For a while, I held Clinton on somewhat of a pedestal of personality. To an extent, I related to the man. I took comfort in the fact that I was not the only man who could subdue my inner-beast for times of desperation and, in the mean time, wear a mask of fake calmness. Like Clinton, I too was looking at the beast behind the man. I was no longer that innocent and docile ‘bar bum’ that had been given ridicule through quiet snickers. I was now an opportunity to make money for Clinton. “Exploit it?” I asked with a condescending tone in my voice. “Exploit it for what, for money, for fame and fortune?” I chuckled amusingly as I turned towards the common patrons of his business and stretched my arm out, as if presenting Clinton the scum of the earth that raided his bar every night. “I’m sure these people are just loaded with money.” ”They are when it comes to these kinds of things,” Clinton said reassuringly, trying to revert back to his calm nature, one that he knew I connected with. I laughed to myself for a moment and shook my head in bewilderment. I took the final sip of my Tequila and ran my free hand through my hair, in an attempt to massage my mental frustrations out. I set the glass down roughly, apparently sharply enough to startle the ‘composed’ Clinton. I began to feel a sense of frustration rising in my body, and struggled to subdue it. I saw Clinton for what he was; he was a dirty money peddler that simply banked on the mental and physical ailments of brainless idiots who didn’t know how else to make an honest living. He was what he surrounded himself with; he was scum. He wasn’t that composed and intelligent man that I once felt was full of wisdom, he was just like every other person in this god forsaken state. I could tell my stare startled Clinton by the way he reached underneath his bar and grazed the edge of his ‘alarm system’ (his shotgun). ”Let’s make something perfectly clear. I don’t fight for others. I fight for myself. I don’t fight to make someone else rich. I don’t fight to make others chant and cheer my name like a bunch of mindless drones. I fight to test my abilities, to take out my frustrations, and to teach lessons to those who deserve it. In a way, you could say I’m quite selfish,” I said through gritted teeth. “And Clinton, I suggest you take your hand away from the shotgun because if I really wanted to hurt you, you wouldn’t have the time to use it.” Clinton looked at me nervously, it was the first time he had ever done so. His eyes were once full of adoration and comfort when it came to sharing dialogue and a bottle of liquor with me. But now, his eyes were clouded with fear and anxiety. I almost felt a sense of sorrow in the destruction of our once fond social relationship. I always felt a sense of warmth when entering “Clint’s Pub”. Even though it was a dark tunnel, full of prostitutes, cheating husbands and wives, and dense dirt bags; it had a light at the end of the tunnel. That light was the commonality between Clinton and myself. Those days were over the minute we took off our social masks and revealed the beasts within. Clinton finally pulled his hand back and rested it on the bar top. I lowered my gaze on Clinton and sat silently for a moment. I could feel my heart beating more heavily, but it was no longer out of anger, but of depression. Sadness has the same physical effect on our bodies as anger, at least in my opinion. They both cause your heart to race from negative emotions, and both leave you with a sense of emptiness that needs to be filled. Anger requires violence in order to be appeased, but sadness…that ones tricky. As I sat quietly, I saw Clinton’s body remained unmoving. I could imagine he too was feeling a sense of remorse. A relationship that was once of social delight was tainted by the prospect of business; such is the case with any social bond. I wanted to say, ‘Let’s just forget about the whole thing’, but those words never worked with any bond. It never worked with Jessica, and it wouldn’t work with Clinton; despite their obvious differences, a social bond is a social bond. Suddenly, the violent slam of a door shooting wide echoed through the loud music and the full bar. I shoot my head towards the door behind the bar and stared at the large and muscular brute that came out with a loud, “Whoo!” of excitement. The man wiped blood from an open gash on his forehead and smiled in total satisfaction as he snatched a terry cloth from behind the bar and happily dabbed the wound. The bulky man, riddled in muscle, looked directly at Clinton and questioned his stiff demeanor. Asking Clinton, “What’s wrong?” After recognizing the distraught look on Clinton’s face, he turned to the one person that sat close to Coldren; he turned to me. This bald-headed God of War, drenched in body art and blood, turned his gaze upon me, the common ‘bar bum’. I read that when animals of the same sex stare at one another, it is always a test of dominance. Whoever loses the stare first is the first to show inferiority. I would not show any inferiority to an inferior. I stared back at him with a hard and cold gaze. No dialogue was shared between us, and the stare was broken equally when Clinton assured this man that nothing was wrong. The two shared pleasantries and spoke of a fight, apparently this man was the victor. After 15-minutes or so of senseless dialogue, the large man took his leave. But as he did, he and I made sure to share a gaze in passing. He shot a simple curl of the lip towards me and a middle finger to show he cared. I only smirked back and watched as he turned his back towards me and went out into the winter’s night. I sat still for a moment, continuing to stare at the man as he disappeared into the night. As an eerie lull, that felt like it lasted an eternity, fell between me, my empty glass, and Clinton, I heard noise from outside. I peered to the windows of the pub and barely made out dancing shadows under the New York street lights, violently bouncing into one another. I could barely hear shouts of profanity and masculine aggression. All of my senses spiked at the violence. I peered over at Clinton to see if he had any reaction, but he remained oblivious to the mayhem that was just a few feet from his place of business. He began serving other customers, possibly to break the lull between us. Meanwhile, blood was being spilled just inches away from the exit door. I briefly questioned why no patron was rushing to see the bloodshed, but was quickly reminded why by the ear-shattering tunes of some mindless rock band on the jukebox. The audible distraction of Clinton’s had left he and his customers numb to the world of violence that surrounded them. I, however, was different. I didn’t wish to draw attention to the scene, or my recognition of it; big crowds usually ruined the beauty of battle with their idiotic chants of blood-thirsty worship. I, on the other hand, saw it for what it was – a natural art form. I calmly grabbed my heavy trench coat and put it on softly. I coolly placed my hands deep within the pockets and made my way towards the exit. Through my serene exit, I could still see Clinton’s eyes following me through the reflection of the window. I paid no mind to his stare and continued out into the chilly winter. Through the brisk winds of the winter night of New York, I could hear a wide variety of sounds. I could hear a child crying somewhere far off, I could hear a vendor shouting advertisements of his products; but through all of the nonsense, I could make out the thuds and cracks of bone and flesh being bruised, beaten and broken. I followed the sounds anxiously for only a few feet, when a nervous man ran directly into me. My large physique overpowered his nervous charge and he fell violently to the ground below. I stared calmly at him for a moment as he raced to collect himself. Meanwhile, a second man raced straight past us and flung himself into the sea of people walking through the busy streets. As the nervous wreck struggled to get to his feet, he lifted his panicky eyes towards mine. His look was only for a second, but I could remember every detail of his fear-filled expression. Sweat was sprayed across his forehead and a nervous drool trickled down the edges of his dry lips. His eyes were wide with terror and his mouth was agape, trying to collect as much oxygen as possible to increase the speed of his getaway. The eye-to-eye contact was brief; he quickly averted his stare from mine and raced past me, huffing and puffing nervously as he disappeared into the crowds of New Yorkers. The nervous wreck had no visible scars to him, and the speed of his escape proved his lack of injury. He may have seen the brutality, but he remained untouched. I noticed that the sounds of violence were much softer now. The roars of raw aggression were now a hum of desperate angst. I walked towards the darker edge of a large building and saw the epitome of human rage before me. The large brutish man, the very one who shared a stare of dominance with me, had a man hanging with a noose. The victim dangled helplessly in the air, as his gurgles and gasps were swallowed by the emptiness of the barren alley. The large man shouted threats towards his victim, exploring his dominance verbally as well as physically and mentally. Then, surprisingly, he released him. The prey of the situation coughed violently and habitually curled into a fetal position before the predator, he who stood strong before his near lifeless prey. I stared at the scene as if it were painting. It was the essence of human struggle for dominance in its purest form. The dominant being standing tall over the inferior being, who is crawled into a ball in an attempt to beg for mercy. My stare became hypnotic…and noticeable. “What a fuckin’ waste of human life you are; just standing there staring,” the man spotted my fascinated stare, and we quickly reinvigorated our own glare. He breathed heavily like a wild boar, with clouds of smoke bellowing from his mouth. “If you’re not going to help then turn around, go back inside, get your drink and go back to sleep in your usual booth.” Through his dialogue he made strong advances towards me. With every giant step he took, my grin instinctively grew wider. I could feel my heart racing with excitement, it was a feeling I hadn’t had since 3 months ago. It was like a drug that I yearned for, that excitement of an impending battle. However, this one was different from the last…my palms began growing sweaty and my nerves began to twitch slightly. I made sure not to let it show as he stood nose to nose with me. I could feel the heat of anger rushing through his body, and I’m sure he could feel the enthusiasm of my own. We stared at one another with angry glares, silently daring the other to throw the first hit. ”Prick,” he said as he ran his large hand across the back of his head and brought back a red-gladden palm. He brushed past my shoulder and headed back towards the pub, shouting his requests for a ‘fucking towel’ and a ‘fucking beer’ towards the empty night. As I heard the door slam behind me, I couldn’t help but smile wickedly. In fact, that smile didn’t disappear, and remained etched on my face for the next 24 hours. It was a smile I hadn’t had in quite some time. It was the rush of a challenging fight that thrilled my inner being. I wanted that fight. I needed that fight. Violence was my drug, and it had been quite some time since I had my fix. I was tired of wearing my mask of serenity. I needed to feel alive again. It was the only thing I knew 100%; and I knew I needed it. This is why I went back to the pub the next night and waited silently for him to arrive. For hours I waited, ordering no alcohol and simply sitting at the bar. Clinton didn’t speak to me; he didn’t so much as look at me for more than a second. It was a lull, but an eager one. The bar was unusually quiet that evening, only a few strays drank behind me; and they drank silently and to themselves. It was as if the world was waiting with me. After hours of patience, the door swung open and he arrived. Our stare quickly returned, but he broke it with a scoff of dominance, as if deeming me worthless through his facial expressions. He briskly walked past me and made a hand gesture towards Clinton as he opened the gates of hell and walked right in; he was a regular. I half-smiled and stood up from my stool. Clinton didn’t seem afraid, and in fact, his look expressed full knowledge of my intentions. He leaned against the bar top and looked directly at me. ”Can I help you, son?” he said with an arrogant tone. I didn’t hesitate. I lifted my coat and flung it over my shoulder and walked straight towards the basement door. I stared at the door, noticing it’s every feature. It’s broken slabs of wooden splinters, it’s decrepit rusted door knob, and it’s frame that was faded with age. It was the gate to hell, and it was exciting. ”What do you think you’re doing?” Clinton asked with a wicked smile on his face. I responded with only one word. “Living.”
