My Immortal

20 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Some sort of piano ballad was playing in the background, a practical interlude on a burnt Evanescence CD Amy'd had on loop all night. I'm so tired of being here Suppressed by all my childish fears And if you have to leave I wish that you would just leave 'Cause your presence still lingers here And it won't leave me alone... The song had meaning and it cut deep. She wasn't walking in the rain, looking for somewhere to get out of the cold...none of the cheese you'd expect. Instead, the little redhead was sitting, huddled on the edge of a bed with a bulletin board of pictures in front of her, just a little collage of she and friends from ninth grade until as recently as one of she and Renee sitting in the snow with Valerian's Garden. She wasn't alone, though. There was a little form tucked away in the corner with some kind of bright yellow Tonka truck that he was exceedingly proud of....and he was something Amy was exceedingly proud of. But he was also a torturing reminder of the times she'd been through...instead of showing the times she'd come out of, looking into his eyes only took her back to where she'd been at 17... Sitting in a hospital for almost twenty four hours straight, just staring at one person. She wasn't a patient, but a visitor to the best friend anybody could've ever asked for while the girl fought with God...and inevitably lost. Amy was the 'winner' that day; because of a pact she'd made with Stephanie - her best friend who happened to be pregnant at the time of a drug-induced car accident - the redhead was going to get a few days to herself to mourn...whether she liked it or not. Amy Campbell had wasted that entire summer in and out of the hospital with the one part of Stephanie they'd managed to save: Stephen, the two and a half weeks premature baby. There wasn't really any time to catch up on how she was feeling, no mourning involved. She couldn't. Whenever it seemed like she was granted a few minutes to be sad, she was slapped back in the face by worry for Stephen or rage at her family for lecturing her on how she took on a task like raising someone else's kid at 17 years old headfirst. Eventually, almost everything got tucked away...she'd JUST graduated that June, and you know that feeling you get after grad where everything's free and you suddenly have your whole life ahead of you? She never, EVER got that. Maybe for all of a few days...but right afterwords, it felt like her life was ending. After all, why shouldn't it have ended? Stephanie had finally graduated, was free of the abusive boyfriend who'd finally run away when she got pregnant, she was 'finally' a lot of things...and then came to the classic tragic end. When you cried I'd wipe away all of your tears When you'd scream I'd fight away all of your fears I held your hand through all of these years But you still have All of me No glory. No fame. Nobody even heard, and out of those who did, only her closest friends cared. But right after, it was all about this baby suddenly put on Amy's lap...no time to think about anything else. Stephanie had only thought of the child's well-being naturally, being not only in an intense state of shock, pain and hysteria, knowing she had a few hours left to live at the time...but it didn't matter, she wanted everything to go right for the little person she'd spent nine months of her life BRINGING life to, and saw in Amy the only way to get it that way. In front of her was the one girl she respected, loved and admired like only a sister could...nobody would raise the baby like Amy would if Stephanie didn't get the chance. It was the 'average' "tragic tale", but it had more of an effect on someone's life than anybody would ever realize. Maybe her parents saw for the months and years after, Amy couldn't look at Stephen without drawing back moments later...but nobody quite 'got it'. Even sitting in her room tonight, she couldn't look at him straight for any length of time - she only ever saw Stephanie. But she also saw what Stephanie could have been and what Stephanie wanted to be for this little boy...the everything she never got the chance to be, but this was the everything Amy never got a chance to earn. She didn't feel like 'Mommy', and didn't want to BE 'Mommy', she wanted to be 'Amy' to him, for Stephanie to be there to see him and fill the role she couldn't quite match up to...but it wouldn't happen, it couldn't happen. For three years, Amy learned the same lesson over again every day: Nothing can be reversed, God chooses certain people to pick on in life and when it's you, watch the fuck out. You used to captivate me By your resonating life Now I'm bound by the life you left behind She looked at Stephen again from her spot, her form sunk down into the bed's matress slightly. Why couldn't she get over it? Their eyes were the same... You'll never see someone's faith in 'the holy spirit' die faster. It was only now beginning to come back, hand-in-hand with the good in her life returning. 'Financially', Stephen had put a strain on Amy...her parents understood she wouldn't give him for adoption and wanted to provide him with the best life possible - for Stephanie - but they only paid half of the expenses and many times, that almost had her giving up on the slight savings she was digging up for wrestling school. The burdens never broke her back, though...she worked as many as three jobs at the same time to get where she is, to become what she wanted to be and even if she fell slightly short of being that person she strived to be seen as, she was here at the forefront of the business she'd dreamed of in EVERY sense of the word, an unsung hero or not. If anybody ever told Aimz she hadn't earned her spot in the business, she'd make sure they walked away a limb short...there was nothing that came to her within the last three and a half years that she didn't earn, nothing that she didn't deserve either. She had sleepless nights and a nearly diseased mind for all of this...but wherever she turned, there was still a whisper of supreme negativity, of the proverbial cross she bore. Your face it haunts My once pleasant dreams Your voice, it chased away All the sanity in me But finally things seemed to be going right. In the last five months, so much had happened: She found the courage to actually be a mother to Stephen, she'd come to fWo, trained ten times as hard as ever and most of all, found Brandon. She wouldn't credit it to divine intervention, either. 'He' might have allowed her to do all of this, but there was nothing she hadn't gotten for HERSELF. She felt as if God owed her at least these things and finally, it seemed, Stephen wasn't quite the haunting image of a long-dead comrade, but maybe the 'hope for a new day'... I've tried so hard to tell myself that you're gone But though you're still with me... Stephanie was there, always, in the form of her blood son...but it only ever served to hurt Amy, not help. It didn't give her confidence of any sort. Tonight, she faced all of the thoughts she'd spent three years hiding from, and it was all her own doing...that was confidence, that's what had her realize that the rest of her life had been a sick scheme by God to get a few laughs...but somehow she still had faith, if only because of all that was suddenly right with her life. I've been alone all along. Maybe this was how a Rat Pack could save "That lost little girl.". Amy certainly had her hero in the end...
