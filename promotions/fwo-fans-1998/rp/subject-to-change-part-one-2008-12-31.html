Subject To Change : Part One

31 December 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Frank Kilcullen’s cragged teeth gnashed from within the thick brush of his bristly black beard. The redder his pitted face radiated with anger, the colder cobalt blue eyes felt as they peered into the sunken, bloodshot pair of Butch Crosby. “Aryah tellin’ me mah boy ain’t good’nuf for ya?” Frank’s fierce words ground from deep within his gravely throat. “Is that roight?” A battle-worn hand, adorned with an aged cloverleaf tattoo, mauled at Crosby’s jowly face. “Oi ghut nhuh rhum ohn huh churd,” seeped out through Kilcullen’s fingers. The muscles that once sculpted Crosby’s body had long since eroded, releasing rolls of fat which refused to be contained by the sweaty white vest he’d pulled over them. He was cowering, stuttering putty in the ex-boxers hands of Kilcullen. “Ah, c’mon…” Frank mischievously smirked, folding up the weathered skin around his flattened nose. “S’not strictly true now, is’t?” The sunken eyes of Butch Crosby then squinted as sweat overcame his thinning eyebrows, “whut hmuv yhuh dhun?” As Kilcullen released his muzzle-grip over Crosby’s face, the pudgy promoter gasped in the heavy, stale air. Taking a few swaggering steps away from the building, Frank jabbed a finger at the yellow posters that were plastered along the wall. “See, on mah way over here oi passed ol’ Yves Marah,” began his menacing tale. “An’it looked t’me loik e’was havin’ a few… car troubles. Told ol’ Frank here that e’snot much hope’ah makin’ yah show t’noight. Ain‘t that roit, lads?”” “Broken axle, oi think’e said,” was snarled from the shadows behind Kilcullen. Crosby’s jaw dropped, flushing his clammy face of all colour and leaving it looking a swollen interpretation of Munch’s ‘The Scream’. For the past five years, the Kilcullens had been other peoples bad luck stories, cautionary tales he needn’t heed. Now, he couldn’t help wondering how much was exaggerated for drama. “What… did you do?” Butch asked, aghast. A laugh reminiscent of ballast being shovelled into a cement mixer erupted from Frank. A look over his broad shoulder infected his two accomplices with the chuckles. “Looks t’me loik y’got a slot t’fill at the top o’that card there,” Frank beamed, radiant from laughter. “So best get’cha a sharpie an’ change that there main event tah Karianne Wolfsername take’non Billy Kilcullen… Loik, now.” “He’s not ready!” Butch appealed, warbling voice slipping higher. “He’s only done two months tra-AURGH!!!” Kilcullen grabbed the promoter’s dirty, sweaty vest, than thrust his punch-sculpted forehead into the orsine nose of Crosby. With eyes watering and blood cascading down into his shock-frozen mouth, Butch slumped down onto his amble behind. “Oi taught ‘im more tough than yah lil’ gym could evah,” Frank decreed, all the joviality surrounding him just a few seconds ago withered away. “Now, oi’ll be takin’ mah seat in tha front row t’watch mah boy.”
