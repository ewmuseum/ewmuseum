EVOLVER_thursday

17 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
He hung up the phone viciously. "Mother fucker!" Eugene turned around and sent his fist into the wall of the overpriced hotel room. All the European hotels were cramped. As some cheap plaster landed on the floor, he stared up at the single flourescent light and, back to the wall, slumped down onto the linoleum. He'd had the conversation in his head so many times, but he had enough relationship experience to know that nothing can absorb the shock of a break-up. Barring the last few months of touring, Eugene had spent the better part of four years waking up next to Jessica. Her face had been on the pillow next to his. Her eyes opened, sleepy and lazy, just inches away from his own knowing gazes. The smell of her hair lingered on his bed. Jessica was a security blanket, and one that life cruelly ripped from his hands. Eugene stood for a moment and went over to the light switch, flicking it up and down to distract himself. He smiled as he imagined those looking into the room's window and believing it was some sort of an SOS. The thought brought him back to his own stranded soul, an oasis of hope in a desert of despair. Or maybe it's the other way around. He walked over to the window, wondering, his mind already outside of it. Rain slashed across the streets below with a tiger's fury, impeding the pedestrians and their midnight soirees. The window was streaked with the water, and so was the reflection that stared back into Eugene's face. He had done it, and there was no going back. But for who? He had decided it was for himself. Even if he could go back, he knew there was no sense. The days when every kiss was as special as the first had long since passed. With them, so did the days of conversation. All signs pointed to one person for Eugene... ...But as far as Amy Campbell saw, the road was one-way only.
