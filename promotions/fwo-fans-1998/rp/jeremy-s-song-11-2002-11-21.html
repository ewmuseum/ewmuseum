Jeremy's Song - 11

21 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
previous parts: see-downfall archive. The sun broke the dreary night, bringing color to the previously shadowed objects. Jeremy Benton, head ducked down to avoid the brisk winds this March morning, pushed through the glass and steel doors of Maine West High School. He paused, blonde hair glowing against the fluorescent lights, and allowed the heated interior warm his thinning figure. Jeremy let his backpack fall from his right shoulder. He lifted it and placed it solely on his right shoulder as he walked down the white tiled steps, ignoring the metallic handrail. Sounds of children speaking in the cafeteria and teachers partaking in their separate conversations were all he could hear. Nothing else, even his thoughts, was relevant. Seas of polyester, cotton, and denim clothed students each gathered at different tables in the cafeteria. The place was like that of any other school, faux wood tables placed in equal intervals of distance apart. Book bags strewn across the floor, Jeremy walked toward a table where peers, friends, and acquaintances sat. Meaningless discussions about weekend plans and the like were the norm. A young woman in a tight pink shirt revealing her naval and blue jeans looked upward toward another friend. A past was shared between the two and it was a past that most knew about. Her name was Emily Hardt, fifteen years old with a young but beautiful face and black hair ran smooth like silk. The two had been in a relationship on more than one occasion. Friendship, love and lust experienced, yet on shaky grounds. Friendship was slowly fading away with the other three emotions that were now only past memories. Preoccupied, no one had even acknowledged Jeremy on his arrival. He placed his book bag on the ground and shot a glance toward Emily. Glance turned to stare as he examined her like a foreign being, eyeing her every movement in overtaking fascination. Friendship was not acceptable for it wasn't what he wanted and wasn't what he felt. In a button-down, meaningless life, sanity was found in this sanctuary of a woman. A place, a person to confide in was all he needed. For that he wanted her. "Hello, Emily." Jeremy spoke with a deepened tone. Turning her head, Emily glanced over and lost herself in the blue lake that was Jeremy's eyes for a few seconds. A slight grin found its way to her face. "Hello, Jeremy," she said with such eloquence and grace, deceitful of her true self. Each little imperfection made her more real, more to desire. Everything she did, everything she said, it all just drove him further and further into obsession. No more words were spoken. The shriek of a bell indicated that first period was about to begin. Teachings were ignored and time teachers spent pacing back and forth talking about a war or a problem, it was insignificant. Forgotten as soon as it entered the brain solely because too much thought provoking things were going on in his life. His head screamed with thought after though running its course over and over until Jeremy finally returned to reality. Minutes on the clock solely passed, followed by the same ringing to indicate the start of fifth period. This, to Jeremy, meant lunch. He walked down the main stairs and down to the front entrance of the cafeteria. Clouds painted the ceiling to compliment the brown, almost stone-like texture brushed on the walls. Hieroglyphics with words of encouragement written below them were painted in an almost beveled way on the wall. The lunch line stretched back as Jeremy analyzed the young adults standing in line, awaiting the next crude meal they were to shell out their cash for today. None of this was of any importance to him. Merely walked his course and sat at an almost empty table with book bags placed on the seats. Eventually, the table was filled with people. One of them stood out amongst the sea of them: Emily. Emotions were now only torturous feelings that only brought him pain. He didn't want to belief that it wouldn't work out or that the one person he felt strongly for didn't love him back. The bell cackled once more. As soon as it began, the free time given to the students had ended. Each of them disbursed to their respective classes where the day would continue. Three o'clock came, and the bell rang for the final time of the day. Jeremy stepped out of his Calculus class and walked down the hallway to his locker. Upon opening it, he placed his book bag and its entire contents inside. Suggestive thoughts of ditching basketball practice were brought on by slight depression and sleep deprivation. Each step only finalized his theory that habit powered over everything else. Each time, a different person would greet him in the hallway. He was, by no means, an unpopular kid. Lots of people knew him but few knew him well. No one knew him well enough, or so it would appear. The locker room lingered with the smell of old unwashed clothes, mildew and sweat. Everything in the room, from the lockers to the benches to the small square tiled floor was tinted with an encompassing cape of green. The steel benches rusted as the legs neared the ground due to the steam and water dripping onto them. Jeremy removed his shirt as he approached his locker. Inside contents were shadowed by the lack of light regardless of the fact that there were numerous in the door to allow what was inside to breathe. The steel cracked against the locker next to it as threw the door open. A pair of blue shorts, a blue basketball jersey with gold numbers on it, a black duffel bag and a bar of deodorant barely filled the large area. The coarse jersey felt rough on Jeremy's pale skin. As he was walking out to the court, his own reflection caught his glance. Defined cheekbones, evenly proportioned features and a long face complimented his tall, lanky frame. "A creature of habit," Jeremy stated to himself as he jogged out to the basketball court. The coach called the team in. Each player kneeled down on one knee as they stared up at their leader. "Everyone, I'd like to congratulate you on last weeks win against Notre Dame. I'd especially like to congratulate Jeremy on scoring twenty-eight points. Everyone give a round of applause for Jeremy." As directed, the team clapped in approval. A few slapped Jeremy on the back as he smiled not out of happiness but out of politeness. None of this brought him any real pleasure, but more of something to keep his busy. It was something to drag him away from the thoughts that weighed him down daily. That was all he wanted, and that was all he received. Thinking of this made Jeremy realize something. Running away was solving nothing. Busying himself with small projects and pointless sports only so he wouldn't have to come back home to drunken parents and no comfort as early as three o'clock. This was not what he wanted out of life. He wanted salvation. As everyone left to grab a basketball, Jeremy walked over to the coach. "I'd like to turn in my uniform," Jeremy whispered. He felt guilty for leaving the team, but he felt disgusted that this was the only way he could deal with his problems. "Why?" The truth would have been too much. Schools tend to take things out of context. Explaining his feelings would get him a green pass to visit the school counselor once or twice a week to talk about these problems. The idea of sharing all of his bottled up feelings with an old person that couldn't possibly understand them at that point in their life was unimaginable. It wouldn't happen, so Jeremy didn't reply. Unwelcoming, cold feelings of the locker room now only made Jeremy feel accomplished, even if only for a few moments. This was all one step toward turning things in his direction. Although, he was lucky Tom was absent today. Even if he was Jeremy's best friend, he would have tried and succeeded in convincing him to stay, doing something he didn't want to do. The two had been friends for five years and confided in each other, Tom more so than Jeremy. Each was a small but significant help to the other. Of course, tomorrow, Jeremy would have to tell Tom that he quit. Tom would, most likely, give a short speech and then say he didn't care. He was very predictable. Jeremy placed his hand against a steel locker and leaned his forehead against his own, staring down at the tile floor. This was it, the end of his once success. Out of all the things he had done, this was the one he did better than anyone else and he didn't even have fun doing it. He removed his jersey and his shorts before folding them and placing the shorts on top of the jersey. Slowly, Jeremy got dressed and placed the rest of the locker's contents in the black duffel bag before waking out and placing the uniform on the equipment manager's desk. A white Ford sounded a beeping noise. Jeremy approached the car and opened up the trunk with a set of keys he pulled from the pulled from his baggy blue denim pants. Placing the black bag in, he closed the trunk door and opened the driver's side door. He stepped in and closed the door before staring off into the empty football field, empty practice fields, and empty tennis courts. The vastly empty campus seemed to be the exact opposite of his life. With that, Jeremy placed a forearm on the steering wheel and placed his forehead against it. Tears rolled down his cheeks and dripped to the floor mat below. It didn't even matter why he was crying, at least not to him. Lost in a dark cloud of emotion and failure. This is what he needed.
