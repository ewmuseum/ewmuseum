Business Lunch

10 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Ready to order?" "Yeah.... let me get the philadelphia steak sandwich platter, with a baked potato instead of fries?" "And to drink?" "Just water, please." "Okay, and you, ma'am?" "Write this down." The waitress looked confused. "Huh?" Poison Ivy continued. "I'd like a bacon cheeseburger," said Ivy, "with a fried egg on top." The waitress wrote it down, although she had a very unappetized look on her face. It was a look she shared with the other person at the table. "Oh!" exclaimed Ivy suddenly, "And a side of gravy, and an unsweetened iced tea." As the waitress walked away, shaking her head, Twitch did the same, only laughing. "What?" asked Ivy, "It's good. Don't knock it if you haven't tried it." "Okay, okay!" replied Twitch, "Not another word on lunch." Ivy sipped her water with a sly gleam in her eye. "Good. Now, was this purely a social call or did you have business you wanted to talk?" "It's business," replied Twitch, "just didn't see why it had to be a formal occasion. After everything you've done to try and relax the employees of this company, a 24-Hour Diner seemed the perfect locale." Laughing, Poison Ivy dropped her napkin in her lap. "Thank ya." "I had a chance to talk over some of your ideas with the guys... it was definitely an unorthodox suggestion you came up with." "Maybe," mused Ivy, "but I think the numbers speak for themselves." She was good. "They do," continued Twitch, "but there's more to this business than just numbers." "You think I don't know that?" The tension was broken up by the waitress arriving with their drinks, and a container of Sweet 'n Low for Ivy's iced tea. "I don't mean to be a bitch or anything y'know, Twitch," continued Ivy after taking a sip of iced tea, "but I don't understand what the problem is and would really like to." "I know," replied Twitch, "Everyone who this would affect talked it over, and it's just not the right time." "We're talking about Cyberslam," countered Ivy. "Right." "The biggest event of the year." "Right." "A proven box office draw." "Right." "I'm not seeing the problem here." "It's--" "Complicated, right?" "It is, whether you want to believe it or not." "Sounds political." "It's a tad political, I can't deny it," agreed Twitch, "Not a game you ever really demonstrated any propensity for." "By choice, though." "You might want to rethink that. This business has as much to do with politics as it does talent. And while your attitude in the lockerrooms has been a model of diplomacy..." "I know, I know," finished Ivy, "It's just that... Well, you know where Eli and I are from, and you know the politics that almost tore that place apart in the mid- 90s." "I know - but nobody thinks you and your boys are forming some kind of nu- millennium 'Kliq.' Trust me on that." "So what is it then?" "Timing. That's the problem." "It always is." Their food showed up - good service here. The conversation stopped momentarily as they both dug in, grateful for the break in the tension. "I know you both had your hearts set on getting this through," said Twitch, finally, "It's just a bad time to push something this different through." "Yeah," replied Ivy, "I knew that... so did Eli. It's just disappointing, y'know?" "All I can really say at this point is to keep doing what you're both doing. Balls to the wall, it literally is just a matter of time." Ivy smiled. It's not what she wanted to hear but it's better than she expected. And like Twitch said.... it's just a matter of time.
