17 April 2003

The first time Eugene lit a match, the wind blew it out before it reached its mark. The flames were too fragile for the strong Santa Ana winds in the San Fernando valley. He dropped the now useless twig to the ground and reached into the pocket of his coat to pull the matchbook out again. Eugene heard Patrick's footsteps come up from behind, but didn't hesitate. Ricky stood at Eugene's side with an expression of utter bewilderment. "Eugene, you don't smoke." The process was successful this time around and Marder inhaled deeply, taking time to brush away the locks that blew into his face. "I figure now is a good time to start." They both shared a tense laugh. That was the most they could allow themselves. Eugene was the first to speak again. "Did you sign those clearance papers?" All Ricky could do was sigh. "Nevermind that, Eugene. How are you? How is everything feeling?" "Rick, I didn't come here to talk about the weather. I live an hour and a half away, and I agreed to meet you at your office to pick up the papers." The annoyed tone in Eugene's voice struck a chord with Patrick. "I don't care about the fucking papers! I want to know that my best friend can walk, and I am not sending you into a wheelchair. If you think I will let you go out there knowing it will be your last match, you are sorely mistaken. I won't do that- not as your physician, and certainly not as your fucking friend." Even with the noise of cars passing by, Eugene could hear Patrick's hard breathing. The desperation in his voice was evident. He cared. But Eugene couldn't reciprocate at the moment. His mind was centered on one Amy Campbell. She'd captured him, twisted him around her finger, and left him. He couldn't handle it. He couldn't handle how much he loved her. He couldn't hand how much he hated himself. He couldn't let Amy go on torturing him. "Rick, I'm fine. I promise. The legs are better, but not perfect. The liquid stitches you put in are healing well. I've had them open on me before, and it's no big deal. In terms of the muscles, I'll be able to wrestler. I won't lie. They're hurting, but it's nothing I haven't dealt with before." It was true. Eugene looked at his hands covertly, peeking at the scars he had gotten so long ago. "Okay, well, I'm glad that you're healing. Just as long as you don't do anything super-strenuous out there, you-" "It's a hardcore title match, Ricky." He was beaming, and Ricky was shocked. "Oh, Jesus. Well, uh, that...Complicates things." "Only if you let it. Now...The papers?" Ricky turned away, not wanting to look his friend in the eyes. "Yeah, I signed them this morning." He reached into his coat pocket and handed an envelope to his friend. "Make sure to fly back right away after the surgery. It's not like you'll have much else to do..." Ricky suddenly caught himself and looked the other way. "Hey, well, listen, Euge, I gotta run, so-" Eugene grabbed the departing man and turned him around. "What do you mean?" "Christ, I'm a doctor, I've got appointments-" "No, shut up. What do you mean, I won't have much else to do." As Eugene slowly loosened his grip on Rick's arm, the doctor's expression loosened, as well. He looked out across the road, at nothing in particular. "Well, those clearance papers alone won't let you wrestle. Management knows I'm a friend, and they know you're a fucking liability, but I understand how much it means to you, so I did a little reasearch to see if I could help you..." Side-stepping to face his friend, Eugene interjected, "And?" "...And CyberSlam will be the last show. This guy I went to school with does some bookkeeping for CWL and he recently overheard a conversation with Nodar and Silver Fox. The fWo will close, and I figure that'll give you the trump card you need. Nobody knows. Just tell management that you do, and threaten to tell everyone else. They'll let you wrestle." Eugene was not at all concerend with that anymore. "What the fuck? It's over? What am I going to do? And now I'll be known as the love-struck sap who went out on an injury. No, no way. I'm going back there, and I'm winning, I'm-" The idea was perfect. He woudl go, he would wrestle, and he would make them all fools. It was going down, and it was going down in flames. Eugene Marder did not want to be there to miss any of it. He had changed.
