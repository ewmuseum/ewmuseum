Quest - Part 2

4 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
An ice flow. They were on an ice flow. Vic felt the ground move underneath him without taking a step. The air wasn’t warming up, yet when he took a breath there wasn’t a cloud that usually accompanied frigid temperature. Alex didn’t seem to be bothered by the cold. He just intently looked ahead as if he could see through the mist that still surrounded them. What are we supposed to be hunting? “Beats me.” Vic caught Alex rolling his head from side to side, much like he himself did when he was tense. “But I think we’re close.” Vic started to walk forward and Alex remained a step behind him. Does your mother know you’re with me? Alex looked at his father. “I don’t think she’s been too thrilled about me hanging around with you.” And you? Any regrets? “I’m a Creed. It doesn’t matter if I’m with you or not. I’m still your son.” His grin was eerily familiar. “You’re in my blood.” My condolences. Alex shrugged. “It hasn’t all been wine and roses. That doesn’t mean I’m giving up. From what I hear, Creeds don’t quit.” Vic felt the cold air burn his lungs as he took a deep breath. I’m sorry you had to go through all of that. I imagine life must’ve been pretty tough for you. I tend to make things difficult for everyone close to me. The ground came to a sudden halt with a crunch. Vic and Alex staggered forward and fell into the snow. The world seemed to tilt and tremble underneath. Vic turned to the side and watched the ice flow split between himself and Alex. His hands were cold and numb which made getting his balance difficult. The flow Alex was now on started to quickly disappear into the mist. “Get him Pops!” Alex shouted. “I’ll be fAAAAAAHHH –“ The silence was louder than the scream. Alex! ALEX! Vic scrambled on his hands and knees and found himself on solid ground. He stood up and squinted into the cloudy mass. There was no sound. No movement. Nothing but the cold. He was alone again. "Vic?" Kodiak Vic Creed looked up from his glass and craned his head to the voice addressing him. To his surprise, Lindsay Troy plopped herself down on the stood beside him and put her purse next to her on another empty stool. "What are you doing here?" "Taking a plane." He slid his glass toward the bartender. "Hit me." Troy noticed the concerned expression on the bartender’s face as he reluctantly turned to grasp the bottle of Wild Turkey once more to top Vic's glass off. "You’ve been here awhile, haven't you?" "Probably." He sipped his drink and set it back down on the bar. "I heard what Dudley did to you. It's hypocritical bullshit. Between what he’s pulling and what Disney is doing to the fWo..." She folded her hands on the bar. Vic nodded and sipped his drink again. Lindsay could smell the alcohol wafting from the glass. She pointed to a bottle of Sambuca and held up one finger. The bartender poured her a shot and after she downed it, she somberly resumed the conversation. "I’m sorry to hear about Slade." Vic paused his glass in mid-lift. "Thanks. He was a good man." Troy could see in Vic’s face that things were far from okay with the Main Man. "You’re not so bad yourself if you'd give yourself some credit, big guy." Troy gave Creed a wry grin. "You’ve got a great kid in Alex, and you’ve got a great career going." Ice clinked against the glass as he drank again. He set the glass down and turned to the Queen of the Ring. He gave her a long stare. "He’s doing okay?" Vic asked slowly. "I haven’t seen him, but from what Brent and Tiff have told me he’s doing very well. Dropping buckets of sweat each day, but learning quickly." Creed wrinkled his brow. "What is this?" "What’s what?" "What are you doing here? Why are you talking to me?" Lindsay blinked once, reached into her wallet, and pulled out two Ben Franklins. "One, your cell phone is off and Alex has been trying to reach you. So he called me and asked me to track you down. Now I can report that you're in one piece, albeit three sheets to the wind." She then stood up, and slid the bills to the bartender. "Two, for my own peace of mind, I just wanted to say that we’re not so different, you and I. I might understand you better than you think, if you ever--on a whim--decided that you wanted someone to talk to. I won't think you any less of a man." She swiftly slung her purse across her shoulder and grasped the handle of her rolling suitcase. "But do yourself a favor, Vic, and get yourself cleaned up and out of the bottle once you leave the terminal. This pity thing really doesn’t suit you." She gave the bartender a nod, "And don't let him pay, either," before patting Vic once on the back and sauntering off through the terminal to her departure gate. Vic didn’t even watch Troy leave. He turned his attention back to his glass. "I’ve got another hour," he said aloud and slid the glass across the bar. "Top it up."
