Getaway - Man in the Mirror

22 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The sky was dotted with glowing stars, and the roads were surprisingly quiet in the early evening. Several storefronts flipped their signs from open to closed as Samuel Braun found himself pacing down the sidewalk, whistling a tune beneath his chilled breath with a couple small shopping bags in his grip. His calves ached with the walking from store to store, so he stopped at a storefront window and observed at the dimly lit insides, as was his nature. While his compatriots responded to new things with their mouths and/or fists, Brawn was more steady, more careful, in his reactions. His nature was also his curse. His preference of observing over "making a scene" didn't help him make new friends or fans. But quiet determination allowed the FWO newcomer to seemingly fall from the sky and notch his belt with victories over the likes of the Flying Frenchie and Eli Flair. Without fanfare. Without blogs set ablaze with hyperbolic "Greatest Wrestler in the World" nonsense. He was a wrestlers wrestler, and that was enough for now. Brawn stood firm and looked at his reflection in the bakery window. He smile revealed his pride. The evidence of his hard work stood before him. It seemed like he was popping pimples just last week. Now his face had settled in a sharp, handsome structure and his body had ballooned at least twice its size. The man in the reflection wasn't easy for the crowd to adopt at first. No tattoos. No face paint. Just the kid that kept getting three counts. That was what got attention, and out of the complex apparatus tumbled the newest face of FWO. Brawn looked around and watched an under-lit fountain shoot jets of water into the sky. A young man tipped his hat as he rode past on a bicycle. The buildings glowed greasy like flickers of candle-light. He reached into his pocket to produce his map, but his hand came out empty. He quickly searched his front pockets and realized he did not have a map at all. His throat tightened and the notion of being lost in Paris rattled him. He looked back into the window and saw the wrinkles that dug into his forehead. A church bell rang out an ethereal melody in the background, and his shoulders dropped and his eyebrows relaxed as he saw the ornate pastries lining the Baker's shelves. For once, the interminable Brawn was lost. And that was fine.
