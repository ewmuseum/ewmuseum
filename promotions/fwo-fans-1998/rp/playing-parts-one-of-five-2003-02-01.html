Playing Parts - One of Five

1 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The renaissance Chicago diner was packed with scurrying dish washers and waiters filling the hungry needs of each and every customer awaiting a plate of food or a cup of coffee fresh from this morning's pot. On the outside, the diner was built out of an old, abandonned housing project splashed with faded colors, paint chips and futile memories from previous decades as flickering red neon strobes in custom typeface spelled out the infamous South Side Chicago's friendly meeting place and dining area. The small cafe was dubbed "Eddie's" after it's founder, an old african-american man in his late 60's, always friendly and courteous with every man, woman or child that crossed his path. He had built the all-purpose cafe, coffee shop, one-stop diner restaurant from a bankrupt apartment complex a couple days before the planned destruction of the location. Eddie, a kind-hearted man and righteous being, used the dozen apartments above his business venture to shelter the needy who slept on park benches, dumpsters and tipis built out of cardboard boxes and rugs thrown out by careless people onto alleyways and back streets. But that was no more, after Eddie passed away local business men whisked away the homeless from their new homes and turned Eddie's into, strictly, a place of patronage. Inside the diner, old, brown leather benches, classic steel stools and tables built out of pure, solid mahogany wood covered with marble sheets were set up to preserve the retro feel Eddie's always had. The local employees were fired and replaced by new, work oriented waiters, cooks and bus boys turning the community hang out spot where meaningful discussions of politics, sports, life and everything in between were held into no more than a profit ground for the new owners. At one of the tables sat a trio of men, all shaped into athletes and wearing their morning jogging suits. One of them, sitting closest to the counter looked over his shoulder to the far side of a wall and examined a clock. It read seven-thirty as sunshine reigned sovereign over Chicago. Are you sure I am getting my big shot, Randy? The man with the youngest complexion and most athletic build spoke up. His voice rang with rawness and a hint of cinnamon flavor, fresh from the bite of his glazed cinnamon roll. It wasn't the best thing for his health but a couple sweets here and there wouldn't do much wrong. The man facing him took a sip of his black coffee and swallowed hard underestimating the capheine drink's heat. "Listen, I talked with the fWo trainers and Silver Fox, they're ready for you." The young man nodded, pleased with the answer. Good, good. He slumped back into the bench and let out a sigh of relief. Both of his hands motionned towards the cup of coffee sitting infront of him, he carefully palmed both hands around the ceramic cup and slowly brought it up to his lips. He tilted it forward and gorged down three gulps of the steaming coffee then sat the cup back down on it's coaster before letting out a soft "Ahh" to announce his delight with the taste. As others drank their coffee or ate their meals, he sat and gazed around at the people, judging them silently. He drew his head to the side and observed a man clad in a navy blue business suit. The man appeared to be in his mid-40's and probably held a part of the stereotypical "american dream" ; a big house, beautiful wife, 2.5 kids, 2 cars and a 9 to 5 desk job in some glass building in the city, but the young man didn't want to draw any conclusions. He watched the man's every move, anticipating the next, something that was part of becoming a good wrestler. The specimen closely examined was paying for his quick breakfast before heading to the office and making small talk with the waitress. As he started to pull away from the counter and towards the exit, a shiny piece fell from his jacket's pocket. Unaware, the man continued to exit the diner when suddenly our young hero jolted out of his seat and scooped it off the floor before jogging outside the shop. His two breakfast and jogging partners simultaneously turned their regards to the diner's door. They saw through the tall and wide, rectangular window their buddy kindly stop the man he had been watching and hand him the piece that fell to the tiled floor. The rushing man quickly responded with a "Thank You" and a smile before walking off. Satisfied with his work, the youngster came back into the diner and reunited with his two companions. Curious, the man sitting next to him politely asked. "What was that all about?" Enthusiastic, the young man responded. He dropped a nickel, so I gave it back to him. Both men accompanying him were speechless but with a big smile on his face, he changed the subject. So, about the character I'll be playing...
