Old Friends

22 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Julie didn’t say a word as she stepped into the elevator. Vic had been ill-tempered for the past week and she didn’t want to risk setting him off. He hadn’t said a word since they boarded the plane earlier this morning. Twelve hours of silence, twelve hours of tension. She couldn’t wait to get to the hotel room and go to bed. The elevator began to climb before coming to a quick halt at the sixth floor. Creed frowned as the doors slid open. He wanted to get back to the room as well, but sleep was furthest from his mind. After seeing the picture of Darryl Vishanti standing with his daughter, Vic took every chance he could to call his little girl and make sure she was okay. The doors opened. Four large men in suits stood in the hallway. A smaller man stood in the center of them. “Been a long time Vic.” Julie turned to Creed. She wanted to ask if he knew who these people were, but as soon as she saw the look on his face she realized she didn’t need to ask. Vic’s body tensed. Jaw clenched. Veins bulged in his neck. The smaller man saw it as well. “You wouldn’t make it out of the elevator before my boys cut you down. So stow the attitude and come out nice and slow.” Vic stepped into the hallway. “Vishanti if you go near my girl again, I’ll bury you.” Darryl turned away from him. “Leave the bags behind. Your woman can take them to your room. Our business won’t take long.” So as Julie escorted the luggage, Vic escorted Vishanti down the hallway. “What business are you talking about?” asked Creed as he eyed up the four bodyguards. Each one of them was packing heat. Probably wearing a Kevlar vest too. These guys were pros, a step up from the street thugs that Vishanti used to employ before Vic sent him to jail. Another man was waiting beside the door to a suite. “I heard you were running with the big dogs now Darryl. Can’t afford the penthouse yet? Man you’re still small-time.” The lights were dim as the entourage walked into the room. “I’m only using this room for business. That’s why you’re here.” Creed slowly looked around the room and evaluated his situation. Three more bodyguards in here, seven total. All carrying concealed weapons. Evaluation: He was fucked. Darryl opened a small leather briefcase. “Have a seat Vic.” Creed folded his arms in front of him. “I’ll stand.” “Suit yourself.” Darryl examined the contents of his briefcase and smiled. “I heard you managed to kick the TAR habit after my untimely vacation. That’s unfortunate. You were great for business.” “You used to bet on my matches,” said Creed. “I don’t know if they had TV’s where you were locked up, but I was still winning matches after I cleaned up. You were probably still running your business in the pen. Only there you were betting with cigarettes and reach-arounds.” “Oh Vic, how I missed that sense of humour.” Darryl nodded to the nearest bodyguard who walked out of the room. “No Vic, the gambling was fine. But the real money was when I started using you as a marketing tool for selling TAR. I had plenty of clients who couldn’t wait to try it once they heard that Kodiak Vic Creed was on board and using.” The bodyguard returned with company. The hulking Guido stepped into the room and immediately lunged at Creed. “You broke my brain!” he said in an angry slur. Two bodyguards held him back. Vic frowned. “You’re lucky I didn’t break your neck.” “As you see Vic, you hurt my brother quite badly. I demand satisfaction.” “He tried to cripple my sister.” Creed glared at Vishanti. “You want satisfaction? Get a hooker.” “Very funny. No, you managed to put quite a cramp in my finances. But all that’s going to change.” Darryl lifted a small vial from the briefcase. “Thanks to this.” “And just what the Hell is that?” “This? Why this is an old friend of yours. Actually it’s more like the big brother of an old friend of yours. My chemists have managed to improve on TAR’s original formula by adding an extra synthetic testosterone chain to the molecular mix. This is T2.” He tossed the vial to Creed, who caught it gingerly. “You two are going to become great friends.” Vic stared at the small glass vial. “You can take this back and shove it up your ass. I’ve got enough to worry about.” “Wrong Vic. This is all you have to worry about, because this is all you have control of. See, I’ve got a long list of clients who are looking to upgrade from TAR. What athlete wouldn’t want a powerful, untraceable steroid? You’re the perfect poster boy for T2. With the fWo under a ‘drug watch’, the fact that you’re using will help show the other customers that it is untraceable. Since the fWo can’t operate in the United States, you’re going to help open up the international markets.” “What part of ‘take this back and shove it up your ass’ are you unclear about? I’m not taking this.” Darryl rested both hands on the table and leaned forward. “I’m not asking you Creed. I’m telling you. Either you take it, or Sarah’s going to be taking something a little more recreational. She’ll need it for when I put her out on the street to work for me, all because her Daddy wouldn’t.” Vic roared and lunged forward. Two bodyguards quickly tackled him to the ground. He struggled to get out from under the mass of humanity on top of him, but the butt of a handgun smacked him in the side of the head hard enough to make him see stars. Stunned, he didn’t have time to resist as the two men picked Creed up and restrained him. A third man pressed the cold steel of the gun barrel against Creed’s temple. Darryl looked disappointed. “Vic Vic Vic. There’s no sense in fighting me. You’re just some professional wrestler. You’re a very little fish in my ocean, and I’m the Great White Shark. You do this for me, and I’ll forget that Sarah even exists. I’m a businessman, Vic. Time for you to do some smart business.” The stars faded. Creed‘s eyes came back into focus. “How do I know you’ll leave her alone?” “You don’t. That’s why I don’t just kill you. I want to leave you hanging, wondering. I want this to torture you.” What could he do? His mind was racing, trying to find a way out of this mess. It was clear that he couldn‘t fight his way out. Violence wouldn‘t solve this problem. There was only one thing he could do for the moment. Play ball. “How do I take it? Orally? Injection?” “Right on both counts.” Darryl pulled a small brown package out of the briefcase and handed it to the nearest bodyguard, who then handed it to Creed. “It’s very potent, but it breaks down in the atmosphere. It has to be absorbed quickly into the body, so you have to inject it into the soft tissue under your tongue.” Vic looked uncomfortable at the thought of jabbing a needle under his tongue, which brought the hint of a smile to Darryl’s face. “You seem like a handy guy. I’m sure you can get your own syringes.” The bodyguards escorted Vic to the door. Vishanti followed behind them. “Now remember Vic, I’ll be checking in on you to make sure you’re taking it. If you don’t take it, I’ll know. That will upset me Vic, and if I get upset then Guido is going to pay a visit to your sister. He’ll finish what he started with her, before working over your little girl. It won’t be quick either. He’ll take his time.” The men shoved Creed into the hallway and closed the door behind him. Vic spun around, locking glares with the bodyguard standing in front of the door. A voice in Creed’s head told him that he had to pick his battles, and this wasn’t one he could win. Not today. With the brown paper package in his hand, a defeated Kodiak Vic Creed pushed open the door marked ‘Exit‘ and disappeared into the stairwell.
