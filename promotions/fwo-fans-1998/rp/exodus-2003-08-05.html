Exodus

5 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"I'm trying hard to forget that cold October day when love challenged freedom to a fist fight. Freedom looked victorious, but no one was prepared for what would happen on that baseball diamond when love reached beneath her plaid jumper, pulled out a switch blade and drove it...directly through the heart of St. Angelis. Any notion of self government was left bleeding on the pitcher's mound." - Alexisonfire, "A Dagger Through The Heart Of St. Angelis" "I should have proven myself by now." Amy was in an unusual position. Her hair was tied back into a loose, scruffy ponytail with strands falling limp over her cheekbones - Exposing a tattoo on the back of her neck. Nothing was being hidden by her boyish black tanktop or low riding jeans, but nothing had to be hidden, in her eyes. She was around her family and, according to what she would have wished in a perfect world, they'd eventually get used to their daughter as she was or not at all. Though the somewhat minor, but incredibly visible tattoos, lip and eyebrow ring were as bold a statement as the bright red hair she'd been born with, something ten times as natural contrasted Amy herself just as boldly, despite being a part of her: The child at her side, enthralled with a plastic book that matched his reading abilities at four years old and the man just barely in sight, sitting in the connected living room - enthralled himself in the childhood stories of Amy he was getting from her ceaselessly embarassing younger brother. But partially in thanks to the contrasting images in her, the redhead was once again being scrutinized for yet another decision in her life - This one having been pretty well thought-out, or so she'd assumed. Not well enough for her parents, though. Her family so far had congratulated Amy and Brandon on their engagement, even her notoriously overprotective brothers loved Roche...but, as usual, her parents offered - whether or not the couple was willing - an entirely different, much more negative view. Oddly enough, though, her mother's doubts were minimal...she'd watched the two and almost predicted this coming - Though in her version, Amy would be bearing her first official granddaughter. The patriarch of the family, however, had a much different and harder to break standpoint. He loved his daughter, but had always thought her too reckless and 'stupid' to be trusted. In many minds, he didn't understand how much she did tend to think on things. But he wasn't a superstitious man - bad luck was to blame for none of his daughter's mistakes...they were all her fault, no other's - A result of her stupidity, no less. It was a skewered perspective, but one he was adamant about. Amy, however, was continuing her argument... "The one thing I know about myself, regardless of what you or anybody else might think, is that I don't set out and hurt people. In all of my 'craziness', wild tendancies, whatever - I've never done a bad thing, not to anybody. I've been self-destructive and just plain retarded but I don't hurt people badly, and certainly not deliberately." She sighed, "I never have and, no matter what you might think of me, never ever will." Her father would hear none of it. "That's all well and good, but you're airheaded when it comes to relationships. You have no idea what to do in most situations." "I don't, do I?" "Well what would you do if you walked into the doctor's office next week and found out you were pregnant or something?" "I'd take leave from work, have a child and marry Brandon in the process." This was an argument he couldn't win. Even if Amy didn't know what was right for her, it was obvious the man she'd come to love over nearly a year honestly did. Nonetheless, a father wasn't to give up his daughter without a fight. "Look at Ste..." Forrester paused, not wanting to call the boy's name for fear of him suddenly perking to the private conversation turned spat. "Look at the child you have now for Christ's sake, Amy! What do you think this is going to do to him?" "Give him a proper family. It's what he needs. It's what I need. This is my life," Amy's stare was glaring, defensive...grown up. "I'm twenty one years old - that might not be much, but I've outgrown the partying phase, the childish phase, that whole 'trying to find my lot in life' thing. I've settled down, in case you hadn't noticed, and taken my son as my own. You threw a fit a few months ago when I moved in with BL and took him from your care into ours, but it was time and it's gone wonderfully so far. I've explained things as they are and he's understood for the most part. When he's misguided, I show him right...I know what right is now, and it's not too damned far from where I am with Brandon, his boys and my own." "And you," She continued, "Need to stop grasping at straws to keep control over me." Without another word uttered by Amy or her stumbling father after the rather reeling blow, she calmly walked off, out into the yard with Stephen before a war erupted. Unintentionally, though, it left Brandon inside to bear the brunt of the battle, should things slide his way... And Redemption follows
