And you

22 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Why do I do the things I do Why do I say the things I say Why am I this way Why do I run away from life Into all the wrongs and rights To you "Cally!" There's only one reason why Rosalyn Callasantos heard her name called from down the street - her CD player was between songs. Cally pulled the headphones from her ears as she heard the familiar scrape of a skateboard on patchy sidewalk. "You're gonna freeze to death, Randy," she said, as Randy Knox pulled up next to her on his board. He was wearing a black backwards baseball cap, black hooded sweatshirt, zipped, and black jeans. Compared to him, Cally was buried under a mountain. Winter coat, knit hat, heavy sweater, turtleneck, and unseen fuzzy socks. "Not a'tall," replied Knox, "It's not cold yet." "You're insane." "Maybe." They stood around for a moment before Knox suddenly whipped his backpack around and pulled out a box. "Before I forget... Merry Christmas and Happy Birthday, rolled into one." Cally smiled. "Thank you!" She grabbed the box and unwrapped it quickly but carefully. "Hope you like it," said Knox. Cally pulled out a pair of suede gloves, wrapped around an envelope. "I know you've been wearing those gloves without thumbs for the past two months," explained Knox, "and I did want to get you something else, too." She smiled as she opened the envelope, at which point her jaw dropped. "Patti?" "Yep." "How?" "Ivy knew someone who knew someone or something... it's on the 30th at the Bowery, I thought we could go see Patti on Tuesday, and then of course, there's Countdown on Wednesday, so you'll be sick'a me by then." She gave him a hug. "Never. Thank you, Randy!" Knox held her bag as she pulled the gloves on. "Want to walk me home?" "I was hoping you'd ask." And you better think before you act Better speak before you know What you're getting yourself into And you better see between the lines Better read into my mind Without looking in my eyes And you Knox picked up his board and Cally's bag, and walked slowly alongside her. She limped slightly from a nasty bruise on her leg, the by-product of the last official class at Coop's for 2003. As Cally shivered, Knox put his arm around her and tried to warm her a bit. "Are you nervous about next week?" she asked, "I know Lou is." "Naaah," replied Knox, "I'm okay. Ivy told me what I need to do, what I can expect. It's no biggie." "I'd be scared," said Cally. "Why? It's seventeen minutes against Luis. Same thing I did yesterday in the gym, same thing I've done a dozen times in the past six months." She giggled. "Remember last year when we did Fiddler on the Roof at school?" "Yeah. I worked on the set, you were Tzeitel. Why?" "We did a good show, right? For a highschool play, at least, right?" "Course." "If that was on Broadway, same sets, same people acting... damn, I'd be frozen." Knox squeezed her fingers. "You're frozen right now." She was glad it was dark - she was blushing. "But still, it's pretty much the same people going to Coop's that went to see the highschool play... this is the Garden, on New Year's Eve." Knox laughed. "Way to build my confidence, Cally." "Oh!" she exclaimed, "I'm sorry! I didn't mean--" "No," he interrupted, "it's okay." In a dream I hear the phone And I remember someone's home Waiting for you Don't remember what I say Don't know why I feel this way Do you "What's your plan for Christmas?" asked Knox, changing the subject. "Mom wants to have dinner at home with my aunt and cousins... my father and his wife invited me out but Mom told me I couldn't go." "Did you want to?" "Yeah," said Cally, "but you know how my mom gets." "I know," he replied, "same as my parents. Stubborn to the end." She smiled at him. "But... do you want to come over?" He looked back at her. "I'll try. I know my parents wanted to go to my grandparents' in Yonkers but I'm hopin' to get out of it... though it's probably fruitless. That's why I wanted to give you your present tonight, dunno if I'll be here Thursday to give it to you on your actual day." The reality of the moment started to sink in. This was conceivably the last time the two would see each other before December 30th. And both knew that it was a day neither of them would truly be able to relax during, not even when they saw Patti Smith live in concert. And it's not like they're a couple, but they both know how the other feels. Fear and apprehension are terrible things. And you better think before you act Better speak before you know What you're getting yourself into And you better see between the lines Better read into my mind Without looking in my eyes And you "I'll be around this week, I think," said Cally, "got a lot of work to do before midterms but I need to hit the gym, too... the holidays are always torture on my tummy." "Pfah," replied Knox, "That's such bull. But you should take it easy, I saw how you landed wrong when you were workin' with George. And everyone knows you never let your ankle heal right." "I'm fine." "Why are you doin' this, anyways?" asked Knox, "You've managed to hurt yourself in every match you've had except for the two that we had, and that's probably because I knew what your problems were and worked around 'em. What're you tryin' to prove?" Cally picked up her pace. "Dunno what you mean." "Yes, you do." He spun her around. "Someday," she said, "but not today." And you better think before you act Better speak before you know What you're getting yourself into And you better see between the lines Better read into my mind Without looking in my eyes Cally kissed him on the mouth, apprehensively. "Someday." As she turned around and headed down the street to her building, Knox stood there, dumbfounded. He dropped his skateboard on the sidewalk and put a foot on it, watching his friend, his horror movie buddy, who just apparently threw down the gauntlet, who apparently was intent on letting him know that things were going to change. In eight days, things would change on one level - Knox would become possibly the youngest wrestler to ever perform on an fWo show. In seven days, he and his best friend would be at Patti Smith, enjoying the music. And she kissed him. Everything had changed, and nothing had changed. But nothing could remain as it was. 'This isn't the end,' Eli Flair had told him when he left the business to be with his wife and daughter, 'this is the beginning.' "So is this, old man," said Knox to the empty air. He turned around and skated down the road towards home. And you... - Lennon
