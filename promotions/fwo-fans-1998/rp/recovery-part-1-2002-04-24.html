Recovery - Part 1

24 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
* * * I'm dead. With the searing pain down his left side subsided, Vic looked ahead and saw the white light. "Guess that's the light I've heard so much about," he thought to himself. "I would have expected hellfire and brimstone. Must've been a saint in a former life." Slowly the fog parted, and Kodiak Vic Creed realized he was staring into a doctor's pen light. "Welcome back, Mister Creed," said the doctor. "You put quite a scare into us." "Try being on the other end," muttered a relieved Creed. He tried to lift his head, trembling with the effort. A nurse lifted his head for him, slipping a pillow underneath and setting his head back down on it. Vic shot her a frustrated look. "Great. So what do I do if I have to piss? You gonna hold that for me too, sweetheart?" "We have that taken care of," the nurse told him as she lifted the blanket. Creed looked down. "Sweet Jesus. You people are sick. If I had the strength, I'd -" "But you don't," said Mitch Wilson as he walked into the room. "So save what little you do have." He walked past the nurse as she left the room and stood at the bedside of his old friend. "You know Vic, you really look terrible." "Thanks. I was going for that 'run over by a freight train' look. Guess I got pretty close." Shaking from the effort, Vic lifted his arm and shook Mitch's hand. "It's good to see you. Did Slade come with you?" "You know how he feels about hospitals," Mitch said as he unrolled the magazine in his hand. "Take a gander at this." He tossed the magazine on Vic's lap. Vic reached with both hands, slowly closing them around the magazine and opening it to the bookmarked page. The bill of sale fell out as Vic began reading the small news article. "You can pay me for the magazine later," Mitch said. "Cheap bastard," grumbled Creed as he continued to read. "Who the Hell is this guy with Tempest? I know the girl likes animals, but dating outside her species?" "Not that article," sighed Mitch. "The next one." Vic read for a minute. "I'm dead. This thing says I'm dead." He set the magazine down. "Mitch, why does this magazine say I'm dead?" "I guess people saw you rushed to the hospital, heard about your massive heart attack -" "Which I recovered from, obviously" noted Creed. "- and all of the sudden, there's a Victor Creid being buried in Alaska." Mitch raised an eyebrow. "I think there's grounds for the assumption." Vic pulled himself up to a sitting position. "Get me a phone. I've got to set these people straight." Looking at the Alaskan Juggernaut, Mitch could already see that his illness had taken it's toll on his body. He had lost much of his size, and what remained was soft. Mitch shook his head. "No. You're going to stay dead until you earn the right to live." Creed opened his mouth to protest. "Don't say a word, Vic. It won't matter. Slade has already made the arrangements." Mitch opened the closet door. "You're coming back with me. You'll stay with Dad until you're mobile. Then, you'll start your training." "No problem," Creed said. "I just need to swing by my place to pick up a few things." "If you're talking about steroids, forget it." Mitch tossed Vic's clothes on the bed. "You're cleaning up. Drugs almost killed you this time." Mitch shot a cold look. "Dad's not pleased. Now get dressed. I'll get your nurse and a wheelchair." Vic grabbed his pants and slowly slid his leg out of bed. For the first time that he could remember, he felt weak and helpless. To top it off, the man who was like a father to his was apparently "not pleased" at him. "Talk about the living envying the dead," grumbled Creed as he struggled to free himself from his blankets. Things were going to get worse before they got better. He just knew it.
