Just Another Night

22 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Okay.... think. If you were Poison Ivy.... which you are.... what would you write a column about?" Ivy has been staring at a blank page on her computer screen for nearly an hour now. She took time off to make herself a drink - Gin and Tonic - and watch a bit of "The Stand" on television, but mostly it's been staring at a blank page. Eli's Hollywood condo is nice.... but it's not home. And Ivy misses the sounds of New York City. "Damn it all to hell." The blank page mocked her. Ivy got up and turned the television off. She hobbled toward the kitchen, her knee wrapped in an ACE bandage. On her way she turned on the CD player - "No One" by COLD came on. It's been an interesting few days for Ivy Lillian McGinnis. Her boyfriend, 'Triple X' Sean Stevens, has been spending a good deal of his time lately with an old friend. "I'll explain everything when I get back to California," he said. But she missed him terribly. Eli Flair has spent the past few nights with his girlfriend Angel. Her doctor called her at her drummer's apartment on Wednesday night - worried about complications with her pregnancy. None of them remembered, but when she became pregnant, Angel was rollin' on ecstasy. And she smoked up at least once before she knew. Hey, rock & roll, right? She was a twentysomething woman making a living on her own - nobody had the right to tell her what to do. But it doesn't make things any easier. Ivy pulled the icepack from the freezer. She closed the door to see a picture stuck to the front with a magnet - Ivy and her mother. Her mother, who died of a terminal brain tumor earlier in the year. "What would you do now, Mom?" asked Ivy, taking the picture down, "Where would you take your life from here?" She hobbled back to Eli's den, the picture and the icepack in one hand, and an ice cold beer in the other. Essentials of life. She sat down at Eli's computer, and continued to stare back at the blank page. The icepack found a home on her sore knee in less than six seconds, but the page remained blank. It's nothing personal.... The words made her shiver. Not because Ruben Ross said them.... not completely, at least. No, she remembered the last time someone said that to her. Remember when I said it was nothing personal? I lied. "Mistake after mistake," she mused to nobody in particular, "The past three years have been full of 'em." Inspiration? Maybe? "In this business, everyone wants to be real. Everyone wants to "shoot" without knowing what the word means. Everyone wants to "shoot" without knowing what the ACTION means." She reread the words she just typed. Not the greatest in the world, but certainly a good opening. It's nothing personal, just business. Everything in this business is personal, even the fake stuff. Even the stuff you plan out and agree on. Everything. "Talk to me, Mom," whispered Ivy, "You always knew how to get me back on track." The picture remained silent. "Between this shit with Tempest and Stormwindz, between Sean and Lucky, between Winters and Davis, between Eli and Ruben.... I don't know what to do anymore." She hit a button on the remote control and the CD player skipped to a new disc and song - "Frenetic Amnesic" by cKy. What, does Eli have something against Korn these days? Or The Doors? Or something that doesn't make Ivy reflect on her life's decisions? "Life was a lot easier back in the day when it was pretending to fight with Deac over the definition of a hero," said Ivy out loud, "or against Superman and his politics in Lecompte's. Or against Smooth and his crazy ideas. Eli wants this to be the Year of Flair.... well it's tough." She spun around in her chair and readjusted the icepack on her knee. "What would you do, Mom?" The picture of Ivy and Lily McGinnis did not answer. "Right," she replied, "That's for me to figure out." Ivy took a deep breath as she spun around one more time in the chair and faced the computer screen. "No excuses, Mom," she said with conviction, "and no backsteps. Just fuckin' do it." She chuckled. "Sorry 'bout the language." Ivy McGinnis.... Poison Ivy, the Manager of Champions.... began to write.
