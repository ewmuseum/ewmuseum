Good Times, Bad Times, 1 of 2

13 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
This is the first of two parts to the story, called Good Times. And no, there's nobody yelling out Dy-no-mite! in this story. I promise. Just call me Norm. I have my favorite stool at TC's and when I show up, I don't want anyone else sitting there. But it's different tonight. Pint of Brooklyn, I said to Tommy, the bartender. "Same," said Lou. Tommy? Rosie isn't working. She has the night off and me and her and Lou and Sally are going out. We're all pretty low rent in our personal lives: for the most part we like to just hang at the bar, or we stay in and do something. It's as much practical as it is personality. I've recently had such little time at home that I'd rather not be out for all hours of the night, and the other three are on pretty limited budgets. But it's not every day one of us signs a contract that effectively doubles my take home, and I wanted to celebrate. First, it's a nice dinner at Isabella's downtown a ways, then wherever the night takes us. Don Hill's or something in the meat packing district. "Tab," said Tommy, putting a pint in front of each of us. Yeah, I told him, I'll settle up tomorrow before I head out. "Man," said Lou, "this is awesome." What is, I asked. "You, ya fool!" said Lou, "Gettin' this fat money deal with the FWO. I knew you were gonna make it there someday." Really, I said. What made you think that, especially considering they just spent three years closed. "Since that night you and me opened Countdown," said Lou, "what was it, six years ago?" 2003, I said, December. So yeah, five and a half or so. "You had the goods, kid," said Lou, "Miss Ivy saw it, the brass saw it, we all knew you were the next--" Don't say it, please. "--big--" Dude. Seriously. "--thang." I really hate that phrase. What's it mean? Why can't we all just come in and do what we do, and go home? "Man, don't do that," said Lou, "that fake modesty crap. You know you're the stuff. You knew back when we were all workin' in this place with Coop, you were head and shoulders better than any of us could ever even dream of bein'." Felt bad, man, I said. Didn't want to discourage anyone. "I know," said Lou, "That's the main reason why we all deferred to you like you were the man, because you knew how to be a leader and make the rest of us feel like we could do anything." You had the goods, man, I said, why don't you call Miss Ivy and see if she's got a spot? We could tag it up like we did back in the day. "Please," said Lou, "I'm good in gyms and backyards, but if I ever got a shot at the show, I'd jerk the curtain on my best day, and if you try t'argue the point I'll slam you on this table and drop the hammer." Okay, I said, trying not to laugh. You win. Somewhere in the back, someone flipped on the jukebox. Pink, by Aerosmith, filled the bar, and we listened in silence for a minute or so. Then, someone whistled. I turned toward the door, and understood why. "Wow," said someone at one of the tables, "Rosie, you're hot!" Yes, yes she is. But I don't think it's possible to fully articulate. I'll try, though. Rose is here four to five nights a week. She knows practically everyone in the bar by first name and knows exactly what they drink every time they come in. They know her and they like her, and they do tend to look after her as a twenty one year old girl bartending in what is not exactly the safest neighborhood in the Bronx. More than that, they know her as a somewhat tomboyish girl who spends ninety percent of her day in tank tops, T-shirts, jeans and cutoff shorts, opening bottles of beer with her hands and trading dirty jokes with the regulars. They never saw her like this. Heck, I've never seen her like this. Rose stood in the doorway in front of Sally, dressed in a black and red slip dress, slightly blowing in the breeze. Her hair, usually up in a ponytail, was cascading around her face, the red streaks accentuating the natural brown. She even painted her toenails, and was showing them off with those shoes that aren't shoes but are really just a bunch of leather straps holding the sole onto her foot. Man, she was gorgeous. "Tommy," said Rose, "Margarita, Patrón." She sat down next to me, and I leaned over and kissed her on the cheek. You look incredible, I said, you're amazing." Rosie smiled, which was all I was going for. "Make it two," said Sally. She looked great as she always did in a skirt - and - sweater combination, but I honestly don't remember any details about her. Rosie had my attention all locked up tonight. To our gorgeous dates, I said to Lou, and raised my glass. "Naaah," said Lou, "To you, RK, for buying all our drinks tonight." "Cheers," said Sally. "And to Mr. Cooper for bringing us all together," added Rose. Hear hear, I said, and we all drank. "Lookin' good, Cally," said Branson, one of the regulars. Rosie still smiled, but looked back toward me. She really wasn't used to this kind of attention. Don't blame him, I told her, you're stunning and guys are going to comment on it. "That's why I don't do this very often," said Rose, "I prefer it when you're the only one commenting." I smirked, and leaned in and kissed her on the lips. "C'mon," said Lou, "we don't wanna see that." I'm buying your drinks tonight, I reminded him. "As you were," said Lou. So, I said, are we just about ready? "Soon as we're done with this round," said Sally, "I think we'll be good to go." Good, I said. I looked around, from my girl to my best friend, to his girl. These really are the best of times.
