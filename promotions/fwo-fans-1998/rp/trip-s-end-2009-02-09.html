Trip's End

9 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“…room at the end of the hall. No visitor’s after one, and no hotplates. Cash in advance.” William Magnum Decker barely comprehended the words coming out of the motel manager’s mouth. After days and nights of nearly non-stop driving Decker had finally made it to Seattle. The only thing he wanted now was an uninterrupted bottle of strong cheap alcohol and a bed to pass out in. And the only thing between him and his immediate (if somewhat sad) dream was the droning little toad of a man behind his bulletproof glass. Decker nodded and passed a couple of wrinkled bills through the ridiculously small opening, and a key was passed back. No fancy keycards here; this was durable, dingy steel – tough and solid, kinda like Decker himself. He smiled as he thought this, picked up his shabby suitcase and shuffled on down the hall in search of his grimy little piece of this ghetto Xanadu… Half an hour and half a bottle later, Decker sat on his rough, rented bed and tried to relax. 30 minutes ago he was dead on his feet, but the alcohol seemed to revive him rather than deliver the knockout blow he’d been expecting. The room itself held no fascination; he’d wasted plenty of his life in equally seedy, grungy flophouses. The dark sounds outside his dirty window were the same sounds he’d heard in the worst parts of a plethora of big cities across the country. His consciousness should have been overwhelmed by the lethal combination of alcohol and ennui but here he was, awake and aware in the bowels of the Emerald City. Maybe it was that fact that he’d finally made it here and was one step closer to his target. He knew this ‘Fairview Reed’ character would be at Key Arena on Wednesday night and he’d arrived with enough time to scope out the area – check out the layout and security of the arena and figure out where and when he’d make contact with Reed. He’d been promised a big payout for this case. And now, with this goose-chase across the country to find this guy he was going to have to add gas, lodgings, and other expenses to the bill. And then, finally, the alcohol kicked in and he lay back in bed. Big money on this one... The bottle rolled out of his fingers and William Magnum Decker smiled in his sleep.
