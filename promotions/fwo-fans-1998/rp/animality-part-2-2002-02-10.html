Animality - Part 2

10 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It was a plain wooden chair, the seat of the chair sitting maybe two feet from the dusty linoleum floor. Mufanga stared at the seat of the chair, and moved to lift himself up on it, To stand on it as he was asked. Except, he didn't. He couldn't. Mufanga stood there motionless, trying to will himself onto the ridiculously low platform. And he couldn't. "See what I mean?" Kelly wrung her hands, "I came here tonight hoping to figure out -- why I was worried about Kagos. And it seems like a good idea now. He's afraid of heights." Kelly directed the last at Kagos, and held the slightest question. "He's not," Rider remarked from the kitchen. He chewed on the cigar, but he had not lit it. "He flew into town with me. On an airplane, moron. He's not afraid of heights. Maybe he's afraid of chairs." Kagos, perplexed, shot the sleazy agent a look, which the fat man ignored. "Look, Kagos, you know that there's more going on here than meets the eye. You aren't afraid of heights... and you aren't afraid of furniture. Maybe you just have stage fright... performance anxiety." Rider paused, aiming that last comment at Kelly, with a waggle of his bushy eyebrows. "Besides, it won't be long till they figure it out anyway, but you probably all ready know that." "They? You mean the fWo?" Kagos asked after a moment of contemplation. "They don't have to know about this. It's just a quirk. I'll get over it." "What? Kagos, man are you --" Rider sputtered until the black man turned and leveled a dark stare in his direction. "I know what I'm doing, Rider." He looked back at the rest of the people in the room. Kelly... and the silent trainer, Jean Paul, who watched him with an intent gaze. "I do. I know when I get out there in front of the fans, I'll be able to pull it off. I hit the Wildive on Crisp. from the top rope. There ain't nothing wrong with the Wildman." "You were so shaky." Kelly said. "Even on TV I could see that you were shaky up there... you were acting as if it was a ten story drop from the turnbuckle, instead of a ten foot one." Kagos glanced at the woman again and sighed heavily. "I was fine." "Do you want my opinion?," the old trainer spoke up at last. "No." "Yes, he does," Kelly said. "Enough is enough." Jean Paul said, picking imaginary lint off his black turtleneck sweater. "Now I know Mufanga Kagos is not inhuman, although he claims to be a 'jungleman freak' in his promos," he pointed a finger at Rider who still chewed thoughtfully -- and silently -- on his cigar. "But he's not the freak you have made him out to be. And now his career rides on that aspect of Mufanga Kagos. The Animality of Mufanga Kagos. So Mufanga... What. Are. You?"
