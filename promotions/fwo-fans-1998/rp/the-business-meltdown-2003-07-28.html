The Business : Meltdown

28 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
As I arrived in Anaheim that warm summer day, I felt the sudden urge to fall to my knees. And while carrying a valuable souvenir in my gym bag for my wife Kate, you can see what sort of dilemma I was in. And while this could have all been written off to dehydration... It was something more. I shrugged it off, wobbling around center city Anaheim and looking for a way to get to the arena. Being shunned by the locker room isn't exactly one of those perks of the business you usually hear about when you sign your name on the dotted line, but it's there. It's rare, but it's there. And Koji? Koji is one scary man. I always think by traveling with him, I'm opening myself up to ax murder or murder suicide pacts. It's just easier to travel alone. We'll meet up at the show. In public places. I heard about the place Koji used to work from my good friend Eddie Cheno. Cheno's been through one hell of a rough spell lately, his mind has been wandering and he doesn't seem to be himself, but he told me about the place he's working at, the place Koji used to work at, and well, I guess colorful wouldn't be the right word, but it seems to be the first one that jumps out. No wonder people gave me crazy looks when I showed up there. Twice. And I thought to myself, with the world against you; why not get in good with the most lethal man on the roster? Bing. Bam. Boom. I'm outta there like a man with a plan, hailing the nearest cab and telling the driver to take me to the arena. It was about 1 o'clock or so, and I didn't look to check if his license matched his face, seeing as I was in a rush. And I needed to get into some sort of shade, and fast. I shook off my own delirium and tried to sit comfortable in the back seat of the moving vehicle. But to say I wasn't used to such a situation would be an understatement. Give me the wheel, that's where I'd feel at home. Then again, I wouldn't trust myself to drive in my current state. I don't really remember the trip there. It was like Mexico, a little haze, and little daze, very little memory beyond the recognition that I threw up out the backseat window. To say it was like taking some sort of drug would be an understatement. But yet, somehow, I'm standing in front of the arena with my bag in my hand, and spit on my chin. I make myself presentable, and head into my place of work in one piece. That is, if you considered a beaten and worn down body one piece. See, the previous night at a house show, Dragon and I were practicing for Meltdown, and I was dropped on my head weird during his awesome Wrist Clutch Exploder. The fans popped big, but so did the tendons in my brain, so to say we won't be doing that at the pay per view would be an understatement. Unless I'm stupid, which I am, so you never really know what I might do. Ask me my middle name and I'd look at you funny. Not because I don't know what it is, but because I don't have one. And then you'll go on your tantrum of me not knowing what my middle name is and telling me to sit out tonight's match. I'm telling you. I. Have. No. Middle. Name. Is that such a leap? But no, the doctors are there, right there when I walk in, and they want to see what I'm like. Fox is there too, making sure I'm alright, as if I was important to this company. Sure, and Switzerland is important to preserving World Peace. To say I was in tip top shape would be lying. To say I wasn't able to go out and compete? That's another lie. But that lie makes me angry. That lie restricts me. The other, well, that's more of a compliment. So, I'm told I "could" have a slight concussion. And I'm all like, "Yeah, well, I 'could' have an easy bake oven here in five minutes with cupcakes for all of you, only I wouldn't share because you're all mean to me!" And for some reason, I remember pouting. They're shining this light into my eye, and I'm telling them why that isn't such a good idea. Eventually, after a bunch of tests and what have you, they give me clearance, and I go out there and have what I would consider to be one hell of a match. The fans were into it, Dragon and I felt like Kindred souls in that ring, and I gave the fans what they wanted. My hand tapping the mat three times. What can I say, I love my job. And I'm sure my son was watching on, and he saw his father quit on national television. And then he turned to his mommy and asked why his father can quit a match and he can't quit Pre-school. He's such a little scamp. They're what keep me going. Because without them, I'd be a recluse loser who traveled alone around the country, putting myself on display for the amusement of others. I guess this business really is a carnival show.
