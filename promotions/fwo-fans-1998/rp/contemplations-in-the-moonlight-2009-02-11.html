Contemplations in the Moonlight

11 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The full moon overhead tried desperately to bully its way through the scattered clouds, and the few rays that did win the battle reflected brilliantly off the surface of Lake Washington. Once again Siobahn McPhineas found herself staring off into the distance, a cup of cooling Earl Grey clutched in her pale perfect hands. She could hear the cold winter wind blowing outside and despite her wish for a breath of air she was glad she was safe and warm on this side of the huge, picture window. This expansive, magnificent house on Mercer Island was indeed exceeding their expectations. Mr. Reed initially complained and fussed over what he called ‘woefully unsatisfactory’ space in the library, but once he had been assuaged everyone had fallen back into habits last seen back in the days of Hurtheart Manor. Mr. Yip had taken over the highest room of the house and was diligently working to put his own personal touches in the kitchen. Burlington Werner joined the shadows of the basement and the ample garage. The Assistant was ecstatic to discover the home gymnasium and could be found there almost exclusively. And Mr. Reed… well, some things would never change. Mr. Reed kept to himself in the library, reading and researching and giving himself over whole-heartedly to his arduous quest for answers. The rest of the house was hers, so to speak. It was funny, she thought, how five people could be so close – as near as family – yet be so removed from each other under the same roof… In truth, Siobahn found herself at a bit of loose ends. There was little for her to actually do. The terrible pursuit of The Hunting Party had been circumvented successfully. Their entourage had traveled the height and width of America and brought a good deal of material goods with them. They’d found the refuge they’d been seeking for months and settled into a routine of comfort and familiarity. And Mr. Reed was an active member of a professional wrestling troupe. There was little for Siobahn to do but await Mr. Reed’s assured discovery of their next clue and keep their household running smooth and steady. In the distance, a wolf howled. Surely it couldn’t all be this easy, could it? Siobahn sipped her tea and scowled at the night.
