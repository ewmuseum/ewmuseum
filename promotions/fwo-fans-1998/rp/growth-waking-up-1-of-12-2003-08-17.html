Growth: Waking Up (1 of 12)

17 August 2003

Growth: Waking Up Dreams are beautiful things. They provide escape, to a different world. They feed off your life, your memories, your emotions. They epitomize a part of you that you know about, but don't know about. Oftentimes you lose your dreams. You forget them, or wake up and then turn over again, letting them flop from your brain itself. But sometimes, sometimes dreams are so vivid that they stay with you. They haunt you, and they come back to you, over, and over, and over again. Sometimes dreams... dreams are nightmares. Donna was so beautiful. He could see her eyes, her hair. The glow of her face shining with the clouds behind her warmed him up inside, just as her the touch of her hand did. He stared up at her, in her naked beauty, as she was atop him. Her smile beamed, and he felt pure. She bent forward, as if to touch him, to kiss him, reaching out her hand. Her fingers hit his chest as her lips met his, and he felt it. A vice like grip coming across his heart. It wrenched, and hurt. She began to squeeze, to squeeze, to squeeze... "NO!" He screamed, as a loud noise from the fan belt quickly brought Kevin Masters out of his dream. He sat up in his seat, pulling the handle on the side, bringing the seat back into its upright position. His friend Mitch looked over at him, a look of concern on his face. Mitch kept hold of the steering wheel of his specialty van, designed to accommodate his disability. "Hey, you okay man?" Mitch asked, concerned. Kevin tried to tell Mitch he was alright, but couldn't find the words. His mouth was dry, his heart racing. "Yeah... Just give me a sec." Kevin replied, forcing the words into a sentence. "That was some dream you were having, you started to shake. I didn't want to wake you though, they say that isn't good." Mitch explained, trying to reassure Kevin that he was there. "Yeah..." Kevin hesitantly replied to his friend. "Want to talk about it?" Mitch asked. "It was about Donna, she was... we were having..." Kevin's voice betrayed him as it broke as he spoke. "Hey man, it's cool. We can just drive." Mitch said, looking at his friend, and then back to the open road, "Hey check it out! Welcome to Long Beach!" Kevin eyed the sign at the side of the road. It had been a long trip from the gray skies of Seattle, and he just felt bogged down. His sleep had been a mere attempt, and mirrored his sleep habits as of late: Non existent. He smiled at the sign, and squinted as the setting sun glinted into his eyes. He realized at that point why so many photos of Californians had them sporting sunglasses and a tan. "Yeah man! Hey, I think this is our exit!" Mitch said, sounding truly excited. "Awesome dude." Kevin said, feigning enthusiasm. He had planned to start his life over around this time, but not in this way. Although Mitch was his best friend, he wasn't who Kevin dreamed of driving the car. No, he wasn't who Kevin dreamed of at all. But that didn't matter, because in reality Kevin didn't care much for dreaming anymore. And the future of the past was not the present... Not in the least. Life... It never is easy because fate didn't mean for life to be easy. Dreams aren't life. They aren't reality. Sure they really occur, but they aren't real. Not in the sense that tragedy is real. Not in the sense that pain is real. Dreams are transient to be real. They can't be held. They only can be remembered or forgotten. In the end, dreams are no more than memories. But in that time when the dream is the present... they take over everything. They envelope life itself. They become reality. But at that point where one wakes up, and emerges from the dream. At that point where the dream and reality collide... what is that point? That point of the collision of two realities? What do you call it? There is only one answer, but many names. It is nothingness... it is the destruction of both realities for an instant. It is death.
