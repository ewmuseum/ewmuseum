Sweet Child O' Mine - Two of Seven

23 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
SWEET CHILD O’ MINE Part Two – Elijah “I’m sorry sir… we’ve had no American women admitted to maternity in the past several weeks. Are you sure you’re in the right place?” Eli Flair rolled his eyes and ran his hands through his hair. “Are you sure?” “Sir,” replied the nurse, “Take a deep breath and think for a moment. Was your wife coming to this hospital?” Eli turned and looked back at her, no humor in his face. “Why the flying fuck would I be here if she wasn’t?” “Get—no. NO NO NO! Just get the fuckin’ door!” He spun back around to see Angel, flanked by her boys, coming in the door. “Angerl!” “Eli! You made it!” “Chair!” called the nurse at the desk. Eli rushed to Angel and kissed her on the lips. “Are you doing okay? Does it—what happened to your hand?” She looked at her badly bandaged knuckles, “Eh, nothing… got into a fight with—“ “—Everything,” finished TJ, “Window’s gone, Nine’s hair is holdin’ on for dear life, and our poor driver’s been verbally immasculated.” Eli looked back at Troy. “Immasculated?” “Don’t ask,” replied Troy, “just take an arm.” “No—no, it’s okay,” insisted Angel, “I can walk just fine. Umm, Troy?” “Ya?” “I’m sorry.” The two hugged, just in time for the orderly to show up with a wheelchair in tow. Angel looked back at it and immediately took a step back. “No… no wheelchair. How far do we have to go?” “It’s about a hundred paces to the elevator,” explained the orderly, “and up to the sixth floor. But you’d be much more comfortable in a wheelchair, ma’am.” “No,” insisted Angel, “No wheelchair, no special treatments, no treating me like an inviolate. Got it?” Either through knowing Angel in general or witnessing her behavior on the bus, Eli, TJ, Mickey, Troy, and Nine all nodded their heads nervously and stepped back. She laughed, but it was cut short by another pang of labor. Instinctively, she reached out and steadied herself on Eli’s shoulder. “You,” she said to him, “can come with me. And bring the chair… just in case.” Eli and Angel headed for the elevator, walking as one, while the rest of the boys follow ambiently. They know they’re not gonna be allowed upstairs, but they’re also not sure where else to go right now. “Excuse me, gentlemen,” said the nurse at reception, “but there’s a waiting room up on six. Y’all can ride up in the elevator with your friend.” Is the city of Toronto really ready for all this?
