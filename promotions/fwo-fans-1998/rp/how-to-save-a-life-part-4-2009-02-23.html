How to Save a Life: Part 4

23 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Ivy kissed Sean, and both of them still felt the electricity pass between their lips. She held him close for a full minute before swinging her leg back over him and collapsing on the pillow next to him. Sean leaned back as well, and put an arm around her shoulders, and pulled her head to his chest. Almost immediately, he knew something was wrong. "Ivy?" he asked. "Hmmm?" "What's wrong?" "Nothing," she replied. Trip moved his hand to her face and wiped his finger just below her eye, and held it in front of her to show her the salty tear. "Really?" She took a deep breath. "Ivy," continued Trip, "we've been together for the better part of seven years. You have two after - sex modes: you either want to cuddle for the rest of the day or night, or you're up in ten seconds to get a sandwich. What you don't do is cry." This was the moment Silver Fox had told her about. She needed to tell Sean everything that had been going on in her mind for the past few months. More than that, she needed to... She needed to stop the internal monologue, for starters. "William offered me a job at TV this past week. He wants me to be on his staff, I guess in a managerial/consultant role." "That's great," replied Sean, as he sat up slightly, "You're a natural at that part of the biz, and it's about time you got paid for it again. When do you start?" "I... haven't accepted yet." "What?" he asked, as she rearranged herself and fell back onto his chest again, "Why?" "Because it's still too raw." "C'mon, Mama Bear," said Sean, trying to get her to laugh, "We've been through this. It wasn't your fault." "I know that!" exclaimed Ivy as she sat up, the tears now falling freely, "It doens't mean it doesn't hurt like fucking hell to remember what happened that night, does it? People died, Sean... people who worked for me. People who trusted me. You don't just forget something like that... and when you're suddenly, possibly in the same position you don't go into it without thinking about how easy it could happen again." He stared at her for several seconds. "These aren't new feelings," he stated, quietly. "How long?" "It was different in the moment. When I was helping William with all that stuff, all I thought about was getting the show to seamlessly move on. Afterwards I was shaky and nervous... I think patches of my hair started to go gray again." "How long?" "It's like I'm terrified of my heart." "Ivy!" Ivy returned to her spot inside Sean's arms. "Months. Ever since Eli told me he made the deal with Huph." "What the hell, Ivy?" asked Sean, as he stroked her hair, "Why haven't you said anything?" "I just--" she started, as she waved her hand around, "You just won the EPW belt and were coming back into the FWO and Eli was trying to get back into the headspace for a full tour. You guys had enough going on." Sean rolled his eyes, though grateful that Ivy couldn't see him do it. "You've taken care of everyone around you for so long, it never occurred to you that there's plenty of people - myself at the front of the line - who'd be happy to return the favor?" "I don't want to be a burden." "Stop right there. We're a partnership," cautioned Sean, "You are not, have not, and will not ever be a burden to me." "Fine," replied Ivy, as she threw her hand up, "you wanna fix me, Sean? Fix me. What do you think is wrong?" "Honestly?" he asked, "Nothing." That was unexpected. Ivy rolled her face around so she could look directly at him. "What?" "There's absolutely nothing wrong with you. You were so damn good at everything you did in this business for so long it got to be second nature. Then Neo and his army took that away and now you're simply faced with a tangible memory of how bad things can get. You know what that means?" "What?" she asked again. "You're human." "If people are relying on me, I can't afford to be." "Bullshit, Mama Bear," said Sean, "If you take this job, people will be relying on you, but you'll be relying on other people right on back. What do you always say, every one of us is here because of teamwork. Why can't that apply to you, too?" She didn't answer. "You can't do it all on your own, Ivy," continued Sean, "and nobody but you ever expected you to." She didn't have to.
