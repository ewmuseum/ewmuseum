...It doesn't mean you have to listen.

28 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
September, 1998... Your average "cute" young redhead was setting chairs up onto tables after dark at a McDonald's restaraunt. The expression on her face was a product of her environment: bleak, dim and grey. Back then, she was only known as a mild rebel with her outcry never going beyond a few earrings, some outlandish clothing and the loudest headphones you could find always slung about her neck. The majority of her "heroes" could be found behind a guitar though her ambitions were low and, at that point, she'd given into her seemingly inevitable fate of working for a mainstay of the corporate world, wearing a uniform every day after school to save for university...only to become a doctor, lawyer or whatever: basically, just another clone tucked behind a suit. Even then, she felt no indication of what was about to happen... The store manager, coincidentally the only other person still in the building at that hour, came struting to the front of the store toward the end of the girl's already 8 hour shift and tapped her on the shoulder to break the redhead's lonely little session of humming what was undoubtedly some "horrible" new-age song to herself. "Amy," He'd said, gesturing toward the newest set of holes in her ears. "Don't you think you're getting a few too many of these?" The girl had turned on her heel to face the uniformed man and spoke in her usual tone, which was always passed off as the usual brand of teenaged sarcasm. "Uhh...no?" "Well I think you are. I'm afraid you're going to have to take a few earrings out, it's just not appropriate." He'd come to a dead stop when her eyes met his. The tension rose a step...authority had tried to break her for the last time and that's when she knew it. "Says who?" She still stared defiantly. "It...it just makes you look trashy. Like you don't care about your body. Customers don't want someone who doesn't care for their cleanliness enough to mutilate their body handling their food..." "I'm mutilating my body?" He was middle-aged at best, from that generation who'd always been incredibly set in their ways. "You are. I know it's a trend now, and all you kids are doing it..." "Fair enough." Amy'd snapped, definitely...it didn't just seem like any average mood-swing. "Care to tell me how an earring is unclean, or are you just gonna claim that I 'don't look normal enough' and fire me so I can bitch, moan and start some petition like all of 'us kids' do?" He stuttered, "They're not 'unclean', per say...but you're going to take them out, or I'm going to have to...ehm...lay you off." Then the real surprise came, his threat was met with a smile. At that very moment, the young girl had been hit with a revelation of sorts: Here was yet another person criticising her for material things, for expressing herself and taking control over an aspect of her life. She 'needed' the job, but how was a few extra dollars worth sacrificing her freedom to express herself and ultimate happiness? If you do something you don't want to, you're not going to be happy...and with only one life to live, why not do what you want and please yourself, instead of concentrating on people you didn't even like, and certainly didn't need to take commands from. Control was the name of the game...and she decided to take some for herself, so she worded her response accurately. "You wanna tell me what's what? Cool, keep doing it..." Her boss at the time was confused, and he didn't speak a word until she finished her statement, openly laughing at him. "But it doesn't mean I'm gonna listen." With that, she dropped the mop she'd clung to for a pay cheque and walked out of the restaraunt. Amy Campbell had discovered freedom, independance and how to keep control of her life, her goals and what she wanted. She didn't quite understand the significance back then, but it would be her last job involving a uniform or set rules and her realization that night would transform her into the woman she's becoming today, over four years later.
