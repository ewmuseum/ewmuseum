Fatal Cries, Part IV

13 August 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A long four years had passed since the child's father killed his mother then himself. Everything in life had been going perfectly for the boy, Honor Roll student, popular with his peers and a guy everyone liked to be around. That all came to an end after the accident. In mid-July the young man had been in a car accident, killing his girlfriend. He had survived with minor injuries, but was charged with OUI and his license was suspended until he would turn eighteen. Everything that had gone right in the past four years were completely forgotten. He was home alone, his foster parents, which were his Aunt and Uncle were gone out for the night. His cousin was away in the military. So it was just him. He stood in front a large mirror, staring back at himself. He was a complete wreck. Bruises from the accident still shone on his white skin. Small cuts lined the right side of his body, as well as his forehead. He looked up and down his body, he was only wearing a pair of jeans. His eyes were filled with confusion as he brought his right hand and the bottle that was in it up to his mouth. Tipping his head back, he began to consume the contents of the bottle. The bottle was filled with alcohol. Since her passing he had basically been alone all hours of the day. His only friend, was away for the summer, so he resorted to alcohol as a means of comfort. It seemed like he was either drinking, or passed out somewhere inside of the house. Everything about the accident had ruined his view on life. He didn't want to live, but he hid his feelings well. He wore a mask of happiness around his Aunt, Uncle and cousin. He wanted them to think he was happy while he was being destroyed from the inside-out. "What have I done?" he asked his reflection. "I–I, she, killed... " He stopped talking abruptly. The words weren't there, he couldn't get them to roll off of his tongue. He went to go back to the bottle, but it was empty. "Fuck!" He shouted and threw the empty container to the floor. He looked around his room, it seemed empty to him. He saw his desk, the dining room chair that he used, a bureau and his bed. His room was the entire basement of the house, it was furnished, but there wasn't any ceiling. Large wooden beams ran across the ceiling, supporting the floor above. He walked towards the bed, kicking the empty bottle out of his way. A half-twisted, half-drunk smile came over his face as he lifted the mattress up. He began mumbling something under his breath as he reached his arm where the mattress was. He pulled out a familiar .45 caliber Magnum. Swallowing hard he returned in front of the mirror. He looked himself over once more. He clenched his right hand over the handle of the gun. He turned the safety off with his index finger and glanced down at the polished silver handgun. He stared back at himself. His mind was trying to process a million thoughts into a nanosecond. Just standing in front of the mirror he broke out into a cold sweat. It dripped down from his hairline and over his face. He wiped the sweat away with his free hand and swallowed hard again. He continued his cold, stoic stare into the mirror, unblinking. A few tears rolled out of his eyes as he cautiously brought the Magnum to the side of his head. "Goodbye," he whispered softly under his breath. He pressed the end of the gun into his temple. His heart had leaped into his throat. He was having trouble breathing and the sweat was beginning to form faster. He closed his eyes tight. He could see nothing and feel only the end of the .45 magnum. This was the end of the road for him. He could feel his temple throbbing against the gun. His hand had turned red, his knuckles white. The palm of the hand had become cold and almost slippery. His index finger, moved up and down on the trigger. One wrong jerk or movement would end everything with a bang. He hesitated. His hand loosened the grip on the handle. And for the first time in his life he heard it. "Come on... " it pleaded. "Just pull it. It's not hard, it's really easy. And think, you only have to do it once and you're done with it. Done with everything for that matter." He shook his head. Was this real? No, he was imagining things. But he heard it again. "What the hell are you waiting for!?" It screamed into his head. "Just do it already! Nobody cares about you and nobody ever will! You're nothing! You've never amounted to anything. You're useless. Your parents knew this, they treated you for what you were. DIRT!" He tried to shake it off again. But he could only hear those last few words ringing into his head. Maybe it was right. He had never done anything. He was nothing to anybody. Did his Aunt and Uncle even love him? Not in his mind. He pressed the gun to his temple once more. He swallowed slowly and clenched his teeth. His finger was ready. Inching its way to the trigger. But he dropped it. The gun fell harmlessly to the floor, followed by him. He got to one knee and looked back at his reflection. It was almost laughing at him because he couldn't get the job done. His body had seemingly gone from a light tan to an old grey. He couldn't figure out what he had just heard. He was trying to decipher his imagination from the real thing. He looked down at the Magnum and let out a sigh. He picked it up carefully with both hands. He switched the safety on. He went over to his bed, lifted the mattress and slid it back under. He looked back into his harassing reflection. "Maybe I am nothing." Doubt filled the Fallen Angel's young mind. He felt worthless and empty inside. This was just the first test to stay alive.
