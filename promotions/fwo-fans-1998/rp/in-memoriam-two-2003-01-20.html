In Memoriam: Two

20 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The rare Arizona rain storm pelted the windows of Kyle Dillon's childhood home. He had been living here since his mother died. As the radio went on about a flash flood watch, Kyle sat at the kitchen table he'd eaten thousands of meals at and stared at a half-empty glass of water, unmoving. Dark rings circled his lower eyelids and his mouth hung agape. He hadn't slept in days. How was he to sleep? With the death of his mother, he was left with one person to care about, and now she had been kidnapped. Kyle had searched on his own without relent. He'd hired private detectives and contacted the authorities, but no leads had turned up. Exhaustion had played on his mind and he was no longer able to search...he wanted to, but simply couldn't. There was no logical order to his thoughts and his sanity had begun to leave him. Jack Lundin could make a man crazy, but knowing that Katie was somewhere, needing him, tortured him. With her gone, Jack Lundin didn't matter. He hadn't heard the door open, but the shadow that moved into the light forced him to turn. He expected an attack. Several times, just as it seemed he'd found some sort of clue that would lead him to the kidnapper, he'd been blindsided, usually ending up unconscious. But Kyle could not take the defensive. He was too tired. So he slowly turned, his eyes barely staying open and his mouth hanging limply. He was not ready for what he saw. "You look horrible," Katie mumbled to him. Kyle could not respond, but he knew there was no way he could top her black eye, fat lip, mussed hair, and drenched frame. Silent tears began to roll down his unshaven cheeks as he stood and she rushed towards him, her arms reaching around him in a deep embrace. He feared kissing her, wanting to cause her no additional pain. "C'mon," Kyle said. "Let's get you to the hospital." Kyle could not help but cry as he told the story to his parents' graves. "I've never felt so helpless. It was horrible. I wanted to say something...or do something...to make it all better. But how can you possibly make up for that kind of trauma. I knew it wasn't my fault that she went through what she did, but I couldn't help feeling guilty. "The whole time she was gone, all I could think about how miserable I was without her...not knowing where she was. I...I never could have imagined it would have been so terrible." "I don't know, Kyle. You're being very selfish. You have no idea what I've been through, so don't tell me I'm being unreasonable!" Katie screamed, her words muffled by her sobs. "I don't mean to make you unhappy, but I just can't trust you." "What?!? What are you talking about? I didn't do--" "You just don't understand! I don't trust anyone anymore. I can't trust men. When I sleep at night, I hear his voice whispering in my ear. I feel his hands against my breast and I wake up screaming. I don't know why I don't feel safe around you, Kyle. I just don't." There was a long pause as a silently crying Kyle Dillon fought for the right words. They didn't come. "So...is this it then?" he asked. "I just don't know. I'm going to go live with my parents for a while and see this psychiatrist in Phoenix. He's worked with a lot of veterans from Vietnam, helping them through everything. My doctor thinks he can help me," Katie informed him as she finished packing her suitcase and zipped it closed. She began to leave and Kyle started after her. "But when will I see you again?" She turned back to face him, and he knew instantly that this ordeal was infinitely more painful for her than for himself. "I don't know, Kyle. Maybe next month. Maybe next year. Maybe never." She took a step towards him and put her face near his as she continued, her right eye still swollen shut from weeks earlier. "Don't wait for me, Kyle. You need to live your life. I don't want you waiting around making yourself miserable because this happened to me. If someday the time is right, we'll find each other." Kyle gasped through his tears. He did not trust destiny as she did, but knew he could not stop her. He sat at the kitchen table as she left through the front door. As he heard the door to her car close and she sped away, his attention was turned to a half-full glass of water on the table.
