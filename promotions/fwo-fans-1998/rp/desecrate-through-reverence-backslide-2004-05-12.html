Desecrate Through Reverence: Backslide

12 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
''...So that was when he raped you?'' ''Of course not! He made himself a cup of tea first. What the fuck do you think?'' ''Miss Campbell, I understand that you've gone through a l--'' '''A lot in the past few days, but we need conclusive answers to take any action.'?'' The police chief stuttered as the redhead finished his sentencefor him. ''Look,'' said the girl, ''Back there you've got a smashed up apartment. You've got my boyfriend in the hospital. You've got a fuckin' monkey busted in half and there I am, pretty bare and bruised like a whipping boy. What more do you need me to say? Sorry, but I couldn't quite determine the size of his d--'' ''Enough.'' Amy's lips pursed. Sitting there in a black tanktop and pajama pants she was forced to find and put on by the police upon their arrival, she was unmoving. Even with makeup run down her face after attempts to clean herself off, the redhead didn't lower her eyes for a moment. She stared the policeman straight in the eye, despite discomfort in the bare white room. ''We'll put you in a hotel for the night, granted that you're here again in the morning to finish paper work. We'll arrange for an appointment with a psychologist, and you can speak to the resident counsellor down the hall if necessary.'' ''Off the record,'' he continued, ''You're handling all of this curiously well.'' ''I've seen worse.'' ''So says your record,'' the man chuckled as he flipped through one of many folders scattered on the table in front of him, then took a moment to look back at Amy, whose stare was unrelenting, ''You've been through - and done - quite a bit, but it really doesn't quite explain this level of detachment fr--'' Amy interrupted, ''Not detachment.'' ''Most women...most anyone,'' he corrected, ''Would have at least shed a tear by now. I'm rather surprised by your strength, Miss Campbell.'' ''Hardly strength. I just don't fuckin' cry.'' she said, ''It's a waste of bodily fluids I'd rather let keep me alive than dump out for the sake of attention. Besides, crying would limit this wonderfully intimate question-and-answer period we're having, Mister Officer Beckers.'' She motioned to the 'mirror' adjacent to the table. ''Bet the deputies don't deal with break-and-entering, rape, assault and animal slaughter all in one often, huh? How're they holding up?'' ''Obviously, this ordeal's thrown your sense of humor off. I hope I'm not the only one able to brush that sarcasm away,'' he replied. ''Are we done here? I'd like to think I've got some shit to sort out.'' ''Be my guest...the receptionist will provide you with the motel information and a taxi.'' Amy stood up shakily. She absolutely abhorred the little white room, and hated the Moncton police force even more. In Atlantic Canada, this sort of thing was rare enough that the blissfully ignorant RCMP would've been better off letting the victims deal with things on their own. Of course, Amy had a lot of dealing to do...and she couldn't help but wonder how obvious it was. On her way out the door, she tossed a folded sheet to the floor. ''Thanks for the fuckin' pamphlet. I'm served and protected.'' She'd dealt with it on her own. On her own two legs, not so much as glancing back at that life when she ran. [Back to "Walk This Way"] or [FAST FORWARD to "Memory Lane"]
