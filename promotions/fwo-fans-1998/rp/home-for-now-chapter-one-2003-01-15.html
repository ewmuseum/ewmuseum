Home For Now - Chapter One

15 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The lessons he'd learned were invaluable. The places he'd visited were a change from the norm. The people he'd met were at least tolerable. Then why aren't I happy? Eugene played with the Rubick's cube in his hands, but his mind was elsewhere. They say that there's a simple algorithm for solving these things. I wish life were that simple. Jessica was at home in their bed, probably. Maybe reading, maybe staring at the wall. Most likely not sleeping this early. They were both night owls. The only person that seemed to sleep as late as Eugene in the fWo was Amy, but their relationship was nonexistant at best. As a result, Marder found himself alone in the earliest hours of the morning. He felt hundreds of years older than a year ago. It wasn't lack of trust that made him call Jess four times a day. Despite "that one time," she was faithful and loving. Was it love? He'd thought so. Yes! No. It wasn't he was slowly learning that. Or was it a slow acceptance? Whatever it was, it stung his heart like antiseptic on a fresh wound. He didn't wish for this. If their love was just a gentle, lapping flame, ready to be blown out, then so was he. And mortality wasn't something he was ready to approach. A chuckle almost escaped his throat. You're indestructible. A pang of pain from his left knee brought his mind back to Earth and his hands to his lap, dropping the puzzle on the floor of the airplane. Next to him sat Pierre Delacroix. The hero, icon, and inadvertant babysitter of Eugene's younger years. Pierre was cordial to Eugene ever since the rookie's, so what was missing? Still, he felt like he didn't mesh. An individual among a sea of others. No cohesive group. Still, better than some sort of modern-day "Kliq" forming. Eugene's knee was getting to be a bigger and bigger nuisance, both in the ring and out. Surgery was not an option, and neither was time away from the mat. Eugene knew that he had to capture the fans with his ability, not just with his trash-talking. The results weren't bad so far, but the true test was still to be held in the ring. You gotta admit, main-eventing Slaughter within your first month ain't bad... Pride would not cloud him, he'd decided. Sure, he'd done pretty well, but there was a hell of a long journey ahead of him, one that he was both eager and apprehensive to begin. He had to establish himself, but the flood of newcomers would make it all the more challenging. Nevertheless, the climate was right. No rooted powers backstage as of yet. He knew that he needed skill, charisma, but also finesse in his dealings away from the cameras. The business was a cruel mistress. Hell, all he had to show for it was a bum knee, a lonely heart, and a chip on his shoulder. Avondale was working stiff, but so was Eugene, and both were pissed off. It would be a difficult situation to diffuse. Eugene was never really comfortable with the management's decision to make Jason's substance abuse part of their program, but now that it was out there, it was too late to stop. Nobody really knew how Jason was actually doing, and that bothered Eugene a lot. First and foremost, he didn't want to be the one to prove that alcohol and professional wrestling don't mix when a botched swinging neckbreaker ends up living up to its name. But he also didn't want Jason to be another causalty of the rigorous schedule and constant pain of professional wrestling. Too much thinking. Eugene layed back in his seat and picked the Rubick's cube up off of the floor. Jess was probably asleep by now, but he wanted to call her. For love, or reassurance? Love. He didn't really know.
