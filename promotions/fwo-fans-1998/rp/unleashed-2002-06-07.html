Unleashed

7 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
There were cynics on the payroll of the FWO, people who believed that there had been far too many spectacular matches on the house shows, far too little problems. If not karma, then at the very least the law of averages demands a bad night every once in a while, a card full of poorly wrestled matches, with crowds that are neither large nor particularly enthused. After all, if every match were great, than great becomes the average, until it's nearly physically impossible to top yourself. These cynics were not particularly large in number, although they did hold members amongst the boys, some of whom were beginning to wonder if the excellent, yet non-televised matches that had been taking place over the last month or so would be quickly forgotten if, come Monday, Unleashed went over as well as the proverbial lead balloon. Granted, the FWO wasn't some startup, and there fans, numerous and well-documented, were anxiously awaiting their return to the airwaves. Better yet, the talent were generally high profile, and were handled with care on the shows. With a few modifications, the roster was essentially the same as it had been at CyberSlam, and people had paid to see them wrestle there. The only cost to watching Unleashed was turning off the piss-and-puke show over on the Hillbilly Network. There would be a few small hurdles at the onset, no doubt. Crowning a new Internet Champion and new Tag Champs for starters. Yes, there were cynics, but The Flying Frenchie, Pierre Delacroix was not one of them. To him, there was a clean slate. Oh, to be certain, he wasn't going to stop reminding everyone within earshot about all of his achievements, nor was he going to stop acting better than his fellow wrestlers. After all, wasn't that one of the better elements of being better than your peers, the ability to discuss it ad nauseam? The house shows had presented ample opportunity to get back his timing, as well as allowing him to step into the ring with some different competitors. Burns, for one, was someone he'd have to keep an eye on. Additionally, he'd been reminded of how much easier it was to work a tag match, and had started seriously considering focusing on that. In that, Unleashed offered a pair of opportunities: the first, to return to television with a win. Granted, nothing was guaranteed, but while he had faced some rather unexpected losses in the last few months, the Texas Kid was not, in his mind, quite of the same caliber. The second, was to delay the pitch that he would soon have to make to some people, regarding establishing a tag team capable of thumbing their noses at all of the less capable teams out there, which of course would be any tag team without Pierre. That was still to come.
