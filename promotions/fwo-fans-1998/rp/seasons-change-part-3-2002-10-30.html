Seasons Change (Part 3)

30 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
11:32PM As Paul Adams eased the car along the road towards the school, a faint scent caught his attention. It smelled of things old and new... wood and paper and leather. However, as he got closer to his destination, another smell mingled in with the others, overpowering them...choking them. It was almost as if something was... ...Burning. He saw the glow and the haze from a short distance away and hit the gas. It couldn't be the school... no, not that. Anything but that... but something inside of him told him it was true. And as he tuned the last bend, he saw it. The modest building that had spawned his legacy, and would be the birthplace of future generations of his sport, was spewing forth a sick black smoke as the flames licked away at the insides. He slammed on the brakes and jumped from the car, almost taking the seat belt along with him, and rushed towards the small crowd that had gathered in front. Two fire engines were battling the blaze, while the majority of people were curious onlookers out in the middle of the night to see destruction in action... witnesses to the death of a part of him. For what seemed like hours, he stood there... screaming inside, watching the firefighters wage a losing battle against the most unpredictable and ruthless weapon of nature.. When all was said and done, they were able to put the fire out, though it was far too late. He spoke to the Fire Chief, who promised a full investigation. "This was no ordinary fire" the Chief said, shaking his head from side to side. "I've seen plenty of this in my years on the force... this was arson, I'd bet my badge on it." Arson? But why? Paul struggled to come up with a reason for this. The place was practically sold, and it's not like the insurance he'd collect would cover a full rebuild of the school. The deal was sure to be dissolved once his prospective buyer learned of the recent tragedy of this evening. The income he was to receive for the sale would be lost, and he would need to make a decision on what to do with the smoldering shell of a building. But there was more... there were students who had paid for their training, who would require refunds, trainers who would need to be laid off and collect unemployment benefits... Financially... this could ruin him. Just then, his cell phone rang, singing a happy little tune that mocked him. He glanced at the number and noticed it belonged to his neighbor back in Colorado. It was extremely late, even by Colorado time, for him to be calling... "This is Paul." "Paul... it's Dean. I've been trying to get a hold of you for hours..." "Something came up here, Dean... something bad... it might take a bit of time to clear things up here..." "Well I've got... some bad news here Paul..." Dean said, hesitation showing in his voice. "What's happened?" Paul asked. Surely things couldn't get any worse than they already had... right? "Your house... I have no idea how it happened... it just seemed to come from out of nowhere..." "What about the house?" Paul snapped, for a moment showing the tone and authority that had made him a feared competitor in the wrestling ring. "Th..the craziest thing... we were eating dinner...and I happened to look out the window because I thought I saw something going by the house..." "...and?" "...2 minutes later the place burst into flame. It was like Hell itself took up residence there. They had to call over an engine from the next town to help out and contain the fire... ...but it's gone, Paul. Your house is gone. I'm so sorry..." No. This was definitely not happening. Not to him. Not now. "...Paul? ...Hello? Hello?"
