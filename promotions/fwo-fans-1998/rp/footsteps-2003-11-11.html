Footsteps

11 November 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The snow gently fell from the grey skies above, covering the virgin ground with a thin white blanket. Winter was funny that way. Autumn was more colorful, but towards the end everything was brown and dead. Winter brought the snow, which covered up the ugliness under a sheet of pure whiteness. It was almost like wiping the slate clean for a fresh start in the Spring. It was like that for people as well. They accumulated so much into their lives, good and bad, and at some point it just became too much. The Summer of their lives would bring so much onto themselves that they needed an Autumn of change and retrospection. Many needed the Winter, the cleaning of the slate. A do-over, a fresh start. Bill Norton was lucky in that sense. His slate had been wiped clean. But as his coffin was lowered into the ground, the only fresh start he would be having would be as a compost heap. The priest finished his sermon, zipping up his heavy winter coat to protect him from the chilly Alaskan breeze. He looked over at the sole mourner at the funeral. "Was there anything you wanted to say to the departed?" he asked. Kodiak Vic Creed watched the grey box, sprinkled with snowflakes, disappear into the ground. Bill never did like the snow. Guess he wouldn't have to worry about that anymore. "No," Creed said flatly. The priest nodded and left the big man alone, walking through the dried leaves and branches which littered the cemetery. Bill's death had come as quite a shock. He'd always been an upbeat kind of guy. Even when he and Vic used to travel on the Indies, driving for twelve hours crammed in a rusted-out Gremlin only to get paid gas money didn't get him down. Bill would make a joke about it, taking the tension off Creed (and saving more than one promoter from being strangled). Even after he'd stepped away from Wrestling, Bill had a positive outlook on life. He was just starting up a comic book store in Anchorage when he was found hanging from his ceiling fan by the neck. Vic was angry at his dead friend. Suicide was a quitter's way out. That's why Vic didn't think that Bill's death was a suicide. He just wasn't the quitting type. If Creed had honestly thought his friend had killed himself, the Main Man would never have come to the funeral. Something in the back of Vic's head was telling him that this was wrong somehow. Best to come by and pay respect to one of the few people on the planet who had actually earned it from Kodiak Vic Creed. The footsteps weren't quiet in the crisp afternoon air. Branches snapped under some heavy feet. Dead, brittle leaves were crushed underfoot. Two men, Vic noted without turning around. One heavier than the other. The bigger man had a bit of a limp. The footsteps stopped directly behind him. "Hello Vic, long time no see." The next sound Kodiak Vic Creed expected to hear was a gunshot. Darryl Vishanti resumed his steps and stopped directly to Creed's right. Vic didn't turn his head. He kept his eyes locked on the open grave in front of him and wondered if he was about to get dumped in the hole as well. Vishanti was a criminal with a very serious grudge against the Main Man. People like Darryl didn't forgive and forget. They didn't let bygones be bygones. They cut your throat and let you write your apologies out with your own blood. "It's been quite some time since we've spoken. How have you been?" Darryl wasn't the least bit interested in Creed's welfare, unless there was a way he could make a buck off of it. Vishanti followed Creed's stare. "That's the funny thing about Death," he said. "You never really see it coming until it's too late." Now Vic expected to hear the click of the hammer being drawn back. Bang. Darkness. Footsteps again, this time the heavier man came up on Vic's left. With his eyes hidden behind his sunglasses, Creed looked without turning his head. A rather large man stared at the side of Creed's head, nasty scars cris-crossing his face. One of his eyes was cloudy and dead. "You remember my brother Bruno, don't you Vic?" "Yeah," Creed replied as he looked back at the grave. "He's the tough guy who crippled my sister because he didn't have the balls to come after me." Darryl smiled. "No Vic. You were our prized earner. You were the racehorse everyone was putting their bets on. We couldn't put down our stallion, but you had to be made to listen." Vic felt Bruno's stare. He turned his head to face his sister's attacker. "Didn't I beat you into a coma?" Creed asked. "I could have sworn I heard that skull of yours crumple when I beat your head in with that chain wrapped around my fist. You remember that Bruno?" KVC was now quite certain that Bruno wasn't armed. If he had a gun, he would have jammed it under Creed's chin and blew the top of his head off. "He doesn't remember much about that," Darryl answered for his brother. "He's blind in one eye. He received some brain damage. But as you can see, he's still very much awake. He doesn't care too much for you though." "The feeling's mutual." Vic turned back to the hole. "What do you want Darryl?" Vishanti stuffed his hands into the pockets of his overcoat. "I want what I've always wanted. Money. You're the fWo World Champion. You get a startling amount of press in the mainstream media. I'm here to cash in on that." "I already have people to take care of my business for me." "You misunderstand me Vic. You might be big in the United States, but you're something of a cult icon in Europe." Darryl shook his head slightly. "I can't understand the phenomenon myself. Maybe it's all the in-breeding, bad medical care, poor diet, who can say really. All I know is that there's a group of wealthy European gentlemen who have nothing better to do than to wager on your matches. They see you as a gladiator, I suppose, waging wars for their entertainment." "They bet on my matches?" Vic asked with a tone of disbelief in his voice. "Yes they do Vic, and they bet heavily. When they found out about the relationship you and I have, they couldn't wait to send their money my way." "You're laundering their money?" "Some of it I'm sure is dirty, judging from the amounts they courier to me. I have no problem cleaning it for them, at a reasonable transaction fee of course. You intrigue them Vic, and the more interest they have in you the more money that comes my way." "Congratulations. What does any of this have to do with you meeting with me?" "I'll come to the point Vic. You still work for me. You've given up everything that means anything to you just to become World Champion, and that's admirable. But you and I both know why you did it, and it has nothing to do with the belt." Darryl pulled his hand out of his pockets and stepped in front of Creed. He looked up at the big man. "You have your bosses with the fWo, and you do what they tell you to. I understand that." He reached up and slid the sunglasses off Vic's face and slipped them onto his own. "I'm just here to make sure you understand that you have a higher power to answer to. Me." Scenarios rushed through the Main Man's head, all involving maiming and dismembering the Vishanti brothers and doing all sorts of disturbing things to their entrails. But while Bruno might not have been packing, Darryl always had a gun on him. There would be another time, another chance to end this. "This isn't like before Vic. Before, we might not have been able to touch you because you were in the spotlight. But the pressure of your position in not just the fWo, but in professional wrestling, must be intense. With your sociopathic tendencies, who would be surprised if you just snapped one day and put a shotgun barrel in your mouth. As you see by the hole in front of you, we've gotten better at helping people put themselves out of their misery." Darryl turned towards the grave. "Poor Billy didn't have much of a chance." The pieces fell into place and Creed felt the blow like a knee to the stomach. "Why?" Darryl turned back to Creed. "To get the pleasure of your company Vic. I guess we could have send a card to you, but I felt that this would get your attention a little more than a Hallmark moment would." He nodded towards his brother, and Bruno backed away from the big Alaskan. "We'll be in touch Vic. It was really good seeing you again." He touched the frame of his newly acquired sunglasses. "Thanks for the shades." The Vishanti brothers walked away from the cemetery plot, leaving Kodiak Vic Creed very much alone. He listened as the footsteps faded away, a car door slammed in the distance, and a vehicle drove away. The cemetery became silent as the dead it held. Inside the Juggernaut, though, he was screaming.
