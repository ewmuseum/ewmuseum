Candles of Pain

3 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The good news was that he was back in the States. The bad news was that he didn't feel like he'd live long enough to enjoy it. Kodiak Vic Creed grabbed the toilet bowl with both hands. His knuckles were white, yet there was little actual strength left in him. He was a man with great physical power, but right now could summon none of it. There was nothing left inside him. Or would be nothing left at the rate he was throwing up. His pulse pounded in his temples as he struggled to raise his head. Chunks of vomit clung to his chin. Vic wiped his mouth and looked at his hand. Blood. He was starting to throw up blood. There really was going to be nothing left inside him. Not the best way to celebrate your birthday. Vic splashed cold water on his face as he knelt in front of the sink. "Shit on me," he mumbled softly. It took nearly a full minute for Creed to get to his feet. With slow, shuffling steps he made his way out of the bathroom. Was it really his birthday? He had lost track of the days of the week. Everything seemed to run together in one big chaotic blur. In the past week, Vic had slept only a few hours at a time. Sometimes he'd be up all night. Other times he'd be up for an entire day before falling asleep. Vic collapsed on his bed, his face buried in his pillow. His eyes, the only thing he had the strength to move, turned to the bedside table and the small bottle resting on top of it. The vial of the synthetic steroid T2 was only half-empty. The longer he took the poison, the more he found he needed it to function. Without it he could barely move. When he did move, his muscles ached so badly he almost screamed. His thoughts were sluggish. He was a prisoner inside his own body, and his prison cell was becoming more and more of a torture chamber. For over ten years, Kodiak Vic Creed endured hardships in and out of the ring. In the ring, he'd suffered injuries ranging from a blown knee to a fractured skull. Outside the ring he'd survived a plane crash and being shot in the head. It was obvious he couldn't be killed. Nothing could stop the Alaskan Juggernaut. But this... it was like dousing yourself with gasoline and setting yourself ablaze. The pain was practically off the charts, and to avoid the pain he had to take T2. When he took it, it was like someone flipping a switch from Normal to Psychotic. Matt Loco had to physically restrain him a number of times to keep him from hurting some poor idiot who simply asked for an autograph. He didn't sleep when the T2 shifted his body into overdrive. He'd be up for days at a time. The lack of sleep and his heightened aggression messed with Vic's mind. Paranoia, mild hallucinations, those were just some of the side effects he suffered with. Damned if you do, damned if you don't. Creed's sluggish thoughts turned to his daughter. "If I would have pulled out quicker, this wouldn't be happening," he grumbled into his pillow. This was all Sarah's fault. Darryl Vishanti couldn't hurt the Main Man directly. Sarah was Creed's Achilles Heel. With every agonizing muscle spasm, with every round of bloody vomiting, Vic found that his unconditional love for his daughter was becoming more and more strained. Some days he vowed to take the pain until he could figure out a way to get out of his situation with Vishanti. Other days, Vic knew that if he stopped taking T2 Darryl would take care of the situation for him, ending his torture at the cost of his daughter's life. For such a young girl, Sarah had certainly taken her toll on the Main Man. He'd lost toes due to frostbite because of her. He'd been forced to give up his hard-earned money to support her. Now he had a fresh round of trials she was putting him through. If he didn't think of a way out of this mess soon, it would cost one of them their lives. And since Creed couldn't die, it was pretty clear who the corpse was going to be. Matt Loco unlocked the door and walked into Vic's room. "Wake up sunshine," he said as he set a styrofoam cup of coffee down on the table. "Time to greet the day." Vic raised his arm slightly and gave Loco the finger. "Very funny." Matt ignored him and opened the curtains. There was a verbal protest of sorts, but the pillow in Vic's face muffled them. "We've got places to go Vic, and people to see." As Matt walked by the dresser, he snatched a t-shirt at tossed it at Vic. "And by the way," Matt said with a smile. "Happy Birthday!" To Kodiak Vic Creed, celebrating a birthday was like celebrating another year closer to death. But if things didn't change soon one way or the other, this would be the last birthday the Main Man would see.
