A Road Paved In Broken Glass: Part One

3 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“I could tell you all that the road to pro-wrestling was a long one, but that would negate the pain I endured along the way. Allow me to take you back to where it all began…” October 12th, 1988. The bleachers were packed, and the gymnasium was filled with noise. For a seven-year-old boy, it was a pretty tremendous thing to be in the middle of this. Milo Samus tried to push the crowded gymnasium to the back of his mind as he focused on his opponent, and schoolmate, Bobby Newman. The two boys, in their wrestling stance, locked eyes as they circled the ring. They’d trained hard, and were both looking forward to their first ever, wrestling meet. “Go Milo!” A proud Kevin Samus yelled, with a smile, from the bleachers. Kevin was a huge wrestling fan, and seeing his child taking part in the sport, etched a smile on his face. He often told his son stories of wrestling events he’d been to, while visiting the United States on business trips, and it was obvious that Milo loved wrestling as much as his father. “You’re going down Milo.” Bobby sneered, continuing the stare down. “Nuh-ah!” Milo said in quick retort, making his first move. The two boys locked up momentarily, with Milo attempting for a headlock. Bobby managed to push Milo away though, and the boys began scaling the ring again in a circular fashion. “That all you got sissy?” heckled Bobby. A sneering grin formed on his face. Milo grew angry, and lunged forward, executing a collar-and-elbow tie-up. The gym grew louder as the action intensified, but the boys did their best to ignore. Bobby saw his opening and was certain he could easily drop his opponent with a single leg takedown. He must have hesitated a second too long though, as Milo saw it coming and quickly went for his head, locking on a guillotine choke-style hold into a cradle. “Yeah Milo!” Kevin Samus was on his feet; cheering for the unbelievable display his son was giving. “That’s my boy!” he yelled, nudging the man who stood beside him. The referee hit the floor, and signaled that Bobby’s shoulders had indeed been pinned to the gym floor. Milo had won the match, and slowly rose to his feet. Words could not describe the excitement he felt, and he proudly threw his arms into the air. The referee pushed the boys together, and they hugged for a moment. “Good match Milo! Enjoy it while you can though, cause we’re having a rematch soon.” “Thanks Bobby.” Milo said, joining his schoolmate in brief laughter. The referee held Milo’s hand in the air, and the school-master announced the winner. “Congrats champ! Now come here and give your old man a hug!” Milo ran over to his father, and gave him the biggest bear hug his seven-year-old strength could conjure. The crowded gym continued to cheer for the athletic display the youngsters had just given them. This was a moment Milo would not soon forget.
