Respect (1 - Holiday)

3 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Some Christmas. While it was great to be home with the family and not in Japan for the first Christmas in two years, after dinner, when the extended family had gone home and I'd gotten so frustrated with Splinter Cell that I nearly broke my Xbox controller, I was left sitting with my parents, thinking about what had happened at Twilight. I guess my mood over the game got me to brooding, and Sean Stevens immediately came to mind. More specifically, the complete lack of respect that he had shown me before, during, and after the match. To make things even worse, he was still being cheered, even louder than I was, which I'm sure didn't make him think any more of me. Where had I gone wrong in that match? "Lance? Lance, honey, what's wrong? It's not that game, is it?" "Nah, mom, it's not the game. I'll eventually get the hang of it, and it definitely is awesome so far. I've got other concerns on my mind." "It's work, isn't it?" "Yeah...I mean, it's so good to be back out there, and to be doing as well as I've been doing, but..." "It's that punk, isn't it? That...Stevens?" "Yeah, dad, it's Sean." "Don't let him get to you, son. You've got more talent than he does, and it's only a matter of time before you get a chance to show it." I nearly fell off of the couch right then and there. Coming from my dad, that was essentially the equivalent of Samuel Smooth getting down on his knees and telling me that I was the linchpin that held the fWo together. I mean, sure, we'd patched up our relationship over the past year and he'd come to accept what I was doing, but I never expected that kind of glowing praise from the most stoic man I'd ever met. "Thanks, Dad. I'll try to keep that in mind." "Well, son, you do that. Now, if you'll excuse me, I'm off to bed. Some of us still work at the mill and don't have cushy wrestling jobs to fall back on." A year ago, that would've been filled with venom and I would've become even more melancholy, but Dad rubbed the hair that was finally growing back in on my head as he walked to the steps, and the smile on his face and the twinkle in his eye was all I needed to know that I was doing something right this time around. As dad made his way up the steps, the telephone began to ring in the other room, which would invariably be one of my aunts or great-aunts, and she'd keep mom tied up until well after I went to sleep. Alone with my thoughts, I was excited by the prospect that was forming in my head. More than a inkling, yet still less than a plan, I hit the power button on the Xbox as a smile crept across my face.
