Family Matters (Part 5)

10 January 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Note: The following events took place last week prior to Slaughter in Japan. It had been a couple of days since Monte had received the call. Nathan had spent a great deal of time in critical condition, as doctors worked feverishly to make sure they could stabilize him. Finally, they were able to remove the blood from his skull and repair the wound. Now, though he was still being held in Intensive Care, family members were allowed to come and visit him. However, as the doctor had predicted, Nathan was still in a coma. The injuries to his brain and skull were going to take a while to heal, if at all. Despite the coma, many family members made their way to St. Luke Hospital to visit him, even though he was unable to acknowledge their presence. On this late night, with bits of snow falling in the cool air, a long black limo pulled up in front of the hospital. Slowly stepping out of the limo was Monte Burns, his long blonde hair pulled back in a ponytail. After nodding to the driver, Burns walked through the entrance and part the front desk. The women flashed him a grin from their papers, but Monte paid them little attention. As he reached the end of the hallway, he took a left down another hallway before reaching a set of double doors. To the right of the doors was a waiting area for ICU visitors. Burns stepped into the room and was glad to see that no one was there. Tonight was not a night to be making small talk. Monte reached out and buzzed the intercom on the wall. "Can I help you?" asked a female voice on the intercom. "Yes, I'm here to see Nathan Burns," Monte said. "Come on back," replied the woman. Burns turned around to exit the waiting room, as the double doors slowly opened. Walking past the nurses' station, Monte took a deep breath as he neared his brother's room. Though Nathan was in the coma, Monte was still nervous about seeing his brother. Every time the two seemed ready to patch things up, something happened to derail their relationship. The blow up at Thanksgiving had been the latest example. Perhaps the two brothers were just not going to get along. After all, Monte and Nathan didn't choose to be brothers. Fate had decided that. That was always the way Monte explained it whenever someone would ask him about his family problems. People who knew Monte could never understand why he valued close friends more than his family. Monte thought it was obvious, since we have the opportunity to choose our friends, whereas family is thrust upon us out of our control. Still, as Monte entered the room where his brother lay unconscious in the hospital bed, he felt a lot of emotion well up inside of him. Nathan, a man that had been so athletic, vibrant, and full of life, was relegated to a bed with tubes running into his body and bandages wrapped around his head. The constant beeps of the machines monitoring Nathan's condition slowly faded into the background as Monte gazed down at his brother. So much time had been spent fighting, blaming, and arguing over the years. Battles over money and respect had been more important to the two of them than anything. John had always been the brother who was level headed, saw the big picture. Monte and Nathan had been competing since the day they entered the world. No matter what they played, winning was the most important. They learned that from everyone around them, including their father. And now, as he looked down at Nathan's body, it didn't seem like he would be doing much battling in the future. Taking a seat in a chair near the bed, Monte reached out and touched Nathan's hand, hesitating at first before becoming more comfortable. He tried to look over his brother's face, but all he could see was Nathan's pale face staring back at him. Monte glanced up at Nathan's strawberry blonde hair, noticing its similarity to Monte's red-streaked blonde hair. The two had always been about the same size, even though Nathan was the older brother. As he thought over the similiarities between his brother and himself, the thought finally hit Monte. "This could have been me." Monte sat back in the chair, frightened by what he saw before him. In spite of all the love from his family, Nathan was a lonely man. He had no wife, no girlfriend, no children, and not many friends. He had given all of his life to the business, from the time he was a child until now. He travelled all over the world, from Japan to Europe to South America, in search of work. The payout for the evening didn't matter much to Nathan, only the thrill of competing in the ring. He had spent all of his time and energy on becoming the best wrestler possible. Love and romance were not a part of the equation. Fun was possible, but it was not something he actively sought. Intimacy and closeness were next to impossible for him. All of this was true for Monte as well. Until now, he had never really thought about where his life was heading. Monte had always just assumed that he would be in the wrestling business forever. It was what he loved, what gave him joy and happiness. How could he do anything else? Unfortunately, as was the case for Nathan, Monte did not have much of a personal life. His friendships with Lance and a few others were really all he had. He didn't socialize much with the other fWo wrestlers, usually thinking they were either beneath him or that they didn't like him. Being so committed to wrestling, it was just as likely for him to end up in the same position as Nathan. Laying in a hospital bed in a coma, alone and unknown. Raising up from the chair, Monte looked down at his brother once more, wishing that he could say something to him now. He thought back to the times when he had wronged his family, from forcing his father to sell the company to jobbing Nathan and John in matches to forcing his own father to compete against him in the ring. After all that time and some much needed thought, Monte finally understood what he had done. He realized just how much pain he had caused. With that knowledge finally in hand, Monte leaned over near his brother's ear. "You were right, Nathan," he whispered softly, "I do regret what I've done." After giving his brother one final look, Monte slowly walked out of the room and past the nurses' station once again on his way out of the ICU. As he headed down the hallway back to the entrance, Monte felt a weight had been lifted from his chest.
