Observations

19 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Footsteps echoed through the hallway. Kodiak Vic Creed heard them. "Excuse me sir, but it's almost midnight. Visiting hours are over." He heard that too. Vic stood in front of the door to Slade Wilson's hospital room, watching the sleeping old man through the tiny window. The blankets barely moved as Slade took slow, shallow breaths. He did look old. Vic found it uncomfortable to see him so weak, so fragile. Slade was as hard as a coffin nail. Now his cropped hair had grown out and pressed flat against his head. His muscles had started to atrophy from lack of use. He was fading away, and there was nothing the Main Man could do about it. Feeling helpless was a sensation that didn't sit well with the Juggernaut. "Sir," the nurse started again. Vic continued to watch Slade breathe. "I'm not disturbing him. I'm staring through a pane of glass. The closest thing I had to a real father is lying in that room dying, and I'm standing in the hallway because I don't want to disturb him." His voice was hard as he spoke in a low tone. There was another reason Vic wouldn't go into Slade's room, but even he didn't know what it really was. I don't deserve to be in there, Vic said to himself. As he thought the words, the sensation he was feeling seemed to confirm the statement. But why? As he considered arguing with himself, Creed noticed that the nurse was still standing beside him. "Look," he looked at her name tag, "Betty. I don't get to see Slade very often, and don't know if I'll get a chance to see him again. Just give me this one chance." Betty gave Vic an understanding smile. "I know who you are. My son's a big fan of yours." That struck Creed as odd, and he immediately felt that Betty was indeed a Bad Parent. Wait, what? Wasn't Vic fighting the good fight now? Why would having the Main Man as an idol and role model be a bad thing? He suppressed those feelings for now. "Thanks. I know it's late, so I'll try not to be here for too much longer." "Okay," Betty said with a nod before leaving him alone once again. Footsteps echoed away from the Main Man, slowly leaving him with only the sound of his own breathing and the occasional beeping noise from some random piece of medical equipment. It still smelled like a mad mix of sterile hospital and stale piss. Slade deserved better than this. He deserves better than me. The thought jumped into Vic's head before he knew it. "Where did that come from?" he muttered to himself, somewhat surprised at the revelation. He looked at the window and momentarily caught his own reflection. At least, he thought it was his own reflection. There was something different in what he saw, and though he couldn't place it exactly he knew he didn't like it. Vic's eyes focused beyond the surface of the glass and he watched Slade for a few more minutes. It'd been a roller coaster year for the Main Man. First, he lost his daughter. At Cyberslam, he lost his career. After that he gained a son, a wrestling son, as well as a return to the fWo. Now he was in danger of losing the man he cared about as much as a son would care for his father. Not Vic's own father of course, since he loathed the man's existence and was quite happy when he finally died. In every part of his career, Kodiak Vic Creed did what he could to honor his mentor. He hoped he could show Alex the ropes the way Slade had shown him. Vic's big scarred hand touched the glass. "I'll make you proud, old man." With that, he left.
