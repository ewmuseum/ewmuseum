A journey of a thousand steps, Part 8

4 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Mike stumbles into the office of Dr. Talbert, the second time he has been there today, and the door shuts behind him. A little bewildered at first, now he has a moment to consider the words of his wife, and for a moment he's tempted to open the door again and ask what she meant. Refusing to believe she meant what he really thinks she meant, but worried that's the case nonetheless, he considers the consequences as he turns from the door to the office and back again. Luckily for his sake, the rational side of his brain had reasserted itself at this point. It told him that, no matter what she meant, if he walked through this door before doing as he was told, there would be absolute hell to pay. Better go see his psychiatrist. He turned and walked back to the room he had left in a fury mere minutes before. Rachel greeted him as if he had just taken a quick trip to the bathroom. “Mr. Baer.” “Hello Rachel.” Mike opened the door again and found the two doctors sitting in the same place. This time, Jacob merely rose from his desk. “Mike, let's save the introductions for now and start over. Here's what we've...” Mike interrupts Dr. Talbert and walks across the room towards the desk. “No, no. My wife asked me to do this thing and that's what I'm gonna do. Dr. Talbert, I'm sorry. Again. Sir, I don't remember your name from before, but I'm sorry. I'm Mike Baer, and I understand you're here to try and help me.” Mike extends his hand. Dr. Regan rises from his seat and gives Mike a firm handshake. “Dr. Kelvin Regan. I'm an old classmate of Jacob here. No harm done. How about we get started? Mike, why don't you have a seat. Jacob, I believe you were saying?” Mike sits down on the couch as usual. Dr. Talbert begins again. “Right. Mike, I'm going to lay it all out on the table. When you were first brought to me, I diagnosed you based on the symptoms I saw, and ever since then we've proceeded down that path of treatment. Since you have only recovered to a certain point since then, and not any kind of point of satisfaction, I began to think my original diagnosis may have been incomplete, or worse, wrong. Worried that my close relationship with you personally may cloud a new diagnosis, I brought in my friend Dr. Regan to offer his opinion. He's got a good hypothesis about what may be wrong, and he's generously offered to lend a hand in your treatment.” Mike sat in shock, processing all Dr. Talbert said as he spoke. Hey tries to form his thoughts into words. “So I... we...” Mike felt some of the anger rise in him. He pauses and breathes. He remembers the mental exercises taught to him. He thinks of what he wants to say first, and begins again. “So, was I right? Did we really waste all this time? Please tell me we...” Dr. Regan began speaking after “time”, interrupting Mike by accident. “Mike, let me... I'm sorry. Let me just stop you there. I want to assure you that this possible diagnosis we've come up with would have been extremely unlikely, maybe impossible when you first were brought to Dr. Talbert. I'm still not sure that, without all the data and notes and everything I've read in your file that I would have come up with this even 2 years ago. Maybe not 6 months ago. This is small consolation, but your case is not an easy one to solve. Don't think ill of your friend over this. It would be as if he built a whole house, but I hammered in the last nail and took all the credit.” Mike ruminates on that point for a moment, also thinking of his next question. The obvious one should be first. “So doc, what's wrong with me, and how are we going to fix it?” The doctors nod to him, take out their papers and begin to lay out a plan.
