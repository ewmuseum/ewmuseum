EVOLVER_intro

17 April 2003

Many people are unaware that two distinc types of evolution exist. The kind we all learn about, the kind that killed the dodo and put the humans on top is called macroevolution. Macroevolution is responsible for opposable thumbs and partially-developed gills. The other type is microevolution. Microevolution entails the belief that individuals themselves can evolve within the couse of their respective lifetimes. Maturation and growth are similar, but not synonymous. Evolution was something else altogether. Eugene Marder wanted to evolve. Eugene Marder wanted to evolve, and once came very close. Everyone experiences it once in a while. Depression. Depression. Worthlessness. Loneliness that can only be fathomed by those who've felt it; loneliness that, when strikes, immediately invites images of grisly war movies, of soldiers with big caves in their chests where a heart used to lie. Four days after his twentieth birthday, Eugene Marder was feeling this to a particularly sobering degree. Ironically, nothing about him stayed sober. He drank for two days and layed slumped against a wall, not quite sleeping and not quite awake. Not quite thinking. Not quite alive. The blinds let a bit of light in, creating lines of illumination on his face. The clock, as far as Eugene Marder was concerned, did not exist. The gun was easy to get. It took a short drive to his friend Alex's house, followed by false claims of burglaries in the area. "For protection." It felt heavy in his hands, but even heavier on the cold floor next to him. It pulled at his eyes, tugged at his throat, until he was finally ready to listen. "I control my future. That is all I control." Eugene Marder cradled the gun in his hands. It was cool to the touch. "It's time to go." He found the gun in his mouth. When certain metals meet in an acidic enviornment, the oxidize. He felt his fillings oxidize with the Smith & Wesson. Strangely, he didn't feel like the gun should be removed. "What am I doing? This is a revolver. This is my dad's revolver." His voice didn"t rise, showed no emotion. Curiosity, maybe. "No. Not revolver. Evolver. It's time to evolve." Though he'd never handled a firearm before, cocking the gun seemed second nature to Eugene. A figure appeared in the door. "What the fuck?" It sounded like Patrick. "You're not real. I'm talking to myself." The truth was, Eugene didn't care. Eugene felt the gun being pulled from his hands and he felt himself fire. He'd later learn that the bullet went straight through the tendons in Patrick's left arm. From that day, Patrick couldn't hold the afflicted arm still. His four years of optical surgery learning became useless. Patrick's friendship preempted his passion, and it still does. But the desire to evolve burns deep in Eugene. To find his next evolver.
