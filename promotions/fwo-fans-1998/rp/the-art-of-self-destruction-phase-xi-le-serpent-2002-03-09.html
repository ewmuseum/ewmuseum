The Art of Self Destruction, Phase XI (Le Serpent)

9 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Life wasn't real anymore. He woke up from his sleep and found himself laying in the middle of a desert. The sun was beating down on him, nothing but golden sand for miles upon miles ahead of him. He stood up, holding one hand up over the top of his eyes to keep himself from being completely blinded. His feet were heavy as he tried to put one foot in front of the other. As he slowly moved his way across the gigantic sand dunes, he stopped and looked down near his feet. A sun colored ribbon wove back and forth through the sand. It stopped in front of him. It had a forked tongue that came in and out of its mouth, a large and flat head, two beady black eyes and slender body. "What are you?" he asked. The creature's tongue slid in and out of its mouth before it replied. "I am something more powerful than a king." He looked shocked at the slightest notion of the idea. "But you're so small, and you haven't got any legs, how can you move?" The creature continued answering the questions. "My touch is more powerful than the touch of a king's finger." "But you're barely thicker than my own fingers." "The size of one means nothing when you don't know what it can do..." "What can you do?" The creature opened its mouth, two large pointed teeth came out, a liquid dripped from the tips of the teeth. "I can take you further away than any ship or airplane can take you." "But you haven't got any haven't gotten any legs. You just kind of move back and forth through the sand," he said, still bewildered. "Don't be so naive." The creature moved through the sand and wrapped itself around his ankles. It looked like a golden bracelet. "You're not from here, are you?" "No," he replied. "Well," the creature started. "With one simple touch, I can send you home." "What do you mean?" "Anything I touch, I return to the soil from which it came. If you want to go back to your soil, just tell me and I will send you–" "No, stop..." "Very well," the creature hissed. "Just remember, I can bring you home again..."
