Family Values - Chapter Two

20 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
November 21, 1994 Spencer, Nebraska "Angela, turn that crap down!" cried Henry Davison, CPA, "I can hear it outside!" He turned to his wife, Helen, with a disgusted look on his face, "Can you believe how loud she plays that filth? I don't know why you spoil her the way you do." Helen didn't look up, she simply poured herself a glass of Scotch Whiskey and began to drink from it, all the while dropping chopped vegetables and hunks of beef into a pot. Henry made himself a gin and tonic, reading the newspaper, ignoring his wife and her dinner preparations. In the background, Frankie Valli and the Four Seasons remind them of Oh, What a Night, in December 1963. The house is a small, single story building. Just enough for two parents and a teenaged daughter. Usually, however... not big enough. In Angel's room, she reluctantly turns down her CD -"Bloody Kisses" by Type- O- Negative. It just came out last year but she hadn't gotten a chance to buy it until yesterday. She doesn't even look away from the computer screen, however. This eighteen year old hellraiser is doing massive amounts of research on the Maquis de Sade for a Literature class at community college, though she doesn't feel like going back in the spring. At least not yet. She's just out of highschool, a very unhappy time for her - the lyrical scraps she wrote and sang with her friends are essentially all that got her through. Her concentration breaks for just a moment to take a look to the left of her desk at a picture of White As A Corpse, the band she's sang for for a year and a half now. Smiling at Travis, her best friend, she pushes away and begins to gather the clothes she dropped when she entered the house today. "Angela, dinner!" called her mother from the kitchen. She caught a glimpse in the mirror - her hair was dyed an insanely unnatural red, and she was dressed in an oversized black Ministry T-shirt and black spandex pants. Good enough, it's just her parents. "How was school today?" asked Helen, spooning out bowls of stew, "Learn something new?" "Was fine," replied Angel, quietly, "Working on my paper now." "Pass the pepper," said Henry, monotone. Angel did so. "I bet you can't wait for tomorrow," continued her mother, trying her best to keep something of a conversation going, "Long weekend for Thanksgiving. Don't forget, we're going to my sister's for dinner Thursday and Friday, but we'll be home Saturday, and the rest of the weekend will be yours to do as you please." "No, it won't," interjected her father, "Your uncle Mark is going to be coming to dinner Thursday, and then spending the weekend here, and you will be here for it." Angel didn't get the chance to answer, but her eyes went wide. "Henry," chided Helen, "We'll be spending two whole days with our family already, would it be the end of the world to let Angela go out on Saturday with her friends?" "Yes, it would," said Henry, raising his voice, "She hasn't seen my brother in four years now. He's always sure to call on your birthdays and holidays, but you're constantly short with him. Young lady, you will keep a civil tongue in your head when we're at Nora's." Henry sat down, and resumed eating. Angel didn't dare say anything to either parent - they've both had two to three drinks already tonight. It had been four years since she had seen her Uncle Mark. Four long years. No matter how hard she tried.... she'd never be able to forget their last meeting.... Or what it cost her.
