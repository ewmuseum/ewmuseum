Family Matters (Part 3)

3 January 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
On a late night in Hartford, Connecticut, the St. Luke Hospital parking lot was virtually empty. Very few cars were parked, and none could be seen moving around. Only the puffs of cigarette smoke coming from workers on break showed any movement at all. Then, a long black limo pulled up into the parking lot and drove around to the emergency room entrance. Before the driver could even get around to the passenger side of the limo, the door swung open. Monte Burns stepped out of the car and rushed inside the hospital. Somehow, mainly due to his private jet, Monte had managed to traverse the long distance between San Francisco and Hartford in a relatively short amount of time. Obviously, the emotion of the situation had already begun to take its toll on Monte, as he could only vaguely remember the flight to Hartford and the limo ride from the airport to the hospital. For the first time in a while, his only concern was the welfare of one of his family members. Burns wasn't sure how or why that was the case. All he knew was that when Lance told him Nathan had been in an accident, his heart immediately dropped. Worries about titles, matches, and victories faded to the background in an instant. If he would have had time to think, perhaps he would have simply shrugged it off and remained in San Francisco. That was what normally happened when family news reached Monte. But this time, something seemed different. As Monte stepped through the doors and walked briskly towards the emergency room waiting area, he immediately caught sight of his mother and father. "Monte!" his mother cried out. Monte met his mother in the doorway and reached out to embrace her in a warm hug. He noticed the redness around her face, obviously indicating that she had been crying quite a bit. As he hugged his mother, Monte looked to the side to see his father standing in silence. Daniel's face looked as white as a ghost, his eyes seemingly staring far off into the distance. Burns knew exactly what that look was all about. "John is on his way," said Sarah as she let go of Monte. "We were only able to reach them about a half hour ago." "How are you holding up, Mom?" Monte asked, even though he knew things were not well. "I'm doing fine," she said in a half-hearted attempt to sound convincing. Then, she leaned over and whispered in Monte's ear. "Your father has been very quiet, son. Maybe you should talk to him." Monte nodded his head and walked over to the wall near where Daniel was standing. Daniel's facial expression remained the same, with Monte noting that his hands were fidgeting as well. Burns stood silent for a moment, unsure as to what he should do. Slowly, he placed his hand on Daniel's shoulder before speaking. "Dad, I know...," he stopped short and thought for a moment before starting over. "Dad, I think maybe you should sit down. I know you and Mom have been here a while and you have to be tired." Daniel turned to look at Monte, but it was more like he was looking through Monte's head. A parent never wants to see their child get injured, but it seemed that Daniel was taking this especially hard. Monte knew why and tried to console his father further. "This could have happened to him anywhere, Dad," Monte offered. "Just because he got injured in the ring doesn't mean that's your fault. It was an accident." The words seemed to help Daniel a bit, as he moved his head back around and ran his hand back through his hair. However, Daniel remained silent as he patted Monte on the shoulder and walked over to sit down. Monte sighed and looked down at the floor, wondering whether he had helped or hurt the situation. Sarah reached out and grabbed Monte's hand with a smile before walking over to sit next to Daniel. A moment later, a doctor with slightly greying hair and a beard walked into the room. "Mrs. Burns?" he said as he looked over at Sarah. Sarah nodded and rose to her feet. Along with Daniel, she walked over to the doctor, followed closely by Monte. The doctor led them to a separate room of to the side to discuss the situation further. "My name is Doctor Jacobs. Here is what I can tell you so far on Nathan's condition," he began with a deep breath. "As a result of the fall, Nathan suffered an epidural hematoma associated with a cracked skull. What that means is that a crack in the skull severed meningeal artery, one of the small arteries in the skull." Holding tightly onto Daniel's hand, Sarah covered her mouth with her other hand, obviously feeling emotional hearing about her son's injury. Monte and Daniel sit in silence as well, trying to understand what kind of impact Nathan must have endured. "However, the rapid deceleration caused by the impact on the ground may have injured the brain itself," continued the doctor. "That's something we will be examining over the next several days. Right now, we've stopped the brain hemorrhaging and are performing surgery to remove the blood that has already accumulated." In taking a deep breath, the doctor paused and looked across at the Burns family. Monte knew immediately that this pause was not a good sign. After a few moments, the doctor finally continued. "I also have to tell you," he said, "that we're not sure when your son is going to regain consciousness. It could be only a couple of days. But it could be longer."
