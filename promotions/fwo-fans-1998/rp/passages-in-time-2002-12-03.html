Passages in time

3 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
My name is Yukio. It's a silly name, given to insolent children born to tired parents. In my country, it means "standing loud". This isn't something that most children would want to be saddled with. But my name is like my aspect. I am not loud, nor usually standing, but if you think this is a joke of mine then you would be mistaken. My therapist recommended that I keep this journal. I believe this to be nonsense, but the idea is to offer a reflection on my life though my words and thoughts. She contends that over time I will see myself as I actually was rather than who my current memory tells me I am. I say this is pointless, for my current memory is all that matters. A memory cannot harm you. My arms are usually sore at the end of the day. When I was younger I worked long hours and for many days on the docks outside of my prefecture. I admired the large men who would work well into the night because they had no other choice. The money was bad in those days and even though the great nation of Japan was starting to grow with technology the boom had not reached the simple people in the city yet. These men would work because they had no choice. I admired the way they would push themselves to keep going despite their pain. When you do not have options you discover what kind of person you are. This is what my grandfather told me, and I believe it was one of the wiser things to come out of the old fool's mouth. I do not mean to be disrespectful. But I am honest here. I got into wrestling for the same reasons. The money. The wrestlers are not all bad, and I do like the mixed atmosphere better in wrestling leagues that offer positions to men and women rather than the alternatives. This is my first time working for an extended period of time in America. I can't say I like it very much. The people here take their prosperity for granted and whine over the smallest, most pointless things. These are not problems and anyone who has a television is not going through any real hardship. What I will do today: walk down to the local market and buy some mangos. I will eat them hot, not chilled. I will enjoy the sensation of the skin. This is still a waste of time. Goodbye, myself.
