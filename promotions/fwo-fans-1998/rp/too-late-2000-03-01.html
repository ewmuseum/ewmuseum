Too Late.

1 March 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
fWo: Fans Wrestling Organization Too Late. March 01, 2000 Blackness. That's all we see, or at last until we begin a slow pan back to reveal that it's actually the latest in sleek, top-of-the-line cell phones, and is currently in the possession of Perfect. He sits on the edge of the elegant hotel bed, sneaking occasional glances at the big-screen TV to his side that is bookended by large green plants. Despite the fact that a perfectly serviceable phone sits on the genuine oak nightstand next to the bed, Perfect prefers to use his own personal phone. Yeah, yeah. No matter what happens, win or lose. Hopefully he'll be down long enough, and then boom! You guys do your thing, and I'll finish him off. He'll never see it coming. Yeah, yeah. I'll see ya at the show tomorrow, big guy. He flips off the phone, turns to the camera. Just putting the finishing touches on a little present for ya, Iceman. By the way, could you possibly tell me what the flying hell you were thinking when you put your career on the line?! Idiot. Even if we ignore the fact that I'm more charismatic, more talented, and just plain better looking than you, the fact still remains that we've faced each other twice before, and both times I did something you'll never do - I used my brain. Something that I've never seen you do in your matches, and something you sure as hell didn't do when you offered to put your career on the line. I mean, come on. If you wanted to retire, have some balls and just say so, don't make it look like you're trying to go out fighting. Tony, don't be a hero. It's only gonna cost you in the end, my ex-friend. Of course, it's too late for that. It's very late, in fact, in your career - you've got less than twenty four hours to prepare. Twenty four hours, buddy. Can you feel the hours going by, the minutes flying past, the very seconds ticking? Tick, tock, tick, tock... I think you can, Tony. When you go to sleep tonight, make sure to relish it. Take a mental picture and keep it in your head forever, because it'll be the last time you ever - ever - can go to bed and say to yourself, "Hey, I'm a pro wrestler." That's unfortunate, Tony, but the fact remains that you brought this upon yourself. You've never had the sense to follow in line, Tony. If you had only came to me after Warfare on January fifth and said to me, "Scott, there's no way I can beat you - I want to join you.", this whole thing could've been avoided. But no, that's not the Tony Barrett way. No, the Tony Barrett way is to keep stubbornly charging, horns out, head lowered, never once taking the time to stop and see where you're going. And why? Why, Tony, why? Is it because of some misguided sense of company loyalty? Face the facts, pal. Thursday morning when you wake up and realize that you'll never see the inside of a wrestling ring again, the fWo isn't gonna give a damn whether you live or die. We're nothing but cattle to them, us quote-unquote "talent". Just a warm body to throw out there to make some money off of, and when we drop we're replaced. Us in FTfWo saw that and we got out of the system in time. You don't seem to see that Tony, but I do. And I know that for you to risk your career because of your company loyalty is one of the dumbest things you can do. Or maybe, just maybe you're doing this because you want to prove it to the yourself, me, and the world - in that order - that for once in your life, you can be better than me. That you can take it to the next level. Tony, if that's the case, the only person you're fooling is yourself. Not once in your life have you been at my level, and you never will be. That's just a fact. Unfortunately you're too busy charging and ramming into trees to see that. And maybe in the middle of the night tonight you'll wake up and you'll realize that you're horribly misguided. The only problem with that is that it's already too late, Tony. The match is signed. I'm ready. And one way or another - in the ring or otherwise - you will retire tomorrow night. Been nice knowin' ya. Perfect chuckles, before turning back to the television as the camera slowly fades to black.
