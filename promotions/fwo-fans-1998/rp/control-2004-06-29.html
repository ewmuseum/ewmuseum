Control

29 June 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"How are you feeling Mister Brandish?" It was a redundant question, but one Darryl Vishanti asked anyway. He wasn't concerned about the well-being of Nick Brandish. The man was nothing more than a huge slab of meat that Darryl made use of. Looking at the huge man standing in front of his desk, Darryl could clearly see the many bruises and deep cuts around Nick's face from the Hardcore Title match he had over a week ago. Lindsay Troy did some nice work on the big man. "I'm fine," said Brandish. "I'm touched by your concern." Darryl caught Nick's sarcasm and made a mental note of it. He stared at Nick's emotionless face. "First of all, I'd like to thank you for doing such an excellent job in your Hardcore Title match. Between that and your loss to Victor, you've made me quite a sum of money." The corner of Nick's eye twitched at the sound of Creed's name. Darryl knew there was friction between the two men. The deep-seeded hostility existed even before he had hired Nick to kill Vic's daughter. The girl had also been Brandish's goddaughter, yet Nick didn't hesitate in accepting the job. Once he'd finished the deed, Nick picked up his payment and left Darryl's office without a hint of remorse. He was all business, a cold-blooded machine. "Victor had the same opportunities I've given you." Darryl deliberately tossed the name at Brandish again just to savor in the reaction he got in doing so. It was all about control, and that included his employees. "I made a substantial fortune from him doing what he was told. Hopefully you and I can continue a similar arrangement without any of the unfortunate incidents that Mister Creed has to suffer through." Without looking, Darryl could feel his brother Bruno straighten his posture as he stood behind Darryl's chair. Bruno was loyal to his big brother, almost to the point of dependency. Darryl took care of his brother, since it was his dealings with Vic Creed that brought around Bruno's assault and subsequent brain damage. He was a little slower now, and that much easier to manipulate. Bruno was also Darryl's personal bodyguard. He took his job seriously, and didn't hesitate in pulling out his Glock 9mm as Brandish took a step closer to Darryl's desk. "Pay me," Nick demanded in an even tone. The sudden appearance of a gun didn't seem to bring about any hesitation in the huge monster. "All in good time." Darryl leaned back in his chair and pressed his finger tips together. Behind him, Bruno kept his weapon trained on Nick's face. "Now I understand you might be able to work your way into the title picture for another division?" "I haven't been paid for my fight against Troy," Nick said. "No way am I looking at another until you pay me." "There's no need for hostility. I have your money safely locked away." Nick looked at Bruno, then back at Darryl. "My money is safer with me." "Trust me, you'll have your money soon enough." A dark cloud seemed to pass over Nick's face. "I don't trust anybody." "Neither do I, Mister Brandish. That's why I'm holding your money until I see more results, showing your loyalty to me." Two huge hands came to rest on the edge of Vishanti's desk. The joints creaked as Nick put his weight forward. Bruno tensed and for a moment the barrel trembled. "You don't need more results. You need to give me my money." Not wanting to soil the carpet with brain matter unless it was absolutely necessary, Darryl raised his hand and motioned for his brother to lower his weapon. Bruno brought the gun down, but didn't take his eyes off of the Monster. Satisfied things were once again under control, Darryl asserted his dominance. "You're the hired help, and you'd do well to remember your place." "You'd do well to remember who you're talking to." Nick's gruff voice had just enough menace in it to take the edge off Darryl's subtle smirk. Insolence was not to be tolerated. It wasn't with Creed, it certainly wouldn't be with this oaf. Darryl leaned forward and narrowed the gap between his face and Nick's. "We're not going to have any trouble, are we?" "That depends on how fast you can get my money, or how fast your boy is on the trigger." This was getting tiresome. Tiresome, and frustrating. "Nobody threatens me." It was Brandish's turn to smirk. "I do." Enough was enough. There was no reasoning with the big man, so Darryl decided to be more direct. He opened one of the drawers and pulled out a stack of papers. He waved them in front of Nick's face. "Do you know what this is?" Nick tilted his head to the side. "I look psychic to you?" "This is a list of people you've worked for, jobs you've done, and people who were unfortunate enough to cross your path." Brandish eyed the thick pile of papers and almost appeared proud at the amount of documentation. "You'll be adding two more names to that list in a couple of minutes." "If you refuse to cooperate, these pages will be leaked to various people in both law enforcement and the underworld. Your life will be forfeit." "My life was forfeit a long time ago. If that's the best you've got, you'd better hand over my money. That's the only paper that'll keep you alive." Darryl wasn't sure if it was wise to call Nick's bluff or not. His brother, however, wasn't taking any chances and held up the gun again. "Maybe I should just kill you." "Maybe." The desk seemed to heave a sigh of relief as Brandish stood straight, taking his weight off of it. Darryl could feel the tension stirring in the air and most of it was coming from his half-wit brother. He could feel that the situation was starting to spiral towards the abyss and, as he made eye contact with the Monster, he could see Nick knew it as well. Brandish turned his attention from the senior Vishanti to the junior and nodded at the gun pointed at his forehead. "It works better if you take the safety off, short bus." Before Darryl could say a word, his brother looked at the switch directly above the trigger guard. Nick's hand closed around the edge of the heavy wooden chair beside him and he swung it, one-handed, into Bruno's gun-arm. The muzzle flashed an angry yellowish-white in Darryl's face and drove a bullet through the top of the desk. Before Bruno could bring the gun back, Nick slipped around the desk and drove his massive shoulder into Bruno's chest. Both men stumbled backward and Bruno's back slammed into the wall. Nick's forearm pressed against Bruno's throat, his other arm wrapped around the elbow of Bruno's gun-arm. Chaos. While the two large men struggled behind the desk, Darryl fell out of his chair and began crawling towards the door. He'd tried to stand but the bullet hole in his thigh prevented him from supporting his own weight. Shock was keeping him from feeling the burning pain of the shattered bone in his thigh. It let him block out the smell of the blackened meat surrounding the bloody bullet hole. Only one thought flashed like a glowing neon sign in Darryl's head
