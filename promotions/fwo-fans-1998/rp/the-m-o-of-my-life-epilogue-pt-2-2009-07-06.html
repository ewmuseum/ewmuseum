The M.O. of My Life - epilogue pt.2

6 July 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
December 2008... “How much?” he asked again, as he rubbed his furrowed brow gallingly. ”Seven Fifty,” the bartender replied with a faux kindness that became synonymous with New York bartenders. The dark-haired daunting patron lifted his sunken eyes and stared blankly at the simplistic tap domestic beer that stood upright, sweating condensation onto the bar counter. “Seven Fifty for a domestic beer?” he asked once again with an increasingly annoyed tone. “That’s the price, sir,” the bartender replied as he nervously scanned the bar, desperately looking for a needy patron to defer him from the tension-filled moment. It had been two weeks since Mike Levine started his bartending job at T.G.I.F., and though the hours were bad, he couldn’t complain with the plentiful amounts of money that were drunkenly tossed his way by the end of the night. In those two weeks, Levine had learned quite a lot about ‘reading’ the people that sat at his bar. He could read the lonely housewife desperately looking for male company, he could read the newly single teenager turned feminist, he could read the depressed/borderline suicidal victim of marital disloyalty; he could read them all – that was until this night. Never before had he seen such a genuinely scary individual come forth for a drink; the patron’s eyes were stained with rings of exhaustion and frustration, his face was noticeably daunting pale, and his demeanor was all around stoic and frightening. He wore loosely fitted and ragged black jeans with a long, almost mafia-like, trench coat. Beneath the trench coat he wore a strangely clean black T-Shirt was some sort of crude graphic written on the front that was half-hidden by the trench coat. It read P.I.W.. There was a scary lull between the mysterious patron and himself. Levine nervously tapped his feet to break in a fruitless attempt to break the eerie silence, as the patron rested his head onto an open palm and stared blankly at him. A cold sweat began to form upon Levine’s forehead as he scanned his surroundings nervously looking for the simplest distraction that would request his presence…but nothing came. In his mind, he could see it now… ‘I’m gonna’ fucking die’ he thought to himself as he carefully reached into his pocket and began running his thumb across the keypad to precisely strike the numbers ‘9’, ‘1’, and ‘1’ in that order without looking. Thankfully, it was not necessary. A twenty-dollar bill was extended by a large, muscular hand. ”Don’t worry, I got this round.” said a calm, strong-faced, blonde-haired giant as he smiled reassuringly behind the raven-haired murderer. “And I’ll take a Jack and Coke, please.” Levine nodded sporadically and accepted the 20-dollar bill with glee, capturing his much desired exit promptly. “Yes sir, right away.” Joshua Listerin fixed the collar of his sports jacket and calmly sat beside his prospective client, who seemingly ignored his presence and continued to blankly stare forward. “You look good,” Listerin commented sarcastically, noticing the angry features upon the PIW veteran’s face. “Better than the last time I saw you, at least. Than again, I think 2-day old road kill looked better than that.” His own hearty chuckle would be the only reaction he’d receive from what he thought was a good, ice-breaking joke. Krow found no amusement in the failed attempt at comedic satire and only sipped his beer. “So…how long were you in the hospital after that brawl?” Listerin asked as he was handed his Jack Daniels and Coca Cola concoction. “One week? Two weeks?” “One day,” Krow coldly replied. ”One day?!” Listerin coughed in mid-gulp, as his eyes widened in astonishment. “They let you out that early? What kind of hospital…” “I let myself out,” Krow interrupted impassively, taking a calm sip of his drink. “You sure you should be out and about that early?” Listerin queried, before coming to his senses and realizing just who he was talking to. “You haven’t changed a bit, have you?” Listerin shook his head disappointingly. ”When are you going to learn? How long do you think your body has before it reaches its expiration date, huh? You’re not helping yourself by refusing any sort of medical attention. Trust me, when I was active in the ring…” Krow interjected rudely. “What do you want, Listerin? Or better yet, what have you been wanting from me?” Krow turned to the man formerly named Paladin of CWL fame and narrowed his eyes. “Why have you been following me? And why…” Krow pulled out the sheet of paper left in his pocket weeks ago, following his brutal battle with the bald-headed behemoth at Coldren’s Pub. “…did you leave this in my coat?” Paladin smugly smiled and pointed playfully at the three bold letters on the very note he had written. They read: F. W. O.. “What? Don’t know what those letters mean?” Paladin chuckled amusingly. ”Don’t insult my intelligence, Listerin. I’m not one of your family-friendly fuckheads from the land of the rich. I know what those letters mean, now answer my questions,” Krow grunted frustratingly, his patience beginning to wear thin. “First of all,” Paladin calmly began as he rested his glass back on the counter and turned his eyes towards Krow. “I wasn’t following you. Last person I thought I’d run into in a damn Underground fighting league was you. I was following the man you were so gallantly fighting in that cesspool.” Paladin’s face turned a bit sour. “You know, it’s wrestlers like you two that make the rest of us look like low-life degenerates. To think that men who used to make millions wrestling in front of sold out crowds have lowered themselves to fighting idiotically in a dingy basement of a low-end dive is beyond my comprehension.” ”Wait,” Krow cut-in, lifting his hand. “You were following him? Why?” Paladin paused. ”You don’t know him?” he asked. ”Should I?” Krow replied. Paladin paused again, attempting to read Krow’s attempt at arrogance, but finding him to be completely oblivious of whom “Graphic Violence” was. Paladin eased into his seat and retrieved a photograph from his suit pocket and placed it onto the bar counter. Krow’s eyes quickly fixated onto the picture. He recognized the bald-head, the rough facial hair, and the body littered in tattoos; this was the man who nearly ended his life. The behemoth. ”What’s his name?” he asked. ”You’re serious? You really don’t know who this is?” Paladin questioned. Krow shot an irritated glare at him and asked again, this time with more force in his tone. “What is his name?” Paladin cocked a confused eyebrow up. ”Steven Shadows,” he said, looking for some sort of recollection in Krow’s face, but finding none. “He’s known as Graphic Violence around our sport.” Still, Krow showed no expression of remembrance. ”You really don’t know who this is,” Paladin said as Krow hungrily stared at the photo. “Haven’t you been following the sport at all lately?” Krow closed his eyes for a moment and curled a lip. ”I haven’t followed that idiotic sport since those three letters ruined it,” Krow sharply retorted. ”Well, I don’t know how to tell you this, but those three letters just might be the most inviting three letters of your life right now,” Paladin said as he eased further into his seat and began digging through his inner-coat pocket. “I work for those three letters. I’m one of their main talent scouts. And that man, right there, used to work for us.” “Used to?” Krow snorted as he slid the photograph towards Paladin. “So, what does this all have to do with me?” “Look, I know you. I’ve known you for quite some time, Damon. And if there’s one thing I know about you, it’s this…,” Paladin said he pulled a small, neatly folded, stack of stapled papers from his coat pocket and began to unfold them. “You can’t stand when someone gets the better of you.” ”He didn’t get the better of me,” Krow harshly responded. “Did you beat him?” Paladin asked rhetorically, as Krow fell angrily silent. Paladin continued unfolding the paper and set it down on the bar counter. “I know you want to get the man back…but I also know that with age, you’ve gotten smarter and a lot more slick than you were in the past. So I know you want to do it in a way that nothing can happen to you legally. You’re not that same, blind kid you once were back in the indies. You’ve grown up…just like me. And just like me, you know a good deal when you see it.” Paladin pointed towards the papers with his pen. Krow’s eyes followed. “Right here is a contract, an FWO contract to be precise. In it is not only a lot of money, but a lot of freedom. Signing this paper means you can do whatever you want to Shadows, in any way. You want to stiff the man into oblivion – go right ahead. You want to kill the man – go ahead. In the end, the FWO holds all legal responsibilities – not you,” Paladin said as he brought the pen towards Krow. “In two days, I’m meeting with Shadows. And if he knows you’re there, I’m sure he’ll sign on the dotted line. And then all you have to do is wait.” “Wait for what?” “Wait for our grand re-opening, of course,” Paladin emphatically stated with a broad smile and wide arms, as if symbolically embracing the idea. Krow responded with an annoyed twitch of the eye; finding that the FWO was re-opening was not one of his fondest of thoughts. Krow turned his attention and quickly scanned the contract before him. Each line made the knot his stomach grow tighter, each legality caused his blood to boil, and every mention of “Fans Wrestling Organization” made his eyes gleam with more fire. The mere thought of selling out to such a company sickened him. This was the very same company that put his own home-away-from-home out of business. This was the very same company that forced Jim Johnson, entrepreneur of the Pinnacle of Insane Wrestling, to put his tail between his legs and whimper away like a cowardly dog. This was the very same company…that scorned his wrestling career in the 1990’s. The Krow of ten years ago would’ve taken the contract, crumpled it into a tightly knotted ball and shoved it down the throat of the calm, daunting monstrous blonde-haired CWL Legend. But the Krow of today calmly reviewed the contract and saw its grand benefits. Freedom. Violence. Destruction. He licked his lips at the endless opportunities, the vast amounts of opportunities, and the possibility of tearing something he hated from the inside. Moments later, he signed on the dotted line. Paladin reviewed the signature. ”You signed it…Krow?” Paladin questioned bewilderedly. Krow pressed the pen down onto the bar counter and lifted his eyes sternly at Paladin’s. ”Let me make something perfectly clear,” Krow said in a low, yet firm, tone. “If I am to enter this company, everything…I mean everything…that you know about me stays with you and you alone…” ”Damon, it’s kind of hard to hide all the shit you’ve done over the years…” “I’m talking about what I’ve done recently. I’m not talking about Action! Wrestling, or P.I.W., or even my little stint in LoC. I’m talking about before all of that.” Krow’s face was expressionless. Some would find his demeanor threatening, but to Listerin, this was the look of a dead seriousness. “Like the name you keep calling me. No more. Do we have an agreement?” Paladin’s stare was unmoving, stuck onto the glare of the Satanic God. He could sense the seriousness in his voice and the sternness in his conduct. He could also feel the slight, yet firm, tug on the opposite end of the contract. Paladin lowered his gaze for a moment and reassuringly smiled. ”You have my word, Krow.”
