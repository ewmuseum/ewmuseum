Back to School

20 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
If you've seen one trashed, passed-out lump on the couch, you've seen them all. Of course, your usual lump is just that: trashed and passed out. Your usual lump also normally doesn't have the equilibrium to wrap themselves in a blanket, or use a key to get in the appartment... But Darcy Markson never thought of that, so when he saw a human-sized heap on the couch, completely covered by a wool quilt, he never took a second to identify the intruder. Instead, he grabbed the first weapon he saw: a heavy glass bottle that was set atop the counter in the kitchen/living room connecting area, and stood over the small lump on the sofa. It didn't make a sound, and he stood there for a moment watching the soft breathing from under the blanket, before giving in and poking at it with a toe. At seven o 'clock in the morning, it wasn't the best time to surprise anyone... Especially not Amy Campbell. When the twenty year old girl jolted up and ended up falling entirely off of the couch, onto the hardwood floor, all she saw was a blur...a blur she swiftly mashed in the groin with a bare, but rough heel. But when she woke up a little, and what a wake-up it was, she clearly made out the form of her longtime boyfriend standing above her...holding a bottle above his head, wincing from the sharp pain that had proven to be the long-promised outcome of waking Amy up that early. Of course, the bottle was quickly tossed to the couch Aimz had come from as he kneeled down to help her up, though his face was growing beet-red, either from the embarassment of mistaking his girlfriend for a crackhead burglar, the fact that her first defense was ramming him in the jewels, or both. "Damnit, Amy!" Instead of accepting Darcy's extended hand, the redhead rubbed the back of her head and slid around to prop herself against the makeshift bed she'd taken the night before. After a look at the clock, she grinned, "Jeez, Darc'...isn't it a little early to be trying to kill me with..." Her usual crooked grin spread to a smile when she looked beside her and saw that his weapon of choice that morning was a bottle of olive oil. "...kitchen products?" Light laughter floated throughout the room, but Darcy didn't budge an inch; he was not amused. Even when Amy cocked her head to the side and looked up at him with the brighest, most innocent expression in her mismatched eyes, he wouldn't even sigh. "This isn't funny." Markson muttered, "Not at all." The brightness in Aimz quickly dimmed and she pulled her knees to her chest, letting her elbow rest on them to support her chin as her gaze up into Darcy's eyes became considerably more morose. "What's wrong?" He still wasn't giving an inch, and her clueless questioning only seemed to further berate the tall, dark-eyed 'teddy bear'. "Uhhh...Amy? I know it's not like you to realize the obvious, but YOU HAVEN'T BOTHERED COMING HOME IN TWO WEEKS!" With Darcy's sudden outburst, Amy sank down a little, letting her legs slip across the floor as she tried to explain. The only explaination she gave, however, was spoken in little more than a mewl. "Well...I dunno......I needed to...sort my head out..." "Oh, really? I sure hope Brand Frontier had the decency to help you, like the great man he is..." He'd begun pushing the limit, and Aimz was fast to let him know that as she snapped up to her feet. "Don't even start." "Start what?" He growled, "Start looking up his number in the middle of the night, wondering if my girlfriend happens to be there?" "Darcy..." Amy let her tone serve as the second warning. "Well you tell me, Amy...what the fuck am I supposed to think when everytime I turn on the TV, you and that guy are tagging or when I go through the paper, some fucker's printed a thousand pictures of you hugging him from all anlges?" She huffed and tried to walk off, stepping over to look out the window while sarcastically gasping. "Forgive me, father, for I have sinned! God forbid I hug a friend." "Well y--" He would've started again, but Amy interrupted with, "Well...? Well you're assuming too much? Well, maybe you should stop to think why I came back here and didn't run off, even though the opportunity was definitely there? Well maybe I'm not such a self-centered bitch and I do care? Well WHAT, Darcy?" There was a sudden change of heart. When Darcy looked into Amy's eyes and thought for a moment, she was right. She had come back to him, and he had no right to speculate she'd done anything wrong, other than neglected a few responsibilities. Seeing even deeper in, he'd realized how badly she'd been cut by his plainly implying that she was some little tramp, running off and sleeping with other people on her spare time. "I'm...yeah, I'm sorry. I didn't mean to...well, shit, I just don't know. Seeing all that stuff people were writing did get to me after a while." He tried to grin, though. "Guess I should practice what I preach, huh?" It seemed both were having changes of heart, though. Amy hadn't spoken after a few moments, instead she was reviewing plain facts. If Darcy had run off for two weeks straight, she would've killed him, especially if everybody was saying that he was cheating, whether or not she thought it to be true. 'Practice what you preach.' rang all-too-true to her and instead of just forgiving him, Amy sounded off with a barrage of apoligies all her own. She was still learning to get over her own stubborn tendancies...but it was coming along, and the man she'd been almost avoiding for two weeks for fear of the very criticism she'd just recieved was always the one to show her this, even though he never did so intentionally. Bang, bang, bang. It was the same formula for every argument they had: Quick snapping, heated words, then the same regret sank in, at the same time, every time. Somehow, through the split-second blur in every spat, there was always something to be learned. Aimz was home again and, as usual, it promised to be another entirely new learning...or re-learning, as the case may be, experience.
