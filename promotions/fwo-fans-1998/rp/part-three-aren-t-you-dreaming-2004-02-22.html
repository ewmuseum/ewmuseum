Part Three: Aren't you dreaming?

22 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Here, let me pay," said Fox, as he stood up, "You've got enough on your mind." "Pay for what?" asked Ivy, "I own this place, remember?" Out of nowhere, Ivy hugged him. "Listen, I wanted to tell you..." "Tell me what?" "I wanted to say I'm sorry," continued Ivy, "Sorry for not being there. Sorry for not being strong enough for me and Ted." Fox smiled. "You can tell me that when you next see me." "What?" Ivy was confused. "But the first thing you need to do," continued Fox, "is wake--" ... "---up!" said Ivy, as she sat up, startled. There was nothing around her, but silence. And darkness. It took a moment, but it all came back to her. She was in a small hotel room in Australia, on tour with Valerian's Garden. The fWo remained in Florida, suffocating under the foot of the Mouse-- "Fox..." -- and Silver Fox, as far as she knew, was still in New York City, in a coma. Ivy got out of bed and rummaged through her bag, her dream still fresh in her head. No, it wasn't Fox, but it was Fox's words that echoed throughout. And even though the usual analysts would say it was simply Silver Fox's image telling Ivy what she already knew - but Fox was one of the few people she listened to. They were right, and they were wrong. As Ivy dug into her bag, she came across a polaroid, wrapped in a plastic sheath - and a smile crossed her face. From left to right, Silver Fox, Poison Ivy, and Pierre Delacroix. Backstage at Twilight, she thought. The old man, the young girl, and the Frenchie. That was the night that Fox had told her about Disney and about his complete control. "Finally, the fWo can be what Beaven wanted it to be from the start," Fox had told her, "There's no 'evil owner' or power struggle now. Disney officially owns the company but as long as we're turning a profit, they're completely hands-off. This is it, kiddo - this is our destiny." "What, exactly, is our destiny?" she'd asked. The answer surprised her. "This wrestling company survives and thrives, not because some all-powerful phantom's pulling the strings, but because we're working together at it. That's why Dead never succeeded. That's why Jim Dudley didn't succeed. This company's future isn't set in stone, because this company hasn't written it yet." In other words, Fox... the future is what we make it. "You're goddamn right." There it is - Ivy's phone book. She flipped through the pages, nearly to the end, and grabbed her cellular. Punching in the number, it rang... and rang.... and rang........ "Hello?" "Buckly? Ivy." "Listen, can I call you b--" "Buckly, there's plenty'a time to play Counterstrike later. Right now I need a favor." She heard the former Kid Cool, the former fWo Webmaster - sigh under his breath. Ivy laughed, the Disney folks had been trying to fire him for weeks now, but they can't seem to get a hold of him, or the passwords. Come to think of it... "Why'd you answer the phone, Buck?" "I was close to it," replied Buckly, "Had to get a new box of Slim Jims and all." "Has anyone from Disney contacted you?" "Not... that I know of." She smiled. There was still time. "This'll take an hour, tops, Cool... and you'll be King of the Frag again. Seriously." It wasn't too late. Ivy just knew.
