Blade Job - Chapter 9 - Backstage Antics

9 October 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Ben, please, you're shouting again." Candi Sloat pleaded. "Where the FUCK is your husband?" Jacobson demanded. "If he had anything to do with that.. that... bullshit match, I'll wring his greasy neck myself. "It wasn't him,' the gorgeous blonde explained. "He really can't help what Bayer does. He just has such a weak-willed personality that he can't control the Grizz." "Right," Ben spat, settling on one of the chairs the medical attendants sat out for him, only after he threw the stretcher at them. "Then where the hell is the Grizz? I'd like a word with HIM." "Lookin' for me, Boy-ah?" A deep throated rumble sounded behind him, and Ben leapt from the chair, into the waiting arnms of what seemed to be dozens of wrestlers. They stood like a barrier between Bayer and Jacobson, and Ben was wild with the kind of desperate anger felt by stray dogs that are teased by small children. A desperate, helpless, blind rage. "You BASTARD." Jacobson screamed across the living wall of Indy wrestlers. "That match was for Philly. That was his sendoff. How could you-" "The way I heard it, boy-ah... lil' Philly got his sendoff a couple days ago. Get my... point?" Ben screamed, but no words formed. Just a gutteral, howling roar rushed from him as he leapt over the wrestlers holding him back. He reached, trying to grab a piece of that smug smile and rip it off that fat hillbilly's face, and could only scratch three deep fingernail grooves into Bayer's cheek before twenty strong hands held him down. "I'll kill you, you fucker!" Ben screamed hoarsely, struggling to stand against the weight of the restraining wrestlers as Bayer strode away. "I'll KILL YOU!!" In the corner, behind the achingly beatiful Candi Sloat, stood Detectives Doyle and Somers in matching tan trenchcoats and standard-issue shoes. They glanced at each other and scribbled in notepads as they watched the Wildman struggle against security. They had slipped back here hoping to get an innocent word with Jacobson after his match, but watching this display of aggression - of madness - this was much more than they could have hoped for. They had found their killer.
