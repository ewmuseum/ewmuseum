Tigres and Tears

10 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The music in the El Tigre was quieting as the club neared closing. The sun would be coming up shortly and the bartender personally couldn't wait. He'd wanted to leave since two in the morning but as long as there were people drinking, he had strict orders to keep the liquor flowing. When Mr. Jackson gave you an order, you followed it. He nonchalantly wiped down the bar as he approached the cute young man brooding into his glass. He always was a sucker for the somber, mysterious types. "Another double honey?" David Cross didn't bother to look up. "Yeah." The bartender poured yet another double whiskey. Cross couldn't even feel the liquor burning down his throat anymore. Hours of drinking had numbed him, unfortunately only physically. Mentally he was still in pain and no amount of alcohol had been able to take it away. Pills, booze, nothing at all was taking the aching emptiness away. He lifted his head and stared into the mirror across from him, showing just how bad he was doing. His skin was pasty and didn't look real. His eyes were drawn into his head, with black rings marked underneath them. The insomnia wasn't helping his misery. Maybe it was causing it. No, Dr. Cambridge seemed to think that his sleeplessness was a direct by-product of his problems. "Explain to me why you decided to come to a gay bar to get drunk?" Cross sighed. "It was the only club near my place that was open at this hour." "You're a fucking mess," Alex said as he stared at Cross with his hawk-like gaze. He turned back to his own drink and sipped it. "I don't think that Cambridge is any damn good." "She's helping," David mumbled. "She's listening to me. She says I'm making progress." "Well you're obviously doing great," Alex said sarcastically. "What did she say today?" David drained his glass and slid it a few inches in front of him. "She thinks I'm suffering from depression." Alex feigned surprise. "Depressed? I can't imagine how that could be. Your life is a smoldering pile of shit." The insults didn't put a dent in Cross anymore. There was just nothing left to hurt. "Thanks for the vote of confidence." "Well let's look at it. Your mother used to beat you when you were a kid and told you that you were adopted. You burn your head to a crisp doing that backyard wrestling crap. Tyler, your only friend, kills himself. You get your head patched up but get blown up in Tel Aviv where you're waist deep in body parts. You pick up a nifty scar in the process. Your adopted mother gets killed and you find the love of your life just in time to lose her. Did I miss anything?" "You missed the part where I lost a puppy when I was six." The techno music in the background was making Alex's head hurt. "Man I'm depressed just talking about it." David couldn't hear the music. "Dr. Cambridge thinks I might have deep emotional problems. She said something about me having a possible dissociative disorder." Alex finished off his own drink. "Yeah, you're nuts. So what now?" "I was thinking about Michelle." Michelle. Alex felt his heart race and decided to say what he felt. After all, it's not like David could feel any worse at this point. "So was I. She was one of the only people who could ever make me smile. I really like her Dave." He was right. David didn't seem to care. "Then you try reach her. I'm done. I've lost her for good." That royally pissed Alex off, and he let David know it. "So that's it? Williams wins and gets to shit all over her and you? I thought you were some kind of badass." "You're the badass. I'm just not like all those other guys who can just suck it up and go on. I've tried. I can't take it anymore." David's voice started to tremble. He began gently sobbing. "So much misery. So much disappointment. When do I get to be happy? What did I do to deserve so much pain?" Alex slid off his stood and stood beside David. "C'mon man, suck it up. The queers are looking. Now what happened to that daredevil? Where's the guy that dove off the top of the FanTron?" David rubbed the tears from his eyes. "You pushed me. I didn't want to jump off there." "Oh yeah, that's right." Alex blinked. "Good times." David looked at the empty glass in front of him. He thought about the pills in his pocket. Soon the pain would be over. He didn't know where Michelle was but she'd be better off without a loser like him in her life. Everyone would be better off without him. "So that's it. You're just giving up." "What do you want me to do? Nobody in the fWo wants to work with me because I'm a loser. I wanted to become a professional wrestler since I was a kid. It was my dream. I want to walk away, but wrestling's all I have left. I'm hanging onto an anchor and it's just dragging me down. What else can I do? I just can't take it anymore. I've lost everything." "I haven't." Alex sat down and stared at David's glass. "I'm not letting Michelle go. I'm not rolling over and dying. If Cody thought you were bad, he's gonna shit his pants once he has to deal with me. I've got nothing to lose, and nothing's more dangerous than a man with nothing to lose." The bartender looked out of the corner of his eye. The young man with the funny scar on his face was talking to himself again, and he sounded like he was getting irate about something. When he saw blood running down the side of his face, the bartender grabbed a paper towel and slid it over. "Are you okay honey?" The young man looked up and stared at him with one good eye, and one crimson eye. "My name's Alex, cum-catcher. Not honey." "Bitch," the bartender grumbled under his breath as he spun on his heels and stormed away. Maybe he wasn't that cute after all.
