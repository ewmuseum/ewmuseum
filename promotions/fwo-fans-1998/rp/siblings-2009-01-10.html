Siblings

10 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It's late. That was the first thought that went through Ivy's head. Her eyes fluttered open to see the clock radio in front of her. The LCD screen read 3:22. All she could see otherwise was the general outline of the room's furniture. The shades were drawn, and only the barest hint of light from the street managed to penetrate. Damn it, she said to herself, I'm awake now. She was set up like the 'little spoon,' and so she very carefully eased herself out of Triple X's arms. Shannon still occasionally woke up in the middle of the night, and, lately, he had been calling for Daddy instead of Mommy, so Ivy decided not to wake him. She did, however, kiss him on the cheek and pull the covers to his chin. A quick grab from the floor of Trip's boxers, and a Yankees jersey from the closet, and Ivy was out the door. They had rented sight unseen; it was really immaterial, what kind of setup they had, long as they could go to sleep and eat their meals in relative security, but Ivy had to admit, the realtor had done them well. It was a two - level condo with four bedrooms on the second floor. Two of them had their own full bathrooms, and there was a third for the other two rooms to split. Downstairs, there was a living room, full kitchen, another full bathroom, and a small, off - the - side room in which Ivy had set up her laptop and radio. For a moment, her eyes crossed one of the closed bedroom doors. Was Eli awake? If he wasn't, would he complain if she woke him up? She hated when she couldn't sleep, but having someone else around to talk to always made it better. Discretion, she thought. There'll be plenty of sleepless nights to come. Ivy walked downstairs as silently as she could, and made a beeline for her office. Everything was as she left it: her laptop was quietly running, the radio was on standby, and the cheap coffee maker had an old pot sitting in it. She sat at the desk and tapped a few keys. The FWO Wrestling website immediately popped up, and, typing in a long, complicated login, she waited. While waiting, she poured a cup of coffee. Its age would have driven anyone else away, but Ivy thrived on it. "Mike Bear," she said to nobody in particular, as she keyed in his official biography, "What's your story?" He was a former fWo World Champion, and he had fought in the Asylum. It was years ago, before she'd ever heard of the place, before Ender had become the God of Fight, before she'd made her deal with the devil. Still, he might know something. It was Ivy's nature to know who she was working with. Then she noticed her phone was beeping. It was plugged in on the other side of the desk, and a quick look showed one new text message. YOU AWAKE? It was from her brother, Brian, and it was sent four minutes ago. What was he doing up so early? Well, there was only one way to find out. "What the fuck are you doing up at this hour?" she asked, as she spun around once in her chair. "Going to work," replied Brian on the other end, "Oscar called me, Miranda went into labor." Oscar was in charge of the kitchen at the diner that she and Brian jointly owned, his wife was pregnant with their second child. "Did she deliver?" "Not yet. But I told him to go, that I'd be down to pick up the slack." "Good," replied Ivy, as she sipped her coffee, "I told him his schedule was as flexible as he needed it to be after the kid was born, just do me a favor and email me with whatever you give him?" "Will do," said Brian, "Which doesn't answer the other question." She was silent. "What the fuck are you doing up?" "Couldn't sleep," she answered, "so I thought I'd get some work done." "Let me guess," said Brian, "You're sitting at your computer drinking that horrible coffee sludge, wearing Sean's underwear and pinstripes. And you're obsessing over the roster you've got." She looked at herself. "Well....... which jersey am I wearing?" "You're doing research, so probably Casey." Ivy leaned back in the chair, trying her best to cover up the large number 37 on the back of the jersey, but couldn't help but stifle a laugh. "How do you do that?" "I know you, little sis," he said, "So what's bothering you about the roster?" "Nothing," she said, "well, nothing in particular at least. I just don't know half these guys." "Is that a problem?" "No, not as such," started Ivy, "it's just really strange for me to walk into a locker room as just another woman. I used to know the entire roster backwards and forwards. I knew everyone's strengths, weaknesses, who they worked best with and who they hated. I knew when people got to the arena and what everyone's post - show habits were." "You could still do that." Silence. "You know it's your choice, right?" "No, it isn't," said Ivy, "I gave up my rights to know everyone's business when I allowed Neo to walk out of the Garden with Jack's title belt and drop it in the ashes." "Still blame yourself, huh." "Buck stopped here." Brian sighed over the phone. "I guarantee you it's water under the bridge for most of the boys." "It's water under the bridge for me, too," replied Ivy, "I'm past it. I'm just not gonna to deny the facts is all." Brian was silent for a few seconds, and Ivy drained her cup. She turned her attention back to the computer and pressed a few keys. "You're doing fine," said Brian, finally. "I know," replied Ivy, "The first show is in four days, Beaven wants Eli to wrestle one of the new guys, and we're gonna see a world title match. He's doing it smart, we're starting slow and letting the brand build traction again." "That's not what I meant," he said, "I mean... Mom and Dad would be proud of you." Ivy considered this. "Good to talk to you, Brian," she finally answered, "listen, I'll give you a call when I'm back in New York on Thursday. Please tell Oscar to call me with the details as soon as Miranda delivers." "Sure thing," said Brian, with defeat in his voice. They had all been trying to get through to Ivy ever since the fWo's return was announced. To a degree, everyone had been successful, but she still carried a lot of guilt over the prior closing. She was one of the most self - sufficient, and most stubborn people he had ever known. To that end, she was very much her father's daughter, but she also carried their mother's nurturing tendencies, and Ivy was usually not happy unless she was taking care of someone. While Shannon occupied most of her thoughts these days, she didn't go to work with him, so it was a jones that she would never fully fill. "Love you," said Ivy. "You too," replied Brian, and the call terminated. Ivy sat in silence for nearly a minute, staring at the tiny phone in her hand. Brian was right, of course. Everyone who had told her that the events of Countdown 2005 weren't her fault was right. She knew it, she just refused to accept it. Wednesday, she said to herself. After Wednesday, I'll know if it's really okay. After Wednesday, I'll know if we really did survive it. For now, she returned her attention to the screen in front of her, and her attempts to familiarize herself with the new fWo family. It was a long time before she went back to bed.
