January 15th, 2004 - Room 214

15 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
January 15, 2004. New York Presbyterian Hospital Room 214. Jasmin leaned over the hospital bed, kissing the sleeping Pierre on his forehead. She'd spent the better part of the last two weeks in the hospital with him, and didn't feel particularly guilty stepping out while he dozed. About one hundred feet down the hall was what the hospital called a family room, though in all reality it was just another variation of a waiting room. The furniture was better than it was in the Emergency Room waiting area, and it was definitely more comfortable and relaxing, but that's all it was. Jasmin had a small bag over her shoulder, carrying a few essentials for her break. She strode into the dimly lit family room, pleased to see that it was vacant for the time being. Laying claim to the couch she stretched out, and rummaged through the sack. The New York Times, having been read through twice already, was passed over, as was a tawdry romance novel she'd found at a bookstore a few blocks down the road. Finally, she retrieved a small blue square, as well as some headphones, and what appeared to be a jack of some sorts. She unfolded the square and powered it on, then connected the jack to the headphones, and then to the Game Boy. It had been loaned to her by Allen Brown, though she imagined she'd have it for a while now, not thinking that she'd be heading to Florida any time soon. Little jewels fell down the screen, while an anthromorphic cat yelled at a blonde man in a red gi. Jasmin allowed herself a giggle. After everything she'd been through the last several months, she had earned it. Now that Pierre was out of the woods, it was easier to laugh, to smile. He'd be in the hospital a little bit longer for some continued testing, and it would be a while before he could walk unassisted, let alone wrestle, but for a man who'd been shot and left to die in a fire, he'd been incredibly lucky. If she hadn't been so immersed in her game, if she hadn't been facing away from the window that looked out into the hallway, she might have thought differently. She might have seen a figure slowly making his way from Pierre's room. As it were, she missed seeing him entirely, as he pressed his hands against the glass and watched her forlornly. He didn't waste much time with this, and shambled down the hallway, continuing away from Pierre's room, having done what he needed there. It would be twenty minutes before Jasmin got up to stretch, and even then she would almost miss seeing the bloody handprint.
