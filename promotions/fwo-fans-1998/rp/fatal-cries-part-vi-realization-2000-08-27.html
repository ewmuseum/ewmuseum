Fatal Cries, Part VI (Realization)

27 August 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Fatal Cries, Part VI (Realization) He woke up again, for the third straight night since being put inside of the mental health institution. His feet touched the cold floor and he shivered. Everything seemed so cold these days to him. He was only a few days from being realized from the facility. Then he'd return to his cold basement room and to those thoughts. He could never sleep through an entire night anymore. Something would always bring him wide awake, whether it was another patient yelling out something or the screaming of a patient being transferred somewhere else. It was eerie, really. Everything was white, the walls, floors, bed sheets, clothing, it was all white. White was the sign of purity and everything that was supposed to be good in life. People who were black inside were forced to where white. He always thought that was a foolish thing to do. He could tell that this was a place used to brainwash people into thinking they were sane. He knew it was the doctors, that were insane, not him. They were trying to mold him into a person that wouldn't remember his past at all. He knew that he was there because he had tried to kill himself, over and over again. He thought he couldn't have failed that last time. They were supposed to think he was just sleeping, but somehow, someway his aunt and uncle knew something was wrong. "It was their fault," he muttered. Those were the first words he had spoken since they admitted him to the hospital nearly two months ago. They were worried about what he could do to himself next. After he had slit his wrist three months ago, he thought for sure he had done it. But then the girl that lived across the street found him laying in a pool of blood that his wrist had created. Then, finally after the overdose on the sleeping pills, he was put into this place. "I never did anything wrong," he remarked. "I don't deserve to be here. This is not my place." He thought he was right that he didn't belong here. The blackened hearts of murderers who got off on insanity pleas were the ones who belonged here. Not an innocent boy that tried to commit suicide. So very untrue, though. He was just as deserving as the rest of the criminals and oddballs that were in here for the rest of their lives. The one difference was that he was here as a rehabilitation to try and straighten his life out. On the outside, it seemed to be working well, but on the inside he was only eating himself up more. His body was torn between itself. Each half of his brain fighting the other for what was supposed to happen and what was happening. It was truly a losing battle, in the end he would lose everything he had, including life. But on the outside he tried to make it look like he was improving and feeling better. He had to. It was the only way to get out. He knew that the doctors would come in three or four times a day, trying to analyze his thoughts, feelings, emotions and whatever else they could think of. But they only got lies. They never got what they truly wanted out of the boy. In a sense, he controlled them. He knew his way around their questions, twisting the truth into his own reality. He lifted his feet back up from the cold floor. He turned his head towards the small window against the far wall, a sliver of moonlight found its way into the room and cascaded some light into the room. He let out a long sigh. He knew this was the last night he would ever have to sleep in this room again. He wouldn't miss the hard bed, the cold floor and the solitary confinement it had. The room was everything he didn't want or need. He really needed people, no matter how much he wanted to be alone. But was being alone so bad? It did have its advantages. He had grown accustomed to it in the mental institution. It gave him time to sort everything out. He was still trying to figure out things about himself and his own life. He didn't even know who he really was. Nobody ever called him by his name. His parents just beat him relentlessly, calling him ‘boy,' ‘child,' and some profanities. His aunt and uncle never talked to him, almost as if he didn't exist. Just another item collecting dust in the cellar for the next yard sale. And then there was that girl from across the street. He had known her since he was in first grade, but couldn't remember her name for the life of him. She had saved him from dying. Her name and face were just a mystery though. The doctors were nameless as well, they were just stuffy men in suits trying to worm information out of him. He didn't know who or what to believe in life. He laid back down on the bed. For once it seemed a little soft and comforting. He put his arms behind his bed and looked up at the dark ceiling, his eyes wandering across the darkness looking for a sign of something. His mouth opened wide, like he was going to yawn, but nothing came out. It was all just now starting to come back to him. "Mary, Ben," he whispered. "My parents. Beating me daily, until I could stand no more. They were named Ben and Mary... Ben and Mary... Winters." His mind raced. It seemed like it was working for the first time. Everything flushed into his head at once. Everything he was supposed to know and learn in time that he had forgotten had hit him like a pile of bricks. His eyes filled with excitement as everything quickly rushed back into his head. "Aunt Lucy, Uncle Joe," he whispered. "They took me in after my father killed my mother and himself. They have a son, Trent... he's nineteen and goes to the University of Michigan. I'm their nephew, Trent's cousin. I've lived there since I was twelve, four years ago I moved from Detroit to Dearborn." A large smile came across his face. He shot up from the bed into the upright position. He stood up and walked over to the door and began pounding on it. He needed to get out now, he was okay, everything was fine. He began to yell aloud to absolutely nobody. "Monica Davidson," he yelled. "She saved my life, found me in the basement when I slit my wrist. Without her I wouldn't be alive right now! I wouldn't be in this room right now!" Someone must have heard the yelling, because the boy quieted down as he heard footsteps approaching his room. He saw a face in the high window on the door. It was his doctor. "Excellent, you're finally coming around," the doctor said. "Now tell me one more thing. Who are you?" The boy looked at himself and slumped down onto the floor, tears began to form in his eyes. "I am nobody," he choked on the tears. "I'm nothing to everybody, I'm nobody. No one cares about me, I am nothing." The doctor looked a little shocked from the response. "No, no. Think harder, you know who you really are." "You're right, I am NOTHING!" He yelled. "I've never been anything, or anyone and I never will be." "You need to look deep inside of yourself," the doctor was almost yelling. "Now tell me, WHO ARE YOU?" The boy looked confused. His eyes slowly looked up at the doctor, then back at the floor where they closed. His entire life began to flash before his eyes. He wasn't nobody, he knew exactly who he was. He just didn't want to admit it to anybody, not even himself. His tearful eyes glanced at the doctor before going back down to the floor. It was right on the tip of his tongue. He just needed to open his mouth, the words and name would come out. He slowly got back to his feet and walked over towards the door. His head was level with the small window, he glared into the doctor's eyes and his mouth opened. "I am Donaven Winters." The End.
