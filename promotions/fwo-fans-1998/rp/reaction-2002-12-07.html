Reaction.

7 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A huge grin came across Donaven's face, his slate eyes lit up. "I guess I'm number one on Scott Wasner's most wanted list." The fans began to boo as "Seizure of Power" came in over the loudspeakers. Winters raised his arms in his trademark crucifix position before sliding out of the ring. Trash rained down on him as he made his way up the entrance ramp. Before he disappeared behind the curtain he flashed a devious grin and Warfare went to commercial. Scott Wasner switched off the television. He looked at the afterimages on the screen for a few moments, his face showing a surprising lack of emotion. Wasner was in the living room of his Seattle house, sitting on a leather couch. The decor around him still spoke of his earlier days, when money had been no object and the more expensive a thing was, the more likely it was to find its way into his house. He wasn't entirely sure why he hadn't gone to Warfare this week. Some of it was respect for Travis, the once-again owner of the fWo. The man had given him his break in wrestling, and Wasner didn't want to keep screwing up his shows. Mostly, though, it was a gut feeling. He knew the man was looking for was a coward - he already knew - and it was clear he wouldn't reveal himself when Wasner was in striking distance. So, Wasner decided to sit back and let the man responsible for sending the letter come to him - either by bragging on the show or literally coming to him. It wasn't his first choice. He very badly wanted to barge in Warfare and take out every ounce of the hate and anger that had been infesting him. Knock somebody out. Break things. Turn the fWo backstage area into a crescendoing opera of screams and shatters until he had his man. What he would do then, he wasn't sure. Self-control and reason had won out, though, for now. So it was Winters. One question begged itself. Why? Wasner couldn't think of a reason, but he found he didn't care much. Questions were for later. Why was for when Wasner was through with Winters... if Winters could still talk afterwards. He realized the phone was ringing. Reluctantly, Wasner picked it up. Probably another idiot asking him to join their fed. "Yeah." "Scott, don't do anything stupid." It was Tony, better known to fWo fans as Ice. The man formerly known as the Big Blond Machine had left the fed at the same time as Wasner, when his run under a regrettable if somewhat amusing Vanilla Ice gimmick had come to an end. He had done plenty of work for the fed - both as a wrestler and a road agent - and management had been kind enough to accomodate his request for a voided contract. Now he made a good living as the owner of a seafood restaurant off the Columbia in his native Portland. "Tony..." "Don't blow me off, Scott. I know you. I know how you operate. You're gonna do something really stupid on Sunday, I just don't know what. And I'm telling you, as your friend, don't do it." "Tony, were you even watching? Do you know what this guy did? When I finally found Stephanie again last May, I was lucky she was even willing to look at me. And with all the things I've screwed up in my life, that I went back home without her wishing I was being torn apart by rabid wolves was pretty much a good thing. You know, I don't think I could ever really make it right... but at least we made it better. A little. And then Winters goes and does this? It's not like, you know, I had any thoughts of getting back together with her. Dreams... sure. But I knew that was all I had. We agreed that we were bad for each other, and that's still the absolute truth. And damned if I know how Winters found out about that anyway... but the point is, she still means more to me than anybody else, and Winters destroyed that. She took out a restraining order on me because of him, Tony. I still can't believe she didn't take me to court because of him." "I know. I'm not gonna say I understand what you're going through, because I respect you more than that. But I understand how you could be thinking what you're thinking. But Scott... don't do it. Stay away from Slaughter. Call Stephanie, tell her about him, get her to take him to court. Do this the right way. The civilized way. For once in your life, act like somebody normal." There was no reason he should say no. Why not? Winters deserved jail time, or whatever he would get for this. And instead of doing something exceedingly stupid, instead of being the old Wasner again, he would take care of the matter like anybody else would. The smart way. "Tony... you know I can't call her. And you know you can't stop me from doing what I'm going to do, and you know you can't convince me." Wasner could almost see him hang his head on the other end of the line, and he did the same thing himself. There was silence for a few moments. Then, "What are you going to do?" Wasner thought for a moment, then answered truthfully. "I don't know. I really don't know. But I think once I've done it, I'm going to regret it. Maybe for a few minutes, maybe for a few years, maybe for... ever." "Scott... why are you so fucking self-destructive?" He had no answer, but he didn't need one. Tony had hung up. Wasner set the phone down and got up off the couch. He stood for a moment, processing, then came to a realization. Just because he had refused Tony's request didn't mean he still had to go. He wasn't bound to it. He knew as well as anybody else that Tony was right. A part of him realized that this was his last chance. He had two choices. One would lead, more than likely, to a peaceful resolution. The other promised disaster for a lot of people. "Tomorrow morning," he said. He didn't know where Slaughter was being held, but he could find that out. Tomorrow morning, before the sun rose, he would go. And on Sunday, he would do what he had to do. ----- Two messages from Scott Wasner's answering machine. "Thursday, December 5, 2002. 6:43 AM." "Scott, pick up if you're there. Dammit, Scott, tell me you're there. Tell me you realized how stupid you were being. Come on, pick up. Wake up, pick up, whatever. Scott? Pick up! Fuck." "Thursday, December 5, 2002. 8:12 PM." "Scott? This... this is Stephanie. I guess I'll just get to the point. Of the the gals I work with - one of my girlfriends - well, her husband watches wrestling, and he saw the show yesterday... and... well, she told me what that Winters guy did. I never liked him. I mean, I never talked to him or anything, but those few times I saw him in the locker room... there was something about him. Something. Something bad. I don't know why he would do something like this... but, uh... well, I'm gonna talk to a lawyer tomorrow and I'm gonna see what I can do about it. I'm withdrawing the restraining order... of course. I don't need to tell you that's not an invitation to come out and see me. But you at least deserve not to have that hanging over your head. And, Scott... don't go off and do something stupid. You know better. You should. I don't want to see you get hurt, and I don't want to see you do something you're going to regret. If I turn on the news tomorrow, or the day after, or Monday, or whenever, and I hear your name, I swear I'll find you and kick your ass myself. OK? And... I need to - no. Forget it. Just, for once in your life, do the right thing. The smart thing. And call me when you get this." "End of messages."
