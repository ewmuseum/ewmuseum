Tough Choices - Part Three

10 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
March had come in like a lion at the Temple of the Golden Phoenix. Japan had suffered an unusual cold snap, which mean another foot of snow that had to be shovelled off the Steps to Enlightenment outside the sacred building. Despite its distant location from any major city in Japan, the road through the foothills was still well-travelled by those looking for spiritual harmony. Not to mention the Golden Phoenix's annual three day Tea Festival. It wasn't really the tea that bought people in droves to the Temple. Much like the Shaolin Monks, the Temples' Disciples put on an artistic display of their amazing physical talents. Some of their detractors called them Shaolin Clones, due to the similarity between many of their acts. However, the Temple was home to not just the monks of the Golden Phoenix. At any time, the Temple housed many different visitors from around the globe. They too put on a display, to both thank their host and entertain the visitors. Put two trained Sports Entertainers in the mix, and you've got quite a show. "The Mecca" Alex Creed and his friend, David "Crucifix" Cross, put on a brief wrestling match of sorts. Alex, dressed as a fashionable Westerner, sat among the event's spectators during the performance. When a young girl stepped out onto the floor and began a slow ceremonail dance, a Black Ninja quicklined from the room and snatched her up under one arm. The girl would scream and, on cue, Alex would stand up and charge the floor. He'd confront the Ninja, calling him a coward in Japanese and challenging him to face him, if he dared. The CruciNinja, of course, dared. The girl was tossed to Alex. He'd catch her, just as the Ninja kicked him in the head. He'd fall to the stone floor, thankful for the padded vest he wore underneath his suit jacket, and let the girl roll away safely. He'd kippup to his feet and from there, for the next five minutes the Hero and the Ninja would exchange both wrestling and martial arts blows. They'd culimate the finish with the Ninja being dispatched in various ways, the Hero standing victorious, and the little girl standing off to the side, clapping and laughing with glee. On the third day, Alex and David stole the show with their finish. The two fought their way onto a nearby stage and the Mecca landed what appeared to be a knockout blow. Alex, wearing padded forearm and knee guards under his clothes, climbed atop a scaffold set up behind the stage and nailed a beautiful Shooting Star Press, driving both himself and CruciNinja through the stage. The normally reserved crowd actually popped. After the festival was over, Master Yoeda thanked them both for their performance. "You have such skill," Yoeda said with almost a hint of a smile. "And you," he said to David. "You... you shovel the stairs tonight." The two changed out of their costumes, somewhat gingerly. "It really would kill him to compliment me I think," David said and poked at the bruise on his shoulder. "How's your elbow?" "It's not broken," Alex replied while slowly straightening his arm. "I'll probably need to ice it for a day or so, assuming Master Yoeda gives us a break." "You can help me shovel the steps tonight. I'll shovel, you ice your elbow." Somehow, despite the physicality both men displayed, neither one suffered serious injury. Alex's elbow took a stiff whack when they fell through the stage, and that was the worst injury either of them took. Surprising, considering both of them were bumping on stone with maybe an inch of padding on their bodies for protection. The Festival had just made things worse for Alex, as far as the Wrestling Bug went. His mother had recovered to the point where she could travel, so she met Alex stateside for a MMA competition. Alex won the match, and afterward spent the evening discussing his future with Sally. "Wasn't tonight much more satisfying than wrestling?" she asked her son. "It was a different kind of rush than what I get when I'm wrestling," Alex told her. "But yeah, it was pretty cool." "Brent, Lindsay, Tiffany, they were all so proud of you." Sally touched her son's cheek. "I was proud of you." Alex smiled. Sally kept her hand on his face. "Please stop wrestling." Alex pulled away. "What? Why?" "Why? How about the fWo being taken off the air because of everything that's been going on! The riots!" The color started to drain out of Sally's cheeks. "Between that and Vic, I don't want you wrestling anymore! It's too dangerous!" "What if I wrestled somewhere else?" Alex couldn't believe what he was hearing.
