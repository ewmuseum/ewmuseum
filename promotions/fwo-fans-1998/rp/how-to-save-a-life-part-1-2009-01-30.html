How to Save a Life: Part 1

30 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"You've got a visitor." The old man with the silver hair looked up from his book to the orderly at the door. From what he understood, only family was allowed to visit, and he had no family to speak of. "Who is it?" he asked. "Your daughter," replied the orderly. He breathed a sigh of relief. "Thank you." The orderly closed the door and the man with the silver hair suddenly felt nervous. For the past three years, ever since he entered the facility, he had essentially been waiting for someone from his old life to come and snuff him out. No loose ends, and all. After three years, a visitor finally showed up, someone who identified herself as his daughter. That narrowed the possibilities down to one. But what was he going to say to her? What could he say that would make up for everything. There was a knock at the door again, and he realized he was out of time. "Hello, Fox." Silver Fox turned around and looked into Ivy McGinnis' face. She stood at the door, her arms folded across her chest, waiting. Fox took another deep breath. "Hello, Ivy. Come in?" She stepped towards him. For three years, she had sent him cards on his birthday and Christmas, books, photos, newspaper clippings about things she had been working on, and all of the various rumors that had surrounded the FWO since William Beaven first tried to get things started again. At this point, Ivy McGinnis truly was his only family. On the other hand, this was the first time they were face to face since before he was clean. He had disappeared from her life as Smith's plans came to fruition, and there was an awkward empty space between-- Of course, the fact that Ivy just grabbed him and hugged him negated any residual awkwardness. "I missed you," she said, with her arms around his neck. Slowly, his arms went around her waist. "And I, you." They let each other go, and Ivy reached into her bag. "Listen, I'm sorry I haven't come to see you before. There's really no excuse, but I've been incredibly busy. Between the Garden's tour and recording the new record to Shannon and motherhood I didn't really have a moment to myself for a really long time." He gestured to the chair next to his desk, and he sat on the small bed. "I understand completely," he said, "Life goes on for most of us." Ivy sensed a change in his mood, but she stopped herself from asking about it. "Anyways, I have some letters for you--" "What?" he asked suddenly, "From who?" "From... some of the boys," replied Ivy, confused, "William has started the FWO up again. We've had three broadcasts so far and a handful of spot shows and I think there's a good buzz about it. But I happened to mention last week that I was going to try and come out to see you, and Pierre, Ric, Deacon, Matthew, and Shadows all wrote you a note and asked me to deliver." She handed over the small handful of sealed envelopes, and Fox appeared to relax again. "Thank you," he said, "So, the company is reopened again? I'm glad to see you're a part of it; they need you." Ivy smiled. "I'm just a cog in the wheel, Fox. I actually didn't want to go back. Eli brokered the deal himself." "I thought he retired." "He did," she replied with a shrug, "but he wanted to help William out with some name recognition, and he convinced me that it was the right thing to do. I'm kinda glad of that, actually." "But you're just a manager now," stated Fox, with a touch of resignation in his voice. It was Ivy's turn to take a deep breath. "That's how it has to be," she replied, "You know what happened on my watch." "It wasn't your fault." "I know that," she retorted, "and even if I didn't, everyone's been so careful to walk on flippin' eggshells around me when it comes to Countdown, it's practically been beaten into my brain." "'Flippin'?" "I'm trying to watch my language now that I'm a Momma Bear," she explained, "even when the little prince isn't around. Would you like to meet him?" Fox smiled. "I'd love to." "I'll try to bring him next time." "Good, I'd like that," said Fox, as he cocked his head and studied her. "But you're not really happy being relegated to the ring, are you?" Well, thought Ivy, isn't this why you wanted to talk to him to begin with? "It's... I don't know. I liked being involved. I liked helping. I mean, after Spyder no-showed the first show--" Fox clicked his tongue disapprovingly, having had a long history and longer memory when it came to Spyder. "--and I managed to get in touch with Slugger for the main event, I liked the fact that I was able to pull that together for everyone. But to do it all the time? Zimmerman, of all people, asked me about it this past show and I told him pretty bluntly that my choices are to either come back out and be in that arena and do what's needed, or hide under the bed. That's not a choice to me. But I think about everything that we went through and everything we didn't make it through, and I can't help but wonder if he's right." "What's happened would have traumatized anyone," replied Fox, keeping his voice level, "the fact that you're refusing to let it keep you out of the arena is a testament to your character." "Maybe," said Ivy, "but between Neo's army in Nassau and the fire at the Garden... it's been years and a lot's changed, but I just don't know. William and his new talent coordinator have both offered me a consulting job but... Right now I can go to the ring with Eli or with Sean, do the match, and be done with it. I can have time for a life, and time for my family. If I go back behind the curtain I'll need to be able to see in six different directions at once, and I'm just afraid..." "You're a leader," argued Fox, as he shook his head, "not a follower. And there's nothing wrong with being afraid." "I know. I really do. But if something happened again, I don't know if I could deal with it." "If something happened," repeated Fox, "what difference would it make if you're just a manager or if you're part of the staff? You never had enough confidence in yourself, kiddo." Their conversation was suddenly interrupted by a knock at the door. "Visiting hours are over," sounded the gruff orderly's voice. "Looks like our time is up," said Fox, as he stood up and hugged her, "Thank you for the letters, Ivy." "Thanks for listening to me babble," she replied, "I'm sorry for just going on and on like that." "No, no," said Fox, "You say what you need to when you're here. I've got no problem listening." She squeezed him a bit, then they let go of their embrace and Ivy walked out the door. "I'll be back, probably the same time next week." "I look forward to it," replied Fox, as the door closed behind her. He walked to the desk, where the small stack Ivy left for him sat, waiting to be opened. It's nice to get friendly letters for a change, he thought, and it was nice to see her again. Fox had never seen Ivy so unsure of herself before. He wondered if it was all Countdown 2005 that had put her in such a state, but quickly caught himself. Countdown 2003 hadn't been all that easy on himself, either. Whatever she needed, if she could find part of it by coming to visit, then the least he could do was listen.
