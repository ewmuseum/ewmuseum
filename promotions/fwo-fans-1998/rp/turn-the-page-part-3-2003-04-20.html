Turn The Page - Part 3

20 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Sunday, April 20 Vic Creed stepped out of the taxi and walked into the hospital. He'd just returned to the United States when he heard the news about his sister. Marilyn, whose only crime was being the sister of Vic Creed, had spent the past year learning to walk again after a terrible beating at the hands of people who wanted to send Vic a message. She was also still partially blind in one eye from the beating. Maybe that was why she didn't see this coming. Vic walked up to the front desk. "Marilyn Creid," he said. The nurse pressed a few buttons on the keyboard in front of her. "I'm sorry sir, but she's in intensive care. Only immediate family can see her right now." "I'm her brother." For now, he added to himself. Vic walked down the hallway and stepped into the elevator. Sarah was safe now, placed in foster care until she could be placed with a foster family. She was in the governments hands now, and Darryl wouldn't take the chance to try anything with her. Too many eyes would be on him if he did, and that's the last thing he needed. That also meant the Vic wouldn't be able to have any contact with her either. As the elevator doors closed, Vic also saw another door closing. After all the emotional sacrifice he'd made for his daughter, he'd made one last final one for her own good. She needed to be far away from him, whether she thought so or not. If he was lucky, she'd hate him so much that she'd run away if she saw him coming. I'm not safe to be around. Even I'm not safe around myself. The room had an antiseptic smell that Vic was all-too familiar with. The respirator clicked and hissed, the EKG monitor beeped rhythmatically. Other than that, the room was silent as death. Or near-death, which was where his sister was. She'd been floating in and out of consciousness, a concern with the swelling around her brain. Vic was footing the bill for her medical expenses, and would pay for the wheelchair ramps at her house and the twenty-four hour medical attendants she'd have to have with her. But this was the last time he'd ever see her. The room was dark, but Marilyn was bathed in the green glow from the monitors which surrounded her. She looked horrible from what Vic could make out. It was hard to even tell that the woman lying in the bed in front of him was even his sister. As he watched the tubes running into her nose and down her throat flex with the oxygen being forced through them, he knew that she hated him with a loathing he couldn't even begin to imagine. Twice now, she was forced to take punishment that he should have taken. Twice he had escaped unharmed. For five minutes, Vic stood at the side of Marilyn's bed. Every cut, every bruise, every tube and bandage was committed to memory and filed away. Darryl may have used Marilyn to send a message, but it wasn't the one Vic received. This is what happens when people get near me, Vic told himself. This is why it's best that I'm alone. Having absorbed all he needed to, Vic took one final look at his sister. Darryl might come after him now, but Vic wasn't worried. He'd been shot, bludgeoned, caught in a plane crash, and had a building blow up around him. Yet here he stood, bigger and better than ever. The Alaskan Juggernaut couldn't be stopped, and Vishanti would learn that if he tried anything stupid. Vic turned his back on his sister, physically and emotionally. "I'd say I'm sorry," Vic said as he walked away for the final time. "But somehow I don't think you'd care." The door closed behind him as he left the room, leaving only the sounds of the machines to keep Marilyn company. But somewhere in the near darkness, a single tear rolled down the side of Marilyn Creid's face.
