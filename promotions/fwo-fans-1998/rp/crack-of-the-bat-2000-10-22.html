Crack of the Bat

22 October 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Slaughter had ended a few hours ago, but he still sat in his dressing room. Clutched in his left hand was the silver aluminum baseball bat that he had found in his locker room earlier in the night. In front of him was the wreckage that had once been a table before it met his and the bat's wrath. Anger had replaced the outward expression of sorrow and that solemn feeling of being lost. Things were changing again, nearly weekly. He couldn't keep his true feelings straight anymore, Monte Burns was controlling the strings and he had him dancing upon the stage. Everything Burns wanted him to do, he'd pull the string and it'd happen just like that. As angry as he was, he was completely powerless. Sure, the score was tied at two apiece, but Burns had the upper-hand. Both of them knew it. He had only thought of the notes as idle threats until tonight. The bat had been the strongest point of everything that had been the past two years of his life, it represented everything that had gone wrong. It represented the Grade III concussions, the scarred losses of people he considered friends. A person he hadn't talked to in over a year, and somehow, someway, Monte Burns knew about all of this and was using it to his advantage. There was nothing he could do now, but to wait for Halloween Hell to see if the threats from the notes, bat, cheese and roses would all come true. "It's all happening so fast," he said aloud and to himself. "Monte... Burns, you have everything you want, just don't do this to me, please. I'd get down on my hands and knees to get you to stop from doing this to me. It's all I ask and it's all I want. I don't want you to go through with whatever it is you're going to do at Halloween Hell." He paused, the grip of the bat grew stronger, his knuckles turned white. "I'm serious Burns," he repeated. "This is all over a measly piece of gold. Don't turn this into a personal matter, Monte, trust me and don't do it. If you make this personal... I can promise you pain, I will turn everything good in your life bad. Burns, you can bribe my former friends, throw wads of your dirty cash into their faces and delve into my personal life, but you will not be able to do it without a price." He began to raise the bat up from the floor, moving it back and forth in a methodical sort of way. "Personal, Monte," he said again. "Personal is bad for both of us. I know what this bat means more than you do. I know what it feels like against the side of my skull better than you do. And if I must, I will make you very, very familiar with this bat if I have to. Don't make the wrong mistake, Monte. Don't make it personal, because I will bring you down hard, no matter what the cost. I will bring you down." He slowly stood up, putting his other hand around the bottom of the bat and he swung at the furniture again, smashing the already broken table into smaller pieces. He had gone from depressed to angry, quite a deadly combination.
