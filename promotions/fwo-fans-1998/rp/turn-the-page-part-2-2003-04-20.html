Turn The Page - Part 2

20 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Thursday, April 17 I just watched a man I've known for years throw his life, and the life of his family, down the toilet. Kodiak Vic Creed has, for as long as I've known him, looked out for himself before anyone else. He was hungry, even back when we wrestled in Japan. He was always learning, always trying to pick up a move or technique. But the bottom line was, after it was all said and done, he wanted to be the best. The big dog. He wanted to be me. Or what he thought I was. The man didn't have direction before he came to Japan. He was drifting along without real focus. I showed him the big picture. I showed him what he could be. Between me and his trainer, Slade Wilson, he began to see things clearly. I lit a fire under his ass and he became a success. Well, successful for a wrestler. But Vic only saw the picture I let him see. There's a whole other world that he only caught a glimpse of. It was a dark place, one with plenty of bad people and even worse monsters. Things he saw in the ring couldn't come close to comparing with what this dark place held. Now there, I was the big dog. We'd been friends for some time when I got the word that Vic had started a job as a bounty hunter. I thought it could be a problem, since his new job would put us on opposite sides of the fence. When he started bringing in some of my people, measures had to be taken. So I set him up to get shot in the head. Me, the godfather of his daughter. Unfortunately, the lucky son of a bitch survivied. I relocated operations, Vic got a new job, and I thought we'd never bump heads again. I got back into the wrestling business to take my operations to a more global scale. Things were progressing nicely. One morning, I got a phone call from one of my people. Apparently Vic had pissed off the wrong people... again. But this time he wasn't supposed to be taken out. He was supposed to suffer for awhile before the end came. It was easy for me to get involved. Vic brought me into the fWo to "help out my career." He has no idea how much he's helping me. Vic walked out of the bathroom. For a guy who just flushed the only thing keeping his sister and daughter safe, he looked quite at ease with himself. He finished packing quickly, since we only had about two hours before our flight back to the States. We checked in at the airport and had about fifteen minutes to wait before boarding. Normally he'd be chatting it up about wrestling, practicing a promo, or talking strategy about his Cyberslam match. But today he didn't say a thing. He just sat there with that look of quiet calm on his face, like a terminal patient who'd just come to terms with his own death. About an hour into our flight, I knew what I had to do. It was time to earn my pay. I went to the bathroom and crammed myself into the small piss-closet. Once the door was closed, I made the phonecall. "Darryl. He's done." I knew Vic would know it was me that made the call. His family's blood would be on my hands. But I'm used to bloody hands. They'd be plenty bloodier before I was finished.
