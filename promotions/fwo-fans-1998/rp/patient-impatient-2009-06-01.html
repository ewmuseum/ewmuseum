Patient, Impatient

1 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Well you've certainly earned the nickname 'Wrought Iron Kid'," the doctor remarked as he pushed the glasses higher on the bridge of his nose, the convex lenses reflecting blue abstract shapes in the x-ray glow. "Your medical history is remarkable..." he turned around and smiled. "Your body is the stuff of medical journals." Brawn missed the compliment. His head was too overwhelmed--eyes on the linoleum. "Thanks, I guess," Brawn replied. He sighed, tapping his heel against the examining table. "What's the matter?" the doctor asked, leaning against the wall. "Is your wrist hurting now?" "Only a little bit." Brawn replied. His shoulders drooped and his teeth clenched on the flesh in his cheek. "So how long am I out?" "Couple weeks." He paused and craned his head to the side to get in Brawn's eyeline. "You will be able to participate at Meltdown." Brawn looked down at the cast on his wrist and grinned wide. "However, after watching the tape, I must recommend that you not. If Jeff Garvin finds out that his plan to injure you has backfired, he will come after you with something twice as terrible... more terrible than you could ever imagine. I've administered to some of his victims in The Squared Circle. He's ended careers with the kind of routine apathy one reserves for grocery shopping." "I don't remember asking for your opinion," Brawn replied firmly, gravel in his throat. "I'm on borrowed time. You deal with my bones, I'll deal with the bonehead." "Very well," he replied with a weak grin, looking away and landing his eyes on the glowing X-Ray display. "One hairline fracture. After all that punishment. It's unbelievable." "Good genes." "That's not what I heard," the doctor said, "One of my colleagues told me about your father." The words escaped casually as he jotted a few notes. Brawn's hand balled into a fist and his body turned to stone. "Rough deal," the doctor said. He paused and sucked on the cap of his pen, eyes skyward. "You know, come to think of it, it makes sense that you wanna get back as quickly as possible. Don't want to leave any money on the table." "What's that supposed to mean?" Brawn was standing now, taking the stance of a Grizzly bear. "Nothing, I was just told you've been putting up a lot of money for his treatments..." "You're awfully fucking brave suggesting that's the only reason why I'm out there every week..." Brawn growled, fierce and quiet. The doctor patted the folder. "Well, you were on the pro baseball fast track before that accident..." Brawn pants and the room bends in the wake of this painful reminder. "The money isn't as good in that FWO circus, but I would presume it's pretty good for a high school drop out..." The doctor suddenly felt the air sweep between the floor and his feet as Brawn drove him into the wall. The man crumbled to the floor, plaster dust trailing behind his scalp. Brawn began scanning the room over his shoulder and let out a quivering sigh as he retreated. "Look, I'm sorry." Brawn said, scratching his head and looking sheepish as a nurse arrived to investigate. He reached down to take the doctor's twiggy fingers and lead him to his feet. The doctor slapped his hand away. "Good luck with Garvin. You animals deserve each other," the doctor muttered, snarling.
