Christmas Rest

26 December 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Note: The following events took place on December 25th, 2001. Darkness has fallen upon Southern California on this Christmas evening. Christmas lights cover even the largest of homes, with figurines of Santa, reindeer, and other symbols of Christmas sitting in their yards. The streets are virtually deserted, as families sit down to dinner inside their homes. At the home of Monte Burns, his mansion on the west coast, the lights on the home are not nearly as pronounced. Some lights here, a few lights there. Inside of the house, a small tree sits in the family room, adorned with a few ornaments but not very many. The house is empty, with Monte's butler at home with his family. Outside, at the rear of the mansion, Burns sits in his jacuzzi, drinking champagne and looking up at the dark sky. "Merry Christmas," Burns mumbles to himself. "Merry fucking Christmas." It had been three days since his last battle with Donaven Winters. The war that had raged for four months concluded in a Last Man Standing that neither was willing to lose easily. While Winters has come out on top, Burns felt more embarrassed that Monica Davidson had turned her back against him and returned to her friendship with Donaven. Never had someone turned against Burns and his wishes. Rarely had he been told his money wasn't good enough. Then, Monica and Donaven came along and beat him. They both beat him, and Monte knew it all too well. For now, Burns would move on and focus his attention on other things. But he would always have that thought in his mind. Winters had beat him. Winters and Monica had beat him. As Monte took another drink from his glass, the cell phone laying next to the jacuzzi began to ring. Monte glanced over and gave thought to simply letting it ring. After all, it was Christmas, and even though he was not exactly spending time with family, he still felt a bit reluctant to take time to have a conversation. In the end, Monte put down the glass and answered the phone. "Hello," Burns answered. "Ah, Lance, how are you today?...That's great. I'm doing fine...No, just myself...Yeah, I'm alright. Just a few bumps and bruises. I'm taking some time to recuperate...That's good. I hope everything went well...Great, great...Yeah, I did see that...No, I have no clue what's going on with her and Eddy...I know. She's very hot, but I don't know about this situation she's in...Why? We're talking about Hong Kong gangs and shit like that. I don't want to get mixed up in this...Definitely. I'm positive...Alright, sounds good...I'll catch up with you then...Happy Holidays, Lance...Later." Monte ended the call and set the phone back down near the jacuzzi. He leaned back in the water, allowing his long hair to slip into the water and submersing his entire body in the soothing waters. It had been a while since Monte had taken the opportunity to get some rest. Life as an fWo superstar was nothing easy, with lots of traveling and plenty of appearances to keep. Combine that with being the CEO of a Fortune 500 company, and Monte's life was not one that allowed much room for rest and relaxation. However, he had taken steps to insure a little bit of rest at some points along the way. Holidays were no different. One thing about holidays that was different, however, was that he was alone. While most people spent holidays with their families and friends, Burns did not have that luxury. He had alienated his family long ago, and Monte's lone best friend Lance Fortune was spending time with his family. Years ago Monte had resigned himself to spending holidays alone, something that he did not mind at all. Most his time during the course of the year was spent alone with his thoughts, pondering what moves to make as a wrestler and as the CEO of Platinum Gold, Inc. Last month during Thanksgiving, Burns had attempted to spend some time with his family, and that had backfired. Another argument ended another failed day of reconciliation. As he sipped from the champagne, Burns couldn't help but smile. He was alone, and he was convincing himself that he was loving it.
