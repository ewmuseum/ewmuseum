Before

7 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
On the sleepy, clogged arteries of Seattle's streets, Brawn was a beating heart, buzzed by several cups of coffee. The buckets of rain were a customary accent to his experience in the city. But Brawn longed for the warm, sunny season springing back home. He sat at a street light, watching the rubber wipers sweep away the streaming water. He checked his teeth in the mirror. Clean and white. There were bags under his eyes, and he fell into a brief caffeine-induced panic. 'They have makeup girls,' Brawn reasoned, and the electric blood flowing in his body gradually calmed. 'Cute ones.' Flashbacks to the night with Birgitte in Paris helped soften the dread he felt regarding his impending commercial taping. There was a time when hocking products were the last item on Brawn's agenda, but his dad was getting much better, and he needed the money. The wheels of Brawn's sedan lurched forward. He noticed a man struggling mightily to push a shopping cart up a steep hill. A kindred spirit. Maybe he was lusting for attention, deceiving himself about golden-hearted intentions. It was abundantly clear that the Jeff Garvin partnership was a fatal error, and three disappointing weeks may as well have been a six month sabbatical. His stomach was an elevator in free fall at the thought. But then again, the company wasn't exactly setting the world on fire, and Brawn had come to form a few opinions. He pitied characters like the closet bed-wetter Fairview Reed. Or Affirmative Action, the crotch grabbing minstrels that were turning back the clock on race relations every week. Or the quiet-as-a-whisper Jade Argent, who needed to funnel smack in his veins on a regular basis to convince himself he was actually decent. At least Impulse now had a play toy to fall back on. Or the tantrum-throwing Jeff Garvin, who would soon discover the error of attacking someone with youth, focus, strength, and energy on his side. Maybe after everything was settled EW Torch would quit licking the nut sack of an expired piece of meat. His teeth gnarled with thoughts of other "wrestlers"-- K-Wolf, Graphic Violence, KroW, and killjoy-- more deserving of warm beds in prison cells than being rewarded with title belts and free reign backstage. High Flyer, once a huge draw, had allowed himself to be distracted and vulnerable to defeat. His list of favorites was woefully small, but Brawn was sure they knew how he felt. Seattle never really felt like home. Brawn hungered for the road, the world outside of the dreary, damp city. Maybe he could then see his colleagues in a different light, with a renewed respect and admiration that was there when he began. But now he saw them for what they truly were, aimless, mechanical bodies unaware of the big picture. It was time for him to put inconsistencies behind him and start performing to his potential. "Brawny commercial set this way!" A handwritten sign read.
