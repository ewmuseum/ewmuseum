Travelling Circus - Chapter 6

9 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Absolutely not." The ominous tone in John Rider's voice stopped Mufanga Kagos in his tracks as he swung the thin trailer door open. Kagos turned back to the small fat man sitting amid the heaps of wrestling magazines. "What did you say?" "I said absolutely not." Rider said sharply. "You will not throw your career, a career I worked my ass off to mold... you will not throw it away over an stupid lie in a tabloid dirtsheet." "It's not that simple, it's-" "It is that simple, you think-headed moron," John said, his voice raising as he became visible frustrated with the young black wrestler. "You've only been here a month. You've been in a Slaughter main event. You fought at Cyberslam, and not many can boast that little feat. You will be fighting for the Cruiserweight Title during this tour, and you want to walk out because a few people backstage are giving you a mean look?" John waited until Mufanga's hand was again on the doorknob, waited until he pushed the door outward before speaking again. "It's time to either get out of the spotlight, or prove you are worthy of the attention. Here.." In disgust, Rider threw the PWI Magazine on the floor at Mufanga's feet. The title, "PWI's Top 500" glared accusingly up at the tall black wrestler. "You aren't listed in there... I checked. Five hundred wrestlers, and you aren't there. Now my question is this. Do you want to be in that magazine next year? Do you?" Mufanga felt a chill, his entire frame shaking under the impact of Rider's words. He took a deep breath, his shaky and grateful whisper reaching the ears of his former manager. "More than anything." Then Mufanga was gone, and Rider was alone. Alone with his thoughts, and alone with a million questions that didn't have answers. With a resigned and determined sigh, John Rider returned his attention to his magazines.
