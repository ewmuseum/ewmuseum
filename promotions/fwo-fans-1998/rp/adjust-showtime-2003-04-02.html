Adjust: Showtime

2 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The building was only a quarter of the ways full, but it was an excited quarter, wrestling fans from all over the Arizona area had gathered together on this evening to witness what had promise in being a showstopper of an evening. The show had eight matches filled out, with some of the top indy talents out there all apart of the show. The Southwestern Wrestling Alliance needed a good show. They had nine minutes before it was show time. The promoter of the SWWA was a fellow by the name of Kyle Morris, who practically saw the SWWA as a hobby, rather than a business, because his real business was his restaurant chain that was steadilly growing. Kyle was a gruff, behind-on-the-times old man, who had been trying to push his rather talentless son, Benjamin Morris, as the next big thing. Hence why he was in the main event of the evening. On the other side of the coin, you had a guy like Allen Speck. Allen Speck, real name Allen Brown, had been wrestling for five years now, and he previously had thought that it would be impossible to get a call from any of the big named promoters in the promised lands. Partially, it was because the male contingent of the Morris family almost invariably made the poor guy look bad. He was booked heel in a feud with Ben, which had the amusing effect of giving him the loudest face heat he had ever gotten in his career. Eight minutes. During the match between Speck and Morris that gave Morris the third of his five SWWA Regional Championships, the fans, in unison, made up a new chant especially for Benjamin Morris. It was a chant worthy of Philadelphia. It was "YOU FUCKING SUCK!". When Morris won the match, most of the fans all left the arena in disgust, not wanting to see Morris celebrate. It would have been a tell-tell sign to pull Benjamin out of wrestling for good until he learned how to not suck, but the Morris family might have been the most stubborn and difficult-to-reason with people on the planet when it came to who got pushed. Seven minutes. Allen Speck, himself, knew what he was getting himself into in his match, tonight, against Benjamin. Benjamin was a sloppy wrestler, a poor seller, and always tried to keep himself on te offense, even though he's supposed to be a face. Allen's own trainer, Joey Malone, still had a scar on his right elbow from when Benjamin botched a Rock Bottom and Joey's elbow struck the steel steps. On top of that, Benjamin was an egotistical bastard and HATED to be booked to lose cleanly. Like Kevin Nash, except with a blonde crewcut and an unbearable ugliness. Six minutes. Allen paced nervously in the backstage area, the crowded locker room that the show's talent was in. The only two people who weren't present were the two male Morris family members, who were doing something that Allen wasn't specifically sure of. Potentials, guests, has-beens, and never-will-bes lined up to make sure they were booked for the evening. He knew that this would be his last show. He wouldn't have to deal with that idiot and his father any more. He no longer was going to be told that he wasn't good enough to earn a shot at the big leagues. Five minutes. A dorky-looking young woman, with frizzly hair and thick-framed glasses walked up to Allen, as he paced. Her name was Leigh Morris Landers, and while she was the sister of Benjamin Morris, she was a lot nicer than he was. She, herself, used to be a wrestler before an injury put her on the sidelines for over a year. She was finally going to get to return to the ring tonight, much to her own delight, "You know, Allen, you look kinda nervous." "Understated." Allen said, before he sat down. Fortunately, he was in the main event and didn't have to wrestle until the end of the show, but he was still nervous as all hell. "Oh, I know the feeling." Leigh hummed. Four minutes. "Yeah, but this doesn't happen to me, y'know? I'm usually all calm, and collected. Brother's gotta keep his cool, yeah?" Allen said. "But...?" Leigh asked, knowing that there was something to Allen's tone. "I got a call on my cellie. fWo guy. Silver Fox, I think his name was. Paperwork's all together, I'm headin' for the other side of the world after this show." Allen said. Leigh bounced happilly and nearly cut off Speck's circulation with a hug that caught him off guard. "The fWo! Ohmigod! Allen, I'm so happy for you!" she said, excitedly. Three minutes. "Y-yeah, that's... great... just chill, darlin'... kinda need... to breathe." Allen said with what little air he was given. Leigh got the hint and let go of him, and just kind of looked up at a dozen or so eyes that had been watching this. "Damn, you two," one of Allen's fellow co-workers, Robert Falk, said with a smile, "I didn't think you had a 'thing' going." Leigh stood up and gave Robert a wide smile, "Oh, that's it, Robert. I'm totally going to stiff you in our match." Robert's smile faded, because he knew just how stiff the daughter of Kyle Morris could be when she really felt like it. The last time someone had been stiffed by Leigh, they had a concussion and couldn't walk straight for a week. With that being said, Robert mumbled to himself and wandered off to talk to his brother, Scott. Two minutes. Leigh shook her head, and turned back to Allen. "So, what's the matter? I already know you don't like to work with my brother, but this is the fWo! This is what you've been waiting for!" she said, smiling at Allen. Allen couldn't return the smile, as he was still way, way too nervous. "Yeah, but see, I'm nervous because they put me with Ben. I don't wanna get hurt before I get my break, y'know?" Allen said with a frown. One minute. Leigh looked up at a clock on the wall, and remembered that she had the curtain jerker. She quickly leapt up to her feet and called out to the Falk Brothers, "Hey! We're up, you two!" As the two Falk Brothers stood up and headed out, Leigh turned to Allen and she smiled at him. "Good luck, Allen!" she said cheerfully, before she skipped away, prepared for her curtain-jerking tag team match. Allen sighed to himself and looked up at the clock, brooding to himself about his opportunity. The seconds ticked away, until the second hand hit twelve. Showtime.
