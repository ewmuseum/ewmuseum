Bricks in the Wall

23 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
As I did every evening, I stood across the street from the diner. It was a tedious chore waiting for the traffic to go by, waiting for a red light, or a driver kind enough to let me across. When someone does, a kind wave and nod was all I needed to give them as a small thank you. It was the final time I'd cross Stephens St en route to the diner. When I left, it'd be the final time back across the street. Bricks in the wall. I was frozen in place when I saw them brutally take her. I watched wide-eyed as they began to beat her. Punching her stomach, where the young she carried with her would inevitably be killed. Fists rained down on her defenseless body and I could do nothing but stand in a dark alley watching terrified. Standing in place like a stone statue. Bricks in the wall. I blinked, my eyes opened and focused. This was really happening and I couldn't do anything about it. I watched as their ripped off her clothing in the dimly lit street. One of the two thugs covering her mouth with an abrasive hand. The second man quickly dropped his pants and entered her. I can't begin to imagine the thoughts running through her mind as compared to mine. She must have been petrified. Bricks in the wall. When they finished their deed, they dropped her headfirst on the pavement. A sickening thud went through the quiet street. The pregnant girl didn't make any sound at all. I carefully made my way towards her. When I did, I was frozen all over again. A chill ran through my body and I shivered. Blood was seeping out her nostrils and mouth. Her eyes were half-open and rolled back. Her face was flushed red. Bricks in the wall. The thugs had killed two living beings. The pregnant girl and her child. They did this without second thought. She had nothing to live for. Homeless, poor, living off whatever people would throw at her. I remembered throwing quarters at her in the mornings and laughing as she scurried to grab them. Now I look into my grey heart and realize how cruel I was and still am. Now a girl I knew only because I saw her wretched face every day was gone. One of life's little simplicities had disappeared and would be buried under the fertile earth. Bricks in the wall. For maybe the first time in my life in the past decade, a tear of true sympathy leaked from the corner of my eyes. Maybe that's when I realized that there wasn't an ounce of humanity in my body. I got back to my apartment and wanted to break down and cry like a child. But I'm a grown man, I'm too strong for that. For once I sincerely felt bad for someone. It wasn't just the same beating I was giving out week after week as a wrestler. This was about me being a person. Bricks in the wall. It's time that I leave everything behind and start new. Sure, this is just a journal and I'll be the only one who reads it, but it's a promise to myself. I need to start again. A new outlook on life, the scars on my body just a distant reminder of a painful life. A life that was mostly numbed with drugs. A life where I was hated by everyone and loved by no one. Goodbye's are always hard, but I figure it's easier to do it like a band aid. One rip, right off. It will sting for a few seconds and then forgotten. That's all I really want. I want everyone to forget me and everything I did to people out of the cruelty of my heart. I want to be a regular person. Walk through a busy street and not be recognized. I want normalcy. So for now, I'm going to blend in with everyone else. Bricks in the wall. Just a chipped brick where young children use chalk to draw their images of purity and innocence. Everything I wanted while growing up, yet I never had. I somehow survived and it made me bitter, angry, depressed, hateful to the rest of the world. When I disappear and everyone forgets about me, I'll just be... Another brick in the wall.
