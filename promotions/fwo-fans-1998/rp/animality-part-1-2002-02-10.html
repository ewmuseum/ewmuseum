Animality - Part 1

10 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Mufanga would have been lying if he said he was happy and comfortable with what was going on. He wasn't, and he didn't think that 'brainstorming' with John Rider was going to help matters or make things anymore understandable or acceptable. Universal hadn't really told him anything after his hiring; he hadn't cleared up any of the fog. As a matter of fact his hiring had only raised more questions than he needed answered. Universal only said that part of the bargain... and the gimmick... was John Rider. The greasy, overweight, balding leisure-suit-wearing shitstain of an agent, John Rider. The man that bailed on Mufanga as soon as it was clear that he wouldn't win Survivor. Then again, glancing at his sweaty manager as the freight elevator descended, Mufanga thought that maybe John Rider in all his crypticism had answered some of his questions long ago. Questions that he clearly hadn't thought to ask aloud, and the answers were those that she wasn't ready to hear. Something had happened upstairs in Universal's office, only Mufanga didn't have the slightest clue of what that something was. He really didn't like being in the dark like this. He was trusting Rider right now, trusting his proven instincts, but that didn't mean that he was happy with it. He planned on keeping a close eye on his own career, and didn't need the fWo or John Rider laying out what was right for him. He didn't plan on leaving until he had a few answers that he could be relatively happy with. And, most importantly, he wasn't going anywhere until he was convinced beyond a shadow of a doubt that his gimmick was safe. The freight elevator reached the bottom floor and John Rider threw up the gate, loudly announcing their arrival. A head swiveled to look expectantly in their direction, a quiet conversation lulling as a telephone was put back into it's cradle. Mufanga Kagos took in the unpretentious and windowless surroundings, wondering why anyone would chose to live in the rather darkened environment. Still it was clean, immaculately clean for the 'typical wrestling trainer' and decorated spartanly and eclectically. His eyes quickly skimmed a wall of swords and weapons and he looked away with a shiver. Something told him that those were more than decorations and display pieces. The knowledge unsettled him. Why would a wrestling trainer need those types of weapons? "Ah, Mufanga, you're still with us, I see," the trainer, an ice-white aging man, greeted him with a friendly nod of his head. "I trust that your 'welcome' conversation with Universal was suitably enlightening?" "Not exactly," Mufanga gave John Rider, his newfound agent, a hard look. "As enlightened as I could get them," Rider immediately objected. He looked around and then decided to settle into an occupied armchair. Tossing his hat on the coffee table, he tossed one leg over the arm of the chair. "I'm not going to do all of Universal's work for him." The old man glared at Mufanga's partner -- if that was what he truly was -- Mufanga knew that Rider was simply in this for the money, as always... but he still wasn't prepared to go as far as calling him a partner. "What's that supposed to mean? And I hope you're comfortable." "Quite, thanks," Rider reached into his shirt pocket and produced a cigar. "It means that I told them what I think that they need to know. That Mufanga is ready for the fWo. That he is over that little bump in the battle royal. That he can keep up with the big boys. The rest is up to you." "Do you speak English at all?" The question came from a woman who stood near the kitchen area with her arms folded across her chest. The brunette looked either bored or disgusted, Mufanga couldn't tell which. It was usually both. "Or is like cryptic part of your oh-so-cool routine? Because if it is, it needs a lot of work." Rider stared at her a long time, too long then turned his attention to Kagos. "Is she always like this?" "Only to people who dress badly," the old man answered for him. He looked to Mufanga, who still hovered nearest to the exit. "Come in, sit down. I'm guessing that we need to talk." "You could say that," Mufanga hung back still. "Considering that I still don't know what's going on." As a matter of fact, Mufanga was only told that they were going to a new gym, to see a new trainer... and to get Mufanga over his recent apprehension about ringwork. Not that Mufanga didn't still love to wrestle. He did, and always would. It was the top rope that frightened him, and that was death for a high flyer. He still had no idea why Kelly was here, or how she knew he was coming... or whty she was alone in this old man's basement apartment. A pang of surprised jealousy shot through him like a knife. "What exactly did you tell him?" The trainer's attention returned to the fat agent, John Rider, who had just bitten the tip off of his cigar. "Don't dare spit that on the floor. There's a trash can in the kitchen." The look that Rider gave the old man could only be described as petulant. He spoke around the cigar bit, swinging his legs to the floor. "Just what he needed to know to trust you. And trust himself." "Cryptic. Cryptic," the woman sang out. She strolled into the living area, flouncing onto the sofa with overdone and overacted exasperation. "I swear, I don't know how he ever taught you anything, Mufanga. You can't even understand a word that he says." "Perhaps we should start at the beginning," the ancient trainer removed his round spectacles, his attention on Mufanga and the woman. "My name is Jean Paul. I train wrestlers to win, and to impress the people. I also train them to survive will impressing them. Is it safe to presume that you came here tonight because you had an unusual experience? Something happened that sent you here... it always does. What was it?" "No," Kelly spoke up. "Nothing. I don't -- He fell off the top rope and hurt himself. He won't admit it, but he's scared to wrestle now." Mufanga looked at her in surprise, his eyes asking the question his lips could not. [Are you sure about this, Kelly?] [There's something weird going on here, Mufanga, but it's not bad. They aren't going to hurt us. I don't know how I know that, but I do.] "A fall?" Jean Paul asked with obvious interest. "A fall that hurt Mufanga Kagos? That must have been a mighty fall." "Oh that figures," Rider rolled his eyes, "They always worry about the dark, mysterious and broody ones. Never us normal, homely guys." "That fall made you take me here? In the battle royal?" Kagos asked, turning to face Kelly. He looked a bit embarrassed to be the subject of Kelly's worries, but he still plowed forward, turning again to plead to the old man. "It was in a battle royal. A guy named Xaxon hooked me for a superplex, but I froze up a bit and accidentally flipped us both off the turnbuckle and onto the cement. It was months ago... Just that fall, Kelly? It was nothing. I still wrestled Crisp. I still won, too." "No," Kelly took a deep breath and crossed to the kitchen table. Mufanga followed her, curiously. "Trust me, Mufanga. This might be the only way to get to the bottom of this." Indicating a kitchen chair, she looked to Kagos, "Go ahead?" "What?" Kelly looked down at her hands. "Mufanga... stand on the chair, please." "Oh, for the love of-" Mufanga stared at her in complete amazement. "Stand on a chair? You brought me down here to stand on a chair? What does this have to do with anything" "EVERYTHING!" Jean Paul, the old trainer, shouted from across the room. "Everything, and nothing. Stand on the chair."
