Tetris

4 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Sometime the only thing worse than thinking about too much is not thinking about anything. Buddhists like to go on retreats where they sit in a room and just stare at the walls for hours at a time. They don't think about anything, they just let thoughts flow as they may. Well I've been doing plenty of that here in the hospital. I stare at the walls, faking my catatonic state so the orderlies don't bother me. I don't focus on anything and just things flow. It can be trippy. It can be outright frightening. Things you've locked down or hidden away, consciously or subconsciously, come bubbling to the surface and overwhelm you. I've had plenty of horror and pain in my life. Reliving it isn't pleasant. Sometimes you need to hurt before you can heal. Society is filled with people who think too much. They put too much emphasis on the trivial, skewing their priorities terribly. After weeks, months, of just sitting and looking at myself inside I started to feel like Tetris. You remember that game, right? Different shapes fall from the top of the screen and you turn them around, trying to fit the pieces together before they stack up and overwhelm you. That's how life is for many people. Day to day, they're watching shapes fall and try to fit them together. Only with people, it's interaction with our environment and how it interacts with us that determines the speed and shape. Then one day, that reverse L gets dropped in the wrong place. It can be fixed. Worked through. Heck, maybe we can even get a Tetris out of it. But for a better part of the population, they spend so much time worrying about that L that the next shape falls on top of it. Then another. And another. Stress builds while we try and reorganize things. Try to fit the pieces together. Eventually the shapes (and stress) build to the point where they reach the top of the screen and it's curtains. For some that means therapy and medication. For others it's death. These past few months has been like Tetris in reverse. Like a Disk Defrag, where the system runs a scan over my mental hard drive and puts files back where they should be. A little simplistic, but it fits. Once we can reformat the way we think and the way we approach things, life is manageable. The shapes are easy to fit together. We know how to handle those zig-zags when they come. It all fits. Life isn't difficult. People make it more difficult than it has to be. All you need is the focus and you can Tetris every day. I am by no means well... yet. But I think I've accomplished all the healing I can do in here. Meanwhile, orderlies are abusing and sexually violating patients. After all, they're just a bunch of crazies right? Who cares about them? They're sub-human at best. Those bruises on their backs? A fit most likely. Bleeding from the crotch or anus? They violated themselves in a self-destructive craze. The stories? Tales from a loon. Nobody believes it, and the cycle continues. I'm not planning on changing the way things work. I just want to get out of here. I don't need a hospital any longer. I'm not sick. I'm just waiting for my ticket out of here. Just waiting for the Devil to come calling.
