12/29/2001 4:00AM The funeral had dragged on for hours, or at least it seemed that way. Friends of the family had passed in and out of the funeral home, and the church, each saying the same things, with the same look on their faces. "I'm sorry"... "Be strong"... "they were such good people..." "if you need anything... just call." Paul Adams now stood at the cemetery, and nodded as the seemingly endless line made it's way past him. He was a young red-haired man, clean-shaven and dressed in his finest suit. His next door neighbors had handled these arrangements, the whole process, having been friends of his parents for many years... for he had no family nearby. The cemetery plot had been paid for years ago, and was a family plot. There were a few people in it who were mere names and stories to him. People he had never known. Now today, two more would be there who he knew barely more than the others. Strangely enough, this was to be his graduation day from the academy. It was just as well... he hated the damned place anyway. People told him what to do all day, and when to do it. It had given him focus and he had learned much... but wanted nothing to do with their way of life. It was his parents' wishes...not his. That was why when they said they could unfortunately not make it to the graduation, he had spoken up in protest. After all... it was their dream, not his. The least they could do was to see it through to it's conclusion. Fate, of course, had other plans for them. A head-on collision changed all of that forever in an instant. The doctor said they felt no pain... but it left him with a void... and almost a sense of satisfaction. He felt guilty for it at the time, as he should be mourning the loss of the two most influential people in his life. Yet, they were never around... always busy... too busy for him. "Sorry Paul." The faceless stranger extended his hand. Paul took it, shaking it firmly and managing a "thank you" while already looking ahead to see who was next in line. The day was a bit windy for early June, he noted. Funerals always had ugly weather anyway... or it seemed to be the case. "It's your fault you know." "What?" "Your fault, Paul." The man smiled and continued on. "Selfish little bastard. Making them drive all the way up there when they didn't have the time to" an old woman cooed as she hugged him and kissed him on the cheek. "All parents deserve a son as inconsiderate and self-centered as you." Young Paul broke into a sweat, and felt his feet sinking, as though they were in cement. He couldn't move from the spot... and the line at the cemetery continued on... "Your parents were great people. Such a shame you'll never amount to anything" a neighbor said, patting him on the shoulder. "All they wanted was the best for you... they gave up one of their vacations every year to pay for that school." The local butcher said, shaking his head solemnly as he passed. "I wish someone had done that for me." "The least you could do is be sorry... you killed them. If you left well enough alone... they'd have been fine." "I didn't know... I didn't know..." Paul said softly. "I swear...I didn't mean for it to happen..." He desperately tried to move away, to escape the scene, but was held fast. The people kept on coming through, criticizing him, chastising him...judging him. It couldn't go on forever... it couldn't. But he looked for the end of the line...and though he thought he could see forever...the line stretched beyond.
