Shadows of Society: Part One

7 June 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Unknown Time: Unknown Location: Unknown The midnight sky was jet black. There was a new moon and only the brightest stars shone. Most of the people were tucked away in their beds sleeping for the next day of work, but alone on a park bench was the outline of a man. His entire body was shrouded in the darkness, the sound of his boot was heard tapping lightly on the pavement as he sat deep in his thoughts. The only sound near the man was his own rhythmic breathing. In the distance he heard a scream and his outlined head looked in the scream's direction. But all he could see was dark. After a minute the screams subsided and the man went back deep into his thoughts until a familiar voice pierced through his brain. "Failure," the voice rang out. "Once again you're nothing but a failure. You've succeeded in blaming everyone but yourself. Accusing everyone of every thing that's happened to you. Do you realize what kind of imbecile you are? Face the facts Donaven, anything that's gone wrong in your life is not their fault. It is strictly your fault. You could have stopped things from happening, but you didn't want to take fault. You found it easier to deal with the pain by throwing it upon the shoulders of this Society. Don't you think that Society has enough burdens of its own? Don't you think that you were to blame? Just once, Donaven, take the fault. Don't put the blame on them." "Shut up!" Donaven shouted to the voice. "You don't know what it's like to have everything you cared about stolen from you. You're just making blind guesses on my life. You don't even know me!" "Oh, if only you could be right, Donaven," the voice was amazingly calm. "You caused your father to kill your mother and then shoot himself. You were drunk and swerved off of a sharp right turn killing Diane. You tried to kill yourself just to gain pity from Monica. And you blame it all on Society? What kind of sick and twisted human being are you?" "My father," Donaven said while trying to regain his composure. "My father beat her to death because she wouldn't have sex with him. It wasn't my fault that he went over the edge. And Diane, she tried to take my beer and wouldn't give it back. She caused me to miss that turn. She caused me to drive off through the guardrail. None of it was my fault." "Of course it wasn't," the voice kept its calm tone. "It wasn't your fault that you got hammered at a party and decided to drive drunk in search of another party? I don't see how you can blame Diane, but not yourself, Donaven. It was your fault and all you have to do is admit." Donaven stood up from the bench, his dark silhouetted figure moved silently across the park. "Show yourself," Donaven sneered. "Show your face, show me who you are. If you know me so well, you should be able to show me your face. There's no point in hiding, I'll eventually find out who you are and then I'll kill you, if not come very close to doing–" Donaven was cut off as the voice rang out again, this time from a different part of the park. "You'll never figure out who I am," the voice said confidentially. "You've never met me before. But I know you, Donaven. I know you better than your parents could have cared to know you. I know you better than Monica could have wanted to know you. I may even know you better than you know yourself." "How," Donaven asked. "How do you know me?" "That," the voice started. "Is something I can't share with you. You will know who I am in good time, Donaven. All in good time." Donaven sat back down on the bench and dropped his head an angry look washed over his solemn face. He scowled as he looked back up into the darkness. He heard footsteps going away from where he was sitting. He knew it had to be the man he had spoken to. And he had to know who it was. In the darkness he whispered to himself. "It's just the light of day keeping me from this man." Donaven stood up from the bench and started to walk off. After a moment or two his shadow finally faded out.
