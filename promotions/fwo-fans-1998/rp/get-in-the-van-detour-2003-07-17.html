Get in the Van: Detour

17 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The bus ambled along the Interstate Highway, most of its inhabitants sound asleep. A late show last night was capped off by their lead singer stage diving into the pit before yet another battle for their money. Angel is quickly becoming feared for her tenacity by club owners, and respected for her convictions by other bands that face the same problem. Angel is also, at the moment, the only member of the band awake. Something's been happening to her for the past six weeks, since Scarred was released for all to hear and enjoy - and if she can force herself to sleep for three broken hours a day she's surprisingly well- rested. Good for her, she can devote the time to her daughter. At the moment, Angel is reclined on the tattered couch that lined the right side of the bus, dressed in her husband's long sleeved COLD T-shirt and exercise shorts, doing the one thing she is almost always doing while in transit during her daughter's naps. Notebook in hand. I was told once, that I wouldn't appreciate being a local band until I was on the road all the time. I laughed at the man who told me that, but in a lot of ways, he was absolutely right. I've never been just a singer. Back in Spencer I was a singer-slash-student. On my way to Los Angeles I was a singer-slash-stripper-slash-waitress-slash-whatever else I could find. The money Zed paid us was barely enough for drink, smokes, and recreational drugs, let alone trying to live on it. With my more self destructive habits a thing of the past and more money coming in from performing, I can actually do just this to survive, but being a mother is far more important. And just like everything that has to do with the band is getting more and more pronounced, there are some days I feel as if I need to shed my skin and leave for the sake of sanity. Today is the Seventeenth, it's Amy's birthday and I want to be able to see her - see my darling EJ, and everyone else that I've picked up on the strange, winding journey that has become my life of the past few years. It's funny how they've become closer to me than my own family, but only on first thought. I look at Aimz and her problems with her parents and I know that she understands me. I look at Brandon and I see a dedication to his children that I didn't understand until MJ became part of my life... and I'm dually jealous of how close he is to his boys and how much I wish my own father was like that. I look at Sean and see a man driven to succeed by the people around him and, even moreso, by himself. Like I said, it's only funny on a first thought. I've got no relationship to speak of with my family. I've got a little girl that I'm terrified of making mistakes with. I'm driven by my situation, and moreso, by MYSELF, to succeed. Actually, it wasn't really funny at all. Nobody BUT a wrestler would understand what it is I'm doing and have done to myself already, the single- minded purpose that my life is encompassed by, the character of Angel Nevenrose that I become on a nightly basis. Who else could understand the addiction of the road that makes me unable to sit still when there's work to be done? Who else could understand what I've given up for my dreams? Amy once told me that the hardest fight she's ever had in her life was trying to find somewhere she belonged. I sympathize, but I do not share her longing. I believe we as artists have a responsibility to get in the van, or bus as the case may be, and take our message wherever we can. We are the final breed of road warrior. We are the last nomadic tribe of the civilized world. The Driven Artist... sounds like something out of Stephen King. Angel laughed quietly at the thought, before returning pen to page. I kinda like that. Closing the book, she pulled out her new phone and looked up Amy's number in the directory... she had an important call to make.
