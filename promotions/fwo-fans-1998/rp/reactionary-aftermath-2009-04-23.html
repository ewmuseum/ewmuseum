ReActionary Aftermath

23 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
So apparently I'm supposed to thank you for this, I said, holding a towel to my still - bleeding skull. "Oh, is that what he said?" said Miss Ivy, "You should thank him with a boot up his fu--" I laughed, and the back of my head hurt in the laughing. She's been trying to watch her language ever since she became a mom in real life, and doesn't always succeed. Have to tell you, I said, this wasn't really what I had in mind when I signed the contract. "It's part and parcel of the game," said Miss Ivy. She leaned over William's desk and pulled a bottle of something from his top drawer. Miss Ivy had kicked William out of his office after my match with Vince Jacobs, and told me to take my time in getting my head together. She looked at the bloody splotch that Vince had left me and told me she didn't think I'd need stitches, so the best thing to do was to just hang out until the bleeding stopped, clean up, and go home without worrying Rose. But, part and parcel of the game? Miss Ivy, I said, I just spent over a year working for Craig Miles, of all people, and at no time was I cracked over the back of the head with a chair after a match. This is decidedly not part and parcel of the game. She didn't say a word. I felt bad, Miss Ivy and Eli seemed to take special notice of me after Angry Johnny and Coop died, and I've always felt that if I ever said anything that might imply she didn't give me all the tools I needed, I'd be spitting in her face in the metaphoric sense. But there are some things she didn't prepare me for. "Yes, it is," said Miss Ivy, "but it's not something that I could've told you about." Why not, I asked. "Because you have to experience it for yourself," said Miss Ivy, "because me and Eli, and Fizzy and Coop all taught you how to fight, but why to fight?" She leaned back in the chair and handed me the bottle. It was extra strength Tylenol. "You need to figure out for yourself," said Miss Ivy, "why to fight. SVJ fights for himself and his own goals, and as you can see, he doesn't care who he hurts to make them a reality." I thought about that for a second. Vince Jacobs was a junkie. The spotlight, and the hatred of the fans was his junk. He was addicted to being the center of attention, and when the referee told me his foot was on the ropes after the three count registered, I took part of that away from him. So I understand why he would've been angry that I beat him, I said, but I still don't get why he attacked me. "Probably because of me," said Miss Ivy, "He and I have a history that goes back a couple years. He thinks I'm a cunt, I think he's an asshole who's built a career on hype covering mediocre skills. It works for us." Wonderful, I said, So he wasn't attacking you, he was attacking me? "That's pretty accurate," said Miss Ivy. This is why I wore a mask in New Frontier, I said, because I didn't want to be either favored or targeted because of Coop's. Do you think I should've left it on here? Miss Ivy reached into another drawer and pulled out a bottle of what looked like Jack Daniels. She took off the top and drank a swig. "No," said Miss Ivy, "because you're an established name now. And even without the pricks like Jacobs who do it because they're overcompensating, a wrestler with your talent will always be in someone's crosshairs." She took another swig and re-capped the bottle. I checked the towel, and it looked like the blood had at least slowed down in its flow, though I still kept my head down and the towel pressed. Listen, I said, is it cool if I hang out here for a bit, just until my head stops? Miss Ivy smiled. "Sure, RK. If you're not out by the time we're ready to leave I'll come get you." Solid. She walked around and her hand slapped down on my shoulder before she opened the door and walked out. I sat in silence for a few seconds, chewing over what she'd said. We never taught you why to fight. That was something I had to figure out on my own. With my head still bent down, I pulled a slice of paper from William's desk and wrote him a note: Mr. Beaven: After April 29th's Silver Pyramid match, I would like to make it known that I have an open contract on the table, willing and able to wrestle on every episode of reAction that has room for me until the June event of Meltdown. Please let me know if this is feasable. Thank you, -RK Impulse Why to fight, I said to myself, because I can.
