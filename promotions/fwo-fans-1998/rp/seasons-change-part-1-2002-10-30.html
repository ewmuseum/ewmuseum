Seasons Change (Part 1)

30 October 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Thing always turn out differently than we hope..." It had been a few months since Paul Adams had stepped foot on the East Coast. After leaving the fWo, he had traveled west, stopping in several states along the way. His travels landed him in Colorado, where he bought a small cottage for himself just outside of Castle Rock and hoped to live an existence of peace and quiet far away from the hustle and bustle of city life. Yet now here he stood on the shores of the Atlantic Ocean, gazing into the night sky and watching his breath flow away into the cool autumn air. He reminded himself that his business here was financial, and nothing more. He was in town to sign some papers, to break the last connections he had with this place... for this former home provided him with no friends or family. Not anymore... Ken and his family had moved, shortly after Martha's death. He had accepted a job in San Jose, or so he had said in the email Paul had received. They hardly spoke at her funeral, and he had felt the void between them long before then. He couldn't comfort his friend over the loss of his mother, and certainly found no comfort or peace within himself. She and Karl had helped him so much when he was getting started... Karl with his training, Martha with a warm meal and even a place to stay under their roof. Paul shrugged to himself, noting that it was probably both of their faults, and yet nobody's fault at all. Friends drifted apart sometimes. "Things always turn out differently than we plan..." So now, the wrestling school was up for sale. He just didn't have it in his heart anymore, and knew that his training would only interfere with the potential of some of his students. A teacher has to be as passionate about his lesson as a student has to be about learning. If it isn't 100%... someone will fail. He had found a potential buyer for it, the owner of a smaller local federation, who would purchase the place under the agreement that the name would be changed. While the name had remained "Koala's Wrestling Academy" in memory of his mentor who left the business to him, the thought of another businessman making money off of the name just seemed to be in bad taste. Luckily for him, the buyer agreed. His house would be converted into a 2 family home and rented out through a property management company. The income it provided would be enough to keep him from dipping too far into his savings, hopefully for a good deal of time. After all, he didn't want to be 50 years old and traveling from town to town signing autographs for 20 dollars each just to try to scrape some money together to make ends meet. He had seen that far too often in his travels. Legends in the business were left with nothing to show for their work but a group of 50 people who might remember them from the 70's and 80's willing to shell out money to spend the time with them that it took to smile and click the camera. Selling the school hurt his pride, just slightly. He had put so much work into it, and while he hadn't turned out the next big superstar, he had graduated a few solid workers who were getting some recognition amongst the true fans of the business. But as for the house... his parents' house... it was never truly home. Home had always been the road, the hotel rooms, the midnight diners, the locker room... the ring. Paul glanced down at his feet, staring at his shoes for the moment. One year ago, there would have been wrestling boots there... his casual slacks and collared shirt would have been replaced by his black pants and tank top... and his hair, now tied back neatly in a ponytail would have obscured part of his face. But those days were long behind him. He was no longer a wrestler. That was his past. A soft chiming of music broke his silence as he reached into his shirt pocket for his cell phone. He had been meaning to cancel it for some time now, since he hardly ever used it anymore. It was useful for emergencies, but not much more than that. Yet, the number showing up on caller ID seemed vaguely familiar and confusing all at once... "Hello?" "Is this Paul?" a familiar voice echoed back. "Yes, it is..." "Paul, I need to talk to you if you have a minute. There's something important going down, and well... I thought you needed to know about it.... it has to do with the fWo..." The fWo? Now? For a moment, Paul Adams felt the adrenaline rush he once felt when he thought about the home of his former career. Many a battle had been fought there... ..but just as quickly, something brought him back to his senses, reminding him once again that his future was different now. His path had changed... for the better. "Sorry..." he replied into the phone. "You've got the wrong number." And with a click, he closed the connection, ending the conversation where it stood. "The fWo" he thought to himself with a small chuckle. "Who knows what sort of trouble they've gotten themselves into now..." The man who was once the Boston Strangler checked the time on his phone and started back towards his rental car. He had a meeting in the morning to discuss the pending sale, and then it was back to Colorado, back to his new home, back to a real life. "Things always turn out differently than we expect..."
