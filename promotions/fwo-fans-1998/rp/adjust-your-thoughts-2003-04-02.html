Adjust: Your Thoughts

2 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Allen Speck lay alone on his bed in his hotel room, his left arm in a sling. He tried to play a game on his brand new Game Boy Advance SP, but it didn't do a whole lot of good to have only one hand to control the action in Megaman Zero, and indeed, the sound of the stupid red robot's death just simply caused Speck to curse, turn the Game Boy off, and put it away silently. He had difficulty sleeping in the two days since his injury, which had been diagnosed as a disloacted shoulder. He also had not gotten back to the fans Wrestling organization, as he was now convinced that they'd never take someone in like him, just some kid that got injured in an independent match. Knock, knock... Allen tried to ignore the fact that someone had taken a bit of interest in his plight, and thought about what had actually possessed him to become a wrestler. Well, for one thing, he was the youngest son of Fred Brown, a man who used to wrestle back in the 70s and 80s. However, his father was a demanding individual, not to mention the fact that he wanted to stray away from his father, he was a known jobber. His two older brothers(who were twins and ten years older than Allen was), Jordan and Robert, had faded into wrestling obscurity, since the twins gimmick never really got over, and the bland wrestling style they employed didn't help their cause. Allen, not wishing to make a similar mistake, met up with Joey Malone five years ago, and while Malone was still getting used to the sport, he was able to teach Allen some important things about the business. After many years of hard work, Allen was able to refine his abilities to the point where he could at least carry a ten minute match. Of course, not a lot of good it'd do him with a busted arm. Knock, knock, knock... Some time later, he figured out for himself that being "just a wrestler" wasn't enough to make it. You needed a character, too. You needed to be something for the fans to connect to. Wrestling wasn't just about cool-looking, but dangerous maneuvers. Those who thought that were dorks who sat around and watched a couple of Japanese guys beat the crap out of each other while a couple of announcers screamed in unintelligible gibberish. So, that's when "Allen Brown" faded and "Allen Speck" rose. He wasn't just going to be a show-stopper. He'd be the show-stealer. He'd be the man that took that risk, take that bump, eat that chairshot, and do all of those other things that a wrestler of his mantra would do. KNOCK, KNOCK, KNOCK... "Oh, for the love of God, the door's unlocked already." Allen shouted. The door creaked open, and in walked his friend, Leigh Landers, from the night before. Leigh was as annoyingly cheerful as ever, and she looked at Allen, dressed in his bathrobe and boxer shorts, and wearing a frown as well as he looked at the frizzly-haired woman. "I'm taking it that you're not having a good day?" Leigh asked, innocently. Allen simply glared at her to shut her up.
