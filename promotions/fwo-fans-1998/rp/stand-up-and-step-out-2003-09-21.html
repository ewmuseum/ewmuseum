Stand Up and Step Out

21 September 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Journal Entry – I’ve gotta get my own place. My contract with the fWo hasn’t been exactly rock solid. When you’re paid per appearance, there’s no guarantee that you’ll be pulling in enough bank to afford your own swanky pad. Me, I’d settle for a freezer box in an alley right about now. My mother doesn’t like me very much. She hates the fact that I’m a professional wrestler. She hates the fact that my father left after she gave birth to me. Everything wrong that happened in her lift – losing her job, putting on about a hundred pounds – is all my fault. I think she took a course on twisting reality at one point, because she could somehow blame me for an earthquake in India. Now my brother, he’s the family success story. He’s the manager of a local Wal-Mart, and on the weekends he leaps tall buildings in a single bound. When he’s not rescuing cat’s out of trees, he’s helping old ladies across the street. I can’t blame him for the way Ma is. I blame Darwin. After all, every once in a while evolution takes a step backward. David just stood there while his mother slapped him across the face with her meaty hand. As her hand crashed against the side of his head, David could catch the not-so-faint smell from her Camel cigarettes, as well as a subtle fishy stink that he didn’t want to consider the source of. Ma Cross rubbed her hand. “Look what you made me do,” she muttered as she pursed her cigarette between her lips, blowing a jet of smoke out of the corner of her mouth. “You’d better hope I can still use the remote.” “Is that what you’re using for a vibrator these days?” David felt the hand print burning on his cheek. “Guess you wore out the other three.” Ma’s hand flew again. “How dare you talk to me like that!” Instinct took over. Before he even knew he was doing it, David’s hand snapped up and intercepted the slap while his other hand rocketed towards his mother’s head with a counterstrike. He managed to stop it about half an inch from her mouth. Sadly, her cigarette suffered the full effect of the punch and crumpled in on itself. Both David and his mother stared at each other in shock. Still, he waited for his mother to withdraw her hand before he lowered his. “I…” was all he could say. Part of him was glad he stopped himself when he did. Part of him thought he stopped an inch too soon. “Get out,” Ma Cross said as she pulled the crinkled cigarette out of her mouth. “You dare raise you hand to your own mother? Get out of my house!” “But Ma, I didn’t mean…” Without a moment’s hesitation, David’s mother stormed into the small kitchenette and grabbed the cast-iron frying pan off the stove. Cross didn’t wait for her to come back. By the time she turned around to face her son, he was gone. The rain fell from the night sky in buckets. He hadn’t grabbed a jacket when he left his mother’s apartment, so it wasn’t long before David’s sweatshirt and jeans were soaked. His teeth chattered in the cold evening air as he walked down the sidewalk. He ignored the stares he got from people as they passed by him. Having a huge cross-shaped scar on the side of his face was bad enough. But since he shaved his head, as well as his remaining eyebrow, the scar stood out that much more. The Video Barn was still open, so David formed a plan. He’d make a phonecall from the rental shop and let his brother Richard know he was coming down for the night. If David was lucky, Video Barn would help turn his night around by having a copy of Daredevil in stock. No matter how bad the day way, there was something about watching Jennifer Garner in leather that never failed to put a smile on Cross’s face. He pulled the door open and stepped inside. All he wanted was to make one phone call and rent one movie.
