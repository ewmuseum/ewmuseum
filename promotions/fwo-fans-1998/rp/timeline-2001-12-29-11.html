12/29/2001 7:00AM The elevator slowly made it's way down to the lobby level, the lights blinking on each floor that it passed while a soft "ding" could be heard in the ceiling speaker. The Strangler was suddenly thankful to only be on the fourth floor, as to ride this slow from the penthouse would border on the unbearable... even worse if there were any music in the elevator. He (not so) fondly recalled the time he stayed at the hotel in Pittsburgh on the seventeenth floor, stopping at every floor on the way up, crowded in with 20 people, and listening to a horrid instrumental of "When a Man Loves a Woman"... a lesser man would have needed therapy. Finally, with a triumphant ding, the elevator stopped and proudly slid open it's doors to welcome him to the lobby of the hotel. He quickly stepped off, heading in the direction of the front desk. Surely someone there would be able to help him with the information he needed. "Good morning sir!" the cheerful young woman behind the counter said, smiling her best "I don't want to be here but I have to make it look good" smile. "What can I help you with today?" "Actually..." the Strangler began, before pausing to correctly word his question. "Would it be possible to get in touch with Darryl? Darryl Bailey, to be more specific. He works here, and we heard he had a little trouble last night... and wanted to see how he was feeling." "Smooth, Paul. Real smooth..." he thought while mentally kicking himself. "That doesn't sound like you're actively seeking him out or anything..." "I... umm..." the girl looked confused. "Hold on a second please..." She stepped quickly over to speak with a man, who by the suit and tie the Strangler judged to be the manager... or at least someone with authority of some kind. After a quick hushed conversation, both returned to speak with him. "Hi, Mr. ..." The manager let the last syllable hang, waiting for the name to use in conversation. "Adams, sorry" the Strangler said, giving what he hoped was a genuine looking smile. "We were just talking about possibly getting in touch with Darryl Bailey... would you be able to help us?" "I really wish I could, Mr. Adams, but the hotel has a strict policy on privacy... we don't give out information on our guests, even visitors don't know the room number but instead must meet the guest in the lobby. We try to give that same respect for privacy to our employees as well. Even in a case like this, where Michelle tells me you wanted to know how he was doing, and your intentions seem good... it's just company policy, I'm sure you understand." "Of course, of course" the Strangler nodded, cursing in his mind. "But please, if you speak to him, pass along our best wishes. Could you at least say if he's doing all right?" "I'm sorry, really. That's a borderline issue... and with a police investigation still not concluded... I'd rather not overstep my authority." The manager shrugged helplessly. "But if I speak to him today, I'll let him know you were asking for him." "That's just fine. You have our permission to have him call us in our room... or at this number" the Strangler said, quickly writing down the number for his rarely used cell phone. It was for emergencies or important issues only. He hated the damned things otherwise. "I'll do that. If I speak to him, or see him, I'll personally make sure he gets this" the manager smiled, placing the number in an envelope before sealing it. "Is there anything else we can do for you?" "Yes, actually... what time, and which way is breakfast?" "You're in luck, it just started... and is in the bar restaurant area. Go back past the elevators, and head straight down the hallway that runs between them until the very end. It's a full spread, so bring your appetite." The Strangler thanked both the manager and the smiling (but not very helpful) service representative, and headed towards the restaurant, cursing his luck. There had to be another way to get in touch with him, he figured. The police might know his address or phone number, but that was even more risky than asking at the front desk. Hotel workers didn't think to ask many questions... the police were different. He figured he'd find out some information somehow... he just needed to get to the right person to do it.
