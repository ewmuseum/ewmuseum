The Business - Death

14 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I don't have words that can properly describe the feeling, eating away at my gut. I heard about it. Who hasn't. Word travels fast in this crazy world of professional wrestling. I respected Ivy. There's nothing more to it, and it saddens me that I'm forced to speak in past tense about her. Chalk her up to the chaos that is our sport. To the reality we hide from the world. She was always a class A act, and from what I knew of her, that was for both inside the ring and out. And her mind was sharper than a tack. And... I don't really have any bad things to say about her. That's hard to say in this business, about anyone. Hell, even I could probably slander myself to the point of no return in the public eye. All the mistakes I've made and all the wrong doings I've justified. But Ivy... She... she was one of the good ones. I was on my way to Phoenix when I heard it over the radio. I planned on visiting Joey, since he always said I could spend the night on his pull out couch if I ever came into town. I remember hearing the words drift in and out of my ear. You know radio's nowadays with their shock treatment. Nothing's real unless it startles you. So here I am, driving, my hands off the wheel and my mouth wide, and my car suddenly rears off the side of the road. Good thing there was nothing but desert on both sides. It was sort of fitting. The whole world felt dead to me now. How the rest of the boys cope... I won't know. Me? I'm going to see my family. It's what I do when I'm at a loss for words. And all I can say to Ivy is... ... I'm sorry. Sorry for your death. Sorry for the problems that were in your life. But most importantly? Sorry I didn't get to know you better.
