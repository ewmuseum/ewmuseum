Sweet Child O' Mine - One of Seven

23 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
SWEET CHILD O’ MINE Part One – Troy “Go, go, go, go, go—GET OUTTA MY WAY!” The icy roads would’ve made driving dangerous enough tonight. “Emergency here! Can you fuckin’ drive, slappy?” A large bus, a lead foot, and a pregnant woman make it even worse. “Troy, will you drive like a sane person?” Troy Parker has been driving the Valerian’s Garden tourbus for just over a year now. He’s spent all of his adult life in the music industry, playing roadie, driver, and what- have- you for bands as big as Kiss all the way down to Sal’s Birdland/Artificial Joy Club. But for some reason… this girl and these four guys have really snared him. “Pick a lane, numbnuts!” Of course, being a roadie and a driver for various musical acts was a decidedly different kind of pressure. “Troy!” The haggard, pain- filled voice of the center of this particular crisis is what caused him to finally turn around. “Angel… how ya doing?” “I’d be doing a hell of a lot better if I wasn’t afraid of vehicular death, Troy,” retorted the singer, “I’m in labor, my hand is fucking broken and my driver is panicking. How’d you be doing?” Troy turned back toward the road – the cars in front of him had finally started to move. Nine stood next to him, pointing out directions. “It’s not this turn,” said Nine, “it’s the next one—no, that one! Damnitt, Troy!” “Let me up,” said Angel, sitting on the couch. “Angel—“ started TJ. “No, Travis!” she interrupted, “ow… let me up.” She pulled herself to her feet, a length of gauze falling free from where part of it wrapped around her hand. The bus ran fairly smoothly, but between the rocking that remained and the pains in her guts and hand, it still took a minute or so to make her way across. She steadied herself by grabbing Nine by the hair – but he didn’t dare tell her to let go. “Troy.” “We’re almost there, Angel.” “Get up.” “What?” “Get up,” repeated Angel, “Let me drive.” Sitting on the couch, Mickey and TJ both began to laugh. “Angel,” replied Troy, “We’re almost there… now you sit down and relax… best thing you can do.” She started to shake her head. “I’m serious—“ “You missed the other turn!” cried Nine, “AAAAAHHHHH!” Another contraction hit her, as she tightened her grip on Nine’s hair. “Troy…” she continued, “get out… of the fucking… driver’s seat.” He stopped at another red light just in time to feel Angel’s hand wrap around his shirt collar, as he was flung out of the driver’s seat by a woman with no patience left. No sooner had Angel sat down, than she floored it, running a red light and cutting off an old, beat- up Jetta. “Hey!” said TJ, looking out the window, “Angel! Respect the Jetta!” As she buzzed past another clump of traffic, she glanced down at Troy, still sitting on the floor of the bus, speechless. “I’m sorry, Troy,” she finally said, “but when it comes to how much this hurts… you’re quite the ineffectual driver.” That finally did it – the rest of the boys in the bus started to laugh. Hard. And so it goes… like something out of a bad sitcom.
