12/29/2001 2:00PM "Thank you so much for the new television, Paul... and the sweater too... I really like the color. It reminds me of my bridesmaids dresses in my wedding... but that was so long ago." Listening to Martha rattle on and on about things gave the Strangler a bittersweet feeling in his heart. She was one of the kindest people he had ever met, having made sure he was doing well when her husband was training him to wrestle... but it stretched further than that. She was the closest thing he had to a family anymore. If Karl had been a father figure to him, she would have easily fit in as a mother. "Would you like to talk to Ken?" "Sure" he said hesitantly. He waited somewhat nervously for Martha to hand the telephone off to him. It had been quite a long time... 8 months or so since he had talked to his friend, and Martha's son... "Hey Paul. Merry Christmas, belated of course. Happy Birthday, also belated...." "Thanks... how are Karen and the baby?" "They're doing fine. I actually sent along some pictures to your house. They should be there whenever you get back to Boston. If you get some internet access, we've also got some up on the website that you could look at... he's such a big kid now... a few months old and I swear he's going to be a scrapper. It looks like little Karl will inherit my dad's physique as well as his name." "And how is Martha doing...otherwise?" "Well... she has her good days, and her bad days. Christmas morning, she didn't want to open her presents, because Dad wasn't there. Growing up, we never got to open them until Dad got home...which sometimes wasn't until after New Year's day... but she had forgotten that he's been gone for a couple of years now. I seriously don't know how much longer it will be before... well... we have to put her in a full care facility." "Is there anything we can do? We'd like to help if we can..." "No Paul... it's being taken care of." "So you're saying you don't want our help?" the Strangler asked, trying to disguise the hurt in his voice. After all he had done for them, as thanks for all Karl had done for him, to be treated like this..."We're not good enough to include in your family in times of need anymore?" "No, it's not anything like that..." Ken said quickly. "You've done plenty already for us, financially and in friendship, during this whole process. Let me handle the details, and you concentrate on your own business. I don't want this to be a burden on you. It's not your problem..."
