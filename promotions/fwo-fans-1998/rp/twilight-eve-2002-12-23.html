Twilight Eve.

23 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The Holiday Inn in Dallas. It wasn't the lap of luxury, but it would do for Scott Wasner for now. He was staying under an assumed name, although he doubted that would stop either Winters or a really dedicated fan from finding him if they set their mind to it. And, as aliases went, "Phil N. D'Blanc" was not one of his better efforts. No matter. Winters he could deal with, and as for fans, well... if he had any left, he could deal with them too. It seemed to him that he probably still had plenty - the fans of the fWo were a pretty forgiving bunch, and what was attempted murder and things of that nature to them anyway? Wasner was laying on the bed, his head against the backboard. The TV was on, tuned to FOX. The Seahawks game was starting, and he considered it a minor miracle that it was on here, considering that some times this season the Seahawks hadn't even made it to the Portland stations. The room was decorated in a vaguely Christmasy style - not Wasner's choice, to be sure. Not that he had anything against Christmas. In fact, usually, he loved it. But this year the relentless cheeriness of the season was pressing down on him like a weight. The small fake Christmas tree on the counter next to the television was the worst of it. It was taking all his willpower not to get up and hurl it out the window in a misguided, holiday-specific attempt to emulate Led Zeppelin. The reasons for that, of course, were quite clear to anybody who had watched the fWo in recent months. Donaven Winters was first on that list. Winters, the sick bastard who had decided to come in and destroy his life for no apparent reason. He was doing a pretty good job of it, Wasner reflected. Winters had driven him to do things that he never would have considered in the past. Scott Slugger had done such things in the past sure - but the difference, of course, was the reality. When Scott Slugger did things, he gave the victim a helping hand back up to his feet as soon as the cameras stopped rolling. When Scott Wasner did things... not so much. Through all this, there was still a question he had to ask. Why? Why was Winters doing this to him? He didn't know, and it wouldn't have surprised him to find out Winters had no reason at all other than that he was frickin' nuts. The other question was, why was he letting Winters take him down to the level he had been taken down to? Why not just deal with him like a normal person? That was a question he wasn't as eager to answer, so Wasner turned to the other reason, this more recent. The video. He hadn't seen it - he had been in a meeting at the time, with the man who had come to his aid recently (which was something he greatly appreciated, though his friend had told him at Warfare that he would be unable to be at Twilight), and he had only learned of it later on. The opportunity for an attack on Winters was gone, and he hadn't felt like one at the time anyway. There had been anger, of course - but at the time, the anger was not directed at Winters so much as at her. He knew he had no right to be angry at anybody but Winters, but that didn't change anything. The last time they had seen each other, they had agreed that that was it... but still, knowing she was with other men (or at least, another man) was a different thing than seeing it. He had gotten Stephanie's message, the one she had left on his machine, a few days late - after Slaughter - and just to hear her voice again had given him one of the few warm feelings he had had recently. Then, Warfare... He shook his head. Once again, another road he didn't want to go down. Introspection was becoming less and less comfortable for him these days. He much preferred examining things like Donaven Winters' broken body at his feet than his own feelings. In the days since Warfare, the anger had been moving back to Winters again. Twilight was tomorrow, and there would be an opportunity to vent all of it - all of the conflicting emotions that had been swirling about him since his return. Yes, he had drawn blood from Winters twice already, but as they said, third time pays for all. Wasner got up off the bed and walked up to the Christmas tree, its fiber optic colors sparkling prettily. "Yeah. I'm gonna give myself an early Christmas present," he said aloud. He had just the gift in mind, too. The lights from the tree threw quickly-changing colored shadows on his face as he spoke again. "Donaven Winters' head on a platter."
