Fatal Cries, Part V

23 August 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The circumstances of one's life can be anything from being born in a gutter or being born in a gold plated crib with the proverbial silver spoon in one's mouth. Is what happens between birth and death fate? Or is it an act of God? It's been debated for centuries. There are those we believe in a high power and then there are those who don't. These people who don't have anyone to go to in times of need. There is nobody to pray to, or to try and find salvation in through the difficult times. He was one of them. Was there any real reason for him to believe in some sort of God or Savior? Not in his mind. Not after everything he had gone through in the first few years of his life. There was no point. And if there was this ‘God' looking over him, he had certainly been brutalized by him just as much as his father had done. If there was ‘God', he had probably laughed at the child as he was beaten within an inch of his life, day in, day out. It was ‘God's,' amusement, but after all, to him, God was dead. As he had been for so many times since his girlfriend's death he found himself sitting on his bed. He had wasted the entire summer down in the basement, trying to deal with the grief and the burden of having taken someone's life. "I killed her," he whispered under his breath. "I really killed her." He stood up from the edge of his bed and looked around his bedroom. It seemed so empty he thought to himself. He had turned to drinking, but it wasn't helping. He grew more and more depressed by the day. Thoughts of suicide continually crept into his head, replacing everything positive. He wanted to go away, to just crawl into a small hole and be forgotten forever. He felt as if this would never end, a downward spiral of attempting his own suicide and drugs that helped him along the path. He was beginning to think that life wasn't worth living at all and he wished he could just stop at any given time. He walked over towards his desk. Out of his pocket he produced a small key and slid it into the lock. He heard the click of the drawer unlocking and he pulled it out. Inside of the drawer was an ordinary kitchen knife. The tip had been sharpened and even had a dull red color. He let out a sigh and took the knife into his left hand as he sat back down on the edge of his bed. He had tried to slit his wrist once before, but he had been shaking so much that he missed and cut his hand open a bit. He looked down at the small scar on the palm of his right hand and laughed. A small mistake. He was ready this time, there wouldn't be any mistakes. This would be the last time he ever saw himself. He looked down at his wrist, it almost seemed like it was talking to him. "Come on and just cut me open!" it mocked him. "Make us both happy and do it now. Nobody is gonna see you do it. They'll just find you slumped onto the floor in a pool of your own blood." His wrist had a point. It happened quickly. His wrist opened up, blood began to pour out. After a few minutes of staring at his wrist he began to feel faint and dizzy. He lost control of himself and landed on the floor in a heap. Blood flowed freely, sinking into the cellar's concrete floor. Life, for the Fallen Angel had seemingly ceased to exist.
