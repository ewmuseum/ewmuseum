My Life - Part 2

2 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“The usual before you head down there?” Clinton asks me. I looked up at him and nodded my head which prompts him to throw down a shot of Jack Daniels. I take my shot and crack my neck from left to right. I look at myself in the mirror behind Clinton for a few seconds before turning around and following him to the entrance of the basement. Two things have been the same every time I am about to head down to the basement. One is Clinton gives me my shot of JD. Second is this bar bum in the same seat, same trench coat, and same face passed out on the table. “I’ve been comin’ here for the past twelve weeks and there he is, again. Why do you keep letting that bum pass out in your bar Clinton?” He chuckled as if I should know. “Him? He never hurt no one and always pays his tab. He’s harmless, so why not let him drink away his pain and put money in my damn pocket, ya know?” He had a point. Clinton proceeds to give me the rundown of my ‘opponent’ as we walk down the stairs. “This guy is from a bar downtown. 'Parently the resident badass for that area. He’s six three, two hundred and fifty five pounds. Righty.” When this all first started Clinton had told me it would make me feel alive again, give me the fire to compete and he was right. But after winning my first five bouts, word quickly began to spread to all the bars in New York City. I convinced Clinton that there was an opportunity for the two of us to make some big money. “How much is ridin’ on this one?” I asked him. “Ten grand. Biggest pot yet.” I laughed. “And it’ll be like takin’ candy from a baby.” Clinton stopped to turn around and look up at him. “I hope so, ‘cause I aint got ten g’s to just fork over to this piece of shit bar owner if you lose.” I slapped my hand on his shoulder. Clinton doesn’t have anything to worry about but he wants me to assure him of that. “Clinton, its money in our bank. Relax.” Now I know what you’re thinking, this is ‘Fight Club’. You would be somewhat correct and that is exactly what I thought when Clinton first introduced me to what he had going on in his basement. Clinton's reasoning made complete sense to me actually, and it was not to blow up some credit card building. He told me that it was not something he planned but rather it just happened. Fights in bars were common and especially in this Irish pub. In order to keep the customers coming and the cops out, he simple told guys to take the conflict downstairs. No killing, no punching in the balls and no eye gouging. Those were the rules. Fight to a KO or a tap out. And wouldn’t you know it; after the fight was over they’d head back up to the bar and buy each other drinks. Go fuckin’ figure. And that is how it started for him. Since my arrival however, it spawned into a money making opportunity. Cash. Tax free. Let’s face it; everyone likes cash without the government taking half of it in your pocket. I made my way to the ‘ring’ if you could call it that. I took off my shirt, shoes and put on my three once gloves. I turned around and looked my opponent in the eyes and that is when I knew I had him. I could tell he knew who I was from my FWO days and his jaw dropped ever so slightly. There was a small select group present to witness the event. Clinton was walking around taking everyone’s bets and money. “Now you guys know the rules. No crotch shots and no eye gouging. We need to be somewhat civilized. No time limit. Fight till you quit or get knocked out.” I walked to the middle and touched gloves with the poor schmuck. “Good luck” I told him. We both walk towards each other with our hands raises by our heads. The first minute is nothing more than gauging my opponent. I can tell he is timid and nervous. He throws a two punch combo followed by a leg kick. Now I have a sense of this man’s power and it is nothing to be impressed by. I know now that this fight will be done in minutes. I throw my left jab which prompts him to raise both of his hands. I follow up with a right hook straight to his ribs which cause him to hurl forward towards me. I got him in a Muay Thai clinch; and this is where his night begins to go very badly for him. He desperately tries to get out of my grasp but I easily over power the guy. I pull his head down while raising my right knee up. One fluid motion and his nose broke. I felt the bone crumple as my knee connected with this face. I follow up with two more knees before letting go. His eyes are watered up now and his vision is blurry. Now I have a choice here. Do I simply swing for the fences, knock him out, and put this poor guy out of his misery. Or do I take him down and pound on him some more. Right. He staggers backward. Left. His legs are wobbly. Right uppercut. He’s down and out. Some guys groan at the outcome while others are obviously very happy. Money was being exchanged and collected. I slapped the guy on the chin and help him sit up. “You alright kid?” I ask him. He just looked around for a moment collecting himself. “Yeah, I think so.” “You know where you are, what year it is and all that other bullshit? Do we need to send you to the hospital?” He rubbed is jaw and then smiled. “Nah, I’m good. I’ll just pop my nose back into place. I’ve broken it so many times I can’t remember.” I grab his hand and help him up. He looks at me with that same look on his face that Clinton had when I first met him. Here it is again. “You’re that Graphic Violence guy from the FWO, well…when it was still around. Right?” he asks me as he pops his nose back into place. Must say, I was impressed with that. “Yeah kid, that’s me.” Of course he needs to know why I am doing what I am doing but he’s afraid to ask. “If you don’t mind me askin’…why the hell are you fightin’ guys like me to make a buck?” I tell him it’s not entirely about the money. “Actually kid, I don’t mind at all. Let me buy you a drink upstairs. After all, I just made a pretty penny knockin’ you on your ass.” He laughs and says sure, why the hell not. Mostly everyone heads back up to the bar, orders drinks and watches some sports games on the TV’s as if nothing happened. “Clinton, shot of JD for me and whatever this kid wants.” I was twelve and zero and just pocketed five grand with ease since I split the earnings with Clinton down the middle. The pots were getting bigger and the guys willing to come down and fight me were as well. The word was carrying around New York City pretty quick. The man known as Graphic Violence was willing to fight anyone at anytime. Big money to be made if you beat me. As I drank my shot of JD I thought to myself…things are going to be very interesting very quickly.
