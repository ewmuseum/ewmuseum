Day of Decision

30 September 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
All he wanted was a drink. Silver Fox had made himself a scotch on the rocks with a splash of water, and was looking forward to a night in his hotel room, drink in hand, relaxing with his thoughts. Or stressing over the next show. Or stressing over his collection of roster misfits. "I'm out, Fox." He didn't want this. Eli Flair sat on Silver Fox's hotel room bed while the road- worn production manager leaned back in the recliner. Yes, his room had a recliner. "What do you mean, you're out?" Eli had his hands crossed, his elbows on his knees. "I mean, I'm out. I can't do this anymore." Not this. Not now. "EJ," said Fox, "Think about what you're doing." "I have," replied Eli, "I can't do this anymore. I miss Angel and MJ like you can't believe. And with Winters taking off to lord knows where, there's nothing for me to finish here." "Wrestler of the --" "Look at me, Fox," he interrupted, holding up his scarred, tattooed arms, "I appreciate the fact that you were looking at me as one of the people you wanted for the finals, but look at me. I'm not a hero. I'm not marketable. I'm not someone you want to represent this promotion." Fox sighed. "What about Creed?" "Creed? Look at that entire thing. From the beginning, that was all in place to put Creed over as an unstoppable monster. He has all the tools, all I offered was someone that the fans loved to feed to him to strengthen his reign even more." "You know that's not true," argued Fox, getting up, "You did some great bank with Creed, and that's money for something in the future." Eli shook his head. "That's not me. I'm not the champion type. I'm the guy that makes the champion look good by either being a sympathetic babyface for a heel to kill or an overpowering Extreme nightmare for a babyface to topple in a fair contest. That's my lot and I'm grateful for it." "But?" Fox waited. "But," continued Eli, "it's not worth it anymore." Silence. Fox knew how to get him to elaborate. "I told Ivy when she was lying in that hospital bed... this business should never be the most important thing in a person's life. The trick is to find that piece that's more important. And I've found it, Fox. I found both of them." "Angel." Fox said her name, but he didn't have to. They both knew it. "You know how I work, Fox... I work safe, I work hard, and I never let anyone look bad unless they're trying to. But I've been distracted to all hell in the past few weeks and it's shown. Now, with Winters pullin' out--" "That's immaterial. We'll have something new for you in no time." Eli waved him off. "No." What? "I don't want something for the sake of doing something. I want to help someone get over. I want to be over. I want to do something meaningful that the fWo fans will remember for all time. But I also want to step out of the way when I know I can't do it to the best of my abilities... the kids here deserve that much." "The kids want you around," argued Fox, "The kids look up to you." "The kids'll be fine," assured Eli, "They know what to do. Remember Spyder talkin' about the Pack and how we were holding all the cards? He was right about one thing... those who can't need to move aside for those who can. And right now, I can't." Eli stood up and looked into the mirror. "I'm old, Fox." It was the first time he'd ever admitted it. "Somewhere, the business passed me by. I don't have the passion for it, and I don't know that I can get it back." Silver Fox downed his drink. "You know that's not true." "Then I'll make it true." "Why?" "Because if I stick around this business will kill me." More silence. How can a man argue with that? "I don't know that it's forever," continued Eli, "But I can't present it any other way. I can't make a committment to this promotion that I don't know for sure I'll hold onto." A thousand arguments flew past Fox's eyes. A thousand different things he could've, should've, would've said to make Eli change his mind. A thousand different ideas for things he could do to re- spark the King of Extreme. But the truth of the matter is, Silver Fox is old too. He's tired too. And he doesn't have the energy to tell someone that they should choose a wrestling career -- that they should choose anything -- over their own family. Instead, Fox extended his hand. "Good luck." Eli shook. "Take care'a my kids here, okay?" He then turned around, and walked out of the room as Silver Fox sank back into his seat.
