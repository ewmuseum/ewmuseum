Conversations.

7 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Geoff Green walked briskly through the backstage hallways of the Alamo Dome. Every Wednesday was a hectic one when you were the fWo's head cameraman, and recently that had been more true than ever before. Especially today - what with this being the first show since the changeover back to Beaven. He had things to do, places to be, and people to meet. Green turned around a corner and walked headfirst into another man. Green stumbled backwards, knocked to a knee, then regained his bearings and looked up at Scott Wasner, flanked by a platoon of uniformed security. Wasner stood free, but the security still took up positions around him as they provided their, er, escort service. Wasner raised a hand to stop the group, which reluctantly came to a halt. "Sorry, I didn't even see you - oh, Geoff. Geoff! Man, it's been a while." Wasner extended a hand, and Green took it, letting himself be pulled up. "Man, has it ever... so how are you anyway?" "I'm alright. It's been pretty crazy lately." "Yeah, so I've heard. Hey, speaking of, next time you see Travis, could you tell him I'm sorry about this? I didn't mean to... you know... screw up his show and all, although considering what was going on at the time, who knows, he might be happy about it. But tell him anyway." "I will. I take it you really aren't back to wrestle then?" "No. Not a bit." "Not even once?" "Not even once. Besides, even if I was... this isn't my fWo. Not anymore. I don't mean that in a bad way... at least not anymore. They've moved on. Somebody like me... I've got no place here. I mean, the fans... yeah, they cheered..." Wasner paused in reflection. "They cheered. But what were they cheering for? A name? All of them know Scott Slugger's famous, but I bet three quarters of them or more couldn't tell you why. What do they care about somebody who did everything important he ever did in 1999 and 2000? They're a lot like me when it comes to the roster right now - I couldn't tell you who half these people are, where they've wrestled before, or what they stand for. Two years ago, I could've done that with everybody on the roster. My fWo... in my fWo, we never would've let somebody like Wrotkowski come back, whether we had a choice or not. He's a joke, and more than that, he's a cancer. You know that flush you heard earlier tonight? That was the ratings for the first quarter-hour going down the toilet." Green chuckled, and Wasner continued. "We knew better. Back then. And Dudley... Jesus Christ. I remember all those times I was campaigning to bring him back. When I was the only one who wanted him back. Then he comes back and does what he did? I don't know what happened to him while he was gone, but that was not the Jim Dudley I knew. Just like this isn't the fWo I knew. I've been passed by, Geoff... and the thing is, like Phil Collins said, I don't mind. I really don't." Wasner stood silent, and behind him, the security officers fidgeted impatiently. Something occurred to Green, and he looked at Wasner thoughtfully. "What were you going to say you wished?" "Hmm?" "When you were talking to Winters. You said, 'I only wish', then you said you didn't." Wasner frowned. "Hmm. I don't really remember. You know how it is - well, I guess you don't, but when you're out there, in front of a crowd - even when you're as... serious... as I was, sometimes you get caught up and you don't really know what you were saying. It must've been that, especially if I took it back." Green nodded slowly. "Ah. I see." Seconds passed. Green spoke again. "Scott... what was that all about?" Wasner's eyes became hard, and his mouth went from a frown to just short of a sneer. He spoke slowly and very, very coldly. "Geoff. Don't. Ask." Though Wasner had been easygoing and personable through their talk until now, Green saw in his eyes what the crowd had earlier - a steely glint that spoke of pain and a man tiptoeing towards somewhere he'd rather not go. "I'm sorry. I'm an idiot, what the hell was I thinking?" Wasner's face returned - mostly - to normal. "The answer, my friend, is not a damn thing," he said with a hint of a smile. "But... you give Travis my message. These guys are getting a little impatient. If there's one thing I've learned in my life, it's don't piss off big goons. Unless, of course, it's absolutely necessary," he added with a smirk. "Alright. I'll give him your message, and I'll see you around. I gotta get going too. Lots of work to be done." "Of course," nodded Wasner. He then started walking again, and the security officers followed him. Wasner disappeared around the corner as Geoff Green watched. Green saw the last security man step out of his view, and continued watching the spot were Wasner had left. Finally, he turned around and went back to work. ----- It was night. Scott Wasner leaned against his car in the parking lot. It was cold, plenty cold, but he wasn't wearing a jacket. He found he didn't mind the cold. Liked it, in fact. "You know, there's a freeze warning in effect. Heard it on the news." Wasner turned toward the familiar voice. He hadn't even heard its owner approaching - and since its owner wouldn't be trying to camouflage his steps, that wouldn't do at all. "Hey, Geoff. I hadn't noticed." "Right. I bet." Green walked up to Wasner out of the darkness and put his hand on Wasner's car, leaning against it. "Oh... you don't mind, do you?" Wasner shook his head. "Nah. It ain't no Viper, that's for damn sure. The Viper is... well, it's probably halfway to Japan by now. I got this one for $2000 at a used car lot. It's an old Benz, 1985 or so. Turbo Diesel. Those things are great. They run like a cat and they're built like a tank." "What color is it? I can't exactly tell." "Light blue." "Oh. Good color." "Yeah." "So nobody's shown up, huh?" "Nobody but you." "Damn." "Yeah." The two men continued leaning against the car, their breaths blowing plumes of white into the air. "I meant I wished I had done it myself," said Wasner in a neutral voice. "Hmm?" "What I almost said I wished. I wished... I had done it myself. Killed Austin Davis." Green rubbed his hands together, then brought them to his mouth and blew on them. Even in his heavy clothes and coat, he still felt a chill. It wasn't entirely from the weather. "But you didn't." "Yeah. I didn't. I never even thought about it. But the first time I heard Winters say it... I've been watching the shows, and I was actually at the first show in Seattle, what a clusterfuck that was... but the first time I heard Winters say it, the first thought I had was 'I wish I had done it.'" "I see." "Davis... well, shit, I don't have to tell you our history. But I never thought I could actually wish somebody was dead... and that I was the reason. Not even when I was at my most fucked up. But Davis... yeah. Winters said it, and that was what I thought." "Scott... we all think stuff like that. I can't tell you how many times I've thought, 'Damn, I could just kill so-and-so.'" "Geoff... were you even listening? I didn't mean it like that. I meant I really wished I had killed Austin Davis. I even thought about how I would do it. Gun... knife... what it would feel like... what the FUCK does that say about me?" Green said nothing. Puffs of his breath floated away into the sky. "Fuck it," said Wasner. "Nothing's happening tonight. Whoever it was, he's not here. I'm going back to the hotel." "Alright." Green stopped leaning on the car and stood up. "I'll see you around, Scott." Wasner opened the door. "No... I don't think you will." Wasner stepped into the car and shut the door behind him. He turned on the headlights, waited for the light to say it was OK to start the car, then turned on the rumbling diesel engine. He popped the brake and pulled out of his parking spot, leaving Geoff Green standing behind, watching. Absentmindedly, Wasner flipped on the radio and found a song he liked. He sung along. "The gray of evening fills the room, There's no need to look outside, To see or feel the rain. Then I reach across to touch her, But I know that she's not there. Rain keeps running down the window pane. Time is running out for me. Can't you see what you are doing to me? Can't you see what you have done? As I try to pass another long and sleepless night, A hundred crazy voices call my name, As I try to pass them by, I almost can believe that she is here, Here in the glow of the night. Do you know what you have done? Do you know what you've begun? Do you see we shall never be together again? All of my life."
