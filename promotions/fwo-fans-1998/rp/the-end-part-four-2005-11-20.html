The End - Part Four

20 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It wasn't the little girl who had clawed up her face that had Betty so worried. It was her sociopath father that terrified Betty to the core. When she first learned of Rave McBain's plans, she couldn't believe the man she loved was capable of such hate. It was only after listening to some of the horror stories about Vic Creid that she finally agreed to go along with him. Creid was a sub-human piece of vile garbage, one that courts never seemed to be able to touch. An innocent girl would be hurt through the course of events, but that was nothing compared to the innocent lives Vic had destroyed in the past. Now that animal would be after her. Him and that damn daughter of his. Betty's only thought was escape. When she ran into the stair well, she ran up rather than down. Trying to run down seventy-five flights of stairs wasn't going to work. By the time she reached the bottom, Vic would have taken the elevator down to the main floor. So, pursued by the young Creed girl, Betty opted for the roof. At least up there, she could take the observation elevator down to the main floor. She'd underestimated the fury of the near-naked thirteen year old who had almost plucked her eyes out. Sarah was right behind her the entire time, and if Betty wanted to escape she would have to get her hands dirty. Surely she could take care of one little girl. She could hear Sarah screeching like a banshee behind her, Betty's blood probably drying on her fingernails. The little vixen had her father's anger, and Betty stood by while Rave did everything he could to make it fester. He'd thought, once the chips were down, he'd be able to manipulate the young girl into doing what he wanted. He was wrong. Now he was probably dead, and if Betty didn't get to the elevator... The Sky Walk on the eighty-fourth floor was the world's highest open-air observation area. There was a rotating restaurant, but it would be closed tonight in preparation for a special banquet for a visiting Chinese delegate. He wouldn't be arriving until tomorrow, but the restaurant was closed so the staff could take their time in decorating it. The caged-in walkway would be accessible, and it would be a simple matter for her to run around to the other side where the Walkway lobby was located. "Bitch!" Sarah shrieked as Betty shoved the push-bar on the Emergency Exit. A sudden gust of wind almost took Betty's breath away as she ran out into the concrete walkway. To her left, the cement base of the restaurant acted as a wall for the walkway. To the right, four feet of concrete topped off by six feet of reinforced wire fencing. Huge skyscrapers seemed tiny from up here, but Betty hadn't come here to admire the view. She was a little busy trying to stay alive. With the wind blowing around here, she could barely make out Sarah's voice somewhere behind her. She wasn't about to stop and turn around. Keep running, was all Betty kept thinking. Get to the lobby. Get to the elevator. Something struck her in the back of the legs, and Betty dropped to her knees. She quickly rolled to see how close Sarah was to her. It was Sarah who had just tackled her. Betty scrambled back and managed to pull off one of her shoes, throwing at the young Creed. The shoe struck Sarah in the face, slowing her enough so that Betty could get back to her feet, kick her other shoe away, and start her run for freedom once again. She's run barely twenty yards when she stopped dead in her tracks. Yellow Caution Tape had been stretched across the walkway, woven back and forth like a neon spider's web. She looked over the tape and saw that the maintenance crew had removed some of the fence that had been damaged last week when an antenna had blown off the roof of the restaurant. They'd kept it quiet, since the antenna could have caused considerable damage if it had missed the fence entirely and sailed through a window of a building twenty floors down. Betty ripped the tape down and ran through the rest like a sprinter crossing a finish line. The damn wind was really howling now; there was no way for Betty to hear just how close Sarah was. The walkway was well-lit, so Betty just chanced a quick glance over her shoulder. Sarah was doubled over, her hands on her knees, gasping for breath. "Maybe if you spent more time on the treadmill and less on your back." Betty's words were lost in the wind. Unfortunately, someone else found them. "Guess you should have taken the elevator," Vic said as he tightened his grip on the bat. Sarah charged again. There was no way Betty was going to get past Vic. When Sarah got close enough, Betty dropped to one knee and grabbed the young Creed's arm. She twisted and flipped her, using her own momentum to slam her against the fence. The temporary bindings snapped and the four foot length of wire popped out, disappearing into the night. Sarah's eyes were wide with fright when she grabbed at the concrete lip. "Dad!" she screamed in terror. Betty got up and turned around. "Your choice Vic," she yelled at the advancing Juggernaut. "Revenge? Or save your -" It was an underhanded flick of the wrist, but Betty didn't react to the bat spinning toward her until it was too late. She quickly tried to back up but the bat became tangled in her legs. She shrieked when she fell to the concrete, and grunted when Vic drove his knee into her stomach. He kicked her in the groin and stomped down on her head twice. "Stay," he ordered. "Daaaa -" Vic quickly retrieved his bat and ran to the hole in the fence. Sarah's bloody fingers couldn't hang on to the smooth concrete, and her fingernails had started to pull away from her tiny fingers. Vic just made it to her as her grip gave out. Vic slid the bat out to her, and she was able to wrap her hands around the taped handle while her father tightened his iron grip around the fat end. "I don't want to die," she sobbed as she dangled almost a thousand feet above the street below. "You're not going to die baby," Vic yelled to her. He looked into his baby girl's eyes. "I love you Daddy!" Sarah yelled. Those words. Time froze while Vic looked into the face of his flesh and blood. Everything he had done in his entire life, and she was the only thing that was Good. Family, friends, everything else he'd touched had been corrupted and twisted into a caricature of what they'd once been. He'd ruined lives, ended lives, hurt people. He'd walked a cold, dark path for most of his life. The Darkness was home to him. Sarah was his light. Even now, after being tortured endlessly simply because she was his daughter, Sarah could still look him in the eye and say she loved him. That word almost seemed alien, one he'd heard or used for his own purposes but never really felt. Not until he looked into those blue eyes. He felt something inside him move, burn, and for the first time in years he felt alive. The first time since he thought she'd been killed. She'd been dead to him. Now she was alive. A floodgate had been opened in his head, and Vic was awash with memories. He relived Brandish and Chavez violating her corpse, Miles taunting him with her death, Graphic Violence destroying her tombstone. He'd broken down and wept in front of a national televised audience when Steve blew apart her grave marker. The world started to move again, and Sarah's voice was becoming more anxious. "Pull me in Daddy," she wept. "Please!" At that second, Victor Cried had a Moment of Clarity. This girl made him weak. She'd almost driven him to commit suicide. Nobody had been able to hurt him as badly as she had. This little girl had more power over him than he had over himself. Vic Creed was stronger than anyone. Nobody was going to have that kind of power over him. Nobody. The light behind Vic's head cast a shadow across his face. Even that didn't prevent Sarah from seeing her father's eyes. Something happened in that second, and she could literally see his soul die. She screamed "No!" and reached for the ledge with one hand. "I love you baby," Vic said as he let go of the bat. Sarah Creid grabbed wildly and screamed as her body disappeared into the evening sky. Vic stood up and walked over to where Betty had been. He slowly followed the trail of blood to the Emergency Exit and found her fumbling with the door handle. "Let me get that for you," he said as he grabbed her by the hair. "P-pleaf," she slurred through her broken teeth. Bloody drool ran down from her crushed nose and cracked jaw. "I owe you a debt of gratitude," Vic told her. He picked her up and draped her over his shoulder. "What we're going to do is go down to the parkade, get in your car, and drive to another hotel. When we get there, I'm going to repay you for your helpfulness in rape dollars." Betty didn't have the strength to fight him. She could only hope that she'd die from her ruined face before Creed could start with her.
