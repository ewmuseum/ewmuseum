Girls' Night Out - McGinnis

8 July 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“So, now that he’s taken care of I’m supposing it’s down to business. If’n ye don’t know I’m sure ye’ve guessed by now that I’m Siobhan. And this is my close, dear friend The Assistant. And before ye ask, that IS indeed the only name she’s ever known. She’s been with Mr. Reed far longer than I have and that’s about all I know.” Siobhan shrugged her shoulders. “As for my own self, I’m from County Kilarney, Ireland. I’m the only daughter in a long line of brothers, and I know nothing about bein’ Mr. Reed’s manager! Sure’n there’s scheduling and paperwork and settin’ matches and all that, but really – what can and should I be expectin’ in this business? “What am I getting’ meself into?” Ivy McGinnis immediately started to laugh. The rest of the table, as well as a number of other patrons, looked in her direction, but that just made her laugh harder. "Are ye okay?" asked Siobhan. She took off her glasses and put a hand on the side of her head. Ivy's face was turning red from laughter. After nearly a minute, she had mostly stopped. "I'm sorry," said Ivy, "that's just a funny question. What are you gettin' into? You're about to enter a world of mysogynistic bullshit that can pile so high it'll blow your mind." Most of the table remained quiet, which prompted Ivy to continue. "I started off fourteen and a half years ago, when I was but a wee lass of twenty," explained Ivy, "in the CSWA, which was a company known for ridiculously good wrestling and ridiculous women. They had a breed of overweight country bumpkins like Hortense and Melba - good girls, but definite caricatures; and Teri fucking Melton, who was going by the name Miss Hooters. You can imagine what her draw was." The rest of the table, capable, competent women themselves, agreed. "So, take the two polar opposites of shallow, surface characters that they had and add me in there. I didn't fit in. Oh, they tried - for about two months they got me to wear the bedazzled, sequined bullshit and high heels. Who the fuck can move around in heels, anyways?" "Anyways, on that first run me and Eli were there for two years, give or take. Slowly, they figured out what I would and wouldn't do, and they figured out why the fans actually liked me, and it wasn't because I was running around half naked. Although, they tried." "That can't have ended well," said Brittany. "Oh, they tried," replied Ivy, "They threw me and Teri into a tuxedo match at Anniversary 1996, and even though I was doing it under protest, Teri actually tore my top off and... started to laugh. Apparently she thought her plastic floatation devices were better. But whatever, that was an isolated incident, and I told Merritt after that, in no uncertain terms, if they ever try to put me into something like that again I was out of there. And you guys need to understand, nobody talked to Merritt like that, certainly not some twenty one year old chick who he thought he hired for eye candy. But after that, and again when Eli and I made our return in 1998, Bill Buckley, and Rudy Seitzer, and even Merritt himself started to include me in their booking meetings and planning sessions, and I always came prepared. I was an actual asset to the company as more than just an on-air talent. I had a weeklyish column in the Tribune, my own office in the CS Enterprises building, and was pretty much the only person who was never afraid to call Merritt on his decisions when I thought they were wrong. And even when he disagreed with me, he always listened to my recommendations and at least explored the possibility." She stopped for a moment, and took a drink. "And none of it mattered." "What happened?" asked Michelle. "June 26th, 2003 happened," replied Ivy, "Eli had a World Title shot against Mark Windham. Mark was one of the first people in the company to treat me like an equal, and, aside from Ray and Rudy, and of course Eli, encouraged me to keep speaking my mind and giving my ideas. Not that I wasn't sure of what I was saying, but as a twenty one, twenty two year old, what do you really know? At least I knew enough to knew I didn't know anything." She stopped her story as the waitress came by, and the group ordered another round of drinks. "Anyways, Mark was pulling this weird tweener thing, and that match was supposed to be a catalyst for a full on heel turn. But he started to improvise as the match dragged down. Not only did he chair Eli, but he chaired me, then decided it would be a hoot to hike up my skirt and tease like he was about to spank me. He didn't, but he did toss me over the top rope after laughing like a lunatic. We've since patched up our friendship, but I've never really forgiven him for it." All was quiet while the drinks arrived, and Ivy sipped liberally. "Eight years after I first entered this business and changed the rules on what a woman's role is, one of the pillars of the company did his best to tear it down for some cheap heat. So, Siobhan, you wanted to know what you're in for?" "I did," replied Siobhan, tentatively. "You might win over the fans and get everything you want on your own terms," said Ivy, "but a lot of the boys, and even a good deal of the management is never going to see you on the same level as the men. All I can really tell you, is if you're approached to do something you really don't feel comfortable doing, simply say no. I've got the Beav's ear, if it's reasonable I'll always have your back." She leaned in her chair and crossed her legs. "Anyone else want to give Siobhan some advice?"
