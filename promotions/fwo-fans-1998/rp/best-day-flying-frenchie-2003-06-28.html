Best Day: Flying Frenchie

28 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Pierre Delacroix approached the table and pulled out a chair. "I overheard somet'ing about free drinks?" His question was genuine, and his accent, while still obvious wasn't as powerful as The Flying Frenchie's was during the shows. Starkey gave a half nod. "Not quite free, but certainly cheap. You want a drink, you've got to tell a story. What's your best day?" "Business or personal?", inquired the French superstar. Starkey shrugged. "Your call. What about him?" Starkey nodded towards Ric Chronos, who had joined Pierre at the table. "Don't drink," Chronos said curtly. With a nod of his own, Frenchie accepts the offer, calling over to the waitress, requesting a Heineken. His order is met by a few surprised faces. "I blame Damnation. It's an acquired taste." Pierre rocked back in his chair, sipping away at his beer. "I'm sure most have you have had zat women who just clicked wit' you. Zat woman who made you believe in such silly t'ings as fate, and soul mates, and all of zat nonsense. "Her name is Jasmin, and she's been ze single greatest driving force in my life, as well as ze second biggest source of aggravation. First, if you exclude me. "I met her eight years ago, went out for drinks. We connected instantly. It truly was amazing. She was a wrestler, second-generation, and as she described her life, her goals, I was dumbstruck. I had been looking for somet'ing, anyt'ing, and it became perfectly clear zat what she was describing was what I wanted. Well, zat and her. "I t'ink we bot' ignored zose feelings for a little while, as she trained me. It wouldn't have been practical to act on zem. Wrestling is such a close contact, physical t'ing, zat if we had been lovers already, I can't imagine finishing a training session. But in time, it happened, and zat's when I knew." Having drained his first Heineken, Pierre reached for a second, already before him at the table. He took another sip before continuing. "Zat day, it wasn't anyt'ing spectacular. She'd been training me for a couple of months, and I'd finally started to not, what's zat expression, suck donkey." Pierre paused for some polite laughter. "We went out for dinner. Not'ing fancy, and not'ing we hadn't done a dozen times before. We had a little to drink, but not all zat much. Perhaps just enough to give ourselves an excuse, but not so much as to actually take ze decision away from us. We got back to her apartment, and, well, we're all adults here. I imagine you can fill in ze blanks. "It was not one of my more memorable session, trut' be told. It was awkward in a way zat every first time wit' a partner is, but somehow more. We bot' wanted zis to be some grand statement, to live up to mont's of fantasies. But zat's impossible. It simply could not of met such lofty expectations. And zat's when it happened. "When we finished, she didn't excuse herself to wash up. I didn't get up, grab my clotes and leave. She just rolled over into my arms and I held her. And in zat moment, it came to me zat she fit perfectly in my arms. It was as if we'd been designed for each ot'er. We talked, we laughed, and zen we stopped. We just lay zere in silence, perfectly comfortable. We drifted off to sleep toge'ter, and awoke in ze same position ze next morning. "She has brought me a lot of joy, and a lot of sorrow. But I've never been as content as I was zat one night." Finished, Pierre Delacroix smiled a thin smile, before emptying his second bottle.
