Into the Snake Pit - Part 1

26 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The stars had disappeared behind nasty gray clouds, which only added to the stifling humidity in the air. The two men stood in the alley, not moving an inch from their position on either side of the steel door leading into the club. The alley was a maze, twisting behind several buildings before ending at the rear entrance to the establishment known as the Snake Pit. Unless someone was familiar with the area, had they somehow made it to the door they'd have no idea which building it led into. Few people ever made it through the alley. There were predators in the shadows, feeding on the ignorant and foolish who had stumbled into the darkness. Even those who survived the alley only lived long enough to see the two men draw their weapons. Muzzles flashed, bullets tore into flesh, and that was the end of them. The reward given to survivors of the alley was death. Tonight, something prevented the two devout guards from reaching for their guns. Maybe it was the sheer size of the man who had stepped out of the shadows. Maybe it was the blood splattered on his face, his clothing and his hands. Whatever it was didn't matter. What mattered was that they hesitated. In that moment, the big man closed the gap between them. "I'm here to see the Serpent," he said in Thai. The two men looked at each other, and then turned back to the giant. The light shining above the door illuminated the man from the chest down. They could see the blood on his scarred hands and splatter marks on the sleeve of his suit jacket. In his huge right hand he held a postcard for the men to see and extended it toward them. One of the men stepped cautiously down the two stairs and quickly snatched the card away. He turned the postcard over, studied it briefly, and handed it to the other man. "Go ahead," he said in Thai to the giant. The big man passed between them and disappeared through the door. "Did you see the scar across his throat?" asked the man holding the postcard. "Idiot. Don't you know who that is?" snapped the other man. "Who?" "That's Nikolas. The Serpent must have called him home." He watched the other man casually stuff the postcard into his pocket. "The scar on his neck was a gift from one of the Serpent's enemies. He cut Nikolas's throat and left him for dead several years ago." "Why do you think the Serpent summoned him?" The first man resumed his place beside the door. "Probably to finish the job."
