Lord of Strife III

20 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The lord of strife tore up the highway on his way to the Grand Canyon. He did not know why he headed there, but was led by a silent urging. The id he'd become so familiar with called him there, though he did not know it. He saw no one for hours and that delighted him. However, it gave him too much time to absorb the landscape. Most would count it beautiful, but he had seen nothing so ugly in years. This playground of the gods reminded him of that he most loathed: his own mortality. The constant roar of his motorcycle fell silent on his ears. The sound was tranquil, but an occasional sputter reminded him that he needed fuel. That would have to wait. He had reached his destination. Dismounting, Wavyrn walked the path he'd known since childhood. After a few hundred feet, he found himself standing at the edge of a sheer cliff. He looked down to the Colorado River. The drop was several thousand feet. There was a bench there that he did not remember, and the lack of wear there told Bryan that it was new. For no reason he could gather, Wavyrn removed his boots, giving his feet the fresh air he had not felt on them for hours. His socks followed, and he stuffed them in his boots. In a move that would forever puzzle even himself, Wavyrn took his boots up and threw them into the canyon, watching them fall out of sight as they went. Reaching inside his weathered leather jacket, Wavyrn produced a shine chrome revolver. He fingered the instrument delicately as it sat in his lap, seeing with his hands. Slowly, he brought the weapon to his face and turned it in, staring deeply into its rifled muzzle. Into his mouth it went, and the sour, metallic taste made his eyes water. As he fingered the trigger he remembered everywhere he'd been and everywhere he longed to go. Closure would be best, he was sure, but he removed the gun from his mouth regardless. It followed his boots into the canyon, but he did not grant it the same attention as he turned back towards his bike. They would have to kill him. He would not do it to himself. What are you to do when you learn that your most intimate, sacred moments no longer are such?..or ever were to begin with? What do you do when you realize that, for year, you were being watched every single minute of your day? What do you do when someone tells your life, but has no perspective? What do you do when your tale is mangled in a matix of lies and blasphemy? You go off the handle. A few years ago, I realized that answer to these questions. That was when I first read Lord of Strife and when I realized that Trent Harrison robbed me of my life. This was something not even the Disciples did. Sure, they robbed me of rest, peace, and sanity, but the life I had already lived was mine to keep. They didn't mar my memories with their own words for reasons I'll never understand...and that's exactly what Trent Harrison did. And now, I wait for his Downfall. When I sat at the edge of that rock with my gun in my mouth, I hoped to kill myself, but, as is the story in all I do, I failed. I knew my reasons for every action I took their, despite what Downfall would have his readers believe. This is one thing I've always taken pride in: while my sanity may be fleeting, I've never lost control. I've always known what I was doing. I am not a raving lunatic. There is one named Winters for that. His eloquence deceives many, but Downfall's words are little more. The substance lacking therein shine brightly on my mind's eye, where deceit is unmasked for the truth it hopes to hide. Downfall stole my life, but with it I would have willingly parted if it may have saved the other thing he stole. Life...MY life is fleeting, but that other thing of which he robbed me was priceless. He'll never know how it feels.
