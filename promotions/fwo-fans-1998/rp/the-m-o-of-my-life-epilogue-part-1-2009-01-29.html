The M.O. of My Life - Epilogue part 1

29 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Each step that I took sent a jolt of pain through my entire body. It was a painful reminder of the physical torments that my body suffered from the agonizing battle just mere days ago. Normal men wouldn’t be walking after such a fight of massive proportions; but being a ‘normal’ human being was not my forte or prerogative. The chilly winds of New York’s brisk winter felt like soothing packs of ice against my various bruises and bumps. I opened my trench coat in the frosty winds and allowed my body to feel nature’s ice-pack. It felt good. But as I did so, my hand ran across the crumpled paper that rested in my pocket for days. I intriguingly pulled it from the confines of my coat and held it out before me. I didn’t need to open it; I had read its short message about 60 times to myself. [detail withheld] F W O I was no simpleton. I had been in that business long enough to know what those letters stood for. They were the same three letters that I once scorned at, the three letters that represented everything I was not…and everything I was against. It was the corporate leader of that damn business, the model in which everyone followed and attempted to mirror. And while the rest of the world praised their work and kissed their ass, I shot a sole middle finger towards the overrated pile of feces that they were. They were the reason that my home crumbled to the ground. They were the reason that the federation I rebuilt shattered into small pieces. They were the sole reason that the Pinnacle of Insane Wrestling, a federation that was rebuilt through my injuries and my blood, closed forever. The weight of pressure, created by FWO, crushed Jim Johnson, owner of the PIW, and sent him running with his tail between his legs like a beaten dog. His weakness sickened me; even more so after looking at the three letters that reigned victorious over him. F W O. But despite my hatred of them, I questioned how the message was slipped into my coat. Better yet, how did such a note find its way into my life? I realized I was no superstar; I was only a cult icon. When I walked down the streets, no one recognized my face. After all, I was no Flying Frenchie. I was no Scott Slugger. I was no Ric Chronos. I was no sports entertainment superstar. So why was this promotion looking for me? I hadn’t entered a wrestling ring in nearly half a decade. I hadn’t as much as made appearances. I was just one of the many who simply dissipated into the crowd of never-was. And as I sat down on a cold bench of the Fort Greene Park, another thought arose. Could this be a prank? Or worse, a trap? Could someone have recognized me and felt the need to mentally torment me with mystery? Or could one of my former victims have located me and wanted revenge? Many candidates capable of such an act ran through my mind sporadically. Sharc. LLB. Reed Young. KC Hight. Sylo. Names that would be esoteric to the uneducated ear, but names that I fully knew would give their own limbs to extract a form of revenge upon me. However, given my heightened senses, it would be nearly impossible for any of the candidates to have escaped my view; to have someone avoid me long enough to place such a trap was improbable. And then, another thought occurred. When was this note delivered to me? Was it during my brutal battle? Or was it during my time spent at the Hospice? “That man,” I muttered to myself as a shot of self-recollection emerged from the back of my mind. I tore off my trench coat and launched it into the nervous audience. They quickly scattered in fear for a moment, but one of them neatly placed my coat on a nearby chair…how kind. (My Life – Part 4) It was as if my mind pressed pause on a mental video. It paused at the precise moment that my eyes locked on the massive spectator who had neatly placed my trench coat on a nearby chair. Suddenly, features of this man became apparent, for they were very unique amongst the crowd in which he attempted to blend with. He was a large man, much larger than myself or that bald-headed behemoth. While he was dressed in a façade of dirty shirt and slacks, his neatly combed blonde hair and made-up face revealed his professionalism. He was not just a spectator like the other blood-thirsty cowards…he was something different. He was not someone I knew. Not a victim. Not an enemy. Who was he? There was only one was to find out. I walked over to a nearby payphone and dialed each number cautiously. With each number pressed, I began remembering more and more features of the mysterious blonde-haired monster. And as I reached the final two numbers, one feature struck me as intriguing. The T-Shirt he wore was one of the most unique features about him. It was a T-Shirt that no normal spectator would be wearing, a T-Shirt that was given only to a choice few at the time of it's existence. Someone picked up from the other line, but before he could get a word out I interjected sternly. “Who are you? And what do you want from me?” His T-Shirt read C.W.L.
