A History Written in Blood, part 2

21 December 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"How did it happen? Hmm, well gradually I suppose," Tempest said with a shrug. "When I was still a child, every now and then, someone would come along and offer me some food or a little bit of money or a coat, to carry a package to some other part of the city. I was hungry so I didn't ask what was in the package nor why the person couldn't deliver it themselves. In retrospect I imagine I delivered a lot of very illicit things to people. But it didn't matter, it was survival." She stood and retrieved her drink and took a long swig before continuing, "As I got older, I started to get into fights, over food, shelter, those sorts of things. But Daddy got me some martial arts training when I was younger and I relied on that. Then after awhile I was asked by one of these people to fight in a ring. It wasn't much, but I won. And so I came into that profession." "And that was all?" Jacobs asked suspiciously. Tempest nodded with a sigh and said, "Yes that was all. Sure it was largely a skin show at first, but I was not a prostitute." Jacobs sensed a disconnect and said, "I hadn't really associated the two exactly, but you've said that before. Why is that?" Tempest nodded, recalling the cultural difference once again and said, "In the houses of these triads, most anything could be bought. They were opium dens, fights took place and there was a lot of gambling on them as well as Mah Jongg and other things like that. And there was prostitution. There were fighters who were also prostitutes, as well as some who did it all, from pit fighting to undercover work and assassinations. It was all a matter of opportunity and what one was willing to do. Personally I always considered the pit much safer than the bedroom around there." Jacobs nodded slowly, the horror of the life Tempest described slowly closing in on her. "So you fear that people might misconstrue your time there? That they might think you were a prostitute?" she said. Tempest smiled and said, "Sort of. You see in China it makes no difference. I was property; I might as well have been a prostitute. There's no distinction on that low level. A social outcast is an outcast. And inside that world there is no distinction at all." She chuckled ironically and said, "In some ways it was very much like a family. I was lucky to be involved with a group that had a very strict code of honor, almost a throwback to bygone days really. If I got hurt in that pit, I could count on being taken care of. So you'll never hear me call someone a whore as an insult. There are too many whores I called friends and who helped me when I needed it." Jacobs nodded, somewhat confused, and said, "And this is what you feared the public knowing? Tempest I can't see how anyone would look down on you for..." Tempest turned on her heel and stared at Jacobs as if she'd grown another head and said, "Kristen, you don't get it. This is the sort of thing for which I would be returned to that world by any employer in Asia. It's a disgrace, it's something no one wants to be associated with. Besides the simple fact that no one wants to have trash in their company, there's also the fact that once associated with a triad, it's very difficult to sever that association." "And have you?" Jacobs asked pointedly. Tempest blinked and looked mildly flustered as she said, "Of course I have." After a long silence Jacobs tried a different tack as she said, "Well... you obviously didn't just fight women, your style is too different from that even now. How did you survive and evolve into the Tempest we see today?" The corners of Tempest's mouth curled upward into a smile. She seemed to like this topic better and said, "Well, the unfortunate truth was that women didn't make very much for their fights. The only way to raise the fee and the bets was to take greater risks. It didn't hurt that I was already a high flyer and got some attention because of that, but I had to wrestle men as soon as I could manage it. Being acrobatic helped a lot and still does though. What I lack in size I can make up for with gravity." She snickered. Jacobs nodded and said, "But how did you become an acrobat in that environment? It's not the influence that many other luchadores have had." Tempest sat back and smiled, remembering as she said, "Oh I never aspired to be a fighter. It happened by accident, and out of necessity. When I was little, my father took me to the circus. And they had these wonderful clowns. They were acrobats as well as clowns and it seemed to me that their feet never quite touched the ground. They floated on people's laughter and flipped and flew and made people laugh as well." She smiled sheepishly and said, "So I begged my father for gymnastic lessons. I wanted to be a clown when I grew up. I guess some people would say I succeeded." She chuckled and Jacobs smiled a little finally and asked, "How ever did you escape that nightmare world though?" ...to be continued
