12/29/2001 2:00AM Damned California rain... For someone who prided himself on his foresight and planning, the Strangler had picked a hell of a night to go out without an umbrella. No more than 5 minutes after the taxi driver left, the clouds began to blow in... the skies clouded darkly, and opened, pouring buckets of rain down on the streets, the cars...and him. He could see the hotel up ahead now, the neon shining softly like a beacon in the downpour. The Strangler quickened his pace a bit, though by now it didn't really matter much... he was soaked to the bone as it was. After crossing the final street corner, and stepping over a raging torrent that made it's way towards the flooded sewer, he finally reached the front door. As he pushed his way through the revolving door, he noticed a doorman nodding in his direction on the other side of the glass. He had a policeman with him... "Evenin'" the doorman began, with a nervous smile. "What room number are you going to?" "...room 424" was his reply. The doorman nodded again, and checked his clipboard before pointing out the name to the police officer. The two men had a hushed conversation before the officer spoke up. "Are you Paul Adams?" "Yes. Is there some sort of problem here?" "Well, sir..." the officer began, before looking around the lobby and motioning the Strangler towards the security desk area. "...this is best discussed somewhat privately." The two men walked over to the desk, while the doorman eyes him suspiciously. Something about the situation didn't seem right. "What has happened?" the Strangler asked. "Is everything all right?" "Do you know a person by the name of Darryl Bailey?" the officer began, looking over a small list of questions he had in a notepad. "Is this the same Darryl who is a bellhop here? If so, then yes, as an acquaintance... we just met today." "Were you at a pub called O'Leary's this evening?" "Yes, for a short time." "Did you run into Darryl?" "He was there, but we didn't get to speak... we were meeting someone else there." "And who was that someone?" "A business associate. His name doesn't matter right now." "Well sir," the cop continued, "I think it does..." "All right" the Strangler shrugged back. "His name was... Mcdonald... Jack McDonald." Great. He had just lied to a police officer in a statement. But he didn't much want people knowing his business, and what did it matter anyway who he was talking to? Besides, what was this all about? "Do you know where we could find this Jack McDonald?" "He...didn't say where he was staying. He's not a friend or anything, just someone who wanted to try to work out some business opportunities... wrestling related. But what is this about anyway?" "Mr. Adams, the young man we mentioned, Darryl Bailey, was severely beaten outside of O'Leary's pub earlier this evening. Your name came up when we asked him if anyone he was acquainted with was at the bar this evening. There are a few others of course, who I'll be in contact with. Your story places you at the scene of the crime at the exact time it happened. You also have had contact with Mr. Bailey. Normally, this makes you a suspect in the investigation... except for one detail:" "What's that?" the Strangler asked, confused. A suspect... him? Darryl, hurt? It didn't make sense. "The kid will swear it wasn't you. He caught a glimpse of the guy's feet and leg, probably while he was getting his ribs kicked in. Dark cowboy boots, and black jeans. He remembered when he saw you at the bar, you were wearing blue jeans, as you are now, and a pair of Doc Marten boots, black... also still worn. He also said the voice didn't sound like yours... the kid is pretty observant, I just wish he had seen the face. " The Strangler had a pretty good idea of whom the face would match, but couldn't let his own face give that knowledge away. He would deal with that issue later... for now he was concerned with the police officer. "Is he... going to be alright?" "I think he'll be fine... Right now, I'm just trying to completely rule out suspects, and get as much information as I can. Sometimes a witness can make a mistake in the description... so we need to cover all of our bases. Of course, for all we know, it could have been a simple mugging, or someone didn't like the way he looked at him... these things unfortunately happen at a bar, and more likely when alcohol is involved..." The rest of the conversation more or less melted away, as the Strangler became lost in thought. On the surface, he still nodded along and discussed things with the cop for a little while... but underneath, he had his questions as to exactly what the hell was going on...
