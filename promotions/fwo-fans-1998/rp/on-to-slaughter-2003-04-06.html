On To Slaughter

6 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It was nearly five o'clock when they arrived in Woodgreen, England. The first thing they happened to see was an old market. The porch was dimly lit and the outer walls of the home were covered with deer antlers. How Pretty Fly got as far as Greenwood without even as much as loose pocket change was still a mystery... some may say they clang to the side of a boat like their great ancestors did many, many moons ago. Others are smart enough not to try and explain their ways. All it does is lead to headaches. They stood at the curb, their hair tangled and matted with sweat from the long journey, staring at the shop. England was a scary place to be; people used words like 'Bloke' and 'Twat' and for some reason, they were always whacking each other with blunt objects. Not like in France where they use harmless bread loaves, but steel pipes. There was no telling what sorts of lunatics were waiting for them out in 'England' and they did not want to know. All they wanted to do was knock on this man's front door, snag some directions, and get the hell over to Slaughter. Nate and Steve-O walked slowly to the front door and gave it a good knock. "Dude, I dunno' if this is such a good idea... what if he's drunk, you know how the English get when their drunk," Steve-O said. "Don't be such a wuss, bro. We'll be fine, it'll only take a minute." The door sprang open and a man popped his head out. The store clerk, no doubt. Nate scrunched up his nose; this guy smelt really bad... like REALLY bad. His hair was half grey, half dark-brown and hung down in loose strands. "Wha' you fellas' be doin' round 'eer? Eh? EH?" the drunk demanded to know. He looked like one of those people you watch on Unsolved Mysteries; bringing stories of alien probing and midnight seduction within the mother ship.
