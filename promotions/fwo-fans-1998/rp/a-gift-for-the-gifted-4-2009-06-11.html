A Gift For the Gifted (4)

11 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Sam packed and re-packed his equipment as he spotted the man in shirt and tie in his peripheral vision. The man was stalled in the queue to vacate the stands. Dad stood nearby, swaying back and forth as if he were trying to balance on the ends of a teeter-totter. Sam tucked his glove in his bag for the fourth time. The shirt and tie was close to freedom. Sam didn't want to make any quick moves in his direction; He wanted the shirt and tie to enter his space without pressure or confrontation. Jerry, of course, would not wait. “What did you think of my boy?” Jerry shouted at the man, who stood startled for a minute, trying to find the voice that addressed him. Jerry's arm stiffly pointed at Sam, who was slowly rising to his feet. “Hell of a ballplayer, coach!” The man shouted enthusiastically. Sam's eyes evaded the man's glance. “I'm Jerry Snezik,” he said, furiously shaking the mans hand like a maraca. “This is the Samuel Braun.” Sam gripped the man's hand firmly. His mind was floating with abstract thoughts, but his mouth offered the simplest salutation. “Hi.” “Great game out there!” the man said with a smile, switching his gaze to his father. “You two look so much alike.” “Thanks, I guess,” Dad said, not really quite knowing the correct response to his comment. “Pretty soon we'll be watching you in the pros, eh?” the man asked, pinching at his clipboard. “Yeah, you couldn't do anything about that, could you?” Jerry asked slyly as Sam watched the eyelids of one eye clamp together and release in a deliberate wink. The shirt and tie's expression turned cold. “No, I couldn't.” the man said, looking at his clipboard with low eyebrows as if validating his reason for being there. A nervous chuckle from Jerry. “So which club are you from?” “I'm with the Parks and Rec department. Sent me out here to report on how the games worked out today. Parking, Amenities, Field condition. The usual boring stuff but at least I'm getting paid, right?.” Sam, Dad, and Coach Jerry all looked in opposing directions, as if ashamed to be in each other's presence. The four men stood closely together for several moments, before the shirt and tie retreated. “I'm sorry for the misunderstanding, fellas,” he gravely remarked, every word an overwhelming weight pressing on Sam's brain. “But you played a great game. I'm sure some club'll want you. Just stay off those 'roids, kid, cheating's no good for baseball.” “Thanks for the tip,” Jerry said as he quickly ushered the man away leaving Sam with his dad. It was then that the lights in the stadiums went dim, a signal that the lights would turn off in less than two minutes. “I'll go start the car,” dad said, wheezing. “I'm proud of you son.” Sam felt his head being pulled down and the lips of his father's briefly touching his wet scalp. “I love you.” Sam felt like crying, but couldn't. He felt his bile splashing around in his gut, but it stayed put. Silently, he stood with his eyes wide open, trying to understand the decision he made and the harder decisions to come. The trailing sound of dad's coughing was a fitting soundtrack to Sam's mind, both absent and absorbed. He picked up his equipment bag and slung it on his shoulder. The stadium lights extinguished, leaving Sam in the anonymity of night. He took Jerry's baseball bat and wrung the handle in his hands. He took a mighty swing at a nearby light pole and heard the bat splinter on impact. The sound of several rubber super balls followed, their pitch receding as they bounced away. He dropped his fractured gift to the earth and began walking towards the headlights in the distance. *
