You Are Cordially Invited...

9 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Her mind was awry, it seemed, with thoughts, the faded spark of anger and every other emotion that was stirred and swirling around within the redhead who always tried not to show her methodical thought. But her absent-mindedness had blazed through when her foot caught on the backpack she'd had laying on the floor near the bay window. All the laws of physics kicked in then, along with the domino effect as she shot her arms out, ready to topple any instant. What those laws of physics hadn't taken into account, however, was the presence of Brand Frontier, there to lunge forward and catch Amy by the waist before she could tumble. Brandon stifled back a smile in an attempt to keep the conversation on the level it deserved. He had heard the rumors of Amy Campbell's out of ring grace but this was the first time he had experienced it first hand. Laughing now would not be a good thing, especially holding the temperamental girl in such a close proximity to himself. Instead he began to put her to her feet when a flash of light caught him in the corner of his eye. Amy too was caught by this sudden burst of illumination and she looked back, up over her head. Instead of setting herself back up she held her position as the realization of what just happened occurred to both of them. It had been the same thing they'd seen night after night for years: not anything natural, or even industrial lighting...it was the familiar blink of a camera. Coming from where, they couldn't be sure...both had been distracted enough not to label an exact spot, but both were studying the row of buildings beyond the windowpane. It suddenly occurred to the two how last week's pictures of them came to be, and possibly who the 'photographer' across the street, as the case may be, was. The knowledge came as a relief, but at the same time a curse...both knew full-well that even if the person taking the pictures was still watching, which they undoubtedly were, the snake wouldn't hesitate a moment to plaster images of the odd situation all over the press, regardless of Aimz and Brandon discovering the camera. Amy couldn't help but smirk a little, though, still glancing out the window from the corner of her eye. "Hey, Brandon?" "Hmm?" The Paragon was still distracted by staring out through the glass, not quite as amused by the discovery as Amy had been. "You can finish picking me up now." Looking out into the church Brandon Roche smiled to himself. So many friends and family had made the long trip to Nova Scotia that he couldn't help it. Twice as many as he had expected had shown, new seats needed to be added at the last minute. The place had been decorated beautifully; Amy's mother had taken care of that. Brandon's mother, too sick to make the trip, had offered her support over the phone, blessing them with her own good thoughts and prayers. The mighty nervous groom pulled a bit at his bright blue bowtie. It sure was hot in here. In the audience Brandon could see a wave of faces both familiar and not. Deciding not to go the formal route, splitting up the wedding party by bride and groom, both Brandon's and Amy's friends and families intermingled. Amy peered through a well placed set of flowers and ribbons. It was such a vantage point that no one could see her. Her nerves had gotten the best of the fiery redhead and she just had to see how many people had come. Her jaw dropped when she saw the full house. From Brandon's side of the invitations she noticed and could name quite a few. Troy Knight's father Lawrence Knight, who had wrestled as The Last Knight in the E.W.A. sat front row, where her lover's father would have sat if he made it to this joyous day. Next to him was Jon Hawkman, Brand's tag-team partner, again from his E.W.A. days. She could barely see Jarod's little head sticking up over the pews next to Jon. Further back there were all manner of older workers from the wrestling business, more importantly people they didn't know they would really call friends had shone.
