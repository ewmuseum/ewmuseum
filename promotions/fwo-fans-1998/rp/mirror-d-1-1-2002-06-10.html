Mirror'd, 1.1

10 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Shoving a sheath of red hair to the side, Ric fingers the remains of the surgery scar on the back of his neck. Finding a good point, he presses in, and with a sudden thrust he tilts his head sharply to one side. A loud "crack" reverberates through the small hotel room, and Ric is satisfied with the results. "The Divine Right" of Brand Frontier had put an extra kink in his spine, and the momentary relief made the last evening's events seem a little easier to swallow. The Frontier kid was a decent enough wrestler, Chronos had to give him that. Not every rookie could push Ric this hard, and even the ones who might have usually new better. Although Brand's obvious ploys to barricade Ric's path to the World title exposed Frontier for the fool he was, Ric had to grudgingly give him the respect due an opponent of Brand's caliber. A respect that Ric felt oddly lacking when the young punk looked back at him. Ric moved forward slowly, taking the time to stretch out the knots in his back as he slid across the room. Stepping into the small bathroom of the hotel room, Ric paused as he caught a glimpse of himself reflected back in the mirror. He studied his features hard, seeing the lines where there had been none just a few short years before. In those lines he could read again the list of injuries that had afflicted him, feel again the pain that jarred him awake at night. He could see that, although he had quite some time left in him, he was growing older. "Is this what the world sees?" he thought to himself. "A half broken, third generation wrestler, worthy only of being remembered as a footnote in the history of the fWo?" That unbidden thought steeled him, his resolve growing deeper. "No," he countered in his mental debate, "they will not see this. They will look at the man they had forgotten, and they will see that he is still strong enough to fight off the Frontiers, the Creeds, and the Kellys. Instead of a meek, slowing wrestler, they'll see the vibrant, experienced warrior who commands respect, who takes what he wants, and who can NOT BE STOPPED with a goal in mind." That goal was nothing other than to be on top of the heap - only there, with the fWo World Title firmly in hand, could he silence the doubters and the naysayers. The way to the top was through Creed, arguably the #2 man in the federation at the moment, but Frontier's claim of past injustice seemed ready to push Frontier into a position ahead of Chronos - and that was something Ric could not allow. If Frontier wanted to touch Creed, he'd have to do it with Chronos firmly in his path. Two could play at the roadblock, and there was no way Chronos would allow Frontier to get to Creed first. After all, this wasn't just about personal vendettas - this was about the business. Chronos had bleed for wrestling - it was what he lived for, sacrificing everything to make it where he was today. Frontier had yet to really pay his dues, and this type of competition was nearly an insult to someone of Ric's stature. Frontier needed to learn some harsh lessons about the business, and Chronos was just the type of guy to teach him.
