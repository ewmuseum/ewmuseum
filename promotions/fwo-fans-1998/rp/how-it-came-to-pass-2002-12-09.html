How it Came to Pass

9 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
K-Mart. Once the grand store of bargain hunters, home of the "Blue Light Special." But this one had seen better days. No longer open, it's paint had faded and chipped away, it's roof sagged under countless winter snows, and the sign now read "K- ar ." It now stood over an empty parking lot, its asphalt now cracked and potholed as if a bomb had exploded. But the lot wasn't completely empty. Towards the eastern side, sitting next to an old chainlink fence, was a recreational vehicle. It's dirty shell seemed to fit perfectly with it's surrounding, this vintage 1970 beheamoth. I'm sure you're all seen the model, complete with olive green 'W' on the back portion. Walking briskly towards this icon of the American Roads was a nondescript young man. A smile cracked his face from ear to ear as he lept over a large puddle of standing water. Humming a happy little tune, he made his way to the side door and opened it. Well, tried to open it. "Dammit, Mouse," he said to himself, "you're so friggen paranoid..." His name is Mick Pepper, by the way, this wrestling hopeful who now dug into the front pocket of his jeans and pulled out a set of keys. Searching through them, he finally took hold of one and unlocked the door. "Mouse!" he called as he stepped into the dark interior. "Mouse!" "I'm in the back room," came a muffled reply. Mick tossed the keys onto the counter and made his way back. But, of course, this door was locked too. "And just what are you doing back there?" inquired Mick. "Uhhh..... ironing." muttered Mouse from behind the door. Mick just shook his head and walked back into the living area. Mouse's real name was John Lake, but he'd been called Mouse for almost 20 years now, having picked up the name in high school. And whilke a name like that might be assigned because of size, Mouse got his because... well, he was shy. Really shy. "Well hurry up!" said Mick as he took a seat and pulled an envelope from his jacket, "cause I got a surprise. I just got us a wrestling gig. And this one... comes with a contract!" "Contract?" asked Mouse, as the door cracked an inch, "You mean we're going to be bound to wrestle in some school gymnasium for a year?" Mick smiled again, "Nope!" he answered as he began to read over the sheet of paper before him, "Try arenas. This is a cantract for the Fans Wrestling Organization!" "WHAT?!?!" Mouse almost screamed, and the door slammed to punctuate his exclamation of horror, "We can't wrestle for the fWo! We suck! And I don't mean Steven Richards suck, I mean we really REALLY suck!" Mick took a pen and signed a piece of paper, "Come on Mouse, think about it! You and me, fWo Tag Team Champions! And even if that is a lofty goal, think of the experience we'll gain. Think of the exposure! Think of the-" "CROWDS!" came a squeaked reply from the back. "This isn't 100 people gawking at us like our old job, we're talking 20,000 screaming fanatics!" "And 10 million watching on TV," added Mick with a malicious grin, to which a loud groan what his reply. "My stomach hurts," whined Mouse, "I need to lie down or something..." "Fine, you do that," said Mick as he got up, "I'll drive the first leg. We gotta get to Colorado Spring by Sunday... seems I'm wrestling for the United States Championship!" The engine drowned out Mouse's groan at that news, and Mick pulled the RV out of the parking lot, and started their journey. To the fWo. To their destiny.
