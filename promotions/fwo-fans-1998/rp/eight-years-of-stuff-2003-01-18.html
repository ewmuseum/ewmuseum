Eight Years of Stuff

18 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"I've been sittin' here tryin' to find myself I get behind myself, I need to rewind myself Lookin' for the payback, listen for the playback They say that every man bleeds just like me" "Talked to Trip last night?" "Yeah." "How's he doing?" "Better. We didn't just talk, we talked." For eight years now, this unlikely duo has been conquering the world of professional wrestling, one arena at a time. Eli Flair, reclusive Hardcore Wrestling pioneer who has spent the majority of his time earning the respect of his co- workers, peers, and fans by a seemingly tireless work ethic, an awe- inspiring personal dignity and blunt honesty about his personal life, and a complete lack of patience for locker room politics. Poison Ivy, on the other hand, handles the business end of their career, doing the interviews that he won't, and playing the political game via her biting commentary dubbed 'The Ivy League' - that offers no apologies. "I feel like number one, yet I'm last in line I watch my youngest son and it helps to pass the time I take to many pills...it helps to ease the pain I made a couple dollar bills, still i feel the same" For the most part... they've been successful. At the moment, the duo have just finished dinner at a fairly upscale restaurant to celebrate eight years of business together. No Angel, no Triple X, no Rat Pack. The two that kicked off a revolution eight years ago decided it was only fitting to celebrate their "anniversary" as a duo. The trouble is, conversation was forced and sporadic. The relationship these two once had - beyond family and friendship - had deteriorated over the course of the past six months almost to the point of no return. "Be right back," said Ivy, getting up from the table. Two empty cups of coffee sat between them, as well as two empty plates. One was home to a slice of key-lime pie (Ivy), and the other, chocolate mousse cake (Eli). As the waiter came by to clear their dishes, Eli grabbed his arm. "Two glasses champagne," he ordered, "Whatever is your most expensive brand. And the check with that, if you please." It's not a matter of losing a friend... just of shifted priorities. Where they used to be alone in the locker room - 'outsiders amongst outsiders' as Ivy liked to say - they've got an entire cast of regulars. Eli Flair used to be focused one hundred percent on his wrestling career, on nothing in his life being more important than the show; than every night in front of a new crowd of people. But while the faces in the crowds changed every night, the people in the back remained a constant. And over the course of eight years, it's inevitable, friendships would form. "Everybody knows my name, say it way out loud a lotta folks fuck with me, its hard to hang out in crowds I guess that's the price you pay to be some big shot like I am outstretched hands and one night stands, still i cant find love" In Poison Ivy's case, the unlikeliest of all - "Triple X" Sean Stevens - became much more than a friend. "...and when your walls come tumblin' down, i will always be around..." "Ready to go?" Ivy was back. "Wait a sec, Ivy," replied Eli, "Sit down." She did so, draining the last vestige of coffee from her cup. "How'd we get here, kiddo?" "I'm not sure what'cha mean," replied Ivy, "How'd we get where?" "Here," answered Eli, "To this point. Eight years ago I was a basketcase and you were a PMS- laden ball of attitude. Somewhere along the way... we both mellowed." "I don't think either of us mellowed," countered Ivy, "I think we've learned. I think we've taken what we could from the shit we've done and had done to us... and to be honest, we're both a lot better for it." She leaned back and crossed her legs. The faintest bit of scar tissue is visible beneath her stockings, the circular mark left by a broken glass bottle. "I mean, who'd ever have thought you'd be a mentor to these kids? Who'da thunk it that I'd be some kind of locker room leader," continued Ivy, "Tell me that five years ago, tell you that five years ago and I'd have called ya crazy." "People don't know about the things I say or do They don't understand about the shit that I've been through It's been so long since I've been home I've been gone I've been gone for way to long" Eli shook his head in disagreement, "I don't think either one of us are what you could call model citizens. I don't particularly like people, and neither do you." "Oh yeah? Then you explain the phrase 'Rat Pack' to me." "That's different." "Is it?" "... No, not really." "Face it, EJ," said Ivy, "We're both adults now." Maybe it was how she said it. Maybe it was the look on her face. But something... in that statement has caused Eli Flair to laugh out loud. "You and I are two of the last links in this business from this generation and the previous. And I know Trip's never forgotten how we helped the CSWA to take a second look at him." An ambient look crossed Ivy's face for a moment, "There's a lotta things about Sean that've changed me." "Obvously." Looking up, Ivy's face suddenly reddened. "Shut it, old man." "I can see your point, though," said Eli, "The Family... everything we tried to do for the fans blew up, but for some reason we really clicked with some of the boys." "And girls," admonished Ivy, "Or have you forgotten about Tempest and Aimz?" "No, no," assured Eli, "Haven't forgotten the girls. Look at where we've come to, tho - Trip, Brandon, Alex, Amy... travel buddies, confidants... friends." "Not too shabby, aye?" "Maybe I forgot all the things I'd miss Oh somehow i know there's more to life then this I said it too many times and i still stand firm You get what you put in, and people get what deserve" "Not at all." Finally, the waiter returned with two glasses of champagne. Ivy sat up suddenly as she took one of them from his hand. "What's this about?" "A toast," answered Eli, lifting his glass, "To Team Extreme." "To Alicia, to Randy Harders, to Shannon," replied Ivy, "and everyone who's gone before us." "And to Steve Radder," continued Eli, "Deacon and Chris Shepherd, Mikey Randalls, Troy and Mark, and everyone who's told a story in the ring with us." They clinked glasses and were inches away from drinking, when Ivy suddenly stopped. "Still I ain't seen mine, no I ain't seen mine I've been givin' - just ain't been gettin' - I've been walkin back that line So I think I'll keep on walkin with my head held high I'll keep movin' on, and only god knows why." "What?" asked Eli. "One more," replied Ivy, "To the Rat Pack." Clink. "The Rat Pack," agreed Eli, "the ones who keep us sane." Salute.
