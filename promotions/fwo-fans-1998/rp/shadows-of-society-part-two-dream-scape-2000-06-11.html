Shadows of Society: Part Two, Dream Scape

11 June 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June Tenth, 2001 Time: 11:28PM Eastern Standard Time Location: Room 211, Motel 6, Norfolk, VA "Dreams are the touchstones of our characters." -Henry David Thoreau The door to room 211 at the shoddy Motel 6 in Norfolk swung open with a loud whine and creak, the old hinges begging to be greased again. A hard rain had been falling for half an hour when Donaven Winters stepped inside of his dimly lit room. His hair was matted down and his black shirt was soaked and clinging to his body, bringing out the faint lines of his chest and torso. He let out a large sigh as he threw down his luggage and kicked off his combat boots. His white socks were a dark grey from the water that had sept through his boots and he took those off as he sat down at the lip of the bed. After taking his shirt off and tossing it into a corner of the room where it draped over the back of a chair he laid himself down on the bed. A small smile crossed his face as his heavy eyelids slowly closed over the slate blue eyes. He had his body curled up onto the small twin sized bed, although his feet still hung over the edge. The bed was a disgusting sienna brown with a motif involving oblong circles and different shades of dark reds and browns. Though the colors of the small bed were far from his mind. He had worked an easy match earlier in the evening, getting the victory over Jackhammer. The only thing on his mind was sleep, the dream world, but for an insomniac sleep was hard to achieve. Except on this night, he was feeling overtired and quickly fell asleep. The smile on his face slowly faded and eventually became a frightened frown. His eyes blinked beneath the eyelids and the water from his soaked head began to dribble down his face. They say that dreams are the gateways to our pasts, presents and futures. Dreams can link things that a person may not remember. Things that may have been repressed and made to be forgotten. Dreams are what people seem to forget about, unless something odd happens in them. Dreams can be a hellish nightmare about a horror movie two little children had just watched. And dreams can simply be the end. Donaven Winters was dreaming all of his worst nightmares at once. Ben Winters bellowed at the top of his lungs as a young eight year old Donaven Winters ran silently through a back alley just outside of the rundown apartment building. His father was drunk once again and felt like pummeling on the little boy for his own joy. Donaven gasped and panted as he reached the end of the alley, nowhere to escape, just a ten-foot high brick wall in front of him. He sat down amongst the rubbish and began to sob, he heard his father yell out again, hearing the sound of a baseball bat tapping against the brick. Almost instinctively young Donaven started to lift cover himself in the disgusting garbage, trying to hide himself from his own father. "Donaven!," Ben screamed. "Where the hell did you go, boy!? I've got somethin' really nice for you out here. Just come on out and get it. I got you the skates you asked for last Christmas, Donny. I finally had the money to buy them for you, and I did. Now, just come on out from wherever you are. We'll go back inside and I'll give ‘em to you." He knew he couldn't let himself fall for that trick again. Even at eight years old he knew how deceptive his father could be. Any money he got went straight into another fifth of vodka, rum or gin. For Ben Winters, money equaled hard liquor. For Donaven Winters, hard liquor equaled serious beatings. He always wished that his mother would help him, but she just watched it happened, crying silently. This was almost becoming an everyday occurrence, Donaven's sobbing finally drifted himself off to sleep, buried under the rubbish of the poor. On the bed in the small motel room Donaven's body turned onto it's back while he slept. The usual and assumed look of peace while sleeping wasn't there. Instead his face wore a look of depression, angst, sadness and confusion all at once. The sun shone brightly down on the lake, cascading a yellow glare and reflection from the dark water. Diane Wilson laughed lightly as Donaven smirked after telling a joke. The two were out somewhere on Lake Superior enjoying the hot day on July Seventeenth. Donaven was paddling the canoe as Diane just sat there, watching him and smiling. They were basically all alone in the middle of the lake. It was so peaceful, serene, quiet. There was no one that could possibly disturb them. In his sleep a small smile crept across Donaven's face, in memory to one of the good times. Once again he rolled over, this time on his right side. His body kind of stretched itself out and he let out a cough. Donaven tried to get back his Miller Genuine Draft from Diane inside of his 1988 Cutlass Supreme Oldsmobile. The two were only sixteen years old and had just come from one party, heavily intoxicated. Diane had taken Donaven's beer and wouldn't give it back. As he played a very dangerous game of trying to drive his car and retrieve the beer his foot slowly pushed down on the gas pedal. Diane was laughing hard in her drunken stupor as Donaven tried his best to act seriously while trying to get his drink back. Little did the couple realize that there was a very sharp right turn around one of the ledges that jutted out where the road should be straight. Donaven's right foot had pushed the accelerator to the floorboards of the car. Being drunk, trying to get his beer back, driving drunk and doing stupid things like not paying attention to the road– He missed the turn. Diane's scream was heard as the car went into the metal guardrail at 95MPH. Donaven finally realized what he had done and couldn't correct it as he watched Diane's head fly into the windshield, cracking it. His own head hit the steering wheel, that was the last thing he remembered. Donaven shot up straight out of the hideous brown colored bed in the Motel 6. Sweat was pouring off of his face as he threw the covers off of his body and stood up to go into the bathroom. He looked at his face in the mirror, it was a mixture of tears, sweat, confusion and realization. "I," he whispered to himself, trying to slow down his breathing. "I killed Diane. It was my fault..." Donaven dropped his head down and it made contact with the sink. The tears came almost naturally as all of the repressed memories came back, haunting him all at once. Donaven had just taken the first step into a new frontier. It wasn't always Society's fault.
