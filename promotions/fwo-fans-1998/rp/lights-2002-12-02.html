Lights.

2 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The florescent lighting of the Rosemont Convention Center overcast every piece of memorabilia on the large arrangement of tables. Faceless individuals gathered around to bask in undying appreciation of the possessions of others. While winter winds blasted against the exterior concrete walls of the building, the swell of the crowd made the place exude some sort of humanized warmth. Lines of people appeared in front of a large stage. One long table ran across the back part of this stage with a microphone stand placed at the center, in front of the tables. An unknown individual—tall, attractive, and young—walked out with a measure of white index cards hugged to his chest. The people watched in anticipation as the man grabbed the microphone. “Welcome everyone to WrestleZone, a yearly convention held right here in Chicago, Illinois. Today, we have four wrestlers from various federations.” The man spoke in a booming, enchanting voice. The fans reacted with excitement to the first three names, and anxiously awaited the fourth. The face of the announcer contorted into a small grin. “And we would like to thank this fourth individual for taking time from his busy schedule to stop by and sign autographs for you folks. Everyone welcome to the stage...Downfall!” On cue, the dark man stepped out from the from behind the red velvet curtains. The crowd beamed. All of other visitors the convention were from the shadowed barracks of unpopularity. Security guards stood at the head of each line. One by one, people approached Trent with various artifacts and memorabilia to sign. Some praised his talents. Some preached their undying love and adoration. Some merely walked up, got their signed pieces, and took off without a single word. Those were the oddest form of people. Those were the ones that etched themselves in Trent’s mind. The hour had passed. The announcer from before paced up the steps. “I’m sorry, everyone, but these men have important things to do. Come back tomorrow for the third and final day before we pack up and head out of here. Everyone give these guys a round of applause!” Screaming fans were the departing message for these four men. All walked to the backstage area and gathered what personal effects they brought with them. Trent grabbed his leather jacket and threw it over his toned body. He began to walk out, amidst the fans that were once cheering for him. On this level—their level—he was no longer a bastion of wrestling legacy. He was just another man. Nothing about him screamed “Superstar”. ...but maybe that was what everyone found so intriguing about him. Money, fame, and success did not scathe his lifestyle. Everything was routine and similar to the past, minus the traveling. Trent stepped out of the convention and walked through the parking complex. The patchwork of Chicago sounds mixed themselves as background music for the scenery. The city was an unmistakable icon of Americanism. Whereas travelers usually found themselves absorbed in the city’s eccentric nightlife and busy daylight, Trent drove plainly around, searching for a small place to eat. Everything was big, detailed, and gaudy. Simplicity was lost in places like this. Everyone wanted to feel rich, or feel important. Nevertheless, Trent pulled into a Rainforest Cafe. A younger man lost somewhere in his late teens or early twenties approached. A green apron covered his collared dress shirt and pressed black slacks. “Smoking or non?” Trent shrugged. The man assumed apathy toward placement and seated him near a window. He touched the glass and left a warm handprint on the cold surface. The waiter looked toward him and coughed lightly. “Aren’t you that wrestler?” he asked with a whisper. Trent nodded in assurance. The recognition was odd and new. Trent sat in silence for the rest of his dinner, but eyes shifted toward him occasionally. Giddy whispers filled the background, overshadowing the noise of idle conversation. ”So this is strange...” – Dashboard Confessional
