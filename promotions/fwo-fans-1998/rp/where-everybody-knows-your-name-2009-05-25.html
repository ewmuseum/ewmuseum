Where Everybody Knows Your Name

25 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Since when does Cally listen to classic rock?" asked Brian McGinnis, as he and his baby sister Ivy approached the door of TC's Bar and Grill in the Bronx. "Jukebox, I guess," replied Ivy, "and by the sound of it, there's a lot of people inside." "Good," answered Brian, "Good for business, right?" "In theory, just surprising, this time of day." Ivy had taken her brother up on his suggestion to spend a few days back home. The continued stress of being the Vice President of Talent Relations had worn on her for weeks, and she desperately needed a break. Although she owned or co-owned places to live in Seattle and Greensboro, and had her man Triple X Sean Stevens' place in Orlando, the word 'home' meant only one thing: New York City. Her plane landed at 11:52 PM, and, when Brian picked her up, Ivy told him she wanted exactly two things: one, to go to Coop's, just over the Bronx border, and, two, to stop at Duff's on the way and down a shot with Jimmy. Shots have been fired, and TC's awaited. "What the--" Ivy opened the door, to see a bar nearly half full. Typically, that was a successful Sunday night for TC's, but this was Memorial Day Weekend, and the local watering hole was typically fairly far down on a list of priorities. For that reason, there had only been one person scheduled for the night. Behind the bar, Rosalyn Callasantos worked at a fever pitch, serving up drinks as fast as she could. After every one, she made a quick mark on a pad behind her and moved on to the next person. Without a second thought, Ivy pushed through the two - deep crowd and joined her beleaguered employee. "Miss Ivy?" said Cally, surprised, "What are you doing here?" "Talk later," replied Ivy, "Let's knock these guys out. What's happening?" "Bachelor party," explained Cally, "they're all on one tab so just keep track of what you pour and we'll add it all up." Ivy nodded. Brian got behind the bar as well and bar-backed for the ladies, keeping everything they needed well stocked. The ladies weaved in and around each other despite the limited space, and worked in tandem to serve the thirsty patrons as quickly and efficiently as they possibly could. Finally, after many minutes, the rush slowed to a crawl, and the members of the bachelor party collectively regrouped to the other end of the bar. Brian, used to working hard but not prepared for a sudden grind of an evening rush, sat down and leaned over. Cally filled a glass with ice and water and put it in front of him. "Damn, kiddo," said Ivy, "that was good." Cally shrugged. "About average. If I'd known they were coming, I'd've scheduled someone else tonight." "So it's this busy regularly?" asked Brian, his eyes wide. "Yeah," said Cally, shrugging her shoulders. "Johnnie, Miss Ivy?" "Sure," replied Ivy, "Wait---no. Absolutely fuck not." Cally's hand froze on the bottle. "PBR," replied Ivy. "Gross," said Brian. "Make it two," continued Ivy, ignoring her brother, "one for each of us." Cally put the bottle back and reached into the cooler, pulling out two cans of Pabst Blue Ribbon. She cracked both tops and handed one to her boss, and they hit the two together. Ivy took a long drink, leaned back, and closed her eyes. "Yeah, man... that's the shit." "It's shit, alright," said Brian, making a face. "I mean, this is what I've been missing," replied Ivy, "You know what I was doing three months ago?" Cally shook her head. "You were in Afghanistan?" Both McGinnis' looked at her, and Ivy laughed. "Touche, Cally. What I meant is that I was a member of the FWO's locker room camraderie, after shows we'd go to Redbeard's and get a platter of wings and a few pitchers, talk about the show, and get home at three or four in the morning, on the far side of buzzed." A wistful look crossed her eyes. "It was fun, y'know?" Cally took a few steps back to get a drink for an individual customer, but nodded that she was still listening. "Now, I'm one of them - the management team." "You're management here too," pointed out Cally, "Six," she said to the customer, "Thanks. But even though you're my boss I don't see a problem havin' a beer with you, even while I'm working." "Professional wrestling is different," said Brian, "The boys have always had a distrust - even unconsciously - of the bosses." "RK trusts you," said Cally. Ivy laughed. These days, people referred to Randall Knox as Impulse 99% of the time, but Cally, even when they were in highschool together, was always the only one who called him by his initials. "That's because me and Eli, and Angry Johnny and Coop were the first authority figures Knox ever associated with wrestling, and we never did or would fuck him over. Another month of Dennis and he'll have a different view." "So why'd you take the job?" asked Brian. "Because that's not the job I was taking!" replied Ivy, her voice carrying over the entire bar. Some of the patrons turned toward her, but quickly went back to their conversations. "I was hired as the head of Talent Relations," continued Ivy, more calmly, "I was supposed to be the liason between William and his staff, and the locker room. Mediating arguments, being an impartial arbitrator, handling complaints on both ends, and all that jazz. William's got me booking matches, finding talent, dealing with the press... it's too much." "So why do you do it?" asked Cally. "Because I'm good at it," said Ivy, "Because I used to enjoy helping the FWO." "Don't you anymore?" "It's not the same," she continued, "Years ago I would help out Fox, and we'd talk about it afterwards. We'd watch stuff together and figure why it did or didn't work, and go from there. It was a back and forth. With William, I feel like I'm his personal assistant who runs the store while he sits in an ivory tower and counts his money." "He does go to the shows, right?" asked Brian. "Of course he does," said Ivy, "and I am being a bit unfair. He does whatever he has to - but the boys are partly to blame for coming to me instead of going where they should be, and I'm partly to blame for letting 'em." "So your problem is you." Ivy and Brian both looked at Cally. "What?" "William Beaven is your boss, and you're doing your job and his?" asked Cally. "In a matter of speaking," admitted Ivy. "You're my boss, Miss Ivy," continued Cally, "I tend bar, I do the deposits, I manage the schedule, I keep the upstairs loft clean, mainly because I'm the only one who uses it. I do the bar laundry because I can pull the money from the register. But if I found your clothes mixed in with the wash, besides the occasional emergency spills, I'd sit you down for a talking to because I'm not your housekeeper." She took another long drink of PBR. "I think you need to talk to Mr. Beaven." Ivy looked at the twenty one year old Cally, and then back at her brother. She laughed. "See, Brian? This is why I wanted to come here before I went home." Brian smiled. "So you know what to do now?" "I know what to do now." Ivy held up her can of beer, and Brian tapped his glass against it. "But for now, I think we owe Ms. Callasantos the courtesy of our assistance for the rest of the night."
