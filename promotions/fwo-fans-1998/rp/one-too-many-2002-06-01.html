One Too Many

1 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Larry wiped down the bar for the sixth time in the past half hour. Business was slow in the lounge, with only a few patrons ordering anything at all. Most of them were seated at various tables, chatting with each other or watching ESPN. Larry considered turning off the television, or at least changing the channel, to drum up some more business. People were distracted from their drinking. Except for the big guy in the corner booth. He was sitting by himself, draining Bud after Bud like he was on a mission to extinguish every drop in the bar. That seemed to bother Larry a little more than the lack of business that the rest of the lounge was providing. The big guy looked like a rough customer. He was sporting a shiner, and had a finger taped to a wooden splint. He was a scrapper, no question about it. But while the rest of the place was drinking to enhance their social mood, the big guy seemed to be drinking to escape something. The waitress stepped to the side of the bar. "Two more Bud for the big guy," she said. Larry picked up two tall glasses, placing the first under the spigot. As he filled the glass, he noticed the waitress looking over in the big man's direction. "He sure can put them away," she said quietly. "Over a dozen in the past hour." Larry handed her both glasses. "I hope we don't have to carry him out at the end of the night. He looks like he'd be a handful." He shot a smile at the waitress. "But I'm sure you wouldn't mind getting your hands on him." She smiled, slapping Larry on the shoulder. "Shut up!" She set both glasses on the tray and carried it across the room. He did look quite yummy, she thought to herself. But he didn't look like he was in a very social mood. When someone is drinking with a purpose, it's best just to let them be. Plus, she knew who he was and knew better than to bother him unless he wanted to be bothered. "Here you go, Mister Creed." She set both glasses beside his huge arm, plucking the empty glasses off the table and placing them on her tray. He slid two bills over to her, but otherwise remained motionless. "Can I get you anything else?" Vic turned his head slightly. "No," he said simply. As the waitress produced his change, Vic shook his head. "Keep it." She paused. He'd been telling her to keep the change since he started ordering. But over the past half hour, he'd been paying more. Normally she wouldn't object to a sizeable tip, but Kodiak Vic Creed was known as something of a powder keg. The last thing she wanted to do was get on his bad side. "Thanks Mister Creed." He gulped down the first glass and slid it over to her. "Might as well take that one back with you," he said as he licked the foam from his upper lip. "And call me Vic." "Okay... Vic." She thought it was a little odd that he hadn't even looked at her since he sat down an hour ago. He was definitely going over things in his mind, and everyone else was taking a back seat to his thoughts. "Sarah, don't pester the man," Larry teased. The big Alaskan turned his head and, for the first time, looked at the waitress. "Your name is Sarah?" he asked. Sarah looked into Vic's bloodshot eyes. He was sporting a bit of a mouse under one of them, and his nose looked as if it had been broken and freshly reset. He looked pretty roughed up, which was something she'd never seen in all the times she'd watched him on television. "Yes it is," she said with a smile. He turned back to his second beer. "That's a nice name," he said faintly, as if lost in thought. "That's my little girl's name." "What a coincidence." Sarah quickly wiped up the rings of condensation around the table. "Well, enjoy your beer." Vic looked up at her again. "You must be a wrestling fan. You knew my name." "I've seen you on T.V.," she said. "My brother and I watched fWo all the time until that thing with Ric Chronos happened." Vic nodded lazily, the alcohol finally starting to faze the big man. A few tables away, a man wearing a Manchester United soccer jersey snapped his fingers. "Chop chop," he said loudly. "We're parched over here." Sarah turned, but Vic gently placed a hand on her upper arm. "I don't get to see my little girl very much anymore. It's nice to talk to a Sarah, even if she's not my Sarah." He set his second empty glass on her tray, the foam remaining on his moustache. Feeling slightly uncomfortable with Creed's hand on her arm, she tried to disarm him with charm.
