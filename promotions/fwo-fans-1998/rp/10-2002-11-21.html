previous parts: see-downfall archive The hotel room was devoid of any kind of obvious movement. Subtle whispers of deep breathing escaped from under a bed sheet. Morning greeted all with its splashing sunlight. Today, the weather remained biting cold but allowed the clouds some rest. All the previous night's events had began to wear down the young man. Two hours would pass before a knock of the door would break the silence. Disregarding Downfall's obvious apathy, the knocking continued. "David Harper with the WWR," yelled the man. Trent threw the coverings to the side and threw on a complimentary bathrobe. He walked to the door and placed his hand on the knob. A check through the peephole showed a short man even younger than Trent decked out in a brown sleeveless sweater, a collared pinstripe button up dress shirt and a pair of brown slacks that completed the outfit. Add to that a clipboard and everything's complete. A mop-like rag of hair remained at the top of his head. The color was odd, like a patchwork of different reds. Trent opened the door and allowed the man to enter. "We have a lot of stuff planned for you today, buddy." A rush of confusion overcame Trent. He didn't remember planning anything, let alone associating the small individual standing before him. For a moment, he thought to dismiss him as a phony, but the man then extended his hand. "My name is [name withheld]. I've been hired as your own personal assistant. Today, we're going out on a photo shoot and then stopping by some indy promotion to hype up the next Friday Night Main Event. That sound good to you?" Trent stopped paying attention somewhere in the middle. Now, he fiddled around inside one of the drawers, searching for something to wear. He produced a Dashboard Confessional t-shirt, a pair of oversized black canvas pants, and a piece of bungee cord he tied around his neck. Roger shied away as Trent threw on his clothes and began to lace his black Converse All-Stars. All speech was irrelevant. Downfall ignored his new employee's attempts at idle conversation and watched the road ahead. "We're going to a studio in downtown Chicago. You familiar with the area?" asked Roger in some sort of attempt to coax his manager out of his silence. After a numerous attempts, he too basked in the beauty of silence. Trent hugged his trench coat to his chest, ducking his chin underneath the now unfolded collar. The Chicago winds broke against his pale yet rough skin. A shiver shot up his spine, assumingly from the frost inducing cold. Glass buildings surrounded them. Trent couldn't even guess which of them they were about to enter, but the walk was short. Elevator music played in the background as the climbed up numerous floors. An uneasy feeling filled the elevator, accompanied by an awkward silence. Roger sighed outwardly as he contemplated the boredom of his new profession. "Here we are," Roger stated as the elevator door opened with a high-pitched ding. Just as he passed the threshold, a random individual approached. The man was decked out in a suit and, in all likelihood, somehow related to the photo shoot. Trent eyeballed the cameras, the make-up cabinets, and the various items strewn about the oversized room. Somewhere, a cell phone rang.
