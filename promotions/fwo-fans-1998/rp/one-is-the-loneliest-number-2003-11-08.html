One Is The Loneliest Number

8 November 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The following took place on Friday, November 7, 2003. Jeremy Savage writhed anxiously in his tuxedo as he stood idle in the backstage of the awards show. He fidgeted with his bow tie impatiently as he stood behind the thick burlap curtains. He reached down and played with the band out of habitual impulse, constantly believing that it possessed a life of its own, and it was actually trying to strangle the life from him. The atmosphere was inappropriately warm, and he frequently found himself wiping away the perspiration on his forehead beneath his long, neatly-combed brown hair. Nevertheless, the entire situation was one that he didn't want to be in. "Your father was a great man." a voice behind him spoke, which made him jolt with surprise. His neck swiveled slightly back at the graying old man and flashed a heartless, wry smile. "I guess it depends on who you ask, doesn't it?" The man could easily see Jeremy was uncomfortable, so in the awkwardness of the moment he smirked nervously and sauntered off. Alone once again, Jeremy brought his ear closer to the curtains to eavesdrop on the introductory speech being given on the other side. The man's voice was rich and bold, and his speech shamelessly entertained the audience with praise upon heartfelt praise. No matter how much he hated his own father, Jeremy couldn't help but agree to some of the labels being thrown out: 'pioneer' 'journeyman' 'champion' . However, it didn't take long for the speaker's eulogy to get out of hand. Much to Jeremy's dismay, the man lathered it on like the foamiest of shampoos; going to great lengths to massage the intellectual scalps of his audience with totally unnecessary and undeserved rhetoric. Jeremy felt he was about to get sick because of the man's ridiculous speech, but he quickly came to an abrupt halt to finally do his job: to announce the winner. "And the Lifetime Achievement Award goes to Peter Savage! Accepting on his behalf is his son, Jeremy." An overwhelming positive reaction quickly erupted throughout the banquet hall. Jeremy knew his sad excuse for a father was popular in the industry, but he had a sneaking suspicion that the introducer's kind words had an added influence on the response. Jeremy took a deep breath, and hid himself beneath a mask of fake feelings. His face turned to a look of humbleness as he ascended the stairs and onto the stage. He took a few steps before being blinded by the abrasively hot spotlights overhead, which reminded him of a desert-like environment. Time slowed down as he casually peered into the crowd, which was full of suits giving him a standing ovation... or rather, giving his father's memory a standing ovation. He knew the standing patrons were rooting on the great career of his fathers and not his, but he still felt tickled to be subject to such appreciation. He took to the microphone and snatched the award from the presenter's hand without a simple handshake, or even a look for that matter. He placed the award on the podium's surface and looked back into the crowd, but something bugged him enough to keep from speaking. He saw that the consensus of the attendants were single males. Steely Dan once suggested in a song that one was the loneliest number, and he couldn't help but agree in light of the depressing sight. Jeremy squinted vainly towards the back of the room, trying his hardest to spot someone of the female gender, but there wasn't a woman in sight. The entire wrestler population was either coupled up with an old friend or with the always-reliable alcoholic beverage. Jeremy knew they wore masks, just as he did. They were lonely and feeble; not to mention normal, which probably hurt these epic caricatures the most. These old men could never regain the vitality of their younger years, and the ones that tried always ended up looking silly. He didn't ponder this depressing thought very long because something bigger, and better, was on his mind. He felt blessed to know he could find comfort in this particular something, no matter how his career turned out. For the first time that evening Jeremy thought intensively about his wife, Shirley. That was pretty much all he could think about, actually. He couldn't place his finger on exactly why, but he missed her more than words could describe in that brief moment in time. He smiled widely as he twisted the wedding ring on his finger. Shirley and him planned out his speech ahead of time, but he completely forgot its contents as he stood before a packed house of wrestling legends and promoters. His eyes lowered to his chest pocket, where a small group of note cards were innocently nestled. He looked back up, deciding against giving the speech; they were all lies, anyway. The only thing on his mind was to get out of there, and see his beautiful wife once again. In spite of the minute-long awkward pause, the crowd succumbed to feelings of befuddlement as Jeremy lowered his head and uttered two words that would never do any justice: "Thank You." Jeremy non-chalantly strolled off stage, as a wave of confusion flowed across those in attendance. As he neared the exit backstage, he could hear an orchestra sound track swiftly kick up in the distance as a small stir arose in the crowd. Jeremy frowned and thought about what might be going on; it was then that he realized he was empty handed. He quickly turned and climbed back onto the stage, where he walked to the podium and grabbed the trophy that he left behind. The microphone caught his voice faintly as he grabbed the gold statuette. "Whoops!" The crowd gasped in disbelief as Jeremy shrugged it off and disappeared again; this time through curtains. As he headed towards the exit, he passed several gawking faces before barreling through the exit. This time, he didn't forget to do what he had planned on doing all along; He made sure to toss the award in the garbage on his way out.
