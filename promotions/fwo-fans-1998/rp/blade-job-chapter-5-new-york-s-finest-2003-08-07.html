Blade Job - Chapter 5 - New York's Finest

7 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
There was a time once when all homicide detectives were big, tough bastards; when you couldn't become a homicide cop unless you looked like the meanest SOB in New York City. The perp would be sitting in a room down at the station, cool as ice on a winter's day, knowing his people were movin' and shakin' to get him sprung out of here in a jiffy as long as he kept his mouth shut, when suddenly the light beyond the door would disappear. The perp would glance at the hall outside and all he'd see is black. Then slowly, it would dawn on him that was because the cop at the door filled all the available space. Homicide cop would say nice and soft "I wanna talk to you," and seconds later the perp would be spilling his guts to The Man, stringing himself up by his own wagging tongue. Truth was, in those days, there was no need for a Homicide cop to pull out a rubber hose... the look of The Man, the size of the motherfucker- combined, they were rubber hose enough. The two men in the cramped police-issue double-sided desks were throwbacks to the good old days. Detectives with the Homicide Division of the NYPD, they looked line a pair of linebackers with the Giants. "Fuck, I hope this ain't a round-the-clock, " Somers said across the desk to his partner, who was rifling through yellow-backed manilla files. "Has the makings," Doyle confirmed. "Remember last year? The hooker on 12th and Main? Almost didn't get go home for Christmas that year." "Yeah, that was a bitch of a case..." Doyle snorted and threw the file across the desk in frustration. "But not as weird as this one." They were working on the MacKay case, reviewing crime scene photos, autopsy reports and officer's case notes. Doyle had elected to handle the case personally, dropping the rest of his caseload after hearing the circumstances around the drowning and and was currently catching up on what he had missed. After several hours of reading and rereading reports, Somers looked up, removing his reading glasses and rubbing the bridge of his nose. "What was that last phone number that MacKay called that night? The techs dredge it up yet?" he asked. "What?" Doyle asked absently before catching on. He sat upright, taking his size 13 feet off the desk and scuffled through the growing piles of paper on his desk. Plucking up a computer printout, he squinted as he ran his finger down the list of numbers. "[detail withheld]. A Mister Ben Jacobson. Age, twenty-nine. He's a rassler for that f-dubbya-o. Makes sense, seeing our boy was a rassler too." Somers stretched, his back cracking like a tommygun. "Well, lets start there. Let's see what Mister Jacobson was up to that night." Doyle turned back to the papers that towered over his desk. He started to get up, then stopped himself and sat down on the leather chair in a huff. "Tell me this, Somers... what makes a man angry enough to cut another man's throat? And then parade him around like a fuckin' ragdoll?" The reports had come in earlier that day, with the blood-spatter analysis... both detectives knew that the killer had not just killed Kevin MacKay. He had mutilated him. Somers sighed, rubbing and scratching at his his two-day beard. "Let's ask Ben Jacobson."
