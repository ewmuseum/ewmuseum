Introduction to Pain

13 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The pain was intense. It dominated his thoughts, his movements, intertwining itself with his very being and making it so that every breath he took, every contraction or expansion of his muscles, sent liquid fire running the entire length of his side. To make matters worse, it was spreading. The pain was already in his back and working its way across to his other side. As unbelievable as he thought it to be, this pain was worse, far worse, than the excruciating agony that he had been in for nearly two weeks after Graphic Violence had attacked him on his final iNtense. As he had rushed to the airport after Slaughter, Lance Knight had known that he was in trouble, but he had no idea how bad it really was. He hurried through the airport, having just switched his ticket to an earlier flight, and in a matter of hours, he'd be back home in Pittsburgh, where he could hopefully rest and recover in the comfort of his own apartment. By the time he reached his gate, he was completely out of breath, his head was literally dripping beads of sweat and still more tiny rivulets of the saline fluid were running down his face and soaking his gray fWo Survivor 2 t-shirt. As he handed the gate attendant his ticket, Knight noticed that his hand was shaking uncontrollably, and while it crossed his mind that he should be worried, a stab of pain, sharper than the constant throb that he was becoming frighteningly used to, shot up into his shoulder, causing him to cringe in pain. After she had collected his boarding pass, the airport worker waved Knight onto the jetway, and he rushed to the other end and onto the plane. Stepping into the plane was an ordeal in and of itself. As he ducked his head, Knight was once more greeted by another hitch in his side, but this one was mercifully short. He looked at his ticket stub and headed towards his seat, but he was stopped by a hand on his arm. Knight turned, wearily dreading the confrontation he was sure to follow. He was relieved to see that it was a male flight attendant who had caught hold of his arm. "Mr. Knight? You've been upgraded to first class, sir, compliments of USAir. We received a call from the terminal and they said that you looked like you could use the extra amenities first class could provide, so enjoy your flight sir." Before Knight could even answer or thank the flight attendant, the man had whirled on his heel and returned to the area at the front of the cabin. Knight staggered into the first class section of the cabin, where a friendly female stewardess pointed him in the direction of his seat. Seeing Knight struggle to place his carry-on bag in the overhead compartment, the stewardess moved in close and placed her hand on Knight's side, eliciting a groan of pain from Knight that caused her to back cautiously away. With some difficulty, the young fWo star slid into his seat and did his best to relax. "If I can just make it though this flight," he thought, "then I'll be fine." ***** The next thing Knight remembered was being shaken painfully awake. A voice kept telling him over and over that they were there, and than an ambulance was waiting. Using all his might to move limbs that felt like they were made of marble rather than flesh, blood, and bone, Lance slowly made his way off of the plane, and as he exited the jetway in Pittsburgh International Airport, he found himself surrounded by several paramedics, who helped him onto a stretcher. Through the fog in his mind, Knight picked up several words: "shock", "hospital", and "removed".
