Intro To The Biz: Intros, Ideas, and Images

7 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Luke Starkey arrived 15 minutes before his appointment, just to be safe. The lobby of this office building was immaculate, and had he let himself Luke would have been awed and overwhelmed. As it was, he was already feeling a bit small and insignificant. If the representatives of Disney decided that they would rather not have Luke as a member of the fWo's roster there was absolutely nothing he could do about it. They literally held his future in his hands. Sitting in the amazingly comfortable oversized chair, Luke had time to reflect before his appointment. Never in his wildest imaginings could he have guessed that he would be here, in the offices of the Walt Disney Corporation, hoping to become a professional wrestler. A strange chain of events had led him to this moment, starting three months ago with Luke pulling a stranger out of the path of a speeding truck. That stranger had turned out to be Alex Newsome, who wrestled under the guise of Lance Knight. The two struck up a fast friendship, and Luke found himself plunged headfirst into the world of professional wrestling. He loved almost everything about it: the costumes, the action, the spectacle. For the most part it was grand entertainment, although some of the people he'd met did seem to have difficulty separating their in-ring persona from their real personality. He'd met a lot of great people, got to travel throughout the country, and even thought that he'd had played some small part in seeing Alex through his rough times en route to the United States Championship. The last three months had been among the best of his life. Simply put, Luke didn't want it to end. Sure, he could stay on as Lance Knight's personal assistant, but truth be told Lance didn't need him. Lance had a lot of other things going on in his life, and while they were still good friends Lance really didn't NEED a personal assistant. And truth be told, Luke was ready to move on, hoping to make the transition from lowly personal assistant to lowly in-ring personality. At this point, Luke would be extremely happy doing the job against Rat or Dog or even hapless, falling-apart Ken War. A polite, sultry voice broke into his thoughts. "Mr. Starkey, they're ready for you." Luke stepped into a plush meeting room, trying not to look like a gawking tourist. Michelle was there, sitting at the head of an immense, majestic table that probably cost more than most folks earn in a year. Strategically placed around the table were four people Luke had never seen before, all looking up expectantly when he entered. Michelle was quick to stand and smile, offering a greeting that was just the right mix of honesty and insincerity, and gestured toward the only open chair around the magnificent wooden table. She and Luke sat at the same time, and then, like she'd been waiting for an unseen starter to fire his pistol, she took off. "Luke! Thanks for coming; it's great to see you! I'm terribly sorry about the delay, and unfortunately that means we'll have to make this short and sweet. First, some quick intros. To my right is my assistant, Mitchell Cardigan. You'll probably be seeing much more of him once you begin actually taking part in the fWo programs. To his right is Mikhail Romanov, who will be in charge of your hair and make up. Over there is Moon, who's been assigned as your wardrobe specialist. And right here is Montrose Jones, who will be coaching you in dialog and movement. People, this is Luke Starkey
