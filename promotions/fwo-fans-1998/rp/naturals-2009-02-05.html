Naturals

5 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Deep within the smoky confines of Redbeards, a hole-in-the-wall dive populated night after night by the usual suspects, sat Eli Flair and Jeff Garvin. FWO reAction had just concluded, with Garvin returning to the Key Arena in a full-on sprint, hoping to make in back in time to use up some of the interview time he’d been promised. No such luck. “Fuckin’ Julie,” sighed an exasperated Garvin, taking a sip of beer, “You’d think she’d learn to cut the shit and let me focus on doin’ my job, but no, she drags me away for a scrape. A goddamn fuckin' scrape. What was she thinking wearing high heels, anyway, knowing the parking lot would be icy?” "Next time, man, she should just put on the heels when she's inside. Or skip 'em altogether. I don't think Ivy's worn heels for over a decade now. No worries, though," continued Eli, as he gestured to the bartender for another round, "there's always next week. And with the whole Tribute deal, a lot more people'll be watching." "You don't know Julie," responded Jeff, shaking his head, "She lacks common sense. Or she's so obsessed with defying me that she'll do the exact opposite of whatever I tell her. I should try reverse psychology, see if that works." Eli thought about it for a second. Ivy certainly had her moments of pain - in - the - ass - ness, but it was always tempered by a pragmatic desire to do the best for the show. "Well let me ask you this - what, specifically, does Julie contribute to the show?" Garvin thought for a moment as he stared blankly into his glass. "She, uh... um... sometimes she goes and gets me a water? Well, she used to... now I get my own water." "Well," replied Eli, holding back a chuckle, "and this is only my opinion, but if she's not contributing to the show, then what she needs at the dance is secondary to what you and your opponent need." Garvin coughed on his drink, and Eli held up his hand. "I'm not a marriage counselor, and I'm certainly not giving you that kinda advice. But the point of the matter is that we're in front of fourteen thousand live on a weekly basis and who knows how many more on television, and nothing can distract you from that. I fight for Ivy as an equal partner because she contributes more than half the roster, but if she was just eye candy I'd insist on taking care'a myself first and her second. Am I making any sense whatsoever?" Garvin nodded. "You are, but here's the thing: I don't know how to put my foot down when it comes to Julie. And even if I did, I don't know that I. She's the first and only woman I've ever been with; before her I was a hapless, wrestling-obsessed jock from Memphis, and now... sure, not much has changed, but at least now I've got money and people know me. She's pushed hard for me in the past when I would've just sat idly by and let myself get fucked over. I guess I just suck when it comes to the business side of the business, heh." "Hmmm," said Eli, "It's pretty tough when you're married to your valet, I take it. I get that she's pushed hard for you and I respect the fact that you're taking care of her. Maybe she just needs more to do? I could ask Ivy to talk to her about getting more involved in the show. Couldn't hurt?" Garvin thought for a moment. Julie was a difficult person for most people to interact with, but he also knew Ivy possessed thick skin and could probably deal with his wife's craziness, at least for a little while. It couldn't hurt to test the waters, he concluded in his head. "Y'know, that might be just what she needs, Eli—a friend. Julie doesn't have very many friends that are women; she tends to drive most of them away with her incessant bitchiness. But maybe Ivy could help her find the balance in her life that she needs to calm herself the hell down. And having Ivy work her like a dog would sure be enjoyable." Eli laughed. "No problem, sir. I'll talk to Ivy tomorrow about, *cough*, "working her like a dog. C'mon, next round's on me."
