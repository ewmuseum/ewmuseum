Blade Job - Chapter 6 - The Sloats

8 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The bell over the door tinkled lightly as Ben entered the restaurant. The walls were covered floor-to-ceiling with built-in bookshelves which overflowed with leather-covered volumes. The atmosphere was intimate and soothing, with small tables and comfortable chairs scattered throughout the long, narrow room. Even this early in the day, candles flickered in hurricane globes on the tables. The soft sound of Chopin wafted through the room. A small bar with gleaming brass barstools stood at the back of the restaurant. This wasn't the type of restuarant to attend alone, and Ben wasn't one to be the exception. He saw the pair that he was to meet at the far end of the narrow eatery, sitting casually in a dimly-lit booth as the candlelight shadows danced like ghosts on their waxy features. Horace Sloat was almost the epitome of what you would expect an Indy federation president to look like. He wore the sterotypical knockoff gold rolex, the gold bracelets and chains, with large gaudy rings on almost every finger. Curly hair bristeled from the open collar of a loud Hawiian shirt, only partially toned down by the sensible sportscoat he wore over the ensemble. He looked more like a pimp than the head of a wrestling federation, Ben mused... but then again, the two occupations were actually much alike. If Horace Sloat was dressed like a pimp, the girl beside him played the accompanying part, dressed in thigh-high leather boots that met the shortest skirt Ben had ever seen. Though it wasn't her skirt that kept Ben's attention, it was her skin. Quite simply, the girl looked dead. Her pale, sallow skin was seemed to wane under his stare, her long, straight hair was so blonde it appeared snow-white in the candle's glow. Her heavily-lidded eyes seemed to look everywhere but at Ben. She studied her fingernails, the swirling grains in the heavy wooden table, the patterns in the wallpaper above their booth. For a second , Jacobson had thought he recognized her, had seen her before, but soon he realized the connection he made. She looked familiar because she looked almost exactly like the character Jerry Hall played in the first Batman movie, a Harley Quinn to Jack Nicholson's Joker. She was a living ghost that seemed to play the "heroin-chic" part to perfection. "Ben," Horace Slot began in a gravelly voice that sounded like marbles thrown into a cement mixer. "Let me introduce my new wife, Candi. Candi, meet Ben Jacobson." "Nicetameetcha." Ben said as he offered his hand across the table to the pale woman. She didnt so much shake his hand as she caressed it, all the while looking at her fingernails on her other hand. Not a glance from the intriguing woman. "Forgive her," Sloat huffed, while shooting a scathing glance to his wife. "She's not one for long introductions. Let's get down to business, shall we? Have a seat." Ben slid into the booth opposite the Sloats, glancing around the fancy restaraunt with it's starch-white tablecloths and waiters with flimsy french accents. He wasn't surprised when he noticed that he was the only black man in the building. Ben looked over at the garish promoter. "What is it, Sloat? Whats so important that it couldn't be said over the phone?" "What?," asked Sloat, suddenly indignant. "Can't a guy buy a friend a dinner without having to explain himself like a criminal?" "Sorry," Ben said. "Ok, then, let's-" "Care to order, monsieurs?," A voice said behind Ben, cutting him off and startling him with it's snooty and abviously fake French accent. "Our specials today inclu-" "We'll all have the steak." Sloat bellowed across the table. "Make mine rare, and well-done for the lady and my guest. Now be off." The waiter gasped a tiny effeminate breath, raising his skeletal hand to cover his mouth and the thin moustache on his lip. He turned on his heel and skittered back to the kitchen, mumbling a litany of exotic-sounding curses under his breath. "Philly Killer is dead, " Sloat said, startling Ben back to the here and now. "Kevin? Yeah, I heard." Ben said. "I want to use his death as an angle, see? I know you and him tagged together a few times, so what I see is you coming into the IPW and rasslin' the guy that he was feudin' with. A big guy named Dan Bayer. They were main eventing this month until MacKay went and got hisself killed, the stupid bastard. So I-" "Hold on," Ben cut in. "I thought Kevin drowned in the shower.. what do you mean 'got himself killed'?" "I hear that the cops wanted the coroner to say that he drowned, as to not leak any information to the papers about the killer, but let me tell you, Keven MacKay was killed, and killed good. Looked like an animal just tore him apart. His throat was cut open ear to ear, I tells ya, and... hey, Ben. You ain't lookin' so swell." Ben's stomach did a long, stumbling lurch in his gut. He swallowed hard, raising a monogramed cloth napkin to his mouth. Out of the corner of his eye, he could see Candi Sloat now staring at him intently, her fingernails now, for the time being, forgotten. "I'm okay," Jacobson cleared his throat. "Just struck me by suprise, is all." "Okay... so here's how I see it," Horace Sloat waded in, never skipping a beat. "You come up to New York, we book you over Dan Bayer, and we can end that feud. Waddaya say? It's not like you are doing much other than pushing papers for the fWo right now anyway, right? We can get you up there, keep the ring rust off of ya, and pay you well for the appearance." Ben sighed. He should have known that Sloat would use Kevin's death to further his own agenda... he knew what the flyers and radio promos would day. MUFANGA KAGOS TO AVENGE PHILLY KILLER'S DEATH. Still, he needed the workout.. and Sloat was right about his role in the fWo these days. Ben's mind raced as he glanced over to Candi Sloat, weirdly attractive for all her pale, waxen pallor. She had gone back to studying the other patrons of the restaurant. Well, thought Ben. He wasn't exactly headlining at the fWo was he? Why not headline the IPW? "One show? One match?" Ben asked sharply. "Right." Horace gleamed. "And I go over?" Ben asked as an afterthought. "Of course." "Alright, it's a deal." Ben said as a steak dinner was set in front of him by the same highly insulted waiter. Suddenly Ben wasn't hungry at all. What do you know? Ben thought... Apparently selling out kills your appetite. He picked at his salad much like Candi as Horace Sloat became a tornado of food-swilling ferocity, with gold-ringed fingers ripping meat from the bone. Soon, he paused long enough to see that he was the only one eating. "What... you don't like your steak?" Horace asked, insulted. "Nah... I'm just not hungry, I guess." Suddenly Candi spoke up, as Horace shrugged and again plowed into his food. Ben was startled as these were the first words he had heard her say all night. He was looking down her nose at him, her heavily-lidded eyes seeming to dance in deathly pale skin. "Your meat looks fine to me... " Ben looked up to see that she was now looking directly into his eyes, a piercing stare that sent a jolt into his spine like a bolt of lightning.
