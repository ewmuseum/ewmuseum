A Road Paved In Broken Glass: Part Two

3 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“Although it was long ago, I remember this day as if it was yesterday. I was only seven years old at the time…a boy forced to become a man. It was one of the most painful events I'd ever endured, and it changed my life forever." November 18th, 1988. "Give it back!" Milo yelled, as he chased schoolmate Bobby Newman across the schoolyard. It was his favorite toy; the baseball his father had brought him from one of his visits to America. The two continued running, and made their way through the back door, and into the school. "No running in the hall" demanded the schoolmaster in a very agitated tone. The two boys however, continued running, paying little attention to the authority figure they had nearly plowed over. The pursuit continued, and the boys were now running through the schools art-wing. Navigation while running proved to be very difficult, due to the crowd of people and displays that were scattered throughout the wing. Nonetheless, the chase progressed. "You'll never catch me!" Bobby yelled, to the trailing Milo Samus, in an attempt to intimidate him. A crowd of art observers was forced apart by the boys as they ran, paying little attention to obstacles that stood in their way. Bobby grew fatigued, but due to his stubborn nature, he continued to run. "Bang" Milo froze, in a state of disbelief at what lay before him. Bobby Newman slowly rose to his feet, and brushed porcelain shards from his clothing. He had sent a statue of the lord smashing to the ground, an act that could get you in a whole lot of trouble in a Catholic school. "Now look what you've done!" Milo said, as a crowd of people gathered to see what had happened. "Now where’s my base…" Milo said before he was cut off by the voice of the schoolmaster. "BOYS! GET INTO MY OFFICE NOW!" Milo had never even been to detention before, let alone the schoolmaster’s office. Regardless, he and Bobby did as they were told, and went to the office. The walk was a rather silent one, and the two boys knew they were in a LOT of trouble. The wait wasn't long, before the schoolmaster called Bobby into his office. Milo sat patiently in the waiting area, looking at his shoelaces, which were untied, and dangling to the floor. About a half hour had passed, and Bobby Newman finally made his way out of the schoolmasters office. Bobby’s eyes were beat red, and it was easy to tell he had been crying for some time. "Milo!" said the schoolmaster in a stern tone. Milo rose to his feet, and slowly made his way into the office, not raising his head to make eye contact with the angered schoolmaster. "Have a seat Milo" ordered the schoolmaster, pointing towards a wooden chair which sat across from his desk. Just then, the phone rang. The schoolmaster glared, momentarily, at Milo before answering. "Hello?" "Lola Samus? Your son is in my office right now actually…" "Oh my god…” Milo didn't like the sound of this, and He got a sick, queasy feeling in the pit of his stomach. He continued to listen to the conversation. "God rest his soul, I will drive Milo to your place immediately." the schoolmaster said as he hung up the phone. It was a short drive, but the schoolmaster never spoke a word the entire time. He kept his eyes firmly focused on the road, but would periodically glance over at Milo. "This doesn’t seem right…" thought Milo, as he watched out the passenger-side window. The car came to a stop, and Milo’s mother Lola was waiting on the front lawn. There was a police car parked in the driveway, and Lola looked to be very upset. Milo thought for sure he would be going to jail for breaking the statue, and he began to sweat heavily. He stepped out of the car, and his knees felt a lot like a bowl of jelly. Lola walked towards Milo and grabbed him; she wrapped her arms around him and began to cry. It was at this point Milo knew there was more to this then just the statue. "What is it mom?" questioned Milo, trying to prevent himself from crying. "Its your father" replied Lola, breaking down further more, she continued, "His plane went down over the Atlantic". Milo was completely shocked, and it felt as if his heart had sunk to the bottom of his chest. He broke down into tears; this could not be happening… "Is he okay?" asked Milo, as he tightened his grip around his mother. "No Milo, your father is in heaven now" replied Lola, crying harder now then before. Milo knew his life would never be the same… he’d just lost the best friend he'd ever had.
