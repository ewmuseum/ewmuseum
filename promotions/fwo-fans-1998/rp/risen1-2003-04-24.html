Holy Family Medical Hospital. A large brick complex on the outskirt suburbs of Chicago, Illinois. The fWo saw it as a place Trent Harrison could heal in peace. Every day was the same: soak in the morphine stench as the television played reruns of television taken straight out of the eighties. There was nothing of interest there; nothing to live for. The luckiest were those on life support. Sweet life. As he cuddled the cotton blue bed sheets to his throat, a knock on the door echoed against the tile walls. A nurse with a simple gray name tag reading "Dallas" walked into the room. Her curly red locks hung over her shoulders as the gaudy red lipstick and thick layers of blush masked any natural beauty. The wheelchair held in front of her sickly thin body was a common vision. Trent rolled over to his opposite side and stared at the empty scene outside the window. Trees, stars, and the faint headlights of cars dancing across a backdrop of black. A sky blue gown covered his body. She came to the edge of his bed and turned the chair adjacent. "It's time, Trent. Let's go," she stated with a rasp voice laden with apathy. He rolled over onto his side and threw the blankets off. A simple as a brace held his knee in place. Trent slumped into the faux leather and balanced his cast on top of the foot rest. With the same emotionless look, he tilted his head back and looked up toward the woman. Skin pale as day, and eyes black as night. She looked starved and empty, deprived of any optimistic view. Through the off-white hallways and over the tile floors, Trent watched a mother hugging her bleeding, dying child to her chest. A group of individuals in the whitest lab coats drenched their hands in the reddest blood. Death was no longer an issue. The nurse pushed a white swinging door open and pulled the chair in. Trent slowly stood and looked at the man before him. He was slightly toned underneath the blue work shirt hanging off his body--one could tell by his high cheekbones and angular features. The man stretched out his hand and stared into Trent's eyes, as he did every day. "Hello, Trent. Ready to work today?" "Yes, Shane," he sighed. Trent had given up his silence in practice for the real world. To keep his job, he would need to be more than just the silent warrior. Shane readied the leg press as he took off helped Trent remove his brace. A few plates lifted up with ease. Shane nodded in the background. "Good, good. We'll have to go a little higher every day, at least until you're back to where you used to be. Ten more." As Trent was finishing off the warm up chapter of his day, his eyes wandered to the other trainers. At one station, a woman sat in her wheelchair, completely silent. Her hair was a disheveled black mat that covered most of her right eye and hung in front of the other. A white gown hung past her knees slightly. Trent stood up from his station and steadied his uneasy leg. Shane tugged on his gown and motioned toward a set of parallel bars, but Trent swatted his hand away. Other physical trainers watched as the man stepped toward the station. Muttered voices of the injured complaints of victims filled the room. As he neared, Trent eyeballed the female standing in front of the woman. He stood beside her and looked over. "Wont talk, or can't?" he inquired in a monotone, fractured voice. "Can't, and no one knows why," the woman replied. She stepped back from the scene and held her head in her hands. Trent smiled for the first time in years. With a slow movement, he kneeled at the base of the wheelchair. Gray eyes peered into the outside world with a wanting and pessimism congruent to his. Again, a cracked grin appeared... ...and she turned to him.
