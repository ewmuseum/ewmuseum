Song from the Heavens, Humming from Hell - 5 - Part Two

18 November 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
FLASHBACK to August 20th, 2002 "Hello Detective, how are you today?" Craig Kelzner was a man of few words but with every sentence his tone seemed to bounce up and down. Detective Hughes smiled back at him, "Just fine," Hughes eyes focused hard on the name tag Craig wore, "Private Kelzner." Craig paused for a few moments, not only did Hughes already know his name, he got his rank incorrect, Craig spoke with a nervous response, "Uhm it's Lieutenant Kelzner." Hughes grinned, "Of course. Look let's get to business, I will be needing to see Norbert Jaeger." "Jaeger?" Craig was hesitant once again however snapping back into reality he quickly followed up with, "Of course Detective, right this way." The two men walked down the long hallway and Hughes followed just slightly behind Kelzner who was still speaking, "Jaeger eh... I know that one, the German scientist, specializing in chemical warfare. Used to be a Nazi." "It doesn't concern you to know who he is Lieutenant, just to lead me to him." Hughes said abruptly. Kelzner couldn't believe the Detectives response, however Hughes was well respected and it was better that Kelzner didn't push his limits. I mean, no one would ever dare question Hughes on why he was here. Hughes was a legend, a smart man who had basically been given higher privileges than most of the people on the force. Kelzner lead Hughes to a small room in which Jaeger was already seated waiting to speak to him. He was an old man and had been in prison for ages, longer than Hughes had ever been alive, or Douglas for that matter. As Hughes dead body had been buried in the back of his house last night, faceless. Hughes/Douglas sat down in the seat and looked hard at the old man, who stared down at the table. He sensed Jaeger was tired, "Well boy," said Jaeger in a tired tone with his German accent ringing down hard on his English lingo, "You wanted to speak to me?" Hughes turned and looked to Kelzner, "Thanks, you can go now," Kelzner abided by what he said and left quickly, as the door shut Hughes/Douglas turned back to Jaeger who was now looking him hard in the eye, "You've never met me, however, what if I was to say I needed your help? And with it in exchange I can promise you freedom?" Jaeger laughed, "Freedom? Boy, I'll be dead in a few years anyhow, what does freedom mean to me?" "Not just freedom," Hughes continued, "But revenge." Jaeger sat back in his seat, "Against who?" Hughes smiled and then his face shot to a serious expression, "Them. All of them. What if I told you I wanted to get them back to, all those dirty pathetic whores out there. What if I could promise you the victory you were deprived of so many years ago? Wouldn't you love to live to see the downfall of America?" Jaeger shook his head, "You can't be serious, they are your own people." Hughes glared harder, "They are not my people. I want to see every single one of them burn till they turn to ash." There was a hard moment of silence exchanged, Jaeger broke it, "Your actually serious." Hughes nodded, "So help me, I need to know how to make a weapon of mass destruction." Jaeger grew serious, "You fucking serious." Hughes leaned in and grabbed Jaeger's wrist, "LISTEN to me, help me, help me kill them all." Jaeger relaxed and the tension eased, "You want to know, I'll tell you if stealing uranium seems too tacky you can buy it. Unmerited uranium is available at any chemical supply house for $23 a pound. Commercial grade (3 to 20 percent enriched) is available for $40 a pound from Gulf Atomic. You'll have to enrich it further yourself. Quite frankly this can be something of a pain in the ass. You'll need to start with a little more than 50 pounds of commercial-grade uranium. (It's only 20 percent U-235 at best, and you need 10 pounds of U-235 so... ) But with a little kitchen-table chemistry you'll be able to convert the solid uranium oxide you've purchased into a liquid form. Once you've done that, you'll be able to separate the U-235 that you'll need from the U-238. First pour a few gallons of concentrated hydrofluoric acid into your uranium oxide, converting it to uranium tetrafluoride. Now you have to convert your uranium tetrafluoride to uranium hexafluoride, the gaseous form of uranium, which is convenient for separating out the isotope U-235 from U-238. To get the hexafluoride form, bubble fluorine gas into your container of uranium tetrafluoride. Fluorine is available in pressurized tanks from chemical-supply firms. Be careful how you use it though because fluorine is several times more deadly than chlorine, the classic World War I poison gas. Chemists recommend that you carry out this step under a stove hood (the kind used to remove unpleasant cooking odors). If you've done your chemistry right you should now have a generous supply of uranium hexafluoride ready for enriching. In the old horse-and-buggy days of A-bomb manufacture the enrichment was carried out by passing the uranium hexafluoride through hundreds of miles of pipes, tubes, and membranes, until the U-235 was eventually separated from the U-238. This gaseous-diffusion process, as it was called is difficult, time-consuming, and expensive. Gaseous-diffusion plants cover hundreds of acres and cost in the neighborhood of $2-billion each. So forget it. There are easier, and cheaper, ways to enrich your uranium. First transform the gas into a liquid by subjecting it to pressure. You can use a bicycle pump for this. Then make a simple home centrifuge. Fill a standard-size bucket one-quarter full of liquid uranium hexafluoride. Attach a six-foot rope to the bucket handle. Now swing the rope (and attached bucket) around your head as fast as possible. Keep this up for about 45 minutes. Slow down gradually, and very gently put the bucket on the floor. The U-235, which is lighter, will have risen to the top, where it can be skimmed off like cream. Repeat this step until you have the required 10 pounds of uranium. Now it's time to convert your enriched uranium back to metal form. This is easily enough accomplished by spooning several ladlefuls of calcium (available in tablet form from your drugstore) into each bucket of uranium. The calcium will react with the uranium hexafluoride to produce calcium fluoride, a colorless salt which can be easily be separated from your pure enriched uranium metal. Now that you've acquired the enriched uranium, all that's left is to assemble your A-bomb. Go find a couple of stainless steel salad bowls. You also want to separate your 10 pounds of U-235 into two hunks. The idea is to push each half your uranium into the inside of a bowl. Take one hunk of your uranium and beat it into the inside of the first bowl. Uranium is malleable, like gold, so you should have no trouble hammering it into the bowl to get a good fit. Take another five-pound hunk of uranium and fit it into a second stainless steel bowl. These two bowls of U-235 are the "subcritical masses" which, when brought together forcefully, will provide the critical mass that makes your A-bomb go. Keep them a respectful distance apart while working because you don't want them to "go critical" on you... At least not yet. Now hollow out the body of an old vacuum cleaner and place your two hemispherical bowls inside, open ends facing each other, no less than seven inches apart, using masking tape to set them up in position. The reason for the steel bowls and the vacuum cleaner, in case you're wondering, is that these help reflect the neutrons back into the uranium for a more efficient explosion. As far as the A-bomb goes, you're almost done. The final problem is to figure out how to get the two U-235 hemispheres to smash into each other with sufficient force to set off a truly effective fission reaction. Almost any type of explosive can be used to drive them together. Gunpowder, for example, is easily made at home from potassium nitrate, sulfur, and carbon. Or, you can get some blasting caps or TNT. Buy them or steal them from a construction site. Best of all is C4 plastic explosive. You can mold it around your bowls, and it's fairly safe to work with. But, it might be wise to shape it around an extra salad bowl in another room, and THEN fit it to your uranium-packed bowls. This is particularly true in winter, when a stray static electrical charge might induce ignition in the C4. A responsible bomb maker considers it impolite to accidentally destroy more of the neighborhood than absolutely necessary. Once the explosives are in place all you need to do is hook up a simple detonation device with a few batteries, a switch, and some wire. Remember though that it is essential that the two charges -- one on each side of the casing -- go off simultaneously. Now put the whole thing in the casing of an old Hoover vacuum cleaner and you're finished with this part of the process. The rest is easy. " A few moments of pure awe and silence once again rang true to the room, Douglas/Hughes broke it this time, "Wow, mind if we go over it one more time?"
