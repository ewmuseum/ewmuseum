State of the Industry

28 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
State of the Industry In a word, crap. Ok, we need more than a word. Credit where credit's due, the industry is still there. Feds are putting on shows week in and week out. Tickets are still selling, t-shirts are still being bought, pay-per-views still being watched. But it's not about money, not about merchandise. It's about the attitude. People just don't care anymore. Wrestlers go out in front of the audience every week and give it everything they have; blood, sweat, tears, all to put out a product people enjoy, a product people want to see more of. But after cameras turn off, the lights dim, the athletes file to the backstage... well, the fans don't care anymore. No one at the back exits looking for autographs. No groups of kids shouting excitedly about one good spot or one swerve. No one cramming internet chatrooms with the good and the bad of each show, picking them apart, giving us feedback into what we've done great, and what needs improvement. It never used to be like this. I can remember tears being shed the night that Ivan Stanislav and Tempest kissed for the first time. I can remember the shocked expression on every face that witnessed the EKG of KVG stopping. The pain people felt when they saw Nerva brand another woman. The shiver that went through ever single person with the appearance of one Mortimer Ulysses Dredrick. People used to love wrestling. People used to idolize wrestlers. Ask any kid of the time what they wouldn't give to be a Machina or Reno Davis (or Aimz to cover both genders). How much they'd dream about just one night in PIW or Action! or Club. It was all-encompasing; a dream and a reality at the same time. Not now. What do we have now? Corporations who buy out the companies, put them behind glass walls and parade them around like freaks. Violence breaking out at shows. Hackers attacking official websites. People badmouthing everyone and everything because they just don't care anymore. No one cares. So why should the wrestlers care? What do we get out of all of this? Glory? The chance to tell a story? A paycheck? All it is now is a job. Something that pays the bills. There's no fun it it, there's no rush to it. We go out, do our thing for 10 minutes, and come back. bad match? Well, the fans don't care anymore anyways, so why risk injury. Why work out elaborate stories and captivating spots, when people just watch, yawn, and move on. You want good wrestling again? Start being good fans.
