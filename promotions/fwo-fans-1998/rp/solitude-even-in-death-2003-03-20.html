Solitude - Even in Death

20 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Amy's phone had been shut off - rather shattered thanks to a collision with her brick wall - for two days. She wouldn't even glance at her computer, nor was she anywhere near turning on the TV. It was Wednesday evening...a war was breaking out, but that war was something so far from her mind, it was all but nonexistant. She was consumed instead by a loss that, to her, was more than any third world country or US air base... Brandon Roche was dead. There was so little hope that he would've survived having what was basically a building fall on him that Amy had given up entirely on even the small glimmers of positivity and disbelief she'd once had. She'd never said 'I love you' and now never would...he was taken in his life's almost happiest, definitely most complicated time, it seemed. God had taken two lives in their happiest times, everything had been so clear... It was clearer now, though. Now all Amy had to do was sit...sit and waste air. Sit and wait for freedom by death, death by starvation...she was too stricken to eat, too hungry to move, not strong enough to cry. There was nothing she could do but sit...nothing to life. For the first time, the redhead wasn't even thinking of her 'adopted' son... Amy laid heavy on her small couch, holding a bottle of white rum that was playing a lovely role in killing off her system cool against her chest, which was covered well by layers upon layers of shirts and blankets. She was shivering, though...violently. Obviously not from cold, though...it was terror of facing the rest of her life. She'd found the one thing that made her really want to wake up every morning, and months later it was snatched away, leaving absolutely nothing but this solitude unless she guided herself to a grave. That wasn't even coming well, though: Aimz had never had the guts to try anything "stupid" like direct suicide - she was hoping to end at the hand of alcohol poisoning, according to the array of bottles set next to the couch, half of which were already emptied. She glanced to a digital clock set in the corner; Warfare would be wrapping up soon and the thought made her sick - everybody was continuing life as normal, regardless of how many friends they'd lost or what they'd been through. Why couldn't she be quite so strong? NO...why were they quite so stupid?! There was little time for anger, though...a series of knocks threatened to break her door down. Amy didn't budge... The door did. The next thing she knew, Renee was seated on the couch over her legs, patting her on the side. "How you feelin'?" "Guess." Amy groaned, trying to roll over but not quite finding the power. Renee'd come to see her the day before, apparently tipped off by a rather surprise call by Ivy, who'd remembered meeting her months before and tracked down her phone number. The blonde knew how to handle Amy, and hadn't forced anything down on her - she wanted to give her time to heal. "Well I saw something cool on TV...pretty interesting stuff, wanna turn it on?" "No." "C'mon!" "No!" "You're gonna turn it on or I'm gonna do it for you, then shove the remote so far up your ass you wo---" "Why?" She interrupted, it did seem like Renee was determined...but that was little surprise. Amy figured this was when her longtime best friend would get forceful...but Renee had something very, very different to say... "Ah, nevermind..." She began, pretending to give up. "It's not that important." The younger girl stood up, threw her arms in the air and stomped toward the door in quite a display...just before chuckling, turning around and grinning at Amy. "...just your boyfriend kicking the shit out of Erik Kelly."
