Playing Parts - Five of Five

3 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It was now 4 A.M. standard eastern time and Steve Arden was back at his Chicago apartment, only a few hours after Sunday Slaughter. His official debut wasn't the type he had expected, fans were rowdier than soccer crowds between two rival nations and security was anything but tight. He had expected to get some cheers or any positive feedback but the crowd did not give, they jeered at him the entire match and even threw popcorn bags and soft drink containers at him.. Hey, at least he got a reaction right? He had stepped in his apartment to find no one as usual thirty minutes ago, reeking of dissapointment but what could you do with such a gimmick? He just had to play it off the best he could. Fast forward to the present and Steve's black duffel bag was tossed onto a chair in the kitchen and the tunes of Alicia Keys' "Fallin" was set to a comfortable listening volume as he lay on his mattress, arm bend under his head used as a pillow. He drifted off in the music but paid close attention to the lyrics. They spoke of love, something he possessed very much so when it came to his profession but sometimes it felt like it just wasn't worth it. Steve always had the idea of becoming a professional wrestler, since his days as a child in the Chicago countryside. He thought back about being a pacific boy but the art of wrestling moved him, he knew it was all an act for the entertainment of the fans and that's why he enjoyed it. His life long dream was becoming a reality day by day now but the art was no more than acting and ratings, for without them, promotions would fail and close as quickly as they opened but he felt it was an insult to the sport. His father taught him values, his mother taught him traditions and Steve Arden took them to heart. Wrestling used to be a tradition and values were subtly added; family, respect, honor, he had learned to work by them to get by and those who deserved it would be promoted but in our day and age, politics ran the show, he thought. Steve tiled his head to the left, the clock read 4:07 A.M. What would he need to get through the night? His horrible arrival to the fWo felt like a dagger to the heart, ripping through organs and penetrating a part of him that was considered a vital asset and life could not continue without medical help or a miracle. But suddenly the phone rang, piercing through his right ear and giving him a vain hint of hope. He picked up the receiver, silent, waiting for someone on the other line to speak up. "Steve, is that you?" A call from God perhaps? Nah, it was just his trainer, Randy Ellis. Yea, it's me Randy. What are you doing calling me at this time? It's 4 in the morning man.. "I know, I know but I just had to call you right away. I'm still in Puerto Rico." What's wrong? "Nothing, in fact, I've got some great news for you." Steve smirked, Great, I could use any kind of news right now. His trainer chuckled on the other side of the line, "After Slaughter, I met up with Silver Fox and we talked some more about you. He saw that maybe this 'Action' Steve gimmick wasn't the best thing to develop your presence in the fWo. So we discussed ideas and came up with a new gimmick for you, Steve." Steve raised his eyebrows and grinned softly, happy with his trainer and lifelong friend's work. Excited, he asked about his new persona. That's great! So, what's this new gimmick going to be about.. Hopefully it's not something as mid-90ish as the 'Action' character. He chuckled to himself and expected a similar one on Randy Ellis' behalf, but it never came. Nervously, he checked with his trainer. Randy, this isn't something as bad as the last gimmick, is it? "We figured the movie star gig was pushing it too much.. so we brought it back a little..You know, to match with your look and stuff.." Steve started to get impatient with the indirect answers Randy had given him thus far. Tell me what the gimmick is Randy. He said, stop beating around the bush and just spit out out already. Randy sighed. "You are now, Stevie Arden.. The Californian Surfer Dude." Arden was shocked. Bad gimmicks turned to worse and he was definitely not happy. Steve hung up the phone without a giving his trainer formal goodbye and threw it on the couch in the far corner of the room. He decided it was best to doze off instead of continue fuming about the matter, and so he did.
