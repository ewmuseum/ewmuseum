previous parts: see-all pieces previous to this The blasting sun beat down on a vast abyss of corn and dirt. Trent pulled the collar of his button-up plaid shirt to his face and wiped off a measure of sweat from his forehead. He looked toward the sun for a few moments, trying to somehow break away from this lifestyle. Blue denim overalls were stained with the remnants of the day's work. The autumn harvest was approaching, reminding the young country boy how primitive his lifestyle seemed in lieu of life experience. A cool breeze shifted each plant in one direction. Simultaneously, Trent placed his rake in the back of a brand new Ford pick-up truck. The dark but dirtied gloves soon followed. He walked across the front of the car and jumped inside. Clouds of dirt picked up alongside the truck. The vehicle became nearly invincible against the pink and orange sunset sky. Nothing but vast fields of vegetation and grassy patches. The scenery was plain in these parts. In the distance appeared a white speck. Soon, that speck turned to a picture perfect house with white siding and a black-tiled roof with yellow shutters and pink drapery. This was the only color seen for twenty acres in any given direction. It was a welcome vision, although a familiar one. Trent pulled the car into the same dirt driveway and stepped out. The lights were on and so were the sounds of family interaction. Trent contemplated a quiet night in his room. The house smelt of store-bought cookies. Over the years, boiled tomatoes began to smell like that. The stairs creaked under Trent's weight as he tried to escape to his bedroom. Mother's inhuman hearing picked up on the sound immediately. She brought herself from the kitchen with a curious hobble. "Trent, how are you enjoying the new house? I thought you'd like it. You know, as a way to get away from everything," Mother inquired. Even the unrelated mention of past events caused Trent to disappear with uneasiness. Although the interior was the same, the memories were contained in the soil. Cracks of the earth seethed with her perfume. Every day he farmed the new fields, he ached. Melodrama was his entire life. Mother paced around the house with an evolving sense of failure layered over months of building depression. The carpet was worn as her feet dragged over a familiar area. Apathetic toward his mother's apparent pain, Trent remained in the same plush recliner. Mother stopped for a moment and looked down at her creation. "One of those wrestling places called today. They said something about a rebuilding phase and coming by to see if you were interested." The door emitted a knocking noise. Although who was present behind there was obvious, Trent answered.
