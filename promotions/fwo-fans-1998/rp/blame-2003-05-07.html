Blame

7 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Griff Truxell still had a job. As surprising as it was, the last, great fWo event: Cyberslam; wasn't at all the last, great fWo event it was supposed to be. Scott "Cool" Collins had returned and most every title had switched hands, but if one knows Griff then one knows he couldn't care less. No, the one thing that should have happened didn't. The fWo didn't fold, the company was still going, wheels grinding. Don't think for a minute that it was out of the fire yet, though. Sponsors were pulling out left and right, thanks to the antics of several roster members. Griff could be included in there, a dozen chairshots to a defenseless man's head didn't seem to please the network executives too much. Rumors backstage were that the fWo would be gone from NBC television by the end of the month. Others speculated that being kicked off of FOX wouldn't be too far behind. Yet, these things weren't important to Griff Truxell. The things that mattered, the things that really meant something to him were what went on in that ring. Griff sat on the edge of the bed in a Detroit Mariott hotel. His elbows rested on his knees as he stared at the blank television screen. He rubbed his goatee rhythmically, far off in thought. Griff had been in the fWo for over a month now. A month of being employed by one of the greatest wrestling promotions in history...and nothing to show for it. He had tried to blame Fox and Smooth for this, but they had enough trouble on their hands by themselves. Besides, his attitude had got his program with Covington put on hold indefinatley and he'd lost every match he'd been in except for a tainted win over Deacon. Padrona, Dynamo, Flair... Flair was the exception though, as much as Griff hated to admit it. The other hacks, Griff couldn't have worked with them even if he wanted too. There was no point, they were that far below him talent wise. Flair though, Griff had Flair right where he wanted him, and then let him slip. "...wasn't for Eron, that conniving little bastard," Griff thought to himself. Funny thing was, Eli Flair was the first person to actually try to talk with Griff since he had joined the promotion. Griff didn't want to listen, but he did. Griff wanted to dismiss Flair as just another hack like the others, but he couldn't. Griff could see that Flair was the same as him, or as close to the same as Greatness as anybody could get. Flair was a veteran, he'd been in this sport just as long or even longer than Griff, and he seemed to be the only person who had an inkling to any knowledge of what they were talking about. Griff didn't want to trust Eli Flair, but he did, and he didn't want to care about what Eli had said, but he did. "The nights and travel go a lot smoother when you're having fun. When's the last time ya did that?" The Truth? Griff couldn't remember.
