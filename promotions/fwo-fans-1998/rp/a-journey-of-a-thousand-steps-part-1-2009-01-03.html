A journey of a thousand steps, Part 1

3 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Your typical psychiatrist's office. Soothingly lit, well furnished. A reasonably sized window looks across the street at a office building, apparently a few stories up. Art on the walls that conveys an air of professionalism, and yet is not particularly eye-catching. After all, the doctor doesn't want a patient to be distracted during a session. Of course, that supposes that the goal is to make the patient better and not just to pay off another boat thanks to never-ending sessions, and thus, never-ending checks. The latter certainly doesn't apply to the well dressed man sitting behind his mahogany desk, pouring over notes spread across the dark brown surface. His bright white oxford shirt, subdued blue tie and neatly pressed slacks give us the impression of a serious man, and Dr. Jacob Talbert takes his job and his Oath seriously. His proudest moments are when he can bid his patients a fond farewell, but unfortunately, in the realm of psychotherapy few are ever truly cured and sent on their way. There are few Dr. Talbert wishes he could wave goodbye to more than his longtime patient Mike Bear, not so much for his own sake, but for Mike's. The year is 2004, and it's been 5 years since Mike had first been brought to him in a straitjacket, and it's been 2 years since Mike's stint in Asylum, nearly 3 since he wrestled in the FWO. He has survived thanks to revenues brought in by a chain of wrestling schools he owns, but those are managed by his wife Brittany. Truth be told, Mike has done little in the past 4 years except come to sessions 3 or 4 times a week and try to live day to day. Jacob had even moved his office to Philadelphia where Mike lived so he could see him as often as Mike needed, and so that Mike would have the support structure of his friends and family around him at the same time. There were people who needed help in every city, after all, and Jacob had was known as a reputable psychiatrist in this town as well. When you're consistently mentioned by the World Champion of the FWO on network TV, you had better make sure your record in impeccable, as word travels fast, though he had never sought to be famous. But as good as Dr. Talbert was, Mike never seemed to be on the road to recovery. Why didn't he get better? How could he still not be able to control his anger, his delusions? After all this time, Jacob decided he was too close to the situation. He couldn't stop being Mike's psychiatrist, though that could be interpreted as part of the problem. After all these years together, the two had become friends. So Jacob did the only thing he could do. He called in a favor. A voice emerged from the phone on Jacobs desk. “Dr. Talbert? Dr. Regan is here.” “Thanks Rachel. Send him in.” Jacob rises from his desk as a man in a charcoal suit enters through the door opposite. He appears to be in a good mood. The two men meet halfway and exchange a hearty handshake. “Jake! How've you been? Still on the prowl, eh?” “Hey Kelvin. I do alright. Great to see you. It's been, what?” “Five years right? Not since the reunion. Good times.” “Seriously though, there'll be plenty of time to catch up later. I assume you got the materials I sent.” “Of course, I should know better when you're in Doctor mode. Let's get down to business.” Jacob and Kelvin move towards the desk. Jacob offers his friend the seat in front of the desk and then returns to his seat behind it. “So you've been having problems with this patient, then?” “Oh don't be glib, Kelvin. You read the file. The word problems doesn't begin to encompass what I've gone through with Mike. It was 1999 he was brought to me and it seemed so simple. The same company calls me back after dealing with that Mike Heftel fellow I told you about years back, tells me they have this guy in development, they really like him and have invested money in his training, but he's got severe anger issues and delusions of persecution.” “And you rightly assumed delusional disorder, persecutory type.” “That would have been your diagnosis too?” “Just as you said. I read your notes. I saw the tape. At first glance that's how it seems.” “Maybe it was... Wait, at first? What did you see?” “I have a few ideas, otherwise I wouldn't have come out here. However, why don't we go over everything first? Let me get your personal take on this. Only so much can be communicated through second hand. I don't want to go on about a hypothesis and have it be easy refuted by something you could have told me.” “Same old Kelvin, I see. You've got to be more bold sometimes.” “Hey, you have your way, I have mine. Now, you treat him for delusional disorder.” “Right, he seemed to respond very well to treatment. All these years later, I regret not looking deeper. Did I compromise my principles under pressure from the FWO? Did I send him out not fully convinced I had adequately done my job? Was I so convinced in my own abilities after Heftel returned to work so quickly” “Jacob...” “that I let my own ego cloud my judgment?” “JACOB! You are being too hard on yourself. You aren't God. You can't predict the future, and feeling sorry for yourself isn't going to make your patient any better. Most cases will get worse from a perceived lack of confidence in the person who's supposed to be the mentally healthy one. Pull yourself together man!” Jacob takes a few deep breaths. “You're right, Kelvin. I've just been at this so long it can't do much but erode your confidence.” “That's why we went to school for this. To be strong for the weak.” “Ha! If you had ever seen this guy in person tear through your office like a tornado, you'd know how ridiculous the word 'weak' sounds.” “Jacob, do you want to take a break? You look like hell.” “I'm fine. I've had enough breaks on this. So Mike goes back to work, but he keeps having episodes of extreme rage. Loses complete control and just rampages through everything in sight. Then around, where was my note? Here, October 2000 I get a call from one of his friends he had some sort of blackout.” “But isn't that what you described before? Losing control?” “No, that's how he described it. Tom, I mean, the friend who called. Said he was rubbing his head and he seemed to suffer a paralysis and lost time. I had never seen or heard of that happening to him, and yet there's nothing in particular in his records to explain it.” But there is an explanation...
