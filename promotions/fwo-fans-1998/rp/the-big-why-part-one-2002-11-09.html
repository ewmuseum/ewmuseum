The big why (part one)

9 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
They say that prison makes a person stronger. I don't get that at all. Prison gives a man cracks he didn't have before, and some people pass this off as strength. The ones who are afraid of the men they are becoming, mostly. These are the men who stand still, say little, and put on the persona of a man who lets everything wash over them like the tide on an a rocky beach. This isn't strength at all, but you would be hard pressed to tell any of them that little fact. Fear is a powerful motivator. When I was looking at ten years I would have done nearly anything to get out of it. Like a dear in the headlights, and until that moment I never understood that expression for anything. When a nightmare looks you right in the face only the quickest of minds runs, and even those aren't so quick in the end. In the end you get caught, either by the law or your own fear. More than a couple people I knew who did the rabbit shot themselves after their gained freedom. There is something about the chase that makes victory seem hollow. With nobody to run after you, what is the real point of running? I would have done anything to get out of it, and I tried my best to test those limits. I came clean on everything and everyone. Friends I had for years meant nothing to me in the space of a cigarette and the angry glare of the city DA. I sang like a bird, and I didn't feel bad about doing it. If it would get me out of ten years, I would have gladly brought my own mother down to the station and whored her out like a hooker on Arvada to every cop in the place. Or so I tell myself anyway... but perhaps that's more of a lie than I'm letting on. The whole reason I was in the position I was in was because of her, or my love for her, and in the end it didn't matter anyway. Because I still did the ten years, and she still died alone in her house. By the time I got out the grave was cold and the house was living with new residents for the better part of six years. I never got stronger, even though I did my complete sentence there. I was up for parole a couple times after mom died, but it just didn't seem to matter anymore. Silence, as it turns out, is not golden in the police station. There are moments when talking just doesn't work. About myself; I'm tall, a good six feet and a half. People in school used to make comments about basketball, but I was never very agile and certainly not very interested in sports through school. I could take one look at myself in run down sneakers and patched pants and know that I didn't have a place with the finely groomed men in the East Lincoln High Basketball team. Go Lions. Being big young does give you some advantages. I was poor, but unlike my smaller brother I didn't have to fight my way through school for respect. When you're tall and big like I am people just give you this wide path and leave you alone. You're still poor and nobody is going to care about you, but by the same hand you're not going to get kicked in the face for it. You ever notice how poor people look ratty beyond just their clothes? It beats me why that is, but it was true. I had the scruffy hair and the unshaven face, the long hair and the unkept skin. Patches of acne grew in small fields on my forehead and back, and there always seemed to be a smell that followed me around. My brother, he looked like a rat although he tried a lot harder to clean it up. But you can't really. Poor stays with you. My mother was sick all though High School. My father, well, he drove trucks for a living, and I don't want you to have the impression that he was a bad father. He wasn't, and he cared about us just about as much as you could imagine. In my years in prison that was the thing that always surprised me the most... just about every guy in there had no father. I know that the women's rights people will tell you that a father doesn't matter, and I know what they are trying to do. A lot of women don't get a choice in the matter, they just managed to get knocked up by a real prick. Women need our support a lot more than they need our sympathy, just like kids need a father. So, funny thing. One year I was in prison the warden got this program going for Mother's Day. He worked a deal out with Halmark to provide cards, and everyone sent their mothers a card for Mother's Day. Real nice, and although my mother was dead by that point it was really something most of the guys took to. In prison a day where people take to something that isn't violence or rape is a good day, and we took it. The little program worked so well that the warden did the same thing for Father's Day several weeks later. But guess what? Only about fifty people participated. This from a prison that houses thousands of people... and what do you make of that? I know that women can make it on their own, but you take one look around that prison and tell me fathers don't matter. My father was a great father right up until the day he fell asleep at the wheel and drove his truck into the side of stone wall on Interstate 90 in Montana. One minute our family was struggling with a dad, the next minute we were handed a check for fifteen thousand dollars and told that he shouldn't have been driving so late. I was angry, hurt, and lost. I liked my father, but it didn't wreck me. Life hands you shit, you don't lay down in it. But let me tell you, there sure was a lot of shit that year to deal with. My mother continued to get sick and while the politicans argued over state legislated health care, my mother waited in hospitals hoping that one of the doctors would work pro-bono. I sold blood, and I worked after school. My brother dropped out and started boxing for money. Little runt... boxing. Jesus H. Christ. The thing about it is, when you're poor and you get sick you're really screwed. You can't work that way, and without working you can't get better. You just fall into the trap and cycle into oblivion... That's why I'm here now. I hate wrestling. But I'm in it. I'm in oblivion.
