Tough Choices - Part Two

10 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
There was a loud crunch and David's body flew through the air, spiralling like a top, and he landed hard on the temple's cobblestone floor. It took a moment for his vision to clear, and he slowly stood up to face his opponent once again. Tzu Ni had become a much stronger fighter since the last time David fought him, and it didn't help matters that he hated the gaijin with a passion. He hit like a bull, moved like a panther, with the tenacity of a wolverine. This was supposed to be training, but Tzu Ni wasn't pulling any punches and hadn't for the past ten minutes. On the south end of the large training hall, three men sat on large red cushions. Master Yoeda sat motionless between the two younger men, studying both combatants with guarded interest. To his right, young Ping wore a similar style of robe as Master Yoeda, identifying him as a member of the Golden Phoenix Clan. His robe was white, like Master Yoeda's, but the lack of gold trim identified him as a rank novice rather than a Master. He, too, watched both fighters but seemed to concentrate more on Tzu Ni's devastating style. The third man, an American, wasn't watching the fight. He couldn't. Alex Creed wore a decorative black scarf over his eyes, which were still recovering from the damage done by his father, Kodiak Vic Creed. He wore a loose-fitting shirt with matching pants, both supplied by the Temple. Despite not being able to see the fight unfolding in front of him, he could still hear it. Especially that last blow. Tzu Ni watched David stand, and tried to hide his disappointment. He'd just crushed the gaijin with a Burning Phoenix strike, yet he somehow managed to get to his feet. Feelings of doubt crept into the young apprentice. Had he executed the move properly? If he hadn't, did Master Yoeda notice? If he had, how could the gaijin dog still be conscious? David wasn't sure himself how he was still moving. He clutched his chest and forced himself to breathe normally, ignoring the pain as best he could. Tzu Ni was hurt as well, he could tell. But it was Tzu Ni who had just pulled off the big move, and now David could see the uncertainty that had set in when it hadn't taken him out. It almost had, had it not been for David's uncanny ability to take an ugly beating. Alex sat in the dark and took in as much as he could from his remaining senses. The smell of sweat from the combatants (and flowery incense from Yoeda). The tingling of the herbal lotion Yoeda had provided for his eyes. The sound of bare feet shuffling quickly across the stone floor, followed by the muffled thump of fist meeting flesh, foot striking bone. Not exactly what he expected when David invited him to come for a visit after the attack at Countdown 1. It had been hard to leave home, especially so soon after his Mother's heart attack. But with her insistance that he went, Alex made the trip. The fWo was filled with chaos, and the last thing Sally wanted was her near-blind son to be submerged into hazardous waters. Best he took some time away to recover and weigh his options. If Sally had her way, wrestling for the fWo would not be one of those options. But it was Alex's benefits through the corporation that helped take care of his mother. What could he do? It was all he'd thought about in the last month that he'd been at the temple, and the answers still weren't coming to him. David watched Tzu Ni, waiting for him to make a move. When no move was coming, David smiled at him. Tzu Ni's face twisted into a mask of anger and he jumped into the air, wanting nothing more than to bury his foot deep inside the gaijin's skull. David quickly intercepted the kick with an inside crescent kick, not only knocking the foot aside but trapping the leg and driving Tzu Ni to the ground. The frustrated apprentice rolled to his feet. Cross closed the gap and stunned him with a left-right combo of thai elbows. An elbow uppercut snapped his head back, giving David enough space to drive his foot into Tzu Ni's stomach. The blow jerked him clean off his feet and drove him backward into one of the many wooden pillars surrounding the ring. The hollow thud of skull meeting tempered wood made young Ping flinch. "Enough!" Yoeda snapped. Tzu Ni froze in place, just as David sent a fist whistling toward his throat. The blow stopped inches from his Adam's Apple. Both men stood at attention and bowed to Master Yoeda. The turned to face each other and gave a quick, shallow bow. Tzu Ni spun and stormed out of the ring, pushing aside the acolytes who approached him with wet cloths to clean away the blood. Things were tough all over, Alex thought to himself. For now, I'll just be glad I'm here at the temple where things are a little more sane. David walked over to where Alex was sitting. The Mecca held up a towel. "So how did we do?" Alex asked. Cross took the towel and wiped the blood from his face. "We? You did a fine job handing me the towel. As for me, well I don't think Sue over there is going to send me a Christmas card this year." "You are disrespectful of Tzu Ni," Master Yoeda said as he approached the two, Ping following closely behind him. "Now is your friend ready?" Alex turned his head. "Ready? What am I ready for?"
