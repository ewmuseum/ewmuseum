Temptation Awaits. One.

14 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Closer to the prize at the end of the rope All night long I dream of the day When it comes around then it's taken away Leaves me with the feeling that I feel the most Feeling comes to life when I see your ghost Caroline Jessop was at a loss. Her right eye began to cloud with tears as she stood in the shower, pondering what had happened. She'd thought given a little time away from Chicago, out at college while her mother and new stepfather got acclimated to married life, would clear her air. Of course, she hadn't expected to fall in lus...lov...something with her roommate. Pulling down her best G.P.A. since the fourth grade was another nicely unexpected surprise. But when she got home, it seemed like everything was different. The friends she'd made in high school didn't seem so cool. The places she'd once revered in Chicago had become footnotes in her mind to similar establishments around the university and in her adopted town. In a clearer moment she wondered how much her feelings for the roomie had clouded things in her mind. Maybe the things were better in San Diego. Maybe she was so stupid giddy in love she couldn't think straight. Hell, there was a tear in her eye. She never cried anymore, not after The Incident. She tried to chalk it up to frustration more than anything else. Frustration over the stepfather and his kids, frustration over being in the freezing cold instead of basking in the sun at Mission Beach, worries about her upcoming schedule... Well, they all had merit. But she knew the biggest reason. She hadn't said anything. And she wouldn't be able to say anything for another while face-to-face. It had to be face-to-face, for her sanity and for the dream to be realized. Caroline washed the tear from the corner of her eyes and washed her face in order to quell the flood behind the eyes. She'd been emotional before. But since, she'd felt cold and detached, apathetic. So little made her feel alive anymore. She was it. Her whine of "Angelina" bounced off the shower walls and throughout the empty house.
