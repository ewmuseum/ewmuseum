Part Three: I Think I'm Gonna Make It

13 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Morning brought with it a sense of disorientation to Dolores Wakeman. She recognized her bedroom, her furniture, her bathrobe on the chair and her slippers underneath. But she heard something from her kitchen downstairs... disturbing, considered that she lived alone and her son was somewhere in Canada, wrestling for the most successful promotion of the twenty-first century. She heard something - someone moving around in her kitchen. Judging by the opening and closing of cabinets, this was a person who was not familiar with her home. Dolores rose and steadied herself... steadied herself? That's right... Ivy McGinnis and a bottle of Jack Daniels. Dolores Wakeman wasn't hung over, but she was feeling the effects of the evening. That woman... was one of a kind, thought Dolores. She had never seen anyone with such untamed fire in her soul, and a heart, fragile as glass. To hear her speak of years past, and the battles she fought and survived both in the ring and in the boardroom... then to watch her openly weep the loss of the only man she ever truly loved... It all took her breath away. This woman's complexities had complexities. And it seemed-- --coffee? Dolores smelled freshly brewing coffee. The intoxicating scent filled the room, and she headed downstairs. The house was old, but very few creaks and squeaks betrayed the fact that she was on her way. Movement continued, but it was less hurried... she was learning her way around. "Morning," said Dolores, as she enjoyed the slight flinch in Ivy's shoulders as she turned from the refrigerator. Her eyes went from the young woman to the kitchen table, and the feast spread out across it. A half dozen bagels, flavored cream cheese, lox, preserves, a corn muffin, a banana nut muffin, a blueberry muffin, everything... and Ivy handed her a cup of coffee. "I didn't know what you liked on your bagels," explained Ivy, "or even if you did or not... so I just guessed. Cream or sugar?" "This is... fine," replied Dolores, "How'd you sleep?" "Oh, best night'a sleep I've had in a year," said Ivy as she sipped her own coffee, "I feel completely refreshed and alert." Dolores looked as if she had something else to say, but sipped her coffee first and the thought was cut off. "This is really good. You make this?" Ivy nodded. "I can barely boil water when it comes to home cookin', but I make a mean cup of coffee. Did you want anything else for breakfast? I saw a diner in the same strip as the bagel shop... it's no prob--" "Why are you doing this?" asked Dolores, interrupting her, "You really don't have to buy me breakfast and get up early to make the coffee and... Not that it's not appreciated, Lord no..." Her voice cut off as Ivy's eyes shot down, and she sat down at the table. Dolores noticed a slight bulge in the knee of her pants, the telltale sign that the surgically repaired knee underneath it was wrapped up in a bandage. "I'm sorry," continued Dolores, "I didn't mean to--" "You've got nothing to be sorry for," interrupted Ivy, "You're great, Dolores. I feel like you get me." Dolores sat down at the table across from her. "You're not someone that needs to be gotten, Ivy... you just needed someone to talk to, I think." "I've got plenty of people I could be talking to," replied Ivy, "but none'a that would've helped." Eyebrow arched. Where was she going with this? "It's too bad I didn't meet you and Matthew three, four years ago," continued Ivy, "you would've really loved my mom. She was really my best friend... she got me. It didn't matter what the problem was, I could go home with a bottle of wine, lie on the couch with my feet on the head and my head hanging upside down off the seat and just talk for hours... she always figured me out. I never understood those kids who didn't get along with their parents, y'know? I talked to my mom about everything." Dolores nodded slightly and sipped her coffee. It was starting to make sense. "I haven't had a conversation with her in three years now," continued Ivy, "I see the same sense of family in you and Matthew. It's the same sense of family that the fWo used to have. But that wasn't real family - that was a corporation that just worked well together..." "...and when you were here over Christmas and saw how Matthew and I interacted..." "...blood's thicker than water, thicker than business." The women looked at each other for a moment. "I'm sorry," said Ivy, "It was selfish of me to come here and throw all this on you." She grabbed her coat from where it hung on the back of the chair, but Dolores grabbed her wrist. "Ivy." "What?" "I don't know the whole story, what you've been doing the past few years... but if I helped you at all last night then there's nothing to be sorry for." "...what?" Dolores smiled. "It sounds to me, like you've considered yourself a burden on everyone that's come in contact with you. The only person you really felt like you could talk to was your mother, the only person who theoretically loves you unconditionally. And who was I over the holidays? Mom, all over again." Ivy shifted uncomfortably, but Dolores calmed her down. "Really... it's okay. It was wonderful getting to know you last night... it makes me feel safer knowing that you're one of the people my son looks up to. And you, dear girl, are welcome back here any time. Just... give a call so I can set up the guest room again." There was no visible emotion of any kind for this invitation - just a simple smile from the Psycho Bitch as her eyes drifted from Mrs. Wakeman to the pile of books pushed to the side of the table. "What are you studying again, Dolores?" "Psychology," smiled Dolores, "but I didn't really need any of that to figure you out." Ivy looked intrigued at this statement. "All I had to do was listen to you." Smiles again. "So get back up there on that tour," continued Dolores, "and tell that boy how you really feel." "Yes, Mom," replied Ivy with a laugh. "But first," said Dolores, "What do you put in this coffee?" Five minutes later, she was sorry she'd asked.
