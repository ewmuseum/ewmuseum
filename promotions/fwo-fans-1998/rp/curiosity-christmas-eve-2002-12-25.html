Curiosity, Christmas Eve...

25 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Well right now, I'd just as soon kill him as kiss him..." Yet another fight. Things were getting more difficult every day with Darcy, from Amy's eyes. This time he'd forgotten Christmas alltogether...well, alltogether with the exception of himself and even though she tried to hide her feelings half the time, Aimz was still a young woman so, naturally enough she played the daughter and ran to her mother to vent. "So I came alone. He's no fun to have around Stephen anyway, all he does is sit there and bitch then ask when we're leaving." Thankfully enough, before her mother could speak they were interrupted by the sound of plodding footsteps and an upreached hand sliding against a rail coming up the stairway. When Stephen Campbell looked at his mother, he saw a gorgeous girl wrapped in the most exotic, curious clothing he'd ever seen; She was an encyclopedia of knowledge that his grandparents never let him see on TV or hear in any storybook, a treasure trove of wisdom on almost any topic. When anyone else looked at her, they saw a lost kid in spikes, chains and anything that wasn't in the laundry hamper topped off by makeshift bandages concealing gashes on her forehead. You could tell she was bright, near-genius even but at the same time, she had little practical sense and generally made a mess of living life and keeping up her mental state. Contrasting views but, even though half the time Amy didn't know it, she had the respect and admiration of the majority if not just for dealing head-on with the hand she'd been dealt some twenty years ago. No matter, Stephen had run down the stairs to see his mother for the first time in two full weeks, against all shouts and warnings of 'Slow down!' and 'Someday you'll fall and break your neck!' his grandmother shot at him. His first spoken word of the moment was predictable enough as he flung small arms around Amy's neck, helping to drag her down further than she was already kneeling to grip him. "Mommy!!!!" "H----waaaaaiit a minute..." She was about to greet him as usual, but leaned back and looked over the clothing he'd been tugging at to loosen. Amy saw the boy she'd taken as a son, her son, dressed in a small dress shirt, slacks and a tie. With lips pursed, she turned up to her mother, "What's all this about?" "Oh! We're going to church." Aimz' eyes widened. Church? She hadn't set foot in a crowded chapel for years, and for good reason: the church-goers, as they were, didn't particularly take to her 'sort' of people. She was the 'trouble maker', a mischievious and hard to handle girl from the time she took her first step. Never quite fit in...never quite wanted to. But it was Christmas eve, she'd have to make an effort. After all, the redhead had absolutely nothing against organized religion, and believed in God if not just so her head wouldn't explode in pondering the origin of that first spark of life. Even if there hadn't been an ounce of anything else 'normal' within her, at least she wasn't conforming to the sudden trend of being some anarchist for the sake of fashion. "Uhh...church? Sounds good." Nonetheless, her answer surprised her mother, not to mention her son, who immediately objected "I don' wanna go t' boring ol' church!" But, as with any average 4 year old, the subject quickly took a leap to "Can I open a present early?" And before Bev could interject, Amy smiled warmly. "If you're good..and only if you come to church with us! Now go tie your shoes...just shout down if you need help, 'kay?" Mimicing his mom, the little boy shouted "'Kay!" and was off before he could take a breath. Bev had watched her daughter interact with the child in awe, and chuckled mostly to herself. "I'll make a mother of you yet!" "Told you I could handle things if I really wanted to." Amy's confident smile mirrored her pride at playing mother for this short period of time. "Soon enough, you'll have life sorted out...then he can move in with you." More laughter, this time from the redhead. "Maybe. Won't be for a few years yet...but maybe I should take him on the road with me for a week on and off, y'think? It'd be great for him to meet BL--err--Brandon. You're always talking about good influences, things to make Stephen grow up 'right'..." She chuckled again, eyes seeming to glitter with some odd sort of admiration. "Well he's the best influence a person could get." Her mother's tone suddenly sank a notch, though she still grinned slightly. "You talk about that man like he's a God. Don't let any of that cloud your vision." "Huh?" Aimz felt an angry chill up her spine. She knew where this would lead. "Look...there's nothing going on..." "I know, Amy. But I know you, and...ah, nevermind." Bev waved hand laiden with dainty little rings at her daughter. "It's no use." "No. You know me, and...?" She was expectant, questioning. "And I know you have a tendancy to jump. No matter how inviting he is, I've got to beg you not to jump without thinking." "Do I really look as stupid as people act like I am?" Amy's face had regained a shade of red not uncommon when something raised her ire, and her mother knew to back off. "Really. Can I go anywhere without someone telling me I'm going to fall for Brandon? The man's married, mom." "I know, I kn---" "No, obviously you don't. Now can we try to have a peaceful night?" She eased back against a wall. Even though she'd been leaning, Amy got dizzy fast. She'd refused medical attention when she'd obviously recieved some kind of head injury at the hands of Graphic Violence, and had a sneaking feeling it would come back to nip her in the ass at any moment. "I came here to escape stress..." And that was just it. She'd been smart this time and avoided another oncoming argument with Darcy, foresight told her home was where to be. Home 'playing mommy' for a few days...after all, it was the holiday season. Home was where people actually cared... Though something in her couldn't help but wonder what Brandon's home was like just then.
