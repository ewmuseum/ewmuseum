Wish There Was Something True

20 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Nobody has faith in the English language anymore. Words are spouted out from their wry mouths and every passing person digests and believes the lies. Just like they follow their orders blindly. Have people become so dependent and foolish that they can accomplish next to nothing without someone hovering over them and barking out some semblance of direction? Example: I could walk into the middle of Times Square with a megaphone in my hand, stop traffic, stop everyone's daily routine and yell. I could yell into this megaphone some of the most horrific statements the language could provide. I could say I killed someone. I could say: "I killed John Doe." Clear as day, the people on the streets listening, I've told them all I killed another man. Even if I didn't kill another man, would they believe me? Yes. This is why words have become such a falsity in our society. Words are how rumors about others start. Rumors start out small, quite possibly being completely true. The rumor gets spread from the wry mouth, person to person, eventually the once valid thought has been whispered between so many ears that it gets twisted like steel. Twisted through the half- truths and the gullible nature of us that it becomes completely false. These rumors that become stretched out farces can cause more trouble than the whisperer could have ever imagined. Example: The human memory isn't all that it seems. Fragmented thoughts, images and ideas all come together to form a memory of an event, time or place. Memories pieced together from the mind of a witness to a homicide or even a petty theft case can be shoddy, at best. So, can it be said that nearly all crimes relying on a witness to make the case and prosecution against an individual; a basic estimation of thought and deception of one's mind? Yes. From here it gets much worse. Men and women trying to get themselves ahead of the vicious cycle of life will do anything, at any cost to better their own situation. This happens to include people who think they have some special ability to see into the future and predict and possibly better a person's life based on a few basic facts. These "psychics" will give you all the information you need over the phone for upwards of $3.99 per minute. Certainly, this profession of mostly women must be on to something. They've done what the rest of the human race doesn't; use common information to create a false future or thought of their pathetic lives. People will invest their hard earned money into these moneymakers and believe what they're hearing. We are a sickly gullible race. Example: Late night commercials, cleverly titled "infomercials" smother sleepless men and women in the middle of the night. These drowsy people are half awake and barely paying attention at best. Without their true inhibitions, they end up calling sages like "Cleo" and believe every word she feeds them. She pirates their money into her own profit and gain. The American Dream. She makes a fortune off of the idiocy off of her own kind. She has proven to actually be smarter than other people, no matter how foolish she looks on her shows. People call. They all want something to believe in, no matter how untrue it is. And is taking millions from these truth seers wrong? Yes. With all of this in mind, I sit here in this dark apartment, brooding over what I've thought. It all makes perfect sense, it's as clear as day. Yes, I was considered mentally insane more than once in my life. That could make my words a little hard to believe. In April I tried to slice myself apart with a broken mirror because something inside had taken over and I couldn't live with the fact that I wouldn't be in control of my own body. I came out of the hospital with a clean bill of health, a perfect patient. I went to the Rockies and then New England to get away, get my head straight. I stayed away from the fires that had burned on the inside. I didn't want to be the same man that for the past twenty-five years had been seen as a poster child for Prozac. Sure, my hair is black, I look grungy overall, but that's who I was. I've changed now. Twenty-six years on my way to hell. I want to turn back, but I can't. People will believe all the lies, deceit and falsities that run rampant in our society. But they won't believe the truth. And by January first, 2003, they will all pay dearly when they realize what they thought was a lie, was the bloody truth. The Countdown to Oblivion has begun with one question unanswered: Why doesn't anyone believe that I killed Austin Davis?
