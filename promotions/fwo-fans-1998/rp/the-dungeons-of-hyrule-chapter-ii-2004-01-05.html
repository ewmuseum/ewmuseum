The Dungeons of Hyrule: Chapter II

5 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
September 2nd, 2003; Early Afternoon Business in the Dungeons of Hyrule video game store certainly picked up later in the day, as there were now as many as nine people in the store. Four people were in the balcony area, two male teenagers, a little girl, and the little girl's mother. Four others were on the floor, a teenage woman, two little boys, and their father. The last one was in line, having his purchase of Splinter Cell being rung up by Stone Brown. Stick was running interference in the balcony, trying to help the two male teenagers find a copy of Super Mario Kart for the Super NES. After he finished registering the purchase, Stone turned his attention to the teenage girl, since she was now approaching him with inquiries on where the Justin Timberlake CDs were. Stone was a bit flabbergasted that this girl had absolutely no idea that this was a video game store and if the owner of the place ever found out that Justin Timberlake CDs were being sold here, he'd have his head. So, obviously, someone bailed him out. "Ma'am, we don't really sell male prostitution in here. Might wanna try the Wal-Mart, I hear that the suck over there is never-ending." said "Codemaster" Allen Brown as he approached Stone and the girl. Offended, the girl stormed off, another customer soundly chased off by he who was the Codemaster. Oh well, there would be others. Stone was surprised by Allen's presence, but Allen just spoke, "You'd think that some people would notice the shape of the building and realize that it ain't much of a CD store." "Allen! When'd you walk in?" Stone asked, with some degree of surprise. "Pretty much now." Allen said, before he high-fived his cousin, then hugged him. In a manly, heterosexual way, of course. After the embrace, Allen looked around and nodded his head in approval, "Place seems to be doin' pretty good right now." "Yeah. Business is boomin'. How's your business?" Stone asked. "Pretty business-like." Allen replied, simply. He looked around, "So, where's Sticky?" "Balcony." Allen backed up to a central area of the floor and looked up, before he found his tall friend. It occured to Stone that Allen had brought his things in, via a large gym bag that was being held over his shoulder. Allen then journied up the stairs and nodded his head to Stick as if to say "Howdy", before he took out the keys from his coat pocket and unlocked the door that would lead to the stairs that led to the second floor of the Dungeons of Hyrule. Allen's abode wasn't really much to right home about. It was a simple three room affair. There was the large living space, which contained several of Allen's personal video game systems that he did not take with him. Of course, since the Codemaster only ever took his Gamecube and Game Boy Advance SP around with him(due to their smaller sizes), this was hardly worth noting. In front of the television set was a small little table-like thing that was barely a half a foot on the ground, and it was mainly a spot for Allen to prop his feet up. Obviously, there were comfy chairs or beanbags situated near this coffee table. There were various posters of video games posted on the walls of the living area, a noticable display hanging just to the right of the door where something like a sword could be placed, and there was a makeshift kitchen in the back of the area, seperated off by tile floor instead of carpeting, and a little counter that jutted out from the wall, stopping only to leave an opening for someone to get through. Allen placed his gym bag on his comfy chair and opened it up. He pulled out his Megaton Hammer and casually placed it on the sword display, where it belonged. Then he pulled out the Tome of Eternal Darkness from his bag, and put it on a display on a small stand next to the entertainment center. He pulled some clothes out of his gym bag and placed them in a nearby closet. Then he pulled out his Gamecube and placed it on the table. He hesitated for a moment, because he felt the vibration of his cellular phone go off in his jacket pocket. Taking the cell phone out and flipping it open, Allen spoke into it, "Speck." "I heard you were back in town." the familiar voice on the other end of the phone said. "Robert." "So, Allen... how's my favorite little brother?" Robert B. asked of his brother. "You don't really like me, Robert. So why don't you just cut to the chase and ask me why you're callin' me?" Allen shot back. He *so* didn't want to have this conversation right now. "Oh, nothin'. Just wanted to see if you were in the neighborhood." Robert said. Allen could sense that something was rotten in Denmark and there could have quite possibly been a red exclamnation point hovering over his head, followed by the infamous "alert" noise from the Metal Gear Solid games. "Peachy. If you don't mind... brother, I've got to get myself settled back in." Allen said, with a certain bit of ice in his words. "Fine then. I'll be in touch." Robert said. Allen heard a click on the other end, and so he casually flipped his phone closed and tossed it aside onto one of the beanbags, deciding not to dwell on that awkward conversation. Allen's brothers have been notoriously jealous and angry at Allen for making it as far as he did in the wrestling business while simultaneously continuing to own and occasionally operate the Dungeons of Hyrule. While his brothers helped to create the establishment, they never bothered to continue ownership, whereas Allen was smart enough to keep his name entact in the deed. Robert and Jordan just were not as savvy at keeping the big picture in mind, and that was why they worked the day jobs they now worked, as opposed to working venues across the globe. Meanwhile, Allen, who was six years younger than his twin brothers, learned from the mistakes made by his brothers and never repeated them. He trained with a little-known wrestler for damn near free instead of blowing his money on getting trained by Killer Kowalski. He kept the video game business alive instead of abandoning it in favor of wrestling. And, most importantly, Allen used his natural charisma to get himself over, instead of simply using the ever-overused powers of twin wrestlers to try and build intrigue into his matches. Jealousy happens. And Allen Brown accepted it. What else could he do?
