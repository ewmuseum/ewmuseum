How I Used To Be (5/5)

17 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Dear Stephanie, I'm writing this on the plane, so forgive me if the penmanship is a little sloppy. Ha. OK, that was stupid, but that's me, and these pens aren't the kind with erasers. Oh hell. I don't know what I'm doing. I guess if you're reading this, it means I went to your house and you weren't there. Yeah, I suppose that's not exactly endearing me to you either, with the whole "stay the hell away from me or die" thing. But this is still something I have to do. It's not for me, and it's not about you... it's about my son. Our son. I grew up mostly without either parent, and even today that's still shaping me - how I've been, how I am, and how I will be. There's a hole - yes, that has cheesy soap opera written all over it, but it's still true. I feel it in everything I do. I don't think about it all day every day, but it's always there in the corners. It's a feeling nobody should have. I'm not asking to be around all the time, or for free access to your house, or any of that. I know what the answer would be. I know I've made a lot of mistakes, but I don't want my son to have to pay for them. I don't want to be an empty space in his life when he looks back from my age. Whatever I can do - a video record, letters, something - I want to do. He deserves that much. And maybe, one day... I want to be the man I can be - not the man I have been. I know it's a lot to ask, but please. Think about it. You know how to contact me - the cell number hasn't changed, or if you prefer, neither has the email. KTF - still. Scott ----- Thursday, May 8, 2003 9:16 PM Scott Wasner flipped through the television channels, watching none of them. The cell phone was on his dresser and his laptop computer was plugged in. It had been for the last 3 hours, though he hadn't actually done anything with it in all that time, besides clicking the annoying AOL pop-ups that asked you if you wanted to sign off. He finally shook his head and turned off the TV. It was a clear night in Miami, and the stars were bright. He stood up and walked to the window. He had a clear view of the city from his room, and it was almost staggeringly bright, active, and alive. He watched it for a while, mesmerized. Out there, lives were beginning, lives were ending, lives were changing. "You've got mail."
