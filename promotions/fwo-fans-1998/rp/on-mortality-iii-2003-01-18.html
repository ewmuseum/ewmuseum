On Mortality III

18 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The blackened night, adorned with all your ignorance, caresses my soul, a velvet scruple yearning for that which defies knowing: Death...an end to all that does not exist to the undying. Much alike a broken record, I find myself echoing words that I already spake. The troubles of the world are not what all make them seem. A troubled dollar, limited in its own effect, coupled with your panic, runs its roots much deeper. It eats at you and you worry where there is none. And so you do what? Unite? Put men in charge? Form committees? You make me laugh, as you take your worries with you into your union...your committee. Heh. It will be your end. The world does ache, though, just not where you feel it. The pains of the world are not so easy as humanity's. Though not so easily arisen, they run all the deeper and cannot be anointed with all the salves and ointments it holds. You destroy it, and for this it will kill you. Do you hear that? Escape...it calls my name. More frequent have its summons grown. Nearly constant now, there is no escaping them...no escaping it. It holds no power over me. I have no almighty, and if I did, it would not be the instrument with which immortality now dies... Though it shall have that effect. There was a time when I trusted my mortality as I trusted man. I trusted man and was betrayed. The knife, having torn the flesh upon my spine
