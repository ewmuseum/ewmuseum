Family

8 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
By some sick twist of fate, Lance Knight had booked a weekend to come home and see his parents (so that they could celebrate Thanksgiving) because he'd been too busy (Mob Rules and Japan) to do so over the actual holiday. Plus, with Twilight being pushed back to either the 22nd or 24th (depending on who you talked to) and then Countdown on 29th, 30th, and 31st, there was no way Alex Newsome could guarantee he'd have any quality time with his family. The sick twist? He'd wanted to get a weekend away from the road since coming back (too early?) from his knee injury, and this weekend had seemed the time, especially with a Calgary to Pittsburgh flight seeming like peanuts compared to one from Tokyo or Seoul. However, he'd especially wanted to escape the arctic Canadian temperatures he'd been exposed to, and with his dad boasting about how it'd been in the mid-sixties all week, Pittsburgh was gonna feel like Jamaica for this hometown boy. Too bad his flight was in a holding pattern for forty-five minutes because Pittsburgh was getting dumped on. Eighteen inches of snow - someone had a sick sense of humor. But none of that mattered now, because he was sitting in the most comfortable chair in his parents cozy living room, the green La-Z-Boy rocker-recliner that he'd bought for his dad nearly a year ago for Christmas, taking advantage of its heating and massage features, a can of Pabst Blue Ribbon in his hand and the third quarter of the Packers/Lions Thanksgiving day game on the television. Thanks to TiVo, the Newsome family tradition of turkey, beer, and football had been narrowly preserved, and Lance wouldn't have missed it for the world. "Hey, Pops," Alex shouted across the living room. In spite of the mere five or so feet between them, the time in the mills had done a number on his father's hearing, and most of their conversations came in shouted bits and pieces, much to his mother's chagrin. "Eh?" was Ed Newsome's only response. His black hair was fading to gray, and he had to wear glasses now when he watched his evening programs, but he was still as physically imposing as he'd ever been, and when his head whipped around to stare at his only son, it was still enough to make the younger Newsome jump. "I was just thinking, y'know, you might need to get rid of that ugly old couch by Christmas time." Alex winced at the pained expression on his father's face. Until May of 2002, they'd been constantly at odds, but ever since Grendel had brought Ed Newsome into his personal vendetta against Lance Knight, both men had realized their stubbornness, and they'd been nearly inseparable on the infrequent occasions when Alex had made it home. As a result, he knew immediately that he'd slipped up, even before his father corrected him. "Your grandfather, bless his soul, gave me and your mother this couch for our wedding, and I'd sooner throw you out than get rid of it!" He finished with a flourish, motioning like he was grabbing someone by their collar and waistband and tossing them out of the door. When he was angry, Ed's accent reappeared, and he gestured more than he would normally. Deciding that changing the subject would be for the best, Alex drained the last of his can and put the chair back down. "You ready for another one?" "You tryin t'get me drunk so you can finally whoop me, boy?" Much better, as the smile had returned to Ed's wrinkled face, years of squinting against the heat of molten steel having carved their way onto his features. "You know it, old man!" Alex exclaimed as he disappeared into the kitchen, narrowly ducking his father's beer can as it whizzed by his head. "Nice shot," he called from the other side of the refrigerator door, and he kept his head in the icebox long enough to miss the one-fingered salute he received from his father.
