Waste, Part VI (Epilouge)

1 September 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I need forgiveness from the people I truly care about, I need support behind my back to help me spit it out I am gonna win, I can't afford to blow this one, I hate myself sometimes, I love myself, I need this way of life because it holds me. I hate myself sometimes, I love myself, Contradiction's the way of life Happiness is wealthiness is healthy now - "I" Taproot Donaven Winters stood in front of the mirror in his hotel room, a small smile pasted across his face. He couldn't believe that he was smiling for once, but he didn't know why he was smiling either. Jon had called, as he had hoped. Jon had also saved his life. His eyes diverted from the mirror down towards the nearly empty trash can. He saw the bottle of sleeping pills in there and he almost wanted to go back for them. But he resisted. The three he had taken were beginning to set in. He was feeling a little drowsy and retreated onto the bed. It creaked loudly as he laid himself down and closed his eyes. * At the same time, Jon Crisp was also smiling. He had Jen in his arms as they just sat together on her couch, enjoying the silent moment. Eyes that had been filled with extreme rage had turned peaceful, somber and happy. Jon was in a state of happiness as he looked at Jen, who had begun to fall asleep in his arms. But his mind kept wandering towards Donaven, and what he was going through. He tried to shake it off. He kept trying to concentrate on what he was doing at the present moment. Jon's eyes began to feel heavy too, soon Jen and Jon would be asleep, together in each other's arms. The way it was supposed to be. * Donaven's eyes snapped open and he shot up, sitting erectly on top of the bed. Something wasn't right, he glanced at the clock nervously: 2:12. It was later than he thought. The past three hours had seemingly gone by very slowly and he couldn't understand why. A strange relationship had started to form between Crisp and himself and he didn't know if it was good or bad. "Is Jon," Donaven hesitated. He looked once around the room, still feeling alone and insecure before continuing. "A friend?" The reply he wanted never came. Just the low humming of the air conditioner that was propped into the room's only window. His eyes kept darting towards the trash can, he reached one of his arms forward towards it, but his fingertips seemed just inches away from being able to grasp it. He sighed and his arm dropped back down. He laid back down on the bed, staring straight up at the ceiling, cell phone clutched in his other hand. * Jon Crisp's eyes slowly opened. Somehow Jen had gotten out of his arms and had gone elsewhere in the house. He knew he was alone for at least a few minutes and he looked at the television, which was showing a west coast edition of Sports Center. He turned it off and leaned back against the couch, putting his legs up as well, nearly laying down. He kept looking at the phone, waiting for it to ring. Donaven wouldn't leave his mind and it seemed earlier in the night that he was turning into him. "What the hell is happening," Crisp thought aloud. Inside, Crisp knew what was going on, the two were becoming friends, somehow. It didn't seem right though, they had gone through bloody battles and to end up as friends... it seemed out of place. Crisp shrugged it off and closed his eyes again, falling quickly back asleep. * While Jon Crisp slept on his girlfriend's couch, Donaven Winters uneasily slept on the bed of a run down hotel. His body shifted sides over and over again, trying to get itself comfortable. His hand opened up, and stretched itself over the bed, the cell phone fell to the floor with a light thud. He had finally settled in and soundly fell asleep. The night had been disturbing and eye opening at the same time. Three new things had happened on this night for him that he had never expected. He was speechless. He smiled. And most importantly, Donaven Winters had a friend.
