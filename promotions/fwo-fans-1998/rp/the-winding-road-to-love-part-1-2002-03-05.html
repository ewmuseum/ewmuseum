The Winding Road To Love (Part 1)

5 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The offices of Platinum Gold, Inc. were strangely quiet on this Monday morning. Many of the workers seemed to be relaxing at their desks, possibly still recovering from an eventful weekend. Since the staff at Platinum Gold was younger than most offices, that wouldn't be surprising. However, normally they still managed to come in during the week and put in some hard work around the office. Today was a different story. As one of the secretaries walked through the offices delivering some messages, the elevator could be heard arriving at the floor with a beep. The doors slide open and the company's CEO, Monte Burns, steps out and starts down the hall towards his office. Followed closely by one of his assistants from downstairs, Burns walks further down the hall until he reaches his secretary's desk. Burns turns and looks back at the offices and sees the lack of movement, talking, and work that is going on. The assistant sees this and takes a step back against the wall. He knew that Burns often became incensed at laziness from his workers and did not tolerate anything less than maximum effort. The assistant prepared for a storm. Instead, Monte said nothing. Burns turned back and grabbed a few things from his secretary's desk and headed for his office. The assistant glanced from Burns to the office and back again before following Monte into his office. Burns took off his sports coat and laid it across one of the chairs, as he stared out of the large office window. The assistant stood silently at the doorway for a few moments, then stepped forward to ask a question. "Is there anything I can get you before we go over this, Mr. Burns?" he asked calmly. The assistant waited for Burns to answer, but Monte didn't seem to be paying much attention. After a few moments pass, Burns finally swivels around and looks at the assistant with a blank expression. "No, I'll be fine," Burns said with a sigh. He started to turn back around to gaze out of the window but then stopped and turned back to the assistant. "In fact, do you think we could hold this off for a while? I need some time to gather my thoughts about some other issues." "Sure, that would be fine, sir," said the assistant with a bit of confusion. They really needed to go over these reports, but not many people could turn down a request to delay a meeting with Burns. Most of the employees didn't exactly enjoy his "talks," i.e. bitch sessions. Burns gave the assistant a nod and then turned back to the window, as the assistant exited the room and closed the door. Looking out at the people walking up and down the streets, Burns knew that he was in no state of mind to discuss business matters right now. For the past week, he had been doing little other than thinking about Tempest. He thought back to their talk the day after Body Count and how she had dismissed him. Monte realized that he hadn't been the greatest person in the world and that he had done some things in the past against her. Still, Monte had been trying to change things around since his brother's accident on New Year's Eve. Many of the characteristics of the Monte of the past had disappeared, although "The Golden Child" was still determined to be the best wrestler in the fWo. Those two interests had butted heads yesterday on Slaughter, as Monte's feelings for Tempest battled with his desire to win a tag team match that saw them on opposite teams. As it turned out, Burns did pin Tempest to win the match, but as soon as the bell rang, he knew he had made a mistake. Burns looked down at Tempest on the mat, clutching her ribs in pain, and knew that he should have been consoling her rather than kicking her. The officials helped Tempest to her feet, and Monte realized that if a person had feelings for someone else, that person would have been there to help them out. Monte knew he had made a mistake. Correcting that mistake would be the real trouble.
