Survivor

1 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Retrieved from an unnamed Internet message board and distributed. November 1, 2002 Seattle, Washington 6:22 AM, local time You did this, y'know. All of you. I wasn't always this fucked up. I used to be normal. Really. I used to be that guy that was everyone's friend - after I dropped out of highschool and got away from all the bullshit politics, that is. Kids can be so cruel. I can be worse. If you weren't in a gang, if you didn't have someone guarding your back, you were a target. You were on the bottom. Kinda like the wrestling business. I did what I did to survive. The physical pain of my style freed me from the emotional pain of Alicia's death. The fans loved it and wanted more, the boys on top loved it because they felt threatened. Yeah, threatened. By lil' old me. I won't lie or try to placate the masses with false humility - I'm good at my job. One of the best. There's not much to it. Go to the ring. Talk, or not. Wrestle a match in some kind of style, win or lose. It's no different, in and of itself, from a thousand other professions. But when you bleed every night and cut your opponents to shreds with your fists, the people who would conceivably make less money should you succeed are glad to see you doing everything possible to shorten your career. The fans wanted to see the King of Extreme because blood is exciting. Some of the boys wanted to see the King of Extreme because it meant I stayed away from their main event money. Everybody wins. Eventually, tho - the cream always rises. A generation passes the business by and suddenly I'm cool. Yeah, I'm cool. Imagine that. Then I've got the belt and I'm not so cool anymore - the Paragon of Virtue is the one they want to see. Apologies to Eddy Love, but I'm being honest here, and Deac is the more virtuous of the two. They didn't want me to be the hero so I became the anti- hero. Staking a Claim. Melissa through the table. You know the drill. Then the fWo fired me and put two kids in a match for a belt I still have posession of, and in the span of however long a clusterfuck of a match they managed to destroy just about everything that I've worked for. Hardcore isn't about weapons, it's about no limits. It's not about how many times you get hit with a fuckin' chair - it's about how many times you can get up and give the fans something to remember. And you're surprised I jumped the rail? If Renshaw had actually known what he was doing I'd applaud his efforts, but sadly the kid is a goddamn corporate tool. So why am I here? The corporation doesn't want anyone who doesn't bend over for their pay. The corporation doesn't want anyone who won't conform to the persona that the corporation wants them to have. Angel is six and a half months pregnant, and while my career has every reason to end, her music is just starting to reach people. And quite frankly, after eight years of this shit, I'm tired of having to justify myself to a world that no longer seems to care about the antihero. If they ever did. So why am I still here? Because I decide my fate, not you. Not the corporation, and certainly not whoever it is that sent two clueless kids out to mock me, like lambs to the slaughter. Because I'm not done in this business, I've still got a lot of blood to spill - mine and others' - and I've still got a lot of memories to make for both myself and the people who paid money to see us. Because I took pride in the work I did in the fWo. Because, as overlooked as I sometimes can be by the mainstream wrestling media, on an individual basis I'm constantly approached by people who tell me that I'm an inspiration to them - that my bluntness when it comes to my life and my own personal drama helps them deal with their own issues. Because I want to leave a legacy for my child that doesn't involve fading away. Because the fWo is my home. Take your pick. Angel is the one who turned me around - I know this now. She did something I never expected anyone to be able to do for me. She made me care. Tonight, last night, whatever - I saw a group of men and women come together, not because they were being paid, not because they were working some kind of wrestling angle, not because they were trying to make a statement of attitude. They stood there, face to face with a Board of Directors who called them 'difficult' and did not take a backstep for one all- encompassing reason. Because the fWo is their home, too. The fWo is running another show next wednesday, and I plan to be there. Whether they want me there in an official capacity or not is irrelevant. I'm going to be there and I want what's mine. And to hell with the corporation. This is my story, and I decide how it ends. Not them. -EJ Flair
