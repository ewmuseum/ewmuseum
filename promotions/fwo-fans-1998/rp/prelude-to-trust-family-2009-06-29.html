Prelude to Trust - Family

29 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Before you go on, you should probably have already checked out Meltdown, night one. Go ahead, I'll wait. Well, no, I really won't. But this will still be here when you get back. I didn't really want to deal with the post - show stuff tonight, but it's important to the FWO and the company's Seattle roots for the roster to be visible after a major event. If I'd pushed, I probably could've gotten out of it considering my head is pounding after the steel that cracked it, and the on - site medic was worried I might've had a concussion. I don't. Thank goodness. But it's not my nature to 'get out' of things. So I'm here, pounding head and all, and I'm actually having a good time. "Can I talk to you for a second?" Oh no. I must have a concussion, I'm hearing things. "Knox. I'm talking to you. I need to talk to you in private." Auditory hallucinations shouldn't be able to grab me by the sleeve of my shirt, should they? I turned around to the familiar voice and, to my surprise... Miss Ivy? "Yeah," said Miss Ivy, "I'm back." I gave her a hug. I missed you, I said, what are you doing here? She shoved me playfully. "I wasn't gonna miss your debut on FWO Pay Per View," said Miss Ivy, "speaking of which, how are you doing?" I sighed. Miss Ivy looked rested and relaxed, though, like her month off did her really well. I'm doing okay, I said. I'm just confused about Lowell, we had another eight minutes we were supposed to do before the finish. And when I went to talk to him after the match he had apparently already left the building. "Yeah, I went backstage almost right away after he did that," said Miss Ivy, "I don't understand it either, but he's been confused for a while and--" No, I said. "What?" I hesitated. That may be the first time, ever, that I've interrupted Miss Ivy. I said no, I said. Try to say that three times fast. I saw his face afterwards, I said, when I was coming around and he was going up the aisle. He knew exactly what he was doing. I just don't know why. Maybe I shouldn't have said that. Miss Ivy leaned against the wall and folded her arms across her chest. She looked really upset. I don't know why he did it either, I said, and maybe it was a mistake, but trust me, Miss Ivy - Lowell knew what he was doing there. "I need to find him," said Miss Ivy, "but that's gonna have to wait. Are you gonna be okay tomorrow?" Tomorrow? The Meltdown match itself. Yeah, I said, I'm fine. "Good," said Miss Ivy, "because I need you to do something for me. Get to the arena about an hour early and go here," and she handed me a folded piece of paper, "there's a few others I need to talk to tonight as well, but I need you to keep this to yourself. Don't discuss this with anyone at all. Can you do that?" I can do that, I said. "Good," said Miss Ivy. She hugged me, suddenly. "I'm proud'a you, kiddo," said Miss Ivy, "Winning and losing are immaterial in the long run. Whatever's going on with Lowell, you guys will settle up somewhere else. I'm sure of it." Miss Ivy let me go, and thumped her fist against my chest. "Keep it up, kiddo," she said. I stood there for a second and watched her walk toward a larger table with more of the roster sitting at it. She leaned in inbetween Alias and Michelle Masters, but with the rest of the noise in the place I couldn't hear a word she was saying. And I was still pretty peeved about what happened tonight, but I was optimistic, too. Miss Ivy's right, it's just one match. And, again - Miss Ivy's back. This is gonna be good.
