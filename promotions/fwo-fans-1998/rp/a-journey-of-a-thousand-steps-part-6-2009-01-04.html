A journey of a thousand steps, Part 6

4 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Walking down a nice street in Philadelphia is a man who appears anything but. Among the pristine office buildings paneled in glass and the men and women in suits speaking on their cell phones about business dealings and God knows what else is a hulking figure dressed in well worn blue jeans and a gray hooded sweatshirt, the hood pulled low over his eyes. A scraggly beard juts out, the kind of unkempt facial hair seen on World of Warcraft players everywhere. Yet, this man doesn't seem like the computer game type. Around four inches taller than most of the well-to-do types around him and built like the most intimidating of bouncers or possibly a linebacker. You can make out under the hood a stern visage that betrays a man who has been to a personal hell beyond his parents' basement. He turns into an office building and walks right past the front desk to the elevators. They know him here, though they wish they didn't. He couldn't care less. Truth be told, he doesn't care about much at all these days. Mostly he tries to remember the good times, when he could just do things without people taking care of him. When he was free of being a burden to those around him. And especially, when he had arenas full of thousands of people chanting his name. Those days seem long gone now, though when his psychiatrist calls *him*, it could be interpreted as a good sign. That's what his wife said, at least. Thank God for her, he thinks. What other reason to keep going if it wasn't to someday make it up to her for causing so much pain. Why else come to this appointment. Why... His thoughts trail off as he reaches the office of Dr. Jacob Talbert and opens the door. The always chipper Rachel is at her desk as usual. He had wondered whether she put on an act for patients, or if she was as saintly as she appeared. He supposed he'd never know. No one leaving or near the office. Typical, everyone's afraid he'll just go nuts again and maybe hurt somebody. Probably right, too. Rachel's face lights up as she sees him walk in. “Hello Mr. Baer! Please go right in.” Another appointment. Nothing will change. After all this time, what could they fix? He walks through the waiting room and opens the door to the office he had been in hundreds of times, and yet this time, something is different. Dr. Talbert rises from his seat and walks to the door to shake his hand. “Mike, how is everything today? How do you feel?” He doesn't hear the words of Dr. Talbert. At least, he makes no indication that he did. Instead, he stares at the other man in the office, dressed in a charcoal suit, who had risen with Dr. Talbert and is standing about 5 feet away. What was his problem? Had he done something else horrible and forgotten about it? Was he under arrest again? Why couldn't he remember? Only one way to find out. “Who's the suit?” His blunt response seems to trouble Dr. Talbert, but he recovers quickly. The other man steps forward and offers his hand as he is introduced. “This is an old friend of mine, Dr. Kelvin Regan. We were in university together, and I brought him in to help us help you. I think he's got some good ideas.” “Pleased to meet you Mike.” While the other two are speaking, he just stares at the hand being held out by the man he had never met. Just like usual, things just happen without him thinking. “So after all this time, you can't fix what's wrong with me. And you bring in some guy who I've never met and he's going to wave a magic wand? So you just wasted years of my life? Is that it?” “Mike, I...” His snaps his head up to look at Dr. Talbert. The look that never fails to frighten his doctor. Talbert steps back ever so slightly, and speaks slowly, though you can hear a slight tremble. “Mike, we need to try something different. Dr. Regan is a good doctor who I've known for years and I think his outside perspective might help us...” “No! I'm just starting all over again with this jerkoff who can't help me either! I'm 33 fucking years old and I'm going to spend the rest of my God damn life as a mental case because I wasted 4 years seeing a fucking shrink who can't do his God damn job! You want to help me so much!? Why don't you just fucking kill me and get it over with!” He storms out of the office and slams the door behind him. Without turning around or breaking stride, his last words are for Dr. Talbert's assistant. “Goodbye, Rachel.” With that grim farewell, he throws open the door to the hallway and walks out. Back in the office, Jacob and Kelvin are a little shaken, but otherwise ok. Jacob takes out his cell phone and makes a call. “Yes, it's Jacob. No, not well. He's coming down, I imagine. I hope to see you soon.” Kelvin looks at his friend quizzically. “What was that about?” “What? The call? That was to his wife.” “What is that going to do? I've read the file, who knows where he could be by the time he gets here?” “Kelvin, you say you've read the file and you don't think I have a plan for when he storms out of the office? Those are some of the good days. Brittany is outside the building. I used to use bouncer types to subdue him, but, well, let's just say their health care costs were a bit too large of a burden. High turnover, too.” “You're not saying...” “However, he has not once ever struck his wife. So, she gets to help out with Mike's recovery effort and I pay her a small stipend for the trouble, though she never takes much more than transportation costs.” “Sounds like a good arrangement. Now what?” “Now we go over the treatment plan one more time, and then we wait.”
