Busy Busy Busy

30 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Dear Ms. McGuiness, Please allow me to introduce myself. My name is Siobahn McPhineas and I am the manager of FWO wrestler Mr. Fairview Reed. This is my initial endeavor into the world of professional sports entertainment and I find myself severely lacking in basic knowledge of the wrestling world in general. Therefore I’d like to invite you to an informal gathering in the hopes that I might glean some helpful hints to point me in the right direction. I’ve made reservations at McP’s Irish Pub & Grill on Coronado Island the night of July the 7th. I imagine that we’ll all be in the San Diego area for the show already, and after time spent on the road we’ll all be ready for a night out. You needn’t respond, but I do hope to see you there and look forward to making your acquaintance. Most Sincerely, Siobahn P. McPhineas She blew a stray strand of luxurious auburn hair from her eye and put the letter in the envelope. Siobahn COULD have tried to phone everyone on her list or sent on mass email to everyone but she was old-fashioned enough to believe in the personal touch. She added the sealed envelope to the growing stack on her desk and reached for another sheet of stationary. Siobahn paused to look around the room, the room in the huge rented house that she’d been using as an office for the better part of six months. Once it had really felt like an office; now it was a cluttered mess of half-packed boxes and stacks of random papers. She rolled her eyes and sighed at the thought of all she still had left to do in the next few days. Her keen mind was working overtime trying to keep everything in order and keep track of all the minutiae of the move… And it wasn’t just a move; it was a return to life on the road, a life she had thought behind them. The last half-year had only proven to be a brief respite though, and the Fans’ Wrestling Organization was about to be on tour once more. And as the FWO went so did Siobahn’s entourage. Burlington Werner had informed her that their semi truck was in top shape and sitting patiently on the storage lot, waiting to be loaded with Mr. Reed’s precious plants, experiments, books, etc. Mr. Yip was complaining about the lack of cooperation he was receiving from the FWO’s front office; trying to get a tour schedule was proving near impossible. Mr. Reed was (as ever) holed up in the library, only occasionally showing his face ask something of utmost importance (to him) or to oversee the handling of some of his more delicate equipment. And The Assistant was simply plodding along like a tireless workhorse, packing and carrying and lifting and generally making ready for their impending departure. Siobahn was going to be greatly relieved when this was all over and they actually got on the road. And at least they’d had some practice in living out of hotel rooms and their RV. And at least they hadn’t had to look over their collective shoulders these last six months. America had indeed proven to be a haven from The Hunting Party. The Assistant appeared in the doorway with a plate laden with a sandwich in one hand and a cup of steaming tea in the other. Her long blonde hair was pulled back in a tail and a smudge of dirt marred one perfect cheek. She smiled at Siobahn as she delivered the unexpected lunch. “You, m’dear friend, are a godsend!” Siobahn hadn’t realized how hungry she was until she saw the food. The Assistant’s smile grew wider as she delicately placed the plate and cup on the messy desk. One giant hand gave Siobahn’s small shoulder a quick squeeze of support. “An’ just t’be sure ye remember, we’ll be venturing out t’morrow night, taking a trip up t’ Tiger Mountain.” One more thing that needed to be done before leaving town, perhaps the MOST important thing. The Assistant nodded and gave a thumbs up as she left the room. Siobahn took one quick bite and one quick sip – she still had so very much to do… She sighed and picked up her pen once more.
