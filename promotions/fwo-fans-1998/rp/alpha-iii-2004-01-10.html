Alpha III

10 January 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Jack Harmen was able to do more than his father ever could. Do more than his brother, do more than he ever imagined. But everyday Jack looks back at the decisions he made, and wonders if they were the right ones. We all do that. Right? Then again, some things don't come by decisions we make. They just... do. ** Jack walked down a small city block, head hung low as the sun could barely be felt. Covered up in a large parka styled jacket, camouflaged in style to highlight the rise of G.I. Joe, he walked, kicking the occasional pebble and stone. He wrapped himself up, shivering quite a bit with the chill lowering the temperature to single digits. Not even noticing that his final kick of a stone had richoeted off the ground, and clanked into the back end of a Chrysler. Jack immediately flinched, and rushed over to the spot of the damage that he caused. Yup. A cracked plastic cover and a busted taillight. Easily replaceable, but at the young age of twelve, even the slightest sign of damage ruffled him up. A creaking sound. But just as that sound hit, like a wave, Jack tunneled his vision toward the busted taillight and just sort of starred at it for a moment. A moment became a minute. And a minute became... ...her ripping him away from the car, scolding him like the son she had. Pointing her fingers and screaming at the top of her lungs, this woman was a bit hefty, and held a rolling pin in her hand, waving it about toward traffic as if she was a police officer. "In my day, when someone would do something wrong, they would be beaten with a paddle. Do you wish that?" She asked, grabbing the young boy by the shoulders and looking him into the eyes. Tears started to well up in the corners, but it just created a glare from the temperatureless rays of the sun. The woman, who had been doing nothing but screaming the majority of this time, finally lowered her guard for a second, motioning the boy into her home. "Why don't you come in for some hot chocolate son, while I call your parents?" Jack sniffled at the mere mention of his parents, but followed the woman into her home. Or would have, if he didn't stop to take a look at the surroundings. It wasn't anything luxurious. In fact, using the word luxurious near describing this world is practically criminal. A crummy row house, connected to numerous other homes of the same down a line. That's why they're called row houses, or town houses. Because an entire town could be fit into them, herded like sheep and left out in the pasture to fend for themselves. "My mother," Jack called out, stopping the woman's stride.
