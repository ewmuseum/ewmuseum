The Business - Soft Suicide

10 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The world is filled with people slowly committing suicide. How else could there be a world with the business that I compete in. The pills, the drunks, the driving to shows, the flights. Drunk driving, fighting on a plane after taking enough pain medication to OD on. It's just soft suicide. I'm not saying that I'm saddened by this, or that I'm... what's the word? Ashamed of my job. But what's true of the matter is that we go out there, and we're killing ourselves for the enjoyment of others. Suicide by tiny increments. I remember a time when I wanted to slit my own wrists just to make the pain stop. And now, I wouldn't dare let myself get near a razor, for fear of not having that pain in my life. The world is filled with people who are emotionally stunted, feeling the burden of adulthood. They feel used, beaten down, destroyed, worn out. They feel like we are. Imagine everyday, getting hit over the head with a two by four. You'd think, after a while, you'd get used to it. And you sort of do, but the human body was never meant to take this punishment. We aren't vampires, we aren't immortal. I wouldn't want to be immortal anyway. To live forever but have no ties to the world. It wouldn't truly be living. If I had all the time in the world. Time would become meaningless. And life would become meaningless. So, I'm sitting here, wondering why I'm thinking about life and it's meaning, bathing in the sun of Hawaii. Why the flew me out to this island for a simple segment with Scotty Jay I'll never know, but I do know one thing. I'm not complaining. True, I can only window shop, but hell, it's as if I'm in the Mall of freakin' America. I may not be able to buy anything, I may not find what I'm looking for, but I'm enjoying myself to the best of my abilities. But I won't be staying here for long. I have to fly back on Saturday for a show in New York, inside the Elks Lodge. I've never wrestled there, and the building is being torn down at the end of the month. It's a shame, for it is truly a wrestling landmark. A historical monument that society has shunned. I get to wrestle in the ring Shawn Michaels and Marty Jannety tore the house down in 1993. I get to wrestle in the same building Shane Douglas announced his retirement to the ECW faithful, and was met with the famous "Please don't go," chant. I get to wrestle in the place where Sabu jumped off the Raw sign... But most importantly, I get to see my family.
