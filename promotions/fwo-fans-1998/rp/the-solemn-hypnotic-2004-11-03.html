The Solemn Hypnotic

3 November 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
She woke up first. She always woke up first, though he never knew it. Every morning, every time 7 AM glowed bright red, she walked to the curious all-night Chinese convenience store. Every morning, every time, she'd buy two tear-away lottery tickets and, on occasion, one might even pay for the other. Next stop was the McDonald's for the same thing she ordered every morning at 7:30 - hashbrown, apple juice and no, I don't need any ketchup. Half the time, she didn't even bother with the little cake of potato bits. Either cold at the center, greasy or generally uninteresting, something could almost always convince her away from it even though nothing - not even her finicky eating - could pull her from spending those few cents on worthless fried vegetable every morning, every time, all the time. Every morning, every time she'd walk casually back to the steps of the apartment with a little grin, pondering why this ritual even existed. Her life in general held zero organization, her tattoos went without a theme...not even her eye color matched. Why, then, did this happen every morning? Walking up the steps and past the old woman who gathered her morning paper every morning, every time AT that time, she'd astound herself; Returning with nothing but the clothes on her back, just like every morning, she could swiftly step out of her coat and back onto the mattress that laid on their tarnished floorboards. She'd slip under the covers and watch him for a moment, but the bare windows that bore no shield from the stark white light of morning would always keep her from any sleep that might linger from seeing that silently sweet expression in his tired eyes when he woke up every morning, every time... 'We'll be ninety before there's any room for routine.' Amy tossed her pencil away from the notebook as that thought crossed her. She was deeply invested in one of the few slots of free time that snuck their way into a regular working week. Things were boring without Brandon, who was taking the kids to the park while his wife laid in bed with her arch nemesis...that, of course, being the common cold. She sniffled and looked back to her random story, then crumpled the paper and tossed that, too, across the dimly lit room. Amy was NOT cut out for this cold/flu crap. Four children quickly made that boredom a memory. Aimz hadn't heard the door open, but took notice when Sean - the elder of Brandon's sons - came into the picture as he hopped up onto the queen-sized mattress and began dictating their trip to the redhead, making sure to note every wrong move his younger siblings had made with the grin of a wiseman. "...But we came home 'cause we was hungry and kin'a missed you." He finished just as 'Dad' poked his head in the door. "Sean, can you take the others and go get cleaned up for dinner?" The little boy replied with further glee, though he casted Amy a 'Do I have to?' glance. He reveled in being the oldest - the most responsible - of the four. When Amy nodded, he didn't hesitate a moment to scurry around his father and out the door, easily heard shouting for the smaller boys - Jarod and Stephen - to follow as he guided Amy's only daughter, Scerinn, by holding gently onto her chubby toddler hand. When he heard the sink running in the bathroom across the hall and Sean handing out orders in his very big brother manner, Brandon grinned and knelt beside the bed. He used a huge hand with unusual grace following to brush the hair off of Amy's forehead and got met with a small cough. "Are we any better?" he inquired, "Still seem to have a bit of a fever." Amy groaned and rolled to her side, blurting a confession quite frankly, "I'm dying." Brandon chuckled, "You can throw up a thousand times...but catch a case of overblown sniffles, and you're on your deathbed." "Hey, I have athsma! That's no laughing matter!" she defended. "It is when your mom and doctor admit that you grew out of it by fifth grade." "They can't tell for sure, y'know." He smiled and kissed her lightly, "Either way, I seriously doubt you're dying. Otherwise, I wouldn't risk catching this deadly virus." Amy pouted and rolled to her other side with a huff, sniffle and an overplayed coughing fit. ...And to that came Brandon's incredibly sympathetic reply, "What did you get for dinner anyway?" After dodging a thrown pillow, he rolled his little redhead over. "Beats me," she shrugged, "I ordered the special at that Chinese shack down the block. Should be here any minute, whatever it is..." "So you are trying to poison me!" winked Brandon, "And by the way, we've got a radio promo tomorrow morning at KTLA, the address was on a voicemail you never checked. I know you're sick, but we've got to make this one...it's going to start looking pretty terrible - not to mention stirring up rumors - if we don't get our butts in gear and actually make these appearances together more often." She knew he'd say that, and had already checked that message...just magically 'forgot' to mention her obligation to accompany him. Brandon knew that as well as she did, of course. However, Amy had fully prepared a protest and let out a final hack before beginning... "Bu---" "Guuuuuuys!" Sean rolled his eyes and smacked his own forehead upon stamping into the room to find his dad stroking his step-mother's forehead, "No gross love stuff. We all got clean to eat...let's goooo!!!" Urgh. No room for protest. "Start without me," replied Amy, turning to Brandon, "I'll be out in a sec...just gonna call Claire and see how she's doing with that new job. Plus, my brother called about seeing him in Boston next week and I said I couldn't talk. Oh yeah, and I think that delivery kid has a thing for me, but I can't really understand anything he says...so don't leave him a tip, just in case he's getting the wrong idea." With a wink and nod, Brandon accompanied the toddlers out of the stuffy bedroom as his wife picked up the phone, pressing to speed-dial and biting her lip. 'I am Claire and I hate schlong, I once was here but now I'm gone. Leave a message at the tone and I'll hit you up if I come home.' "Hey, Claire...me again. Sick as shit, seeing how you're doing. Call me back or whatever." Nothing out of the ordinary. Nothing at all.
