The red-headed Amazon sat down on the edge of her bed, her housecoat opening just enough to reveal the curve of a breast and the ridge of a muscular thigh. She lit another cigarette and tossed the lighter onto the table before dialing the number she'd been waiting to call. The shower hadn't relaxed her at all and, as she flipped her long dripping locker over her shoulder, she doubted this call would either. "Hi, you have reached Michael Hunter. Leave your name and number and I'll get back to you." Yeah right. "Hunter, it's me. I'm on a land-line. Pull your hand out of your pants and pick up the phone." There was a moment of feedback as someone on the other end answered the phone. "I was expecting to hear from you," Michael Hunter said. "Have you been watching TV lately?" she snapped. "Your boy is advertising his presence on prime time." "He what? What are you talking about?" She saw right through his act. "So you did know. Professional Wrestling? That was the best you could do?" "They thought it'd be a good testing ground for him. I haven't seen him disappoint yet. He is functioning in society, and his profile has remained intact." "Obviously there's a problem. Otherwise I wouldn't have been called in. You know I've got a history with that prick." She flicked the ash from her cigarette into the palm of her hand while she cradled the receiver between her ear and shoulder. Filthy habit, but the life expectancy in her line of work wasn't long enough to worry about lung cancer. "I've just learned that our boy has been involved in some extra-curricular activities." Hunter paused. "Nothing serious yet though." "Extra-curricular... for Fuck's sake Michael!" She rubbed her forehead with the back of her hand. "What has your pet monster been doing? Did he kill someone?" "Reports are sketchy, but it appears he has been involved in a few homicides." "A few? How much is a few?" "The estimate is currently at seventeen people. The information we're getting is months old, so we don't have all the facts yet." "How does someone kill seventeen people and not make the news?" He'd been busy since the last time she'd seen him. Deep down, seventeen seemed like a conservative estimate to her. "It was a small criminal cartel based out of Los Angeles. Once the primary was identified, the incident was covered up." The primary. How rich. They still talked about him with a cool detachment, like they had nothing to do with putting a man who should be on death row, back on the street.
