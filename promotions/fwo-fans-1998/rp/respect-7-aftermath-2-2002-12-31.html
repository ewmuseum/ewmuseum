Respect (7 - Aftermath 2)

31 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Who knew victory would hurt this badly?" As I wearily dropped to the padded chair in my locker room, I was reminded of the infamous ancient general Phyrrus, who defeated the Romans at Heraclea but still suffered such losses that he was immortalized by the term phyrric. While it was true that I had come out of the match with my first victory over Sean Stevens, had the costs been too great? I was barely able to move my arms, and the severe pain that greeted even the most minor movements sent waves of dizziness crashing over me. Surely there had to have been some other way? Better not to think about that now. I needed to enjoy my victory, but I couldn't forget that I had a final match with Stevens tomorrow, one that I badly wanted to win and still had no stipulation for. On the other hand, I couldn't focus too much on the damage I had sustained during this match, or I'd be at an emotional low going into the grand finale. Suddenly, the thoughts of Phyrrus and tomorrow's match were washed from my head by something that Eli had said just yesterday, when I was telling him of my plans for "the loop", as he liked to call it. "After it's all over and we're all BUDDIES... if you're good we'll tell ya about the Respect match." Eli had been trying to keep me in relatively good spirits so that I wouldn't go into the match too determined to kill Stevens, but he had ignited the flame of an idea, just the same. If I had taken the context correctly, then perhaps it had taken a match for Eli to earn Sean's respect, so why wouldn't the same thing work for me? With the idea firmly planted in my head, I quickly rose, hoping to catch Silver Fox in a rare moment of relative peace, something that was hard to come by on nights as hectic and important as this. However, all thoughts of the match and Silver Fox quickly left my head, as I found myself dizzy. Falling forward, I tried to catch myself before I hit the floor, but my weakened arms simply collapsed beneath me, leaving me to fall into the warm embrace of unconsciousness.
