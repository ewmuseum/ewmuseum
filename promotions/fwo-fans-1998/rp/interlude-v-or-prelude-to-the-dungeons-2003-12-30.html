Interlude V, or 'Prelude to the Dungeons'

30 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
In the ring, he was the Codemaster. The Blackest Brother in the Hyrule Kingdom. He wielded a weapon forged in the hellfires of Death Mountain. He wielded a book born out of the eternal darkness of the Ancient, Mantorok. He unintentionally happened to own a sword shaped out of a key. But outside of the ring, the Codemaster traded in his robes for a sweater and some jeans. The Codemaster traded in his Megaton Hammer for his car keys, and his Tome of Eternal Darkness for a cheapass Pontiac. Actually, he didn't really trade any of that stuff, since he owned everything anyway. But it's the principle of the matter, and the principle was that the Codemaster was only the Codemaster whenever he had to be the Codemaster. Outside of the ring, he was simply Allen Brown (or Allen Speck as most people called him), the youngest of three brothers in the Brown family of Salt Lake City, Utah. He, like his brothers, was a second generation wrestler, as their father, Curtis Brown, wrestled predominantly in the western United States. Allen's brothers, Jordan and Robert B., weren't much of a success, as the fact that they were somewhat uncharasmatic twins rather sealed their fate as "the twins team that always switched". So, it was Allen who ended up with success, working the indies for five years until the fans Wrestling organization called him up, and he became the Codemaster thanks to Allen's primary passion of video games. So, anyway, the Codemaster was on leave after Unleashed: Road Rage. Why? He just was. Don't question it, he's a fickle brother. So, he planned to work at his other place of business until the fWo needed his mighty drawing power and amusement value to save the fWo from its slump. Because whenever the fWo was in a slump, it needed a new design, and that was definitely Codemaster territory. Now, Allen's "other" place of business wasn't anything illegal or immoral or anything like that. No, the Codemaster just happened to have owned a business of unique design. It was on the outskirts of Salt Lake City. It was a small building, built out of stone, brick, and other materials. It was shaped like a castle, and with four castle turrets on the corners of the building. Now, what's important about this tiny little castle outside of Salt Lake City was the fact that the owner of the building was one Allen Brown. It wasn't just his place of residence, on the second floor of the building. It was also his place of business. You see, the first floor of the building consisted of one of the largest video game stores in the state of Utah, and it belonged to Allen. It had been in business for seven years, and started by Allen and his brothers. However, his brothers soon left for the glamour of the wrestling ring, leaving their charasmatic and optimistic brother to own the store. He eventually became the sole owner. So, eventually, after two years and after his brothers faltered in their careers, Allen hired his cousin and best friend to mind the store and went to Phoenix to get trained by someone other than his father. The store, known as "the Dungeons of Hyrule", eventually was moved to its new castle building after enough money was amassed to do so. The store, still in Allen's ownership, was occasionally minded by Allen whenever he happened to be in town either via a tour, or via a break from the action of wrestling. It was here that Allen Brown would stay, for four months, until he was asked to come back for Mob Rules. It was here that the next chapter of the Codemaster's ever-strange tale would be written.
