Noel, We Hardly Knew Ye

1 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Lowell Graham, number 419." At the front desk, a heavyset man nodded, then disappeared into the back from behind a closed door. He had been having computer problems for weeks now. It crashed several times while he was typing up the fourth, and might he add brilliant, installment of his Internet column. He wasn't happy. Lowell rested his elbows on the counter, humming a show tune to a Broadway play he had seen live on HBO. The man returned, carrying the black, Del laptop in his arms. He sat it down on the counter, then calmly shoved it off into a waist receptacle that was positioned below. "What the HELL?" He uttered in a stern voice, and blinked his watery eyes in surprise. That was Noel. A beautiful machine with well over fifty-two gigs of memory on her hard-drive, as well as the best sound card money could buy. Estimated worth: $4,000.00. In his eyes at least. So many great memories... Discovering the wonders of hacking expensive pornography sites. The laughs they shared as he used fake screen names to verbally attack members of the Colorado Avalanche. They were now sitting on a mound of trash; pop cans and banana peelings littering her magnificent exterior. Pepsi- he was the spokesperson for said company. He couldn't, without losing a truckload of green, pick it up and hurl it to the floor. It would need to sit there, soaking in wet garbage until her owner figured out what this all was about. Hesitantly, Lowell reached across the counter and grasped the cashier by the scruff of his uniform; gritting his teeth and demanding answer. "Why did you kill my Noel! Tell me, or I'll..." He hadn't exactly thought this through. He collected a single pen from a black and yellow plastic-holder that stood next to the cash register, then bit off the top and spat it out to the floor. If he didn't talk- an accident, a very messy accident might just happen all over his clean new shirt. He sneered, half-squinting, tilting the broken end of the pen dangerously close to his collar. "Sir, it was broken. Nothin' we could do. Sorry." His words were like daggers in his heart, or perhaps his crotch- maybe both. It did hurt quite a bit. He had lost a loved one. His greatest loved one. "You lie?" "No." His hand went numb. The pen slipped though his fingers and bounced on the tile floor, leaking its blue contents out onto it. "This can not be. She was always so healthy." He was trembling, slowly slipping down the side of the counter with his back to it... clutching his stomach; it hurt so much. "There's no way to revive little Noel? I'll pay anything!" "Sorry. No," grunted the PC Medic employee, before he turned and left to take his standard daily lunch break. * When he returned home, sluggishly moving through the door and out into the living room, where he would collapse in a heap of self pity, Darren sat at his desk, making important visual changes to the Lowell.com web-site lay-out. Somehow, through the audible clicking sounds of his constant typing, he could hear his boss's child-like whimpers. He didn't care if he was sinking into a pit of depression. Go ahead- cry. But it was becoming a distraction. He couldn't think... and without being aware of it, he had changed the font to hot pink. Soon the homosexuals would flood the forums with disturbing sex tips and, dare he even think it, stories of wild orgies. Darren shuddered. He took his hands off the keys, stood up and walked into the living room. Lowell's face was buried in a pillow and he was blowing his nose. He cringed, then carefully tapped him on the shoulder. "What?" The Human Advertising Machine had calmly rolled over and risen both eye brows up, like to say: Is there a problem? Darren politely smiled and replied, "Dustin," that was Lowell's birth name, few know it... and even fewer have permission to speak it, "are you okay?" With an unpleasant metallic taste in his mouth, so dry that it hurt to speak, the blue-haired sell-out, with his pride apparently not harmed at all, replied, "Well... Yeah? Why wouldn't I be? I'm Lowell. Are you saying that I am so lonely that it hurts to think? IS THAT WHAT YOU'RE SAYING?" Darren backed off. "No." "Well it is! I need my precious Noel back! She completed me...
