The Lost Week: Nauseous

24 December 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
San Diego, California What the hell happened? One minute, he'd had the FTB. Then he got chaired in the head. One minute, he'd hit the POD. The next minute, the count was so slow the evil luchador refs would've looked on in envy. One minute, he was going for the Black Sabbath, the next his groggy eyes saw Chronos being moved out of the way. What the hell happened? Where the FUCK had Universal been? He couldn't even look at anybody. The walk back up the ramp had been the longest of his life. The cheers did nothing for him. And by the time he'd gotten checked by the trainer and dressed, the particulars were long gone and the Sports Arena was empty. He looked east towards the sky. As he shivered internally, he saw home. ******************************************* Puerto Nuevo, Mexico He'd never been good at facing people after this. He didn't think of the family or friends that'd shown, he just felt a pressing need to get away or else. In and out of his apartment in a flash, he'd packed two suitcases and killed the lights. Twenty-five minutes later he was at the border, waiting. An hour after that, he'd gotten to a hotel and checked in for the night. But people around him were having boisterous parties and he didn't fall asleep until well after four a.m. *************************** He'd gone to a store and bought vodka, tequila, and a few other assorted goodies. He walked back to the hotel and drank the vodka. When that got boring, he drank the tequila. And when that got boring, he drank the both of them together. His head. Lord, his head. It felt like someone was using a steamroller with exacting speed to flatten a grape. Flashes shot on the sides of his brain, fraying his optic nerves. Dissapointment? Anger? Depression? Desperation? Slowly he pulled himself up on the sofabed and disengaged his legs from the table. Grumbling, he pulled out a piece of paper from his wallet and got a hotel pen off the counter. On the paper he wrote three names. One was Ric Chronos. Another was Austin Davis. He made it most of the way through writing Chris Universal before his body resisted or his concussion reflared or his B.A.C. didn't allow him a rest of the night. The Pacific Ocean waves pounded against the shore.
