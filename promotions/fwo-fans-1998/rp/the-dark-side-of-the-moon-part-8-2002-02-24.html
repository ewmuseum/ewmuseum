THE DARK SIDE OF THE MOON Part 8 - Brain Damage And if the cloud bursts, thunder in your ear you shout and no one seems to hear and if the band you're in starts playing different tunes I'll see you on the dark side of the moon 1:15 AM Poison Ivy is the most impatient woman in a twelve mile radius. Bar none. She's been pacing the waiting room at Mercy Hospital for.... fuck it, for too damn long. Her mother is in intensive care, and out of respect for the other patients she's only allowed one visitor at a time. Right now, Ivy's big brother Brian is in there with her. Earlier tonight, Brian and Ivy had it out. There had been tension building up between them for a year now, ever since their father died. Brian finally vented all of his feelings of frustration toward her, over how his life was lived under a magnifying glass by their parents and she got away with murder. They reconciled and hugged.... just in time to hear a message on the answering machine telling them to come to the hospital. Ivy has a cigarette in her mouth - the first one she's had in almost a year. Even with the ciggie calming her nerves, her hands are shaking. Twice now, she's tried to sit down, and both times she couldn't sit still. "Ivy." She spins around to see Brian standing there with a somber look on his face. Ivy dropped her cigarette on the floor and walked toward him. "How's Mom?" "She's stable again," replied Brian, "but they don't think she'll make it through the night." Ivy backpedals a step or two, and starts to cry again. Almost as quickly, she regains control of her emotions and holds her head up high. "She still taking visitors?" "Yep. Go see her, Ivy." Ivy opens the door to Intensive Care, and feels another tap on her shoulder - it's her brother again, and he has an envelope in his hands. "Mom was lucid for a few hours today, apparently - and she wrote us both letters. Here's yours." She takes the letter, but it's almost an afterthought. Ivy negotiates her way through the mini- maze of ICU and finds her mother's bed. Lily McGinnis is unconscious, with tubes in her nose, the IV buddy in her arm, and a ton of machines around her body. Ivy sits down next to her and takes her mother's hand in hers. "Hi, Mom.... it's me. Goddamnitt, I can't stand seeing you like this. Fuck.... I don't know what I'm supposed to say here." She kisses her mother's hand. "Brian and I fixed everything, we're done fighting I think. I know how much you hated how bad things had gotten.... neither one of us really knew what was wrong for so long.... but that's over with." "I'm scared, Mom.... The only thing that's gotten me through the past two years has been the fact that you were always there for me. You always gave me good advice, whether it was going back to wrestling, whether it was how I should handle Hornet and Teri.... when I was out of my mind after that night with Sean, you calmed me down and pretty much told me exactly what I needed to hear." Ivy's train of thought is broken as she feels her mother's grip tighten in her hand. She looks at her face, and sees her eyes open just a crack. "Ivy...." whispered Lily McGinnis, "Ivy...." "I'm here, Mom," replied Ivy, taking her mother's hands in both of hers, "I'm right here." "Take...." Lily's voice was strained, but still distinct, still with that air of strength behind it, "Take care of your brother...." "I will, Mom," replied Ivy, "You just save your strength." Lily McGinnis closed her eyes, a smile forming on her face. She looks like she's got some kind of weight off her shoulders, but nobody knows for sure. Ivy smiles and kisses her hand, oblivious to the bustle around her. A nurse pulls her away, Ivy fighting her for a moment before the odd sound hits her mind. It's a high- pitched squeal, and she can't place it until it's too late. Flatline. With a brain tumor, after spending a week mostly unconscious, the medical staff knew there was nothing much they could do except cover her still form. Brian reemerged from the waiting room - there's no reason to keep him out now - and takes the still- stunned Ivy away from the room. She doesn't even realize that there's a sealed envelope in her hand, written in her mother's fancy script. She won't realize it until she gets home. To Be Concluded
