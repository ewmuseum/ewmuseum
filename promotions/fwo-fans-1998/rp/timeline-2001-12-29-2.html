12/28/2001 10:00PM "O'Leary's Pub, please." "Sure thing buddy." Lou was a good cab driver. From his 20 plus years of navigating the streets of this city, he knew the quickest route to get to any destination, regardless of the time of day. But the many years on the road had also provided him with a keen sense for reading what was going on with his customers at the moment. He had seen women rush to the cab after a fight with a boyfriend, or a businessman late for an important meeting. This passenger seemed to be weighing quite a bit on his mind... "Girl problems, big guy?" The Strangler looked up to where the taxi driver was smiling at him in the rearview mirror. "Huh?" "I said, are you having girl problems? You look like the weight of the world is on your shoulders. I know when a guy's got girl problems he heads for a bar to hang out with his buddies. It usually gives time for things to blow over or be put into perspective..." "Actually... it's not girl problems" the Strangler replied. "Just meeting up with someone at O'Leary's" "Well your buddy picked a good bar, that place is where there's plenty of action. They've got some good live music there too." Lou smiled a grin that was missing a few teeth. "If I was a bit younger, I might go there myself, but they don't need an old man like me dragging the party down...y'know?" "He's not a friend, really..." the Strangler began and then trailed off. The thought crossed his mind before he pushed it away. "He's more of a business consultant. He's been offering to help out with some of the smaller details of our profession. Our focus on our targets hasn't been as sharp as it used to be..." "What are you a hitman?" Lou asked with a chuckle. "Or maybe a football player? You're built a bit like a linebacker, I'll give you that...but you're not as tall as some of them..." "We wrestle, actually." The Strangler said, glancing out the side window and wondering how it was that taxi drivers were able to get people to open up so easily. Maybe it was the solitude of the cab, shut away from the world as you traveled to your destination, or perhaps it was simply boredom to pass the time... "Really? Ah that's it! I thought you looked a little familiar... you're that uhh...Strangler guy, right? I don't get to watch much of it, but I do once in a while. It's a little too violent for my tastes. You guys take some high risks for injuries there..." "Everyone takes risks... some just do it with their neck on the line on television" the Strangler replied. "After all, a policeman takes risks... a fireman takes risks... a soldier takes risks... even the door to door salesman is taking a risk. That's life." Lou clammed up for a moment, considering the risks his profession had. There were accidents, and the chance of being held up on the late night shifts... which is why he didn't drive very late anymore. After all, his eyes were beginning to go just slightly, and he wasn't comfortable driving at night to begin with. "Actually... Strangler, can I call you that?" "Sure, if you'd like" the Strangler shrugged. "Most people do. It's like Paul Reubens being recognized only as Pee Wee Herman... we've been typecast." "Well, I wanted to let you know there was an accident on the main street. Some kid decided to race the cops, and hit a light pole, taking the whole thing down. Two other cars were hit... kind of a mess downtown. You don't mind if I take the longer way? It'll get you there faster in the long run, trust me." "That sounds fine" the Strangler nodded, looking in his wallet to check that he had enough for the fare. To his dismay, he found that he had no cash at all. Thinking quickly back, he recalled that he had left it in the safe back in the room. A good idea, so long as one remembered where they kept things. Thankfully, he still had his ATM card with him. "Actually sir..." "The name's Lou. I don't have any fancy name, just Lou." He smiled back, extending his hand for the Strangler to shake. "Ok, actually Lou... we're going to need to make a stop, wherever the nearest ATM is along the way." "Oh, sure, sure." Lou nodded in assurance. "There's a few along the way. We'll have you all set by the time you get there. Like I said, trust me."
