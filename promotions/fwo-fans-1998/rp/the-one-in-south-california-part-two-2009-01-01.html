The One In South California - Part Two

1 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Pages 18 to 36 of The One In South California. *** *** *** *** *** *** INT. DINER-LATER The waitress has brought the food for Cassidy, who is still in the bathroom, and she's decided to make small-talk with Dean. WAITRESS So, sweetie... that your sister or...? Dean continues to pay attention to his tea and shows slight amusement at her question. DEAN No. WAITRESS Oh. Oh, no, I'm sorry, I just meant, y'know. The way she acts and the way you act, you're just a little more-- Dean's eye catches hers and he smirks. DEAN Got it. And we're not related. Cassidy returns and stands behind the waitress, hand on her hip. After waiting for a few moments, she makes her presence known. CASSIDY So are you ever going to move your fat ass or are you just gonna stand there and flirt with my husband all day? I can just get all my stuff and move into this booth over here, I'll fill out a form or whatever for the goverrmint. Residence: Booth 4, shitty diner, Californ-i yay. DEAN We're not married, Cassidy. Finally, the waitress has had enough and begins to point her pen at Cassidy in an accusatory manner. WAITRESS Y'know, sweetie, you're really outta'line. You're lucky you've got a man willing to put up with this kind of stuff, don't take that for granted. He's gonna leave you for someone with some manners someday. Cassidy is absolutely taken aback by the fact that this woman decided to stand up for herself. CASSIDY Oh really? Listen, cunt: I don't know who the fuck you think you are, but he's not interested in your stretch-marked tits or that snapped-rubberband cooch of yours, so back the fuck off. I know you're 25 with a 15 year old kid at home, but I don't give a fuck. And if you're expecting a tip for your service today, you can just march that... Ghostbusters Marshmallow Man ass of yours back down to the welfare line right now, because you're not gettin' shit but a split lip, you General Lee moonshine whore. WAITRESS Excuse me?! Dean watches, sipping his tea. Cassidy shoves the waitress's arm, just enough to be antagonistic. In response the much larger waitress shoves the waify girl, who grabs hold of the back of the booth to brace herself and laughs. This really sends the waitress over the edge, and she actually takes a swing at Cassidy, much to the horror of just about everyone in the restaurant, except Dean, who has come to expect this behavior. Just as someone comes to separate them, Cassidy drills a hard kick right into the stomach of the waitress; upon feeling someone grab her, she instinctively reaches down and pulls out her Makarov pistol from around her ankle. With both hands, she holds the pistol expertly, aiming it around in a circular motion so that everyone is be fully aware that she is not to be messed with. Dean takes action at this point, jumping up and picking her up and pulling her toward the door, a seasoned veteran of this sort of situation. Once he has her out the door, he steps back in and throws a twenty dollar bill on the table, while the small group of people in the restaurant look on in horror. Dean shrugs and smirks. DEAN It's uh.. It's like Raekwon says: Shame on a nigga who tries to run game on a nigga. CUT TO: INT. PUBLIC TRANSIT BUS-DAYTIME The BUS DRIVER stares unhappily at the road ahead. He has long, greasy brown hair; the sleeves of his city-issued, uniformed t-shirt are short enough to show off a majority of his poorly-done arm tattoos, and there are certainly a lot of them. His beard is thick and long, covering most of his jaw, making him look like late-era Jim Morrison. He's a man in his early 30s, who's given up on life and takes no greater pleasure than exercising his authority as driver of the bus when he needs to. He's not much of a talker, except when ranting about his low wages. Lowell behaves nonchalantly, attempting to seem inconspicuous, but only succeeding in coming off as awkward. LOWELL So, what's up? Whooot's up? (Clearly amused by himself) Day's goin' alright? 'Notha' day, 'notha' dolla? Lowell's attempt at chummy small talk is met with incredulousness from the bus driver. BUS DRIVER It's goin'. LOWELL I bet you deal with a lot of assholes. The people in this city, they're animals, they'll piss anywhere. It's disgusting. BUS DRIVER I guess. The bus driver glances to the coin receptor, which Lowell sees but plays up like he doesn't. LOWELL Yup. Lowell covertly steps past the bus driver like it's no big thing that he didn't pay the fare. Nuñez remains standing just inside the door on the main level of the bus. BUS DRIVER Hey, hey! Money. Lowell spins around and laughs, again, fakely. LOWELL Money? Hey, how'd you know what they called me in high school? Lowell turns to Nuñez and points, first at him, then at the bus driver. His mouth widening into an even bigger, disgustingly arrogant smile. He smacks himself in the side of the head as if having some great epiphany. LOWELL (CONT'D) No way! No way! Nuñez, y'know who this is? (Snapping his fingers, excitedly) Shawn's cousin! Kid fucked that girl who wore those 70s style jeans. She had the fat ass, remember? Shit. I don't know how I failed to recognize you! BUS DRIVER I ain't that guy. And I don't know no Shawn, neither. Come on, now. Money. Let's get 'er goin', we've got people waitin' here. Lowell throws up his hands and shakes his head in faux disbelief. LOWELL Well, you look just like him! I'll tell him you say hi anyway. I'm sure he'll get a kick outta this. Hey! Maybe sometime when we all have a night off we can get together and play, I dunno... Risk? Hmmm? How's that sound? Like a plan, eh? Like a good fucking time, right? And check it: Nuñez is Mexica, so he can do all the cooking! Nacho platters 'n what have you! And you and I and Shawn can just sit out on a patio and pound back some wine coolers! (Examining him again) It's like you guys were separated at birth! The bus driver taps the coin receptor, prompting Lowell to sigh and dig in his pocket, hauling out a handful of cigarette butts, a condom--out of its package but unused--and 35 cents in mixed change. He tries to jam it all in the coin slot, wedging cigarette butts in the opening. LOWELL (CONT'D) Consider 'the Man' paid. He smiles, moving past the bus driver. The bus driver's hand stops him, mid-stride. BUS DRIVER Hey, hey, hey, not so fast. (Examining Lowell's "payment") `Fuck is this supposed to be? The bus driver picks through the cigarette butts to find a quarter and a dime. BUS DRIVER (CONT'D) Nope. This ain't enough. Lowell isn't quite sure what to do. He turns to Nuñez, who just stares back at him without verbal reply. LOWELL But that's all I got! BUS DRIVER Then you ain't riding this bus. (Motions to door) Off. An unamused Nuñez pushes through Lowell and drops enough change for both of them. They both take their seat on the bus. Finally, the bus begins to move. Several people murmur in the background about the hold-up. NUÑEZ Y'know that was our payphone money, right? Lowell confidently reaches into his pocket, pulling out a black Nokia. Lowell passes the phone off to Nuñez, who looks surprised as he examines the phone. NUÑEZ (CONT'D) I'm half-expectin' candy to fall outta this muh'fucka. Lowell beams with pride. The bus shakes as it goes over a bump. The elderly groan. LOWELL Nope. It's the real deal. I stole it off that kid in line. NUÑEZ The nigga you pushed? Lowell makes his best attempt to suppress laughter. NUÑEZ (CONT'D) Ya sneaky muh'fucka. 'At's good. 'At's real good, nigga. A few moments go by, before Lowell reaches in his other pocket and produces an iPod. LOWELL Swiped this, too. It's old but I figure we can sell it for at least some cash. NUÑEZ 'At ol' thing? `Fetch a buck ninety five down at the pawn shop. CUT TO: INT. DEAN'S MONTE CARLO-AFTERNOON. Dean, lighting a cigarette as he drives, pulls up to a red light. Cassidy sits in the passenger's seat, visibly still angry about the altercation earlier. DEAN You calm yet? You're gonna need to cut that shit out pretty soon. Low pro. Brandishing a firearm isn't good if we're moving weight. Cassidy doesn't even look to him, somewhat irritated with him for intervening. Suddenly, her phone begins to buzz in her pocket and George Carlin's voice can audibly be heard as her ring tone: "You know what no one talks about in public anymore? Pussy farts." She almost frantically reaches into her pocket and pulls out her phone, not recognizing the number. CASSIDY Hello? Oh, hi Lowell! Dean motions for her to hand him the phone. DEAN Where are you? ...Oh, did you? ...Oh, good idea. Causing a disturbance and getting your ass kicked out of a public place. There's a plan. Dean smirks with a glance at Cassidy. DEAN (CONT'D) What? We're halfway upstate. Nah, we're going back home. That guilt shit isn't going to work, Low. Look, I'll call you if anything changes. Well, Cass will. ...What? Fine. Fuck. But these promises better come true, motherfucker. We're talking Mary of Nazareth, white as a wedding dress, still sportin' a hymen purity. Top of the line shit. And Lowell? Don't fuck with me right now. I'd put a brick in your face just to prove that I'm a hardass. He snaps the phone shut and tosses it into Cassidy's lap. DEAN (CONT'D) Store that number, it's Lowell's new phone. FADE OUT. INT. PUBLIC TRANSIT BUS-DAYTIME Lowell scrolls through the stolen iPod's roster of songs and artists, while Nuñez sits, spine curved against the back of the seat, resting his eyes. LOWELL Crap, all crap... Tell me, how can someone have downloaded eight thousand songs, and all of them be shit? Does that make any sense to you? Because it doesn't to me. He's got every Bright Eyes album ever in existence and about a gig's worth of self-recorded pansy-ass emo garbage, but not a lick of hip-hop? No Biggie? No Easy E? No Wu!? Shit, I'd settle for some Lil' Wayne over this trash! Lowell groans disparagingly and shoves the iPod back in his pocket. Amidst the shabby dregs--hookers and seedy individuals peering through sunglasses at underage girls--sits a beautiful young woman--AMY, 22--a UCLA laptop bag on the floor pinned between her feet. She's conservatively well dressed, with auburn-coloured hair tied back in a loose ponytail. Looking at her, Lowell's eyes glint with misdeed, a perverse smile snaking across his mouth. He makes futile attempts to make himself look attractive, fussing with his hair and clothes, and stands up, moving across the aisle and plunking himself down in one of the seats adjacent to her. LOWELL (CONT'D) It smelled like farts over there. Lowell stealthily points to Nuñez, who is busy catching some rest. LOWELL (CONT'D) Mexicans with their spicy foods, you know how it is. Amy is clearly intimidated and creeped out by Lowell. AMY Uh.. he looks black. Maybe a little Puerto Rican? LOWELL From the outside yes, but he's got that firey Mexican temper, always wanting to "box me out". Luckily, I pack a one-hitter-quitter in my right hand here. (Balls his hand into a fist) I haven't used it yet, but if someone were to try and slip something into your drink at, like, a party, or, like, touch your boobs, or whatever, I'd definitely put 'em to sleep! Maybe permanently, I dunno. I don't know exactly how much force is behind this stunning bolo of mine. But what I do know is this: it's waitin'--oh, it's waitin'! It's eager to crack someone on the button! That'd be its Christmas. It'd like that. It'd take that out t'dinner, maybe a movie afterwards-- (Shrugs) Maybe a Richard Greer flick, whatever it wants, whatever it likes. And then home, no pressure. No "how 'bout a handy before ya go inside?" No "y'know what'd be great?" while nosedivin' 'er into its crotch. Nunna' that! Amy's digust is growing by the second, not only caused by Lowell's rude manners, but also his lack of hygiene. AMY Is... Is that a urine stain on your shirt? Lowell grabs the least disgusting part of his shirt, twists it up, and dampens the end with his tongue, using it to try and scrub the pee stain out, which proves difficult, if not impossible. LOWELL This ol' thing?! Noooo! It's a apple juice stain! Nuñez and I went apple picking yesterday! But I can totally see why you'd think otherwise! AMY And you juice the apples yourself? LOWELL Of course! Nuñez and I are always trollin' the apple orchards, lookin' for the beauts as we call 'em! We're picky. Y'see, not just any will do. I mean, shit, just last week we drove--what was it?- three days straight to make it to this big apple convention in... I forget what state, but wherever it was, those apple-pickin' nuts were fuckin' craaaay-zeeeee! We partied like we just tested positive for AIDS! In fact, we're lookin' to break into the AJ biz! It's tough though 'cause industrial-size juicers cost an arm and a leg. Right now Nuñez uses his fat Mexican head to butt the apples from the tree, while I carry him on my shoulders. Then, we gather up the apples, toss 'em in this old tub we found, and jump around on 'em. We wear Lugs. AMY Um, that's sort of weird. Why don't you both just use your hands? That'd be more productive. LOWELL Well, we could, yeah, but where's the fun in that? To me, hands equal commercialism. Back in prehistoric times, apes wanted a mango? BOOM! They headbutted the shit out the mango! They were true bosses! Then, we got soft, civilized. We lost touch with nature. Hell, why do we still drive around in cars? Why not just make planes? Why make cars when we could be zippin' around in personal jets? Hoverboards and stuff. George Jetson did it, why can't we! But not everything is about efficiency, babe, some things things are better left being taken care of through simple means. Lowell gives up trying to scrub the stain out of his shirt, and instead merely attempts to conceal it. LOWELL (CONT'D) So, that's how I got this stain on my shirt. Apple, not pee. Why would I have pee on me? (Nervously laughs) I'm not a savage! I don't wipe my dick off with my shirt after a piss! Give me the benefit of the doubt! I'm a nice guy! I'm normal! Amy doesn't buy that for one second and does her best to avoid eye contact. AMY My stop's coming up. My boyfriend's going to be there. Just so you know. She's no longer intimidated by Lowell, having seen through his facade, but she has grown tired of his bizarre attitude, becoming increasingly agitated by his presence. LOWELL That's cool. I'm sure he's a nice guy. He's probably a closeted homosexual, but that's not to say he's not nice! You should just, y'know, be on the look out for HIV. And meth. I hear the queers love meth. God love 'em. As soon as the bus stops, Amy is gone. Lowell jumps out of his seat and dashes after Amy. He snags his arm on the pole and swings around in comedic fashion, stretching his other arm out as the doors part, allowing Amy to exit, and then clamp shut again. Lowell turns to the bus driver, placing his hand on his shoulder in comraderie. LOWELL (CONT'D) That bitch was somethin' else. Lowell pretends to be upset. LOWELL (CONT'D) I dunno what I'm gonna do without her... (A devilish smile forms on his face) ...Maybe all the L.A. women I want? Perrrhaps. We shall see, bus driver man. (Brief pause) Hey, why are we going so fucking slowly? Don't they teach you how to use the gas pedal at the Bus Driver Academy? My hard-earned tax dollars should be going toward efficiency and helping me get from place to place on time. BUS DRIVER You again? I've had about enough of you, I'm gonna throw your ass off this bus in about five seconds. LOWELL Yeah, well, I could probably walk faster than this shitty bus anyway! Nuñez violently pulls Lowell down into a seat at this point. LOWELL (CONT'D) (To bus driver) Yeah, you're lucky my Mexifriend over here decided he wasn't gonna let me stomp your ass. It would have sucked for Shawn if his cousin died a violent death as a scumbag bus driver! The visibly upset bus driver begins to increase the speed of the bus. LOWELL (CONT'D) (Gleeful) See, Nuñez? I'm a fuckin' people person. A real go-getter. A sucka free fuckin' BOUSE! I make things happen. CUT TO: INT. DEAN'S MONTE CARLO-DUSK. Dean reaches down and uses his hand to survey around the car, stopping near Cassidy's feet. DEAN Check the glove compartment and see if there are any cigarettes in there. Cassidy does as she's told, rooting through the glovebox and under the seat. CASSIDY Nope. Nothin'. DEAN Then we've gotta stop at this gas station coming up. Dean makes a slow turn into the parking lot of a mostly-empty gas station in a barren area, with nothing but a motel nearby. DEAN (CONT'D) I'm gonna leave the car running. Don't get yourself kidnapped. Once Dean has entered the gas station, he examines the cigarettes behind the counter. DEAN (CONT'D) Do you have any Benson and Hedges? The teenage store CLERK looks to him as though he's speaking a different language. CLERK Uh, no. DEAN Lucky Strikes? CLERK (Laughing softly) What is this, like, 1950? DEAN Then what the fuck do you sell? I'm not gonna smoke Winstons and shi-- Suddenly, Cassidy appears next to Dean, who is completely unenthused about seeing her. DEAN (CONT'D) Tell me you turned the car off. A moment of horrible realization for Cassidy. CASSIDY Hm..? The sound of tires screeching permeates the room, and Dean bolts out the front door, followed immediately by Cassidy. Dean's Monte Carlo begins to pull out toward the road. Instinctively, Dean reaches under his shirt and pulls an IMI Baby Eagle pistol out of his waistband, aiming it and blowing one of the back tires clean out, and startling the driver into slamming on the brakes, spinning the car out. Cassidy runs around behind Dean, who slowly marches so calmly toward his car that he might as well be yawning. The CAR THIEF, a young white male bursts out of the car, nervously brandishing a butterfly knife. DEAN Oh, a knife. Really? Okay. CAR THIEF Yeah, that's right. ...You're not gonna shoot me, asshole. DEAN Oh really? Dean raises his gun and points it directly at his forehead. CAR THIEF Fuck you. If you were gon' shoot me, you already would have. Dean lowers his gun to his hip. DEAN You're right. I'm not gonna shoot you. The kid begins to laugh at him, backtracking toward the car. CAR THIEF I knew it, you big pussy. You wouldn't-- Suddenly, several rounds are PUMPED into the young man's back, with blood SPLATTERING all over the car and even splashing onto Dean's clothes. Dean turns and looks to Cassidy, who stands in a shooting position, hopping around and pretending to be a secret agent with her poses, humming the James Bond theme song. DEAN Hey, Bonnie, good going. Now we have a homicide. And god knows what disease in the blood that's all the fuck over the place. Dean turns and looks to the door of the mini-mart and sees the teenaged clerk standing with a conflicted look on his face; he can't decide whether that was the coolest or most horrific thing he's ever seen. His eyes then return to Cassidy. DEAN (CONT'D) And do you ever pay attention to, you know, potential witnesses? Cassidy is still admiring her gun. CASSIDY A great ballplayer never knows who's in the stands. DEAN What the fuck is that supposed to mean? You're lucky this place is the type with fake security cameras. (Turning to clerk) You guys have a bathroom? The clerk points him to the side of the building. Dean steps forward, and kicks the head of the deceased to ensure that he's expired. DEAN (CONT'D) Cass, take whatever he's got, get whatever you need out of the car. Dean then pops the trunk open, grabs Cassidy's duffle bag, tosses it at her feet, then grabs his own and disappears into the bathroom. Cassidy crouches down next to the body and pats him down, pulling out a thick wallet, and finding a small bag of cocaine in his pocket. She sets them atop her duffel bag and then steps into the car, popping open the glovebox again, this time pulling out a Sharpie. Soon, Dean emerges from the bathroom, surprisingly looking far more presentable than one would ever expect under the circumstances; his hair is less puffy and instead looks much longer, having been washed and tucked behind his ears. He's wearing patched houndstooth pants, but now has a long-sleeved brown tee over a white button-down shirt with its collar exposed from beneath. CASSIDY Mmm.. You're all dressed classy, I'm all... mmmmphhhh... (She shivers) Excited from what just happened... (She begins to reach down toward the zipper of his pants) How 'bout-- Dean looks down to her with his bored eyes, all-business. DEAN What'd he have on him? Cassidy pouts momentarily and then hops over toward the deceased. CASSIDY Hmph. Well, he had probably a gram of blow and... DEAN Beautiful. She reaches down and begins to count the money, then stops and hands it over to Dean. CASSIDY Fuck it. This much. Dean flips through the bills, silently counting them in his head, before folding them up. He looks down at the body, which has "I AM A STUPID DICK" scrawled across its face in red marker and then looks down to his right at Cassidy, who is beaming with pride at her work. Dean actually finds this adorable and feels affection and pride toward his little girlfriend. DEAN Get something, wipe down the handles of the car, the bag of coke, the obvious print places. The cops'll write this off as gang shit, they'll be relieved. He steps inside the mini-mart again, with the clerk who clearly wants nothing to do with him. DEAN (CONT'D) Listen. (Extending the wad of money.) I need 5 packs of Marlboro Reds. (He reaches into his pocket and pulls out another small folded wad of money, then tosses it, along with the other, onto the counter.) And for you to stay cool. When the cops show up--and the cops will show up; hell, you might want to cover your own ass and call the cops right now--just tell them that you heard a drug deal go bad. Or a botched robbery, vigilante thing. Tell 'em 2 guys got into it with that kid out there and they were yelling. Tell 'em they were Asian, keep 'em scratching their heads. I don't give a fuck. You can go on back to your mom's house in time to catch the opening theme to tonight's Two and a Half Men. There's $452 here. That can get a lot of girls drunk. The kid cautiously pockets the money and retrieves the cigarettes, his eyes never leaving Dean, who smirks arrogantly back to him. DEAN (CONT'D) Thanks, kid. CLERK Hey--are you guys like.. Murderers? DEAN (Tucking a cigarette into his mouth) Absolutely not, man. CLERK But you've killed people? DEAN Let's not ask questions we both know the answers to. CLERK Who do you kill? Well not who, but, tou and your... girlfriend is she? You didn't seem to care. Like, it doesn't bother you that that's a person out there. DEAN (Completely calm) Listen, we're not responsible for the world missing out on another LeBron James. Or Nikola Tesla. The only thing to consider with that squirmy fucking cancer that we extracted is that he would have killed me without a thought. Or her. Or you, Honor Roll. Or whatever soccer mom was driving next car he needed after he crashed mine, or whatever teenager had a watch he could pawn. Innocent people. Fuck him for playing cops and robbers in someone else's back yard. And for not knowing or caring whose back yard it is. (Lighting the cigarette) You can't lament and mourn everything that happens. Trust me on this one, kid: if you keep on thinking so hard about things, you're never gonna get anywhere in this life. CLERK But why do you do it? DEAN If I didn't, I wouldn't be doing anything. CLERK Yeah, but, I mean, comparatively... DEAN Yeah, well, it may be the devil or the Lord, but everybody's gotta serve somebody. Just as Dean begins to step out the door, the kid speaks up. CLERK Wha-wait. What are you gonna do about the car, leave it? Can't they trace it back to you? Dean smirks and steps out of the shop.
