Burnt Offerings [.03]

27 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I stared into her eyes, those beautiful eyes that held all that was gentle and sweet in my world. Whenever I looked into them, I fell victim to her aura, one that I would not escape. In fact, I was happy to be captured. She carefully read over the contract, her lips slightly moving as she read it to herself. I didn't have the heart to make the decision in the office, so I tucked the document into my pocket and walked out with my head held high. I didn't want them to know that the choice was racking my brain, so I gave them the best bluff I could and ran out with my tail tucked between my legs. Cowardly? Perhaps. Smart? Absolutely. Natalie Dischner, the one true love in my life, sighed and looked up at me. "Interesting." I blinked once. No, twice. "That's it? You've been reading that contract for the past ten minutes, and all you have to say is..." "...interesting." She said, finishing sentence. "There's nothing special about it, Tom. It's all cut and dry. You would be contracted to fWo and they would promote you and whatever projects you decide to undertake." I snatched the contract back, quickly skimming through it again. "That's impossible," I snapped back. "There's got to be some loophole, some sort of angle that they put in this contract to undermine me later..." Natalie shrugged. "Not that I can see. Maybe they are being sincere about this offer." "You don't know this man, Nat," I replied. "He's a slime ball. Scum of the earth. I wouldn't trust him to hold my ice cream cone during a hot day, not to mention handling my business affairs." "I don't doubt he's a slime ball, but that doesn't mean he didn't hand you a legitimate contract." She perked an eyebrow up. "Even slime balls need to earn a living." Suspicion was the only word I could use to describe my emotions at the moment. Not only did I not trust Tallison, but I let that mistrust cloud my own instincts. "I've waited months for a chance like this," I said. "I am a wrestler. What could I possibly bring to the rest of this world if it doesn't involve me knocking the hell out of someone or leaping off of the top rope with a clothesline?" She rubbed my check with the softest touch, trying to comfort me. "Tom Lexian, I don't think you give yourself enough credit. Now, who is that other wrestler that has made such a splash in the entertainment business?" I rolled my eyes. "Oh please, don't mention him." "No, no, he has made a huge impact," She insisted. "Now, what was his name? The Boulder? The pebble? Oh god, I know it was some inanimate object..." "The Ro - ," I began to say. "Oh, forget it." She made a low growl in her voice, frowning disapprovingly at me. "The point is that you could do it too. You have everything that these hotshots in the entertainment business want." "So you want me to do this." Her eyes drifted away. "Tom, I know you love the business. It's been your life for so long, sometimes I don't think you know anything else."
