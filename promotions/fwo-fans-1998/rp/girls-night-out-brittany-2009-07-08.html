Girls' Night Out - Brittany

8 July 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Brittany took a long swig from her tallboy and chimed in. “I guess I'll take that invitation, Ivy.” The other women leaned forward a little, curious. “You all don't know me that well, I'm... well, I don't make many female friends, but I guess that's part of this business. It's a boys club, and it always will be. I've been in and around the ring since I was a teenager and I always hung out with the guys... Ah man, I'm rambling. Point is, thanks for putting this together, Siobhan, this is nice for a change.” Ivy smirked. In her years in the business, she learned to spot someone with a story they were afraid to tell. “Brit, just say whatever you want.” Brittany shook her head. “No no, I've got something for our party organizer, here.” She pointed her beer towards Siobhan. “That's the main reason we're here, right? Let me tell you about Cyberslam 4, 2001. FWO management had just changed hands, and the people who made the decisions were going to make Mike Bear, my then boyfriend, into the focus of the promotion. As much as both of us wanted that, to climb to the very top after working so hard, there was a part of me that I should have listened to, telling me not to let them put that much pressure on him. I don't know how familiar you all are with Mike, but he really isn't playing a character. Most of the mental problems he has shown on FWO programming were real, maybe played up a little in his more lucid moments His condition was unfortunately misdiagnosed way back in 1998 as a borderline personality disorder. His doctor couldn't have known, really, but that didn't help his therapy sessions. For years he was patched up and sent out there with his mind in pieces because he sold t-shirts and put butts in the seats, and we were still young and stupid enough to think it would just go on forever.” There were more than a few raised eyebrows. Brittany paused, then shook her head, having lost her train of thought for a moment. “Right! Cyberslam. Mike was a top draw by the end of 2000, so they put the World title on him at Countdown, and almost immediately the cracks started showing. He'd fly to see his doctor every week to try to keep it together, and luckily he was working with some real professionals in Mitch Wilson and Chris Kidman who made sure he looked good in the ring. Tom and Fear helped out a lot with the day to day stuff outside the ring. That's why he's always been part of a group, to have a support structure around him. Still all of that was just a band-aid on a broken arm. A week before Cyberslam, Mike just lost it and went on a frenzy through Florida.” Michelle's jaw was hanging open. “Wait, c'mon Brit. I remember that, that was an angle. Wilson attacked Mike's father to mess with him. That was on TV.” Brittany sighed. “That was to cover the truth. Mike ran out of the arena that night, just cracking. We went looking for him, trying to keep it quiet that the guy headlining the biggest pay per view of the year just went missing. Mitch actually came up with the idea of attacking Mike's dad. Steven Bear and Slade Wilson knew each other from the old days, and Mitch was in the process of selling his wrestling training academies to us, so it all fit together. Except, of course, Mike was still a mess. To this day I don't know why they didn't go in another direction. Mitch and Chris were taking time off, but there were options. Whatever the case, we finally tracked Mike down and Mitch and Chris put on a hell of a show. Mike even pulled himself together long enough to look great in the ring for one night. And then that crazy sob had to go and propose to me in the ring afterwards.” Some at the table awwwed. Ivy turned to Brittany, wide-eyed. “Are you kidding me? I remember reading about that! You didn't know that was coming? That was the first time he asked, and after everything you just said, you said yes?” Brittany thought for a moment, and then just shrugged. “Love makes you do crazy things. And when Mike's around, there's a lot of crazy things. But really, I said yes because at that point, I saw what was happening to him and all I wanted was to help him, when the rational response would've been to run away. That's how I knew to say yes. Anyway, after Cyberslam, it was clear Mike was uncontrollable and unreliable, and being engaged certainly didn't make him any saner. They took the title off of him the next month and put him in a program against his friend Fear to see if he could be salvaged, but really they had moved on, building around Chronos, Kelly, and Ross. Mike was prone to blackouts and fits of anger. He doesn't even remember much of those times, and it's just as well. A few months after Cyberslam they put him on the shelf indefinitely, and they released him from his contract a couple months later.” Brittany took a drink, and then waved her hand in a cricle to sum up. “And then there was the 3 months in Asylum, more blackouts and fits, more therapy, a correct diagnosis, and finally the mostly lucid Mike Bear you see today.” Siobhan was a little confused. “I thought ye said he wasn't playing a character. He's been bringin' stray cats into the arena, from what I've heard.” Brittany smiled a nervous smile. “Well... some of it's an act, now. Mike likes to play it up on camera, it helps him keep it together, actually. The cat, on the other hand, that's a long story. He definitely has a good reason for the cats.” Ivy smiled knowingly. “Yeah, good luck with that. Crazy cat-man,” she said, with a little sarcasm. Brittany stuck her tongue out at Ivy. “I'm still a wee confused,” Siobhan said. “That's a fine story, to be sure, but what's the lesson?” Brittany took a long drink from her beer. “The lesson is, know who to trust. The lesson is don't settle for than you're capable of, but don't exceed your capabilities, either. This business has chewed up and spit out a lot of people who knew what they were doing, and as you said, you're still learning. Know when to say no. Learn to spot when the short-term benefits are outweighed by the long-term drawbacks. And for God's sakes, don't fall in love with a man you have to physically beat up and drag into therapy.” Brittany finished her beer while everyone else at the table tried to get over their shock. “Who else has got one?”
