Animality - Part 4

25 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
At three o'clock in the morning, Mufanga Kagos woke, grunting and tussling briefly with the tangled sheets. he'd drunk a few too many beer with dinner, which was most unusual for him. The buzz was gone... and now his bladder was killing him, however it was not merely the call of nature that had distubed his sleep. He had a terrible nightmare about Bodycount. In that nightmare, several dead men - all duplicates of Mufanga Kagos himself- had risen up from their places of rest on the canvas Bodycount mat, and rose in a robotic stiff-limbed manner to confront him. He had run out of the ring, clumsily piling out between the ropes, but they had come after him. They searched the darkened arena for him, moving jerkily, his dead dopplegangers calling his name in their flat monotone voices. Now, he lay on his back in the darkness, staring at the ceiling which he could not see. The only sound was the nearly inaudible purr of the digital clock on the shadowed nightstand. Before he made the trip to the fWo, Kagos seldom dreamed. And he had never had a nightmare. Not once in twenty five years. But after his entrance into Survivor, all of that had changed. The pressures of being in a national promotion was taking it's toll on the Wildman of Lagos, both mentally and physically. He dreamed once or twice a week now, and more often than not, the dream was a bad one. Most of the time it had to do with him being chased or pursued by images of himself of faded images of his past. He didn't need a hundred-dollar-an-hour psychiatrist to tell him that those dreams were about the fWo, and his feelings about his performance so far in the federation. Bodycount was a total loss. He didn't eliminate a single person, and let Boston Strangler, a man he had pinned a week before, put him out of the competition. Pathetic, really... He got out of bed, stretched, and shuffled to the bathroom without turning on a lamp. He began his business in the dark bathroom, his calloused barefeet cool on the tiled floor, waiting for the splashing sound, almost deafening in the early morning silence. Turning his head slightly, a flash of refection stabbed at him from the shadowy bathroom mirror perched above the porcelain sink, and Mufanga's heart seemed to stop for a second. The need to go stopped at once as if someone turned off a faucet, as Kagos' internal organs seemed to freeze. He was looking in the mirror, and it was not himself looking back. The man looking back at him seemed ancient, almost mummified with his black leathery skin. The dark eyes seemed to hold life, but not an intelligent, wise brand of knowledge. Moreso a flat, dull animilistic hate seemed to eminate from the man's eyes. This man, this stranger, that was looking directly back at Mufanga Kagos through the mirror. Mufanga moved an arm, slowly raising his hand in the dark bathroom. Suddenly, he snapped on the light, and looked closely at the -window?- mirror that hung not a foot from his face. It was nothing. Or Rather... It was just him. Mufanga Kagos. There was no sign of the ancient, wild, terrible thing that he had seen in the mirror moments before. He blinked once, twice, waiting for the animal to rear it's head once again. But nothing happened, and he began to feel foolish standing like this, staring intently into a mirror at three in the morning. At last, convinced that his weak, tired eyes had decieved him, he went back to bed. In the morning, he didn't remember any of this ever taking place.
