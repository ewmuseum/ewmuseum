Shots

7 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Saturday, December 6th Queens, NY 2:21 AM local time "Alright," said Brian McGinnis as he sat down on the floor in front of the coffee table, "One bottle of Imperial Scotch Whiskey, two shotglasses." "Pour 'em," called Ivy from the back of the apartment, "and don't even THINK of drinkin' until I'm out." Brian filled the two shotglasses to the brim and capped the bottle. He paced around a bit, looking over two months' worth of mail sitting on the kitchen counter. It was a stroke of luck - good or bad, who can say - that landed Ivy home for the weekend. She had wanted to take a weekend at home just to take a breather before the fWo's final push toward the end of the year, but what were the odds that the one weekend she took would see nearly a foot of snow dumped on the city? She should play the lottery. "Alright," said Ivy, emerging from the bedroom, "Let's have it." Brian smiled. He picked up the two glasses, handed one to his sister, and clinked 'em together. "To a bunch of prima donna assholes," said Ivy, downing the shot. Brian dropped his glass back to the table. "Go on, let it out." Ivy collapsed on her couch and put her feet up. "I just don't understand... I was out of the company for what, three months? Besides that one night back, I mean." "Something like that." Shot number two poured. "It's like, one day everyone's working as hard as they can for the company, and the next, nobody cares." Shot number two killed. "Nobody gives a shit about the work Fox is doing, about all the stress he's under," continued Ivy, "Everyone is all about their shit first, their shit best, complaining over programs getting short circuited, people leaving..." Shot number three poured. "People quitting their company when they're in the midst of a major feud is a problem," pointed out Brian, "and you can't blame anyone for being concerned about their own program first and someone else's second." Ivy rolled her eyes as she downed shot number three. "That's bullshit, and you know it. That's what caused fucking, hundreds of promotions to shut down. You go back and look at the fWo last Winter, last Spring, even, around Cyberslam. Everyone was on the same page, everyone was intent on helping everyone else get better." "You guys were on the verge of getting shut down," reminded Brian. Shot number four poured. "If Cyberslam was the end of the road," he continued, "of course everyone was gonna put their best foot forward." Ivy shook her head. "No. I see what this is doing to Fox, to Twitch and Smooth. They're working overtime on their overtime." "Everyone in wrestling does that. You worked for ninety four days straight over the winter, what, twenty hour days?" "Then why am I here?" asked Ivy, "Why did I need to take the weekend off?" "Because you deserve some time off?" "No," argued Ivy, "Because I can't take this stress anymore. Because there's Deacon and Chris, there's Craig Miles of all people... and everyone else in that lockerroom makes me... not want to be there." Shot number four, killed. "What about Lance Knight?" Ivy froze. "What about him?" "You guys are friends, right?" "Yeah..." Shot number five, poured. "But I screwed it up in Japan," said Ivy, as she looked down, "I kissed him." "You what?" Ivy looked at her brother. "I kissed him, and I haven't really talked to him since." Shot number five, killed. "I don't buy it," replied Brian, "He's not the type to do that shit." "I..." Ivy was stammering, "have been kinda... avoiding him." Brian looked at her, the silence spoke volumes. "How can I look at him again? We were such good friends and in a sake-induced stroke of idiocy I ruined it. What's he thinking, that if Sean's gone I'll just move on to the next guy in line? I hate the fact that he's probably thinking I just used him like that. I hate thinking this way!" She took her glasses off and wiped her eyes. "Are you going to talk to him?" asked Brian, "You really should." She reached for her phone, but Brian grabbed it. "Maybe... on Monday. When you haven't had half a dozen shots of scotch." Ivy smiled. "Y'know, all those times I called you a deadbeet? I didn't mean it, B... really." Brian McGinnis laughed. "Yeah, y'did. But it's cool. Be right back." He got up on semi-steady feet and headed for the bathroom. Brian McGinnis was worried about his baby sister - that much was clear. She'd always been the most self- sufficient of the McGinnis kids, all intelligence, all sense, but no room for other opinions or paths. Which was fine - she was good at knowing what needed to be done. The past year, however, she's undergone a drastic change. Ivy was rarely the angry bitch that he remembered her from since she was sixteen. She smiled more. She cried more. She spoke of love as something that could actually happen to her. And it did, for over a year. Ivy and Trip seemed perfect for each other. Brian washed his hands, thinking through an alcohol - induced haze. Maybe he'll give Trip a call and see if he's busy. Maybe he'll talk Ivy into taking Monday's fWo show off and hanging out with the kids at Coop's. Ivy was talking about pushing for Randy Knox to wrestle another one of the kids on the final fWo show of the year. If she had good news for him, he'd really enjoy hearing it. "Ivy," he called, as he headed back into the living room, "I think--" She was asleep. Brian laughed at his sister, how some things never changed. He picked up the phone and dialed a number. "Kim? Hey, it's me. Ivy's having a bit of a bad night, I'm going to crash here." ... "What? No, no... will be home in the morning. Get the kids up early, we'll all go out to breakfast." ... "Love you too. Bye." The wife and the four kids understood how having Ivy McGinnis for a sister led to some interesting nights. But, as Brian headed for Ivy's guest room, he couldn't help wondering... when does his sister get a less interesting night? Doesn't everyone deserve it?
