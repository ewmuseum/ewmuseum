A Task Given

24 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The cold wind bit deeply, like a knife of frost scraping his very bones. The scraps of newspapers and rags he wrapped around himself offered less protection than the cold bricks of the dirty alley, but just barely. He situation wasn't so bad during the day; at least then he could shuffle from shelter to shelter and get a hot meal and human contact, but he dreaded these winter nights. Even the bottle of cheap wine he'd consumed couldn't keep the chill out for long, and so he lay under a heap of filth, body shivering uncontrollably, teeth chattering incessantly. Eventually, physical exhaustion overtook him, and he fell into a fitful sleep. He heard the breathing first; the deep, rich breaths of a man in his prime. He cracked one eye open, fearful of finding a group of young punks ready to torture him for "fun" again. Instead, a pair of huge bare feet stood before him on the frosted concrete. His eyes instantly traveled up twin legs, solid as ancient oaks, over a bare barrel-chested torso, and up finally to a face straight from his detox hallucinations. He whimpered in awe and fear, even as the creature bent closer. If this...thing...was daunting before, now, this close, hot breath finally delivering some small measure of warmth, it was truly frightening. He squeezed his eyes shut and smiled sadly in epiphany. As pitiful and wretched as his life was, he wasn't yet ready to give it up. He waited anxiously for the blow that would signify the beginning of the end...and waited...and waited... Something landed on his face, something light, yet solid. Cautiously, he opened his eyes to find a photograph settled across his nose. His shaking hand snatched the picture from his face and he studied it nervously, cursing as fear and cold and withdrawal kept his eyes from focusing. The photo was of a handsome young man, well built and strong, an athlete maybe, and completely unfamiliar to him. There was a paperclip on one corner, and as he turned the photo over he did find a face he recognized: Ulysses S. Grant. His hands shook, but this time with hope and greed. He couldn't remember the last time he had even DREAMED about fifty dollars, and now here it was, right within his grimy hands. For a moment he was lost, oblivious to the world around him except for the small green slip of legal tender before him. Certainly this couldn't really be happening. Certainly he was dreaming. Certainly there had to be a catch. "There's a catch." The voice of the monster was deep, too deep to be human, and echoed like a shout in an empty metal basement. Reality slapped him in the face, and he tore his eyes from the fifty and looked up, way up, at the creature towering over him. "This man will be at the Key Arena tonight. You will be there as well, and you will cause him injury. You shall focus on his knee. Be it with a steel rod, or brick, or sledgehammer, you shall do everything in your meager power to cripple this individual. You will do this: you have already been compensated for your time. Failure is not an option." And then the monster turned and walked away. He watched him retreat until at last the shadows devoured his massive form. He looked down again at the green treasure in his hand, and the photograph of the stranger. What did you do, he wondered, to make such an enemy? He shook his head sadly and slowly, unsteadily got to his feet. He tucked the photo and the cash into his pocket and shuffled down the alleyway, trying to form some semblance of a plan, when a thought hit him like a brick in the back of the head. Why should he do this? He had fifty dollars in his possession, and the young man in the photo had never done him any personal harm... Surely he could lose himself in the underbelly of the city, disappear into the shadows as easily as his mysterious benefactor had. He contemplated his dilemma as he walked onto the sidewalk and into uncertainty...
