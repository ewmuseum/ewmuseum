Destiny's Child - Part Two

17 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Five blocks. Ten blocks. No shoes. Only socks. In the rain. How quite insane. The temperatures had dropped and being soaked to the skin wasn't helping to keep himself warm. Right now, David Cross wasn't thinking about his own well-being. His view of reality was like looking through a kaleidoscope of chaotic swirling colors, focusing on the black dot in the middle of it all. Shivering, teeth chattering, David pushed open the door to the old cathedral and stepped inside leaving wet footprints behind him. Surrounded by the silence once inside, David noticed that his footsteps sounded different somehow. He looked down and saw he had one bare foot. He'd never even noticed he'd lost a sock somewhere. But that was David, always losing something. Shoes. Socks. Love. Friends. Family. Sanity. He dropped limply into one of the wooden pews. With everything that had been dumped on him in his life, all the pain and suffering, all the disappointment and loss, something had to go right for him. Somewhere, David Cross had to find happiness. Michelle was his happiness, his beacon of hope in the sea of dispair. She was a perfect fit for him. They were meant to be together. Fate, it seemed, had other ideas. David didn't believe in Fate. He looked no further than himself to see that. His name was Cross. The name he used as a professional wrestler, which he inherited from his only friend, was Crucifix. He even had a cross-shaped scar over his right eye. But what did it mean? Was it Fate? Was he destined for something great? Something divine? Nobody in the fWo could understand his suffering. As nice as they tried to be to him, none of them sat in the wreckage of the arena in Tel Aviv, surrounded by the dismembered bodies of women and small children that had come to see the show. None of them were covered in blood, listening to the haunting cries of the dying and the survivors who had lost someone close to them. None of them held a charred limb of a small infant while a bloody cross leaked through the dirt and ash on their face. None of them looked to the Heavens and asked "Why?" only to be answered with the sounds of tormented suffering. Dr. Cambridge had known that David was mentally unstable. Session after session, she tried to reach him but had no idea how. She couldn't understand the pain of having a family that was a lie, a fact that was constantly thrown in his face by the woman he falsely called "Mother." She didn't have the training to help him cope with the memories of the obese troll beating him nightly, telling him that he was with her because his own family was smart enough to get rid of him. She could bring the pain out and make David face it; she had no idea how to help him deal with it. The rain pounded against the stained glass windows. David sat in the shadows where he felt he'd always been. He never walked in the light. Never. He always felt like a bug
