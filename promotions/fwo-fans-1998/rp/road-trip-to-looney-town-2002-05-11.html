Road Trip to Looney Town

11 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
David Cross snorted and sat up, realizing he'd been snoring. He blinked hard, letting his eyes adapt to the dim glow of the dashboard, and the headlights shining into the rainy darkness. "Sure is coming down out there," he said to the occupants in the front seat. "Not hard enough," the driver said. "I could still hear your snoring." "Are we there yet?" the young passenger asked impatiently. "Almost," the driver replied. "We're almost there." Cross shifted in the back seat and looked out the window. He couldn't make out anything. No signs, no shapes, nothing. The blackness had swallowed everything whole. "So, where are we heading?" He waited. There was no answer. "Guys?" David slid behind the passenger's seat. "I realize you're probably in a hurry to get to... to wherever it is that you're going to. But Nature's calling, and that's a call I'd rather not take in the backseat of a car. Could we pull over?" Instead of slowing down, Cross felt the vehicle speed up. "Uh, I don't think I'll make it to the next gas station. So there's no need to rush. I don't mind peeing in the rain." A flash of lightning forked across the sky, silhouetting the rows of barren trees on either side of the road. They seemed to reach out from the ground with think, dark fingers. David sighed. "I liked it better when I couldn't see what was out there." The car hit a large bump, and for a moment Cross felt himself float off the seat as the car flew through the air. But as it landed with a loud crunch, he reached for the seatbelt. "Hey look, could we please pull over? You're going to have a puddle o' pee back here if you don't, and those bumps aren't helping matters." The passenger squirmed nervously. "Dad, slow down 'kay? I don't wanna have an accident." "Speaking of accidents," Cross chimed in. "I'm gonna have an accident in about a minute if you don't pull over." The driver remained silent, his foot pressing the accelerator to the floor. They hit a second bump, and Cross smacked his head against the roof before the car crashed down to earth. "What the Hell!" he shouted. "Slow down!" "Don't tell me what to do in my own car, Davie boy. I don't take orders from losers." The driver turned off the windshield wipers. "Sweet Jesus man. I gave you everything. I gave you the chance to live out your dream. I gave you a shot. I gave you my friendship. I gave up everything and gave it all to you. All I asked for in return was your help." Cross felt his stomach tighten in panic. His eyes darted from the speedometer with the buried needle, to the young child crying in the passenger seat, to the shadowy branches of trees passing by at an alarming rate. "Tyler, I'm sorry. Really, I'm sooo sorry." Tyler's eyes moved from the blurred windshield to the rear-view mirror. For a man that bitter, Cross thought to himself, he certainly looks calm. "It's too late," Tyler said coldly. "It's gone beyond apologies." Little Robbie's fearful sobs were getting louder. "Daddy stop the car! You're scaring me! Please stop the car!" "You hear that?" Tyler snapped. "You're making me scare my boy. He's terrified, David, and it's all your fault." Cross lunged forward, but the seatbelt held him firmly against his seat. He fumbled with the release mechanism, watching blurred shadows darting across the waterlogged windshield. "This isn't funny Tyler! I'm sorry for what I did! I'll make it up to you!" Tyler leaned back as his son pulled on his leather jacket. "I... wanna go... home," Robbie gasped between sobs. The big man rested a hand on his son's head, wiping his tears away with his thumb. "We're almost home," he said calmly. "It's almost over." His eyes moved back to the mirror, locking stares with Cross. "It's almost over. You ready David?" Tyler flipped the windshield wipers back on as the car started to shake. A huge tree rushed at them head-on. Cross hammered on the release mechanism, vainly trying to free himself. He couldn't reach Tyler to try and stop him. Trapped. Pain exploded in David Cross' body as the car slammed into the tree. Time slowed for Cross as he watched the windshield shatter. Robbie screamed briefly as the dashboard slammed into his little body. The steering wheel snapped and lurched forward, punching a hole through Tyler's chest, back, and seat.
