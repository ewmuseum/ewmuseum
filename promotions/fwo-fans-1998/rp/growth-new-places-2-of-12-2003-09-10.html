Growth: New Places Moving is always hard. Moving on is even harder. Yet at some point you must let go of your old life, and find something new. Many times people have unresolved emotions; Emotions that they can't let go of until they replace them. Replace them in the heat of the moment. The emotion of sadness, of pain, is the hardest one to let go of. Yet sometimes you find yourself in a new place, a new place with new opportunities that you just have to explore. You just have to. Kevin exhaled a deep sigh. He felt claustrophobic in the new house, the boxes overwhelming him entirely. It was all so much, so fast. He looked around the loft style apartment. It was nice, nothing he could complain about. It had everything he needed really, a television, a second phone line for the computer, ample space, even a small workout area. It had everything... Except her. Except Donna. And even as Kevin tried to make the best of it, that's where his mind went. It went back to Donna. The fact she wasn't there, or anywhere, it haunted him. Haunted him like nothing else. She had been there forever, and he had tried to cherish every moment... He really had. It just proved harder than he had imagined. "Hey Mitch, I'm gonna go for a walk okay?" Kevin called out to his roommate. Mitch quickly wheeled in from his first floor bedroom, "Oh, alright man. Hey, you wanna get some milk and shit for tomorrow morning?" "Yeah, no problem." Kevin nodded, grabbing his keys off of a table sitting in the center of the room. Closing the door behind him, Kevin hit the stairs, racing down the six flights. The setting sun was a surpirisng sight for Kevin as he walked out from the complex into the open. He smiled to himself, enjoying the warmth of the sun. He looked down the avenue, watching the people as they walked by. Laughing. Smiling. Happy. He started north, and he walked. And he walked, and he walked and he walked. He tried to become one of the people, doing his best to smile when he made eye contact, or to wave as he crossed the street. But it felt false. He wasn't happy. So as he approached the musty gym, his attempt at looking kind, being happy, it had faded to a sodden bitterness. He looked up at the sign, "Wresling Tonie." The two lowercase t's suspiciously missing. He ducked his head down, and walked into the gym, looking at the collection of about 300 aluminum chairs surrounding a wrestling ring. Inside the ring was a large man, probably six foot four, biceps larger than his skull. He had bleached blonde hair, and a short goatee, as well as fingerless gloves and black wrestling shorts. He glared at Kevin, talking on the cheap wired microphone over the ridiculously poor sound system. He made eye contact with Kevin, and quickly smirked for the collection of about a hundred local fans. "YEAH! Look at this chump!
