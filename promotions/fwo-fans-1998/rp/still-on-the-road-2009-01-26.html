STILL on the Road

26 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Siobahn McPhineas sighed and looked at the desolate landscape through the windshield of the RV. Northern Nevada had little to offer in the way of visual distractions and after this many days of endless road behind them Siobahn was nearly desperate for some kind of diversion. Mr. Reed was hidden away in his space, a wall of books around him like a proper English soldier in his foxhole surrounded by sandbags. The Assistant was in her customary position in the passenger’s seat but it was hard to watch both her and the tarmac ahead. The radio provided nothing but garish American nightmares screeched by emaciated girls and repeated endlessly. The trials of traveling were starting to catch up with her. Their journey was taking longer than was strictly necessary and that was all Siobahn’s fault – a strict, straight southeast-to-northwest line would have been the obvious way to go, but no one in their party would ever even raise an eyebrow over her choice of route; everyone recognized her latent abilities and followed her lead unquestioningly. And sometimes her lead was dictated by feelings and dreams and premonitions that she had, and no one questioned that, either. Twice on this trip she’d felt an odd impetus directing her and steered the RV accordingly. The first time found them staring at Joshua trees and baked dirt in the middle of the Arizona desert. Siobahn had known when and where to stop and turned that knowledge into a leg-stretching pit stop but otherwise there was no apparent reason to stop at such an isolated, forlorn location. Still, Siobahn knew …felt… this to be a place of supreme importance. She just didn’t know what form that importance might take. The second time she followed her instinct led them straight towards the undeniable allure and great, grabbing, gluttonous glow of Sin City herself, Las Vegas. Uncharacteristically, Mr. Reed had actually asserted himself and declared that they had no time for temptations and distractions and could not afford to delay their journey. Siobahn had seen the wisdom in his words and agreed, but still she knew that somewhere in that wicked city something momentous waited. She glanced in the rearview mirror and saw the solid, reassuring presence of their semi truck. Burlington Werner - his long gray hair pulled back behind him and wearing sunglasses - was behind the wheel, following the RV flawlessly. Though Siobahn could not see him she knew that Mr. Yip was right next to Mr. Werner. Their little traveling entourage. And in turn she thought of their group, and their current journey, and their destination. She smiled to herself – what would the Fan’s Wrestling Organization think of them? Mr. Yip confirmed Mr. Reed’s inclusion into the organization’s roster and Mr. Reed declared himself fit and ready for the demanding physical exertion. It fell to Siobahn’s perfect, freckled shoulders to shepherd them all to the great, green northwest and deliver Mr. Reed to the arena on Wednesday, where a wrestling match would be waiting for him. She had absolutely no doubt of this – Mr. Reed had said that he would arrange for a spot to be opened for him and that was that. After all, she had learned from experience that How or When or Why were utterly immaterial to Mr. Fairview Reed.
