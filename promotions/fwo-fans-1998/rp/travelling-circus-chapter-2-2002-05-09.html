Travelling Circus - Chapter 2

9 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The sound of a sharp knocking, followed by the door slowly creaking inward, surprised John Rider. Once upon a time, Mufanga Kagos always came to visit. But that had been back when they were in the indy feds, running the gimmick to it's fullest potential. John Rider, the flamboyant mouthpiece, leading Mufanga Kagos, the Wildman, to the ring on a leash... Ahhh... those were the days. Sitting in the cramped trailer, watching the fWo on the tiny black and white television with the coathanger aerial. Dreaming up schemes and angles that would get the fans cheering in the endless gymnasiums and Bingo halls as they followed the travelling circus that was Independant Wrestling. That was before Mufanga was drafted to the big leagues, to TV-land, and Mufanga decided to do his own speaking, decided to stand on his own. But that was fine, thought John. There was no animosity between the two men. Mufanga had to do it on his own, and Rider understood that... but now... now that Mufanga Kagos had failed to capture the hearts and minds of the television wrestling fans, now that the blame for the loss of the fWo TV contract was supposedly being placed directly on the heads of the fWo rookies, here he was at Rider's rusted trailer door. Another day's work for John Rider. "Rider, you're still here." Mufanga's surprised voice greeted him from the doorway. "Nobody saw you hanging around backstage after Slaughter, so we figured you had fled the scene." John raised his head to look at the lanky black wrestler, as he slid the "PWI Top 500" Magazine under the pile of dirtsheets on the pressed-wood table with a stealthy gesture. Although he was no longer Kagos' agent, not even employed by the fWo, the connection between them was still strong. The young man standing in the doorway, brown eyes demanding answers, produced in him a strong paternal instinct. Kagos was always the star, the main attraction, the one that always seems in control, and yet Rider worried about protecting him. Not so much from the evil in the world, or whatever wretchedness the professional wrestling world was spewing out this week, but from himself. "Last Slaughter is an apt term... and yes, I'm still here. Where the hell would I go?"
