An Angel in my Corner

24 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Whap Whap The heavy bag jerked and jumped with the impact behind Kodiak Vic Creed’s taped fists. Sweat covered the big man’s body, soaking his wife beater and sweat pants as if someone had tossed a bucket of water at him. A few of the young boxers who also had been working out in the small gym stopped what they were doing to stare at Creed’s seemingly endless barrage of destruction. He didn’t seem to get tired. His punches were as crisp and as devastating now as they were ten minutes ago when he started his assault. Whap Whap Two things kept Creed’s body fuelled with aggression. The same two things that had kept him awake most nights, and had recently prompted a few extra-curricular brawls outside the ring – TAR, and Rage. Both felt like a natural fit to the Main Man, and he embraced them with a great deal of enthusiasm, almost desperation. A lean muscled black kid slowly unwound the wraps from his hands as he watched Creed pummel the duct tape that held the bag’s guts inside. He didn’t know what Creed was doing here – after all, wrestling was fake – but the big man was certainly making himself at home. He was the first one in the gym when it opened, and the last one to leave. He sparred occasionally (much to the horror of his sparring partners) but spent most of his time training alone. And, much like now, he talked to himself. Today he was talking about ice cream. “You don’t need ice cream,” Vic said in a loud whisper between punches. “That garbage will make you fat.” His dead child Sarah stood off to his right with a stopwatch in her hand. “I’m a kid. Nothing makes me fat now.” She looked at the time. “Another five minutes.” “Great.” Whap Whap The other fighters simply gravitated back to their own training, their own little worlds, leaving Creed to his dillusions. He danced to the side, hammering the bag with repeated right hooks. Sarah quickly stepped back. “So have you figured out how you’re going to deal with Uncle Nick?” “Nick’s not your uncle, he’s – was – your godfather.” Vic shook his head, sending droplets of sweat spraying off his face. “And yeah, I do have a plan.” “Good.” Sarah kicked at a can that was about as real as she was. “I don’t like him wearing my head.” “That motherfucker…” WhapWhapWham Jerry really didn’t know any better. He’d just started to get into boxing and was still pretty soft around the midsection, so to speak. The speedbag was out of his grasp so far, but the skipping was pretty keen. One thing he did every time he was in the gym was hit the heavy bag. It was a great way to get rid of the day’s frustration, and after Ed from Accounting used Jerry’s coffee cup all day today he was just chock full of frustration. The big guy had been on the heavy bag for what seemed like hours, so Jerry wanted to find out when he’d be finished. “S’cuse me buddy, but –“ Whap Whap... Craaack Jerry’s right eye was swollen before his unconscious body even hit the floor. Vic stared down at him. His voice was cold an unsympathetic. “You come to me like a bitch and I’ll treat you like a bitch.” Sarah stuffed the stopwatch into her pocket. “Not again,” she sighed. Trainers rushed from across the gym and out of the ring. None of the other fighters dared approach. Creed hovered over the man like a lion over the corpse of a fat, dead gazelle. He didn’t hear the trainers yelling for him to back off, to leave the gym. The only voice that registered at all was Sarah’s, and only because it was in his head to start with. “C’mon Dad, let’s get out of here. There’s other gyms you can work out at.” Vic walked over to the nearby bench and snatched his towel off it. He looked back at Jerry as the trainers helped him to a seated position and waved smelling salts under his nose. “If my kid wasn’t here, you’d be visiting her you little prick.” Creed stormed out of the gym. Jerry looked at the trainer through his one good eye. “Is that guy crazy?” “Pretty much. Now let’s get you on your feet.”
