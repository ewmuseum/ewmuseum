It's not you... it's me

14 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Michelle Hiroshi's eyes were bloodshot and burned from her tears. The medication they'd added to her I.V. drip, while killing her pain, also made her much more emotional. It wasn't like she wasn't already in a bad emotional state though. She'd been attacked, crippled, for something that had nothing to do with her. It was a senseless act of brutality, but that was the North American culture for you. Violence was escalading and no one was doing anything to stop it. She'd already spoken to the police which did no good at all. She was still a Japanese citizen, and the police seemed more concerned with her travel visa than with the vicious assault she'd been a victim of. They asked question after question - who she knew in North America, had she made any enemies - and made her feel more and more like she was responsible somehow. David was with her the whole time, watching her pain and blaming himself more and more for what happened. It had been days since the attack and they still hadn't spoken much. He wanted to know what she was thinking, to tell her that he'd find out who hurt her and he'd make them pay. If inflicting pain was an Olympic event, David Cross was training to get the Gold medal in it. Cross was torn. He wanted to stay with Michelle while the doctors continued to run their tests, but she wanted him to take care of his fWo responsibilities. All day Saturday, Michelle kept telling him that he had to carry on. But was it worth carrying on without her? "Want me to call the nurse back?" David asked softly as he caressed Michelle's hair. Michelle shook her head slightly. "No. It's not my legs that are hurting me." David pulled up a chair and sat beside her hospital bed. "Has the pain spread? Where are you hurting?" She smiled, her eyes welling up with tears again as she touched her chest. "Here." "Your heart? That could be gas from anxiety, but I'm not taking any chances." David started to stand but Michelle grabbed his sleeve. "David you need to go." He looked at the hand holding his arm. "I'm trying to." "No." Michelle looked into his eyes. "You need to leave." David rubbed her arm. "The fWo can wait. You're more important to me than some job." "It's not a job," she said with a tremble in her voice. "It's who you are. It's your life, and it's not one I can be a part of anymore." "What are you saying?" David sat down again. "Once I can walk again, I'm going to see my Father. Then I'm going back to Japan.
