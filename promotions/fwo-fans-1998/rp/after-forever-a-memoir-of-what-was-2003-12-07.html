After Forever (a memoir of what was)

7 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Diamondback rattlesnakes aren't nice. Especially when one bites you. I've been in the hospital for three days now. Every now and again I've been puking and I haven't had much of an appetite. This war with Austin Davis, it's gotten to places I never imagined. He's turned me into someone I don't want to be. I can't help being the man I am now. I have no wife. My child is miles away from me. Austin Davis did this to me. He's the reason I'm in this hospital now. He's the reason why I'm suffering. The nurse pops her head into my room. "Mr. Williams? You have a visitor." I frown. I don't want to see anybody right now. No one can make me feel better. No one can change the fact that I'm pale as a ghost and beaten up inside. Except for Clarisse. She comes into the room with a few tulips in a vase. She's trying her best to be normal around me. She doesn't want to make things stranger than they already are. I cheated on her. In fact, I fucked every single girl I could find while she sat at home with Matilda. Looking at her right now I regret what I did. Regrets, regrets. My life is full of them. She puts the vase on the stand next to my bed. "I just came by to see if you're all right. I didn't like seeing you get hurt." And I didn't like seeing her hurt, especially when it's me hurting her. Just seeing me crushes her heart. I don't know what to say to her. I try not to make it awkward. "Thanks for coming. I'll be OK in a few days." There's an awkward silence, so I ask her, "How's Matilda?" Clarisse takes a seat next to my bed. "She's doing fine. I left her with the babysitter. I'm flying back tonight. I just came to see how you're doing. I was worried the snake bite was serious." I know she wants to cry. When she looks at the ground like that when she talks. Things like this come like second nature to me. I've known and loved her for so long. "While you're here, Clare--" She turns her head away. "No, Cody. I don't want to talk about it." I take her hand. Both of our ring fingers are missing the wedding band. "Please, Clarisse. I never got the chance to say this when I left you. I just wanted to say that I'm sorry for everything I've done. I'm sorry for who I've become. God, you don't know how empty life is now. I can't let go of you." She shakes her head. "I don't want to deal with this now. You've hurt me enough." "Remember in Paris? The two of us, in front of the Eiffel Tower. When I said I'd love you forever, I meant it." "I meant it, too. There are things in life we can't change. I love you more than anything, Cody, but I can't have you in my life. Not the way you are now. I don't want Matilda growing up seeing this." She gets up. "Goodbye, Cody." Before she puts her hand on the door, I say: "No one is ever going to love you like I did." I am Cody's honesty. She looks over her shoulder at me. "Likewise." She walks out the door, and once again I'm all alone.
