Our Story Thus Far (Part 5)

2 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
1995 Tourists are told that Paris is the City of Lights, the City of Love, and to an extent, there is some truth to that. But even the City of Lights is not without its seedy underbelly, and you'd be a fool to walk around the city, even in broad daylight, without being aware of your money was. Pickpockets made their living in the crowded streets, milling around the various street performers. Jasmin made sure to walk opposite of the fire breather, juggler and the mimes, and the large groups that gathered around them. The only performer on her side was a con artist scamming a group of Welsh with a game of three-card monte. Well, him and the purse snatcher. Jasmin didn't know what happened at first, she just knew that she'd been thrown to the ground. She barely saw her attacker's feet as he dashed away, until he fell to the ground, followed shortly by a small, yet presumably heavy, pin. [[You look as if you could use some help. May I help you up?]] [[The juggler, I presume?]] [[I should hope so. I'd make a terrible mime. Pierre Delacroix. And you are?]] [[Buying drinks for the man who kept my day from being completely awful. If you don't mind, though, I'd like to talk to my friend over there first, get my purse back, and maybe make him regret his choice of victims.]] [[And how might you be trying that?]] [[Nothing complicated. Just thought I might pop out of one his testicles. It's easy, if you know what you're doing.]] [[I will regret asking, I'm certain of it, but you know what you're doing in this regard?]] [[Everyone's got to start somewhere.]] Some grievous bodily harm, and a few drinks later, the pair began discussing their lives, their goals, and their regrets. For Jasmin, it had been her inability to follow in her father's footsteps. He had enjoyed widespread success throughout Europe in the 60's and 70's, and had not only taught her the basics of their craft, but also supported her desire to learn a different style, one more suited to a person of her build. As for Pierre's regrets, they were quite the opposite. The younger son of a politically connected family, he'd never been encouraged to find his own path. It was just expected that he'd become one of the idle rich until his thirties, at which point he would begin his own career in politics. He knew he was meant for something else, and on this day he realized it. Talking to Jasmin, hearing her stories, his excitement to become involved with this profession was palpable. So he asked a question: [[Will you train me?]] Of course she would. 1996 They'd been together a year, both as student and teacher, and as lovers. He'd moved in almost immediately, ostensibly for the easy access to training. He took to the craft quickly, inspiring her to train others, both to supplement her income and to create a breed of wrestlers that her father would have been proud to share a ring with. She'd called in some favors, to get her students bookings around the area, and they quickly gained favor with the local promotions. Their dedication and training made them valuable commodities. It helped that most of them learned the European style. Only Pierre chose to follow Jasmin's particular style, in addition to a variety of others. If he were to become successful, to become important, he needed to know how to compete in any atmosphere. When they spoke of his career, they always talked about the future, about becoming a name on a global scale. There was only one way to accomplish that, they agreed, and that was through dominating in North America. When that happened, they would not be together. Of this, they never spoke. 1997 Pierre was away, in Las Vegas, when she met Thomas Desjardin. He wasn't an athlete, wasn't in the business, and wasn't going to leave her. He was a florist, a kind and gentle man, and every time he looked at her, she could tell that he truly loved her. Some day, she could feel the same for him. Wouldn't she? She loved Pierre, and needed to be honest. The letter she wrote was touching, honest, and at its core, brutal. 1998 He'd almost ruined his career with the FWO before it had a chance to begin. He could never admit it, but he understood why she would want some stability, which was couldn't promise. Not now. It was only after he'd won the Cruiserweight Title that he'd found out about their impending marriage, and he'd left without much notice, just enough time to drop the title on his way out, to do business the right way. He'd found her the night before the ceremony, did his best to talk her out of it, but she wouldn't budge. Instead, she offered him some small consolation, to be the last man she'd be with before entering the holy bonds of matrimony. They did their best to make that night count, to make it lasting. Turns out, they didn't need to. He left before the ceremony, and begged to get his job back in the FWO. He got it, with the caveat that he'd be removed from the Cruiserweight Division, as a form of punishment. How'd he feel about the Hardcore Division? He felt just fine about that.
