Cure for the Itch (feat. Eli Flair)

15 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Call me Ishmael..." Someone was leaning over Amy Campbell as she sat, perched at a "cheap" hotel bar awaiting someone, anyone she knew to come over and occupy her time. Alas, the only person she knew among anyone travelling with her was Brandon Roche, and he was nowhere to be found. She'd already tried to snatch him up for a trip around Houston to pass some time before Slaughter, but no such luck...apparently he was on his way in with Ana and 'the kids', which meant Hitl--errrr---his loving wife would be certain he kept up as a family man, as opposed to being a friend for the moment. However, this annoying little waiter seemed to have all the time in the world for her. Three times now, Amy had tried to brush him off, yet he continued gawking at her with a peculiar expression. Not surprisingly, he appeared to recognize her...but she persisted, and ended up shoving a ten dollar bill into his half-open palm while appearing about ready to just stand up, walk away and bring her evening of watching people and sulking while avoiding alcohol to an end. She was content to sit up in her room alone, as usual, but right now the redhead just wanted to win her little battle against this accursed bus boy. "Alright, Ishmael...there's your tip." Her tone was half-sarcastic as she continued, but the near star-eyed young man never seemed to notice. "Okay? Now I'm just going to eat and...uhh...." He wasn't budging. Amy needed an excuse, and fast. "...and wait for my boy....friend. Yeah, he's here somewhere and..umm...supposed to meet me for a drink. Have a nice night!" "Yes...yes! Boyfriend, I see! I need to go attend to my tables anyway..." He stuttered, "Um, nice to meet you!" When she turned back with a gloomy frown to a tiny glass of Coke and the eight or ten onion rings she'd fought the waiter a half hour just to have the chance to eat without being stared at, Aimz realized that it just was not her night. She'd looked up from watching the icecubes do circles along the rim of her glass only to see a tall, seemingly well-built man walk in... It took her a moment before she fully recognized him, but the lengthy black hair and piercing eyes were unmistakable. She saw Eli Flair watching her but didn't particularly need him to know that fact, so Amy elected to let her eyes wander back to the fizzling pop which, a few months earlier, would have been mixed with rum to drown out her 'sorrows'. Another stare-driven battle was apparently about to begin. Every time Amy looked up Eli had an eye on her and wasn't shy to relent his gaze, either. Of course, she thought, they worked together...well, in the vicinity of one another, so it wasn't terribly unusual to notice a co-worker...right? Wrong. There had to be a reason his curious eye always wandered to her and when it came time for him to pick a seat, he sank down next to her, easing his full frame down onto a creaking stool.....still staring. Didn't smile, didn't speak...just looked, and now it was with an expectant glint to his eye. Finally, curiosity and what almost seemed like fear got the best of her. "Something wrong?" "You tell me," replied Eli, nonchalantly, "You've been sitting here looking like someone drowned your cat for the past fifteen minutes, and since Brand's not around I'm taking one for the team." He leaned back, taking a long drink out of the bottle of beer half- hidden in his hand. "Something wrong?" She watched him respond with a grin not manipulating her mouth, but one toying around in her eyes. It hadn't even been six months since she'd tape "Brand" and Eli's matches on a weekly basis, watching over and over again in admiration and hopes to help herself improve...and now she spoke to Brandon nearly every day, and here was Eli Flair sitting casually beside her as if there wasn't a difference between him or some guy pulled in off the street for a beer. "I don't ever recall a time when it was necessary for something to be wrong if a girl sat around looking weary..." Amy's only reply was stand-offish at best. She knew Eli was Brandon's friend but with no past experience under her belt, she couldn't be sure about him no matter who else trusted the man, or how much she respected his ability. 'Hey,'The redhead thought for a moment, an amused smirk playing about her lips. 'Might as well trust him. As long as he's here, at least "Ishmael" won't be back.' Eli considered the first comment, the one she'd spoken aloud, for a moment. "You've got a point," he finally answered, "...but in my experience it's better to assume the worst. Besides, we work for the same company now. What affects one person affects everybody backstage. I dunno how things were where you came from but these tours can really wear on your brain." He ran his hands through his hair absent-mindedly, pulling it behind his ears. "And sometimes the daily grind is all it takes to depress someone, 'specially if they're not used to it." "Meh," She mumbled, "I doubt anything I think is going to impact 'everybody backstage' at all. As a general rule, I avoid them...everybody's either got an ego or an issue that I don't want to deal with because I've got enough problems and too little ego of my own. Funny as it may seem, though, sometimes I get sucked into caring, like with your buddy...at least, I think he's your buddy." Aimz was treading lightly, not entirely sure if Eli had a second opinion of Brandon or whatever else he might think or have to say about her closest, and only friend so far in fWo. "Who, Brand?" asked Eli, "He was one of Ivy's projects last winter - she was damned determined to get him to relax and not think about work after we'd left the arena. I think I did see him relax once....but he made me promise not to tell anyone. I know what you mean about issues, though. Trust me - you'll have a lot more fun in this company if you just ignore the bullshit." "Yep...I try to ignore the bullshit. And Ivy, eh?" After chuckling at Eli's mild quip about Brandon's rather 'elderly' personality, Amy'd paused a moment to think back in her recent memory, which was basically the only memory she had left thanks to the Bacardi-soaked year or so after graduation. "I think he's mentioned her. If she helped him at all, I'd hate to see what he was like before she caught hold of him. I dunno if my presence helps him along at all or what...I think he's relaxed a little, maybe, but he tends to act almost like...I don't know, like a nervous wreck. His wife's probably giving him a seriously hard time about me at home." Yet again, she'd let her mind and tongue had wander a little too far into a subject, so she make an abrupt halt on the topic of Brandon's household. "Brand can take care of himself, don't stress about his home life. Everyone in this business deals with it on some kinda level," replied Eli, with a reassuring smile, "Except me, of course. I tell 'em, do what I did and fall in love with a singer. Angel's got the same kind'a nomadic lifestyle as we do. Fits nicely." They both laughed, as Eli's gaze fell on Amy's nearly- empty cup. " Get ya another one of them? What's you drinking there, rum and coke?" Aimz had laughed again, leaving Eli with a wondering expression until she simmered down a little. "Err, sorry. No no...no rum, gotta work on willpower and all that nice stuff that doesn't involve letting my life depend on some crazy little chemicals. If you knew of me a month or so ago, you'd probably know why I'm refusing...so try not to call me rude." She smiled, "...or wimpy. Believe me, three months ago I could've probably consumed my own weight in alcohol without...well...while almost not facing the Grim Reaper." "Heh," said Eli, as he continued to drink his beer, "Don't worry 'bout it.... everyone's got their own thing, right? I shouldn't drink, myself, either." He drained his beer with a pensive look on his face. "Yeah, I shouldn't drink at all. But I'm not one for taking good advice." "That makes two of us!" The conversation was quick to slip into idle chatter and fluttering laughter. Whatever had somehow drawn the two together that night seemed to have done so to open the doors of what could easily become a strong friendship, because when they spoke on about 'absolutely nothing at all', any onlooker would have sworn they'd been friends since high school. Once again, though...Amy couldn't box herself up. There was a small circle she seemed to slowly, unintentionally be getting integrated into with peers she'd wanted so badly to avoid. In all of these blossoming friendships, and though she tried to veer from the fact with a passion, fWo was starting to seem like home.
