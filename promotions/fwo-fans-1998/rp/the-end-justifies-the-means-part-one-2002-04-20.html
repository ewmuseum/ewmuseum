the end justifies the means, Part One

20 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Now, he knew only hate. Sunday, April 14, 2002: Sphere kicks out at the very last second, and Wretch, angered, tosses Sphere into Crisp, who slaps him on the back, and tags himself in. Crisp crashes a forearm into Wretch face, and quickly whips him into the ropes as the fans boo him on. Crisp sneers, and ignores Wretch, which is a mistake as Wretch executes a running Hangman's neckbreaker when he returns, and goes for the pin. One. Two. Crisp uses his momentum to reverse the pin. One. Two. Wretch kicks out at the last moment as the fans cheer Wretch on. Crisp lies on the mat, motionless as Wretch gets up, and looks around. He smiles, and races from rope to rope, and the fans known what was in store for them next: Rating Saver Legdrop Wretch executed his finisher, and hooked the leg, as Sphere entered the ring, and Tempest took him down with a bulldog. One. Two. Three. Wretch and Tempest were the winners, and Tempest and Wretch raised each others arms into the air as the fans in Fresno cheered them on. An angered Crisp, however, forearmed Wretch from behind, as did an angered Sphere. The two men pummeled the winners as the fans booed at them... Until Monte Burns comes out with a chair. Burns runs down the rampway, sliding into the ring, and pasting Crisp from behind with the chair. It was one thing that Crisp lost the match. He didn't have a problem with that. But it was another thing that the lost to Wretch. Cleanly. One thing had been forgotten in the entire hamburger debate, which had been forgotten in itself. That was good for Crisp, as he didn't care for it period. The hamburger debate was forgotten when Wretch went off to film parts for a new movie. But still, one thing had been forgotten in this feud. Neither had beaten the other cleanly. That was supposed to happen at Cyberslam V. One person was supposed to beat the other cleanly. Now, the big payoff, something that was supposed to jump start the career of a former Heavyweight Champion in three other companies, one who was once an Icon, and not just in name... ...the big payoff is now gone. This was not new to Jon Crisp. If only the public knew... ...and all he knew now was hate. This was not always the case.
