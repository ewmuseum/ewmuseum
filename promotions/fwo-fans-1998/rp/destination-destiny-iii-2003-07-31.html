Destination, Destiny: III

31 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The car ride seemed oddly similar to the one they taken before. A few key differences helped Allyra make the distinction, however. First, she was not spending her few conscious moments struggling, and second, she knew exactly where Eugene was taking her. She couldn't say that this destination made her any happier. The Illinois Sheriff's Departmet had done a good job. It took them two days to find the motel room, and two seconds to kick down the door. They were more than a bit surprised when they saw a man and woman in bed together, a blissful smile on the face of the abducted. She told them that she wouldn't press charges, and they were immediately gone. Trent had cracked...He wanted to find her. She knew that Eugene felt this, as well. Now the road led back to Harrison, to a conflict that was boud for cataclysm. What was it they said about the best-laid plans? She sighed and turned towards the window. They were in Chicago already, closer to future with every turn of the wheels. Destination, destiny. ********** The grey house stood quaintly in the summer rain. The drops fell, lukewarm, and evaporated in a matter of seconds. Trent Harrison stood in the doorway, staring at the car that just pulled up his drive. Reluctantly, Allyra opened the door and took a step outside, suddenly turning towards the motionless driver. "Why aren't you getting out, then?" He didn't answer for a while, only locking eyes with Trent. "I don't belong here. What we had there- that was not what you want. Go to him." She thought to turn, to grab him and kiss him. She thought to shout out to the sky, "I AM BETTER THAN THIS!" She thought to close the door and drive far away with this man. Somewhere, she knew that he was right. Behind the cold reason, behind the unforgiving logic, there was a something resembling truth. He watched her progress up the path to the house. Trent stood, arms folded, waiting for an explanation. Eugene looked away, for the first time. Despite his words, he was hurting inside. He turned, watching a horror movie with his hand over his eyes, but fingers spread, ready to hide again. Their embrace was an earthquake. It shook him, them, the whole world. For an instant, he was convinced that nobody existed but the three of them, linear and coherent. He turned the engine back on as the duo went inside. Reaching under his seat, he found an open bottle of Jack Daniels and drank more than he should have. He was tired, weary of travel and of feeling. He found his head down on the horn, definatly. The noise brought everyone outside except for the two people he had just said goodbye to. The early evening arrows were missing their mark. Slowly he backed out of the driveway and onto the street. The ordeal was over, but the scars would run deep. He looked up at the sky. He was paralyzed, done. Nothing could hurt him anymore, he ran free of perdition. "Stand up, turn and face me," the waxing moon called. "Don't just sit there, thinking about your freedom." Spirits weathered, he drove home.
