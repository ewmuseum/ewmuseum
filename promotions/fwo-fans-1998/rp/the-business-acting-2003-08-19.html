The Business - Acting

19 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I could sit here and babble on and on about how my world is constantly changing, and make numerous cliche's to things getting better or early worms, but I won't bore you with all that crap. I'm sitting here moments before Ruahh~! goes on the air, and there seems to be a last minute change. Like usual, the roster is the last to know when something's amiss. Seems like my three way number one contenders match isn't going to be happening on Monday, and there's a fair likelyhood it won't happen at all. So now, all I have on my plate is a little segment involving Bowman, which should be fun. But it's not why I came here tonight. It's not why I left my family at home and decided to walk through those back doors tonight. It may be a way to entertain the fans, but it's cheap. It's a cheap way to draw a reaction. It's harder to get fans on their feet by just wrestling. Look at all the cheap shots to local sports teams and all the interviews and segments that take place around the world of wrestling. In my heart of hearts, being an old school fan, I truly believe that anything we say will have less of an effect than the things we do out there in and around the ring. Talk is talk. I didn't pay for talk. It's like going to an action movie and having Arnold blab for fifty minutes about politics. That's not why fans buy the tickets. And sure, I may be one of the more talented performers in this respect, it may have been what truly got me noticed, but there's an inherit lack of detail in a wrestler's promo which separates it from the art that is a truly great wrestling match. I love to wrestle, it's why I'm here. I'm not here to act. I'm not here to say cute things, or voice my personal opinions and clog the airwaves with my dribble. I'm out there to preform, like only a true sportsman preforms. When did becoming a wrestler mean taking acting classes? When did we lose focus on the product inside those ropes. When did we CHANGE the product? And most notably... Can the common fan even remember a wrestling show that never left ringside? Because all the shots to the head I've taken over the years have purged that information from my skull. I wonder if that was reality, or just one of my whacked out dreams. My imagination usually gets the best of me. I'm not saying cutting a great promo is easy to do, because there's so much to think about. Where to stand, when to deliver. Timing is everything, it makes or breaks your performance, especially in comedic aspects. But we're still a sport. And our sport isn't called Let's make a movie. It's called wrestling. Sometimes we all forget that.
