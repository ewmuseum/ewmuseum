Two Roads Diverged

6 December 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"There's a different air about him than when he first arrived." Brent DiPetrillo looked on with fatherly wonderment as he gazed at Alex Creed through the windows of his office. He pensively held a hand to his mouth. "He's let go of some of his inhibitions, and the rate that he's picked up on things so quickly is something that I haven't seen in years." "Eight minutes in," Tiffany checked the stopwatch she held in her hand and looked at her mentor. The normal five three-minute rounds of a traditional muay-thai match had been completed, but Tiffany had wanted to see how Alex's stamina held up for one ten-minute round. "The progress he's made compared to the other kids who started out at his age is impressive, Brent. He's employing moves that I don't start teaching until six, seven, or even eight months in. And he's doing them well." Brent nodded as Alex blocked a high kick by twisting the top of his Roscoe's foot and driving his knee into his calf. "Do you think he's ready to go back?" "Those evals were done weeks ago, and whoever's in charge over at fans Wrestling's been sitting on them for whatever reason. Makes my blood boil." Tiffany scowled and snorted. "That's not what I asked you," Brent half-smiled. "I asked if you thought he's ready." Alex fell to the mat after unsuccessfully blocking a low-swing kick, but quickly kipped back up and sent Roscoe down with a cross-switch kick. Tiffany smiled on in approval. "Yeah. He's ready. He's more disciplined, more focused, and he's taking his failings in stride and working harder to make sure the same mistakes don't happen again." "You've helped him work on that temper of his too." "Well, from what he's told me, that's the Creed temper. Vic would probably do well taking a lesson from his son." She checked the stopwatch again. "He wants to stay here longer, I know that much. But he's not making any money here, and if he's not making any money then he's not helping his mother out any. Nowadays it's damn near impossible to make it on a waitress's pay. Alex understands that he needs to get back in the ring, as soon as possible." "That kid's had a hard life, no doubt about it," Brent said with a sigh. "No benefits of a father figure but all the negatives associated with being the son of a man not well liked. He's had to grow up way too fast. But then again, so did Lindsay and Alaina. And so did you." In their private conversations, Tiffany and Alex had found a close connection that came close to most sibling bonds. Both shared stories of their upbringings, and for the first time in a long time Tiffany felt comfortable sharing her life with someone somewhat close to her own age. Her father had raped her mother at a post-college homecoming dance, and he died of a drug overdose that same night. Tiffany's mother dropped out of school shortly after she was born, and raised her with little to no help from her grandparents. When Tiffany was six, her mother was killed by a drunk driver while walking home from 12 hour shift at a busy espresso bar. She was then shuffled around from foster home to foster home until she was finally adopted by a couple who were friends with Brent and Elena DiPetrillo. Tiffany's foster father, Rad, trained under Brent at the academy and taught there as well. "If Alex wanted to stay here longer," Brent continued, "I'd set him up with something. Granted, it wouldn't be nearly as glamorous or pay as much as I'm sure the fans Wrestling organization would be willing to give him, but he and his mother wouldn't be starving by any means." "He's very appreciative of everything everyone's taught him, but I've got a feeling the ring is where he belongs. He's not looking to fill his father's shoes, he's looking to make his own footprints." The stopwatch beeped. Tiffany rose from her chair and strode over to the door to call the time. She smiled with pride as Alex bowed to Roscoe and then to her. Alex was taking the road not traveled by his father. And in the long run, that would make all the difference.
