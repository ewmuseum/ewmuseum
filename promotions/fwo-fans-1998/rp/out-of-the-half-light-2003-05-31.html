Out of the Half-Light

31 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Once, I was asked why I do all of the vile, repulsive things that I do. This person looked into my eyes, and our eyes locked upon each other. His were blue, vibrant, and full of life. Mine were dark, gloomy, and long were dead. With innocence not yet corrupted by a dark society and a darker world, he really could not understand why I am the way that I am. How I wish to still be like him. There was nothing I could do. Nothing I could say. To make him understand. That when you look upon this world, when you take off the rose-colored glasses, nothing is as it seems. Does that woman seem to love you? She wants something from you, and when she takes it, she'll be done with you. Do those people seem to depend on you? They won't depend on you forever. Everyone has a hidden motive. Mankind was wrong all along. We don't have souls. We don't have absolute morals. We're just animals. Animals scraping at each other, fighting each other, and doing whatever they can to find that single, unattainable asset. Total happiness. Oh, sure, you might find temporary pleasure, whether it's by volunteering to clean up an old park or rubbing your hand against your penis. But you will never find total happiness. Because we're all just animals. Flawed mammals that have no point, no purpose, no matter how hard we try and create one. So, when that person asked me why I do all the vile, repulsive things I do. I gave him no answer. Because deep inside, he already knew. He just refused to admit it. And for all of the vile, repulsive things that I do. I could never strip away his innocence.
