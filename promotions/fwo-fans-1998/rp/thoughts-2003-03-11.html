Thoughts.

11 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Scott Slugger saw the ball clearly as it traveled toward him. I've got to hit this one... it's all on the line now. Slugger reached back, marked the angle of the ball coming in, and swung with everything he had. The ball flew over the net... and brushed the edge of the table as Mitch Wilson swung and whiffed. "Yes!" Slugger threw his arms up into the air from the left side of the ping-pong table. He wiped the sweat from his brow and picked up the Vanilla Coke bottle. He raised it to Wilson, who scowled, in an imaginary toast. "Booyaka, 21-15, I win again. Face it. You suck." "Blah blah blah. I'm glad you're proud of your world-beating table tennis abilities." "Most proud," agreed Slugger. He looked around. They were outside, in a courtyard of the hotel that Slugger, Wilson, and a few other fWoers were residing in for their time in Israel. A palm-like tree provided a modicum of shade above the ping-pong table, but the oppressive desert heat still beat down from above. Slugger walked out of the courtyard and sat down in one of the chairs set off to the side. It was set up in a walkway, and the second-level walkway above provided a great deal of shade - probably the most to be found in the entire exterior of the hotel. Even in the shade, the chair - made of vinyl or whatever the hell it was they made lawn chairs from - was sticky. Slugger grimaced. "Jesus. OK, we can't go back to America, fine. But do we really have to come out to the middle east like this? It's one big freakin' desert!" Wilson walked over to a Coke machine which was vending 20-ounce plastic bottles like Slugger's. He put in a dollar and got back a Mountain Dew. "Yeah. This bites the big one." Slugger closed his eyes as Wilson walked over. "Can you imagine what it's like back in Washington right now? It's probably 65 degrees... a brisk breeze... and maybe just the beginnings of a drizzle. I would kill for that kind of weather." Wilson nodded. "And it's been forever since I've seen an episode of The Shield or Angel or something on TV. It's such an incredible pain in the ass, taking the time to download those, and then watching them on your computer, and they're all fuzzy... and you know, I heard from Tony that the first Mariners game of the year was televised a few days ago. Not that I missed much. Apparently it was like, 'Late 2002: The Lost Game.'" Wilson leaned against the wall. "Sucks." "Yeah." Slugger took another drink. He sat in the shade with his eyes closed. Wilson looked around at nothing. Slugger sat up. "Why are we here?" Wilson cocked an eyebrow. "Well, we're here because five billion years ago, there was a cataclysmic explosion, that literally - " Slugger whistled, cutting Wilson off. "No, Mitch. Jesus. I mean why are we here, going all over the world to Hell and back just so we can go out and fight some idiot every night for fifty thousand fans who don't really give a crap anyway? What's the point?" "Make money. Prove we're the best at what we do." Wilson said it immediatley and in a way that said it should have been obvious. Slugger shrugged. "Yeah. I guess." He paused. "What has wrestling brought me, Mitch?" "I'd say fame, money, respect. Also a shitload of titles." He added this last with a crooked grin. "Hmm," Slugger said contemplatively. "All true - more or less. But it's also brought me a drug addiction that I still have to fight every day, nearly an attempted murder rap - or worse - and..." He trailed off. "And what?" Slugger didn't reply, and Wilson came to think he wouldn't. Then - "And a son I'll never see." Slugger looked up at the sky above the courtyard. "It was May when... it happened. Ten months ago. Wherever they are, I have a son now." Wilson waited to see if Slugger would continue, but he didn't. Finally he shook his head. "So go see Dr. Fucking Phil. Look, if you've got issues, Scott, fine. I probably would too if I were you. But I've got problems of my own, problems inside the ring, not out, and if you're not going to be at my side, I need to know right now. I've got fucking Chronos and his goons, for one, and then I've got Kellen Kinkade thinking God knows what, so if you've gone and decided to quit, or something like that, you better tell me." Slugger looked at Wilson calmly. "I'll be there. I'm not gonna bail on a friend and leave him in a lurch against a bunch of assholes like Frenchie and Chronos and... that other guy... and yeah, I want to find out what Kinkade is up to myself." He sighed. "I dunno. The heat must just be getting to me or something. Or - heh - maybe I just need to beat the crap out of somebody to right my mind again. I've got Borst tomorrow night, don't I?" "Yep." "Perfect. That guy's a wiener anyway. All 'bloody' this and 'bugger' that. Somebody needs to tell him he's not some vampire on Buffy. And he calls soccer 'football'! How annoying is that? Yeah... beating the crap out of Borst should do wonders for this malaise. Of course, if that doesn't work, there's always Wazerizers." "Wazerizers?" "Vanilla Coke and Southern Comfort, top with whipped cream. Wonderful." "Ah." They were silent for a moment. "You got a bad feeling about this?" asked Slugger. "Oh yeah. I don't like being here, and I don't just mean the heat." "That makes two of us." The two members of Ragnarok sat and stood, waiting for the sun to go down and the heat to relent.
