Blade Job - Chapter 7 - Ben in IPW

9 October 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It was the smell that hit Ben Jacobson first, as it always did. The stinging stench of sweat that seemed to rise from the brown canvas mat like a ominous fog. The scent of popcorn and beer that made it's way to the ring from the rows of fans that sat in so claustrophobic-close. And as always, underlying everything, the coppery hint of blood that only wrestlers and soldiers would recognize... the smell of oncoming battle. The Brooklyn Ballroom was once a theatre, in better and more prosperous times. Balconies rose high on all sides of the auditorium, and the red felt curtains still hung in the same places they had decades before. Hand-carved mohagany railings decended down long staircases that once housed tuxedo'd theatre affictionados, and now was home to rabid fans of the IPW. Horace Sloat had bought the run-down theatre for a song in the early nineties, and spent surprisingly little money replacing the stage and orchestra pit with a wrestling ring. On the antique marquee out front, where once the words "Hamlet" and "Romeo & Juliet" proudly stood, now hung the phrase "Mufanga Kagos VS Dan "Grizzly" Bayer" in blood-red letters, eight feet tall. But now, all that didn't matter at all... what mattered most to Ben right now was the 500 pound behemoth that stood on the other side of the ring. Dan "Grizzly" Bayer. Quite Simply, he looked like that old WWF wrestler named Hillbilly Jim, if old Jim had a mean drinking problem and grew up torturing small animals. The man called Grizzly wore old tan workboots that matched faded and stained denim overalls. He wore no shirt, and hair thick as a New York mink coat covered his huge barrel chest. He had a wooly brown heard and thick bushy eyebrows, made strange by the fact that his head was chrome-bald and just as shiny. His tiny weasel-like eyes darted and shifted about the ring. The match was booked as a grudge match, and the fans ate it up. They loved the Philly Killer, and they HATED Dan Bayer for killing him, as their five dollar programs said they should. That was one thing old Sloat could do, thought Ben as he looked out at the wild-eyed fans, he could promote a match with the best of them. The fans chanted the Mufanga name as the referee called for the opening bell. And then things went sour fast. All five hundred pounds of Dan Bayer charged across the ring, surprisingly quick for a man of his immense size. Ben, who was now instantly Mufanga Kagos, Wildman of Lagos, ducked nimbly out of the way and placed a perfectly-pulled enziguri kick to the back of Bayer's head. Bayer sold nothing, and turned about quickly. Although Ben and Bayer had not spoken before the match, it was assumed that Ben would go over in a dominant way, and would put an end to the feud that Kevin "Philly Killer" MacKay's death had cut short. It was, as Horace Sloat has put it just hours before, a one time deal that would have Mufanga Kagos squash the villian in a convincing manner, letting Bayer get no offence, and then they would play the video tribute packaage for MacKay. It was to be a fine send-off, with Kevin's mortal enemy vanquished by his friend, Mufanga Kagos, and all things put right at last. Apparently no one told Grizzly about this. Dan Bayer reached out and grabbed Mufanga by the throat with both hands, lifting his bare feet clean off the canvas. Growling a gutteral "Ruarrrgh", he thew the black man the length of the ring, where Kagos hit the turnbuckle in a boneless heap. Bayer charged in, and all the weight of the giant hillbilly crushed into Mufanga, pinning him to the turnbuckle. The breath went out of him in a shot, and the smaller man fell to the mat. "What... the fuck?" Mufanga gasped as his dreadlocks were grasped in one ham-sized fist and he was jerked, howling, to his feet. "Welcome to tha' IPW, boy-ah." Bayer growled into the black man's face, just before he reared back and dropped Kagos again with a ferocious headbutt. Ah, The good ol' IPW, thought Ben, as his head reeled. This and all the Indy feds just like it. They operated on a different set of rules, and it ususally meant the survival of the fittest. Ben grew up in Indy feds, and he knew the foul in-ring booking changes that hopeful maineventers used to get their characters over. This was just like any other stiff squash match that Ben was part of, but still it felt different somehow. Ben took a hardway DDT from Jake Roberts almost a year earlier, a DDT that had Ben spend most of early 2003 in a neck brace, but there was a motive to that. Roberts was an asshole, but a smart asshole. He knew that Jacobson was angling for Roberts' place in that old westcoast fed, and he made sure that Ben wasn't going to wrestle anytime soon. But this... this was different. Dan "Grizzly" Bayer knew that this was a one-shot deal. He knew that Ben was going to take this win, but Bayer was poised for a Title run in the IPW. After this match, Ben was gone anyway, back to the fWo. What the hell was he doing this for? Either way, Ben Jacobson had seen this type of grabass booking before, and he knew how to get out of it. He'd just rebook himself a win. Right now. Hardway.
