12/29/2001 10:00AM Hurry... hurry... hurry... A lot was riding on this... namely his reputation, and a little bit of pride. A lot of trust had been put in him for this. In his entire wrestling career, the Boston Strangler had never been late for an autograph session. Not once. Today was looking like it might be the end of the streak, what with the clock striking 10, and he wasn't yet at the card shop. He always wondered why signings had to happen in the smallest storefronts known to man. He had seen prison cells with more square footage... Finally, he made it to the back of the store, slightly winded but none the worse for wear. The security guard posted at the door checked his ID, nodded, and let him in. The Strangler made his way over to the table that had been set up, and checked over the prints that he was going to use for that particular session. It was a pretty good photo, one of him standing with the belt after the Hell in a Cell match with Erik Kelly. That match had taken quite a bit out of him, he recalled... and this photo was snapped at just the right time... before things got very ugly. Funny that this photo represented the last time he had felt true victory in the fWo... it was probably his proudest moment of the year, and judging by the photo one couldn't know that what happened in the next 5 minutes changed the fWo completely. Al, who owned the shop, checked over the merchandise, and stepped forth to unlock the door. As usual, they expected a wave of people, of varying ages and walks of life. Such were the fans of wrestling. The door opened, and to the surprise of the Strangler, about 7 people came in. He couldn't believe it. Seven people... In no time, the autographs were signed, the small talk was over, and he was left to wait for the session to be up with nothing but idle time on his hands. Al seemed to be taking it better than the Strangler could have hoped, but he still felt pretty guilty about it. People just weren't showing up for this. But who could blame them? Fans followed a winner... something he hadn't been for the last couple of months... but he couldn't help but feel like for all he had done for them over the years, they had turned their back on him when he needed their support the most. Seven goddamned people. "Maybe the ads didn't get out in time... maybe people are away for the holidays still, you know this is a tough time to get them in here... I'm sorry to be wasting your time..." "It's OK, Al. We're sorry not enough people are showing up" the Strangler replied with a slight shrug. He tried to tell himself that it was just business, and that these things happened... but inside, he felt terrible. "Maybe... I don't know, hell, maybe they're shopping? I heard there are some big sales going on to try to boost the awful Christmas revenues..." "...Or maybe, Al... they just don't want to see the Boston Strangler right now" he thought to himself.
