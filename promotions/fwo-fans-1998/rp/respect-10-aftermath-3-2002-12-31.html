Respect (10 - Aftermath 3)

31 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Well, I did it." And I had done it. However, realizing how close I had come to crossing the line that I swore I'd never cross, I wondered it if was all worth it. The only consolation that I had was that I hadn't actually crossed the line, and I had still managed to do what I had set out to do. Though I had very nearly ended a man's career not once, but twice, less than twenty minutes earlier, somehow, I still managed to feel good about myself. Thankful that the crowd had stopped me from bludgeoning Sean, I could only hope that his words in the ring were sincere. After a shower, I'd go find him and Eli, and congratulate Sean on another great match, apologize for how I had acted, and wish Eli luck on his match for the night, which was sure to bring the house down just like they had the past two nights. As I made my way to the shower, a realization came over me that put a little more spring into my step, in spite of the various aches and pains that filled my weary body: It had all been worth it. However, troubled thoughts continued to torment me: in relentlessly pursuing my goal, I had almost lost sight of what had gotten me this far in my life. I had nearly cast aside all decency and humanity and tried to maliciously end the career of a fellow competitor, a man that I had once hoped to call a friend, and whom I now could only pray would give me that chance. I stopped, the warm mater cascading over my bruised and battered body, and was forced to question if I could've actually ended Stevens' career by shattering his knee. The realization that I could've was a bittersweet one, and it made me regret my actions all the more. Part of me tried to rationalize that I hadn't actually gone so far, crossed that final line that would've made me grow to loathe myself. But the rational part of my brain was outweighed by a familiar voice that told me just by putting the chair around his knee, I had, in fact, crossed that line. No, I told myself, just because I could've perpetrated so heinous an act, that doesn't mean I actually would've gone through with it. I could've stopped myself, just as I did with the microphone minutes later. But was that truly the case? I couldn't remember much about my thought processes during the match, but it stood to reason that I hadn't just positioned the chair where I did for dramatic effect, didn't it? The shower forgotten, I rushed from the stall, the last remnants of soap still clinging to my body. Nearly knocking the stall door from its hinges, I began to vomit, the force of each upheaval driving my already sore head hard into the toilet seat, which sent another wave of nausea through my body and forced another stomach spasm. When the nausea had finally passed, I drew myself up slowly, and stumbled back into the shower. The once-warm water now nearly scalded me, but I didn't care. I needed to wash the last vestiges of the past month from my body, and this shower would serve both as a wake-up call and a baptism: there was no way I could ever let something like this take control of me so relentlessly again. I had reached the point of Faust's dilemma: was gaining the world really worth the price of my soul? Thankfully, as Faust had, I had chosen an alternate route, and now my only concern was ensuring that no permanent damage had been done. Quickly throwing on my clothes, I made my way as hastily as I could from my locker room and down the hall. Knocking on the doorway, I could only hope that the two men I hoped to see would receive me. And then, suddenly, the door opened, revealing an emotionally and physically drained Sean Stevens. And though he was clearly surprised to see me, I was relieved to see that he didn't look too thoroughly upset. "Sean, listen...We need to talk..."
