Silence

23 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
She blew her hair out of her eyes, lowering them to her half drunk glass that lay before her. The rings on the coffee table had taken their toll on the old oak finish, but who was there to really notice? Her finger ran seductively over the ring, the repetitive motion produced a musical sound that filled the room. Tilting her head and looking out of the glass screen door to her side, she saw her son playing in a sandbox by himself. That's when the sand pit became a desert in her eyes, and he was trapped in there alone. The sands overtook him like a wave. A sigh. The silence now that she'd stopped rubbing the top of her glass was too much to handle, so she grabbed the remote to her expensive stereo system and pressed play, before a string version of "Pretty Good Year" by Tori Amos played. Leaning back in her leather couch, which could double as a recliner, she looked around at all the things that they've afforded over Jack's illustrious career. A digital flatscreen television, numerous stereo speakers that simply surround you in sound, various nic-naks and statues that add character to the house. But it was still missing something. It missed him. ** He stared out of the airplane window, feeling a bit relieved that he actually got a seat nearby it. Without much else to amuse his time during takeoff, he loved to just look over the side of the plane to the little people below them. See the buildings the size of legos and the cars driving around as if they were a self-controlled match box cars. The seat next to him wasn't anyone he knew, a plain old passenger like any other. Seemed like most of the boys steered clear of him lately, for whatever reason. Maybe it was because they weren't sure where his in ring personality ended and his actual self took over. But that probably wasn't it. They probably all just hated him. He scoffed to himself, looking up to the stewardess button above his head and praying that the plane would level off shortly. He wasn't a huge drinker, but the problems of his life had begun to weigh down on him, and the occasional rum and coke, while not the solution, helped him forget about the hate and the anger for at least a few moments. The seatbelt was unfastened. And yet no one had noticed. It was almost a metaphor for his entire life thus far. He had the two pieces of his life, his family, and his career, and they could never hold together. They could never click. That's when the plan leveled off, and the fastened seatbelt sign switched off completely. Now, he didn't have to fix his problem. At least, not until tomorrow's flight.
