Fight Club Part 2

29 December 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I can remember the moment I lost all hope. It was liberating. Your wife is dead. They were unable to recover the body. They told me I was marketable as a pissed off kid who had lost everything. My parents left. Alicia left. When my parents left, I lost my future. When Alicia left, I lost my hope. I understand you're in a bad spot right now, but the fact remains, you signed a contract. Either you honor the dates you've agreed to or you're in breach of contract and you'll be blackballed from the business forever. Probably not now when you've just started. Losing all hope was my ticket out of there. We want you to really open up and grab those heartstrings with both hands and pull. The more sympathy you can get, the better. People are here. People die. We kill them. We don't. We all look the same in the end. We're sick of hearing about your poor dead wife. Come up with something else or you're gonna find yourself out of a job real quick. What you did doesn't matter. What you're going to do is important. It's better to base your life on a potential future than a lucrative past. That way, when the midtown bus plows into you and changes you from thinking, functioning person into cold, lifeless object, the people who pretend to care can talk about wasted potential and hope other people who pretend to care notice how deep they are. You didn't suffer in the past, I suffered in the past. What you went through isn't shit. I'm in pain so I'm going to cause you pain. When you have that lucrative past and that potential future, people who are better at what you do because they say so will take every opportunity to tell you that they're better at what you do, because they say so. You're a disgrace to everything we stand for, everything we've worked for. You should be fired. When the people who do what you do better than you do it get hurt because you do it better than they do, they blame you for their incompetence. They grew up being told they can be the best at everything and anything, and when they find out it's a lie, it's your fault for succeeding. I know this because Eli knows this. What could my motivation be for beating you again? I've already done it twice. I have nothing to gain from doing it a third time. It's only a big deal to do something better than someone else if they're famous for it. Who cares when Christopher Celebrity beats up on Joe Whoever in the back of an alley, or in the middle of an arena. But when Joe Whoever is suddenly on the tip of everyone's tongue, and Christopher Celebrity is sweeping floors, it's the biggest deal in the world. You've got the name recognition and the worldwide respect. I've got a bingo hall and an oversized ego. But I beat you twice when you were nothing, therefore I'm more successful than you. I am Eli's smirking victory.
