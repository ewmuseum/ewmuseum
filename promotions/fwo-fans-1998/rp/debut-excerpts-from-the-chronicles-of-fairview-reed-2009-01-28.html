Debut (...excerpts from The Chronicles of Fairview Reed)

28 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Our arrival is imminent, and as the sporting coliseum looms before us I cannot help but tremor with a bit of uncertainty and nerves. That I will be in the ring tonight there can be no doubt – I have taken the necessary steps to ensure my inclusion in tonight’s spectacle. I still do not see the wisdom or practicality of allying ourselves to this wrestling federation but Mrs. McPhineas’ judgment and forethought have proven to be beyond reproach; in all matters mundane and material Mrs. McPhineas is invaluable. And thus I shall let myself be directed by her and take a few precious moments from my research and participate in tonight’s event. Mrs. McPhineas and Mr. Yip have assured me that I will not be required or expected to attend this falderal but once a fortnight, and that we all shall be safe upon the soil of the Colonies and not be forced to travel overseas. I have also been informed (with great enthusiasm and eagerness!) that Mrs. McPhineas has secured a residence for our entourage and that for the first time in many, MANY months we shall have a base-of-operations if you will, a place that we can rest our weary heads without fear of terrifying midnight flights or vicious visceral nightmares. As much as I miss Hurtheart Manor I am forced to recognize its flaws and shortcomings and believe our new homestead shall be the fortress we require, and leave it to Mrs. McPhineas to find such a dwelling on an island! We shall be doubly protected indeed! The only impediment to rest and relaxation on Mercer Island is a brief stop at the Key Arena. I shall take the precious time from my research and perform – a monkey dancing to corporate organ music – but my appearance shall be as brief and perfunctory as can be; I have business much MUCH more important and pressing than engaging in faux physical combat. Again, I must not dwell on this and trust in Mrs. McPhineas. I am certain that she knows what is best for us and is confident in this course of action.
