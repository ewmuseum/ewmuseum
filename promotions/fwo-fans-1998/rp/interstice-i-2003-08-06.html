Interstice - I

6 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Eugene stared into the cereal, stirring his spoon around to make words out of the Alphabits. He'd gotten "CAB," and was now regretting that he'd eaten most of the marshmallow vowels. Breakfast at two in the afternoon meant time off of the road, and the feeling of novelty was beginning to wear off for Eugene Marder. Restless, he pushed the bowl aside and sat down unceremoniously in front of the television. Nothing on. He settled for a re-run of Even Stevens on the Disney Channel and leaned back on a recliner, staring around the apartment. The small one-bedroom affair in the heart of Los Angeles' artsy Silverlake area had been his home for the past four years, though his roommate had long since moved out. It was definitely the apartment of a twenty-two year old would be the first thought of any visitor. The contents of a gym bag were strewn around the front door, and the kitchen housed a heap of dishes under which one could only assume lay a sink. An expired milk carton stood on the countertop, precariously close to the edge. The coffee table that stood in front of a beat-up couch was smothered in sports and video game magazines while a hodge-podge of twisted wires and video game controllers adorned the living room area as a whole. In the corner, by a leather chair and a reading lamp, stood several acoustic guitars, with various accessories being the primary occupants of an adjoining bookshelf. The room would be sure to give Christopher Lowell an aneurism, but it suited the occupant's desires. In his youth, Eugene was always surrounded by pristine, almost sterile, conditions, and he supposed he was still in his rebellious phase. He considered packing the gym bag again for the afternoon's training session, but decided to remain where he was, only half-watching the program. After two days of simple relaxation following his suspension, Eugene was massively discontent. He decided he would began training again, partly out of bordeom, but mainly because he wanted to impress when he'd return. It was hard to tell his family why he wasn't performing on the two fWo shows that were so close to home, especially since he couldn't blame it on some phantom injury. Thanks to the marvels of the internet, they all knew about his run-in with the law. Luckily, not many were aware of the details surrounding Eugene and Allyra's time together. Either way, Eugene had resolutely promised himself and his family that he would come back to the fWo in better shape than ever, ready to rescue what was left of his floundering career. He knew it would take a healthy dose of convincing with a dash of begging to persuade Silver Fox to allow him to wrestle seriously again, but he knew it was possible. Though his blue-chipper status was gone, he could re-start with more vigor than ever. Having taken the easy way in with Survivor II, Marder had taken things for granted, and he'd be the first to admit it, now. But, like everthing when exposed to time and circumstance, he had changed. Between the hours at the gym with Mark, his trainer, and the independent work-outs he'd regimented for himself, Eugene's days were largely boring. This was the age when he began to realize that he was an adult. His friends in the area were getting married, advancing their careers. The doctors and lawyers secretly scoffed at his line of work while the mechanics and restaurant managers envied how he had it easy. Meanwhile, Eugene was stuck with no love interest and no friends to share a beer with at four in the afternoon. Sure, he'd get together with some of his buddies, but every time they pencilled him in for a late power lunch, he felt more and more like a burden. Boredom, therefore, was a constant. Even Jack, Eugene's best friend and doctor, was now Dr. John Taylor, M.D. For these two weeks, Eugene wasn't even Martyr. The phone rang, interrupting all other thoughts. Eugene hoped it would be someone of substance, someone calling from fWo, telling him that they'd need him to come in earlier. He muted the TV and jumped to his feet, in the process painfully hitting his ankle on the corner of the coffee table and knocking over a half-empty mug of mango juice. While the phone rang, he watched the juice seep into the carpet with a look of helplessness, then hopped to the receiver on one foot. "Hello?" His voice was chipper and welcoming. "No, thanks, I already get the Times." He hung up the phone with audible disappointment and looked at his feet pensively. ********** It was early evening when he'd decided to take the hour-long walk home from the gym, leaving his car behind. He had nothing better to do at home, and he figured he would just take the bus back the next day. A cool summer breeze in Los Angeles was hardly anything one would want to waste, anyway, so he began the trek, weary but content. Training had been hard, but, ultimately, satisfying. He felt like when he'd return, it would be in a very impressionable way. By the time he was nearing his house, it was already quite dark, somewhere near ten o'clock, Eugene guessed. The last part of the hike was uphill, but he savored the walk. It gave him a chance to see the neighborhood a bit, something he hadn't enjoyed in a while. Silverlake was a unique town, as evidenced by the mat sitting on his front porch alone, eating cashews and drinking a Gatorade. Eugene smiled at him and walked on. Further up the hill, Eugene caught a glimpse of the exterior of Spaceland, a rock club he'd often attended when he was young. It was always a twenty-one-and-over venue, and reminiscing about he and his friends sneaking through the back put a smile on his face as he walked by. Soft music was playing from the inside, and it only assisted in taking him back to his youth. Suddenly, he stopped in his tracks. Of all the peculiar sights he'd seen living in the area, he was witnessing something that genuinely surprised him. Outsidea house's door, a skunk was eating food from a dish. Having never observed a skunk before, or even known that they live in the heart of populated cities, the small animal piqued his curiosity. Eugene stared for a minute or so, wondering whether it was a pet or a wild animal. Neither would seem more out of the ordinary. Chuckling to himself, he turned to walk home. A face. Sunken. Hollow. Emaciated. Weathered. He noticed it even before he felt the feeble hands grabbing his shoulders. His immediate instinct was to pull free, but he stayed. The hands felt old, weak, powerless. Somehow, he knew that the person who had stopped him madly in the middle of the night was not after the contents of his bag. The ancient face stared for a few more seconds, sputtering, then smiled a toothless smile that made Eugene wince with shock. "He's coming!" The man standing in front of him, who by now Eugene had noticed was at least a foot shorter then himself, cried the words with passion and genuine fear. To Eugene's silent reply, he added, "For you! He's coming for you!" Swallowing hard, Marder narrowed his eyes. "Who is? Who are you?" "He's coming and you won't even know!" Eugene took a few steps back, heading toward his house. "He'll do to you what he did to me!" The wispy white hair, the maniacal cackling, and the smell of chalk was too much for Eugene. He turned, stumbling, and ran uphill as fast as he could, slinging his bag back over his shoulder. He cast one final glance toward the man, who was advancing slowly, the laugh now risen to a roar heard which Eugene was sure the entire city could hear. Home was a sanctuary. Eugene made sure that no strange figures lurked about when he slid the key into his apartment door and went inside, leaning against the wall and slumping to the floor. He wasn't sure if he was still hearning the laughter or if it was imagined, but somewhere, it was present. Suddenly, he began to question it all, wondering whether the scariest moment of his entire life had, indeed, been imagined.
