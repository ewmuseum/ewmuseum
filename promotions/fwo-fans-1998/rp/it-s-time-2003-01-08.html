It's Time

8 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Tuesday, January 7, 2003. Late evening. Scott Wasner. A hotel room in New York. Wasner was sitting on the edge of the bed. He wore a Koren Robinson jersey, sweat pants, and a sharp scowl. His cell phone was to his ear, and though it wa clear he was talking to a friend, it was also clear that he was angry (though not at the person on the other end of the line) and in pain. And who could blame him? He had only lost the only woman he'd ever loved - for the second time - and his son, a son he only found out about days before. He had also been hanged, if only for a moment. Such a chain of events weren't generally known to make men all flowers and sunshine. "It's time. After what happened at Countdown... I can't justify it anymore. I can't keep you on the shelf." Pause. "Right, exactly. If you were there, none of that shit would've ever gone down. That sick bastard Winters would've never had the chance to hang me, and St - and nothing else would've happened either. All of that could've been avoided. I was a moron not to bring you in before this." Pause. "I wanted to wait for the best possible moment. Yeah, in the rankings of all-time backfires, I think that's a consensus top 5 if I've ever seen one." Pause. "Right. Sunday. No way it could be tomorrow. I'm still hurting like hell and I haven't even left New York anyway. Don't ask me why, since this place is a shithole. Bunch of Yankee fans... Don't forget your visa, since we're gonna be in Canada now. Some idiot got caught doing what I always used to get away with." Pause. "Yeah, got it in one." Pause. "No, nobody specific. Not yet, anyway. Winters... what do I have to fight him for anymore anyway? I lost all that. All that's left is what he did to me... and he's hardly the only guy on the list of people who have viciously attacked me. I'm not forgetting about him... I'm sure we'll run into each other again before our lives are over... and I know that if we both went after him full-bore we could end him once and for all... but for now, I'm going into a holding pattern. Body Count's coming up... I think I'd like to focus on that right now. It's been too long since I main evented a CyberSlam. I want it." Pause. Wasner laughed. "Right, if you don't get there first." Pause. "Not yet, no. It's not time yet. I know what that did to me last time... but this is different. You and me... we're invincible. In-fucking-vincible. Anybody who gets in our way... will be dust. Dust in the wind, baby." Pause. A long one - some of it by Wasner. Finally, he started speaking again, slowly. The anger had gone from his face, and the pain was ruling it. The cocky, angry edge to his words - Scott Slugger seeping into Scott Wasner - was gone. "Oh. Yeah. That's exactly what I wanted to talk about. Is the way about a week ago I got my heart stomped out and pissed on. What's wrong with you, man? You used to have more tact." Pause. "No, nothing to be sorry about. I just... shit, I don't know. All of that... and you want to hear something? After Countdown 3. I should be in a hospital at the time, or on my way to one. I'm walking out to my car. My neck feels like it's unhinged. My head feels like the Stay-Puft Marshmallow Man is dancing the cha-cha on it. Everything on me hurts, and my face is covered in dried blood because I'm too sore to get in the shower and wash it off, or even wet a rag to do it. This 13-year old punk bastard... I don't know how he got into the wrestler's lot, but he did... he comes up to me. And he says, 'Hey, man. Autograph?" And he thrusts this t-shirt or something at me. Like I owe it to him. Like I OWE IT TO HIM. The best I can do is shake my head, because I can't exactly talk at the time. And you know what he says? 'Fuck you.' 'Fuck you.' I know, New Yorkers, but still. I don't know why I didn't turn his lights out right there... but I didn't. I just kept walking. I think the little shit spat at me, but I don't know for sure." Wasner paused - not to let the other person talk, but to gather his thoughts. "These are the people I'm supposed to cater to? To play to? To kowtow to? You know, they've been booing me the last few weeks too. Some of 'em. Because I've been going too far in what I've been doing to Winters. I'd like to see what they'd do if somebody violated their life like that. They sit back in their seat or their couch and they munch their nachos and drink their Vanilla Cokes and cheer their heroes and boo their villians and they never think about hey, these people I'm watching aren't just little men on their XBox, they're real people. They have a real life. They hurt. They bleed. They die. Look at what they let happen to Mick Foley. He'll be lucky if he's not in a nursing home in five years because of what he gave them. His body. Because they wanted to cheer just a little more. I'd like to see what they'd do if they were me. Fuck me? No. Fuck them." Pause. "Yeah. I know. Listen, it's getting late. I'll see you Sunday." Pause. "Yeah, I can't wait either. I'll talk to you later." Wasner pressed the button to end the conversation, and tossed the cell phone aside. He continued sitting on the edge of the bed, and tried very hard not to cry.
