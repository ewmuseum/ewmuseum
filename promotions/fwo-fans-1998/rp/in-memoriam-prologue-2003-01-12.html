In Memoriam It was cold for Arizona. Of course, the morning sometimes got that way, especially in the middle of winter. The morning frost had just begun to melt in the cemetery as Kyle Dillon entered it on foot, carrying a bouquet of carnations and entirely alone. He'd avoided coming here for years, even when his parents were buried, he refused to walk upon this lawn of death. The visual presence of his own breath reminded Dillon of the ghosts that lay within as he began his search. He didn't even know where they were buried. Why had he allowed himself to be such a...a...child? Kyle knew he was still growing. He may have been twenty-seven years old, but there was so much left to learn about this game we call life. He had been searching for some monument or marble headstone with such grandeur as to illustrate the quality of his parents, which is why he was so surprised to see the simple footstones that he found. Richard Dillon 1954-1999 Mariah Dillon 1955-2002 Upon the sight of these simple reminders of the people he had been closest to, Kyle didn't know why he had expected so much more. They had been buried as modestly as they had lived and died. God, they were young. It wasn't fair that such wonderful people to die before they could truly enjoy the fruits of their labor, but the rough, nearly impoverished lifestyle they lived brought them to their ends. After taking several minutes to reflect on where he had come from, who he was, and where he hoped to go, Kyle crouched at the head of their graves and traced the letters on each his parents' footstones. Feeling did not bring any sense of reality to this event, and Kyle found it hard to fight back the illusion that he would return home to find his mother and father there. The first tear did not come until he divided the flowers and laid them just before the footstones. It was not followed. "Well, you guys...I'm here. I don't know why I let it take me so long. Fear, I guess...a fear of having to accept that this was all real. I'm sorry, and I miss you guys.
