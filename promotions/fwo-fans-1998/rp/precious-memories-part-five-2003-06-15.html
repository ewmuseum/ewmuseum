Precious Memories ~ Part Five

15 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Again, we are whisked back to that magical year of 1995... Well, as it turned out...the bum was a squealer. Right after that strange girl from the alley had left, her blood splatter leaving a trail on the pavement, he had come to. His pride was aching and hurt moreso than his skull - and he just wouldn't stand for it. That evening he had gone to the cops. He would tell them everything about the incident, complete with a few imbelishments that could not be attributed to his drink-damaged mind. The old man knew precisely what he was doing. It all ended up working out according to plan. When he had reported the incident to the police, they immediately put out the word on a teenaged girl, nearly six feet tall...caucasian, black hair green eyes. Last seen wearing dirty black pants, a ripped Misfits t-shirt, black motorcycle jacket and beaten Doc Martens. There weren't too many girls around like that in those days and well, the sight of such a woman is still somewhat of a rarity. Especially ones with significant injuries to the face and body. The police assured the bum that they would be able to locate her. They were right. ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- The officer wasn't so kind when he found the young woman. Jade had fallen asleep on a park bench that morning after she left Bill's place. He had given her a spot on his couch, but she had spent the night tossing and turning uncomfortably. The couch had been perfect...staying in the house made her suffocate. This place was much better suited to her liking. The nightstick plunged sharply into her shoulderblade, jerking Jade violently from sleep. Excrutiating pain jolted through her back, shooting into her chest. Reflex brought her to a sitting position, only to be greeted by another blow to the back. That blow caused her to double right over and gasp for breath. "Rise and shine, Miss Hunter!" The cop said wryly, enjoying every moment of this. He had dealt with her before in similar scenarios. Just about every single one of them on the force had. "Get up! You've got quite the day ahead of you!" Jade let out a pained groan and angled her head up to look at the officer. She vaguely recognized his face but had no idea what his name was. A hoarse cough followed. "Don't have much to say this time, huh?" He laughed in a sick little way, still brandishing the nightstick. It was practically burning a hole in his hand. "Get on your feet, now." The cop raised the nightstick slightly, his arm pulling back at the elbow as his grip tightened. Still in a great deal of pain and short of breath, lungs burning, Jade stood up. She wanted nothing more than to retalliate, but her body just wouldn't let her. "Boy, that's a pretty nasty cut you got there. I wonder where you might have gotten something like that..." He commented with a calculated casualty. With her lips pulling into a sneer, she raised her eyes to the cop. Still, she said nothing. Perhaps part of her knew that silence was her safest option. But that reasonable part rarely came through in her. It was more likely the case that those blows from the nightstick had really done some damage. "Hmmm. Well, you know I have a little theory about that." he answered his own question. "I think you might have done something pretty bad yesterday, and had to be punished for it. Something tells me that you assaulted an old drunk and his friends jumped you as payback. Tell me, what kind of broken bottle did they use?" He sounded so proud of himself for that one. Again, no response. "I asked you a question, Hunter." he insisted. Jade noticed that he was raising the club again. She braced herself for another hit. "You better answer me. You're already in a lot of trouble." He cautioned her like a parent to a child. Jade glared up at him through her black mask of hair. The officer just shook his head with a laugh that turned into a snarl as he drove the end of the club into Jade's gut. She yelped and doubled over again. This time, her lungs completely emptied. The surge of crushing pain momentarily convinced her that she was about to die. As she struggled for just one breath, the policeman just stared down at her. He reached for his cuffs. "That's just about enough for now. You're coming with me." He said as he secured the cuffs around the choking teen's wrists. She was yanked up forcefully and stuffed into the back of the waiting police car. ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- The first stop en route had been the local emergency room. That day, the police had been feeling generous enough to allow her face to be stitched back together. But, the luxury of being someplace like a hospital was short-lived. And now, here she was, back in a small cell at the station. There had been a total of four people in there that afternoon. Two of them were women picked up the previous night on charges of prostituion and the other was a man who had been charged in a bar fight. Jade had claimed a spot on one of the benches against the far wall. She had been stretched out there for her entire stay, not uttering so much as one word. Jade was too busy listening. Not to the sounds of the police station, or the conversations of her other inmates...but to her oldest friend and dearest compainion. Doctors would later refer to it as a psychotic hallucination, created by Jade's mind for the purposes of coping with the slightest bit of difficulty. It was a manifestation of her illness. But to Jade, it was a source of guidance and comfort. It was something divine, quite possibly the voice of God himself, though he never claimed to be. He never claimed to be anything but the truth. "...you see Jade, its not that bad, is it? You've been here before, my dear." he spoke in a whisper. The voice always spoke like that. "No. Its not that bad. At least its warm..." she answered quietly. Oh, the others in the cell and the jail knew that she was talking to herself. At first it had seemed quite strange, but that had drowned her out now. The girl seemed crazy...it was best just to leave her to her own devices. "That it is. It doesn't seem like you hate it that much. You're here enough." "Too much." she said with a blank expression. "Why?" "Its all part of your plan, that's why. Everything that you see, say and do has a purpose. Everybody has one...its just that not everyone can see it. You can, Jade. You're special." It spoke with the voice many imagined that a storybook character would have - whimsical and enchanting. "But you know this already." "Yes. I do." she said, her voice sounding nearly robotic. "Something is bothering you though. I can tell." Jade just stared ahead, at nothing at all. Something definitely was bothering her. For the first time since she had known him, the voice wasn't comforting her so much. She had been puzzling over the reason why all day long...and thought that she had finally reached a conclusion. It was because of Bill Randolph. There was something about him that was interfering with the workings of her best friend. Jade didn't know whether to hate him for it, or love him for it. The truth was that she wanted to be wanted more than anything. At this point in her life, she didn't know that...and probably wouldn't for some time. Sitting alone in that cell, Jade had wanted someone on the outside to be worrying about her...to be out in the car searching for her. But...there was no one to do that. Who? Her deceased father...the man who had died under "mysterious circumstances" as reported by the newspapers and TV? The man who's death headlines she couldn't get out of her head. Or what about her mother? Damn crack-addicted whore never stuck around long enough to see her daughter's first birthday. Jade shook her head, trying to release those thoughts. "You shouldn't waste your time thinking about them, Jade. That won't get you anywhere. I, will get you somewhere." His words brought her back to reality, back to the jail cell. "Yeah...you're right." ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- "Hey Hunter!" a male voice, a real, physical male voice called out. The jingling of keys followed. "Get up! You're getting out of here." Sounds of a key fitting a lock and the door opening ensued. Jade rubbed her crusted eyes as she sat up. Her entire body moaned with stiff pain. As she opened her eyes, the blurry picture turned clearer. The policeman escorted her out of the cell. "What's going on...?" she asked in a scratchy voice. "Someone posted your bail. He's over there." Squinting a little, she saw something that took her breath away. There was Bill Randolph, looking as relieved as she had ever seen anyone look. He hurried over to meet her. "Holy shit! I was so worried about you, kid!" he exclaimed, wrapping her in a big bear hug. Jade just let him do it, though not returning the emotion or embrace. "Bill..." "Shut up. Don't say anything. You're coming home with me." he said with a big smile. Finally, he pulled away from her. When he turned his back to lead her out of the station, Jade cracked a tiny smile. At the same time, though...she was filled with a sense of dread. What would her friend think? Until he came out and voiced his disapproval, Jade would try not to think about it. All that mattered now is that this kindly semi-stranger had done a miraculous thing. He gave a damn about a hopeless case.
