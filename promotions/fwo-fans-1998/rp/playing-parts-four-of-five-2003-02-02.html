Playing Parts - Four of Five

2 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The sunny side of Pine Street was always something people enjoyed on the weekends. Chicago residents, out-of-towners and flocks of tourists came to the different shops and cafes to sit back, relax and share memories from their visits. The local street was a great place to meet people, in any weather they scurried on the sidewalks or stood near a street corner to engage small talk with new and old faces. This was one of the better places of Chicago's great city, considerably less touched by the crimes and violence, it encouraged gathering spots and hang out areas. Steve Arden walked down the sidewalk with a happy step and expression on his face, nothing could ruin his day and his regular polite, always helpful self came back. As a young child he had been laughed at and discarded for being such a goody 2-shoes kid. He offered anyone help, for any task and this sometimes backfired on him. Although it wasn't his fault, he was born in a religious family and grew up in the church. His father was a local pastor, respected and loved by the community while his mother was the organ player at every mass and wedding in town. They never lived in absolute bliss, money was sometimes an issue but they understood the far more important virtues needed to live happy. Steve's father owned a small cottage house on the outskirts of the Chicago pollution in the country where chickens and horses roamed the free land. He never was a farmer, but lived old-fashioned. He met Mary Lombardi, a bright young woman of italian descent and exquisite beauty. Her olive oil skin struck him during a song hailing the Virgin Mary, a coincidence? Perhaps, but he saw it as fate. After the traditional closing ceremonies he hurried to spot her and introduce himself. They instantly clicked and fell in lust. Blessings from her father let them see each other and later on join together in holy matrimony. They moved closer to the city and conceided their first child, Steve. He had an italian shape and olive oil skin but his father's distinct and proud eyes. As a kid, Steve grew up helping around the church and learned the values of being a good man at a ripe age. His father taught him the word of God and brought him to church every weekend. Do unto others, it was a motto he unawarily took upon himself throughout his life, but to him it was just a natural thing. And so, Steve walked continued his path down Pine Street. Coming to a street corner, he saw on the opposite side of the road an elderly woman trying to cross the street with three grocery bags in every hand. She waddled down the lane reserved for the people who travelled on foot with misery. Physically, she looked exausted, her skin a frail white tone and wrinkled to the core, her back arched toward the front and her figure could be blowed away by the slightest winds. Arden quickly jogged towards her and politely asked to help her, she accepted with joy. Steve grabbed the bags and slowly brought them across the street. The old woman happily thanked him, complimenting him of being a wonderful young man. His nature remained, and it would have to stay that way as an fWo Superstar. He knew the gimmick wasn't pleaseant, but he had to go through with it. Here passes 'Action' Steve Arden.. And The Show, Must Go On.
