Reacting to reAction - Ground Zero in T-Minus Two

4 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
This is too much. I've locked myself in the basement to mat out all of the activity upstairs. Miss Ivy and Rosie and Lowell are all watching television, or, Lowell and Miss Ivy are watching television and Rose keeps getting up to make them another bowl of dip. It's not Miss Ivy's fault; she offered every time but the first, but Lowell doesn't want her to leave. He's a bit touched in the head so I'll cut him some slack, I just hope he knows that my girl isn't at his beck and call. Besides, I've got homework to do. Killjoy is two days away and I'm determined to break the current cycle. Today I've been watching two things obsessively: his match with Vox Nihili at Cyberslam, and his promo from February 4th. I'm not surprised at anything I'm seeing here, but I'm disappointed in a lot of different things. I'm not surprised that Alias and Karina did what they did. Killjoy had been quite a presence for them in the weeks leading up, and neither members of Vox are the type to take things lying down. Cliches aside, when I think of them, the word that comes to mind is escalation. I'm not surprised that Miss Ivy turned a blind eye to the fight. After Killjoy's promo centered on her, she took it as a personal slight professionally, and any mention of Shannon by anyone anywhere in this business is fighting words to her. But I am disappointed. She let her personal feelings influence her actions as a member of the company. I know she's an emotional person whose passion sometimes bubbles over, but for years all anyone who ever stopped over at Coop's would talk about is her cold, logical business acumen. And she already knew whatever it is she knows about Killjoy by Cyberslam. I knew that when I read her column. She had to have known what Karina and Alias were hoping to do, and she still went along with it. "Do it. DO IT. There's nothing more I can do to you except let you see my face. This face - the face of every broken dream you've had to face alone at night. The fact that's now driving you past the point of whatever you've tried to hold back in yourself your whole life. This is the flood that I bring to you. Take off my mask and release both of us, Chrissie. Free us both, eternally." There's something admirable about the depths to which Killjoy is willing to go for his mission, whatever it is. Something that speaks to me and the depths I was willing to pointedly not go to accomplish my own. Killjoy is willing, it would appear, to kill himself for his beliefs. I am unwilling to compromise my ethics for my own advancement. An irresistable force, and an immovable object. There's a right way, however, and a wrong way. To understand his mission would very likely lead a good deal of this roster to take it up themselves. Viva Wrestling Revolucion, I know it well. Of course, I embraced the Revolucion because to me it represented what I wanted out of this sport - an emphasis on wrestling and the rest of the dog and pony show in the backseat. It wasn't until El Presidente was holding a World Title belt from its rightful owner and threatening slash punishing me for speaking against it that I realized the Revolucion was a lie. But my dedication to what I believed it represented was real, and that's all I need to hold onto from that time in my life. We are free because of me. "Respect is something we will bring you back because I am its reckoning. For every Dusk I dust aside, for every Krow that thought he saw the Devil until he looked straight into my eyes, the FWO will come closer to understanding why I am here and what we want. Your inclinations to comprehending our intentions are integral to the careers in this locker room. Maybe in the end, you want what we want... a brave new world, the doors of perception no longer closed by capitalism and market research. The blood we've spilt for that ring is an oath built on respect, but I think you're starting to forget what that was, not who he is. Don't worry, we are free to help you remember." We are free because of me. Maybe the journey is the most important part of the understanding. I understand Killjoy. I understand being dedicated to a person or an ideal, or a cause with so much of yourself that it's become your identity. The Messenger Is not Important. I understand him, and I admire him. I just don't agree with his means. In another life, I could see myself inviting him to the pub, buying him a beer, and telling him a dirty joke or two. Instead, we're about to beat the crap out of each other. All over ideals.
