Our Story Thus Far (Part 3)

2 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Pierre Delacroix had known Alex Rolander for all of ten minutes, and already he disliked him. A short, obese man, who carried about him what could only be described as a stench, he would never be a man to make a great first impression. His appearance wasn't what made him so unlikable, however. Rather, it was the venom that spewed from his mouth. The first words he'd heard from his new promoter was, in fact: "Little tall for a Jap. Guess you'll have to do." From the airport they traveled to his small gymnasium, which was in all actuality little more than his garage, with a ring against one wall and free weights scattered about. "Your first match is tonight. It's a tag, so just watch Bauer and try and keep up. You understood, right? You speakee the english, boy?" "Oui. Er, yes. Who's Bauer?" Bauer, it turned out, was an honest-to-God decent human being, using the nom de ring of Otto Destrukt. He was the German half of the Axis Powers. He assured Pierre, repeatedly, that none of this was his idea. Alex, it seemed, was big on gimmicks. So big, it turns out, that he had been negotiating to bring in Doink The Clown for when High Roller Wrestling went big. Then there was the matter of pay. He was guaranteed twenty dollars a match, plus one percent of the gate, if the gate ever exceeded one thousand dollars. Which was to say, he earned twenty dollars a match. Given Mr. Rolander's propensity for stunts, this lead to one interesting occasion when the ring had been set up in a local aquarium. While nobody on the crew knew exactly what it had cost, the rumors had it pegged well over twenty times what the workers had been promised. "Amazing. We got out t'ere and risk serious injury for dozens of gawkers for peanuts, and he tosses out several grand for fish." Diedrech Bauer, having settled into his role of mentor to the still green Pierre, tried to assuage his concerns. "What can I say. We're not dolphins." Still, his tenure in HRW went well enough. It would have been difficult to get that kind of experience wrestling in France, and being in Nevada, he'd been able to wrestle a handful of luchadors, to learn their style and perfect his. The crowds, if such a small collection of human beings could be called a crowd, never really took to him, but if it was any consolation, they never really took to any of Alex's characters. It was tough to openly cheer the likes of David Harllison, bad ass biker, or Bruce "The Bat" Mannwein, blatant trademark infringer. Aside from the experience, there were other advantages of living in Vegas. Hot and cold running showgirls for one, as well as other tasty treats. He might not have had the notoriety of a wealthy and famous household name, but he was still a twenty-four year old with a foreign accent. You'd be amazed to find out what an aphrodisiac that was. Or how much trouble it could get you into.
