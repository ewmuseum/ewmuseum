Getaway - Drink, Drink

24 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Mike Bear and Pierre Delacroix, formerly of The Underground, are teetering in a Parisian tavern that is suspiciously well stocked with Heineken, the traditional drink of the legendary team. ...at least it *was* suspiciously well stocked. Empties surround Bear and Pierre, both on the table before them, and the floor around them. The bar itself wasn’t as buzzing as it normally would on a Saturday night, primarily because Mike Bear and Pierre Delacroix, formerly of The Underground, were teetering in it. Oh, and Mike Bear was shirtless, for reasons that would be difficult to explain. For some reason, a large drunken tattooed American did not help bring in the customers. Behind the bar, Francois, the proprietor of this particular haunt, was too busy counting a very generous tip to pay much heed to the two wrestlers. “Really, so Eddie was elected Premiere of his homeland of…” Bear cut him off. “Hand to God! He got 96% of the vote. It’s a shame about the other 4%. Who knew he could be so vindictive?” Pierre shook his head. “So many lives wasted. On a completely unrelated note, I’m just so glad you guys could come. Just so very glad. It’s great to have friends around. We’re friends, right?” Pierre extended his arm around Bear’s shoulder, but in embracing his friend, his weight shifted, and Pierre fell off his barstool, and onto the floor. "Wow, you are a total lightweight anymore. You're lucky Tom and Fear aren't here, they'd have you standing on your head drinking Schlitz. Hold on, there's your phone. Should I answer it? Don't care” Mike flipped open the phone. “Hello cheese?” There was a brief pause "No wait, cheese can't dial a phone. This is the Frenchie's phone, his lunatic friend speaking, who are you?" Another short silence followed. “Jazz! Can I call you Jazz? No? Fair enough. So, Jazz, what’s going on? Really? Perfect! I’ll let him know.” Mike folded the phone shut again, as Pierre clambered back to his feet. “Ah, how is my lovely wife?” “She didn’t say. But Brawn came back, safe and sound. She thought you wanted to know.” Pierre nodded his head slowly. “Good. Good.” Bear opened another bottle. “So how’s it going with the kid? You turning him into another dastardly video tape stealer?” Frenchie lightly shoved Bear. “Oh, let’s not bring zat up again! I’m pleased as hell so long as I can keep him away from ze power tools!” “Hey, it's been many years since I drilled holes in somebody's face... as far as anyone knows..." As Bear completed that thought, several locals who had a better grasp on the English language made their way to the door. "And speaking of kids, what is the deal with you and Zimmerman? Hell, you teamed with the guy, why's he such a douchebag?" "Look at him! Talent galore, but he's five foot not'ing, weighs a hundred and not'ing. You could knock him over wit' a hard stare. But he keeps getting back up, and keeps winning. Besides, I'm French. I don't have much room to judge people for arrogance." "Yeah, I got the tiny part just looking at him. My wife's almost his size. He's just out there freaking out in front of the fans. You, I mean, the fans usually don't love you, but they respect you. Scotty's out there going nuts, and you should remember that I know nuts." "It's very tough to argue wit' zat. And Keit’ will get t’ere, so long as my psycopat’ic friend doesn’t tear him limb for limb.” Bear snorted. “I’m not making any promises. He almost assaulted Brittany, it was dirty what he did on the clusterfuck show to Creed. I'm not gonna let just him walk away. He'll be lucky to walk *again*.” “I can’t tell you how to handle your business, Mike. Nor am I capable of walking in a straight line, or telling you how many fingers you’re holding up. I’m afraid I have to tap out. Do you want to get a cab back home?” “I was hoping we could ride a mime home, but barring that…” Losing his train of thought, Mike Bear stared off into space for thirty-two seconds. "Wait, what was I talking about? Hey, what is the deal with mimes? I mean, they made silent films because they couldn't do sound. Were the first mimes mute? Seriously, I..." "Mike! How ze hell are we getting home?" "Good question. You, bartender guy, how do we get to the Frenchie's house?" "Je ne parle pas anglais." The bartender points outside, where several mimes are waiting by an equal number of rickshaws. "That is by far the least likely thing that has happened today. Well, maybe third least."
