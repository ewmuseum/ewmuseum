Mojo Risin Prologue: LA Woman "Drink up, boys," said Angel, "we've got a toast to make." She emptied the bottle of red wine and raised her glass. All around her, the rest of Valerian's Garden did the same. The empty bottle was on top of the headstone. Yes, they were in a cemetery. "To the journey," she said, "to the city of Paris." Indeed, the band had spent the better part of a year crisscrossing the world, and had finally found themselves in Paris, France for two shows. One down, one to go, and an important side trek for the poetic soul inside their lead singer. "To the poets that went before us," continued TJ. "And to the ones that were taken, far too soon," finished Nine. "Blessed Be," said Angel, "bottoms up." They all did so, except for Mickey, the recovering alcoholic who placed his full glass on the stone in front of them all. The stone that so many had seen before... covered in wax, flowers, and other such monuments. JAMES DOUGLAS MORRISON 1943 - 1971 KATA TON AIMONA EAYTOY As they emptied the wine glasses, a strong breeze blew through the clearing. "I think someone's here," commented TJ, "can't ya feel it?" Angel laughed at him as she shoved paper towels into all the glasses and put them back into her bag. "You're just paranoid, Teej. Ain't nobody here." "Oh really?" replied TJ, "Well, Little Miss Witch, aren't you s'posed to be the one who believes in ghosts and spirits and all that jazz?" "Okay, okay," said Angel, as she held up her hands in defense, "point taken. But don't you think Jim would have better things to do than hang around his own grave?" They all looked at the site again. "Not that there aren't a steady stream of... interesting... people in 'n out." The band packed up their trash and headed out of the clearing, mostly in silence. "So, didja get anything outta that?" asked Nine, "Other than a nice souvenir photo for MJ someday in the future?" "I don't know," replied Angel, "I don't really know what I expected to find. But you know how I feel about it, guys - as a singer and a poet I just had to pay my respects to the first and greatest of us." "Look!" said Mickey, as he gestured to one of the many- graffitied monuments. He moved in close and held his finger directly under a very small piece of writing. "What's that say?" asked Nine, "Bad eyesight 'n all." "Team Extreme, class of 1999," read Angel, "Eli Flair, Randy Harders, Ivy McGinnis. Keep the faith, fight the power." "Damn," said TJ, "Eli 'n Ivy were standing right here half a decade ago. Where were you half a decade ago, Angel?" "Fighting with your brother over the fact that he smoked too much and didn't work enough," replied Angel, as she hip- checked her guitarist, "and gettin' smacked around by this dumbass." The tension grew. Mickey took a tentative step away from the group as they continued to walk. "Hey," continued Angel, as she moved over next to him, "You know I'm kidding, right?" "Still feel like shit, Angel," replied Mickey, "that won't change." "You've changed," reminded Angel, "and that's what matters most." "Sure," replied Mickey, apparently unconvinced, "and I bet everyone who knows the truth really believes it, too." Angel stopped him in mid- step and shoved him backwards another six.
