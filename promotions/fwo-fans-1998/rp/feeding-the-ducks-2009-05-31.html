Feeding The Ducks

31 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
‘What a beautiful day for a walk in the park.’ Lowell walked along a pathway admid a beautiful innercity park. He admired the foliage for its greenness, the sun for its cancer-giving powers, the big-breasted women for their ability to make his ED-affected penis stand on end. Most of all, he admired himself for getting out of the house and taking a much needed stroll. He’d worked hard the past few weeks, getting back inside the ring, dropping KSZ with not one but two clotheslines, and delivering a promo that a stagehand called “reprehensible… grounds for termination.” Fuck you, stagehand, with your Jesus ears. Lowell stopped and sat down on a patch of grass next to a pond populated by a dozen or so ducks. An elderly gentlemen in checkered pants fed them pieces of stale bread. “Sir,” Lowell called out, prompting the old man to turn around. “Those ducks… they just want your bread, right?” The old man shrugged. “I `spose so, yes.” “Doesn’t that make you, like… hate the ducks? `Cause all they want is your bread, and once all the bread’s gone, they just swim away like ‘fuck you, y’ole geezer, we’re finished, that’s the extent of our relationship.’ Y’know?” The old man tore off another piece of bread and held it out as if to invite Lowell to come and help him feed the throng of ducks that had waddled up onto the grass. Lowell stood up, walked over, and took the piece of bread in his hand. The old man smiled, turning and gazing out across the pond. “Doesn’t work like that… sorry, I don’t even know your name, young fella.” “Lowell. My name’s Lowell.” Nodding, the old man replied, “Well, Lowell, ducks are fickle creatures. They want to eat. Most of ‘em `round `ere rely on the hand of people to feed. They’ve become… what’s the word? Dependent on us.” “I see what you’re saying. It’s sorta like... I’m this entertainer, and I have all of these fans… but they don’t know they’re my fans yet. They just see me delivering award-winning promos and clotheslining nerds, and they think, ‘That’s cool,’ but it doesn’t go any further than that.” Whether the old man understood half of what Lowell had said was a mystery, but neither ‘promo’ nor ‘clothesline’ caused him to bat an eye. He simply responded, calling upon his infinite old-timer wisdom, with, “That’s life.” “Yeah, I guess so.” Lowell knelt down and presented the piece of bread to a duck that looked like it had been bitten repeatedly on the face by the other ducks. This duck was a reflection of him—a pariah of sorts that the world had discarded like trash. The piece of bread fell from the unsightly duck’s beak, landing admist the grass. The other ducks immediately darted over, pushing him out of the way, so that they could fight over it. Rage smoldered inside of him as he watched what was transpiring before his very eyes, something remiscent of his childhood, and his time spent in the wrestling business before finding his good friend ‘cocaine.’ He’d seen enough. Suddenly, he punted a dick twelve feet back into the pond, creating a huge splash of water! The old man turned to him, shocked. “What’d you do that for!?” Lowell couldn’t be stopped, and he couldn’t be reasoned with. He wasn’t going to stand idly by and watch himself, er… the duck be bullied by a bunch of arrogant quawking assholes! The sound of bones crunching beneath ratty Converses accompanied the gory image of blood-spattered feathers exploding in every direction, floating to the grass below, as the horrific scene seemed to crawl excruciatingly to a merciful end. The look in the eyes of the elderly gentlemen told the story. Ten dead ducks lay in a pile next to a hyperventilating Lowell. “You sick bastard!” the old man growled, backing up, trembling in anger. He pointed to Lowell, shouting for all to hear, “This man is a bad man! He killed those ducks!” What made the situation all the more unfortunate was that, admist all the sadistic stomping and kicking… Lowell had inadvertedly stomped the duck he was supposed to be sticking up for. “Oh my god, no! Ducky! Fuck! I always do this!” Lowell cursed in frustration, before kicking off his bloody sneakers and running away from the advancing crowd of pedestrians. Never could he do a good deed. Everytime he tries, he winds up making the situation worse… with a mod of people chasing after him. Still, though, killing those ducks had been somewhat therapeutic, and Lowell wondered, as he ran like a coward, whether the same approach would work against his colleagues, those jerks that had denied him his rightful place a top the money-earning ladder of accomplishment and respect. There was only one way to find out…
