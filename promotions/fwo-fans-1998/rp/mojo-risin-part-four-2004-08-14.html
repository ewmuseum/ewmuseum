Mojo Risin': Part Four

14 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Mojo Risin' Chapter Four: Stuff Ivy McGinnis has a very interesting look for professional wrestling. She does not dress to shock. She does not speak to shock. She does not look shocking. Everything she is, does, says, and wears is all a part of who she is, at a fundamental level. Except for the glasses. Her trademark wire-rimmed glasses are there to allow her to see more than a vague blur in front of her. "Thank fuck," she said as she crouched down on the hot asphalt, "for the small favors." Aside from a small bend on one of the ears and a scratch out of her direct line of vision, Ivy's glasses survived the car accident in perfect condition. She bent the ear back to normal and wiped them off on the one area of her shirt that isn't already stained with blood or sweat. Twenty feet away, surrounded by a pile of possessions and sitting on a red cooler, Eli Flair studied a map intently. "Found 'em," replied Ivy, as she joined him, "now... where are we?" "Somewhere around here," said Eli, as he gestured to a section of road, "figuring we left Kingman around nine, and crashed a bit after ten." He showed her the section of map he referred to, and she nodded her understanding. "You were going pretty fast, too - no?" "Eighty," replied Eli, "about normal for a road this length and no other cars around." "So figure we got about eighty miles down the road," continued Ivy, "which would put us... about sixty miles away from Williams, give or take." "Yup." "Damn." "That's a long way to walk it," observed Eli, "especially in this heat." "Walk?" He arched an eyebrow at her. "Yes, walk. Have you seen any cars pass by?" "Not as such..." "So we're on the side of the road somewhere in the California desert, the car is on its side and totalled so we can't drive out. Your phone is busted so we can't call for help. We haven't seen a single car pass by so we can't count on being picked up. What choice do we have?" Despite the laid back tone in his voice, Eli braced himself for sarcasm mixed with contempt. "Well when you put things logically," replied Ivy dryly, "I guess we've got no choice. First thing's first, we need provisions." Eli exhaled sharply. She was sticking to the truce, as he intended to do as well. Small favors were the keys to survival. "Provisions?" he asked, "What d'you suggest?" She peeled out of her bloodstained shirt and dropped it on the ground. The black lace bra she wore underneath had survived the impact relatively intact, and quickly replaced the ruined shirt with another from her open bag. Ivy then dumped the entire contents of the bag onto the ground. "Here," she said to Eli, "empty your backpack. Only bring what you absolutely need to survive." "What?" asked Eli, "Why?" Ivy didn't answer. She retrieved an ACE bandage and a tube of sunblock from the pile at her feet and looked expectantly at him. "'Scuse me, EJ," she said, kicking him gently in the outer thigh. Eli got up, and suddenly understood. The cooler. Filled with water, gatorade, and 'that Stoli shite that Angel loves so much', not to mention filled to the brim with ice water that used to be completely ice. "Ivy, have I called you a genius today?" "Haven't called me that in months, asshole." "You're a genius." "Your compliment is noted and not accepted." They both laughed. Eli dropped everything out of his own bag, save two clean shirts and one clean pair of athletic shorts, which he tucked into the back of his waistband. Deep breath as the cooler opened. Deep breath as the cooler opened. "This was a nice bag," said Eli, "but... survival, right?" "Right." Four bottles water each, five bottles gatorade each. Eli's mystery box and Angel's Stoli. They filled their respective bags to the brim with ice and water. The bags immediately started to drip, but that was part and parcel of the entire deal. "I don't think the cooler itself is gonna survive the trip," said Ivy, "because I ain't carrying it and it's too awkward to walk down the road with." Eli stared at it. The cooler itself was empty, save about two inches of freezing water. It didn't take long for both of them to figure it out. First, Eli dumped about half its contents over Ivy's head, then the remainder over his own. "Now we're ready." "Almost," replied Ivy, as she pulled a slender butterfly knife from a side pocket in Eli's backpack, "just one more thing." He winced as she sliced through her hair, letting the wet glop fall to the street with a minimal splash. "What was the reason for that?" asked Eli, still staring at her. She'd made a clean cut of it, her hair now falling just below her earlobes. "More hair equals more heat," explained Ivy, "now I can actually get a tan on the back of my neck and not feel like something's dragging me down." Eli looked his best friend over. The haircut, as minimal as it was, looked good. "So, shall we?" They looked toward the east - the sun was still rising high in the sky. "Let's do it," replied Eli, "next stop, civilization." One step at a time, there's the ticket. The sun beat them down, but it was nothing they couldn't handle. For two people who routinely traveled in uncomfortable cars, buses, and planes - for a man who routinely wrestled for more than three quarters of an hour and more under hot ring lights, discomfort was a way of life. "Let me ask you somethin'," said Eli. "Sup?" asked Ivy, as she dabbed her head with the same bloody shirt. "Why did you end up working with Disney?" Ivy stopped walking. "Y'know, for all this shit about a truce--" "Calm down, Ivy," interrupted Eli, "I'm not trying to be a dick at all. I really want to know what happened to change your mind about them." "It's a long story," warned Ivy. "S'all well, we've got plenty'a time." Deep breath. Ivy reached into her bag and rubbed her face down with a hand drenched in ice. The sun was getting hotter and hotter. "Shell shock, really," she began, "this entire year has been one massive flow of shell shock. I don't know how much you know about how I came back in after you retired - slash - quit last year..." "All I know is you hacked a column about Crisp onto the server and Fox offered you a job," replied Eli, "Was in the midwest for most'a that stretch, didn't get any specifics."
