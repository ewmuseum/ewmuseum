A journey of a thousand steps, Part 5

3 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Back in the office of Dr. Jacob Talbert, he and Dr, Regan are still going over Mike Bear's case history. Jacob is looking through his notes again, describing the situation to his friend Kelvin. “He's admitted to the hospital after a particularly serious shot to the head where they uncover a more serious head trauma sustained fairly recently and some reported abnormalities in the motor control area of his brain. I've always been tempted to link the two, but no one has ever deduced the original cause of the trauma, nor the exact nature of the abnormalities. Whatever the case, it sure didn't help matters.” “The case history said he returned to work a week later. He put in extensive on the show he had that Saturday.” “AGAINST my advise. I was consulted, and given his history I told him he was risking his health, maybe his life if he went through with the show. He wouldn't hear of it. Said the fans would expect nothing less than his best. Then he goes out and absorbs several more severe shots to the head. I couldn't even watch. He was never the same after that night. He tried and tried, but he could barely muster up the strength to talk on camera. His appearances were cut back. I spoke with a few of his coworkers, they said Mike was like a shell off camera. The mood swings were worse. Occasional bursts of energy, some bouts with depression, mostly he just appeared vacant.” “And he still appeared on TV?” “They refused tell me how they got him out there to perform. I watched him on TV, and I could tell something was wrong. He had definitely gotten much, much worse, but they needed him to stay on and finish what he had started. Two months to the day after I told him not to do the show, they gave him his walking papers. I saw him that night, July 28, 2001. He was fairly lucid, said he was proud he could do one last thing for the company. At the very least he didn't take the shots to the head that he used to during those two months. He was handled much more delicately, knowing that he could fall apart at any moment. Still, he needed a lot of work to pull himself together. We met everyday after that, in conjunction with his doctors. He looked as if he would at least be able to get back to the condition he was in before, but...” “Jacob, like I said, you can't always save everybody. You're not God. I assume you told him not to do the... cage fighting, was it?” “Yes, Asylum. Appropriate, I suppose. That's where he belonged, moving from professional wrestling to cage fighting. I begged him not to do it. I begged his wife to convince him otherwise. His friends, his parents. Anyone close to him, I tried to get to help me stop him, and they all said the same thing. 'You just can't change Mike's mind.' As if that should just be accepted as fact. I wish they could have seen how vulnerable he was at that point, but they sure saw afterwards. Five months back in the weekly grind of a television show. Interviews, promotion, not to mention the fighting, and always on the road. Five months and we were right back where he started a year ago. Exactly one year after he was admitted to the hospital with the head trauma, Asylum fired him too. It started all over again. The mood swings, the vacant staring. He came home after that, his friends and family would look at me with eyes that asked, 'Why didn't I listen?' They all thought he was this invincible superman, but his mind is just so fragile.” “And ever since then, you've been trying to put the pieces back together?” “Oh I got him to the point where he was lucid, back where he was, but he's still not in control. His temper is as bad as it ever was, though he's never lashed out and his friends or family. I've been told of times he's come close to assaulting people, destruction of property. He's made restitution in those cases, but I need to have get control of his emotions.” “Jacob, have you ever considered that he has a borderline personality disorder?” Jacob looks up from his papers at his colleague, then back down towards his desk and put his hand on his chin, ruminating on the possible diagnosis. After a minute to think about it, he presses the intercom on his phone. “Rachel, could you please contact Mike, ask him if he can come in this afternoon?” “No problem, Doctor.” “Oh Rachel, one more thing. Could you order in some lunch?” Jacob raises his eyebrow to Kelvin, who shrugs. He turns back to the intercom. “Anything's fine.” “Will do. I'll let you know when it's coming.” “Thank you Rachel. Please hold any other calls unless it's an emergency.” “You're welcome, Doctor. I'll take care of it.” Jacob turns off the intercom. “Bless her. Best one I ever had.” “Jacob, you didn't...” “What? No. No! God, no. Never mix business, and all that. She's just fantastic as an assistant. Nevermind that. Thank you, Kelvin. The suggestion, your hypothesis. It hadn't occurred to me, though it should have. I *was* too close to this for too long.” “Sometimes an outside perspective is the best way to solve a problem.” “Well, it's your diagnosis. Are you in?” “If I wasn't, we could have done this over the phone. If you want my help, I'll do what I can.” “Then let's get to work. We can only pray we're not too late.” Jacob goes to pull a book off of his shelf and Kelvin pulls out some of his own notes as we leave this scene.
