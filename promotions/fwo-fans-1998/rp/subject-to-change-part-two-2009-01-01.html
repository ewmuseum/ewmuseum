Subject To Change : Part Two

1 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Billy Kilcullen’s trembling hand ran through his limp, mousy hair. The cold sweat that clung to his pale, pimpled forehead soaked into the white athletic tape that encased the hand, slicking his long hair back. Deep lungfuls of air were sucked in, puffing out his gangly frame for a few seconds. “Yah can do this,” Billy unconvincingly told himself, words timid even in his own company. “Yah can do this.” Raised among a family of strong, broad oaks, Billy Kilcullen was still a mere sapling. The white elbow pad he tugged up his left arm hung loose, white and green cloverleaf pants, held up only by a tightly tied drawstring, seemed made for a more developed frame and boots, a size too large, followed behind his feet rather than encasing them. He planted his damp face in a towel, soaking up the sweat that was helping stick his gear to wiry limbs. No sooner did he remove his face from the towel than each pore on his lightly freckled, boyish face expelled another cold bead. A knock at the door sent a icicle-stab chill up his spine. “One minute, kid.” Everything Billy Kilcullen had learnt in training was lost in a sandstorm blowing around his head, swirling with every word he’d ever heard about Karina Wolfenden and every firm push in the back his father had given him before he was ready to take the step for himself. “Ocgh,” he gagged. The first two timed he’d vomited that evening had purged his stomach of any solids, so it was a syrupy, dark brown liquid that gushed out into his gym bag. He panted, eyes darted around the room. Window barred, air vent too narrow for even his wiry frame to wriggle though. The door burst open, and the flabby figure of Butch Crosby, nose straightened thanks to a heavy band of tape, was stood over the son of his attacker. “Think y’ready, boy?” growled the rhetorical question, sweet stench of vomit wafting around the room betraying the answer. Any concern for Billy he‘d shown earlier that evening had fermented into vindictiveness. “Heard she once burst a guy’s nut with one of those kicks, y’know. Sure you’ll be fine though, with those two months of trainin’ and all. Don‘t want to let your Pop down… do you now?” As Crosby waddled out through the door towards the ringside slaughterhouse, Billy stood, crossed himself with a trembling hand, then shuffled out after him.
