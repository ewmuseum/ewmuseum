Of exodus and redemption (part three)

28 December 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"And of course, there was your run in the United States..." David Spencer shrugged to the camera and sipped at his tea. The young interviewer was pretty he supposed... pretty for British television anyway. They didn't waste the "A" talent on wrestlers after all... this was a public interest story that would appear on one of their more tabloid style programs, or as a nice featurette between the night's soccer tallies. "Every wrestler of our generation has gone to the US for a run, Nancy. It's a common enough thing to do, but I suppose that history tells us that. McGregor had a long run in the CWL in the '90s as their world champion, you had the Bulldog in the WWF and certainly the Dynamite Kid and Cross Jerkins were famous in their own rights. The United States gives us the unique opportunity to explore our craft in front of a new audience. They aren't used to our style of wrestling. It's a flashier brand of wrestling, which is strange really. The US was all about the technical hooker style for decades before this showmanship became the in thing there. We brits were in front of the times and called ourselves progressive, then came the 80's and people like McMahon, Hailer and Race and suddenly we're all old school over here. Classic wrestling I suppose they call it... it's a nicer name for old fashioned and behind the times." The interviewer smiled a vacuous smile that made it quickly obvious that she hadn't understood nor cared about anything Spencer had just said. David smiled back at her and sipped from his tea. Ten years ago this would have bothered him. Ten years ago and twenty million dollars ago he would have cared. "Now, in this clip...," the young woman gestured to a nearby television. David guessed that this footage would be split in over him at this point for those watching at home. "In this clip you're wearing some kind of skeleton costume... is this old school Mr. Spencer?" David Spencer laughed. On the screen was himself in his Death costume, surrounded by the original Dark Riders. War, Pestilence and Famine. Those were strange days... War didn't speak to the rest of them behind the curtains and generally stuck to himself during the whole period they were saddled together. Pestilence showed a mild interest in everything, but at that point they were attempting to build up his whole character into an Austin Powers remake... and Stevie Roberts was interested in becoming a road manager anyway. Famine was clearly interested in Spencer and his style of wrestling, but Spencer suspected this had more to do with Famine sucking up out of fear of getting cut. His push in the Dark Riders had been a favor to Jim Dudley anyway... some kind of relation that came into play when the fWo were strong arming him to work 18 dates a month on the road. "No, that's United States wrestling there. You see, it's more about the costuming and the storylines. Far be it for me to tell them they are wrong, because... let's face it... they were cashing the checks, right? But perhaps I'm just old fashioned, but a little flash always took away from the product. Wrestling... athletic skills... those were what people wanted to see and I believe still want to see. That's why I'm starting the CWA..." The young woman glanced down at her notes. "Commonwealth Wrestling Academy... correct?" "That's right." Spencer was nodding. "It's a school to teach people the old style of wrestling and working. Of course I'll be helping out when I can, but we've got some good instructors lined up and perhaps people will stop paying attention to the flashy, little substance feds and pay a little more attention to what this game... this sport is really all about. And if they line my pockets in the process... I mean, am I right?" Spencer laughed a deep laugh while the interviewer smiled politely. She didn't understand the differences that Spencer was drawing, and didn't particularly care. She had this assignment because the station director was slipping it to the second bar... a young know nothing called Julie. "Well, that's all well and good Mr. Spencer, but wasn't it the flashy American style wrestling that you did in the Fans Wrestling Organization that made you as wealthy as you are today? Isn't it that money that is funding your school?" Spencer frowned. "That's some of it, but I've had a twenty year career. I've done movies..." She glanced down at her notes. "Yes, one with Rowan along with 'The Slayers Part Three' and 'Mr. Butler'... but none of them highly successful in the box office..." "That." Spencer said the word like a sentence. "That is a matter of opinion. My work speaks for itself, and let me tell you that plenty of film makers and the fWo are in constant contact to see if I would be on their televisions again right now. In fact, just two weeks ago I was in negotiations to be a surprise tag team partner at an fWo Pay Per View. I was promised a title run, several main event matches..." The woman was looking off camera at the stage manager who was pointing at his watch and giving the sign to move things along. "No offense intended Mr. Spencer, and we are almost out of time anyway. One last question though... any regrets?" David Spencer looked at the woman with the remnants of a frown on his face, then at the camera. For a moment he saw him, Ryan Moore... Joy... falling off of the steel entrance ramp and down to his death. He heard the rush of silence that followed... the screams. Spencer closed his eyes, then looked back at the camera. "Regrets? Not a one, love. Not a one." On the television David Spencer flashed his winning smile that was shown so prominently on the posters for 'Galaxy Cop'. Text ran on the screen letting the British audience know how they could get in touch with Spencer for information about his wrestling school. On the hotel bed Fallen clicked off the television and sat looking at the dark screen. No regrets, Fallen. Did you hear that? John Skyler heard it. It made what he had to do tomorrow much easier.
