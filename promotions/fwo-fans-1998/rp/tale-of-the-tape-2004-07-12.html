Tale of the Tape

12 July 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“Hurry up Hollywood!” Mitch Wilson yelled as he leaned back in the plush leather recliner. His body sank into the chair and he wondered just how much it cost to rent a luxury hotel suite like this one. He reached over to the end table beside him and picked up his can of diet soda. “If you take any longer my drink’s gonna be flat.” “You’re head’s gonna be flat if you don’t zip it,” grumbled Kodiak Vic Creed as he walked out of the kitchen of his hotel suite. He tossed the bag of pretzels at Mitch. “There you go, one bag of unsalted pretzels. Nice and bland, just like your promos.” Mitch pulled the bag open and shot Creed a dirty look. “At least I don’t need to wear a bra when I go out in public. When are you going to drop that weight?” Vic twisted the top off his bottle of water and plopped himself down on the couch. “Probably start next week. I’m not used to carrying this much weight. My knees are killing me.” Reaching over his head, Vic probed the end table blindly for the remote. Mitch smirked and pressed the Play button. “Too slow big guy. Besides, doesn't the guest get Remote privledges?” A one-finger salute popped up from the couch. “What’re we watching now? If it’s another one of your new Puroresu tapes -” Vic turned his head and stared at the 52” screen across the room. “It’s our latest camp. I had some of the guys work with a few indie wrestlers.” “Must’ve killed you not to be in the ring.” Without having to see his face, Vic knew Mitch was cringing. Primetime’s backdrop driver had all but finished his wrestling career. But Mitch wouldn’t be kept down. It wasn’t his style. For about ten minutes, both men watched the screen as Mitch’s instructors ran drills with people who claimed to have been wrestling for at least a year in various independent promotions. It was painful to watch. “This is better than a comedy,” Vic said with a chuckle. “These guys are horrible!” Wilson looked at Creed with a slight bit of amusement. “It gets better. Just watch.” After a few minutes, a new wrestler appeared on the screen. A young man with shoulder length chestnut hair and the body of Adonis stepped into the ring. He had good mobility, executed his moves crisply but controlled, and still had that ‘enthusiastic rookie’ look in his eye. “This kid looks like he’s got potential. How old is he?” “Eighteen,” Mitch said with a smile. Creed sat up and watched the youngster. “He moves well. Got a good ring presence.” “He’s a natural,” commented Mitch as he tried to hide his amusement. Vic continued to watch the kid during a training match. He was green, but he had ‘it’. When the kid went to the top rope, Creed was curious as to how he’d finish the match. The kid leaped into the air with a shooting star press, twisting it into a hurricarana for the pin. For one of the few times in his life, Kodiak Vic Creed was impressed. “Holy shit, this kid’s gonna be a star someday.” Mitch nodded. “I thought so too. It killed me not to work with him. But he comes by his talent honestly. His old man is in the business as well.” “Second generation wrestler huh?” “Yep.” “Do I know the kid’s old man?” “Actually yeah, you do.” Just then, text scrolled across the bottom of the screen announcing the results of the match. “The Mecca” Alex Creed def. Brickhouse Brown via pinfall. Vic shook his head. “Nice name. Tell me it’s just a coincidence and that he’s not trying to rip off my name.” “His real last name is Apostle. He didn’t think it had a good wrestling ring to it.” “Apostle? Damn, I knew an Apostle when I was a kid. Sally Apostle.” Creed’s eyes clouded over in thought. “I was fourteen when my family went to Florida for one of our rare vacations. I met Sally on a beach. She thought I was eighteen. I sexed up that beach bunny every day. Man that was a great week.” Vic ended his reminiscing. “I wonder if he’s any relation?” “I think he is.” Mitch couldn’t hold back any longer and started laughing. “What the Hell is so funny?” “The muscles in your neck are cutting off the blood to your brain, old friend.” Mitch pointed at the television. “That’s your son.”
