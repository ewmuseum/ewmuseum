Buried Hatchets

3 July 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Once, when I was little, my father made a rare appearance to the trailer park and was greeted by my dog, Hatchet. In a moment of excitement, Hatch introduced himself by pouncing on my him and running his rough-textured tongue across his face. Two days later, I found my father burying Hatchet in our backyard. Told me he was "sick," but as usual, I had the hardest time believing what he had said. Euthanizing my dog was completely uncalled for in my adolescent eyes and in the end only mounted to the tremendous pile of reasons why I hated him. When fWo shipped me off to rehab, I found it completely unnecessary at first. People told me I was "sick," but I wasn't seeing it. It didn't take long before I knew how Hatchet must have felt. I wasn't hungering for a change, but I was suckered into a hearty helping of it. I didn't like the taste of change at first, but I eventually did. After only a week, it was all I could think about. Rehab is a sword that can be easily dulled by a willingness to actively participate. I attended every activity, keynote speaker, and group discussion. Sure, I thought some of it was goofy at first, but it all worked in the long run. Now I welcome alcohol with the same enthusiasm I would an enema. I didn't realize how much I'd changed until I returned home to Shirley. Even now, she is suspicious that I was switched with someone else. I couldn't be happier that she is content, but this positive change of lifestyle has its own pitfalls and obstacles. We named our tag team Fatal Attraction because people really wanted to kill themselves after seeing how completely frictional opposites managed to make it work. We were destined for disaster by the way we pestered each other, but it is how our relationships thrived. We gauged our growing by the intensity and subject of our arguments. It might sound odd, but I miss them terribly. We haven't had an argument since I've returned home; it's not that we're trying to avoid it. I've tried to start fights with her but the quarrels never pan out. Tough Love has disappeared, leaving only the latter behind. For that I couldn't be happier.
