A Morning At Zom's I, Sloan

22 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The Bic lighter flickered to life, it's small flame making contact with the Camel cigarette. The Camel lit and the lips pursed around it inhaled deeply. Sloan Ambrose took the Camel out of his mouth and slowly exhaled the smoke. He flicked the first ashes into the white plastic tray that sat on the bar top to his right. His heavy brown eyes scanned the rest of the diner with heavy interest. This was one of Sloan's favorite things to do, sitting at the bar of Zom's, watching people engage in their everyday activities. Some called him nosey, but he'd swear to you he was just curious. Sloan was still a young man, only twenty-eight. Short, but thick chestnut brown hair that was usually gelled and slicked back, revealing a sharp widow's peak. Thick brown eyebrows stood over his always moving eyes. A crooked nose from when he broke it in a bar fight, a small scar ran across the left nostril. His mouth was notorious for always having a lit Camel dangling from one end. Zom would always yell jokingly that the cigarette was gonna drop on Sloan's Detroit Free Press one day and set the entire diner on fire. Below his mouth and on his face was a five o'clock shadow and on the end of his chin was a thick soul patch. Sloan had big, powerful hands, on his right ring finger sat his 1992 Dearborn-Edison High School class ring with his garnet birth stone displayed proudly. He had never gone to college, as he graduated high school he got sucked into the seamy underworld of pit fighting. Sloan knew that it was a dirty job, but it was his best option to make some quick cash to support himself for living day-to-day. He hated it, his family hated it, but it was a living. Sloan came to the diner every morning around 8:15. There was always a new edition of the Detroit Free Press tucked under his right arm. He would sit on the third bar stool from the left and order the same cup of black coffee, eggs over easy, home fries and french toast. It was a comfortable routine for the somewhat conservative Sloan. "You know, if you eat those things every morning, it's gonna kill you eventually." With a grin, Sloan turned his head to see his closest friend, Valerie Manson settling in to her seat at the bar. She smiled back and reached a hand up to wipe a small piece of egg off of Sloan's upper lip. "Fighting every night's gonna kill me too, but I still do it," he retorted sarcastically. "So is smoking, but..." "It takes the edge off," Val stepped in, finishing the sentence she had heard so many times.
