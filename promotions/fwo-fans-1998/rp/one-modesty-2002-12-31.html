One: Modesty

31 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Humility is having a modest or low view of one's own importance. It is the great equalizer amongst a persons inner thoughts and his ultimate actions." Brandon looked over his trophy room. He didn't come home as much as he should, even when the tour was light he was finding more and more excuses to stay away. Times with Ana were tough and she was growing farther and farther away. He had done everything in his power to bring them together but nothing was working. Ever since he came back to the fWo it seemed like they were on a slow drain. The deep mahogany wood shone with the brightness of the small droplight situated in the middle of the ceiling. The anti-glare glass protected the trophies and awards the man known as Brand Frontier had collected in his six years in pro wrestling. Each belt or award was displayed with a picture and a plaque. Oddly the picture next to each of them was not of Brand Frontier earning the title, or accepting the award, rather it depicted the person that Brandon had defeated or beaten to achieve a particular accolade. This in fact was not merely a trophy room for everything he had been given in the sport; it was also a shrine to those that came before him. The ones that paved the way for his meager success. Brandon absently walked to the largest of the display cases. The spotlight on this one was evident and as the Paragon looked down in to the case the golden color of its occupant gleamed off his rigid cheek. The Elite Wrestling Associations World Heavyweight Championship. At this point in his career Brandon considered this to have been the ultimate prize. Looking at the pictures of Condor and Vic Creed, the two men he defeated to claim it Brandon wondered what he had done to truly deserve such an honor. That federation was long since dead but it's spirit and what it meant to him lived on. Maybe, one day after paying his dues and earning what the fWo had given him he would be able to look at the Organization's championship in the same way. For now though he had his sights set on Ric Chronos. In mere hours they would be tearing down the house at the fWo's Twilight Pay Per View. That thought brought Brandon out of his nostalgia trip. He needed to go. This match was another chance for Brandon to say thanks to the owners of the company and prove that they were right in investing their time in him. This was also a chance to lock up with Ric finally. One on one. It had been many months and Chronos believed Brand Frontier was using his past to get ahead of the game. He needed to show Chronos that all he wanted was to earn the right to be there and if that meant putting Ric down for the count then so be it. Two: Forbearance
