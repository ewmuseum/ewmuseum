Rotary Club Transcript 8.7.09

7 August 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Thank you for inviting me to speak here at the Breaksgood rotary club. I haven't been back since the funeral and it's comforting to see so many familiar faces. Some of you no doubt are heartened, or despise, the idea that the twenty three year old man standing before you commands a seven-figure salary in the FWO for performing on television an average of 10 minutes every week. I have not earned any of it. Perhaps it's my upbringing that, in hindsight, robbed me of a proper, carefree childhood and any semblance of personal satisfaction. I worry I may never be satisfied. I grew up in a household that placed a high premium on competition, achievements, and success, and It has proven difficult to break that mold. I was firmly taught to keep my mouth shut, put in the work, and good things will come. They don't. The company I work for is a loosely affiliated band of warriors, nomads wandering across this country pitching tents and putting on shows. We are a circus, where the criminals and clowns are not only accepted but relentlessly rewarded and embraced. Our fans love to live vicariously through these social misfits, when they themselves lack the courage or good conscience to behave that way in their everyday lives. You can decide if that is a comment about the state of our society. The good guys are a minority, but we press on. I beat one of the FWO's most esteemed names on it's biggest stage: Cyberslam. I was one of the final wrestlers standing at our recent Meltdown event, an evening that will be forever burned inmy memory. But somewhere along the line I lost my way, as so many people in this profession do. My hunger for accolades had become so deplorable that I resorted to tactics that I reasoned would fast track me to the top. Frankly, my attitude has not earned many friends. I've been stubbornly set on taking everything the FWO has to offer without giving anything in return. I've tried to entertain without doing anything worthy of the description. I've turned my back on the fans that enthusiastically supported me when I emerged as a new face in the crowd. It's so easy to forget that the fans are what makes our company tick. I've given them no reason to line up with me as I begin each new chapter of my career. Success is overrated, and cannot be measured. I was set on earning everyone's respect except my own. Not anymore. I am a kid, a kid with extraordinary responsibilities and freedoms. Sometimes I need to reflect, as my uncle once put it, just how I became "too big for my britches." The FWO has given me a week off. I've been trapped in a cage that I myself constructed, but I believe I am beginning to take steps towards finally respecting myself and the company I am so proud to be a part of. I don't know what you all may take from this talk, but I hope you'll think about the importance of being yourself, working within your means, and remembering to give back to the people who have given so much to you. Thank you."
