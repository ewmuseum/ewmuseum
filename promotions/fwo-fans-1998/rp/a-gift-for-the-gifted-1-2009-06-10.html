A Gift For the Gifted (1)

10 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The descending sun threw long diamond patterns on Samuel Braun's face as he clawed at the metal links of the fence. His hand slid into his tight polyester back pocket, producing a few sunflower seeds that he anxiously shoved into his mouth. He nibbled ferociously on their shells, hoping to placate the nerves that were beginning to activate within. “Do you need a cough drop?!” Jerry barked at Sam's dad nearby, all indignation. Sam winced and closed his eyes. He had a hundred thoughts swimming in his head, but his mind lingered on his coaches' question. Dad politely declined. Sam hated his father's cough, a painful hack that sounded like dry bones grinding together. But he hated his coach Jerry even more, a tyrant of a man who supervised dad in the limestone mines. Dad took pride in working hard. Jerry was proud of collecting a paycheck and navigating the nuances of health code compliance. “Guess how many championships I'll have after tonight?!” Jerry eagerly asked to no one in particular. “31,” droned a chorus of 8 teenagers. Sam continued watching and waiting, slobbery sunflower seed shells intermittently blown from his lips. He heard footsteps, the hoof-like sound of metal cleats on concrete. Five plump fingers tightened on his shoulder. “Hows my ticket to the Iowa Baseball Hall of Fame?” Sam watched the field, where a double play was unfolding in the consolation bracket championship. “You listening to me, son?” Sam resented the familial term, but understood why it was so readily used by Jerry. He was the only coach Sam ever knew, lording over his success from t-ball to all-state high school status for three straight years. Jerry graciously arranged a visit by a Major League scout to tonight's game. Sam didn't want to think about that now. “Just thinking about who I want to ask to prom,” Sam said through the side of his mouth, knowing this would mortify Jerry. “You better be thinking about baseball,” Jerry snapped back solemnly, his voice barely audible. “Listen, I got something for you.” Sam's eyes didn't break from the field as he heard a metallic rustling behind him. Jerry returned and poked him in the stomach heel-end of the object. Sam looked down and was temporarily blinded by the sun reflecting off of the sparkling barrel of a new baseball bat. “This bad boy is made out of nitrogen-cooled titanium alloy developed by NASA,” Jerry remarked, adding, “They make space ships out of this shit.” Sam handled the bat like a cob of corn, observing the bright teal label on the metallic barrel. “What's that?” Dad asked as he buttoned up his uniform top and joined them at the fence. His eyebrows furrowed as his pupils moved to Jerry, then toggling quickly to his son. He expelled a sigh. “You know we can't afford that,” Dad muttered to the ground. “You don't need to pay me back. Boy's earned it.” Jerry's five fingers clamped down on Sam's shoulder in the front. “Son, you've given me exciting years of baseball. You deserve this.” Sam looked to his dad, who was looking away as if he didn't hear the exchange. “Thanks, coach,” Sam said hesitantly, still unsure what prompted this unusual outpouring. He was reminded as he took a glimpse of his father, pale and holding himself up with a nearby fence post. Dad saw that the game being played was over, the players slapping hands at the pitcher's mound in solidarity. He walked off to gather Sam's teammates and equipment so they could get settled in their dugout. “Too bad you have to go wood for the championship,” Jerry remarked as he produced a second bat and handed it to his player. His eyes scanned the immediate vicinity. “This sucker's a beast to begin with, but I put a few things inside to give you a little more pop.” Sam's eyes returned to the bat. His gut was an elevator in free fall. “Like what?” Sam asked weakly. “I put some of those rubber Super Balls in the barrel,” Jerry replied quietly. “It will give you better distance.” “I don't know, coach...” “I know, you're a hard ass,” Jerry said. “You've taken 20 out of the park this year without it, but I want you to look as good as possible out there.” “Yeah, but...” “Look, you're gonna make me a legend tonight. 31 championships will break the coaching record. That's all I've ever wanted in life. The least I could do is set you up for some big bucks in the pros.” Jerry's head craned backwards at a forty-five degree angle, accentuating his bulbous nose. Sam's eyes followed the gesture. A man in a dress shirt was approaching in the distance, the breeze pushing a tie over his shoulder. A clipboard was tucked under his arm as he took a right and began scaling the bleachers. “Up to you, kid.” *
