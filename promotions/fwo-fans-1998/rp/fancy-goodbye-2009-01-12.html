Fancy Goodbye

12 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The Pier's dining room was dimly lit, a blended wash of maroons and browns punctuated by dim stale yellow lamp light. Plastic ship steering wheels dotted the wood paneled walls. White Christmas lights shrugged from the periphery of the ceilings under the pretense of class. Soft jazz music chirped from ceiling speakers. There was an ambient clang of glass and silverware, wind chimes letting out an abstract melody. Brawn couldn't stand it. "Annie, we didn't need to come here, really," Brawn said, squirming slightly in the over-sized chair. He looked around to the couples in the establishment, whispering sweet nothings over cod fillets in what he imagined to be a pre-cursor to mechanical, elderly sex among Precious Moments figurines and forsaken crochet kits. "We're doing this for me, not you," Annie said as she slurped a blue substance from a glass in the shape of a tremoring fish. "I can't afford this fancy stuff. C'mon, it'll be fun." "Yeah yeah," Brawn replied, looking to the side and seeing eyes directed at him. What am I supposed to do? He sighed, wiped his moist brow and took a drink. "Not everyday a celebrity comes around," Annie said hushed as she pointed her forkful of salad at him before stuffing it in her mouth. "The people at Liberty bank must be sad to lose you--" "It's strange," Brawn interrupted, smiling and nodding to a gawking old woman. He tapped the perspiring stem of his glass. "It wasn't like this when I was playing baseball. Did you see the kids lined up for autographs at the ribbon cutting? My wrist feels like it's missing." "It was sweet of you." Annie said. She sighed and looked into her plate, "Dad would be proud." "How is he?" Brawn asked, much more eagerly than he would have liked to project. "He's hanging on," Annie replied. "He's like you. Strong. I know he wouldn't say it, but he's thankful for the money. He can now afford the treatments--" "Stop," Brawn said, choking. "Please, don't." It was then that a waiter kicked open a stand and lowered a gigantic tray. Brawn's nostril snarled as a plate of shells and glistening, pearly flesh landed in front of him. "What is this?" Brawn asked with consternation. The waiter scratched his pencil mustache and lifted his head. "You can't say things like that here," Annie reprimanded, kicking him under the table. Water rumbled in their glasses. She unfolded her napkin and placed it in her lap. The two were alone once more. "After living on crackers during your three year limbo, I thought you might like a fancy meal for a change," Annie explained, lifting a shell to her lips and briskly sucking in and swallowing the contents. Her eyes clamped shut and her head shivered before her face relaxed and opened in a wide, ecstatic smile. "Your turn!" "No...no," Brawn said, waving his hand over his plate. Annie reached across the table and lifted a shell and nudged it on Brawn's hand. His hand reluctantly stretched out. He took it into his paw and lifted it up. "To FWO." "To FWO," Annie repeated in a burly tone, "Your new job." Brawn paused. "My escape plan." Annie produced a subtle, stunned frown. The eavesdroppers nearby applauded as he leaned his head back and poured the shell contents into his mouth. A milky white liquid dripped down his throat as his teeth tore into the chewy meat. He swallowed and dropped his head down to his plate. He ignored the weight of his sister's sorrowful expression, curling his fingers like a pirate over buried treasure. "Mmm. Who's next."
