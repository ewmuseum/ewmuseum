i. stevemartin Out of his mouth came a storm of jokes. All of them landed. He tested out the crowd with his weakest material and they were falling out of their chairs. Sides were bursting. Smiling lips were running out of room on their faces. After all those failed attempts: atonement. It was all autopilot from there. Zavala covered the War In Iraq. Gay Marriage. Micheal Jackson's trial. Everything was clever, witty, and well versed. Even the vocalized streams of conscious were finding a favorable flow in his audience's ears. He was a success. After about a half and hour, he finished with a closing joke and the place shook with applause. Standing ovation. He found himself in a state of disassociation, so detached from his own being that he had to be assisted off stage. Once he reached the back, everyone was there to congratulate him. The club owner gave him a congratulatory pat on the back. After the compliments died down, he returned to his dressing room, where a lime green LCD light blinked in the darkness. He swore the night couldn't get any better. But after looking at what was on the screen, he was immediately disproven.
