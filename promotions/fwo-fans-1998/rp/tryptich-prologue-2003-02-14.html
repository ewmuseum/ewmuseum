Tryptich - Prologue

14 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
TRYPTICH Prologue - Portrait of an American Family "My family was like anyone else's. Parents both worked, the 'neighborhood kids' all watched out for each other. My dad grew up on the same street we lived on, his friends were my friends' dads. When they were teenagers they started a gang called the Eliminators. Not much of a gang, they were just a name for the neighborhood delinquents to call themselves." "They didn't do very much 'gang- like' either. A few brawls with the gang across town. For the most part, pops straightened out when he turned 18 and realized he could hit jail instead'a juvie." "That, plus he met Mom. She really straightened him out, gave him a good set'a priorities... really made him care about his future. He didn't do much with the gang anymore but hell, he and his best friend started it, they all lived on the same block, they all worked out at Coop's so it was really just a formality, if you will." "'Sides... what's formal about a buncha dumbass kids hanging out and causing trouble? You don't need a gang for that, just three friends who get bored easily." "But yeah.... nobody ever learns. Pop gets laid off from his job as the manager at the liquor store and his dumbass best friend talks him into robbing the place. Great idea, Pop... get yourself shot when your son's thirteen years old. Way to set an example." "...no, that's not fair..." "He was great... he always spent time with me, he was a father and a friend... that's really rare. But try tellin' that to a thirteen year old kid who only understands that Pop's not comin' home because he did somethin' dumb." "So I'm thirteen, I do somethin' dumb... by this point the Eliminators are all the founders' kids... including my best friend Paul Fizzbin and his little bro Johnny, who we made the mascot. But even though I missed Pop, life went on pretty much as it had been... hung out on the corner, started lifting at Coop's, went to school whenever I felt like it.... typical teenaged bullshit." "Same as everyone else." "Until a family of interlopers disrupted everything forever. I can honestly say that if Joe and Lily McGinnis hadn't moved into my neighborhood, I wouldn't be here now." "Skinny nothing of a boy. Skinny nothing of a little girl. And a brown haired beauty, the likes of which I would only see one other time in my life." "Can y'blame me? What else does a fourteen year old bag'a hormones think when he sees a sixteen year old girl in a pair of tight jeans? Sue me, but it was love at first sight with that perfectly shaped.... shape." "And before y'all think I'm some kind'a sexist bastard, try and remember I married that woman." "The gang didn't matter much - they never did. My friends know by now... if you don't hear from me, it's business as usual. Somethin' good happens and I'll tell you. Somethin' bad, you'll find out soon enough. But an obligation to hang out every day was more than I was willing to do for them guys, so s'just lifting at Coop's, an occasional rumble with the Sykots across town, and shopliftin' booze." "Typical kid stuff." "Paul and me really threw down over Brian McGinnis once - and I admit my reasons for stickin' up for the kid were purely selfish - I wanted to meet his sister. But it wasn't much the same after that anyways, Luty stabbed Paul with a switchblade during a fight at school when the Syk's were pickin' on Johnny. Paul bled to death because nobody called a doctor." "Someone called the cops, tho... and I think Luty's still doin' time for that one." "If I hadn't ditched that day I would've been in the middle of the fight with Paul, might be dead, might be alive. I didn't care about that part, just that I wasn't there to back up my boy when he needed me. See, I'd pretty much figured out I wasn't gonna get outta that neighborhood. Pop didn't, Ma didn't, Fizzbin didn't... ain't nothin' about me any different than all them. And sure, it was run down, it was small, and we didn't have much in the way'a material stuff, but it was still home and I loved that street." "Hookin' up with Alicia tho, really made me start to think about wantin' more. But it was about then that Ma died and I knew I wouldn't be able 'ta get it. She worked in the tunnel, and carbon monoxide's a cruel way to die. Pop might've gotten what he had comin' to him, figurin' what he was in the middle of at the time, but Ma deserved better." "So anyways, this wrestling thing catches on and somehow I'm able to convince people I'm actually good at it... I could either walk or take the subway to the matches so it wasn't like I needed to recoup transportation, so I was doin' pretty well for myself. Plenty'a old timers reduced to working the NYC gyms, and they were so desperate for an audience it didn't matter who asked what - everyone'd get a two hour story outta it. But I loved the stories they'd tell of the road.... made me want to hit the road m'self and see what's out there." "It was the best moment of my life to that point when Ali told me she wanted to come with me if I hit the road lookin' for a job. From Salt Lake City to Colorado, Mexico City to Montreal... I made a pretty decent living, even managed to save some money for Alicia to head back to college, for us to get married." "The big break was around the corner, too... big- ass elimination tournament in Greensboro, North Carolina. Soon as Ali and I completed our dates in San Francisco, we were headed back to the east coast. That's when all hell broke loose." "It's been eight years and I still don't know what happened... but everyone's heard the legend by now. Golden Gate bridge, rainy night, reckless driver knocked my girl off the bridge. They found the car, they never found her." "One McGinnis woman saved my life when Ma died... another was about to do the same." "But that's a story for another day."
