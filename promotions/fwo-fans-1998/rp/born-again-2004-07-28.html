Born Again

28 July 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The makeup was no longer caked on. Black eyeliner and lipstick didn't exist on his face anymore. He sat out in his car in front of the house, mulling everything over. It was late, but a porch light was still on and he stared intently at it, waiting to pick his moment. Each time he closed his eyes, his mind flooded with memories. The same memories he had whenever he looked over his scarred and battered body. His name had become synonymous with hatred and evil. People cringed at the sight of him. The ghostly makeup, hundreds of scars, some self- inflicted, others through disturbingly grotesque acts. He was a man without friends, yet hordes of enemies. People he let into his world always found it undesirable and quickly left him, alone and desolate. Just like now. Many times, on many lonely nights he lie awake in bed, staring at a dark ceiling wishing he could turn it all around. He knew there would be much to cover, a lot to go back through. Change is inevitable in every person's life, but he could never figure out how to make the change. He badly wanted to be the good person even he didn't think he could ever become. The porch light of the house was his glimmering beacon of hope. His chance to throw the past behind him and start all over again. If only he could get open the door to his car. Shadows moved behind the drawn curtains and he slowly opened the door. He eased himself out of the seat and stood up, staring straight at the front door. With each uneasy foot in front of the other, he walked up the front steps and onto the porch. Slowly he reached the door and knocked. The door clicked. And opened. Donaven Winters balled his hands into uneasy fists and bit his bottom lip. The man at the door looked taken aback and cautious. Winters breathed in deeply and let out words he never thought he'd hear himself say. "I'm sorry. I'm sorry for everything I've done to you in the past three years. Sorry for all the pain and suffering I've put you through. I'm sorry for attacking your family. Sorry for everything. I hope you can forgive me someday. But it won't be to my face, because this is probably the last time you'll ever see me. I'm sorry." Winters turned around and didn't wait for an answer. He got back into his car, started it and drove off into the future. Standing at the door, Eli Flair smiled slightly. "Last time I see you? I highly doubt that." As the car roared off down the road in the darkness, one thought was finally true. Donaven Winters is dead.
