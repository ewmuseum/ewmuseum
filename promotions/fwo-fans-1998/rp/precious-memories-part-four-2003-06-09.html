Precious Memories ~ Part Four

9 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Well kid, are you gonna talk or what?" Bill Randolph said as he brewed a cup of green tea for his long-time protege and friend. This was a ritual of theirs. The old kitchen, still decorated in the fashions of the 1970's -- rust orange curtains over the sink, yellowish gold linoleum and chestnut brown cabinetry -- had seen hundreds of these night-time meetings. It had become a safe-haven for Jade over the past ten years. No place had ever felt more like home. At the present moment, she was seated in one of the creaky old metal frame chairs. Her bruised fingers ran along the chipped table top, eyes fixed on their motions. "Yeah, I'm just...waiting for my tea." she said softly and with a hint of a smile. "Its done, actually." Bill replied as he brought to steaming mugs over to the table. He took the seat opposite of Jade. She gripped her mug between both hands, much like a small child would. For a moment, she just stared at her own distorted reflection in the murky liquid. It was easy to lose oneself in something so trivial when being avoidant. Jade was great at that. "Bill?" she asked, still transfixed on the aromatic beverage. "What is it, kid?" "....is this a new kind of tea? It smells really good." She was dodging around her real question and both of them knew it. Bill did her a favor and went with it. "No...no, this is the same kind that I always buy." "Oh. Guess it's just been a really long time since I've been here." It had been a very long time. Jade hadn't been in this house in over a year. She finally took a sip from the mug, enjoying the warmth as it traveled down to her stomach. "Yeah, I'd say so. Shit, must be...well, over a year now." His tone indicated that this fact really stung him, deep down in some buried place. The unspoken tension was immeasurable and put a thousand miles between the two, even though they sat just feet apart. Jade flicked her eyes to the balding, older man, then to an invisible spot on the floor. She just couldn't bring herself to do it -- to say what she really wanted to. To do so would be to risk losing the one good thing in her life. Right now, it just wasn't possible. Bill knew it, too. He could read Jade like a book and always had been able to...ever since their first meeting all those years ago. Perhaps that is why they had an instant connection. Or perhaps it was something more than that. Jade shifted in her seat and looked around at the vintage decor. "Still haven't gotten to redecorating yet, huh?" she asked, completely detatched from her own idle banter. "Haven't had the time. I've been busy with the gym and all. Got some pretty promising prospects down there. Nothing like y--" Bill stopped himself from saying that word. That one little three letter word that would probably make Jade get up and leave. She looked up at him and sighed heavily. The mug found its way to the table and Jade folded her arms. "Why don't you just say it. I know its nothing but a lie, but if it'll make you feel better..." "I thought you'd say that and you know what? Its fucking stupid. One of the dumbest things to ever come out of your mouth and trust me, there have been a lot of 'em." That certainely got Jade's attention. She just looked at Bill with heavy eyes. "How dare you think I'd go and fill your head with a bunch of bullshit. You know what I think about you and that hasn't changed at all." The old man was sincere. He shook his head. Jade looked as if she could break down into tears at any second, but she stopped herself. God forbid she ever show a slight indication of weakness. She would resolve never to do something like that to herself...not even in front of Bill Randolph. Such things were saved for a bottle of Jack and dark room. "Then why are you so fucking smitten with that Kevin Masters guy, huh? Or why did you start raving about those assholes down at the gym?" "Oh Jade..." he said like a concerned father to a child who has gotten the wrong impression. Bill was always sympathetic because, well, Jade was still very much a child. "Do you really think that?" "Why shouldn't I?" The woman said with a touch of bitterness. "Because you're smarter than that. I know you're still pissed off and I'd be lying if I said I wasn't still a bit hurt. I would have tried to make you stay last year but..." She flinched. Even mentioning the words "last year" made her want to rip clumps of her hair out. The subject of the previous year was not one that Jade was open to discussion about. Too many bad things had happened...and she was still in the process of realizing that the year was actually over. It would be a very long time before she would even utter those two words. Nearly losing Bill was just the tip of the large iceberg that was last year's bullshit. But, here she was, back in his life...and he back in hers. No it wasn't the same at all, and who knew if it ever would be. When she had called Bill earlier that day, part of her had expected him to just hang up. Jade certainely would have if she were him. What she had done one year ago was practically unexcusable. Somehow, Bill forgave her...though he didn't directly say it. The tea invitation was more than forgiveness. "Yeah, I know Bill." she said in a low, hushed voice. Jade raised her eyes to Bill and the two looked at each other in silence for a while. They always had that ability to communicate more without words. Words were over-rated for each of them. They got in the way of conveying true intentions and feelings. This was pure...it was real conversation. Jade broke a half-smile. "I can't believe we're sitting here, doing this." Bill said, shattering the silence. "...Not talking." "What's so unbelievable about it? We used to do it all the time." "Exactly." They both smiled this time. "Here, let me warm that tea up for you..." Bill grabbed the mug from Jade and went over to the microwave. She watched him and couldn't help but marvel at how quickly their conversation had turned into something reminiscient of ones they used to have. Though she would still hold a grudge against Bill Randolph, Jade was able to sit there that entire night and talk(and not talk) with her dearest friend. Inside, she was convinced that he was a little too hung up on a certain fWo wrestler...but she made herself forget about it for the present time. Even someone as proud as her was capable of setting something aside for one evening. Sometimes, old friends are more important. Sometimes. They would sit there until four in the morning, just when the birds began to sing.
