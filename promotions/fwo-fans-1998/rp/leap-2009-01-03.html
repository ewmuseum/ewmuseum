Leap

3 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Friendly the Frog saw the young girl approaching. Her tiny limbs strangled his green felt leg. Something brushed against his massive frog head. Through the mesh he saw a baby boy, reaching out from the nest of his mother's arms. The woman placed the baby on the ground, atop the hurricane debris of wrapping paper and dirty shoe prints. The post-birthday party ghost town. "Can we uh--" "Kitchen?" his muffled reply. She nodded. Samuel Braun raced down the empty road, snow drifts passing like cresting waves of shaving cream. The blood rushed through his veins. He tapped an abstract percussive beat on his steering wheel and followed the melody on the radio with his lips. "Didn't want you to take your head off in front of the kids." "I understand," Samuel Braun said as he observed the head's neotenic features before placing it on the marble. He looked up and met her gaze. She paused. A lusty expression materialized. Samuel Braun wiped his sweaty brow, and looked down on the table surface where a salty drop had landed. He saw himself in the glossy surface. The offers came, but his contract restricted him from taking them. Samuel Braun was the hallmark of FWO's development system. Everyone knew it. The kid had it. Destined for stardom, they said. He felt the ebb and flow and pull of a company who wanted him to succeed or fail depending on mood. After the pruning there seemed to be nothing left. "Sorry," he said as his green arm rubbed out the drop. "Hot under there. Mind if I change?" "Oh, no. Fine, fine." the answer came quick. He waited. She didn't move. Samuel slowly unzipped the front of his costume and peeled it, revealing a tight physique pressing through a white v-neck t-shirt. He leaned over and grabbed his duffel bag. Slipped into a sweat shirt and denim jacket. Stuffed the costume inside. He reached into his jeans and pulled out a crumpled piece of paper, pink. Braun learned to resent the place that called Fans Wrestling Organization. Weeks became months. Months stretched into years. He quietly saved his paychecks, and resorted to odd jobs in the interim. "You have 15 days to pay." "Thank you for coming. Madison and her friends enjoyed every minute of it," she cooed. "Glad to hear it. You have a nice day, now," Braun said as he moved to the screen door. "You too." Samuel Braun heard the screen door clap behind him as he stood shin deep in an un-shoveled sidewalk. He opened the door of his salt-stained, overworked Ford and threw his garments in the backseat. His warm breath created clouds as he looked down the long empty highway. The brisk cold wind, or something else, caused water to mount on his eyelids. "Fucking FWO." FWO was back. Brawn was back. He felt hunger. He felt life. He climbed into his car and the engine wheezed, gurgled, and ignited. He sat in the freezing automobile, peering out from the ice crusted windshield. Braun blindly reached into his denim jacket pocket: new message.
