How I Used To Be (3/5)

14 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Thursday, May 8 3:08 PM Interstate 95 runs up and down the east coast, stretching from Maine to Miami. The latter was where Scott Wasner had joined the highway, headed north in a steadily increasing rainfall. He kept it at a steady ten miles per above the speed limit, a nervous lightness in his chest keeping his foot heavy. The folder sat on the passenger's side seat of the Saturn. Wasner occasionally reached out a hand to touch it, not thinking about it as he did so. It was mid-afternoon, and the combination of gray skies, intensifying rain, and the time being a short while before the rush hour meant the roads were fairly clear. Though the Florida countryside passing by might have, in other times, caught his eye, this time it did not. He was focused on what lay ahead, eight miles down the road in the quaintly named (or at least he thought so) town of Golden Glades, Florida. He passed the last car in front of him and moved back into the other lane. Every second he got closer to the inevitable, the reason he had come here, and for one of only a few times in his life, Scott Wasner was scared. The importance of this situation surpassed any other he had faced before... and there were so many ways it could turn into a disaster. And after all, the more important something was, the more likely Scott Wasner was to blow it. In that way, he was the exact opposite of his famous and celebrated wrestling character, and sometimes he wished that he could just be Scott Slugger. Even with all of the hassles that came with the package, Scott Slugger was confident, he was smooth, he was... right. Always. Even when he lost, Slugger found a way to turn it around in the end. He never made any real mistakes. Scott Wasner was none of those things. As the Saturn rolled on, its tires running across the macadam and spraying rainwater in its wake, he thought back to a time when that had been proven more than any other... ----- Even now he only remembered part of the night of December 30, 2002. The first half of the day was completely out of his reach. There was no block - it simply was not there. There was a white buzz, a white fuzz, and nothing. He suspected that was probably for the better. Most of the second half of the day was the same. He remembered snippets of the Countdown show itself. Calling out Winters. Searching backstage as the show went on around him. Finally, a hand connecting with his face as he looked uncomprehendingly first at Stephanie - whom the hand was attached to - and Donaven Winters, laid out, bloody, and unconscious on the floor. What his memory returned to now was the middle of that night, alone in his hotel room. When he had come to after blacking out earlier, there had been questions from the usual authority figures, but nothing had come of it. Stephanie was nowhere to be found, which had a lot to do with Winters' not being in jail at the time. Nobody had heard from her again. It wasn't the healthiest response, but it was understandable. She was not the first and would not be the last to run from their problems instead of dealing with them. He knew that all too well. Beyond that, the majority of the night's events had happened off-camera, and as for what was on tape, there was little way to conclusively prove they weren't simply staged as a part of the show without the victim's presence. When the night had finally ended, he had staggered back to his hotel, not wishing to drive for fear of injuring someone else. He was by himself in the bathroom, looking in the mirror and trying to figure out who the man looking back at him from the mirror was. Surely it couldn't be him - the man in the mirror was haggard, dark circles were under his eyes, and some dried blood still was on his face. He looked dead. So he had set about matching his look to reality. A quick scan of the bathroom had turned up no razors - he only had an electric, darn the luck. His luck with the medicine cabinet was better. A bottle of Advil. Crude but effective. He opened it and filled his right hand with pills. He looked at them for a moment. So small... Before he could think about it, he lifted the toilet lid and dumped the handful in, quickly followed by the contents of the bottle entire, then flushed the toilet. He couldn't do it. Worthless, he thought to himself. Can't protect anybody, can't save anybody, can't muster up the guts to make everybody else's life easier and remove yourself. Worthless. He staggered into the main room and fell on the bed, half on and half off, and spent the rest of the night that way, drifting in and out of consciousness. ----- He shook his head. Much like his on-show alter-ego, he had kept himself going for the next month or so by turning his self-pity into a nebulous, undefined, targetless anger. In the end, it had petered out, and finally he had come to a realization - he had to deal with it, somehow. That was when he found Zito. He looked out the window. Exit 10B. That was him. He had a choice to make. Keep going. Try as best he could to forget he had ever been here. Take the exit. Take whatever came from there. He flipped the turn signal on. ----- 3:33 PM 1629 Revello Drive of Golden Glades, Florida was an unassuming, modestly-sized house of red stone and brick. It sat on the far end of a street filled with similar houses. Scott Wasner had parked the SC2 across the street in the rain ten minutes earlier and sat in the car, looking and watching. The bright red Camaro was in the driveway, and the sight of it brought back another flood of memories, bittersweet. He finally screwed up the courage to get out of the car. He didn't have a coat, but didn't mind. For one, it wasn't cold. Even in the rain, Florida was still as warm and humid as ever. And for two, his Northwest roots caused him to shrug off anything short of a minor monsoon anyway. He stepped out into the street and immediately jumped back - he had missed the car coming across the street by inches, and it honked angrily at him as it continued on its way. He checked the road, then walked across and up the driveway. He stood there for a moment, then walked up the steps to the door and knocked. For a moment, nothing. The door opened.
