Life Of Indifference [1] J-Pro

25 March 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I'd come to realize what a truly superficial world I was living in. A world where image is everything, a world where Hollywood's finest grace the big screen with their bleached white teeth, rippling steroid enhanced figures and plastic reconstructed faces. A world where self-mutilation is self-improvement. Knowing this I faced the facts, hiding my disfigured truth was the only choice. I wore the horned mask. I became The Jersey Devil. September 25th 1994. "Six months mopping floors and living on bread and water, this better fucking be worth it." Kellen Kinkade grumbled as he sat in the reception area awaiting the call for his meeting. He was in Japan and it was sink or swim time, not a company in the western world was willing to employ him because of his disability. If one in the east wouldn't then the chances were none of them would. "Mr. Nazaki will see you now." A voice cut into the silence, the receptionist had stepped from behind her desk and was now opening a door for Kinkade and gesturing him in. He got to his feet and made his way briskly down the hall and through the doorway. "Ah Mr. Kinkade, please do take a seat." Mr. Nazaki spoke from a seated position behind his desk as Kinkade approached and extended a hand "Konichiwa Mr. Nazaki, it's a pleasure to meet you finally." "Just as it is a pleasure to meet you." Nazaki replied as Kinkade took a seat before him, before any further words could be exchanged a contract and pen had been slid across the table "I trust everything will be sufficient, if you could sign your name at the bottom and date it that would be most excellent." Kinkade almost couldn't believe it, for the first time in a long time the opportunity to wrestle was sitting before him a signature away. A chance to ply his trade without any of the prejudice and discrimination that often came with his injury. It took him less than a couple of seconds to scribble down his name and date the contract, he didn't want to read it and quite frankly he didn't need to. "Thankyou Mr. Kinkade, welcome to J-Pro wrestling." Mr. Nazaki got to his feet and the two exchanged another firm handshake, Kinkade turned to leave... delighted with the outcome of the meeting. "Oh, but before you go there is something I must ask." Nazaki spoke up, and Kinkade froze... fearing that the truth of his injury was about to come to light and tarnish his hopes of seeing the contract stay in one piece. "Yes?" Kinkade reluctantly questioned. "The mask... I like it, but tell me Mr. Kinkade, why do you wear it all the time? Do you use it to hide a secret or is it merely a tradition?" Nazaki smiled as Kinkade thought about his answer. "Tradition, lets just say it's something I wear because of my father." Kellen's answer was a very well twisted version of the truth. "I see, tradition is a very important trait in J-Pro. It is nice to know that you respect it, I look forward to seeing you wrestle." Nazaki smiled once more as Kinkade made a brisk exit; the fewer questions asked the better. October 16th 1994. Tokyo Dragon pushed The Jersey Devil onto the top rope and a road echoed throughout the masses of fans watching their bout. A top rope brainbuster was one of the many signature moves Dragon possessed to put an opponent away and it looked like The Jersey Devil was about to experience it first hand. It was no surprise that a roar of approval and support went up as The Devil was hoisted into the air, Tokyo Dragon was wrestling in his hometown on home soil against an American opponent. What was surprising was the equally loud echo of support as Devil gripped the turnbuckle and shoved Dragon to the canvas with a hefty thud. Fans rose to their feat as Devil poised himself on the top rope and executed a crisp, picture perfect shooting star press down on Dragon. A hook of the leg followed and the three count that came with it marked not only an upset but with it a surprising reaction for a newcomer. Kellen Kinkade rose to his feet and gazed out across the sea of Japanese fans chanting and standing for an ovation, as he helped up the favourite Tokyo Dragon and exchanged a handshake with him he knew that he'd taken a huge first step toward finding success in Japan. He clambered between the ropes and made his exit down the narrow aisleway, exchanging high fives with his newfound fans before finding himself on the backstage hall. Making his way toward his locker room he suddenly found an individual trying to beckon him. "Psst, American!" A small man whispered as Kinkade slowly approached. "Listen man, I've got to go and take a shower... but I promise I'll come back and sign an autograph for you soon." Kinkade replied. "No want autograph, want to tell you something." The small man said back, almost offended by Kinkade's autograph gesture. "Oh alright then, go ahead." Kade folded his arms and listened. "I see you wrestle American, you good. You strong, you fast and you in good shape... but you also wasting time here." The small man shook his head and sighed, before producing a piece of paper "This my number, when you want make some real money... you call it." "Thanks." Kinkade took the paper to humour the man more than anything, the small individual quickly scuttled away as Kellen tucked the paper into his boot and headed in the opposite direction. If he'd known the trouble the paper would bring, he'd have burned it on the spot.
