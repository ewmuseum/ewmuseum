the gentle whisper

26 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It was long ago, the last time I sat alone in a room with Matthew. A gentle melody was playing in the background, the soothing voice of a French vocalist comforting us as I inched closer to his muscular frame. He pretended to ignore me as I inched closer towards him, almost hugging him like a scared little child would hug their father, but I knew he noticed me. He liked to pretend I didn't matter- that it was of little concern to him whether I lived to see the next sunrise. But I always knew, in the back of my mind, that without me he would fall to pieces. The music hit a crescendo and we kissed. It was dull, the kind of kiss that would tell you something was wrong. A small voice screamed within my mind that he was cheating on me, but I knew better than to listen to her. However, I did know that something was wrong with Matthew. So I spoke up. "Is there something wrong?" His only response was to move his head backward, allowing his long black hair to drape down his uncovered back. "You're distant towards me, Matthew. I'm not entirely sure why." His gaze turned towards me, empty and yet somehow frightening. He motioned for me to get up, so I got up off of him and stood up. He looked down at the dirty couch we had been lying on, and a disgusted look came over his face. His eyebrows arched upwards as he looked back towards me, and finally, he spoke. "The two of us mean nothing." he said almost sadly. "There was never anything between us and there probably never will be." I wasn't shocked he was saying this. I had always known, somewhere in the back of my head, that my feelings for him weren't mutual. But to hear him say it was an entirely different experience. It hurt, yes. But in the back of my mind, I was almost relieved to hear him finally say it. The air was filled with the gentle playing of violins. "What do you want me to do?" I finally asked him after a long pause. He looked at me, and then suddenly threw a television remote he had been holding in one of his hands into the stereo, causing the beautiful music to suddenly stop. "For one thing, find a new taste in music." he spat. "This garbage is just like you." I felt a tear welling up in one of my eyes, but I resisted the urge. "It's just as gentle as you are." he continued after I had no response. "It's disgustingly gentle. It makes me want to throw up. You make me want to throw up. Learn not to be gentle, learn to be strong, then you can come see me again. Then, perhaps I will want to see you again." There was another long pause as we both stared down at the equally dirty carpet of our apartment. "Get out." he finally spoke. I nodded, and meekly returned to our bedroom to collect my things. I did turn my head one last time to look at him, and watched him walk over to the broken stereo to pick his remote up. Without a word, he walked back over to his couch, leaned backwards, and turned on the TV. It was as if the fact that he had just shattered my heart to pieces meant nothing to him. It was as if he was lacking a very soul. I shuttered at the thought of Matthew's existence. I had no idea how a man so cold, so callous could have ever possibly won my affections. It's because you're cold and callous. came her voice inside of my head. It's because we are cold and callous. I rejected her words, shaking my head as if I thought that would silence her voice while I packed up my things. Instead, it only made her louder. You have been gentle, as he said. You have been weak. Yes, I realized, she's right. I have been gentle. I have been weak. If only I knew how to get stronger. I know how to get stronger. I can show you. I nodded, a devious smile creeping across my face. There was no doubt left in my mind- I was going to be stronger. I would never be gentle again. And then, Matthew would take me back. But I wouldn't take him back. I shot Matthew one last look as I opened the door to our San Francisco apartment for the very last time. I watched him cross one leg over another as he drank from a small glass of cola. I smiled again, for I knew tonight was the last time I would ever whisper gently. I closed the door behind me, never to open it again.
