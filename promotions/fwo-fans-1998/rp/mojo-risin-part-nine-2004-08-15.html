Mojo Risin': Part Nine

15 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Mojo Risin' Chapter Nine: Rise of the Phoenix "You kids've finally figured it out, I think." Eli Flair woke up with a start, and looked around the meager campsite. Ivy was sound asleep on the other side of the dying fire, and there was no sign of anyone else around. "Gotcha." Behind him, his mentor Terence Cooper strode into the glow of the fire and sat down on a log that wasn't there. "Terry," said Eli, "Good t'see you." He knew, at that moment, that he was dreaming. Terry Cooper had been dead for approximately a year now, killed in the same incident that left three bullet scars on Poison Ivy's body. And somehow, the sight of the old man didn't put him off in the slightest. Eli Flair, the recipient of many head wounds through the years, was used to such visions and hallucinations. They had always had a positive outcome for him - helped him to see things he had missed. "So we're close, huh?" asked Eli as he, too, sat down on the log that wasn't there, "Close to civilization, sure - but I have a feeling that's not what you're talkin' about." "Smart man," said Coop, "I was talking about you and the kid over there. Be careful with'er, Eli - she's got a pretty bad head wound. Keep an eye 'n all." "I have been, Coop," replied the King of Extreme, "and pretty damn well, I think, considering how much time we spent fighting this year and how little time we've just spent getting things back together." "True, you're doin' well," observed Coop, "but what happens when you rejoin society? What happens when you go back to the fWo, the place where your greatest fights had several witnesses?" Eli sighed. "I guess we'll cross that bridge when it's in front'a us." "It already is," reminded Coop, "You can't take one moment of solidarity - compared to you ten year career, two days is just a moment - and retroactively apply it to an entire year. You two owe it to each other, and to the idea of Team Extreme, to figure out what's what before you decorate your stuff all over the rest of the business." "So what then?" asked Eli, "Admit that I was wrong? Expect her to admit that she was wrong? What're the odds that either of those things'll happen?" "Point," replied Coop, "but who said either of you are wrong or need to admit it? Two different - opposit - points of view can be just as legitimate as the other. It all depends on your perspective." Perspective was something they both knew about, all too clearly. "So I'm trying to protect the balance of heroes and villans in the fWo, and she's trying to protect a number of different people and ideals, most of which are working at cross purposes. Great. No wonder she's losing her mind." "She's only losing her mind because she's two days in the hot desert sun, slowly bleeding to death from a head injury that also gave her a concussion. And you're feeling overly protective because it was a head injury, and even though the gash you've got on your shin is now infected it's not affecting your judgement. But she's a lot more together than you're willing to risk." Eli looked down at his shin - at the raw, irritated flesh around the scab of dried blood. It ached, but it certainly was nowhere near the worst pain he'd ever felt. Still, as part of his subconscious, this Coop figure knew. "Whatever you decide," continued Coop, "decide fast. Because once you set foot inside fWo territory again, things are going to move pretty damn fast. What with the war and everything." The war? "Miles." "He's got nothing holding him back anymore with Nixon released, and he's got no problems running roughshod over anyone - anyone who gets in his way." "How do you know this?" asked Eli. "Because you know this," replied Coop, "I thought you knew the rules in here." Right, thought Eli, Everything is me. "So what do we do about Miles?" "I'm glad you're thinking in the sense of 'we' again, it means you're thinking collectively. Good." "Well?" asked Eli impatiently, "What do we do?" "I have no idea." "Why not?" "Because you don't know what to do." Right. Idiot. "You two were probably one of the greatest duos in the history of the sport, do you know why?" "Do you?" asked Eli, with a smile. "I do," replied Coop. "Then I guess I do too." "You two, your relationship was more than friends," explained Coop, "It was more than family. It survived through an implicit trust that you both had for each other that rarely develops in this sport due to its very nature. As far as this business itself goes, can you honestly say you've ever worked as closely with anyone?" "Not even close," replied Eli, "and that's been missing this year. Haven't had anyone to throw ideas at or to catch ideas from, y'know?" "There's your answer," said Coop, as he got up and headed out into the desert night again. Eli turned and followed. "What d'you mean?" asked Eli, "My answer to what?" "Think about it, kiddo," replied Coop's voice as it gradually faded out into nothing, "you already know the answer." ---and Eli Flair woke up with a start. The sun had started to rise. "Goddamn," moaned Ivy as she held the palms of her hands to her eyes, "Hangovers don't make my head ache as much as this damn bruise." He watched her get up - the laborious motions of a concussed individual attempting to will mobility out of a body that has been walking for two days straight, sleeping on the ground, and had a bum knee to begin with. "What?" asked Ivy. "I'm just amazed at us," said Eli, "we survived." "Course we did," replied Ivy, "It's the power of Gatorade. You've seen those commercials, right?" "I think the dreams of our next meal is what kept us goin'," replied Eli, "That and each other." Ivy smiled. She walked over to him and stuck out her hand to help him up. Strange sight, considering she was nearly two hundred pounds lighter. "C'mon, ya bast'd," said Ivy as she clapped a hand on his shoulder, "there's a greasy and largely unhealthy meal waitin' for us, seven miles away."
