Home - Travel

16 November 2004

Angel stopped at the curb, shocked by what she saw. “You… picked up the baby’s car seat?” Eli smiled. “Course I did. Nothing but the best for my girls. Wait until you see the apartment… had the bathroom redone.” She raised both eyebrows. “We’ve got a bathtub now, my dear.” Angel collapsed into Eli’s arms, very overly – dramaticized. “I love you, wrestle-man!” “Now now,” said Ivy as she tossed Angel’s bag into the back of the car, “no lovey shit. You’ve got plenty’a time for that.” TJ laughed as he gently put his bass into the trunk next to the bags. “You sure you’ve got room for me, Ivy? I can call up some friends—“ “Ain’t no friends,” interrupted Ivy, “This is family. And you’re staying in my spare room, no argument.” The Psycho Bitch looked around at the rest of their party. Angel held Mariella in her arms while Eli held both of them tightly, and TJ was patiently waiting, leaned on the door, returning the recognizing stares of a few passersby. Maybe it wasn’t recognition – a man with platinum blonde hair and a fur coat, along with the unusual looking family on the other side of the car were certainly a sight to behold. “Shall we?” Eli snapped out of it. “Definitely.” It was a no – brainer. Angel had let her driver’s license expire, Ivy never had one to begin with, and TJ, like many non – residents, feared driving in New York City. Angel sat shotgun while MJ was strapped into the car seat and was joined on either side by TJ and Ivy, but she was well – socialized and didn’t look upset at her mommy and daddy in the front seat. TJ and Ivy were like a favorite uncle and aunt, and this nearly – two year old was completely at home with them. “What are your plans?” asked Eli, “Time off? Recording right away?” No answer, and he noticed TJ looking at Angel, who was looking out the window. Ivy didn’t get it either. “You’ve been writing, right?” continued Eli’s line of questioning, “I mean, you were on the road for months… that’s always where my most creative promos came from.” “Oh, I’ve been writing,” answered Angel, “but I’ve also been fending off nosy partners.” “Read some’a the stuff,” interjected TJ, “It’s some of Angel’s best work, ever.” Angel smiled as she looked out the window. “We’re gonna put this down in the studio in what… three weeks, Teej?” “Something like that.” Ivy caught the look on TJ’s face. She raised an eyebrow and he silently shushed her. Abruptly changing the subject, she tapped MJ on the nose. “Excited about moving to the big city, lil’ Angel?” MJ smiled and looked away from her surrogate aunt. “Yeeees,” she said. As they entered the tunnel, Ivy instinctively held the baby’s hand. “The kids want to hang out before I hit the road again,” said Eli to Angel, “I left it open, didn’t really know what you needed to do t’unwind tonite, or what exactly you had in mind, or whatever.” Angel took Eli’s free hand and kissed it. “We’ll take a raincheck, I think,” she answered, “I need some alone time, I think…” Ivy and TJ both coughed. “…unpacking and stuff,” continued Angel without missing a beat, “and without commentary from the peanut gallery.” She sent a loving glare to the two in the backseat. “The lease on the place in LA is still paid through March,” said Eli, as they continued to make idle conversation, “so we’ve got an easy place to go to for vacations and quick trips west and all that jazz.” “Lovely,” answered Angel, “Smog and gangbangers.” “Hey!” “And four fifths of Garden,” continued Angel, “I was counting you guys amidst the smog.” TJ smacked her in the back of the head. “Children!” said Ivy, “Don’t make us turn this car around.” “Too late,” answered Eli. The car shot out of the tunnel, into the bright pre- twilight of a fall Manhattan afternoon. Eli expertly negotiated traffic like a person can do only if they’ve been born and raised on New York City streets. They sped through the late afternoon traffic, thankful that they were still a good twenty minutes early to be trapped in the heart of rush hour. “Need me t’drop you home, Ivy?” “Naaah,” replied Ivy, “We’ll help you unpack before headin’ out. Teej, you’re still crashin’ at my place for a few, right?” “Unless you’ve changed your mind.” “Nope.” For twenty to thirty minutes, the car was mostly silent. It had been a long day for most of the inhabitants, and there was a strange sense of urgency pressing down on all of them. Ten months, Eli and Angel were apart… being together again, being whole again would take some getting used to. As Eli slowed down on the approach to the parking lot for his building, he laughed silently to himself. A purple Ninja was parked in his usual space, with a “WELCOME HOME ANGEL” sign taped to the left handlebar. Angel laughed. “She’s a sweetheart, Eli,” commented the Gothic Diva, “If we deign to go out later, we’ll have to have a beer with her.” “Definitely.” They exited the car, and each grabbed a bag. TJ left his stuff in the car, as they were pretty much dependant on Eli to take them both to Queens later, so that was immaterial. Besides that, Angel was a minimalist at heart, and had three bags in total, two of which were packed to the brim with toys for the baby. “Welcome home,” said Eli to the two year old in his arms as he opened the door to the apartment, “This is where you belong, sweet pea… this is where you’re always welcome.” He kissed his daughter as she smiled shyly. Angel joined her family in the embrace, as Ivy looked at TJ. “Listen, Teej,” said Ivy, “What say we go buy this little angel some ice cream? You want some ice cream, Mariella?” MJ nodded happily. “Let’s go.” Ivy took MJ in her arms and kissed her. Eli and Angel both sent their nonverbal thanks to the Psycho Bitch. “Give us a call when you’re ready to go home,” said Angel, “Eli’ll drive you both, no problem.” “Right,” laughed Ivy, “Seeya in a bit.” Ivy, TJ, and Mariella headed down the stairs as Eli watched them go. He turned back toward the door, where Angel stood, biting her lip. Angel gestured to him with her fingers to come toward her. Eli did so.
