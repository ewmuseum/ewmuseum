Domino Effect - Part 2

29 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Part 2 - Sunday, December 15th, 6:35 PM The Magi tossed and turned on his hotel room bed. His head was pulsating, his temples throbbing, his stomach ached and his muscles were weary. He was a classic victim of his past self, for 18 hours ago he took his health for granted and treated himself to a night out. He now buried his head well beneath his cool pillowcase and begged the gods above for a few hours of sleep to cure his weakened state. Alas, his time had run out. He had a match to prepare for in less than two hours and could not miss it. It was an opportunity to shine, to prove his worth on a grand scale once again. He had made several promises to himself while he was out of work, promises to make the most of any small opportunity he received. Tonight's was a big one. Cruiserweight title match. One on one. A grudge match with an old rival, a chance to lay some heat to rest. These thoughts rushed through his tortuous head and at once he let out a mighty growl, muffled by his pillow. "A waste," he thought out loud. How could he compete on a championship level when he felt like shit? He hopped out of bed and turned on the TV, which had been turned up to it's full volume. The echoes of it's rumbling static nearly blew the Magi back under his covers. He made his way over to his dresser and poured himself a glass of whiskey without giving it a second thought. What harm could it do? If nothing else it might loosen him up, take his mind off his physical pain and enhance his in-ring performance. A little couldn't hurt him, this much he was convinced of. Time skipped ahead as everything beyond the first drink seemed unreal. Pouring, drinking, he repeated the process and couldn't exactly remember when his headache disappeared, but once it had it was too late. He convinced himself to take more- for his own good of course. Somewhere in his mind he could hear the first domino tumble. His next memory was of a Japanese man in a green mask gracefully twirling his body through the air, almost in slow motion, until finally the base of his foot met with the side of Jason's head. He couldn't even remember the three count... the sweet bliss of unconsciousness relieved the pain, regret, and remorse that surely would have followed. Back in the hospital Jason's daydream was interrupted by the crashing of his bed through two wooden doors. The lights were slightly dimmer in this room. "Jason we're going to see if you can stand up," Dr. Fields said. Two nurses helped Jason roll to his side and place his feet on the cold floor. As he began to shift his weight he could feel a crescendo of pain ripple from his bruised knees up to his mangled ribs. He let out a surprised grimace and fell backwards against the bed, stabilized by the staffers. The pain killers were wearing off, and the pain that would soon encompass his entire body would be unfathomable. Moments later he stood in the center of a small room, leaning gently on a metal walker, clad in lead protection gear. From behind a wall the voice of another doctor spouted instructions before brief flashes of light filled the room. Taking his mind off the pain in his ribs, knees, and wrist, he searched for something nice to think about, but his quest for favorable memories turned up empty. Instead he looked back on exactly what got him into this mess in the first place.
