Chillin' With My Sleeves Tied in the Back

25 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It wasn't the loudest scream he'd ever heard, but it was nonetheless disturbing. Especially at three in the morning. Tyrell continued down the hallway, clipboard in hand. Unlike many wings in the hospital, the psychiatric ward was often louder at night than it was in the daytime. Sleep, nightmares, or the darkness in general were the homes of demons for many of the wing's patients. Those who weren't sedated and were free to indulge in their personal horrors often voiced their terror quite audibly. Standing under one of the few lights in the hallway, Tyrell checked off several names on his list. So far, most of the patients he had to look in on were sleeping or restrained. Either way, they weren't about to harm themselves or anyone else. That just suited Tyrell fine and dandy. A quick scan told him there were only three more people to look in on. His eyes stopped on the name John Doe and he frowned. Ever since John Doe had been admitted, Tyrell felt uncomfortable around him. Maybe it was the fact that nobody could find out who he was. The fact that the psycho had cut half his face off was also quite disturbing. Nurses had told him that John had been quite physical when he was found by police and lashed out at them. Crazy as he was, John had managed to send four cops to the Emergency Ward before he was restrained and shipped to the Psych Ward. The restraints remained on while the doctors worked on John's face. Even after he was sedated, the restraints were nice and tight. Once he came out of surgery and he was fitted with a straitjacket, John was quite docile. In fact, he was practically catatonic. He just sat in the corner of his room, staring ahead with his one good eye and his one funky-looking dark eye. According to the notes on his chart, John Doe was coming out of the jacket tomorrow. Tyrell doubted the diagnosis was a good one given the level of physical damage Doe was capable of dealing. For a small guy, John Doe was a serious ass-kicker. If he had to guess, Tyrell would wager that John had quite a bit of martial arts training. Realizing the possible danger involved, his sedatives would be upped in dosage once he was free to move around. Tyrell walked up to John's door. He had no idea if they'd ever find out who the mystery man really was. There was something familiar about him, which bothered Tyrell to no end. Maybe once they could speak to him properly they'd get some answers. Until then, they'd have to settle with the vegetative John Doe. Lifting the penlight from his belt clip, Tyrell slid the small panel to the side and shone the light through the window. He jumped back, startled, as a black eye surrounded by white gauze stared back at him. John's wrapped face was pressed against the glass and Tyrell could hear his muffled moans of pain. He quickly stepped to the side of the door and checked John's notes. Yep, he'd received his injections for the evening. He shouldn't be awake, and certainly not feeling any pain. He took a breath and stepped back in front of John's door. "Hey buddy," Tyrell said as he stood close to the glass. "Feeling a little sore huh? Say, why don't you go sit down in your corner and try sleep it off. I'll make a note for the morning shift to get you your medication first thing." John pulled his face away from the glass and shuffled into the darkness. Tyrell slid the panel back over the glass and stepped away from the door. That guy gave him the creeps, and Ty was glad to be on his side of the door. He couldn't guess what nightmares a guy like John Doe had and wasn't anxious to find out. He turned the penlight to the clipboard. After staring at that eye, Tyrell wasn't going to be able to sneak in a few winks before shift change. He'd be lucky to sleep at all before his next shift. "The crazies are gonna make me crazy," he muttered to himself as he continued on his rounds.
