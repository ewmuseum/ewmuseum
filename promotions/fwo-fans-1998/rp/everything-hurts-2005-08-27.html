Everything Hurts

27 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Sally Apostle opened her eyes and tried to slow her rapid breathing as she stared at the ceiling in the dark. It had been awhile since she'd had the nightmare where Vic held a handgun to her pregnant stomach and threatened to blow her baby's brains out. Easy girl, easy. Take a breath, Sally ordered herself. It was just a dream this time. You're safe in your own bed, in your own house. The rain drummed against her bedroom window. Sally focused on the sound, diverting her attention from her rapidly beating heart. Her mind shifted from the fear from her dreams to the loneliness of reality. The last time she'd had that nightmare, David had been there to comfort her. As odd as he could be sometimes, David made her feel safe. He was the first man to do that in a long time. A very, very long time. Alex was in Kindergarten the last time a man had given Sally a sense of security. Dean owned the gym that Sally went to at the time. He was her personal trainer as she struggled to lose the stubborn baby fat she hadn't been able to drop. Dean had a great smile and a six pack you could grate cheese on. They went out on a few dates, and Dean was as charming in a pair of jeans as he was in track pants. After he charmed his way into her pants, Dean got a little possessive. Nobody at the gym dared look at Sally when she worked out. If any man approached her, Dean was up in their grill. He apologized to Sally later, saying he was dieting for the Venice Beach Invitational and it was making him irritable. When the contest was over and Dean almost slapped out the kid who delivered groceries to Sally's apartment, it was time to break it off. Dean, however, wasn't about to let Sally go. He followed her whenever she left her home, intimidated any guy who tried to talk to her. Finally, a restraining order was written up and Dean got the hint that Sally was serious. A new apartment. A new gym. A new start. Alex would be the only man in Sally's life, which meant focusing her extra energy into her workouts. When he was old enough, Alex started going to the gym with his mother. He'd go off and do his own thing, and eventually built himself into the fWo superstar he is today. Alex became a success. Sally became lonely. So where did David fit in? Was he just a booty call? Or was he filling the emotional void Sally had inside her? She'd never know now. A hard rap against the glass. Sally didn't recall the weather forecast calling for hail. She stood up and walked over to the window, hoping the glass wasn't broken by whatever had struck it. No, there was no damage
