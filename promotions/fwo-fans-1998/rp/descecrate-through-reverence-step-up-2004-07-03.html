Descecrate Through Reverence: Step Up.

3 July 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"So, Amy...I hear you gave the technician some trouble when she needed those blood tests done." Awkward way to start a conversation, but the social worker-slash-psychologist spoke those words the moment Amy sat down on the three-seater couch at the end of the room. Vivian eased into her own swivel chair, nothing but a small glass coffee table separating them as she laid out a small mountain of paperwork. Amy cringed over the number of "Sign Here" marks she noticed on the papers as she replied rather simply with a "Yeah. I'm not a fan of people sucking my blood with needles." Vivian O'Brien was blunt and considered the best at what she did...at least, for an underpaid Atlantic Canadian social worker. She majored in psychology and its various non-medical fields, and had ended up beginning her career at a Canadian Child and Adolescent Services center in New Brunswick, Canada. Fifteen years so far. Fifteen years, and she'd never seen the likes of Amy Campbell-Roche. This young woman wasn't easy to label, despite her painfully obvious insecurities and recorded history of obsessive compulsive, bipolar and panic disorders...not to mention jail time for numerous small offenses and an anger management problem that her career choice did nothing to help. She'd spent days reading Amy's files, preparing for this new client of sorts; A fairly confused girl who wanted to adopt back the accidental daughter she couldn't handle previously. Now two years older, a smidgeon wiser, married and accepting the son she adopted as a promise to her best friend who died delivering him after a car wreck at 17...Amy Campbell and Amy Roche seemed like two entirely different people, but they were both still crammed into one body. By the looks Amy was giving her during their initial small-talk and paperwork, Mrs. O'Brien could make the fair assumption that the girl was as intelligent as her high school IQ evaluation said. She picked up on every move Vivian made - every gesture, tiny expression or minute change in tone, Amy was analyzing. She obviously didn't trust psychologists, a trait easily explained by her insecurity...but the fact that she was so watchful and intent, almost too smart but trying to veil that fact made even the veteran psychological analyst a bit nervous. Physically, the little redhead didn't seem overly intimidating...especially not for a professional wrestler. She was filled with tattoos, but had a small frame that was more tone than muscle. Her handshake showed off an unusual upper body strength, but that's all Vivian had noticed...of course, aside from the pages upon pages describing her quick and sometimes vicious behavior in the psychiatric evaluation she'd been given when the appointment was made. Amy still held a childlike appearance. She looked young, slender and rebelliously adorned - better suited to be a pop punk princess on MTV than a pro wrestler, but that was sheer opinion. When asked, she explained the meanings behind the tattoos she had and made it very apparent that the girl didn't slack at all mentally. What really stood out, though, was the combination of naturally bright red hair and two-toned eyes - one so icy blue that it could've been mistaken for fake and the other a mahogany brown. Such a stark contrast, Vivian thought, that it could've been an easy metaphor for her nearly split personalities. So, indeed, this was one for the books. Unfortunately, she was well-aware that Amy wouldn't let it be that way. She'd see this girl a few times, but not often enough to help her. After all, the appointments had only been made for the adoption process and not for psychiatric care of the mother. Even still, the temptation to pry was too much and, after what seemed like dozens of forms were filled out, she stared Mrs. Roche dead in the eye and began a questioning that Amy had obviously seen coming. "What's your life like right now, Amy?" "Stressful." "And your marriage...?" "Stressful." "Do you know where this stress may be generated?" "Do you watch TV? I'm on it." said the redhead, "And let's put analysis away for another day, I'm only here to see my daughter. Vivian had been cut off at the pass. That was fine, though - they'd have future encounters. For now, she'd fufill Amy's current purpose... "Then..." she said, standing up and stepping to the door which, when opened, revealed an older woman holding a small hand, "Allow me to introduce you." That grasped hand was connected to a baby blue dress, mass of wavy red hair and two blissfully innocent doe eyes. Amy's mouth was agape, and Vivian continued speaking. "If you need...we'll go into the other room." she smiled, looking to the rather unresponsive mother. "Get yourself aquainted with her." At that, O'Brien and the older woman travelled quickly into the connected office area, leaving Amy Roche staring across the room at a little girl who was equally curious. She spoke while pointing a pudgy finger, "You...mommy?" Amy swallowed hard as she looked into the eyes of her biggest challenge yet...real motherhood. Accountability. Responsibility.
