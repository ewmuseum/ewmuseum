Best Days: Jack Harmen

28 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
His green hair bobbing from side to side, Jack Harmen, aka High Flyer or Fly to his friends, jammed out to his personal cd player in his hand. He held a brown suede gym bag in the other, which actually looked more like something that would be used for bowling purposes. His baggy black jean shorts were filled to the brim with personal things, such as additional cd's, his cellular phone for those long distance calls home, his wallet, keys, and pretty much every empty skittles or starburst wrapper from the past two weeks. Whatever the reason, he didn't seem to be throwing them out. He walked into the bar, taking in his surroundings before popping yet another starburst into his mouth. He wasn't used to being in a place like this, an avid avoider of social situations involving all things alcohol related. But he slowly walked over to the bar where his fellow co-workers sat and had on a smile as he did. "In a haze, these days, I pull up to the stoplight..." He sang, sitting down at the nearest stool before pulling his headphones off and down onto his neck at the request of Luke. "Hey guys. Heard about free drinks, and that's my kind of pricing." There was sort of an awkward moment, which Kevin's story was the cause. Jack didn't understand, his face still beaming as if it wasn't 2 am. He sort of was playing the role of the Frenchie here, even though the Frenchie was, well, here. "There's a catch," Luke was the first one to speak up, raising his hand as the waitress that was there earlier in the evening walked closer to Luke to once again give him an eye full. "There's always a catch," Jack said, rolling his eyes. "Just as long as I don't have to sign up and pay for the next four drinks at double the price." "No, no, no," Luke said, shaking his head as he went. Jack proceeded to simply order a can of coke from underneath the bar. "We're just relating the stories of our best days to one another." "Best day?" Jack thought for a second, looking around at all the distracted faces. It seems like a lot of the guys were no longer looking at one another, instead, directing their attention to either outside distractions or Jack himself. Jack received his coke and took a few sips. "Why are you all looking at me like that?" Jack stopped, taking a look at the faces around him, Avo, Lance Knight whom he only really knew as Lance, Brand Frontier, and all their eyes were pointed in his direction. Except Avo's, whose eyes could barely remain open and it's a wonder how he remained upright. "So, you want me to tell my story now I'm guessing? Although I feel as if I'm missing something here," Jack said, a little unsure of the whole situation. He could feel the small amount of tension, and wondered if his entrance was the cause of it. "And, might I add, I just got here!" he protested, taking yet another sip of his drink. It was an attempt to break up the tension, but it was futile, since the wrestler's didn't get that he was joking. See, Jack hadn't really gotten into many friendships with the fWo wrestlers since his entrance, so it was hard for him to dive into something so personal as a best day with people whom he thought, for the most part, were strangers and acquaintances. He'd been trying to walk the fine line backstage between veteran and newbie, and neither really afforded him time to get close with anyone. PA announcements could be heard in the background, and a lot of the guy's ears picked up whenever one was being announced. That didn't stop the wrestlers from listening intently to other's life stories, but it was a distraction they had to fight through. "I think I might need something stronger than this," Jack said, motioning to the coke in his hands. Jack raised his hand to the bartender, pointed to the bottle of rum behind him and made a motion with his fingers that would measure to about a shot. The bartender poured it into a double shot glass and the same waitress that has eyes for Luke brought it over to him. Jack thanked her, topped it off with some coke, and gulped it down. Jack then made what could be quite possibly the most squeamish looking face ever. He slammed the shot glass down, and rested his head in his hands. "Best day. Best Day. Think Flyer think!" He shouted to himself, raising a few eyebrows from the few other patrons inside the bar. "Ah-ha! Well, we were in Detroit." Jack looked out above the bar, as if looking up to a small imaginary bubble that held the actual visage of the memory. "The cameras were noticeably hot that evening, as I remember sweating like a pig." He laughed to himself, shaking his head as he went. "I was in this feud with my tag partner at the time and best friend, Tony Davis. See, there was this whole deal where we were both insanely over with the crowd, and the board wanted to milk it. So, we were in the process of a breakup angle that Tony and I were batting ideas around for. See, five months prior, I messed up both my knee and my elbow in this car crash. My knee was already in horrible shape, but I was just coming back from surgery, getting the ring rust out of me, you know, so I wasn't exactly wrestling every night." Jack could feel the pain that car crash caused in his joints. See, the pain may go away, but the memory of the moment never does, so the pain is always there, eating at you in the back of your mind. He shook his left leg, trying to forget about the imaginary pain that was there. "See, the back story is always a pain. Anyway, I don't even really remember the reason, but Tony was the cause of the car crash, and it was supposed to only take me out in character, put me on the shelf so I could marry Kate and what have you. But the damn door smashed in and sandwiched my leg between the door and the steering wheel, so my light fake injury became oh so real.
