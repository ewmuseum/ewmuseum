On the Road

7 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
[David Cross lays unconscious in the empty bus station, slumped uncomfortably in one of the hard, orange plastic chairs. Cross' steady snoring is interrupted by a passing janitor, who jabs him in the ribs with his broom handle. Cross sits up, stretching his arms overhead as the janitor disappears into the bathroom, having traded his broom for a mop. Cross looks at his watch.] Cross: Almost one in the morning. Still no sign of Crucifix though, which can only be a good thing. A taped hand smacks Cross in the back of the head with enough force to send him tumbling out of his chair. He lifts his face off the floor and looks up. Crucifix: Such disrespect. After everything I've done for you, and this is the appreciation I get. He stomps on David Cross' hand, who pulls his hand under himself and rolls to a seated position. Cross: I do appreciate what you're doing. Hey, I'm helping you with your travel arrangements, aren't I? Crucifix: You're doing what the fWo monkeys are supposed to be doing. They're still not getting the picture. I'm not going anywhere, and I'll hurt as many people as I have to until they realize that. No thanks to you. Cross: What's that supposed to mean? Crucifix: When you wore the mask, people just laughed at you. They're not laughing now. I make an example of Brand Frontier, showing that all those muscles and a few moves do not a wrestler make. But every time I turn around, there's you scurrying off to warn Frontier about me. I'm beginning to question your loyalties, David. Cross: I... I just don't see why you need all that barbed wire. I know what it's like to be hit with that stuff. I know what it's like to have some gorilla try to knock my head into the eighth row with a chair, or use my body to break tables into firewood. I just don't see what's wrong with going to the ring and giving people a good show. Crucifix: That's exactly what I do. Be it on the mat or in the air, I can do it better than most. And the fans? They're an ignorant bunch, especially the ones here in Canada. Cross: What makes Canadian fans so different? Crucifix: It must be the cold weather or something. Maybe there's a lot of inbreeding up here. Who knows? But these are the same people that cheered for the Orange Goblin back at Wrestlemania. Cross: I used to have a Hulk Hogan lunchbox. Crucifix: That doesn't surprise me. Guess I can just chalk up your warning Brand Frontier as gross stupidity. I'm still facing him in Montreal, right? Cross: It's going to be a tough match. He's a great wrestler, and he's done quite a bit since he started here in the fWo. [Crucifix slaps Cross across the face.] Crucifix: I'm not about to let Frontier get the best of me. I'm certainly not going to let you try and talk me down, either. I don't care what he's done in the past. He's weak right now, at a time where he can't afford to be. I'm skilled, I'm focused, and I know what must be done. Stop being such a mark, David. [David rubs his cheek.] Cross: I can't help it. I'm a fan. Crucifix: At what cost? Your face? [David's hand moves from his stinging cheek to his forehead.] Cross: Shut up. Crucifix: How unfortunate for you. Your love of wrestling cost you your face, and your failure at wrestling gave you a new one. Cross: SHUT UP! [He swings a looping right hand at Crucifix, who easily traps the arm and drives a knee into David's chest. With the arm still trapped, Crucifix straightens Cross up and hammers his face with stiff kicks. After the final kick, he releases the arm, letting Cross fly over a row of chairs and crashing to the floor. As David stares at the ceiling, blood trickling from his nose and split lip, Crucifix stares down at him.] Crucifix: Remember your place, David. You had your chance, and were too weak to succeed. Where you have failed, the Angel of Agony will succeed. Brandon is only the first. Soon, they will all crumble. And their pain... will be my pleasure. [The bathroom door opens and the janitor quickly scrambles out, his belt buckle undone and a long strip of toilet paper hanging out the back of his pants. He looks around for the cause of the commotion, but all he sees is a young man sitting in a chair, wiping blood from his nose and swollen lip as he plunks coins into the small television beside him.]
