Path.

8 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Amy rolled over for the umpteenth time. Vic Creed was in her dreams, again and in the worst way. She wasn't having any corny nightmares, not "reliving" anything or screaming in her sleep - it was a simple, unsettling dream. The same one every time. Always seemingly innocent...it happened again and again, but she couldn't even remember what 'it' was. Even the dreams and horrible thoughts were routine, as with everything else since she'd found herself alone...and the redhead knew that checking on her son a thousand times a night wasn't habit, but an attempt to escape how empty even tossing and turning in her nightmares seemed. Why the hell couldn't she be losing this sleep over wedding plans, maybe being nervous over taking Brandon and the kids to see her family over Christmas - something that meant something, that wasn't just this overwhelming sense of being unsafe, insecure... But there reality was again. She had no clue where Brandon was and no idea where Vic laid...somehow, the first scared her the most. Where Creid could destroy her phsycially, Brandon had the ability to easily - even accidentally - destroy Amy emotionally and get himself in painfully over his head. It all hurt mostly because he was different. Brandon wasn't a cheater, he wouldn't dare lie to her and he wasn't desperate or by any means a loser like those before him - "men" who'd live with their parents until moving in and living off of her, little boys who'd say "I love you" just because it meant they could look at their lives and say "Ha. I might be a friendless cry baby/heavy drinker/druggie/general loser/all of the above, but at least I've got a girlfriend.". He was in a position to have almost anybody he desired, but chose Amy above them all and that fact had her sure that when he said those three words, when he defended or complimented her it was genuine - it was real, not the action of someone desperate or codependent. That's why it hurt...because, of anybody who she'd ever been seperated from, he was worth having. And more importantly than his meaning every word was the fact that she felt the exact same way. She knew he'd return if not just BECAUSE of how real everything was, but it was the waiting that fucked it all up. Having to explain to Stephen every day where his surrogate father and brothers were - missing Sean and Jarrod HERSELF - it was wearing all too thin. Worse yet was talking to her parents as they tried convincing her it was all less than love. Amy might not have been able to explain what love is, but of anybody on the planet she's probably fastest to explain what love isn't after so many demonstrations. People didn't even have the decency to let her hurt. Call after call, question after answered question; Constant reminders that right now, she was alone and couldn't do a thing to help the life connected to hers. But then, the realization came... She understood this. Amy was quickly growing to learn exactly what Vic did to people, and how easy it was to get clawed into mindgames you'd normally assume he was too muscle-bound to play. What, short of murder, was going to stop this for all of them and how the hell was she to take it upon herself to protect everything dear to her against a man three times her size? Well...everybody needs goals.
