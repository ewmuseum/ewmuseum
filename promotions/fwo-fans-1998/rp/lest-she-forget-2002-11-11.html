Lest She Forget

11 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Monday November 11th, 2002. 10:12am Rememberance Day service, home. "...lest we forget these brave men who left their homes as young as sixteen to fight for the freedoms we enjoy today. Lest we forget..." She listened intently to the elderly man who was reading the names at the cenotaph...the usual array of John Smiths, Peter so-and-so's, but then one that struck near to Amy: the only Bernely on the list, though one of many un-related Campbells. Her grandfather had died in 1996, thanks to an onslaught of medical problems that would plauge any 83-year-old man, including a liver condition, courtesy of his earlier alcoholism and emphysema that came from years of abusing his body with cigarettes. The death had struck her hard, though she wasn't even 12 at the time...but she was old enough to understand, and that's what made it the most difficult. He had adored the little redhead, always had a new pet name for her. Though his health and mental state were failing in later years, he always knew her when she spoke, even if he couldn't remember her name at first...it was unmistakably Amy, the skinny little girl with prose, posture and wit to match any adult's. Of course, there were always little scares with his health, and he probably should've been dead by the time his grand daughter was five...but Bernley stuck around as long as he possibly could. One day, Amy remembered, her grandmother had called to say she was taking him up to the hospital to get him checked out, apparently because he "just had a little cold". By the time Amy had come home from school, her father was crying, and her older sisters were there in the car, which was a little unusual considering the high school got out two hours later than elementary... She'd known it then. Her grandpa was going to die...and that definitely hit hard. He'd died that night, and Amy could easily remember the seclusion; She hadn't wanted to go near any member of her family, or any human being for that matter. One of her oldest brothers had left where the family had gathered to mourn, and he'd made the drive back into town to see Amy. Her grandmother always worried, and apparently she'd "flipped out" when she found out the little girl refused to speak to anyone...the last thing she wanted was her grand daughter to have a fear of death. So that night, her brother'd spoken to her, and one statement still rang in her ears... "Just grow up, live life and do the right thing. Don't do anything to hurt yourself, he'll watch you and help you whenever he can." Aimz had failed there...miserably. She was standing at the cenotaph with her grandmother and when she looked around at the others, she saw no one visibly under 55...and no one who hadn't given her a sideways glance or two already. Of course, the girl who'd "ruined" naturally red hair by tinging it with pink, the one who'd marred her own flesh with tattoo ink and piercings, the same one they'd admired the courage and heart of years ago when she came to lay a wreath for her grandfather...well, that girl just didn't fit right now. She'd worn the most appropriate clothing she could find: rather snug black pants, minus the wallet chain, and a simple burgundy t-shirt underneath a zip-up West 49 hoodie. Amy'd tried her best to cover her tattooed forearms, though the ankh, a religious symbol she'd once believed to be the key to eternal life after death, on the back of her neck showed underneath her loose ponytail. The stares never stopped, though. A few children passing on their walk to avoid the ceremony had stopped her, asking questions and wanting signatures for some reason that seemed crazy to Amy now...they admired her? She began pondering everything...the kids who looked up to her, her brother's words eight years ago and her grandfather, who was looking down somewhere, maybe trying to guide her. When there was a short pause in the service, Aimz leaned down to mutter into her grandmother's ear... "Grandma," She began, but paused a moment, innerly chuckling over how, at twenty years old, she still referred to her family by the same names she had for as long as she could remember. Her next words struck the older lady as very odd, though. "Am I a good person?" Tears almost came to her eyes as she squeezed her grand daughter's hand, looking up into eyes that were adult enough, but seemed almost like a child's...still very open to the world, and learning about every aspect of it. "You're a wonderful person and one of the most kind-hearted people I've ever met, if you believe that." "Well why have I been screwing up so much?" "Only you can answer that," She managed a weak smile and planned her words to carefully avoid a religious argument, since the redhead had always seemed eager to argue the existance of dieties. "You need to remember the people who want for you to be the woman we all know you can be. Your grandfather would have been heartbroken to see when you went sour...all that ruckus when you were a teenager." That's when Amy nodded up toward the sky, into the bleak, gray cold... "He can see me." ...It was also when her grandmother smiled brightly, clasping the soft, un-marked, much younger hand. "So he can. Make him proud." Amy Campbell was slowly, but surely, planting her feet back on the ground and finally growing up, it seemed. She'd finally started noticing her reasons to keep on the right path, and they weren't nearly as selfish as her reasons to veer off of it...these ones actually meant something. So she smiled, but it would be her last smile for a while. Little did Amy know at the time, there was a soon-to-be journalist who wanted nothing but to make her path seem more corrupt...
