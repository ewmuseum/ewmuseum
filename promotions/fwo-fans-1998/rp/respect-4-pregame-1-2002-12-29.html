Respect (4 - Pregame 1)

29 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"I've gotta come out of the gate strong and establish myself in this one. I relied on my wrestling ability too much in the last one, and Sean still had enough left at the end that he pulled that counter out of his ass and put me away." Sitting alone in my locker room, I slipped the burnt compact disc into the portable cd player and the sounds of M.O.P.'s "Cold As Ice" filled the headphones. I smiled in spite of myself, as this had been Alex Lawson's theme when he had been one of the most dominant heels on the independent scene. Alex had been responsible for training me after Dom had fallen ill, and I had actually wrestled my first professional match as his tag team partner in the fed he had been working in at the time, Lethal Championship Wrestling. LCW had been a good fed, but I hadn't done well enough in my debut match to earn a shot there, and I'd been forced to go back to the school until my UPW tryout. I'd met up with Alex a few times since then, once on a tour of Japan two years ago that both of us had worked, and then again last spring, when the fWo had put three Evo matches on one of their house shows in Philly. For the life of me, I could never understand why a man like Alex would've stayed in so small a fed, wrestling in a night club, but it was about more than the money for him. He'd actually helped to design and build the club, even doing most of the ring construction work himself, and it was that investment that had kept him in Evo in spite of offers from other, bigger feds. It was thinking about Alex that brought me to formulate my strategy for the match. Alex was only an inch or two taller than me, but he had packed a great deal more muscle onto his frame. He was the one responsible for the vast majority of my techniques in the ring, and essentially the one that urged me to focus on developing my suplexes, as he had. I tried to think back to some of the lessons he had given me, but instead what came to mind was the match that he had worked on the house show. Wrestling as Duel, he had dominated that match from bell to bell with a variety of throws and suplexes that'd make most of the guys I wrestled with in Japan envious. Coming on strong was most certainly the key, but I'd need to maintain the intensity throughout the match, which would likely be the biggest challenge I faced. That had been a tag match, and Alex had had the opportunities to regain his wind when he started to wear down. I wouldn't have that luxury. That would make a quick start all that more important. If I could come out aggressively enough and put Trips down hard a few times, that'd most likely allow me the opportunity to get my air back if I started to blow up. There was too much riding on this match to not win, and aggression had to be the key, as far as I could tell. Inflict as much damage as possible and then pick up the win, but at the same time, I couldn't afford to be too forceful, lest I get reckless and give Sean the opportunities that he needed to pick up the win. Yeah, I had my plan, now it was time to get ready.
