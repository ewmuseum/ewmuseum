Soul Taker

16 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Kodiak Vic Creed stared at himself in the mirror, wondering about nothing and everything. He hated being like this. He hated the fact that things seemed to be just out of his grasp. People he trusted (as much as he trusted anybody anymore) had betrayed him. Not only him, but Creed's only source of true innocence - his daughter Sarah. She was his soul; the only thing that came out of his life of rage, violence, and corruption that was actually pure. Good. Innocent. Yes he drove her away. It was for her own good to be away from him. Everything Vic touched became dirty, soiled, tainted. If he'd only sent her away, her heart would draw her back to him. That's why he was so callous towards her. He had to drive that wedge between them to sever all emotional ties his daughter may have still had for her father. Now his sins may have tracked her down. Over the past two days, Vic hadn't left his hotel room. He tried to formulate some kind of strategy to deal with Darryl Vishanti before he could try to inflict his will on the Main Man. Vic wasn't about to let some punk (a well-connected punk, but punk nonetheless) get the upper hand on him. Right now Darryl held all the cards. Vic wanted to sway things in his favor. His cellular phone chirped. Vic slowly walked out of the bathroom and looked at the phone as it vibrated on his bed. The Caller I.D. was blocked so he had no way of knowing who was calling him. He didn't need to know. Vic snatched it off the blanket and pressed the Talk button. "Speak." The voice was smooth, composed, with an authoritarian tone that always made Creed think of Agent Smith. "Hello Vic. So good to speak to you again." "Fuck you." "Why, that's no way to talk to your financial superior. I'm a much richer man now, thanks to your... obedience." "Obedience my ass. You wanted me to win a match I had no intention of losing, you pissant." He knew Vishanti was trying to push his buttons. Unfortunately, he was succeeding. "That may be Vic. That may be." Every word mocked him. Vic's grip tightened on the phone and the case started to crack. "But I guess we'll put that to the test at Twilight." "Fuck you." "For someone who calls himself the Main Man, you certainly don't sound very manly using such vulgarity. Swearing makes you sound desperate." "Then I must fucking be at the cocksucking end of my goddamn rope, assbite." It took more effort not to crush the phone in his hand than it would just to splinter it into tiny pieces. "Actually Vic, I believe you are. The pressure of being World Champion has obviously been taking it's toll on you. I think you need a break." "Blow me Princess." "No Vic, I believe it will be you who will be blowing me. You see, at Twilight you're competing against Amy Campbell. While she is probably a worthy adversary in your wrestling world, in the gambling world she's what is known as a long shot. One that would pay off handsomely if she were to defeat you for the World Title." "I'm not laying down Darryl." "I think you misunderstood me Vic. I was not making a request. I was giving you the outcome of your little match. Amy Campbell will beat you for the fWo World Title, and I will collect several million dollars from my European counterparts." "You don't pull my strings. You never did. If you want to kill me, meet me in the locker room after I beat Aimz into a bloody pulp. You're more than welcome to try there." "As much as it would give me a great deal of pleasure to do so, I'm afraid I'm going to have to decline. Besides, there are other ways to influence you." If there's a God in Heaven, it won't be about Her. The line was silent. "Daddy?" Kodiak Vic Creed looked up into the Heavens. Fuck you, he cursed at the Almighty. In his heart, Vic burned down a hundred churches and killed a thousand Saints. He wanted to die right where he stood so he could confront Him and tear His throat out. Either that, or there was no one looking out for the Innocent. In which case, Vic might not be going to Hell in a handbasket. Darryl's voice came back on the line. "I heard that you spoke with your assistant. Don't blame him for this Vic. I knew where she was the moment she left your side. You see, her Foster Parent work for me. I always knew Vic. I always had her." Son. Of. A. Bitch. "I know you won't go to the police. We both know you can't save her from me any more than you could save her from yourself." "You so much as touch a hair on her head -" "Yes I know. You'll kill me, torture me, blah blah blah." Vishanti's voice took on a stern tone. "Let me tell you how this is going to work. You're going to wrestle at Twilight. You're going to lose your title. If not, your daughter will be involved in a fatal car accident." He almost bragged about it. "I promise you she'll be beaten to within an inch of her life, but she'll be very much awake when we start the car on fire." The phone shattered in Creed's hand. Plastic splinters dug into his palm, but he was beyond feeling pain. His guttural roars could be heard down the hallway as he punched a hole in the wall. Vic grabbed the table and shattered the mirror hanging on his wall before throwing it against the door. His bed was flipped against the wall with a roar and a swipe of his hand. On the street outside the hotel, pedestrian scattered as a dresser crashed through a window on the fourteenth floor and careened through the air before exploding from the impact against the sidewalk. Tens of thousands of dollars of damage done in an instant. Further damage to his knee as he kicked down his hotel room door. None of it mattered to Vic Creed. He knew a long time ago that he was going to Hell for what he'd done in the past. He had no idea he'd go there while he was still alive.
