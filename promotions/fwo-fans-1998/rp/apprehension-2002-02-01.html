Apprehension

1 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"It's like every time you sense you are about to succeed, you stop trying!" The woman exclaimed in a voice filled with both frustration and dread. Mufanga Kagos forced his mind through a quick meditation exercise, trying to stave off the inevitable reaction. Being the subject of the wrestling world's recent news, speculation and critique was eating up reserves of patience that he simply didn't have. Physically it had been too many months since he'd had the freedom to be an active wrestler, and mentally it was worse, it had been too much time since he had been in front of an fWo crowd. Last Sunday he had been a wreck, practicing his little speech over and over before his locker mirror, studying his lines. He wanted to sound sincere, cause God knew he was, but didn't want to get out there and freeze up. His memories of the Survivor contest told him he could deal with this, could deal with a match in an fWo ring. But his last fWo match, the Survivor Battle Royal, he fell from the top rope in a blown spot that left him laid out on the cement floor. That fall dealt him an injury he had never really healed from, and put a kind of fear in him that he thought would simply render him useless as a wrestler. He was a high-flyer. A risk-taker. What would he do if he climbed the top rope against Crisp this Sunday and simply froze? His carefully controlled and regulated emotions were drowned beneath a dark murky memory of days and weeks spent curled around wounds that never really healed, wounds that his body still ached faintly from. But he had gotten used to that before, hadn't he? Those feelings were overwhelming, the remembrance of how the sound of flesh on cement went from being a warning to ready himself for another attempt to break him, to a source of terror, to simply another sound. "As your semi-official fWo guardian Ben, I know you are still hurting, but you have the fan support now. You have to step up to the plate and deliver," the woman told him. Unlike everone else, she still called him Ben. It was one of the things he loved about her. "Is that what you are?" Mufanga Kagos asked. "I've been wondering." "Sort of," she replied, Kagos could sense her casual shrug even if he couldn't see it. "I know that it always has a danger to it, but...you know-" "I know. Play the people," Mufanga Kagos sighed. "But the people do love kamikaze missions, don't they?" "Do you really want to begin your fWo career like this?" the woman asked with an overbearing tone. "Without me there?" "It's better this way." Mufanga Kagos asked. "First you were there for me, and then John Rider. Now, it's just me. This is something I have to do on my own." She groaned in frustration, but he cut her off with another quick promise, before hanging up the phone. "I'll call you after the match. I'll be fine." And he would be fine... wouldn't he?
