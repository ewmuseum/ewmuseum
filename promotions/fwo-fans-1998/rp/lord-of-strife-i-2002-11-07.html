Lord Of Strife I

7 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
They chased the villain into a bunker, where he met with one of the leaders of his cult. None of the soldiers knew what words were exchanged. They didn't need to. They only needed to know that this man, Bryan Wavyrn, was a neo-terrorist and that the only way to keep humanity safe was to destroy him. But when the door opened, it was not the young threat who emerged, but his mentor. After a moment's hesitation, they did what they knew needed to be done. They did not shoot to kill, just to stop Dr. Martan. He wasn't going far, anyway. Yes, he was an old man, but he had been responsible for the death of thousands of innocents. The world would not ache to miss him. With fury, they stormed in on him, making sure he would not die. He was a leader and would have knowledge that could end the carnage he had been so instrumental in implementing for nearly twenty years. Wavyrn had watched it all from the bunker and did not emerge until he could be sure the coast was clear. What he had just been told shook him. His world was about to change...or was it about to end? These words are not my own, but you are familiar with their author. You have seen him on your televisions, but you may not know his name. He hides his identity from you. Hell, he hides it from himself. In his relentless flight from his past, he's forgotten who he is, but I remember. I will never forget. His name is Trent Harrison. You know him as Downfall. Before he came to be a wrestler, Harrison was an author. His novels were sold in bookstores from the shelves of science fiction and fantasy sections. What I know, however, is that there is no fiction or fantasy to his rhetoric. I know this because I lived it. Of course, you do not believe a word I say. Why would you? None of you believed my words before when they were against a faceless entity you were apathetic to. Now I make accusations against a man you much admire. Why shouldn't you approve of him? Eli Flair does, and Eli Flair gets beat in the face with metal and wooden shit for a living, which, of course, makes him an authority on the matter of character. This story I recount above has already been told to you, and the first mouth from which you heard it was my own. The Dr. Martan of this novel, Lord of Strife as Downfall has chosen to title it, is the Burkenstock I told you of already. I must admit, Harrison was rather clever with the name. I don't think I need to say who Bryan Wavyrn is. So Trent Harrison knows my story. Not only that, but he knows the parts of my story that I thought no one did. Not only that, but he's known them for some time. "What?" you say. "I do not even believe your story, but if I did, this proves nothing." There is more, and you will hear it. I do not know how Harrison knows so much, but I sense I am close to an answer. In the meantime, I will cover my bases and leave nothing to chance. Because chance, my friend, is a villain greater than any man could ever be. ... Oh, that girl I hit, that was an accident, but no, I do not care if she dies. My hands are stained already. Additional blood will dirty unnoticed.
