Where Everybody Knew Her Name

25 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
When it hit, it hit like a fist to the face. I'm used to Sunday nights being slow, easy, and low stress. Considering how much business this place does on Saturdays, we can easily deal with it, and as the only person scheduled for most Sundays, you'll never hear me complain. Speaking of which, you're probably wondering who I am and what I'm doing here. No, I'm not Impulse, Randall Knox, RK Impulse, whatever you want to call him. My name is Rosalyn Callasantos, but people pretty much refer to me as Cally. Only RK and my mother call me Rose, and only RK calls me Rosie. But he's off somewhere doing his wrestling thing, so unfortunately he couldn't be here tonight. You're stuck with me. Sorry 'bout it. At the moment, forty three young to middle aged men just walked into my bar and began to order drinks. That sounds like the setup to a joke or something. "Forty three men walked into a bar..." Sorry. Someone else might've been overwhelmed, and I could've easily been, but I've found my bearings in the past two years. Okay, I said, we need a little bit of order here. "This man right here," said one of the others, clapping a young looking guy on the shoulder, "is getting married in four days. We're here to celebrate the death of life as he knows it." He took a credit card out of his pocket and handed it to me. "Money's no object, honey," he said, "everything this group orders goes on that card, and keep 'em coming." Despite the fact that I didn't really like being called honey, I got to work. I took the guy's card and put it with the other running tabs, and opened up the notebook that Miss Ivy kept underneath the bar to a fresh page, and started to keep track of what was ordered. The first few guys were good and organized, but after that, the entire party pretty much swarmed the bar about two or three deep. And they changed my music. I heard Poe scratch away, and some Beatles song take its place. I'm sorry, I know the old saying, everyone discovers the Beatles at some point in their lives, but I haven't gotten there yet. Slow and easy, Rosie, I said to myself, willing my lungs to hold together. The tightness was in my chest, but it never got as far as hacking for breath. And all of a sudden, I was joined. Miss Ivy, I said, as, quite literally out of nowhere, Miss Ivy was behind the bar. What are you doing here? "Talk later," said Miss Ivy, "Let's knock these guys out. What's happening?" Bachelor party, I told her, they're all on one tab so just keep track of what you pour and we'll add it all up. Out of the corner of my eye, I saw Miss Ivy's brother, Brian, come behind the bar too. He immediately went under the counter and started checking the keg levels. Perfect, we've got a bar back. This is my home for hours at a time, five nights a week, so I moved pretty well back here. Miss Ivy has spent less and less time here since January, but she picked up on my rhythm really quick and followed it. That was pretty cool, actually - since she owns the place she could've insisted that I follow her rhythm, but that's not her style. I held my tongue on all the questions I wanted to ask her - why is she here? How is RK? Do you know if he's coming home soon - because it wasn't the right thing to do. After RK's first few weeks as a professional wrestler I realized that the last thing he really wanted to do when he got home was talk about professional wrestling for more than a few minutes. Finally, the rush slowed to a crawl, then stopped altogether. I took the papers with the drink orders collected, and started to tally them up. Out of the corner of my eye, I noticed Brian had taken a seat on the other side of the counter, so I fixed him an ice water. "Damn, kiddo," said Miss Ivy, "that was good." Was it? We're busier than that on Saturday nights, and three weeks ago I was working alone. About average, I said, if I'd known they were coming I'd've scheduled someone else tonight. "So it's this busy regularly?" asked Brian. Yeah, I said, as I picked up a short glass and a bottle of Johnnie Walker Black, Johnnie, Miss Ivy? "Sure," said Miss Ivy, "Wait---no. Absolutely fuck not." I waited. "PBR," said Miss Ivy. Pabst Blue Ribbon it was. I pulled a can out of the cooler and put it on the bar, and closed it up. "Gross," said Brian. "Make it two," continued Miss Ivy, "one for each of us." I went back into the cooler and pulled another can out. I opened both cans while handing one to Miss Ivy - being able to open cans or bottles on the fly is a skill every bartender needs to survive. We toasted, and drank. "Yeah, man," said Miss Ivy with her eyes closed, "that's the shit." "It's shit, alright," said Brian. "I mean, this is what I've been missing," said Miss Ivy, "You know what I was doing three months ago?" You were in Afghanistan, I said, as I shook my head. They both looked at me, and laughed. "Touche, Cally," said Miss Ivy, "What I meant is that I was a member of the FWO's locker room camraderie, after shows we'd go to Redbeard's and get a platter of wings and a few pitchers, talk about the show, and get home at three or four in the morning, on the far side of buzzed." She looked a little sad for a second. "It was fun," said Miss Ivy, "Y'know?" Out of the corner of my eye, I saw one of my regulars gesture with an empty glass, and I backed up toward them, keeping my attention on Miss Ivy's conversation. "Now, I'm one of them," said Miss Ivy, "The management team." You're management here, too, I said, as I put the glass in front of my customer. Six, I told them, while they handed me a ten and gestured to keep it. Thanks. But even though you're my boss, I said, I don't see a problem with havin' a beer with you, even while I'm working. "Professional wrestling is different," said Brian, "The boys have always had a distrust - even unconsciously - of the bosses." RK trusts you, I said. Ivy laughed. "That's because me and Eli, and Angry Johnny and Coop were the first authority figures Knox ever associated with wrestling," said Miss Ivy, "and we never did or would fuck him over. Another month of Dennis and he'll have a different view." "So why'd you take the job?" asked Brian. "Because that's not the job I was taking!" said Miss Ivy. Some of my regulars turned their heads at this, but Miss Ivy lowered her voice. "I was hired as the head of Talent Relations," said Miss Ivy, "I was supposed to be the liason between William and his staff, and the locker room. Mediating arguments, being an impartial arbitrator, handling complaints on both ends, and all that jazz. William's got me booking matches, finding talent, dealing with the press... it's too much." So why do you do it? I asked. "Because I'm good at it," said Miss Ivy, "Because I used to enjoy helping the FWO." "Don't you anymore?" asked Brian. "It's not the same," said Miss Ivy, "Years ago I would help out Fox, and we'd talk about it afterwards. We'd watch stuff together and figure why it did or didn't work, and go from there. It was a back and forth. With William, I feel like I'm his personal assistant who runs the store while he sits in an ivory tower and counts his money." "He does go to the shows, right?" asked Brian. "Of course he does," said Miss Ivy, "and I am being a bit unfair. He does whatever he has to - but the boys are partly to blame for coming to me instead of going where they should be, and I'm partly to blame for letting 'em." So your problem is you, I said. "What?" asked Miss Ivy. She and her brother looked at me. William Beaven is your boss, I said, and you're doing your job and his? "In a matter of speaking," said Miss Ivy. You're my boss, Miss Ivy, I said, I tend bar, I do the deposits, I manage the schedule, I keep the upstairs loft clean, mainly because I'm the only one who uses it. I do the bar laundry because I can pull the money from the register. But if I found your clothes mixed in with the wash, besides the occasional emergency spills, I'd sit you down for a talking to because I'm not your housekeeper. They looked like they were really listening to me, so I took a drink of my beer to give myself a moment to think of something else to say. I think you need to talk to Mr. Beaven, I finally said. MissIvy looked at Brian, and back at me. She laughed. "See, Brian?" asked Miss Ivy, "This is why I wanted to come here before I went home." Brian smiled. "So you know what to do now?" asked Brian. "I know what to do now," said Miss Ivy. Miss Ivy held up her can of beer, and Brian tapped his glass against it. "But for now," said Miss Ivy, "I think we owe Ms. Callasantos the courtesy of our assistance for the rest of the night." I smiled. It's unsettling to see Miss Ivy so worried and unsure of herself, but if I was able to help at all, than I'm glad I was here. And if you were bored, I promise that RK will take over next time.
