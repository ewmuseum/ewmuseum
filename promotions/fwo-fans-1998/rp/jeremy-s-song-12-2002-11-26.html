Jeremy's Song - 12

26 November 2002

previous parts: see-downfall archive Bland colors and darkness blanketed the black shingles and stained brick house. The place resembled a prison with windows strategically placed amongst the dark gray bricks. Matching concrete pathway led to a rotting door made of breakaway wood. The faint glimmer of the brass doorknob immersed in dirt and the tall, green grass were the only shades of color to be found. The only noise filling the empty space was the sound of a truck honking its horn on a distant highway. A dim porch light placed near the top right corner of the door gave the only light for a reasonable distance. Jeremy walked up the pathway and approached the door. Blue light overtook the window, thus indicating that someone was watching television in the family room. A nudge on the brass handle, and the door remained closed. A hard pull, and the door finally flew open. The warmth of the house was a welcome feeling as Jeremy placed his leather book bag in a corner near the door. The floors and walls were wooden paneling, while the ceiling was an eggshell white plaster of some sort. With all of the lights off, the only things visible in the family room were the brown couch, the television now playing reruns of "I Love Lucy", and a side table holding up an empty bottle of vodka, a bottle of scotch, and a beer. All of this was ignored. Jeremy walked into the room and took a seat on a recliner a few feet from where his father sat. The man was in his boxers and a wife beater shirt that hung off his thin frame, matching that of his son. A glazed over look coated his eyes as he laughed at the black and white screen. His son looked over and nodded out of disappointment. There was nothing to be proud of. Others can claim their family worked hard for what they had, regardless if they lived in some rat hole in the outskirts of town. Jeremy couldn't say this. Sitting around all day, his father drank while his mother went out to work at no potential job at a local McDonalds. There was hardly any food around the house but alcohol was abundant. Jeremy's eyes wandered over to the side of his father's face. Slouched backward the man didn't acknowledge his son's own existence. Life wasn't normal for Jeremy. Parents, to everyone else, are people you can talk to about anything and people who trust you. In this world, parents were drunks who forever imprinted your burlap textured couch. He was running out of people to turn to. Already he had run out of people to turn to. "I quit the basketball team," he stated bluntly in monotone. Like every other male, Jake Benton (his father) was a sports enthusiast. Jeremy playing basketball wasn't important him but telling people his son played basketball was. A showpiece was all his son was good for. Without that, Jeremy was nothing to him. Jake turned his head and stared at his son. "Oh, and why da fuck did you do that?" A shrug was the only reply he would get. "You know that's all your good for. Without basketball, what else are you going to rely on? There are people with college degrees sleeping on the street using them to wipe the shit from their ass crack. You're a good for nothing fucker." The drunken slur, maybe he didn't really think that way. Alcohol weighed heavy on his father's brain but even when sober he made the same comments. No excuses, except for the fact that even his own family didn't want him. Jeremy pushed himself up from the couch using his arms and walked away. Upstairs, a closet sized room fitted a bed and a small end table with an alarm clock on it reading nine o'clock. The digital red numbers read clear. "Oh, and that Tom kid called, askin' what happened at practice. Guess that little faggot didn't quit the basketball team, eh?" The message was ignored. Jeremy sat at the end of his bed and looked at the stained walls. Water dripped to the brown carpet below as he thought about his life in general. The mention of Tom caused memories to flash through Jeremy's head, now treading off sane territory. Tom was Jeremy's best friend. In the coarse of one's life, one finds someone that other people associate him or her with. The relationship between the two had come to that point. Both played on the basketball team. Both went to the mall together. The two were best friends. What more could be said? One memory stood out like a sore thumb. A bathroom with blue tile flooring and white porcelain sink and toilet was the scenery. Inside a mirror was the reflection of a broken man in the formation of Jeremy. Eyes swollen and red from hours of crying in front of rooms of people. Broken down, nothing would stop him at this point. A black object lay on the surface of the sink. Large, metallic, the cold feeling of the metal against his hand as Jeremy picked it up and fell to his hands and knees. Tears welded up inside of him after years of coming home to a family that didn't care, going to school and putting on a falsified life so everyone would think he's happy.
