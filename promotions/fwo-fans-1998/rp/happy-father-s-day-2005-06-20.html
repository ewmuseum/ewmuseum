Happy Father's Day

20 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Three thirty in the morning. Cabbot Wilson looked at his watch again just to make sure he wasn't seeing things. He'd just received a phone call from his client, Kodiak Vic Creed, asking him to come to his room. As was customary, Cabbot had his own key despite having a room to himself. When Cabbot walked into the room, he was a little taken aback. He'd walked in on Vic before, during, and after performing various lewd acts with women of low moral fiber. He'd walked in on Creed shooting up various, well Cabbot never asked what he'd been shooting. This was the first time he'd walked in on Creed in the middle of a drinking spree. Five large whiskey bottles lay empty on the bed. The sixth sat on the table beside the Main Man. "Vic, is everything okay?" Creed lifted his glass and toasted his agent. "I miss your old man, Cabbot. I miss a lot of things." Vic sat at the table, wearing only his black silk boxers, with his legs stretched out in front of him. A homemade card, drawn in crayon, lay on the table underneath Vic's hand. Cabbot could make out some of the letters in the dark and realized it was a Father's Day card... made by his daughter Sarah. Cabbot pulled out the other chair and sat down. "I miss Slade too." "Bullshit. He treated you like shit. We all treated you like shit." Wilson pulled the whiskey bottle out of reach of Vic's right hand. "I think you're a little drunk Vic." There was only a slight slur in his voice when Creed spoke. "I wish. I've got so much shit running through my system that I'd have to take a handful of sleeping pills just to get some shut eye." Less than twenty-four hours before Creed had to appear on television and here he was drinking his bodyweight in alcohol. "You should try and get some sleep. We've got Ruahh on Monday, and a Pay Per View at the end of the week." Vic reached out and pulled the bottle closer to him. "Look at me. I'm a bastard of humanity. Do you know how many lives I've ruined? I killed babies, abused pregnant women, destroyed families, raped, brutalized. If I hadn't been rich enough to have one of the best legal teams in America, I would have been locked away without any chance of parole. You know what the sick part is? I did it because I could. It came naturally to me. That's why I tried so hard to keep the people I cared about far away from me." Vic moved his hand and looked down at the card. "I guess I'm still doing it, too." Cabbot looked around the hotel suite, making sure they were alone. He didn't need Vic's drunken babbling being leaked to the Internet. "You know what I liked about being the World Champ?" Vic asked. "It meant I'd reached my goal. Everything I'd done, all the sick, shitty things I'd done along the way, they were all worth it. The end justified the means." Kodiak Vic Creed then raised his glass. "Brand Frontier, champion of all that is good, took that from me. Now Deacon, God Almighty's favorite wrestler, has the belt that I sacrificed my daughter for. You think God's going to give me another chance to have that belt? You think God wants me to be able to say The end justifies the means?" "I didn't think you believed in God," Cabbot said. "Sure I do," Creed replied as he topped up his glass once again. "And I hate the motherfucker." Watching Creed gulp down the whiskey was making Cabbot nauseous. "Vic, take it easy." "You wanna hear something funny? He won't let me die." More drunk talk. Well, best not to get the big ape angry. Still, it was too late at night to be doing this.
