Vic Creed's Forever in an Instant

28 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
December 24th, 2003. Moments after the fWo Unleashed Christmas Show. Time stretched for an eternity, but for Kodiak Vic Creed it was the blink of an eye. While the trainers worked on Creed's knee, forcing him to let them freeze the knee and examine it, he almost seemed serene. He hadn't been himself throughout the entire match, but now that it was over Creed seemed at peace with what had transpired. The trainers all assumed he'd been hesitant about wrestling Aimz. Maybe he felt guilty about his treatment towards her. They had no idea that Creed's behavior had little to do with Amy Campbell at all. On the ride back to the hotel, Vic was silent in the back of the taxi. He didn't even wait for his car to come and pick him up. Employees guessed that he had a hot date waiting for him back at his hotel and he didn't want to waste time waiting for his car. They had no idea that the date Creed was expecting was one he hoped stood him up tonight. Vic walked through the lobby, making a beeline straight for the elevator. He didn't stop at the front desk to check on the stack of messages he had. The desk clerk looked at the pile of notes she had accumulated for him since he left the hotel earlier in the day. She waved at him and called his name, but she guessed he didn't hear her or just ignored her. She had no idea that there was only one message Creed was expecting, and he didn't want to get it. The card slid into the lock on his door, and a green light lit up. Vic turned the door handle and pushed it into his room. The lights were off, the air as thick and black as molasses. Moving across the room to his bed was almost like slow motion. He didn't feel the pain in his body. He didn't see the bed in front of him His entire focus was on the cellular phone ringing on the bedside table where he'd left it earlier in the day. Vic picked up the phone. "This is Vic Creed. Speak." The voice on the other end was a woman's voice. His publicist perhaps. "Mister Creed, I'm afraid there's been an accident. It's... it's your daughter." She didn't need to say anymore. Sarah was dead. Vic's thirteen year old daughter was dead. Why? Because he'd chosen a gold belt over her life. Vic's soul was now dead, and he'd killed her.
