Faith in Peru

18 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Chris, It's not another hurricane. No, this storm is something of a different sort. This storm grabs me by my thoughts, whips me around with reality, and reminds me that the storm's eye I am wanting is sunken into the head of this 7 year old dying girl. This storm would break your heart, it's breaking mine. Perhaps it was the poverty that made me realize how wrong I was, perhaps my own history. Perhaps it was the fact that my own words kept grabbing me by the throat and chokeslamming me through a 'no gimmick' table. "They screw up my entrance." "They mess up my airline tickets." "They send me to horrible place to sleep." I had spent so much time whining about things that don't matter, that I forgot all about what does. For someone who touts humility as much as I, you'd think I'd get it by now. It's like... I had said 'Faith is the evidence' so many times that I had forgotten what 'Faith' is. 'Faith' is an action word. "My faith is in Christ", though in that case the word is shown as a noun, it moves nonetheless. It moves toward Christ, and airline tickets, and missed shows, and confusion backstage, and blown pyro doesn't touch that simple fact. Faith moves. And so here I am, looking out the window of my hotel, wondering what I should do with my faith, or better yet, how my faith in Christ is moving me. And that little girl, she provides me all the answers that I need. I'll be at the show tomorrow, Chris. Tell Padrona we'll go over our stuff when I get there. And Chris, pray for me, no, scratch that, pray for the next little girl you see on the side of the road... and then do something. FAITH is the evidence, Deacon --------------------------------------- Chris put the letter down, wondering how important it is that he write the next chapter of his book. He and Big D were always a team, and today, they would do what they hadn't done since the early days - get back to the basics. Chris left the room, and his keyboard, behind. Faith was moving again, that much he knew.
