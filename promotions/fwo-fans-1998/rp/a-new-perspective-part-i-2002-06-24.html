A New Perspective (Part I)

24 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It is raining in Milwaukee. Looking up at the lights that make the downtown streets glow, one can see a thousand droplets of rain slowly drifting to the pavement below. To most people, the cooling rain is a welcome relief from the standard mid-June heat wave. Down the street a taxi cab turns the corner with great vigor. It rushes through the wet streets until it comes to a screeching halt in front of the hotel entrance. The back door opens and out steps a man most recognize as The Magi. He reaches back into the cab for two leather suitcases and a bomber jacket. He gazes for a moment at the glittering sky and thoughts race through his mind that regale the gorgeous, peaceful sight. He quickly dashes these thoughts and turns his mind elsewhere. "Dammit! This is suede!" He sneers, examining his newly purchased sportcoat. "Hey!" The cabbie gets The Magi's attention. "That's $32.50, sir." As the driver informs him of his fare, he stops for a brief second to think about the last time somebody called him "sir", or even the last time they made the effort to feign courtesy in his presence. Whenever it was, if ever it was, it was too far in the past to be recalled now. The Magi removes his snakeskin wallet from the inside pocket of his suede jacket and removes a $50 bill. "Have a good night now," he says with a smile. The driver is quick to offer his deepest gratitude's. "Thank you, and you as well sir," the cabbie extended his hand to the Magi. "Jason," he replied. "Have a good night Jason." The driver tipped his cap and drove off. Picking up his bags he made his way to the doorway of the hotel. He is tapped on the shoulder by a young man in his late twenties, holding an unlit cigarette between his fingers. "Hey, do you have a lighter on you?" He asks. The Magi takes a short pause before offering a response, almost as if he's searching for the "correct" thing to say. "Sorry, I don't smoke. It's a filthy habit." Once inside, The Magi signs in, checks his bags, and makes his way through the halls towards the elevator. All the while, he smiles, laughs, and lightly converses with the people around him. Isn't it better this way? He enters the elevator and pushes the button for his floor. He is alone for a moment, and quietly stands listening to a light jazz rendition of "I Want It That Way". He suppresses a light feeling of repulsion. The doors open and a middle aged woman carrying a light shopping bag enters the elevator car, and stands close to The Magi. Another first experience for him. "Oh, I love this song," She genuinely says with a giggle. "Doesn't everybody?" The Magi spits out.
