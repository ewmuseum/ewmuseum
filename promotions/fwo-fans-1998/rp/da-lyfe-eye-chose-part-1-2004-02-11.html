Da-Lyfe-Eye-Chose, part 1

11 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I read a book the other day, - "The Holy Grail" ... it changed my perspective on various things. Life ... love ... wrestling. It talked about Jesus Christ, not as a messiah, or as the most devout Christian would have you describe him, - "The Messiah", or even as a "Profit of God" for that matter. It told a story about a man living in France, with kids, and a wife. I was befuddled at first, because all my life, I was led to believe a certain way. I guess that's where the problems started. Do I believe the stories? No, emphatically, no. I was raised the way I was raised, and I believe in what I believe in. It's gotten me this far, why change now, right? The last time you saw me, I was in trouble with the law. A rape charge, that wouldn't go away until I dug deep enough into my pockets to see to it that it did. I didn't rape that girl, in fact, the sexual encounter we shared was totally against my will, but I wouldn't expect you to believe that, so I'm not going to go to deep into all of the specifics. Point, blank, period ... the woman who took me to court was a money-starved-skank, willing to do whatever, to whoever to get where she needed to be. I was her meal ticket. She had no compassion for my career, and how the public might perceive me, so long as she got rich, and while I could've fought it, and probably could've won, I chose to take the easy way out, because I had the finances to do so. From there, I sat back and did some reflecting. I'm a fairly wealthy man, I've been in the wrestling business in some form or fashion for nearly ten years, and I'd assume It'd be okay to say I was fairly successful. Three world titles, various lower level championships in a couple of independents around the globe, not to mention two very important title reigns in two of the big dogs of professional wrestling, the "CSWA", and the "Fans Wrestling Organization." I was Triple X. They told me to be charismatic, to use my good looks to my advantage with the fans. "Don't smile too much, but make sure you're not frowning all the time, either. Be something in between," they would advise me. And, I did it. I was a whore to this business, on air, there wasn't a tweener who was as good as I was. I played that good-bad-guy role as well as anybody out at the time, all the while, backstage ... I was the perfect corporate kiss up. They saw a budding relationship forming between me and another popular woman, who happened to manage a friend of mine, and decided to exploit it, and while she fought it, I convinced her to just go with it. My best friend died, got cut up real bad by some thugs in an alley, in New York, and they started mentioning it on air to play the sympathy card. I decided that enough was enough after the rape charge started to spread. I couldn't do it anymore ... I was no longer interested in being a champion, no longer satisfied with promoters smiling at me, and patting my back, at the end of shows. When it was all said and done, I still wasn't getting the same cheers as Eli ... nor was I close to a world title push. Hornet, for whatever reason, prevented me from breaking through that impossible CSWA glass ceiling, that they still, to this day, swear is invisible. I had nothing left. No desire to fight, no longer was my eagerness to succeed carrying me. I was through, exhausted, down right... ...tired. So I took my life into my own hands, decided to be the person I was, in the confines of my own home, when nobody was around. Those long, pretty golden locks? ...Gone. The clean tattoo-less, mocha-complexed frame? ...No more. That stupid ass, XXXstasy name, slash gimmick? ...Over and done with. I am, who I am. Either you accept me for it, or you can kiss my ass, while you watch me walk out of your door. This is the life I chose, or rather the life that chose me. If you can't respect that, your whole perspective is wack ... maybe you'll love me when I fade to black...
