Reckoning

21 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Sunday, March 17 Vancouver, BC 12:45 AM PST "Scott? Mr. Universal will see you now." Scott Wasner looked up from the television in his locker room to the man standing in his doorway. A lanky, red-haired man wearing an fWo polo shirt and slacks. So this was the harbinger. The backstage gofer, the nothing, the nobody - heh - who got to escort the legendary Scott Slugger to his doom. Wasner set aside the controller for the Nintendo he had been playing - Madden had just royally fucked him late in a game, so it didn't bother him much to be interrupted - and stood up. "Lead on, MacDuff." The attendant smiled with pleasure. "Hey, you know my name! Cool. It's really good to see a wrestler who cares about us little people." Wasner had to restrain himself from rolling his eyes. Whoosh, right over his head. Typical. And one mother of a coincidence, too. Wasner and MacDuff left the room and walked silently down the adjacent hallway. It surprised Wasner as he walked that he had no fear of the upcoming confrontation with Universal, nor any fear of losing his job - the proverbial strike three. Certainly he should have been afraid, but he could not muster the energy to be concerned. A week of contemplation of his fate had left him at a point where he no longer cared. It wasn't as if he had nothing to lose - he had plenty, what was left anyway - but rather that nothing he would lose seemed to matter that much to him anymore. It was a strange feeling, not like any he had ever felt before. A fascinating condition, this truly not giving a shit. Wow. I'm scared shitless of cockroaches, but losing the source of my livelihood doesn't faze me. Not... one... bit. He let out a giggle. "Something funny, Scott?" asked MacDuff. "Very much so." "Mind telling me?" "Very much so." MacDuff grunted and continued to lead Wasner down the hall. They were the only two in the hallway, and their footsteps echoed off the walls as if they were in a tomb. Wasner was briefly struck by a peculiar sensation of unreality. This is nothing. This is a joke, this is a dream. I'll wake up in my bed in my house. No, not even that. I'll wake up and I'll be in a hotel room in New Orleans in September, thinking of how funny the mind sometimes is and wondering exactly how the hell I'm supposed to get back home and wondering if the M's really are going to break the wins record. Yeah, that's the ticket. Then it passed. Oh, this is real alright. I'm walking right to Chris Universal's office, and he's going to tell me my test came up positive for cocaine and painkillers, and he's gonna tell me that's strike three - I know Chris, he'll make sure he uses that exact phrase - and he's gonna tell me I'm fired, and I'm gonna walk out, and I'm gonna drive back home to Seattle and... And what? What would I do? Call Vince McMahon? Shyeah, right. "Hi, Vince, this is Scott Slugger, I just got fired for drug abuse, wanna hand me a hefty contract?" Call some other fed? Jolt? Asylum? Somewhere else? Not bloody likely, for myriad reasons. A return to baseball would be highly unlikely, although Steve Howe proved that there were always exceptions. U-Dub had already hired someone else to coach their baseball team, and there were no other universities at which he wanted to work. If they'd even accept him anyway. So where does that leave me? That leaves me as Jake Fucking Roberts, for God's sake, putzing my way around the indies for $50 paychecks and probably dead in ten years. Now he was afraid. Oh, he was afraid and more. "Here we are." MacDuff stopped at a door with "Chris Universal" on it. He opened it for Wasner - how kind - and moved aside. Wasner entered into the room. "Chris." Universal looked up from his desk. Strangely, he did not seem grim, or angry, or any other expected emotion. "Scott, good to see ya." Good? "Got your results back, as you probably guessed - and of course, clean bill." CLEAN? Wasner tried not to let his surprise show on his face. He tried his best to put on an "Everything's Cool" face and voice, and though he would have sworn he was failing, it seemed to satisfy Universal. "Awesome, good to hear. Good to get that out of the way." "You bet." "I mean, really, how stupid would I have to be with my history to do anything to jeopardize this, huh?" Nervous rambling now, not good Scott. Universal chuckled. "You ain't kidding. So, you and Austin got any plans for that match yet? Cut it off now, Scott, you're free, who cares how, now get a move on. "Not right now, Chris, but I'll try to talk to him when I see him. If he'll listen to me, crazy bastard." Another smile from Universal. "Anyway, I gotta get going - thanks for the heads-up, Chris." "No problem. MacDuff!" The attendant stepped back into the room. "Bring in the next one, if you would." "Of course, sir." As this went on, Wasner stepped quietly out of the room. He managed to make it back to his locker room and then collapsed onto the couch. Oh my God. How'd it happen? Somewhere along the line my sample musta gotten switched. That's the only explanation. Man, they say don't use a deus ex machina in fiction, but that deus sure comes in handy in real life. Jesus. Oh... whoever got mine is fucked. Hopefully it's nobody here. He sat up. "Fuck it, not my problem," he said aloud. "Not my problem." He noticed his hands were shaking from the near miss. He dug into a bag and pulled out a small bottle. He popped a few of the small tablets out and threw them down his throat. Better. He was better. It'll all work out. It'll all work out. It'll all work out.
