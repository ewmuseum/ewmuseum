Blade Job - Chapter 2 - Kevin MacKay

30 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"It is never wise to seek or wish for another's misfortune. If malice or envy were tangible and had a shape, it would be the shape of a boomerang." - Charley Reese. The red message light was blinking on Ben Jacobson's answering machine when he walked in the door. He hit the play button as he clicked on the living room lights. His own voice said, "Hi, this is Ben. Please leave a message at the beep." Ben idly flipped through the previous day's mail as he listened to the messages. "Ben, it's your mother. I just wanted to talk to you. Call me, dear." His brother's voice. "Ben, its Hoss. Where the hell are you? I've been calling everywhere for you. Give me a call, okay?" The final voice was somewhat frantic, and vaguely familiar. "Ben? It's Kevin MacKay. From the IPW? I really need to talk to someone. It's urgent. Please call me at [detail withheld] as soon as you get in! Please!" Kevin MacKay? Ben thought. Oh, yeah, the tall lanky Indy wrestler in New York's IPW. Went by the stage name "The Philly Killer" back then, even though he grew up in Kansas, and never lived in Philadelphia in his life. Ben tagged with him in IPW... one of those cameo appearances that were supposed to help ticket sales in Indy feds. The fWo loved shit like that. The last time Ben spoke to Kevin was at one of the June IPW shows in Brooklyn, but it was a cursory type of conversation, just running through the moves that they were going to be trying that night. Why was Kevin calling him? Picking up the phone he dialed Kevin's number. The answering machine picked up and Ben left a short message. He walked into the kitchen, tossing a frozen dinner into the microwave. This is really pathetic, he thought. I've lived in this place six months and haven't even finished unpacking. Of course, he had a dozen excuses for the boxes still stored in his bedroom. A full schedule of workouts and fWo appearances, the fun of discovering New York for the first time, Disney marching into the fWo and telling him he needed a "makeover". He shuddered slightly, remembering the terror he had felt as two reed-thin Disney yes-men explained to him how he could market himself as "New Lion King". Ben still believed that Wippit Guud had put them up to it. Guud... that was a subject that only now Ben had allowed himself to think about. Wippit had completely ruined his life with his childish pranks and stupid jokes. Ben had snapped and tried to cripple the little bastard... only it ended up that the little bastard almost crippled him. Even then, for Ben's troubles he was quickly and unceremoniously taken off of fWo television. He had refused to be assigned a new gimmick, and had thus had been put on what the business calls "Autograph duty". AD consists of going into town a week before the fWo and sign autographs, do radio and TV commercials, and plug the upcoming live appearances of the guys that have taken your place in front of the camera. You gotta love fWo logic. Turning away from that dangerous line of thought, he walked into the bedroom to change while waiting for the microwave to ding. Pulling a pair of shorts and a "Say Hello to Block" T-shirt out of the closet, he knocked a small cardboard box off the shelf. Cursing, he bent to pick it up and the contents scattered on the closet floor. They were photos that he had forgotten he even possessed. Flipping through them, he found snapshots of Jason Winters and Kanaida in a time-frozen lockup at some unknown highschool gymnasium in Texas, a few of Hoss and his Mother. He froze as his eyes fell on the last few photos. They were of Wippit Guud, one in front of the gates at Graceland. He was wearing a goofy grin and a t-shirt that read "The REAL King is named Lawlor." Another of the two of them in Toronto, wrestling for the UWA, when Guud had convinced him to work for what was then known as the best federation in Canada. Those were the days that both of them, along with a revolving cast of other wrestling misfits, drove around the continent in a crappy minivan, looking for Indy federations that paid per appearance. That was before the backstabbing... before the mockery and public humiliation. Quickly, he put the photos away, pushing the box and the memories far, far into the back of the closet
