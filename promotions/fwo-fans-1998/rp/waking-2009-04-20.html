Waking

20 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“Doing leads more surely to talking than talking to doing.” Morning daylight pushed through the white curtain, casting the entire room in a mimosa-tinged glow. Electronics beeped and footfalls stamped on the linoleum outside of the door. Samuel Braun sat in a chair next to the hospital bed. His father was prone, motionless. Multi-colored vines flowed from the openings in his body, funneling air in, sucking liquid and toxins out. The condition of the sallow, emaciated body had barely improved despite the constant transfusion of money from his son's bank account. Sam. The wrestling rags, so accustomed to crowning rising stars themselves, were pained to see this Brawn character materialize from nowhere and command the respect of fans and peers without making himself a minstrel, a microphone warrior, a jester for the masses. A recent issue framed him as a bratty, mercurial figure, slandered in 5000 words by a handful of anonymous sources whose outrageous claims were printed without hesitation. "WHO IS THIS MAN?" the bold headline asked. Brawn had already provided the answer. It was a paradox the wrestling world still could not accept or understand: the question was being answered quietly, week in and week out, with grit, determination, and second efforts. While other superstars were talking into mirrors via combs, or re-designing their ring attire, Brawn was learning from his old matches and spending many lonely evenings in the gym. Brawn wasn't heartbroken. There were more important matters to address, like paying the overwhelming medical bills, skipping meals, or staying out of windows during sleepless nights, wondering when the room would stop spinning. These were the tidbits that the press craved, but Brawn kept them starving. He feared the emergence of new perspectives he couldn't control. One man's humble workhorse is anothers narcissist prone to exaggeration. No one wins in the public eye. Brawn lifted a magazine and read one of the story titles on the cover. "Life is Cruel." He looked up to his father, who groaned, and wheezed, and returned to the depths of his subconscious. killjoy let Brawn's silent secret fly at reAction, and the consequence of that secret was on display next to him. Sam didn't know how killjoy found out. He didn't want to know. He could only hope that the recent blood-letting would keep the masked maniac at bay. Sam reached back and rubbed the throbbing reminder on his head, grimacing the same way he did when Jeff Garvin approached him at reAction. The synapses had been colliding into the walls of his mind's labyrinth at the time. His decision to align with FWO's latest lockerroom cancer was a mistake. Why would any sane person associate with a grouch that couldn't even negotiate a spot on the card? A golden glimmer jumped from a nearby clock and briefly blinded Brawn. He shielded his eyes and gritted his teeth. His eyes watered. His frivolous anxieties diminished with the quick distraction. He lowered his hand slowly when the light passed. It was then he noticed the cloudy, distant eye of his father glancing at him.
