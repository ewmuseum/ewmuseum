The Gift of Pain

24 December 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Vic walked into the hotel lobby, a newspaper tucked under his arm. Life as a professional wrestler often meant being away from family and friends during the holidays. It was something most wrestlers accepted; it was a sacrifice that came with the job. A bitter pill to swallow, but one that went down nonetheless. Still, as Kodiak Vic Creed lumbered past the carolers singing by the front desk, he found he had to suppress the urge to reach out and snap their necks like so many matchsticks. He didn't want to hear about Peace on Earth, or Joy to the World. Peace and Joy were feelings that he had to do without. Happiness was an option Vic had long since forsaken. All he had to embrace was his damned soul. He stepped into the elevator, standing off to the side. An elderly couple stood close together, their hands embraced affectionately. After so many years together, their love was still strong. The bile began to rise in the Main Man's throat. Holidays like Christmas were times of reflection, which was the last thing Vic wanted to do. He knew he'd burned his share of bridges, made wrong decisions in his life. He resolved himself to the fact that his brain was wired differently than other people. Drugs or no drugs, he chose to solve problems with violence. If someone crossed him, he hurt them. Plain and simple. Violence was all he knew. It kept him from true happiness, but it provided him with success. It was a sacrifice he had to make. The bitterness, the rage he felt inside, kept him strong. Vic stepped out of the elevator and opened his newspaper as he walked down the hallway. With headlines filled with war and sadness, he couldn't help but feel bitter. "The world's going to Hell in a handbasket. And they say I'M a bad person," Vic said to no one. But it was true. He was seen as a bad person, a title he had to admit he had earned time and time again. It was that notoriety that led the court to take custody of his ten year old daughter away from him and give it to his sister, the one who had actually raised young Sarah from infancy. He'd never get her back; he couldn't undo the past. He wasn't sure he'd want to even if he could. It's not like he knew how to raise a daughter; his father hadn't done a great job of child-rearing either. Still, that didn't mean Vic didn't love his daughter, or miss her... Creed took his key card and swiped the lock to his hotel room. Maybe he'd watch a little TV, hit the gym later on, or maybe load a few syringes and chase it down with a bottle or two of spirits. If he was feeling ambitious he'd hit one of the night clubs hosting a Christmas Party, find some woman filled with too much Christmas cheer, and bring her back to his room so she could ride his Yule log. Flipping on the lights, Creed noticed the message light flashing on his phone. He pressed the button, retrieving his voice mail. "Probably BQ asking if I knew what hit him," Vic said with a sarcastic grin. "Vic?" said the voice message. "It's Marilyn." Stunned, Vic's jaw hung slack as his sister spoke. "I guess you're not there. Well, I managed to track you down thank goodness. Haven't heard from you for a bit so I wanted to give you a few updates. I'm walking, which is a Christmas gift in itself. I need a walker, but the doctors are telling me I should be able to move without one eventually." "You're a strong one, just like Mom. But you wouldn't need any help walking if it hadn't been for me," Creed said. "I dropped the ball, and Vishanti made you pay for it." "I also got the picture you sent me, and don't say you didn't send it because I know it was you." Vic could almost see his sister smiling as she spoke. "Yes, that's the guy." His fist clenched, knuckles cracking. "Ah, a Christmas gift for me." "Well, I should get going. We've got friends coming over and I've got to get the turkey out of the oven before it burns like last year. So Merry Christmas, and here's someone who wants to talk to you." Half filled with hope, half with regret, Creed waited. "Daddy?" For a brief moment, Vic's heart filled with joy.
