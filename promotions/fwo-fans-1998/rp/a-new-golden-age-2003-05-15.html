A New Golden Age

15 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
He lifted his hands away from his face and stared blankly into the mirror. His dark blue eyes were bright. Brought out by the white makeup that had been caked on. The crimson red lipstick that was smudged from one corner of his lip down the left side of his chin. Finally, hair that had once been blacker than a flock of crows in the middle of the night. The hair was white. Nobody notices the simple things. Changing the color of his hair changed his entire appearance. He knew he liked the new style. It fit his shifting personality. He wasn't as brooding as before. He wasn't as gloomy or as filled with angst. Something had procured this out of him. A woman. She was attractive, and she grabbed his gloved hand and looked up at him smiling. "Things will be different," she smirked. In agreement, he nodded. There was still no expression on his face. Through the years he only knew of two: Deadpan and anger. Showing signs of happiness would always be difficult, that's the way it would always have to be. But the woman brought some feeling into his greyed heart. Something inside of him kicked at his heart, like a small insect burrowing its way through his body. Straight into his heart. He felt something with her. It was new. But was it good? "Things are different," he replied to her softly. He was the shrew and she had slowly begun to tame him. But even though he looked a bit softer on the outside, inside was the same unbridled feral rage that was ready to burst through the seams. She was keeping him in check. She was keeping him primed and on the edge. They both knew that in less than two months, hell would once again be unleashed upon the wrestling world. No one would ever expect it to happen, either. He clenched his free hand and glanced down at her. She was a beautiful woman, something he never thought he could have. After they met, things happened quickly. They fell for each other hard and fast. They were like two high school dandies. Somewhere, he loved every second of the time they spent together. He didn't show his affection very well. It was jagged, nervous and cut off. She was more open. She cared for him like nobody else ever would and ever had. The past five months, without her he would be either underneath the ground, in a hospital or buried under garbage in a forlorn alleyway. She was the Dawn that his Night had always needed. Donaven Winters was no longer color blind. He could now see the vibrant colors of the world. Black, white and grey tones no longer set the medium of the day. Life was vivid and bright. Along with the color came a new edge. This was the beginning of Donaven Winters' Golden Age.
