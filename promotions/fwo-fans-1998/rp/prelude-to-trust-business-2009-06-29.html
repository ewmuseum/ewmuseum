Prelude to Trust - Business

29 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The phone rang. "Hello, Ivy." Ivy McGinnis put her feet up on the table in the basement of her Seattle condo. "I'm surprised you still have my number in your phone, Beav," she said, as she sipped from the glass of red wine in her hand, "considering the last time we talked." "I wasn't going to get rid of it," replied William, "I knew you'd be back." Ivy smiled. "You're right. Also glad to see things have been running smoothly, tonight went really well I thought. For the most part." "Really?" asked William, his voice betraying excitement around the edges, "You were there?" "Course I was," replied Ivy, "My boy Knox had a match, for starters, and I might've gotten burned out on the day to day aspect of the job but I still love the boys and am interested in how they're doing. That's why I'm calling, actually." Her voice had dropped in pitch, and Ivy could tell from William's sudden silence that he realized she had shifted from friendly chatter to business talk. "I would like to join the company on the road," she continued, "but there need to be some changes." "Talk to me," said William. "You're the boss," said Ivy, "and you need to stay the boss. The buck cannot and should not be stopping with me. I'll deal with a good number of your headaches - I can handle the boys - but you're in the big chair and you really need to be more hands - on with the booking and the conflict. Can we agree on that?" William sighed. "Nothing I can't live with." "Good," replied Ivy," because I have something to take care of tomorrow." "Killjoy." "Yes," said Ivy, as she took another drink, "Killjoy." "I assumed as much," said William, "What's on your mind?" "He lost the match," continued Ivy, "and he needs to lose the mask." "I agree with you," said William, "but we can't force him to take it off when we can't find him - when he can take it off in private and blend in with the rest of the crowd." Ivy laughed. She stood up and started to pace. "No, he can't. If he takes the mask off anywhere in the arena the gig is up, which is why he has to take it off. Plus, we can't let Pierre get shoved into this position again; it really isn't fair to him." "Again?" "Four years ago," reminded Ivy, "He beat Spyder in a career match that Miguel decided to ignore. Sure, his contract specifically stated at the time that he couldn't be fired without written authorization from the owner of the company, but at the same time... if we can't protect the most senior member of the locker room from shit like that, who's to say we can enforce anything, right?" "No," said William, "I agree with you, but how do we enforce it?" Ivy smiled. "I've got a plan." "What is it?" "No," replied Ivy, "I'm sorry but this one needs to stay really, really close to the chest. I'll let you know what's happening tomorrow at the arena, after I talk to the people I need to talk to." William sighed. "The buck is supposed to stop here, remember?" "Touche," said Ivy, "but this is different. Killjoy is different. I know him, Beav - and if I don't play it this way he's going to get off scot free. Trust me." Silence. "Okay," said William, finally, "We'll play tomorrow your way."
