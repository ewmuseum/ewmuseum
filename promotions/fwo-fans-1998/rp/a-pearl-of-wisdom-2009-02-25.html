A Pearl of Wisdom

25 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“Ivy? Hi, it’s Pearl. What’s good?” Pearl Ladune, Alex said to himself. My pitbull librarian. To someone who met Pearl for the first time, it might seem like an odd combination. Even now, Alex watched her from the other side of the desk and was amused at the contradiction. To look at Pearl was to look at what could best be described as a “soccer mom in a business suit.” Her long, ash colored hair was tucked up into a bun, held precisely in place with strategically placed pins and bands. She wrinkled her nose while staring at her monitor, then pushed her square-framed glasses higher up on the bridge. Today was a pin stripes and skirt combo that made the late thirty-something look like she would be very much at home in a court room. Everything she did seemed very calculated. Yet beneath that exterior was a driven woman. She’d been married to a professional wrestler back in the ‘90s, so she knew the business. She’d been on the road with him, handled his bookings and such, and always made the most out of what little they had to work with, be it money in the bank or his talent in the ring. Peal had a mind for wrestling, and had she had the good fortune of being born a man rather than a woman, she could have done very well in a managerial pursuit. But as a woman, man in the business seemed to see her as a pair of breasts with feet. Now that she wasn’t in her twenties, and her curves had softened, she couldn’t even be seen as that anymore. Needless to say, their loss was the Mecca’s gain. Naturally Alex was hesitant when Pearl first approached him. He wasn’t in the best mindset to start with, and his emotions were overriding his common sense. Pearl seemed to catch on to that almost immediately, and simply offered up references for him to check at his convenience. But before she left, she handed him a big red apple. “Now eat something decent,” she said with a smile. “Thanks mom,” he’d replied at the time. But the gesture, and something about Pearl, did remind him of his departed mother. He looked at the list of character references, and there was many of them, and hoped she was as good as she seemed. She was. “Thanks Ivy. Always a pleasure.” Pearl hung up the phone and looked at the notes she’d scribbled on the pad beside the computer. “Your world has not yet crumbled young man,” she told Alex. “The FWO understands your behavior as of late, and are willing to be a little more lenient with you in regards to this bitterness you seem to have.” Alex leaned back in his chair and put his feet up on the desk. “No spankings. I appreciate that. I’m really trying Pearl.” Pearl stood up, note pad in hand. “I know you are Alex.” She swatted his shoes with her pad. “Feet. Off. Now.” Alex smiled and took his feet of the desk. Pearl read her notes again. “The reason I called you in today, is that I might have some news on David.” “Let me guess. You know people in Afghanistan.” “I know people who are currently in Afghanistan.” “You know people everywhere, don’t you?” “I’m a very fortunate woman.” “So David, is he okay?” “I’m waiting on confirmation, but my source tells me he’s in a hospital in Kabul.” Pearl gave Alex a comforting smile. “Your friend is alive.” “What happened? Is he hurt?” “I’m not sure of the extent of his injuries. But he should be coming home soon. That’s the important thing.” Pearl walked to the side of her desk and sat on the edge. “Which means that you can now get yourself in check and see about winning your title back. You think you can handle that?” “Pearl, I -“ “Alex, if there’s one thing I’ve learned is you can’t change the past. From what you told me, you did nothing wrong in Afghanistan. Nothing. David is alive, and when he gets back to the States I’ll make sure he gets squared away. You worry about you, I’ll worry about everything and everyone else. Fair?” “Fair enough.” The phone rang, and Pearl leaned back to answer it. “Pearl Ladune.” “Is it about David?” Alex whispered. Pearl covered the mouth piece. “No, but I have to take this call. As soon as I hear anything, you’re the first phone call I make.” “Thanks Pearl.” Alex plucked his jacket off the back of his seat and slipped it over his shoulders on his way out the door. His smile continued all the way to the elevator. “David,” he said to himself, happy that his friend was alive. More importantly, he was coming home. + + + Blood stained teeth. Gauze wrapped around his head, including his left eye. Bloody bandages wrapped around his right hand With his remaining good eye, David Cross stared up at the ceiling of his hospital room. “Alex,” he hissed. He wasn’t smiling.
