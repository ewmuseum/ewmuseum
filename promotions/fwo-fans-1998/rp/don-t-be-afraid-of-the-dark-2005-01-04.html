Don't Be Afraid of the Dark

4 January 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Master Hiroshi walked up the back stairs to his tiny apartment. Normally he'd come in through the front, but about five blocks back he felt that he was being followed. He wasn't concerned about a confrontation; after all, he was a master of Golden Phoenix Kung Fu. He could shatter cinder blocks with his bare hands, if he was so inclined. Since the dojo closed down, he had little desire to raise his hands against anyone or anything. Which was why he'd tried, apparently unsuccessfully, to lose his shadow rather than confront him. Rather than lead them to his home, Master Hiroshi took the stairs up to the roof. Either his stalker would hang back, or they'd come out onto the roof. In any case, the pursuit would end there, seven stories above the street. Hiroshi stepped through the doorway and felt the tiny stones under his feet as he crossed the roof. He had no idea who could be following him, but he would soon find out. The whirring of a large circulating air fan masked any faint noises, but Hiroshi focused his hearing as best he could. There was ten feet between him and the doorway which led out onto the roof, and he could cross that space in a heartbeat. Even in the setting sun, Hiroshi could see the doorknob quite clearly and waited for it to move. Perhaps a mugger had been following him, thinking the old man an easy target. He would soon find out just how wrong that was. "Waiting for someone?" asked a voice from behind him. Hiroshi spun around. There was no other way onto the roof, but sure enough someone was sitting cross-legged on the edge of the roof. The ledge was only one foot wide and two feet off the roof itself, and the shadowy form seemed quite at easy on his tiny seat. "How did you get up here?" Legs untwined and straightened, feet deliberately placed slowly on the rooftop. In the fading sunlight, the bald man in the long coat appeared smaller than Hiroshi. Yet he moved with a smoothness, a natural grace, which told him this person was no mugger. The man turned his head slightly, and the orange rays of the sun shone off a metal plate on the side of his face. "David?" The man bowed slightly, his right fist extended and covered with his left hand as he did so. "Master Hiroshi. It's been a long time." "Indeed it has. I heard you had returned to Japan."
