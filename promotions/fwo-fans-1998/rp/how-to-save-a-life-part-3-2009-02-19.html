How to Save a Life: Part 3

19 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"I think you should take it." Silver Fox was sitting back on his bed, newspaper in his hand, looking at Ivy McGinnis. "You want this, you're good at it, and you'd be a genuine asset." "You think so?" asked Ivy McGinnis. She sat in Fox's desk chair, with her back against one of the armrests and her legs dangling over the other. "I really don't know." "C'mon," replied Fox, "Are you still stressing about what happened with Neo?" There was no dancing around it today. Fox was going straight to the root of things. "No," she replied, "I've pretty much come to terms with the fact that there wasn't anything I could've done differently." "You're right, you know." "But it still gets to me," she continued, "William wants me on his staff, which means, what? I'd be management again. I'd be responsible for the rest of the boys making their money." "In a way, isn't everyone responsible for everyone else making their money?" "Semantics, Fox." "Exactly." Ivy laughed. "You are so annoying," she said, "Things went down on my watch, Fox. Regardless of where blame goes, I was the one who had to call everyone to tell them not to bother coming to work again. After last week's debacle in Afghanistan, I don't think I have the strength to have all eyes on me again." "What about this quote in the paper?" asked Fox, as he turned to the second page, "'All that was on my mind was my two year old son at my brother's house, and that I wanted to hold him again. I wanted Sean to hold both of us again. Any of you have a problem with that?' There's a lot of eyes on you right now." "That's different. That was just me." "What do you think was going on when you were on my staff?" asked Fox, "You were just you, which is exactly what I wanted. It's what worked." He leaned forward. "Look, this business is financed by men in suits who, by and large, couldn't last sixty seconds in the ring. You're a real person, Ivy - and that's what the FWO needs right now. That's what William needs right now." "He's in over his head, but he's doing his best," replied Ivy, "The last thing he needs is to be distracted by another road agent or consultant or whatever it is I'd be. His attention is already divided up enough." "You told me Ric was a road agent now, right?" asked Fox. "Security, actually," replied Ivy, "Why?" "How much direction does William need to give him?" "None. He knows what he's doing." "There you go." She hesitated. "No, it's not the same." "It's exactly the same," replied Fox, "Ric Chronos and Ivy McGinnis both cut their teeth in the same ring, the only difference is that you did it with your words and he did it with his wrestling. There's a lot of upside and zero downside to your taking this job." Ivy swung her legs around to the front of the chair and stood up. She picked up a plastic cup of water and took a drink. While she turned away from Fox, he stood and stepped right next to her. "When I hired you for my staff," he said, as he put an arm over her shoulder, "I knew what I was getting and that's why I wanted you." "Yeah, but you were there," countered Ivy, "You were in charge and everyone knew it. Now, William is still trying to convince himself that he's in charge, which makes it really hard on the rest of the staff." "What, you're afraid of being the adult in the room?" asked Fox, "What was it that Sean calls you?" She laughed, and her cheeks reddened. "Mama Bear." "From the stories you've told me so far, you're already playing Mama Bear for the rest of the roster. What's the harm in making it official?" Ivy took a deep breath. "You make a good point, but--" "What am I saying?" asked Fox, "You shouldn't be talking to me about this. There's someone else that should be getting all this information." She turned toward him and looked into his eyes. "Sean." "He's got his own problems right now that we're trying to get him through, he doesn't need--" "Shut it," interrupted Fox, "No, he doesn't need to hear about yours, but he wants to. When he put that ring on your finger, it was better or worse, no?" Ivy nodded. "These feelings you've been talking to me about the past month or so... they'd fall under the heading of worse, I'd say. Am I right?" She nodded again. "Then," said Fox, "you need to stop wasting time with an old man like me. You've got your own life out there. Go live it." Ivy hesitated for a moment, then she turned and hugged him tightly around the neck. "Thank you," she whispered. "Any time," he replied, "Mama Bear."
