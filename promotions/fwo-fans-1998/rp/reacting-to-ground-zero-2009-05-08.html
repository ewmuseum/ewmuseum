Reacting to Ground Zero

8 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
You know what, I don't even care that I lost. I don't care that I wrestled my heart and soul out there and ate the Snapmare Driver anyway for my troubles. And it might've been the ringing in my ears, but I'm pretty sure I heard a bunch of cheers for me anyways. That's what they said my goal should be: the same goal every successful wrestler has. Coop told me I was the most natural wrestler he ever saw in his sixty - odd years in the business, that I had a feel for how each opponent moves and could effortlessly adjust my own movements to match. But, he said, there's a big difference between being a naturally skilled, naturally gifted wrestler and a wrestler who can actually make a living at it. There's hours, days, weeks, months, and years of refining worth that separates the two. I'm sitting in the locker room, oddly nostalgic over my career so far. Two wins, one loss, and a Silver Pyramid that could get marked down as a wash, but I feel like I'm behind where I was at the same timeframe in New Frontier Wrestling. I didn't win a single match for three months, but I was on the map. I was the kid who didn't care how big the house was, or how high up on the card I worked. If there's a show in some bingo hall in Wyoming, I'm still there because I was booked there and am happy to do it. I'm still that kid, just a year older and a little more cynical. Miss Ivy told me that wins and losses don't matter in the grand scheme of things. This business remembers those who made their mark in every other way. You can make more fans losing on the biggest scale you can, than winning in the opening match. Fans don't remember winning and losing, they remember heart. You've got heart, kiddo - don't lose it. It's probably because there's a lot more eyes on the FWO than there are on New Frontier. NFW is profitable and thriving, but it's still considered the best kept secret in professional wrestling, and I think El Presidente likes it that way. We don't get spotlight seekers in NFW, we get people dedicated to the craft. Looking at the history of the FWO, it's easy to see how people could lose their way. This company has had more than its share of tragedy and power struggles and double crosses and triple crosses, and it's hard to imagine how anyone could go through all of this and still maintain optimism. I looked around the locker room. It was nearly empty; most of the roster had already left for Redbeards. I should probably check that place out at least once. Of course, when things go sour is when we'd need optimism the most. Forget the mainstream, said Eli Flair, slightly edited. Forget the popularity and the status quo and the dirt sheets and the negativity you'll find on every website. You need to do what you're gonna do regardless of how it looks to the majority. Change with the times, but never change because of a trend or a fad. You might get overlooked by the office more often this way, but you'll have a hardcore group of fans who appreciate that you speak for them, and when push comes to shove, those are the ones that'll be paying to see you wrestle. I've taken a lot of hits from opponents and prospective opponents for my fairly upbeat worldview, but what do they expect? I'll suddenly stop wrestling for the joy of it and demand my money up front, and refuse to go on until it's physically in my hand? If there's one person in a crowd of thousands that bought a ticket solely to see me, then I owe that person the absolute best match I can do. How dare I not bust my hump to do it? It's a little scary how rare this point of view is. You're gonna hear a lot, said Angry Johnny, wearing this very same leather jacket, about doing too much, or not doing enough, or making it too hard to follow your stuff. The only thing that holds water in any of it is that, given a choice, do ten things instead of twenty, but make those ten mean as much as the twenty. Make what you do mean more, and there's no stopping you. Other than that, the only thing you will ever do wrong as a professional wrestler is not be true to yourself. Angry Johnny was my hero growing up, and when we became friends during my training, he was never anything less than completely generous with his time and his encouragement. I wonder what would've happened if he didn't die when I was seventeen. I certainly wouldn't have worn a mask, since he would've gone to bad for me with Miss Ivy and I wouldn't have had any reason to hide. But that was a liberating year. Anonymity is a rare thing in this profession. I just miss my friend, is all, and it's pointless to play the what if game. I'm teaching you these hooks, said Coop, because you respect the history of this sport and you respect the men who have come before you. I'm hoping you'll never have to use any of them. It's time to stop dwelling on what was. What is, is that I put out an open contract and Killjoy was just the next in line. After all, some of us don't play lackey to Vince Jacobs or protege to the Flying Frenchie to get our foot in the door. Some of us fall back on wrestling as best we can, week to week, and letting the chips fall where they will. Tomorrow's a brand new day; ante up.
