Tough Choices - Part One

5 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Alex looked up as the vise closed around his head. The pressure, God the pressure was overwhelming. It wasn't a machine that was crushing his head, though. It was his father. How the Hell did he get that strong? What was he shooting into his veins these days? Had he seen the thumbs moving toward his eyes, Alex might have looked around the room. He might have looked over at a locker door, a chair, the wall, or even the ceiling tile. Alex didn't have the luxury of foresight, and now he'll always remember that the last thing he'd ever see would be his father's hateful face. "Alex, we're here." The Mecca sat up and felt the taxi come to a halt. It was true what they said about losing your sight, and how all the other senses became more sensitive. A bit of a curse now, having been in the taxi for over three hours since they'd left the airport and created quite the boquet of odors in the vehicle. There was the smell of B.O. that simply comes from travelling halfway across the planet. His aunt Marilyn's perfume had faded, but her deodorant still had a bit of fragrance left. The cabbie, well he stank from the moment they got in the cab. Marilyn negotiated the fare while Alex helped himself out of the car. The cold January air almost took his breath away when the first small gust blew into his face. He'd really lucked out by having his father's sister offer to escort him from California to Japan. It was the first time he'd ever met his aunt, but they quickly learned they had plenty in common. The cane Marilyn walked with was courtesey of Kodiak Vic Creed. Alex was probably going to have to learn to walk with a folding white cane, thanks to Daddy Dearest. "Hey Mar?" Alex asked as he heard the taxi move ahead and pull off to the side of the gravel road. "Are my eyes open?" Soft, caring hands took hold of Alex's forearm. "You know they're closed," Marilyn said. She began to guide Alex up a cobblestone path. "Just keep those sunglasses on and keep your eyes protected." "Yeah, because it'd be terrible for me to hurt my eyes," he said sarcastically. He listened to the sound of Marilyn's footsteps crunching over the stones and tried to match his cadence with hers. "There's nothing wrong with your eyes that can't be fixed, Alex. The doctor told you that." Alex focused his attention on the footsteps. "That didn't give Mom much comfort. She still ended up in the hospital." "Nobody knew about her heart condition. I know as well as anyone how stressful being involved with Vic can be. This was all just too much for her." Marilyn had spoken with Sally while she was hospitalized. Sally had heard broken reports about what had happened to Alex at Countdown 1, as well as the riot that took place at the end of the show. Luckily she had the phone in her hand when she had her heart attack, or she might not have had the strength to call 9-1-1. "Yeah, she's in the hospital and I'm running away to Japan so Vic can't get to me." With the fWo Internet Title, he added to himself. Alex felt funny about being the champion of a promotion that was tearing itself apart. He was lucky that he'd been attacked early enough in the show that he managed to get out of the arena before the bizarre cult-like invasion. The silver lining to having his eyes nearly gouged out of his head. "This is just a little vacation Alex. Your Mom's going to be fine as long as she knows that you're safe. Even Vic wouldn't try anything here," said Marilyn, almost believing what she was telling her nephew. Here being the Golden Phoenix Temple, high on a hillside that was just about as far away from civilization as possible. Alex could only imagine the carvings along every pillar, the rich color of the tapastries. The distant location didn't stop the temple from having a souvenir stand just inside the front doors. T-shirts hung along the back of the kiosk, while statuette of golden birds and posed marial artists stood proudly in the glass display case. A number of pamplets, conveniently written in Japanese, Mandarin and English, were spread out across the counter top. Marilyn picked one up and noticed it was a temple history leaflet with a donation envelope in the back. A young man, dressed in royal blue robes trimmed with silver, smiled from behind the stand. His smile grew even wider when he noticed the leaflet Marilyn was reading. "Welcome to the Temple of the Golden Phoenix," he said in surprisingly good English. "May I interest you in a tour?" Alex turned his head in the direction of the voice. "Actually I'm here to see one of your disciples. David Cross?" The monk looked surprised. "You're here to see the Devil Chicken?" He motioned them forward with his hand while his smile disappeared. "This way." Marilyn and Alex fell in behind the monk, who led them up a flight of brick steps. He then took them down a hallway with a hard wooden plank floor, with walls made of the same brick as the stairs. "Sounds like you have some interesting friends Alex," whispered Marilyn as the three stopped in front of an unmarked door. "Devil Chicken?" Alex shrugged. He knew there'd be a story behind the name, but he couldn't imagine what it might have been. The monk knocked once and stepped back. The door swung wide open, and the three were met with the image of the former professional wrestler once known as Crucifix, standing with his hands on his hips, wearing a red feather housecoat. "Greetings, and welcome to my humble abode! And by humble I mean unfurnished, with a tiny clay pot to pee in and eight feet of stone floor to sleep on." The monk simply shook his head and walked away. David cocked an eyebrow and looked at Alex's travelling companion. "Hey, aren't you Vic's sister?" She smiled politely and let Alex into the room, guiding him to a large red cushion against the far wall. "Yes, that's me. I'm Marilyn." David watched the two walk past him. He noticed the way Alex held Marilyn's arm. "Uh, nice to meet you Marilyn. So... What's going on here?" Alex reached out and felt the cool stone under his fingertips. He tapped the cushion with the toe of his cross-trainers and slowly sat down. Marilyn watched Alex carefully before turning her attention back to David. "Sally didn't tell you?" David shook his head. "She asked if Alex could come and hang out here. She said he had to recoup from an injury." Cross knelt down and placed a hand on Alex's shoulder. "Alex, let me see your eyes." The Mecca was defensive. "It's fine, Dave. Seriously." "Oh, well that's good news then." David dug his fingers into Alex's shoulder. "Take off the glasses." Alex hung his head, but did as he was told. He slipped them off and held them in his hand. He then lifted his face to David's. There wasn't much light in the room, but Cross could see that the whites of Alex's eyes were now deep red. The blue of his iris was clouded over. David spoke to Marilyn without taking his eyes off Alex. "You're heading back Stateside, yes?" Marilyn nodded. "The cab's waiting for me outside. I've got an evening flight tonight." "Good. On the off chance you see your brother, tell him I'll see him soon." There wasn't any need for David to further explain his meaning. Marilyn could see it in his eyes, and understood his anger better than anyone. She gave Alex a few more words of encourangement before taking her leave of the boys. After Marilyn had gone, David turned his attention back to his best friend and almost son. "And as for you, it looks like you won't have to worry about me pranking you by moving the furniture around." "Doesn't sound like you have any furniture," Alex commented. "Just my pee pot," said Cross. "And I don't want you knocking that over. For everybody's sake." He helped Alex to his feet and caught the young man smiling at him. "It's good to see you again." David didn't touch that statement and simply smiled back. "Likewise, kiddo. Likewise."
