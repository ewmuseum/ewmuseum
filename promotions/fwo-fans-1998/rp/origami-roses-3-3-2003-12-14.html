Origami Roses (3:3)

14 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It was nearing 8:00 am, and Jeremy's eyes were closed. He was fast asleep. His face laid on what seemed to be the very last rose. His arms cradled his head; one of his hands still grasped onto a pair of scissors. A flickering candle was inches away from his head, which was nicely groomed and prepared. His attire was of a finer-than-usual quality; he was cloaked in a burgundy sweater and khaki slacks. He didn't hear the limo door slam outside, as Shirley stood firm in the cold. Clouds of breath escaped her mouth as she looked up and admired her home, her palace, which she was returning to. She bent down to grab her suitcase, but it was only then she noticed a pink rose on their snow-covered driveway. She lifted it close to her face, to observe it with befuddlement. She smiled as a look of romantic admiration developed on her countenance. She started her step, and noticed that the rose trail led to the stairway. She looked ahead, and saw that it would lead her up the incline to the front door. By about the fifth homemade rose, Shirley picked up her step into an excited jog. It soon matured into a sprint as she rumbled up the wooden stairs. She grabbed the brass door knob, and burst into the cabin. Jeremy jarred awake and looked up at her as she stepped in the room. She noticed that he had been snoozing. He buried his face in his arms and cursed the mortality of his body, which wanted sleep when he wanted to stay awake. His grand plan was foiled; one of his steps to marriage recovery had been botched. Shirley dropped her luggage near his feet, and gazed at the countless roses on the living room floor. She took off her shoes and walked into the rose-filled room. She noticed that the carpeting was barely visible. It seemed that paper flowers, and only those paper flowers, crunched beneath her bare feet. She basked in the atmosphere as she slowly spun, noticing the fire and softy playing Christmas music. Her knees were becoming weak, so she turned and collapsed into a chair. Tears came to her eyes, and it didn't take long for her to break into a full-scale weep. Jeremy popped up from his chair and sat next to her on the couch. He sniffled quite loudly, before composing himself. He softly grabbed her chin and pulled her face to his. "I wish I could have loved you more..." Jeremy whispered, but he was obviously losing himself in emotion as well. His eyes shut as the tears flooded off of his usually-rigid jaws. The two kissed, emitting sigh-like cries in the process. It didn't take long before they were on the floor, in a tight embrace of love that couldn't be broken. They hugged. They kissed. They did things that satisfied married couples do. The rest can be left to the imagination.
