Lost Time - Cycle Two

26 May 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
She hopped a plane from Baltimore to Orlando with little more planned than marching up to the arena where SCW was having their show, and letting Linda Johns know what was going on. As soon as she stepped outside in Orlando, she got a feeling in the pit of her stomach that threatened to gnaw its way out through her skin. "What if I'm fighting Linda Johns for something that no longer exists?" Poison Ivy had to know where she and Triple X stood. As she slid into the back of the taxi, she made a decision. "Where to?" "Highlander Court. Off of Murkirk Road" "You got it." Ivy didn't even think about what she would say to him. She couldn't. If she walked into his apartment and saw him with Linda, with anyone else... at least she'd know that she'd lost her chance. But before she can do anything else... she has to find out. "Wait," she said suddenly, "Pull in here." The driver looks to his right to see a massive supermarket. "Here?" "Yeah," replied Ivy, "And keep the meter running. I'll be awhile." "Keep the meter running?" "Trust me, I'm good for it." Forty five minutes later, Ivy reemerged from the market with two bags filled to the brim with food and food fixin's. The cab was still there, undoubtedly curious over what she would be doing. "Thanks for waiting," said Ivy as she slid back inside, "Highlander Court, again." Ivy looked down at the things she'd just bought, and her nervousness increased, tenfold. She didn't know how to cook. She'd never pretended to know how to cook. She was riding a wing and a prayer that she'd be able to fulfill her promise to Sean that she'd make him dinner some night... and hoping that he'll be receptive to the offer. As the taxi pulled up to the building, Ivy paid the fare (not nearly as much as she'd thought) and slid out, heading up the stairs. Every step was complimented with an agonizing thought about Trip and where he might be. She pressed on, refusing to let them get to her. At last, she was at his door. No answer to her knocks, she used her key to open it up, tiptoeing into his bedroom. He wasnt' there. However... the pile of mail on his table, his overnight bag tossed in a corner of his room, told her that he was in town. All she had to do was wait.
