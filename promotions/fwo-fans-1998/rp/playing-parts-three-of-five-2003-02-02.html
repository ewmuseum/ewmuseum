Playing Parts - Three of Five

2 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Steve Arden stood inside the uptown Gold's Gym in Chicago and watched athletes of all sorts, boxers, wrestlers, bodybuilders and karate experts along with their trainers lift weights, strike punching bags or perform short exhibition matches in the ring set up like a centerfold in the middle of the gym. His whole life he had trained in this very gym, but today felt like it was his first time, for only a couple hours away, he would debut in one of the most respectable squared circles the wrestling world has to offer; the fans Wrestling organization. Though he had wrestled before, Steve Arden had never worked for an organization with such exposure and the stories of turmoil in the promotion ran through the grapevine but he cared less. If he was going to get his big chance, he was going to have to live through it as best as he could. The gym was small, despite it's trademark name. The walls were uneven and varnished with a grainy white color, suspended from them were different accessories tarnished and ripped but still the only ones they could afford. The all-purpose boxing/wrestling ring was damaged to a further extent, but still used. The blue mat was covered with a black build up and mildew induced by blood, sweat and tears through the years, the turnbuckles were ripped, exposing it's foam padding and even the ropes were devoured by time, the steel cables uncovered by small chips here and there on the three layers of ring ropes. From the back door frame Steve saw his 2 trainers, Michael Levine and Randy Ellis march towards him both holding a small stack of papers, most likely his fWo contract. He was proud to finally be an employee of the infamous wrestling legacy but something was wrong. Across the years he had seen drastic changes, wrestlers came from being recognized for their wrestling skills to being praised for their colorful gimmicks and microphone skills, on the outside he was fine with it, always remaining courteous and respectful to them but on the inside, it ate away at him and now, he was on the verge of becoming exactly that. "Ok, here's the contract they just faxed us, Steve. You want to go over it one last time and check everything out?" Yeah, sure.. They skimmed the pages and flipped each sheet, turning the chapters on their lives. For one, Steve Arden would become a major player in the game just for signing this contract. His pay checks would swing up at least 50% from the measily independant payments he got now and his name would be all over billboards, Pay-Per-View commercials and wrestling magazines. Secondly, Michael Levine and Randy Ellis would be recognized as top trainers in the sport, boosting their client numbers considerably. It was a win-win situation no matter what. It looks good to me. Steve brought forth is liking as did his trainers. "Guys, I think this will be a turning point for all of our careers and we should honestly be proud of what he accomplished." The trio nodded and turned to a nearby table, pen in hand, Steve sat on the plastic chair and sighed a deep sigh, knowing his life was about to change completely. He brought the pen up to the designated area for his signature and signed the contract, he was now officially part of the fans Wrestling organization. Mike and Randy clapped in delight and patted their protoge on his back. He stood up and threw down the pen on the wooden table, finally smiling. Guys, I think I'm going to go back to my apartment and work on this actor character. It's not the best gig in the world but when God gives you lemons, you make lemonade right? Alright, see you in Puerto Rico guys. He smiled at his trainers and shook their hands, swinging on his black leather jacket. His steps were small, but confident strides. After all, it was a new day. It was the day of 'Action' Steve Arden..
