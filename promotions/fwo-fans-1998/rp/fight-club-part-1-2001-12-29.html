Fight Club Part 1

29 December 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Public embarrassment. Block. Been there, done that. It's insignificant. Return. What Donaven Winters did was start something rough. Defend. It's the same thing it's always been. Parry. Man walks into ring. Man gets the upper hand. Man pays for it in blood. Sweep. You can hit me, kick me, knock me off a scaffolding. Like a New York City cockroach, I just refuse to stop. Scrape. You can put me through tables. Stred my skin. There's an open wound on my forehead that hasn't been healed in three and a half years. Claw. Donaven Winters thinks he's got the advantage. Donaven Winters thinks he's got one up. Donaven Winters can exchange his name with Dr. Silver. Pound. Attaxx. Tear. Random Opponent Number 438. Break. Donaven Winters is not special. Donaven Winters is not unique. Donaven Winters is doing nothing that hasn't been done to death. Strain. It's the same thing I've been told my entire life. Humans aren't designed to bring happiness to each other, we're here in order to lighten our own misery by dumping it on everyone else. Burn. Take two people who have more than the typical amount of misery and bad things happen. Take two men who have a history of abuse, given and received, tape the results, and sell them to underground distributers who do business behind strip clubs and classy restaurants. I feel no pity for Donaven Winters. The first rule of this business is that you separate your mind from your body and learn to distinguish between the two. To not allow yourself to emotionally get involved when things get rough. I feel no emotion for the blood I plan to spill. You can use a torn muscle for hours without problems, if you can disconnect the pain from your mind. I do not fear for my soul for my actions, both taken and planned. This is freedom. Losing all hope was losing all fear, and losing all fear is freedom. This is what we do for a living. I will treat this man like an insignificant piece of shit. I will then be told that my parents are to blame for raising me in an environment of violence. I will then be told that I am unfit for society. Again. I know this, because Eli knows this.
