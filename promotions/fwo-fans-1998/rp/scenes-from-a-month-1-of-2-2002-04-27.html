Scenes From A Month. (1 of 2)

27 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Sunday, March 24, 2002 Calgary, Alberta, Canada 9:42 PM MST Scott Wasner opened the door to his locker room and stood stock-still. "Hold on," he spoke aloud. It was a truly bizarre feeling. He literally had no sense of where he was or what had been happening. The night was a blank, no matter how hard he tried to recall it. This was not a good feeling. Wasner shook his head. Still, nothing came back. He shrugged and stepped into the locker room, closing the door behind him. He walked into the bathroom, where the white powder was still laid out on the counter. Oh, stupid. Stupid motherfucker. He had left it there, even through the segments being filmed, even while he was out in the ring, where anybody could walk in and see it. Then he shook his head. What the hell did that matter? If he hadn't been caught by the test the week before, and he hadn't been caught this week, it seemed pretty obvious. He wasn't going to be caught, period. Wasner calmly brushed the powder off the counter and flushed it down the toilet. This calls for a celebration. Hell... a permanent celebration. They can't fucking touch me. How cool is that? ----- Saturday, March 30, 2002 Toronto, Ontario, Canada 6:45 AM EST Scott Slugger swam out of sleep like a diver coming up from the ocean. He could hear the sounds of the street below him, and he did not recognize his bed. Hotel room. Yeah, that made sense. He tried to roll out of the bed, but found he could not. To his left, blocking his path, was a naked woman. Hmm. That can't be a bad thing. He tried to roll out to the right. His path was also blocked there. Wow. That really can't be a bad thing. If this is what being coked out of your mind leads to, put me down for it! He smiled and woke up the woman to his right. ----- Saturday, April 6, 2002 Montreal, Quebec, Canada 12:37 AM EST Scott Wasner stumbled out of the bar's bathroom and back into the main area, now properly done up for a night on the town. As he walked, he bumped into a large, burly man with a leather jacket and a goatee. The man stopped and turned back toward Wasner, who was still walking. He reached out and grabbed Wasner by the collar, stopping him in his tracks. "Hey, asshole! Next time, why don't you watch where the fuck you're going?" Wasner blinked, surprised at this man's sudden outburst. He then rolled his eyes. "Dude... shut the fuck up." The man reached back and pistoned a fist into the face of Wasner, who took the blow and rolled with it. Wasner then came back with a stinging right of his own, knocking the man down to the floor. Wasner looked around, then grabbed a pool cue off of a nearby table. By now a crowd was gathering to watch the fight - if it could be called that. Wasner calmly slammed the pool cue over his knee, breaking it in half. He tossed one end away, then bent down into the face of the man, holding the sharp, broken end of the pool cue to his throat. "Apologize." The man snorted. "Fuck you!" Wasner dropped to one knee, not coincidentally driving that knee into the man's crotch and bringing forth a yell of pain. Wasner leaned in closer, and pressed the cue down to the man's throat. "I said, apologize." The man looked into Wasner's eyes and saw something that made him decided that this was not a battle to be fought. "Hey... hey, man. I'm sorry. I didn't mean to do anything. I'm sorry, honest." Wasner looked down at the man, eyes blazing. After a moment of silence, he spoke, leaning down right into the man's face, going eye-to-eye with him. "That's right. You didn't. Just like you don't mean to call any cops after I leave, correct?" "Hey. Sure. Whatever you want, man. Just... don't do something you might regret, OK? I'm sorry." Wasner slowly got up and tossed the remains of the cue aside. "I don't regret anything." He walked out of the club, and nobody stopped him. ----- Monday, April 15, 2002 Just outside Fresno, CA 10:52 PM PST Scott Wasner eased the Dodge Viper onto the tracks. He looked out his window. The warning lights were flashing, the bells were ringing, the posts were dropping. Train a-coming. Wasner readied himself in his seat, then gunned the engine. The powerful revs of the massive engine blared through the night air. Wasner saw the light. It was just a pinprick at first, a small dot on the horizon. It grew larger as it got closer. "Showtime." Wasner popped the car into gear and started forward. The train continued to approach. The Viper was blazing down the tracks now, doing well over 100. The train sounded its horn, a deafening sustained note. Wasner chuckled in glee. "Hooooonk yourself, motherfucker!" He leaned on his horn. They were coming very close now, and the train's horn sounded again. Wasner imagined he could almost see the engineer in the window, mouthing obscenities. It was now or never. Wasner swerved off the tracks, leaving the train and its horn to skid down the tracks, proverbial foot on the brakes. Wasner didn't lift his foot off the gas, blasting through the night. "Ha ha, WHOO! DAMN! I've always wanted to do that!" ----- Friday, April 26, 2002 Laguna Cliffs, California 11:58 PM PST The cliff roads were twisty, that much Scott Wasner was aware of, but for obvious reasons he did not care. Slugger swerved the Viper around one cliff, all but scraping the small metal divider separating the road from nothingness. "Ah... this is living." Matchbox 20's "Bent" played on his radio, and Wasner turned it up. The roads were slick and wet, and it would've taken a skilled driver to navigate them successfully at normal speeds. Wasner was going well over 100. He hadn't looked at the speedometer for a while, so he wasn't really sure of his exact speed. Wasner, a mad grin on his face, let the car drift towards the edge of one of the cliffs, then violently yanked the steering wheel back towards the road. He did not particularly know why he was doing this - a psychologist could probably tell him something about it, he supposed - but he also did not particularly care. Wasner repeated the drift-yank motion once again, but this time, something went wrong. The tires on the Viper's powerful back wheels failed to grip onto the slick asphalt, and the car began to fishtail. "Oh... shit!" Wasner tried to right the car, but he couldn't get it to move. He slammed on the brakes. No effect. The car spun towards the edge of the cliff, slamming into the metal. Wasner fell unconscious. The song was over.
