On Mortality I

7 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
They took me to the hospital while I was unconscious; the EMTs that is. They pumped my veins with their anesthetics and performed their surgery. It was a surgery I neither wanted nor asked for, but it was done anyway. I had a vertebra with a small crack that they somehow repaired. They said this was the cause of my pain. I should be out for three months, if I can return at all, they say, but I intend to return much sooner than that. And so I've sat in my apartment and continued my contemplation of mortality and my apparent ability to avoid it. I was shot six times not too long ago. Six times in the chest and stomach. How does someone not die from that? The only person I've ever heard of doing anything like that was Don Corleone, and he's simply a product of imagination. At first, the thought of eternity danced about my soul, and I was able to smile about it. Seldom do I smile, but the thought of unending existence without the human cancer that dominates this shattered orb was enough to make me giddy. I would outlive all of you and would work to rebuild what you have demolished. As eternity played on my brain, I became much less enthralled with the idea. Forever, you see, is a very long time. I've known nothing but running and struggling to live, even since the fall of the Disciples, and the thought of existence without these things terrified me. I have become comfortable in my horrors, and going without them is not a change I am ready to make. So I planned to test this immortality that has been cast upon me. I found the razor blade, it's shimmering edge drawing my eye and it's finality drawing my heart. Now was not the time, but I needed this reminder of what I knew I'd someday have to do. And so I took a felt-tip pen and wrote the word "Escape" along it's flattened side and taped it to my bedroom mirror, a perpetually present reminder of what I would one day have to do.
