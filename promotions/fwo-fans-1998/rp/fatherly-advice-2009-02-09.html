Fatherly Advice

9 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Saturday night and everything’s alright. For the young twenty-something crowd with disposable income and a valid driver’s license, Saturday night is a time for quiet personal reflection. A time to get in touch with their inner selves. Usually while being held upside down over a keg of beer. For Alex Creed, it was a time to go to the gym. A little part of him felt sad when he noticed how few people were working out. Guess they had better things to do with their lives. For Alex Creed, the gym was a big part of his life. Two hours later, well after the moon had climbed high in the night sky, a freshly showered Alex Creed pushed open the door to the gym and stepped out into the cool evening air. The smell of sweat was replaced by the scent of exhaust, but at least it was a dry smell. That didn’t make much sense, but that feeling wasn’t new to the Mecca. He was tired, sore, and just looking forward to getting a bite to eat at the hotel before going to sleep. Standing on the sidewalk, Alex watched a car drive by filled with people his age, speeding off to good times of their own. Sometimes he missed that. A normal life. Alex didn’t see the limo navigating through the traffic behind him, approaching methodically like a shark moving in on a bloody swimmer. He did look over at it when the car pulled up to the curb twenty feet from him. When the driver got out and walked around to Alex’s side of the car, hairs stood up on the back of the young man’s neck. “Your father would like to speak to you,” said the driver, before opening up the car door. He motioned inside with his hand. “Please.” The Mecca stuffed his hand into his coat pocket and pulled out a small pair of earphones. “I’ll pass. I’d rather walk. Or set myself on fire.” “Get in here Alex,” growled a voice from inside the limo. Alex looked at the driver, then at the dark interior of the vehicle. “Whatever,” he sighed and stuffed the earphones back into his pocket. After stepping into the limo and having the driver close the door behind him, Alex felt somewhat uncomfortable. It wasn’t claustrophobia, but there was something in the car that seemed to suck the air out of him. That something was sitting on the seat beside him, larger than life and colder than death. A mountain of a man, snowcapped with short-cropped white hair, and blue eyes as cold as a glacier. Victor Creed didn’t look at his son at all as the limo pulled away from the curb. Alex didn’t look at his father either. “What do you want?” he asked. “Heard you’re back in the FWO,” said Creed the Senior. “That’s right,” replied Creed the Junior. “You left FURY Pro?” “Yep.” “Think that’s the right move?” “It’s called loyalty. You’ll probably have to look up the definition.” “Always with the smart mouth. Still, not a good business decision.” “It’s not always about money.” “Sure. In FURY Pro, you got to live out of your own home. You trained all the time. You fought once every few months, and were paid a handsome sum to do it. With the FWO you’ll visit your home a few days a month, if you’re lucky. You’ll barely squeeze in one workout a day, and the FWO isn’t throwing around Disney money anymore. So again, why go back?” “I told you it’s not always about the money. The FWO wants me back.” “Please. They want you? Maybe they want your belt because Beaven can’t afford a new one. But you? You think you’re not replaceable?” “I bring something different to the table. Plus, the fans seem to like me.” “For now. They’ll turn on you.” “I guess you’d know about turning your back on people.” “Just those who aren’t worthwhile. I’m sitting here talking to you though, aren’t I?” “Overlooking you trying to gouge out my eyes, of course.” Block after block disappeared behind them. Neither Creed spoke, or even looked at one another. Through the tinted glass Alex recognized the neighborhood. He wasn’t far from his hotel. Since he hadn’t even told his father where he was and the man had tracked him down, Alex wasn’t shocked that Vic knew what hotel he was staying at. “Wrestling is a waste,” Vic said finally. “You can do better than that. You don’t need to wrestle; you’ve proven that already.” “Maybe I want to win the FWO World Title.” He didn’t have to look at his father to know Vic was frowning. THAT thought brought a hint of a smile to Alex’s face. “You’ll never get the shot, Alex. Kid Cool isn’t that smart.” “I’m not going to the man and asking for one. I’ll earn the shot. I’ll be so ‘half-man/half-awesome’ that it’ll be a no-brainer. He’ll have no choice but to see the talent.” “He wouldn’t know talent if it drove over him in low gear.” The limo pulled up in front of the lobby. Alex heard the driver get out of the vehicle and close the door behind him. He looked out the tinted window and waited for his door to swing open. “So thanks for the lift and stuff.” Vic turned and stared at the back of Alex’s turned head. “Get rid of Cross before you go to Afghanistan.” Alex wouldn’t turn around, and spoke to the glass in front of his face. “He’s my friend. Probably one of my only ones these days. By my count, that’s one more than you.” “I might have been a miserable bastard to you, but I always had a reason. There was always a method to my madness. Cross is off his tit. You never know what he’s going to do. He doesn’t even know what he’s going to do!” Alex felt relief when his door finally opened. He could see into the lobby, and felt as if salvation was only a few feet away. “Whatever. I gotta go.” As the young Creed stepped out of the limo, Vic leaned over and said, “Just… be careful.” Alex stopped and turned around. “How very fatherly of you.” The door closed and Alex quickly walked into the hotel. Vic watched his son disappear from sight and then picked up his cell phone. “He’ll see things soon enough,” he said with a frown.
