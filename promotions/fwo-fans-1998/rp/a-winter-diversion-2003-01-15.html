A Winter Diversion

15 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"This is crazy, Mick!" "I know! It'll be fun!" Behold Mick Pepper and John Lake, collectively known as The Freak Show. Behold, the view at the top of a mountain in Banff, Alberta. Behold, Mouse... in skis! Mouse stared down the expanse of white powder laid out before him, a gentle slope on a 'green' rated ski run. He could see several skiers making their way down the mountain at a leisurely pace. But he wasn't fooled. "Mick, if I go down there, I'm gonna get killed!" Mick appeared next to Mouse and looked down, "What? Killed on this? This might as well be the bunny hill!" "I was thinking," said Mouse, "we have a match later tonight, and it's a good drive away. Maybe we better call it quits and let me rest up. I don't need to try skiing today." "Mouse, you're the one who wanted to get out more," countered Mick. "Ok, so I'm having second thoughts. That's allowed isn't it?" Mick chuckled to himself, "Ok Mouse, it was a miracle of fate that you were able to get in the ski lift, let alone have it take you all the way up here. It won't take you back down. There," he said, pointing down the run, "is the only way down. If you can make it down, we'll call it quits and head out." Mouse stared down the hill, the wind blowing about him. And stared. And stared some more. "Dammit, Mouse!" shouted Mick, "GO!" Mick suddenly shoved Mouse, and while on solid ground that would accomplish nothing, in skies on a ski hill, it was just a matter of friction (or lack thereof). Mouse began to slide forward. "No!" he cried in alarm, "Wait! No no no noonono noooooooooooooo..." Mouse was suddenly moving, and unlike the rest of the hill, was doing it in a straght line. And gravity has a very accellerating effect on a 700lb object. "Heh," noted Mick, just before he pushed off himself, "he was right. He IS going to die." Mick set off down the hill, hoping whatever crowd Mouse ran into survived the encounter.
