Turn The Page - Part 1

20 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Thursday, April 17 The small glass vial between Vic Creed's fingers seemed harmless enough. It looked like a small test tube filled with water. Vic knew what it really was: a bottle of the Devil's piss, which he injected under his tongue. A derivitive of the TAR synthetic steroid, TAR-2 (or T2) didn't have the same painful side effects that the original had. No, it had all sorts of new Hells to unleash on the user. Vic stood up. He'd been forced to use T2 by Darryl Vishanti, who used the safety of Vic's family as leverage. But the longer Creed used T2, the more the liquid fire ate away at his body from the inside. When the constant agony began to affect his emotions, his mind, Darryl's revenge was complete. He had Vic on a tight, tortuous leash, and he wasn't about to let go. "Vic?" asked Nick Brandish as he watched the big Alaskan walk into the bathroom. Nick was the only other person on the road (other than Erik Kelly, apparently) who knew about Vic's T2 use. But only Nick knew, or suspected, what it was doing to Vic's mind. He was becoming more violent, more hostile. If Creed wasn't careful, he might become another Nick Brandish. The white bathroom lights brought out the dark circles under Creed's eyes. He didn't sleep much anymore, and when he did he was front-row center of a number of horrible nightmares which usually involved disfigurements, violence, and torture. He knew that the demon that teased his soul was sick, but he had no idea how sick. It was refreshing in a sense. Vic knew he was a bad person, and just in case there actually was an afterlife, he had a good idea what Hell was going to look like. Nick stood in the doorway. "Are you sure you want to do this?" Growing up, Vic's father was little more than a bully towards him. He had control over Vic. When Vic finally left home, he made himself two promises. One, he vowed to be a success and cram it down his old man's throat. Two, nobody would ever, ever control him again. He looked down at the glass vial in his hand. If he stopped taking T2, Darryl would know. Retribution against his family would be swift and painful. But if he didn't stop taking the poison, Darryl would win. He'd maintain control. "Hell, I can't even control myself," Vic said to himself. "Why should Darryl control me?" No one controls me, Vic thought to himself as he pulled the stopper off the top of the vial. He poured the contents into the sink, then flushed the vial down the toilet. Maybe Darryl wouldn't find out what he'd done. Either way it didn't matter. If he found out, Creed's family would suffer. It wouldn't be the first time they'd paid for Vic's sins. It was part of the glory, and the curse, of the Creed name. Vic himself was untouchable. He was unstoppable. And now, he was a man in control of his own destiny. Of course, it also meant that the lives of those around him were in peril, but sometimes sacrifices had to be made for the greater good. Besides, Vishanti would have to find out that Vic wasn't playing ball anymore. And he wasn't about to say anything, at least not yet. So how could Darryl find out?
