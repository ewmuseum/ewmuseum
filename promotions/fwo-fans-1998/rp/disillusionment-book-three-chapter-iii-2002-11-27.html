Disillusionment: Book Three - Chapter III

27 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
I killed a man today. He wasn't an officer for the Disciples, either. I figure I've probably killed a dozen of them. Although, I can't be sure, as I've never hung around long enough to watch them die. This man, was just a normal guy, but I had to do it. It's the one thing I always said I would never do: commit a crime against a human being who was not a part of the Disciples, but I had to do it. Of course, there is guilt. It's only been twelve hours since I did it, and already the guilt is eating away at my soul. I had to do it though. He knew too much. Had I not killed him, the Disciples would have captured him just minutes later and taken him into captivity. The pain that they would have inflicted upon him there would be much worse than the instantaneous death I offered him with a point-blank shot from my ten-millimeter. I had to do it. You can understand that, right? It was like euthanasia. The quick death that I brought that man was the lesser of two evil, as it prevent him from suffering an extended, indefinite period of grueling pain. That's not that bad, is it? To kill a man to save him? Ah. Who the fuck do I think I'm kidding? There were no excuses for my actions. None. I just did it. It was a spur of the moment thing, and now my eternal soul will have to pay the consequences when I reach the pearly gates. It wasn't in self-defense, either. I would love to be able to claim that it was in self defense, but that man, who's name I do not even know, never showed me the slightest hint of aggression. I feel like a monster. I am a monster! Let me take you back to it, so I can let you know how it happened. I was running. Not just avoiding the Disciples, but actually, physically running. There were a half-dozen or so officers on my toes, and I had to escape them. I ducked into a convenience store, hoping to find a second of relief. I had my gun out and must have looked suspicious, but the clerk at the counter showed no fear of me. "Hey, you're that Jack Lundin guy who the Disciples are accusing of rape and murder." "What did you say? How do you know about the Disciples?" "Stephanie O'Brien was my daughter?" "What?!?!" "Don't worry. I know you didn't kill her. I know that you would never do that. Stephanie told me all about the Disciples, and all about you." "Well, Mr. O'Brien, I'm sorry about what happened to your daughter." "Yes. So am I. Tell me, was it the Disciples who killed her." "Yes sir, she died in my arms." "Oh dear, that must have been terrifying." "Believe me, old-timer, I've seen some terrifying things in the past few years, but this one was by far the worst." "Are you running from the Disciples now?" "Yes, they are probably only a couple of minutes behind me. Why aren't you running from it all if you know as much as I do?" "I'm an old man, Jack. I don't have a lot left to live for. I wouldn't last long in a chase. I figure I'll just let the Disciples find me and kill me like they did my daughter." "You don't understand! Mr. O'Brien, your daughter was an exception to the rule. Traditionally, the Disciples don't kill on site. They take prisoners and then they torture before they kill." "I'm not worth it to them Jack. I'm too old to be held as a prisoner." I could hear the jeeps that carried the Disciples' officers off in the distance. They still had a ways to go, but they would be here in less than a minute. I looked at poor Mr. O'Brien, not knowing what he was in for...not knowing the raw pain they would put him through, and without thinking, I put my gun to his skull. "Jack?!?! What are you doing?!?" The old man was almost in tears. "I'm sorry, old-timer, but it's better this way." I pulled the trigger. BANG He fell to the floor...lifeless. I left through the back door and hightailed it into some nearby woods. I watched as the officers entered and searched the store. They knew that Old man O'Brien was onto them. I could tell by the way they entered. They didn't bother to be polite like they do around civilians. They went in with the intention to attack and capture. I doubt they even suspected that I ever went in there. They were after O'Brien, but he was gone. Deep down, I want to believe that what I did is right, that saving a man from the torture he would have gone through would have been a greater crime than killing him, but, if that's true, then why do I feel so guilty? You believe what I did was right, don't you? How could it have been wrong? Damn. I was already having trouble sleeping, ever since I saw what they did to Stephanie. This wasn't going to make rest any easier. Knowing that you've killed an innocent man...it's the worst possible feeling a man could ever imagine. All I can think about right now is the blood that coated the counter after I shot O'Brien. The sound of the leaden shard ripping through his skull and his neural flesh. I've already thrown up twice today at the thought of it, and I'm starting to feel queasy again . I never asked for this, and now that I have it, I hate it. I hate it more than anything I could possible have any feelings about. My hatred for the Disciples pales in comparison to the intense hatred I feel for my situation. Maybe it's because I know that my situation is of my own manufacture. If I hadn't been such a curious bastard, I would have never known about the Disciples, and Burkenstock would have never called me the "Chosen One" and I'd be lying before a fire with someone I love. Now, all I look forward to is cold, hard concrete
