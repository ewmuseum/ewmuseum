Destiny's Child - Part Three

17 May 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Dr. Sandow adjusted his glasses as he walked down the hallway. He didn't even notice anymore the smell of stale air and urine that always seemed to linger in the hospital's psychiatric wing. The occasional screams that seemed to echo down the corridor was a little disconcerting at times, but the nurses who frequented the ward understood the terror that prompted the cries. He stared at the rows of doors that lined either side of the hallway. Each one had a small black nameplate beneath the tiny observation window in the door. "Nurse," he said to the woman walking in front of him. "How is my patient doing?" The nurse stopped in front of one of the doors and looked at her clipboard. "We've had to increase his phenothiazine dosage," she observed from her notes. "His hallucinations are starting to subside and he's becoming less hostile. We're still not ready to take him out of restraints though, at least not until his old wounds have healed properly." Sandow stepped up to the door and looked inside. "Still no idea who he is?" The nurse shook her head. "No sir. His fingerprints haven't turned up anything and he has no I.D. And his little episode, we can't even show his picture around to identify him." Dr. Sandow didn't feel much pity for the lunatic inside room 3A. Bound in a straitjacket, John Doe sat motionless in the corner of the empty room with his legs stretched out in front of him. His head was still wrapped in gauze from attempts to save what was left of his face. "As soon as he's more docile I'd like to talk to him." "Of course Doctor." The nurse made another note. In the dimly lit room, the doctor managed to catch a slight bit of movement. "It looks like he's still awake." "Not likely. We sedated him pretty heavily." Still, Dr. Sandow was sure he could see John Doe's eyes... one in particular. "Any word on what the eye tests brought up?" The nurse flipped through the pages on the clipboard. "Severe nerve damage and leaky capillaries. That's why it's so red. Eerie looking thing. He hasn't opened it much though. When he was chopping away at the tissue around his eye, he might have nicked some more nerves. It's probably very sensitive." "I'm sure it is. Let's check around and see if anyone recognizes that type of eye trauma," Sandow told the nurse. "Let's put a name with the face." "Or what's left of it," she commented. Dr. Sandow shot her a stern look. "The outside can be fixed easily enough, even for his kind of damage. It's what is inside that I'm curious about. What could cause a person to do that kind of damage to themselves? In a church no less?" Had Dr. Sandow not left the window to continue on his rounds, he might have seen more movement from his mystery patient. He might have seen the lips move behind the mask of medicated bandages, whispering in the darkness. "I see it now," the voice hissed with hope. He opened his eyes, the white of the left one contrasting greatly with the almost black right. "So beautiful."
