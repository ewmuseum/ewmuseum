the end justifies the means, Part Two

20 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The fWo career of Jon Crisp began on May 13, 2001. It began innocently enough, with Mike Heftel as the usual opening match. Heftel had done this before, and it was never a problem. "Alright, and maybe some time in the match, I can get the dreaded drop toe hold in," Heftel said to Crisp, three minutes before they were to take the stage. Crisp looked at Heftel, unsure of whether he meant it. Heftel quickly took a picture of Crisp's expression with a polaroid camera, and laughed. Crisp blinked, and laughed along with Heftel. "Let's go put on a show," said Heftel. His music hit, and he began to walk down towards ringside. Meanwhile, Crisp looked out, and saw the crowd cheering for Heftel. It was a massive crowd, one of the biggest he had ever performed in front of, despite the fact that he headlined in Canada for months before this. So Crisp did the only thing he knew what to do at the time: He threw up, into a trash can that stood next to the curtain. This was about twenty seconds into his music, however. Quickly, he ran out. May 13, 2001 Slaughter Recap (credit: www.fwowrestling.com): "Down with the Ship" hits the speakers, bringing out Mike Heftel to a rather nonchalant reaction from the crowd. He walks down confidently, until he gets to the ring. He moves to slide into the ring; however, he loses his momentum as his legs refuse to go with him. Rather, they hang down over the apron. Eventually, he falls over, back onto the mat. He decides that the steps are the real way to go, and he uses them. As he warms up, "My Hero" hits the speakers, bringing out fWo rookie Jon Crisp for his debut. The fans, unsure of what to think of him, give him a rather mixed reaction. That is, until he climbs into the ring and grabs a microphone. "Atlanta, Georgia...home to the 1998 NFC Champion, Atlanta Falcons!" The fans erupt in a cheer, at least for a few seconds. "It just shows that the NFC's decline didn't start with the Giants. The Falcons sucked, too." The fans begin to boo, somewhat. Some cheer, realizing that he probably is correct.
