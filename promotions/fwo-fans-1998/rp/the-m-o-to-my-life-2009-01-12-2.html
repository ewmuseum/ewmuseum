The M.O. To My Life

12 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“Please, just wake up…” That voice; it was harmonious, yet troubled. Even in times of distress, her voice radiated a beauty like none I’d ever heard before. It was the only voice I’d ever known that could capture my undying love. I wanted to smile at her, and speak softly into her ear. I wanted to let her know that all was well and that I’d be fine. I wanted to tell her that we could start where we left off. But I couldn’t. “Oh god, please don’t die.” Her voice quivered gently as it echoed in my ears. I could feel the warmth of her hand as it pressed upon my bandaged wrists. She gripped slightly, as if physically telling me to hold on. I wanted to hold her hand and feel her warm embrace, but I couldn’t. Something was holding me back. “What have you done?” she asked with a soft and trembling voice, as her grip faintly shook from emotional anguish. I wanted to look deep into her beautiful sky blue eyes and tell her that I loved her, and that that love would always keep me alive and close to her. I wanted to cast my eyes upon her astonishing beauty and watch as the wind brushed through her golden locks. I wanted to be temporarily blinded by the sunlight bouncing off of her silky white skin. She was a vision of angelic loveliness, and I wanted to see her again. But I couldn’t. …Death wouldn’t let me. I could feel the cold, raspy breath of death against the back of my neck. It was the only sensation that I could feel. My limbs rang with an eerie numbness. I closed my eyes and awaited my fate with silence. Slowly Death’s long black overcoat dragged across the ground, as his boney structure rattled with every step he took. His breathing patterns grew louder and more frequent as death’s excitement rose. Through his long and hard breaths I could’ve sworn I heard a sickening pleasurable laughter. I understood why. I was the highly anticipated prey that he could never catch. I was the prize he could never achieve. I had escaped his touch time and time again, but after years of frivolous attempts, he had succeeded in his desire…and he basked in his victorious glory. As his elderly giggle grew louder, I opened my eyes to see his boney finger making its way towards me. I stared silently with a cold disposition, I was not about to give death the pleasure of seeing me in a frightened state of mind. However, I believe he took even more pleasure at my attempts at sturdiness. His finger shook with excitement as he drew closer and closer towards my chest. And I was mere centimeters from feeling Death’s touch… I smiled. Suddenly, the numbness throughout my body began to dissipate and feeling began to revitalize. I could smell a sweet scent of flowers, and it made Death growl. I could feel the comfort of a soft pillow against my head, and it made Death clench his fist in rage. I could feel life returning to every inch of my soul and Death rose in anger. His touch could not reach me, because something was stopping him. My desire to live. “Looks like you failed,” I said with a cynical grin. Suddenly, a wave of light enveloped around Death and with a mighty roar, he was sucked into the heavenly portal. I watched the remnants of death’s robe dance in the air in front of me as they dissipated into the shining light. As I watched my narrow escape from death, her voice broke my victorious silence. “What have you done? Why did you do this? What did you prove?” Her voice of elegance was now a voice of disappointment. I wanted to answer her, but something was stopping me. Another foreign emotion… Fear. … “I’m sorry!” My boisterous roar was followed by a violent awakening. My eyes were wide with a mixture of shock, terror, and anger. I threw out my hands before me like a homeless beggar, except I was not asking for monetary wealth, I was asking for emotional forgiveness from the only other human being I loved. The only problem with my plea was that it was made to the wrong person. Although her voice rang in my ears for a few moments, with her slew of disheartened questions reigning upon me, they were soon conquered by the sounds of reality. The blurry images of sky blue, white and purple began to collect amongst themselves and form objects. Walls painted in a serene mixture of blue shades stared at me from across the room, holding up peaceful paintings of flowers and farmland meadows. Feeling began to return and I quickly found myself in a comfortable bed adorned with cheap sheets and Ikea-brand flat pillows. Steel bars surrounded by bed, keeping me from further injury. I quickly looked to my left, searching for a clock or sorts. But to my left was a large window decorated with flowers and cards of ‘get well soon’ (which I would later discover that many of them were sent my Clinton Coldren – all of them asking not to sue). It was dark outside, and by the height and position of the moon, I assumed it was about 1:00 AM. I shook my head for a moment, to knock the proverbial cobwebs from my mind, when suddenly a stinging pain took over my entire jaw line. As I clutched the edges of it with comforting pressure, I was quickly reminded of the source of my stinging injuries. It was the same source for all of my injuries; the heavily wrapped left arm, the various stitches around my scalp, and the variety of bruises that decorated my body. It was all because of him. “He nearly killed me,” I said with a violent rasp in my voice. My fists began to clench and shake angrily in frustration. “He nearly killed me” I repeated, with a louder and more furious tone. “That son of a bitch nearly killed me…” I slammed my fists against the steel sides of my bed and caused one of the edges to snap violently to the ground. I sat for a few moments, stewing in my rage as the sound of steel hitting tile echoed in the empty room. I closed my eyes momentarily and took a few deep breathes to relax myself. Breathe in. Breathe out. Breathe in. Breathe out. Breathe in. Breathe in? Breathe out. Breathe out? I questioned if my breathing technique was echoing in the small room and held my breath to confirm that it wasn’t. There was another pair of lungs breathing beside me. I slowly turned to my right and discovered my room mate. His bald head shined in the moonlight and his various bandages patented marks of my triumph. He wheezed horribly with uncomfortable pressure, while the majority of his body remained stiff as board. It was the beast; the behemoth that put me in this God forsaken predicament. It was that son of a bitch who exposed my weaknesses and placed me in a damn robe like some feeble, elderly man with dementia. I attempted to rise from my bed to confront my foe, but I was held bound by a variety of different tentacle-like chords. One by one, I ripped them from my body. Each one depicted a sign of weakness, something I refused to show any of my rivals. After I removed the marks of vulnerability from my body I made my way to the bedside of this man. I searched persistently for some sort of chart or Identification that could reveal this man to me, showing me his every weakness, his every injury – to make the job of killing that much quicker and easier. As my search proved to be fruitless, I hesitated for a moment as I stared into his unconscious state. His forced slumber was not peaceful. While his eyes were shut, they winced frequently while his lips curled and quivered with disgust and anger. Within in his mind, he was still fighting. I watched his slumber in silence for a moment and felt the corners of lips curl upward. I grinned. I realized that if this man was fighting in his dreams, like I was, then he and I were destined to finish our battle where we left off. Call it a respect amongst foes, but I found myself hesitating in my pursuit to kill him. I couldn’t kill a man who brought me to the brink of death, an area I hadn’t visited by force before. He was an enemy that differed greatly from any other. And for that reason alone, I couldn’t kill him. Not now. Not like this. Our battle would have to continue elsewhere, when we were both ready for the second round. Until than, we would both have to wait anxiously for our rematch. However, I wanted to make sure that he knew a rematch was imminent. So I left a note at his bedside. I knew that each word would send his mind into a flurry of rage. I could envision his eyes going red with power and his blood boiling from frustration. It read: “Being alive is a luxury. I only let you live, to see you die at my hands.” I laid the note at his bedside counter, gathered the clothes I found in a plastic bag beside my bed, and soon after took my leave. As I exited the room, I heard him call out to me. Through rough snores and heavy breathing, he murmured, “I ain’t done with you, yet.” I paused for a moment, and my muscles tensed. I slowly peered over my shoulder, expecting him to be charging…but even in his sleep, he was aware of my presence. I grinned slyly and responded over my shoulder. “And I with you. But until then, take comfort in every breath you take, for it could be one of your last,” I raised a gentle hand over my shoulder, bidding him adieux. “Until next time, don’t stop living; I want the pleasure of taking that from you” I slipped on my leather trench coat (the only article of clothing that I wore that had not been stained with blood from the battle) and traveled through the dark corridors of the silent hospital. As I continued to travel through the empty halls, I could feel the presence of death in each of the rooms I passed. In room 414 was a man who suffered from a fatal case of lung cancer, possibly after voluntary suicide through years of intentional nicotine-laden inhalants. In room 408 was a woman who was suffering from heart complications, perhaps after gorging herself in McDonalds, Burger King and a wide variety of alcohol throughout her miserable and lonely life. Because of their negligence of life, death was beaming down on them all. Yet, here I stood, though injured possibly beyond repair, having escaped death again. As I left the hospital and entered the brisk winter night of New York, I reached into my pocket in search of my cigarettes. What I retrieved was something entirely different; a crudely folded white note. I held the mysterious note up towards a nearby street light and examined it briefly. I hadn’t left anything on my person besides a pack of cigs. I slowly opened the two-fold paper and held it towards my face. My eyes beamed curiously at the words etched in black pen. It was ominous with its mystery, and it left no explanation of its personal origin, only its mission. On the dirty white note was a 10 digit number with three letters written below it. Three letters that left a stench of rotten business in my nose; they were three letters I often scoffed at, even more so now due my growing detest of the wrestling business. I read the letters aloud, as if convincing myself of their presence. “f. W. o.”
