The Art of Self Destruction, Phase I (The Cabin - Home)

5 February 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The cabin was out in the middle of a dense forest in Michigan's upper-peninsula. Snow was falling hard from the ominously darkened skies. A howling wind swirled the snow about in mid-air as the cabin weathered the storm. Soft and white, the snow had piled up at least three feet, right underneath the two, small, box windows that sat four or five feet away from the door. In the center of the peaked roof, it sunk down in spots, the snow weighing it down over the years. The light grey shingling barely hanging on by its staples. Those old shingles were a sign for something much deeper. A young life that's decaying away on the inside. Rotting from the core out, just as an apple does. There are many, many different meanings of life that can be put into different perspectives from different people. From psychologists to taxi drivers, everyone has their own opinion on what life really is. Everyone hears the different interpretations from every person that's around them. But what happens when someone doesn't listen? The shingles fall. Cracked and grey, no longer needed and discarded aside like they never mattered. Almost as if the shingles had never meant anything to anybody. As if they never provided a steady guard from the pounding rains, heavy snows, hard winds and the hot sun. No, shingles don't need to be loved, or cared about nearly as much as a human life does. But when both are neglected, they fall into a category of uselessness and are thrown away. Nobody cares about them. And after all, why should they? Shingles are replaceable. But is a human life? Hardly. * * * Inside the cabin was barren, the walls were stripped and grey. Everything was compressed into one room. A small kerosene space heater sat in the center, an old weathered desk was pushed into a back corner of the wall, and an antique dining room chair was in front of it. In the opposite corner was a small gas range, and a night stand that had been turned into a makeshift counter. A bag filled with paper plates and plastic cups and utensils sat on top of the stand. Bolted into nearly every beam behind the walls around the room were kerosene lanterns that were lit to provide what little light was inside of the room. Although the heater was in the cabin, it was still unbearably cold, especially in February and in northen Michigan. The entire cabin reeked with an old must and was nearly empty, except for the bear necessities and a man that was sitting in the chair that was positioned at the table. He was bent over the table, a pen scrawling furiously into an opened notebook. His fingers were beet red as they pressed into the pen. The pen's ink was a dark red, the ink that would come from a gel pen. He kept drawing small signs and symbols onto the paper, hieroglyphics almost. On his face, a stoic, emotionless look was worn as his hand worked the pen into the paper. His brow was furrowed and little beads of sweat had formed at his forehead. He was hot and cold at the same time. The space heater did enough to keep him warm. But on the inside, his entire body and brain were being manipulated. The light was one and nobody was home. * * * It's cold outside tonight. The wind, it's blowing pretty hard, snow is coming down out there. Look out the window. Watch it come down. Pretty hard... You know, it's been a long time since we've been alone together like this. We haven't had any of our own time. It's about time we get it. I know how much you like this cabin... this place has ties to everything you've ever done in your miserable life. Actually, looking around this place, it's exactly what it's like living inside of here. Dark, desolate, boring, uninteresting, cold... I could go on. Maybe we both need some interior decorators. Man, you need to lighten up a bit. What are you writing anyway? That ink almost looks like... blood. Nah, it's just ink, you're not smart enough to end your life now by writing some sort of suicide note in blood. Even though... it'd be something different from your utterly hopeless blandness. Wish I could read whatever the hell it is you're writing. Those are some odd symbols or signs you have going on down there. What's going through your head right now? Oh... wait. I am your head. Ha ha ha! Why are you doing this to me? Because I control you.
