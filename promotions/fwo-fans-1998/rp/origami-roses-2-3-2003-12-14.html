Origami Roses (2:3)

14 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It was his reluctant belief that he had made a mistake at the floral shop. Making origami roses couldn't have been that hard, but the direction manual illustrated otherwise. His fingers didn't grab supplies for a simple crafts project but an advanced-level masterpiece. There were over twenty steps, and the book warned that the process wouldn't work if one step was overlooked or botched. His eyes started at the first step and scrolled horizontally across the long fold-out. He did this several times. What started as a blank leaf of paper transformed into an intricate pattern of folds and tears. The multi-layered final product was beautiful beyond description. That is what marriage was all about for Jeremy Savage. A union is whatever one makes of it, he surmised. Life-partners fold and twist their relationship experience into layer upon layer, only to come out with something incredible beyond words, or absurd beyond comprehension. A weak approach makes the completed product look loose and fake. On the other hand, too much force leads to undesired folds and tears. But in general, it is completely up to the parties involved regarding whether or not the marriage is a success. Whether they turn it into a rose or weed isn't a very difficult decision, but it is still one that needs to be made constantly in the confines of a relationship. Jeremy realized the difficulty of marriage as he did the difficulty of putting together the sculpture of paper. He screwed up many times, and ruined about 3 of his kits before getting on the right track. His first completed rose was wilted and crooked. But they got better. With great effort, Jeremy soon became a master at his craft. Every finished rose became flawless. If he put this much effort in his marriage, the Savages wouldn't have many problems at all. But then again, this was his personal opinion. Whenever he was in a bind, he could use trial-and-error to channel in good and prune away the bad. The constant repetition of cutting and folding beneath the dim light of a lamp was slightly therapeutic for him. He was making something that came from nothing. His inner thoughts revolved around Shirley with every manipulation and newly-fashioned creation. He wasn't concerned about her reaction because he knew she would love it. Nevertheless, he was excited to see her response. Her smile. Her dancing eyes. He let out a deep sigh, setting the scissors on the table top. He stretched his fingers by bending and spreading them. It was time for a quick break. He looked up and squinted at the clock on the wall, which read 1:30 am. He was deeply surprised that he had spent the entire day working on the Origami roses, but he soon realized that a great many still needed completing. Shirley would arrive around 8:00 the next morning, so he needed to complete the remaining preparations with haste. Jeremy rose from his chair, and bent over to pick up the empty plastic bags that were strewn on the floor. He stuffed them in a trash can near the kitchen, then walked towards their bedroom. He stopped at the door. He had prepared what would happen when she got there, but how was she going to get there?! Jeremy was very worried, but he quickly discovered a lapse in his memory. He had called up the limousine service in the afternoon, and they had agreed to pick her up when her flight arrived. So, her entrance was well-assured. He heaved another tremendous sigh before entering his room, where he planned on showering. Hopefully the water could bring some life into his tired body. After bathing, he would tidy up the cabin and get ready for her arrival. His heart was unusually warm and loving on this crazy night, and it was his firm belief that he was ready to finally reveal it to her. That, and the pink Origami roses.
