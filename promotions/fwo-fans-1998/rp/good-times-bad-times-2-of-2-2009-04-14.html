Good Times, Bad Times, 2 of 2

14 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
This is the second part of my story, appropriately called Bad Times. And they are. Not as bad as they have been or could be, but still. This dude really needs to get out of my way. "I'm sorry," said the dude in the scrubs, "but if you're not family you can't go in." After most of a perfect night, this is the last thing I wanted to deal with. Listen, I said, I'm Rose's boyfriend. I rode here in the ambulance with her, I'm responsible for calling her mom and getting her home. And you're telling me I can't go see her? "Those are the rules," said the dude in the scrubs. Lou and Sally were sitting in the waiting room, each with a cup of coffee in their hands. They weren't here nearly as often as I was, and figured, probably rightly so, that they would just be in the way if they were flanking me while I fought with this guy. "If you want to go take a seat and wait for a family member," said the dude in the scrubs, "we can let you in then. But you are not allowed in the ICU unless you have a family member inside." Where's Loretta, I asked. "Loretta is on her break," said the dude in the scrubs, "but she'd tell you the same thing." No, she wouldn't, I said. I walked back to the waiting room. I'm not usually a guy who gets angry at things, but times like these, the beaurocracy of the hospital is infuriating. We had gone from dinner to the Lenox Lounge for a night of jazz. TC's was more of a rock and roll, bluesy kind of place, and when we weren't there we would typically trek into Brooklyn for a night of metal at Duff's, but tonight was supposed to be about a more discerning taste. Rosie was having a great time. She doesn't have nearly enough of them. Currently she lives with her mom in a small apartment in the Bronx, working five to six days a week at the bar, up from four days because her mom's hours at work were recently cut to part time. She doesn't get enough time to herself, and doesn't get nearly enough pampering and someone taking care of her. That's not fair. She can take care of herself pretty well, I just... well... prefer to do it myself. "What's the deal?" asked Lou, when I returned to the waiting room. They won't let me in, I said, some new guy is guarding the door. "That's total crap," said Sally, "Don't they always let you in?" Loretta does, I said, but she's not here right now. I'm sure she's not supposed to, but she knows the deal and takes care of it. Lou and Sally both got out of their chairs and hugged me. I appreciated it, but I really wish I could be doing the same to Rose. Halfway through the performance, she excused herself to go to the ladies room. No big deal, it happens, but when she didn't come back after five minutes I asked Sally to make sure she was okay. She wasn't. Sally reemerged on her phone about twenty seconds later, and grabbed both Lou and myself by the shoulders. Turns out, Rose had another episode while in the ladies room, only, this time, she managed to spray the entirety of the mirror with coughed - up blood. At least the floor was clean for when she fell to her knees with her hands over her mouth. "Aw, don't tell me you're back again," said a familiar voice. I turned around and came face to face with Loretta. She wore her nurse's scrubs, an unzipped windbreaker, and smelled of cigarettes. We made it almost three months, I said, that's gotta be a record. "And this is the fourth time this year," said Loretta. "That can't be a good sign." Lou pointed to the chairs and I nodded. Go home if you want, I said, I'm gonna stay here for a bit. I can give you money for the cab when I get home. "Aight, we might do that," said Lou. He shook my hand and gave me the one - armed hug. "We'll hang for a bit, though, and if we do leave, talk to ya tomorrow." Loretta smiled at them, and we left the waiting room. My anger and frustration quickly drained as the nurse led me back to where I started. "We never like to see y'all come in," said Loretta, with that accent that was still strangely Southern despite the fact that she'd lived in Harlem for almost fifteen years, "but Marjorie's gonna be sad she missed ya." Well, you'll just have to make sure Marjorie knows we tried to schedule Rosie's breakdowns around her time here, but sometimes that girl is just inconsiderate. Loretta and I laughed. It was really all we could do. "Maxwell," said Loretta to the dude in the scrubs, "please let the boy in to see Miss Callasantos." "He's not a relative, Loretta," said Maxwell, "No exceptions." "For the love of God, Max," said Loretta, "This boy is here a dozen times a year with that little girl and he's never caused any trouble. All he wants to do is be there. Now, I'm tellin' you, let him in to see Miss Callasantos." Maxwell looked at me, and at Loretta, and back at me. I could tell he was hating me right now because he was getting overruled. "Fine," said Maxwell, "come on in." I didn't blame him; he was just doing his job. But still, how long would it have taken for him to page Loretta and all that? "You know the rules, I assume," said Maxwell. I do, I said. Quiet. Don't touch anything. Rosie is in bed 15A because it's closest to the door. How sad is it that my girlfriend has been to this hospital so much she has her own reserved bed in ICU? I walked quickly and quietly through the room, easily avoiding the doctors and nurses who gave me a nod of recognition. She was in the last bed in the ICU, because she was expecting to be moved to a regular room or discharged outright within the next hour or two. Her shoes were next to the bed, and the beautiful dress she had previously been wearing was hanging over the back of the bed. There were a few spatters of dried blood on it, but I'm sure she could get them out. Rosie was reading a magazine that I'm sure someone snuck in to her. Her eyes raised when she saw that I was approaching, and she smiled. "Hey stranger," said Rose. She had tubes in her nose and a pulse monitor, but no IV. "Feel like taking me home to do naughty things to me?" I laughed quietly, in deference to the rest of the room. Years ago, Rose was caught in a fire and her lungs were severely smoke damaged. To this day she has pretty bad asthma, and will occasionally go into a coughing fit. She and her mom are used to this, and deal with it as best they can, but when Rose starts to cough up blood and can't pull in any air whatsoever, that's when the ambulance is called. This makes four times since 2009 started. Granted, the first three were all in January so there's been almost three incident - free months. The dirty jokes and innuendo is her coping mechanism. Her father was caught in the same fire and he died twelve or so years ago; she's confided to me in tear filled nights that she feels like she's been living on borrowed time ever since. And she's only twenty one. Did they tell you how long you had to be here, I asked. "Dr. Mayview said they're planning to move me to a room in the next half hour or so," said Rose, "and they want to keep me overnight but that I can leave in the morning." Okay, I said. Want me to stay with you? "Actually," said Rose, "could you go to the pub and get me a change of clothes? I think I have some emergency changes in the room upstairs and I'd rather not do this kind of walk of shame." In the locker, right, I asked. "Right." I leaned over and kissed her on the lips. I'm going now, I said. Where's your purse? "Loretta's holding it," said Rose. I didn't know if I'd make it to the bar by the time Tommy closed up, so I wanted to get Rose's keys to get inside. Plus, I would need her keys to get to the emergency loft upstairs anyway. Lou and Sally are still here, I said, I'm going to ask them to stay until I get back just in case you need some familiar faces. She smiled. That smile could melt your heart. Listen, I said, when I come get you tomorrow, I think you should come with me to Seattle for reAction. And it's out there. We've been dancing around the subject of my wrestling career and the fact that I think she could be an asset in it for the past year. It was a touchy thing when I was wearing the mask, because Rosie's presence would have given me away and ruined my entire goal of trying to make it anonymously, but ever since, we've alternated on thinking it was a good idea and thinking it wasn't. What it always boiled down to was that Rose wants to be with me, but didn't want to just be shoehorned somewhere because she was - at the bar she was the go-to on everything and liked being that. What it always boiled down to was that I wanted Rose to be with me, but I was always worried about her health and what would happen if she suddenly had an episode in another state or country. She knew everyone at this hospital and they knew how to handle her. I don't want her to have to go through this somewhere else. "I'd love to," said Rose. And that was that.
