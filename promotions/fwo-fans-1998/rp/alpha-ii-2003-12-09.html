Alpha II

9 December 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Then, he was just Jack. A small kid raised on the sport he grew up around of professional wrestling. As a child, he shared nothing but love with the men and women who risked their bodies and their careers in the ring and watched with focused and attentive eyes. Even though he was diagnosed with faint Attention Deficit Disorder, Jack never turned his head away from the ring. Except for that faithful night where he sat front row, as his father's neck contorted into an L shape position. His mother, at the time, was shielding his baby sister from the wreckage, overlooking the middle child like every single family sitcom has done since their existence. Jack watched as the crowd fell to a silent hush. When Joe Landersell's arch nemesis that evening didn't pin the limp Joe, and instead waved to the back and knelt down next to his opponents twisted neck, people weren't sure what was going on. There wasn't a thought of "kayfabe." The magical tricks of the business had yet to be revealed, so during this break of character, the crowd was in a state of shock. Paramedics ran out from the back as the sun lights cooked what was the remains of Joe Landersell. That night. That night was ripe with death, with gloom, and most of all, sorrow. The stench of liquor was so strong that Jack could hardly fall asleep in their motel bed. Japanese late night television wasn't exactly helping with it's epileptic flashes of light, color and sound. But it did help drown out the shot. Jack didn't see his mother alive again. You see, to coop with her husband being on the road nearly three hundred days a year, his mother had fallen to alcoholism. And none of her ignorant sons and daughter could tell the difference between a normal mother and a booze hound. So when Jack saw her with a bottle that was near empty in her hand, he didn't think anything of it. When she went into the bathroom to sob her broken heart out, Jack didn't think anything of it. And he never heard the self inflicted gun shot to his mother's skull. But he eventually found out. You see, he was the first one up the next morning, and had to drain his lizard. The papers said it all... Joseph and Sarah Harmen, dead at ages thirty two and thirty five respectively. They are survived by their two sons, Jake and Jack, as well as their daughter Tammy. Funeral services will be held Saturday.
