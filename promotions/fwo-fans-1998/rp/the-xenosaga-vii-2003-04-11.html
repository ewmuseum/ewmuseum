The Xenosaga: VII

11 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
George Douglas was your average British citizen, meaning that he was irritable, drunk a lot of the time, and probably wouldn't play a really big role in this tragic tale of torture, bad video games, and bad movies based on video games. In fact, his role in this tale was limited to looking up from his gardening and seeing a black man in a black robe that was covered in feathers, chocolate syrup, and popcorn. The man, the Codemaster, dashed through his garden, leaving being little crumbs of syrup, feathers, and popcorn. George Douglas found this strange and turned to his wife. "You don't see that every day." His wife nodded, and both of them stared at the Codemaster as he dashed away as fast as he could. * . * Meanwhile, James Cagle, though with his gooified brains from having to endure his own torturous Mortal Kombat: Annihilation movie, was on the hunt with Crocodile Davy Dingleberry. Finally, after some blind wandering towards the direction that they felt the Codemaster had gone in, Dingleberry stopped, dropped chest-first to the ground, only stopping himself using his hands, and seemed to be sniffing the ground. "What're you doin', Dingerburry?" "Oy'm pickin' up ees scent!" Dingleberry said. "Dingerburry, Ah can see the trayel er chawklit see-rup and pawpcorn, you ain't gotta dawdle by showin' off your sniffin' 'bilities." Cagle retorted. "Shut up and let's go." Dingleberry shot back, before the two Tallywhackers ran of. * . * George Douglas had just recovered from seeing the Codemaster run off like a crazy guy covered in random amusing things to be covered in, but now here was the thing that took the cake. He looked back up from his gardening and saw an Alabama redneck in overalls and an Australian who was meanacingly wielding a boomerang and was apparently also trying to sniff something out at the same time, for that added bit of strangeness, and they were also following the same path the Codemaster took, as if these two were actually following him. George sighed to himself and again turned to his wife, "Honey, I think it's time to call the bloody orderlies. I think I'm going insane." She didn't argue.
