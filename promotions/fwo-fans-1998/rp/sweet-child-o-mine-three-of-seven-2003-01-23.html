Sweet Child O' Mine - Three of Seven

23 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
SWEET CHILD O’ MINE Part Three – Sex Stanley Xavier is actually having a good time tonight. Valerian’s Garden was supposed to play Randalls’ Bar tonight… but the show was hastily cancelled when the lead singer had a sudden case of labor pains. All was not lost, however, as one of the bar’s regulars is a jazz saxophonist, and it was no problem for him to call the rest of his band and play an impromptu set, thus keeping Randall Knox’s paying customers happy. The jazz band’s drummer couldn’t make it, however. Fortunately, Stan “Sex” Xavier was on hand. He’s filled in well for them, and has actually enjoyed himself. But his thoughts are not with the music at hand… they’re with a young lady lying in a hospital room right now. Whatever happens with the band at this point is anyone’s guess – and he’d like to keep things going… but you don’t know what the future will bring. Sex can remember the first time he ever met Angela Davison. He and TJ had just hooked up in a sixth- rate nowhere town in Nebraska and alongside TJ’s brother, played their favorite metal and glam rock covers in his basement. And while TJ could sound like David Bowie on his good days, he was no lead singer. Then outta nowhere he showed up to practice with this nothing of a wannabe goth. She’d never sung in a band before but TJ seemed to think she was worth a shot. “Got something y’wanna try out with?” asked Sex, tapping his foot on his bass drum steadily. She surprised him by requesting that they play “Sober” by Tool – a song that she’d apparently just heard a few days before. But she blew ‘em all away with her rendition – eyes closed, hair in her face, and she belted out the words like she was about to burst into tears. Even before the song was over… they knew. Even after Sex and TJ relocated to California and hooked up with Mickey and Nine – they knew they wouldn’t be a band unless they lured Angela – by then, she’d been rechristened “Angel” – out to sing. The jazz trombone player looked back at him with a wink – he’d apparently been impressed by the new drum roll he’d laid out on ‘em. But why did Angel always cry when they listened to “Prison Sex” by Tool – and that one line, ”I have found some kind of temporary sanity in this”? None of his business, she’s a grown woman and she can take care of herself. Besides… she’s gone from a quiet and shy girl, only coming to life when she sings, to one of the most confident, driven, and ambitious women he’s ever known. Much like the woman he spoke to on the phone about a half hour ago. ”Apparently, they’re there,” said Poison Ivy, the buzz of a wrestling show nearly drowning out her voice, “and Angel’s the only calm one of the bunch.” “I’m not surprised,” replied Sex, “she’s been ready for this thing for about six months now. And I guarantee you she’s gonna spend six days in labor just to make ‘em all sweat about it.” Ivy laughed away from the mouthpiece. “She spends six days in labor and I’m gonna tell her it’s all your fault. You get a lotta shit for no- showing the concert?” “Actually no,” said Sex, looking around, “We didn’t get any money up front, the club wasn’t selling any tickets, so it was just a matter of finding a new act.” “Did they?” “Some jazz band. They asked me to fill in for their drummer.” “Not bad, not bad… you play jazz?” “It’s not all that complicated… anyone with a drummer’s ear and the ability can keep time. I’ve always wanted to play in a jazz band.” “You think you might make a career change?” “I…” “…don’t want to prejudge. That’s fine, I wouldn’t want you to.” “Ivy,” said Sex, taking a deep breath, “let me explain this to you. I have been playing in bands with TJ, and with Angel since she was sixteen. Quite frankly, I know her better than you do. She doesn’t do anything half- assed, ever. It’s all or nothing. You follow?” “I think so.” “Good. Now, she has been putting her entire being into Valerian’s Garden, just as much as she’s put into this relationship with EJ. Add a baby to the mix, and I don’t know if she can balance the scales.” “You’ve lost me.” “Okay… how’s this? She loves EJ. She loves the idea of being a mother. She loves to sing. And I know that losing any of these would be devastating to her, but if something’s gonna be sacrificed, it’ll be the band. And trust me,” with an air of caution added to his voice, “there’s no Valerian’s Garden without her.” “Let her have the baby first… then we’ll know what she’s planning.” “I plan to. But I’m also doing what I can bet Mickey, TJ, and Nine are doing at the hospital right now, and that’s making a plan for the future that doesn’t involve Valerian’s Garden.” He can remember the conversation verbatim… and he can’t believe some of the things the two spoke about. Leave Valerian’s Garden? Play in a band without TJ or Angel? But if Angel decides to raise the kid full- time… there won’t be a Valerian’s Garden to leave. As Stanley “Sex” Xavier returns his attention to the jazz number that the band is playing – something by the master himself, Sonny Rollins – it’s small comfort to know that he’s got plenty of options in the music business, whether the Garden remains or not. But no matter where he might go, he knows… he knows that it wouldn’t be the same. There’s only one Garden.
