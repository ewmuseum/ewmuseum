The End - Part Five

11 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Nobody gave the nurse a second glance as she walked down the hallway. One of the orderlies looked up as she passed the admission desk. Her red hair was tied up in a bun, and maybe with some makeup she might have been something to look at. She might have been a little on the hefty side, but many nurses had to be a little on the stout side to lug around patients during the course of their duties. Then again, you'd look hefty too if you were wearing a form-fitting kevlar vest under your hospital whites. She slipped into the patient's room unnoticed, moving like a shadow through the doorway and drifting into the room without making a sound. It wasn't like the patient could hear her; he'd been in a semi-vegatatitive state since he'd been admitted weeks ago. A professional wrestler with brain damage wasn't much of a threat, especially to a trained killer like her. Mercy walked over to the bed. It was late at night, and nobody would be by to check on the patient until the morning. By then, the neurotoxin she was about to inject into his bloodstream would have destroyed the synaptic tissue in his brain. Full vegetable, only good for harvesting organs. Not that there'd be much worth saving. It was almost funny to think that Nick Brandish might actually end up saving some one's life rather than destroy it. The air mask remained strapped to his burn-scarred face. Burning tattoos off of someone's face was pretty sick, even for Brandish. The top half of his bald head had several rows of stitches from where the surgeons had to stop the bleeding around his brain. It was ironic that Shawn Stewart, an ex-cop, was the one who almost beat Nick to death. On a Pay Per View, no less. If ever there was an event worth paying to see, that was it. She looked down at the monster's closed eyelids and watched the flutter. Was he dreaming? What did something like Brandish dream about? Whatever it was, there would be plenty of blood involved. He was an animal, and like any sick, dangerous animal he had to be put down. She pulled out the small black box and opened the lid. One syringe, with one full dose of neurotoxin. Hours after the injection, Nick's heart would stop beating. By that time, most of his motor functions would be gone. When the door opened behind her, Mercy quickly palmed the box and slipped it back into her pocket. "Excuse me," she said as she turned around. "Visiting hours are over." The huge figure stepped out of the doorway. "Hello Dawn," Vic Creed said as he stood with his hands on his hips. "Or Mercy, I believe you're going by these days." She tried not to show her surprise. "I said, visiting hours are over." "Silly bitch." Vic let his trenchcoat fall open as his arms dropped to his sides. He could see her eyes betray her anger, even if her face didn't show it. She obviously remembered their last meeting, and hadn't forgiven him for what he's put her through in the past. But her feelings didn't concern Creed. This wasn't about her. "Haven't you heard? Your job is finished here." She looked down at the blanket covering Brandish's chest and watched it slowly rise and fall. "He's not dead," Mercy said calmly. "Yet." Vic took a step toward her and studied her body from behind his sunglasses. She didn't so much as flinch as he approached. "He is on paper." Vic reached inside his coat and pulled out a folded piece of paper. Mercy quickly snatched it from the Main Man's hand. "He died thirty minutes ago. Tragic." It was true.
