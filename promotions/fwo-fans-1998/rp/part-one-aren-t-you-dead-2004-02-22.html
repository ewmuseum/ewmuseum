Part One: Aren't you dead?

22 February 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"This isn't the way." It was the main thought that reverbated in Ivy McGinnis' mind for the past week. Watching the fWo continue its Disney-induced stupor, she could barely stomach a full show anymore. But she watched it - she felt a responsibility to keep up to speed with it all. If only she could write about it. She's been in the depths of depression, separated from the job she cares more about than her own future. Ivy stretched out in the 'Center Field' booth - the one closest to the kitchen was always left alone unless Ivy was in town. She'd left her unlaced boots standing at attention on the floor beneath the table as her feet rested on the opposite side. Only twenty nine years old... but her emerald eyes, hidden behind her glasses, looked much older. "It's not the end of the world, y'know. Stop looking like someone murdered your dog." It can't be... Ivy spun around, face to face with the man she missed more than nearly anyone else. "Fox? But--- how?" How, indeed. But Grant Mitchel stood in front of her just the way he did on New Year's Eve - leather sports coat, dark button-down shirt, and black pants. It was-- "--a miracle," whispered Ivy, as Fox sat down next to her stretched out feet. "Mind if I join you?" asked Fox, "You look like you could use a friend." "Don't know how much company I'll be right now," replied Ivy, "but sure." She moved her feet so Fox could put his coat down, but continued to stare at him. "So... how're ya feeling?" "How do I look?" "Y'look pretty good for a guy that was in a coma for two months," said Ivy, as she suddenly looked away with a laugh stuck in her throat, "Jeez, this is all wrong... you can't be here." "I'm here, aren't I?" "What happened?" "I think we need to focus on you right now." Deep breath, Ivy. All those nights you just knew that all you needed to make everything in your brain make sense was Silver Fox to bounce your thoughts off, time to pay the piper. "You wanna know what's goin' on right now, Fox?" Let the healing begin.
