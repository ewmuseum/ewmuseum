Full Circle

8 December 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Salt Lake City, Utah The ballboys loitered amongst themselves, and played their game of half-court ball on the parquet of the Delta Center. Soon enough, the Utah Jazz and Golden State Warriors would be engaged in NBA basketball in front of the always-frenetic Utah faithful clad in the home team's purple and white. But that wasn't for a few hours yet, and so they got to live a dream, if only for a short time. On the other end of the court, another young man walked onto the playing surface. He was a little taller and a little more muscular than the other ball boys, and his countenance--his strut--seemed to signify that he wasn't in fact a ball boy at all. His black-stripe-on-green-warmup-pants swished softly against his Nikes. They would've thought it very odd that he was wearing an old school Charger uniform, numbered 79 that he had christen 3 Hate Me on a basketball court. But the young man looked down at his mp3 player, and then he looked at the magazine in his hands. There were a coven of Utah fans, but this was not a happy scene. All their eyes were on the same man. Their expressions ran the displeased end of the gamut, from despair, to anger, to resignation, to disappointment. Some of their hands were on their heads already. Their fate was certain. Some of them were praying to their God that the ball would not go in. But they all knew it would. Bryon Russell could only look behind him. In front of him, Michael Jordan had just shot the ball that would win the game. And the picture captured that moment before the ball went in. Ruben Ross stood on the floor, where Michael had shot his shot. He looked at the basket and back to his magazine. He thought of the similarities. August 26th, 2000. Everyone had gone but him in his matches. He'd essentialy retired Greg Davis. Red Dragon was long gone. Juno Law had quickly gone to tag wrestling but had never beaten him. And Mitch Wilson had been retired back at CS4. He'd just pushed them off him, a little. The space had opened up. His shot and his aim were dead on. For the most part they were relegated to the Buzz Bin of history. He was still around. The ball boys started towards him, but he'd seen what he'd come to see. He walked out the way he came in, silent, without a sound. After all, he'd be here tomorrow. And as he left, he heard the opening clarion call of what had been Aren Archer's music: How many people want to kick some ass?
