Desecrate Through Reverance (15): A Fork For Your Road

27 June 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
In Orlando, Aimz always complained about missing the even temperatures of her Eastern Canadian home. She was rather happy that Brandon wasn't with her at that moment as she prayed for Floridian weather upon stepping out of her rental car in a mid-June chill, clad in a Damageplan sweater over a t-shirt and dressier black pants. A chuckle from her company made up for any of Brandon's missing humor. "Don't even," the frozen redhead laughed to her best friend, who wore a tanktop rather comfortably. "Thanks for coming, though. I don't have the slightest fuckin' clue what to do in here." Renee Williams and Amy Campbell-Roche stood before a door reading 'Child and Adolescent Services', a small strip on the side of a hospital Aimz had no intention of glancing to. "First off," began Renee, "Tone the language down, and gimmie the sweater. Social Services isn't really down with giving kids back to their moms when they're wearing a shirt with a great big middle finger on the front - especially when she's cursing to make a sailor cry." Amy sighed, "Fuck you." "Love you too, hun. Now go, I'll wait back at the car. Just tell the lady at the window inside who you are and what you're there for." "I'll manage." said the redhead, shedding her sweater to reveal the red t-shirt underneath. "If I don't freeze first." "Besides," she continued, "How bad could I come across NOW? They just followed me around for a week and still deemed me a fit parent." Renee giggled, "Physically fit, maybe. I'll give them that." "Thanks for the vote of confidence." winked Amy as she walked into the air-conditioned hospital. Dear Lord, it was chilly in there. She halfway jogged to a small reception area and spoke through plexiglass to an elderly woman, "I'm Amy Roche, here for my five o'clock with Vivian O'Bri--" "She's running a bit behind this afternoon, so if you'll take a seat in the waiting area I'm sure she'll find you." The lady smiled, barely even noticing she'd interrupted the Amy. At that, the halfway-nervous, halfway anxious redhead stepped into the most dimly-lit waiting room she'd ever seen. This was an environment to help keep children happy? "I know." Who? "...Too sterile for my tastes, too." "Sorry," Amy stuttered, peering through squinted eyes at the short-haired woman sitting across the tiny room. "I didn't quite catch tha--" As she spoke, the corner of her eye caught a wide and innocent smile. Damnit, not here! "MOMMY! IT'S AIMZ!" Too late. The woman sitting across the room had been looking her over, but now she seemed to be closely examining Amy as her son ran towards the tattooed redhead. "If wrestling ain't real, why you always look so hurt?" Amy laughed and used the elastic around her wrist to ponytail her hair as she spoke. At least the little boy hadn't asked for a photograph, but his mom may as well have taken one...she was still staring. "Wrestling's pretty real, man. We don't exactly fake that pain." "Are you and Brand really married?" he beamed, but his mother's expression changed slightly, "Where is he? Can I see him?!" The woman finally spoke again, this time to her son. "Jeff, don't pester the poor woman." "Hey," said Amy, turning back to the tot, "It's cool. Yeah, we're married...but Brand's in Orlando right now doing some work." "Why are you he--" "Jeffrey, can you go play in the toy basket?" The little boy looked to his mother, to Amy and back again. He nodded, waved at the redhead and quickly switched his attention to Tonka trucks. Amy shifted uncomfortably, pretending to read a magazine among the few on the coffee table in the center of the dark waiting area. That woman was still looking her over! What the hell was going on? Her face was recognizable, Amy concluded. But many faces looked farmilliar to the nomadic 21-year-old. Finally, though, she had her answer when the gawking girl spoke up. "You're Amy Campbell, yeah?" she said, "Leanne Cooper." Leanne Co-- "I stood next to you at graduation." Amy's face lit up with realization, "And I took your ex to prom! Man, it's been a while...you're living in Moncton now?" "We live with granma 'cause mommy says daddy's an a---" "Ooookay, Jeff. Back to your toys, buddy. Let mommy and Aimz talk." Leanne continued, this time to Amy. "I...uhh...almost married the guy you took to prom. Almost, meaning...well...I was in love before I found out about some things." "Matt wasn't a very nice guy." said the redhead, standing up to sit next to her old aquaintance. "At all. But...I found out before there was a ring on my finger, at least. It made things cheaper, but there's still...well..." Leanne nodded towards her son, playing in the corner. Amy nodded in recognition and Leanne continued, "So...what brings you here? I mean, you're off being Hollywood wrestler or something right? No disrespect intended." "I'm just picking someone up." Well, it wasn't TOTALLY a lie. "Then I'll beg a question." "Shoot." Leanne looked to her son, then back to Amy. "How the hell do you get out of here?" "The door?" "No, I mean here. Home. Canada. How do you get better things?" "You escape. Sad as the government there is, the states is the best place to go for a living." said Amy, scribbling on a paper scrap from her pocket. Very suddenly, a vibrant woman of about 5 feet jumped into the room. "Amy Campbell? I'm Viv." The redhead smiled at the woman and stood. "'Kay, just give me a moment." She handed Leanne the scap of paper she'd been writing on, "That's my cell. Call anytime, day or night. I don't really sleep." she said, turning to the little boy still in the corner, "See ya, Jeffro!" The tot waved frantically as Amy followed the social worker back through the door. Leanne Cooper was a name she'd never honestly known. But, whoever that woman was...she reminded her too much of herself. Too much of herself, and just enough to help. For now, though, she had a daughter to catch up with. [BACK to "Life Under The Weather"]
