Sunday Bloody Sunday

24 January 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Sunday, January 20 London, England 9:55 PM London Time (4:55 PM Eastern Time) The pain was back again. That much Scott Wasner was conscious of as he dragged himself up. The cut had been yelled, and the segment was over. Slaughter was being filmed ahead of time that night, and being tape-delay broadcasted back to the US - unlike Tokyo, London time did not lend itself to a live broadcast. Davis, Strangler and Creed had walked away immediately - they were unconcerned with his health at best. I don't blame them... no reason for them to be. I've done the same thing myself. But whether they suspected so or not, their kicks had taken a toll. Especially one especially stiff shot laid in by Davis towards the end of the segment. He had been wrestling for many, many years, but he had never felt a kick like that. Not even close. I never liked Davis, the annoying little bugger. Agh... He was sure it would look good on TV, but that was hardly on the top of his list of concerns. The director gave him an odd look as he stood up. "Uh, Scott - the segment's over. You don't have to sell it anymore." Wasner gave a half-hearted smile (I think my ribs are going to explode, is that possible?) and begin to walk off in the opposite direction. Well, more like stagger off. He turned around the corner, out of the director's sight, and doubled over. Holy fuck... It was a combination of things. Davis had truly blasted him, he was still feeling the pain from last week - not a good sign - and, it was quickly becoming apparent, he was not entirely ready to return to the ring. If he hurt this bad following a stiff beatdown segment... Wasner stood back up and thanked God that his dressing room was right there. He stepped in the door and threw himself on the couch, clutching his ribs. Oh... my... God. The need to urinate was becoming strong, and he pulled himself back to his feet and staggered to the bathrom. Oh, shiiiiit. Red. This is SO not good. He had experienced this a few times before - most of the time after especially grueling matches - his Hell In The Cell with Eron came to mind - or taking especially hard bumps during segments - such as the time Ice had thrown him through a car window. He had done the stunt himself, as was his custom on pre-taped segments, whereas most stars would have used a stunt double. It was, after all, pre-taped - no reason to put one's self on the line. Leave it to the magic of the editor. He didn't subscribe to that creed. Stupid. Wasner snapped himself out of his woolgathering as a thought hit him. He zipped up and headed back to the main room with the thought still on his mind. Roach. He had kept the card of the diminutive pusher since being given it. I meant to throw it away, honestly - I just got caught up cutting those promos for the syndies afterwards and forgot about it. Since then, something had always come up (you don't trust yourself, Scott) when he had had (you think you're weak and you're gonna backslide, so you kept it) the opportunity to (and maybe you didn't want to stop, did youyou were right). Wasner set his travel bag down by the couch and dropped down onto it again. Oh, too hard. Pain. He reached out and dug through the bag, then came out with the card. OK, Thomas "Roach" Preston... I think I'm going to pay you a call. But just this once. And not for coke. Oh God, no. Just a few painkillers... I've never had any problems with those, just coke. I'll be fine. It's just... God, I'm not ready yet, I shouldn't be back. But I can't exactly go up to management and tell them hey, I need to take a little time off even though I just came back, can I? No, I can't. So just this once... just for a little something. Because it hurts so bad. ----- Seattle, Washington 9:55 PM Pacific Time Stpehanie Griffith had never noticed how lonely the wind sounded. Strange but true... all those hokey old stories are true. I'll be damned. She had been watching Scott on his show, but the beating he was taking tonight was too much for her to look at. She flipped the channel with a grimace. Scott can keep doing that, he's great at it, but don't expect me to watch it. Finding nothing, she turned the television off and leaned back in her chair. While she was not needy or clingy - at least did not like to think of herself as so - this kind of lifestyle was not anything like what she had envisioned. He's out there, on the road, all the time... if he could just spend a little more with me, things would be a lot better. I don't know if I can take this kind of relationship. She closed her eyes and listened to the wind. It was loud tonight, a high whistling sound, as lonesome as a Bob Seger song. She was alone, it was alone... a pretty fair matchup, I guess. She couldn't call, not really - the time distance was too much. Not to mention the price. Not that he - we can't afford it. This house is proof enough of that. The house itself was very large, located at the top of a hill in the posh Queen Anne district of Seattle. It was large and rambling. So large, in fact, that Stephanie wondered more than once if Scott had ever managed to reach all the rooms. She thought not. How could somebody live alone in a place like this anyway? It's so big... so cold. When you're alone. She could not call up her friends, because she had none - no real friends anyway. There were a few other women at her job - a nice accounting spot, in what Scott often referred to as that big-ass black building, she thought with a smile - but she knew none of them as more than a working acquaintance. There was Jimmy, probably the guy she worked with most, but she found him a bit creepy - he seemed to have a thing for her, and that was not a place she wanted to go. So she sat in the chair, listening to the wind moan outside. After a while, she begin to go to sleep. Before she faded away, she was struck by a brief but terrible certainty that over in London, something had happened today that would change her life - Scott's life - and many more lives than that - in ways that she couldn't come close to foreseeing... but it would not be for the good. Then that feeling slipped away, leaving her mind like water down the back of a duck. She slept, her sleep undisturbed by dreams or nightmares, alone.
