Family Values - Chapter Seven

2 July 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
November 22, 1994 Spencer, Nebraska 10:40 PM local time Maybe she was wrong about Uncle Mark. Squeezed into the back of her father's Mid- Life Crisis car, Mark seemed to be pushing himself against the opposite door. At Aunt Cheryl's, he barely looked at her, let alone talked to her or tried to touch her, even so simply as a pat on the arm. It made Angela think of the security she had hidden in her purse - it made her feel guilty. This man, her Uncle, was obviously a good man, right? Her family seemed to think so - her father particularly. "Sweetie? You feel okay? You look a little pale." "Paler than usual, at least." Her father laughed at her, but her mother seemed genuinely concerned. Angela quietly excused herself and went outside, wearing only a vinyl shirt with massive sleeves and a long black skirt despite the cold. She shivered but made no effort to warm herself - the cold air sharpened her senses. "THIS is the source of my nightmares?" She smiled. "THIS is the cause of my shakes and anxiety?" She laughed to herself, looking inside at the small, thin man who was steadily working on his third water and... water. He hadn't had an alcoholic drink all night. "Maybe I don't really know what happened," said Angela to herself, "Maybe he was drunk and my mind has just made it worse than what it was." Yeah. That was it. A shy fifteen year old with an overactive imagination turned an honest mistake by a good man into the Worst Thing Ever. "Angela," called her mother from the back door, rubbing her hands together in the cold, "We're getting ready to go." "Okay Mom," replied Angela, feeling a little silly for letting such a nothing event turn her into the Stereotypical Neurotic Girl. And whatever happened, it gave her the ability to write. That's a good thing... right? The Davison House 10 PM "It was good to see you again," said Mark to Henry, "Take care." Henry and his wife were headed to bed. Angela was in her room, busily writing the words to a song called "Hunter" in her notebook. She had said goodbye to Uncle Mark, and had even given him a hug. The nervousness was gone. She was listening to "Intolerance" by Tool on her radio when she felt the need for a cigarette. Tiptoeing outside, she heard the sounds of her parents still awake. Knowing that her cigs would not go over well if they were still up, she grabbed her coat - a long black coat - and headed outside. Her backyard was the beginning of a long stretch of woods. About a half mile back was the highway - she knew the layout well. Walking to the highway and back at a leisurely pace would be enough time for three cigarettes, enough to get her through the night. "Got a light?" asked a voice from behind. She spun around to see her uncle Mark standing there, with a cigarette of his own in his mouth. "Sure," she said, tossing him her lighter. He lit the cigarette and tossed it back to her - and she could see his motorcycle parked off the side of the highway about thirty feet forward. There wasn't a sound in the air. Suddenly, she felt nervous again. "Uncle Mark?" "What is it, Angela?" "Do you remember the last time we saw each other?" "Of course I do," he said with a rueful smile. "You... did something when you were drunk," she stammered, "I wanted to ask you---" "I was wrong," said Uncle Mark, almost immediately, "It wasn't something I should have done, and I'm sorry." His bluntness surprised her. "I hope..." he continued, "...that someday we might be able to have an actual relationship that doesn't have such a cloud over it." Angela smiled. "Maybe in a little while..." "Why wait?" "What do you mean?" "You didn't know what you were doing last time I saw you. You were what, fifteen?" "I'm... confused." "I lost my temper," continued Uncle Mark, unabashedly, "and I hit you in the face when I should've been showing you what to do." As he continued to speak, Angela took a step away from him, suddenly feeling sick. "Showing me what to do?" she asked, shocked. "Yeah," continued Mark, barely stopping, "But you're eighteen now, right? You've had some practice." He took a step toward her. She took one back - nearly falling over. Her knees felt weak. "You're family," she countered, "you're blood!" "Best place to go," he continued, "Family is always there." Her cigarette fell to the snow, extinguishing immediately. "You're fuckin' sick," she whispered. "Tsk, tsk," said Uncle Mark, practically leering at her, "Such language." Angela turned to run, but the snow, combined with her own physical state of near- shock refused to let her legs move and she sprawled face- first into a snowbank. In less than two steps he was upon her. He stripped his belt from around his waist and wrapped it around her throat, pulling tightly while he groped up her skirt. "But... but you said you were sorry," choked Angela through the leather noose, "You were so... different today..." "I am sorry," countered Uncle Mark, "You were too damn young last time I loved you. But now, you're a woman, ripe for the breaking." He slapped her hard across the face. "Stop struggling, goddamnitt!" As Angela closed her eyes, tears squeezed out the sidez - and images long since repressed began to emerge. "Love" he called it. She knew better. He chose her because she was small, quiet, and weak. Easy to control. It was the control that turned him on - the control and the violence. The unerring mind's eye played a collage for her - every time he hit her, every thrust of his body, every time she gagged on him. For no reason at all, she thought of the day she met TJ - the day she first heard Maynard James Keenan's voice. The strength it gave her - the determination that it filled her body with when she sang the songs she scribed. There was all the strength in the world just six feet away from her, as she looked to her right and saw her purse, cigarettes strewn all over the snow from its opening. Uncle Mark held both her wrists down while he unzipped his pants - and a fierce determination came over Angela Davison. I'll show you what your love did to me, she said to herself, I'll show you what you taught me about control. You're in for a surprise, dear Uncle... I've been here all along. I remember everything. Angela balled up her right hand and let a punch fly to her uncle's face. The action startled him enough to let go of her hands, and he staggered backwards, surprised to taste blood on his lips. Taking that moment for all it was worth, Angela dove for her purse, paying no mind to the sliver of blood that had frozen to the ring on her right index finger - frozen to one of the five points of the Pentacle she wore on it. "You fucking bitch," he growled, more scared than anything else. Here was his neice, a girl he loved and wanted to show how much... and not only does she hit him and cut his face, but she pulls a weapon on him? A weapon. A gun. Her father's, stolen from his den. Hidden in her purse. Her security. "You ruined my life," she whispered, squeezing the trigger. A bullet whizzed past Uncle Mark's head, embedding itself in a tree just behind him. They were both stunned - by the fact that Angela had missed or that she had pulled the trigger at all, who can say? As Uncle Mark took another angry step toward her, she held the gun back up in his face. "If I hold my breath," she said to him, as well as to the voice in her head, "then I won't miss this time." A smile crossed her face. "If I hold my breath," she repeated, "then everything will be okay." Time seemed to stand still. Uncle Mark made a last, desperate grab for the gun. Angela, the scared little girl, wanted to run. Angel, the confident, fierce woman who wanted to avenge the scared little girl, wanted to put a bullet in between his eyes. Fortunately, this was the moment that the scared little girl took that one final step. The moment that the scared little girl faced the demons of her past and told them that she would run no longer. And Angel pulled the trigger.
