Recovery - Part 2

26 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
* * * Vic doubled over and threw up all over his feet. Behind him, Slade Wilson smiled an evil smile. "Wassamatter Vicky," Slade smirked as a bead of sweat ran from his grey crewcut. "Too much running for you?" Creed sucked in a breath, spat, and wiped the last bit of half-digested chicken from his chin. "No," he said, gulping air. "You're just a terrible cook." Running, walking, and sweating. Three things that hadn't stopped since Mitch Wilson dropped Kodiak Vic Creed off at the home of Slade Wilson, Mitch's father and Vic's former trainer. Slade wasted no time in getting Vic mobile - making him walk around the house, to the end of the driveway, and eventually down the forest path to the secluded section of oceanside beach. Once Vic could walk to the beach, he had to jog it. Once he could jog to the beach, he had to jog in the sand. For miles. Usually until he threw up. Then he'd have to run a few more miles. There were no rest breaks during Slade's training session. You rested when Slade decided to rest, or if you dropped dead. Unfortunately, Slade never seemed to get tired. At the end of every day, dropping dead felt like a very real option. They had just finished covering several miles of beach, running in about a foot of ocean water. Much like they'd done for the past several days, Slade Wilson pushed Creed past his limits. He wanted to break Vic down before rebuilding him. He'd work the injured heart, the lazy lungs, and try to sweat out the toxins. Over the first few days, Creed's sweat smelled like a garbage dump cooking under the hot sun from all the chemical impurities being purged from his body. Now he smelled like rank sweatsocks. It was a step in the right direction. Deep down, Slade felt close to the big Alaskan. The two shared almost a surrogate father/son relationship. He knew about Vic's upbringing and his determination to become not just a pro wrestler, but a successful pro wrestler. The boy was a natural in the ring, and had a hunger for wrestling much like his own son Mitch. Unfortunately, sometimes Vic got lazy and depended too much on his strength. The elder Wilson would educate Vic that one dimension could only take you so far. Slade opened his canteen and sipped at the water. "Save some of that moxy. Someone's coming to see you." "He's already here," a gravelly voice said from the nearby forest path. Creed looked over to the side as a gigantic blonde man stepped out from the shadowed path leading to the beach. "My God... Thor?" Thor Magnussun lumbered forward. "Long time no see, Victor. Let's see what you've learned." Thor was a powerlifter/strongman competitor from Iceland. He also used to train with Slade as more of a shoot wrestler. He wrestled overseas as the 'Icelandic Colossus'; a dangerous man with a reputation for hurting people in and out of the ring. "Oh Sh-" was all Vic could say as Thor speared Creed in the stomach. Both men flew into the surf, and Vic managed to throw up again. From there's things became a blur of action. Bodies flew around the beach (mostly Vic's body), blood was spilled (mostly Vic's blood), and at the end only one man remained standing. It wasn't Vic. "Thanks Thor," Slade said as he slapped the big man on the back. "It was fun," Thor grinned as he looked down at the bloody and near-unconscious Alaskan Juggernaut. "See you later Vic." "I hope you get hit by a bus," Creed spat as he looked up at the sky. Thor made his way up the path, disappearing into the trees. Remaining motionless, Creed looked over at the elder Wilson. "So what was that all about? Someone piss in your fiber this morning?" "He beat you," Slade stated matter-of-factly. "Well no shit he beat me! You had me run until I could barely move!" "So you didn't have the energy, the stamina to beat him. Is that your excuse?" asked Slade as he knelt down beside the bloody Creed. "I couldn't catch my breath, you bastard." Creed smeared blood from his lip with his thumb. "Why didn't you put him in a rest hold? Neutralize him on the ground until you got some energy back? You kept trying to lift and throw a man who was bigger and stronger than you are." "Gum me, old man." The truth was a brutal slap in the face for the Main Man. He'd been in unfamiliar territory, fighting someone who was actually stronger than he was. Slade stood up. "So what did we learn here?" Vic sat up, wiping a trickle of blood from his nose. "You tell me." Slade sipped his canteen of water again. "First - Your stamina is terrible. If you can make your opponent suck air, you've got him beat. You found that out the hard way. Second - There's always going to be bigger and stronger opponents out there. Just because you can't lift him over your head doesn't mean you can't beat him. Third - We've got a lot of work to do." Slipping his canteen back into his belt, Slade helped Vic to his feet. His hardened expression softened slightly. "Every time you fall down, I've been there to pick you back up, kick your butt, and send you on your way. But I won't be there forever, Vic. You and Mitch, you've been my greatest accomplishments. Don't let me down. This whole thing that's happened to you, I'm hoping you learn from it." For a brief moment, Vic felt that bond - the bond that he never shared with his own father. But as quickly as it came, the moment passed. "Now haul your ass up the hill!" ordered Slade, his drill sergeant demeanor returned. "You've had enough time to rest. We've got to get you back in the gym and in the sparring ring before you can even think of wrestling professionally again!" Once again, Vic started to run... but with a smile on his bruised face.
