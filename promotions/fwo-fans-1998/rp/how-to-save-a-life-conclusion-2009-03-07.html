How to Save a Life: Conclusion

7 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"So he was in the supply closet," said Eli Flair, "for three days?" "At least," replied Ivy McGinnis, "I couldn't leave him there; it's against my nature." They stood at the door to one of the three bedrooms in their Seattle condo that didn't have a permanent resident. Eli Flair was dressed casually in a plain black long sleeved T shirt and olive green cargo pants; Ivy McGinnis wore a deep red slipdress and matching sweater. She had been out with Sean Stevens, celebrating the successful defense of his Empire Pro Wrestling World Heavyweight Championship against The First. Between the match, the flight, and dinner, Sean was wiped out and sound asleep. This, of course, left Eli and Ivy to look at the newest arrival to their little corner of family: Lowell Dot Com. "What happens to him now?" asked Eli. "He was babbling about some man that was after him," replied Ivy, "once he wakes up--" "If he wakes up," interrupted Eli. Lowell had been mostly asleep since Wednesday night. He had gotten up twice since they brought him in, both times to use the bathroom. However, he had been down and out for over twenty four hours at this point. "He'll wake up," contiued Ivy. "hopefully he's just gonna sleep through any withdrawal symptoms and any DTs. I've never seen someone go through that and I'm happier for it." "Angel should be here for that," said Eli, "she and Teej helped his brother detox some years back." "How's everything back home?" asked Ivy. Eli's wife Angel was less than approving of his current return to wrestling; though she'd never say anything to either of them about it, Angel was the type of woman who wore her heart on her sleeve. Her occupation, lead singer of a rock band that defied classification, was aided by this, but her relationships often suffered. "MJ is enjoying school," replied Eli, "and Angel finally has most of the hardware set up in the basement. They don't have the recording technology up and running yet, but the soundproofing is done, so Valerian's Garden finally has official practice space on the east coast again." Ivy turned her attention back to Lowell. "You'd never guess he was living in the arena by the look of him, would you?" "Why did you bring him home, anyways?" She hesitated. "Like I said, it's my nature," said Ivy, "and it's the only thing I could think to do to pay you and Sean and Fox back." Eli cocked his head. "You three pretty much saved my life this winter," she explained, "by convincing me that I needed to face up to my fears and my insecurities in the FWO, and by pushing me to be who I am at the show. I've been thinking ever since William offered me the job, how can I repay you?" "Forget about it." "I can't. You guys did something incredible for me. The only thing I could do, since you and Sean are pretty together and Fox is getting the help he needs, is to put my energy toward helping save someone else's life." "Enter: Lowell," replied Eli. "Exactly." "I'm not saying you're not doing a really great thing for him," said Eli, "but you know, you don't owe anyone a thing. You've been Den Mother for the FWO for five years, easy, now. Any kind of advice or support anyone's given you has been a long time in the coming, and none of us are really even with you yet." "Shut up," replied Ivy, as he gaze returned to Lowell. He waved his hands in front of him like paws as he slept. "So what happens now?" Eli took a deep breath. "What?" asked Ivy. "What happens now," said Eli, "is that I say my goodbyes." Ivy's gaze snapped back to her best friend. "What are you talking about?" "My job's done, pookums," replied Eli, "it's time for me t'go home." "No," said Ivy, "Job's not over yet." "Yes, it is." "How? We were here to give William a big name boost, and we've only just scratched the surface." Eli chuckled. "See, that's what's called a lie." Ivy smacked him in the ribs. "Ouch!" said Eli, "Look, this company was going to come back with a vengeance whether we were here or not, Ivy. Pierre was back, Jack was back, Ric was working in the office, Zimmerman and Wakeman were back, Shadows was back, and so forth. They even dug Mike Bear up outta the mothballs and he's been kickin' ass. They didn't need us." "So why were we here?" asked Ivy. Her voice cracked a bit. "For you," said Eli, "Because I knew you were stuck on Countdown '05 and needed to get shocked back into action. I just didn't think you'd walk into something nearly every week." For a moment, Eli thought Ivy was going to cry. She took her glasses off and rubbed one of her eyes, but just as quickly replaced them, and, with a smirk on her face, hugged him tightly. "You're an asshole," she said. "Love you too, dear," replied Eli. "So what's the plan, then?" "I got my big goodbye back in 2005 after Deacon took the belt from me," said Eli, "thought I'd just come to reAction on Wednesday, wish the boys the best'a luck, and just slip out the back like I was never there." "You know," said Ivy, as she let him go, "you have this grand delusion of being the Ninja that nobody recognizes, but everyone knows you were there, and everyone appreciates what you've done for the sport in general and the FWO in particular. Sneaking out ain't gonna be as easy as you think." "Just watch me." "Do you really think Sean or me will let you do that?" He thought about it for a second, and his attention went back to Lowell. "Can't I just send him in my place?" Ivy laughed. "I think he'd need at least twenty minutes preparation to do an Eli Flair impression. Try tomorrow?" "Bitch." "Dick." "Cunt." "Fuckhead." "I'm gonna see you guys all the time," said Eli, "but it won't be the same, will it?" Ivy turned away from the door and hugged him with one arm around his waist. "It never is, man. The time spent in this business is priceless, and there's no substitute." "Good," replied Eli, as the two walked toward the kitchen, "cause I wouldn't've traded it for anything." And I would have stayed up with you all night Had I known how to save a life
