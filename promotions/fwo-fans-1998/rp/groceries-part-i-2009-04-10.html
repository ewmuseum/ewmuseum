Groceries (part I)

10 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Somewhere off in the distance, a wolf howled. Siobahn McPhineas thumbed the button that set off the ‘beep’ and electronically locked their SUV. She gave a quick questioning glance to The Assistant and, satisfied by the nod she received in return, headed inside the store just before the clouds opened up again – spring in Seattle was rainy, after all. As a rule no one in their group went out alone and Siobahn and The Assistant found themselves paired often for these domestic chores. Mr. Reed was far, far too engrossed in his research. Mr. Yip was as stubborn as a heartsick elephant. And Mr. Werner… well… he was indisposed this evening. Groceries and other items were needed on a regular basis and as the de facto leader of this gathering of oddities it fell upon her shoulders. She smiled. The rain felt good upon her face, The Assistant (who was as close to a best friend as she’d ever had) was with her, and she knew what was expected of her tonight – bring home the groceries. Things had fallen into a sense quaint little sense of routine for their entourage; Fairview Reed would fight in a match on Wednesday nights and Burlington Werner would patch him up directly afterwards. Mr. Reed would then return to his library, exiting only when The Assistant insisted he work out in the home gymnasium… Mr. Werner would be in the garage most likely, his nervous energy directed to dissecting engines and putting them back together in an almost surgical manner… Mr. Yip would be squirreled away in his attic kingdom for the most part (which, in all honestly in a house like this, was rather expansive and an absolutely PERFECT fit for him) but Siobahn had glimpsed Mr. Yip and Mr. Reed in deep conversation in the library from time to time. Siobahn had the run of the house and sometimes felt so alone she despaired of intimate human contact again… Steering the bulky metal cart was still awkward to her. She was more accustomed to the quaint, quiet personal markets of County Kilarney, not these soulless concrete and tile monstrosities. She sighed and pulled out her paper shopping list – old habits died hard. She took a moment to formulate the best plan of attack and turned to The Assistant. The Assistant’s attention was clearly focused elsewhere. Siobahn knew damned well not to interrupt her while she was in this state. The best she could do was stand by and wait…
