Faithful Questions

18 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"We all have our part to do." Deacon said to the middle-aged Jewish man named Tsion. Tsion's hair had seen better days, his eyes also. He wore bi-focals to supplement the reading. He didn't wear anything to help the hair. He had been wise enough to know that issue was a lost cause. Deacon handed him a drink of water before moving on. "Thank you," Tsion said with his middle eastern dialect. He had spent all day under the hot sun, and like many from the fWo, he was trying to do his part to dig out any survivors to the blast. This was, after all, his job. He had often thought of how sad a situation this was. His country had so many bombs and tragedies on this scale and of this type that it had turned into a regular gig to pay the bills. Deacon moved on down the line, giving water to the next person working in the rubble. "How's your shoulder?" The old man asked. Deacon smiled a knowing grin and the man responded with the same. The old man took his water, downed it in record time, and handed the plastic cup back to Deacon. Deacon moved on down the line. Deacon spent all of Monday working on the site, not rescuing people or saving the day like he does in the ring, but giving some water to the REAL heroes in his presence. A few days prior, he had written a letter to Chad Merritt, complaining about how this land had lost their fervor for God. A few days later, he had seen that fervor for some god kill nearly a hundred people and injure plenty more. Deacon had also seen that love of God come out of people to help, many of them risking their own lives in an effort to save others. "You ready?" Chris slipped up behind him. "No," Deacon began. He exhaled heavily before continuing. "But I will," he said as he thought about how things had changed from a few short days ago. I saw a young boy while I rambled around the city today. He was amazed by my size, and it's fairly commonplace to get mobbed when I go into the streets. I was amazed by the boys energy in spite of obviously not being well-fed. That's the toughest part of this, we've visited so many lands where so many people are struggling not to pay for their cable tv but for their very lives. And I know I have so much... and I know that there is nothing I can do... and I wonder where God is in all this mess? I know better, wiser men than myself has asked the same question, and I know the answer... it's just sometimes the questions scream a whole lot louder than the whispering answer. As he left the scene of such destruction, Deacon knew that he was leaving a holy land, not because of what happened there almost 2 millenia ago, but because of what was happening there today. Was God responsible for such unspeakable evil? Deacon knew that answer. Was God shown in the response? Deacon knew that answer as well, and that was all the evidence that he needed to put his faith in a God in heaven above. God was still in control, and he would be in control as every person moved forward. On the side of the street, a young woman put a flower near the arena. Tears flowed freely down her face as Deacon walked by. Deacon had seen such destruction... and then such loving response. God was there, but as Deacon stepped into his car to go to the airport, he took a glance back at this woman, and he was left without the answer to the most basic question
