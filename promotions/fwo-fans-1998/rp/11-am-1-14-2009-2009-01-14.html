fWo — Roleplay archive

11 AM, 1/14/2009

14 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Doesn't seem real, does it?" Eli Flair leaned against the corner, his elbows resting on the top rop on either side of the turnbuckle, and he stretched his feet out in front of him. He was dressed in his street clothes, and was staring at the house lights far above his head. "How so?" asked Ivy. She was walking the perimeter of the ring, taking in the familar viewpoint of the mat - at - eye - level. "Did you ever think either of us would be active members of a roster again?" asked Eli. "You?" asked Ivy, "Not a chance. Me? Don't forget Sean is still thriving everywhere he goes, it's not unheard of that he might ask me to come in somewhere at some point." Eli nodded. "Point." He pushed himself forward and walked to the opposite corner, and, in one fluid motion, turned around and vaulted himself to a sitting position on the top turnbuckle. "One major difference with me, though." "No fear, no pressure," answered Ivy, "I'd have to say, the fact that we're not here to win the belt again is mentally liberating in a twisted kinda way." "Hey," replied Eli, as he hopped to the mat and leaned over to grab her hand and pull her (rather effortlessly) to the ring apron, "the point is to give the company whatever name power we can at the outset. If Beaven and Huph want me to take a belt for a bit, then I'm playin' ball. If they want me to spent the next six months putting the rookies over, same deal." "You know, you give too much." "It's my worst failing." He smirked at her as he backed up so she could enter the ring. "Seriously though, Ivy... our legacy is pretty well entrenched. Ain't no reason to start making demands now, is there?" "I never said you'd need to go Diva, now did I?" "You were thinking it." "...You never actually went diva during your career, so we'd be justified, now, wouldn't we?" "Blasphemy." "It's nice to see some things don't change," said a new voice from the floor. Eli and Ivy turned at the same time, since the voice was a familiar one. "Phil!" cried Ivy, as she ducked between the ropes and jumped into the middle aged man, her arms around his neck, "How are you? Where did you come from? What hasn't changed?" Phil Haggan smiled as he held Ivy by the waist so as to not A: drop her, and B: break his neck holding her up. "You're the first ones here today. You two were always the first ones at the arena before a show. And I work for the fWo, remember?" "I thought you worked for Disney," replied Ivy. "True," said Phil, as he put her down, "but I got a call from Mr. Hupfer a few weeks ago with a job offer, mainly due to several notations in my e-file from former commissioner McGinnis." Ivy's face reddened. "So thank you, Ms. McGinnis," replied Phil, "the time I worked in the fWo locker room was some of the most fun I'd had at Disney, and I'm glad to be doing it on the books from this point on." "It's good to see you," said Ivy, as she hugged him again, "Eli, you remember Phil Haggan, right?" "More or less," answered Eli, as he leaned over the top rope and shook Haggan's hand, "Good to see you, sir." "Likewise," said Phil, "Listen, busy day as you can imagine. I'll see you both later." "Bye!" said Ivy, as he exited. She turned back to the ring, and Eli, who was leaning on the top rope again. "Is it a rookie mistake that I've got a really, really good feeling about things this time?" "Naaah," replied Eli, as he turned around, hooked his arm over the top, and fell backwards over the top rope and landed on his feet, "I think it comes of having nothing to prove." They started towards the locker rooms as well. "Nothing to prove, huh?" she asked. "Nothing to prove," repeated Eli. "Then don't do the backwards flip tonight," replied Ivy, "I don't know how I'd explain to Angel exactly how you cracked your head open." "I don't miss, you nonbelieving bitch." "Dick." "Twatwaffle." "Cuntbunny." "We're supposed to be the mature veterans, aren't we?" "Eh, maturity's overrated."
