Recovery - Epilogue

26 April 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
* * * Kodiak Vic Creed smiled as the walked down the paved path. He passed under the branch of an overhanging tree, stopping to take a deep breath of fresh air. The almost tart smell of wood bark hung in his nostrils as he continued on his way. A layer of freshly fallen snow blanketed the ground, causing the sun to sparkle from both above and below him as it shimmered off the icy flakes. A beautiful landscape. If it weren't for the tombstones, you'd think it was a park rather than a cemetery. Vic stepped off the path, cutting through several plots to reach the far corner of the cemetery. Off to the side, all by itself, sat one lone plot. A simple tombstone poked out of the snow like a cold, grey thumb. Vic looked town at the tombstone. An eerie calm fell over him as he read the name across the front. Victor Creid. "Now I know how Scrooge felt," said Creed. "Bah humbug. How could people think I'd be buried in a plot in the middle of nowhere? I'd have a shrine built, a big tomb. Kings had them, pharaoh's had them, and I could kick any royalty's ass." "But here I am," Vic said to the tombstone, "and there you are. Your time is over. I wonder what you thought about in your last minutes? Did you think about the pain you were in? How unbearable your suffering was? I heard that you screamed like a bitch, begging for morphine." "Maybe you thought back to the past. Maybe you had a hint of regret for things you did, people you hurt. You messed up a lot of people, Victor Creid. You left scars that'll never go away. There's some wounds that time just won't heal." Vic stared at the tombstone, almost waiting for it to reply. "Guess the silver lining to your reign of terror and mayhem is that you're dead, and nobody gives a damn. I heard your wife is already seeing someone else. She's not wasting any time in getting on with her life. Probably had more sex in the weeks that you've been gone than she did for years while you were alive." "Your kids damn sure don't miss you. After all you put them through: the insults, the put-downs, not to mention the beatings. It always had to be your way. Hell would freeze over before you told them, or even showed them, that you gave a damn about them. You told them they'd never amount to anything if they didn't do things your way. You were always right, weren't you?" Vic cleared his throat, hocking a large phlegm gob at the tombstone. It landed with a splat, slowly running down the front before starting to freeze. It looked like a nasty scar across the front of the polished, smooth stone. "Well screw you, old man. I've amounted to more than you ever did. I'm the Main Man, and I've proved it time and time again. I'll prove it for years to come. You? You're just fertilizer. Worm food. I hope they choke on you, Dad." The anger began to subside in Creed's eyes. He sucked in another deep breath, spun on his heels, and exhaled a frosty white cloud. "Ah, it's great to be alive," Vic said as he strolled back toward the path. He lifted his face to the sun, feeling it's warm embrace. "Sucks to be you, Dad. Sucks to be you."
