Family Values - Chapter Three

30 June 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
December 24, 1991 Spencer, Nebraska 4:22 PM, local time "Angela, will you come out here for a moment?" Fifteen year old Angela Davison was always a bit shy. She had one or two friends in school, she felt her parents didn't understand her, she liked her English and History classes, not math, Phys Ed, or Biology. She left her room and headed down the hall to the living room - catching a momentary glimpse of herself in the closet door mirrors. Mostly, she likes what she sees when she looks at herself - but her skin is too pale, and her chest is too small. According to her, at least. But her jeans make her butt look good and her "Guns 'n' Roses" baby tee shows off her flat stomach. "Coming, Mom!" she called. Angela ran a hand through her red-gold hair, following the familiar hallway to the front door. She saw her father, Henry, walking in the door, shaking out the cold and the snow, with an unfamiliar man next to him. Her mother had given him a big hug as well, and he gave a wide smile toward Angela. "My God," he said, astonished, "She's gotten so big, hasn't she?" "Angela," said Henry, "This is my brother, your Uncle Mark. You don't remember him, do you?" Angela shook her head, looking somewhat confused. Her mother, Helen, laughed, "That's not surprising, dear. You haven't seen him since you were three years old." "Hello, Angela," said Mark, holding out his hand, "It's been a long time." After a moment's hesitation, she shook it. "Go get dressed," urged Henry, "We're going to dinner with Mark and then we're going to church this evening." "Your uncle is a permanent Deacon at his church in Denver," added Helen. "Alright," replied Angela. She liked church - not so much the constant standing/sitting/kneeling, and the way the priest talks at everyone instead of to them... but she loves the music and she loves the moments of silence. "Hurry," repeated her father, "You know how packed Ronny's gets at dinnertime." She disappeared back into her room, quickly changing into a white blouse and wool gray skirt. Taking a few quick swipes with her brush, she decided against makeup. It's not really her thing. Besides, her family is waiting.
