Reacting to reAction - Ground Zero in T-Minus One

5 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
So why did you do it, I asked Miss Ivy. "Do what," she said. We sat in the covered porch behind the rented condo - one of the few in the complex. I was glad it was covered, because it's been raining since we got here. Of course it is, it's Seattle. Why did you set Killjoy up for that handicap match, if you can call it that, at Cyberslam, I asked her. She didn't answer right away. "It's complicated," said Miss Ivy. No, I said, don't do that. You've spent the past six years telling me things are complicated when you didn't want to explain them, and I need some answers. "Not these," said Miss Ivy, "You don't need these answers and you don't want 'em." So you do know who's wearing the mask, I said. She reached to the floor and picked up a nearly empty glass of scotch, and took a sip. I drank from my can of beer, never really could get used to the harder stuff. "I'm not really sure who's wearing the mask," said Miss Ivy, "but I don't want you to have to deal with the person pulling the strings." That's not really answering my question, I said. Why did you put him in that match at Cyberslam? She didn't answer - again. I've never seen Miss Ivy this stressed out by anything before, and it made me really sorry that I brought it up to begin with. I've got a match with him tomorrow, I said, so it's part of my reality. I've been studying him incessantly, and I've been listening to what he's said, and I think he's got the right idea, but going about it wrong. Miss Ivy's eyes drifted to mine. "You think so," she said. It's obvious he cares about this company, I said, and a lot more than any of us can really admit to. The problem for me starts with him taking anyone in his way, whether they really are or just happen to be his opponents for the night, and treating them like they set fire to the FWO back in 2005. It was a sore spot, but if I can't talk brutally honest with Miss Ivy, who can? "Knox," said Miss Ivy, "you're new to the FWO, and you've got a lot less time under your belt than most of the roster, even the newcomers. There's stuff that happens here that doesn't happen anywhere else, not even in Craig's NFW--" It's actually President Mayfield's NFW now, I corrected her. "Damn," said Miss Ivy, "that's even worse. But regardless, please trust me on this. In a few months things might be different and I think you'd be a real asset in this case, but for now, please just wrestle your match, don't raise his ire, and come out of it in one piece, okay?" Don't raise his ire, I asked, Did you see how he dismantled Tony? "Tony is kind of a pain in the ass," said Miss Ivy, "please don't be like Tony." That's not the point, I said. Tony Davis didn't do anything to Killjoy, and he got taken apart in record time. I haven't done anything to Killjoy, but he sought me out for a match which means if he gets his way, I'm already stepped in it. She looked at me. "Don't say it," said Miss Ivy, "I hate it when you do this." I smirked. If Killjoy knows who I am already, what's the point in getting quiet? I should get louder and make sure he never forgets my name. Miss Ivy shook her head and rubbed the right side of her face with her hand. "Just don't come back crippled," said Miss Ivy, "There's no way I'd be able to explain that to your mom." Don't worry, I said. I've got a plan.
