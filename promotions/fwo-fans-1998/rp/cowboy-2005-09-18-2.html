Cowboy

18 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A few years had passed since Olivre's first cowboy experience, and he found his new residence in a smaller town in Texas. His aspirations to become a cowboy were the same, but his surroundings had greatly decreased in size and quality. His families' move was his bidding in the first place because he hoped he could uncover a lost western society un-tampered by the modern world. What he found disappointed him. He hoped to find tumbleweeds, saloons, and saddled horses but instead found a lot of the norm; stray garbage, ATM machines, and automobiles. Nevertheless, his quest to be a cowboy continued as he was roped into a ranch hand job on a local farm. He always continued to hope that one day he could be a cowboy. That day came in the summer of 1993... "The last few years you've spent with us have been heaven-sent," The wrinkled rancher gushed at Olivre, who was towing a heavy bag of fertilizer over his shoulder. He nodded in response and allowed the satisfied rancher to continue. "It seemed only yesterday that I met you for the first time. You said you wanted to be a cowboy." His crescent smile at the recollection ceased as he became distracted by Braley's predicament; he was fighting to keep the bag balanced on his beefy shoulder. "Just--Just put that down a minute, Ollie. I thought that it was foolish at the time, but I promised me that if you would work hard for me I would give you the chance. You've done everything I've asked when I asked it, and to cut a long speech short, I'm gonna give you the opportunity to be a cowboy...a traditional cowboy... starting today." "To be a cowboy?!" Olivre repeated, trying to hold back his delight. When the rancher gave his nod of affirmation, Braley lunged out and hugged him tightly. "Thank y so much, sir!" "Don't thank me yet," the rancher warned, "It ain't as glamorous as you'd think. You might not like it." "Oh, I'm sure I will," Olivre shot back confidently, "I'm ready to ride on out there on the open range saving damsels in distress and ropin' bandits." The rancher stopped a moment to laugh hysterically. His eyes began to water like a newly tapped faucet, his tears pure and free-flowing off his prickly jaw. "Bandits?!~?"
