Post- Slaughter Drinking

19 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Toledo, Ohio Monday, May 19, 2003 12:15 AM The bar was like any other in the world. Smoke- filled with dim lighting, muffled conversation, the clink of a game of billiards, and the soft sounds of pub rock - right now, 'Roadhouse Blues' by The Doors - the only sounds. Most of the dwellers stopped what they were doing as the two new ones entered. The regulars stared at the cute blonde girl with the nice ass but didn't dare approach her with the mountain of a man standing behind her. The women admired the mountain of a man, but the cute blonde girl with the nice ass looked like she itched for a fight. Consequently, the two found a seat in the corner and fell/collapsed into the chairs. The waitress was over, post- haste, to take their order. "Whiskey," said Poison Ivy, holding out a measurement with her hands, "and whiskey." "Beer," added Eli Flair, "Red Stripe if ya got it, Molsen Golden if ya don't." As the waitress left earshot, Eli stretched his arms high above his head and cracked his knuckles, his wrists, his elbows, his neck. "Another show, another match, another payoff." "Congratulations," replied Ivy, "You're on a roll - and you're getting your rep back as a bar- none workhorse." It was that moment that the waitress returned with the drinks, and Team Extreme toasted continued success. As Ivy drank, however, Eli simply stared into the bottle. "It's not gonna be enough, is it?" She hesitated. "I didn't think so," he finished, "Goddamnitt." "It's not as bad as you think," reassured Ivy, but the words were empty. She knew very well that it was as bad as he thought. Minimum guaranteed money plus nightly payoffs gave Eli an annual salary in the high six figures, subtract transportation, lodging, food, as normal wrestler expenses on the road. Subtract supplies and pain medication for nightly 'homemade surgery' in the locker rooms. Subtract a hospital visit following the Asylum's MIND GAMES event, and remember that Eli didn't get paid for that fight. Subtract the cost of the Garden's touring after their record label dropped them, without paying for any of it. Subtract the cost of a recording studio and payments on a Los Angeles condo. What's left? Subtract the cost of a four month old daughter. Subtract Angel's contract with Artemis Records. "I'm so fucking close to landing Angel a deal," said Ivy, taking a long swig of whiskey in the silence, "but it's not gonna fly unless the album rules the world." "It will," replied Eli. "I know that, you know that, and the boys know that," countered Ivy, "but I think Angel could use convincing. And I think you and I need to come to a realization as far as the time between then and now goes." "What's that?" "You need to make more money." He drained his beer, the bottle clanging on the table as he dropped it down. "Only one way to do that, and it's been made clear to me in the past that I'm not welcome in the main event level matches in the fWo. Ain't no way I can survive night after night like Cyberslam, either. Got a plan C?" "Correction," remarked Ivy, "You and the other guy didn't work well together. New administration runnin' the show now, what could it hurt to just ask the man for a shot?" Eli thought about it. Nothing to lose, true... but the stories he'd heard from Brandon about the Alaskan Juggernaut didn't make him eager to approach the subject. On the other hand, sometimes all it takes is one thing to get between two men, no matter how small, and the distaste they have for each other makes the stories grow another leg. "Speak'a the devil," said Ivy as her gaze drifted over Eli's shoulder. She suddenly shot up out of her seat and made a beeline for - for what? Eli turned around and saw her heading for a table on the opposite wall... and immediately followed her when he saw who was sitting there. 'Kodiak' Vic Creed and 'Nasty' Nick Brandish, sharing a pitcher of beer, engrossed in some kind of conversation. Neither man noticed the blonde ball of attitude headed in their direction, but Ivy isn't the type to do things quietly. He just hoped-- why was he even worried? Creed and Brandish were two of the boys... and two professionals, to boot. Never had a problem with them in the past, barely talked to them in the past. "Hiya, Vic, Nick," said Ivy with a laugh as she leaned on the wall next to the two. "McGinnis," acknowledged Creed, not looking at her. "Now that we're all buddies," continued Ivy, not missing a beat, "you guys busy?" "Yeah," replied Creed, "Don't" --he looked at her to see he was too late-- "sit down." She looked over both men, sizing them up. It's a unique skill Ivy has, she's usually pretty accurate at determining exactly what it is her targets want, and dealing as much of it as she can without compromising what she's looking to get. "Congratulations on a good run so far," she said to Creed, "It's a welcome change." "Thanks," replied the Main Man, uninterested. "Down to it, I wanted to know what the odds were that you'd be interested in a few matches with the King of Extreme himself, Eli Flair." As if on cue, Eli got to the table at that exact moment. "Sorry 'bout that, gentlemen," he said, "Ivy... the time and place for business is at the arena... not in a bar. I don't feel like talking business right now, Creed doesn't look like he wants'ta be talking business right now... let's leave it for the show." Ivy didn't move. Her gaze remained on Creed, unwavering. "How 'bout it?" Vic Creed polished off the remaining half pint in his glass, and poured himself another one. "Y'got balls, McGinnis, will give ya that. And you, Flair - you've impressed me a few times. Even stomached your little wife's music. But you don't want the belt, trust me on that." Eli's eyes narrowed. "I could handle it, Creed." "Oh, I'm sure," retorted Creed, "Trust me, Flair... you don't want any part'a the belt." He downed the pint in one gigantic gulp, and motioned for Nick Brandish to follow him out of the bar. Creed nodded slightly toward Flair and Ivy as he passed them, in condescention or respect, nobody knew... and headed out of the pub. "Well, there goes that plan," said Eli, smirking. He headed back to he and Ivy's table. Ivy stood there for a moment, swirling her drink around her glass. "Oh ye of little faith," she said, just loud enough for nobody to hear her, "we've only just begun to fight."
