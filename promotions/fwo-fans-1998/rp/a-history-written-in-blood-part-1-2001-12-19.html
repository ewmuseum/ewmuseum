A History Written in Blood, part 1

19 December 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The elegant swish of silk in the low light caught the camera's attention and it followed the hypnotic movement to find Tempest swaying to a subdued electronic rhythm. She wore a short magenta dress decked with fringe that parted in a wide swath across her middle, which rippled with soft muscle as she danced. She sipped at a glass of some exotic looking orange drink as she looked down from the raised suite she was in onto the dance floor below with an expression filled with yearning. She turned as the door opened and Kristen Jacobs walked in. Jacobs seemed amused at finding her there and said, "Well, you seem to be doing better than I would have thought. Hanging out in the clubs once again?" Her tone was mildly accusatory, but not overly so, and still mostly curious. Tempest smiled wanly and sat down on a leopard print covered chaise lounge, motioning her toward another in covered in pink polka dots. "I love to dance," Tempest said. "It's an escape, and a way to burn off energy too." She glanced back toward the balcony and said, "But I'd really rather be down there, with all the people. In Hong Kong I would save up money for weeks just to have enough to go uptown and dance, just to get lost in that sea of people. No one knew who I was and even I could forget for a little while. Now I don't want to be there too long for fear someone will recognize me." Jacobs looked at her and said, "Because of your past or because of what you're doing to Eddy Fate? Which is it that you're more ashamed of?" Tempest's head jerked to face Jacobs and her gaze was piercing as she spoke in a rapid, clipped tone and said, "I have nothing to be ashamed of as far as Eddy is concerned. I did what I had to do to try and keep myself and my career intact and that's what I'll continue to do. If it means I have to win back people's respect over his broken back, so be it. And if he's who I think he is..." Her eyes narrowed and she said, "Then he's much less innocent than you and everyone else thinks." She sat back and took a deep breath as Jacobs tried to regroup and said cautiously, "That may be. I don't know. But people used to think you were a lot more innocent too Tempest." She tried to sound reassuring and said, "I know that whatever happened has you scared about your future. But without your background I'm not sure the fans understand why. That's why you should do this, come clean and tell them the truth." Tempest looked at Jacobs and seemed to be sizing her up for a moment before saying, "Are you sure? Will they want to hear the ugly parts? I'm not sure they want to understand me anymore." Jacobs shook her head and said, "They do. A lot of the questions I'm emailed every day and find in letters from fans and are asked by correspondents are about you. People want to make sense of it. And Tempest, they can forgive a lot. I'm not sure that whatever it is you're so ashamed of would be so bad to an audience outside Asia." Tempest nodded and said, "That's true. I've thought about that, about what it would be like to have been raised in a country where they just pay women less instead of drowning them at birth." Jacobs blinked, taken aback for a moment and said, "Um, exactly. So why don't you start at the beginning?" She looked at Tempest and sat back, clicking on a little tape recorder, where Tempest could see it. Tempest sighed and said, "Well, the beginning is easy. I was born..." She chuckled. "I was born to a nice mother and father who lived in Hong Kong. My dad was British and my mom was Chinese. I don't know a lot of their story because my mother died when I was a baby. So my dad raised me alone. At least until they killed him." Her eyes clouded over and the loss seemed fresh upon her face. "Who killed him?" Jacobs asked, shocked. Tempest's eyes blinked rapidly, holding back tears, but at the same time it seemed as though she were weighing the question, and deciding how to answer. "When I was nine, my father was gunned down in the street. It was a bright sunny afternoon and I stood there holding his hand, while people just stared. I remember screaming and crying and no one came for a long time, until the police came and took him away. No one would help. And I didn't know why for a long time." Jacobs stared aghast and said, "Well, why?! I mean that's inhuman, what were they thinking? It..." She was cut off by Tempest's sharp laughter. "I told you it wasn't a pretty story", she said, toying with her glass, her expression volleying between a sanguine smile and the grimace of pain that it was so close to. "My father was a doctor. And he treated the poor in Hong Kong. In a lot of cases that meant opium addicts. He was able to help them, see them through withdrawal and return them to health. But there were certain people who didn't like that." "You mean, the dealers," Jacobs said with a touch of resignation. Tempest nodded and said, "Yes, but they're not exactly like dealers in the states. They're much more organized. They're part of a larger organization and this one happened to be one of the deadliest, a triad known as the Ghost Arrows." The utterance of the name caused her shoulders to tense up and set her eyes in a hard stare. "And these Ghost Arrows killed your father?" Jacobs asked. Tempest nodded and said, "Yes, and no one dared do anything about it. They had too much power. It took me years to discover all this though and I spent most of those years starving on the streets of Hong Kong. I couldn't even get into an orphanage, because everyone knew that I was hated by the Arrows and didn't want to draw that attention on themselves." Jacobs shook her head and tried to digest this as she said, "But how did you survive? And why didn't the Ghost Arrows kill you too? Was it because you were a child?" Tempest chuckled although it was a bitter sound and said, "Oh they would kill children, that wasn't it. The let me live because it was better to watch me suffer. That way I was a constant reminder to anyone who might make trouble for them, of what the results would be." She stared off into the flashing lights a moment and said, "But I survived the way all little street urchins survive. I stole, I begged from tourists too ignorant to know better and eventually I fell in with other elements of the city that existed on that level." Jacobs looked horrified and said, "You became one of them, a member of one of these triads." Tempest snorted and shook her head as she said, "I can see there's a lot of explaining left to do. To say I was a member of a triad is like saying that a guppy in an aquarium full of fish, in the home of a large family, is a member of that family." For a long moment she stared straight ahead and then her lips moved as if trying to speak but nothing came out. She cleared her throat and tried again saying, "They owned me, just like a pet. Or a dish, to be discarded when broken." ...to be continued
