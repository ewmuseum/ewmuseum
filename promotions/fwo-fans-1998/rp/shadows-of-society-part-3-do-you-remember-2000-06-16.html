Shadows of Society: Part 3, Do You Remember?

16 June 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June Thirteenth, 2001 Time: 2:17PM Eastern Standard Time Location: Mary Winters' Burial Place "As memory may be a paradise from which we cannot be driven, it may also be a hell from which we cannot escape." -John Lancaster Newman The cemetery had beautiful rolling green hills, dotted with grey and white tombstones. Many people of the Detroit suburbs liked to use the cemetery as a route during a daily jog. Families would come every day to put flowers in front of a loved one's tombstone. But on this day the sky was overcast a dark grey, almost black. The threat of rain looked inevitable, the cemetery for once was desolate, not even a groundskeeper or two were in sight. It seemed as if only one living human was lurking in the cemetery. Donaven Winters stood with his head down, his shoulders hunched up as he looked upon a gravestone that had been broken in half by teenagers getting off by vandalizing the memorials for those who have passed on to a ‘better life.' A small scowl had formed on his face as he kicked some of the slate rubble aside. He slowly brought a hand to his face, brushing his hair out of his face. As his eyes narrowed, they focused on the busted tombstone. "Here lies," he started through his teeth. "Mary Ann Winters. A great girl with a great heart. 1959-1985, R.I.P" Donaven scuffed and kicked dust at the tombstone. After closing his eyes, a small smile crept onto his face and he spit on the stone. "That's what you treated be like, mom," he scowled. "You treated my like dirt, like spit. Nothing more than trash. You and dad both. The daily beatings, the hell both of you put me through. Do you remember the homeless shelter? Do you remember the roaches? How unclean that place was? The water that looked like it had gone through Detroit's septic systems many times over. Whenever we went out in public you lied to grocery clerks, you told all of them what a great kid I was. And then you took me home, took all of those drugs and then beat me. Our family was the bottom feeding, scum sucking humans that lived off of the state." He slowly turned his back to his mother's grave, his head was lowered in disgust. "You pretended to love me," he whispered harshly. "You pretended to be happy that I was yours, but when it was you, dad and I, you were a bitter, drug addict bitch. You and dad used to argue all the time and then would beat me, then you'd throw me into my room and lock the door. For days at times, I was only five years old, you locked me all alone, no food, no way to use the bathroom. Do you remember any of this? Do you really know what you've done to me?" For a moment tears ran down Donaven face and he angrily kicked the tombstone as he turned back around. "You wanted me to die," he shouted. "You would have left me there for months if dad's drinking buddies had never shown up. If your whore friends hadn't shown up, wanting to see how cute and adorable I was. And you showed me off like I was some sort of prize. Like a proud hunter showing off the head of a mounted twelve-point buck, you made me look like the greatest thing ever. You lied through your teeth and then went and got stoned. Dad would come home drunker than hell and then you two would have sex for hours, leaving me there, sometimes right in front of you. Telling me to shut up, not to move. Now, sixteen years later, look what you've done to me, all of these thoughts won't go away. You've damaged me, killed everything good I could have been. And I should thank you. I should thank you because it's your special day? Well then, I'll give you my thanks. Mom, thank you for turning me into a reject, into this– human waste, this waste of flesh and bone. And most importantly... Donaven stared at his mother's name that was etched into the gravestone. "Happy fucking birthday."
