Chosen...but proven?

21 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Finally accomplished something...and she's entirely oblivious to the fact." Renee Williams, the young woman who'd grown up basically mothering Aimz had always played the role of best friend, which she was glad to since both girls were the same age and almost entirely identical in tastes. In this instance, though, the 'mothering' side had kicked in as the blonde tipped her head towards another mutual friend of Amy's and spoke, both comrades watching a flurry of pink-tinged red hair spin around...and around...and around as Aimz spun herself on a small office chair. "...but that's just Amy for ya." Renee and the man, an old friend who'd played for some of their favorite local bands since they were all in their early teens, had been talking for quite some time and staring at her all the while, Amy'd noted. He was tall and laiden with spikes and chains, his clothing not dissimilar to her chosen attire of camouflage pants big enough to swim in and a tiny West 49 t-shirt, promoting the Canadian "skater" store and clothing line. When she slowed the motion of the chair to a final stop, and shortly after the room stopped spinning around, churning the pit of her stomach, the redhead hopped up to her feet and smiled at both friends before her. "Whatcha' saying?" Renee sighed and smiled, chuckling a little. "Nothing, nothing." After looking at the blond and then to her practical brother, Ryan, carefully studying their faces, Amy's took on an urgent expression. She'd sensed that, once again, someone had something to say to her, some extra 'wisdom' or direction that they'd assume would help. "No, really...what were you saying?" After a moment, the previously silent partner in all their 'crime' kicked in, brandishing an unusual grin as he looked at the blank stare and little sparkle in Amy's eyes. "We were just talking about how kinda down to earth you are. Do you even realize how well your name sells? If someone writes something involving your name, people read it." "No." Aimz muttered, "Writers sell lies, not me." "Maybe, but you're a practical icon now..." He retorted. "There are girls out there who want to be like you." The redhead just laughed and shrugged it off. "And what's there to emulate? I dress to be comfortable and say what I think...there's not much to want to be. The people I used to idolize did the exact same thing, and they were a whole lot better at it." "You don't have enough condfidence!" Renee interrupted and patted her on the back as they began a trek outside of the little brick room, out into the crowded hallway of The Pavillion, a little "hardcore" all-ages club they'd known well for years. " "No...I just don't like being 'special'. Unique and individual, fine...but I'm no trend-setter, and I'm not worth treating differently. That's why I like being home...people just know me for being me, not being on TV. They treat me the same as they always did." That night, they were set to watch a band brought from halfway across the country, a punk act dubbed 'Choke', a logo that frequently adorned Aimz' chest on hoodies and shirts. She was an avid fan of the band...that's what she saw as icons. She didn't see herself as important or vital to entertainment as those people even though she'd met them, drank with them and actually given them a place to stay overnight when their small 'tours' brought them to town. Even though they weren't on TV, didn't make nearly as much money as she did, and probably wouldn't get recognized on the street for autographs, it wasn't important...what mattered to her was the heart of the people, and what they spilled out in their music. Wrestling didn't give her any real form of expression, so why should she be held in high regard? Only her 'style' and occasional words really said anything about her for people to see... What was the point? Simple: People in the world right now desperately need something to direct them, someone to show them a successful way of being. It was just like she needed Darcy, or the music she listened to. Unfortunately, Amy was a little too down to earth, niave even. She'd didn't seem to have the confidence enough to understand the full power of her reach. She didn't think she'd proven anything yet...but what did she have to prove? Everything, and the outside world had everything to prove to her before she'd trust it. {Sorry for chopiness, needed to get some thoughts out. Had very little significance to the 'story', but the ending works in.}
