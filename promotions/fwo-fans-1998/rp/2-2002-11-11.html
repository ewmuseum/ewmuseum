Previous Parts: downfall archive Algebraic equations and idle lectures. A symphony of pencils dragging across paper filled the room. Trent picked up his pencil and pressed it against his paper, but a mind-locking euphoric feeling kept him at bay. Her pineapple hair, apple lips, and pear figure made him hungry for her presence. His pencil dragged across the paper, forming a silhouette of her perfect features. The bell rang. Smog clouds of infantile children and tired souls poured from the doors. Lagged behind was the dazed and confused Trent. Salvaging what was left of his mind was his current concern. Thoughts of Diana burned like a dry forest wildfire across his brain. Nothing could tear him away from his perfect reality, not even the frail and meaningless words of his dearest friends. The school was build around the cafeteria. The circular room had huge glass windows. It was like a brimming cage that contained many different species and variety of adolescents. Inside sat the quiet sheep waiting to be devoured, but a sympathetic knight rode to her rescue. A transparent teardrop fell from the encasement of wavy locks. The lethargic and brash populous of the high school had broken down what was left of her psyche. Diana was exposed for the sensitive and hateful person she really was. Suddenly, the sweet innocence she had was lost. Her appeal almost faded, but the vision of eyes too tired to cry and an expression so dead to emotion filled Trent with a new appreciation for her. No longer was she the innocent, quiet girl without a person to call her own. She became the most respectable, most deserving individual he had ever came across. Trent's mind raced with these thoughts as he quickly entered the cafeteria. His backpack slid off of his body and to the floor. He sat down quietly and placed his hand on her shoulder. "I think we'd be good together." Time stopped. Existence closed. The two remained celestially intertwined in their own fabricated and imaginary world. Their arms wandered like unyielding beasts charging toward pray. The passion was borderline violent, yet any watcher would observe it as a regular teenage romance. Reflective tears trickled to the white tables below. Even though the initial reaction seemed painful, Trent felt no regrets over his proclamation. The tears ceased. The happiness overtook that faux wood table, even if for just that moment. And melodrama didn't seem so bad.
