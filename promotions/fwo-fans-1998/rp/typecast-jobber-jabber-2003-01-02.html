Typecast Jobber Jabber

2 January 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Thursday January 2nd, 2003 1:17pm Halifax International Airport, Nova Scotia If she was anyone else, the twenty-year-old would've been embarassed to be seen in public clad in torn, but incredibly comfortable jeans and a t-shirt tied off at her hip...but this was Amy Campbell. A mass of hair, frizzy from the sweat of nightmares endured during the previous evening's sleep was tied back in a clump at the back of her head, with strands falling loose over high, well-formed cheekbones. On any other day, she might've bothered a little with her appearance, but she probably could've shown up naked as the day she was born and not get any sort of reaction; she was home, half of these people had actually seen her the day she was born. The redhead chuckled at that thought as she approached her means of transportation for that afternoon: a rusted-out '88 Jetta, diesel engine and all, with a mass of blonde hair at the wheel. That mass of hair cleared only momentarily to turn after hearing the familiar foot falls of a lifelong friend approaching. It was Renee as planned, icy blue eyes bright as ever and the same old perpetual smile forming dimples on her cheeks. She watched Amy hobble wearily up to the car, and saw a slight bounce return to her step when their smiles met as she rounded the hood, slipping quickly into the passenger's seat while tossing a duffle bag to the backseat. The mutual smiles continued for a silent moment before Amy noticed the absence of a rear-view mirror and threw her head back a little to giggle, "You know, you've had this car since I was sixteen, and it was an antique even then..." "Do not speak ill of the Jetta!" The blonde snapped back with a grin, as she always did when her best friend poked fun at her first real investment. She didn't speak again until they'd left the parking area, straight out onto a seemingly endless stretch of highway, power lines and evergreen trees. "Always with the car bashing! Soooooooo-rry! if I can't be as special as Miss 'Pro Wrestler Sala---OOOOH, wait! That's right, you don't have a car." Both laughed, but Amy complimented hers by cracking Renee on the shoulder, pressing yet ANOTHER comment from her younger counterpart as she rubbed her wounded arm, which still rested on the wheel. "You really need to learn that not everybody lifts weights on a daily basis! Be gentle!" "Yeah RIGHT! I might be fit, but the only thing I do on a daily basis is eat!" She laughed. Renee slipped a hand easily around her friend's slim wrist, squeezing it with a laugh of her own. "Maybe you should eat a little MORE."...a laugh that only got her smacked again. " Christ, Ames! Watch it! Fragile little bones, y'know." "Fragile my ass." Amy grinned. "How many times did you take me down in high school?" "Oh blah!" She'd waved the redhead off, and tried to venture to a topic that had most-likely been on both their minds. "So..uhh, seriously though. You and Hero Boy, eh?" Finishing it off with a mischievious grin and a nudge to the ribs, Renee realized her mistake as soon as Amy raised her head to reveal a suddenly troubled expression. "Where the hell did you hear about that?" Amy's longest-held friend had pointed to a local newspaper's wrestling section, which was open for her reading on the dashboard. Aimz stared forward, and immediately saw a picture of the fWo New Year's Eve party...focused on one particular moment and two particular people, followed by a headline that read "Tabloids Right Again". "See?" The blonde continued pointing innocently. "It's everywhere." After staring into her lap for a moment, fists clenched, Amy sighed. She'd seen this aftermath coming. "Darcy's gone, isn't he?" "Yup, and I won't repeat the message he left." Renee tried to smile for her friend's sake, "I know you're not worried about that, you're much better off now." "Has anyone in my family heard?" "Ehhhhh...........unfortunately, yeah." Amy swallowed hard. "Shit." With that news, she elected to wait until the next day to visit her parents. If they'd seen the papers, all hell would be raised the moment she was within their sight. "Ah well...did Darcy look pissed?" The other girl released a fit of laughter and tried to catch her breath as she spoke. "You...you need to ASK that?!" Another burst of giggles. "He was beyond pissed. There's one jerk-off you won't have to worry about re-surfacing for another year." "Yeah, right..." On the tail end of another sigh, Campbell had raised her eyes to stare blankly out the window. Her forehead wrinkled in worry, she couldn't help but feel nauseous at the prospect of her 'future' at this point. "...Now all I need to do is pay rent, get my own car and somehow eat at the same time."
