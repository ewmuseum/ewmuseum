12/29/2001 12:00AM The city of Sacramento still bustled, though the witching hour was upon them. Most major cities were that way, especially on a Friday or Saturday night... but something about the crowds in the street seemed extra jovial, almost as though they had been saving up for this week of celebration. From Christmas through New Year's Day, many people would find much to be happy about. Also, with the recent events... maybe they were looking extra hard for reasons to be happy. The economy wasn't in great shape, many had been laid off... the nation was still on a hair-trigger alert over the actions from 9-11 on... ...But little of that concerned the Boston Strangler as he stood outside of O'Leary's, waiting for a taxicab to arrive. The slump of retail sales and excuses for lack of profits meant nothing to him when he could not break the slump he was in... and he had no excuses to fall back on for it. At least, that's what he was led to believe during his "talk". If that was any sort of a pep talk, he didn't feel much better coming out of it. Losing to XXXstasy was a tough pill to swallow. He had him locked in with the Massacre... only to find himself pinned. It was a mistake he had made before, back at Cyberslam 2000... and that was a subject he didn't feel much like thinking about. Instead, he reflected on the conversation that they had at the table, once a round of beer had arrived... "You know you're better than this, Paul." "We know that... of course we are." "So what's holding you back? What do you think is the one thing that is keeping you from succeeding? Is it confidence? Preparation? Skill? Intensity?" "No.... none of those things seem right. Nothing has changed." "Maybe, Paul... that's the problem. Nothing has changed. They have, but you haven't. The business is changing, more now than ever before. You need to evolve with it to survive. I've done it... and I can say I've had a tiny bit of success. Maybe you should think about that." "Where would we begin?" "That's for you to decide, I can't go that far. I will tell you this though: I probably know you better than most of the people in this business, and in a ruthless career like we both are pursuing... you're probably the closest thing I have to a friend. So I'll be brutally honest with you... you need to make a change somewhere in the way you're going about this. They'll take your money from you, your power to control your own career moves... but they'll keep taking unless you remind them why you became one of the most feared men in this sport. Think about it Paul... and trust yourself." After that, they had finished their beers and parted ways. The Strangler wandered the bar, looking to catch up with Darryl and apologize for brushing him off previously. He had caught sight of him earlier in the bar area, and noticed him staring at the two of them at the table. After making the rounds a couple of times, the Strangler had given up and gone to find a taxi...where he stood now, wondering if one would ever show up. On yet another corner, Darryl Bailey wondered the exact same thing as he watched the tow truck drive away with his car. It was odd, as though someone had sabotaged it while he was in the bar... but with no enemies, he couldn't understand why. It would be something for him to think about when he awoke a while later, and could tell the police very little about his attacker who struck him in the head from behind. The only thing he recalled were some sketchy details of clothing, and the man telling him to "mind his own god damned business"...
