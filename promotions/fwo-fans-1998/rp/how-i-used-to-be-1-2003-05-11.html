How I Used To Be (1/?)

11 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Thursday, May 8, 2003 Scott Wasner had always liked Florida. He had vacationed there once with his parents, when he was young - Disneyland (or possibly Disneyworld - he could never keep them straight), Epcot Center, the usual - and took a liking to it immediately. It was a land of opposites, a state of duality, a vital and living mixture coming together in a way all its own. Throbbing heat and cutting rain. Blissful calms and menacing storms. Glitz and gutters and glades (though he had obviously formed these articulated ideas much later in life). He had always vowed to come back when he had more time - while he had been to the state plenty of times since his youth, it had always been as a wrestler. Scott Slugger, playing to and hiding from the crowds, never with a chance to enjoy anything. Now, finally, he was back. Wasner stepped out of the walkway from his plane and into Gate 7B at Miami International. He rubbed his eyes and looked at his watch - 2:31, AM not PM. It was the edge of the night, just before the very first small light begins to seep in. He shook his head, wishing that Detroit had an earlier flight. The lobby was all but deserted as he headed to baggage claim where he would meet the man he had hired weeks ago. The stores were closed, and Wasner saw only a few men sleeping in their seats at the gates. Airports always had a strange feeling at this time of day - a sort of still calm before the storm. He had seen it too many times in his life. Wasner took the escalator downstairs, leaning on its moving handrail and trying to keep his eyes open. He succeeded, and turned in the direction a helpful blue sign overhead told him was baggage claim. The carousels were spinning, and for once fate was on his side. The baggage from his flight, a Delta that had been nearly empty, was there. He walked up, watched it for a bit, then took a pair of leather bags. He turned around and came face-to-face with the man. The man's name was Larry Zito (just like the Miami Vice detective, not just like the Oakland A's pitcher, as Zito was fond of reminding everyone who would listen, and most who wouldn't). He was a private detective, and looked a great deal like a character from his namesake's television series. Zito's hair was slicked back with what looked like a gallon of grease, and Haggar slacks, a white jacket and a Hawaiian shirt completed the eye-stabbing ensemble. Zito had a twitching little rat's face. He was the best at what he did, and from what Wasner had inferred, he wasn't opposed to working around the law. Wasner didn't know if he had had to do any of that for him, and didn't want to find out. "Zito," he said. "That's my name. Larry not Bar - " "Jesus, Lar, I only hired you a few weeks ago, I haven't forgotten your damn name yet. How could I?" Zito smirked. "Yeah, yeah, yeah." He then turned serious. "What you wanted... I've got it all right here." He reached into his jacket, pulled out a folder, and handed it to Wasner. Wasner reached into his pocket and pulled out his wallet, then a few appropriate bills and passed them to Zito. Zito smiled a rat's smile and nodded. "Pleasure doin' business with ya, pal. You ever need me again, you just call Larry not - " "Yeah, I will. Thanks." Zito stood for a moment, nodding idiotically, then turned and left, which lightened Wasner's mood considerably. The less he had to do with the detective the better. He looked at the folder, though he knew he didn't need to. The name was correct. He opened it and looked inside, shuffling photographs. On the fourth, his chest hitched and he forgot to breathe for a moment. There were more, but he didn't need to look at them. It was only eleven miles to Golden Glades, but Wasner thought he would make that drive tomorrow. Or... technically, later today. A night's sleep would do him a lot of good. He picked up the bag he had set down and put the folder in it, then started for the nearest Hertz establishment. Fifteen minutes later, he put the keys to a green Saturn SC2 into his pocket and stepped out the airport doors. The air, hot and humid even at that ungodly hour, hit him like a thick fist. He stopped for a second, then continued out to where his car would be, the manila folder marked "S. Griffith" secure in the bag he held in his right hand.
