Jeremy's Song - 13

26 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
previous parts: see-downfall archive Eyes red and surrounded in black circles from lack of sleep, Jeremy stared down at the phone and shifted his glance toward the clock every once in awhile. It was eleven P.M., but he could wait no longer. The plastic receiver felt cold against his pale skin. He placed the object against his ear and kept it between the side of his head and shoulder. Every number dialed brought a little more satisfaction. Soon, the phone began to ring. Nervousness ran through Jeremy's body. Anticipation was in his veins as his shaky pale fingers pressed the gray telephone numbers. The loud beeps brought him closer and closer to freedom. Saying what he thought to others was a pastime long forgotten. The telephone rang clearly. Jeremy shifted nervously and waited on the edge of his bed. A faint click was heard from the other side, "Hello." A soft, melodic voice spoke to him. So familiar, yet so distant, he swallowed his spit. "Hey Emily, it's Jeremy. What's up?" His voice was nervous and broken. "Nothing much, really. You haven't called in awhile. Why today?" No words were spoken and the silence was clear. Jeremy stared off into the gray walls and wandered into past memories of happier times. "Jeremy, you need to get over this." "What are you talking about?" he asked as he was brought back from dreamland. A sigh could be heard on the other line. "You're such a fucking stalker. You can't even take a hint. I try to be nice with you and I try to be friends with you, but you can't realize that I never want to go out with you again. I'm not trying to be mean, but for fuck's sake, you need to get this through your skull. It's over; forever." Click, the connection was broken and Jeremy slowly lowered the receiver to the base of the machine. Silence filled the room; the only sound was a faint train whistle off in the distance. He stood up and grabbed his coat from the end of his bed before opening the door to his bedroom and slowly walking down the stairs. The blackness of the room was clear but the blue light glowing from the warm television screen was the norm. Jeremy looked at his father, staring at the television screen. Two steps and he found himself standing before the man that had pretty much neglected him his entire life. No hateful memories filled his head, because he had none with this man. Their relationship was a distant one, regardless of how strong a bond there should have been. "I'm leaving. I just thought I'd say that I love you, even if you don't care about me. I could die any day, dad, and I realize that. Just thought you might care." With that, Jeremy turned around and opened the door. Slamming it shut, he walked around the edge of the house and past the grassy field behind it. Frost caused the grass to be engulfed in a white covering. The sound of the grass under his weight was faint, only matched by the now stronger train whistle in the background. Finally, a pair of train tracks had come into plain view. Jeremy kept his pace and turned his head. A blue commercial train speeding around a bend. The cold metal of the tracks stung as Jeremy sat down on them, his denim pants giving little protection from the cold. Whistling louder, the train came closer and closer. Ten yards within distance, there was no possibility for stopping. Jeremy stood up and walked to the center of the tracks. Inside of the blue steel car, a conducted waved his hands frantically. The man stood, staring forth as the moonlight radiated off the cleansed steel. The train came within a few feet. The sound was inevitable. That of bone and flesh echoing off a steel train. The body slammed against the ground, causing the back part of the skull to crack open against one of the wood tracks. His leg flung to the side and was immediately taken off by the rolling wheels of the train. The gears slowly began to stop, but it was too late. Everyone, physicians, doctors, and all the people that would attempt to bring Jeremy back from this horrible accident would fail. A gaping hole in the back of his head was enough to end whatever plans he could have had later in life. A fight with some high school girlfriend was enough to push him to the breaking point. The family that never cared for him was a contribution. Out of all of this, there was one glimmer of hope that got no recognition in the consideration of this act. Jeremy's best friend, Tom, sat at home while this happened. Oblivious, he only wondered why Jeremy was not at school the day previous to the event. Tomorrow, he'd learn.
