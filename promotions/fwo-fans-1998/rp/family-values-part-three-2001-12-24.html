Family Values (part three)

24 December 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
She waited for the tearing of the cloth and the hard pressure of fists or hands on her skin but it didn't come. There was the panting, the heavy breathing and laughter from the boys... then silence. Her eyes were clamped shut, but she understood that something was wrong... perhaps the boys had gotten scared? Had run away? But then she felt a fist tighten around her wrist and jerk her upward. She screamed, and buckled her knees together. If they were going to rape her here and now, she was going to make it tough for them and make sure they had to fight for it with blood and sweat. She thought of what her brother would do... strange Henry who was so angry and violent... Henry would fight his way out of this. For a moment he even thought she could hear his voice... "Let go of her. Let go now." She opened her eyes with a start and there he was. Henry, shirt off and dark eyes glaring at the group of boys that surrounded her. Dirt powered down from his grubby hand, and she realized that he held a handful of rocks. It was so rare that she heard his voice, and she was surprised to hear it now. It sounded so cold... so hollow. But it was welcome... he was one boy against six, but she suddenly realized that she was safe. Something in his eyes... something in his eyes was dangerous. "Make us freak... make us. You just stay out of this... stay out of this or you're going to get killed, freak." But the boy's voice shook. Billy Simpson from three blocks over... she remembered that years ago he had fallen off his tricycle in front of their house and had cried bloody murder under his mother had come and whisked him away. That was years ago, and now he had a thumb hooked inside her panties and if Henry hadn't come over... "Let go of her. Last chance." Henry wasn't joking and the boys knew it. One grunted and ran... Eric Phillips who she had crossed the street with in Kindergarten. He had always been the uneasy partner in the gang, and now faced with Henry's pale and angry face he bent and ran. The others were not however... and she felt their warm, sweaty hands leave her arm and advance on Henry. "Okay freak, you're going to the hospital now... little bastard who doesn't know who his father i..." Henry pistoned his fist out with the rocks in his hand. The fist collided with Billy's face and expanded as Henry shoved the rocks into Billy's eyes. Billy howled in pain as the other four boys, Jon Meyers, Don Foreman, Sean Kiel and Wally Heinelstien all advanced on Henry. Henry spat on the ground and kicked out with a thick swing of his boot. The boot impacted Wally in the groin and he vomited warm yellow syrup all over the ground and Henry's legs. Henry jammed his hand out and grabbed Jon by the nose, digging his fingers into it and jerking upward. The flesh of the nose tore and he screamed as blood gushed out and turned his white muscle shirt into crimson haze. Sean was moving in, but Don looked suddenly frightened and ran the direction Eric had gone. She watched in horror as Henry grabbed Sean's hair and pulled out a bloody patch of hair and scalp. Eric screamed, and Henry shot a fist into his mouth. Teeth broke and exploded as Henry pulled back his fist and looked at it. The skin had been ripped off and his knuckles were bloody and white. Henry spat again and turned on Billy again. Wally was on the ground between them and Henry placed a hard boot into his ribs as he headed for Billy. She was suddenly scared again... the look in Henry's eyes was darker than she had ever seen it. Dark and beyond hope... beyond sanity. "Henry... Henry don't... I'm okay now." Henry glanced at her, and shook his head. He turned back to Billy and his face broke into a smile. She gasped... it was the first time she had seen him smile and now she longed for old times of dark, numb glances. "Sissy... you're my sister, and I'm your older brother. I protect you because that's what older brothers do. I'll protect you as long as I live... hear? That's my promise to you... I'll always protect you." Henry grabbed up Billy's head and Billy began to scream loud braying howls that echoed in the neighborhood. Henry slammed Billy's head down onto the concrete and Billy choked and coughed great wads of blood. Henry's gaze tightened, and he raised Billy's head up and rammed it down again. Billy's screams were cut off and all she could hear coming from him was a strange gurgling and gasping. She began to cry and shut her eyes against it, but even in the darkness she could hear the wet thud and slam of Billy's head hitting the concrete street again and again. It seemed like hours later, but it couldn't have been more than minutes when people arrived. She wouldn't open her eyes at first, but she heard Billy's head hit the ground a final time. There were mutterings and screams and a moment later a police car siren was heard getting nearer. When she felt the hard hands grasp at her she screamed, but the adult voice told her to hush and rocked her in strong arms. She heard nothing from Henry, and opened her eyes. He was being held down by a policeman. There was a small crowd there, and someone had placed a blanket on top of Billy. The white sheet was stained dark red and one of the policemen had apparently vomited at the sight of him before the blanket had been used to cover him up. Henry was looking at her, and she looked back at him. His eyes were alive, but full of sadness. Henry was drug over to the police car and tossed inside roughly. She watched him, her eyes not leaving his for long minutes until the car had pulled out and driven away down the street. She didn't know it then, but it would be eight long years before she saw her brother again. "I said I'm leaving you... I'm sorry." Neo looked at Mitsy and frowned. Why now? Why tonight? He could feel the old anger rise in him, but fought to keep it back. The way she was looking at him now brought back old memories and Neo just sat silent as he watched her drag a suitcase to the hotel door. "You want to go? Go. I'm not going to stop you... Neo's not good enough for you? That what you think girl? Then get out... there are plenty who are lined up to take your place. Get out." She turned to him, tear streaked cheeks shining in the light cast from the table lamp. "It's... it's not like that. You're different Henry..." "Neo." He cut her off briskly and raised his fist. She flinched, and Neo smiled his sneer of a smile. She still knew some rules it seemed. "You know what to call me, girl." She looked at him, that same soulful glance that made Neo uncomfortable. What was she playing at? Why was she pulling this stunt now? "Hen... Neo. Listen... this isn't good for either of us... you know it somewhere inside of you... you understand how this can't be good..." Neo rolled his eyes. "You want it more gentle? That what this about? I hear you screaming girl... you can't deny that you get off on it... bad girls don't pretend to be good girls real well. Not to Neo, not to the people who know 'em." She closed her eyes, and Neo had a brief pang of regret and hated himself for it. He sighed, and reached for his wallet. "Fine... take this." He tossed a wad of bills to her, and she caught it with surprise. He rolled over on the bed away from her, and after a few moments he heard the door open. She drug the suitcase out of the room, and he heard that she was crying. The idea brought a smile to his face. "Neo... no, Henry... I'm sorry. I'll call as soon as I'm settled." He was silent, and after a few moments he heard the door close behind her. He turned, and looked at the closed door. He frowned and shook his head as he reached for the remote for the television. "Fine. Go out on your own. But I promised I'd protect you as long as I live... and no matter where you go that doesn't change. That's what older brothers do... so run away girl, but you'll be back. You always come back." Neo turned on the television and was lost in the images. He didn't notice the tear drop down his face, and would have hated himself if he had.
