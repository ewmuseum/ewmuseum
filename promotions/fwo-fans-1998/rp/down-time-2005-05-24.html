Down Time

24 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Click. Click. Click. "So this is what guys my age do on their days off?" Alex Creed muttered to himself. He lay on the couch, casually flipping trough the TV channels hoping something of interest would magically appear. No program had stayed on the tube for longer than ten seconds for the past two minutes. He'd even cycled through all the available channels twice, just to make sure he wasn't missing anything worth watching. "That or video games," David Cross said with a sigh as he leaned back in the recliner, popping the footrest out in front of him. "I hear that some of them even engage in shenanigans." "Shenanigans? That's a word?" "Are you kidding? Of course shenanigans is a word. You've never seen South Park?" "That's the cartoon with the fat kid, right?" "... yes, the cartoon with the fat kid. I couldn't watch that show. They were always so mean to Babs." Sally stepped into the room and tossed a small wrapped package at each of them. "There you go boys. Two sub sandwiches." "Thanks Mom." Alex sat the remote down and unfolded the wrapping. "Tuna on whole wheat. What'd you get Dave?" David smiled as he held his sandwich overhead like a trophy. "This is a crowning achievement of the great sandwich artists, my good man." Alex wrinkled his nose. "Dude, it smells like Death. You got Death on white bread in your hands there." "Different strokes for different folks." David took a bite of his lunch and smiled. "Mmph gud rrtng." "Tremendous." Alex stood up and set his sandwich down. "I'll go grab us something to wash this down with. So that would be skim milk for me and gasoline for you?" "Unleaded." David took another bite. With Crucifix enjoying his sandwich more than any person in their right mind should, Alex walked into the kitchen and noticed his mother sitting at the dining room table. He opened the cupboard door and pulled out two glasses. "Whatcha reading?" he asked. Sally remained focused on the document. "Your fWo contract. So far I'm not seeing anything that will help get you out of this thing." Alex thumbed through the small stack of mail on the counter. "What's all this?" "Contract offers." Sally looked up and smiled. "You've got a great future ahead of yourself, kiddo. I just wanted to get you out of your fWo contract before I started looking at the other promotions." "This one spelled my name wrong," Alex muttered before going to the fridge. "How hard is it to spell Creed, anyway?" "I was thinking about you going back to Apostle." Sally returned to her reading, dragging her finger across every sentence. "Let's cut all the ties from this nightmare." "But more people know me as Alex Creed." "Well I don't know why you bothered using his name in the first place." "At first, I did it out of respect. Now I'm doing it to show him up." "You're not having anything to do with him!" Sally snapped with such urgency that Alex almost dropped the milk. She stood up and crossed the room in four long strides, grabbing her son by the upper arm. "Does he have to cripple you before you learn your lesson?" Alex set the milk down. "Well I think a little light maiming might be too subtle." Had he been expecting it, he could have moved. But when Sally slapped him across the face, Alex was taken totally off-guard. He stared in shock as he felt his cheek burn. Sally's hand flew to her mouth, her eyes wide and filled with both surprise and sadness. She quickly turned and ran to her bedroom, slamming the door behind her. Cross rushed into the kitchen and saw Alex standing in the center of the room. He noticed the mark on his cheek and followed the young man's stare. "You okay Alex?" "Truthfully man, I don't know. I just don't know." "Was it about Vic?" "It's always about Vic. That son of a bitch doesn't even need to be in the room to mess up people's lives." Like bare wires conducting his own anger, David's surgical scars began to burn. Vic Creed was a man without a soul, without a single redeemable quality. With everything he'd done, all the lives he'd tortured, David felt there was a special place in Hell for the Main Man. He didn't deserve happiness. He didn't deserve to know that his daughter was alive. "I'll talk to Sally," David said as he rested a hand on Alex's shoulder. "Don't worry."
