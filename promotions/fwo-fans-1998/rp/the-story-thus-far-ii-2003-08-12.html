The Story Thus Far - II

12 August 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Monday, December 1st, 1986. A young Jordan Copeland sat down on a snow embankment on a street corner known as Aylesworth watching the other children having a snowball fight. He desperately wanted, and desperately needed, to play with the other children, but they wouldn't allow it. They were all white children, and he was black. They didn't want him to play in their game, for some strange reason, they assumed that he would ruin their fun. Jordan had lived in the neighborhood for three years, every time he would go outside, he would have to play by himself or with one of his caring foster parents. It wasn't the same as playing with his peers because, as most people know, people around the same age usually had the same sense of humor and usually were interested in the same things. Jordan continued to watch them, looking dejected and distant. This had been happening every day he went outside to play since he had moved to Scarborough, but now, it was becoming too much. He knew he couldn't let the other kids see him cry, so as he felt the tears welling up in his eyes, he slowly began to trudge through the snow towards his rather large suburban home. As he walked through the snow with his snow boots making a crunching nose, a man stepped in front of him. It was a man whom he didn't know and hadn't ever seen before. He appeared to be an African American man in his 40's, and he looked homeless. The homeless weren't really a problem in Scarborough, they usually just hung out outside of a restaurant hoping to catch you on your way out with a load of food and some change and feel sorry for them. This guy looked lost and strung out on drugs. He had on a very worn brown Cleveland Browns jacket, some brown jeans, and some brown work boots. He had on gloves that were torn at the tip of the fingers, leaving his fingers exposed to the cold. "Don't worry about them, son," the old man said in a scraggly and slow drawl. A homeless man had never approached Jordan on his own since moving to Canada, but he knew what the homeless and the addicted were like. Jordan looked at the once handsome man who was ravaged by addiction's eyes before looking back in front of him as he walked towards his home. The man kept pace with Jordan, step for step. "You don't need them to be happy," he continued. "One day, sometime in the not too far future, Negroes like you and myself will rule this world... it's the Lord's plan. We'll have our own big houses, our own fleet of brand new vehicles, and there will be four to five white, houseboys for each black man. They ruined my life. They fired me due to 'substance abuse' yet they sent their white workers to rehab and still paid them. I live off the streets.. begging people so I can survive. Those honkeys got to know, what goes around, comes around..." Jordan ran past the man before he could continue his paranoiac and drunken rhetoric. It was fairly evident that he had more in him, more venting to do, but Jordan didn't stick around to hear it. Not understanding exactly what the man had just told him, Jordan bolted to his home. The homeless man looked at Jordan as he ran into his home before nodding his head and continuing on.
