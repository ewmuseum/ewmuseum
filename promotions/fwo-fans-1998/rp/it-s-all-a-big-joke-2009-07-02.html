It's all a big joke.

2 July 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"TC's pub, this is Rose," said Rosie, when she picked up the bar phone after two rings. It was a fairly slow night, which was good, because I wasn't really in the mood for lots of people right now. "Oh, hi, Miss Ivy," said Rosie, as she looked in my direction, "Have I seen Knox?" I gestured no. Miss Ivy was someone I didn't want to talk to right now. "No, no, he hasn't come in," said Rosie, gesturing to me to give her something. I shrugged, put my hands together and leaned my head on them, "He said he was probably going to go home and sleep for a while, and that he might be by tomorrow." I leaned on the bar and picked up my pint glass. It was never good to make Rosie lie, because she was horrible at it. She shrugged, but continued to nod. "Sure will, Miss Ivy," said Rosie, "Thanks. When are you coming back east again?" Rosie nodded again. "No problem. Bye," said Rosie. She walked over to me and tapped me on top of the head. "Hey," said Rosie, "Miss Ivy wanted me to tell you, next time I saw you, that you should really leave your cell phone on so when you're hiding from her in the bar you can at least keep track of who calls." Thanks, I said, I needed that. "So why are you hiding in here," asked Rosie, "What's going on?" Just work, I said, had a bit of a frustrating weekend and don't want to think about it. Rosie held up a hand when two people entered the bar and sat down on the other end. She walked to them quickly and, although I couldn't hear what they were saying, I could tell they were semi - regulars and knew what they wanted right away. "Sorry," said Rosie, "You were saying?" Well, I was saying I didn't really want to think about it, I said, but nice segueway." Rosie smiled, and pulled her hair out of the band holding it back. She'd cut her hair recently, it was a lot shorter, but it looked really good on her. Can we get some tunes, I asked. "Sure," said Rosie, as she hit a few buttons on the iPod dock under the back wall. I rolled my eyes as I recognized the first few violin chords of 'Soothsayer Lies' by LOURDS, but Rosie danced in place just a bit. "Come on," said Rose, "It's physically impossible to be in a bad mood when Lourds is singing, so just out with it." Okay, okay, I said, laughing despite myself, After the Meltdown match, I sat in the locker room for a long time, trying to figure out what went wrong. "That whole thing with Lowell, right?" asked Rose. Partly, I replied, but the Meltdown match itself, too. Six minutes, I was in the ring, give or take. "So?" asked Rose, "You got to make it an early night. Besides, I thought you guys hated battle royal type stuff. Miss Ivy always said Eli did, at least." They're hard, that's very true, I said, but that's not the point. Three months I've been working my tail off, wrestling as hard as I can for as long as I can every time out - like I always do. I wanted to do that in Meltdown, too - but I was tossed in about six minutes. It just makes me wonder if I'm wasting my time, I said, if that's how little they think of my work. "It's only been three months," said Rosie, "you always said you had time to make your mark." I know, I said, and I do. And I will. But I came into this company with a reputation as being the Marathon Man. Three months into my NFW stint I went nearly two hours in the JTP Invitational. I ran my hand over the top of my head, feeling the short hairs. Rosie looked worried, and I don't blame her - she had never heard me come down on myself or another company like this before, and she wasn't used to it. Well, I don't blame her - I never do this. You know what it is, I said, After the match, the locker room was filled with good energy. Everyone was congratulating the Flying Frenchie - rightfully so, I should say - on his victory, which was well deserved. A lot of the newer guys were also talking about how happy they were with their run, how they have a lot to build on. I raised my arms and shrugged my shoulders. The Marathon Man who lasted six minutes, I said, I've got nothing to show for the past three months. When I walk back in on the 8th, I'm essentially going to be in the same position I was when I wrestled my debut match against James Renshaw. It's frustrating to be standing still, no matter how hard I run. Rosie leaned forward and kissed me on the mouth. Damn it, I've missed her the past few weeks, between New Frontier Wrestling, TEAM, and the FWO, it seems like I'm never home anymore. Which is why I wish she'd just come on the road with me. But she likes this bar too much. "It sounds to me like you need to accentuate the negative," said Rosie, "You're so used to being able to pull something positive out of any situation, and now that you just got punked - essentially - for two days, it's got you all turned around." Maybe, I said, I just wish there was something I could point to to get me going again. Rosie stood up straight. "You're not starting over," said Rose, "I know it might feel like it but you still have wins over all those guys you have wins over, and you had that really exciting match with Karina Wolfenden and that Xender guy." Xander. "Whatever," said Rose, "The point is, everything you do builds on everything you did, so even if you have a night or a series of nights that look like a complete wash, you still have the means to move from that point instead of going back to zero. Am I making sense?" The thing is, she was. She had never spent any time as an according - to - Hoyle participant in the wrestling business, but she had a keen mind for how things worked. You are, I said, you are. "Excuse me," said another bar patron, and Rosie had to get back to work. I sat there for a while, thinking about the wrestling business and my career, and my place in the FWO. Maybe I didn't have a place in the FWO, maybe the past three months were a wash. Maybe there's no room for a guy who doesn't believe in hatred and crosses and double crosses and Machiavellian style plots. Maybe my title reign in New Frontier Wrestling is a fluke, and I should just pack it up and go home. But when have I ever done the easy thing, says the man who spent a year wearing a mask for reasons that apparently only made sense to him. I finished my drink and pushed it back; if I refill it myself Rosie won't have a business reason to come over and therefore will deal with her customers first. Which is fine, because I need to think. I need to have a plan and a goal beyond what I've been doing so far of simply wrestling a good match and moving on. July 8th, something's going to happen. I just don't know what yet.
