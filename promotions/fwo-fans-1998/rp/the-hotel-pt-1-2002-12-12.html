The Hotel, Pt. 1

12 December 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Wednesday night in El Paso. Scott Wasner stood on a street corner, leaning against the outside wall of the hotel. It was the Hampton Inn, he had observed. Nice to know some things never changed. The fWo would lodge you in a good but not great hotel, but if you wanted a really good one, you had to pay for it yourself. Apparently, Donaven Winters was not the pay-for-a-hotel-himself type. It hadn't been that hard to track down Winters, mused Wasner as he pulled his coat up further on his shoulders. He had found out where he was staying before he went to Warfare. At Warfare, he had played with Winters' head a little - just a little - but the real fun was to be had here. He had never been one to dislike psychological warfare - he had, in fact, used it to his advantage many times, both in angles and otherwise - but until now, he had never realized its fullest potential. Physical attacks were all well and good, but Winters was probably seeing Wasner everywhere he looked now - and if he wasn't now, he would be soon. There was a part of him that still protested over his actions of the past few days, a part of him that realized he had crossed a line himself, but that voice was growing weaker day by day. Some old dead philosopher - he couldn't recall who - had once said that one who would fight monsters must be careful not to become one themselves, but Wasner found that he disagreed. If this was what it was like, then maybe there was something to the old monster thing after all. Movement caught his eye. Sure enough, it was Winters, right on time. Winters walked up to the hotel and stepped in the door. Wasner smiled. It was only a matter of minutes now. The hotel security guard had been most accomodating. ----- Donaven Winters' hotel room was in chaos. The window was shattered, and the curtains blew gently inward and back with the wind. The television screen was smashed in, and its inner workings were clearly visible. The mattress had been thrown off the bed, and the covers were scattered over the room. Every light had been broken. Inside the bathroom, the mirror had been cracked. A spiderweb pattern emanated from the middle to the outer edges. The aluminum baseball bat used to do the job had been left on the counter, balanced on the sink so as not to roll off. Writing was still visible on the mirror, printed in red paint, still not completely dry, which dripped like blood. "BE SEEING YOU" it read. In the main room, the door began to open.
