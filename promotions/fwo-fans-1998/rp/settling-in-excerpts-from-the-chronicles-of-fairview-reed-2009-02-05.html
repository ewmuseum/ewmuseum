Settling In (...excerpts from The Chronicles of Fairview Reed)

5 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
My body is bruised and sore as I write this but as I learned long ago the physical shell is immaterial and serves as little else but armor for the man within… Mr. Werner has seen to my ‘injuries’ and has declared (as I knew that he would) I shall soon be as good as new. …The mice!!! I am so very pleased: The mice are alive and thriving!!! Such valiant troopers! Such hardy beasts! It was my initial apprehension that they would not survive the journey across The Pond, but apparently creatures from the Otherlands need possess a soul to be thwarted by water… These two mice are the very same that entered our plane by way of my abominable, accursed portal… I am encouraged and inspired that these two rodents have stood steadfast and hardy during the trials and tribulations of our past few months – to be hauled and whisked from place to place, thither and yon without a moments notice or a moments peace… Now that we are settling into this respectable and satisfactory haven I have had the chance to examine these mice fully and am immensely pleased to proclaim them healthy and hale! …That thrice-damned portal! Oh would that I could step backwards in time and prevent that catastrophic tragedy from occurring!!! Daily and nightly I curse myself – my ignorance and curiosity. I shudder with rage and fury at myself, that I could be so ill-prepared for the consequences of my dangerous prodding… And the resulting chain of events has been well documented in the earlier pages of this tome and I shall not put my pain to the pen again… I have been poring over the later prophecies of Barastius but am no further in finding The 9 Books. There MUST be some clue some where but I have yet to discover it - such a simple thing it would be to employ Ralphson’s Divination or Pale’s Finding Hidden Objects, but I cannot rectify the cost of such results. I simply must redouble my efforts, and our new home is ideal for such work. The library is adequate if small (Oh, but to weep and wail for the volumes and books left behind at Hurtheart Manor!) and I find myself spending most of my time there. Mr. Werner attends the fire with alacrity and regularity and my Assistant brings nourishment to sustain my efforts even before my awareness of such need. These four walls of words and warmth are the embodiment of my impetus; my inner fire shall only be quenched by the next epiphany hidden somewhere within this wealth of knowledge…
