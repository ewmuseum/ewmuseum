Time.

21 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A hotel room in New York City - top of the line. The decorating and style spoke of class and cash, not necessarily in that order. On one side of the room, a window was open and the curtains were blowing inward in a slight breeze. The window had a commanding view of the city skyline. The sun was going down, casting an orange-red glow over the sky and the towering buildings. Scott Slugger, clad in street clothes (Mariners t-shirt, jeans), stepped into view in front of the window. "Look out the window, Kinkade. What do you see? I'm sure you see a sun, some buildings - well, in one of your eyes anyway. I see more than that. I see time. That may seem irrelevant to you, but not to me. Let me explain. Tomorrow is CyberSlam. For those of you out there who have spent the last five years living under a rock, CyberSlam is the reason any of us are in this business in the first place. From the green rookies - no, not Covington, I mean the real green rookies - in their first match, to the twenty-year veterans, every single wrestler on this earth strives to make it to CyberSlam. CyberSlam is where you make your name. It's where you etch yourself into the stones of history. I was at the first CyberSlam. Not as a fan, but in the ring. I was here when it all started - the beginning of time as far as the fWo is concerned. Since then I've done a lot. Cruiserweight Champion, Hardcore Champion, World Tag Champion, Internet Champion... oh yeah. Five-time fWo World Heavyweight Champion. But it all comes back to CyberSlam. The next year... CyberSlam II. I main evented. The feeling was indescribable. I may have lost, but that's beside the point. How many people have main evented a CyberSlam? Eight. Myself. Scott Collins. The Boston Strangler. Austin Davis. Mitch Wilson. Mike Bear. Erik Kelly. Ruben Ross. This year the list will grow to nine, with Vic Creed. The nine people in that club can claim something that everybody else dreams about. Including you, Kinkade. Where were you when I main evented CyberSlam? Traveling the backroads of America, trying to find your next promotion? Working in a high school gym in front of 50 drunken hicks throwing popcorn into the ring? I don't know where you were, and you know what? I don't care. Because on that day I cemented myself as an immortal. Not the capitalized, meaningless version you sometimes call yourself... but a real one." Slugger looked out the window. The sun was getting closer to being completely down. "The sun rises. The sun sets. Lather, rinse, repeat. It's time. It flows through all of our lives. Changing, moving, building, destroying. How will time look upon you in five years, Kinkade? Ten? Fifty? A hundred? I can tell you how it'll look upon me. It'll look upon me as the epitome of my profession. The greatest. Nobody has done what I've done in my career. You... don't make me laugh. Who are you anyway, Kellen Kinkade? You're a nothing, a nobody. You came up from the minor leagues of this sport, and you may act like a major leaguer, but you'll never be one. You're a fine wrestler... but who remembers fine wrestlers? A hundred years from now, who'll remember Ricky Steamboat? Nobody. They'll remember Ric Flair." Slugger paused again, in thought, the sundown casting an orange glow on him. "I'm not the first man who's ever said this, but I'm the only man who can say it without lying. I am... the best there is... the best there was... and the best there ever will be. You? You're good. Not good enough. In case you haven't read the promotional material, Kinkade, CyberSlam is where men become heroes... heroes become legends... and legends become immortal. It's where the best rises to the top, and where the good are separated from the truly great. And that, Kinkade, is why you don't have a chance tomorrow. You can bring Mitch Wilson, the backstabbing son of a bitch... you can spout your pseudo-psychological bullshit... you can do whatever you want, but it won't make a difference. You don't have what it takes to beat the likes of Scott Slugger at CyberSlam. I'm the last of the truly great ones. There are no more Austin Davises. No more Scott Collinses. No more Ultra-Violets, no more Boston Stranglers, no more Jimmy Dudleys, and I mean the first run. There is the Flying Frenchie, true, but... he's Frenchie, for God's sake. There's only me." Slugger paused again. Only a sliver of the sun remained visible on the horizon, a neon orange disc. "The sun rises. The sun sets. A hundred thousand sunsets from now, I'll still be regarded as the best of all time. You will be dust. Not even a rumor. Two hundred years forgotten. And that, my friend, is why you might as well just etch tomorrow's results in stone right now." Slugger smirked at the camera. The sun disappeared. "Until next time... ... it has been your pleasure."
