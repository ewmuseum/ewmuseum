Patterns (Part 2): "Awaken"

3 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Thursday October 31st, 2002 3:40pm Still home Same old patterns, same old people... With a bounce in her step, Beverley Campbell drifted to the door of her daughter's appartment, hoping to find Amy home and cheerier than she'd been two days prior, when she'd heard news that she'd most likely soon lose her job and what seemed like all hope of furthering the wrestling career she'd been so set on. When she found the door ajar and stepped in, those previous hopes were trashed about as badly as Aimz' living room. The clutter and generally chaotic atmosphere had followed anywhere Amy'd occupied since she was young, so that wasn't at all unusual. What was out of place, oddly enough, was finding the young redhead sprawled across her couch. Napping at mid-day? Not something she'd ever been known to do, even as a child she hadn't slept often enough. Mrs. Campbell didn't want to startle her, Aimz was always a little jumpy and wavering on the side of neurotic so Bev used a soft, calm tone in whispering "Amy?" but it didn't stop her daughter from jolting from her sleep, rolling over the edge of the soft sofa onto the not-so-soft tiled floor while eliciting a loud thud and a groan to match. When her eyes finally opened, she shook her head a little and sat up against the couch. "Wha.....what're you here for?" "I just wanted to see how you were doing." She muttered as she sat down, offering Amy a hand to help her back into her seat. "Not so good, hmm?" Aimz' response was breif and concise enough to evade any deep questioning by her perpetually nosy mother. "Not feeling as well as I could be." "Why not? You're not still down about OSW, are you?" Well, so much for avoiding questions being asked. She started, paused, then began again after a moment's thought, carefully planning what to tell and what to keep to herself. "Nah...I'm just in a bad mood, I guess. I let some crap get to me and freaked out on Darcy for no reason...so he's gone to cool off. Not too good." Bev just sighed, shook her head and mumbled "You'll never change." "And just what's that supposed to mean?" "Amy..." Her tone was descending and it was obvious she was disappointed that her daughter still had the same childish mood swings. "You've got a lot of growing up to do..." Aimz was shocked, to say the least...usually her mom didn't nag in person, probably to avoid a fit on the part of her daughter, who held an almost eternal grudge if so much as a negative word was spoken. "What?! I'm twe--" "Age doesn't directly reflect maturity, you know." The elder Campbell snapped back. "You abuse everybody around you, and it's all to suit you. A person can't live a happy life when everything they do is so selfish." The look on Amy's face said it all: Bev had hit the nail on the head, but her daughter would never dare to risk her pride in admitting so, especially after how offensively it was worded. None-the-less, she tried to remain relatively calm, not exactly open-minded but not terribly combative. "Nobody in my life's unhappy!" "That's why Darcy had to go calm down before he got irrational and walked out, right? I honestly wouldn't have blamed the poor man for disappearing again, I would have done the same if someone had ever dared to step all over me like that." "You're nuts." She growled then finished with, "And NEVER bring that up again." "Don't talk to me like that, Amy. If you want to think of things in a self-centered way, just think that you'll be much happier if you keep the people around you happy. It'll cut down on arguments and draw more GOOD people to you." Her voice raised, but lowered again. "And maybe you'd get criticised less...you always complain about people nagging you, but you bring it upon yourself. Look at the good in life and don't focus so much on the things that aren't quite going exactly how you want them...you'll be MUCH happier, and so will everyone else." Amy stared, organizing what was just said. "......so you basically just said I'm a selfish bitch who needs to care about others more?" "Not my exact words. But, if the shoe fits..." And then...? Pure silence. It seemed to have lasted two or three awkward minutes before Aimz spoke, surprising her mother with what she finally admitted. "You know what? I don't say it often, because often it's not true, but......." She took a deep breath and swallowed hard, trying to muster the nerve to slash her own ego. "......you're right." Bev smiled and leaned in, finding Amy open to a hug for the first time in a long time. She noted the sign of deep thought in her daughter's eyes and elected to leave then, just as the phone rang. Hopefully, it was Darcy and Aimz might actually find the strength to admit her mistake, like she had to her mother. Amy's mom left with nothing short of a whisper as the redhead grabbed the phone, hoping for the same as her mother had. It wasn't the ray of light she wanted in apologizing to Markson, though... It was even better.
