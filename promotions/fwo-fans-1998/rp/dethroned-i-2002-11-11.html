Dethroned I

11 November 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
November 2002 And then the rain began to fall. He did not much mind the rain. Others around him covered their heads and unfolded their umbrellas, but he had no reason to shy away. He allowed the cool drops of dew to fall lightly on his exposed head. As he walked he could feel the water slide through his long black hair before sinking peacefully into the back of his neck. The cool water on his face was welcome relief to a mild fever that made the early night unenjoyable. Next he made his way under the tracks, giving him a momentary reprieve from the showers. Above him he could hear a train approaching, screeching metal and thunderous rumblings foreshadowing its impending arrival. As he came to the end of the block he turned the corner around an old brick building who's damp exterior gave off a lustrous shine in the cool moonlight. The music inside was loud and emanated all the way down the block. He leaned against the wet bricks and listened for a few brief seconds while he took a few final drags on his damp cigarette. The long walk had worn him out, but he enjoyed the reprieve and the music in the air, until the passing L-Train masked all other sounds. He tossed the butt into a puddle by the curb and proceeded to the doors. The show had started hours ago, so the lines had cleared out. Approaching the doors, he was stopped by a large, bald man. The man said nothing, simply held out his arms and pointed to a black sign that stated "No Admittance past 11:00". "Look, I'm just here to see a friend of mine, I don't want any..." Unable to finish his sentence, he was interrupted by a muscular man who put his hands on the black man's right shoulder. "It's ok Dave, he's a friend of mine." The bald man looked around and reluctantly stepped aside. "Jason!" the muscular man exclaimed, "Don't mind the new guy, he'll get to know you. How the hell are ya?" The two exchanged smiles and friendly pats on the back. Jason stepped inside the entrance of a small club, doused with flickering lights and engulfed with loud hardcore rock music. The smell of sweat and alcohol wafted through the air. "I'm hanging in there Paulie, you know how it is," Jason said. "We didn't expect to see you back so soon, when'd you get in?" "Late last night. I heard Shane was playing again so I thought I'd stop by." Jason brought his hand to his thin pale face and pushed his wet black hair to the side. "Yeah, you missed him though. Good set, good set. Jesus Jase, you feelin' alright? You don't look too good." Jason turned his eyes away from his friend and looked around the club for a few moments. "Yeah well, you know. I've got a fever, maybe the flu, something like that bullshit." Paulie's face reflected his concerns. "I guess the road isn't a kind place to be if you're figthin' a bug either," Paulie remarked casually. Jason's eyes again flickered as he faintly nodded with half a smirk. "Well I'd see a doctor if I was you, I mean you're an athlete you know? You gotta keep an eye on your health." "Yeah, I gotcha. Thanks man. Shane's backstage?" Paulie caught himself staring at his friend. His normally light skinned face was now whiter than a ghost, and his cheekbones and jaw seemed to be trying to escape the snow-colored flesh that clung to it so tightly. "Yeah, he's back there with the other guys," Paulie said. "Let me take your coat and out it someplace for you." Jason took off his long black trenchcoat, handed it to his friend, offered his gratitude and walked into the crowd. Paulie watched his friend pass, worried about his appearance. Though his friend wore a sleeved shirt Paulie noticed that his muscular definition was fading. He barely looked recognizable. Jason made his way over to the bar and ordered a drink. On stage was the band "Eighteen Visions", churning out powerful riffs and screaming poetry from the bottom of their lungs. He gazed into the violence and unruliness taking place in the crowd for a few moments. Flying bodies, torn clothing, banging heads, flailing limbs ... it was home. At least, it normally was. Tonight, he wanted none of it. He felt weak, jaded, absent minded. He just wanted to sit down for a second or two. He lingered in the back and finished his drink, and while he did he played out the events of the last six months in his head. Time passed. Thoughts wandered. "What could I have done differently." He retraced circumstances, trying to locate where things went wrong. When did it all fall apart? How did he lose what he worked so hard for? How could he let himself crush his own dreams? Lost in jaded memories. Draping over the sun. Will this be our swansong? Our last dance? Come take my hand and we'll watch fate destroy us. The lyrics of a song that echoed against the walls of the club echoed inside his head. He had not been listening to the music, but he heard the words nonetheless. Was it over? "Come take my hand and we'll watch fate destroy us." He had hope at the beginning of all this. He had optimism. But time had passed, opportunities had been scarce, and second chances were merely fantasies. He had hope, once. But now hope was slowly slipping away.
