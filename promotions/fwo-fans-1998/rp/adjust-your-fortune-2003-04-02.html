Adjust: Your Fortune

2 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Two and a half hours later, and Allen Speck's time had run out. First, he came out to his theme music, which was a loud, obnoxious-sounding heavy metal song that totally didn't fit with Speck's natural mood. Speck held his arms up in the air to get a reaction, and it was mostly cheers, despite being booked as a heel. Allen nervously entered the ring. The ring was a bit smaller than most traditional rings, which made it possible for Allen to do some insane corner-to-corner stunts. It was, however, well-built for an independent federation's ring, and well suited for tonight's action. The fans were enjoying the show, having witnessed a barnburner of an opener, and an excellent technical exposition between Charlie Harper and Mack Shields in the midcard. Allen Speck, himself, was a notoriously insane spotfest wrestler before, though he's been trying to refine his abilities to a more "sports entertainment" feel. He liked to play to the crowd. He liked to taunt his opponent. It was those little things that made him very popular amongst the three hundred screaming fans in attendance. Then, a White Zombie song hit. "I'm Your Boogieman". Massive boos, because this was the theme music of the reigning five-time Southwestern Wrestling Alliance Regional Champion, Benjamin Morris. Out came a six foot, three inch, and two hundred and fifty pound man with a blonde-haired crewcut. He had a large, oblong nose, eyebrows so thin that you wonder why he doesn't just shave them off, and generally looked like a tool. He also wore bland black trunks and black boots. He also wore the bulky, awkward-looking championship around his waist, and stood with his chest puffed up despite having what could only be described as a beer belly, like he was high and mighty and proud of his accomplishments. Allen glared at him and shook his head in embarassment. Benjamin Morris, the son of wrestling legend "Nitro Beaver" Kyle Morris. Benjamin Morris, the man who claimed to be better than the best. Benjamin Morris, the man described by even his own little sister as "the worst wrestler she's ever seen". How the man still wrestled despite the fact that he was wrestling's equivalent of the human disaster area would remain a mystery to Allen Speck, but he did know that he needed to make sure that the fans got their money's worth. As soon as Benjamin came within range, he rushed the ropes and came flying over the top rope with a corkscrew plancha, which caught Morris offguard, sent the crowd into the frenzy of a lifetime, and sent the crowd in an uproar. Speck fought to his feet first, and quickly sent Morris into the ring. Once back in, Morris was getting his no-selling groove on, as he immediately returned to his feet, tore the belt off of his waist, and quickly clotheslined Speck in midair as he tried to come in with a springboard dropkick. The fans exploded in boos, as Morris took a moment to catch his breath, already winded from the first thirty seconds of action. No, Virginia, this was not a good sign. So, Morris just gained the advantage. Does he try to do another move to keep Allen Speck on the canvas? Does he taunt Speck while he's down? Does he stomp away? No. Even better. He just locked in a chinlock for two minutes. Though nobody could speak for Allen at the moment, the fans were about ready to tap out from the boredom. Finally, even Allen noticed the ridiculousness of this, and fought out of the hold with a few elbow strikes to the stomach. Morris was stunned, and Allen ran into the ropes, bounced back, and caught him with an almost blind leg lariat to the head of Morris, which knocked him down. Speck quickly kipped up, but noticed that Morris was again no-selling his offense. "Oh, for the love of GOD," Speck thought, "Even Triple H sells." Speck paused for a moment before he spun around in a complete 360
