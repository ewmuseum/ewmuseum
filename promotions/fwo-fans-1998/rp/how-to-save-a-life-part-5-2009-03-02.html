How to Save a Life: Part 5

2 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Ivy, I appreciate the fact that you've been coming to see me so often in the past few weeks, but things don't change very much for me here, you can take a few weeks off." Silver Fox gestured towards the door, but Ivy McGinnis sat down at his desk. "It's different today," she said, "I just showed up, pretty much, to thank you." Fox looked confused, so Ivy continued. "I've talked to Sean, I've cried in front of him, and I've taken William's job offer. As of this Wednesday, March the fourth, I'm officially working for the FWO front office again." Fox beamed. "That's what I wanted to hear," he said. "Congratulations, Ivy... you're going to be a great asset to him." Ivy stepped toward him. "That's... only half the reason I'm here." "What?" She sat down on his bed. "You lookin' for a job, punkin?" asked Ivy. Fox's eyes darted from side to side. "What are you talking about, Ivy?" "I'm talking about you," replied Ivy, as she took his hand, "You're wasting away here and I think you could be so valuble to the company if you were on the payroll again." "I appreciate it, Ivy," said Fox, "but I--I....I....... I can't be part---" His stuttering forced him to the window, where he stared out into the gray Seattle sky. "Why not?" asked Ivy. Why not, indeed. Fox went over all the reason in his head that Ivy would understand, why he couldn't tell her the truth about himself, about Smith and Neo, about Miles and Disney, about the real reasons the FWO was dormant for the past three years. "I just don't think it's a good idea," said Fox. "Why don't you come to this week's show?" asked Ivy, "You can see how things are run and you can decide then." "No!" replied Fox, too quickly. Ivy looked momentarily hurt, but she recovered quickly. "Why not?" "There's things, Ivy..." said Fox, "Things you should know, but things that I can't really divulge. Please trust me, I really shouldn't leave this room right now." "Okay," said Ivy, "What if you didn't have to?" "What?" "What if you could just watch the shows and talk FWO with me once a week or so," she continued, "and gave your thoughts based on that?" "That would be incredible," said Fox, "but that's not how it works." "Why not?" asked Ivy, "William doesn't know how to run a wrestling show so he's making it up as he goes, and he knows how t'do that. And he's willing to work around your schedule if you're willing to give your thoughts... isn't that the dream?" Fox looked away. "That would be ideal," he said. "That's what William is offering," replied Ivy, "He specifically said to me, any way I could convince you, he's willing to pay for. If you'd prefer to contribute to the FWO by talking to me once a week and keeping your involvement secret, I'm fine with that. The point is to get your mind moving again... then maybe you could be part of the company again." Fox, again, didn't answer. "What if you could stay right here?" asked Ivy. "What do you mean?" "What if you could stay in your room," she continued, "and just give us your thoughts on what's in progress? All the benefits of working the road, and none of the travel." Fox thought about it. That certainly seemed to answer all his concerns. "That might work," he said. "Great," said Ivy, "then I'll be back Thursday Morning with a run sheet and a review." She wasn't going to let up, thought Fox. Ivy wanted him to be part of the show again, and she even presented him with a scenario that would allow him to contribute without forcing him to leave the room where he's as safe as he can ever be. "I look forward to it," he said. IVy hugged him. He felt almost normal again. Almost.
