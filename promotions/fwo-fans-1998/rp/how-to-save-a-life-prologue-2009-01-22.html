How to Save a Life: Prologue

22 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It was just past 4 AM, according to the digital clock in the bedroom, but Ivy McGinnis wasn't in a position to lament the hour. Judging from the retching noise that came from the bathroom, she had more pressing matters at hand. She stood up on shaky legs and spat into the sink, and looked at her reflection in the mirror. The tiny scars that surrounded her emerald eyes punctuated the dark circles below them. So far she had been able to cover the rings up with makeup, but it had now been nearly three weeks since she had had a good night sleep, and it was going to inevitably catch up with her. Eli and Angel had pulled MJ out of school next week and were planning on making a vacation out of it; Ivy and Sean were going back East tomorrow to hook back up with their little guy, Shannon, and it would be something of a family vacation. Technically, she had exactly one day to get her head in the right place. So what was wrong? Earlier tonight, she and Geoff Green had both commented on the fWo's reAction as 'The greatest comeback in professional wrestling history,' and while she was sure it would be, she also knew that time would be the ultimate judge. On camera, she was able to portray Zenlike calm and confidence (except when she needed to play the Psycho Bitch while at ringside), but as soon as the cameras shut down, the fear and doubt crept back into her and seemed to camp in her throat and chest. That's not entirely true, she said to herself, you were fine when you were fixing the FanTron. Damn it, she replied, that's not what you're there for. You can't be there to patch knees and wipe noses anymore. You're a mother, you've got responsibilities to Shannon. The words made her feel sick again, and she sat down. Of course she loved her son, and of course she would do anything for him, but the truth of the matter was that Shannon was Sean's son, and, she would most likely give herself an ulcer between the bitter jealousy she felt over the relationship the two of them had formed, and the intense self loathing that she felt for herself over that same jealousy. It was strange to think that five years ago, even two years ago, Ivy McGinnis would have been considered the toughest, most hardassed, 'ice-water-in-the-veins' person in the entire sport. Her critics would have a field day if they could see her now. Those critics could blow her, in the metaphorical sense. None of them would ever understand the toll that the wrestling business had taken on her. Fourteen years ago she walked into her first locker room (Coop's, for obvious reasons, didn't count) with one objective: Fulfill a promise she made at her sister's funeral service and watch over Eli Flair. That was a heady enough thing to lay on a twenty year old's head, but she considered it par for the course, not only on her sister's memory but on the fact that the McGinnis family had all but adopted Eli when he was seventeen. In the past fourteen years, Eli grew from relying on her for everything but what happened in the ring to a World Champion, to a wrestler who transcended Championships, to a father and husband and bonafide legend to the industry, who was now in the fWo solely to give back to that same industry with name recognition and working with the youngsters who would be the company's future in the years to come. Of all the people in the world who would be able to understand what Ivy was currently feeling, Eli would be the most sympathetic, the most constructive, and the most understanding, if for no other reason than the fact that they'd known each other for more than twenty years at this point and knew each other's peaks and valleys like their own. She'd never tell him how she felt. After fourteen years of being Eli's sounding board, he would certainly feel he owed her the courtesy of reciprocating, but she would never bring it up. This was not Sean Stevens' problem, either. A lot of the issues that currently swam through Ivy's mind were in place before they finally got together for good. He loved her as much as he loved their son, and he would do anything to protect and support her, but Sean's career had had its own peaks and valleys. He was currently riding up the biggest peak to date, both in the fWo and elsewhere, and she would not and could not burden him with her own neurosis and risk dragging him down again. The problem began and ended in Ivy's own mind. As Eli Flair gradually needed her less and less, she began to make plans to leave the business and go back to her pre - wrestling career goals, only to find she couldn't remember what they were. All of a sudden, she was twenty six years old with a half dozen years and thousands of miles logged, and found that she couldn't leave the boys behind. Silver Fox had provided her with a compromise when he offered her the consultant position. He could see she was losing herself at that time; virtually all of the close friends she had in the business had left the company, and due to her own sacrifices, she had nothing at the time to go home to. His faith in her reared its head again when the Tort Media board offered her the on- and off- screen role of Commissioner before-- Before. Failure on that level has tortured her ever since. It was a relief to shift her focus to New Frontier Wrestling and the ULTRATITLE; it allowed her to scale back and just have fun. Craig Miles had plenty of people working underneath him, she could just be part of the show. It scared her too much to even consider having the boys rely on her for a paycheck. Plus, her role of the Ol' Perfessor, or, as Sean liked to call her, 'Mama Bear,' was easily filled by both Shannon himself and through touring with Valerian's Garden. Road managers often became a surrogate parent for musicians while on tour, and though the Garden was on the high end of the maturity scale, as musicians go, the analogy still held. Still, the fWo has been back on the air for two weeks and, both times, Ivy has been instrumental in averting a disaster. Calling Slugger was a one shot deal, she had rationalized; he was from Seattle and still lived there, since he was available he was the logical choice. Plus, she knew he would be more receptive to accepting her offer as opposed to Kid Cool's. William Beaven had come a long way since his days as the BONI champion, but a lot of the old timers would probably still see him as he was. The FanTron was a different matter. Her months working with the former technical staff, to say nothing of the Garden itself, had honed her ability to come up with quick, cheap fixes on the fly for equipment that would otherwise need expensive repair and replacement. Before the night had ended, Jarrod had already asked her opinion on two other trivial matters. And there was Karina Wolfenden. Why did you have to say something to her, Ivy asked herself, K-Wolf can handle her own career, she's built a pretty solid reputation for herself without your help. It's the same cycle starting all over again. You get attached to the boys and girls on the roster and end up with a great big metaphorical fist up your ass. And if I use the word 'metaphorical' one more time, I'm going to force myself to throw up again, which finally got a smile out of her. Between Mommy, Den Mother, and Tech Geek, was there any room left for Ivy Lillian McGinnis? And was there anyone she could talk to who would both understand her issues (thus disqualifying her brother) and she wouldn't feel like she was unduly burdening(thus disqualifying both her fiance and her best friend)? Ivy stared into the mirror, into the deep pools of emerald green that comprised her eyes. Her gaze shifted to her unkempt blonde hair and the gradual but ever - increasing gray that was seeping in. She was only thirty four, but her hair was going gray. Neither of her parents had a single gray hair when they died, and they lived to their fifties and sixties. As she studied the gray, standing out in the midst of the blonde like streaks of silver, a smile formed on her face. There might just be someone she could talk to. Here's hoping that particular door wasn't yet shut. And maybe she could get back to sleep tonight.
