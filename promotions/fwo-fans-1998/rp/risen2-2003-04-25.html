Well, I suppose it's time I write my own story. I couldn't tell you why I refused to speak of those years. My life was wrought with depression and apathy. When you truly care for nothing, you speak of nothing. Some wish to know what I was thinking all of those days, but all is lost in a vast abyss of newfound desire. A desire to see the rose red lips and cheeks of Dallas Wroth. Every lethargic morning was spent pacing through the small personal room I had been given. A television glowed--even in the daylight--with a blue light that reflected off the colorless walls. A melodramatic soap opera played an omnipresent role. I ran toward the sound of flesh against oak. I threw the door open before Dallas had a chance to walk in. Those same bags remained under her eyes. I smiled toward her, and she cocked her head a bit. The chair smelled of a mix of perfume and anesthetics. She looked down at me, and I looked back up at her. "I hope things get better for you some day, Dallas." There was a quick pause in all life, but she continued to push me down the hall. "You don't think I was sincere? I see you meander into my room every day with the same dead look on your face. Like I said, I just hope things get better for you." Metallic doors parted ways as Dallas hustled me through. My forward motion helped me limp toward my current concern. Those cold eyes reflected everything I used to see. In them was the death of Diana, the explosion that caused all of this, and my rebirth. And, for some reason, I assumed she saw the same in mine. I kneeled down against the wheelchair and crossed my arms against her lap. She placed a hand on my head and ran her fingers through my hair for a moment. I looked up at her for a moment and began to fiddle with the beaded bracelets she wore. My eyes shifted toward her wrists as I smiled once more. "I can't thank you. It would just be too frivolous. Just know that I care." Her nod had sufficed. Shane walked over with the same bland and meaningless clothes wrapped around his body. I pushed up against the arms of her wheelchair and stood up to him. "Can I have a break today? I'm doing fine, and I don't really think that anyone's concerned with me getting better at this point. Not even myself-" I stopped as Shane nodded his head in agreement. He grabbed a lab coat off a hook near the doorway and stepped out of the room. With a quick glance, I looked to my life and tilted my head to the side. "I respect your desire to not speak," I kneeled down by her once more. "The last few years of life were caught in the wake of depression. My vow of silence was due to the fact that I thought nothing I had to say mattered. That still may true, but now, I know that there's something to hope for." The woman in the chair motioned for the female trainer standing beside us to leave. She did so, realizing a mental breakthrough was far more important than the physical being. Chicago streets and wrestling fans christened me Downfall, but that name was not for this occasion. "My name is Trent Harrison, and the love of my life died years ago." The scent of the air shifted from that of sweat and dried blood to love. Surrounding actions no longer affected my makeshift universe. For hours, I told her stories of my days as an author and why I was here. She listened, smiling once in awhile. It didn't matter if she said anything back. All I wanted--at least for today--was for someone to care about what I had to say. Call it a selfish desire, but it was my time to be selfish. The physical training center was closing for the night, so Dallas and her personal nurse returned and carted us off to our rooms. And that's when everything fell apart. I sat in the room for about two minutes, contemplating the day's events, when the phone began ringing. "Forwarded call for Trent. Fans Wrestling Organization, should I put them through?" "Yes, please." An unfamiliar voice appeared in the back. "Downfall? I hate t'call and tell you this, but the committee says that if you're not on Warfare with a personality that sparks a little reaction in the crowd, you can just spend the rest of your days in that hospital." "...wait, so I'm expected to come back with a leg that's somewhere at half capacity at this point?" The voice went soft. "Guess so, son." I nodded, and hung the phone up quietly. The closet was wide open, the same tattered black denim jeans and cotton shirt hanging there. I grabbed my arm bands, necklaces, and put myself together. For over an hour, I scoured the hallway in hopes of finding my love's therapist. My hands dragged against the lightest blue walls as I tried to divert the stares. All was in vain. The woman looked at me with a smile. "I'm not supposed to tell you, but I see you're the only good thing going on in Sara's life right now. C313, but try not to make it too obvious that you're patients wandering off." The brass door knob was cold to the touch. At first I was nervous, but then I threw the door open and nodded. Somehow, she knew what was going on, and I waited outside the room as she changed into her clothes: a simple sunflower dress made of white, yellow, and brown silk. It looked like something out of a springtime movie, but I neglected to mention such things. We left, and I led her toward the train, to a phone... ...I didn't even know where I was going. All I knew is that I had to make it back.
