Tomorrow

26 July 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
She got home at 6:30 in the morning. The bus pulled away as soon as the door closed, and Angel immediately pulled the boots from her feet. Silently thanking her gods that they had buckles and straps instead of laces, she staggered toward the bedroom, discarding clothes as she went. No real reason, other than she knew once she sat down, even to take something off, she'd be out. By the time Angel stood over her daughter's crib to check on her, she had gone down to her bra and spandex shorts - they were a necessity for this gig, as she was wearing a short skirt. It was just for a day... but she missed her little angel like you couldn't even imagine. "I'll be there," she'd said to Eli, "If we need to leave the moment all the crap is loaded and drive all night, I'll be there." She smiled as she remembered her words - she hadn't let the man she loves down. Collapsing into bed, she was asleep before she even hit. But the impact woke up her husband, who wrapped his arms around her little waist and pulled her close to him. Even sleeping the sleep of the dead, he saw the smile on her face. Slowly, Eli eased his way out of bed and grabbed the tattered and torn "Beyond Good and Evil" T-shirt from his floor and pulled it on. Hell, he could've gotten out of bed with an alarm clock buzzin' and Angel wouldn't wake up. But even still, respect for the woman was most important. It was certainly a nicer 'balcony' than he had in the Bronx... that was simply a fire escape. From here, Eli could see the sun rise, the city in the distance coming to life after the night of debauchery that Hollywood was famous for, everything. Tomorrow was the big one. The showdown with Kodiak Vic Creed. The match that he had been preparing for, in many ways since his arrival in the promotion. Forget about Kelly and the title shot he'd received at Countdown to Oblivion... Fox and Smooth had wanted him involved in some fashion, being champion and all, and the Flair/Ross program was one of the hottest ones running. This one was for real. And yet, all he could do was think about Brand Frontier. Why? Brand and Creed... that was none of his business. That went back farther than most wrestling fans' memories can reach. That reached a level of personal hatred that even Eli, with his extensive history, didn't know could exist. Creed and Angel at Uprising... his blood boiled. The verdict was in, that Creed would be battle- ready tomorrow, but he wouldn't comment on whether his neck bothered him or not. Fine. Eli wasn't The Deacon. He wasn't Brand Frontier. He didn't have the pressure of being a hero. He knew that sometimes, if you need to take out a villain, you have to be willing to be worse than the villain. You can't have so much integrity that you wouldn't break Vic Creed's neck, given the chance. He wouldn't give you that same consideration. Steven Shadows didn't think Eli Flair had it in him. Troy Windham didn't think Eli Flair had it in him. Kevin Powers never knew. ATTAXX certainly never knew. Donaven Winters knew... Donaven Winters simply didn't care. Does Creed know what Eli Flair is willing to do to win the battle? Does Creed know that while Eli Flair is looked at as a hero by a vast majority of wrestling fans... he acts in a way completely the opposite? Eli shook the thought from his head as he returned to the bedroom. Coffee was on the brain now - still a novelty to him that he owns a coffee pot, a fully stocked kitchen, and all the domestic amenities of home. He is home, after all. The clock on the wall read 6:42 AM. Another six hours or so and their weekend houseguests would start to show up. They'd have fun, they'd hang out and do nothing, they'd head to the studio and mess around, all the while trying to pry Aimz away from Mickey's mixer and guitar electronics. They'd all have their fun tonight, because tomorrow was all about business. Tomorrow was all about the fWo. Tomorrow would see the King of Extreme, the ultimate Anti- Hero taking on the Alaskan Juggernaut, the Ego Destroyer. It was always a question as to how far a man would go to be the champion. How far would he be willing to go for something he believed in. Would a man compromise his integrity for his beliefs, if placed in the position? The answer for Eli Flair was a resounding "YES." Someone had to go this far. Tomorrow.
