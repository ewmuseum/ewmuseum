Of exodus and redemption (part seven)

28 December 2001

LONDON DAILY TIMES NOVEMBER 16, 2001 AMERICAN WRESTLER FALLS ON HARD TIMES BACK AT HOME. To audiences around the world David Spencer was an ordinary, if large man with a heart of gold. The champion for the common man, David Spencer was a long reigning mainstay for the British based English Professional Wrestling League. Over his career David Spencer had fought in over eighty televised events from the BBC. Spencer, a devout Catholic and self professed "family man without a family", had an illustrious career in Europe before his attention turned to the states and a new territory to delight and entertain. The United States presented problems for Spencer however, and the former main event caliber wrestler soon found himself struggling for placement on the tops of wrestling cards [events]. Spencer, a known name and high-ticket item was too good of a resource to waste on smaller venues, and thus he was quickly repackaged from the man we had all grown to know and love into a dark and brooding character. "At first I was shocked. I mean, I've got a tradition that spans over a decade as a hero to children. Of course, Americans being what they are this didn't mean anything. I hadn't been in the states for four months before they are telling me that our style [European style] is washed up... old fashioned. They present me with this mask and gaudy costume and tell me that my name is now going to be Death. Well... that might work for some but seriously, you know? Seriously..." David Spencer was repackaged as "Death", a masked man in black who lead a team of four men called the "Dark Riders" inside the American based fWo, or Fans Wrestling Organization. Spencer led the promotion to new heights as Death, even though he was a staunch critic of the gimmick. While at the fWo, Spencer collected the highest salary inside the organization, a fact that bothered fellow workers so strongly that at least one of them left the fWo in protest. One "Jim Dudley" was said to be infuriated with the amount Spencer was making and boycotted several events before his eventual release in 1998. Spencer played the Death gimmick for nearly a year, drawing record attendance and setting the stage for the fWo to quickly rise to the top of the American wrestling markets. At the top of the fWo, Spencer's run as Death was credited as being one of the most original and powerful "bad guy" gimmicks the wrestling world has ever seen, a fact that amused the former champion of the common man David Spencer to no end. "I mean... here I am the former hero of a country being booed at by thousands of people a night. During the first run as Death the fWo actually had to hire additional security to prevent the fans in the arena from jumping the fences and charging in at me. Nobody has come close to getting that kind of guttural response, no matter what the press releases in other organizations say. I know, I know... this isn't a healthy style of wrestling, but certainly it was profitable." David Spencer would only wrestle in the fWo for another year after his first run, but the time spent made him an even more wealthy man. Feeling that the time to return to England was right, David Spencer traveled back to his native land. But it was a different England from the one he had left, and Spencer would find that his former golden reputation had been tarnished by abandoning Europe for the big money in the states.
