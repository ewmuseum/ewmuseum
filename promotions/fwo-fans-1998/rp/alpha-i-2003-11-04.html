Alpha I

4 November 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The world watched on in horror at the wreckage before them. Like a NASCAR car crash, no one was able to avert their eyes from the destruction before them. They always expected Landersell to fall victim to his flashy offense, but as fans, they never thought they'd see it first hand. They never thought they'd see another man crash and burn through a table head first. They never thought they would smell the stentch of death in the air as one of the most beloved and forgotten jobber to the stars lay in the ring, crumpled in a position that was anything but normal. But I guess things are getting off to a bit of a quick start, aren't they? Maybe I should travel back a bit in time. You know, to a time where Joe Landersell lived. ** Seven pounds, eight ounces. Held in Sarah Harmen's arms was the bountiful baby boy with crystal blue eyes and a firm yet delicate hand, squeezing her finger in what she perceived as affection. After thirteen hours of labor, the only thing Sarah wanted to do more than hold the child she bore in her arms, was place him back in her womb, where she knew that he would never be harmed. Unless you consider suffocation harm. Joseph Harmen ran into the room, not wanting to miss a second of the reality that was his life. Sure, he had been apart of birth before, but never had the birth been his. Long story. Complicated ending. Not the point right now. He held a small glass filled with water in his hand, and stopped abruptly from his jog, splashing some of the water onto the hospital floor. He shrugged, running his hand through his shaggy uncombed hair, and breathed a sigh of relief. Sarah smiled at him, who calmly took his stride over to her, and began to pour some water into her mouth. It was as if he was the mother, and she was his baby. Childbirth had really taken its toll on Sarah Harmen. In fact, they were worried they were going to have to do a C-Section. And that can't be anything but painful. But all those dreary thoughts washed away like rain soaking the light snowfall that had fallen outside. The temperature had not been anyone's friend lately, changing from rain to snow sporatically and without anyone's permission. Yet, right now, Joseph Harmen didn't care for the outside world. He didn't care that he had a match the next night to be preparing for. He didn't care that his six year old son was running around the hospital chasing around the children in the pediatrics section.
