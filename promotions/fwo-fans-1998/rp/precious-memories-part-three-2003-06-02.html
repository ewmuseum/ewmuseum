Precious Memories ~ Part Three

2 June 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
1995, once more. Later that same day... Jade never did like attention much. Going completely unnoticed from day to day was just fine. To be able to successfully conceal oneself was to be better able to get away with what needed to be done. Inconspicuous animals are the best hunters, are they not? That afternoon Jade had walked across Philadelphia with a flayed cheek. It reminded her, later on, of a piece of fileted fish. The open wound blew away her inconspicuous front, making her feel as open and on display as the raw ground-beef for muscle fibers on her face. Heavy rains and dark clouds had given way to bright sun. How metaphoric life can be sometimes. Jade scowled, gluing her eyes to the rough concrete sidewalks. All along the familiar route, Jade was met with shocked gasps and horrified looks from passersby. "Oh my god! Are you ok?" "Honey, do you know how badly you're bleeding?" "You need to go to the hospital right away!" "That's disgusting..." These were all things that she had heard numerous times before, even in her young life. There were seldom kindly hello's and waves. No tipping of gentlemen's hats. Not even a smile. The young girl with the raven hair and the bright emerald eyes was always just given a quick nod, or nothing at all except the occasional comment such as the ones that had flooded her ears that afternoon. Jade had grown accustomed to it, though. Walking around in such a ghastly condition was bound to illicit this kind of reaction from other people. But, Jade would have prefered no reaction whatsoever. Wouldn't it be nice if life worked that way? Alas, it never does. And Jade, like so many other people before her was forced to walk this walk of shame and discomfort. Her security had been shattered in the whiskey bottle of an old homeless man. If she possessed the capacity to feel sorry for herself, that would have been the appropriate time to begin. Instead, Jade just drew deeper into what little sanctuary her own thoughts could provide her with until she reached that old familiar gym. The walk had only taken about twenty minutes total, but it had seemed like an eternity to her. Bill Randolph's gym was located above a slaughter house, right in the middle of one of the worst parts of the city. The acrid smell of old blood and rotting bovine carcasses filled the air with a thick syrup of decay. It stuck to your nose for days. Cattle moaned on their death march on the other side of the brick wall. Jade loved it here. This was home. Without the dead cows...the blood, the shit in the streets...it just wouldn't seem right. She wanted to come home to this every night -- needed to. Things could always be worse. She could have been born a cow. Jade didn't feel much different from one of those animals as she climbed the narrow, walled-in staircase with its uneven steps. Huddled into this area, bleeding and filled with an impending sense of reluctant terror, Jade wanted to turn back...but had no choice. The alley was out of the question for tonight. After what happened, there was no way she could go back there lest she wished to not wake up in the morning. The urgent sense of dread pressing like a weight on her chest was not for entering this place. It was for what coming here meant. She needed help. It was something that she never asked for. Not even once in her life. Jade would never allow herself to stoop that low. Weakness was something that she could not tolerate in herself. It was the worst thing that a person could be. Bill Randolph knew better, though. Whenever she showed up, she was asking for help in her own indirect way. He would never mention that to Jade, though. Even she had to recognize the implications of her random stays. She just chose not to acknowledge the true meaning behind her actions. The interior of Bill Randolph's gym bore a striking resemblance to the Mickey's gym from the Rocky movies. It was old and in a state of moderate disrepair. When Jade walked through the warped wooden door, she found the lights to be on, but could not see anyone. Everyone who used the place was done with their workouts by this point. Bill was around somewhere. He had to be. Jade stepped over to one of the benches that ran the perimeter of the room and sat down. The second her body hit the bench, she realized just how exhausted she was. She had lost a lot of blood -- so much so that it had turned her skin and white wife beater red. Bruises had already formed on her body and beneath her eyes. The only thing that had really stopped bleeding was her split lip. Jade allowed her head to rest against the wall. "Hey! Who's out there? Don't you know this place ain't open right now?!" a deep male voice bellowed through an open doorway. Bill Randolph stepped out moments later. He was an older man, around fifty years old. His hair was graying and thinning out at the top. Though getting wrinkles and a sizeable beer gut, it was easy to see that he had once been a pretty decent-looking man. He still had some good-sized biceps, actually. The skin of his face looked slightly red, either from too much stress or drinking. When he saw Jade, he froze. "What in the hell happened to you, kid? You look fucking terrible." The older man said with concern as he walked over to Jade. "Nothing. Just some...hobos or something...and whiskey." Jade mumbled her words and closed her eyes tightly. "Christ, look at that cut. That's really bad...." he remarked while leaning in closer for a better look. "Its just a cut. Get away. I'm fine." She tried to sound authoritative, but her voice was rather uncharacteristically weak. There was no concealing how badly she was hurt. "No it isn't. Jade, I can almost see your cheekbone. You're gonna need stitches to fix this one." Bill reached out to touch the area around the wound. That illicited a swift reaction from the injured Jade. With great speed she reached out and bent his hand away from her, bending his wrist back at a sharp angle. Bill was shocked and gritted his teeth. "I'm...just telling you...God! Let go!" Jade just stared at him coldly for a few moments. She looked as if she could have killed him. But, she ended up releasing the grip. Bill rubbed his hand. "I know what you were going to say..." she persisted. "You were going to take me to the hospital. I hate hospitals." "When have I ever taken you to a hospital? Hmmm? Name a time." Bill waited for an answer, but Jade didn't give him one. "That's right. I also know you came here because you wanted me to help you. And that's just what I'm going to do." "Help. I didn't say that fucking word." Jade said the word help as if it were a cursed word. Bill looked at her with the stern expression of a concerned father. He knew that he had hit an exposed nerve, but decided to ignore it. Jade would get over it. She usually did. "Think of it more as a favor then." Before Jade could say another word, Bill got up and disappeared through the doorway that he had came out of. Moments later he returned with a small first aid kit...and a needle and thread. Jade raised her head a little and watched him. She was having a hard time keeping her eyes open. Bill kneeled down beside her. He opened the kit and pulled out some peroxide and a couple of cotton balls. As he began to dab the cotton, Jade spoke up again. "What...are you doing?" she said with a thin voice. "Showing you how to do this so there's one less thing you need me for. Everyone has to be self-sufficient. Its the only way you can get by anymore." Bill didn't necessarily believe that, but it was what Jade wanted to hear. If it would make her feel better, then it was worth saying. Now up on his knees, Bill rubbed the peroxide on the open wound. The cut sizzled instantaneously and Jade failed to react. She just layed back and allowed the sensation to come. For her, it was a beautiful experience. Physical pain was something she considered to be a lesson...something to learn from. Over the course of the next half hour, Bill Randolph stitched her wound up and made sure it was perfectly clean. Jade savored each and every pierce of the needle. It was one of those things that she would never forget so long as she lived. This was one instance in which it wasn't so bad to be helped. But she would never entertain that thought for a second. From this day on, Jade would see Bill for what he really was -- the only person ever to give a damn. He was the closest thing to a father that she would ever have. That night, she would pass out on his old yellow and brown plaid couch. For once...everything felt safe. Too bad these things never last...
