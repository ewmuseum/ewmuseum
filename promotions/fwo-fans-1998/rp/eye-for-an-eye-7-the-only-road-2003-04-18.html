Eye For An Eye [7] The Only Road

18 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Life is a series of twisting roads, each filled with bumps and obstacles, which prevent it from ever being an easy ride. Now and then the roads come together, it's inevitable... the road to the final destination can change in the blink of an eye or a split second decision. We all meet crossroads in our life, and we choose a road to venture down. But not all of us get to make a choice. For me... the choice was already made. December 14th 1992. "Face it boy, that girl ain't ever gonna call you back." Vince Kinkade sniggered from a comfortable seated position in his armchair, situated in the corner of the front room. Kellen Kinkade sat anxiously beside the phone... everytime it rang he picked it up and everytime it wasn't the person he expected, his heart cracked a little more. "No point moping about it son... women are like that, hot and cold... one minute they love you and the next minute it's hate, take some advice from me Kel... drop her and move on, you're young... got that whole football career ahead of you, make the best of the talents you have, don't let a damned woman grind you down!" Kellen shot a glare at his father as he took a sip of his whiskey-spiked coffee. "Since when did I ever ask you for advice? You aren't exactly the model husband to my mother are you? Nor in fact are you the model father... one son in juvenile, a daughter heading the same way and another son... ... who couldn't give a shit about what you have to say." Tension filled the air as Vince glared back across the room at his son following his harsh words. A quick sip of his coffee calmed the rage boiling within Vince. "Fair enough son, you don't have to listen to a word I say... you just stay over there waiting for your phone call, I'll come back in five... six months and see if you're ready to talk." Kellen sighed. "You do that, why exactly are you still here anyway? Isn't it tradition for you to vanish at Christmas? Don't you have a wrestling date to work?" Punches certainly weren't being pulled, with every second that passed by the father and son talk got more personal in the wrong kind of way. "I own WWC now boy... you know that full well, my days of rough dates and schedules are over, I pull the strings now... I call the shots, hell... I could work from home if I wanted to. The day Valentine passed on was the day that my hard work paid off, see that's what life is about Kel... it's not about chasing around women that aren't interested, it's about working hard and making investments for the future." Kellen shook his head. "Investments well made eh dad? You only had to sacrifice your family, your body... and by the sounds of things your mind as well... wrestling didn't make a life your you pops, it took one away." Vince squeezed his hand into a fist so tightly that his fingernails pierced the palm of his hand. "Sacrifices are what make us boy, you of all people should know that... especially seeing as you're making a few as we speak, so much potential and so little brains... you're chasing a football dream that passed you by years ago, you're chasing a woman that dropped you like a sack of shit months back, sacrificing the present for the past son... that's what you're doing." Kellen was now the one clenching his fists. "Just what are you implying?" He barked at his father. "No need to get irate boy, I'm simply pointing out the fact that most footballers your age have already got a contract by now if they're gonna make it... maybe you just aren't as damned good as you think you are, maybe you didn't make the cut... and maybe it's time to consider other options... ... I've said it before and I'll say it again, but as long as I live and breath as the owner of WWC... there's a spot on the talent roster waiting for you." Kellen and Vince exchanged a long, silent stare. "And I've said it before and I'll say it again... as long as I live and breath, I will never set foot in the industry that has torn my family apart... ... thanks but no thanks, pops." Kellen Kinkade got to his feet and exited the room a man with hopes and dreams still intact, but Vince Kinkade smiled to himself... he knew that the days of hopes and dreams were numbered, numbered in his favour. March 1st 1993. "Time breaks us all boy, there's no shame in that... the strongest, the fittest, the best... we all pay the price of time sooner or later. Pride is the hardest pill to swallow Kel, but the sooner you get it over and done with... the sooner you can move on with your new life." Vince smiled to himself but his smile was brought to an abrupt end as his son took a grip on his shirt. "Stop calling it that, this isn't a life... it's an existence, I'm only doing this because I have nothing else left, no girlfriend... no career, nothing." "Good!" Vince exclaimed as he swatted Kellen's hands away "You know more than anything what requirements this industry has... the demand, the dedication... you're in this now, there won't be a family or a wife... not one that you can maintain at least, this is all about you and what you can achieve... I won't let you make the same mistakes that I did in my youth." Kellen's face twisted with rage. "So I'm a mistake now?" He sneered, tossing down an elbow pad that he'd previously been trying to yank on as he headed for the door of the gym. "Walk out of that door and you can say goodbye to all of this, fame and riches are things you're not going to take out of here if you walk... you can rest assured that you aren't going to attain either by flipping burgers for Ronald McDonald." The words were delivered like a knife to the heart... they stopped Kinkade in his tracks. He swallowed hard because his father was right... pride was the hardest pill to swallow. "You ready to do this?" Vince said sternly as he folded his arms. Kellen stood silent for a moment, contemplating that which he was about to embark upon. "Yes." Kellen picked up the elbow pad and pulled it on, before clambering through the ropes of a wrestling ring. "Great..." Vince began "... then we shall begin, we'll start with the mat basics and move you onto the high spot stuff when you've grasped that." Vince stepped through the ropes and immediately place his son in a headlock, the training session had begun... and whilst Kinkade fought to break free of the wrestling hold, he'd already surrendered a part of his soul. May 22nd 1993. "But I'm not ready!" A nervous Kinkade cried as he peered through the curtain at the crowd gathering around the ring. His father placed a stern hand on his shoulder. "I wouldn't send you out there if you weren't ready boy, you've learned the ropes... now you've got to go out there and show the world what you've got." Vince released his grip, but the nerves continued to stab at Kellen Kinkade. "Come on dad, these people aren't going to buy it... we're talking about guys that weigh well over three hundred pounds here, I'm what? One hundred fifty... two hundred at the most? It's not going to sell." Vince shook his head. "The only person who can plant the seeds of doubt is you boy, you've gotta dig those things up before they start to develop into weeds, because when they do they'll start to root... you can do this, just do some of the flying stuff, it'll go down well." "I hope you're right." Kellen said as he took a deep breath and exhaled, before parting the curtain and making his way down the aisle. "And introducing next... from Short Hills, New Jersey. Weighing in at two hundred pounds, the son of the legendary "Iron" Vince Kinkade... ladies and gentlemen, Kellen... KINKADE!" The sounds of the announcer pierced his ears, filling his mind with yet more doubt... as if being a rookie wasn't enough; he now had the shoes of a legend to fill. Blinded by the flashes of cameras and deafened by the screams of the crowd, he tried to focus on his opponent for the night. Landslide. Landslide was a 385lbs monstrosity, Kellen couldn't lift him off the ground so his tactics for the match were already limited... his father was right, he would have to fly. Kinkade slid under the ropes and went through the motions of what he'd been taught, a quick clamber onto the ropes and a raise of the arms to get the crowd behind him as the heel across the canvas stared back menacingly, and then it was time to engage the foe. Kinkade hopped down, pacing across the ring and standing... not quite nose to nose with the huge Landslide, extending his hand for a pre-match shake. Landslide returned the favour by slapping him hard across the face, and that's when the rot started to set in. Landslide had hands like a bear, and what should've been a simple slap across the face to rile Kinkade and the crowd up turned into a disaster, as Landslide's shovel like hand hit Kinkade across the side of the head and ear, almost knocking his head off and forcing air into his head, making him dizzy. Kinkade fell to the canvas when he should've stood tall and proud. The crowd booed Landslide insanely, as the man himself looked somewhat shocked that his opponent for the night had gone down to a simple slap. After a few seconds of confusion Kellen Kinkade staggered up to his feet somewhat shaken, as Landslide made his move, whipping Kinkade hard across the chest with hard chops. Kinkade was fresh meat... his chest immediately glowing red from the stinging blows. Kinkade went back into the corner as the official stepped in and broke it up... but in true heel fashion Landslide wanted to strike a final blow. He overshot it and punched Kinkade in the face, hard. Landslide backed away as Kellen lay a crumpled heap in the corner, a lot of thoughts raced through his mind but none were more prominent than the ill feeling that he was being shot upon. He staggered to his feet as his eye slowly drifted into a mix of purple and black bruising, kicking Landslide in the bread basket several times, enough to double him over for the first proper move of the night. A simple suplex. Kinkade had his doubts, but he'd been reassured that Landslide could get through the necessary flip to complete the move... hooking the big man's head he threw himself backward, only realizing midway through that something was horribly wrong. He was being sandbagged. Deadweight, that's what Landslide was making his best attempt to be and it worked.... the 385lb monster crashed all of his weight down across Kinkade's ribs, knocking the air out of the youngster and leaving him down and out on the canvas. Things were not going to plan. Landslide got to his feet and raised his arms triumphantly much to the disdain of the attending crowd, in the background a winded Kinkade tried to struggle up to his feet... fearing that he may have cracked or even broken some ribs. With Landslide's back still turned... he slowly clambered up the ropes, still dazed from the hard shots earlier on and gasping for air after the hard hit to his ribs, he knew that if he could hit the perfect finish he'd win the crowd over. A cross body from the top rope and a cover would suffice. Kinkade leapt through the air... the intended finish flashing through his mind and disappearing in a second of judgemental error, in a dazed state he'd misjudged the leap and completely overshot Landslide... clipping his head on the way over and landing in a heap behind him. The crowd were silent... they didn't know what to make of the glaring mistake, the official head the crackle of Vince Kinkade's in his earpiece and passed the message to the two wrestlers. "Vince is pissed, he wants you to go home... now!" Landslide shook his head, a promising career as a wrestling monster was about to be flushed down the toilet. Kellen Kinkade rolled him up and the three count was registered, but there was no mega pop for the victorious rookie, just an indifferent reaction and a few claps. And a "We want Vince!" chant. A disenfranchised Kellen rolled under the ropes and his made his way to the back like a scalded dog, as Landslide got to his feet and followed. Kinkade parted the curtain to be greeted immediately with his father. "Good job son, go and get showered up and we'll talk about the match later on." The message was encouraging but at the same time merely a means of getting Kellen out of the way, so that he wouldn't witness what was about to occur. Landslide made his way through the curtain and was greeted immediately with a punch in the face. "You're lucky that I don't fire you right now you piece of shit, you play deadweight with me boy and I'll play deadweight with your livelihood... I can fire you in the blink of an eye Chris, don't ever forget that... if you shoot on my son or go against the grain again, it'll be the last thing that you ever do in this promotion, do you understand?" Landslide considered punching out his employed, but then thought better of it. "Yeah, I understand." Vince Kinkade smirked to himself, he'd set an example that his son was not to be messed with... but he'd also failed to realize that physical abuse was not the only form of abuse. May 29th 1993. "How's it going KK?" Kellen Kinkade looked up at one of the wrestlers in the backstage area, who'd greeted his arrival with a devious smile. "I'm good thanks man... you?" "Never better." The worked replied with a chuckle, making his way toward the ring for a match. "Hey KK, who've you got tonight?" Another wrestler piped up. "I don't think I'm booked... dad wants to introduce me slowly so that I'm not being pushed on people, you know?" "Oh yeah... I know all about that, we don't want him pushing you on people do we?" The worked said before uttering "Don't want him pushing you over them either." Kinkade didn't hear it as he pushed through the locker room door and tossed down his gym bag, as he did... one of the senior workers and a personal friend of his father made his way across the room. "Hey kid, a moment of your time?" Larry Dupri asked as he sat beside Kellen. "Sure thing Larry, what can I help you with?" "Err... well kid, it's not so much what you can help me with and more what I can help you with, I wanted to have a little chat with you about the politics and games that go on back here." Kellen narrowed his eyes. "I wasn't aware that there were any, everyone has been fine with me so far... just today a couple of guys said hi as I arrived." Larry sighed. "Yeah kid... well, that's partially what I wanted to talk to you about... this little nickname they've given you, KK?" "Yeah..." Kinkade replied "My initials right?" "You would think so... that's what so clever about it... ... it stands for Kareer Killer." Larry said, avoiding eye contact with Kellen. "The boys seem to have it in their heads that if they make one wrong move in the ring with you they'll be out of a job, it's a very cruel game they've got going on so I just wanted to give you a heads up and let you know that you shouldn't pay any attention to what they think." "Thanks." Kinkade replied in a sombre manor, as Larry gave him a pat on the back and went back about his business. Kellen could've made a fuss, he could've gone to his father and had the problem bled out of the system, but he didn't. Because being a career killer was the least of his problems... ... now he was a joke too.
