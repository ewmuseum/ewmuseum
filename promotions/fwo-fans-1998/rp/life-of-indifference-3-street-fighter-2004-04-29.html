Life Of Indifference [3] Street Fighter

29 April 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
So I've told you about greed but what about that other basic character flaw we all share in curiosity? Because no matter how confusing my statement may seem, curiosity is a flaw. Just look at a few shining examples. The cat... and the fact that curiosity killed it. Or how about Alice and her little trip down the rabbit hole? These of course aren't factual so how about the number of people that die every year because they're curios as to whether or not the Japanese are telling the truth about poisonous blowfish tasting good? Curiosity kills. I should know, it nearly killed me. July 10th 1995. "So what you're trying to tell me is that you want me to fucking dive? Then what the hell was the fight a couple of days ago all about... even Kenshiro can take a friggin' dive." Kinkade wasn't pleased, but his boss was adamant. "Not about taking dive, wanted to see if you capable of doing right damage before you hit ground. I keep telling you American, Yakuza's get one sniff of this being fix and we both in trouble... big trouble!" Matsumoto grabbed Kinkade by the collar and shook him to stress his point event further. "Right." Kade grunted, still not happy with what he would have to do in a few nights time. "Excellent, then you know plan... first round you mess him up. Rip at face and make that dirty dog bleed, but whatever you do... not knock him out." Matsumoto wagged his finger "Second round I no care... you come out and when he hit you, you go down. Stay down for while then make your way to betting table to collect your loser fee, then you come find me in back and we leave fast." "Loser fee huh?" Kellen raised an eyebrow "So how much does the winner get?" "No matter." Matsumoto snapped quickly "You win you get big money, but you also get angry Yakuza who lose all their bets... you like your fingers, you lose!" "Understood." Kinkade sighed... he knew what he had to do. July 13th 1995. "You do this and we make good money, don't mess up." Matsumoto delivered his final words as he rubbed the shoulders of his fighter. He and Kinkade stood in the middle of a circle of money waving observers all waiting to place a bet on the fight that was about to take place. "If you tell me one more time about what I have to do, you'll be the next thing I mess up." Kinkade growled over his shoulder. "Haha." His mentor chuckled "Just do as you told, now get up and fight!" Matsumoto backed away as Kinkade got to his feet and did a few stretches, taking note of certain surroundings as he did. As the rain slowly started to fall from the sky he saw the Yakuza gang members attending slowly back under a canopy to shelter, he also saw the betting table where he'd collect his fee for taking part in and losing the fight... and he also saw a fire escape that lead up the side of the nearest building. His opponent stepped into the ring, several inches shorter than Kinkade and at least fifty pounds lighter. They didn't have long to stare one another down and consider a game plan because the fight was almost immediately called into action. Kade's opponent advanced but quickly found himself overpowered, as Kellen took him down to the concrete floor and proceeded to send a couple of body blows flying into his ribs... sending them black and blue within a matter of seconds. The other fighter struggled to get free but Kinkade simply pushed his weight down onto him and found enough space to fish hook his mouth and tear away, producing a scream from the fighter to accompany the stream of blood running down his face. Remembering that he wasn't supposed to knock the other man out, Kinkade rubbed his forearm firmly into the other man's nose until it popped and broke under and weight and also started to bleed. A couple of seconds more struggling were followed by the verbal cry that brought the first round to an end. Kellen returned to his stool and sat, where Matsumoto sprayed bottled water over his face and into his mouth before congratulating him with "Good good, hard part done... now all you need do is fall." Matsumoto took a look across to the other gathering of people who were surrounding their man, trying to patch up his bloodied mouth and nose and temporarily stop the bleeding. Their rushed efforts were ended by the call for the second round and the other worried looking fighter made his way up to face Kinkade again. The two slowly circled before Kinkade's opponent threw the match ending punch... but it was not match ending as it was supposed to be. The blow flew right over the top of Kellen's head and to completely silence most of the cheering Yakuza attending, Kinkade threw a right into the back of the fighters head that send him to the ground with both eyes rolling back into his skull. A loud declaration of the other fighters knocked out status send most of those attending the fight into frenzy, it wasn't often that the Yakuza lost their bets and instantly most of the gathering were rushing to the betting table to collect their winnings. Kinkade looked to Matsumoto whose mouth screamed "YOU FUCKING IDIOT!" that couldn't be heard for all the cheering, Kade quickly pushed and bustled his way to the front of the crowd before collecting enough yen to ensure he wouldn't be having to fight anytime soon, stuffing it away into his jeans he made his next move. Rushing quickly through the celebrating people who were holding their winnings high, Kinkade caught a glimpse of several Yakuza members dragging Matsumoto kicking and screaming into a limo, he could also see several more of them trying to push their way to him but that didn't stop him from hopping onto the nearest car and making a leap for the fire escape to freedom. As his hands gripped the bottom rung he realized that one thing hadn't been a factor in his grand scheme. Rain. He felt his grip slip and he felt himself fall and hit the floor hard. It was the last thing he'd feel for a while.
