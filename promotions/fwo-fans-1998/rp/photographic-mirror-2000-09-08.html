Photographic Mirror

8 September 2000
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
A mirror is the photograph of the present and ever changing future. A photograph shows the events that had transpired in the past. The good times and the bad times of life. Whether it be war, a joyous celebration a photograph can bring out a thousand words. Every single person who looks at a photograph will have a varied opinion. Their meanings are all different and they are all right in certain ways. On the other hand, a mirror shows the reflection of the present, the now. Everything about it is clear, a perfect representation of what is actually happening in front of one's eyes. The present of the mirror can be a tool for improvement, or a shameless self-battering of harassment towards oneself. The mirror tells no lies, a person can look directly into their own eyes, they can't even deceive themselves. The mirror is the truth. He stood in front of his dresser, a large oval mirror hung on the wall and a framed photograph of a female sat on the surface. His hands were pressed down on the edge of the dresser, his body leaned over, his face staring into the mirror. The room was nearly pitch black, except for a small scentless candle that was on a small hanger that came out from the wall. The words came out mumbled, nearly crossed together. "Where did it all go wrong?" His eyes moved down towards the small three-by-five photograph of the young female. A giant smile was across her face, her hazel eyes beaming. The small glance at the photograph brought a slight, crooked smile to his face. "She was always holding her true feelings in," he remembered. "The smile, those bright eyes, they were just a coverup for everything that was wrong in her life. Everything that she wanted to talk to me about, but she couldn't. She wouldn't. She was too hesitant and those who hesitate are already lost." He let out a long sigh. "But it was me who was lost. She hesitated and it gave her an opening to take advantage of everything I had. She left me with nothing, this... empty feeling I've had since last year. She left me for dead, my core rotting from the inside." His left arm swept across the top of the dresser. The photograph and it's frame went sliding across the top and it eventually met the edge and fell to the floor. The glass of the frame shattered, then broke. "Everything breaks in the end," he murmured. "Nothing can stay perfect forever. Not a friendship or a relationship. Nothing. Once it's broken, it can be fixed, but it will never be the same again. I hope she realizes that..." He trailed off in mid-sentence. He had started to stare at his face, his steel blue eyes locking with their reflection. A slight frown formed across his face. "I try to look at my face everyday, but I can't. I'm so repulsed by everything I see. What was I created for? What purpose do I serve anybody? I'm just useless, my face is shallow and meaningless. People who see me everyday, just see me as another face in the crowd. Someone they've never seen before in their lives until I go out and kill somebody to make the front page of the Detroit Free Press. That's not going to happen though. I'd rather be nameless and meaningless. I have no purpose to anybody or anything. I can stare into the mirror everyday and tell myself how useless I am and I'll take it..." He took a deep breath and looked deeper into his on eyes. "Because it's the truth. And the mirror never lies to me."
