Somewhere I Belong - Slither (Four)

19 April 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Few people ever 'got' Amy Campbell. Whether it was her parents, peers, authority figures or the police - one look at her seemed to convince everybody that she was doomed to fail in anything. Thanks to that, she spent most of her life trying to prove them all wrong, which usually succeeded... Until one of those people decidedly brought it crashing down. People in positions of power in her life - of which there were few - tended to enjoy using that power to help satisfy their opinions of Amy, to bring her independant streak to the very brink and back again. This is why she made sure few people had that power, but some things were inevitable. Things like land lords. "What the fuck is this bullshit?!" Amy'd never gotten to know her land lord - another deliberate move. They were up close and personal now, though: She was wagging an Eviction Notice with his signature about a centimeter from the button tip of his chubby nose... ...And he was smiling?! "It's an eviction notice, of course!" Ray Conrad grinned. He rarely interacted with Amy and, judging by her exterior, was safe to keep it that way. "It means you can be off of my property within the next twenty four hours, or I can have you arrested." "I know that!" "Oh, that's right. Sorry about that, I forgot you probably have a record about as long as that roll of new carpeting I put down last month. Which, by the way, you owe me two hundred dollars for staining with alcohol." "Fuck you." "I'm married." "This isn't fair!" "Life isn't fair." "Fu---" No, you already used that one. "GOD, I hate people like you." "Live with it." He smiled arrogantly, "You stomp around at all hours of the night. You blast that cursed tripe you call music constantly...oh, and you're a week late on rent." "I was in England!." "Then you should've paid earlier. When you pay to live under my roof, you live by my rules...and my rules happen to say that if you're late, you're out." She was about to speak again, about to give some sort of defense or fling the best insult she could muster...but Amy was defeated. When she'd said 'I hate people like you', she'd had no idea how appropriate it was until Ray used the 'While you live under my roof...' line. He was the mirror image of her parents, her father especially - Just another self-appointed authority figure on a power trip. Come to think of it, everybody who pulled her strings like that was a spitting image of her parents... ...And now she had to go back, to live under their roof - by their rules - all over again.
