Abuse of Power - Part 1

11 August 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Samuel Rocca was third generation rich. His grandfather, Dr. Martin Rocca, had started the family fortune through hard work and cutthroat negotiations. Blessed with a fearsome scowl and a short temper, Martin Rocca was a menace in the board room. Menace if he was sitting across the table from you. If he was on your side, you could sleep soundly under a blanket of security. Sam's father, Dr. Anderson Rocca, followed suit and added to the family's portfolio. He was an intelligent man who had his mother's patience but his father's desire to live the good life. In only ten years he'd doubled the fortune left to him by Martin Rocca through his business contacts and his lucrative medical practice. Money wasn't left to waste with Anderson. He indulged himself with the finest vices money could buy - from drugs, to alcohol, to sex, Anderson partook in them all. No one knew about this dark side of the good doctor. Not so much because he chose to hide it, but because he felt it wasn't anyone's business. No one had done what he'd done, could do what he did. He was a lord among commoners and they were all beneath him. He felt no shame in snorting cocaine off the chest of a ten year old girl in Thailand. His corruptions were somehow noble because they were his corruptions. When Anderson decided it was time to continue the family line, he took a wife who bore him a son, Samuel. The woman Anderson married was of no consequence to him. Her existence in his manor was only to carry his seed for nine months and give him a son. Once Samuel was born, her duties were that of babysitter and caregiver. So inconsequential was she that from her son's first minutes until the day she killed herself nine years later, Samuel never even knew her real name. Early on, it was evident that Sam had adopted qualities from both his father and his grandfather - Martin's temper and Anderson's lust for the forbidden. When Sam was eight years old, he was expelled from Saint Mary's Academic Institute for bludgeoning a classmate with a statue of Jesus that sat on a shelf in one of his classrooms. His father tried everything to convince Dean Edmondson to let his son remain with the renowned Institute. Many of the school's graduates went on to great things in many distinguished fields. That was the future Anderson saw for his son. He wasn't going to be denied the glory of a successful son. If Sam was going to be a success in life, it wouldn't be at the Institute. Edmondson slid a thick folder across his desk and presented it to Dr. Rocca. "These are the recorded incidents that your son has had with other students in his short stay here. While we appreciate your generosity towards the Institute, we cannot in good conscious allow him to remain. He's a danger to others, and potentially to himself. Our resident councilor feels that Sam might benefit from professional attention to deal with his emotional issues." The peasants. They couldn't understand that Sam was frustrated by having to deal with them. They didn't deserve to breathe the same air a Rocca did. The good Doctor hired tutors to school the child in his own home. For ten years, Samuel's world was with tutors. He had little interaction with people outside his family, and by the time he turned eighteen his lack of social skills was painfully clear. He was demanding, overbearing, and filled with a narcissistic rage. During his tenure in medical school, Sam often felt himself slighted. He was quiet, and people mistook his loathing for interaction with them as shyness. But like his father, Sam partook in vice. By his second year, he had amassed almost a cult-like following of other drug users. He got the best and sold the best. The godlike status he felt fed his ego like gasoline to a bonfire. The sheep were there to be used. The mouth breathers weren't even human. Which led him to being placed in the psych ward of his father's hospital. For Sam, taking care of these so-called people gave him the same rush his father got in a Bangkok brothel. These were barely people. They were broken. Nobody wanted them. They were like toys to him, and Sam enjoyed playing with them. The rush of power he felt when he'd beat them, maimed them, raped them, was better than any drug. And now Sam was getting ready for another fix.
