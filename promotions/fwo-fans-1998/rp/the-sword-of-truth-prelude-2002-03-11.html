The Sword Of Truth, Prelude

11 March 2002
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Detective Leonard Braswell sipped his coffee, inwardly cursing as the too-hot beverage scorched his tongue. This was the last thing he needed, particularly after dealing with the steroid monkey again. "That's not a fair judgment!" his conscience screamed at him. He glanced over to the interrogation room, where the "steroid monkey" - Ric Chronos - was being questioned again, this time by Braswell's partner, Lt. Geena Richards. So far, he had refused to answer with more than a "yes" or "no" to anything he was asked, and what should have been an open and shut case had taken more than a week even to process. Even though the blood tests they had given Chronos upon his arrests showed no trace of steroids, it was hard to find another reason why the guy would have raged like that - on national television, no less - and then refuse to explain himself, or even speak with one of the half-dozen lawyers the fWo had sent over to work on the case. Braswell sighed inwardly - he could tell through the one-way mirror that Richards wasn't getting anywhere. They had hoped that Richards - assets - would perhaps loosen him up some. Braswell knew that he was intimidated by Richards looks, but looking down at the slightly less than trim stomach that was only barely contained by his dress shirt and brown tie, well, he figured that he could probably be intimidated by a lot of things. If only something would give. Hell, Chronos could be in and out of the grand jury hearing if he plead self-defense, and if he didn't walk away scot-free, Chronos would at best get probation ro community service. But the guy's adamant refusal to cooperate was just extending this entire thing well beyond reasonable limits, and for no apparent reason. If Braswell didn't know better, he would say that he thought Chronos might be hiding something. Chronos' record only held a DUI and one Public Intoxication, both of which seemed to have been resolved after Chronos started attending AA meetings. He took another sip, and looked up, as another detective entered the observation room. "You working on the Chronos case?" Braswell nodded in affirmation. "You might want this. Just came across the fax for you." Braswell started to gulp down another swallow, but stopped midway, staring at the sheets of paper passed over to him, and a stoic grin crossed his face - right before the coffee seared his throat. "Gotcha, sucker." Braswell stormed into the interrogation room, interrupting Richards in mid-sentence. "So, Mr. Chronos - you want to tell me about Huang Chin, known Chinese tong boss working on American soil?" "No." "I think you will - here's a lovely picture of you and him from a few months back in Chicago. That ring any bells?" "No." "What if I told you the would-be assassin was under Chin's employ, eh? What would you have to say to that, Mr. Chronos?" Chronos' face remained an impassive mask, and if the detective had scored a hit, it would not be reflected in Ric's expression. "I think that I would like to speak with my lawyer now."
