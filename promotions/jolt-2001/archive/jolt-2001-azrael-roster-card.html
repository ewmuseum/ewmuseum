Time: 5: 30 PM, EST Date: January 5, 1994 Where have I come from and where have I been. Where was this started and what have I seen? What do you want to know? My past? Why? What worth does it have on the here and now? Oh well, I guess you’re persistent. Annoying, too. But that’s beside the point. My life started at nine. Yep, before 1983 I haven’t a memory except a locket and tales, none of which are my own. Maybe I blocked it out. I think Froid, or some such other wack-job, would call it repression . . . or whatever the hell he’d call it. I know I’ve sure as hell met enough of those idiots in my life to last five more life-times. Oh, you wonder why? Well, first time was in ’80, except I don’t remember it. Phelan told me ‘bout it a lot, when I was younger. Oh, yeah, Phelan’s my father- an idiotic, backwards, drunken sot. Hey, maybe I do take after him a little bit, ‘eh? But yeah, enough of these anecdotes and side-tracks. I was hospitalized at the age of six the for the first time, psychological, that is. Elizabeth, that is to say my mother, died around that time. I don’t really know what caused that, probably her death. She was pretty good, although I don’t remember her well. All I do know is that, before her death, Phelan was a semi-decent, if a little sadistic. Afterwards . . . well, we’ll get to that, won’t we? So, I’ve been told I spent a couple months in a hospital at the age of six. Onto the future. Three years later, as I lived with my father, I began to realize what a truly sick, and twisted, fuck he was. He’d always blame me, for one, for Elizabeth’s death. According to him, and I’m not one to say otherwise, for I don’t remember, I knocked over some candle or some-such. She ended up burnt to death. Can I get a light, Doc? Thanks, I appreciate it. See, I hate to talk about this shit, you know. I don’t even remember her, Christ. How fucked up is that? I do remember Phelan, though. See these scars on my hand, my good man? Well, I have Phelan to thank. Ah, you look so disgusted, well, it wasn’t by my choosing. Of course, he used to say I brought it upon myself. I’ve learned every twisted fuck like him says that. Always your fault, you weren’t good, you’re an evil child- At this point the patient got violent forcing us to continue three days later. Time: 5:30 PM, EST. Date: January 8th, 1994 Ah, nice fresh bruises you got there, Doc. Well, anyway. I was talking about Phelan, right? Ah, yes. Dear ol’ dad. He had a stash of coke, you know. And, no, I’m not talking about the soda. Well, at fourteen, I found that stash. Every week I’d snag just a lil’ more till I’d built up a tidy little baggy of my own. You know the street value of a few grams of coke? I see, you don’t. A gram goes for one hundred dollars. Guess you didn’t know that. Well, I did. I had about three grams of coke, by the time two months had passed. Sold it, made a tidy chunk of change. Even with me snagging a little from it for my self. What, you’re shocked? I’m sure you’ve heard worse, so don’t even feign that bullshit with me. Hell, my cellmate Payday was into heroin at twelve. Oh well, that’s all you idiots do anyway, aren’t I right? Sit there, look compassionate, bah. Just a bunch of little wastes, anyway. Oh, you want me to get on with my story. Okay, I guess so. A few months passed, I turned fifteen, and Phelan found me out. Oh man, that was ------ up. Oh, can I say ------ up? Or will that offend your little recorder? Anyway, I barely came out of that situation with my life. I probably should’ve been hospitalized- I think I fractured a few ribs that day. But, ‘course, I didn’t go to the hospital. I think my chest was black and blue for two months. Well, I dropped out of school at sixteen. Was gone from Phelans' house before then, however. I think one of us would’ve ended up dead if I stayed there. And, while it wouldn’t’ve been a shame, I still didn’t want to go to jail. I ended up here anyway, didn’t I? So, dropped out of school, moved out. What next. Ah, yes. I got involved with a small time wrestling corporation. Nothin’ big. It was a pay-check. Maybe my depression helped, ‘eh, doc? ‘Cause I know I sure as hell didn’t give a damn what happened to me, for sure. Broke a few ribs, ------ up my knee, uncountable concussions. The sick fans loved it. It was the first time I’d ever been in front of a large audience. I’d never been a people person, still ain’t, hell, that’s what got me here. I dislike and distrust others. I find’em to be stupid sheep, actually. Don’t worry, doc, I think the same of you. So, I wrestled. Met up with a girl a few years ago, she became my valet. Sweet thing. Hell, we were almost married, know that? I still have the ring, even. Of course, I should say will when I get out. Engaged to be married. Semi-successful in my wrestling. Hell, I see you lookin’ at my arms. Do you know how much time and money these things were? A lot. A helluva lot. But, I could afford it. As I said, I was pretty successful. Things couldn’t get any better. And, damn, they didn’t. I was at a bar some night, five years ago. Mother ------‘ drunk bastard started to grope my girl. She turned him down and the mother ------ put his hand up her dress! God damn, I’d had eight shots by that time and I was in no mood to be screwed with. So, grabbing the shot glass, I jammed it right into the mother ------s face. Ground it in, to tell you the truth. Man, how he screamed. Blood came right down his face. Some weird --- liquid dribbled down too, clear. Oh, that was his eye? Oh well. What, you don’t want me to continue, Doc? Why not, you asked for my history, so here it is. I dragged the ass outside, slammed his face into the concrete. Don’t remember much from then on out. I do remember waking up in the prison cell, my head burning, ribs aching. Oh, that was the police? Did I do anything to them? Oh well, guess you don’t want to tell me. You know the rest, Doc. I got sent up here after a short trial. Haven’t heard from my ex-fiancee in awhile. Don’t expect to, to tell you the truth. Maybe she got some sense into herself.. I guess that’s all. Can I get another cigarette from you? Thanks. Time: 12:30 PM, EST Date: August 20, 2001 So, I’m about ready to leave this place, ‘eh, Doc? Hope I don’t depress you, but I damn well want to not see your face again. Good, it doesn’t upset you, I’m glad. What the hell are these? Oh, some Tranqs? Cool, maybe I’ll end up using them. So, what have you diagnosed me as, good doctor? You know my record, I’m sure, and you know I’ve been diagnosed as everything under the sun. Why try? I’ve been ADD, ADHD, Manic-Depressive, Depressive, Schizo, Multiple personalities. You going to add your own little interpretation into the melting pot that is Azrael? Damn it, you ---hole, don’t call me that. He’s ------‘ dead, I’ve told you that before. Explained it so many times that it seems I’ll never stop. He died, Doc. Caleb died years ago. He’s nothing- he’s a ghost of my childhood, a memory I’d prefer to forget. What, you think it’s unhealthy? Well, screw you too, Doc. It’s my life, my mind, and I can do with it what I what. So just back off. Good. What, you want me to take this? What the hell is it? Fluanxol? And that is . . .? Oh my god, an anti-psychotic? I guess you do think I’m crazy, don’t you. Oh, you think I’m schizo, too? Oh well, you’re not the first to think so. Probably not the last, either, truth be told. Who cares, though. Soon enough I’ll be out of this shit, into the big time. Didn’t I tell you? Some group, jot or jolt or something or other, contacted me. They want me to wrestle again. Yeah, I know, it’s awesome news. I won’t have to look for a job, thank god. So, doc, this’ll be the last time I bum a cigarette from you. Thanks, I appreciate it. Well, Doc Alecard, I better be going. Put the ---- I’ve got into boxes.
