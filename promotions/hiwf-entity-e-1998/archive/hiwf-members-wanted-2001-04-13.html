HIWF (High Impact Wrestling Federation, 2001) — Rules

Members Wanted

13 April 2001
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Anyone interested, please contact [detail withheld] . HIWF RULEBOOK v. 1.0 I. What is the HIWF? The High Impact Wrestling Federation, or HIWF, is an e-wrestling league. If you don't know what this is, you might stop on by the usenet newsgroup rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy, where the e-wrestling FAQ is posted quite frequently. The HIWF is a booked league, meaning roleplays and angles determine who wins and loses. However, to make things easier on our writers, we simulate matches using the TNM 7 simulator, created by Oliver Copp and available at tnm7.com . TNM allows for good wrestler personalization as well as a wide variety of gimmick and non-gimmick matches. For those of you who worry about simmed feds, the sim is used only a guide for our writers, who have the right to change the logfile to fit the storyline of the match or for match flow purposes (and will probably have to for matches to make sense). This is the second version of the HIWF. The first version had their first card on April 4, 1997, and ran successfully until August 20, 2000, when it merged with the Supreme Wresling Federation to form the Unified Wrestling Federation. The SWF officially folded on April 1, 2001, and with nothing to unify, the HIWF is once again a separate entity. The current HIWF will keep title histories of the old HIWF, as well as the SWF and UWF, up on their website, but they will also be a separate entity from the current HIWF. Like all e-mail wrestling leagues, The HIWF is supposed to be entertaining, you know...fun? If you take stuff like this seriously then you are weird and strange and we don't want to know you. If you honestly find someone's flash offensive, mail President J. Adam Hawkins at [detail withheld] , and say so, it will be given honest consideration. So above all else, we want you to have fun, and if anything interferes with that, we will do our best to remedy the situation, as soon as it is brought to our attention. II. Basic Guidelines IIa. Wrestler control You are now allowed to control either one singles wrestler or one tag team, male or female. We do ask that male handlers handle male wrestlers and female handlers handle female wrestlers if at all possible, but at this time that is not required. If you choose to control a singles wrestler, you will be allowed to team up with another singles wrestler to form a tag team. Please bear in mind that anybody controlling a tag team must fill out two applications, one for each wrestler. IIb. Flashing While the HIWF uses the TNM 7 simulator, flashing is also very important. To put it bluntly, if you don't flash, you probably won't win very often. This is league partially booked. That means, if the bookers feel certain people aren't flashing enough, or with enough quality, they will lose, whether or not that's the result given by the simulator. You will not be told when this is done, but for the most part it probably won't be hard to figure out. Every match won't be booked, if both participants are actively flashing in the period before their match, the sim will determine the winner. We're not going to play favorites. If both wrestlers are making an honest effort to actively participate, the winner will be simulator based. However, if one guy is doing witty, 50 line flashes, and the other is responding with repetitive 2 line flashes, the one who puts more time and thought into the league will be booked to win. There are too many simulator-run leagues where the participants who put a lot of time into their characters and role-playing lose to those that chose a better list of moves when creating their wrestler. IIb1. What if I don't flash? If we see a wrestler who hasn't flashed for several weeks (and he hasn't explained to the president why he isn't flashing), a warning will be issued, that if they don't start flashing, they will be de-pushed. They will have a week to get their butt in gear and start participating, or they will be either be dropped form the league due to inactivity and/or only used as jobbers to get active wrestlers over. We don't want to be mean, but the point of e-wrestling is to get involved. If you don't say anything, no one cares about your wrestler, and you miss out on the best part of e-wrestling, the interaction between the wrestlers. IIb2. How do I flash? Flashes are to be sent out to everyone in the league, fedheads included. Flashes are sent to the entire mailing list by sending an e-mail to [detail withheld] . Flashes should not contain other wrestlers without the handler's consent. If we see people attacking each other physically in flashes (without consent), we will ask that people disregard the flash and will warn the person not to do so again. Swearing will be allowed, but try to keep it reasonable. I've seen people swear every other word, and it just ends up being a horrible flash. Also, remember that our shows are supposed to be televised in most cases, so you should attempt to keep the language down to what you'll what you'll usually hear on WWF Raw is War. IIb3. The Chain Letter Rule Sending a chain letter out to the HIWF mailing list will result in your immediate expulsion from the league. I'd also prefer to keep the HIWF mailing list for HIWF stuff only. Any jokes and other non-HIWF material sent to the list will be a one warning and then expulsion policy. Should you want to discuss what's actually going on in the "real" world of pro wrestling, we'll allow that in some cases, but please make sure to include "OOC" in the subject line. If the message contains results from wrestling programs, including PPVs, please put the word "spoilers" somewhere in the subject line. Also, advertisements for other e-feds may be allowed, but must be approved by the President beforehand. IIb4. The Bandwidth Rule Basically it breaks down to this. In every piece of mail you send to the HIWF mailing list, there must be at least 50% new material written by yourself. This means no quoting 15 lines to give a 2 line reply. The purpose of this is to kill the long and wasteful messages. We've already seen the flash once, and we don't need to see the entire thing again. If you need to reply to a specific quote, fine. Cut out the text you don't need, if you only have one line to write in response to 10 lines, chances are you only needed one of those ten lines to reply to. Also, if there's leftover stuff at the bottom, delete it. This includes other people's signatures. No one wants to read other people's sigs multiple times, and if you think no one wants to read it, chances are you shouldn't include it. This also applies to quoting matches. Don't quote 15 lines of text and then say "yeah, see how I dominated" or something like that. If there's something important that happened that you want to refresh people of, cut that little event. Just like in replying to other people's flashes, everyone has had the opportunity to see the match, if they didn't, they probably didn't want to. Sorry if this was a bit long winded, but I (and others) are tired of re-reading peoples flashes in a desperate attempt to find new material. Now, that being said. This rule will be a three strikes and you're out policy. I'll notify you every time I see it, and ask you to please stop. I don't want to be a jerk about this, so I'm not going to do this if you slip up and once a month for 3 months send out flashes of this type, that's not 3 strikes. But if I see it happen 3 times in a 3 week period, I probably will bust you and get rid of you. I can't think of any other way to do this. III. Cards IIIa. Weekly cards The HIWF will present two televised cards in a given week, plus periodic pay-per-views and "Clash of the Titans" spectaculars. We will also have a weekly recap show. IIIa1. HIWF Superstar Challenge This is our biggie, the Raw of the weekly shows. This show will initially be one hour and feature four or five matches. If a show gets expanded to two hours, this will be the one. IIIa2. HIWF Wrestling Warzone Our weekly Tuesday card will feature four or five matches and be approximately one hour. This would be the HIWF's version of Smackdown. NOTE: Until the HIWF is established, Wrestling Warzone will not take place. As soon as there are enough wrestlers on roster to warrant the second show, it will be added. IIIa3. HIWF Rewind Airing on Sundays, Rewind is a weekly recap show, featuring highlights of the previous week's action, as well as clips from certain flashes and interviews with the wrestlers. This show may or may not include "exclusive" matches taped one on of the weekly shows. If a pay-per-view is scheduled for a Sunday, Rewind will air live from the site of the PPV. Consider this the WWF Live Wire of the HIWF. IIIb. Special cards. "Special cards" include any and all pay-per-views, as well as the "Clash of the Titans" specials. IIIb1. Pay-per-views Approximately six times a year, the HIWF will present a pay-per-view event. These events will feature the (hopeful) conclusion of any key feud, as well as several title matches. These events are usually at least 7 matches long (though they could be less based on the type of pay-per-view event). Pay-per-views may resemble regulation cards, or they may resemble the WWF and WCW style pay-per-view cards (Royal Rumble, Survivor Series, etc.). While most feds have a strict PPV schedule during the year, only several in the HIWF are etched in stone. They are: ???: HIWF Anniversary Spectacular -This event is held to commemorate the HIWF's first card. Since this is a new version of the HIWF, that date has not been determined. This event is our version of WrestleMania. Late July-early August: HIWF Super Bowl of Wrestling -Our secondary PPV. October 31: HIWF Halloween Hell -This event is to celebrate All Hallow's Eve. This will feature more specialty matches than any other pay-per-view, as well as title matches. Other PPV events will be scheduled as time goes on. IIIb2. HIWF Clash of the Titans The HIWF will present between several special events on the Fox Sports Network entitled "Clash of the Titans". It will be used as the old Clash of the Champions events were...as a huge card to fill the gap between pay-per-views. The number of matches held will be dependent on the event, but will feature a minimum of five. Please note that this card will always be held on Tuesday nights and will air in place of Wrestling Warzone. IIIc. Other specials IIIc1. The Annihilation Awards This event will take place the day before the Anniversary Spectacular. It will be an awards show, similar to the Slammy Awards, where the handlers vote on their favorite (or least favorite) wrestler in a number of characters. IIIc2. The HIWF Charity Golf Classic To be held in the late Spring, this event is a break from the norm of e-wrestling, and will be done simply for fun and "charity". All handlers wishing to participate may do so. This is the one event where the winner is chosen at random. The wrestler's name will be placed in one hat and a score placed in the other. A wrestler and a score will be determined together, and that determines where you finish. Other than that, everything goes. IIId. House shows The HIWF will present periodic house shows. These shows are not televised and will usually be released in summary form. However, they will be "taped" and footage of any title changes will air on the next televised card. These cards may also be released as a special "home video". IV. Interference Interference is based more or less on things sent in to the particular writers. It is used to further a feud or start an angle. It can be done as a run-in during a match, or as a post-match attack. For the purposes of this rulebook, in-ring interviews and backstage vignettes will also fall into this category. All interference should be sent to [detail withheld] by noon the day before a show. IVa. Why didn't my interference go through? or Why was I included in someone else's attack? Usually, if interference is sent in but isn't used, it is due to one of four things. 1. We didn't receive it on time. 2. Somebody expected it and sent in a counter attack. 3. We received several attacks for the same spot and just couldn't keep everything separate. 4. We forgot. Very rarely does number 4 ever take place, but it does happen on occasion. The writers will make every attempt to include everything. IVb. The "fire" rule In four years of running an e-fed, you see people who want to try some strange attacks. In general, we ask you to be realistic. We call this the "fire rule" because one of the most commonly sent in attacks is to set someone on fire. If we let such an attack go and try to put it over, then we forget the fact that if it was real, the person would probably die. This is one time where just because they did it on Raw doesn't mean we're going to let you do it. Attacks that could do serious damage in real life (unless agreed upon by all parties involved) simply will not be used. V. Strategy A match strategy (or "strat") is simply what you plan on doing during the course of a match. It can be as simple as "work on the knees", or as complex as detailed spots to go into a match. The more detail the better (it could improve your chances of winning), although it's up to the writer to make it fit in with the match flow. NOTE: Just because you strat yourself doesn't mean you will. Very often your opponent will also send in detailed strats as well. Remember, the card writers as a whole will often determine the winners and losers. Detailed strats help, but are not the sole thing we go by. VI. Injuries If you're going to be out of town for any length of time (or will be unable to flash for some reason), the best way to take time off is an injury angle. This way we know not to use your wrestler until you can roleplay again, and your wrestler doesn't get used as a jobber. Send a note to President Hawkins at [detail withheld] to notify him you're leaving. You might also want to contact a certain handler about allowing his wrestler to injure you. That puts you into an automatic feud once you return. :-) VII. Ratings Ratings will be released approximately twice a month: once on the first of the month and once after every PPV or Clash. Ratings will be based on win/loss records and flash quality since the previous ratings period. VIII. Alliances Alliances (also known as stables) are allowed, but every member of your alliance must inform me of their membership, and each Alliance must have a captain, who will be in charge of collating attacks and such. Alliances will be limited to 5 handlers (between 5-10 wrestlers, depending on how many tag teams are involved). IX. Tag Teams Tag Teams are the same as alliances, both members of the team must inform me that they are forming a team. One member of the team should then send the president a note giving the name of the team and the name and description of a finisher. If you are a handler controlling a tag team, you must fill out one application for EACH wrestler, and send a team name and finisher along with it. X. Belts Face it, even though this is all meant for fun, if you weren't interested in titles you wouldn't have any reason to roleplay and remain active. These are the rewards for those who have done an exceptional job. All titles must be defended once every 30 days. A wrestler may hold a singles title and a tag team title simultaneously, but may not hold two singles titles at the same time. Titles can only change hands by pinfall or submission unless specified otherwise. Please note that only men are eligible for titles that don't have some reference to women in them. This isn't meant to be mean or sexist, but simply put, I put in a women's division to give the women an outlet. Intergender matches are fine as far as I'm concerned, but they need to be kept in moderation in order to mean something, so for the time being we're going to keep men and women in separate divisions as far as titles go. Xa. World Heavyweight Title This is the big one, the one everybody strives for. This title will almost always be defended in the main event of PPVs and Clash of the Titans events, although there are exceptions. Xb. World Tag Team Title Basically the World Heavyweight Title for tag teams. Xc. Women's Title Pretty self-explanatory here. Xd. Intercontinental Title This is the secondary men's title. Traditionally the secondary champion is the top contender to the World Heavyweight Title but rarely receives a shot. Still, holding this title generally makes that wrestler number two in world in the fans' eyes. Xe. Hardcore Title This title will usually be defended in falls count anywhere matches. However, this title may be defended in matches with almost any "hardcore" stipulation. Other titles may be added as the HIWF grows, but which titles and when they'll be added are to be determined. XI. How do I apply? You can either copy and paste the application at www.geocities.com/hiwf2001 and e-mail it to [detail withheld] , or you can send an e-mail at the same address and ask for one to fill out. XII. How will I know I'm accepted? If accepted, you will be sent an e-mail congratulating you on your entry. Shortly thereafter, you will be sent an invitation to join the mailing list at yahoogroups. The introductory roleplay will be put up on the mailing list either in the next card or at the next Rewind, whichever comes first. At that point, you may begin flashing.
