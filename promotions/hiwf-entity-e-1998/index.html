Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

This High Impact Wrestling Federation dates its own first card to 4 April 1997 and describes itself, in a 29 May 1998 recruitment ad, as having “been running ever since.” It ran partially booked, on a TNM simulator whose result was overridden by roleplay only when one side's was excellent or neither wrestler sent one, carrying about twenty wrestlers against a cap of thirty-two, three cards a week, and eight to ten pay-per-views a year.

On 31 May 1998 its president wrote to a newsgroup audience thanking readers for praising a recent golf-themed card and announcing that the federation would begin posting its weekly broadcasts there from that Friday on — “a task that is not easy when you've been running for over a year.”

Its titles turned over constantly through the second half of 1998, across a World Heavyweight, a United States, a Television and a Cruiserweight Championship. Its Parade of Champions pay-per-view, 21 June 1998, crowned a new World Champion when a masked “King Pinnacle” Tim S. Hersh upset Nighthawk with the Legion of Pain's interference, unmasking himself the next night as Mr. Shh!

That November its own on-screen authority figure, President J. Adam Hawkins, stripped the Night Stalker of the Television Championship for holding it alongside the World Heavyweight Championship at once, against the federation's own one-title rule, and turned a tournament already announced to find a new World Title contender into an on-the-spot Television Title tournament instead — won by “Stone Cold” Steve Anderson, who had wrestled it masked as Tyrone the Psychopathic Soldier.

Several other title changes across the year are known only from a wrestler's own later on-air claim or a champion's billing on a later card, with no document showing the match itself. The federation's last held card, 20 November 1998, books a rematch for 12 December; how it went isn't in the surviving record.
