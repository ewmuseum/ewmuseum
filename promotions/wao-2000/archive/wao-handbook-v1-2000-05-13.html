What is the WAO? 1.1. Formation of the WAO *Kayfabe* GEN-X TV ... A new national cable network, has become quite popular on television with its popular syndicated re-runs such as Married with Children, and other raunchy broadcasts, has witnessed the popularity of wrestling in which television ratings have skyrocketed in the past three years. Trevor
Fleming, GEN-X TV's kingpin, has visioned instead of allowing a promotion to buy a time slot on its network, has decided to create his own ...
_the WAO_. WAO ... Wrestling Anarchy Organization was planted into developemental stages. Being that Fleming doesn't know much about the wrestling business, he has hired a crack staff of workers to build the WAO to a promising promotion. He found now WAO President, Richard Perry. A former talent scout and mastermind for hundreds of independent promotions, was immediately signed to a lucrative contract to run day-o-day operations.
Now with Perry hired, he has traveled the world over for the best crew he could work with, and he's found it. Now all there's needed are talented, charismatic
wrestlers in which wrestling fans would love to watch every week. *Non-Kayfabe* Some of you might know who I am, some of you don't. For those who don't know, i'm [name withheld], former fed head of many different promotions. Now that i've been away for awhile, I feel that I have matured enough to become a highly-dedicated fed head once again. I have produced great feds such as DYNASTY in 1998-1999,
SPWA in 1997, and others. Now I feel that e-wrestling has become quite boring, I hope that I can _liven_ it up to bring it back to what it used to be. 1.2. How to make the WAO successful (credit goes to [name withheld]) 3.1 Roleplaying The WAO does not impose a limit on the number of flashes from each character per week. _HOWEVER_, chances are if you are scheduled for action and don't send in a flash, you won't be getting a check mark in the win column. Anyone who fails to produce flashes regularly, or remains silent for long periods of time, will be ejected from the league. In actual flashes and role-plays, swearing should be kept to a minimum. Unless absolutely necessary (and by that, agreement on the management's part is required), nothing stronger than ass should be said. If necessary, use [Bleep] in place of most swearing. Nothing that could get a show kicked off the air by the FCC should be used (racism, constant swearing, etc.). Wrestlers are _NOT_ permitted to physically attack any and all officials, ring announcers, television announcers or interview personalities _UNLESS_ you receive permission. Stylized text is _NOT_ used in the WAO. One exclamation point is usually enough, and adding extra ones will only hold up the shows as they're edited out anyway.
3.2 Strategies Strategies, or strats for short, are needed in order to win matches. It's up to you which kind of strategy you send in, a long drawn out one or a simple paragraph or two. "I wanna win, 'cause it'd be cool," is _NOT_ a strategy.
Focus on your opponent's weakness, and explain how your wrestler plans to wrestle against them. Failure to send in strats for long periods of time will result in a league ejection, much like non-flashing wrestlers.
3.3 Angles/Match Booking The mark of a good league is how well it's storylines and angles are thought out. Angles are what determines in most cases how and what matches are scheduled for the week. Flashes and strategies are dependent on good angles, otherwise proposed attacks may not be run unless they're for a specific purpose.
The easiest way to get matches: ask for them! Sending in challenges to other wrestlers are looked favorably on, and in most cases will be scheduled whenever possible. Again angles come into play, and those challenges that seem to "make sense" will be given preference in schedule dating. Title matches usually are given to those who prove themselves in past matches, and simply asking for a title match without reason (or storyline) won't get you one, guaranteed.
