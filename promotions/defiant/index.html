An angle-booked federation that built itself a rulebook, three championships and a Hall of Fame page, announced two shows at Madison Square Garden and the Arrowhead Pond in 2003, and left no result of either. Twenty-one wrestlers are recorded from its two roster snapshots and staff pages. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Defiant Wrestling wrote its rules down first. Eight of them, in plain order: apply through the form and do not roleplay until the President writes back; no formal contracts, because your personal life takes priority, but honour the verbal ones; profanity and sexuality are fine and racial slurs are not; roleplays have no length minimum or maximum, and a ten-line one usually will not get the job done. It is a federation talking to its own handlers about how it intends to behave, and it is the longest single piece of writing the promotion left behind.

It also says outright what kind of federation it was: an angle-based one, whose President enjoyed creating exciting and intricate storylines. None of those storylines survives. Its own history page, its headlines page, its columns page and its hardcore page were all captured as empty shells; four of its log files came back as genuine, zero-byte files; its Hall of Fame page has a heading and nothing under it.

Twice it announced a show called Wrath. The first, on 26 March 2003, was four standard matches at a Madison Square Garden it spelled with an extra letter, one of them booked against a slot its own roster still listed as Vacant. The second, on 9 July, was at the Arrowhead Pond in Anaheim, and promised that the owner would be on hand to reveal the details and demands of what should be expected in his new company, and that the audience would meet the announce team. Both results pages were crawled. Both came back with nothing in them.

In between, the whole federation moved. It had been at dwrestling.theewe.net since February; by June it was a hosted sub-site of 360° Wrestling Entertainment, running the same design and the same show, with a fresh roster of five names and five slots it had not filled. Only Capo made the crossing, from a nine-man roster to a five-man one, booked on both Wrath cards four months and a continent apart. The staff page carried over the owner and nobody else, and gave the office of President to a different man than the rulebook had.

Three championships are named on its own title-history index — a World Title, a set of Tag Team Titles and a Hardcore Title — each with a page of its own that nobody ever captured. No champion of any of them is named anywhere in what survives, and neither announced card puts a belt on the line.
