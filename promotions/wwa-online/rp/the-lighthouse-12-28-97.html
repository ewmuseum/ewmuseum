The Lighthouse (12.28.97)

28 December 1997
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
The Lighthouse by Lucien Chateaux As we approach the new year and the return of the WWA with the Born Again PPV, there are many questions still unanswered: Why the hiatus and threatened shutdown of the WWA? And, when the return was announced, why the mass exodus of top-flight talent? I have taken advantage of my considerable contacts in the wrestling community to line up interviews with some of the major former talent of the WWA and hopefully get some answers. I present for you here the first of these interviews, obtained yesterday with the inimitable "Lightning" Larry Castle. Larry Castle burst on the wrestling scene in 1987 at the age of 24 in the now-defunct Stormy River Wrestling promotion. The elder son of famed referee Frank Castle, he grew up around wrestling his entire life. Popular and talented, he rose quickly in the ranks, and teamed up with his younger brother Andrew once Andrew entered the promotion a year later. They won the SRW tag belts on two occasions in 1989-1990. Set for an SRW title shot against Rip Roberts in 1991, a leg injury forced Larry out of the match, and he was replaced by his brother, who won the belt. Larry split with his brother soon after and went to wrestle full time in the Texas Wrestling Federation. He formed "Bad Apples" with Cecil Washington in late 1991, having gone rulebreaker. Bad Apples joined with the rest of Rancher T.T. North's stable in 1992 and Larry met "Deadeye" Dave Wilson. It was during this alliance that the roots of The Band of The Hand were formed, the metaphor of a hand clenched into a fist showing up more and more in both Wilson's and Castle's interviews. T.T. North's stable fell apart, and eventually Bad Apples did as well, when Washington refused to attack a downed opponent. A bitter feud evolved and Washington defeated Castle in a Loser Leaves Town match in the fall of '93. Castle went to Europe and improved his standing, signed with ILCW when it formed in early 1994. He and Wilson were reunited, feuding with High Impact: Knights of Armageddon and The Kenyan Lion. Good luck got him a title shot at Fidel for the ILCW World Title in October, 1994. Castle took the belt and held it until dropping the title in early 1995 to Tiger Moon. Castle and Wilson began their epic feud with Jeff Ladd, which resulted in the formation of the Band of The Hand as a major force. Castle would briefly hold the tag team titles with Wilson and then later the European title and hold it for much of 1995. The Band of the Hand went through different incarnations while and Castle was without titles throughout 1996. Castle completed the Grand Slam in 1997, taking the ILCW International title. In late 1997, Castle stunned the world by showing up in the WWA with his ILCW International title and claiming to have restored the Band of the Hand with Wilson, Arron Stone and his brother Andrew. The group would run roughshod over the WWA and eventually add "Perfect" Tommy Chapman to the ranks. The WWA decided to recognise Castle's title as the Intercontinental title and then made him defend it against Chris Page. Castle and Page traded the belt before Castle finally lost the IC title to "Nightmare" Nick Steele in a "Crucifixion" match at Halloween Horrorshow '97. The WWA then went on hiatus, amid wild rumours of financial problems and backstage politics. When the WWA announced it's return, Larry Castle, Dave Wilson and Arron Stone were *not* on the list of wrestlers still with the promotion. I caught up with Castle at his home in Vancouver, British Columbia. ************************************************************************ Chateaux: I'll start with the most obvious. Why did you leave the WWA? Castle: Lack of respect. Let me rephrase that. Lack of good faith. The simple fact is that the WWA higher ups did not want me there. In their minds, the "cons" of me there, outweighed the "pros". Chateaux: And what were those? Castle: The pros were that I was a world class wrestler, I lent the belts and matches prestige, and that I am a solid draw who knows how to entertain a crowd. The cons were that a lot of the muckety-mucks don't like me, and that I scare the other wrestlers. Plus, the Band of the Hand was controlling all the gold, and was dictating what happened too much in the WWA. We could not be denied shots or titles, and we had too good a chance of winning that gold and holding onto it. Chateaux: So they asked you to leave? Castle: Not at all. They tried to push us out. Last minute changes on booking our flights, cramped quarters, insufficient physical therapy, even veiled threats from other wrestlers. Chateaux: Wrestlers threaten each other all the time, you can't mean-- Castle: There's in ring and out of ring threats, Lucien, you know that. You should've heard the talk in the backstage locker room. 'If the Band of the Hand shows up in my match, I'm going to do this,' 'If I see the Band anywhere, I'm coming out after them.' Things like that. Chateaux: I thought you *wanted* the Band of the Hand to scare the other wrestlers. Castle: (laughs) Absolutely. But we scared them enough to have them put aside differences to worry about us. You might say we were a victim of our own success. Add that with the stress of knowing that management is hoping you have a reason to leave, an injury, something, -- it becomes too much stress. Conditions before Halloween Horrorshow were at an all time low. I no longer felt safe working in that environment. Out of ring. Chateaux: So let's go to the Horrorshow. You lost to "Nightmare" Nick Steele. Are you blaming that loss on the WWA management? Castle: Not at all. Steele beat me, pure and simple. I underestimated him. I knew he couldn't match me in a straight wrestling match, and given his babyface ways, I didn't think he could manage toe-to-toe with me on the outside. I was wrong. He's better at getting down and dirty than I gave him credit for, and I paid the price. It wouldn't happen again. Any re-match, straight wrestling or brawling, and I would have the belt around my waist again. The backstage conditions were tangential to me losing that belt. Chateaux: And after Horrorshow? Castle: I went to the man with the biggest problem with us, Sebastian Biggs. He's the main board of directors guy who deals with the wrestlers. I laid out my complaints. He told me that I had no proof they were trying to push me out and that I had a contract. If I wanted to break it, it was on my legal shoulders. Then he warned me that accidents happen and I should study the insurance rules in these matters. Chateaux: and what did you do? Castle: I sued the league. That's one of the main reasons for the hiatus, they wanted this hush and hush and they were seriously worried about capital. Chateaux: Legislation is still pending, I presume? Castle: Settled. I can't discuss details. But they and I can both go our merry ways now. But due to my suit, everyone had their contracts re-examined and there are no longer any long-term agreements with restrictive clauses in the WWA. And a lot of guys took the opportunity to walk from an organisation that treated them like expendable meat. Chateaux: But many stayed. Castle: Everyone isn't treated the same way. Chateaux: Of those who stayed, two are members of the Band of the Hand. "Perfect" Tommy Chapman and your brother. Castle: Tommy was never truly Hand. He's got the talent, he's got the drive, he's got the attitude. I don't let losers into the Band, Chapman is not a loser. But the core of the Band of the Hand has always been a family thing. The Castles, the Wilsons and the Stones. Everyone else is only there to fill out spaces necessary. We needed a light-heavyweight, we got ourselves one. The fact he decided to stay makes no difference to me one way or the other. Chateaux: And your brother? Castle: Another story. The fact is, he's a big boy, he can make his own decisions. I would have liked some solidarity, sure, but he's got issues with Page. It's a personal thing. He takes that feud way too close to his heart, and it eats at him. He needs to work through all that. Chateaux: People have wondered about some of the other talent that left the WWA. Did they leave in solidarity, as you put it? Castle: Dave and Arron did, sure. But not the rest. I think the Oddball was let go and not re-signed as too unstable. I'm not sure why Doug Christian didn't come back, although I suspect it was an offer from Jack Morgan to sign with the new Canadian promotion assuming that gets off the ground. That's where Morgan is, obviously, teaching. The Kenyan Lion is in Kenya. Chateaux: Wrestling? Castle: Nope, politics. He was always talking about Kenya backstage. Rattling on about his dad and the strikes, the struggle for human rights, etc. The big election. So, as part of my plans for softening him up for Andrew, I started getting on his case about it. If he was such a supporter of the people, what was he doing living in luxury while his family struggled for worker's rights in the streets, in front of police guns? Stuff like that. He never could resist arguing with me, even if he was afraid to step in the ring with me. SO I worked my way under his skin. That and with me giving Andrew the strategy tips he needed got the Band the world title. But when the hiatus came, the Lion took all my shots to heart, and last I heard he went back home to join his dad. He may never wrestle again. Chateaux: Speaking of retired wrestlers, what do you think of Jeff Ladd doing commentary for the WWA? Castle: It's stupid. The man should be in the ring. I may not like Jeff Ladd, we feuded for two years straight it seems, but he belongs in a ring, not behind a microphone. So, he had to fight a woman. Big deal, it's the 90's Jeff, get over it. Athena's a damn tough woman, anyway. Even if he lost that match, so what? He had a bad day. Big deal. The promoters had you do something stupid, so you go off into your room and sulk? That's idiotic. He needs to get over this obsession he has with the whole "Living Legend" Jeff Ladd thing and don the tights. He's still got 1 or 2 good years in him. Hell if he goes back to the WWA with me not there he's got 4 or 5, maybe. Chateaux: Well, that brings me to the next question. What next for Larry Castle? Castle: A break for a bit. One of the reasons I came back to North America is because Deborah wanted to do some Bush pilot work up here in the north. I'm definitely letting her finish her current contract. Then she and I need to talk. I know she loves it here, Yukon and Alaska flights are her favourite. So I'm in touch with Jack Morgan. If he can swing this Canadian fed based off his school, I'll join if at all possible. It may not be prestigious or great-paying, but Deb gets to still be in the area and do what she wants, and I get a chance for a series I've never had -- Band of the Hand against the Morgans. "Lightning" Larry Castle vs. Jack Morgan headlining a card is a major thing even if we hold it in someone's basement. Chateaux: Would Stone and Wilson be joining you? Castle: I hope so. It's not for sure. The odds are good. They both are interested in doing some TWF work, we all have history down there. I might consider a short run that way, but up north is better for me. Chateaux: And if Morgan doesn't get it done? Castle: Then to Japan, I think. With ILCW a joke now, effectively gone, Europe is not someplace to be. Way too many politics and backstabbing. You're not likely to get a clean match, an honest ref, or even paid by the promoter. No thanks. Talent is all Europe has right now, and it's going to flood out of there soon unless something changes. Neither I nor the Band have done a serious long-term run in Japan, and that may be very interesting. We'll see. Chateaux: So we will see you wrestle again? Castle: Eventually. I might take a break, but I'm not done yet. I'm only 35, I've still got a lot of years in me. Chateaux: Any predictions for the Born Again Pay Per View? Will you even watch it? Castle: I'll watch Andrew, at least. Frankly, I think the card is mostly non-matches. The two battle royals interest me. I can't see Donovan holding that title against Ameri- Amanda Reese. And Reese is always good in multiple match nights. I think she'll walk out with gold unless someone really cruises through the battle royal untouched. In the men, I think you have to watch Khali. With so many people gone, he's got a clear shot now, and he's an opportunist. Plus he is great in the Battle Royal format. Chateaux: And do you expect Khali to be facing your brother? Castle: He's hungry enough. It's just whether or not he can keep focused. He won't let me advise him. Call me Monday. ************************************************************************ Larry Castle also expressed a desire for all his fans in the WWA to keep an eye out for where he, and the Band of the Hand, pop up next. Castle seems calm and relaxed. He looks better than he has in months, and I think a break from his hectic schedule was needed for both his wrestling career and his marriage. Hopefully, we will be treated to this great wrestler's abilities in the ring once again soon. Lucien Chateaux. -------------------------------------------------------------------------
