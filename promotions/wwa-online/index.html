A Usenet-era WWA styling itself "the WWA Online," running on TNM through a 1997 title-tournament summer before a winter hiatus its own fan press narrated as a mass talent exodus. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The WWA Online's own account contradicts itself on how old it was. A third party's January 1997 newsletter reported an “up-and-coming WWA” debuting “in late January” as a brand-new startup, while its own head booker, in an August 1997 membership drive, traced it from “handouts given to players once a week” through a BBS discussion board and a series of e-mail leagues into “the amalgamation that we have today of one league, with one world title and 4 years of history behind the wrestlers who are there.” Both are its own words, months apart; neither is adopted over the other. It ran on the TNM 6.2 Plus simulator, and at least one of its own posters complained of the program's limits at length — no lumberjack, flag, or Carson City Silver Dollar matches, wrestlers “kicking out of finishers left and right,” matches running either three minutes or “in excess of 20.”

Its first pay-per-view, Beach Bash '97 (3 August 1997, Muscle Beach, California), crowned a World Heavyweight Champion by tournament final: The Oakland Oddball over “Nightmare” Nick Steele, the finish coming when Oddball's manager, “Maniac” Michael Gates, hit Steele with Oddball's own surfboard while the guest referee's attention was elsewhere. The same card ran a six-man gauntlet for a newly created Ironman title, won by Rip Roberts after his brother “Golden” Tony Roberts interfered against “Perfect” Tommy Chapman. From that card on, the promotion moved to three cards a week, Thursday through Saturday, plus a weekly round-up show built around its own nine best matches.

A card built around Athena earning a shot at a still-uncreated women's title, and winning it by an interference finish, led the promotion to establish a WWA Women's World Championship and recruit for it directly: “there is now a demand for female wrestlers in the WWA... check us out.” Its own president went further in a public discussion of gender in e-wrestling, arguing that “in fantasy leagues, women should be given equal opportunity to wrestle against men.”

By its December 1997 in-house newsletter, the WWA Insider, the Women's World Champion Stevie Power had been stripped of her title after a car accident left her unable to defend it, the belt awarded instead to her challenger Rachael “The Black Pearl” Donovan ahead of a battle royal for a shot at it on a 21 December card, Born Again; the World Cruiserweight title sat vacated after a champion's departure, with an internal memo from its own president, Don Napolitano, hinting it might simply be dropped rather than defended; and the folded League of Professional Wrestling's own former owner, Sandra Bullet, joined its booking staff to run its women's and planned women's tag titles.

Its last surviving document, a 28 December 1997 retrospective interview column, finds the promotion mid-hiatus, its Born Again return already announced and already shedding roster: a trio built around “Lightning” Larry Castle, Dave Wilson, and Arron Stone — wrestlers who had spent 1997 controlling its own championships as a stable calling itself the Band of the Hand — was missing from the return's own list of wrestlers. Castle, interviewed at his home, attributed the departure to management wanting the group out, a lawsuit that followed, and a settlement that ended long-term restrictive contracts across the roster.

How, or whether, it continued past that account isn't in the record.
