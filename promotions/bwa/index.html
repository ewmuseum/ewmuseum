A UK national promotion founded in September 1997 from merged regional promotions, whose own on-screen President crowned its first Heavyweight Champion in an eight-man tournament the night the fed debuted. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The British Wrestling Association's founding Players Guide, dated 3 September 1997, presents it as “a National Promotion based in [the] British Isles,” several regional British promotions merged into one stable platform under a returning veteran promoter, its Monday Night Carnage broadcast booked for national television and Sky Sports 1 satellite feed alike, with United States syndication already “being discussed,” alongside a Thursday magazine show, Raw Footage.

Carnage debuted on 22 September 1997 with an eight-man single- elimination tournament to crown the promotion's first Heavyweight Champion, opening on Bishop predicting his own coronation as, in his own words, “the Avatar of Extreme.”

The final delivered a stranger story than the buildup promised. Facing Bishop for the title, Ace Malone broke off mid-match to argue with an unseen “Tommy,” shouted “NO! I won't do it,” then simply stayed down for the pin, leaving a visibly stunned Bishop to be handed the belt as the promotion's first BWA Heavyweight Champion.

Raw Footage picked the mystery up three days later without resolving it, its own commentary team calling Malone “unstable” and “a loony” on air. The same broadcast saw “Shotgun” Steve Lawless, beaten by Malone earlier on the debut card, demand a rematch in Malone's name and in the name of his own mentor, Logan, and get one signed for the following Monday's Carnage.

Its surviving record ends there, on 25 September 1997, three days after the debut card, with Ace Malone's outburst and the promised Lawless rematch both still unresolved. No further record survives.
