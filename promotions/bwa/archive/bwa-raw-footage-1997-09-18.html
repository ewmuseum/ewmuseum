[Queen's classic song, "We are the Champions" plays, as we cut to the studio. Larry Jenkins is sat alongside his broadcast colleague Jerry Connors, and Connors already seems to be having a joke at the expense of his so called friend.] [LJ:] Good evening folks, and welcome to BWA Raw Footage. I'm- [JC:] Yeah we know, "I'm Jennywenny, and with me is the Master of Disaster Jerry Connors. [LJ:] Not quite right Jerry. Folks, I'm Larry Jenkins, and this is the infamous Jerry Connors. [JC: thinks for a second] Infamous.... Yeah, I like that. [LJ:] I'm glad you do Jerry. We've got a tip top show for you all tonight, jam packed with the latest news and rumours from around the BWA, plus some great action for you from the first volley of house shows that are already going on around the country as we speak. [JC:] Tip top show? [LJ:] Nice alliteration don't ya think. [JC:] Not entirely. For all those morons out there, we've got some action, some _fighting_, but they won't appreciate some of the talent that will be on show tonight. [LJ:] I'm sure that the BWA fans who have already sold out the Coliseum for the 22nd, and the debut of Monday Night Carnage will appreciate the action tonight, and indeed all BWA action just fine. [JC:] Whatever you think Jenkins, but those scrubs no absolutely nothing as to _real_ quality. [LJ:] Well, we better move things along here somewhat, and take you to the first of five taped matches for your pleasure. This first encounter, sees the arrival of "The British Badboy" Miles Alexander, as he meets Tik Durpin. [JC:] Alexander is the local boy, and in my opinion has got his sights firmly set on the truth. He looked at the _foreigners_ that are on the BWA Payroll, and said what most of the British People are thinking, they're crap, and he's going to be the man to prove it. =-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= "The British Badboy" Miles Alexander Vs Tik Durpin [J] Taped at the Bournemouth International Centre Bournemouth =-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= Miles Alexander certainly has the attitude of which his nickname describes. He has been said to be the local hero, but down in the South at the BIC, nothing could have been farther from the truth. Getting a hot reception would not do the reaction he received justice. The fans despise this man, and Durpin would have been just the man to inflict that humiliating defeat on him. Durpin was more than a match it seemed for the power brawling style which more than describes the attack used by Alexander. Durpin, while not being as powerful, did seem to have a technical edge over his opponent, and on the brief opportunities that he had to make his mark, he used them to good effect. However, Miles Alexander always seemed to have something in reserve for these few occasions when Tik made a mark. A low blow here, and a rake to the eyes there pretty much dented any offence that was offered against him. It was to be the power of Alexander that saw him through this match, in the end, much to the displeasure of the fans, quite easily in fact, would be the power, the brawling prowess if you like. Alexander was hot on the offence, and Belly to Belly's, and Belly to Back suplex's were a favourite move. A memorable moment would have had to of been the atomic drop followed by the running clothesline to the back of the head, which would prove to be the beginning of the end. What followed stunned the fans, and was damn impressive to be honest. The British Baddrop be it's name, and a Razors Edge into a piledriver is what it is. Durpin was out for quite a while after it had been hit by the Badboy. [Miles Alexander via a pinfall] [LJ:] Miles Alexander stunning the fans in Bournemouth with his great ability, and have no doubt folks, he may not be the nicest guy around, but he sure does have great talent. [JC:] And that is what it is all about, great talent. In a league of crap, he is the shining light at the pinnacle. [LJ:] That is something that we will no doubt find out in the coming weeks and months. [JC:] We better role one of the champions elect's stats so all the boys and girls watching this can look at the 3d character model, 'cause most of 'em aint going to able to read his stats. [LJ:] Jerry! The Lost Boys, one of the greatest tag teams of all time perhaps, clash with the team of The Goon, who we will see more on later, and Tearchaser. =-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= The Lost Boys (w/ Wendy Addams) Vs A.K.A. Taped at the Manchester G-Mex Arena =-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-=-= [LJ:] Prior to the match itself, The Lost Boys had these comments to make. **Greg and Frank Addams stand in front of a wall that has the BWA logo painted on it.** [Greg] FINALLY! You're going to be D.O.A.! **Fade** In the first ever tag team match sanctioned by the BWA, The Lost Boys, Frank and Greg, were more than ready for anything that A.K.A. would throw at them, infact, you could say that they were rather hyped to say the least. Greg, using his ring smarts, and all round smarts when compared to his brother, would set the tempo in the match, controlling much of it, and attempting, and in most cases succeed on deciding who would be in the ring with who. Wendy at ringside was as always looking stunning, or should that be, Slutty? Her brothers did her proud in the ring, and her visible appreciation was evident. Tearchaser was in the ring for much of the action, as Greg kept him cornered from his partner The Goon, and allowing himself and Frank the chance to dish out the punishment, and dish it out these guys are able to do with a large amount of accuracy. Frank however, did show a small amount of vulnerability on his side of the team, and something perhaps that may be picked up on in other matches. His somewhat preoccupation with fighting on several occasions gave The Goon a chance to get into the ring, the tag being made. However, Greg was always there to somewhat clear up the mess made by his brother, obviously, he was not interested in clearing up the blood of Tearchaser that had spilled from his nose in a rather brutal beating from Frank. But both teams came out showing there stuff, and proving to a public eager for the stars in action, that the tag scene already had two great teams in it. The end of this match came when Wendy became involved, or more appropriately _got herself_ involved. Tearchaser was distracted, weren't we all?, and went after her after some stinging comment. Frank and Greg took the opportunity their sister had given them, and Frank locked on the figure four on The Goon for the submission. [The Lost Boys via a Submission] [LJ:] And a great impression given by both teams. Weakness apparent of both sides, but the something that showed the great strength of The Lost Boys was Wendy. _She_ won the match with the distraction of Tearchaser. [JC:] He didn't _have_ to go after he. He was stupid enough to fall for it, and it lost his team the match. Simple. You shall need it in ABUNDANCE. {Fade} Fury, the psychotic high flyer outweighed the smaller Nomad, but never in the duration of this match did he even appear to have any chance of overcoming the technical mastery of Nomad with his own, and by no means poor, technical game. Nomad, already encouraging the fans to think of him as the "rulebreaker", wrestled an extremely clean match, err, well at least at the start of it. Some things don't last for ever, and in this case, fair play eventually went out of the window. Nomad went from the clean breaks on every hold, and the downright decency of his sportsmanship manner, to the serious destruction of his opponent with all means necessary, but no matter what happened, always relying on the technical game that would in the end show the fans what a great match there is in store at Carnage. But don't take this wrong, Fury did indeed have his share of the domination of this match. His plancha dives, and all round agility wooed the fans, and showed the fans that he had a lot to offer in terms of entertainment value, even if he had little hope of ever being invited back to their house for tea and crumpets. But in reality, Nomad fought a very good match. He used his wear down holds well, the nerve pinches, the reverse neck locks, and even submission moves including a move made very popular by a certain Lost Boy, the figure four leg lock. But Nomad would put an end to this match with his patented Caravan Suplex, a Superplex. Fury hit hard, and Nomad gained the three count. Leaving the arena he had a few comments to make. [Nomad via Pinfall] {Nomad is leaving after the house show. Mr. Carter, I hope you took notes. Good day. {Fade} [LJ:] Nomad showing a great deal of skill in this match to be sure, but Fury demonstrated something of what he has come to the BWA to deliver. Take note Carter, this is the guy that will show you what technical wrestling is all about. Bishop and Jonathan Blade, perhaps two of the higher fancied men to claim the Heavyweight Championship meet head on in a battle that may only bear warning to what may yet come. Bishop, the epitome of extreme was on form, but the all round game play of Blade kept a great match going for long periods where a lesser man may have fallen. Blade was not a lesser man on this night, and showed that he is a serious contender for the championship. He used a technical game when he needed it, and he took the air when needed as well. Bishop was never at ease, and that must have been the way that Jonathan Blade wanted it. Both men dominated periods of this match using their individual style to gain advantages. The sheer brute force that Bishop showed in his lariats and slams paved the way for what will be an interesting first round match with Jon Tickle. But Jonathan Blade did not even give Bishop a chance to look over his shoulder, to look ahead to Monday night. He had to keep full concentration to the match at hand, especially when the a painful Japanese Arm Bar was applied, one that came close to gaining valuable ranking points. But Bishop was not done. As Blade had earlier when Bishop and locked the Confessional Scorpion Death Lock on, he fought out of the hold, and eventually gained the momentum again. But this match never seemed like ending with an out and out victor. It would be one mistake that would cost the loser the points. However, this was never to come, as when the bell rang, it would be for a time limit draw, and a match that was worth the entrance money on its own.
