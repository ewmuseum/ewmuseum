Allstar Global Wrestling (AGW) is an e-mail-based fantasy wrestling federation, which is booked and utilizes a closed-flash (RPs sent in flash packets) system. AGW was first known as Allstar Wrestling of the Motor City
(AWMC). AWMC was founded in December 1997 by [name withheld] (known for his characters "The Legend" Sean Darring and "Fantasy" Brett Young) and has been a well-regarded fed (#4 top fed in 1998 RSPWF Source Awards, various RSPWF
200 nominees, etc.) during his tenure. The fed, so the storyline goes, was formed when TDW (Top Dog Wrestling) ran out of money. So they moved to
Detroit and renamed the fed Allstar Wrestling of Motor City. The AWMC wass based out of Detroit, Michigan. In May of 200, the storyline continues that
AWMC has received a deal for International coverage, and with this came a facelift, and Allstar Global League Wrestling.
Now..back to real life ;). In June 1999, [name withheld] stepped down from running the fed as well as all administration, and new bookers took over the fed. Several new policies came in. All members are encouraged to work with other handlers to develop angles, alliances and feuds. We took this policy to encourage more community among handlers and to take e-w back to the old school. In July, AWMC bought out the ADEF promotion and merged operations.
In August, AWMC signed agreements with [name withheld]'s Ultra Japan Pro and [name withheld]'s Cross Continental Wrestling for future talent exchanges. Also, AWMC bought out Carl Blair's Unlawful Wrestling Council in January. In February 2000, [name withheld] stepped down as Head Booker of AWMC. Stepping up was [name withheld], former VP. [name withheld]gave the league a new facelift, by eliminating one show and making just one show a week, AWMC Explosion. In May of 200, [name withheld]
broke away from the AWMC roots of a regional/national league, and fictiously expanded Internationally. Titles were renamed as well as a new Weekly TV
Show, AGW Global Warfare. PPV's are held once every two month, with the Signature PPV, "Cruise Control" held in May. The AWMC Supershow remains Cruise Control, signifying the legacy that Allstar Wrestling of the Motor City left. 2.Who runs the AGW? *The Real People* The AGW is run by [name withheld], [name withheld], [name withheld], and [name withheld]. 3.What titles are there in AGW? a. AGW World Title The top title of the AGW. It signifies that you are the top wrestler in the federation with the belt. Top contenders for the belt will be decided by booking as well as other factors. However, a good feud will easily replace the top contender.
b. AGW North American Title This is the second-best title in the fed. Top contenders for the belt will be decided by booking as well as other factors. However, a good feud will easily replace the top contender.
c. AGW Extremist Title Encompassing the company's hardcore and shootfight matches. Only the Hardcore and Deathdefying need enter the division. This division has produced some of the msot memorial matches in AWMC/AGW history.
d. AGW National Title A merger of the old AWMC Middleweight and TV belts, the national Titles keeps in touch with AWMC's regional roots. It is defended in normal
AGW-sanctioned matches with a 15 minute time limit. e. AGW World Tag Team Championship Titles Titles awarded to those wrestlers who team for action. These titles are on the same tier as the top singles belt.
f. AGW North American Titles Tag Titles awarded for those who team together. Second tier to Tag Team Championship Titles. The titles will signify who's who of the AGW. These _aren't_ the main goals of the fed. If you've joined just to have your name placed beside them, go ahead and leave now.

Rules and Regulations ################################## 1.How are the Matches determined? -Roleplaying -Heat/Angles (You do not have to be Heel to get heat) -Strats -Effort (If you put forth effort, you'll eventually get a push.) 2.What are the roleplay rules? Two flashes and one in-ring per week is the limit on roleplaying, though one great roleplay can sometimes beat out two average ones. Numerous RPs sent in for one match will be frowned upon. Several RPs, whether weak or strong in quality, inconvenience bookers and break the rules. Focus instead on producing the best roleplays you can within the limit. Descriptive, consistent (with the character), creative roleplaying ("RPs", "flashes")
helps bring your character wins. Use of inappropriate terms (from excessive swearing to "pussy" and the like) is grounds for immediate release and jobbing out of final contract
We used to have a mega "RP Guide" to close every single loophole some of our more disruptive members would squirrel out like porch rats to get away with.
For now, we'll go with some ground rules. Credit these to GLCW (thanks Andj): a. The occasional curse word is ok, but try and censor it out using a *beep* or *censor* or whatever. b. The Happy Clause - I can make some of the people happy all of the time, but I cannot make everyone happy all of the time. Keep that in mind at all times, please. c. The Spunky Clause - From here on out, we're shooting for realism. If I don't think something you write would get aired on national TV, then don't send it to me. d. The Egomaniacal Tyrant Clause - The basic premise of this rule is "No Bragging Allowed". Anyone who develops a serious chip on their shoulder will go through what's known as a "humbling process". I don't care how many titles you have won outside of AGW, or how many people you have defeated outside of the federation -- if you're coming into AGW, don't expect an
insta-push. e. Character Assassination - The basic premise of this rule is that a person is not allowed to attack another character in a way that the other character has potential to be 'ruined', without getting the permission of that character's handler first. This includes any and all attacks that would leave a character injured, overly humiliated, or permanently scarred.
[add to that:] f. The Diggs Clause - AGW administration reserves the right to refuse flashes of inappropriate material (i.e. racism, personal shots, various retardedness) as well as the right to refuse to count the same inappropriate material towards a match. If it's refused, that's the end of it. Anyone who tries to talk me into running an inappropriate roleplay will be canned REAL quick-like. This is just a game. It's not life. If you lost, you lost because I thought you should've. If you wonder if you were out-rp'ed, you were. If you think you need some more heat, you do. If you take it out on me, don't expect to get any reaction, except reference to
this rule. If you're seriously ticked, then you're seriously fired. If you've ever been a fedhead, you know that it can be a hard job, and the less aggravation I have to suffer, the better.
g. The Brain Clause - Just because you write a strat that covers all the grandest moves, every counter imaginable, provisions for interference and the rest, don't have a fit if it isn't used or the match goes contrary to your plans for world domination. The whole concept of pro wrestling is that the wrestlers vouluntarily submit themselves to being hit with the moves that are tried against them. We all know that any wrestler could realistically get out of any hold, or avoid any move if he really wanted to. But he CHOOSES not to, because that's the way the show is done. Just because you have a counter or protection doesn't automatically mean it'll be used. Sometimes an angle is better furthered (while accomplishing mutual goals) by a different outcome. If you can't sell something that turns out contrary to your master plan and decide to take it out in roleplays, you'll have plenty of practice applying to other feds, guaranteed.
h. The Strat-Or-Can Policy - If you're booked for a PPV, and you don't Strat/RP without a pretty damn good excuse, your gone. Chances are, if you haven't shown interest in your match, then no one else has, so we won't bother doing a Play-by-Play for it. We will not tolerate laziness for PPV's.
We treat each PPV like Cruise Control, so please, strat for your match. Failure to do so without a VERY good excuse results in immediate dismissal from AWMC. We are VERY strict with this policy. (See - Sin City Showdown II:
Proving Grounds) i. Use of other handlers' characters in your RPs without permission is prohibited. 3.How do I get matches? First and foremost, activity gets you matches. If you don't roleplay, you won't get matches and/or your opportunity to beat someone who is active regularly is diminished. Even when you don't have anything going on, there's no reason why you shouldn't try and contribute something to the fed every week. There are standard ways of getting matches (i.e. you get signed randomly to a match; you challenge someone in a roleplay and send a 'formal'
request; you send in the 'formal' request for the match), but activity is the primary way to get booked. It is the bookers' discretion on whether a challenge or given match is signed or not.
4. Are sneak attacks allowed? Attacks and interference are allowed in moderation and administration has the right to refuse a sneak attack. Try to keep it somewhat realistic in the context of wrestling. All members are expected to be professionals and
'sell' attacks and incorporate them into flashes, strats, etc. However, if you do have problems with the way your character was portrayed in an attack
(used in a flash without your permission; portrayed in a way inconsistent with his character, etc.), e-mail the President and AGW administration can decide if it's worth acting upon. Attacks and interference will never be used to affect the outcome of a match, unless (among other reasons) the party being attacked is already booked to lose, one of the parties in the match has something developed, or (extremely rarely) if an angle might benefit from the attack/interference. Don't ask to cost someone a match; if a member has worked on a match, they'll get consideration before attack/interference requests. You can ask for your attack to be considered if the person is booked to lose. However, be aware it might not get used.
5. How is new talent brought into AGW? New recruits are issued 90-day contracts with the federation. During that period, a new signee is evaluated for her/his compatibility with the fed and the new signee can get a feel for the organization. During the 90-day contract period, new signees are obligated to stay with AGW and will complete their duties as assigned. New signees can work with handlers, develop angles and make themselves known in the fed. At the end of the
90-day contract, the AGW or recruit has the option to terminate the contract or make an "extended" contractual agreement, which is basically says the handler can stay as long as s/he wants and either party can drop from the AGW at any time for any reason.
6. How many wrestlers may I handle? Members are permitted one wrestler or one tag team. Tag team members may wrestle singles bouts but are ineligible to earn singles rankings.
