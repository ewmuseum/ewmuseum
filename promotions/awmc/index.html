A Detroit-themed federation whose weekly Explosion and Motor City Madness broadcasts carried five title lineages through 1999, its operator stepping down mid-year to a booking committee. Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Allstar Wrestling of the Motor City opened in December 1997, its own FAQ tells the story as a fed called Top Dog Wrestling running out of money, relocating to Detroit, and renaming itself in the move. It ran out of Detroit, Michigan, with a weekly rhythm of two broadcasts: Explosion on Sundays and Motor City Madness on Wednesdays.

In June 1999 its founder stepped back from running the fed and its administration entirely, and a new booking staff took over. The change came with new rules: handlers were limited to one wrestler each, and members were pushed toward working together on angles, alliances, and tag teams — in the new staff's own words, a push “to take e-w back to the old school.” The singles roster turned over almost entirely between its own January 1999 pay-per-view and a complete roster listing circulated that October.

Five championships ran through the roster's turnover: the AWMC Championship, the AWMC Great Lakes Heritage Championship, the AWMC Extremist Championship, the AWMC Motor City Championship, and the AWMC Tag Team Championship. Sin City Showdown, a two-night pay-per-view run 30-31 January 1999, settled the AWMC Championship on Kuwasi “Blackfist” Shakur over “Rolemodel” Paul Styles inside Hell in a Cell, and closed a running AWMC Extremist Championship mystery when it turned out Craig Miller had been wrestling behind the belt all along as the masked “Sanchez” — unmasked, he regained the title outright. The AWMC Extremist Championship itself went vacant on 1 June 1999 and stayed empty until a new champion was crowned that October.

The fed staged its second annual Golden Turnbuckle Awards on 9 December 1999 at the Bayview Yacht Club in Detroit, three days ahead of its own Above The Law pay-per-view, with former stars hosting and a Hall of Fame induction on the same card across twelve nominated categories, from Wrestler of the Year to Hardcore Hero of the Year.

Its own schedule, circulated that August, projected shows into January 2000, and its own late-November update still billed new champions across all five of its titles for a full December pay-per-view card. No closing notice survives, and no result past that December survives to confirm any of it.
