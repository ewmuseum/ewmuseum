A Usenet federation founded 30 August 1996, closing within this record after a 1 November 1996 pay-per-view that included a sixteen-man 'Haunted Arena' gauntlet and an inconclusive casket match. Twenty-six wrestlers are recorded. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Cyber World Wrestling Federation opened its recruitment on rec.sport.pro-wrestling.fantasy on 30 August 1996, inviting “really wrestlers from any fed (eg: WWF, WCW, EWC, etc.)” into a booked promotion run on TNM 6.2, built at launch around three singles belts — a World, a US, and a TV Title — and two tag titles, World and US Tag Team.

By its sole surviving pay-per-view, “Nite of Living Hell” on 1 November 1996, the actual title picture had moved: a World Heavyweight Championship held by Havoc, a Television Championship, a Television Tag Team Championship, a World Tag Team Championship, and a Hemisphere Heavyweight Championship.

That card turned over three of the five. The champion tag team More Ice imploded mid-match when Hugh Morris turned on his own partner, JC Ice, costing them the Television Tag Team title to the Balls Brothers; a scheduled Hemisphere Tag Team defense was scratched after an injury and replaced by a non-title gravedigger match, won by Rusty Racer; and JC Ice, who charged the ring ahead of the next match to attack the champion with his own old belt, lost the Hemisphere Heavyweight Championship to Cawthorne anyway, dropping both his titles in one night. The Crusher held on to the World Tag Team belts against MC Masters, and Sy Kopath took the Television Championship from the Earl of Doom in a hangman's match.

The night's feature was a sixteen-man Haunted Arena, fought through a maze of cages suspended above the ring, its survivor guaranteed a title shot; the field thinned round by round until Godzilla emerged the winner. A parallel feud between Chris Stonebreaker and his former manager, Lefevbre, was staged as a casket match refereed by a guest brought in from the WWWF, the Undertaker — who was knocked unconscious with a sledgehammer mid-match when Lefevbre sealed his own valet, Sylvestra, inside the casket instead of his actual opponent and had it dumped into a river; security recovered it, empty, before the broadcast's end.

Godzilla came close to ending Havoc's unbeaten reign in the main event before a foreign object, passed to Havoc by his own second, turned the count; Havoc retained the World Heavyweight Championship at 11:27.

No document past that pay-per-view survives. Two thinner, 1997-dated recruitment posts using the same letters may or may not continue the same operation; neither carries anything tying it back to this record, and whether the promotion ran again isn't known.
