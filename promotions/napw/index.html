A text-based Canadian e-fed, per its own byline the "Official website of NAPW – Online Fantasy Pro Wrestling," with a house venue at Edmonton's Polish Hall and recurring Calgary shows at the Ogden Legion — five championships across two eras, a weekly TV taping recovered nearly whole across its own 2005-2008 run with appearance-by-appearance extraction against every served card, and its own annual awards ceremonies mined for the fed's own record of what it valued most. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

NAPW opened its doors 31 Oct 2005 at the NAIT gym in Edmonton, crowning three inaugural champions on the same debut card — The Plague (World), Ravager (Provincial), and D-X (Tag Team) — and ran a weekly TV taping (Monday, then Tuesday, then Wednesday Night Fights, plus a rotating “NAPW Action!” supplement) alongside a real Pay-Per-View calendar: Black Thursday, Sole Survivor, Complete Control, Tagstravaganza, Anniversary Assault, Lethal Lottery, Cold Snap, Bingo Tango. For a stretch in 2007–2008 it ran joint “Supershow” cards with a sister promotion, REBEL Pro Wrestling, and its own World Champion, Donovan Astros, carried the belt on an overseas tour through REBEL's own sister fed ECFW — Brisbane, Sydney, and Beijing, three title defenses across a single month in 2007. Astros won the title from Ravager on a date the fed's own broadcast called live on air (31 Jul 2007) and held it for three-plus years, still champion per the fed's own Sept 2010 rankings — the classic era's own longest reign, closing a chain that also runs through a Bickle-Cartwright title switch the served recap itself cuts off mid-finish, corroborated instead across three later broadcasts.

The company staged its own death in September 2010 — a mark named “Arthur Graham,” fresh off a lottery windfall, “purchases” NAPW in a full in-fiction press conference and reinstates Commissioner Terry Brandon for one last hurrah, a seven-show farewell tour running into December. The real announcement, plain and out-of-fiction the same week, said the mundane truth: the promoter just had less time for it. NAPW came back anyway, a few years later, same promoter (site byline “Ro,” later “Ro-Bot”), same Commissioner still installed, running under a WordPress blog through at least 2016–2018.

The revived era's own World Tag Team Championship carries a four-month reign by a touring outside act, The Phenomenons — won in a four-way at Tagstravaganza (28 Jun 2014), lost to Those That Would Inflict Ill at Flavour Explosion (27 Oct 2014). The reign's own best-remembered moment isn't on any card at all: the Phenomenons spent their Tagstravaganza purse renting Robosaurus and wrecked the arena driving it at their first defense, Beach Blanket Bodyslams (29 Jul 2014) — independently confirmed by NAPW's own 2014 Year End Awards, whose “Jack Jones ‘HOLY HELL’ Moment” category named it #1 at 21% of the vote. Full title ledgers for all five championships are on Championships; the Phenomenons' own local NAPW-era scripts, recruitment through the Flavour Explosion build, are served whole on Roleplays.

Storylines

The programs its own fans actually voted for, at both Year End Awards ceremonies the record holds — 2006's, run against a full results archive, and 2014's, run against an era whose own results pages were never separately crawled, so its own story survives only in what the ballot itself says happened.

2006: D! turns on NAPW Written by AI, 2026

NAPW's own debut-anniversary card, Anniversary Assault, closed on the fed's own 2006 Match of the Year: the House of Horrors, a five-man steel-cage brawl that didn't end until four wrestlers were handcuffed to the cage itself, leaving one man five minutes alone with the rest at his mercy. Ravager won it. What happened after is the angle the record actually remembers: D! — NAPW's own three-time champion, its self-styled "savior" — ran into the cage with a steel chair and turned on everyone in it, including his own protege, before climbing onto the announce table and knocking the commentators out cold too. It closed the show, and the year: D! took 2006 Wrestler of the Year and Face of the Year on the same ballot that crowned the turn itself Match of the Year, while his own program with Ravager — run across singles and tag competition all year — won Feud of the Year outright.

2014: the Abbey Graves era Written by AI, 2026

No results recap survives for NAPW's own revived, 2013+ era — only card-preview posts from the fed's own WordPress-era blog, and the 2014 Year End Awards ballot the fans themselves filled in after the fact. Both agree on the shape of the year: Abbey Graves defended the NAPW World Championship against Declan Black at the two-night River Valley Rumble/Beach Blanket Bodyslams outdoor summer cards — voted the year's Best Match — and ran a parallel program with Jay Deschain that the same ballot named the year's Most Compelling Feud, an angle the ballot's own "HOLY HELL" Moment category remembers by one beat in particular: an injured Graves suckering Deschain into a title match at Heritage Havoc. The same ballot is also the only independent confirmation that the incident the Phenomenons' own roleplays narrate — a ROBOSAURUS, at Beach Blanket Bodyslams — actually happened on the show itself, not just in-character: it topped the "HOLY HELL" Moment vote outright.

REBEL Pro Wrestling

Crossover booking with NAPW (2005)
Five joint "NAPW/REBEL Supershow" cards ran 2007-2008 (Supershows I-V), with talent crossing both ways; NAPW's own World Champion, Donovan Astros, defended the title on an overseas tour billed as running "with REBEL Pro Wrestling" through REBEL's own sister promotion ECFW (Brisbane, Sydney, Beijing, Aug-Sep 2007).

Affiliate of NAPW (2005)
A league-database record on the hub, acronym NAPW, gives the full name "New Alberta Pro Wrestling" and "Open since October 31, 2005" -- an exact match to this wing's own founding (2005-10-31). No profile page for this record survives in the mirror. The hub's own event and top-ten databases carry 31 event records and 33 ranking records under this acronym.
