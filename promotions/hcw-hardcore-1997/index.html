Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Hardcore Championship Wrestling opened 4 March 1997, a date its own founder states at least eight separate times across its own record, with its first broadcast posted 12 March and its first pay-per-view, March Mayhem, following three days later. Within days it was fighting to hold the name: at least two other e-feds posting to the same newsgroup called themselves HCW too, and this one's own president answered publicly that his own federation had been running since 4 March and asked the newer claimants to change theirs.

It ran as an open, booked federation — handlers signed up to write as many as three wrestlers each, roster sheets collected under threat of firing for non-participation, matches decided by a stated weighting of seventy-five percent roleplay to twenty-five percent strategy. Two broadcasts a week and a monthly pay-per-view carried its own hardcore-gimmick house style, backed by its own newsdesk columns, an in-fiction magazine and an in-fiction television-ratings sheet.

Its championship picture turned over constantly and not always on the record: the World Heavyweight title, created in a 30-man battle royal won by “Wild Thing” Jonnie Adonis at March Mayhem, passed through El Perfecto, the Heartbreaker and Chris Hunter before ending with Lord Doomsday, who fought Hunter to a one-hour draw at the federation's last pay-per-view and was still champion when it closed. A United States title, born from a 32-entrant tournament, was formally retired that July and replaced by a Television title; a World Tag Team title was vacated once when its first champions, having just won it, were fired for disrupting a pay-per-view; and Flash Flood's reign as the original Hardcore Champion was later named the federation's own longest.

Its own award show, the HCW Extremies, was held 25 April 1997: El Perfecto took Best Wrestler and Best Personality, “Franchise” Shane Dreamer Best Heel, Terrorize and Stryfe Best Tag Team, the bWo (Blue World order) Best Stable, Storm Best Manager, and Flash Flood Best Title Holder and Fan Favorite — though he didn't show up to collect either, his plane reported late.

Its own operator closed the federation on 15 December 1997: “HCW is no more, I have taken it apart for bigger things.” What was left of it merged with an allied federation, the World Online Wrestling Association, into a planned superfederation meant to begin operating in the new year.

Two of its most heavily referenced cards, Mega Fight and Cage Wars, do not themselves survive — only their announcements and aftermath do — so several of the title changes above are known only from later broadcasts billing a new champion. A tournament to crown a new Triangle Champion, announced for October 1997, has no result in the surviving record either.
