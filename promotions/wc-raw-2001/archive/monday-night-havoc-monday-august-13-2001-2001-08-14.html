Windy City RAW — Card Archive

Monday Night HAVOC — Monday, August 13, 2001

14 August 2001
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Card Written by WC-RAW President, Douglas Coleman. Lights! Pyro! Flaming Technicolor Midgets on Trampolines (yes, we’re beating the whole midget idea into the ground – shut up, it’s funny)! Buck Madison and Stan Willis welcomed everyone to the UIC Pavilion and quickly got into a discussion about the recent
Handicap Iron Man Challenge laid out by Greco. Buck Madison was surprised that, so far, there hasn’t been a single response.
Willis chalked it up to the fact that no one wants to be in the ring with a washed-up has been.
Avoiding a rather heated discussion, Madison quickly changed the subject to tonight’s card, which looked to be a rather strong one. Willis also noted – a surprise to all that he was in any way observant – that Windy City RAW has recently signed a lot of former XHCW members – including Rob Ericson, who was to be on tonight’s show. With that, Monday Night Havoc went to their first match of the evening.
Logan Zensation v. Sabbath Silent newcomer Logan Zensation was introduced without much fanfare or much of a response from the crowd. Sabbath got a similar lukewarm response, but more because of the fact that his entrance took the crowd by surprise. After he was introduced, the lights began to flicker and the music popped over the speakers. Sabbath appeared and stalked his way to the ring. Walking with him, just behind and to the right of him, was his manager, Master Yojohnahk, proudly waving a flag that had the symbol of Sabbath’s family on it. As Sabbath climbed into the ring, it became clear that Sabbath was hungry and quickly took control of the match. He punctuated his dominance with a vicious huricanrana. Continuing on a path that Madison dubbed
“suicidal,” Sabbath continued to dish out pain, putting Zensation in a painful spiderweb submission, which Zensation was able to break out of thanks to ropes. Zensation attempted to mount something of an offense, but Sabbath quelled it with a powerful DDT.
Sensing the end was near, Sabbath used the opportunity to leave a lasting impression on the audience and the WC-RAW staff. Sabbath slid out of the ring, pulled out a ladder from underneath the ring, and slid it into the ring. He quickly set it up in the next to a turnbuckle and moved the unconscious Zensation into position and climbed the turnbuckle. Sabbath then leapt off the top turnbuckle and did a handstand on the top of the ladder, and then pushed off in a springboard type affect. He flipped in mid air and used the momentum to hit a perfect splash on Logan. The crowd erupted after the perfectly executed move and it was a quick 1-2-3 and Sabbath scored his first victory.
Winner: Sabbath via pinfall. Match Rating: 7/10 Lil' C v. Tony Gravano The audience showed no real interest in this match. Tony Gravano got a small pop – there are still a few loyal fans out there that remember him – and Lil’ C received a few cheers. Both wrestlers made mistakes in moves, which the audience picked up and ridiculed the wrestlers for the missed spots. Finally, Lil’ C gained the advantage and finally was able to overpower the once rising star that was Tony Gravano. The crowd erupted as Lil’ C hit his finisher, the Gangstarana!, and scored the victory.
Winner: Lil’ C via Pinfall. Match Rating: 3/10 Randy Hudman v. Shawn Borders Borders came to the ring with a new found focus, following the Career
Battle Royal and Hudman took the brunt of it. As Hudman stood waiting in the ring, the crowd erupted as Borders returned with his Same Ole
Stick! The crowd soon discovered that this Borders was a lot like the
Borders who once held championship gold as he took Hudman to a brand new level of extreme. Soon, the match spilled over onto the floor as
Borders savagely attacked his opponent. Relentless, Borders sent
Hudman over the guardrail and into the crowd with a clothesline. The crowd went nuts as Borders climbed the top turnbuckle. He posed for a second and then flew into the crowd, slamming into Hudman with a stupendous double jump moonsault plancha. The crowd went crazy!
Referee Dave Stevenson finally convinced both the wrestlers to get into the ring. Randy bounced back, hitting a series of moves, but he was slow and still a tad woozy from the earlier pummeling. He went for a Russian leg sweep, but Borders countered with Russian suplex. The crowd erupted and Borders fed off the energy. Borders picked up Hudman and hit a stiff release high angle dragon suplex. Sensing victory,
Borders motioned for the Border Line! Hudman never stood a chance.
Winner: Shawn Borders via pinfall. Match Rating: 8/10 Rob Ericson v. The Wolverine The crowd never seemed too interested in this match, really. The
Wolverine got a small pop, but the match pretty much went without much noise as the crowd didn’t really know who to cheer for. The crowd started getting to their feet as Ericson stood on the top rope and went for his F***ing Frog Splash. The Wolverine rolled out of the way just in time. The Wolverine quickly capitalized, sealing the victory with his Lethal Powerbomb.
Winner: The Wolverine via pinfall. Match Rating 5/10 Alex & Doug Dunvegan v. Fire & Brimstone Alex came out alone – a sign of bravery, at least according to
Stan. Fire and Brimstone came out and quickly took advantage of
Alex’s predicament. However, Alex used his size and strength to his advantage when Fire got into the ring. Thanks to a few saves by
Brimstone, F&B was able to stay in the match. Dunvegan finally had enough and chased Brimstone out of the ring. However, he ignored
Jaramellio, who hit a powerful standing dropkick, sending Alex into the turnbuckle. Brimstone was tagged in and simply just overpowered
Dunvegan. A few minutes later, Alex found Salvation.
Winner: Fire & Brimstone via pinfall. Match Rating: 5/10 "The Master of Insanity" Hannibal Payne v. "The Real F*ckin' Icon" Joe Brown This match was simply extreme as Joe Brown made his debut against the only man in Windy City RAW to hold every singles championship –
Hannibal Payne. Brown looked rather impressive in his debut, but Payne seemed to be too much. Payne seemed to not figure out that Hardcore rules were not in effect as he was constantly having to be told not to use a weapon, specifically a steel chair that he had brought into the ring. As the match started coming to a head, Payne grabbed the steel chair once again and raised it to pummel Joe Brown once again, but referee Jason Nicholson went to stop it, but was savagely kicked him away. Brown, woozy following a sedative slam, was defenseless as Payne brought the chair down into Brown’s head. Payne continued to attack the rookie, even as the ref called for the bell. Nicholson, showing either bravery or stupidity, got in Payne’s way and was able to separate him from the chair. He helped get Brown out of the ring, but Payne grabbed the ref from behind and sent him into the corner of the ring as the crowd cheered. Payne looked manically out to the crowd and then motioned for his Crazy Train. Security showed up, however, and before Payne was able to get to the defenseless ref.
Winner: “The Real F*ckin’ Icon” Joe Brown [ Cut to center ring, where RAW Windy City interviewer Marty Crowell stands, holding a microphone. ]
MC: "Ladies and gentlemen, I know you were promised an interview with the new Windy City Tag Team Champions, Purgatory. The interview will take place in just a moment, but first, allow me to explain the situation. Currently, our tag team champions are not present in the arena to conduct the interview, citing prior obligations."
[ The crowd boos. ] MC: "However, our champions have graciously agreed to conduct the interview live, via satelitte!"
[ The fans continue to boo, even as the lights begin to dim, and the
RAWTron comes to life, with a slow, steady countup to 10 (In German).
Upon the count of 10, the arena entrance explodes with pyrotechnics, as Rammstein's "Sonne" begins to play. The countup on the screen is replaced by the two grim masks of Purgatory. Dementia raises a gloved hand, and the music fades. Once all is quiet in the arena, he lowers his hand. ]
Dementia: "Let us begin this interview, as our time is precious and seldom can we be bothered to waste it on such pitiful endeavours. Pose your queries, and heed your tongue. You now stand audience to
Purgatory!" MC: "Welcome, Synn, Dementia. Our first question for you is Why weren't you willing to do this interview in person? Why the mystery and isolation?"
Synn: "We do this interview against our better judgement. We could not care less what you wish to know of us. We do not explain ourselves to the likes of you. We satisfy our whims, not because we must. But because we can."
MC: "That..doesn't answer the question." Dementia: "Your question is irrelevant. If you wish us to proceed with this atrocity, phrase better inquiries."
MC: "*shrug* Ok. You have defeated Alex and Doug Dunvegan for the
Windy City Tag Team Titles. Who do you believe will be first in line for a title shot?"
Dementia: "We care not who challenges first for the titles. They who are worthy, shall challenge us last."
Synn: "We care nothing for these trinkets. Purgatory does not adorn itself with jewellery. We seek competition, to quench the thirst in our gullets. The burning realization that our previous victims severely lacked even the most miniscule ability to eminate any semblance of quality. Simply put, they couldn't even fake talent."
MC: "Those are some harsh words against some of the best talent Windy
City has ever had the privilege of offering."
Dementia: "It's unfortunate that the viewers at home must endure such innane acts of desperation. Your so-called best couldn't even produce an effective offense. Harsh words, borne of harsh reality."
MC: "So, you're saying there is no one on the Windy City roster who can give you even the slightest hint of challenge?"
Synn: "The Windy City roster is too busy competing with one another as to who can recite the longest list of accomplishments within one's career. Dementia and I, we do not live in the past. We look only to the future, and let our actions speak for us."
Dementia: "We may hold these cheap trophies you call belts, but only because our heads are not clouded with trivial matters, such as previous exploits, other federations, and tending to wounds caused by the stabbing of knives in the back. We focus on our goal, to dominate all in our path, and leave only redemption in our wake."
MC: "To speak of your accomplishments for just a moment, you have taken on, and defeated, Windy City's best tag teams. What shall be the next chapter to be written in your history?"
Synn: "Due to our disinterest in gold and material gain, our goals for
Windy City are as such, you would not be able to comprehend even the basic structure of those goals. To sit here and attempt to translate them into words is not only a waste of my breath, but also a waste of my time."
Dementia: "Suffice it to say, we have grown weary of the tag team division, and its dehydrated talent pool. We find a viable solution to be for us to branch out, extend our grasp beyond tag teams, and dip our fingers into the puddle that is the singles division."
MC: "So, you are going to pursue the Heavyweight Title?" Synn: "Your lack of attention is disturbing. Why have us give answers, if only to repeat the question? We seek no gold. It seeks us. Should it come our way, as it is with the tag team titles, so be it. But
Purgatory gets no pleasure from tarnished metal. The thrill of the hunt, the scent of fear, the essence of domination....that is what we crave."
Dementia: "We wish for the cries of mercy to echo to all corners of the world. To have our souls tantalized by the seductive embrace of despair. To drink from the goblet, spilling over with psychosexual ecstacy as our victims fall prey to the shattered illusions of reality. THAT....is what we shall pursue."
MC: "Since you seem to be disgusted by the thought of holding the Tag
Team Titles, what are your plans for them?"
Dementia: "Your mindless drivel irritates us. We have answered more than enough of your pathetic questions. You want to know what
Purgatory has in store for the future? I suggest you pay attention, because we shall provide you an answer. The time is not right for that, but when it is, you shall not need to ask any questions.
However, we do have one for you." Synn: "If the ends justify the means, what do you perceive our ends to be? Synn is in, and so, we begin!"
[ The arena is instantly cast into darkness, the RAWTron goes black, the PA system goes dead, the crowd screams. Suddenly, the lights return, but the RAWTron is blank, and lying in the middle of the ring, is Marty. ]
"The Franchise" Khan v. "Sadistic" Steve Liermann This return match woke the crowd up as “The Franchise” and Former World Heavyweight Champion Khan came to the ring. He walked to the ring slowly, making sure he connected with his fans. With
Cassandra by his side, his new music, “It’s Over Now” by Kurupt, accompanied him to the ring. Liermann came to the ring second and stalked his way to the ring with a determination he has rarely shown. The two old rivals duked it out immediately as climbed into the ring, showing the two still had no love for each other. Liermann gained an advantage, sending Khan into the ropes. He went for a clothesline. Khan ducked and went for his Snakecharmer, but
Liermann ducked it and took Khan down with a leg sweep. Liermann pressed on the attack, hitting his spinebuster slam in the ring.
Liermann was a man on a mission and in obvious control in the ring. It appeared that Khan didn’t have a chance. Liermann climbed the top turnbuckle and went for his Lierbomb, but Khan showed he still had a little life left as he rolled out of the way. Khan then pressed attack, slamming Liermann to the mat with some vicious power moves.
Khan kept looking into the crowd and the audience, as if he was expecting an attack. These moments of indecision ended up costing him bigtime as Liermann took control of the match quickly with a Millenium
Special. As Liermann started working over his opponent, softening him up for his diamond dust, the opening of “Mimicry for
Nothing” – Reese Whattington Hurst’s music – came over the speakers. Reese came running out of the backstage area and came running down the ramp. Liermann ignored Khan for a moment as he awaited Reese. Reese, however, was suddenly cut off by some mysterious man who jumped out the crowd and hit him to the ground with a clothesline from hell! Liermann stood quizzically for a moment and smiled and returned to Khan, hitting his Diamond Dust and scoring the clean victory.
Winner: Steve Liermann via Pinfall Match Rating: 9/10 As Liermann made his way to the backstage area, Moose came out of the audience and climbed into the ring. He stood in the corner and waited for Khan to get to his feet. A moment later….GORE! GORE! GORE!
Khan was, needless to say, knocked unconscious. Moose then demanded a microphone and started jawing at the unconscious Khan.
Moose: You know something, Khanald, Alex Dunvegan had the same look in his eyes the night I gored him. Completly cutting off my ties with him. Ya know, I hate him almost as much as I do you. But you're different, you actually managed to aviod me every chance you got, not giving me the world title shot rematch I wanted more then anything alive. YOU DENIED ME THAT TITLTE, I MADE YOU PAY, I COSTED YOU THE
WORLD TITLE AND WE DON'T HAVE TO SEE YOUR UGLY MUG AS THE WORLD
CHAMPION! Next Sunday Night, me, you, Last Man Standing match. Let's see what's the most dominate move, the Gore, or the Snakecharmer.
Let's see if you can stop me know. Moose gets up and drops the mic onto Khan. Moose slowly walks away as
Cassandra enters the ring. Moose turns around with lightening like quickness preps for a Gore. But Cassandra quickly exits the ring before Moose can take off. Moose cracks a sick girn as he drops to the mat and slides out of the ring. He just walks away as Khan lays there, still with eyes glazed over as Cassandra just tries to wake him up.
Hardcore Title Match Lance Ammo (c) v. Reese Whattington Hurst Hurst made his way to the ring and was obviously still woozy from his earlier beat down. Ammo, in a classic Ammo-Core match, showed no mercy. Ammo just dished out more and more pain on the somewhat defenseless Hurst. Ammo just smiled as Hurst writhed in pain.
Surprisingly, the fans seemed to be cheering with each smash of a stop sign. Ammo ended the match with a brainbuster and retained his
Hardcore championship. Winner: Lance Ammo via Pinfall. Match Rating: 7/10 World Heavyweight Championship Match WAR (c) v. "The Real S#@!" Jamal Byers Despite a short summary of this match, this was simply the BEST match of the evening. War and Byers fought back and fourth throughout the entire match and both recorded several near pinfalls. The crowd was into this match throughout, cheering each vicious move after vicious move. The match spilled out to the ringside area and the match took a vicious turn as Byers slammed through the ringside table with a pedigree! Byers, taking a bit of damage himself from the aftermath of the move, groggily picked up a bleeding WAR and dragged him into the ring. Byers pressed on the attack and seemed to be close to gaining the World Championship and went for a powerbomb. However, WAR countered with his World War III, pulling out a victory from the clutches of defeat. As WAR had his arm raised, Byers grabbed the belt and slammed the championship into War’s forehead, opening the wound again. The ref tried to stop him, only to receive a Wrap. Byers went back to WAR and hit him with three consecutive Wraps in the middle of the ring. He took the world belt and threw it on him and walked out, with the crowd booing him.
