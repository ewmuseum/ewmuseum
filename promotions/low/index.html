Founded in June 2002 by a group of wrestlers out of the collapse of True Xtreme Wrestling, Lights Out Wrestling ran a co-written television serial and a graded writer's academy across two domains, went dark in the autumn of 2003, came back the following February and closed in September 2005, its site staying up into January 2006. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Lights Out Wrestling opened on 11 June 2002 out of another federation's collapse. True Xtreme Wrestling had just folded, and LOW's own history page tells the founding as a scene — a group of ex-TXW wrestlers wanting to start again, a room of chairmen, and a man billed Trigger being handed a company he had sworn he was finished with. They named it after the show TXW used to run: ''Lights Out… That was the name of TXW's television show, wasn't it? Tuesday Night Lights Out.''

It ran two shows a week — Realm on Saturdays, Aftermath midweek — and five championships, with a King of the Death Match crowned once a year alongside them. Its own masthead carried a strip of champion portraits, each image titled with the belt and the man holding it. No title ledger of the fed's own was ever captured, so that strip is most of what survives of who held what.

The whole first year ran as one story. Mike Meyhu turned on Trigger, took the presidency, and operated for months behind a mystery CEO called Kevin Miller before the fed unmasked Miller as Meyhu himself at a pay-per-view called War Wounds. The anniversary card, In The End, put the two men back in the ring against each other for the company: ''On June 11th, 2002, a dream was born. And on June 11th, 2003, there is a risk that it will die.''

The roster wrote the shows themselves, segment by segment, and every card closed on a credits block naming who had written which piece of it. The fed ran a school for that: Lights Out Academy, its own sub-site, which graded members out of a hundred in three separate disciplines — roleplaying, matchwriting and segment writing — published the standings, and made graduating a route onto the roster. Its own rules gave two paths to graduation, both demanding straight A grades: seven roleplaying pieces plus five segment-writing pieces, or a faster ten-piece spread across all three disciplines, either one ending in a roster spot and a weekly match to write for it. Its August 2003 standings show fifteen students working the Roleplaying school alone, Mr. Sake leading it at 87.

It went dark in the autumn of 2003. The roleplay board's last post of that year is dated 17 September; nothing follows until a cluster of test posts on 10 February 2004, when the roster came back and started over. Seymour Almasy's own run belongs to the weeks just before that silence — seven roleplays between 18 August and 13 September 2003 and a match against Iron Lung on the Wakeup Call pay-per-view, the last of the seven titled ''Alone.'' Writing on the fiftieth Realm the following May, a member put the closure and the return plainly: ''When the LOW closed I didn't join another fed because there was nothing like the LOW.''

The second run reached January 2006. The last post on the fed's own community board is dated the 10th of that month, on the out-of-character board, under the title ''Well…''; the site's last genuine capture is a roleplay page sixteen days later. No closing announcement survives.

Storylines

LOW closed every show on a Credits block naming a different member for every segment they wrote, and its own history page tells its whole first year as one continuous serial. The arcs below follow that same shape — recapped from the shows actually read for this pass, every beat linked to the card it happened on.

The Winning Team, and the face behind Kevin Miller Written by AI, 2026

LOW's own history page tells its founding year as a single war. A masked CEO calling himself Kevin Miller — face never shown, name assumed fake — ran the promotion from the shadows through an enforcer, Justin Malice, and a stable called The Winning Team that absorbed The Shadow Riders, Sean Dahmer and Luke Justice as it fired LOW's own vice president and commissioner in turn. Mike Meyhu, staged the entire time as the promotion's own reigning champion and Trigger's ally against Miller's regime, embraced Trigger on camera "to the fans' delight" moments before the fed's own payoff, at the pay-per-view War Wounds: Miller's face lit on the LOW Tron resolves, in the history page's own words, into "the face of Mike Meyhu" — and Meyhu drops Trigger with the Meyhu Meltdown before the crowd can finish reacting.

The rematch is the anniversary card, In The End (11 June 2003), billed outright as "loser leaves LOW" for ownership of the company. Trigger is thrown from the top of a steel structure mid-match and appears dead; he rises anyway, hits two Warrior Realms, and is finishing Meyhu when Tristan Belmont — LOW's own reigning World Champion, elsewhere on the same card locked in his own sixty-minute Iron Man defense — runs in and turns face for the first time on record, driving Meyhu through the announce table before Trigger makes the count: "TRIGGER HAS DEFEATED MIKE MEYHU! TRIGGER IS ONCE AGAIN OWNER OF LIGHTS OUT WRESTLING!" The same show closes a second thread the same night, unresolved: Belmont's own World Title defense against Jesse Jamester goes to sudden death tied at two falls apiece, and before either man scores the deciding fall, Eric Russo and Jason Kage — The Shadow Riders, Miller's own former recruits — run in, beat down both men, and announce their own retirement from the ring in the same breath. The served text never returns to declare a winner of the title match itself.

One night at Realm 50 Written by AI, 2026

The fed's own Wednesday Night Realm 50th Edition (circa 5 May 2004) changes three of LOW's five championships in a single broadcast. President Chryste suspends reigning Realm Champion Cyrus Skar mid-match for conduct outside the ring, then hands the vacant title to his opponent on the spot: "Since you're suspended…the winner of this match, and NEW LOW Realm Champion, Malik Katsuya." Angelus Sorrow takes the Xtreme Championship from Iron Lung in an Ultimate Taipei Match, and a debuting unit, The Saikyo System, wins the Tag Team Championship off The Metallo Zords in a ladder match the same night.

The same broadcast stages an interfed angle: a Six Man Fifteen Foot Ladder Match for Primetime Central's own Global Championship, with Jesse Jamester — introduced as having left LOW and "now resides in Global Wrestling" — defending against five men including Hessian and Josh, the latter announced twice in the same segment as "the Global Wrestling Heavyweight Champion." Hessian climbs down with the belt: "THE NEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEW PRIME TIME GLOBAL CHAMPION… HESSIIAAAAAAAN!!!!!"

CATASTROPHIC, and Annihilation's reach Written by AI, 2026

CATASTROPHIC (23 October 2004, Philadelphia) closes on an Asylum Match for the World Championship — Annihilation, Eric Russo, Luke Justice and Mike Meyhu climbing a tiered steel structure toward a belt lowered from the ceiling on a wire. Annihilation — by the broadcast's own account, "climbing the ranks from the lowest ranks of ICA" for "more than two years" — is last standing over a motionless Russo and unhooks the title. He puts it on. Then, with the crowd still cheering, he wraps the same wire around his own neck. The fed's own champion banner shows the World Championship vacant six weeks later; the surviving record never dates the vacancy or says what became of him.

Affiliations

TXW (True Xtreme Wrestling)

Predecessor to LOW (2002)
Lights Out Wrestling was founded by a group of ex-True Xtreme Wrestling wrestlers when TXW folded, under the same owner, and named itself after TXW’s own television show. A separate 2005 forum post reads “I love low/txw, whether it’s the same or not” — a fan-side echo of the same claim. This is the fed’s own staging of its own founding, rather than an out-of-fiction corporate record.

Primetime Central (PTC)

Member of LOW (2002) (as of 20 Apr 2003)
Sent a delegation to PTC’s own “New Era” interfed supercard, 20 Apr 2003, whose Superstars page groups the whole card by member fed — the single largest delegation on the card. The fed’s own site ran across two sequential domains, lightsoutwrestling.net and lowonline.net, confirmed as the same promotion’s two hosting eras.

Dominant Wrestling On-Line (DWO, 2001)

Crossover booking with LOW (2002) (October 2003)
The DWO's own card of 9 October 2003 bills three arrivals it attributes to LOW's collapse: "The DWO is thrilled to say the least at being able to house two of the recently deceased LOW's main stars on our show this week", naming Jason Warrior and Reality/Mayhem. The card's own results page was never captured, so the billings are recorded as billed appearances and nothing further.
