Academy: Matchwriting Grading

captured 29 October 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
>>Navigation Main • Graduation • Tutorials • Forums Roleplaying • Rules • Grading Matchwriting • Rules • Grading Segment Writing • Rules • Grading Grading Structure In the matchwriting section of the Lights Out Academy, a match will be posted on the forums every week. It will have the competitors involved, information about them, and a possible stipulation to the match. To be graded, you are to post a completed match involving these competitors and the stipulation, and it will soon be graded. With that said, we have made a list of the criteria you will be graded on. Originality: 15 Points If you happen to watch wrestling on television, you'll notice how mathces get repetitive, and at times even boring. In Lights Out Academy, we try to teach you how to avoid those sort of 'average' matches, and introduce your own ideas and concepts into the mix. Here, your creativity will be put to the test. Fifteen percent of your grade based purely on the originality of your match. Sneak attack beginning? Incredible spot on the ending? All of these will be factored into this score. Length: 15 Points Compared to quality, quantity means little to nothing, but it is still important to give the reader a lengthy, accurate description of what is going on. Most matches would be at least fifteen minutes in real-time, and if every move is recorded, this would be quite a lengthy text. We will be grading on the standards of Lights Out Wrestling, which requires a minimum of 15 kilobytes in filesize for each matchup. This translates roughly into 3000 words of text. You will be given 1 point in this section for every 1 kilobyte that your match is. There is no shame in falling short, but to pick up the maximum points, write a match that meets and breaks through these standards. Once you get into it, it is quite easy to get carried away. Description: 15 Points Straight action is boring. Very boring. Punch, kick, punch, kick, punch, kick, powerslam. Not very interesting, huh? At the same time, you could transform that into something like, Wrestler 1 throws a punch, and it connects with the opponent square in the jaw. He stumbles backwards, but is able to throw out a kick, tripping Wrestler 1! Wrestler 1 staggers about, trying to regain his balance, and in doing so plants another fist into Wrestler 2's face! Wrestler 2 does not fall, but he soon does, with a kick-punch combo from the foe. Wrestler 1 is not done, not nearly. He pulls Wrestler 2 to his feet, kicks him in the midsection, and carries him up and over with a powerslam that rocks the ring! See? The exact same move-list, flavored up with a little description. This is one of the most important grading categories, and you should be sure not to overlook it. We want you to be descriptive in all senses. What is the arena like? What are the fans like? Analyze every bump and brawl, every cheer and shout. Put yourself inside the match, and you will be impressed with the results. Plot: 15 Points This is a fairly simple category to score high in, as it deals with only three things. Beginning, middle, and end. In other words, organization. Have your wrestlers introduced, and start things off slow, but then pick up the pace in the center of the matchup. Go to a peak near the ending, and then let things roll to an enjoyable conclusion, with the victor truly victorious. Five points possible for beginning, five points possible for middle, five points possible for ending. Can't get any easier than that. Announcers: 5 Points Almost anyone will admit it: when writing in a format that includes comments from the announcers, these comments MUST BE INTERESTING! Failing to perfect color commentary is a fault of many matchwriters, but through this program we hope that it will not be a fault of yours. When a match is posted for you to write, it will include information about the announcers. It is your job to ensure that you capture these announcers' personalities, and use them to the best of your abilities, to ensure entertaining yet informative commentary. Spelling/Grammar: 15 Points Some may not care about this section. Some may not think it to be important. We at Lights Out Academy, however, DEFINITELY DO! There is nothing more distracting when reading a match then having to stumble over misspelled words, or deal with poor use of the English language. Some tips are to run your match through Spell Check on applications such as Microsoft Word, to make sure that no two sentences in your paragraph start with the same word and that they vary in length and complexity, and finally, proofread, proofread, proofread! Requirements: 10 Points When you are writing a match, you are capturing the hard work of two or more competitors, that have spent the entire week roleplaying to the best of their abilities, hoping to score a victory. That victory may not always come, but they want to at least see their character performing well in action, and that part is up to you. When a match is posted for you to write, you will be given valuable information concerning the wrestlers involved, as well as the format in which results should be written. Use this information at all times, and you will score high in this category. Overall Quality: 10 Points There are some things that a grader cannot put their finger on... Certain aspects of your match that make it an exceptionally enjoyable read. After reading your match, the overall taste that the grader is left with will go into this score, and it will determine if you have been able to peice all of the aforementioned information together, or if it has slipped between the cracks. The only peice of advice offered here is to lose yourself in the match... You will exceed even your own expectations.
