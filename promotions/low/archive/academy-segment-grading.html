Academy: Segment Writing Grading

captured 29 October 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
>>Navigation Main • Graduation • Tutorials • Forums Roleplaying • Rules • Grading Matchwriting • Rules • Grading Segment Writing • Rules • Grading Grading Structure The grading structure for in-cards in LOA is as followed, and will be followed out with every graded in-card. Below we will show the following criteria that LOA is looking for in in-cards and the amount of points it will be for a certain criteria. Originality: 20 points The originality of a in-card in LOA is the same as you might see around other great federations. In LOA we like it to be something special, a mix of something and stirred together. If you happen to watch wrestling on TV, it is usually filled out with sneak attacks and interviews. They’re are VERY unoriginal and the least to say, boring. In-cards can be taken anywhere! The key to originality is imagination! If you use your imagination, you are bound to get a high score in this! Using your wrestler backstage is permitted, but try to put a twist in it and you will be doing well. Length: 15 points Yes, length is required in in-cards. How ever, we are not looking for pages worth, we are looking for length that will not carry on and will get the point across. We do not like to see 5 lines of an in-card on the in-card forum; these sorts of in-cards will not be graded at all! You can put the length of an in-card in with detail and description, which is what makes a in-card great. Remember, a good length in an in-card will satisfy everyone. Description: 20 points Description is needed in in-cards! It gets very boring when you have no description at all. Joe Schmoe comes down the road on his motorcycle. He stops and gets off. He walks towards the arena. Jim Bo catches up with him and asks him for an interview. Wow, if that was on TV, I’d push the TV off the stand. Here, let’s put some description into this. The roaring sounds of a Harley Motorcycle are heard around the great city of New York! Cars stagger out of the way for the approaching Joe Schmoe. He races down the street, at top speeds of 120, nearly missing a turn and skidding out in the road. As he approaches the road ahead, he gears down and slowly comes to a stop, with his hands off the steering and taking off his gloves. He regains control and stops slowly away from the arena. Joe Schmoe shows his bravery, yet stupidity, by not wearing a helmet. He stops and kicks out the kickstand, and lets the motorcycle fall to the kickstand, and stand there. Slowly he gets off of the bike by putting his leg over the seat. As he does so, he grabs the keys to his Harley and pockets them into his blue jeans. He straightens out his loose black shirt of his picture on it and takes off his sunglasses, exposing his blue eyes. His blonde hair waves in the wind while he walks with his black boots across the pavement. From the corner of his eye he can see Jim Bo approaching with a microphone, trying to catch up and dragging a long wire for the microphone in his left hand. Joe stops and turns around and crosses his arm and looks at the approaching Jim Bo with a grin, who stops, nearly colliding with Joe. Instantly, Jim Bo mutters out and asks if he can get a interview. Ok, there. A bit long, but it has description that will get you that 20 points needed for description in your graded in-cards Plot: 15 points Yes, a plot is needed for your in-card! You can not have your wrestler walking around in the backstage area, doing frankly nothing and believe it’s an in-card. Oh no, you need a plot! A storyline. An angle! You need something to run with for your in-card! The formation of a stable and the retiring of a wrestler is a plot. The working out of your wrestler is not a plot; it has no reason to be posted at all! You must have a good reason for your in-card to be posted! A plot is what makes a good movie; and a plot is what makes a good in-card. Having a plot is the whole reason for an in-card. Without it, it’d be writing and gibberish and a waste of time. Spelling and Grammar: 10 points Though it might not seem like a lot of points, spelling and grammar is CRUCIAL to an in-card. First of all we must know what you are speaking of. We don’t want to see spelling such as “hey every1 how r u 2day?” That WILL not be allowed, period! You must have correct spelling and grammar. If you believer that you need help, please ask. Also we must know what is going on. If we see something like “Bob,runs,into,a,wall”. Yeah, you can see the problem can’t you? That is very poor grammar, and in the end spelling and grammar will bite you in the butt if you don’t remember it! Remember to do a proof read and spelling check after you are finish with an in-card. Overall Quality: 20 points. Quality means how good something is. How you describe it and how everyone wants to continue to read it. If you merely put in small detail, and very little detail, the reader might find it hard for an image to come to his or her mind. The point is to get a picture in their mind, and as they view that picture, they will continue to get a clear scene in their mind. The whole point of this is that you do not steer out of control, but stay in the plot of the in-card. If you follow this, you are sure to get a high marking for this. Over all points: 100 points.
