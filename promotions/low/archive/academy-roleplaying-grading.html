Academy: Roleplaying Grading

>>Navigation Main • Graduation • Tutorials • Forums Roleplaying • Rules • Grading Matchwriting • Rules • Grading Segment Writing • Rules • Grading Grading Structure Description: 25% The best of the best can describe VERY well. The best description is when someone who reads your role-play can say I knew where he was, what he looked like, and how he was feeling. The emotion, the scenery, and the appearance of yourself and other characters in your role-plays, are displayed to that point it makes you receive a perfect score of 25% in this category. I as Jamester see description as the highest point in any Role-Play. Such as this example I put together. “The dim faded lights reflect off his smooth creamy face as the beads of tears roll down his chin and make there way through his wild prickly forest like beard. His face in awe as his daughter’s neck is shredded in front of him. The knife rips through the skin piercing Jesse’s heart with tragedy and horror. His body curls up as the tears begin to roll quicker! His eyes shut and all he can say in a hollow voice is NO! Breaking the silence his sobs now become a quivering pattern of how much Jesse loves his daughter. He lies there curled up against the blood stained wall holding his daughter in his arms as the blood rushes out and covers his shirt and arms. He is losing her… He is losing everything he’s ever wanted in life. The love of his last hope has been drained from him. Jesse bends his head over letting his hair cover his daughters face as his piercing eyes look down at her, the blue eyes are the breaking point of this mans madness, once they are seen you cannot accuse him of anything. They are his innocent plead to help himself. There Jesse reaches over, his hand twitching and both arms covered in crimson red blood, he grabs the guilt knife that had sliced her neck. He picks it up still twitching; his eyes are seen through the clean side of the blade as he examines it. As he pulls it to his face he sobs even more, the room near pitch black besides the spinning light that hangs above him and glares a circle down in the center of the room. Jesse puts the knife up to his throat, and looks up then says a prayer silently, and pulls the knife quickly slicing his neck and falling backwards where he lies to die. Though before he dies, he puts his fingers to his throat and takes the crimson red blood only to rub it across his lips, then bend over and kiss his daughter on the forehead leaving a bloody lip print as a goodbye note. He fades away with his head on her lap and the blood pouring all over the concrete floor.” Quality: 25% Quality is how well a role-play is all together. Did you present a well-based role-play that can be easily understood and followed by the members of your efed or was it just a rambling on note you wrote just to get something up? As a member of LOW many who do not present a good story are most likely not going anywhere. Make sure the story is well put together, the role-play flows, the length is just right, and there are few mistakes in any of the categories for this category itself to even be over 20. This is a mainstream category so please work hard on this one itself. Storyline: 20% The storyline of your character is as much if not the same as your gimmick. Many confuse storyline with angles and say well he’s just this guy who wrestles. That isn’t going to get you anywhere, because LOW wants talent that has an edge to them. Every member should have a creative standpoint that points him out from all others. Mike Meyhu for one is a guy that has that, the gimmick and storyline to further a feud. He’s known as the number one brawler in LOW, or was until retired. But that it held true in all his role-plays, all his in-cards, and all his matches. A name that carries itself for your character gets you over. Tristan Belmont is known as a Warrior, that gimmick though used is great for him since Trigger was known as his mentor. See each of these make the character bigger as a whole. Make sure your character has a firm gimmick that will work within storylines of all types. If you make a character that does not fit in right, it may be the end of what looked like a LONG road. Keep that in mind. Organization: 20% Organization is a MAJOR part of being in LOW. Why? Well it’s not just grammar; it’s readability, and the style you use. All together this category is for those who don’t read over they’re work, which don’t write in a certain organized way and makes it all flow in different directions. Basically this category will kill you if you don’t watch out. Grammar or spelling as it’s known is 10% of this whole grade. When you spell something wrong it’s half a % off so you better double check by proofreading. Those who don’t read over there work will get failing marks in the academy, and that is no lie. Readability is the other 10% of this grade. When someone reads your Role-Play it should make sense. It should have a quality to it that brings you to the championship of LOW. When someone reads a Role-play it must be flowing so that the reader doesn’t have to scroll up to find out where and what he is reading, or so he isn’t lost. This is important and something I myself want everyone to be better in. The readability of any Promo is a main focus, without it, you’re a headless chicken running into everything and falling down often. Length: 10% Length is not to be based on lines. If I EVER at anytime see a grader say that the member had a short amount of lines I’ll freaking snap them in half and boot them from the academy. For length is whether you can make the reader feel he’s read enough, and still wants more in your next role-play. If you can’t attract and audience how will you EVER be great in LOW? Overall: 100%
