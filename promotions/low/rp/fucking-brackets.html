Fucking Brackets....

captured 11 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
San Antonio, Texas July 16, 1988 A small boy sits still, lying in a small hospital bed. His arms are folded in front of him, a tube running from his left wrist up to a small bad of liquid next to the bed. He stares blankly at the room’s blank wall, not blinking, not moving. The camera zooms out a bit, revealing a woman and a man dressed in a long white coat peering in from the door. The woman looks troubled, whilst the man seems a bit interested in what the child is doing. Woman: I don’t get it. Why would he do this? Why!? Man: Ma’am, please try and stay calm. Is there anything that was said or done over the past few days that would have upset Curtis enough to try to end his own life? The woman continues to stare at the boy, watching him stare at the blank wall. Man: Ma’am? The man, most likely a doctor from what he has asked the woman, taps her on her shoulder. The woman shakes out of her trance and looks around nervously. Woman: I’m sorry. What were you saying, Doctor….? Doctor: Dr. Banks. Ma’am, I know this is a difficult time for you and your family, but I really need you to focus. Can you do that for me? The woman nods silently. Dr. Banks: Thank you. Now, was there anything said or done to Curtis that could have caused this? Woman: Ummm… The woman looks down to the ground, replaying the last few days over in her head. Woman: No, nothing out of the ordinary. Dr. Banks: Okay. I understand that your family just recently moved to San Antonio? Woman: Yes. Dr. Banks begins to walk down the hall, motioning for the woman to accompany him. The woman takes one last look at her motionless son before heading down the hallway with the doctor. They turn left a few yards down the hall and enter a small office, most likely Dr. Banks’. Dr. Banks steps behind the desk and sits down into a nice leather chair. Dr. Banks: Please, Mrs. McPherson, have a seat. Mrs. McPherson looks down at a small, wooden chair next to the desk and takes a seat, folding one leg over the other. Dr. Banks: So, is your family new to the area, or did you just decide to move to the city? Mrs. McPherson: No, we’re new to the area. We just moved here from California. Dr. Banks’ eyebrows spring up, a bit surprised by the answer he had received. Mrs. McPherson notices his reaction and becomes a bit curious of what he’s thinking. Mrs. McPherson: Is that bad? Dr. Banks: No, no, no. It’s just…..how did Curtis feel about the move? Mrs. McPherson chuckles a bit. Mrs. McPherson: Oh, he hated it. Curtis loved California. He was always hanging out down on the beach, that’s where I would always find him. He loved the ocean. He loved to surf… Mrs. McPherson begins to get a little choked up, her eyes watering up with tears. She looks down and grabs a tissue from her purse and dabs her eyes. Mrs. McPherson: Sorry about that. It’s just…he was so happy in California. I’d do anything to allow him to go back there. Dr. Banks: What if you could? Mrs. McPherson looks up from her purse and stares wide-eyed at Dr. Banks. Mrs. McPherson: What do you mean? Dr. Banks smiles back at her. Dr. Banks: I have a friend who is a psychiatrist in California. He lives in Newport Beach, which is a big surfing community. Mrs. McPherson: That’s where we’re from! Newport Beach! Dr. Banks and Mrs. McPherson both smile at each other. Dr. Banks: I’ll set up a meeting with Curtis as soon as he is available. Mrs. McPherson stands and smiles at Dr. Banks. Mrs. McPherson: Thank you, Dr. Banks. Dr. Banks: It’s no problem. The two shake hands and Mrs. McPherson exits the office. The camera switches back to Curtis’ room, where the bed is empty. Curtis is standing at the door of the room, smiling from ear to ear. Newport Beach, California July 24, 1988 A young man, most likely in his middle to late twenties sits in a cozy leather chair. The young man is wearing a black business suit and seems to be looking over some sort of paperwork. A large window is seen behind him. The window has a beautiful view of the ocean, visible is a long, wooden pier filled with weekend beach goers. The sun shines brightly on the ocean’s surface, a small glare shoots into the lens of the camera. The young man pulls out a journal and begins to write something from the paperwork he had been studying. Suddenly, a loud knock interrupts his writing. The young man looks towards where the sound came from and tucks the journal into his desk. He stands up from behind it and walks towards a large wooden door next to a bookcase filled with books. Young Man: Who is it? A young woman speaks from behind the door. Young Woman: A Mrs. McPherson and her son Curtis to see you, Dr. Todd. Dr. Todd’s eyebrows launch up his forehead as if he had been reminded of something he had forgotten. Dr. Todd: Ah yes, I’ve been expecting them. Dr. Todd cranks open the deadbolt and twisted the knob, opening the big doors. Mrs. McPherson and Curtis enter the room, a bit nervous and shy. Dr. Todd: Hello, hello. This must be Curtis. Mrs. McPherson steps to the side to give Dr. Todd a good view of Curtis. Dr. Todd bends down to become eye level with Curtis. Dr. Todd tries to look straight into Curtis’ eyes, but Curtis shies away, instead looking at the ocean in the window of the office. Mrs. McPherson: Thank you for seeing us on such short notice, Doctor. Dr. Todd: Oh, it’s no problem really. Mrs. McPherson: No, I don’t think you quite understand what this means to us. Dr. Todd: I understand, and it really is no problem at all. I’m sorry, but I didn’t catch your name, what was it? Mrs. McPherson: Oh, it’s Sandy. Sandy McPherson. Dr. Todd: Oh, okay. Well, hello Sandy, I’m Dr. Toby Todd. The two shake hands and smile before having an awkward silence between them. After a few seconds, Mrs. McPherson feels a yank on her blouse, looking down to find Curtis tugging her to look at the ocean. She glances towards it and then rolls her eyes. Mrs. McPherson: Curtis, what did I tell you!? We can go to the beach after we talk to Dr. Todd! Curtis looks down to the ground, saddened by his mother’s stern voice. Dr. Todd: No, it’s okay. It might be better to get Curtis’ feelings in an environment that he is comfortable in. Mrs. McPherson: Oh…well, okay. Come on, Curtis. Let’s go. The boy grabs on to his mother’s hand and the three walk through the doors of the office. The camera picks up on them once they have reached the steps of the beach and begin to descend down onto the sand. Dr. Todd: So, Curtis, why don’t you tell me a little about yourself? The child ignores the question, instead running through the sand towards the bright blue ocean. He stops at the water’s edge, letting the foam rinse his feet. He smiles, looking out into the ocean, watching the waves form and crash. The camera switches to Dr. Todd and Mrs. McPherson, who are walking along the beach together. Dr. Todd has his hands in his pockets and walks with his eyes on his feet while Mrs. McPherson holds her purse nervously as she watches Curtis at the water’s edge. Dr. Todd: So, how long ago did your family move to San Antonio? Mrs. McPherson: Two months ago. It’s been really hard on all of us, especially Curtis. Dr. Todd: And had Curtis shown any signs of depression or anger before he tried to commit suicide? Mrs. McPherson: N-n-no. He seemed fine before he did it. Although, he was much less talkative once we moved. Dr. Todd nods in agreement. Dr. Todd: That is to be expected after a traumatizing event like moving. Mrs. McPherson’s face lights up with a sudden outburst. Mrs. McPherson: It wasn’t traumatic! A lot of families go through what our family did, and they all come out just fine! Dr. Todd’s eyebrows spring up in interest. Dr. Todd: Mrs. McPherson, are there any troubles at home? Mrs. McPherson breathes in deeply before whimpering out a small response. Mrs. McPherson: T-t-troubles? W-w-what kinds of troubles are you talking about? Dr. Todd: Any sort of abuse going on inside the family? Drugs, alcohol, sexual, physical abuse? Mrs. McPherson stops walking and turns away from Dr. Todd, holding her purse tightly to her chest. Dr. Todd: Mrs. McPherson? Dr. Todd places his hand on Mrs. McPherson’s shoulder, trying to get a response from her. Instead, he notices her grimace in pain as he places his hand. Mrs. McPherson begins to sob softly. Dr. Todd pulls Mrs. McPherson’s blouse down off of her shoulder, exposing large scraps and bruises. Mrs. McPherson quickly pulls her blouse back over her shoulder as Curtis begins to glance back at his mother from the water. Dr. Todd: Mrs. McPherson, who did this to you!? Mrs. McPherson: NO ONE! Dr. Todd: It was your husband, wasn’t it!? Mrs. McPherson: NO! Suddenly, Mrs. McPherson drops her purse and begins to run through the sand. Dr. Todd chases after her, trying to run in his business suit and shoes. Mrs. McPherson begins to scream as she runs, heading towards the water. Mrs. McPherson glances behind her, seeing Dr. Todd struggle to catch up in the sand. WHACK!!! Mrs. McPherson goes down in a heap, clutching her leg and screaming. She looks up to see her son, Curtis, clutching a large tree branch, most likely washed ashore, in his hands. Curtis looks down at his crying mother with an anger glare. A lone tear drips down Curtis’ face. Mrs. McPherson: CURTIS! WHY DID YOU DO THAT!? WHY DID YOU HURT MOMMY!? Curtis gives a small smirk. Curtis: You’re not my mommy! Curtis pulls back the branch, ready to strike again, but before it comes forward, a hand grabs the branch. Dr. Todd stands up, pulling the branch away from Curtis. Dr. Todd: Curtis, what did you do!? Mrs. McPherson, are you alright? Dr. Todd grabs Curtis by the midsection and tries to pull him away, but notices that Curtis is in pain as he pulls him. Dr. Todd lifts up Curtis’ shirt to reveal a large amount of bruises and cuts. Curtis’ entire back seems to resemble one large black eye. Dr. Todd: My god! Who did this to you!? Curtis doesn’t answer Dr. Todd, but instead looks at his mother with eyes full of hatred. Dr. Todd looks from Curtis to Mrs. McPherson, trying to pull all the pieces of the puzzle together. Texas State Courthouse – Austin, Texas January 23, 1989 A court case is in session, with both benches standing up. The jury is also standing as it appears they are reading the verdict for the case in question. The camera cuts to a view of the benches. At one bench stands Curtis, Dr. Todd and a brown haired woman and at the other bench stands Mrs. McPherson, a man that looks like an older version of Curtis, and an old, grey-haired man. All six stand in silence as they listen to the verdict. Juror: In the case of McPherson versus Todd, we award the verdict to Todd and award Dr. Tobias Tyler Todd full custody of Curtis James McPherson. Screams of joy come from the bench of Dr. Todd and Curtis as they celebrate their victory. Dr. Todd lifts Curtis up into the air and twirls him around, both with large smiles on their faces. Dr. Todd brings Curtis down and gives him a large hug, holding him tightly. The McPherson’s sit quietly at their bench, watching Dr. Todd and their son jubilate. Mrs. McPherson places her head in her hands and begins to cry, while Mr. McPherson slams his fists down on the desk in frustration. The judge slams his gavel down on his desk. Judge: ORDER! ORDER THIS INSTANT! Dr. Todd drops Curtis and they both stand next to each other quietly. Everyone else quiets down and looks at the judge intently. Judge: In light of the information brought out during this trial, I have some punishments to hand out. The audience of the case lets out a small gasp and a bit of murmuring goes on. The judge slams down his gavel again. Judge: I WANT THIS PLACE DEAFLY SILENT! All talking in the courtroom stops and the judge waits until he is satisfied to go on with his punishments. Judge: MISTER JAMES CURTIS MCPHERSON… Mr. McPherson looks up at the judge intently, nervously, wondering what sort of punishment he will be given by the judge. Judge: You have been sentenced to two years in Texas State Prison and one year of probation on two counts of spousal abuse. Mr. McPherson looks shocked as he looks at his wife, who has a bit of a smile on her face. The bailiff walks up to Mr. McPherson and locks him into handcuffs, escorting him the rest of the way out of the courthouse. Before leaving, Mr. McPherson says one last thing to his the court and his family. Mr. McPherson: I WAS PROTECTING MY CHILDREN FROM THAT DEVIL OF A WOMAN I MARRIED! I’M A HERO, NOT A VILLIAN! I’M A HERO! The bailiff shoves Mr. McPherson the rest of the way out of the courthouse and then court goes back to normal. Dr. Todd glances down at Curtis, who is looking towards the door his father had just gone through. A tear rolls down his face, but he quickly wipes it away with his hand. Dr. Todd leans down to Curtis and whispers in his ear. Dr. Todd: Don’t worry, Curtis, everything is going to be okay. Curtis nods as Dr. Todd pats him on the shoulder and returns to an upright position. Judge: AND MRS. SANDY ROBERTS MCPHERSON… Mrs. McPherson looks towards the judge, but not with the same look of nervousness that her husband had shown moments ago. She looks calm, almost prepared for what was to come. Judge: You have been sentenced to twenty years in the Texas State Women’s Prison and two years of house arrest for four counts of child abuse and two counts of drug abuse. Mrs. McPherson nods silently as she hears her punishments. The bailiff comes over and places the handcuffs on her and escorts her to the exit. As she leaves the courthouse, she never takes her eyes off of her son Curtis. Curtis stares back at the woman, showing no emotion. Mrs. McPherson goes through the doorway, never to be seen again by Curtis. Judge: Seeing as everything has been taken care of, this case is over. Court is adjourned. The judge slams the gavel down a last time before climbing out of his seat and exiting the court room into the judge’s quarters. Dr. Todd and Curtis rise to their feet and shake hands with the brown haired woman seated next to them. After they are done with her, Dr. Todd tussles Curtis’ hair before motioning for them to leave. Curtis gives him a big hug before walking out of the courtroom under the arm of Dr. Todd. Pipeline Beach – Oahu, Hawaii June 7, 1996 Curtis and Dr. Todd are seen sitting under an umbrella on a beach, waxing surf boards. Both look a bit older than we have seen before, Curtis now looking like a man. Dr. Todd: Well, Ceej, how do you like Hawaii so far? Curtis: It’s great. Beautiful beaches, beautiful waves …beautiful women. Dr. Todd and Curtis share a small laugh. Dr. Todd: Yeah, that was the impression I got when I first came here. I was about your age, except I came with my best friend in the world. Curtis: Funny, ‘cause that’s who I’m here with. The two share a smile and a warm moment before Dr. Todd reaches over and grabs Curtis into a playful headlock. He begins to rub his knuckles into the top of Curtis’ head as they laugh together. Finally, Dr. Todd lets go and they both return to waxing their boards. Curtis: Lucky you let up, ‘cause I was about to go Stone Cold on your old butt. Dr. Todd: Old!? Who you calling old!? Curtis: You, Mr. Grey Hair. Dr. Todd looks up as far as he can and pats his head, laughing. Curtis smiles and sticks out his tongue. Dr. Todd: Alright, Ceej, what’d you say about us hitting the waves now? Curtis: Surf’s up, brah! Both men grab their boards and run towards the ocean, jumping in and paddling out to the waves. A ukulele begins to strum a familiar Hawaiian tune as clips of Dr. Todd and Curtis surfing plays. The clips show Dr. Todd stealing waves from Curtis and watching them both smile and laugh in the process. It shows both men standing on the same board, Curtis at the front, Dr. Todd in the back. Finally, the music stops playing and the video stops playing, revealing Dr. Todd and Curtis sitting on their boards waiting for the next set of waves to come rolling in. Curtis looks wide awake, ready for the next waves to come, while Dr. Todd looks beat and red. Dr. Todd makes his feelings known. Dr. Todd: WHEW! I’m beat. What do you say we head back in and find a restaurant with some burgers and cokes? Curtis: Nah, I need to get one more big wave before I can call it a day. Dr. Todd paddles his board around to face the beach. Dr. Todd: Alrighty then. I’ll be under that umbrella looking for a Hawaiian babe to take home as a souvenir. Dr. Todd smiles and raises his eyebrows as Curtis just laughs and shakes his head. Curtis: Alright, I’ll be in a minute. The next set is about to roll in. Dr. Todd: Alright, later. Oh, and Curtis? Curtis looks at Dr. Todd with question. Dr. Todd doesn’t call him Curtis often. Curtis: Yeah? Dr. Todd: Be careful, alright? Curtis: No doubt. Later. Dr. Todd: See ya. Dr. Todd lies down on his board and begins to paddle in. Curtis watches for a second before turning back to the waves and waiting for the next big wave to hit. He sits patiently for a while, bobbing up and down as wimpy waves roll past. Curtis sighs and then paddles in a circle, turning back towards the beach. He notices that the sun has begun to disappear behind the large Hawaiian volcanoes in the distance. Curtis: Must be getting on six o’clock. He glances to the beach, trying to find the umbrella that he and Dr. Todd had brought, it was still there. Curtis squints his eyes, trying to make out what was going on underneath the umbrella. The camera zooms in a bit, revealing Dr. Todd and a gorgeous Hawaiian woman sitting beneath the umbrella sipping beers. Curtis shakes his head, smiling. Curtis: That’s my man. Curtis paddles in a circle again, facing the waves. His eyes widen to the surprise of a huge set of waves coming towards him. His heart begins to pound in his chest as he quickly paddles around and lies on his board. The wave begins to build around him, Curtis paddling as hard as he can. The board begins to drift up the wave. Curtis quickly springs to his feet and turns the board downward into the wave. The tunnel of the wave opens and Curtis maneuvers the surfboard into it. He jets through the water, letting his hand flow through the wave as he goes. As he rides through the mist, he lets out a yelp of joy. Curtis: WOOOO!!! As he rides through, he notices a small gray shape underneath the wave. The shape becomes bigger and bigger, until it nearly surrounds him on all sides. Curtis’ facial expressions quickly go from being incredibly happy to becoming fearful for his life. The figure emerges from the water, making a loud snapping sound in the process. Curtis lets out a scream as he falls off of the board and hits the water with a smack. Las Vegas, Nevada March 15, 2001 6:15 P.M. An older, much manlier and built looking Curtis walks through a Las Vegas casino, a large wad of gum in his mouth. Curtis walks past the slot machines, money and little electronic jingles bursting through the room. Curtis walks past the blackjack, craps, roulette, and poker tables, not even glancing at any of them. He continues to walk, seemingly knowing exactly where he is heading. Finally, he takes a turn and heads down to a large room in the back end of the casino. He walks through a doorway and enters a room filled with booths and TV’s. Curtis smiles as he walks up to the bar at the front of the room and checks out how the NCAA Tournament brackets are looking. Curtis takes a seat near the middle of the large board and gives it a hard stare. A talkative, heavy set man seated to the right of Curtis glances towards him and then away. Suddenly, the man glances back again, with eyes wide with amazement. Fat Guy: Hey, I know you! You’re CJ Tyler, from XWE! Curtis looks away and rolls his eyes, fearing that he’ll be stuck talking to another stupid wrestling fan. Fat Guy: Yeah, you are him! I bet on you against Violent last week! You won me three hundred bucks, man! Curtis looks back at the fat guy with sarcasm. Curtis: I’m thrilled. The fat guy doesn’t notice Curtis’ sarcasm. Fat Guy: Yeah, it’s a shame about that match with Farthing a couple weeks ago. I thought you had ‘em. Lucky for me, I bet on Farthing. Curtis: Great. Will you excuse me? Fat Guy: Sure. Curtis gets up from his seat and moves down the row of stools, planting himself in on at the other end. The bartender then walks up to Curtis and leans down on the counter. Bartender: Eh, don’t worry about that guy. He does that to everyone he’s bet against and won. Curtis: What guy? The bartender smiles and laughs at Curtis. Bartender: That’s the spirit, kid. So, can I get you a drink or something? Curtis: Yeah, I’ll have a Coors. The Bartender grabs a glass and fills it with beer. He fills it to the top and then places it in front of Curtis. Bartender: One Coors, and for CJ Tyler, it’s on the house. The bartender winks at Curtis. Curtis: Thanks. Bartender: No problem. The bartender nods and then walks down to the other side of the bar, helping another client on that end. Curtis grabs the beer and takes a small sip from it, smacking his lips together once he puts it down. Another man walks up to Curtis from behind and holds a pen and a pad of paper. Man: Excuse me, sir? Curtis doesn’t look at the man, but has a feeling he knows why he’s there. Curtis looks up to the ceiling, frustrated. Curtis: Sorry, buddy. No autographs tonight. The man gives Curtis a funny look and then tries again. Man: No sir, I’m here to ask if there were any bets you would like to make tonight? Curtis looks up at the man, a bit embarrassed. Curtis: Oh! Sorry, it’s my first time in one of these places. Man: I see. Curtis takes another look at the large board in front of him before turning back to the man. Curtis: Ummm, yea. I’ll take Iowa State to cover Hampton by twenty and then I’ll take them through to win the tournament. The man jots down the bets that Curtis has made and then pauses to ask another question. Man: And how much will your wager be tonight, sir? The man looks down and awaits an answer as Curtis pulls out his wallet and counts his money. Curtis: Ummm, how about everything in my wallet? Thirty-two hundred dollars. Man: Okay, sir. Thank you. Curtis: You’re welcome. Curtis gives the money to the man and the man gives Curtis a receipt for his bet before walking away from Curtis. Curtis looks back to the board and takes another sip of his beer. He glances at the time for his game and then to his watch. Curtis: Six thirty, game’s at seven. Okay, just a half hour before game time. 9:15 P.M. Curtis is sitting in the same spot, watching the Iowa State-Hampton intently on the screen above him. A line of five or six empty glasses sit in front of Curtis, each with a bit of foam somewhere inside. Curtis slams down his fist in frustration as Hampton nails a three pointer to cut Iowa State’s lead to four points. Curtis: Come on, ISU! Get your heads in the game! Curtis signals to the bartender for another beer, but the bartender shakes his head. Curtis shrugs and turns his attention back to the game. Announcer: Hampton hits another three and now trail by just one point with just thirty seconds remaining! This has been an amazing second half, folks, as Hampton has held Iowa State scoreless from the field over the last six and a half minutes. Amazing. Curtis slams his fist down again and wipes his forehead with his other hand. Curtis: Iowa State’s still got this. Hampton’s gonna choke. Announcer: Williams get the ball, ten seconds remaining, he turns! He shoots! He made it! Tarvis Williams hits it and gives Hampton a one point lead! Iowa State has a chance at one last shot! The inbound pass, they dribble it up, he shoots! NO GOOD! And fifteenth rank Hampton has shocked number two ranked Iowa State in the first round by the final score of fifty-eight to fifty-seven. Boy, what a finish! If I had bet on Iowa State to win this thing, I’d hang myself. Wow! Curtis stares at the screen in shock, while others who had picked the upset jump around joyfully. Curtis slams his head against the counter repeatedly, trying to rid himself of his own self pity. He moans and groans as the bartender comes up to him, frowning. Bartender: Eh, tough luck kid. But, we’re closing up here in a minute, time to pay for your bill. Curtis looks up at the bartender in confusion. Curtis: But I thought you said it was on the house for me!? The bartender nods his head. Bartender: That first one was. But after that, it was all you. Now, pay up! Curtis grabs his wallet and goes to grab some cash to pay off the ass of a bartender, but finds that his wallet is empty from the bet he had made. Curtis looks up to the bartender with embarrassment. Curtis: I don’t have any money left. The bartender shakes his head and jerks his finger across his throat. Curtis bangs his head on the counter again. Las Vegas, Nevada March 18, 2005 Curtis enters the casino just like he did back on that horrible night in 2001. He does the exact same thing he had done then, except now he was wiser and also better known. Countless people recognize him as he walks through the casino and enters the sports book as he had done just four years earlier. Several faces look to the door as Curtis enters, some showing faces of surprise or shock, while others show indifference. As Curtis walks to a seat at the bar, his cell phone begins to ring from inside his pocket. He pulls the phone out and checks the screen to see who the call is. The ringer doesn’t play for long, but it is easily identified: Californication, Curtis’ theme music. He flips open the phone and places it to his ear. Curtis: What up, Doc? Man On Phone: Ceej, my boy! Where are you? Curtis: In Las Vegas. Man On Phone: Curtis, what have a told you about Vegas? Need I remind you what happened last time you were there? Curtis rolls his eyes. Curtis: I know, Dr. Todd, but that was four years ago. I know a lot more about this stuff now. Dr. Todd: I’m still not sure about it. I mean, you’re doing so well, why do you have to risk it on something as stupid as gambling? Curtis: Relax, Doc. I’m not going to spend all of my money on this thing like I did last time. In fact, I’m hardly going to spend any money on this. Dr. Todd: Shouldn’t you be preparing for that match you have coming up? The one with Haze, Adams, and Demond? Curtis sighs. He hadn’t been expecting a lecture about his training by Dr. Todd. Curtis: I’m as prepared as I’m gonna be. It’s gonna be a tough match, but training more than usual isn’t going to help at all. You’re still coming, right? Dr. Todd: I wouldn’t miss it for the world. Curtis nods and smiles. Curtis: Good, so I’ll see you in Penn. Dr. Todd: Okay, and Curtis? Curtis: Yeah? Dr. Todd: I love you, man. Curtis gets a sly grin on his face. Curtis: I love you too, Doc. Later. Dr. Todd: Bye. Curtis hangs up the phone and continues to walk to the bar. He grabs a seat at the bar and gets the attention of the bartender, the same guy from four years ago. The bartender notices that it is Curtis and has a look of joy and surprise. Bartender: Hey CJ! I never thought I’d see you back here! What can I get you!? Curtis smiles. Curtis: Same as last time. A Coors. The bartender stops smiling and points to Curtis. Bartender: Coming right up, but one thing. You’re gonna pay for this, right? Curtis laughs and smiles. Curtis: Yeah. The bartender smiles back. Bartender: Okay then. The camera begins to zoom out as a man walks up to Curtis with a pen and a pad of paper. Curtis: Yeah, let me have Syracuse over Vermont for five thousand…and Kansas over Bucknell for ten thousand…. End
