Underdog? Maybe. Loser? Not Quite Yet.

captured 23 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Nine months of hell and poverty, all for nothing. I found myself yet again traveling on a road with no path. It wasn’t so much of a bother as most would think it to be. It’s something I’ve grown well accustomed to, at least since I realized that the critics were right. I really was a nobody with a streak of good luck. I really shouldn’t have depended on all that hype to keep a family alive and rekindle a lost relationship. “You blew it again Tess.” Every single critic I had ever met and had the chance ot talk to had found a home inside my head. As much as they knew how to fill my head with the power of hope for a new tommorow, where I could rise towards the top again, they also knew how to choke the life out of me. They also knew how to make me dance into failure like I were their own puppet on a string, and that’s exactly what they wanted. There wasn't any room for people like me anymore. The kind that just wanted to go into a match to learn how to improve themselves. Not the kind that looked for a fight every where they turned. But that’s not what the media wanted. It was time for the spotlight to shine on their new favorites, people like Jason Hughes and Patti Anderson, who might have known how to talk tough, but when the time came they never wanted to step up to the plate and prove themselves. The media lived for these kind of people. The people they could always get a word out of, because they lived for the spotlight. It was their only way of grabbing the attention they so desired. And now they have it, and use it to proclaim their greatness, or at least the greatness they’d like to believe they have. I was like that once myself, and then I learned the same way they eventually will, that all good things must come to an end. “You knew very well you didn’t stand a chance against John Adams.” It was true, I didn’t. But it didn’t stop me from going into the match with a positive outlook. Never before had someone having nearly two feet over me been a challenge. But now, it mattered. After being manhandled by Hessian in RPW, you would have thought I learned my lesson. You would have thought I’d stay away from the extreme type. Especially the large ones. ….But I didn’t. I refused to give in. I couldn’t give in. I had two children to support. So rather than find a place that would be more suitable for such a petite and fragile woman like myself, I decided to go against the odds. I decided to go the last place someone would expect someone like me to be. A place where if I were to survive, I’d be required to keep my defenses at the highest. A place called Lights Out Wrestling. Most would consider it stupidity on my behalf, but at this point I had nothing to lose and everything to gain…back. I lost all my respect when I let my head slip under water against William Curr, a new face in the world of wrestling, and then it only got worse when I lost to John Adams. I was supposed to be the underdog kind. The type you would never expect to win in the beginning, but always managed to walk away with her arms raised high in the end. Yet now I became the slaughter type. The highly overrated slaughter type that didn’t stand a chance anymore. But it wasn’t going to last for long. I didn’t come to this business to be overlooked. I came to be noticed. Something I never was before. I knew what it was like to be appreciated, and I wanted that back. I wanted the respect I used to have. But I knew very well it wouldn’t just be handed to me in a silver platter, this time. I knew that if I were going to get that I’d have to prove something new. Something extreme, real extreme. Meaning that I’d have to show that I could hang with the toughest of them. People already knew I was tough, but it didn’t do much good considering all the tough things I did came in the form of losing and barely being able to walk again, yet somehow getting back up and doing it all again. But now, it was time to prove something new. It was time to prove that not only could I take, I could dish, without having to be extreme like all the others. I wasn’t a wrestler, I wasn’t a fighter, I was merely a woman trying to keep myself and my family alive and well, while making a statement. Matt Meyhu, Cobi Deekins, and John Adams were simply the first that would find that out. Together, they were just what I needed to rekindle my flame, so that I could once again shine brightly in the eyes of those watching. Forget the critics. Forget Aaron Rain and Enigma. It was time for me to learn how to do things on my own.
