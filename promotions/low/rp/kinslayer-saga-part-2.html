Kinslayer Saga Part 2

captured 11 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
He hadn’t noticed how much more he enjoyed the darkness. He used to love walking at noon through the fields. He used to love riding his stallion, Manderb. How he missed him. He missed most of the things that he had. He was a respected man, and a man with power. Now he was nobody. Right now, he didn’t really care about things like that. His only goal in life now was to finally kill his nemesis, if he was really alive still. He had started doubting Ur’s word ever since he thought about it. There was no logic to it, and if he really was in this era, why didn’t Ur just tell him where he was. He knew that Ur was just playing games with him. Playing games so that he would have his mind busy. It was a distraction from the important things. Ur knew that he had complete control over Dagoth. There was nothing to do except his will. The irony of his life, he was an immortal who owed his immortality to the devil and was his slave forever. Dagoth had gotten accustomed to many of the things that existed in this new era, but there was always something new to surprise him. Be it an MP3 Player or an X-Box, there was always something to keep him busy. Once, a very famous author of vampire novels wrote that the most common cause of the death of these immortal creatures was time itself. These creatures couldn’t move on and leave their past behind, making them obsolete and suicidal. Dagoth was nowhere near suicidal. He enjoyed this era. He missed the fields and the trees, but now the trees were light posts and the asphalt is the grass. He enjoyed living this new life. He still looked back at his old life with nostalgia. He missed his wife and his daughter. Dagoth was sitting in his room thinking. He couldn’t help but think about the past, about what had made him what he was now. He looked at himself in the mirror, his long black hair almost covering his coffee colored face. His eyes had changed color to black, he hadn’t noticed until a few days ago. He had trimmed his beard like the people of this era. He only had a goatee now. He looked hard at the mirror. He could see what had happened in the mirror. That day… that dreadful day. The day that had made history, and had doomed the Huns to extinction. It was all because of their jealous leader. Attila feared the power Dagoth had over the army. Unlike Dagoth, he didn’t trust anyone. Dagoth had trusted him with his own life and that of his family and he had been betrayed. The people paid for it. -- He sat up with a start. He looked around and sighed in relief as he saw the body of his wife sleeping soundly besides him. It was still dark outside but the sun would be coming out in a few hours. He got up and went outside. He had grabbed his sword and tied it to his leather belt. Dagoth went to attend his trusted friend, Manderb. He got on his black stallion and went riding in the night. He always loved to take these rides at night. He could see the moon and the bright stars in the sky. This day, it was important for him. This was the day where he would lead the army of the Huns and attack Rome. He was worried though, he had just had a nightmare, a bad omen. He dreamed of a crow. Crow’s were never good in dreams and to Dagoth they were a bad omen. He understood that he had to go to war; there was no way to circumvent it. Attila was expecting him to take Rome by force, and once Rome was taken, they would both be kings, and that is what Dagoth knew had Attila at the edge of his seat. He didn’t want to share his power with anyone. It didn’t matter; Dagoth was not in the search of power. He had all that he ever wanted. A wife and an offspring. He had gotten down from Manderb and let him eat some of the fresh wet grass. Manderb had always been with him, ever since he became a man at the age of 14. He was able to tame this stallion and ever since, he had accompanied him everywhere. He was watching the sun on the horizon. This was one of the things he enjoyed about life. Dawn was always something to see. The birth of a new day. This is why men existed, to look at these things and appreciate them. Dawn was the beginning of life. The irony to him was that no matter how beautiful the birth of a new day, or a new creature was men would always prefer war. He knew all about war. He was the General of the Hun Army. He had trained these warriors all their lives to be the best soldiers they could be so that they could defeat the Romans. They could do it, he had faith in them. He knew though, that he had to lead them and show them that he had no fear. That the descendant of the great Sumerian King Magog would bow to no one. He had defeated so many tribes already that the people knew he was the best warrior in the land. He grabbed his sword and unsheathed it. The shining metal receiving the glow of the sun. He looked at his sword and he not only saw the pain and suffering that such a weapon could cause and would cause, but he saw what protected him and his family from his enemies. His sword was made for that sole purpose of protecting his family and he would never rest if his family was ever hurt. He sheathed the sword and tied the belt around his waist. He took his bow and arrow and went into the forest to hunt for the food of the day. This was going to be a grandiose day, so he wanted to find a deer that could be cooked nicely. He walked silently through the trees with the bow ready. He had very keen ears and the eyes of a hawk. So when he heard the sound of ruffled branches, he knew what he had to do. He set the arrow on the bow and moved towards the sound. He was careful not to make a sound even though he had a huge frame. He finally saw the deer eating some grass. He raised the bow and pulled back the arrow. A normal man would not be able to pull back on the string of his bow. He pulled back as much as possible and aimed the arrow for the neck. He released it. The deer heard the sound and raised his head, but before his feet could react, the arrow pierced his flesh and killed it instantly. Dagoth, satisfied with the shot, went over to the fallen animal and with his right hand, touched where the arrow pierced the deer’s flesh. He brought his now bloody hand to his face and with the index finger made a vertical line on his forehead. This was a symbol of respect. This was to show the gods that he had killed out of necessity. He grabbed the deer and easily slung it over his shoulder. As he made his way back, he thought about all the people that he had killed over the years. All the necessary deaths that he had caused to protect his family. All the Ostrogoths and Visigoths he had to kill, not to mention the countless Germanic tribes. He had killed hundreds in his life. He got back to his horse and tied the deer up. He got on the horse and started moving towards the house. He liked the ride, so he was going slowly. He noticed smoke on the horizon though. He realized that it was in the way to his house so he started galloping towards his family. He saw his house burning. Outside two men were raping his wife and his ten winters old girl. His eyes burned with anger. He jumped down from the horse and charged at the two men. They realized he was coming, and they killed his family with their swords, relentlessly. They pierced that flesh that was his also. He halted in shock. He could not believe what he was seeing. The pool of blood that had formed around his family stained the grass crimson. The two men attacked him with their swords. None were as good or as powerful as Dagoth. One of them was on his knees after giving up his sword. They had both surrendered and were asking for mercy. Dagoth would have let them go, but they had taken away from him all that he loved. “Who sent you? Why did you kill my family!?” The two men were begging for their lives and Dagoth raised his sword. He grabbed it with both hands and slashed it across one of the men’s neck. His head fell down and rolled right besides the other man who in shocked backed away and tried to get up before being kicked back down by Dagoth. Dagoth grabbed the severed head of the man and put it right in front of the man’s face, who was now crying. “Who sent you!?” “…eh… Attila…” Dagoth’s eyes were about to pop out of his head. He could not believe what he had just been told. How could Attila betray him like that? They were supposed to be friends. He didn’t want to believe the man, but he was so afraid that he didn’t think the man would dare to lie. Dagoth wiped the blood off his sword on the man’s shirt. “Have mercy! Please!” Dagoth looked at the man in the eyes, and at that moment the man realized that he was dead just like his friend. The anger just seethed out of his eyes. The man closed his eyes to the inevitable. Dagoth decapitated him like he did the other man. He then dropped his sword to the floor and ran towards his dead family. He knelt down and with one arm hugged his wife and with the other his child. He had been destroyed along with them. He stayed like that for a long time, not knowing what else to do and not wanting to let them go. That night, he burned their bodies and sat around planning how he would destroy Attila. The only thing he had left was the pendant he had made for his wife. That and the memories of them. Memories that would hunt them for his entire life. -- He was touching the pendant at that moment, and it seemed to weigh so much. All the grief and pain that had been passed on to the pendant was kept in there. His soul was lost, and he knew this, but he would find the man who did this to him. Who sent him on a tailspin of hate, anger and loneliness. To historians he was and would always be the antagonist, but they would never understand. The Huns, his own kin had betrayed him. He had to destroy them before they took over everything and ruined the world. Yes, he had killed, he had maimed and he had exterminated his kind. Genocide is what historians called it. Yet they would never understand the monster that had been created. He was evil, he knew it, but Attila was even more, because he was the one that created the monster that would always be known as Dagoth Kinslayer. He was never the same man again. That moment changed his life forever. That moment created a monster that no one would be able to stop. Not the great Attila, not the Roman Caesar and certainly not some man named Palmer.
