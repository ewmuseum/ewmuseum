Money or Gold?

captured 23 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It appears that I’m suffering from an acute case of deja vu. Didn’t I already face and defeat Juvian Ramorez last week at the sixtieth edition of Realm? Evidently the LOW staff was impressed with Juvian’s performance last week that they put him in match against yours truly, in a Two Out of Three Falls Match for my Extreme Championship at the second Pay-Per-View, Catastrophic. But there’s a catch to all of this: we both have something to gain and something to lose. If Juvian Ramorez wins, he takes the Extreme Championship, he dethrones the Extreme King. Juvian Ramorez climbs the Extreme Mountain and takes the crown, however you want to view it. If I win, not only I’m still the Extreme Champion, the LOW staff will reward me a check for ten thousand dollars for being the longest Extreme Champion. Who da thunk it...me being the longest reigning LOW Extreme Champion. I’ve held this title since July the tenth of this year and today marked the third month of my title reign. I guess I could say it has been a privilege to hold this title for three months. It’s been a long time that I’ve held a major federation title and it is a privilege to hold this title. For two years, major federation titles have mocked me, filling my head with nonsense and lies. It had been an addiction for me to win a title and on July the tenth, I win the Extreme Championship against Soma in a Junkyard Match, cutting his title reign at a premature week. I have defended my title against all who opposed me and I have overcome them. People like Soma, Reality/Mayhem, Fathom and The Blue Coral wanted this title and I kept it from them. And now, Juvian Ramorez wants to covet this title even though I defeated him previously on Realm 60. I must say that I was impressed with Juvian Ramorez, but he didn’t defeat me. He didn’t have the ability to beat me. He wasn’t capable of beating me because I’m better than most people think. I was a hot commodity in 4WW and I was fired because of my thoughts on Tyler Blaze. Someone like Tyler Blaze should be thrown in jail due to several counts of embezzlement, not only that, he bought out people to win the SCWF Superior title. Ian Childress, Fey McClancy and Lorena Lovecraft knew this and gave him a second chance. After Maelstrom II, I take a vacation away from the Four Winds. When I come back, I see that lowlife fuck parading around the 4WW locker room. I spoke my mind about him - my mind speaks the truth, mind you - and I get fired. Oh well, easy come, easy go. Let’s get back on track, shall we? This match at Catastrophic shall be interesting. Will Juvian Ramorez become the next Extreme Champion or will I become ten thousand dollars richer? From what I see, this will be the easiest ten large I’ll ever win. So, what’ll it be, Juvian Ramorez, Mr. Vortex Enigma - money or gold? “It’s a good thing you came here for me to examine you today. You were almost risking some health problems.” The words echoed in Uriko’s head. Even though she had been pregnant for three months, she had been experiencing an unusual weight gain. She once told Simon that she was getting fat, but Simon assured her that their unborn child was developing. She didn’t really believe all of it. Since last month, she’s been experiencing pain in her stomach, a pain so unbearable that it drops her to her knees. The morning sickness, leaking and spotting she has gotten used to, but the pain she had been experiencing, she felt that something was wrong. She had talked to friends - who were mothers - and asked them if gaining fifteen pounds in three months was unusual. All of them said the same thing: “It’s sounds pretty unusual to gain fifteen pounds in the third month of pregnancy.” Uriko even asked Sylvia Madison, Simon’s doctor friend. “It sounds like you might have some health problems,” she once told Uriko. “I suggest you go to your doctor and have it checked out.” So she decided to go by herself to her doctor Sunday morning. When her told her that she was almost risking some health problems, her eyes started to sting with tears. She had a feeling in her gut that she might lose her baby after being pregnant for this long. Keeping her composure to the best of her abilities, she finally asked: “What did you find?” He looked at her and he can tell that if she heard any bad news it would crush her. “Well, Uriko,” he began, searching for the right words. “I might as well tell you. You have developed a small tumor---” Uriko started to cry helplessly. Her doctor should have seen this coming. Ever since she has gotten pregnant, she had been calling him all hours of the night complaining about her pregnancy and telling her that she had a tumor would send her off the brink. “Mrs. Kreed, it’s not that serious! Please let me explain. The small tumor you developed is non-cancerous and if we went into surgery, you’ll have a small chance of losing your baby. Uriko started to calm down. She looked at her doctor and she felt a ray of hope shining down on her. “How small of a chance?” “Well, my dear, after viewing X-Rays, the tumor is resting right on the embryonic sac, not inside it. When surgeons go in and operate, the sac will be left unharmed. The tumor hasn’t clasped itself on the sac so that reduced the chance of harming the child to zero, thus, you have little to no chance of losing your baby. On a maximum level, however, you‘ll have a thirty percent chance of losing your child, which is still pretty small.” She looked at her doctor and felt an overwhelming power of grief about to take hold of her. “How would I lose my baby if there’s no chance of losing it?” Her doctor picked his words carefully. “For example, let’s say that the surgeons removed the tumor and somehow damaged the embryonic sac, causing it to leak its fluid. Without the fluid and a tear in the sac, they baby will be prone to diseases and lack of nourishment. Basically, it’ll die. But, with my surgeons, that won’t happen. My surgeons are highly trained and nothing like that will happen.” A sigh of relief escaped Uriko’s lips. “That’s good to know. Do you have any idea when I can go into surgery?” “I’ll have to talk to my colleagues to see when they can fit you in, so I’ll give you a call to let you know.” “Thank you, Doctor,” she said, getting to her feet and shook his hand. “Let me know when I can go into surgery.” “You’re welcome, Uriko, and don’t worry. This cancer isn’t threatening to you or your baby.” During her time in the doctor’s office, she had been dreading about the bad news she was about to hear. After hearing what the doctors had to say, she felt better. This tumor won’t get the best of her, oh, no. She will kick this tumor’s ass. I had been sitting at home, waiting for my bride to get home from the doctor’s office. She had insisted that she needed to go alone, so instead of arguing with her, I let her go by herself and to respect her wishes. She had left about half an hour ago and I began to worry a little bit. I tried calling her cell phone numerous times but I kept getting her voice mail on one ring. I figured that she was in the doctor’s office and turned her phone off. All I could do was sit and wait. When I heard her Toyota pull into the garage, random thoughts began filling my head. What did the doctor say to her? Is it good news? Is it bad news? How will it effect the baby? How will it effect our future as would-be parents? All day long, I’ve been trying to block those thoughts out of my head, but they kept coming back. Something told me that something bad was going to happen to us. When I heard her enter the house, I sprang to my feet. “What did the doctor tell you?” “And a fine how do you do to you, too,” she giggled. “It’s nothing serious, hon, I have a small tumor.” When I heard Uriko say those words, I just about went out of my mind. Perhaps she was delirious about the fact that she was going to lose her baby! But when I see her face, she was smiling and she was happy, it was the same sunny disposition that I grew accustom to throughout the four years that I’ve known her. Either she lost her mind or she isn’t telling me anything. “Just a small tumor? What do you mean ‘just a small tumor’? Have you lost your mind? We can lose our baby this -” She didn’t let me finish. “Let me explain before you go off killing someone. The doctor took X-rays of my belly and she spotted the tumor, which explains why I had been feeling really bad stomach pains. He said that the tumor is resting right on the embryonic sac, not clasping itself on it nor inside the sac, and when I go into surgery, I have a little to no chance of losing our baby.” A sigh of relief escaped my body after hearing that there was no chance of losing our baby. If we lost the baby after the surgery, it would be almost impossible to conceive another child. “Do you have any idea when you’ll go into surgery?” “The doctor doesn’t know yet. He has to get a hold of a few of his colleagues to set up a schedule. Simon, everything will be just fine.” I was relieved to hear that there was no chance of losing our child, but I was skeptical about it. With my big match coming up against Juvian Ramorez this Sunday, I had my doubts. “I hope so, Uriko. I really hope so.” I decided to get away for awhile by myself for the afternoon, to get away from the madness I had endured this morning. I never thought that Uriko would go into surgery sometime in the near future to remove a “non-cancerous, non-threatening” tumor. Yeah, like I’ve heard of a “non-cancerous, non-threatening” tumor before. I’ve heard of non-cancerous ones, I’ve heard of non-threatening ones, but not both in the same category. I decided it was best for me to take a little drive around Springfield to clear my mind. I haven’t had a nice drive by myself since I got married to Uriko, so a little me time was definitely overdue. In the first few weeks - hell, days - of our marriage, we went everywhere together. Wherever I went, she followed suit. She was like my little hip attachment, and my little hip attachment is going to have surgery to remove a tumor. What is a father-to-be to think, let alone do? I could go out and drown my sorrows with alcohol, but that would be like overdosing of cocaine, an experience that I unfortunately remember doing a time or two. One of those OD’s almost killed me, so if my drinking matched my cocaine habits, I would definitely die. Drinking is definitely out of the question. Exchanging one addiction for another wouldn’t bold well for me. I thought that talking to my mom about all of this and get her opinions about it. My mom can be a very opinionated woman so she might make a field day out of Uriko’s up and coming surgery, or she’ll tell me that everything will be okay and not to worry about anything, that I should listen to the doctors. What she doesn’t realize that I’ll be a father for the first time. I know that she would understand the disposition I’m in, but it’s hard for me to bear it. I pulled into the driveway of my mother’s large house in the “richer” part of Springfield on the east side. I thought about moving out here but I decided against it. The rancher on the west side of Springfield was just fine. I turned off the engine and sat there for a moment. Perhaps I shouldn’t waste my mother’s time with my drama. For a moment I was going to turn the engine back on, back out of the driveway and continued to drive. I decided against it and got out of my Land Rover. It’s been awhile since I’ve seen Alexis - my mother’s name - and it would be a shock to see her only son paying an unexpected visit. I rang the doorbell, stood and waited. A few seconds later, no one came to the door and I thought no one was home. I was about to leave when I saw my mother opening up the door. “Simon,” I heard her say. “What an unexpected surprise.” I smiled at her and gave her a kiss on the cheek. “Hi, mom. Are you busy?” “Oh, not at all. Please, come in.” I walked in with my mother, examining her house. It felt lonely inside her large house since no one was inside it. “Where is everyone?” “Michelle’s upstairs working on her homework and Malachi’s at work. So, it’s only me downstairs. Would you like something to drink?” “Sure. How about some iced tea if you have any?” “Coming right up. Have yourself a seat and I’ll get a glass for you.” I sat down in a big, comfy couch and waited for my mother to get a glass of iced tea for me. In front of me, there was a coffee table equipped with a few pictures. The pictures were one of my mom, Malachi and Michelle, a LOW glossy I sent her on her forty-second birthday and a picture of Patti Anderson with the 4WW North Wind Championship around her waist. Her picture was autographed like mine was autographed. I picked up Patti’s picture and examined it. She had a cheesy-looking grin on her face as if to show disapproval of the photo shoot she was in. A smile played on my face, shook my head and put the picture down. “I didn’t think you still talked to our mother,” I said quietly to myself. A few minutes later, I heard my mother’s footsteps approaching me and I see her offering the iced tea to me. “Here you go,” I heard her say and I took the glass from her. She sat down next to me as I was taking a drink from the iced tea and she noticed that I enjoyed the taste of the tea. “Do you like it?” I drained about half the glass before placing it on the coaster in front of me. “Indeed I do. Thanks for the tea.” “Good. So, what brings you out here to see your old mom?” “Just to say hi...and to talk about something,” I said. My voice took on a depressing tone after I said those words. Her eyes reflected concern after hearing the tone in the my voice. “Is there something the matter with you and Uriko?” “Oh, no. Everything between Uriko and I are fine. We’re as happy as can be...” My voice trailed off when the thought of Uriko’s surgery crept into my mind. Then I looked at her, my expression hardened. “I guess there is something wrong, but nothing of the marital variety.” My mother could tell I was about to emotionally lose it at any time. “Go on, honey. Tell me what’s wrong.” “Remember when I told you that Uriko and I are going to have a baby?” Alexis nodded. “Well, since last month, she’s been having terrible stomach pains. I thought it was the unborn child moving around in her belly but Uriko told me that it didn’t give her much discomfort when it moved around. This pain became a bother and a concern, so she went to the doctor this morning and she found out she has a small tumor.” Alexis’ eyes widened and a gasp escaped her lips. “Oh, my God. I’m so sorry to hear that. Are you okay?” “I dunno. The doctors said that the tumor is resting on the embryonic sac, not inside the sac and it hasn’t clasped itself on it. Uriko told me that he said that when she go into surgery that there is a little to no chance of losing the baby.” “If there’s no chance of losing the child, then what’s the matter?” “I scared for Uriko. I’m concerned that she might not make it through the surgery. Mother, I love her so much and I’m looking forward to become a father and I don’t want any complications to rise.” “Honey, there’s nothing to worry about. You won’t lose Uriko and you’re sure as hell not going to lose this child. I bet the surgeons will be very careful in removing the tumor. It’ll definitely improve her health and the weight gain won’t be so drastic. Do you have any idea when she’ll have the surgery?” I sighed and shook my head. “Her doctor has to talk to his surgeons to make an appointment. I just hope that things turn out well.” “Things will, son. Have faith.” I chuckled. “‘Faith’ is the last thing on my mind right now. Perhaps things will turn out well. Knowing Uriko, she’ll come out of this with flying colors.” Alexis smiled. “That’s my boy.” She gave me a hug and a smooch on the cheek. “Feeling better?” “A little bit. May I ask you a question?” “Sure, honey.” The question itself sounded kind of crazy, but I had to ask it. “Is it possible for a woman to gain fifteen or more pounds in the third month of their pregnancy?” “Yes, it is. Why do you ask?” “Well, I’ve heard Uriko’s friends, who are mothers - even Sylvia - that it isn’t really that common or possible nowadays for women to gain such weight during the third month of their pregnancy. “Simon, every woman is different. When I was pregnant with you, I gained almost one hundred pounds after my pregnancy with you. You weighed almost ten pounds after I gave birth to you. I gained about twenty pounds on my third month, so yes, it’s possible.” “That’s what I thought.” I reached out for my tea, drained the rest of it and put it back down on the coaster. “Thanks for the tea, mother. I should be heading out.” “You sure?” “Yeah, I’m sure. Thanks for listening to me.” I smiled. “Of course. Have a nice day, son.” “You too.” We gave each other a hug and I was on my way. After the day was over, I felt better. I had the feeling that things will go for the better.
