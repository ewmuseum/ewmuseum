My Reign Shall Continue

captured 23 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Let me tell you about the title reigns that I’ve had. I’ve been in several independent feds before joining major federations like 4WW, eVolution and LOW. In 2001, I’ve joined a federation called Buffalo Wrestling Alliance and became the first BWA Television Champion in a tournament. It was a twelve-man tournament and I came out on top. I held the Television Championship the longest in the federation, a record-breaking eighteen months. The owner of the federation only saw me as the general of the Television Division and kept me there. I’ve asked for bigger title shots and they refused to give them to me. So, I was stuck with this pathetic TV title. I’ve faced all comers for my title, even some BWA superstars more than three times. After bout after repetitious bout, I told Johnny Buffalo - the owner of the fed - to either give me a decent title or I’m gone. Buffalo, being six feet five inches tall and weighing three hundred and fifty pounds of muscle, scoffed at me and showed me the door, but on one condition: I had to drop the title to Henry Rowengarter - who had the gimmick of “Zeta” at the time - in a Mue Thai Death Match. So, on my last match in 2002, I faced Rowengarter, performed possibly one of the greatest matches in BWA history, and I was bloodied and defeated by Rowengarter. His larger size, martial arts and military background overcame me in that grotesque, bloody match. After the match, Buffalo wanted to see me in his office to give me my last paycheck. He fucked me over and gave me a quarter of a million dollars when my original check should have been two-point-five million. The only thing that pissed me off was that he was laughing at me. For those who know me very well, you don’t laugh at Psymon. As punishment, I broke his right leg, spat in his face and left his office. I left with a smile on my face and my paycheck clenched in my right hand. I walked out like nothing had happened. Unfortunately for Uriko and myself, I was out of work once again. For about three months, I wanted to get out and work, so wrestling took a back seat for those three months. One night, a promoter recognized me and asked me to wrestle in his company, Illinois State Wrestling Association (ISWA). It originated in Chicago and did shows all over Illinois, New York, Pennsylvania, Massachusetts, Nevada and California. Ivan Reigns, the ISWA promoter gave me a three-year, ten million-dollar contract. I thought I hit the big time until I saw the “competition” he’d promise me the first three years of my stint there. After looking back at the competition in ISWA, it makes me ill. Ever single “superstar” there should have been flipping burgers or working at grocery stores rather than telling themselves that they can make it in the Professional Wrestling industry. Only a handful of wrestlers were good and the rest complained after taking a bump in the ring. In the first month I became the ISWA World Heavyweight Champion. Just like my reign as the BWA Television Champion, I faced all comers and defeated. Instead of facing people several times, I made sure I didn’t face them again. I put the less worthy wrestlers on the shelf for lengthy amounts of time, and I enjoyed it. After facing more opponents, they complained - complained - that I’m hurting them in the ring. Pain is the name of the game in wrestling, people; it isn’t a fucking pillow fight. I quit less than a year later, dropping the title for everyone to see. During my time in ISWA, I was being scouted by some federation called 4 Winds Wrestling, a major wrestling federation. I was never told this by Ivan Reigns because he wanted to keep me as a permanent superstar in the ISWA. After my last day in the ISWA, after laying down the ISWA World Heavyweight Championship for everyone to see, I get a message from Ian Childress, the owner and President of the 4 Winds Wrestling. Uriko taped matches I had in BWA and ISWA - behind my back, which didn’t bother me after she told me - and matches in Japan. Ian was impressed enough to have me come down to Phoenix for an interview. During my time in Phoenix, Uriko and I were looking at houses just in case I get a wrestling job from Childress - who I never knew was Nephilim until Ian himself told me over the phone one day on my way to Phoenix - and we saw a nice ranch house on sale. When I got the job as a wrestler, Uriko wanted to be my valet, which I had no problem with. So, we bought that rancher in the west side of Phoenix and moved our shit in, etc, and were a regular on 4WW wrestling cards. During my time in 4WW, I never won any titles, won some awards in 2003 and my biggest rival was Chris West. Chris “Mr. Hardcore Wannabe” West! The 4WW wasted my talents against a man who was nothing more than a mere glorified stuntman. And in return, this rivalry bit me in the ass and became the greatest rivalry in 2003, and West and I received an award for Best Match of the Year with our Japanese Death Match in 2003 at the first 4WW Pay-Per-View. I was wasted potential in the 4WW and it left a bad taste in my mouth. When I was fired in January of 2004, it was a blessing in disguise. Evidently Ian Childress and Fey McClancy couldn’t stand the I spoke about Tyler Blaze and fired me over it. They fired their hardest worker with the wasted talent in the 4WW. Oh well, it matters not to me. Afterwards, it seemed that my wrestling career seemed to go downhill afterwards. I joined eVolution a couple of months later (I believe) and my time were wasted there, too. I was supposed to have some sort of hardcore feud with Tom Mason and it never happened. Before it closed its doors, I left the federation. Then, I joined LOW in April of 2004. Tristan Belmont wanted me on the roster so I said sure, and look at me now - I’m on my to having the longest Extreme Championship reign in LOW history. I have beaten out all competitors who wanted this title - some more than once, I might add - and I’m still on my mountain, sitting comfortably in my throne with my crown sitting straight on my head. For three months, this title has been mine and rightfully so. I have defended it against superstars who were worth of a shot and I’ve defeated them, and the title is still mine. Even in a non-title match, I defeated Juvian Ramorez... Juvian “Vortex Enigma” Ramorez. Why do I have to face you again, because you somehow impressed the LOW staff and they gave you a title shot? No. Absolutely not. They seem to think if they gave you a title shot in a Two out of Three Falls Match for my title, you can defeat me. Evidently I have to prove the LOW staff wrong and beat you in this match and I continue my reign as the Extreme Champion will continue. And as a reward, I get a check for ten thousand dollars from the staff. Isn’t that nice, so charitable? Like I said before, this will be the easy ten thousand I’ll ever win. My reign shall continue, Ramorez, and there’s not a fucking thing you can do about it! “Son of a bitch!” After hanging up with Dr. Sullivan - Uriko’s doctor - my mood completely darkened. After having such a nice day yesterday afternoon after clearing my mind, today had to be the bearer of bad news. All my worries about Uriko’s tumor and surgery were out of my mind, and after getting off the phone with Sullivan, they all came back. I didn’t like answer for Uriko’s surgery date and Uriko could tell by the anger etched on my face. “Is there something wrong?” I heard her ask, but she knows the answer is yes. Keeping my cool the best that I can, I slowly turned to my wife, my eyes fixed on her. My rage seemed to be developing faster than collecting my composure. “I just got your date for your surgery,” I said angrily. Before I continued, I took a deep breath, trying to calm down. I looked back at her. “It’s on Sunday, the same day as the Catastrophic Pay-Per-View. Dammit, Uriko, it’s this Sunday!” Uriko saw me quickly get up, about to reek havoc around the house and her eyes began brimming with tears. I looked down at her and I felt terrible. My lovely bride was on the brink of hysteria and I was the cause of it. “Uriko...I’m sorry,” I began. I became calmer than I was earlier. I sat down next to her, wrapping my arms around her. “I didn’t mean to hurt your feelings.” “You didn’t hurt my feelings, hon,” she assured me. “I can’t go to see you wrestle Juvian Ramorez for your Extreme title and I was looking forward to see it. You even bought me a plane ticket and everything. Now...” her voice abruptly faded. I felt her shaking in my arms. “Now I have to miss one of your biggest matches of your career. It’s not fair, it’s not fucking fair!” She began to cry hysterically in my arms. It was sad to not have her by my side at the Pay-Per-View. I would have to worry about her going into surgery and coming out of it this Sunday. What if something happens? What if complications rise in the surgery? What if... I shook the last idea out of my mind. She won’t die, she can’t die! Not when I’m in Philadelphia wrestling one of my biggest matches. “I know you were, Uriko,” I said, trying to comfort her. “But you need this surgery. You’ll be there with me in spirit, okay?” “O...Okay, Simon. But I’m so scared!” “Me too, Uriko. Me, too.” We continue to sit there, and I continued to comfort my wife. It appears that I have more on my plate than usual. This Sunday is going to be hell for me.
