On the Strip

24 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(The scene opens to a shot of the Las Vegas strip in the mid day sun. The shot pans from left to right as if searching for something. All of a sudden, a black 65' Chevelle comes speeding down the street. The car screeches to a halt in front of the camera as the passenger door swings open. A voice comes booming from inside the car.) Voice: “You getting in or not!? I don’t have all day to be waiting around!” (The cameraman quickly gets in the automobile closing the door behind him as we see the driver or the car for the first time. Sitting in the drivers seat in none other then the superstar of the LOW himself, Eric Russo. Russo is wearing a sleeveless Shadow Riders t-shirt tucked into a pair of faded blue jeans. A pair of black, Ray-ban Aviators sit on his face blocking the glare from the bright sun outside. Russo sits looking out the window at a homeless man sitting across the street as he begins talking.) Russo: “It’s funny, the way things change in the blink of an eye. Just the other day that bum across the street was sitting at the high rollers table. He was laughing and having a good ol’ time. Now look at him, sitting there on the sidewalk, begging everyone that passes by for change and food. You see, he failed to realize exactly how high the stakes were. He got in over his head and ended up paying the price. That reminds me a lot of Matt Robinson. You see, Robinson does not realize what he is getting himself into. He does not realize that the Shadow Riders do not play for simple wins and losses. We deal in much higher stakes... we deal in careers. My good friend Kage says that Robinson is not special, I however see it differently. Matt Robinson is special, he is special because he is the one that I am going to use as an example. Through Robinson I will show everyone exactly why I talk such a big game. Mike Meyhu and Cade liked to talk about how the Shadow Riders were not on their level. Well they we’re right, we we’re above him. I ask you, what did that get them? I beat Mike Meyhu in a boiler room. As for Cade, he never fully recovered from the loss I handed him. You see when we play, we play for keeps. As for Robinson’s new facination with his parents,does he realize that his mommy and daddy can’t save him now? I tell him to bring them, bring both of them. Because he is going to need all the help that he can get. It’s just to bad for him that no matter what he brings, the outcome will remain the same. It will still end with Matt Robinson lying face down in a puddle of his own blood, unable to even utter a word as his career here in the LOW comes to a grinding hault once and for all. As he sits in the darkness and solitude with nothing but memories of the life it once knew, one thought will repeat for eternity. A thought quite familiar, the thought that Matt Robinson just like the rest has become nothing but another falling star.” (Russo turns his attention to the road in front of him as he presses his foot down on the gas pedal. As the smell of gasoline fills the air, the car speeds off down the street. Russo does not speak as he drives the car. After a few blocks, Russo makes a sharp right turn onto a small side street. The car speeds down the street as it gets further and further from the glamor of the strip and deeper into the rundown section of sin city. Russo continues driving for a few more minutes before pulling into the parking lot of a small dilapidated bar. Russo parks the car and opens the door as he speaks to the cameraman.) Russo: “Come on, I’m supposed to meet Kage here in a little bit. Let’s get a beer while we wait.” (Russo and the camera man exit the car and walk into the small bar. As the walk to the back of the bar, a few of the patrons reach out their hands to shake Russo’s. As if he does not even notice them, Russo passes by them avoiding their touch. The two men take a seat at a small table towards the rear of the bar. A waitress who looks to be in her late forties comes over to the table. She is wearing a low cut, white t-shirt which shows off her sagging cleavage as well as a pair of super short cut-off jeans. She begins speaking.) Waitress: “Hello honey, what can I get you.” Russo: “I’ll have a tequila on the rocks and he’ll have a...” Cameraman: “Nothing thank you, I don’t drink.” Russo: “He’ll take a straight up shot of 151.” Waitress: “I’ll be right back with you order.” (As the waitress leaves the table, the cameraman begins to question Russo.) Cameraman: “What’s 151? I never heard of that before.” Russo: “Don’t worry about it, it’s good for you.” (The waitress returns to the table with the two men’s order. She sets both glasses down on the table then walks away. Russo takes a large sip of his drink as he motions for the cameraman to drink his. The cameraman raises the glass to his mouth and quickly takes the shot. The camera begins to shake as we hear the cameraman begin to cough violently. Russo lets out a little chuckle as he watches the cameraman. He allows the cameraman to compose himself before he looks into the camera and begins speaking once again.) Russo: “Did you see that waitress? I hate people like that. You know, the ones who don’t know when it’s time to move on. The kind who try and recapture their glory days by acting like something their not. It reminds me a lot of Matt Robinson. This guy comes to the LOW talking about this and that acting like people actually give a shit what his life is like. Is that supposed to mean something to me, because it doesn’t. The name Matt Robinson does not mean a damn thing to me. As far as I’m concerned he is nothing but another name that is nothing more then letters of the alphabet that we’re strung together. As for him being an actor . . . so what? How in the hell does that effect your career here in the LOW? I could sit here all day and name different roles that I’ve been asked to take part in over my career but I’m not gonna. Do you know why I’m not gonna Matt? I’m not gonna name them because they won’t change a damn thing here and now. But I’ll humor you for just a second here Matt. I’ll go along with your story about how you were the star of this and that and how this is supposed to mean something. Now you have decided to leave hollywood and come and make an appearance here in Lights Out Wrestling. So how do you return? You show up and do nothing but rip-off of something that even you in all of your Hollywood glory could never measure up to. You rip-off of the Shadow Riders. I can’t blame you though, if I was gonna try and be like someone I’d pick us too. There is one thing however that you did not take into account. Just like I’ve said in the past, I don’t play well with others especially cheap knock-offs of real talent that are trying to take my belt away from me. So Matt even though I don’t like you very much, I am still gonna give you a chance. Pack up your bags, book your flight, and get as far away from the LOW as you can because if you don’t you will leave me no choice but to run you out just like everyone else who crosses my path.” (Russo lifts his drink to his mouth and finishes it with one gulp. As he waves the waitress over to order another drink, a drunk man stumbles over to the table. The man looks at Russo in a drunken stare as he begins to talk with a sight slur.) Drunk: “Yer Erick Russso... I’m yer biggesst fan...” Russo: “That’s good, now get out of my face. That stench coming off you is making me sick.” (Looking dejected, the man stumbles off as the waitress arrives at the table once again. Russo orders another drink. As the waitress goes to get the drink, Russo begins talking to the camera once again.) Russo: “I hate the drunks in these run down bars, they are always trying to tell you that their your biggest fan when you know they probably don’t know a damn thing about you except that your famous...” (The waitress returns to the table and places Russo’s drink on the table in front of him. Russo lifts up the glass and takes a sip of the tequila. He then returns the glass to it’s position on the table before continuing.) Russo: “As I was saying, they probably don’t know anything other then the fact that I wrestle for the LOW. It kind of reminds me of Matt Robinson once again. You see, Robinson just like that drunk likes to pretend that he knows who you are. He likes to act as if he knows exactly what he’s getting in to. Robinson is nothing more then a two faced bastard, he talks to everyone as if knows them on a personal basis . . . as if they are his top priority. In reality though Robinson only has one favorite, Robinson only has one person that he looks out for . . . himself. He does not give a damn about the LOW. He is an expert at lying straight through his teeth. I however am not one to take comfort in false statements. That is one of the main reasons that I have decided that after Realm, I will make sure he will never be able to make another statement again. Although I am normally not a very caring and forgiving man in general, this time it will be brought to a whole new level. I don’t like you Matt Robinson and you can be assured that I will make you well aware of that fact when we get in the ring. You all saw that first hand when I took out Ezra. Ezra thought he could take me too but . . . no, instead I decimated him like nobody else ever has. Just as I mentioned then, I do not hide my agendas. I am here for one reason and one reason only . . . I am here for money. I have said it many times in the past, my services are available to whoever has enough money to pay us. I am a very talented individual and we make sure we get paid very well to be just that. Right now, the thing bringing me my money is my Realm title and a little punk like Matt Robinson is not gonna change that.” (A loud clamor is heard off camera. The camera swings around as we see the bartender dragging one of the many drunks to the front door. The bartender kicks the front door open and throws the drunk man out into the parking lot. A large grin slides across the bartenders face as he admires his handy work. He then yells out to the drunk who is trying to drag himself to his feet.) Bartender: “Come back when you have some money you fu(k!ng bum!” (The bartender pulls the door shut as he begins to walk back behind the bar. He notices that one of the customers is staring at him. The bartender lunges foreword and motions as if he is going for a punch to scare the staring customer. The customer falls of his stool as he jumps back in fear of being hit. The bartender lets out a hearty laugh as walks behind the bar. The camera comes back to a shot of Russo as he begins speaking once again.) Russo: “There is nothing more pathetic then a bully. Unlike a real man, a bully is rarely ever able to back up what he says. It kind of reminds me of Matt Robinson once again. Robinson is kind of like that new kid on the block that nobody likes. The one that either out of anger of rejection or necessity becomes the town bully. Strangely enough, this is nothing more then another attempt for them to fit in. A way for them to become a part of the crowd. Matt Robinson worked in the LOW with little fanfare. He has done his best to try and promote himself but those attempts have failed miserably. You see Robinson . . . or should I call you Nuke? Oh well, it doesn’t really matter what I call you so lets just leave it at Jacka$$. You say that I think I am some kind of god. Nothing could be further from the truth, I don’t think of myself as a god... I know I’m one. As for the reason I use the moniker of superstar, it is not to make myself look better then the rest. It is because I earn the right to use that name with everything that I do. The fact that you are a “giant” does not bother me in the least. I have taken down men a whole lot bigger and a hell of a lot more talented then you without blinking an eye. So you can talk all you want about how your a big movie star and how you’re such a dangerous man. Because that is not going to make a difference when you get in the ring with me and I take you down to the mat and show you exactly why they call me Superstar.” (As Russo finishes the cameraman begins to question the whereabouts of Jason Kage.) Cameraman: “I thought you were supposed to meet up with Kage here.” Russo: “Why are you so antsy, you got something more important to do? Kage will get here when he gets here. Until then, you just keep that camera on me cause I still have a few things that I wanna talk about.” Cameraman: “You got it.” (Russo stands up as Kage walks in the front door of the small bar. Russo and the cameraman walk over to meet Kage. Once they are there, Kage begins speaking.) Kage: “Good to see you man. Did you find anything out about our mysterious benefactor?” Russo: “Of course, did you actually think I would come back without any answers?” Kage: “Who was it man? I can’t wait to hear.” Russo: “Get this, it was... hold on.” (Russo turns to the cameraman.) Russo: “You got any money on you?” Cameraman: “No, why?” Russo: “It’s gonna be hard getting out of here with no money to pay the tab or get a cab. Oh well... sucks for you.” Cameraman: “You can’t just leave me here like this!” Russo: “You know what, you’re right. Give me the tape.” Cameraman: “What.. why?” Russo: “I don’t wanna loose this tape if you don’t make it back. Give it to me now or else I’m gonna take it.” (The picture begins to shake as the cameraman reaches up in fear to press the eject button. Suddenly, the screen goes to the Shadow Riders logo with the voice over that says... “The Shadow Riders have arrived so just sit back and enjoy the ride.”. The screen now fades to black.)
