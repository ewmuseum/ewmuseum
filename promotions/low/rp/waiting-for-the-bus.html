Waiting for the bus

29 January 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Realm 67 has past. Dark Haze, victorious. That’s right I won again. I won not only to prove that I am worthy of the title, but to show everyone that I am better. I showed Cyrus I was better than him, I’ve showed The Demond that I was, again, better, and now, I’ve showed Luke Justice that I can beat him. So maybe he didn’t have his best of days, but I’ll give him a rematch if he wants it. I have no problem doing so. The only thing I find to be ludicrous is some of the stuff that some wrestlers are saying. I mean we all know that, yeah, the World Division is rather small, containing one person and all, but so many stars wish to be in it. They all want a piece of Kage. Yeah, I think that’s normal, but how does a wrestler jump divisions like that? They have to go through me, and the problem I see is that none of them have fought me, and those who have, obviously lost. I mean, shit, I want a piece of that World Title too, but at least I manage to try and work my way up the rankings. Nothing is free, and the cost to be in the World Division is showing that you are better than the wrestlers in the Xtreme Division. I’ve already proved that I am better than three wrestlers in my division; who’s next? The good ol’ days were great, and today reminds me of slightly of them. **September, 1993.** “Now let’s get in line. Single-file please.” The teacher remarks. We stood in line at the door and waited until everyone was ready. The class finally got in a straight line. A door holder and line leader were chosen to help out the teacher. This was my favorite time of the day, recess. Our school was rather large and we had a couple if not a few acres to work with. That’s what this was, work. No play was involved, or at least for the main part. We continued to the playground and we finally got there. Alas, time to have fun. Have fun making my money. When there is demand someone will supply. The supply may vary. It could be entertainment or drugs, or, hell, it could be both at the same time. A couple buddies and I walk out to a secluded area, not too secluded, but mainly we were far enough away from the teachers. “Look at that girl ” Master J said. His real name was Jermaine, but we called him ‘Master J’ because he rolled the best joint out of all our friends. “Yeah, she’s mine.” I replied. This girl was hot. She was skinny, had a nice face, and most of all she had tits. No acne yet, she was a beautiful girl. “Bitch, she’s been mine. We’re married.” Mater J insisted. “You’re married, but I stick my ding-a-ling in her every night.” Yeah, I didn’t know too much about that. All I knew was what my brothers had taught me, but I thought I knew a lot, and plus, it was funny. “Shut up ” Master J exclaimed, as I probably made him feel stupid. Master J rolled a joint while we were sitting on the bench. Not too long after it was lit, this Spanish kid walked over. Our school was probably ninety percent black, but there were a few Spanish and white boys. “What’s up holmes?” He says in his heavy accent. “Not much, watcha need?” “I’ll take 3.” By this he meant that he wanted three nickle bags. I only charge four dollars a piece; my brothers taught me that you have to beat the competition some how. Competition, in fifth grade? Yeah, I knew at least five kids who also sold bags. I was friends with a couple, but we all knew of each other, and they all definitely knew who I was. Bad? Not really, we all kept it on the DL, and no one important ever found out. They probably didn’t even know there were drug dealers at this age. I was 10. “Sure, gimmie twelve.” The kid pulled out his saved lunch money and handed it to me. In exchange, I gave him his bud. We continued to smoke and no one realized it. The ones who did, didn’t even care anyway. They feared us. Now, we weren’t necessarily the meanest kids, but if something went wrong, we were all down to throw hands. I was doing boxing at the time, and I was practically a star. I fought kids one and a half times my age. The kid walked off and we kept it at that. I didn’t know him, and he didn’t know me; he just knew a face. “Damn, be smokin’ or be passin’ ” I demanded because Master J was just zoning out and holding the joint.” I sold a few more bags during recess. I’d guess about eighteen to twenty, if you want to be exact. Our teacher blew her whistle, and it was back to class. This part sucked, it was boring and I didn’t care to learn. I had more important things to worry about. I had to worry about helping support my family. My mom tried, but working legally it’s hard to make money. She had two jobs but only made minimum wage. My brothers helped out a lot, and I thought we were ‘hood rich.’ Both of them drove cars with Kandi paint, rims, and the whole deal. I had no better role model. Once school let out , I rode bus number one eighty two. It took me back to Brodmore, my neighborhood, in Bankhead. We called it westside or zone one. Getting off the bus I’d head straight to the store. I’d walk onto the side of it and sit on a milk crate, just me and a couple older friends. They sold harder stuff like blow. I wasn’t much into it, so I let them handle that business, and they gave me their weed business. I guess we were partners. This black lady walks up. He hair is frizzed out, her mouth looks chapped and white, and she is shaking a little bit. “Crack head.” I commented as she came closer. “Hey. You boys got skee.” she asked. “I got dubs, Gs, what do you want?” my friend answered. We called my friend ‘Dub.’ He always had powder and he made more money than I could of imagined. These people were fiens. “I got fifteen.” the crack head lady responded. “A dub’s twenty.” “All, I got is fifteen.” she pleaded. She was desperate you could tell. With the way she was shaking and just the look on her face, you knew she HAD to have it. “We I got dubs and you got fifteen. That doesn’t add out.” Dub wasn’t accept it. “Hey, come here.” she called him over, and he followed her. We could barely make out what she was saying, but, ultimately, we did. “I’ll suck yo’ dick.” “Don’t do it ” I yelled. The lady got furious at my remark, but again no one had the audacity to fuck with me or them. “Give me fifteen, and I’ll do it.” Dub made a mistake by saying that. They walked around back and did what they had to. I just can’t believe his clientele. After a few hours of slanging, our work was interrupted by the store owner. He was Arabian. “Get out Get out ” he yelled at us. We stood firm and just sat there. “We aren’t inside, you stupid fuck ” I had to be a smart ass. “You see the sign ” He pointed to the ‘No Loitering’ sign, just above our heads. “Nigga I can’t read. Shit, I’m only a kid. I don’t even know what that damn sign means.” Although this wasn’t totally true, I wasn’t going to take it. I needed to tell my brothers, so they could have another talk with him. This was a normal occurrence now and then. He seemed to only do it when we were busy. “Quit tryina knock the hustle ” I added. He went inside, so we stayed for a little while longer. So far, I think I made about 200 dollars, minimum. We were on our way back to Brodmore, and BANG BANG BANG The sound of gunshots rang out, and another life was taken. It was a sound we were, sadly, all to used to. It was probably gang or related, so the cops would just show up. They wouldn’t really try and figure out who did it; they just had to please anyone who cared, by pretending that they were doing something. A car screeched out of the neighborhood, and the crime was done. We made it back, and we were just in time to see a guy getting jumped, all over a game of dice. Shit was real around here, you just didn’t fuck with the wrong people. As suspected, the cops showed and started questioning this man, ‘Demon.’ He was in the Bloods, so I obviously wasn’t friends with him, and I steered clear of him. He was a straight killer, pretty high up in the gang but not close to my broters’ status in the Crips. He was bound to get killed one of these days. He had a hot head and didn’t give a fuck about himself, anything, or anyone. Later, during sundown. My boys and I were just walking around the neighborhood, and some random truck pulls through. “Who the fuck’s that?” I questioned. “Looks like a couple white boys.” Sure enough it was, and right behind him was a police car. “Yep, trol’s right behind that bitch.” Is was inevitable that white people would be followed if they came into our complex. Simply they weren’t welcomed unless they were buying something. It’s was rather obvious, that’s what they were doing. “They must be scared. Comin’ up in here and boy in blue followin’ them.” Master J commented and laughed. The truck parked, and it was a smart move. It appear they were getting out, because a door opened, so the cop turned around and headed out. He wasn’t really welcome in here either. The kids got back into the truck and drove around a little bit. I’d never seen them before, so I was guessing that they didn’t really know what they were doing. We walked toward them, and as we got close I spoke. “What you doin’ in my hood?” The boys seemed rather frightened and they replied. “Uh...Uh...do you know ‘Carwash?’” Carwash sold everything. He had crack, soft, weed, painkillers, heroin, anything. “Yeah. What you need?” I then approached their truck. “Just a couple bags...” “How many’s that?” I asked. “Like, five.” “Aight, wait here.” I didn’t know these kids, so I walked around the corner like I was getting them from someone. Shortly after, I went out there and gave him my product. The boys were happy, and they left. I, on the other hand, had made a few hundred, so I decided to call it a night. Master J, Corey, and I decided to head back to the crib, where we would smoke at. Present day. Dark Haze is talking to a random black man, on a bench, at a bus stop. “...Now days, I supply entertainment by wrestling. Back then, I supplied entertainment through drugs. Either way, I get paid.” Dark Haze says, in a stoned ramble. “Um huh...” the man replies, not paying attention, yet he still share the cigar with Haze. “Oh and another thing, back then, that bitch Demon used to talk madd shit to me, my brothers, or our boys. He got his though, when he got shot. But anyway, now, I got this new character The Demond. I fought him in December, two matches ago, and he still manages run his lips.” Haze grabs the special cigar back and inhales it repeatedly, before continuing. “Earlier this week my agent sent me a recording from a radio station...” Earlier this week. Dark Haze is basking in the sun at his private island. Next to Haze, is a table with a twelve ounce crystal glass containing a dark green drink. “I love Hulk.” Haze mutters, as he takes a sip of his drink. The exotic birds sound is carried with the cool breeze. The skies are clear along with the translucent blue water. All of a sudden, Haze’s cell phone rings, disrupting the peaceful, exotic atmosphere. “Yo ” The voice on the phone can barely be heard, “How are you doing?” “Chillin’. Why what up cuz’?” “Well, I found out, last week, The Demond was on a radio show...he was talking about you. He didn’t even mention much about Juvi, who his match was against; he mainly spoke of you.” Haze’s agent explains. “Didn’t I beat him already? What does he want from me?” “Obviously he wants a piece of gold, that’s why he mentioned Kage, as well.” “Why does this man think he’s so good? I know he hadn’t been beaten much, but I showed him is was only because he fought people who were, well, I guess average. The people who he fought may not have been stars, or maybe they were, but one thing is for sure. They weren’t superstars.” “I have no clue. Anyway, listen to this.” Haze’s agent begins to play the recording. Haze listens to it for a few minutes before responding. After chuckling , he finally answers, “I can’t believe he’s pushing Juvi to the side like that. For one thing, Juvian went undefeated as the Xtreme Champion. Despite his injury, which may show signs of weakness, he is a great wrestler, and I wouldn’t doubt him like that.” Haze begins to listen as something may have caught his ear. Haze eyebrows curl and a wide smile comes upon his face. “I just don’t think anyone understands how serious I am about my dominance there in the LOW.” Haze hits the last inch of the blunt between his fingers. “Haze is just the first stop on the road to the title I so deservingly should posses. The Demond, you are asking who I am? You don’t know Dark Haze? I fight you, win, and you say you weren’t giving it your all? You have a lot to learn little buddy, but you did manage to make a little sense. You stated that I will be a stop in the road. You’re right, you will be stopped by me, just like when I came back to LOW and stopped you the first time. Come on now, that was your big break, and you fucked it up.” Haze takes a rests, and puts another trademark cigar in his mouth. “Alright you can push play again.” Haze mumbles with the cigar in his mouth. The ‘Tru Blue Superstar’ puts a flame to the tip and sparks the cherry. Smoke seeps out as he exhales, and he breathes it back in through his nose. “Damn, I don’t even want to listen anymore; I’ll call you when I get back in town. The Demond is boring me.” Haze presses a button on the keypad of his phone and it sounds off. Back to present time... Dark Haze is still sitting on the bench, the sun is slowly beginning to fall. Crickets chirp their wings together, and the start of nighttime is apparent. Haze looks at the man and starts his speech again, “You know, I couldn’t believe he had the nerve to call Kage, Juvi, and I out like that. It makes no sense, he talks so much about his dominance in LOW, and yet he hasn’t showed that to me. I really hope Juvi wins.” Dark Haze and the man sit there for a couple minutes before a bus stops in front of them. The bus leaves and the man is gone, so Dark Haze sits there, alone, on the bench.
