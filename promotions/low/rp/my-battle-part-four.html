My Battle: Part Four

Iron Lung LOW Roleplay #698 Date: 03 / 04 / 2003 Location: In the mind... My Battle: Part Four My Battle: Part Four 03 / 04 / 03 The end of the road was just a few steps away from me. I stood there in my place with the rain slightly downing on me, looking around at all of the creations before me. The buildings all looked the same, almost as if they were all made out of the same Lego set. They were brick and they had glass windows, and the door to enter was always huge. The doorknobs were made of brass, and the doorbell chimes were always classy. No, I was not watching one of those Walgreen’s commercials. I was actually standing there in awe of all the similarities of the block. It confused me as I scratched at my hair, which I did not style this morning. So many questions arose in the back of my mind. I had walked around this block at least three times by now, but this section always astounded me. However, I did not notice it all until this morning. It was as if somebody was calling down on me to pay attention to the little details around me, to stop and smell the roses instead of living a rustic and busy life. I guess it was not so complicated after all; I was just making it seem to be that way. Looking at the people as I passed, I noticed almost the same expression on each of their faces. They looked lonesome and depressed, maybe it was because of the weather. But then again, maybe life truly was not having a good day today. There was so much destruction around us, talks about war and disasters in the air brought our feelings to an all time low. However, to those who did not feel, it was a whole other story. I used to enjoy listening to the bickering of old women and the laughter of children when carnival time came. Now it seemed that everyone just annoyed me with questioning. I had to take an Advil every time I made my way into my room after the long, stressful day. It was not easy to be on the road almost all of the time, and when I did come home, it still was not even my real home. Renting motel rooms was my top priority; I was what you would call a traveling man. I mostly left my bags in my room packed for the next venture. The only bag I took with me was the duffel bag that did not leave my sight. I still clung on to it as I gawked among the similar buildings. I shook my head a bit and then a gentle hand touched my shoulder. I turned around slowly and noticed and old woman pointing to the crosswalk sign that said walk. I helped her cross the street and we eventually ended up on the other side, her politely thanking me for my crossing adventure. I smirked a bit at her hunched over, shriveled body. Then I realized that I may be like that one day, and my face decayed back to its straightness. My expression was rock-like, and my eyes remained like coals in the pits of my head. People always said I did not care about anyone else but myself, but once again they did not know the real me. The wind tossed my hair back and forth, but I did not even bother to fix it. People walked by me, huddled in winter jackets as if the weather was going to get worse any second. I just shrugged my shoulders and took in fresh air, still standing on the opposite corner with the buildings behind me. Just then, I had a thought about what possibly could have brought me to these grounds. My reason could not have just been that I was bored and needed to stretch my legs, there was something eating away at the back of my mind. There had to be a reason for me to stop and finally see the world in less than an eye blink. Maybe I finally realized that time was indeed short, and I had to relax a bit. I had to focus on things more clearly and I had to think over things deeply. I was a rushed person, and going into something of this nature rushed never resulted in a good way. Just look at my prime example, being on a streak and then suddenly losing to Canyon Thunders. I went into the match thinking that I would get it over quickly, but I was dealt with a harsh view of reality for already having the match over in my mind. Sometimes I confused myself. Why did I rush into things so much? Maybe I did need a lesson, so I guess I really should be thanking Canyon instead of complaining about things. So if you are out there Canyon, consider this my truce. You taught me a good lesson in wrestling, never rush and never turn your back on your opponent. Now I knew my final strategy for War Wounds, but was it too late to say it? Time was closing in quickly and so were the walls of tragedy around my body. If I did not have a game plan, my opponents would crush me. They all already knew what they were planning to do, and perhaps I was fearful for the first time. I was fearful that I would be stuck at the bottom of the success ladder again, and it turns harder each time I try to get up it. The first time Canyon knocked me back down due to me not being prepared. The second time it was Canyon again, shoving me harshly back down into the canvas. He reminded me that I was not the only one who wanted the spotlight all for me, and he bravely kept climbing while I struggled to gain my footing. This time it was everyone else staring at me while I was still at the glass ceiling. They were laughing and taunting me while I tried to break into their place. I was on the bubble, struggling to pop it with my victorious pins. I knew many things about wrestling. One of the things that I learned was that you must focus and clear your mind of all reality before you enter a highly anticipated match like this one. I had to become a warrior inside again, and battle my way through the opposition. They were holding me back like a brick wall; I had to be the destruction device. I had to be the metal ball that caved them all in, I had to be the strong iron chain that fueled me with force. I had to divide and conquer, and one by one, they all had to fall…
