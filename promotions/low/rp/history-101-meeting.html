History 101- Meeting

24 May 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
(The screen fills with the logo of the Shadow Riders. Under the logo, a message is written across the bottom of the screen which reads . . . 'Please stay tuned for an important announcement from 'Superstar' Eric Russo, leader of the Shadow Riders'. The scene opens on the busy streets of Seattle, Washington. The camera moves along the glistening street that is still slightly damp from a recent rainfall. The camera then slowly begins to raise it’s shot upwards. It then stops, focusing in on the window of an apartment building. There is a person standing in the widow frame looking out onto the streets. The identity of the person however is obscured , all that can be seen is a silhouette . . . a shadow. The scene quickly switches to one from inside of the apartment. The room is empty, aside form a few token pieces of cheap wooden furniture. The only light in the room comes from street light shining in through the window. In the center of the room, a man sits at a table. He has a sight stubble on his young 20-something face giving the impression that he has not shaved for a couple of days. The man is wearing a white t-shirt tucked into a pair of blue jeans. He unwraps a small cassette tape and pops it into a recorder. The man then unravels the long cord of a microphone and begins fiddling to get it plugged into the recorder. He then turns to the window where we now get our first look at the shadow from the window. Eric Russo stands with his back the man still looking out to the street. He is attired very differently then normal. Instead of his normal t-shirt and jeans, Russo is wearing a darkly colored Gucci suit. He is also supporting his weight on a pair of crutches. Without looking to the man sitting at the table, Russo begins to speak.) Russo: “So let me get this straight, you want me to tell you all about myself so that you can record it. Is that right Jerry?” Jerry: “That’s the plan, I kind of do this stuff as a hobby. I’m hoping that I can one day get a story good enough to fill a book. I’m a writer too.” (Russo pays little attention to Jerry's comments other then the ones that pertain directly to himself.) Russo: “You’re gonna need a lot of tape for my story, it’s a long one.” Jerry: “No problems there, I have a whole bag full of blank tapes right here beside me.” Russo: “Before we start . . . I want to know, what made you pick me for my life story instead of anyone of those other guys in the bar?” Jerry: “To tell the truth, I don’t know. You just look like someone who has a good story to tell. You learn things like that after you’ve been doing this stuff as long as I have.” (Jerry continues in his naturally inquisitive nature.) Jerry: “So, is this where you live?” (As Jerry finishes his sentence, he reaches into his pocket and pulls out a pack of cigarettes. He flips the box open removing one and lighting it up. As he does this, Russo responds.) Russo: “No, this is just a room I like to come to from time to time when I need to get some thinking done. Jerry: “I see. So, what do you say we get this thing rolling? Tell me, what do you do for a living? Russo: “I’m a pro wrestler.” (Jerry lets out a slight chuckle at this answer.) Jerry: “I’ve never heard that one before. I take it when you say pro wrestling, you mean something along the lines of that fake stuff that kids watch on the TV Saturday mornings.” Russo: “No, I mean the dirtiest business in the world. One where you adapt yourself to pain on a constant basis. One where you deaden your emotions for the sake of success. One that is more real then any outsider will ever know.” Jerry: “Hey . . . it sounds like a story to me, lucky huh?” Russo: “Lucky for both of us, I get to tell my story and you get the honor of hearing it. Hell, maybe you’ll even get your book out of this one.” (Jerry pauses for a moment to take a drag of his cigarette before talking.) Jerry: “You said that pro wrestling is the dirtiest business ever right? Tell me then, why do you still do it? Why don’t you just call it quits or something?” Russo: “I’ve thought about it from time to time, but there’s a strange thin about wrestling. You see once the sport gets into your blood, you’re just as hooked on it as a junky is to crack. Once you’re in, there’s no turning back.” Jerry: “You really believe that pro wrestling is legit don’t you?” (Russo finally breaks his gaze from the street as he turns in the shadows to look at Jerry. He begins speaking.) Russo: “You know, we really should get some light in this room before we start. Maybe that way, I’ll be able to change your opinion about the reality behind what I do.” Jerry: “I thought you wrestlers liked working in dimly lit arenas. So as to keep that mystique that you guys surround yourself in.” Russo: “Actually, I love the light. As far as I’m concerned, the more spotlight on me, the better.” (Using his crutches for support, Russo moves towards the light switch on the opposite wall of the room. Once there he flicks the light switch causing the room to illuminate. Russo then turns from the switch and begins heading back towards the table where Jerry sits. Across from Jerry is another chair which Russo takes a seat in as he rests his crutches against the table edge. Finally getting a close-up look at the veteran wrestler who still bears a few slowly healing scrapes on his face from his battles in the ring. This sight slightly startles Jerry who begins to second guess his earlier comments about the legitimacy of wrestling. Seeing this, Russo begins to reassure him.) Russo: “Don’t worry, I’m not gonna hurt you. I want this chance to finally get my side of the Eric Russo story heard considering everyone else seems to have one too. Go figure, the only side of the story that has not been heard is the one of the main character.” (Still slightly taken aback by the real scrapes and cuts on Russo’s face, Jerry begins to question him.) Jerry: “How in the hell did you get all those scrapes all over your face?” Russo: “Same as anyone else would, a series of simple movements, only the movements that caused my cuts were in a slightly more violent manner that you would be accustomed to. Make no mistake . . . I’m made of flesh and bone just like you, but I’m quite different at the same time. I’ve been different from the normal perception of society for almost fifteen years now. Shall we begin with that story? Where do you want me to start, do you want the normal . . . “I was born here and I grew up there” routine or should I start with when I was officially born into my new life as a professional wrestler?” (Jerry is still unsure of what to make of Russo. He just sits enthralled as Russo continues.) Russo: “That’s where we really should start, don’t you think so Jerry?” (Jerry still trying to take in the significance of the cuts on Russo’s face, Jerry responds.) Jerry: “You’re not lying are you . . . pro wrestling is real?” Russo: “Enough with the questions about my sport being real Jerry. It’s time to start my story so just sit back and enjoy the ride.” (With that, the screen fills with the logo of the Shadow Riders. Under the logo, a message is written across the bottom of the screen which reads . . . “The previous message has been brought to you by the leader of the Shadow Riders, “Superstar” Eric Russo. The screen then fades to black.)
