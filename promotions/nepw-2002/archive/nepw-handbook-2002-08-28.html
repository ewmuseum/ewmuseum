Commentators * Shawn Foster * Jimmy Jones 5. Role-playing 6. Strategies 7. Application 8. Outro =================================================== 1. Introduction Hi. Welcome to the NEPW Handbook. This is the beginning of the NEPW Handbook. Read whatever you want to read that you feel is most important. If you like what you see, just fill out the application and send it to [detail withheld] or if you just want to follow the fed, send in an email and request to be added to the mailing list. A. What is NEPW? The NEPW is a promotion based out of the lower northeast of the United States. The states that it runs around are New York, New Jersey, Connecticut, Rhode Island, Massachusetts and Pennsylvania. Rightfully so, the NEPW stands for North East Pro-Wrestling. The main Headquarters of the NEPW are in Queens, New York. B. Who runs NEPW? I'm [name withheld] and I am running this federation. I don't have the ultimate plan to run the best fed ever, but I have a good idea on how to run a fun fed. I've done it in the past, but just because I wanted to have power over a group of people that were in my league, but after a short stay in the N.E.W. before it folded, I learned what running a fed is all about. The angles, the work ethic, the fun and games. That's what keeps everyone interested. I've been e-wrestling since 1994. I have a lot of e-wrestling years under my belt, and I don't give up easy. It's not a good feeling. I know this fed will succeed. =================================================== 2. Shows A. Weekly Shows * The Spotlight This is the weekly show where wrestlers get to say what's on their mind. Talk about upcoming matches or matches that past. Basically, whatever's on their mind. No matches take place here. * Rage This is the actual card. It takes place on the weekends. Usually on Sunday nights. On some occasions, it takes place on Saturday night, depending on how the schedule of things are running. This is a small promotion, so as of right now, this card holds 3-5 matches a week. The roster size won't be more than 20 men for sure. When there is a Supercard, most of the times there won't be a Rage, but on some occasions there will be. B. Supercards As far as Supercards go, they'll be held usually every other month. It's mostly whenever I feel that one should be done. When I feel that angles and feuds are established enough for a Supercard to happen. I'll name them as they come. Some names might stick to become yearly events and some might not. =================================================== 3. Titles A. NEPW Heavyweight Title This is the most prestigious title of the NEPW. Only the best of the best get to have this belt. It's mandatory that this title be defended at least once a month. On a Supercard, this title, like the other two titles, will be defended. B. NEPW Television Title This isn't like the Heavyweight Title, but it's the second best out of the three belts. This title gets no rest. Whenever there's a Rage or a Supercard, the TV Title will be defended. Some might make a name for themselves by defending this title for a good amount of time, then they'll rank themselves for a title shot at the Heavyweight Title. C. NEPW Hardcore Title The NEPW Hardcore Title doesn't hold any value like that of the TV or Heavyweight Titles, mainly because it's a hardcore belt. This is for those who think that they need the taste of blood more often than others. Only those who like to hurt someone, and in return, can handle the pain dealt by others. No punks should try to go for this title, that is if they know what's healthy for them. =================================================== 4. Characters A. President/Owner/CEO [name withheld] Higor "The Rebel" Rosa is the President/Owner/CEO of this promotion. He has most recently been a promoter for the N.E.W., which folded in 2001. He has also ran one promotion in the past, the East Coast Wrestling Federation, which folded after 6 strong months, when The People's Drunk drank the ECWF out of business. He's sobered up a lot, and now is serious about not letting his promotion fold like nothing this time around. B. Backstage Interviewers * Joey Rebel- Born in Las Vegas, Nevada and raised in San Diego, California. He's the face interviewer. He used to be an indy wrestler himself, wrestling under a different alias. He's 6'9 and weighs a slim 289, but still doesn't get pushed around by too many people. * Elizabeth Cunningham- Born and raised in Norwalk, Connecticut. She's more of a neutral commentator. She tells it how it is and doesn't care if anybody disagrees with her. She's a tough woman. Also an ex-indy wrestler. She's taken her fair share of hits from her indy career. * Ricky Preciouz- Born and raised in New York City. He's, obviously, the heel commentator. Never wrestled, just always watched local wrestling while growing up into an immature sleezeball. Brown-noses a lot. He's a skinny punk, but has always been able to talk his way out of trouble. C. Ring Announcer * Ryan Joseph- An Ivy League grad, out of Yale University, Ryan Joseph was a minor in journalism and somehow ended up with a small job, here in the NEPW, as a ring announcer. D. Commentators * Shawn Foster- Born and raised in New York City. Those familiar with [name withheld] and Shawn Foster know that these two were tag team partners for about 4 years. Won multiple tag team titles and always watched each other's back. It only makes sense that whatever one does, the other will do. A job for Shawn Foster as a color commentator was always open for him. He tries to be a neutral commentator, but it's in his heel nature for him to dislike the face wrestlers. * Jimmy Jones- Born and raised in Hoboken, New Jersey. A neutral commentator. Calls it how he sees it. He takes no sides for anybody. He sees no reason for it anyway. =================================================== 5. Role-playing Role-playing is important in this league, as it is in a lot of other leagues. I won't say that it's the only important thing, because strategies and angles help a lot to decide the outcome of matches. Booking matches isn't easy, so if you want to have a good chance of winning your matches, give me quality role-plays and your chances of winning will always be fairly good. Don't go out of character in your role-plays. By that, I mean, don't call someone by their handler's name. Don't say stuff like "I'm a better role-player." Don't mention real promotions, or better yet, don't mention any fantasy promotions either. I mean, you could refer to things that have happened in passed feds that your character was in, but other than to revive a feud, or start one with a wrestler of that same league, there's no need to mention another fed. Oh yeah, and don't mention real wrestlers either. I mean, obviously, if you can't mention real feds, you can't mention real wrestlers, but just to be safe. Don't ever take anything personal and put it into your role-plays or in-rings. That, to me, is a shoot. I will drop you from the roster immediately if that happens. =================================================== 6. Strategies I myself have never been too good at doing strats. But if I know one thing from experience, it's that strategies have a major influence on how matches happen. They help the writer by giving him a description of what you want your character to be like during the match [high flying, aggressive, etc...]. You can list some spots that you'd like for your character to do during the match. Give a description of what your character will do after he wins the match, or how will he react if he loses. How a match is written is more on the guys that are involved in the match than on the guy that wrote it. If neither guy sent in a strat, they can't blame the writer if their character didn't go about in the ring how they normally expect them to be. =================================================== 7.
