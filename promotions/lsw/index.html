A December 1997 Texas-based regional affiliate of the National Wrestling Council, whose premiere "Monday Massacre" broadcasts crowned an inaugural Heavyweight Champion and announced the promotion's own first supercard before the surviving record ends. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Lone Star Wrestling's own 4 December 1997 founding invitation describes it as one of a chain of independent federations that, together, made up the National Wrestling Council, and sets its own opening date as 15 December 1997. A later National Wrestling Council promotional dispatch calls Lone Star Wrestling a charter member of that council and the only one of its affiliates never to change commissioners.

Its premiere broadcast, Monday Massacre, crowned “Deadly” Dave Morgan the first-ever LSW Heavyweight Champion, closing a tournament final decided by a ten count. Commissioner Cooper presented Morgan the title belt in the ring, a black-and-gold strap with the letters LSW worked into its plates, while the broadcast promised a follow-up card, Friday Warfare, out of Odessa High School.

Its record extends into 1999: at Face Off at High Noon 2 (14 April 1999), the National Wrestling Council's own championship, held by Sinister, was put on the line against the promotion's own Avalon Britton. Britton's own manager, Justin Kase, came to the ring in his place, and the title changed hands on a fast count from a compromised referee, with a faction that included the self-declared Prisoners of War backing the switch and hustling the new champion out to a waiting car before the decision was even announced.

No document dated after that card survives, and no closing notice is on record.
