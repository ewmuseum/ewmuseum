Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Underground Wrestling Alliance's own handbook, issued 6 October 2001 as “ver. 1.0,” tells its own history as a third attempt at the same promotion: born out of the collapse of an earlier Epic Xtreme Wrestling after only a few weeks, refounded in Atlantic City, New Jersey by two of that earlier promotion's own people, and carried through a first run good enough, in its own account, to draw notice from name talent elsewhere in the sport.

That same handbook narrates a falling-out between its own two founders, a coup by its own then-assistant commissioner that left the promotion bankrupt when the ousted founder emptied its accounts on the way out, a comeback built around what was billed as one last pay-per-view, and a further change of hands after the promotion's own account says its founder was taken out of wrestling for good by its own reigning Heavyweight Champion. A third run opens under an owner the handbook itself declines to name, run day to day, by its own account, as a two-person operation.

Under this third run it staged one major card a week, Thursday Night TRauMa, worked in full play by play under a standing no-disqualification, no-count-out house rule, alongside a shorter, irregular late-night show and an occasional newsletter. Its own past-champions lists carry a Heavyweight Championship, an Intercontinental Championship, Tag Team Championships, a since-retired Hardcore Championship, and an annual Dooms Day tournament crown.

Its one surviving broadcast, “It's Alive 2001” (“..a good day to die”), taped 7 October 2001, stages itself in its own commentary as possibly the promotion's last show. Its card runs a triple-threat ladder match for a stipulation named for a past champion, an Intercontinental Championship fatal-four-way worked under a special guest referee, and a best-two-out-of-three-falls Heavyweight Championship grudge match; after a fall apiece and two separate ringside interventions, the reigning champion regains the title in the deciding fall.

No document survives past that one broadcast. Whether the promotion's own on-air framing of the night as its last show matched what actually happened next is not something the surviving record settles.
