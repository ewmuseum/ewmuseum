Jay Terror Biography Real Name [name withheld] Nicknames The Established, The Outlaw, The Rebel, Mr. Marlboro Hometown Bow, New Hampshire (Now resides in Philadelphia, PA) Birthday September 9, 1983 Height 5'11 Weight 187 lbs. Fed Titles Current World Heavyweight Champion (Second Time), Former Television Champion Fed Awards Match of the year 2007 (NC-17 Terror VS Knight), Rising Star of 2007, Feud of the year 2008 (Valentine vs. White vs. Terror), 2009 Dangerous Games Winner, 2010 Feud of the Year (Tessa Windsor vs. Jay Terror) Biography For PTCPedia Page: Here! Jamison was born into a family where wrestling was life. His grandfather was a wrestler (not the best by a long shot but he was still a fighter in the ring), his father was a well known superstar that traveled the globe, and now as you'd expect, they hoped for the youngest Talley to continue the legacy. As he was younger, Jamison was always the kind of kid to find trouble. He liked to test people's words and see how far he could go without overstepping the lines. That held true as he grew older. The main difference however was he would learn that going a bit further wasn't the worst thing in the world. His father attempted to train his son on many occasions but his own performing got in the way causing Jamison to care little about the business. Instead, he had fun by running around town with his gang of friends making mischief wherever they could. Between him and five others, they created a group which they called "The Bandstand Bandits" (Mostly because the center of town was a giant bandstand). The six kids, now eighteen and younger, wore masks to hide themselves in the night as they created various raids on different points in the town. From small theft to bullying, the kids made a name for themselves quickly as a group to fear. Jamison would stay with the bunch until he was nineteen when he left the position as lead of the group (Or "Top Terror" as they called it). His reasoning was simple; his father had finally retired and was dead-set on training his son. He was banned from leaving the house until he finished four hours of practice a day. The rules continued to build up causing Jamison to get enough. He decided that he would fight but it wouldn't be for his father's glory or even under his father's name. At twenty one, Jamison moved out and found himself a small wrestling promotion in Concord (CWA-Concord Wrestling Academy). With the little he remembered from his dad he created his own style by finding dumb luck early on. He ignored most training and found drugs to be his path to success. For a good year he experimented with multiple drugs and doing so brought him to a point where he wanted to give up on life. His wrestling career had already turned to muck after two years and he was ready to leave for good. Luckily, someone found him on the side of the road and recognized him from a house show. That someone went by the name of Daniel Franco. He had a background in managing wrestlers but that was a long while ago. As he looked over the incapacitated kid though he felt something that made him want to take the kid under his wing. He woke him up and found him some food and soon after proposed a deal with him. His terms were simple, all Jamison had to do was fight his heart out and he'd receive a place to stay and food to keep him going. Daniel didn't want pay but would rather test and see if he could complete this unique experiment. The duo moved to Pennsylvania to get to Daniel's home and there he enrolled Jamison in another small promotion local to the area. He did what Daniel asked with many arguments along the way. It was in his nature to rebel against rules and authority and Daniel saw this as an advantage in the ring even if it became a nuisance outside of it. Jamison, wrestling under the moniker Jay Terror, was ready to move out and start to get deeper in Daniel's experiment. His first target soon became Global Championship Wrestling (GCW), which was a long-time member of the Primetime central circuit. In GCW, he slung together victories against many greats and managed to win the Television Title in his second match. His rise was inevitable and fans began to catch on to the cigarette smoking kid. He would defend the title on many occasions before finally loosing it to Tyson DeBough in hectic tables, ladders, and chairs match. Jay would recover from the loss however and move up the ladder by fighting the likes of LeStatt Knight, whom he had a notable feud with, and Jordan White. White, a best friend of Jay's, would later be turned on by The Terror himself as he took the head position within Steven Caldera's Establishment. During his tenure with Caldera, he managed to retire Rich "The Renegade" Rollins and take the rights of his bandanna. The following day, Rollins would be found dead in his hotel room. Caldera raised Jay like a son filling the father void that Jay had for all of his life. Terror would continue to fight with Jordan White in what is considered one of the better feuds to ever happen in GCW. It ended when Terror won the GCW World Title off of Vivica J. Valentine and later defended it against White. Fighting in an ACWF match, White would ultimately take the title sending Jay into a new frenzy. Terror would become tangled with Andy Murray next and they matched up perfectly showing a split between good and evil. In the end however, Jay Terror fell flat once again giving Steven Caldera doubts about his ability to lead. He continued to try however until he was later taken down by his own mentor, Caldera, and tossed aside. Spending months away from attention and the ring, Terror recovered and found a home within JUST Wrestling as a way to get back on his feet. After going having a record of four wins and one loss, he would become the Just Wrestling Champion defeating "Normal" John Johnson, Peyote Jones, and Jacob McKail. While doing so he would be thrust into GCW fame again after winning the revered Dangerous Games match after entering as the 30th man. In a turn however, Steven Caldera sent him after Jason O'Neil instead of Andy Murray and the World championship. [GCW] Wins: 25 Losses: 14 Draws: 0 Titles Held: World Heavyweight Championship, Television Championship, Vanguard championship (Unified With the TV title) Other Feds JUST Wrestling Other Fed Titles Just Wrestling Champion Other Fed Awards None Strengths Tenacity, Striking, Disregard for the Rules Weaknesses Strength, Technical ability, Endurance, Disregard for the Rules Wrestling Style Unorthodox and often times all over the place with grace in striking offense Strategy When writing Jay, think of everything mean and illegal a guy can do in the ring. He's an honest to god prick who only cares about winning. If the ropes are close enough, he'll use them to help his pin. If the referee isn't looking, he'll throw an eye poke or do a low blow. He's unethical and takes all short cuts to win. When he isn't winning, he's frustrated. He likes to be in control and often will overcompensate to catch up if he's down. This is bad. When it comes to moves procedure, use the head. A lot of his moves, DDTs especially, are meant to work on his head. He uses neck chokes and headlocks because he wants to soften the spot where his finisher lands. You can work the body with jabs but the ultimate goal of the jabs and uppercuts should be to distract the opponent's hands so he has a free shot at the face or back of head. Yes, he'll go after an opponent with their back to him. The Rich Rollins superkick is a big deal. He'll use it to end matches often or to turn the momentum since it's so quick to execute. He defeated Rich Rollins in a ladder match where their bandanas were hanging above the ring and laster that night, Rich committed suicide in his hotel room. Good story, emotion behind it. In terms of weapons, use 'em. He likes to cheat and if it means he'll have the upper hand then he'll do it. Also, he smokes so many cigarettes that you can notice it in the ring.
