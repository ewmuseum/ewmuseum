The Nashvillain Biography Real Name [name withheld] Nicknames the Heart & Soul of all things Southern; the Man with the God Fearin? State of Tennessee on his Back; NV; Nash Hometown Nashville fuckin', Tennessee Birthday 9/28/73 Height 6'1 Weight 255 lbs. Fed Titles none, boy. Fed Awards see above, boy. Biography - Born in Montgomery, Alabama to Edward and Janice Hicks on September 28th, 1973. - Second child for the Hicks? but Red grew up an only child because his older brother Leonard Jameson Hicks drowned under the care [or lack] of a babysitter before Red was born. - His folks moved them to Nashville, Tennessee when Red was ten because his father gained employment at a country record company. - Grew up totally enamored with the sport of professional wrestling, as was every young child in the South around that time. Always emulated his favorite wrestlers and their signature maneuvers, which sometimes landed him in hot water at school, church, etc. - While in high school he passed on several pleading offers from the football and wrestling coach to join their respective teams, choosing instead to hang around a wrestling school doing odds and ends for owner Carl Bowman. - When he turned sixteen he started training under the tutelage of Carl Bowman, a well known and well respected Tennessee area wrestler who many dubbed the "King of the South". - Started his career being "enhancement talent" while still training under Carl Bowman who urged the boy to use his real name. He did, and became "Rebel" Red Hicks. - Two years into the training Carl Bowman suffered a massive heart attack while on a fishing trip and after a few months of mourning, Red started to train with another Southern legend, Horace Gordy. - At the age of 20, Red started out on his own, dropping the name "Rebel" Red Hicks in favor of a mask wearing ultimate bad guy character he created and dubbed "The Nashvillain", drawing inspiration from his childhood favorites. - Gained notoriety and legit heat everywhere he went for his simple, but ruthless and effective style of wrestling, his penchant for cheating and his over flowing amount of Southern pride, which kind of made him a tweener in some areas of the South. - It was at this time that he met a young man named Geoff Dupree who had tried and failed at becoming a pro wrestler and was now trying his hand at a managerial role due to his excellent work on the microphone. It didn’t take long for the two to hook up, with Nash handling the physical side of things and Geoff handling the psychological aspect. - Many promotions in the North offered him and G.G. spots on their rosters, which Red quickly turned down having never left the Southern United States in his entire life and never planning to, completely happy with where he was presently at. - Was suspended indefinitely three years ago by a Board of Ethics made up of many promotion owners in the South for a situation that to this day has never quite been clarified or made public knowledge. - Worked a menial nine to five job in Tennessee seemingly giving up on the wrestling world forever until G.G. convinced him to don the mask once again and make a run up North. At first he was hesitant but the itch was too much and after putting out feelers all over the South [and receiving no bites] he begrudgingly agreed to move the show up North. Other Feds Noneya. That means: none ya damn business, son. Other Fed Awards again. . .see above. Manager G.G. Dupree Wrestling Style Very basic, very stiff, but very effective. Think bare bones but painful wrestling. Strategy n the ring, Nash uses a variety of basic or "old school" moves, but does it with such ruthlessness and precision that it works for him. He’s also a big time cheat, whether it’s a thumb to the eye, a pull of the tights, yank of the hair, the rare ref bump. . .you name it and Nash does it, some say he’s mastered the "art" of cheating working it fluidly into his moveset. Finisher Move Southern Ressurection Finisher Description electric chair drop/facebuster which can be done with opponents head and face coming down on the top turnbuckle, or with opponent’s throat coming down on the top rope, or can be done regularly.
