COLUMNS Friday, August 05, 2005 GCW Worldwide 8/4/05
GCW is trying to come back with a bang coming off of their phenomenal super card event entitled Dangerous Games where a new World champ was crowned and we witnessed a battle royal for the ages. It’s no secret that post super card shows are usually fairly unexciting as it’s most fallout from the PPV and the introduction to new feuds, but if done right they can be a much needed momentum shove – let’s see how World Wide stacks up.

Nice introduction featuring a little recap of Dangerous Games for viewers who were unfortunate enough to miss the classic spectacle. New champ LeStatt Knight finally where he belongs, atop GCW. We have a new number one contender in Michael Stevens who outlasted countless other men in the Dangerous Games’ battle royal, and a nice shout out to Eva Van Lorne who really went over well at the PPV and is proving she can play with the big boys.

Number one contender Michael Stevens comes out to say a few words. First thing I notice is that there is a mixed reaction with the fans and I really dig this because when Stevens faces Knight he’ll ultimately have to be the one getting cheered. It’s going to make it a more anticipated match if Stevens fits the role as someone the fans can cheer for instead of just two utterly despicable bastards.

Stevens mentions his big win, that he wants to get even with Luke Hanson and do some celebrating, but he’s interrupted by, you guessed it, LeStatt Knight. Stevens cracks a joke or two about LeStatt being a vampire, but Knight remains serious. Again, I like how Stevens is going over as someone the fans can appreciate and I love the fact that these two are starting this feud off right – Knight and Dread did a brilliant job last term – and it seems as though these two will do the same.

This whole altercation was pretty good. I really like LeStatt urging Stevens to take him more seriously and step up his game as if he’s on a platform that nobody in GCW can touch right now. And I enjoyed Stevens counter in that by getting Knight mad he got the advantage. Knight is a little bit stronger on the mic of the two here and my one qualm is that normally I think it’d take more for Knight to lose his cool than the banter that took place. Maybe not.

Mike Hardy backstage with President Steven Caldera. Hardy’s frustrated over his loss to Rollins and Caldera’s trying to reason with the fan favorite. I love that Hardy is built up as a fan favorite in GCW which has an over abundance of heels. The whole playing the game thing was corny to me, but I guess it fits the uber-face approach that Hardy brings to the table. I really like stuff Hardy did with Rollins last temer and I’m quite interested to see what’s in store for him this time around. He’s easy to like.

Mike Hardy vs. Dimensioner – Mike Hardy with a quick and rather unexciting match against Dimensioner. This one might’ve taken two whole minutes and featured two or three legitimate moves. That said, opening matches don’t have to be fabulous. I’d have liked a little more here because for one I think it hurts Dimensioner’s credibility a little bit losing so quickly and also GCW needs to take every opportunity to get momentum back after the late airing of Dangerous Games.

I did like Hardy going over here though and I like that he was in a debut match to continue with the whole ‘starting-over’ theme he brought to the table moments before in his discussion with Caldera.

Jason Dread and Michael Stevens chat it up backstage and how appropriate is this? We have a passing of the torch so to speak as Stevens is really beginning to come across as a face which I’ve made clear I think is a smart play. Dread, while on crutches, is still solid on camera as he announces he’ll be away for awhile and provides a bit of advice to his predecessor of sorts. Stevens showing pity for the old friend was kind of awkward for me, but reasonable I suppose. Spitfire has impressed me thus far tonight. He’s making far more impact than he did in his altercations with Tremendous last term.

Jonathon Borealis vs. Rich Rollins for the Television Title. Probably the biggest TV title match since it’s return to action. Borealis has really given the title some credibility by successfully defending it week and and week out and his performance in the battle royal at Dangerous Games was also great. This match was quite a bit better than the opener. Nothing spectacular, (but it shouldn’t have to be on a weekly show as long as the show airs on time). I’m not surprised that Rollins wins here as he is, at this point anyhow, a better wrestlers than Borealis. My qualm here is why put the television title on a guy whose level far exceeds that of the television title. [Looks at upcoming card] I myself have an inkling as to why, but I won’t say it aloud just in case I could be wrong. Borealis has been a solid television champ and I don’t think I’d mind seeing him elevated IF he got a little better on the mic.

Eva and LeStatt. These two have amazing chemistry and it’s been apparent and never wavered since I first witnessed the two of them together. It’s hard to find two people with this much chemistry on camera affiliated with wrestling of all things. LeStatt is ever mysterious and Eva is charming per usual. Two cunning minds working together? Against one other? I for one love watching it all unfold. Their dialogue, their emotion, their actions – they all seem so realistic instead of forced like what you’d usually see between man/woman on a wrestling program.

Trey Tremendous, Jordan and Rich Rollins backstage. A little glimpse of something to come here perhaps. Just a bit of dialogue exchanges hands here as Trey Tremendous and Rich Rollins jeer one another a bit. I’m getting the distinct feeling that something is going on surrounding these titles and what I really think I like about it is how many people are involved one way or another. IF done properly angles that get quite a few people involved are usually quite entertaining – but maybe nothing is going on, we’ll see.

Trey Tremendous vs. Eva Van Lorne for the United State Title. How’s Eva have all this energy. I guess Knight couldn’t tire the broad out – I bet I could. Good exciting match thus far. Lots of solid back and forth action between two fierce competitors definitely bring a lot of energy to the ring. Regardless of who wins this one Eva is continuing to go over as a prime time player and not a mid card chica. Which is fine by me as she’s been working hard and she’s right in the thick of GCW affairs both figuratively and literally with her actions with the champ.

Knight’s music prompts Trey to look up with the momentum in his favor and it gives new TV champ Rollins the opportunity to catch whack him with the title belt. Eva wins and becomes the first woman to hold gold in GCW. Rollins takes off and a furious Tremendous gives chase. Knight’s music plays again and this time he comes out to celebrate with Eva and they particpate in some smooching…save it for backstage guys. The fans ain’t digging that. BIG win for Eva tonight. I hate to see Trey lose the US belt as he’s been super awesome since getting it including a clutch defense against Stevens at Dangerous Games. That said, something tells me this isn’t over just yet and that the situation is about to get a bit more sticky in the upcoming weeks.

Steven Caldera walks into his office only to find former GCW champ Octavian waiting on him. Octavian is back and he wants a world title shot, but Mike Hardy drops in to let know that you have to start at the bottom and work your way up. A little taunting form both Hardy and Octavian. I personally would love to see the old progidal son step into the ring with Caldera’s new piece of work. I think the two would have some awesome mid-card matches between the two of them and really work to elevate themselves.

Michael Stevens vs. Luke Hanson. Hanson starts off ferociously but is quickly upended by a chair shot from LeStatt Knight and the match is promptly thrown out. I’ll be honest… with Hanson main eventing here I really wasn’t looking forward to this main event as much as I was the TV title bout or the US title bout. But the chair shot contest that takes place between Stevens and Knight is quite fun and I almost do feel sorry for the big lug Hanson and the beating he takes. Maybe you deserve to main event if you can withstand those unmerciful chair shots. Knight finishes with a massive chair shot to the face of Stevens and departs leaving the other two men fallen in the ring. The champ definitely makes his presence known.


PULSE NOTES: This was a better than okay show, especially for coming off a PPV. I think it did a lot to give GCW a lot of their momentum back, especially with all the new faces coming in and so many GCWers participating in the Internet Gauntlet. The first few matches, actually most all the matches could’ve been a bit stronger with the exception of the US Title match which was really pretty good. (Bare with me guys, I’m trying to be in character here – mucho props to anyone who steps up and writes a match REGARDLESS of quality – if it weren’t for you guys the cards would be primarily late if at all).

Something was said about not too many segments on the show, but I’ll say this. One thing I’ve always really enjoyed about GCW is that their segments are always really good and serve a solid purpose to advance storylines and angles and aren’t just pure crap or even there in lackluster attempts at entertainment. I love [name withheld], but five pointless segments a card are ridiculous. Keep the segments coming, but make sure they always serve a purpose. GCW’s awesome segments are part of what makes them one of the top 3 PTC feds in my honest opinion right now. Keep up the good work hombres and I’m looking forward to next card. Oh, and stroke my ego and let me know you enjoyed this review.

-- GCW Pulse

Slightly Less than DG Review
With GCW just beginning to catch fire in the ratings, not to mention both LeStatt Knight and Rich Rollins making the PTC top ten, AND Rollins currently holding the infinite guantlet championship – this hiatus of sorts couldn’t have come at a worse time. It’s no secret that momentum is shot to hell, BUT with a GREAT PPV chances are GCW can begin the climb back to prominence. That said, without their owner, the handlers, under the guidance of Mr. Everything Rich Rollins, took it upon themselves to put on a show in anticipation of Dangerous Games.

The event was effectively called Slightly Less-than Dangerous Games.

Effective intro. I honestly got a kick out of Jhub interrupting the voice over. We had a cool montage of some important stuff leading into Dangerous Games. Something off the change of pace, like I expect this show to be, is always refreshing for a reviewer who witnesses the same ole same ole week in and week out.

First Match. For the television title ‘Scandalous’ Ryan Stephens challenges Jonathon Borealis. Stephens immediately playing mind games with the champ, classic Stephens. He’s really a despicable guy and the fans agree as one plants him with a beer mug allowing Borealis to go on the offensive. Jonathon Borealis has been nothing short of spectacular in defending his television title. This is probably his toughest test since outlasting Stephens for the title in the original battle royal.

I’m digging this match as it does a good job portraying Stephens as a smaller, smart wrestler while also very effectively displaying Borealis’ knack for a bit more of a balls out hardcore type fighter. Despite both fighters truly being rather technical – I like that it branches and deviates and shows that the two are just one-dimensional wrestlers.

Borealis has fought well enough to claim a win, when Red Rock, UWF’s Television Champ bursts on the scene. I like the intensity between the two respective television title holders and you have to respect and enjoy the co-existing of the PTC feds despite what happened with the WMW/GW pay per view. Co-existing is something that should happen more often around the PTC. There was mention of GCW guys showing up at UWF stable wars and we get a preview of things to come here tonight. Stephens wins via DQ, but that’s an afterthought now as Borealis outfought him and this UWF/GCW altercation seems as though it’s about to get a bit more interesting.

Red Rock gets on the mic and proceeds to banter about what a joke GCW is despite the fact that inanimate objects win championships over in UWF. He mentioned UWF HW Jamie Shock being better than Jason Dread and speaks of Rich Rollins leaving UWF. All in all I must admit that it was pretty lame and unconvincing, but I am interested in where this will lead post Dangerous Games.

Commercial Break.

We return to out three commentators discussing Dangerous Games by giving a bit of their thoughts. It makes for an interesting preview and I like the fact that these guys aren’t scared to state their opinions about the operations of the fed.

Backstage with Jordan White and Trey Tremendous. Fun is sure to follow as it usually does with these two. These two don’t miss again. I didn’t laugh aloud this time so minus two points for that, but definitely a cool segment talking about the whereabouts of GCW President Steven Caldera and we get to see Trey in his finest form, reading cue cards.

Our second match of the evening pits LeStatt Knight vs. Eva Van Lorne. Looks like the flirting between these two will come to a halt. At least for a few minutes this evening. Very logical progression with this match, even a hint of sexual tension between these competitors, good stuff. Eva hangs tough throughout most of the match, even making two clutch kick outs before Knight defeats her with the Knight Fall. He IS the hottest wrestlers in GCW right now, and this wasn’t a big surprise. The surprise here is Eva going over a bit with a valiant effort. It will be really interesting to see how these two respond to having to face each other in a match.

Commerical Break.

Main event is GCW’s resident hero and hardcore specialist Trey Tremendous against UWF hardcore specialist The Communist. Communist is okay on the mic, nothing spectacular. We have a few interesting scenarios here. First, is Trey actually defending his hardcore belt against The Commy? I mean can UWF’s Communist be GCW’s hardcore champion. Also it’s stated that Trey defends this belt all the time, but I don’t think he’s defended it since the last pay per view and probably not even on that show. I’ve voiced my displeasure for the belt, but hey what can you do. I just review shows.

Ugh, I just can’t get into hardcore matches, let’s try. Trey face first through a pane of glass, blood pouring from him like a water faucet. I’m still reading because I want to see the fallout of this match and as they have been all night long, the announcers are spot on with solid chemistry and are calling a good match.

Hey, what a coincidence. The Russians sets some tacks up and Trey reverses his own move onto the thumbtacks. I think I’m being overly harsh here just because I’m so biased against this hardcore crap (it’s my gimmick maybe), but I could read through that like I could a Freddy Prinze Jr. movie.

Finally, after another spot that isn’t too ridiculous Trey seems as though he’s about to snag a pin when Digital Morality shows up. GCW’s Rich Rollins comes out to confront his former UWF stable mates while Trey sneaks away. Red Rock comes back out and the three UWF guys surround Rollins, but LeStatt Knight and Trey Tremendous come to the rescue. As you would imagine chaos ensues and we have a HOT rivalry on our hands. Complete pandemonium, and fade out.

Honestly Slightly Less than Dangerous Games is a huge success in my eyes because it accomplished a number of things. First of all it brought a fun atmosphere to the GCW fans in wake of this long await for the PPV with Caldera being in Argentina and all. SLtDG also opened the door for this UWF/GCW feud that is beginning to shape up. While I can’t get into hardcore matches for the life of me, and I wish there would have been some more segments from the GCW clan, all in all it was a nice slice fun which definitely wet the appetite in anticipation of Dangerous Games.

Mucho props to the GCW guys, especially this one guy that made it all happen. He’s a cool kat.

--GCW Pulse

GCW Worldwide 6/16/05 Reactions
First Note: The roster size is approaching a comfortable state in GCW. The place should get an inactive roster page and throw the inactive guys on there THEN be selective with their new additions. Make sure that each guy they hire from here on out is a good fit for the company. There are the old faces and the newer guys for GCW and after Dangerous Games it is time that those two groups start coinciding, intermingling if you will. Also, I think it’d be cool to have a really promising tag division of about 6 active teams. It’s not often you see a fed with a solid tag roster and I think that is something GCW should aspire to have as they grow as a federation.
Second Note: The Hardcore title… I don’t like it. One if you have so many titles and not enough guys who deserve them then drop one. The hardcore gimmick is REALLY overplayed anyhow. I mean the roster isn’t that big and your titles are a lot more prestigious if there are less of them and it will also cause new guys to learn to get together and feud with one another to get over, to garner attention as opposed to talking shit and going for a title they don’t yet deserve a shot at. EVENTUALLY maybe bring in a cruiserweight title if the demand is there for that type of match, but first I’d try to get a stronger tag division and lose the hardcore title. Just my opinion.

Good start to the show. Nice jobbing plugging the Knight/Dread stuff as it is and should be the premiere feud match as we approach Dangerous Games.

I enjoyed the Knight/Eva interaction as I did two weeks ago. They have solid chemistry on camera together and it they usually leave suspense so I suspect and hope that something will evolve from it.

So the battle royal will be filled out by AWA wrestlers. I’m not sure I like this move because after all this a GCW PPV, not a joint PPV. Caldera needs to be careful and keep the primary focus on his GCW guys. The fact that Caldera is returning to the ring to settle a personal vendetta with Low is alright with me for right now as long as it’s a side plot and not the main emphasis in the battle royal. This battle royal allows the GCW to shake up the totem poll and it should serve as such. Because Caldera is a wrestling talent the whole scenario is viable, but again I don’t want him to get carried away with emphasis on himself, it’s got to be about getting his GCW talent OVER.

Rich Rollins and Ryan Stephens chat about some politics. It’s no secret that I like Rollins on the mic and Stephens held his own here as well further developing his character as an intelligent guy with deep pockets. It was good to see these two taking the reins and showing that the old guard and the new guard can coexist and work together. Stephens is working hard to make himself into a believable contender for the battle royal.

Great first match. In fact it was so solid that I’m scared it’s going to make some of the later matches seem a bit watered down. It was much more than I expected. Twisted as fallen fairly far to be opening for GCW, but he fought hard like the champion he had been and was upset and in marvelous debut for Shawn Johnson. The rookie goes over pretty big here and it will be interesting to see if he can keep it up.

The Stephens/Toxic segment seemed a little off for me. I liked the whole premise of it if Toxic doesn’t want to participate, but the way Stephens has been portrayed thus far I wouldn’t think he’d be annoyed about a mention of not having a title belt. And there were some other things that seemed a bit off in regards to Stephens as well. He played the paying off as he probably would however. As for Toxic, I agree with what Stephens told him nobody is going to care if he’s dodging matches except perhaps Caldera. He’s not LeStatt Knight, he’s not Rich Rollins so who cares if he’s dodging matches?

LeStatt Knight comes out and challenges the champ and gives us some good heel lines. He seems back on target this week. Not a whole lot here, but definitely a necessary segment that I enjoyed. He’s really doing a great job of getting himself over as an ultimate heel and he’s made solid reasons for the loss of his US title and the legacy he left. I can’t wait for he vs. Dread to bubble over.

Shawn Johnson. Hmmm. So he’s left the ring already and he’s off talking to Lopsang who is accompanied by his goat. You know it was fairly humorous but it just doesn’t fit in the context with the show. I don’t know what Johnson is going for here. The segment doesn’t have much basis for being there. It really doesn’t. I kind of liked the opening match guru thing and wish that perhaps he would have played that up more or used his mic time to initiate a feud as opposed to talking to Lopsang. I’m undecided here.

Well Mike Hardy versus Capo never exactly takes place, but what does is a whole lot more entertaining. Capo gets a nice free pin and the shit kicked out of him, he’s a non-factor here. Rich Rollins comes out in typical Rollins mockery fashion and is eventually attacked by a crazed fan impersonating UWF’s Tsunami Oni. Turns out it’s Mike Hardy himself and more fun ensues. The announcing crew is on target during all the shenanigans. Both of these guys are doing a great job escalating this feud and getting themselves over. My only qualm here is that Mike Hardy just isn’t that strong on the mic, or at least he hasn’t displayed it to me. I’d let the veteran Rollins carry the mic stuff during the altercations and have Hardy stand there and look like a golden boy. Still, good stuff from these too. I like this as much as the Knight/Dread stuff of late.

Michael Stevens speaks with Sheena Ramone. First let me state that I like the reasons and the history given for why Stevens wants a piece of Dread in this match, but Tremendous barely getting a mention considering fairly recent events just didn’t sit well with me. Also, his dialogue with Sheena seemed like she was just there so that between the two of them they could get his points across and it just wasn’t a believable dialogue between male/female for me, but I suppose that’s how it is with most wrestlers. Stevens is a guy I see making a big impact at Dangerous Games. He’s lingering right there between main event and upper mid-card and I think with a good showing at Dangerous Games (he will) and better mic skills he could claim a spot up there amongst Dread, Knight and for me Rollins.

EVL vs. Dimensioner/Brian Marshall didn’t exactly happen which was fine by mean. The only reason for this might have been to set up Dimensioner/Marshall which really didn’t work for me either seeing has how Dimensioner just flips and attacks Marshall out of frustration!? As I noted before I wish GCW would pursue a few tag teams after Dangerous Games. One of my least favorite parts of the show for sure.

Trey Tremendous/Jordan/Caldera. I love this little scene. I am quickly becoming a big fan of Trey Tremendous and his gimmick. Like some others I thought the “You've got a match against some guy, some time,” from Jordan followed by the “Sweet” from Trey might’ve been the lines of the night. Trey is a guy that is right there on the verge with Stevens and I’m looking forward to another altercation between these two soon enough. I don’t know if there’s room for both of them atop the card. My only complaint here and it’s only because I find flaw with everything is that Caldera seemed a little too “cool” as opposed to more professional and poised as I envision him.

Dread more or less excepts the challenge from Knight. Not quite as intense as I would have liked between the two, but both were fairly solid as I’ve come to expect from them. Maybe my expectations were too high and this was brilliant, but I’ll go with above average stuff from two talented headliners. I wish it would have been a bit edgier but I sense it will be later in the evening.

Borealis vs. Hardcastle. Borealis has a little rant before the match and surprise, surprise I enjoyed Jonathon Borealis this week. He did, he stepped it up this week. He did a solid job playing up his face status which is good for GCW because they don’t have a whole bunch of them. He obviously has quite an ego and he’s denouncing egos so that was a bit awkward for me, but I found myself feeling like he was getting himself over as a newcomer, and underdog of sorts despite being the TV champion and that’s what a good face can do. I’ll say it again. I enjoyed Borealis this week. Oh, and I wonder who might take him up on his challenge?

Now for the match itself. It was a good match which is what I anticipated in a title bout with two p and coming cruiserweights. I enjoyed how the referee was made a factor in the match. I am curious as to what is going to come of the ending of this match and must admit that I didn’t like Borealis a face being assisted out of the ring by security because of the referee. It just doesn’t add up well for me, but perhaps I’m a bit of a traditionalist when it comes to alignment.

Erik gets a beat down backstage and they’re pretty sure it wasn’t Knight or Dimensioner, and they don’t know who it is. Eva comes to his side, but Erik just spouts off a bunch of German and departs, Eva on his heels. Interesting segment. Curious to see where it heads, if served it’s purpose.

Main Event. Trey Tremendous/Jason Dread vs. Michael Stevens/LeStatt Knight First of all I thought Pete disliked Knight, despised him actually. I liked Stevens getting the pin here… I kind of wished it wouldn’t have come at Dread’s expense merely because I don’t want our super stud face to seem vulnerable going into the match with Knight at Dangerous Games. It was a solid, well constructed match, perhaps a bit short but full of logical progression and action.

Post game brawl is a bit confusing for me. We have Tremendous attacking Knight whom he beat last week and Stevens and Dread battling… It makes sense for Knight maybe to go after Trey, but because he has had tunnel vision for Dread this ending brawl just didn’t work for me. How about some added tension for Dangerous Games? How about Dread and Knight exchanging a few blows and Stevens and Tremendous who are logically linked to me anyhow squaring off. Perhaps that would be boring and anticipated? But logical. I don’t know.

Kind of a cool ending there with the camcorder and Tremendous landing a big spot. Kudos on another good show. GCW is quickly moving up my list of PTC feds to watch and follow and they’re paying me good for this gig so I’ll probably keep doing it… IF I GET MORE FEEDBACK… I invest quite a bit of time in these reactions and if they aren’t appreciated I shouldn’t bother…

---GCW Pulse

GCW Worldwide 6/9/05 Reactions
Shows starts with Pete, James Bryan and Jhub commentating. We’re not sure if this is a one time thing or something we should get used to. Some of the dialogue is funny, other is a little forced. I wish GCW had more distinct personalities at the announcer’s table. We get a quick preview of Dangerous Games, thirty contestants. There’s not even thirty guys on the roster so we’ll see how that works out. A preview of tonight’s show and we’re off.
Segment: Tremendous & White. Segment didn’t serve that much of a point other than a few nuances but I really enjoyed it. It gave Tremendous who was fighting for the US title some face time, which he handled well, and made note of Knight being tougher than Stevens, which we already know, but still. Enjoyable first segment.

Segment: Borealis. Okay I picked on him a little bit last week about his segment presence and he responded with a pretty good interview with Sally Ford this week. Again, not a lot of pertinent information but the television title holder deserves face time as well. The verbal banter between he and Ford was certainly clever.

Segment: Knight. Okay, let’s break this down because Knight wasn’t as spot one as he was last week. Last week he exuded this relaxed confidence that I really enjoyed with his character. I actually hope that this warped tunnel vision approach to the title was just helping set up what was to come in the main event as opposed to something he’s going to keep pursuing into dangerous games. I think that a mention of Tremendous would have been rather appropriate amongst all the Dread banter.

Segment: Stephens. A very short segment without much substance here. Yes he is attempting to convey his character as conniving heel from all angles and the women’s lib stuff works, as does the business like approach of having an office, but I think it could’ve been done another way, another time during the show.

First Match: Stephens vs. Marshall. What this match lacked in wrestling and excitement it made up for in logic. It put both characters over nicely. It had Marshall being the temper charged psycho that he is attempting an early torrid pace that he’s unable to keep up with and the key spot in the match, the dive over the ropes outside of the ring. It conveyed Stephens’ as the smart wrestler that he is, though I wish it would have put him over more as the technician he claims to be. Stephens’ finisher stuff is goofy, he should work on those. The match had logical progression and was a really solid opening bout to kick off the wrestling. I enjoyed the cowardice with how Stephens left the ring after being tripped by Marshall, interesting spot. Makes sense to put Stephens over here as he has made himself more of a threat to do well in the Battle Royal at Dangerous Games.
** ½ out of *****

We see some footage of Toxic defeating Twizzy at the pay per view and in doing so won a match he in a quick turn of the tides, but that’s neither here nor there. Segment ensues.

Segment. Toxic. Honestly, this segment just didn’t do it for me. I suppose it’s in the guy’s personality to over hype himself, but that’s exactly what he’s doing here and in the long run it could hurt him. He is basically laying claim to being the best wrestler in GCW. He can be an affective heel with all his banter to the fans, but claiming to be the franchise at this point, I’m just not comofortable with that. Seems to me like he’s setting himself up for disappointment. I’d rather have seen his mic time used to make himself, with proof mind you, a viable contender for the shot at Dangerous Games.

Segment. Jason Dread. My favorite segment of the night thus far. The champ is not only starting to the set the scene with Knight, but the two are showing different points of view on their friendship. Dread gets across as a huge face here which is perfect for their bout come Dangerous Games. The scenario is beginning to unfold and despite being the champ Dread is making himself be the underdog and the babyface and the fans are going to love it. I know I’m getting legitimately interested.

Second Match: Mike Hardy vs. Dimensioner. Mediocre match here but it suffices for an early match on a weekly show. The move sequences were fairly solid, but the match didn’t really tell much of a story or progress in a manner, which put the characters over more formidably. My beef with this match was that there was too much focus on the annoucing crew and them being funny. An occasional funny mark is good for ratings, but it’s those guys jobs to put the wrestlers in the ring over and they didn’t do that here. Because it was a short match it made it seem Hardy was stronger than he actually was here. I might’ve opted for a draw type finish here. All that said, this match was a big improvement from the matches last week (cough: summary stuff :cough). * ½ of *****

Post match segment. Rich Rollins. Action ensues. This was a lot of fun for me. It is becoming quite apparent that Rich Rollins is a prime time player in GCW. His Mike skills are very strong and he carries Hardy here who does convey a stern intensity which indeed adds to this feud. The whole stapling the sign thing until Caldera comes out was creative though I don’t know that an attack with a microphone and getting stapled would leave you in that bad of shape in a week’s time as was mentioned. President's Rant #1- Fame is Overrated

Who doesn't love James Bond? He gets all the women, the gadget, the cars, and saves the world every few years. Hell, Bond has been around for forty years and he never seems to get any older (save Roger Moore's last two movies.) He's the ultimate alpha male, and I challenge you to find me one man who wouldn't jump at the chance to be James Bond.

However...

There's one fatal flaw with the Bond movies. One tiny little detail that ruins it all for me.

James Bond is a famous spy.

In almost all the movies, Bond is famous. People know his name, they're afriad of him. When he's killed in You Only Live Twice, it's front page news. When SPECTRE tries to plot their next move, they always wonder about the involvement of James Bond. James Bond is a famous spy, and that, my friends, is impossible. Forget all the amazing stunts, crazy gadgets and impossibly beautiful women; this is the most unlikely part of the whole crazy mess.

Because Bond is a famous spy, people are always trying to kill him. Of course, this will never happen, because Bond is more or less bullet proof; hell, I can only remember once when the guy even bleeds. But he's always running, and the bad guys almost instantly see through his disguises.

So, I ask, why does everyone want to be famous? Especially in the pro wrestling business, fame only makes you a target, makes you the hunted one. You can be deadly, suave and drive a fast car like Bond, but when you make yourself famous, you get the whole world gunning for you. The spotlight in wrestling may as well be saying "He's over here! Quick, get him!" You've got about as much chance as Daffy Duck arguing with Bugs Bunny over whether it's Wabbit season or Duck season.

Those who last the longest in this business come out of nowhere. He reaches the top, and nobody ever saw him coming. Once you reach the top, once you win the World Title, you can't help but be famous. But because you avoided the barrage of talent gunning for you on your way up, you've got that much more of a chance of staying there. When I won my first World Championship, I was a virtual unknown and a complete stranger to the company. That's the way to do it; because after you get there, you're going to be plotted against and planned for as if there's a bald guy stroking a white cat and ordering his henchman to dispose of you.

So my advice? Be like James Bond, but be a real spy. Do what you need to do, but do it quietly. Don't let your ego run your career, let your brain do it. Don't go looking for trouble, because in this business, trouble will find you.

But if you do insist on being famous, if you can't live without the spotlight, make sure you can at least get a girl named Pussy Galore or Holly Goodnight. Otherwise, it's all just a big waste.

-Steven Caldera, President & CEO Caldera Enterprises, Incorporated
