WorldWide 82 Ford Center — Oklahoma City, Oklahoma ---- Introduction The lights dim in the arena and the crowd begins to buzz. The GCW logo flashes across the screen, as "Wake Up" by Rage Against the Machine begins to play. Finally a heavy guitar riff explodes into the arena, and the fans go wild! Bright blue fireworks explode over the ring as two screaming bolts of light stream down to the stage. The stage erupts with silver pyrotechnics as the cameras begin flying over the frenzied crowd, and the voice of James Bryan chimes in right on cue. Bryan: Hello and welcome to another explosive episode of WorldWide! Last week GCW 2008 started off with an incredible show. Vivica Valentine addressed the world for the first time as GCW World Heavyweight champion only to be interrupted by everyone that considers themselves fit to be number one contender. Of course I’m alongside my longtime friend and colleague, David Yale. Yale: Much appreciated JB, last week was certainly eventful. Not only did LeStatt Knight, Jordan White, Jay Terror and Rich Rollins stake their claim of being the contender to the title but at the end of the extraordinary Main Event, Shaman rose up and made Valentine take notice of him. Bryan: Yes our United States champion appears to be ready to move on to bigger and better things. He seems to have set his sights on Valentine like the rest of the field. I’d hate to be Valentine right now! Yale: The top of GCW is just sick right now JB and even when someone does manage to take that title from Valentine, I wouldn’t want to be them either! Bryan: Last week we also saw the crowning of a new TV champion when Tyson DeBough was defeated by the wacky and loveable Clyde Fox! Yale: Well I can think of a lot of ways to describe him JB but loveable isn’t one of them. Clyde did manage to win the television championship though, so I have to give him props. Problem is that, like the World title, the Television title is going to be hotly contested. Bryan: Quite right, GCW has seen an incredible influx of talent since NC-17 and each and every one of them appear to be on the fast track to stardom. Andy Murray and Thom LeStrange have already formed a mutual distain for one another and does anyone like Chris Bagwell? Yale: I can dig him JB. Bryan: So now we move on to tonight’s action, where GCW fires up it’s round robin to officially announce a number one contender to Valentine’s World Title. Yale: Five superstars all after one goal! It doesn’t get any better than that! Bryan: Tonight we see a rematch of the Dangerous Games Main Event when Jordan White tries to do what he couldn’t back then and topple one of GCW’s greatest champions ever, Lia Ambrosi. Yale: And our Main Event is a rematch of GCW’s Match of the Year for 2007. LeStatt Knight vs. Jay Terror. Does tonight not feel like a Pay Per View JB? Bryan: It does indeed! Tonight we also get to see B&E~! member Chris Storm go one on one with Kimbusa after last week’s attack by Caldera’s hired gun left both members looking for answers. Yale: Also on tap are great matches between newcomers Andy Murray and Chris Bagwell and another with Kiljaden Kincaid, Mark Matthews and Depraved in a triple threat match. Just another step for these rookies to prove they belong. Bryan: One of the more interesting matches of this loaded card is Digital Mortality vs. Tessa Windsor. Tessa has been in GCW for quite sometime but has just been below that elite level but a win tonight over the insanely talented Digital Mortality, a recent signee from the now defunct GLOBAL will put her on the right track. Yale: Mortality is in his first GCW match which does put him at a disadvantage BUT from everything I’ve seen and heard about the man he should be in tip-top shape. It promises to be a damn good one. Bryan: Just like every match tonight! Well fans it’s about time to get this show on the road! WELCOME TO WORLDWIDE! ---- An Englishman Abroad - pt.3: Mr. LeStrange's Evening Out Bryan: "Well folks, this week's WorldWide has got off to a lightning start!" Yale: "As always, J.B, as always!" Bryan: "Absolutely right, Dave, it's a pleasure to be out here at ringside week in, week out," At that moment, Two eerie harmonic notes ring through the arena, stunning the announcers and the raucous crowd into silence. Every light in the building descends instantaneously into darkness as the haunting unison vocals of "Personal Jesus" by Depeche Mode, amplified and delivered through the Public Address system, pierce the pitch-black gloom. "Reach out, touch faith..." As the final note of the introduction echoes about, the lights slide back up to their normal level of luminescence. Without warning, a sheet of sparks cascades from the very top of the MegaTron, accompanied by the pounding bass riff of the song, and Thom LeStrange steps boldly through the conflagration to a reaction that as the weeks have gone by, has rapidly worsened. Where once he would receive an equal mixture of boos and cheers, recent events with upstart fan favourite Andy Murray have left rather a sour taste in the mouths of the GCW faithful. Bryan: "Here we go. I wondered how long it'd be before LeStrange showed his cocky face." Yale: "J.B., it wouldn't quite be WorldWide without him!" Bryan: "No it wouldn't, Dave, but not in a good way." Yale: "And it wouldn't be WorldWide without a James Bryan rant about LeStrange either!" Bryan: "Can you blame me? I think I speak for everyone except you in my distaste of him." Yale: "Say what you will though, J.B., the guy's made a name for himself, he's got himself some attention. Smug though it is, you can't forget his face now! Ya gotta give the kid that!" Bryan: "I guess you're right." Yale: "So you reckon it's time for another instalment of 'An Englishman Abroad'?" Bryan: "You mean another chance to act naive and wide-eyed and try and make us laugh at our own culture?" Yale: "You know what they say, J.B., if you can't laugh at yourself, you're in a sorry state..." "...Your own personal Jesus. Someone to hear your prayers, Someone who cares. Your own personal Jesus..." As the verse of "Personal Jesus" begins to play, all eyes turn to the top of the ramp, where the spotlights converge upon the figure of Thom LeStrange. He stands proudly, surveying the crowd. The crowd in turn stares right back, observing the enigmatically handsome man who stands before them, his soft features set into a strong jaw, and brooding deep brown eyes belying the confidence, bravado and propensity for chicanery that lies within. As always, his attire is impeccable - a perfectly tailored, exquisite suit of grey Prince of Wales check, with matching waistcoat, pocket watch, a white shirt and a silverish tie. His thin-lipped mouth slowly curls upwards into a half-sneer, and he slowly raises his arms up to the sides, as if welcoming, nay demanding the attention of the crowd. Bryan: "Nice to see he's getting the boos and hisses he deserves, especially after what happened between he and Andy Murray last week." Yale: "What the hell is going on with these two? Out of nowhere they're in each others' faces the whole time? Feels like someone lit the blue touch paper and stepped back, and we're watching the explosion." Bryan: "Well I'll tell you how it came about; it came about because LeStrange was too high-and-mighty to help out a fellow human being, let alone a fellow Brit at NC-17." Yale: "And as if that wasn't enough, when Andy Murray asked for an explanation, perfectly reasonably, LeStrange just went off and jumped him. The guy's a nutjob!" Bryan: "Sometimes I find it hard to believe how one man can be so arrogant, so officious, so self-important!" Yale: "He's an upper-class gent, J.B.!" Bryan: "He's an offensive oaf who merely had the fortune of being born with a silver-spoon in his mouth." Thom's arms return to his sides, and he paces steadily but assuredly towards the squared circle, ascending the ring steps and stepping deftly between the top and middle rope so as not to crease his suit. As his music dies to nothingness, he takes a microphone from the ring announcer, and paces the ring a few times, carefully watching the mass assembled before him. Eventually, he raises the microphone to his lips. LeStrange: "I must tell you, assembled rabble, how fabulous it is to be out here again, able to address you all in the spirit of bonhomie, but I fear the feeling is no longer mutual. It seems a numer of you, nay all of you, have rather taken against me, particularly in light of my latest embroilment with Andy Murray." Mention of the name of the Scottish King of Cool evokes a brief cheer, followed by a longer, louder boo at Thom LeStrange for his actions towards the Scotsman. Thom nods his head understandingly, waiting for the booing to end, before raising the microphone to his lips once again. LeStrange: "Allow me to tell you my side of the story. It all began as a simple misunderstanding on Mr. Murray's part. You see, the fact that I happened to be present whilst he and Bagwell had their little set-to does not mean I was in any way his ally. It was sheer happenstance. We merely happened to find ourselves in similar situations. I did not know him - I owed him nothing. And it was he who confronted me last week. And may I remind you, it was he who laid his oafish, gargantuan hands on me first." Another substantial boo from the GCW crowd irks LeStrange somewhat. His desperation shows as his flailing attempts at justification become more and more provoking. LeStrange: "Let me tell you something, serfs, where I come from, your reaction would be different. In England we'd think twice before letting that Scottish boob cross the border. I've often wondered what the point of a north/south divide is if it's not policed. But I understand, you root for your own kind. How was I to have a chance: me, an urbane, opulent, intelligent man from damned fine origins - you could never identify with me! No, no, you stick up for your own sort, this boorish, ham-fisted nincompoop." These incendiary comments merit Thomas James LeStrange a torrent of booing, hissing and abuse, and even a few missiles are launched at him from the crowd. Bryan: "It seems his cool-as-a-cucumber composure has deserted him, Dave. Murray and the GCW crowd have gotten underneath his skin, and he does NOT like it! We're seeing some vitriol here!" Yale: "These people can't respect someone who's just of higher calibre..." Bryan: "Why should they respect this despicable human being?" Thom LeStrange is silent for several moments, attempting to regain his composure, attempting to replace the mask of level-headedness and absolute composure which has slipped. LeStrange: "GCW faithful, I apologise for my outburst. It was highly inappropriate. In the spirit of forgiveness, camaraderie and conviviality, may I invite you to enjoy the next instalment of 'An Englishman Abroad'. Enjoy." The crowd tentatively obliges, an air of awkwardness now in the atmosphere. They turn their heads to face the MegaTron, which now displays Thom LeStrange sitting in now-familiar environs; an antique armchair in an elegantly decorated drawing room, a decanter of single malt whisky and a single tumbler on a small side table next to him. LeStrange: "Greetings, friends, and welcome to the third part of 'An Englishman Abroad.' This week, as every week, I shall regale you with tales of my adventures in this nation of yours, as I attempt to grips with customs and etiquette quite foreign to me. Today, I shall detail a rather agreeable luncheon I recently took." "Last Sunday, after the transmission of another successful WorldWide, I found myself with nought to do, and decided to seize the opportunity to sample the delights of an evening out at an authentic American bar. Consulting once again my trusty travelling companion, the 2002 Gault-Millau guide, I found a number of worthy recommendations, but as I walked into the city centre, I passed a tavern that seemed to epitomize all that I had been searching for. Its name was Barney's. I tossed the Gault-Millau into the glove compartment of my car, and entered its doors, awash with anticipation of the evening to come." "The saloon style interior was instantly appealing. At several tables sat clusters of wounded young sports aficionados nursing the scars of their team's loss with Coors, sucking on Marlboro lights for extra comfort. Some sort of highlights package of the encounter in question was being played on the large televisionbox suspended in one corner of the room. Taking a gander, intrigued at their version of football, I noted that the game appeared to be some rather quaint bastardisation of rugby, in which a forward pass was perfectly legal, the chaps all wore lycra, and there was no scrum. The girls at the sideline scantily attired and kicking their legs reminded me of my favourite music hall revues back in Blighty." "I took a table, and ordered a round of chilled lager to whet my appetite for a stupendous evening feast. Much like Les Deux Garcons, a particular favourite eatery of mine in London, Barney's had no menu. At Les Deux Garcons this is because the day's fare is the spontaneous creation the eccentric 3-Michelin-starred chef Michel Laurent. At Barney's, the reason there is no menu is because there is no food. My request for a bill of fare was met with much hilarity by the fellows ensconced at the bar. I decided instead that this would be a good opportunity to discover local customs first hand, so at their invitation I joined the hearty fellows for a beer or two more. As guest, I felt that etiquette demanded that I pay for drinks, and soon the jovial gathering had swollen to fifteen or twenty locals." "There is a not wholly unpleasant numbing around the temples that is the result of several flagons of draught lager. With continued consumption, a further sensation is produced which can best be likened to having one's eyeballs removed, allowing someone to play table tennis with them, then wrapping them in sandpaper and returning them to their sockets. At one-thirty in the morning we finally emerged, swaying from Barney's into the chill night air. My newly-acquired friends led me towards what they promised would round off my evening. They bundled me into an establishment named "Millenium Kebab". The smell within was rather too pungent for my refined nose, and so I waited outside." "Presently my new chums returned, clutching armfuls of paper packages, one of which they handed to me. I copied them by tucking into it right there in the street, our faces lit by the glare of passing headlights and the glow of streetlights. The repast turned out to be quite delicious - this "kebab" consisted of fine bread filled with slices of spit-fired lamb-flavoured offal (which I could see rotating in the window of the establishment), with a julienne of salad, and a unique chilli jus. Every mouthful seemed to fill some aching chasm created by the volume of beer inside me. Although I was used to long, lingering evenings of seven or eight course meals, accompanied by several different wines and punctuated by a trou Normand - a light serving of sorbet to cleanse the palate between courses - this American tradition had its own peculiar charm. At a grand total of $173.76 (having paid for an evening's worth of alcoholic beverages and a large kebab for each of my new chums), my evening had not come cheap, but it certainly had been an authentic American experience." The MegaTron turns blank again as the video presentation fades to black, leaving some of the crowd chuckling - as descipable as his behaviour had been, they found it difficult to deny the humour of "An Englishman Abroad". All eyes turn once again to the real Thom LeStrange, standing in the ring. Smiling with an air of superciliousness, he raises the microphone to his lips once more. LeStrange: "Thank you, GCW aficionados. As always, you've been charming." With that, Thom hands the microphone back to the ring announcer, before exiting the ring and ascending the ramp once more. At the top of the ramp, he pauses, turning back to face the crowd. As has become customary he takes a long, mocking bow, his brown eyes sparkling irreverently, before walking through the curtain to the backstage area. ---- Olive Branch Rejected The cameras cut to the area immediately behind the curtain, quiet and calm in comparison with the rabid, roaring arena, which is separated only by flimsy black material. The curtain is moving, rippling elegantly, slowly returning to static. The cameras turn, affording the captive audience a glimpse of the intimate backstage area. We find ourselves in a large congregational area, where the labyrinthine corridors of the arena converge. GCW technicians, be they pyrotechnic experts, stage managers or merely assistants, all mill about in industrial fashion, like a colony of diligent GCW-employed ants. In the left of our picture, a corridor leads off. The suited figure of Thom LeStrange, the cause of the flapping curtain having returned from the ring, heads towards it. As he walks, he rotates his head, sighing quietly to himself as he feels the comforting crack of his neck. He stops briefly, and stretches his arms and back, almost as if yawning. Sighing again, this time audibly, he continues his sturdy pace, glad to be rid of the burden of keeping up appearances in the ring (as much as he enjoyed patronising and condescending the assembled masses, it was sometimes tedious, and always perfunctory). Bryan: Well, I thought we'd all seen enough of this pissant upstart, but clearly our production crew thinks otherwise. Yale: J.B., every week I'm astounded by how quickly you've taken a dislike to this guy. Bryan: Well every week I'm shocked how easily you can defend him! Yale: He's not all that bad. I mean sure he's a pompous ass, but he's all talk, J.B.! I'm pretty certain his only means of attack is bafflement by long words. He may have won his first singles match last week, but he's miles away from backing it up! Bryan: You may be right there, Dave. And the amount that this guy talks, even if he had the talent of a Vivica J. Valentine or a Lia Ambrosi he'd still be all talk and no walk. Yale: Alright, J.B., let's not get hyperbolic. A split second after Thom LeStrange disappears down the corrdor, the Scottish King of Cool Andy Murray appears at the other side of the screen, having hastily paced from the other side of the arena. As he strides into the congregational area, his head darts from side to side, scanning the hub of activity in a twisted pastiche of a 'Where's Waldo' book. He drags a backstage assistant to one side, placing a large hand on the significantly smaller man's shoulder and practically lifting him up and placing him down in front of him. Murray: Yo homeslice, you seen LeStrange? Backstage Worker: He just finished his segment, he just headed back to his locker room. Murray: Thanks rudeboy, I-... Jesus, this is getting damned familiar. Like déja vu or something! Andy Murray's face is briefly a mask of contemplation, considering the circumstance he found himself in and how similar it is to the previous week's, and wondering if the universe had aligned itself so that he was permenantly doomed to chase Thom LeStrange around GCW arenas. Shrugging this thought away, he dashes off down the same corridor that we have just seen LeStrange pace down. Yale: Hey J.B., reckon Murray's looking for a bit of retribution after LeStrange started on him again last week? Bryan: Could be, Dave. He's gotta be frustrated that security broke them up pretty swiftly last week, before he really had a chance to give that English ass a good shot. Yale: They were just doing their job. Bryan: I know Dave, but I sure wouldn't have minded seeing LeStrange get dealt a blow before it got broken up. Yale: These guys have barely arrived here, and already it's all kicking off between them. It's crazy! Bryan: I guess things just snowball. Murray turns a corner in the backstage area, and ahead of him we see LeStrange, striding on purposefully. Murray slows his jog to a brisk walk, and calls after LeStrange. Murray: Hey Thom! Expecting LeStrange to hear the call, turn and walk back towards him, Murray stops in his tracks. A second passes. LeStrange continues walking. Murray calls again, wondering how Thom hadn't heard him the first time - it was a fairly narrow, fairly empty corridor, and LeStrange hadn't been that far ahead. Again, LeStrange does not respond. The Scottish King of Cool breaks into a jog again, wondering why LeStrange was ignoring him, and chases the Englishman again down the corridor. He turns another corner to see LeStrange enter his locker room. Murray follows. Squeezing himself through the door as LeStrange was about to close it behind him (which is no mean feat, given the Scotsman's imposing stature), Murray finds himself face to face with Thom LeStrange at last. A curious look has come across LeStrange's face - a mixture of curiosity, superciliousness, apprehension, and distaste. LeStrange: Yes? Murray: Didn't you hear me in the hallway? LeStrange: Which time? Murray: Well... both?! LeStrange: As a matter of fact I did. You were bellowing rather loudly. A look of confusion flashes across Murray's face. Slightly galled by LeStrange's refusal to even acknowledge his call, he becomes briefly vexed, but shakes it off. Murray: Well why didn't you- ... Never mind. Anyways LeStrange, I figured I'm come to here to apologise. You know, white flag, olive branch, that stuff. LeStrange's only response is to raise an eyebrow. Taken aback somewhat, Murray continues. Murray: Maybe I made a big deal out of NC-17 and over-reacted last week, there's no need to be at each other's throats the whole time. We're all new here, we might as well watch each other's backs, right? Murray extends his hand for a handshake. Thom LeStrange casts his eyes down to survey the outstretched appendage. Moments pass as Thom considers it, staring at it intently. After what seems an age, he looks back up at Andy Murray, now rather regretting his peace-making decision. LeStrange: Andrew, I am about to have a shower. I wish to cleanse myself of this godforsaken place and these godforsaken people. When I step out, I do not wish to see you in here, else I shall strike you. Put your hand down, you look like a fool. With that, LeStrange extracts a towel from his bag, and steps into the en-suite shower-room of his locker room. The Scottish King of Cool stands statue still for a moment, mulling over what has just happened, his teeth grinding in his skull. After several moments of deliberation, he defiantly pulls over a metal folding chair, places it down hard enough to belie the calm exterior he is trying to maintain, and sits, arms folded. Five minutes pass, the sound of the shower coming through the door. Murray half expected LeStrange to start whistling "Rule Britannia". Eventually, he hears the shower turn off. Another moment later, LeStrange comes back through the door, in a flannel dressing gown, drying his hair. He looks up, and flinches as he sees Murray still in the room. LeStrange: I thought I told you to leave. Murray: Yeah, about that - I didn't want to. LeStrange: Murray, I'm a man of my word. LeStrange throws the towel to the floor, and steps menacingly towards Andy Murray, despite his size disadvantage. As he takes his last step, he pulls his fist back, balling it up tight, and throws it towards the Scotsman with all the velocity he can muster. Murray blocks it with an agile flick of his arm, flicking LeStrange's arm off to the side. Murray: Why do you have to be such an arse, LeStrange? There's no reason for us to keep bickering and ending up trying to know the back teeth out of each other. Why can't you just accept my handshake like a gentleman and not be such an arrogant little rat? LeStrange, incensed by this, rears back to attempt another punch, but again Murray blocks it, much to Thom's irritation. Murray: Thommy, you're really starting to piss me off. Don't make me do something I don't wanna do here... LeStrange: Who the hell do you think you are? The LeStrange family has over five-hundred years of aristocratic history, of which I am, undoubtedly, the PINNACLE. My name can be traced back to the Norman period. I went to Cambridge University. Do you know even who Sartre is?! And you, you filthy Scottish serf, you assume that a person like you can just saunter in and be best of chums with someone of my calibre? You expect me to come rushing to your aid at NC-17? Then you expect me to shake your hand and forget it ever happened after you tried to assault me?! Why would anyone befriend you after that, and on earth would I stand to gain from it?! Murray: You egocentrical... With that, Murray rears back, and powers forward with an enormous uppercut, his weighty right fist crashing into the fine jaw of the Englishman, sending him flying backwards and tumbling to the floor. Rubbing his knuckles, surveying the sprawled and motionless Thom LeStrange on the floor, a pathetic sight with his flannel dressing gown, Murray sighs and shakes his head, before leaving the room. ---- Kiljaden Kincaid vs. Mark Matthews vs. Depraved Kiljaden Kincaid vs. Mark Matthews vs. Depraved The beginning of "Ironside" by Quincy Jones begins to play, more noticeable to today's generation as the death song from the Kill Bill movies. The lights in the arena begin to blink red along with the siren going on and off. The red light sustains as the intro fades in silence, plummeting the arena into black. With the scream to start off "Deathbox" by Mnemic, white strobe lights hit the entrance and the crowd groans as a man walks out into the arena. A black towel lies over his head, covering his face as he looks down at the ground. His muscled shoulders heave up and down with every breath as he takes a moment to focus his anger before stomping down towards the ring. Andrews: Coming down the aisle from Crowheart, Wyoming weighing in at 280 pounds. "The Maverick" Kiljaden Kincaid! Storming down the aisle towards the ring, it's almost like Kiljaden doesn't recognize that the fans are even in attendance. One at a time the fans begin to shout things at Kincaid with no response at all. His actions are cold, icy, and emotionless. Subdued Violence. Walking up the stairs Kiljaden holds onto the rope as he crosses the apron and steps between the ropes into the ring. With no fanfare at all, Kiljaden paces back and forth a little before heading straight for his corner with the black towel still over his head, waiting for the bell to ring. Yale: This guy gives me a bad feeling. Bryan: It feels like the arena just got a few degrees colder when he came out. There is something about him that is just unnerving. The arena goes dead as the lights slowly fade out to a florescent lighting affect of blue and yellow madness. 'Magdalena' hits the PA and a man slowly steps out onto the ramp way, wearing his usual attire of blue and yellow tights with black boots, no shirt and a leather jacket with a pair of black aviators resting upon his face, casting a shadow through his eyes as he looks around with no real emotion what so ever. He walks with a swag of his own, caring the least bit for anyone besides himself before hitting the end of the ramp way and exploding with fury, throwing his arms into the sky and roaring with a sudden spark of anger. He rips off the sunglasses and jacket, throwing them to the ground and soon after sliding into the squared circle. Andrews: Introducing next, from St. Louis, Missouri weighing in at 275 pounds. 'Marvelous' Mark Matthews! Once inside, pyro blasts out from the ring posts and he drops to his knees, his arms rose with pride and a look of determination stitched to his ever so perfect face. After standing, he walks to his corner and simply waits, with nothing more on his mind than the simple thought of winning with any means necessary to do so. Yale: This guy gives me a bad feeling. Bryan: It feels like the arena just got a few degrees colder when he came out. There is something about him that is just unnerving. "In the Eyes of God" by Years of Fire kicks in over the announce system as the crowd turns to see who is coming out next. On another show loaded with newcomers, the fans give a mixed reaction with the man who is next. Supporting his long beard and military pants, Depraved steps out from behind the curtain, and marches down to the ring. He enters in the ring, ducking under the top rope and heading to the center of the ring. Andrews: Introducing next, from Kent, Ohio weighing in at 260 pounds. Depraved! Eerie he looks around with ring with seemingly no expression on his face. He turns to his right to see Matthews standing there hauntingly. To the left is Kiljaden Kincaid, who is also standing hauntingly. Depraved decides to join them in standing in a haunting manner. Yale: This guy gives me a bad feeling. Bryan: It feels like the arena just got a few degrees colder when he came out. There is something about him that is just unnerving. DING-DING! The three men stare at each other, waiting to see who will make a move first. Depraved finally jumps at Matthews, wailing on him with lefts and rights. Matthews fights him back though, blocking a left and answering with one of his own. He manages to get Depraved pack, kicking him in the gut and tossing him through the middle rope once he doubled over. Matthews doesn’t getting breathing time though, with Kincaid on him quickly. Mark gets forced into a corner, Kincaid drilling him with hard blows to the body. Mark kicks to try and get out, but Kincaid throws in a Forearm to pin him up against the padding. He reaching back with his free arm, looking to try and land a knockout punch, but he is ripped away by Depraved. Yale: Depraved is getting himself back into the mix after Matthews threw him out of the ring. Bryan: And it looks like he wants payback for that, even if it means going through Kincaid to do it. Grabbing Kincaid, Depraved spins him around and catches him with a Belly to Belly Suplex before Kincaid can even tell what is going on. The break gives Matthews time to come out of the corner and attack Depraved from behind. He nails him with a forearm before following it up with a Russian Leg Sweep. Standing up, he grabs the legs for a Boston Crab. Fighting at he can, Depraved is intent on not being turned over. But Mark continues to try and turn, only to have Depraved reach the ropes before he can fully lock the move in. Mark tries to yank him off the ropes and back to the center of the ring, but Kincaid levels him with a huge boot to the head. Rather than stay on Matthews, Kincaid turns his focus on the man that put him on the mat, Depraved. He picks him up off the mat but Depraved slings back an elbow and catches Kincaid in the bridge of the nose. Bryan: These young kids keep getting hot-headed and not trying to capitalize on the advantages given to them. Yale: Yeah, it certainly hasn’t worked out for any of them yet. They need to start wrestling smarter. Aggression only gets you so far. Shoving Kincaid into the ropes, Depraved again takes him off his feet, this time using a crushing Spinebuster. This time it is Depraved who is looking for the Boston Crab, holding onto the legs. Depraved actually does manage to get him turned over, but the success doesn’t last for very long. Coming from behind, Matthews crushes Depraved in the back of the head with a Whip Kick. He picks up Depraved, locking him into a headlock and using it to rush him into the turnbuckle. Throwing knees in on him, Mark tries to wear down Depraved before lifting him up onto the top turnbuckle.
