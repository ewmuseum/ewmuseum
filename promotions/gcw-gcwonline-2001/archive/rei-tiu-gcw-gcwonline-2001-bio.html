Top Rope Reverse Frankensteiner Tips for match writers [The Beginning/Start-up Moves] Rei usually starts off with an explosive bang. Springboard maneuvers and kicks and punches to wear them down. He has a head for interpreting one's use of stamina, so he uses this to his advantage early on. The moves are lightning quick. In physically awesome shape, he uses his stamina to its letter even in the beginning. Springboard Leg Lariat, Springboard Spinning Kick, Heart Kick, Solid punch combos. Just for an example. [Advantage/Having the upper hand] Technical wrestling takes his soul when he has the advantage. Suplexes, submission holds. He tends to focus on almost -every- body part, not just one. If he's going to hinder someone, he's absolutely going to hinder them. Swinging Neck Breaker, Hammerlock Shoulder Jawbreaker, Chinlock/Armlock Combination Submission, Indian Deathlock with Front Face Lock Submission. Another example. More moves listed above. [Being Smited/On The Defensive] Rei believes in the art of technical reversals. It can be something simple like a headlock chokehold, or something a bit more over-the-top like a Reverse Frankensteiner out of an opponent's power-bomb attempt. He's adept at it, often letting his opponent rush in and take the initiative, thereby setting them up for a big fall in the near future. He's also very limber being a lightweight and can maneuver himself very well. Hurricanrana, Swinging DDT, Reverse Frankensteiner, Reverse Rana. Other moves that could be used as reversals as well. Any opportunity for a reversal Rei can take, he does. [Pre-Finale/Ending Minutes] His endurance comes into play. By now, they're both tired, but Rei knows what'll happen if he slows down. He begins to take the high spots and take up with the more dangerous and awe-inspiring moves. Off the top rope Somersault Ax Kick w/ Twist, Corkscrew Plancha, Top Rope Hurracanrana, among others. Insane high-flying circus stunts. [Finishing Moves] Blackout: (DMG ***** Pin/Tap ****) This is Rei's primary finisher which is an off the top rope somersault leg drop with a twist. A mouthful. If connected, it usually does the trick. Dazzling with style, the big drawback to this move happens if Rei connects with the canvass instead of the person. Injury would be a possibility in some match styles. Top Rope Reverse Frankensteiner: (DMG *** Pin/Tap ***) Another dazzling move, it would most likely be used as a more effective set-up maneuver for the Blackout, or, as mentioned earlier, a truly effective counter maneuver. Rarely would he get a win with this, despite its damage potential. [Dirty Sneak Thief Tactics/Cheating and Retaliation] Simply? There are none. Rei's not a cheater and he'll go down into the record books as such. He respects himself and everyone enough in that light to not resort to cheating. In the form of retaliation, he pumps up his performance and adds a little intensity to his move base. He'll try to smother them with effective cruiserweight moves of style and grace. [Combo/Chain Moves] This is often where Rei shows his innovativeness. He'll string together a series of kick combos, followed by some high-spot maneuver. Or he'll take the technical way and string together suplexes and submission holds, immediately then switching back to his high-flying instincts. In other words, Rei varies his chain moves, keeping an opponent guessing. [Pros/Strengths] Vs. Smaller: He'll use technical flair and probably less high spots. Also, his better strength could come into play. Even though his speed is excellent, it probably wouldn't be much of a factor against the smaller. Vs. Equal: This is where he shines. He uses everything he knows; technical, martial arts, the high spots, and submissions. He doesn't hold back anything in this situation. Vs. Larger: Obviously his speed is the immediate advantage. Also his constant understanding of where he is in the ring. Usually uses technical moves to wear down the bigger guys as well as submissions. He'll use high spots, but only after they're worn down. [Cons/Weaknesses] Vs. Smaller: His speed would be his biggest drawback. If not up to standards, the smaller wrestlers could easily pull the rug out from under him here. Vs. Equal: Since he holds nothing back, if his instincts falter, he could wind up being injured. That's his biggest weakness in this category. Vs. Larger: Their strength can easily make him the next rag doll. And if he can't mount a comeback soon after an overpowered onslaught starts, he'll probably be watching the overhead lights for the rest of the night. Definitely a challenge for him. [Quick Summary] As a wrestler, Rei aims for two things in a match; The win and the style and grace with which he does it. An aerial performer by nature, he's not one to let a boring match stay that way. He also straddles the fence in taking the initiative and letting the opponent take the initiative, which depends on their size and his gameplan. He's had lots of martial arts training, so expect lots of kicks and throws. And he's decently strong for his size, which often goes unappreciated by the larger guys. All in all, Rei is a fighter and a performer. He will not go down easily and will often rise to the occasion more often than he should, which could lead to injury if he's not careful. He's also a respectful fighter and won't cheat under any circumstances (he would retaliate, but not by cheating.)
