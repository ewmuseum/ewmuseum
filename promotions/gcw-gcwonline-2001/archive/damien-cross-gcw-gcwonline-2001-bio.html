Damien Cross Biography Real Name [name withheld] Nicknames "The Latin Assassin", "The Last Son of Luchalibre", "The Icon of Violence" Hometown Cielo Azul, Mexico. Currently resides in Brooklyn, NY Birthday 11/25/1979 Height 6'4 Weight 235 Biography In a small city of Cielo Azul, Mexico, the youth were given two options; earn an education and hope to get hired by an American company that sought cheap labor, or become a small-level criminal under the eye of a corrupt police force. A third option, for those were fortunate, was to train as a luchadore and earn a ticket to fame. Damien Cross was fortunate enough to be taken under the tutelage of a local hero. Father Victor Morales was once known as El Nino Oro, 'The Golden Boy', and returned to his hometown to pay back the debt of his opportunity. At the age of 14, Damien Cross quickly established himself as a natural talent. In the Los Locos Lucha Libre ( a small offshoot of Father Morales' school), Damien Cross competed against grown men and small-time champions. By the time he was 17, Damien had grown faster, stronger, and more daring than his small-time company could contain. With a stream of accomplishments, and the support of an entire community, The Last Son of Lucha Libre boarded a plane to the other side of the world. Attracted by stories of burning tables and exploding turnbuckles, Damien ended up in Japan, among the most brutal of fighters. Companies like the Japanese Suicide League, the Tokyo Boxing Club, DragonFire Wrestling Company, and the Nippon Wrestling Association became Damien's new home. In matches that made legends of men like Mick Foley, Terry Funk, and RVD, Damien Cross grew as a wrestler and as a fighter. Dubbed The Latin Assassin, Cross became unmatched in his aerial abilities coupled with the innate brutality that the Asian crowds inspired in him. From underground garages to Billion-dollar arenas, Damien spent the next 4 years carving a place for himself among Asia's best. As his legacy grew, so did his thirst for more. Understanding that the next level of competition would bring him back to North American shores, he announced his retirement in front of a sold-out Tokyo Metrodome, and boarded the first flight back to the United States. Seventeen hours after winning The Japanese Suicide League Grand Title, The Latin Assassin landed on American soil, and returned to obscurity. He spent the next seven days sleeping in Casino bathrooms and wrestling in exhibition matches, all the while sending out promo tapes to any company that would watch them. Eventually, a small company gave him a try-out, and gave birth to Damien's American career. In the fifteen years since he first parted the ropes in a dusty Mexican gym, Damien Cross has performed in companies both large and small. He has been the best in his companies, and has floated around the midcard. In essence, the evolution of The Latin Assassin has been cyclic . And now, he stands to take the next step in completing the circle. From writing his own meal ticket to hoping for the long shot, Damien Cross has arrived. Other Feds SCCW/FUSE, PRIME, and other smaller feds that never too off. Other Fed Titles FUSE Universal Champion x2 Wrestling Style Hybridized Hardcore/Luchalibre/Technical Finisher Move Mayan Driver I, Mayan Driver II, Mayan Driver III Finisher Description Powerbomb lift (I) Pumphandle lift (II) Reverse Fireman carry (III) to piledriver Special Moves Dragonfire Kick- Spinning Sweet Chin Music, Other Moves Various luchadore/RVD/AJ Styles/Sabu-style creativity. Originally trained as a lucha libre purist, but later expanded his aresenal during his time in the Japanese Hardcore scene. Technical wrestling, shoot wrestling, lucha libre, and plain old-fashion teeth-kicking are all second nature to him. A lot of precise moves. Damien is a man of economy. He will do a high spot in order to capitalize on a position, not for cheap pop (ie: take a leap from the top of the ladder to execute a move on a vulnarable opponent, not find one and climb it). He is exceptionally skilled at controlling the pace of the match, but can be prone to bouts of frustration. When the match is going his way, he is almost mechanical in his efficiency, brutality, and methods. When things are not going his way, he can be desperate and careless unless he gets his head back into the game plan. When he loses, it is usually a result of him trying to fix what may not be broken. His lucha libre training has made him creative, explosive and confident. His Japanese training has made him economical, stoic, and very violent. And his years in the US and Canada has taught him how to mesh what he knows with submission wrestling, technical skill and brass-knuckles pragmatism. Entrance: When he enters the ring, he does so in pitch black with a red background behind the entryway. As he steps out onto the ramp, Red and White sparks rain down over the entryway (Goldberg-ish). Then, the lights raise, and he travels down the ramp. Damien, like I said above, is a man of economy. While adrenaline usually sends men bouncing from the rafters, Damien channels it into pure focus. He will slap a few hands when he is confident, and will climb the ropes and greet the crowd. When he feels like he is walking into a real challenge, he is more focused, usually just greeting the fans with a distracted wave or fist pump. He usually steps up onto the apron in a single step, then springs over the top rope. Or, when the pressure is really on, he'll step throuugh the middle ropes and test the ropes absently. In a lot of ways, he's feast or famine. Entrance Music "Bored"- Deftones
