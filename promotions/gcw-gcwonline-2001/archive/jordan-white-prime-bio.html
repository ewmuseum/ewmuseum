Jordan White Biography Real Name [name withheld] Nicknames The Former World Champion Hometown Charlotte, NC Birthday Jan. 16th, 1984 Height 6'2 Weight 235 Fed Titles Current World Champ, former US champ, former TV champ Fed Awards It's not an award, but he beat Rich Rollins in an Ironman match. Rich Rollins. Biography Frank Black was an independent wrestler/cokehead beyond his means whose girlfriend had just dumped him. It was not particularly surprising to anyone except Frank. Luckily enough, he had the right last name and the Sage Black formerly known as Jordan White hired him as a replacement as part of a deal he'd worked out with Steven Caldera. Shortly afterwards, Jordan joined Caldera's inner circle as a well pushed member of the Establishment; an early boon for his fledgling career. He earned a world title shot against Lia Ambrosi and subsequently lost; instead of taking the inevitable removal from the Establishment, he was proactive and left the group on Worldwide the next night. In the time period following, his career languished as he found a seeming compatriot in Jay Terror; this, of course, turned all sorts of around as Jay abandoned their psuedofriendship in the form of joining the Establishment, smashing Jordan around with weapons, and beating him at the culmination of a round robin tournament for a World Title Shot. All in one smooth motion. Vivica J. Valentine, then champion, helped to escalate the situation as she granted Jordan White a title match prior to giving Jay Terror the one he had earned; the subsequent match was a disqualification and saw an incensed Terror interfering to get a measure of revenge on White. White was, even at the time, vocally opposed to the match. The three feuded for a month, momentum shifting from week to week, and culminating in a three-way match for the world title at Warpath; VJV retained at the end of the brutal evening. Jay Terror beat Jordan's ass after the match. Jordan laid there, bleeding. And Terror won the belt at the next Worldwide in an impromptu match; White made his intentions to that shiny gold thing clear and threw himself headlong into a gauntlet of Jay's devising, taking down Tyson DeBough and Octavian with ferocity before being pitted against Murray. The match ended in a disqualification and, in a fit of rage, Jay Terror agreed to defend his belt against Jordan at Dangerous Games. At the final Worldwide before the event, Jordan White convinced Steven Caldera to make the defense an ACWF match and lost to GBJ because of some mysterious man in black. He wasn't derailed by the loss, surviving the ACWF match and becoming the new World Champion one year after his original failure to achieve. During his reign, he defended the belt twice at Worldwides, against Digital Mortality and Chris Bagwell, before, in the tradition of NC-17, being dethroned. In a brutal match with LeStatt Knight, Jordan went the distance only to find out that maybe he was wrong, after all, about monsters not existing. Strengths Athletic to a nearly inhuman level and extremely explosive, with visibly better pacing from week to week. Agile for his size, and capable of exceptional violence. Manager Sage Black Weaknesses He tends to wear himself ragged before he stops to take a break, so he's generally operating at something less than 100%. He's not that great at anything, really, and has made several bad splitsecond decisions over the course of his career. He Wrestling Style All-arounder, with a growing mat game. Strategy Big people: Lots of fast strikes to control the pace, and lots of submissions, since he's only really worth a damn at taking out people's legs. He's not going to use a sit-out powerbomb against Bryan Mayhem on Worldwide. Average size: If trying to brawl doesn't do the trick, it's a cycling of the high-impact grapple moves, submissions, and a more technical style of striking until something starts to work out for him. Cruisers/midgets: He wrestles like Vader. He beats them, he throws them around, he uses a token submission move or two if the chance or need arise. -He's been claimed as something of a natural, which I guess just sorta got attributed to him over the way, so there's that. -He's not a fan of opening with headlocks, tests of strengths, or the grapple to feel out your opponent--he'll try to begin most matches with a flurry, hoping to set them up for an improving transitional game as he moves into the more technical moves. -If he can find a way to make a move drop someone directly on their head, he will do it. He's found it's usually quicker to knock someone out for ten seconds than to wear them down. -He can take a beating and will, waiting for an opening. The tougher the fight, the longer the wait. -He tries to pace himself around the middle of a match, saving stamina for the all-important last minute surge. -He enjoys kicking people in the face, and will take any opportunity to do so. I'm thinking those Japanese cats like Kawada, Hashimoto. And Chono, with the Yakuza Kick. -The Yakuza Kick and the brainbuster can be finishers just as easily as the superkick. It just takes the right level of building them up, with a good match beforehand and a few feinted attempts. -That pump-leg drop is how he shows off. It probably hurts no more than any other leg drop, but he enjoys doing it. -That's a bad thing for him, a decent percentage of the time. Finisher Move 314.47 Finisher Description A superkick, or any imagineable variation thereof; including towards injured body parts or whatever else sounds good. And he can kick with either foot, too! Other Moves Big moves. The things you get clips of in intros. The Thing Kimbusa Did - aka- The Yakuza Kick - Recently renamed, but most likely rarely acknowledged. He runs. He kicks the fuck out of their face. They can be standing, sitting, jumping, crawling; they can be dancing, if the opportunity ever shows up. Sometimes, for sport, he kicks the back of their head. It's a much crueler version, if you wanna be serious about it, and probably wouldn't come up very much. A sheer-drop, nasty looking brainbuster. He's not the kind of man that does a vertical suplex--dropping down is easier than falling backwards and does a hell of a lot more damage. And if the superplex moment comes up? Avalanche brainbuster. Electric Relaxation - Pump leg drop--jumps off with the leg drop, tucks his knees to his chest in midair, then drops back into the leg drop on impact. It's mainly come up when he thinks he already has the victory, and finds that just pinning them doesn't seem exciting enough. The DDT Half boston crab, the tenacious kind where you might lock it on three times in a row after stopping to stomp at the knee. And sometimes, with a bit of extra rotation, it can float over into the Caldera Lock. The Caldera Lock - It's an STF. It takes advantage of his penchant for going after knees, skulls, and necks. It makes some sort of vague, nebulous statement. It comes up when the half boston just isn't enough. He still calls it the Caldera Lock. Expressway to Yr. Skull - He reverses vertical suplexes, when he can, by spinning them into a Diamond Cutter/RKO/I don't know what you kids call it these days. Whatever - Vertical suplex lift, dropped into a sitout piledriver. For those pesky problems that brainbusters just won't solve. The rest Headbutts Spinning forearm/elbow Clubbing forearms to neck and back. And shoulders. And faces. Lots of forearms, really Kick to the gut Running kick to the gut Running kick. To the knee. Leaping shoulder smash Snapmare followed by dropkick to the shoulders/back of the head. He kicks them in the face. Vertical suplex lift into body slam Fisherman's suplex Delayed release german suplex German superplex Double underhook backbreaker Double underhook powerbomb--lifting the opponent for another sitout powerbomb at the end if they're small enough Double underhook piledriver that kinda looks like a powerbomb for a second in there One shoulder powerbomb/Awesome Bomb. Top rope leap into DDT on standing opponent Missile dropkick Diving headbutt Flapjack to DDT Spinning spinebuster Figure-Four All those classic knee twists and knee-focused elbow and boot meleeing. Entrance Music Big Bang by Aesop Rock Ring Entrances The arena lights drop from intense to mellow, casting the entry way in lazily shifting beams of blue and purple that shift to the rhythm of the building bass. He steps through the curtains as the drums kick, as the vocals come in and the smoke rolls across his boots and he, Jordan White or Frank Black or does it even matter anymore?, he's smiling wider than he's every smiled as the audience rises to its feet as one. They roar and he laughs, shaking his head before looking up to them. He lifts a fist lazily into the air and he feeds himself off of their response. Chants ripple and run and he lets them; he tucks his head and he nods it to the beat as he makes his way down the ramp, smacking a few hands along the line before climbing the ring steps, stepping through the ropes.
