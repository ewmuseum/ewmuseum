WorldWide 152 Grant Park — Chicago, Illinois ---- To Finish What You Start Black. And then, light. The setting sun paintbrushes the horizon a milky orange, visible only by looking up and between the spaces of high standing architecture. Production shifts to another camera. All of Grant Park is in a frenzy, a wall of perpetual noise so powerful that it doesn't evaporate into the sky. The makeshift stadium lights pour down across the ring. Terrence Kingsley sits hunched forward in a steel chair in the middle of the ring, his hand limply grasping a microphone and his eyes fixed toward the canvas. A cigarette hangs from his lips, smoke oozing from his nostrils. He's already dressed for the main event, from black tights with multi-hued skulls on fire, to his knee-length snake skin boots, to his hallmark sun-beaten leather jacket with rusted nails lining the seams of his shoulders and arms. There's one difference; a necklace on his neck, a brass cross at the end. Opening his free hand, he slowly rolls the cherry out across his palm. The crowd can barely hear his whisper. Kingsley: I'm scared. Chicago is supposed to be the crowd that marches to the beat of its own drum. That goes against the norms of professional wrestling to root for the opposite of the corporate choice. And, sure, there are plenty in Grant Park wearing Pit Viper branded merchandise who willingly throw support behind him, just as there are in most cities. But they are mere pockets to a louder roar for him to just go away. He stops, for a moment, looking up from the canvas at the vast expanse of people congregating on these grounds like they'd done years before for Barack Obama. One thing about a Chicago crowd, though, is their ability to find weakness and seize upon it. The MegaTron shows pieces of welded chain link behind the curtain.
