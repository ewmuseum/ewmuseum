The History of GCW The history of Global Championship wrestling is deep and rich indeed, and is inextricably linked to the history of its founder. Though GCW’s President Steven Caldera is fond of reminding us of it, it cannot be denied that his history and GCW’s are one in the same, and therefore we must examine his roots to find GCW’s. The story of GCW goes back to the year 2000. It was then that a young Steven Caldera broke into wrestling, finding himself in a small promotion called the American Championship Wrestling Federation. The ACWF was run by a man called Mr. Danger, who quickly saw promise in Caldera. Within one month of breaking into the business, Caldera won his first World Championship belt. It was during this time that Caldera met a young Rock impersonator, who would later become better known as Octavian, linked up with future henchman Khan, met Mike Hardy and wrestled with lifelong friend The Jhub. Mr. Danger himself would become a sometime road agent for Caldera’s future ventures. However, the ACWF shut down shortly thereafter and Caldera found himself unemployed. Then, through a series of odd circumstances, Caldera found himself in contact with the owner of a larger company, the CWU. The owner, Nathan K-------, offered Caldera a wrestling position, but Caldera opted for a spot backstage. Commissioner Caldera was born, and Caldera’s foray into management had begun. K------- departed a few months after Caldera arrived, and Caldera began a rapid rise to power, slowly assuming total control of the company. Despite the suspicious nature of his ascent, Caldera became wildly popular due to quickly rising ratings and the overall success of the company. The CWU thrived for several months until K------- returned and a struggle for power began. Ultimately Caldera stepped down and the CWU closed its doors, once again leaving Caldera unemployed, but setting the stage for GCW to be born. It was in the Cyber Wrestling Union that Caldera first came into contact with CWU mainstays Toad, John Reed, “Blockbuster” Kurt Richards, and future GCW Hall of Famer Rich “The Renegade” Rollins. Caldera spent the next several weeks in hiding, regrouping after being forced out of the company and in quiet negotiations with his many contacts. When Caldera re-emerged, he had established a new company: Global Championship Wrestling. He invested every time he had in the new promotion and signed the majority of the former CWU roster to GCW contracts, forming the foundation for the company. GCW picked right up where the CWU had left off, and quickly surpassed the success of its predecessor due to Caldera’s limitless vision. GCW’s first incarnation ran from February 2001 to July 2002, becoming known as one of the world’s premier wrestling organizations during this time. However, Caldera had formed his parent company, Caldera Enterprises, Incorporated, and his interest in GCW waned. With the established talent of GCW also in need of a break, the company closed its doors and would remain dormant for quite some time. Caldera did attempt to resurrect the company under a new name, X:Wrestling, but the company lasted only five shows. Toxic won the X:Wrestling World Title against Twizzy, but the X:Wrestling Championship history has been merged with the GCW World Title and the two companies are now indistinguishable from one another. GCW remained in stasis for some time. The talent scattered and Caldera began working for a variety of large promotions around the country. During this time a number of the world’s premier wrestlers began an exodus from their home companies, and Caldera was approached to re-open GCW to provide a home for these exiled stars. GCW very nearly re-opened in 2003 with this new incredible list of talent, but the situation calmed down and the relaunch was nixed. After another two years of quiet, the core talent of GCW came together to reopen the company in February 2005. GCW has been running ever since. President Caldera himself has alluded to a possible retirement some time in 2008, but has assured the wrestling world that Global Championship Wrestling has no end in sight. Trivia Imagery
