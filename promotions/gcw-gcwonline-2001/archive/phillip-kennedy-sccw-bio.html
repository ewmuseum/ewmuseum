Phillip Kennedy Biography Real Name Phillip Benjamin Kennedy Nicknames The Las Vegas Lariat, The Sin City Savior, The Big Stack Hometown Las Vegas, NV Birthday July 7, 1977 Height 6'3" Weight 249 Fed Titles None. Fed Awards None. Biography A former professional poker player weaned on the poker boom of the early Aughts, Phillip Kennedy made the transition to the ring during a bad stretch of that career when he needed more tournament entry fees. Taking on a job as a bouncer in a local strip club, he also paid a local to teach him to wrestle. Once deemed ready for the ring, local promotion Sin City Championship Wrestling took a chance on the hometown boy, signing him to a contract that started what could only be described as a whirlwind career for the thirtysomething newcomer to wrestling. After a chance meeting with Kathryn Shaw on his second ever show for the company, Kennedy found himself as the junior member of the dominant Dead Man's Hand stable, alongside a rogues' gallery of Amy "Aimz" Campbell, Alexandra Pierce, Mayhem, and Jadian Bridden. The DMH brought Phillip great success in the form of the company's Gateway Championship and a reign with the Strength in Numbers Championships. Even as he achieved greater success, though, he began to feel great resentment towards Amy Campbell, SCCW's Universal Champion, who he felt was being protected and favored by Alexandra Pierce. Over the course of the company's No Limit Tournament, Kennedy defeated a series of SCCW's best to arrive in the finals against Aimz, before which he promptly turned on the Dead Man's Hand to strike out on his own. After a vicious, personal war with Campbell for the SCCW Universal Championship, an errant panel of steel cage contributed to Kennedy's end courtesy of Amy's Bitchkiller head punt. While Kennedy continued to battle (and suffer several concussions) at the hands of the Hand after that, the loss to Campbell essentially took away his will to continue on. Several comeback attempts fizzled, culminating in the death of Sin City Championship Wrestling. Returning to his native Las Vegas, Phillip took a part-time job tending bar in a major hotel-casino while playing occasional poker. Kennedy fell into the routine of his new life for a year and a half, until a chance meeting with former love interest and manager Kathryn Shaw led to a change of plans. Now returned to the ring, and with an eighteen month plus break allowing his health to get into order, Kennedy is ready to pick up where he left off -- ironically enough, in the same promotion that many of his fellow Sin City expatriates also arrived in. Other Feds SCCW, cameo appearance in PRIME Other Fed Titles SCCW Gateway Champion (longest reigning), SCCW Strength in Numbers Champion (with Katsidy & Reginald V. Lovecraft) Other Fed Awards Member of the SCCW/Dead Man's Hand team that beat PRIME. Yeah. Strengths Brawling. Confidence. Very, very emotional. Weaknesses History of concussions. (Relative) inexperience. Very, very emotional. Wrestling Style Texas-influenced sports entertainment brawling Strategy Phillip Kennedy doesn't want a technical wrestling match. He wants a brawl. He wants to kick you with his cowboy boots, punch you in the face, and lariat you until you die from it. He's only been a professional for around three years, an active competitor for about half of that due to various injuries. He has a relatively basic moveset, but he has it drilled well. He focuses on the neck and back to set up his various finishers, particularly The Nuts. Wrestling him is a good way to beat him, but he will do his damnedest to brawl you all over the arena if possible. Finisher Move The Nuts Finisher Description Running left-armed lariat, thrown to the back of the head/neck Finisher Setup Move No defined setup. Setup Description Ditto. Special Moves Las Vegas Lariat (charging lariat to the chest), Flop, Turn, & River (Triple rolling gutwrench suplex), Viva Las Vegas (Leg-cradle Death Valley Driver), Check-Raise (Undertaker-style no-hands dive over the top rope) Other Moves BASIC MOVES: - Brawling (generally big looping roundhouse rights and left, kneelifts to the gut, stomping on downed opponents and kicks with his cowboy boots. This makes up the vast majority of Kennedy's offense) - Liberal use of throwing opponents into the ringposts, stairs, and guardrail outside, as well as fighting on the floor in general. - Basic wrestling moves (armdrags, hiptosses, and the like, the sort of things that any wrestler should be able to do after graduating from training) - Basic submissions/'restholds' (rear chinlock, front facelock, surfboard stretch, Boston crab, generic arm and leg locks) - Powerbomb - Gutwrench suplex - Stalling vertical suplex - DDT - Reverse Neckbreaker - Calf Branding - Texas Clawhold - Snake Eyes on the top turnbuckle - Snap powerslam off an Irish whip - Diving shoulderblock off the top rope SIGNATURE MOVES: ALTERNATE FINISHERS: The Sinker - Sliding D Elbow Strike. Done in tribute to the deceased Lauren "Savant" Fox, Phillip's mentor in SCCW and the Dead Man's Hand. A rarer alternate finisher, Kennedy will use it against persistent opponents. For example, if an opponent kicks out of The Nuts, but is still groggy, he will load up the Sinker to finish them off before they can fully recover. Drawing Dead - Texas Cloverleaf. Phillip's primary submission finisher. He likes to use it primarily on smaller opponents, cruiserweights in particular, where his size means he can crank down and prevent them from reaching the ropes. FINISHER: The Nuts - Enzuilariat, or a lariat to the back of the head/neck. Phillip's primary finisher, and he can execute it on practically anyone. Most of his offense works towards this move as the killblow. The fact that he throws it to the back of the head means it's a blindside shot, and thus much more difficult to kick out of than a basic lariat (many of which Phillip also throws) SUPER FINISHER: Acecracker Lariat - Nigel McGuinness-style "rebound" lariat, using the second rope as a fulcrum, thrown to the jaw. It's the biggest weapon in the Phillip Kennedy quiver, and it comes out rarely. When it does, though, it spells the end. Entrance Music "Save a Horse (Ride a Cowboy)" by Big & Rich
