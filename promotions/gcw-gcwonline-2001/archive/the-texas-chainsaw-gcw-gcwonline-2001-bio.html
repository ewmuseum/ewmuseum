The Texas Chainsaw Biography Real Name Steve Jackson Nicknames Mudhole Jack, Texas Hold'em, Texas Choke'em, The Texas Chokehold, The Texas Buzzsaw, Texas Rusty, The Pine Forest Pick Axe, The Poison from Pine Forest, Savage Steve Jackson, The Savage, The Texas Savage, The Sly, The Bastard, Backstab Jack Hometown Pine Forest, Texas Birthday June 12th, 1963 Height 6'1' Weight 253 Fed Titles None Fed Awards None Biography Story: 'The Texas Chainsaw' Steve Jackson is an older guy. He's worked all across the heartland, mainly in small, backwoods towns, the kind that smell perpetually of cow shit, where the black dust stains everything that moves, where the only buildings are houses and a bar, and where you couldn't find a decent meal if you tried. A hundred miles from the nearest McDonalds, and a thousand from the nearest Starbucks. He's not famous in any big way, except in Texas, where he's got a reputation, even in the big cities. Why? He's hard working. He doesn't mind putting other people over. Behind the scenes he's got creativity. And behind the scenes he's a very nice guy. So why is he known and respected in gin joints and dirty gyms all over the South? Because of his personality: he plays his gimmick to the hilt. He's rarely been a face. He wouldn't know how. 'The Texas Chainsaw' is one of the slyest, most vicious, and downright meanest damned heels anyone has ever seen. He acts mean, he talks mean, and he fights mean. Ironic that he's a very nice guy, in reality, but he'd never let you know it. In the backwoods of Texas, you don't drop a gimmick. If a fan comes up to you behind the scenes to get an autograph, he'd sooner spit in their face than sign their piece of paper. Word has it that he got his ass stomped by three bikies from the local Gypsy Joker's crew, because they offered him a beer after a match and he threw it in their faces. Chainsaw has been wrestling since he was ten: his old man swept rings for some small time promotion, and Steve managed to get a spot on the undercards. That was in the early '70s, when the big men of wrestling ruled the big towns, and everyone else tried to claw their way up the ladders. Guys like Joltin' Joe Blanchard, Fritz von Erich, and Dory Funk Sr, ruled the rings and captured peoples hearts. One story has it that Steve Jackson got his name after he chased an opponent around the ring, brandishing a chainsaw, in a drunken haze, screaming and shouting and cursing a blue streak. Funny thing was, it was before the show had even started, and there were only four or five people in the crowd. But he worked 'em anyway. Another one says he got his name from years working at the Bear creek lumber yards, where he put in twelve hours a day and still wrestled another three in the night, with enough time left to sink a bottle of Jack and travel to the next town. Another story says he challenged the then Southwest Styles champion Jack 'Golden Boy' Maziavito to a 'last man standing' match: apparently he got his fight, and the three count fall as well. But after the ref disqualified him on a minor technicality, Jackson went backstage, came back, snatched the belt from Maziavito's grasp, and cut it into two pieces with the help of a chainsaw. It's hard to say if any of these stories are true, or if they're all fabrications, the result of hazy memories, barroom chatter, too much beer and whiskey. They didn't have the Internet in those days, and TV was mostly news and game shows. But regardless, fans all over Texas and the Midwest still mention Jackson's name, childhood memories from a day at the wrestling show, where they remember that mean, vicious bastard, one of the best damned heels there ever was. So if, in wrestling's current incarnation, the big name federations like the WWE or GCW represent wrestling's brain, the electric core, then Jackson represents it's heart, it's soul, it's history. Wrestling wasn't always huge, and wrestlers didn't always get movie deals and million dollar contracts. Some of them worked and scrabbled and fought in the dirt and the dust, some of them with a smile, and others with a sneer. Jackson is old and hard, battered and bloody, and he won't be getting a movie deal anytime soon. But he comes from the not-so-hallowed halls of Wrestling, the roots. He comes from a time and place where kids didn't go to the movies; they went to watch the wrestling. Where 'smarks' and pop music had nothing to do with wrestling. Where the good guys were always good guys, even if they were assholes backstage, and the heels always heels and the fans didn't want to know any better. He might get a shot at GCW fame, and he might not. If he does, it'll probably be the most exposed that he's ever been. But damn sure he won't go soft. He's taken chairs and nails and shattered glasses to the head, and always come back for more. He's tough, in that way. He's a bit crazy, the result of two packets a day and a bottle on top of that. He's got heart, but his gimmick is what makes him: he's got great personality, and he's not a bad fighter. Old, sure, compared to the new generation: but his character is vicious and mean and nasty, sly and bitter and twisted, a true Texas bastard. And that's the way he likes it. General Appearance: He's a weathered sort of guy; you can see it in his face and eyes. He's got a few scars, and a few tattoos on his arms. He's of moderate build, not pumped and oiled but hard in the way that labourers are hard. He's got stringy dark brown hair that goes to his shoulders, and piercing blue eyes. He usually wears sunglasses, the old-style aviators. In the early days he wore red-and-yellow trunks with boots and pads to match. But now it's usually just jeans, a bit worn and tattered at the knees, and a pair of cowboy boots. No shirt or pads or anything too fancy. His character's been that way for a while. He keeps a dark beard and moustache, and they're trimmed but not too neat. A bit grey in places, but it's not too noticeable. Additional Information: Most of this has already been covered in the 'Story' section. Basically he's an 'old school' wrestler trying it out in the new school. He's wrestled in whatever places would pay him and whatever styles would pay best. Basically he's a brawler. He can dish it out OK, but he's aging a bit, he doesn't pack the same kind of punch that he used to. But he can take it, for sure, still as well as he used to. As for hardcore matches, he's obviously never gone into anything too fancy, but chairs and tables and sharp objects have all been staples of his in that regard. He has a deep Southern, Texas-drawl, a harsh, raspy sort of voice. He smokes, and drinks. He plays his heel character to the hilt, influenced by guys like Jake 'the Snake' Roberts, another great heel of the old days. He probably wouldn't be the type of guy who would go for the championship belt, but he's a good undercard or midcard wrestler, and a good heel, the kind of guy who will polarise the crowd: but then again, maybe they'll end up liking him. Fans are fickle and it's impossible to tell which way the crowd will go. Other Feds Texas Titan Wrestling (TTW), Jim Blackburn's Wrestling Tours, Pine County Wrestling, All Star Country Wrestling (ASCW), Riverside Wrestlin', Old Mike's Monthly Wrestling Tours, Southern Hardcore Galore (SHG), Deep South Wrestling (DSW), Mis Other Fed Awards Has held everything from the heavyweight belt, the hardcore belt, the middleweight belt, the tag-belt, and everything in-between. Whatever their equivalents are in whatever a particular show or 'federation' is called, in Texas and the Deep South, he has held it. Considered a veteran, but not a champion, most of the titles he's held come from smaller backwoods tours, but he has captured a couple of bigger titles in bigger federations like Southwest Styles. Wrestling Style Brawler/Hardcore/All-Round Finisher Move Texas T Finisher Description This is a pump handle slam. Basically the wrestler grapples the opponent from behind, then puts him in a pump handle stretch, lifts him up onto his shoulders, and slams him down onto the ring. The opponent's back would hit the ring. Finisher Setup Move Amarillo Clothesline Setup Description Basically a strong lariat: Wrestler crouches, then rises up and lunges forward, using a clothesline attack to knock the opponent down Other Moves Moves consist of those that constitute a typical brawler's list: that is to say that he's not a particularly technical wrestler, not well versed in submission holds and some complex throws, but is experienced in a wide variety of attack moves and more 'typical' throws. He has been wrestling for a long time, so knows and sometimes utilises many different moves. However, his repotoire in a normal match consists of fairly simply attack moves and throws, along with his 'trademarks' and finisher. ATTACK MOVES These are used to set up for a throw or submission hold. However, as mentioned earlier, he is not a technically proficient wrestler, and therefore attack moves and throws constitute the majority of moves used in his matches. He will not use all of these in a match, possibly not even the majority of them.
