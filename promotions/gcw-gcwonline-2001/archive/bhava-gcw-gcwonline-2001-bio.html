Bhava Biography Real Name Gerard Roy Nicknames None to speak of. (Any nicknames he gains will be bestowed upon him by others and not Bhava approved) Hometown Chiba, Japan Birthday September 12. Height 5'9 Weight 190 lbs. Fed Titles none Fed Awards GCW's first Zen Buddhist. It's something, at least. Biography The man now calling himself Bhava is perhaps better known to fans on the maritime Canadian and Japanese indy circuits as Gerard Roy, a young up and coming talent with several years of experience who went by the moniker of the Canadian Phoenix. He got his big break in late 2009, receiving a contract to compete in Japan for the small WORLD WARRIORS promotion. He moved to the company's home base of Chiba, Japan after his first tour to compete full time for WW and a variety of other smaller independent companies. In May, he was fortunate enough to win an opportunity to go on a learning excursion to America after beating his key rival in a tough bout. However, three days before he was set to fly over, tragedy struck in the form of an accident in training, with Roy cracking his head on the padded concrete outside the ring on an attempted tope con hilo. The accident left Roy with no permanent physical damage, but retrograde amnesia. For the next few months, Roy's friends on both sides of the ocean helped him piece together his life once again. There is still much Roy does not remember and much he learns anew every day. Thankfully, however, most of his physical talents were unaffected, and soon after, he was able to return to the ring. Given a clean bill of physical health by doctors, Roy was able to take the opportunity he had earned in WORLD WARRIORS, which turned out to be a deal with GCW. In order to make himself more appealing to American fans, he crafted the Bhava persona, borrowing elements from Japanese greats throughout the years. Bhava itself is the rough Sanskrit equivalent of "rebirth" or "reincarnation." Undoubtedly, Roy sees GCW as the rebirth of his career after it was nearly taken away from him. The devil, though, is in the details. PERSONA/CHARACTER: During his time in Japan, Bhava became a practicioner of Zen Buddhism. This often shows through in his on-camera persona as well. Due to Zen's emphasis on letting loose of logical ways of thinking to understand something's true nature, he will often answer questions in what may appear to be nonsensical ways. This philosophy bleeds over into his in ring style as well. His tactics lack the coherent logic (working a body part, trying to set up a submission, etc) that professional wrestling is generally guided by. As such, in spite of having a "conventional" finishing maneuver, he considers it a possibility rather than an end-all, be-all, and will often try to finish with side headlocks, and other odd moves. (He is fond of saying that "something can only end when it has not yet begun.") Other Feds WORLD WARRIORS, Halifax Wrestling Alliance, CHIBA Fight League Other Fed Titles CHIBA Fight League Jr. Heavyweight Champion Other Fed Awards Voted Most Popular Wrestler in the HWA for the year 2009. Strengths Bizarre in-ring tactics, hard-hitting elbow and knee strikes, speed and agility Weaknesses Bizarre in-ring tactics, difficulty with faster opponents, inexperience with the American style Wrestling Style Zen-influenced Japanese cruiserweight Strategy Bhava likes to mix "stereotypical" Japanese moves (knife-edge chops, evil Asian mist, hard kicks), with more modern cruiserweight tactics and an arsenal of Muay Thai knees and elbows to create his own style. Generally, he will use these moves to work towards his "Moment of Zen" flying Muay Thai knee strike, which obviously works best with a disoriented opponent. Once people know to fear the move, he will begin feinting with it, to try and get in opponent's heads. Even with his finisher, Bhava is more than willing to roll up opponents, or win in unusual ways. Throwing opponents off guard is Bhava's MO. If he's not trying to take them down with hard elbows and knees, he's coming off the top rope, or meditating at random times, or using an opponent's ring gear against them. Facing Bhava is an unsettling experience for the most veteran of wrestlers because of his often bizarre behavior because there is a method to his madness. Finisher Move A Moment of Zen Finisher Description Charging, leaping Muay Thai knee strike to the face. Bhava will try to lock the clinch on the opponent mid-air to guide their head into the knee. Finisher Setup Move Anything that knocks the opponent down, and forces them to stand back up. WW Entrance All is quiet, and then, the driving sounds of "Sign" by Susumu Hirasawa herald the arrival of the warrior known only as Bhava. The song is, perhaps, a bit loud and driving for one's perception of a Zen Buddhist, but Bhava himself would be the first to tell any who protest that only through defying Earthly expectations can one truly ever know anything. Of course, he'd say it a lot more elaborately and confusingly than that. Andrews: Making his way to the ring, from Chiba, Japan, weighing in at 190 pounds. . . BHAVA! Bryan: Yale: He emerges from behind the curtain, wearing long, baggy red pants with white streaks of lightning tearing down the legs. His torso is covered with a traditional Japanese ring jacket with "WORLD WARRIORS" written in two lines over the breast, and his name emblazoned on the back over the merged symbols of a Canadian maple leaf and a Japanese rising sun. Of course, perhaps the most noticeable thing about Bhava is the white facepaint that he wears. Over the white is written the word "om," in Devanagari script. He doesn't interact with the crowd at all on his way down the ramp, sticking dead center as the fans gawk. Save for a few red strobe lights panning the arena and his driving Japanese rock theme, the entrance is almost...boring. His focus is almost laserlike on the ring in front of him, and he slides in under the bottom rope deftly. Bryan: Yale: Once in the ring, Bhava makes his way over to his corner. He removes his ring jacket, handing it carefully to one of the attendants at ringside. With that taken care of, wrestling's only Zen Buddhist sits down in his corner, and assumes the Lotus position, meditating on the task at hand. Setup Description Does that really need further description? Special Moves Fisherman's DDT, top rope double foot stomp, straitjacket choke (the gokurakugatame) Other Moves Outside of his "signature" and finishing moves, Bhava's moves constantly change. He dislikes relying on even his best tactics, so expect to see him changing things up constantly. He likes to use "stereotypical" Japanese offense in the ring, such as martial arts kicks, chops to the throat and chest, and evil Asian mist, which he spews in any of a number of odd colors (blue, pink, and white are common). Think a combination of Yoshihiro Tajiri with some of the more well rounded Japanese cruiserweights (Jushin Lyger, Koji Kanemoto) and you have Bhava's general sort of movepool. Bhava also is excellent at using the ring, ringside area, and items such as clothing to his advantage in the ring, often bending the rules with such things while not outright breaking them. Here are some moves he likes to use, with the caveat that his moveset does, as mentioned above, like to pull things in and out on a regular basis. Running palm strike to the face, Dragon suplex, repeated knees from a Muay Thai clinch, somersault double legdrop to the abdomen, kneel-down powerbomb, top rope spinning heel kick, straitjacket DDT.
