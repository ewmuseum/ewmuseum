WorldWide 113 Madison Square Garden — New York City, New York ---- Walking In For the Last Time The parking lot. Cars in columns gleaming, reflective of street lamps and city traffic in the distance. A black BMW comes to a stop rows removed from the camera and a man, his shoulders slumping and his head down, steps from it. The audience, watching on the MegaTron, they do what's expected of them and they chant "Jordan White, Jordan White," and he does what's expected and does not hear them on his trek into the building. He pushes in through a set of steel double doors and paces the hallways, avoiding eye contact with passing GCW superstars and GCW crew alike. A small contingent of techies, abused by Bagwell at some point their career, let up a cheer as he staggers past and it draws, for a second, just the briefest smile from the corner of his mouth; but he takes a turn and it is gone and so are they. The roaring fans, the vibration of tens of thousands screaming to nothing in particular, reaches through the backstage hallways and he shudders as he reads the placards, door to door. He smirks at Christian Zenith, lifting an idle middle finger as he posts up on the wall across from it and knocks on the door beside of him. Caldera: It's open. And the former World Champion, now the formerly employed, Jordan White opens the door to Caldera's office and steps inside. GCW's brain, its heart and its soul, sits behind oak or mahogany or something that doesn't matter to anyone but himself and he almost looks hurt when he sees Jordan--who has made no progress past the entryway. Caldera: Sit down. White: This really gonna take that long? Caldera: We were friends once, weren't we? Sit down, Jordan. White: Frank. Caldera: Then sit down as Frank. I don't care what you call yourself, but you're not done with me yet. After a heavy second he shrugs and Jordan White or Frank Black or whoever he really is pushes forwards, collapses on a diagonal in the chair opposite Caldera. White: Yeah? What's up. Caldera: I wanted to speak with you about your recent, well. Retirement. White: And I wanted to enjoy it. Couldn't do this on the phone? Caldera: I prefer to look a man in the eye when he's walking out on me. What, really, have I done to you, Jordan or Frank or whatever you're called, other than put food on your table these last three years? I'm not really a bad guy. White: I didn't say you were. White: You not comfortable with me gettin' to quit twice? Hopin' you'd actually get to tell me to 'fuck off' one of these days? He sets it down and he smirks. You've heard every superlative they've rested on your shoulders, you know every accolade. One of the most naturally talented competitors the sport has ever seen? That's lofty praise and there's no part of me that thinks it's remotely inaccurate. You might, you may think I'm another embittered boss, another bellicose monster completely blown up on his own worth--but I haven't had an easy life and I wouldn't be where I am today without a few second chances I was given along the way. Think of this, Frank, think of this as an olive branch. As trying to smooth over the divide between us. White: Because you don't like people quittin', or because you're scared I'm gonna see if the comp's hiring? And Steven Caldera sighs and Steven Caldera shakes his head. Caldera: For once in our relationship, take me on my word. Frank, I'm speaking to you as a man--not as a protegé, not as an underling, and I would like to offer you something tremendous. White: What's the catch? Caldera: There is no catch. There's only you, a match, and the potential to continue your employment as a member of GCW. Jordan leans in. White: Shoot. What you got. Caldera: Before I extend this, I'd like to make it clear that, in accepting, you are not obligated to stay. If you win this match, you will not be contractually obliged to ever show up again--this is a choice, Frank, because GCW. GCW won't be the same without you. White: You didn't have to cringe when you said it. Caldera: Old habits. White: I feel that. Elaborate, el jefe. Caldera: How does a match for the World Championship sound? Jordan's eyebrows rise. White: Like a proper send off. If nothin' else. He smirks. Caldera: Just consider staying. That's all I ask of you. White: Shit. I spent the last six months considerin' stayin'. So I know you well enough to know that you don't want to go out like this. Caldera: Then, yes. That. I've got my reasons; you shouldn't ask for them, Frank. I didn't ask for yours. White: But aren't you curious? Caldera: Yes. White: Then I guess we're even. Caldera: Are you in? Jordan White versus new champion Andy Murray, for the most prized possession in the sport? White: Oh, indeed. Always thought I deserved a main event to go out on. Caldera: Think about it. White: I did. Caldera: And? White: Find out next time. Either I show up or I don't. And, in sayin' this, I don't mean to be a dick but.
