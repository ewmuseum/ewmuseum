---------- Forwarded message --------- From: Global Championship Wrestling [detail withheld] Date: Mon, Dec 20, 2010 at 12:48 AM Subject: New announcement: HEY, NEW GUY/GAL! WELCOME TO GCW! HERE'S HOW IT WORKS! Introduction Seeing as how there are, like, 10 new folks on the roster this cycle, I figured now would be as good a time as any to make a forum post on how to get started in GCW. As you'll come to see, the Forums are the most important thing to get to know, so after reading this post, take some time browsing around. The environment here is pretty awesome, so posting around on the non eW forums are a great way to get involved in the GCW community. Also, if at any point you have any questions, I'm always available for a PM and usually available for an IM at [detail withheld] on AIM. I don't use any other messaging services, though, and the majority of folks here use AIM, so it's a good thing to have an account there, even if you just sign on once in a while when you need to catch someone on AIM. How do I get booked? Before every show, I post a Pre-Booking thread in the WorldWide forums. In this thread, I ask if you can do three things for the next week. It usually looks like this. Quote Can you: ROLEPLAY: (Y/N) MATCHWRITE: (Y/N) JUDGE: (Y/N) Simply respond to that, and I'll book a card based on who wants to RP, how many matchwriters we have, and how many judges we have. So far, after 20 shows, I have never had to sideline anyone who's wanted to be booked, which is a testament to how our active matchwriters and judges make GCW a killer fed. Our show cycle is about 10 days. Usually, 6 or 7 of those days are for the RP period. In GCW, it's a one RP vs. one RP style for each match. If you want to do other RPs beyond match RPs, that's always welcome, and it's an alright way to build a character, but it's definitely not needed. A lot of folks here have busy schedules, so putting the same effort into 1 RP as you would put into several is a huge advantage in that you're putting more effort into a single piece that will have more readers. RP styles very greatly in GCW. Whether you favor a more old school approach to RPing that is heavily based on the upcoming card or are just writing an unrelated story featuring the same character, there are a few constants: good mechanics are good mechanics, an entertaining story is an entertaining story, and a well built character is a well built character. Write what you want here. There are very few restrictions on what's acceptable. Mostly, don't use other characters without their permission and all that jazz about being courteous to your peers. Submit your RPs Backstage under the ROLEPLAY link under the Submit section header. Fill out the appropriate fields, preview, submit, and you're done! Matchwriting The Pre-Booking thread is just a gauge for matchwriting. If you answer yes here, you're not locked into a match, but volunteering here is pretty meaningless unless you sign up for a match. After the new card is booked, I will post it on the WorldWide forum with deadlines. This means that the card will be up backstage in the area marked "SEG SIGNUPS". This is a list of all of the seg slots for a given show in order of appearance. Matches are included as seg slots, too. To sign up for a match, find the match you'd like to write, put your name down in the "Writer" slot and Save. If you're signing up for a match, it's BEST to sign up for a match where you are more familiar with the characters and what is going on in their storylines. Matchwriters are expected to push the storylines of both wrestlers and use Non Playing Characters accurately. Matchwriters (and RPers) are encouraged to touch base on anything that should be included in the match. Familiarize yourself by checking out roster pages and reading previous segs and matches with the characters if you haven't. To submit a match, click on the MATCH link in the Submit Section backstage. Select the appropriate event and match, select the competitors, and denote any roster members that appear unscheduled under the Interference dropdown. Please note that matches have specific seg slots, too, and find the appropriate slot number on the SEG SIGNUP page. Matchwriters are given the winners as soon as the judging is in, but if you want to get a match in early, or if you don't want the ending spoiled, you can submit two endings instead. If you do this, please submit both of the endings in a way that's easy for me to edit out the wrong one. I suggest: 1. LOTS of lines in between where to insert the two endings in the actual match and clear breaks and labels between the two endings. A note in the comments field that this match has 2 endings is also required! 2. Stop the actual match where the alternate endings start. Put both alternate endings in the comments field, clearly separated. Then, preview and submit! Presto! Segging Segging, for the time being, is as simple as matchwriting. Claim the seg spots you need on the backstage board. Do not overwrite anyone's name. If all slots are filled, you can always add a segment in between two segments. For example, if you need a seg between seg slot 8 and 9 which are both called, add seg slot 8.5 and claim it. Submit segs the same way as you would matches, except go to SEG under the submit section backstage. Judging As stated previously, RPs cover various styles and levels of relevancy to the WorldWide shows, so keep this in mind as a judge. Unacceptable reasons to pick wins include (but are not limited to): 1. The other one wasn't about the match. 2. I'm more familiar with this character 3. This one had better spelling/grammar* 4. I couldn't finish the other RP** * While spelling and grammar can contribute to a RP getting across better, this should NEVER be the only reason ** If you're going to judge, read both RPs through, at least once. I feel this is common sense. Anyway, every match has 3 judges. So far, with a few exceptions, I have been the third judge in every match. I ask that judges PM me reasoning for their picks and at least a bit of feedback for each RP individually. It justifies the judging process by making it transparent, and it's helpful to the writers. I try to post all the feedback in a thread after each show (I'm a little behind now, but it's coming!) I think a lot of 2-1 decisions have also showed how objective good writing is, and hopefully this means that people know not to take losing too hard. There have been plenty of great losing pieces in GCW, so losing ain't nothing to get discouraged over. If you say you are available to judge in the pre-booking thread, I will send you a PM later in the week with the match I have you judging. I try to get this out as soon as there's anything available to judge on the RP board. You can sign your judgments if you want to, but it's not required. IF YOU, AS AN RPER, HAVE A PROBLEM WITH YOUR FEEDBACK/MATCH JUDGMENT: Deal with it through me through PM and not on the board. This should probably go without saying, but people get frustrated once in a while, and I won't hold anything you say against you as long as it's in private and not about my mother. How to Succeed in GCW Now that you know how to do everything to participate, here's what's required to succeed: A GOOD ATTITUDE: It's night and day that a good attitude is more important than a good win/loss record to me in GCW. There's nothing that kills my motivation to efed faster than backstage bullshit, so don't cause it. If there's anything in e-wrestling worth getting upset about to cause a ruckus, take a look at your priorities, because this is nothing significant. It can be hard work sometimes, but it should always be fun. PATIENCE: As of this posting, there are roughly 500 people in GCW by my estimation. Good work, consistent effort, and especially patience, are paramount to hopes of getting more spotlight, but with the combination of all three, even if you never win the world title, there's plenty of reward to go around in getting noticed, read, and feedback, and the longer you stick it out, the more you will improve your game. That's a Garbage Bag Guarantee. COMMUNITY: I know everyone can't read every RP, every match, and every segment. I don't expect anyone to do that, but I do expect everyone to try to know what's going on as much as they can. Awareness to the things happening around you may be the most important way to improve your game. The shows are a combined effort, and if your stuff shows that you have no idea what anyone else is doing, it's way less fun to read. Thanks for reading, and hope you have some fun while you're here.
