JAGI Biography Real Name [name withheld] Hometown Hypoluxo, FL via Busan, South Korea Birthday 1/25/1986 Height 5'11" Weight 174 lb. Fed Titles n/a Fed Awards n/a Biography Ja Gi Kyung-Moon comes from a family that is notable in his native country for having many regional and national championships in Taekwondo and Judo. The family's dojo is sought out by athletes seeking to refine their technique and have the best possible chance of succeeding in competitive martial arts. While Ja Gi excelled, as had his family before him, he was still considered the weakest and least-skilled fighter within his family. To be the worst of the best, that sort of stigma over years takes its toll on anybody. For Ja Gi, he came to despise the family legacy, to believe that he was cursed to forever live in the shadow of those who came before him. There was no point in following the path as all his blood relatives before him, so he instead chose to abandon his lineage and South Korea altogether, entering into professional wrestling. Kyung-Moon is the first South Korean pro wrestling graduate of the Animal Horiguchi Dojo. He attempted to jump directly into the big leagues in Japan, but without having worked his way up through the internal dojo systems of the Japanese big leagues. Finding it beneath him to toil for 5+ years on the Japanese independent circuit, the professionally-renamed JAGI packed his bags and moved to the southeast United States without even looking for work beforehand. It took him a month and a half to get his first booking in Brandon, Florida, subbing for an injured Scandalous Scott Skinner against Cholic. It was a rookie versus rookie match, so there was no real program to the opener. JAGI took advantage of it and showed off that he was trained well, and had a natural grace and athleticism. Since then, he was booked more and more by the promoter who liked that he worked hard, showed up early, and was charismatic. It was also easy to book JAGI because he was already fairly westernized, was fluent in English, and could carry a promo if necessary. However, Sunshine Wrestling was small. Too small. A glorified backyard with adults. JAGI knew that if he was going to earn a living in pro wrestling, he had to get out of the little leagues. But at the same time, a years-long inferiority complex harbored within the young man meant that he was convinced that even in this new sport, he would not be able to make a name for himself. Relocating to North Carolina, JAGI settled in at the Saturday Avenue Slam Masters school. The head trainers Frederick Glick and Persephone Waters were middle-aged former wrestlers with connections in the industry. Going through their school couldn't hurt somebody in their career. It was within SASM, his third week training, that the Korean fighter met El Rey de Corazones. Rey was training to get back in shape following a career-stalling concussion. Rey saw a good deal of talent and potential in the young man, even if his head wasn't in the right place. Seph and Fred agreed; JAGI didn't need training, he needed the confidence that would only come from proper professional experience. At the same time, El Rey needed a "buffer" to his full return to active competition. There would definitely be ring rust, and while only working out in the ring would fix that, having somebody to watch his back and cover for him couldn't hurt. So after properly meeting JAGI, the luchador made the proposition to join him in a tag team in REBEL-Pro. The offer was accepted and Rey introduced JAGI to R.P. management. However, things soon fell apart. The team won... a lot. But JAGI realized that he was the reason that they were winning. El Rey de Corazones was a glorified jobber, an unimpressive white boy whose only saving grace was that he was 6'4" and 245 pounds. JAGI was the winning formula... JAGI was the one who was the star. So he ditched El Rey, leaving him to the fading mediocrity that defined the man's career. JAGI set his sights on a bigger stage and better pay. To that end, the Rosewood Committee came calling with an opportunity that was too good to pass up.... Though he tries a little, he is still for the most part a legitimate martial artist, as such he may at times settle for a hard attack and then just watch his opponent recover, as opposed to going for the victory pinfall. However, when sufficiently motivated, he will go for the pin or the submission. Quick kicks are aimed to disorient (head) or immobilize (legs) his victims, setting them up for throws that wear down the body and spirit before, finally, the knockout and tapout.
