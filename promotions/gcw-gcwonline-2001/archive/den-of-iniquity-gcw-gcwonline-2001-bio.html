Den of Iniquity Biography Biography Vassago: Born Simon Morgan, Vassago was a troubled youth, never truly grasping any understanding of the basics of human emotions. He’d pull away from a hug, or cringe at words like; friend, brother or family. Words like love fell on def ears, for Vassago was not a boy like any other. From a young age he felt himself compelled to kill, to eradicate especially vital individuals from the world. He first attempted his compelling nature at the tender age of twelve. The boys name was Martin. Martin was the son of his father’s colleague, an annoying kid who loved tossing around the words; friend and buddy. Vassago hated him from the first time he met him. Nonetheless he played the “game” and pretended to be his friend, believing that everyone else knew they were full of shit, just were never willing to admit it. See, his young warped mind believed that everyone felt the same way he did inside, but pretended to live up to the expectations of the world in order to fit in. Vassago did not want to fit in. Martin died in a house fire three days after his twelfth birthday; the authorities ruled it as an accident, a twelve-year-old playing with matches? accident. In the six years following the death of the young twelve-year-old Martin, young Simon became increasingly interested in Satanism. It was through this that young Simon Morgan decided his rightful place was in hell, beside the dark lord himself. A secretly published book had described for him that route to damnation. Titled The Pit, it was the work of a condemned murderer named Jeremy Lemmings who had killed his own mother and brother, and then committed suicide. A paramedic team with too much dedication and a little luck had foiled his carefully planned descent into the Pit. Lemmings was revived, healed, incarcerated, put on trial, convicted of murder and sentenced to death. Rule-playing society had made it clear that the power of death, even the right to choose one’s own, was not ever to be given to any person. (This would be proven again when young Simon’s own attempts at suicide would be thwarted). While awaiting the death sentence, Jeremy Lemmings had committed to paper the visions of Hell that he had experienced during the time that he had been on the edge of this life, before the paramedics denied him eternity. His writings had been smuggled out of prison to fellow believers who could publish and distribute them. Lemmings? book was filled with influential, persuasive images of darkness and cold, not the heat of archetypal hells, but visions of an empire of vast spaces and chilling barrenness. Peering through Death’s door and the door of Hell afar, Jeremy had seen immense powers at work on mystifying structures. Demons of vast size and strength strode through night mists across lightless continents on anonymous missions, each clothed in black with a sinuous cape and upon it’s head a shining black helmet with a flared rim. He had seen dark seas rolling on black shores under starless and moonless skies that gave the feeling of a subterranean world. Colossal ships, windowless and mysterious, were driven through the tenebrous waves by powerful engines that produced a noise like the anguished cries of multitudes. The description wasn’t like anything in Dante, but it possessed a certain romance and irrefutable power. In fact, to a psychotic young man with delusions of grandeur and a hard on for violence, with the unusually high testosterone levels that typically accompany a mental condition, then the Hell depicted would be the ultimate wet dream of supremacy. Where as a normal person would see right away that Lemmings wasn’t recounting what he saw in Hell. He was taking his inspiration from a source as stupidly obvious as it was ludicrous. His Hell was nothing more than the Galactic Empire from the Star Wars movies, to some extent changed, extended upon and filmed through the lens of religious legend. But still Star Wars. The demons were nothing more then hundred-foot-tall versions of Darth Vader, and his depiction of Satan was a dead ringer for Jabba the Hutt. But, to young Simon Morgan, this was certainty. When Simon had studied Lemmings? words, he had known they were truer than any ever inked upon a page, and he had determined to follow the great man’s model. His mother and sister became his tickets to the exotic and enormously alluring netherworld where he belonged. He had punched those tickets with a butcher knife and delivered himself to that sinister empire, encountering precisely what Lemmings assured. He had never imagined that his own escape from the insufferable world of the living would be undone not by paramedics but by his own father. Andrew Morgan, a renowned cardiovascular surgeon, and resuscitation specialist was na’ve in believing the deaths of his family had come from the hands of a stranger. Finding his wife and daughter stabbed numerous times, and his own son with his throat slit never considered that their dreadful fates were the byproduct of his son’s intentions. Using the bypass machine to recirculate cooled blood into Simon so as to lower his body temperature and prevent cell deterioration or brain damage. Air conditioners were set to below fifty to keep the outside of the body cooled, as Andrew and his team diligently worked to save his son. After thirty minutes in Hell Simon was returned to the world of the living. He awoke in the recovery room with a gasp, robbed of his eternity in hell he was more determined then ever to reclaim his place. He allowed the time for his body to recover, and slowly it became stronger than it ever had before. Through physical therapy, and various forms of recreation Simon spent his time in the hospital morphing his once think, lanky body into a mass of strength. By the time he left the hospital he was no longer the tall, skinny pale-faced kid who was made fun of in school, no now he was a true beast, returned from hell with a mission.
