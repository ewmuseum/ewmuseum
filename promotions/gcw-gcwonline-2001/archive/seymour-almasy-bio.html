Seymour Almasy Biography Real Name Jason Seymour Wilson Nicknames Lord Seymour, The Standard-Bearer, The Party Leader, Hometown The Imperial Capital of Archades. Birthday June 12, 1981 Height 5'8' Weight 193 lbs. Fed Titles GCW World Tag Team Champion (w/ Eva VanLorne and Virgil Ambrosi) Fed Awards Hated by more people in GCW than anyone currently on the roster. Biography Seymour Almasy’s GCW run is among the most interesting tenures of the Final Fantasy’s career. Seemingly on track to the GCW World Title early on, his run was derailed by the arrival of four collagues of his from Global Wrestling. Dubbing themselves Dies Irae, Virgil Ambrosi, Vivica J. Valentine, Lia Ambrosi, and Dawn McFarlane set out on their journey to dominate GCW, much as they had dominated Global. Seymour attempted to warn the other GCW talents of their impending fates’only to promptly be beaten down four on one by the debuting group. Almasy fought the group for several weeks, suffering defeat after defeat, until finally, the unthinkable happened and Almasy joined Dies Irae. The final straw may have been McFarlane’s victory over Seymour in the semi-finals of the Rampage tournament of 2006, the closest Almasy ever got to the GCW World Title, and a defeat suffered at the hands of a woman he loved. The full story from here is somewhat clouded due to the refusal of virtually everyone involved to speak, but Almasy’s tenure with the megastable was short lived. Theories for this are wide-ranging. Some believe that it was due to Almasy’s annoyance with Lia going for the GCW World Title after Dawn lost the belt to LeStatt Knight, bypassing him. Others (including Seymour himself) have stated that the influence of G. Ian Childress III (better known to wrestling fans as Nephilim) in the group turned the group from a group of equals to a stable focused around Lia. Others say the pressure of living and working with two people for whom he had strong feelings played a role, coupled with the bad blood between Seymour and virtually all members in the past. Infinite Gauntlet’s 23rd round was the beginning of the end for Almasy’s tenure in the group, with Nephilim entering the tournament in an effort to "beat respect" into Seymour. More troubling was communication that Almasy found on an internet wrestling rumors site featuring a meeting between Lia and Almasy’s sworn enemy at the time, Brandon Youngblood. Almasy won that Infinite Gauntlet round, but paranoia coupled with the above reasons seemed to play havoc on Almasy’s psyche. Whatever the case, what IS known is that Seymour Almasy packed up his belongings one day and left the McFarlane Estate. He did not return. Seymour did not next see any member of Dies Irae until FUSE presented KillZone PTC, featuring Lia defending her title against the Final Fantasy. By now reviled in GCW for joining Dies Irae to begin with, Almasy was the fan favorite in FUSE that night, fighting through Ambrosi’s onslaught to catch her Levin Bolt on his shoulders and defeat her with the Shockwave Pulsar to become the first ever three-time PTC Global Champion. At this point, Steven Caldera, owner of PTC, piled on to the group of GCW entities angry at Seymour. Citing Almasy as caring about PTC events more than his home promotion, a $600,000 charitable donation to the interfed secured Caldera’s prot’g? Kimbusa a one on one match in the first round of GTT6 for the Global Title. Kimbusa avenged his GTT5 defeat, crushing Almasy with the bomb, taking his title, and hurting Almasy perhaps where it hurt the most: PTC. Problems between Almasy, Caldera, Dies Irae, and Brandon Youngblood rose to the point where Almasy was literally thrown out of the promotion after being annihilated by Brandon Youngblood, taking the series of elbows to the head known as the Spirit Breaker until he submitted. Dies Irae joined Caldera in the arena for Almasy’s public firing, and Seymour and his bags were thrown from the arena by GCW Head of Security, Toad. Almasy returned once for an ACWF tag team match, teaming with Steven Caldera of all people, against perhaps the only two people he hated more: Rich Rollins and Angelo Deville. Over a year has passed since these events, and Almasy has resurfaced once more, with an entirely new look, attitude, and approach to the sport. More than willing to talk about his latest endeavor, Almasy claims to have returned to GCW to prove his greatness in the most difficult of environments: a promotion littered with those whom he has crossed in the past. Mortal enemy Chris Storm is one half of the tag team champions, Tessa Windsor a past love interest of Seymour (and current of Storm). Vivica Valentine and Lia Ambrosi, former members of Dies Irae, still remain with the promotion, Vivica its World Champion. Perhaps most gallingly of all, Rich Rollins is once again with GCW, the man who led to Laura Winters? suicide. And this says nothing of the usual list of talent, both known and unknown, who would love to notch their belts with a victory over Seymour. For years, the hype has proclaimed Seymour Almasy to be one of the best in the world, and his record backs up such assertions. Now though, he seems to be competing to put his legacy as far out of reach as possible. Perhaps moreover, though, he seems to be competing to shove his success in the face of Global Championship Wrestling at large. Winning the GCW World Championship in an environment in which he is universally reviled would go a long way towards that end. ==================================================== GCW Match History (Second Run): 1-0-1 Outside GCW History: 0-1-0 1. Almasy has shelved most of his flashy aerial tactics, opting for a simpler, easy to execute game designed to put pressure on the opponent with constant covers and pinning predicaments. The more falls the opponent has to kick out of, the more worried they become, the more they push the match’making them lower their guard and make themselves vulnerable to Almasy’s Euphoria Factor superkick setup, and then one of his finishers. Finisher Move Hinamizawa Syndrome OR Geostigma Finisher Description Rear naked choke submission/Crucifix-style Backstabber. For more information, see the Finisher Setup Move The Euphoria Factor Setup Description Superkick to the back of the head. From here, Seymour will go into one of his finishing moves. Special Moves Fallback Piledriver, Top Rope Moonsault Press, Running Kneelift --> Discus Lariat, Running Shotei, Other Moves Finishing moves: Hinamizawa Syndrome: Rear naked choke/Coquina Clutch. Generally, he needs to stun the opponent to set this up (hence the purpose of the Euphoria Factor to set up), or stronger opponents can power free. Almasy will generally go behind his opponent and apply the choke, pulling the foe to the ground and clamping on a body scissors. I’m going for realism with this. The move will never be on longer than 20-30 seconds. By that time, the opponent will have either tapped out, been put to sleep, or escaped the hold in some way. Preferred escape is the ropes. An opponent can fight the hold, that is, try to prevent Almasy from sinking the choke in by blocking the arm. Generally, once he gets it hooked, an opponent will try to find a way out, claw at Almasy’s choking arm, and then tap if they can’t escape. VARIANTS: Hinamizawa Syndrome, Stage Five: Scissored Katahajime. Ties up one of the arms, making escape less likely. Think of it like Kurt Angle’s grapevine ankle lock. If the Syndrome is escaped or blocked a couple of times, he’ll go for Stage Five. Geostigma: Crucifix-style Backstabber. From behind an opponent, Almasy grabs hold of both of the opponents wrists, extending their arms out into a crucifix, before leaping up to land the Backstabber. This is Seymour’s exclamation point, flashier than the Syndrome, and he uses it when he wants to show off a bit. Setup Move: The Euphoria Factor: Almasy prefers to land his superkick to the back of the head when at all possible. There is no setup, no stomping of feet, Almasy snaps the move off as quickly as possible. He CAN finish with this, but more often will use it to segue into one of his two finishers qwhich require being behind an opponent. When covering after this, he will utilize a style of pinfall in which he is able to hook the opponent’s leg with one arm, using his other hand to count out the three on his fingers. When he hits the opponent in the face with it, he will follow the opponent to the mat and hook the leg in one fluid motion (a la a certain Heartbreak Kid) Signature moves: Seymour will ALWAYS cover after each of these moves unless the match type that he’s in doesn’t allow for pins. This is part of his strategy to keep pressure on opponents at all time, as well as the simple fact that most of these moves might be enough to get the pinfall; he’ll never know unless he tries, after all. The Piledriver: Duh. Seymour executes the piledriver quickly, lifting the opponent to vertical and then dropping straight down as quickly as possible. This is to torque the neck, but it also helps the slight Almasy execute the move to begin with. Running Kneelift --> Left-Armed Lariat: In spite of being right handed, Almasy executes this combination southpaw. Charging a doubled over opponent, Seymour will lift his left knee up into the opponent's face, sending them reeling vertically. From here, Seymour spins quickly clockwise to clock his opponent with a left-armed lariat. Moonsault Press off the top rope. Seymour always pins with both hands on the chest afterwards, and as such, it rarely wins. Shotei: Seymour’s patented palm strike. Now has the habit of kissing his palm before delivering the strike, and he will generally whip the opponent into the rope before doing it, thus, Irish whip, kiss, and palm strike. If Seymour can get one, he won’t complain, but these are part of his new strategy to put pressure on his opponents as much as possible. These moves are not limited to simply this list, but really any way in which Seymour can force his opponents shoulders to the mat. If he IS going for the win with one, you can bet your ass he's going to hook the tights, ropes, or anything else he can get a hold of for extra leverage.
