Dynasty Biography Real Name [name withheld] Nicknames the Bad Boy from Buffalo, the Prodigy, New York's Finest Hometown Buffalo, NY Birthday 6-21-1985 Height 6'2 Weight 229 lbs. Biography Born and raised in dismal Western NY, Danny Francisco was born believing he was destined for fame. It would come sooner than expected but not without it's consequences. Sure, he looks like the typical kid from New York, growing up idolizing Yankee legend-in-the-making Derek Jeter and his New York Yankees. He put on baseball spikes and never thought otherwise as to what his life would be. He was elected AP High School All-American Catcher three times, two-time NY State Mr. Baseball, led his team to three NY State playoff appearances, in which his Senior season ended with a State Championship, building his legend ever more. He was born for the spotlight and the 17-year-old phenom from Buffalo was featured on the cover of ESPN the Magazine with the caption reading "The Dynasty" as he went into his future uncertain of college or pro baseball. New York state's Mr. Baseball signed a letter of intent that Spring to attend the Arizona State University on full scholarship and be their everyday man behind the dish as well as play linebacker - Danny recorded a New York State record 31 tackles over his four years on varsity - in the Fall. Danny's time at ASU was anything but academic. His on-the-field performance was unbelievable - 14 sacks and a bowl game win in the Fall and added 31 HRs, .326 AVG and 66 RBIs which took the Sun Devils to the College World Series' finals - marking one of the most impressive athletic seasons of the school's history. Off the field, as it would publicly be revealed, that outside of his athletic accomplishments, Danny was receiving the star treatment in the classroom and around Tempe during his one school-year attending ASU. Fast cars, expensive clothes, drugs, cheerleaders, college babes, and limitless bank accounts - handed to the stud who was drafted in the First-Round by the St. Louis Cardinals in 2005 and signed a $43 million dollar contract, guaranteeing him $31 million, over the next ten years. His professional career began with a bang when he made headlines across America when he was involved in a clubhouse altercation in which he broke a teammate's jawbone and, later that night, left another teammate's car with slashed tires and smashed windows. He admitted to it over the radio the next day when he was discussing his recent trade to the Kansas City Royals. Kansas City was a bit more complicated. Danny seemed to adjust there, allowing himself to relax away from the big-market buzz. He was jovial, calm, and collected - especially at the dish. Through his first fifty games, Danny Francisco was in the lead for the Triple Crown - 29 HRs, 59 RBIs, .335 AVG, and .412 OBP - as a twenty-year-old, every-day Catcher. In game 51, Danny Francisco's personal life met his professional life. It was like watching a head-on train wreck. Rumors began to surface that Danny had met a woman and was planning on marrying her or asking her to in the near future. The two were inseparable and it was well documented through local media outlets. Another rumor also caught Danny's ear and that one included his girl, a teammate, and acts unspeakable to the common person. After the National Anthem, Danny cracked his knuckles, then cracked his third baseman with his bat. He also fought four other teammates in the fifty-minute, five-person bloodbath that was the epitome of bad sportsmanship. A week later, Danny was released. His natural reaction was to shrug it off and look for another chance. Only one call came over the next week and it was from the Tijuana Tigers. Danny's agent thought it might be worth a shot, do well and make his way back to American Pro Baseball. Danny took the job and made his way to Mexico. After months of barely making enough for a decent bottle of tequila, Danny's drive to become the greatest baseball player lost it's edge. He didn't work as hard as he did for nothing, he did it to make sure his life is one that is considered legendary, famous, and adventurous. Not to fade into anonymity as an amigo in the Mexican Minors hitting .500. The Dynasty began to moonlight as a fall man for aging lucha-legends - somebody to take dives for kids' shows and charity events - for the extra cash. Luckily it was enough to get him into the cantina for some cervesas y senoritas. A year of low-man money was enough to spark an outburst by 'el Dynasty' (as he became to be known around Mexican wrestling) and he showcased one of the finest matches against one of Mexico's greatest wrestlers. He might have been aging but it was a monumental win in the eyes of the fans. His fans. He was soon signing wrestling matches in which his skill was extremely outmatched, but his desire to shine in the spotlight carried him into every fight with an overwhelming confidence. No man could match el Dynasty. His face, pristine yet rugged. His body, powerful and tone. His mindset, unbeatable and composed. Three years after Sports Illustrated announced 'the Dynasty had crumbled,' Danny Francisco finds himself staring at a GCW contract that would lead him back to the states as a wrestling prodigy. Danny Francisco is trying to make the most of his situation. Anybody else in his shoes might crumble under the heat. Danny was born for it. Danny came back to prove it. Other Feds Only Semi-Pro Strengths Athletic Phenomenon, Charismatic, Courageous Weaknesses Inexperienced, Bullheaded, Naive Wrestling Style Unorthodox Highflyer Strategy Dynasty goes into each match with the conditioning and determination of a wrestling legend and the experience and focus of a first-time fighter. His remarkable athletic ability, potential, and raw talent gives him an edge and chance to compete in every match he signs on for. Whether or not he can keep his head in the match and focus on the true task is another story. He watches for opportunities to make a name for himself as the greatest to live which allows for many different outcomes. sometimes it pays off and Dynasty gets some good face time on the GCW recap show but if it doesn't, he might be the goat of the locker room for weeks due to the amateur mistake. A phrase: A lot of talent with a lot of time to grow.
