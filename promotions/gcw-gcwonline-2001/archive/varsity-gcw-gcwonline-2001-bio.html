Varsity Biography Real Name [name withheld] Nicknames 'The All American' Alex Winters, Heisman, The Man With The Golden Arm, Rookie of the Year Hometown Collegeville, Pennsylvania Birthday February 19, 1980 Height 6'2' Weight 220 Fed Titles Cruiserweight Title (Current) Fed Awards N/A Biography Ever wonder what happened to that Hometown Hero after he made good? Well, this is a possible answer to that question. Varsity’s entire gimmick centers around himself being one of those rare Awesome Athletes, capable of leading any team that they’re on to victory. This usually ranges from any sport across the board, but for Alex it was football. A prodigy on the field, one could call him, he was All City and All State all four years of high school. As the quarterback, he managed to turn that school’s football program all the way around. That easily got him a scholarship to the college of his choice (which just happened to be Penn State) and his first two years there were great. However, it was in those years that he fell in love with the sports entertainment industry and his life began to take a bit of a change in direction. Talks of the NFL were always dinner conversation at home and the Winters family had established that he would go pro. When Alex brought up the topic of professional wrestling as an alternative, they weren’t too happy about that. However, it seemed that Alex’s heart was set on becoming wrestler, so they did what any good family would do... they supported him. On campus, he kept playing football, wanting to keep his body in shape and helping to lead Penn State to back to back championships. However, in between semesters, his father had him working with both a personal trainer and a wrestling coach, Tommy Kyle (Ex-Wrestler). If his son was going to be jumping into the dangerous business of pro wrestling, he was going to make sure that Alex was ready for anything they tossed at him. Graduation from college went off without a hitch and Alex shocked the nation when he didn’t sign up for the draft. Instead, he took a year off from football completely, focusing on his wrestling and training. His trainer, taking the young man’s small (in comparison to most of the wrestlers in the industry) size into account, molded Alex into a solid wrestler, giving him a wide variety of moves to choose from. However, as a secondary playbook (so to speak) his coach enrolled him in gymnastics and ballet, to perfect his balance and flexibility. Things were looking up for Alex, as it looked like he was going to be a force to be reckoned with. Boy was he wrong. Near the end of his initial training, Alex couldn’t wait to get into the ring. So he tried out for a local Pennsylvania club, so that he could get the total wrestling experience. This was both a good and a bad idea, as the results were devastating. He barely made it through two minutes in the ring with the big boys. For the first time in his life, Alex Winters wasn’t the top dog anymore. He was starting at the bottom and it sent him into a major state of depression. Major. To the point that he quit the wrestling dream and prepped to head off for the NFL. He never made it to the plane. Little Billy Madison is to blame. The Madisons lived next door to the Winters family for years and little Billy had to be Alex’s number one fan. So, of course, Billy was there that night Alex got his ass handed to him in the ring. So it was only natural that Billy be standing in the driveway when Alex was heading off. He was wearing his autographed Alex Winters Penn State jersey. The jersey that Alex gave to him after his first championship with the team. Billly only had one thing to say: “Win or Lose, You’ll Always Be My Hero.” Cheesy? Yes. Corny? Absolutely. But it worked. Somehow, Billy was able to shake Alex out of his funk and instead of going to the airport... he went to TK’s Gym., to restart his training. That’s what he’s been doing for the past two years. Training and wrestling in small, independent federations, littered across his home state. He’s managed to gather himself a small cult following, as he’s taken up the mantle of Varsity. Rumors have be circulating, though, in regards to him starting to become too big for small time. It’s been said that he’s looking to crossover to a major federation and is currently weighing his options. Recently, Varsity (thanks to the genius of his manager, Styles) has signed a contract with Global Championship Wrestling. Let the games begin... Finisher Description Commonly referred to as a ?running moonsault?, Varsity runs towards the corner, leaping up onto the top rope. Springing off it, he performs a classic moonsault, to come back down across body of his opponent. This is quickly followed by the hooking of the leg, to try and get the win Finisher Setup Move Interception! Setup Description Spinning Spinebuster (Reversal) -- Basically, this is when an opponent comes running at a groggy or out of it Varsity and he uses his 'Heart' and 'Inner Strength' to reverse their attack into a spinning spinebuster that leaves them dead center on the mat. Other Moves Basic Move Set - Springboard Arm Drag - Asai Moonsault (Top Rope) - Corkscrew Body Press (Top Rope) - Inverted Side Headlock Swinging Bulldog - Super Bulldog - Twisting Bulldog - Short Arm Clothesline - Flying Somersault DDT - Swinging DDT - Springboard Somersault Drop Kick - Slingshot Flying Head Scissors - Somersault Leg Drop (Top Rope) - Swinging Neck Breaker - Springboard Somersault Senton - Any selection of suplexes or ranas would also be fitting Trademark / Signature Move Set - Heads or Tails: Not the most powerful, or even useful, move in his arsenal, but it’s in there. Heads or Tails consists of a literal coin toss, in the middle of a match. Done while the opponent is groggy (either on the mat or standing), Varsity will go into his pocket and pull out a half dollar coin. He’ll then Irish Whip the opponent off towards the ropes. While the opponent bounces off, he’ll quickly flip the coin into the air. With timing on his side, his opponent should bounce off the ropes and head right into a strong right hand from Varsity. (This is his trademark crowd pleasing move.) - The Longest Yard: Simply put, this is a Standing Reverse Ankle Lock. This is usually done after a considerable amount of attention has been paid to a particular ankle on the opponent, so that it’s more painful. He’s even been known to let up on the pressure, just to allow some crawling towards the ropes, before locking back down again and dragging his opponent away. (This is his signature submission move.) - Friday Night, Lights Out: Something of an equalizer when doing battle against those with more power than him, Varsity uses this move to even the score. Set up as a reversal to a powerbomb, as he’s lifted, Varsity will roll upwards, throwing off his opponent’s momentum. He locks his arm around his opponent’s head and falls backwards to bring them down to the mat in a painfully DDT manner. (This is his signature reversal move.) - Unnecessary Roughness: Used in times of extreme duress, while trying to get back the momentum in a match, this is a move than can come out of nowhere. Using whatever inner strength he has, Varsity will whip his opponent into the turnbuckle and follow up with a stiff elbow to their face, to keep them dazed. He’ll then lift them up to the second rope, where he locks them up for a Rock Bottom. He’ll then leap backwards off the ropes and instead of slamming them straight down onto their back, he’ll swing his body around his opponent’s and drive them down to the mat, face first. (This is his signature momentum gaining move.) Alternate Finisher Overtime: A 450 frog splash, usually done as a second finisher, if the Touchdown! doesn’t get the pin. The setup to Overtime can be any move that leaves the opponent down on their back, in the middle of the ring. Entrance Music 'Not Listening' by Papa Roach
