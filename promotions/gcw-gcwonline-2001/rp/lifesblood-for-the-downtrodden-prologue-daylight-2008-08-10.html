Lifesblood for the Downtrodden, Prologue: Daylight

10 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10th August I Andy Murray “Honestly sir?” Brendan Perry, full-time lawyer, part-time smug bastard, pulled a sleazy grin across his face as he leaned back in his leather recliner. “We’re going to tear her to pieces. If what you say is true, of course.” Andy snorted; he couldn’t get his head round how a guy who made a career of destroying the lives of fellow human beings could be so damn cheerful about his occupation. But then again, Andy did like the words that were coming out of his mouth, so he nodded in response. “Good. Personally, I hope we take all she’s got,” chimed Rueben Dallas with a hint of malice to his voice that Andy wasn’t used to hearing. Realising that Andy might not be quite as enthusiastic; he turned to face the Scotsman. “Sorry, Andy.” “Sorry for what?” “Well” she is the mother of your child.” “And?” Andy asked. “If this goes your way, sir, the financial implications on her end are going to be substantial,” Perry said as he leaned across his rich mahogany desk. He decanted some still spring water from a crystal jug and glanced at the Scottish wrestler. “Want a drink?” Mouthing the word “no,” Andy shook his head. He took a look at Brendan Perry as he repeated the question to Rueben. Dressed in a suit finely tailored to fit every curve and contour of his body, the lawyer was a direct personification of every clich? and preconception that Andy had for lawyers. He couldn’t have been over the age of thirty-six and yet he worked out of an office fit for Donald Trump himself. “I mean, she’s really going to struggle after this, I’ll be frank with you,” said Perry before taking a sip of water. “I can’t imagine her personal wealth to be too substantial. It all depends how far you want to go with this.” Andy contemplatively nodded his head, allowing himself to slump back in the chair that was a lot more comfortable than any office chair had the right to be. Carefully he considered his options; this was Jane they were talking about, after all; a woman who had brought him many years of joy, a woman who had brought him a child. A jealous, scornful woman who had driven a wedge between Andy and his son so far that their relationship may never recover. A woman who he had divorced twice. A woman who, just a few weeks ago, had tried to turn his career into dust. “Think about it, big guy,” said Rueben as he leaned across, gently tapping Andy on the shoulder. “Personally, I’d take this all the way to the god damn bank, but it’s your call.” Rueben was right; it was Andy’s call. And it was an easier call than he’d thought. “Let’s do it.” Brendan Perry smiled like the proverbial cat that got the canary and rubbed his hands together; looks like his reverse psychology “guilt trip” shtick had reeled him in another hefty payday. “Excellent,” he said. “With what we’ve got I reckon we can string her for defamation of character and slander at least; sure I can dig a couple more things up though?” “Don’t bother, that’s enough,” Andy dismissed. “Oh?” “Look, she might have a little bit of cash lying about from the last divorce settlement, but Jane’s not loaded,” he explained, adjusting the collar of his black Clutch band t-shirt. “She needs to pay for this, sure, but I don’t want to put her out on the streets or anything. She’s got my son with her, remember.” “Totally understandable,” nodded Perry, pretending that he respected Andy’s remorse. In reality, though, he wasn’t happy; he knew that the less money Andy claimed from the trail, the less he’d get in his paycheque. Not that he’d ever admit it, of course. “Well, I’ll get on this ASAP,” he said, beginning to sift through the array of documents scattered across his desktop. “How soon is ASAP?” asked Rueben. Andy wondered if his agent was more keen to sue Jane than he was. “Heck, I don’t see why the writ can’t be issued to her as early as this evening,” replied Brendan Perry, raising his finely-plucked eyebrows. “Sooner the better, I guess,” said Andy. “Alright then.” That Cheshire Cat smug grin reappeared on Perry’s face as he rose from his chair. “Pleasure doing business with you, Mr. Murray. You can expect to hear from me before the day’s end.” Andy tried to rise from his chair, but found it significantly harder than he was used to. Barbs of pain shot up from the sole of his right foot as he attempted to transfer weight on it, causing him to visibly wince as a result. This was the first day he’d been able to walk without the aid of a crutch since Warpath, but he was still very much in agony. Still, he at least had the strength to reach over and shake Brendan Perry’s hand. “Likewise.” “Shit, you alright man” I can call the first-aider if you want,” Perry offered, reaching for the phone on the desk. Andy shrugged it off. “It’ll take more than a mere leg injury to derail Andy Murray, let me tall ya,” he smiled with tangible confidence. “Let me know when it’s done.” II Jane Murray This shouldn’t be happening. Right now she should be with Chris Bagwell, riding high on the crest of an epic, hard-fought pay-per-view victory. She should be living it up, enjoying the good life while her ex-husband’s career lay scattered around him in tiny fragments. Things couldn’t have gone any worse for Jane Murray. Instead of being in the position of having eternal bragging rights over her former partner, here she was, right back where she’d started. Chris Bagwell had kicked her to the curb before he’d even taken to the ring in Toronto. It had hit her like a freight train. Of course, she’d seen his personality and the way he acted on television, and she knew all about the reputation he’d built himself as one of the most reviled individuals in GCW history, but she thought that it was an act. She thought it was a character, a persona that Chris Bagwell adopted every time he stepped inside a wrestling ring. That’s what wrestling’s all about right, gimmicks? Apparently Chris Bagwell didn’t get that memo. Chris? rejection wasn’t even the worst of it though. Her master-plan, her “flawless” scheme that was supposed to obliterate the promising career of Andy Murray had failed in spectacular fashion. With recent speculation surrounding PRIME’s Killean Sirrajin and GCW’s own Paul Samson she figured that the press would pick up on even the slightest hint of steroid abuse and really run with it, but that’s not how it turned out. Instead Andy chose to face the press almost immediately, delivering an incendiary, heartfelt discourse that blew Jane Murray’s allegations straight out of the water. She knew about the cancer, of course she did, and she knew how difficult her ex-husband found it to talk about the wretched disease, so the last thing that she expected was for Andy to come out and straight-up admit it in front of the world. But it had happened. Andy Murray was in the clear, and after defeating Chris Bagwell at Warpath, viewed by many as a future Global Championship Wrestling World Champion. A heavy sigh escaped Jane’s lungs as she rose from the couch and sauntered through to the kitchen; Marvin would be home soon, and she knew how grumpy he got if his dinner wasn’t on the table by the time he got back from soccer practice. As she filled a saucepan with water, Jane ruefully glanced out the window of her Tallahassee bungalow as her thoughts meandered towards another wasted day. Aside from a couple of menial jobs around the house she had accomplished absolutely nothing of any real productivity. The monetary situation was fine; she was still sitting on a nice wad of green from the last divorce, and Bagwell had always been more than generous during their brief courtship. She knew that she would have to get a new job soon though, for lack of having nothing else to do. Jane turned the faucet off and carried the pan across to the gas hob, but she couldn’t keep her mind off what could’ve been. If only Chris? she began in her head, but her train of thought quickly tailed off. There was far too many “if only??s for her to comprehend right now, so instead she decided to jab a needle through the heart of the Andy Murray voodoo doll she kept in her mind’s eye. But was it really his fault that she was in such a state? She had asked herself this question so many times over the past few days she’d actually lost count. Surely Andy Murray was the one being wronged. He saw that somebody was trying to tear his life to pieces, so he stood up for himself? surely anyone in the right mind would do the same under such circumstances? Conflicted and completely unable to focus on the cooking, Jane took a step back and sat down at the kitchen table and tried to make sense of the downtrodden mess her life had become. III Andy Murray “Tell me where I need to pull over,” Rueben said as he drove his faithful silver BMW through downtown Tallahassee. “Nah, I was just going to let you figure it out for yourself,” Andy began, crammed into the passenger’s side like a sardine. Clearly, BMW’s weren’t manufactured with 6’7? professional wrestlers in mind. “Ya know, just to fuck with you.” “You’re a prick, Andy,” smiled Rueben. Beads of perspiration were beginning to build up on the brow of Andy’s agent; clearly wearing the suit jacket was not a good decision. “You fucking love it.” Andy reached into the pocket of his stonewashed blue jeans as the sun suddenly decided it would be a fantastic idea to try and blind the Scotsman. He slid the mirrored aviator shades that had become his trademark over his face. “There you go” just up there on the right. Big blue door. See it?” Rueben squinted in the sunlight, trying to make out whatever the hell it was that Andy was pointing at. “White car outside?” “That’s it.” A couple of seconds later and Rueben had his automobile pulled up outside the gym. He killed the engine with a simple flick of the ignition. “Is that it” Doesn’t look like much.” Choosing to not even acknowledge that comment, Andy pushed the door open, eager to escape the confines of the cramp vehicle. Swivelling his torso round, he put his left leg out first, supporting his entire body weight on it while using the outside of the car for support. Rueben was already out of the car and eyeing up the gym by the time Andy had even left his chair. “You’re going to give this a lick of paint, I hope,” said Rueben as he flicked a flake of loose paint away from the admittedly dilapidated front door. “Of course, you nonce. Rome wasn’t built in a day, and all that shite.” Closing the car door behind him, Andy hobbled around the outside of the vehicle with all the grace of a baby elephant. Dallas turned, noticing Andy’s ailments. “Are you sure you’re okay man” You aren’t moving so well.” “Bah,” Andy said, waving his concerns away. “It’ll be alright by WorldWide. Anyway, enough” let me show you around?” Knowing that at least one of his brothers would probably be around, Andy pushed against the door of his newly-acquired gym, sliding through the gap as daintily as his injuries would allow him. After a short journey through a dank, dusty corridor, Andy and his agent stepped into the main gym hall area. Andy’s brothers, Sid and Cayle, were already there, just like he’d thought. The two of them had been working pretty much non-stop to try and get the place going during Andy’s time on the road and they’d done a sterling job thus far. The seemingly endless amount of windows had been cleaned of all traces of dust and grime, the walls had been whitewashed and they’d even found the time to set up a wrestling ring smack bang in the middle of the floor. “Sidney, Cayle, how’s it going you couple of rim-lickers?” Andy beamed as he stumbled across to his brothers, who had been sparring sportingly in the centre of the old wrestling ring. “Ohhhh fuck, here comes the old man,” Cayle said, faux-annoyed. He turned to face Andy, leaning against the ropes. “Where’s your wheelchair” Bloody cripple.” Eventually Andy shuffled over towards the ring while Rueben paced around the inside of the gym, inspecting it for the very first time. Neither of his brothers were dressed for wrestling. “Hope you’re not kicking his ass too hard, beardy.” Sid laughed. “Just showin” the cocky little fucker a thing or two.” “Showing me a thing or two” Man, I’ve had you on the deck at least three times now!” Cayle grinned, punching the eldest of the Murray brothers on the shoulder gently. “Now now ladies, calm down,” Andy said, leaning back and pulling his good leg up onto the ring apron. He grabbed hold of the middle rope and hauled his right up too. “Let the King show you how it’s done.” Cayle and Sid shared awkward glances as the brother that they knew was injured rigidly ducked through the ropes and entered the ring. “Uhhh, I don’t know Andy. You’re pretty banged up,” Sid said, backing away. “Yeah Andy, don’t risk it,” Rueben piped up from the background as he approached the ring. “You might have a match at WorldWide, don’t want to chance making things worse, do you?” “Nonsense. It’s just a little sparring,” Andy turned to face his younger brother. “C’mon hotstuff, lets go.” Cayle shrugged, genuinely unsure what to do. As the youngest of the trio he felt a constant need to prove his worth to two brothers who had experienced a lot more success in the wrestling business than he had. But he knew that Andy was hurting and he didn’t want to be directly responsible for worsening the situation. Either way, he put up his dukes. “Alright Stephen Hawking, lets dance.” Cayle paced sidewards, keeping on his toes, putting his obvious mobility advantage to full use, yet it was Andy who made the first move. The GCW athlete grabbed his youngest brother by the head and put him into a side headlock. Naturally Cayle squirmed, trying to break free. In desperation he planted a hand on Andy’s back and shoved. “Ahhhh!” barked Andy as his right leg suddenly gave way, sending him stumbling forward into the ropes. “God-fucking-damn.” “Shit, bro, you alright?” a genuinely concerned Cayle asked, helping his brother back to a full vertical base. Sidney shook his head; he wanted desperately to say “I told you so”. “Jesus Murphy,” Andy said as he clutched his calf muscle. “Alright, that’s it. I’m calling Steven Caldera,” Rueben said with a sigh. Before the super-agent could pull out his cell phone, Andy was having his say. “Hell are you talking about, Ruebs?” “I need to tell him you can’t compete at WorldWide.” ?” what?” “Look, you just about collapsed trying to apply a bloody headlock. How on earth are you supposed to last a full match?” Rueben asked. “And with LeStatt Knight picking your opponent, who knows what could happen! Heck, he might end up putting you against a monster like Shaman, or even Jay Terror?” “I’m fine, Ruebs,” Andy said sternly. “No you ain’t,” Sidney suddenly said from across the ring, scratching his thick, dirty blonde beard. “This is your biggest problem man; you don’t have a clue when to quit.” “So what, my leg’s hurt” I’ve worked through worse,” a headstrong Andy replied, desperately holding onto his pride. Chris Bagwell wasn’t going to further stall his charge towards Dangerous Games glory, not a chance. “I’ll just grab a couple of painkillers before the show. No biggie.” “After the shit Jane tried to pull,” Cayle began, “I don’t droppin” some pills is really a good idea right now. Do you?” Andy sighed. His younger brother had raised a good point; what if somebody caught him downing the pills backstage and mistook them for something else? After the recent steroid controversy that wasn’t a chance he was willing to take. “Come on, lets get you back to the house you stubborn old hermit,” waved Rueben. He climbed up onto the apron himself, helping his client through the ropes. “We’ll talk about it when we get there.” But Andy had already made up his mind. He was going to WorldWide, by hook or by crook.
