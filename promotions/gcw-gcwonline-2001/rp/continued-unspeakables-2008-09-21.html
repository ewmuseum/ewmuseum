Continued Unspeakables

21 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 21/9/08 The scene was a bar. A smoke filled bar. Which is odd considering the smoking ban had already taken effect and no one in the bar was smoking. Perhaps the bar was using those smoke machines you get at night clubs to induce a backroom smoky atmosphere, I don't know, I'm just narrating this shit, I'm not a smokemaologist. Dirk Dickwood, a man alone in the world with nobody to turn to sat alone, sipping on whisky. Honestly, sipping, what a puss. He looked like a husk of his former self, dishevelled and hunched over, cold to the world. ? However, his day was about to change, perhaps indeed his luck and his life were about to make a 180. A shining beacon of virtue was once again about to walk about in his life. A fine, upstanding member of the community concern with only his contempt for every other human being. The man who swung open the bar door and with a ray of light shone behind him, swaggered . Dirk Dickwood sat upwards to meet this man eye to eye, a man he knew from his past. That man... was Phil Atken. ? Atken: I'm glad you could meet me, it's a very trying time in my life. I just lost my agent and now the GCW is very upset with me due to "vile sexist language" or something like that. ? Dickwood: First of all, you fired me. Second, I didn't meet you here, you followed me here in one of the most creepy stalker-esque fashions I have ever witnessed. How many wigs and hats do you actually have in that car of yours? ? Atken: Car? What are you talking about, I'm a public?transport man. Anyway, you said we should meet for drinks. ? Dickwood: No I didn't. ? Atken: I'm almost certain you did. ? Dickwood: Still doesn't excuse the stalking. ? Atken: You say stalking, I say surprising an old friend. ? Dickwood: Old friend? We saw each other last week! ? Atken: How's Helga? ? Dirk's eyes lit up in a flurry of panic. The memories of Helga still burned brightly, the mace, the masks, the matador, the inappropriate usage of a viking helmet. The memories all came flooding back to Dirk with two simple words, "How's Helga?". Phil was unaware of the full impact of the words he had just uttered until he turned his attention back towards Dirk, now curled up in a ball and gently sobbing. ? Atken: Honestly, every time. Every single time I bring up that viking whore of a wife/ex-wife/girlfriend/pillaging maniac of yours, you curl up into a ball and ? Dickwood: *sniff* *sniff* phwafff ? Atken: Those weren't even words. ? Dickwood: Twoodle. ? Atken: Now you're just making things up. I am not a twoodle, stop spreading vicious rumours. I could have my lawyer sue you for such things. Dirk sprung up from his ball like form, directly back in to his seat. He had some facts to be a-stating. Dickwood: You don't have a lawyer. Atken: Like that's ever stopped me from making that threat. Anyway, aren't you meant to be crying? Dickwood: I got better. Atken: A true Christmas miracle. Could I borrow Helga? I need her for something. Dickwood: If it's your GCW commitments, I strongly advise against replacing yourself with a woman who thinks she's a Viking. Atken: She could pull it off. Anyway, it's not that, I'd just like some insurance... you know how much I love gold. Dickwood: I don't know, there's that whole "is psychotically insane and may kill members of the crowd" thing. Plus the fact I am no longer dealing with issues in regards to your wrestling career. Y'know, swings and roundabouts. Atken thought back to his childhood, the days of actual swings and roundabouts. The joys of going up and down on a roundabout and around in circles on the swings. Simple, childish pleasures. Joyful and delicious times in life. The times you could get away with murder, figuratively and literally. Dickwood: You said you were going to do this on your own. Atken: What? Sorry, I was thinking about hotdogs riding roundabouts. Dickwood: If that isn't a metaphor for something I don't know what is. Dirk was correct in his insinuation that "A hotdog on a roundabout" may one day be used as a metaphor, yet to date in the entire 200 year history of the entire universe, no one is yet to use it. Atken: Yeah, that John McCain sure is like a hotdog on a roundabout. That was a simile. Dickwood: Back to my original point, you said you needed to prove yourself worthy in GCW, you said I was dead weight in this partnership, you said you were going to skullfuck my first born if I came near you during a Worldwide, and now you want me to ask Helga to step in and help you? Atken: Winning is harrrrrrd. Atken proceeded to make puppy dog eyes and a pouty face at Dirk Dickwood. Dickwood: No amount of thirteen year old teenage girl facial expressions is going to change the fact that you fired me. Or the fact you don't want to re-hire me, you just want my psychotic behemoth of a wife to sit in your corner and most likely run interference allowing you to win a match in some way or form. Atken: A title match! Dickwood: Why don't you let one of your other lady friends do it? I heard you were trolling the circuit again. Atken: Yeah, like you don't. Dickwood: I don't? You think I'd let Helga catch me doing something like that? I'd very much enjoy an uncharred corpse. Phil pondered his good friends plausible pearl for a moment. A moment of quiet contemplation. Atken: I hear it tastes like chicken. Dickwood: I'm glad you care so much about my welfare. Atken: You're on welfare? I mean I know that losing me must've been damaging but I didn't know it was that bad. Dickwood: No I meant... oh forget it. You are purposely dense. Atken: Purposely what? Dickwood: Point proven I think. Dirk then proceeded to lick his finger then put it on his butt. He later sued himself for sexual harassment. Dickwood won the case and had to pay himself $500 dollars in damages. Atken: You know, this comeback thing seemed so easy in my head, beat up a couple of idiots who consistently lose to those a vaginal persuasion, I mean ?they must be worthless? I said to myself, ?you don't need any training to beat these morans? a hobo yelled at me. Dickwood: Were you pretending to be homeless again? Atken: Clearly you've never tried the soup, it is certainly worth it. To get that derailed train back on track, I don't know if I've still got the abilities I once possessed. Dickwood: I've had doubts for years. Did you actually ever have it? I mean if I remember correctly, all your biggest victories came from the political bullshit games you used to play. Playing sides against each other, allowing you to pick up the pieces. Atken: That's what I meant, I'm worried I've lost my talent for corruption. This is a very traumatic moment of realisation for me. Phil hung his head in sorrow, forgetting the candle that was sitting below him. He almost lost his eyebrows for good that day. Then that doctor glued some hair where they should have been. Turns out you really shouldn't trust Dokter Steve's 24 Hour Stitch 'n' Go. I doubt that man even has the dokterate he claims to. Dickwood: Well, if you need a refresher course, I could send you back to the alter of Vict... Atken: I'm not seeing her, I am not going near her. I completely refuse to see her. Dickwood: She's the one that taught you half your tricks. A refresher course couldn't hurt. Atken: Look Dirk, there are things in life I will see, there are things in life I have seen, there are things in life I will see again, that crotch of Satan is not one of them. Dickwood: And I thought I was the one with the women issues. Atken: I don't have woman issues. Dickwood: Says the man who calls them all whores on live television, belittling any success they have in a sweeping generalisation. Atken: Well, I don't have issues with the ones who accept my money. Dickwood: Well, I'm sure she still would... Atken: No, several years ago she was left with a decision, side with the future of the wrestling industry, or the retard who once thought it would be a splendid idea to let a monkey have controlling interest in his wrestling company. She made that decision there, then and forever. Dickwood: Are you honest to god still heartbroken after all these years? That is so sweet. Atken: I'm not heartbroken, I just refuse to associate myself with anyone who has the loosest of ties to that monkey-toting gas bag. Dirk shifted in his chair uncomfortably as came spittle flying out of Phil's mouth. Every word Phil said came served with a side order of globs of phlegm. Atken: Listen you little fuckstick, I came to you for aid, help, wisdom. Losing doesn't do my psyche very good and as you well know, I have a habit of spiralling. For once in my career I was trying to actually address one of my flaws as a person, to try and better myself. I was hoping, with the aid of an old friend, that perhaps, I could use his beautiful maniacal partner to help my out this week, and what do I get instead? You have essentially whipped out your cock and slapped me in the face with what you just offered. That was a metaphor. Dickwood: I honestly think it couldn't hurt to speak to her. Atken: If you didn't want to help, that would be fine. I am man enough to accept that. I will not however accept that... thing... being brought up. It is not a topic of civilized discussion. It is not a part of my life anymore and it never will be again. Dickwood: Clearly there's something deeper here. Atken: Clearly you are a rampaging cockmuncher. Dickwood: You're upset, I'm just gonna... go. Atken: Please do piss off. I'll come up with something on my own. I'm going to prove I never needed her advice, that my abilities are mine. I do not need to consult or consort with those lesser than me. Which now includes you. Dickwood: Have you ever spoke to anyone about this little nerve you have? Atken: Just go. Dickwood: But you were to one that followed me here... Atken: GO!
