Going All In

9 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-9-07 An exquisite wood grain railed enclosure cradled the long oval baccarat table like a newborn child, segregating it from the rest of the main casino floor. A gray suited beast with a craftily hidden firearm stood guard at the entrance; understandable, considering the table dealt with cash instead of chips. The royal blue felt table was flanked on each end with chairs that towered over the table, where laddermen kept an eye on the croupiers and the payouts that were given; their attention to detail was hidden slightly by the formal attire every employee of the casino wore inside this area of the casino. Those two men kept their attention on the three croupiers who handled the cards, and the pit boss who oversaw the rest of the action. Like four saints dressed in black ties and tuxedos, they sang praise to the winners, and promised future luck to the losers. With a certain grace, and motions as quick as mice, they ruled over the game like royalty. But Jayce was far from frightened, it was his associates who held him back. ?It's late and you hardly rested on the plane, maybe you should wait until the morning.? Mario Trellis knew that Jayce wasn't focused on gambling, but once you're in a casino all you can think about is the scent of the money being passed around like hotcakes. ?Besides, this place closes in like fifteen minutes.? Jayce glanced over at the sign with the closing time of the tables posted on it, and a smile creased his features as he looked back at Mario. ?I thought the casinos never closed.? ?They don't,? the elegantly dressed pit boss said from the other side of the finely crafted railing. ?We deal with cash here, so we stop running the tables at a certain time, and reopen them in the morning. However, we are just about to make the last shoe for the evening, if you're interested.? While very welcoming and friendly, the smile on his face was dripping with malcontent. This is how they got people to lose their hard earned money, scamming them late at night when they don't have all of their senses. Jayce watched as all of the cards were tossed onto the table, then scooped up to be stacked before the shuffle. He glanced over to Trellis and Hartman, ?How about you two play with me? I'll put up the money and you guys can keep twenty percent from whatever you win.? ?Are you crazy,? Trellis asked. ?I'm going to bed.? ?All you have to do is sit there, Mario. That's it.? ?No. Something feels wrong, kid.? ?How about you, Rich?? The larger man with the gentle soul, that has been working with Trellis at the gym for more than six years, smiled politely then nodded his head. ?Sure, I'll play with you.? ?Great,? The pit boss replied, as he waved at the security guard stationed at the entrance to let them in. ?Take these two seats right here, gentlemen.? Jayce walked like he had money to burn, as if there was no way he could lose. Richard Hartman did not have that same attitude, he was just there to keep the kid company, and hopefully not lose too much money. As they took their seats, Jayce motioned for a waitress to bring them a couple of drinks, as Mario shook his head from the railing. Jayce took out the money he had in his pockets, handed some to Rich, then placed the rest in front of him on the table. There was a twenty dollar minimum, and as he saw Rich place a twenty face up, Jayce reached over and placed a few more. ?Bet a hundred man, I feel lucky.? Mario's eyes went wide as Jayce was handed the shoe. The croupier lifted his arm into the air, holding up his hand high in the air to keep Jayce from dealing. He then glanced around the table as other people placed their bets, then let his palm fall and beckon to Jayce, ?A card for the player.? Jayce dealt the cards in quick swift motions, one to the croupier then one to himself. Then another one to the croupier and another for himself. After a quick glance around the table, the croupier handed his cards to the person who bet the most on player, since Jayce was the dealer of this hand. The man peeked at the cards then turned them over with a wry grin. ?A natural nine,? called out the croupier. Jayce turned over his cards to find a Queen and a King... Garbage, he had busted out. ?Son of a bitch,? Jayce mumbled under his breath as he passed the shoe to Hartman, who quickly passed it to the next person in line. ?I was sure I was gonna win.? ?Come on now, Jayce,? Mario called from behind the rail. ?You had your fun, now let's get some rest. You have a big night tomorrow.? ?One more pass,? Jayce said without turning to face Trellis. ?I know I can do this.? The box slowly made its way around the table, flip flopping winners the whole time. Banker. Player. Banker. Player. Nothing consecutive until the shoe came back to Jayce. He pushed all of his money in, then instructed Hartman to do the same. He tuned out the complaints from Trellis, then proceeded to deal out twenty straight hands of winners. His pile had grown with every hand, and he never cashed out. He, as well as Hartman, kept letting it ride on every bet. Jayce felt this sudden surge of power course through his veins; it fueled his hand to deal another hand, and this one came up a winner as well. ?How much do I have there,? he asked without a care in the World. It took the croupier a while to count it out, but even after taking five percent for the bank, since that was what Jayce and Hartman had been betting on the entire time, the croupier answered, ?One Hundred Thousand Two Hundred Seventy Five dollars.? A gasp escaped from the crowd that had gathered around the railing around Trellis, who had yet to catch his breath. ?We're going to bet it all.? The place went silent. Even Mario was at a loss of words. ?I- I can't do that Jayce. You're on your own, kid.? Jayce watched as Richard stood up and joined Mario in the massive sea of people that had crowded around to view the spectacle. ?I'll bet it all then,? Jayce said as he turned back to face the croupier, who's face had dropped before he turned to get an answer from the pit boss who casually walked over as one of the guys on the big chairs talked on the phone. ?We need to check with the owner of the casino,? The pit boss said with a crooked grin. ?This is a rather large sum of money.? ?I understand.? Deep down inside, he wanted to say he counted on it. He knew who the owner of the casino was, and he knew that he had probably been watching him this entire time. He felt even more power, as if this was his way to show Shaman that he could beat him at his own game, no matter which game it was. Jayce watched as the man in the chair hung up the phone, the seconds before he gave his answer seemed to drag on for a lifetime. ?He said shut it down,? the answer deserved a fist pump, but he held his composure. ?No more bets tonight.? The answer had caused a sigh to echo out through the crowd, as they too were excited about the bet. This was about more than just the money for Jayce though, and though he fought hard to keep it contained ? he had already won the first wave of the war against Shaman. As a check was written for his winnings, Jayce never turned his attention away from the large mirrored glass on the level above him. He wondered if Shaman was biting his lip, if worry had finally begun to make its long journey through his mind and filled him with doubt. He would soon find out. When it really mattered. Where it really made a difference. =?=?=?=?=?= ?What's on your mind?? That's what Mario asked him as he stood there staring out the window. It was a strange question as far as he was concerned, but he humored him with a response nonetheless. ?I'm scared,? Jayce told him, not even bothering to lie about the situation. ?Shaman's not a small guy, and after everything I've been going through the last few weeks. Well, I'm not even sure if I belong in GCW anymore.? ?What... why do you say that?? ?Look at me,? he replied. ?Do I look like someone that a company like that can count on? They require excellence beyond all else, and I haven't done anything but lose lately. My victories have been few and far between, and I'm surprised they haven't given me the boot yet.? ?You just need to get out of your slump,? Mario said, as he attempted to calm the former Television champion. ?In a few weeks the road to Warpath will begin, and then you can show everyone that you deserve attention. That you want that spotlight. You did it last year, and there is no reason why you can't do it again.? ?But I lost in the first round last year, remember?? ?Yeah,? Mario laughed. ?I also remember that you challenged Danielle Northsky for her spot because you were not given one. Last year you proved that you wanted it. This year, prove that you deserve it.? ?That's easy for you to say,? Jayce muttered as he turned his attention away from the window. ?You don't have to get into the ring with these sick sadistic fucks.? ?I hardly remember you being a saint.? Jayce laughed at the comment. Not because it was funny, but because of the irony of it all. Earlier he had won a large sum of money from Shaman's casino, and as much as he knew he hurt Shaman's pocket books, that was the extent of the damages. How could he really compare to Shaman in the ring? He could barely take the man out when he was using a weapon, and even then he was the one who turned up hurt. There was no way he could win, not with the mindset he had. ?Jayce, you don't know how much faith President Caldera has in you, but I promise you...? Mario puts his hand on Jayce's shoulder. ?You go out there and give it your all, and you'll find out. You'll realize just how much the man that runs this company believes in you.? ?How can anyone believe in a failure.? ?You're here, kid,? Mario said reassuringly. ?How is that a failure??
