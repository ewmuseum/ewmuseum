Please Say You Love Me

2 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 2nd, 2009 He stared blankly into the bottom of his cup. She knew this because she had been watching him for close to an hour now. In that time he had ordered his coffee, drank it in small, slow sips, and hesitantly waved off the waitress anytime she came over to refill his cup. It’s not as if she meant to watch him, she simply lost track of time as she stared at him, mesmerized by his curious behavior. She argued that the only reason she even started to notice him was because she recognized him from the apartment building she lived in. It was common, after all, to be interested in what your neighbors were doing if you ran across them in public. She had to disengage herself from her mental argument when the waitress approached her with the pot of coffee, looking to top her off. Unlike the man she was watching, she politely nodded her head and lifted her cup to be refilled. Taking a sip from the bitter brew, her eyes returned to the man as if the rest of the world failed to exist. The waitress smiled. Failing to notice the waitress was still standing there, the woman was startled when she was lightly touched on the shoulder. Her head tilted up, glancing into the smiling face of the middle-aged woman. “Would you like for me to bring him a piece of pie” she allowed the statement to hang in the space between them for a moment before continuing on, “maybe let him know it is from you.” The woman instantly became flush, though not realizing it at the time. “No, I’m fine” she managed to get out, lifting herself nervously out from the chair. She lifted up her jeans, which had begun to slack down a bit, and adjusted her Mets cap. Reaching into her back pocket, she pulled out a thick brown leather wallet that looked as if it could tell some stories. Pulling a few bills from her wallet, she dropped them down onto the table. She made sure it was clear she was leaving a generous enough tip, giving an awkward smile to the waitress as she did, and turned to leave. Caught off guard, she found herself feeling like she had just face planted against a solid brick wall. Despite stumbling back, she managed to keep her feet under her. Looking up, she notices her wall is the man she’s been staring at for the past hour. “Oh, I’m sorry” he says, shuffling past her. His hand grabbing hold of the door handle and something inside her started to stir. She reached out, calling “wait!” He stopped and looked back at her. His facial features were soft, soften then she was accustomed to seeing. It caused her to run flush again, temporarily paralyzing her when he spoke. “Are you hurt?” “No” is what her mind said, but her lips never moved. They were frozen in place, unable to express how she felt. All she could manage from her remaining muscles was a light movement of her head. He turned away, walking out of the caf? and into the busy street. She darted after him, not even sure why. Her legs seemed to move lighter than usual, a little faster than they normally did. Since he was still traveling at a normal speed, it did not take long for her to catch up with him. Walking up to his side, she glanced up at his face, drawing his attention. Feeling those eyes on her, she blurted out the first thing on her mind, “do you live in the Ascension Condos?” It seemed like an odd question to ask someone, only confirmed by the weirded out expression on his face. Realizing how it sounded, she hurries to try and recover, “it’s just that you look like one of my neighbors.” She gave him a smile, trying to assure him that she’s not some crazy kind of stalker. “I’m actually heading back there way right now, in fact. I dunno if that’s where you were going.” He only replied with a nod, continuing on his way as if to say that he accepted her explanation, but would not answer back. She took his silence as a confirmation and followed beside him. The two started down the street with the woman hugging rather close to his side, at least closer than he was comfortable with. His eyes were always darting around; looking to see what was going on in their surroundings. With each step he tried to move slightly further away, but she never noticed and merely kept close to him. Every person that glanced over at the two caused him concern. He kept wondering to himself “are they taking pictures” Is this going to look wrong? What will people say?” In Scott Drake’s profession, rumors ruled and could make or break your career. They reached the complex without any incident, something that still could not make Drake feel at ease. No matter what, he could not shake the feeling that something was wrong. Maybe, he told himself, he was just ashamed of the fact that he was with another woman, even though they weren’t doing anything. Looking up at him, she came to realize she had just walked home alone. Forget the fact that she never strayed more than a few mere feet from his side, to him she might as well had been a million miles away. “I’m sorry” she said as she turned her eyes toward the concrete in shame of how selfish she had been. Snapped back to reality by her pitiful apology, Scott tilted his head toward her. “I made you walk me home like I was some kind of helpless child, despite you clearly not wanting to.” For the first time since they bumped into each other leaving the caf?, Scott looked at her instead of looking toward her. His complexion softened, “No” he said, interrupting her, “I’m the one who should be apologizing. I was very rude. It seems like it’s been so long since I last spend time in the company of someone new, that I’ve forgotten my manners.” He smiled at her, wrapping her in a warm blanket of comfort. She couldn’t help but smile back. It felt silly that just a moment ago she seemed as small as a bug, but now she could not stop herself from smiling. “You know, you seem like a completely different person when you smile” she observed out loud as she silently wondered which was the real one, the warm smiling man currently in front of her or the cold and distant man that walked her home. It just did not seem possible that both polar opposites could reside in the same body. It dawned on her just how blunt that had sounded and her face was flushed with a deep red hue. “I didn’t mean it like that!” she was quick to point out. Her tone dropped drastically as embarrassment settled in. “I’m sorry, I usually don’t say things like that.” Scott just laughed, wiping a tear from the corner of his eye, “of no, don’t worry about it. Sadly you aren’t the first person to say that, and I doubt you’ll be the last.” Hearing the intoxicating sound of his laughter, that smile crept back onto her face, spreading itself from one ear to the other. Like a drunken stupor, she could not help herself. “You know,” he said, “I just realized I don’t even know your name.” “It’s Cassidy” she replied back, sticking her hand out to shake. He took her hand, shaking it, “and I’m Scott Drake.” Slipping her hand from his, Cassidy grabbed the back of her head, “I know, I think the entire building knows you. Your mail piles up almost every day! Everyone can’t help but notice.” Embarrassment set in for Scott, “yeah sorry about that. I’m sure I’ll be getting a lot less lately.” No matter how many times he tried to keep his address private, his fan mail always seemed to find him. “If anything, I would say it increased.” Scott nearly gagged, it seemed he forgot that no matter how much fan mail he got, there would always be more hate mail, and if there was ever a time in his career he would get hate mail, that time was now. “I’ve never seen someone get so much junk mail before, I tell you, every solicitor must have you on speed dial” she said with a soft laugh, seemingly failing to realize what was actually going on. Scott gratefully took it, as he always hated going into detail about the business he worked in. He turned from her but stopped after taking only a half-step. From over his shoulder he called out “oh, I forgot to ask, you said you lived here too, what number?” Though she knew he was only being curious, she was still relieved that his back was to her. Cassidy’s face had again turned to a deep hue of red, filling her cheeks until they looked like ripe strawberries. “243” she replied. Scott froze in place, “243?” Had her heard her right? Though he wasn’t looking at her, she still nodded in agreement. “You live right across the door from me.” He tilted his head, glancing over his shoulder at her. Cassidy was still nodding. “Yep, for the past two years now.” Scott’s smile quickly faded like the sun was doing in the distant sky. How had he failed to realize, or even notice her? Two years? Thinking back, the past two years had really just been a blur for him, moving with accelerating momentum, never slowing down. It was no surprise he had failed to notice the details around him. Cassidy, however, waved it off and walked past him. “Don’t worry about it though,” she told him, sensing that he felt ashamed for not realizing it until now, “although if I see you around now, I expect a “hi”!” Scott nodded. “Definitely.” And with that she left him, though it pained her to do so. But she knew she had to leave, otherwise it would get harder with each passing second. So she did the only thing she could, Cassidy walked past him through the hall and toward the elevator. Scott would watch her go, allowing her to take the first elevator before he started off himself. He would catch the next one, but by then she would already be at her room. She was sure he would stop as he reached for his handle, making a moment to glance over at the plaque that read “243” and smile like he had before. Then he would open his door and close it behind them, leaving the hall a quiet and lonely place. Standing in just an oversized tee and simple white panties, Cassidy stared into her bedroom mirror. The red still hadn’t fully disappeared from her cheeks and she could still feel the hot ambers burning under her skin. It kept her warm, even on the surprisingly cool April night. If she had kept a journal , she most likely would have started today’s entry by saying “today was a weird day, but it was a good day.” Cassidy had done something, many things really, that she would never had been able to see herself doing. It was a snowball, each usual act leading to another, rolling faster and faster, gaining momentum until it all just became a blur. She allowed herself to fall back first into the bed, to be absorbed by the comforter and wrapped in the sheets delicately. A sweet sigh escaped her, caressing its way into the air. Her eyes closed, Cassidy would sleep tonight, waiting until she could open them and greet tomorrow. Tomorrow would be a good day as well.
