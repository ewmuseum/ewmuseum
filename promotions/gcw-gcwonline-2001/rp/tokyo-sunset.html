Tokyo Sunset

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Recently “Welcome to Tokyo. I hope that you enjoy your stay.” The customs agent says in a sweet, yet forced Midwestern accent as she goes to hand Dorian Ryan his passport. “I’m not going to be here long.” Dorian responds as he takes his passport. “Is this trip going to be for business or pleasure?” Dorian takes a moment to think about his answer. “Neither.” It is a simple, one word answer. When he got the phone call to come, he knew that he didn’t want to be there. “I’m here because I have a friend that’s in need” It pains Dorian to be here. With Dangerous Games on the horizon, this was not something that he needed to pop up in his life at this time. But he’s there anyway, in a land that was no different to him than Minneapolis. He’s spent a great amount of time there. But Tokyo wasn’t considered one of his favorite cities. Not anymore. There was too much pain, too much heartache. As he walks through the airport, he tries to ignore any of the five fans that remember him from his days wrestling out of the Japanese independents. He didn’t come to Tokyo to talk to fans about wrestling. He came because Keiko’s brother was in trouble. And his former in-laws think that he’s the only one that could help out. - - - - Tokyo’s Underground Club Scene was not a place for the faint of heart. The drugs, the booze, the crime. Sometimes, even Yakuza didn’t even come into some of these clubs. Dorian knew that he was treading on ground that he didn’t want to tread. Shoichi Magashima didn’t call Dorian because he thought that Dorian could handle himself there. Shoichi called Dorian because he knew that Dorian could be accepted there. For them, Dorian wasn’t Gaijin. He was one of them. The unaccepted. The unacceptable. That, and the fact that Dorian was a regular here before he met Keiko didn’t hurt either. “Dorian, my brotha.” one of the DJs at the club yelled as he entered the club. Dorian knew him once upon a time. “Towa, how was New York treating you?” Dorian yells back to him as he points off to the side. “Come on down, I need your help with something.” - - - - Towa sits with a bottle of Sapporo Black Label while Dorian is relaxing with a bottle of Suntory Malt’s. “Now, let me get this straight, this cat calls you all the way from Japan to find his son. I heard that you were serious about No Mercy, but I couldn’t fathom that you were that serious.” “Towa, you keep saying words like cat around here, and people are going to realize that your real name is Colby and you’re from Cameron.” “Listen, Dorian, this is my livelihood. I run the show at these places. It’s hard to get to where I’ve gotten with the stigma that I’m American. That may be accepted in the mainstream, but here, it will get me killed.” “Relax. You forget that I’ve been here too.” “There’s a difference, you were revered as a hero against the majority. You were the man that came in and told the Japanese Puro Elite that you were American, and here you were.” “And then I married this cat’s sister. Kinda lost a little bit of credibility there.” “Nonsense. They see you as the man that liberated a hot young thing from the ruling class. They see you as the man that took Keiko Nagashima away from Shoichi and made her your bride. That, to these disenfranchised youth, was more important than being Japanese. That to them, was godlike.” “Now I’m married to an Irish-Italian.” “Even better for them. You, my friend, are a hero.” “Back to why I’m here.” “Yes, Kensuke Nagashima. I’ll keep my eyes open for anything. I have some guys that I associate with that DJ at some of the other clubs. I hear anything, I’ll let you know.” “Thanks. I have a flight that leaves first thing in the morning. If you hear anything tonight, just leave a message with my hotel.” “Hotel, I don’t think so.” Towa says as he reaches into his pocket and pulls out a key. “My place is much better than any hotel that you’re going to find in Tokyo.” Towa tosses the key over to Dorian, who snags it out of midair without any effort. “Thanks.” “Just remember, don’t touch the beer. I had that stuff specially imported.”
