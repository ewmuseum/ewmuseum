Conquering El Dorado Part 1

25 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 25, 2006 In a short couplet, famed by philosophers and poets alike, Robert Frost wrote of a choice. Not just any choice, but the choice; a life altering choice, a don’t-look-back kind of choice, a kind of choice that would decide the direction of the rest of your life. It was dubbed a perfect metaphor for the human existence; a path and a fork in the road dividing two options, both trails rounding inevitable to the same destination. The only distinguishing factor, was the verity that one path, one way, one choice, was something that many before him had trudged through. In one interpretation, the path chosen provided a mystery to him; a curiosity within him that needed to be quenched. In another interpretation, the chosen path was selected exclusively for the fact that it differed from what others had chosen. Why is this specific piece so noteworthy? Well, because of its evident significance to the everyday life and the varying interpretations of this precise road. It was the preference of black or white, good or bad, up or down, the inevitable anecdotal paths to the same objective under diverse circumstances. So what was this destination, this target, this end goal? While some can go on for hours, construing the end line aspiration to be death and the road being that of his morality, when applied to the majority of professional wrestling, the ending is quite simply, fame, immortality within your ranks, the almighty victory over your oppressors? and what you are willing to do to reach it. For me, the path before me represented my redemption, a sense of reclaimed honor, a rise from my haunting disgrace. While the path behind me, laid my greatest defeat, a lingering ignominy, and my definitive failure. While others overlooked the reality that they were provided a choice, I loathed the fact that, through my own preceding blunders, my path had been chosen for me. My past actions had caused a wave of effects that are still being felt this day through everyone around me. I travel the moralistic route, the highroad, the divine path, and the righteous way. I politely wait my turn, timidly accepted criticism, and diligently train to my body’s breaking points, to assure that cheating doesn’t become a factor for me, or my opponent. Did I think myself a soldier of virtue, an altruistic warrior, or some kind of moralistic pugilist? No. I was, quite simply, a Luchador who, long ago, fell from grace. A man condemned by his past actions. I was a man clawing his way back to humanity, with the results of my errors hanging dauntingly over my shoulder; forcing my thoughts to return to what I was and what I had tried so callously to steer from. My path was drawing me ever closer to the Dangerous Games and, in no time at all, Dies Irae would be stepping into my way. With, paths crossed and two goals intersecting, I could do only one thing; announce my presence and end-line goal to a, albeit dominant, force in the wrestling force. So? GCW, prepare to finally meet La Sombra. The end-line goal for me is a path through Dangerous Games with my hand raised high. The end-line goal for me is redemption. The end line goal for me is? victory. ~~~ “I don’t see what you are upset about.” Brack hollered to me from his bike. I hesitated, gasping in between breaths as I ran beside my manager on foot. “What’s not to understand” I don’t appreciate you deciding what matches I will and will not compete in!” He thought for a second, desperately peddling to keep his bike up to my pace. “Knight is a prick” and had no right to throw you into a double booking like he tried. You have to be ready for the rumble and while others are sitting on their proverbial asses in wait for the coming supercard, we have been expending precious energies letting the GCW play you like a fool. We are doing what we need to and taking a week off to train.” Running through Mexico City seemed the only serenity I could find through the coming weeks as the focus on my legs and breath helped to keep my mind off the pain coursing through my body. It had been about a week since my? ?our? loss to Fiery Passion and my body was still punishing me for it. I had spent my nights chewing sticks of gum into submission in an effort to numb the pain flowing through my jaw and lying on my back to keep from putting too much strain on my stomach, which had turned to relative putty after having two boots rammed into my midsection. But, every morning I was greeted to the same pain shooting through my back. I was only one match into Tag Team competition and I felt like I had been hit by a truck. How did I ever expect to make it out of a match that favored twenty other competitors over me? “Yo, JD, are you still with me” You know I only did what I thought was right. We can’t let these people walk all over us like this.” Brack urged between pants. “It is not your place to delegate who and when I will wrestle. You are here to assure that when I step into that ring, I am stepping into it with worthwhile opponents. I came to GCW for a challenge, Brack! Not to be babysat! I asked if you would be ok with me joined the Ascension! That includes the responsibilities that they will throw at me; whether you have something against it or not!” I scowled as I sped through the gravel road. Perhaps it was the bumps and bruises that had aggravated my temper, but the more and more September seemed to creep around the corner, the edgier I had become toward him. My patience was wearing thin. Yet, deep down, I knew that Brack was doing everything he could to prepare me for my matches; everything that he thought was right in reference to my health and what I could handle. Yet, with an event like Dangerous Games, I knew it would be the last chance for me to solidify myself as a worthy roster member of the GCW. I grimaced, catching my breath as I slowed my pace into a brisk jog. “I’m sorry. I just”, my words were interrupted as the, all too familiar, scent of burnt lumber filled my lungs. I came to a complete halt as Brack slammed on the breaks. My eyes peered over a building that lay before me. “You feeling alright, Julio?” Brack asked, clumsily making his way off his seat. “You haven’t seemed like yourself at all this week. We’re in your home country! You should be more at ease here than anywhere else!” “I” I have had a lot on my mind as of late.” I said staring apprehensively over the massive structure. I edged my way closer side as I whispered,” I can’t believe it’s still standing.” “You know this trash heap?” Brack questioned as he set his bike against the wall. “This?” I said, running a hand over the massive walls. ??is where is all began for me.” “But, Julio,” Brack took a quick glance at the structure and then passed his attention back to me. “It’s in shambles. I figured you to be the type of guy to walk into coliseums or mainstream, Mexican arenas. This place is kind of slummy.” A smile graced my face as my mind passed back to my fond memories. “Oh, but Brack, it was a coliseum. This was the proving ground of Luchador wrestling. Anyone who was somebody got their start here. My grandfather practically put this place on the map. It was one of the greatest arenas this side of Mexico. Me, my brother, my father, my grandfather, my uncles, and cousins” we all wrestled here. We became a part of history here.” “So then what happened?” Brack asked, wiping some sweat from his brow. “I mean, with the way you talk about it, I would expect this place to still be thriving. How could a site like this have crumbled to such a” slum?” I yanked my hand from the wall as my past came rushing, full force, back to me; as if the ice cold walls had life still flowing through them. I could still remember my father carrying me out of the arena as my brother was carted out behind me on a stretcher, followed closely behind by a mob of angry fans. “It’s not important what happened to it, Brack!” “I” Don’t understand.” My puzzled manager managed to sputter through gritted teeth as his regular breath finally returned to him. “It’s not for you to understand, Brack.” I exclaimed, suddenly remembering why this particular path was one I had not run often. “But, I need you to do me a favor.” “Anything you need, JD!” “I need you to make the necessary calls?” I took a second to catch my breath and placed a hand on his shoulder. “and put me back into that card with Rufio and the Jackson Brothers.” “I will do no such?” He began to protest but quickly realized that my request was more or less not to be debated. “We need to be training.” “And we will be, Brack” I said as a smile crept it’s way to my face. “There is no substitute or experience! If Ciel and I are to become a viable combination then we are going to need to team with each other as often as humanly possible to assure that we know each other inside and outside of that ring. If that means I need to beat Rufio as well then so be it. We will prove to the world that neither of us is out and down yet.” “I need this, Brack” I pleaded, finally receiving a silent nod my manager as I started down the path once again. I did need this; More than Brack could ever understand. I need to push myself, harder than I ever have. No body likes losing, nobody likes a teams efforts at a victory thwarted by your inability to kick out. Yet, at the next WorldWide, I had the chance to redeem myself, by walking out of that show with two victories under my belt. Two victories and the necessary momentum to make the desired impact at Dangerous Games. I took a glance back at the tattered remains of a once great arena as Brack mounted his bike and sped up behind me. I knew this wasn’t the last time I would see it? and knew that, somehow, answers to an unknown question lied in wake for me there. To be continued
