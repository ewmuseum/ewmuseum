Cary's Revenge For Orkly, And The Introduction to Valchek

28 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-28-08 Orkly was buried later that day. The mourning lasted longer. For weeks after the dumpster dump, Cary Turkington questioned the morals of the world. It was his Orangutan, damn it; HIS Orkly. HIS pal. Not these low-lifes who didn't know shit from shat. For fucks sake, who the FUCK were they to say "Y'know what, Cary and Orkly? You need to stop." No. Fuck that, Cary said. "I did?" ? "Because, surely, I wouldn't use that language. I don't know why you speak like a caveman; you REALLY need to take those words of the vocab, it does nothing but sully your image." Image? Cary, I just finished the Orkly part of the story and you're worrying about a fucking image? "Well, jeez, at least I can be taken seriously over the phone!" You don't own a phone, shit bird. You don't even got enough for a pay phone. "?" Anyways, where were we? Oh, yes. Orkly, 4 feet deep in a dumpster. Cary felt that it was no longer his duty to try and survive. Replacing that instinct was "revenge". Hey jerk-head!, he'd yell to his homeless brethren, get the heck over here! And then he'd proceed to waltz on over to whatever intoxicated bum he decided to torment that day and whip the fucking SHIT out of them. YEAH! YOU THINK YOU CAN GET AWAY WITH IT? YOU THINK YOU'RE GOD OR SOMETHING? NOT WITH ME, MISTER! Until Cary's 18th he went through this every day. His eyes lost color to the blackness that consumed him. The little boy that heart was drained of the good we knew him for. HIS Orkly’it was HIS FUCKING ORKLY, and these fucking dick wads thought otherwise. BAM! WHACK! SMACK! He never killed any of these dudes. Brought them close, sure, but it was never his fists that killed them. Bones broke, skin ripped, hair pulled’but never a death. The urge to just kill these men wasn't there as he realized that not all of them were responsible for Orkly's demise. The feeling of revenge’can anyone say "Memento", for fuck's sake? "Memento." Shut up, Cary. "Well, to heck with you! I wasn't that much of an animal! Sure, I may have laid a finger on one of my fellow broke citizens, but there were always reasons!" ? "?Ok, so it was all Orkly. But you know what? Can I be blamed? Why is it that I now pay the repercussions for what happened as a child? You're vilifying me. Some kind of monster? I don't even like to THINK about combat anymore?" Even with some drinks? "?that's besides the point. That's not ME. THIS is me. Cary Turkington. I stand by my friends, my companions. Maybe I'll act like a fool, do this or that, but never, EVER deny my loyalty. Why, if they struck you dead right here’if GOD HIMSELF dropped a meteor on your head, I would spray aerosol into the sky until he tapped out and struck me down as well. But I can't let my friends, my kin, the smartest men I've seen, fall. I saw the best minds of my generation destroyed by disloyalty, starving hysterical naked, dragging themselves through the?" Ginsberg. "?Heh, you're good. But you get my point. No one understands, especially with the way your present it. I am no monster that fights for no reason, and I never was. Not once in my life was I delusional; there has always been purpose. ALWAYS. I mean, why bother with existence if I'm just a blank slate that could be placed in an Iron Lung and forgotten, allowing the world to expand and contract my chest at ease without asking my heart if it's ok with that lifestyle? No, my friend, I will not let you taint my story anymore. Here is a fiver; use the rest of your money and get us a handle of the Poland Spring." No, no, I got 7 bucks, let's treat ourselves to Mr. Boston. "Oh, that's wonderful! The best news I've heard all day! Make sure you’well, we probably can't afford olives, can we?" You serious? "?of course not. Well, adieu to you. Now, where were we’oh yes, of course! My violent days. You know, I never meant to truly inflict death or whatever on these men. But each who received a fist cosmetically turned into the men who killed Orkly. It was a terrible, horrible cycle, until one day?" BLUUUUUUUUUUE. "Oh gosh, a hassle right now?" The police men rolled up on me absolutely pounding some poor fellow. In my ignorance I thought nothing of it, but apparently you're not allowed to just completely rip someone's face apart in the middle of the day on Broadway. Needless to say, the police offered to put me up for the night, so long as I wore their pretty metal bracelets.. "Name." "Cary Turkington, defender of the underground, destroyer of those who destroy..." "Address." "The world." "?so you're a vagrant." "If you want to be ugly about it, sure." As the booking continued, the officer frustration exponentially grew, but what was I to do? When people ask me questions, I give the first answer that comes to my mind, whether it makes sense or not. So when he asked? "Height." And I answered? "Too tall to ball." I thought nothing of it. He took it sourly. "Ok, fuckface, I've had enough. We know all about your shit’yeah, that's right, thought you were sneaky beating up homeless dudes?" "Well, no. I actually think I'm a terrible sneaker. I don't even wear any footwear most of the time." The officer finished his questionnaire there and decided that I was too important for all this, escorting me to the commissioner's office. It's not every day you come across the most prolific advocate of bum violence, so it's no wonder why Charles in Charge wanted to have a word. "Is this that Turkington fellow you were telling me about?" They threw me on a chair in front of a desk that facilitated the commissioner, a Mr. Valchek according to the gentlemen who dragged me in there. He must've been important or at least good with his fists, because the officers left me in there alone. I wasn't particularly scared of Valchek, but I knew I had to give him respect: the cold, squinty eyes never alerted me whether he was about to rip my head off or if he was going over a laundry-list of chores in his head; the hands with interlocking fingers gripping tightly on the desk that one little shift might break each and every knuckle; the uniform he wore with medals and badges absolutely drowning the fabric, whether they were warranted or not; and that smirk’that "You're to do everything I tell you to do or else I'll rip your intestines out through your belly-button" smirk that made children wish he was Krueger instead. I didn't fear him, but I had to respect him. "Yes sir, I most certainly am." His eyes and nostrils simultaneously flared like he just overdosed on a speedball. "Excuse me, kid, but when I want you to talk, you talk, ok?" That was my cue for silence. "Now, unless you have anything else to say, I'll continue." Of course I didn't. "Cary’wow, what a faggotty name’Cary, it seems here that you are scum." The way he said "scum" was dirty in itself, like he wouldn't utter the word in front of his grandmother. "A hobo who does nothing but inflict pain upon his brethren. Tell me, Cary, why do you do this? WHY do you leave these bums on the street all beaten up so the tax-paying citizens of this fair city have to step over a guy they really don't know is dead or alive?" "You wouldn't believe me if I told you." "Try me." Sigh. "My-friend-Orkly-the-oragutan-was-killed-by-hobos-and-now-I-want-blind-revenge." Silence. "Ok, shithead, forget I asked. I guess it doesn't really matter anyways, because I already got a’uh’plan, I suppose, set up for you. One of those two-option plans." Fuck. "Option one is you go to court and face the charges. Which are’oh, great! Wonderful! It's like we got a copy of the fucking laws with your name at the top! Ok, let's see’indecent exposure?" "I HAD TO PISS!" "SILENCE!...anyways, where were we’oh yes, I.E, 3 counts of assault and battery, 4 counts of aggravated assault, 2 counts of assault with a deadly weapon?" "The salami sticks count as a deadly weapon?" "When you're shoving them down someone's throat to choke them, yes they most certainly do. Oh, lookie here, about 12 cases of regular assault. You know how many felonies that is, Mr. Turkington?" "I'd have to guess enough to get me in jail?" "Bingo. So that's option one. Not pretty, huh? Option two is a bit different. I drop all the charges and you never see a judge. Of course, that means you owe me. A lot." "How much is’a lot?" A lot was a lot. For my whole life I've been forced in and out of positions of servitude and what Valchek offered wasn't much different. Apparently he and a couple of the other high-end officials ran an underground fight club where they'd pick up criminals with no chance of even making the police notes and pit them against one another. The deal was if I won 10 fights in 15 bouts’which Valchek was confident I'd accomplish’my freedom would be rewarded. "And what if I don't want to do that?" "Then every piece of shit on the earth is going to know who you are. Every fucker in county, every low-life drug dealer, every crooked officer, shit even the God damn hobos are going to know who you are. They will hunt you until you are dead or close enough where it won't matter." "Uh, that doesn't work, my friend, unless I'm actually important. And if my name is Verbal Kinte." His teeth clenched together; he must have been a big fan of The Usual Suspects. Either way, I took the second option and was whisked away, living in the basement of a fast food joint. Every day I trained and once a week I was thrown into the cage. It was a weird environment. The place was barren when there were no contests, but if you put two fellas in between that chain-link cage every low-life flooded the place. The air filled with thick cigar smoke, blending with the never-ending bottles of whisky to make each breath of air intoxicating. Dogs, brought to protect the big-wigs, could always be heard above the screaming men with their incessant barks. Every fist had at least a hundred dollars clenched in it. And they all knew your name. "COME ON TURK, YOU MOTHERFUCKER!" "Get down, bitch! Cary, what a bitch name!" "You gonna get 'em, Cary baby! You gon' get him!" And I usually did. The opponents they threw in front of me were personal mirrors without the athleticism or fighting ability. My fights would never go on longer than a few minutes, with a flurry of lefts and right usually making contact at a high enough percentage to knock any guy to his feet. "Imma’Imma gon' kill you, Ca-Cary." "Mhm, sure you are. Now, if you could turn your head to the left and smile for the camera." CRAAAAAAAAACK. By the time I got to my 10th fight, there 9 Ws notched into my belt. I had swagger, I had charisma, everyone loved me and no one wanted to fight me. "Cary! Cary! Cary! Cary!" "Thank you’oh, and thank you to!...Mr. Mayor, looking stunning as usual, and your wife looks marvelous herself’ah, Giaccomo Silva, what a pleasant surprise, hitting the Grey Goose this evening?...Petey Weekes, what's your contract-hunting derriere doing in here?...Mrs. Wilson?" And so on. Fingers and hands brushed up against my torso just to get a feel for what it'd like to be me. I was a superstar’no, THE superstar. No one could defeat me. At least, in a fair match. Unbeknownst to me, Valchek and the Mayor, Frank Reynolds’the most Golden of Golden Boys’put a large bet on my opponent, which Valchek happily matched. Valchek, who isn't the brightest of bulbs, didn't read up on my opponent, a mistake that we would both pay for. When he entered the room, those who remained talking must of had their backs to him. It was as if the Mayor had a demon stashed in his mansion, ready for moments like this. He was at least 6'8", though when you're looking up at someone it's truly hard to determine, and he was built like Schwarzenegger. There was no definite pigment to the skin; every angle projected a new being, like he was constantly refreshing the intimidation factor to prevent his image from going stale. As he moved closer to the ring, the place grew silent except for the thud that accompanied every step. Inside everyone's head was the same thought: Poor Cary. But I was confident. The odds had been stacked against me before’not this drastically, but I've still climbed mountains. Before he made it into the ring I had the plan mapped out: don't let him grab me, and stay low. Those legs dragged too much to pose a threat, so as long as I stayed below the waist (ha-ha., get your laughs out of the way now) I could avoid a stiff shin to the jaw. If I maintained quickness throughout the fight I could pepper away at those tree trunks until I was ready to make my move. The plan wasn't foolproof, but it was the least I could do. The most disturbing part was the total lack of confidence everyone instilled in me as they stayed silent even as the beast entered the cage. That behemoth glared at me, like he knew I was toilet paper, and everyone in the crowd saw it. Faintly I heard someone at the back of the basement whispering into the phone to a 911 operator, asking for an ambulance to be sent in 10 minutes. Awesome. "Ok, gentlemen, no crotch shots, no eye gauges, and no fingers in the butt. Everything else is legal’FIGHT!" When the bell rung, the silence was a nice segue way to the darkness the dropped on me. He threw a faster punch than I expected and before I knew it I was waking up with the sun, the brightness pinching my eyes. My body felt dead but my mind remained alive, which was an unfortunate circumstance because that hit wasted my brain to mush. I couldn't move at all, and as I focused my vision a seagull dropped doo-doo on my forehead. There was too much pain to do anything about it; my arms laid out in a Jesus Christ pose instead of wiping it off. "It wasn't your fault, kid." The voice surprised me. From the corner of my eye I could see Valchek staring off into nothing with a cigarette in his hand, taking a drag every couple of moments. This either meant it was the end of me or a new beginning, and at this point I wouldn't be surprised with either. "What’what happened?" "Your opponent, 'I Kill, You Feel', introduced you to his fist. It didn't go so well between you, and the guys running the place dropped you off here." "How long have I been out?" "Too long. But it doesn't matter kid. You got heart. You got potential.You're pretty damn charismatic, too. So you're off the hook; free to go." Finally. "But before you go, I got another offer. My brother, Hank Valchek, runs Big City All-Star Wrestling. Ever done fake combat?"
