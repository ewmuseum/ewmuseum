Reminisce

15 November 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/15/10 September 24, 2009 Mom and dad love to take us on these family trips. We travel together, as a family, and enjoy the sights, as a family. Dad calls it the "Thorpe Family Express", which is lame, but whatever. It used to be the three daughters and the son, but then Clinton went and became, well, Clinton, so it just became mom and dad and the three girls, Kim, Sarah, and lil’ ol’ me, Alexis. And we go everywhere. It’s crazy cool. Last year it was Beijing, the year before that Rio. And dad. Geez. Dad always has it all planned out. Everything is mapped out, all the itineraries and the layout, it’s crazy. He even knows everything about where we’re going, all the cultures and customs. He’s like our own personal Wikipedia or something. So we’re barely off the flight, a couple of hours set foot in France, and dad knows of this out-of-the-way wine vineyard in Orleans that one of his professor buddies told him about. It’s supposed to be quaint and sublime, in some famous wine region or whatever. It’s cool, it’s on this hill, and I’m down, ‘cause in France, I can drink and no one can do anything about it. I’m not a big drinker anyway, but still, eighteen and no one’s gonna hassle me over a beer? How cool is that? We’re sitting at a table in the patio section, and the view is breathtaking. It’s really unbelievable. I know you’re just a notebook, pieces of paper glued together and all that stuff, but if you could see it…just, wow. Anyway, we’re all sitting there, trying out the different years of Bordeaux, and this kid is sitting like a few tables away with all these old French guys, and they’re just, like, loud. Like really, really loud. We just try to ignore him at first, try to just enjoy the moment. Dad starts to tell us the history of red wine, and as he does, these guys just start laughing. I mean, it drowned out everything around us. Mom’s kinda pissed, so is Kim and Sarah. Hell, so am I. Mom’s urging dad to say something, tell the waiter or go and talk to them, but dad doesn’t want to start trouble. He never does, you know? He’s kind of a, what’s the word…pacifist. I mean, the guy enjoys listening to NPR, for chrissake. Mom and dad are hush-hush arguing like they always do, ‘cause they never start scenes. Sarah and Kim are talking shit, ‘cause they don’t do much else but talk shit. And I’m a bit tipsy, so I’m like fuck it, I’ll say something. They all try and stop me, but I’m gonna say something. This is my vacation, right? So I walk right up to them, and I tap the kid on the shoulder. And he turns around and…wow. I must not have gotten a good look at his face, ‘cause he’s kinda cute. He’s still an asshole, though, so I tell him that I’m with my family and we’re trying to have a conversation, but we can’t do that when people are screaming at the top of their lungs. The bearded guy in a brown beret says something in French, and all the old guys laugh, and now I’m really pissed. But the kid, he’s not really laughing. He apologizes, says they’ll keep it down. I get lost in his blue eyes for a moment, but I don’t want to let on, you know? So I flip my hair back and say whatever. He holds his hand out, says his name is Triumph, which has to be a fake name. I mean, who names their kid Triumph? I call his bluff, but he says it’s true. Shows me his California ID, too. Triumph Frost. Born 1991. Seventeen. San Francisco. Hmm… I tell him to try and keep it down, but he asks if I want to sit down, have a drink with them all. I tell him I should get back to my family, but he insists. There’s something about him, the way he speaks. It’s kinda cool. I look back at my family, and they look mortified. So I sit with him for a few minutes. Everyone else is chatting in French, and I took high school Spanish, so they might as well be speaking in tongues. But him, he doesn’t take his eyes off me. Those nice blue eyes. When I finish up my glass of red, I tell him I have to go. He asks me my name before I go. I tell him Alexis Thorpe, and then I ask him if he’s gonna tell me his real name? He just smiles. September 26, 2009 God, who was that guy? Kim and Sarah won’t stop asking that friggin’ question. I don’t know, he’s just some guy. Sarah says he looked like some German kid, asks if he was German over and over. Kim says he kinda looked a bit Japanese or Chinese. When I tell them he’s from Cali, they scoff. Kim says that I should Google his name, so Sarah fires up the laptop and uses the hotel’s wi-fi to connect. I say that’s totally tacky, and that I doubt he would ever do it. My sisters are relentless when they want something, though. When I say his name aloud, the look they both give me…well, they didn’t believe it, either. And after getting past some links about some puppet dog that smokes cigars, they find some stuff about him. That’s when I get a text. It tells me to put on a dress and be at this cafe in an hour. I wonder… I should maybe think twice, but I do it anyway. I put on this cute dress and matching shoes that I bought yesterday from this funky little shop downtown and go to the cafe. There he is, waiting, sipping a cup of espresso. He’s pretty tall. Was he always this tall? I sit down, and we make some small talk at first. He asks if I Googled him yet. I laugh, try to play it off like I hadn’t, that I don’t do things like that. He wants to know if I was curious to know about him, and I tell him if I was, I’d ask. He says he was curious about me, and then he proceeds to freak me out. He knows the names of my parents, my sisters and brother. He knows where I live, my phone number, where I went to high school. I ask him how he knew all that stuff, and he mentions the power of the internet. I’m about ready to get the hell away from him, when he starts telling me about himself. That his dad died when he was a toddler. His mom died just a few years back. That he has more money than he knows what to do with, but that it’s all meaningless to him. It’s a weird thing, when people are willing to be that honest with you. So I stayed. We talked about so much, I can’t even remember half of it. I’ve never talked with a guy like this for this long. I mean, I’ve talked to guys before, but they all want to obviously get in my pants. But Triumph, he’s different. He seems pure. Real. He walked me home, and he asked if he could see me again. I said I’d give him my number, until I realized he already had my number. And before I knew what was what, he kissed me. Not some peck on the lips or teenage slobbering. A real kiss. He let me go, said he’d see me again soon. When I got to the elevator of the hotel, my head swam. My head filled with thoughts. Will he call back? Is he really that rich? Was he just talking just to talk? Is he the one? What the hell is happening? November 11, 2009 I still remember the beauty of that day along the Pacific like it was yesterday. The smell of the salt-water in my nose. His hand combing through my hair. And the ring. Jesus, what a ring. Carly and Lacie nearly shit themselves when they saw that beast. We've been living together for about two months now, and I don't even think I've explored half of his mansion. Hell, it's more like a labyrinth. Just the other day, I got lost for like two hours. I had no idea where the hell I was, and I was just trying to find a bathroom. It's been good so far. I really like him, he seems to like me. He's only seventeen, and yet wherever we go, he knows everyone and they know him. We don't wait for tables, and they shut down the store when I'm buying clothes. I feel like a princess, like Snow White or Cinderella, and I keep wondering when I'll awake from the dream, back in my Paris hotel room, never having met Triumph Frost. So when we lay in bed, and he clutches me tight, I hold on, hoping that the cruel joke won't be revealed. And if it is, that I get that extra few moments to enjoy it. We haven't told my parents about the impending nuptials, and they don't have many fond memories of Triumph as it is. I can only hope they see the man that I love like I do. I know they won't, but at least I can hope. February 15, 2010 We’ve been married around a month now, and all he ever wants to do is drink and fight. He’s been fighting underground. Bare-knuckle nasty stuff, like straight out of Fight Club or something. He takes me to the things, and I don’t know why. They fight in basements of bars and clubs, even freight yards. One time, it was on an old decommissioned luxury liner. And the fights. Jesus, it’s absolutely brutal. One guy got hit so hard in the side of his head, his eye popped out. I wanted to throw up. Everyone else screamed their heads off. It’s blood and carnage for hours on end, and I can’t imagine any of this is legal. One time the bodyguard at the door patted me down, and I spotted the holstered gun under his armpit. Triumph, he seems to thrive off of this. I’m by his side the entire time, too scared to even make any sort of conversation with these people. But there, it’s like he knows everyone. It's like he's the king of this place, and everyone knows it. These people I wonder if this is the life that I got myself into. If this is the trade-off for the money, the fancy clothes, cars, and jewelry. I ask myself again and again, why put myself through this? Why watch him put himself through this? When he jumps in the cage, I want to close my eyes and cover up my ears until it's all done. But I can't. I watch. And when I watch, every time, he shows me why he does this, because when he's in there, staring down some other battle-scarred fighter, he turns into this well-built violence machine. And when he's on, when he's throwing punches with abandon and blood splatters about... All I can see in his eyes is pure joy. Pure, unadulterated joy. It's like this is his purity, and nothing will ever make him happier. And every time, I can't stop watching. I'm hung on every moment. I'm completely and unabashedly enthralled. And when it ends, always with him as the winner, he jumps out of the cage. He's sweaty and bloody and out of breath, and I grab around his waist and pull him in close and kiss him on every bruise on his face. But when the adrenaline wears off, I realize that he almost came this close to getting the shit kicked out of him, or worse, and I want to throw up. This can't be how it goes for us. I won't become Adrian to his punch-drunk Rocky. We're both too good for that. He wants to fight, I'll find him somewhere to fight. Somewhere other than these dank basements of pure hell. I know I can find him something safer. I will find him something safer. July 15, 2010 My heart's still pounding. I'm still shaking. That guy, that...Mayhem. Jesus, he was so massive, and I tasered him. I mean, I tasered him! Holy shit, he won a title! I can't even wrap my head around it! We bolted from that arena as fast as we could. I was so scared that guy was gonna come after us and rip us apart. Jesus, he just still might. I tell Triumph we have to get the hell out of here, but he's so amped up. He had the title in his hand, and he started cracking up. He was ranting about how ugly the damn thing is, and how that bastard's now got nothing left to show for. "A troll without his bridge," he said. I'd never seen him like that. The blood from where that monster carved into his chest seeped through his sweatshirt. I said we should get him to a hospital. He said forget that, let's get fucked up instead. I could see it in his eyes again. That purity that only he knew. I wonder if this has all been a mistake. August 28, 2010 After that night in Phoenix, after the unpleasantness had passed and we aired our grievances and the pain subsided, everything seemed to right itself. He had walked out with not one, but two titles now. It was crazy, really, when I thought about it. He was the first to do that since some guy named Rich Rollins, whoever that is. I mean, over Tempest, too? F'ing crazy, you know? When we were on camera, around the house, he hated that title he had. Every time he had to put it on or show it off, it was like it offended him to be in the presence of it. I had tried to tell him, that he was a freakin' champion, but he never cared. But now... Now, when I spot him, when he doesn't know I'm watching, I can see something that I haven't seen from him in a long time. A sense of pride. A feeling of accomplishment. I just wish he would admit it to himself. He's been through a lot, but no one seems to care. They see some spoiled brat taking away something they've put years in for. Years of training and suffering and hard work and blood and sweat shed, and he comes along and takes it all away in an instant. He doesn't always help matters with the things that he says and the way he acts like this doesn't even mean anything to him, but still, if they knew him like I knew him, maybe they'd think differently. I know he cares. I know he wants this. I know he wants to win. I know he wants to be the best. I have to believe he does. Otherwise what has this all been for? November 8, 2010 Laid up in that hospital bed, it felt like I was a world away from reality. The pain pills kept me docile, and at times I was lucid, but nothing felt real. It was like I was trapped in my own personal hell. When I closed my eyes to sleep, that monster haunted my dreamscape. I’d run away down endless corridors, hide in dark spaces, and yet he’d find me. His smile, his laugh. I couldn’t escape. In between the nightmares of the things I could barely remember happening I could feel Triumph near. There, by my side, always. The family would come and go. My mom, she just couldn’t handle it. She never could watch the shows, it just tore her apart. So when she’d run from the room, tears streaming down her face, looking at the damage done, I couldn’t say I blamed her. Yet I could hear him always. He would never admit to it, but I could hear him fight back his emotions. Some nights he succeeded. Other nights they did. To hear him cry over this, pissed that he couldn’t stop all this, that he couldn’t protect me…it broke my heart. I know he thinks I’m not cut out for this, and part of me hates that he doesn’t think I can hold my own. But the rest of me, the rest that sees the bandages on my face in the mirror, the rest that feels the dope pouring into my veins, the rest that remembers those eyes of black gleaming at my misfortune…the rest understands. When I’m awake, he’s fallen asleep in the chair by the bed. He looked exhausted. I jumped out of the bed and woke him up, shaking his shoulder. He rubbed his eyes, and asked what time it was. I didn’t have the heart to tell him it was two. I just asked if we could get out of this room for a bit. We drank the shitty hospital coffee and ate from the vending machine, and sitting in that cafeteria, it reminded me of somewhere familiar. Soon, I found myself tumbling through memory after memory. Some connected, some not. It’s funny how a thing can send you falling through your own head. Back to places you haven’t been in years. They might not be exactly how you remember in real life, but it’s that you remember them. Suddenly, you find yourself lost in the deepest recesses of your own mind. And while you might wonder what brought you there, there’s that small piece that hangs, happy that you got there. That you remembered. I worry about him. I'm afraid of him growing distant. It seems like it's always him against the world. He doesn't keep much in the way of company, save for me and that...that damn monkey of his. And with Shipley gone, it's not like people are beating down the door to hang out with him. Then again, it's not likely he'd be opening the door when they come knocking. It's not like he even talks about any one of them, and when he does bother to bring any of them up, it's with begrudging respect for Rasa or pure unadulterated hatred for...ugh, Bryan Mayhem. When it's all the others, it's hardly flattering. Though you'd never know which ones he was referring to, unless you know him like I do. Still, I worry. I think about what he might do some day. I’m worried about how far he’s willing to take this. He tries not to let on that all this has affected him, but it has. Every time he looks over at the battle scars, it brings him somewhere primal, somewhere unforgiving. I can see it in those blue eyes. The ones that might lie to everyone else, but never lie to me. We sat in silence at the table, until he grabbed my hand, his words soft. He asked me not to be there, not to come with him, to let him finish this on his own terms. His own way. I told him I needed to think about it, but that I wanted to be there for him. His grip tightened on my hand, and he asked me to promise him. I looked into his eyes. I hate lying to him. I really do. And so I lied, and promised him that I would stay away. He brought me back to the room and helped me into the bed, and kissed me on the forehead. I think for a moment if this would be the last time I ever see him again. If he would ever be the same after this. If he would make it out alive. So I grabbed his hand and held it close to my chest. I told him it would all be okay. It will all be okay. It will all be okay.
