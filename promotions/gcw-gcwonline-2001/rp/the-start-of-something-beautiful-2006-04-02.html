The Start of Something Beautiful

2 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-2-06 The morphine drips that is life; Drips, drops, drips into a culvert labeled by your name We’re billions of microscopic particles, Trapped in an elliptical barrel And plummeting down endless waterfalls So, how is your life today? *** Peter Colt was a firm believer of barely believing in anything. According to him, it’s what makes the human being stronger. Throughout his elementary years, he was a pessimist and dead to the world. Strangely so, he was born a very bleak child. Doctors and experts alike were both taken aback at how he reacted when he came out of his mother’s womb; motionless, frighteningly collective, and observant. With those exceptions, he was a healthy boy and, through time, he slightly curved away from the personality barricade. Those that knew him as a kid truly considered him to be an angel or an extraterrestrial. These assumptions made things a bit difficult for the rest of the family. The media and nosiness of the neighborhood did all but imprison them within their own home. As the mother, Sister Mason, goes out to get groceries, the rest of the world seems to gaze at her because of the child she’s given birth to; a child that everyone in Sun Valley came to know as “Freakboy”. I guess this explains why there’s so much personal odium for his school years. Thing is, eighteen years of your life is school-life. So, needless to say, Peter never experienced life like you should as an adolescent. It’s interesting though, nowadays. It seems as if he’s come full circle. The childhood horror and the awful glamour of being put on the spot by the media for accusations that are as artificial as a tale have ran their course. Yes, everything seemed to have an aluminum taste to Peter. Success always came with a bargain for double or nothing. As gullible as Peter was in his younger years, he would envision life that much higher up on the ladder. When it came time to sink or swim he’d always lose control of the ship and ram it into a glacier. Failure is as imminent as wind and as common with Peter as oceanic shifting tides. He never understood the phrase, “just walk away”, because he always observed what lay beyond what we’re supposed to see and have and do. Failure forced his brain to convince his soul that nothing remotely positive was coming his way. That, in it, was more than enough to coax him into becoming an agnostic of just about everything. What’s worse was that his mother was a retired nun. It was never fun and games for her, seriously, she was soaked in religion. Refusal to hang out to dry for just once in her life, she holds the bible close to her heart and has artistically painted the walls of her home the colors she believes represents Him; blue and gold, to be exact, and with streams of yellow bridging across the original wall pattern. These days, she uses her home as protection from the “subconscious cesspool of sin”, as she calls it, and has somewhat lost all hope for her son. Yes, hopeless for her very own son and soaked in religion. What’s wrong here? Why has this happened to their relationship? Why is it torn apart like so? There’s no better answer than “colliding personalities”. They are two different spices categorized in two different cabinets. Peter wasn’t a religious boy and he isn’t a religious man. Like an endless barrage of lilly pads migrating down a rivulet, he kind of always went in the same direction. This is the direction used mainly by the free bird, spiritual youths willing to experiment with their lives. Peter loved experimenting. It made him feel alive and refreshingly natural. It hurt him, though, in many ways without ever knowing until later in life, when the dust was cleared. *** Sailing streams of gravity Ever so slowly drifting in perpendicular patterns Fading away like the suffocation of a flame Our last breath is unpredictable? And who can judge any differently? For we are all robots on the loading pad Being packaged and shipped to toy stores We are the playlands of what is real above us Again I say, how is your life today? *** Unfortunately, 2112 Glen Hills isn’t some special nesting in West Hollywood built for the luminary and grown-into-wealth scum. Then again, the hills aren’t all they were thought up to be when “The Sound of Music” came out. Now? Now, we’ve got doll-headed, eyeless children prowling them in search for human flesh. Right, so, this is where our boy Peter lives. It’s on the corner and is next to a barber shop called “Claylays Do” Shop? and a Mexican grocery that carries cheap milk and cigarettes. The traffic is absolutely painstaking through most of the week and the beeps of irritated commuters are enough to send you to a psyche ward if you happen to be a resident. Peter was forced to endure a two-week stay at a low-scale psyche lab in Wyoming, the mountanous destination that our very own 'Freakboy' hitchhiked to. His mother, Sister Mason, noticed that he hadn't called her in months. After a failed attempt at locating him in the vincinity he was supposed to be living within, she called the police and mapped out a search party. They found Peter in a local Wyoming bar, 'Neon Lights', scarfing down beer after beer to drown his woes of realization that God was, in fact, not in the basement of a chapel in Acme. When police got word that he was on a journey to literally locate God in Wyoming, they commited him. To this day, Peter finds it strange that he was picked up and shipped off to a mental asylum in less than two days apart. An added note, he hadn't spoken to his mother in seven months and it remained that way while he bunked within padded walls. To be honest, I don't think either of them wanted to spesk. It just kind of seems a bit awkward. Seven months have passed and the one reason you're going to see eachother is to say 'goodbye and goodluck' to a newly labeled psyche patient and to a deliriously religious mother. Should we stick our hands in the other issues? What about his degenerate gambler of a father who's on the run from strange men who drive black Cadilacs and suit themselves in tuxes with yellow roses peeking out of their shirt pockets? Or how about Anna Lee? A hooker that meets up with Peter while he's in the depths of his own insanity; doing drugs, stealing, and living in the underground subway utility stations in New York City for two years. The utility stations is exactly where he meets her and it's just the beginning of two years of tension, turmoil, and intensity. This is Peter Colt's life. An 'avant-gard' soul with just one wish out of life; to succeed. It's no longer about scoring dope, or getting high until your eyes get blurry, or getting wasted in the middle of nowhere and waking up in the same place, but without shoes, socks, and a shirt in the freezing winter weather. It's about aiming at one goal, one three-point hoop, that you'll forever remember. It's not about creeping to get by, or considering death by self doing. It's abour doing what you think is in your best interest. Peter? What do you say? 'Let's do it.' Ding. Ding. Diiiiiiing.
