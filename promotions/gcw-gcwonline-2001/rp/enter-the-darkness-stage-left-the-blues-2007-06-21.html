The simple answer: a man who appeared out of nowhere in late 2000, taking the wrestling world by storm. Although he made his mark fairly quickly on the wrestling scene as a rookie in the up-and-coming independent World Class Wrestling Federation, having several epic battles with Jester and Berserker, former Asylum members, he wouldn’t hold his first singles title until more than a year and a half later. It was a slow start, the lighting of a fuse that would continue to burn for more than seven long years. WCWF saw Jackson involved in Knights of Anarchy, to the Unholy Alliance, to the mysterious Fate trifecta under the tutelage of Nathanial Duke. Jackson moved on, calling other places home, capturing titles and accolades in some of the day’s most prestigious wrestling organizations. Like the rest of us though, he always found his way back to WCWF, eventually making his way into the hearts of the locker room, and the fans alike. Love him, or hate him, you had to admit that Brad Jackson was a man that never lacked charisma, or the in-ring talent to back it up. Who is Brad Jackson? A man who seems to have more enemies than the United States under the skillful leadership of George W. Bush. A man of few words, who’s been known to write some of the most scathing remarks ever seen in print. A contradiction. An enigma, wrapped in a mystery, locked in a Chinese puzzle box, for lack of a better term. A man I once called friend. I began this article hoping to shed some light on the mystery. It was no easy task, I’ll tell you that. I tried calling his past places of employment. Global. Super Athletes Wrestling Federation. Freedom Independent Wrestling, Xero Wrestling Academy, Pro Championship Wrestling. All but one of those had become nothing more than footnotes in wrestling history, their doors closed forever. Even more recently, Asylum Underground Wrestling, which has mysteriously also closed their doors. I almost wonder if Jackson brings this destruction with him now, if these places would still be thriving if he had never graced their ring. Such thoughts are superstitious at best, and as a seasoned reporter, I know better than to clutter my column with nothing more than ridiculous supposition. Still, it seems almost uncanny that Jackson is tied to so many places that have faded away after all these years. Wrestling, as a whole, is a fickle business, and these days, promotions spring up and die off almost as quickly as their talent gets injured. Disposable heroes, these modern day gladiators, but one man always seems to still be standing when the dust settles. That man is Brad Jackson. We’d all heard about his impressive upset over the feared and revered Spiral in PCW. I was watching that Pay-Per-View, and I’m not ashamed to say I cheered. Screamed my head off. Love him, or hate him, you have to give the devil his due. He wanted that win and after six long months of enduring every punishment imaginable at the hands of the monster named Spiral, everyone around the world wanted him to get it. He deserved nothing more than to have his hand raised in victory. Tell me one fan out there who doesn’t agree, and I’ll show you someone who hasn’t watched a televised match in the last seven years. Jackson is a household name, precious commodity to any federation he signs with. Listen to me, you’d think I’ve gone mad. Quite the contrary. I’ve always been a fan, even though at times I can’t stand the guy. Twelve federations, and seven world titles later, Jackson had found himself to yet another proving ground, the home of the best.
