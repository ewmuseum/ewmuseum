Backseat Thinker

Date: Day after Yesterday Sitting in the back of the rental car, driven by Alex Morgan, his new old bodyguard, he’d had a chance to study both of them. Window down, chain smoking, he silently watched Sheena and Alex. There was an easy camaraderie that hadn’t been there previously. In the old days it would’ve been himself and Sheena in the back, hands all over each other, while the silent figure in the front drove, saying nothing, simply watching the highway pass. Now it was different, the two spoke at length, about everything, nothing, the topic didn’t matter, they could just talk. Always before if the two had been forced to interact it would’ve been a few short mumbled words from Alex, usually egged on by something flirty and bordering on obscene from Sheena. Now, just conversation. It shouldn’t really bother him but it did. These two in just a couple months, a few short weeks, had completely changed, grown. While at the same time he was static, the same man he’d always been a little immature maybe, definitely an asshole, just Michael Stevens. And yet they now seemed to be fully functioning individuals, while in the past it’d been almost as if they couldn’t have survived without him. They were whole, and he was still empty, it hurt, and was just a little bit scary. He tried to tell himself, over and over again, they didn’t matter they were his servants, his lackeys. Underlings, he shouldn’t give a damn about their private lives, instead just remain smugly secure that he was their better, and always would be, the thing was, watching them now, he wondered if any of that was true. Having spent time outside his influence, Alex was clean, you couldn’t smell the familiar reek of stale marijuana smoke, his eyes were clear not glassy. He couldn’t remember the last time he’d heard Sheena make anything close to a sexual remark, hell she hadn’t even flirted with him really since they’d been back together. Sighing deeply, he flicked the used up butt out the window, watching it’s bright orange course over the jet black of the road. Hitting the ground it exploded, a miniature fireworks display, just for him, or anyone else he cared to watch. He’d been watching a lot more of the simplest things in life recently, all in an effort to get back to his roots, back to the man that’d taken the professional wrestling world by storm seven years ago. Somewhere along the line he’d lost that spark, that fire, the drive that’d led to his nickname “The Spitfire”. Such a nice little nickname, bringing to mind images of quick witted, smartassed children, maybe the plucky young paperboy who’d deliver his papers even if he had a broken leg. That’s what you thought of when you heard someone say Spitfire, and for a while you even thought of Michael Stevens. The name really did fit there for a while, he was explosive, exciting, and wouldn’t back down from a fight from anyone. Now, he’d dodge any fight that he didn’t think would be an easy win. Sure the confidence that’d made him nearly invincible for so long was still there, sometimes. Just not all the time, not anymore, and with age had come a peculiar kind of maturity. The maturity that lies in the knowledge that you aren’t invincible, that anyone on any given day can beat you. It’d be a huge lie to say that maturity hadn’t been one of the major catalysts for the behavioral change. Instead of stepping up, any time any place, he’d focus on ruling from the shadows, either by using agents, or just trying to break someone emotionally before he ever had to actually step into the ring with them. It wasn’t a bad way to be really, hell it worked and pretty well, but it wasn’t where he wanted to be, not anymore at least. He couldn’t just turn off his brain, stop playing the mind games, he was good at them, so why not? Besides only a fool or a coward, won’t use every weapon in his arsenal. It was the stepping up more often that he really needed to work on, no more trying to protect his spot, no more trying to use whatever backstage influence he could scrape together to hold down those that might possibly threaten him. Instead he’d have to start proving it again, get back the respect he’d lost along the way. He deserved to have his spot, be up there at the top of the card, it’s where he belonged, but more and more he’d heard rumblings that he was washed up, done. A year ago, it wouldn’t have bothered him, he’d just turn a deaf ear, and use money and clout to crush those who’d spoken against him, and if push really came to shove, he’d step up, and show shades of his former glory. And really that was almost sadder than if he’d really just lost it. If he’d just been broken down, too many years, too many injuries that would’ve been one thing, but no he’d been lucky, escaped any real serious damage. No he could still be great, if he just pushed himself, but he’d been at the point where he didn’t want to push himself, instead he’d prolong his career as much as possible by only doing the bare minimum. Just enough to get by, and justify himself. Now, watching these two, he realized just how hallow all of that was, these two people who he’d surely kept from reaching their full potential had crawled out from under his thumb, and become better for it. This was the example he should be striving for, instead of just getting by, to be whole, to be great, that was what he meant by going back to his roots, it’s what he’d have to do. It was in that moment when the revelation struck, that he realized he hated them. From the deepest blackest part of his heart and soul he literally despised these two, who had grown while he had stagnated. Still he couldn’t help but love both of them as well, for even as great as they had become, they’d returned to him. Perhaps it was nothing more to them than some strange underlying feeling of obligation for bringing them together, or maybe they wanted to help him they way they’d helped themselves. He didn’t know, but either way he still loved them, and hated them. “You want a soda or something?” It took him a minute to realize he was the one being spoken to, even longer to realize the car wasn’t moving anymore. So deep had he been in thought that the world had disappeared around him. Sheena’s voice sounded professional, there wasn’t really any better way to put it. Still maybe, just maybe there was a spark of friendship in the voice, at least he hoped so. There were times when he could speak to them, and almost feel like part of the group. And then there was a time like now, just the boss sitting in the back, the guy who’d call the shots, but that you’d never invite out for a beer. She was staring at him now, probably getting a little impatient. It’d been a simple question and he still hadn’t answered, just looked forward, kinda surprised. He was going to have to say something or he was afraid he’d just place another wedge between them, as if there wasn’t enough space already. “Yeah, a soda would be good. Why’d we stop anyway?” “You know rentals, they never give you more than a quarter of a tank. We didn’t want to push it and risk breaking down.” “Ah yeah, damn rentals.” It was a really weak joke, and you could barely even classify it as such. But it was something to try and break the ice, he hoped for a chuckle, he got a small smile. Well, at least it was something. “So, what’d you want?” “Whatever, surprise me, I trust you.” Always sucks when they don’t laugh at your jokes, but when you’re being serious, you get a laugh. God, had it really come to the point where he couldn’t even profess he trusted these people without them believing it was at best a joke, or at worst an out and out lie? Guess the bed had been made a long time ago, so he had to lay in it, no matter how uncomfortable. Watching her go, he realized he should’ve offered to let her stay in the car while he got the sodas, maybe even let both of them stay while he pumped the gas, and went inside. That would’ve been the nice thing to do, the thing a friend would do. Instead he just sat in the car, watching the big man pump gas, while the lady went inside for refreshments. For the first time he actually felt bad for being an asshole, damn, this wasn’t good at all. He resolved to have a talk with both of them, once they got to the hotel, hopefully he’d get a chance to talk with both individually, but if not he could talk to both at once. For now though he’d wait, and think, maybe try to pick a little bit more of their conversational rhythms, it probably wouldn’t help but hell it was worth a shot. It was always easier to talk to someone once you could mirror the way they spoke, it was supposed to make them feel more comfortable, or something like that. Having filled the tank, Alex slide back into the car and behind the wheel. Drumming his fingers against the steering wheel he didn’t even bother looking back at Stevens. A moment later, Sheena arrived as well, handing back a strawberry soda, she smiled just a bit. “Let’s go Alex.” He started up the car, and pulled back out onto the road. Watching them both silently from the back, Stevens lit another cigarette, and tried to formulate exactly what he’d say. ~A continuation is assured.~
