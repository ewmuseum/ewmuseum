Breaking Away Is Never Easy

26 June 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/26/08 “WHAT”!” Tim Lester was furious as he slumped into his ratty chair at the Motel 6 on Wisconsin Highway 38. He was due for a promotional appearance at the arena for WorldWide 92 in an 90 minutes; it wasn’t time for aggravation. But there he was, getting an earful from his producer, Phil Diamond, on his cell phone. “Hey, it wasn’t my call this time. Don’t fucking yell at me.” “Well, I’m sorry Phil, but I shoot messengers. You should have known that by now.” He put his left hand over his eyes, trying to mitigate the headache he knew was coming. “Look, it’s just a couple of re-shoots. It ain’t that bad.” “Easy for you to say, Napoleon. You’re out there, sitting in your fucking trailer, waiting for your beeper to go off or for your next slasher flick to go into production. Me” I’ve got steady work now.” “Oh look at you, Mister Wrestling Hero, one match and you’ve already got steady work. I thought that losers didn’t last long in that business.” He felt like smashing the phone in a million different pieces, but he wasn’t going to let Phil get to him this time. Remember, Timmy, you don’t have to deal with him anymore after all this. “Real easy for you to say, tough guy. You’re lucky...” “I’m lucky, what” You know what would happen if you laid a finger on me.” “Ooh, I’m shaking in my boots, Napoleon.” “Well, you should be, because if you don’t come back here and re-shoot like the studio wants, they’re gonna sue you for breach of contract. And then they’re gonna file an injunction against GCW blocking them from having you anywhere near their productions. And you’re gonna have to do the re-shoots anyway...” “And they’re gonna lose money on the deal through all the legal fees. We’re not talking Paramount Studios here, Phil, and you know it.” “Oh, you’d be surprised at how well the overseas market for the crap they put out pays.” He shut his eyes hard, almost squeezing brain out of his ears. “Can’t they push it back” I’ve got a match against Jacob McKail, he’s one of the best...” “Listen, I don’t give a fuck if you have a match against the reanimated corpse of Angelo Fucking Deville. The studio’s already under the gun on releasing this fucking DVD. Be here tomorrow or else they’re filing suit.” “But...” Beep. The call ended. His Honda Civic skidded out of the parking lot and onto the road like it was the Camaro he drove in Yeti Cop. The trip was like a blur, going by as quick as a paparazzo’s flash. Not like Tim ever experienced that, outside of the time he was accidentally behind Britney Spears? limo. He burst out of his car, feeling his right pants pocket. “Now or never.” He rushed in through the talent entrance and wildly looked around for someone who might help him. First one he saw was Sally Ford, striding down the hall, looking flustered. Nah, he thought, she can’t help me. He took a hard left and burst into the mess hall, but he only saw Brad Jackson. Nah, he thought, I want to be inconvenienced, not incapacitated. Slipping out of the mess hall, he found who he was looking for... ---- John Quinn was a member of the staff in Milwaukee for a long time. Pot-bellied, but not obese, he had toned arms and legs with little body-fat, the earmark for having worked in a physically demanding position for the time he had been. He was looking forward to a light summer. The Bucks hadn’t made the playoffs, and summer breeds outdoor concerts. Those damn wrestling events though, they annoyed him. White trash fans, boorish talent bordering on sociopathic, shady promoters. He’d seen a lot, enough, he thought, to justify him calling out sick. But he had never met Tim Lester. The stubbled, bedheaded superstar turned to him and pulled a pair of brass knuckles from his pocket. “Whoa, whoa, whoa... what the hell are you gonna do with those?” he asked, fearing the worst. Tim’s look went quizzical. “Oh, well, I ain’t gonna do anything to you with these.” Awkward pause. “I want you to do something to me with these.” Now it was the stagehand’s turn to look baffled. “Huh?” “I want you to break my jaw.” “You... you’re serious?” “Serious as death.” A look of concern came over John’s face. “What, you afraid of whoever you’re going against?” “Oh, oh, God no. If you break my jaw, I can still wrestle. But I can’t speak.” “Why don’t you wanna speak?” “It’s... complicated.” “How do I know you ain’t gonna use this for some...” “Trust me, I’m not. Please, just do it for me.” John looked around the hallway. Sweat beaded on his forehead as Tim insisted the knuckles upon him. Finally, he took them on, slipping them on his left hand, a natural southpaw. He cocked back, aiming at Tim’s jaw line. Just as he was about to... “What the fuck is going on?” Both Tim turned around; John dropped the knuckles. It was Bruce Campbell. “Bruce?” “Yeah, I was just stopping by to check out how you were doing.” John started to stammer. “You can go now, buddy. I’d like to talk to Tim, one-on-one.” John left, obviously weirded out. “Tim, what’s going on here?” “Uh...” Tim was surprised ? not that Bruce was there, but at the timing. “I came here to see how you were doing after I heard you left the biz. Then, I find you here, and you’re letting some guy hit you in the face with brass knuckles?” “It’s... a long story.” Tim avoided looking into Bruce’s eyes. “Really, well, I’ve got some time. Tell me, is the wrestling thing not going as well for you as you thought?” He sighed. He had to tell the truth. “It’s going good so far. I mean, I lost last week, but it felt natural again. It felt good. It’s just...” Another awkward pause. “It’s the studio, isn’t it, Tim?” “I figured you’d understand, you know. I figured that being a big movie star wouldn’t cloud your judgment, you of all people.” Bruce smirked at the wry sarcasm. “Yeah, yeah. So, let me attempt to venture a guess. You got some bad news, like some re-shoots. And you don’t want to do it, but they’ve got you by the balls. So you need a way out, and you figured the only way to do it was getting that guy to punch you in the throat and smash your Adam’s apple. Right?” “Yeah, well, up to the Adam’s apple part. I was going to have him break my jaw.” “My idea was better.” Tim nodded. “Yeah, that’s why you’re in Spider-man and I’m on the cutting room floor for Batman Begins.” “Mhm.” Another awkward silence. “You know,” Bruce said, “the Timmy Lester I know would never be taking the easy way out.” “What” Intentionally injuring myself ain’t exactly easy, Bruce.” “On the surface,” Bruce replied, taking a sip from his coffee, “but let’s just look at what you’re doing. You’re sleazily avoiding confrontation as a quick fix that isn’t going to work. I mean aren’t you the same guy who threatened to throw a studio exec’s son out a four story window if he didn’t buy you safety nets?” Tim nodded. “And the same guy who kicked the crap out of a co-star because he missed the same cue 15 times?” He nodded again. “And the same guy... let’s just forget the pleasantries, Tim, you don’t back down from anything. Why now?” Tim sighed again and looked away, almost ashamed to look Bruce in the face. “I just want to break away.” Bruce nodded. “You know, if you got your jaw broken, you know what they’d do, right” They would just postpone the re-shoots. They’d get you again. And then out of spite, they’d enforce some arcane clause in your contract, and you’d never break away. You’d be in it forever. Do you want that?” Tim looked up, straight into Bruce’s eyes. “Hell fucking no.” “Then you know what you have to do.” “Yeah.” Tim shook Bruce’s hand and walked away. Before he exited, he turned around to Bruce one more time. “Hey Bruce... if anyone asks where I went... just tell “em I’ll be back in time for my match.” Bruce nodded. Tim left let the door slam behind him. He got back into his Civic and turned the key. He had a new place to go. The airport. TO BE CONTINUED
