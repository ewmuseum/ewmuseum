Date: 04/09/10 ---- Maynard Summer 1995 ---- Clayton says we’re perfect for one and other, her and I. I’m not entirely sure, but I hope he’s right. I could commit to the rest of my life with Sara right now and be the happiest man in the entire world. If nothing else, I know that much to be certain. “What are you thinking about, Maynard?” She asks sweetly, brushing my hair behind my ear. She’s sitting cross legged across from me on our favourite park bench. She smiles at first, but pouts a little before she speaks. “I hope it’s about a haircut.” “No,” I laugh. “Not a haircut.” She frowns. “It’s almost to your shoulders!” It was, and I liked it. But I’d shave it to the skin for her. If she asked me to. “So what are you thinking about then?” “Oh, just stuff,” I say. “You know, just trying to figure some things out.” “And what does the ever-confident Maynard McGarrity still have to figure out?” It sounded sarcastic, but there is a lot of truth behind what she said. I mean, I’m about to graduate with honors and with a full wrestling scholarship to one of the most respected wrestling schools in the country: the University of Iowa. Iowa not only won the Division I championship the year before last, but many, many times before that as well, including complete domination from 1978 to 1986. Yes, it was true -- the next four years of my life were already pretty much planned out for me, but that doesn’t mean I don’t have anything to consider. After all, Iowa is a long way from Washington and as we’ve discussed, Sara would be attending WSU in the fall. I know we’re young, but I’m hoping I can change her mind. “I have a lot to figure out, Sar”, I say seriously, running my fingers through her golden hair. Lavender. It always smelled of lavender. “A lot.” “You’re going to be a Hawkeye though,” she sighs, touching my cheek affectionately. “What else are you going to really have time for, you know?” “Oh, don’t be like that ..” “Be like what?” She asks dumbfounded, but she knows what I mean. “I don’t know,” I have to be careful how I phrase myself, in fear of upsetting her further. “You sound bitter, I guess” She frowns at me, apologetic. “I know, Maynard. I’m sorry. But can you really blame me? For goodness sake, it’s hard to get excited about you moving two thousand miles away. I’m happy for you and everything, please don’t get me wrong, but I’m … you know, I’m going to miss you. Maynard I ..” She pauses. After eight months, we still haven’t said those three little life-changing words. I can tell that’s what she wants to say. “ .. really care about you, you know that don’t you?” I smile, despite my disappointment. “I know, Sara. I really care about you too, and I know that this move is really going to put a strain on things, but --” “Astrain? Maynard, it’s going to put more than a strain on things”, she’s got tears in her eyes now. “I mean, how do you expect us to survive this?” “What do you mean? You know I’ll be home on holidays -- summer break!” “Oh! Thank goodness I’ll see my boyfriend for a few days every five or six months!” She cries sarcastically, blinking back tears. “Maynard, we’re just kids!” “Sara, please! Don’t do this. We can talk about this another time, I don’t leave for Iowa for another six weeks!” My hands are on her shoulders, trying to calm her down -- defuse things so that we can talk about this more rationally. “No, Maynard,” she groans, pushing my hands away. “I don’t want to talk about this later. For goodness sake, there is no later for us!” “Sara, please ..” “What’d you expect me to do?” She’s crying hard now, thick wet tears streaming down her face. Her words slur together as though she’s been drinking, she’s becoming harder to understand. “Sit by the phone, patiently waiting for you to call? To write you letters every day? This isn’t a movie, Maynard! I’m not Meg Ryan and you’re not Tom Hanks! Life’s not a fairy tale!” “Sara ..” “I can’t do it, Maynard! I just can’t! I’m not strong enough.” “ .. I love you.” ---- Maynard Present Day ---- Clayton is my best friend and everything, but sometimes he is one seriously annoying son-of-a-bitch. “Clayton!” I shout up the stairs at him. “What the fuck is taking you so long?” The little fucker has been upstairs getting ready for twenty-minutes at this point, and I’m seriously starting to lose my fucking patience. I just want to get to the club. I’m starting to get that fucking feeling again: loneliness, Clayton says but that motherfucker doesn’t know what he’s talking about. Maynard McGarrity is a lot of goddamn things, but lonely just a’int one of ‘em. All I need is a stiff drink in my stomach and woman on my arm, and all things will be cured. “I swear to God, Clay,” I shriek up the stairs again, stomping my right foot as hard as I can. “If you’re not down here in the next five fucking minutes, I am coming up there and dragging you down these stairs by your goddamn cock. You hear me?” “Language please, Maynard,” comes a small but stern voice behind me. I whirl around to meet the black eyes of Clayton’s master, Olivia. She must have come in the front door without me noticing. She’s a short woman. Short and fat. I can’t stand the sight of her. I hate the way she curls her upper lip when she speaks to me. “My deepest apologies,” I spit in her direction, trying my best to sound as impertinent as possible. “It will never happen again, ma’am.” She rolls her eyes as she takes off her coat, hanging it neatly in the closet. “So where are you taking my husband tonight, Maynard?” She asks with an edge. I smile. “Out”, I reply simply. “Will you be home late?” “Maybe.” “Are you going to be drinking? Again?” “No, no! Of course not!” I say sarcastically, my left hand on my heart and my right in the air like a pledge. She somehow manages to look down her nose while looking almost straight up at me at the same time. Pretty impressive. “Maynard, my husband has a job --” “I have a job!” “A wife --” A scrunch my nose at the word. “And a life, Maynard. He can’t be out drinking his face off every other night!” I put on my best pair of doe eyes. “Yes, mother. Don’t worry, I won’t let him have too much. Son-of-a-bitch can’t hold his liquor anyhow.” She frowns angrily, poking me in the chest. “Watch your mouth when you’re in my house, Maynard.” I stand up as straight as I can and put my hand to my forehead in an over-animated military salute. Yet again, she rolls her eyes, this time waddling out of the room at the same time. “At ease, Solider!” Booms Clayton’s voice from atop the stairs. He’s wearing his best blue blazer over a white-with-blue-stripe button-up shirt and an elegant pair of black dress pants. He even shined his best fucking shoes. Motherfucker looks like he’s going to the in-laws for Thanksgiving dinner, not a a goddamn strip joint. “Jesus Christ, Clay. The fuck you wearing?” “Language!” Screams the shrew from the next room. I ignore her, as is my regular routine. Clay checks himself up and down, glancing at the mirror on the wall next to him. “What? What’s wrong with this?” He asks stupidly. I lean in close to that bitch won’t hear me. “We’re going to a fucking strip club, not the goddamn opera!” “So a guy can’t look nice?” “The fuck you looking nice for, anyways? Who gives a shit?” “Again, a guy can’t just look nice? What’s wrong with looking nice?” “You fucking trying to pick up or something?” I laugh. The bastard is not only wearing cologne, but he’s shaved, gelled his hair similar to that Robert Pattinson guy, and I think he even polished the frames of his Elvis Costello glasses. If this son-of-a-bitch isn’t trying to get him some extra-marital pussy, I’ll be damned. “No!” he insists angrilly. “I just take a little pride in my appearance. You could learn something from me, you know?” “And what’s wrong with a Floyd t-shirt, cargo shorts and Chuck Taylor’s?” I ask seriously. I’ve always thought the subtleties of my wardrobe went unnoticed far too often. “He’s right, you know,” comes a shrill holler from Clayton’s ol’ ball and chain. “You look like a homeless man. When are you going to cut that greasy mop of yours anyways?” “Never!” I shout back. No one talks about my hair like that. “Not ever, not for anyone! Never gonna happen! Clayton interjects, sensing a situation rising between me and his woman. “Maynard, we’d best get going.” “Yeah,” I scoff quietly to Clayton. “Anything to get me away from that horrible fucking woman.” “Come on, man! That’s my fucking wife”, he snaps angrily, opening the door and stepping through it. “Yeah, I know. Sorry,” I reply honestly, shutting the door behind me. ---- Maynard Summer 1995 ---- “Maynard,” she pleads, tears still streaming down her face. “Don’t ..” I put my arm around her. She rests her head on my shoulder. “What do you mean, ‘don’t’?” I ask softly, afraid of the answer. “Just,” she begins slowly as though she’s trying to find the proper words, “it doesn’t have to be this hard, you know? There’s no reason we can’t just have a wonderful six weeks and then take it one step at a time after that. You know, ‘no strings attached’”. I can feel my stomach in my throat. My heart sinks to the seat of my pants and my breathing becomes labored. Sara is the one -- the girl I’m supposed to spend the rest of my life with. I think I’m gonna be sick .. “What if,” I start, swallowing hard. “What if that’s not what I want? What if I want more?” “We can’t, Maynard. It’s too far. It’s too hard.” “But ..” “But what, Maynard?” Her cheeks are covered in her tears and smeared eye make-up. It’s hard for me to know how much this hurts her. But if this hurts so bad, surely there is hope for the future, right? “What do you expect us to do?” ”Get married. Run away to Iowa together”, I don’t say. “I don’t know, Sara, but do you really just want it to be over? Just like that?” She shakes her head, burying her face in her hands. “I don’t know what I want, Maynard,” she sobs, frustrated. I put my hand on her lower back, leaning forward to try to catch her eyes behind her tiny hands. “You never said it back. What does that mean?” “I never said what?” She almost-snaps. “What does what mean?” With my heart beating a violent rhythm in my chest, I swallow hard. “I -- uh. I told you that I --”, my voice cracks slightly. The pitch of it raises an octave. “I told you that I loved you.” Silence. For an excruciating moment, she makes no sound. I can’t even hear her breathing. All I can do is hold my breath, my heart still pounding so hard I can feel its pulse over every inch of my body. Then she explodes forward, wrapping her arms around my neck and sobbing uncontrollably. “Of course I do, Maynard. Of course I love you.” I hold her head close to my body and say with a smile, “then everything is going to be okay.”
