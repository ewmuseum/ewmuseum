Pines of the Past: 2 (pt. 2)

16 July 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/16/11 The grave stone, etched with sorrow, crippled the man as he kneeled beside it. His hair was blonde, yet the gleaming moon shed a cast of gray upon it, giving forth images of a much older man. His hand shook, but nothing more. Not a drop from his eyes, nor a thorn to his heart. The sensations within his body were few and far between, and any such sensations were only driven by the unfamiliar atmosphere. It had been a very long time, and the smells lofting throughout the air hinted of times long ago when things were simple, and nothing seemed out of place. Even if it always was chaotic, it was now unperceivable, unreal, and the voice ripping through the smells was only that of Tony. His head jerked to the side and peered toward Tony, who stood a few feet away with his back to a Pin Oak. His lips moved, "What was he like." William addressed the question and turned his attention back onto the grave stone. The sound of roaring chainsaws erupted and sent chills down and throughout his body. His head nodded softly and his response went like this. "I wish I knew." Tony took it for what it was and didn't bother to question William, who was kneeling be forth the grave with his head tilted downward. As he remained knelt at the foot of the grave, Tony walked closer and stood behind and scanned the tomb stone with his eyes. A bit of wind caught his comb-over and sent it dancing upon his head, and his eyes continued to roll back and forth with motions arising from his lips in the form of words. 'Here lies Jack Edward Curr. A Father and a Friend. 5/10/56 - 2/4/02' "He was a man of few words, and when he did speak it was mostly about the business." William stood up from the grave and turned to face Tony, who looked at Curr and took in the statement which he did not expect. "The business," Tony questioned, turning his head to find William walking down the slight slope and towards the car. The sun radiated downward onto the two men, and the blue mass flooded the skies with puffs of white clouds scattering about here and there. "Logging business. You see, he came back after leaving my Mom. I guess you could say he was meant to be out here, away from the buzzing crowds of Chicago and within the pristine forests of Oregon." Tony nodded his head and responded quickly, "At least he was happy, right?" "Yeah, I think he was." William smiled a bit as he let the last word roll of his tongue. After cherishing a memory with his father, he turned towards Tony and signaled him down towards the car. "I don't want to over stay my welcome here, the dead need some peace, too." Tony shuttered at the thought of death and scampered his feet towards the car, where both men quickly seated themselves and were soon after gone. "Where to next," Tony asked as he picked up the road map and proceeded to unfold it. William flicked the turn signal down and turned right out of the cemetery and onto a dusty, narrow roadway. "I need to go see someone. I think I can get some unanswered questions I have about my Father." William's face remained calm, without an ounce of fear nor pain trickling within. "Who's this person?" Tonys' face on the other hand was turning sour, with a lot of unanswered questions brewing within himself as well, yet his answers were locked up in the man right before his very eyes. "You're going to think I'm crazy, but I don't even know the guy, but I remember seeing him and I know I'd recognize his face anywhere. Those eyes he had, big and brown, with purplish, pink shading beneath his eyes. He had a rather simple look, but trust me, I'd be able to pick his face out even if it has been 15 years." William kept his eyes on the road as the words he spoke slithered their way into the ears of Tony, who sat back and didn't say a word for a moment. It was just Shorty after that Tony's mouth parted, "What answers do you need, William? I'm sorry but it's not of any use anymore, is it?" "I just want to know why I saw some things that I saw, things that I don't think I was suppose to see. " "Things you weren't suppose to see, William, what are you trying to say?" "That's just it, I don't even know, but I don't want to jump to any conclusions until I am certain. Just bare with me, I know it's not much to go off of." Tony shook his head, his eye balls drifting upward towards the ceiling. Looks of frustration began to seep through and anger hit the shore. "I don't want to be a part of this little investigation or whatever the hell it is. Susan is going to fuckin' cut my balls off if I don't come home. How long is this going to take, do you have any leads?" "Jesus chirst, Tony, all I know is that Daniel Shy was born and raised in Mitchell, Oregon... about two hours south of here and guess what... that's where we're headed." "Just fuckin' great..." Tony was not buying into this little adventure, but William had more to come. "There was two bodies, I think. Two men that worked for my Father were right there, and I knew something wasn't right. They didn't see me, but I don't think I'll ever be able to erase that image, and who knows if my father was in any way connected...but I need to do this." "Who's to even say this Daniel Shy, poor, sonuvabitch will even cooperate or hell, he may be dead himself. I just think you're goin' a little out of the way here and I understand that you just lost your father but this...this is just madness!" The car halted to a stop and William turned to Tony with the wind, showing signs of aggravation and fatigue. "You have two choices. You can stay here, or you can walk." With that, William said no more and kept a solid mug directed entirely on Tony, and nothing more was needed. Tony shook his head and violently slammed his fist into the dashboard, and thereafter nestled up with his arms in his pits and his eyes out the window. William turned back to the road and continued onward.
