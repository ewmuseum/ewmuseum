Orchestra of Wolves, Part 3: Toil

undated

Date: Today It hit him as soon as he stumbled from the guts of the arena, hours after WorldWide had gone off the air. A sudden numbness beneath his skull and an acute pain in his temples. His vision started to blur and the bellowing his heavy breathes soon became the only thing he could hear over a heart that was pounding like a jackhammer. Dizziness gave him the legs to those of a newborn foal and his knees buckled beneath him, sending his near three-hundred pound frame crashing shoulder-first into the side of somebody's Mercedes. His bag slid from his shoulders as he sat up against the side of the car. Cocking his head back, feeling beads of perspiration building on his brow, Andy squeezed his eyes shut and tried to focus them, when when he opened-up the world was just as fuzzy as before. He tried to pace himself; slow his breathing and calm his heartbeat, but the sudden burst of acidity that shot up his throat like a geyser was unstoppable. Andy hunched to the side and let it come. Soon he was left staring at a noxious yellow syrup of bile and vomit that splattered against the concrete. “There he is,” he heard someone say, but he was too concerned with holding back another torrent of spew to put a name to the voice. Andy gritted his teeth and swallowed but couldn't hold back the second wave. Spitting the remains from his mouth and panting heavily, Andy rolled onto his side and looked up as his brothers approached. “Pick 'im up!” ordered Sidney. To Andy, his elder brothers voice was dull and incoherent, as if he were underwater. Cayle stepped around the mess and tried to prop Andy up, but the GCW wrestler's eyes were fluttering and his breathes were slowing. Andy felt the pressure in his head begin to dissipate, replaced by a dizzy state of delirium. Cocking his head to the side, he managed to hurl another ball of vile liquid from his mouth but couldn't muster the strength to pull himself up. Struggling against Andy's dead weight, Cayle found himself unable to lift him further. He looked up at his elder brother. “Shit, Sid! He's out of it,” he said as Andy raised a weak, trembling hand. “We need to get him to the hospital...” “I'll get the car!” the bearded Murray family matriarch said as he turned on his heel and bolted into the parking lot's shadows. For Andy, there was nothing he could do but wait. He saw the outline of his younger brother kneeling down beside him but could not make out his features. All his senses were beginning to fail him, and he could fight no longer. His mind finally submitted to his body's struggle. * * * * * [i]How much more can I take?[/i] It was a question he'd asked himself a thousand times and still he was no closer to finding an answer. Losing consciousness was nothing knew to the ten-year veteran, but never before had he experienced such an overwhelming barrage on the senses. He'd checked-out of the hospital almost twenty-four hours prior and still a dull headache lingered and his head and a high-pitched squeal ringing around his earlobes. Sighing as he stepped from his car and approached the front door, Andy tried to push back the worry. The war with LeStatt Knight in GCW would soon come to an end, and every second spent worrying was a second wasted. Still, he couldn't help but wonder what might happen should he go out like that in the middle of a match. He hadn't felt truly stable and sure of himself for weeks and the aches and pains were getting worse with every passing day. Now a showdown with Shepherd was on the horizon, and the last thing he wanted was to lose himself in front of the Just stalwart. He shook the images from his head and slipped his key inside the lock. The sport was pushing to the limit and he could feel his body crumbling, but he could not relent. Padding heavily through the hallway, the man GCW fans knew as “The Scottish King of Cool” traversed his Floridian home at a near-glacial pace and eventually found his way into the kitchen. Artificial light bounced back from the varnished mahogany panels and marble worktops as Murray flicked the switch and pulled open a cupboard. Producing half-empty single malt and a crystal glass from the drying rack, he poured a shot that he quickly knocked back. Smooth, smoky whiskey kissed his taste buds and burned the back of his throat and he questioned whether alcohol was the best idea, considering the turbulence in his stomach. “Fuck it,” he mumbled as he effused more of the cool, brown liquid into the glass. Andy was about to cock his head back and down his second shot when a cold, wet nose found it's way up his loose linen trousers and brushed against his shin. It sent a sudden bolt of shock flaring through his body and the glass shattered into a thousand tiny pieces as it slipped from his hand and hit the floor. Furrowing his brow and sighing as Winston, the dachshund pup, lowered his shamed head and backed-off, Andy looked to the mess of glass and scotch on the tiles. “Well done!” he barked at the dog, his voice laced with sarcasm. “Look what you've done...” Shaking his head and turning to retrieve another glass, Andy heard the scuttling of tiny paws on the tiled floor. He turned around with the dog standing just inches from him and grimaced. “Get the fuck out of here,” he growled, grabbing the bottle. Winston was still there when he looked back. “Go on! Piss off!” The roar was louder than he'd intended but it did the trick. The giant wrestler's roar terrified the tiny pup and he scurried from the kitchen as quickly as his little legs would carry him. “I need another drink,” Andy said under his breathe. He pushed open the French doors and stepped out into the damp night's air. * * * * * The sound of glass shattering and the subsequent furore woke her from her slumber, and when she stepped past the cowering dog to find a puddle of glass shards and Glenmorangie on the floor her mind began to wonder. Closing a cupboard that had been left open and grabbing some kitchen paper to mop up the mess, Kayla saw Andy's discarded black travelling bag on the table and remembered. Dropping the paper on the pool, she stepped through the open double doors and found her fiancée standing alone on the decking, resting his muscular frame against the wooden railing. The warmth hit her as soon as she left the air-conditioned palace inside and walked slowly towards the Scot. “Hey you,” she said, sidling up beside Andy and pushing her shoulder against his arm. The chirping of crickets was loud and sounding closer than usual, but not loud enough to drown her soft, midwestern tones. “Hey,” he replied in a low, rumbling voice. She pressed closer to him as he took a sip and looked out across the moonlit garden. Andy was tired and worn down; she could tell from the monotone voice and slouched stance, so she forwent the usual post-WorldWide “how was the trip?” deal. “You feeling alright?” was what Kayla opted for instead. “Yeah. Not bad, I 'spose,” Andy replied, turning his head to make eye contact. “Shouldn't I be?” “No, it's just...” she paused hesitantly. “Sid told me they had to take you to a hospital...” “Oh, he told you that, did he?” Andy said, recounting the one thing he'd told his brothers not to tell Kayla when they got back to Tallahassee. “Yeah...” she began before her voice drifted off. She knew he didn't want her to worry about him and that his recent dealings in GCW had left him more irritable and strung out than she could remember, but Kayla couldn't help herself. “Are you [i]sure[/i] everything's okay, Andy? They said you dropped like a fl-” “I'm fine,” Andy reaffirmed. “Just a knock I took is all. Not worth a second thought.” “But you weren't even wrestling.” Dropping the last of the scotch down his throat, Andy felt the claws of guilt dig into his heart as he looked deep into those round, hazel eyes. He knew as well as she did that this latest lapse wasn't physical. No, it was entirely psychological. Andy's ever waking second was dedicated to dethroning LeStatt Knight and his body was telling him to “stop,” but Andy Murray would go until he could take no more. At least he had the Shepherd match to take his mind off things. “Occupational hazards,” was the only retort he could muster. “Yeah, well. What kind of an occupation leaves it's employees passed-out in empty parking lots,” Kayla said, bowing her head and cocking it to the side. He picked up on the disappointment in her voice. Took note of it. Professional wrestling had already taken one relationship from him and he wasn't about to let it claim another, but his situation was almost untenable. If she found out the true extent of the previous night's lapse then Andy knew the worry would crush her. Andy wrapped an arm around her waist and pulled her close, kissing the top of her head. “It's nothing to worry about, Kay. Seriously. I'll be fine,” he said, putting all his faith in his fiancée's confidence in him. “But you it's happened so many times, Andy.” “It has?” “All those injuries you told me about. Before we met...” “My back's as good as new, if that's what you mean. Haven't had any problems in almost two years now. Besides, that's got nothing to do with what happened last night.” “But...” again Kayla lost her train of thought, trying to piece together words to make the point she wanted. “That's not what I mean. I know the injuries aren't connected and all, but aren't you worried that all these bad things keep happening to you? I mean, LeStatt's got your head in all kinds of strange places.” “Hey, come on,” he said, gently pushing her head up, but she would not smile. He sensed that this probably wouldn't be the best of times to tell her he was going back to Just. “I'm fi-” “You keep saying that, but I don't know,” she shook her head. Looked up. Into his cold, blue eyes where once bright exuberance danced and sparkled. Kayla swallowed. Hard. “Andy, you're pushing yourself too hard. You need to take a step back before something bad happens...” He struggled to quash a sigh as the last words he wanted to hear came from her mouth. “It ain't easy, Kay. I'm not gonna lie,” he said, turning back towards the starry sky. “But when the storms come, you gotta weather 'em. That's what you do when you're a wrestler. I know it's hard to understand right now, but in this business, to truly appreciate the good times, you've gotta go through the very worst, and I'm [i]so[/i] much closer than I've ever been...” Kayla looked back at the Scot, who pulled his arm from her waist and up behind her shoulder. “Okay,” she said. Her belief was waning but Kayla knew there was no point arguing further with him; not in this frame of mind, at least. “But if this happens again...” “It won't.” Andy pulled her forward, kissed her on the cheek, and dared not mention the bout of dizziness that forced him to stop the car an hour ago.
