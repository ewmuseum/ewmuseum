Light Room, Dark Emotions

30 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/30/05 The camera’s scene opens to a blank white room. The walls, the floor, the ceiling, all blank and all white. Located directly in the center of the room is a chair occupied by Coyle O’Conner. The man seems lifeless. His stiffened body makes no obvious movements or gestures, allowing the viewer-interpretation of something tremendous. He is covered in a heavy black long jacket, a skin-tight long-sleeved gray shirt, and black pants. The camera does not catch a glimpse of his face at all, as if it’s being hidden. It’s as if death has overcome all emotions. Coyle’s eyes are locked on the television that stands tall in front of him. You know this because the camera has a view on his back. From time to time, his hand reaches up to the screen to touch it, only to drop down once again. Something happened, but no viewer can make out what it is yet. Coyle, “Wolack talks about how I don’t know how it is to lose something important, and well, he’s wrong.” He pauses and drops his head. A sniff or two could be heard. When he picks his head back up, while the camera still has a view on his back, he clears his throat. Coyle, “My life hasn’t been peachy either, it’s had plenty of disastrous moments. So many, in fact, that Wolack has no right comparing himself to me; absolutely no right. I’ve seen and been apart of so many things that his defectively-flawed mind couldn’t even dream of.” Another pause comes from Coyle. Sigh’s are overwhelmingly bursting from his mouth, letting every ounce of regretful feelings out. Coyle, “It’s a shame Wolack considers himself as someone who has been through worse than me. Even if that were true, however, what would that matter?” His voice slowly becomes enraged, and his glands slowly create adrenaline. Coyle, “Huh Wolack” What exactly does it all mean? Who cares? Who cares about you? Who cares what you think? All that people care about is?” He spins around in his chair to stare into the camera. His eyes are pure white, and a large Celtic-tribal design covers his forehead to his nose. The design of it is beautiful, but it shows death and decay as for the meaning. His eyes are slanted just a bit to show his anger and frustration. Coyle, ??how I’m going to massacre you come Devil’s Night; and believe me, it won’t be pretty.” His eyes scold for a minute. Coyle, “Remember Wolack, you gave me scars that I’m stuck with for the rest of my life. So, I’ll be sure to repay the favor, I’ll give you a particular scar that you’ll remember for the rest of your life. It’s war Wolack, it’s war.” He looks away from the camera, but as a viewer, you can’t tell (eyes white and all). Coyle, “And this match will be in honor of the only one who believed in me?” Coyle stands up alarmingly from his chair, and walks out of the scene. However, the scene doesn’t end. For a moment, there is probably confusion as to what’s going on now. But then, as the camera slowly zooms in to the television, the news could be watched. In a few moments, you can realize that the news story was about Patrick O’Conner, and a gun-down at his pub. Newscaster, “Patrick O’Conner, 1952-2005.”
