Orchestra of Wolves, Finale: Pretty Pictures

19 February 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/19/10 ”Death was just a simple glance across a dimly lit room, And those eyes did it, Those three words did it, Those three words killed him.” - Converge, Heaven in Her Arms. * * * * * The cardiovascular jackhammer behind his ribs pounded with such force that he could feel it's beat in his throat. Thump, thump, thump; dull, weighty, and quickening with every passing second. Foomp. He swung a slow haymaker that barely juddered the heavy bag. Although the crooked timbers from which the bag hung creaked, he could not muster the power in his tired muscles to knock it more than a couple of inches off-centre and found his failing body swaying more than the punchbag itself. Took him a good deal longer than it should've to steady himself on Bambi's legs and swing again. Another haymaker crashed ungracefully into the bag's off-white surface, leaving behind a thick crimson smear that went unnoticed. This time he arched his back, supporting himself by holding the bottom of his thighs with his beaten mitts. Perspiration trickled liberally from his forehead's every pore, dribbling down his glistening face as if it were a melting icicle until it slipped from his nose and chin to meet the splattered pool on the floorboards. A minute later and his lungs were finding it easier to cope. Slowly, he heard track twelve of Bruce Springsteen's Greatest Hits collection ooze from the speakers and through his head's soundtrack of heavy breaths and frenzied heartbeats. Hazed vision refocusing itself, he noticed the redness across his knuckles. Standing as straight as his weary frame would allow, he raised his shaking paws to the light and the longer he stared at them, the worse they looked. Torn flesh and fraying bandages were bound together by the thick, maroon gloop seeping from his open wounds. Asking himself how long he'd been hitting the bag and failing to find an answer, he picked at his right hand's bandage and tried to peel it free of the bloody mess. A sharp pain stung him immediately. He recoiled. Winced. Looked back down at the reddened pulp of flayed skin, dressings, and blood. They'd become one. Grunting, he gazed towards the bag and saw the mess he'd made. Streaks of red and pink everywhere; some long, some short, some thick, some thin; peppering the bag from top-to-bottom, but the picture still hung relatively untouched. Frayed down the torn edge that had once connected it with it's other half, and crumpled from the blows he'd swung, but still hanging from the two staples that held it. Enough. He glanced again at his damaged hands and curled them into fists, though it hurt to do so. With gritted teeth and a fearsome roar he threw a swift left at the picture and bashed new crumples into the subject's face. Barbs of white-hot pain shot from his hand but he wouldn't relent. Another left. A right. Jab, jab, jab. Uppercut. Each shot sent tiny droplets of blood sliding down the photo's uneven surface to the growing pool of sweat below. Right, left. Left, right. Jab, jab. Pause. Line it up. Haymaker. Pulse getting faster, breaths getting heavier. Not a note from the CD player now, only the sounds of his body struggling to keep up the pace. Uppercut. Jab. Jab. Time between punches increasing. Balance failing. Right cross. Suddenly lost his footing on the damp boards. Fell forward, clinging to the punchbag like a young panda would to it's mother. The staples came loose and slowly the battered photograph slid to the floor, landing by the puddle. He watched it fall with fuzzy vision and stepped back from the bag. Carefully leaning over, he tried to retrieve the picture. Couldn't. Legs buckled, no longer able of supporting his 275lbs. He fell to the cold, hard ground like a skydiver without a chute. Sweat splashed around the cheek that hit the deck. He tried to push himself up but his arms could not support him either, so he remained on the floorboards; a broken wreck of a man, devoid of life save for his heaving lungs and batting eyelids. All around him was blurred, but the photograph remained as clear as day. There it was, inches from his face; a pummelled portrait of a black-haired, blue-eyed man breaming from ear-to-ear with an arm wound around someone torn from view. A giant man, happier than he'd ever been, with a shiny title belt strapped around his waist and a flick of blond hair resting on his shoulder. His hand crawled involuntarily towards it but wouldn't get far. He was fading. He was fa. He w. Black. * * * * * Andy Murray in... Pretty Pictures Orchestra of Wolves, the Finale. * * * * * By his brother's side, Andy retrieved the last of their bags from the carousel and slung it onto the trolley. Carefully backing-out through the gathered throng of impatient tourists and travellers, Andy glanced over his shoulder and whistled loudly towards where his son was seated. “Yo, kiddo. Let's make a move.” Marvin Murray drew his head up from a copy of Sports Illustrated and hopped from his chair, nearly knocking his glasses off his face in the process. He shuffled towards his seniors with heavy feet, evoking a harsh “get a move on” from his father. “Sorry,” Marvin said sheepishly as he cantered towards his elders, magazine safely tucked under his arm. The lanky pre-teen stuffed his hands into the pockets of his pressed black pants and hung his head as Andy swung the trolley around and pushed it away from the crowds. “Sure you don't want me to take that?” asked Sid. Years of nicotine abuse had ravaged Andy's elder brother's voice to the point where every sentence sounded so hoarse and gruff that he was perpetually cantankerous. “Thing must weigh a tonne, judging by the amount of crap that junior here stuff into his case,” he said, ruffling an unimpressed Marvin's short back and sides. “It's only a luggage cart. I got it.” “Just trying to be helpful,” Sid replied, shrugging. He dared not push his younger brother any further; he'd barely said a word on the flight over, and while he wanted desperately for the GCW World Heavyweight Champion to liven-up, it wasn't worth the quarrel. Instead he kept his mouth closed as they passed through the baggage collection area and headed towards Terminal 3's main lobby. “Christ, look at the time,” Andy grumbled, rolling his white dress shirt's sleeve up and checking his timepiece. “You think they'd be able to get a three-hour state-hopper from Florida to Texas in without any delays. Schedule's gonna be screwed, now.” “Tell me about it. Anything I can do to help, though, let me know,” Sid said, scratching an itch behind his newly-shorn head. No response was forthcoming. Andy had never been good at dealing with loss and failed relationships, and his brother knew that the best thing to do was usually to give him space and let him deal with it himself. Part of Sid wanted to slap his brother across the face and tell him to man-up. Here he was, days away from the biggest fight of his life, burying himself beneath a mire of bullshit. It was quite ordinary for him to become more reserved and focused than usually before the big matches; he'd seen him blazing with channelled anger prior to his battles with Bagwell and Knight, but never had he seen the man so wrought with doubt. It was as if his sibling was cowering behind a locked door and Vivica J. Valentine was bashing at the door, trying to kick her way in. Sid didn't like it. For a while, he considered not coming to Dallas and letting his brother travel alone with his son, but after seeing the mess Andy had left behind in the gym, there was no way Sid wasn't getting on the plane. They walked through a wide tunnel, passing through the Arrivals gate, and entering the vast, curved expanse that was the terminal's main lobby. Thousands of travellers buzzed back and forth from the doors to the check-in desks, from check-in desks to the departure lounge, carrying, pushing, and pulling their baggage around. For Marvin the sight was nothing new; he was familiar with DFW's interior from ferrying back-and-forth from Andy's Tallahassee home to his mother's Texan residence. “Rueben should be outside, c'mon,” Andy said, nodding towards the line of swingdoors that took up much of the terminal's glass-fronted façade. Eventually they slipped through one, and the brilliant Texan sun was upon them. It was a warm day for February, and Sid rued his decision to wear an old AWC hoody from the moment he stepped into the sunshine. Andy brought the cart to a halt beside the pick-up/drop-off point. A few dozen people stood a few yards away, queueing up for taxis as the brothers Murray surveyed the scene. “See him?” Andy asked, squinting in the sunlight. “Nah. I'll go see if he's further along.” Pulling his sweatshirt over his broad shoulders, Sid traipsed along the outside of building, looking for any sign of Andy's bald-headed agent. Noticing his disinterested son was doing little more than stare at his well-polished shoes, Andy tapped him on the shoulder. “Hey, you okay?” Marvin responded with a coy nod and looked up. He saw the bandages; white, pristine, fresh, and wound tightly around both of his father's hands, their purpose had been bothering Marvin all day, yet with Andy acting so glum he thought better of enquiring. Now he had an opening. “Dad?” “Yeah.” “What's wrong with your hands?” Immediately Andy pulled away from Marvin's shoulder and hid his hands in his pockets. “Nothing, they're fine.” “Then why are you wearing bandages?” “Oh, uh. Just picked up a few burns hitting the punchbag the other day,” Andy said, forcing a weak smile even his son could see through. “Serves me right for not taping-up, huh?” “Ain't no sign,” Sid's gravelly baritone suddenly announced. He strode up to Andy and Marvin, sweating from the heat. “Been all the way up the other end and back. Nothing.” “Son of a bitch,” Andy groaned. “First the flight's delayed, then my agent decides not to turn up. Great.” “You know how his schedule is,” Sid reasoned as his brother pulled out his cell phone and switched it on. “It's not like he could've got in-touch either, what with us bein' in the sky. He probably couldn't wait any longer.” “No answer,” Andy said, shaking his head and snapping the phone shut. Stressing, he exhaled deeply and looked over at the taxi queue. “Alright, I guess that messes up our ride plans. You go over and pick up the rental car and drive Marvin to his mother's like we discussed. Place is just over there.” He pointed over his brother's shoulder. “I'll take a cab.” “You sure? Could easily just take the rental and drop you off on the way back.” Andy had already started hauling cases from the luggage cart before his brother had finished. “Nah, she's on the other side of time, and I've got a presser in forty-five minutes.” “Yeah, but have you seen that queue?” The towering Scot looked across and did a quick headcount. Thirteen. “Fuck it, just go. I'll see you at the hotel.” * * * * * The waiting room was deserted - all they had for company was each other, save for the occasional scrub-clad doctor or nurse fluttering by the doorway. Cherise clung tightly to her three children. At eight, six, and four, none of them were quite old enough to grasp the severity of the situation yet; all they really knew was that Daddy was sick and Mommy was upset. They absorbed her sadness and sat clustered around her, the youngest clinging so tightly to her hand that if he was twenty years older and used the same force he may have broken bones. It seemed like they'd spent a lifetime in that waiting room, but they hadn't even been there an hour. Cherise took a paper handkerchief and caught a wayward tear with it, then took her daughter's head and held it close to her chest. At least she wasn't alone. A door clicked open in the hallway, couldn't have been more than a few feet away. Slow, heavy footsteps began plodding along the concrete floor and there he was beyond the waiting room's windows: her husband. Cherise rose to her feet and hurried with the children towards the door. Lips trembling, eyes wide with hope, she watched him trudge towards the threshold with an opened envelope in his hand and a tear forming in the corner of his eye. She didn't need to wait for Rueben to tell her that the results weren't good. * * * * * Such was Andy's hurry as he burst out of the cab that he forgot to forgot to tip the driver and almost knocked a bellboy off his feet as he hopped up the hotel's steps, suitcase in hand. Skipping through the narrowest of gaps in the revolving door and getting through the other side in a flash, he was completely oblivious to the splendour of his marble surroundings as he sprinted towards the mahogany-panelled reception desk. The abruptness of his arrival caught the Asian receptionist by surprise at first, but she soon regained her composure and flashed a friendly smile at The Scottish King of Cool. “Hi, welcome to the Magnolia. Are you chec--” “Conference room,” Andy said between heavy breaths, not meaning to sound unfriendly. “Where is it?” “Just along the corridor there, fourth door on the left. I'll need to see some I.D., though.” Obviously, she wasn't a wrestling fan. Fumbling around his waistline, Andy eventually located his wallet in one of his front pocket and pulled out it. He removed his Florida driver's licence and handed it to the girl, but decided against a tiresome “don't laugh at the photo” quip. Just as she was finished inspecting it, a familiar voice echoed around the whitewashed walls and marble floor. “For God's sake Andy, where have you been?” Joseph Wallace, GCW Press Officer, strode towards the Champion at a right of knots. Dressed in a black pinstripe suit, tie, and sky blue shirt, rounded off by a pair of designer spectacles, Wallace was a man who compensated well for his crooked nose and rapidly receding hairline by dressing like a king. Brushing past the Scot, Joseph took Murray's licence from the receptionist and handed back to him. “I'll take him off your hands, sorry hun.” “Nice to see you too, Joey,” Andy said as his chaperone placed an arm behind his back and led him towards one of the hotel's five conference suites. “Where were you? We were supposed to start twenty minutes ago,” Wallace said impatiently. “They're getting antsy in there. Short version now; you can tell me the details later on.” “My flight got delayed and my ride didn't turn up. Good enough?” Wallace stopped in his tracks, noticing something was missing. “Hold on, where's your agent?” “He was my ride.” “Fuck,” the Press Officer snarled. “Okay, you're either going to have to fly solo, or I can sit in there with you. I've done it before under similar circumstances, but you'd need to field the bulk of the questions.” “It's fine, I got it.” “Sure?” Andy nodded. He had a hard enough time coping with Wallace's irritating Bostonian accent in normal conversation; the last thing he wanted was to have to deal with it in a room full of prying journalist and camera lenses. “Then show some fuckin' enthusiasm, would ya? I've seen zombies with more life than you today. And where's your jacket?” “It's right he--...” Andy looked down at his suitcase. No sign of his jacket. “Fuck. Must've left it in the car.” “C'mon, Andy! You're getting slack,” Joseph said, quickly removing his own jacket and handing it to the GCW World Champion. “Here, try this.” He extended the jacket towards The King, who reluctantly took it. “Think this'll fit?” “Probably not, but we can't have you looking like a fucking scarecrow for the biggest press conference of the year, can we?” Andy squeezed his tree-trunk arms into the jacket and pulled it across his chest. The breast size was at least four inches too small; no way was he going to be able to button it. “How is it?” “Sleeves are a little short. It'll do.” “Good. Now come on, perk up a little; just 'cause half the planet thinks you're going to get your ass-kicked by a girl this weekend doesn't mean you can skulk around here like Solomon Grundy. You're the face of this company, remember? Show me those pearl whites.” Andy flashed his second forced smile of the day. The irony of Joseph's words compared with the tone in which they were said was not lost on the Champion. Wallace handed him some tissues from the packet nestled in his breast pocket. “Now take these and dab your brow off--... Jesus! What the fuck are you wearing on your hands?” “Bandages.” “I can see that, Einstein. Get rid of 'em." Andy shook his head. “Not a good idea. Busted my knucks up pretty bad.” After gently dabbing the sweat away from his brow and tossing the damp tissues into the waste paper basket behind Wallace, Andy used his reflection in the door's bronze “Conference Room 4” sign to adjust his shirt and newly-acquired jacket. “Well I guess it'll have to do. Go on in.” “But I ha--...” “GO!” Raising both palms in defeat, Andy twisted the doorknob and stepped into a room crammed full of reporters and photographers. All eyes were upon him as he strode up to the raised platform, shielding his eyes from the glare of forty flashbulbs strobing in unison. Finally adjusting to cameras' glare, Murray sat down behind the prepared table and turned down the placecard labelled “Rueben Dallas, Agent.” Finally, as the flashes began to die down, Andy raised his head. “Alright, who's first?” Almost every hand in the room shot up and several voices chimed. Murray dragged his vision across the room and picked out a young, bearded reporter in a red lumberjack shirt with a point and a nod. “Mr. Murray; given the circumstances of your break-up with Vivica J. Valentine,” he said, smooth and well-spoken, “and given that you are due to defend your title against her in just a couple of days, do you really feel it was appropriate to let yourself get caught clubbing with Tessa Windsor and Tempest earlier this week?” Andy exhaled. This seminar would be a long one. * * * * * Sidney Murray was in no mood to hang around and engage in awkward small talk with a woman he had nothing but scorn for. When pulled up the Chrysler rental outside Jane's condo he wasted no time in popping the trunk and bounding from the driver's seat. Rescuing Marvin's bag from the car's boot, Sid placed it down on the tarmac and slammed the door shut as Andy's timid offspring emerged from the back seat. “You got everything?” Sid asked, sparking up his fifth Marlboro Red of the day as Marvin closed his door and checked his pockets. “All set.” “Good,” said Sid, exhaling a mouthful of noxious smoke. “I'll be over on Sunday afternoon to pick you up for the show. See you then.” Nodding, Marvin pulled up the handle on his wheel bag and hauled it up onto the pavement curb. Sid slid his first leg back into the car, but turned towards his nephew before departing. “Oh, kid?” Marvin turned his head. “I know you're young and impressionable, but don't let pop's implosion get you down. It won't do you any good.” Accepting Marvin's nod, Sid sat down in the car and twisted the ignition just as the terracotta-coloured condo's front door clicked open. Marvin turned as his mother stepped onto the threshold, arms wide open. “There's my little bundle of joy. C'mere you!” A woman once proudly tagged “Mrs. Murray” hurried from the doorstep to meet her tired son. 31, formerly of Hastings, England, presently residing in Dallas, Texas, Jane smiled widely as she knelt down and kissed her son's forehead. “Oh I've missed you,” she said, wrapping her thin arms around his torso and looking him straight in his bold, blue eyes. “I've missed you too, Mom.” “How was the flight?” “Good. Well, except for the delay.” “Awww, the flight was held-up? Hope you didn't have to sit around for too long.” “Two hours. Sucked.” “Awww.” Letting go of her son as she rose, Jane took the case's handle from his grasp and shepherded him towards her home. “Come on; you can tell me all about it inside.” Hauling her bespectacled son's over-packed suitcase up the doorstep, Jane followed Marvin into the condo and closed the door. It was a light and airy interior, with painted cream walls and laminated oak flooring punctuated by black fixtures and modest furniture. Not bad for someone who's relocation to the city hadn't been voluntary. “You want a sandwich or something?” she said, leaving the bag by the staircase as Marvin sat himself down on the couch's floral patterns. “You must be hungry after all that travelling.” “No,” Marvin said, his voice flat and unengaging as he slid his shoes off his feet without untying. “I'm alright.” Jane's soft features knotted with doubt. “You sure? You don't sound too perky.” Marvin didn't reply, instead folding his arms across his chest and puffing a sigh so audible Jane could hear it from the other side of the room. Walking round the breakfast bar that separated the kitchenette from the rest of the ground floor, Jane parked herself on the couch's matching armchair and looked beyond her son's thin lenses. “What's wrong, Marv?” she asked, taking his hand in hers. “You're not usually this down when you come to visit.” “It's Dad.” He gave his answer without making eye contact. “He's in a bad mood.” Now there's a surprise, Jane thought to herself. Despite the fact that they shared a son, the only times she'd communicate with Andy were those telephone conversations at the beginning of every month to negotiate when Marvin would make the visits his mother was entitled to. Even those were brief, unfriendly affairs. She had no real sympathy for her former husband, but nobody likes seeing their child in such a downtrodden state. “Honey, you know how he gets before one of his 'big matches',” she said, moving from the armchair to the sofa and putting an arm over Marvin's shoulders. “I know I had to put up with it for years. Just don't let it get to you, he'll be right as rain as soon as this event's finished.” “I dunno,” he said, voice trailing. “He won't tell me what's wrong. I think it's something to do with Vivica.” “He's gone through a break-up, Marv,” said Jane. She had no desire to discuss her former flame and his estranged love interest at any great detail, but she'd need to be sharp and wily to change the topic without further upsetting her boy. “It's always tough, but trust me; things'll be back to normal soon enough. You'll see.” Accepting his mother's words with the slightest of nods, Marvin responded with a quiet “I guess”. A smile stretched across Jane's unpainted lips, lighting up her hazel eyes and bringing her face to life. “Let's see if we can't cheer you up in the meanwhile, Mr. Grumpypants. What time are you leaving for the show on Sunday?” “Early afternoon.” “Great. Plenty of time to--...” “Don't know if I wanna go, though.” “... oh. How come?” Marvin's eyes met hers. “I don't want to watch him lose.” * * * * * Andy couldn't catch a break. “Mr. Murray, how have your lingering feelings for Vivica J. Valentine affected your preparation for this match?” “After weeks and weeks of cowering in a corner and not standing up for yourself, how do you react to the common belief that the Andy Murray of 2010 is little more than a pushover and a wet blanket?” “Do you think it was wise to become romantically involved with someone who you share such an intense, high-stress profession with?” “Given that up until WorldWide 126 you'd spent almost a month avoiding conflict and trying to pretend that Valentine turn her back on you, how are you going to step into the ring and physically hurt her on Sunday night?” Enough! Forty-two minutes had elapsed and eighteen questions had passed, all poking and prodding at The Scottish King of Cool's mental state. There was no mention of the fact that his star as GCW's most popular competitor had long since eclipsed Vivica J. Valentine's, or the fact that he'd just become the longest serving World Heavyweight Champion of all time might have given him over his NC-17 opponent. Just question upon question of unwanted probing inside the mind of a man who saw the separation of his private and personal lives second only to the well-being of his family and friends in terms of sacredness. The last question was the final straw. Irritated and harassed, Andy pointed his finger towards a skinny reporter wearing an ill-fitting suit and ratty facial fluff who promptly rose from his seat. “In recent weeks you've had to endure public collapse of your relationship, the massive invasion of privacy that has followed, and your own on-air mental meltdo--...” Andy raised a hand. “'Invasion of privacy'? Pretty ironic, coming from someone in your line of work.” He scowled at the gawking journalist. This was the biggest wrestling event of the year, and the men gathered before him were supposed to be sport's journalism's crème de la crème, yet Andy could see no distinction between them and the vultures that picked at bones and scraps to fill tabloid column inches. The man tried to continue, but Andy would not let him. “We're done here.” Caught by the flashbulbs, Murray rose to his feet and hopped down from the platform without a moment's hesitation. The press rose from their feet and tried to call him back, but it was too late - Murray was already out the door. The door's sudden slam and the sight of the 6'7” GCW World Champion powering past him caught Joseph Wallace off-guard. “Hey, what the fuck?” he said, pushing himself away from the wall and watching Murray stomp down the corridor. “Where are you going?! You can't be done already!” “I am done already.” “Oh for fuck's sake.” Wallace rolled his eyes and took off after the towering Scot, grabbing him by the shoulder and stopping him in the marble-floored lobby. “All you need to do is sit in there and answer their questions, is that so hard? Get back in there and finish the damn thing, unless you wanna get buried in tomorrow's media.” “I couldn't give a shit,” Andy snapped. “With the shit they're asking in there, they can go to hell if they think I'm fielding any more questions.” “Of course they're gonna pry! You're stuck in the eye of the most personal storm that the wrestling business has seen for years. These people gotta sell newspapers, you know.” “Fuck those guys and fuck their sales figures.” Hastily, Andy pulled the tight jacket from his shoulders and thrust it towards Wallace's chest. “Oh, come on!” Joseph protested, straightening out his crumpled jacket as Andy raised a middle finger and turned away. The King's mind was already made up, though, and he was out the door like a shot. Undoing his shirt's top button and rolling up his sleeves, Andy walked briskly around the side of the building until a familiar voice stopped him dead. “Andy?” It was his brother. “Hell are you going?” Andy turned. His shorter, bearded brother walked towards him; his rugged, weather-beaten features awash with bewilderment. “Ain't you supposed to be with the press right now?” “I can't take any more of their garbage.” “So what, you're storming out?” Andy sighed. “I'm going for a walk.” He tried to turn away, but his brother wouldn't let him. Blocking his brother's path, Sid immediately questioned whether confronting his sibling in such a volatile mood was a wise move. Regardless, he continued. “You should go back inside. Walking out on the broadsheets like this ain't gonna do your image any good at the moment, y'know.” “Think I care what those shithawks think?” Andy said, furrowing his brows. “They can write what they want about me, turn me into a villain if they want to, Sid. I just don't care anymore.” “Well, you fuckin' should,” Sid barked back. “Look what you're doin' to yourself, Andy. You're supposed to be the best in the business, but all you're doin's throwing your toys out the pram and causing a fuss.” “'Causing a fuss'?! Have you forgotten everything I've been through recently?” “No, 'course not. But damnit, you're 'sposed to be a fuckin' professional, so start acting like one.” “This is the biggest match of my career, the last thing I need is these peop--...” “You're right,” Sid growled, poking a finger into his brother's ribcage. “It is the biggest match of your career, and you're doing a fine job of destroying your chances of victory before you've even stepped in the ring! All this grieving and feeling sorry for yourself... you really think that's how you prepare for a fight with a woman who wants to rip you limb-from-limb?! You may be the longest servin' champ of all-time or whatever, but you ain't gonna be for much longer 'less you get yer head back in the game.” He took a step backwards, Andy; taken a-back by his brother's impetuous savagery, but giving his words time to sink-in. He felt his lips quivering as they parted. “Just save it, Sid,” Andy said, softer than before. “I've heard it a thousand times before.” “Then it's 'bout damn time you started listening!” His brother would not relent. “You're throwin' away everything you've worked all your life for, and all for a girl who probably never even loved you in the first place. Even your own son thinks there's somethin' wrong with you.” “There's nothing wrong with me.” “Uh-huh,” Sid grunted with a slow nod. Reaching into the back pocket of his jeans, he pulled out a small white sheet, folded once across the middle. Andy knew what it was before Sid had even opened it. “Then explain this.” Andy looked down at what was forced into his hands. It was the photograph; his photograph, the one he'd stapled to the heavy bag and left discarded in a pool of blood and sweat on the basement floor. His azure blues met his brother's smouldering emerald oculars, and Andy swallowed his words. “Fuck this.” With a moment he'd turned on his heel and powered away from the hotel. With arms raised to his side, Sid called; “'fuck this'? That's your solution to everything at the moment, isn't it?!” * * * * * As her son scampered around the condo's upper mezzanine, Jane clasped her freshly brewed cup of tea and sat down in front of the television. With a quick button-press the dated 24-inch screen flickered into life, beaming forth the image of a smartly dressed newcaster sitting before a blue backdrop. With no desire to watch the same report on the Austin plane crash she'd been subjected to that morning, Jane flicked through the channels as she swallowed a mouthful of warm Earl Grey. Global Championship Wrestling was all over the news channels, and unsurprisingly so. Thousands had descended upon Dallas ahead of NC-17, with thousands more scheduled to arrive over the weekend. By Sunday night, the city would be swamped with rowdy, red-blooded wrestling fans who were in town to see the greatest show on Earth. It made her glad to live in the suburbs. There was no escaping it. The biggest wrestling event to hit Dallas since Wrestlemania X7 was upon the city, and the news anchors were all over it. From in-depth reports on the legality of AXEL Action's contractual obligations to Dan Black, to snapshots of the destruction left behind in the wake of Bryan Mayhem and Shaman's latest off-air scuffle, GCW had taken over the airwaves. She continued to skim and skip until finally, something caught her attention. Pulling a painted fingernail away from the channel button, Jane caught a still image of a man she once called her husband hunched over a table, head in hands. Before him sat a microphone and a name sign, and a GCW NC-17 banner hung on the wall to his rear. A headline flashed below Murray's visage:- ”FURIOUS GCW CHAMPION WALKS OUT OF PRESS CONFERENCE.” Two newsreaders materialized before the image as it shrunk into the background. “BREAKING NEWS now from Global Championship Wrestling,” the suit-clad male chirped. With teeth brighter than a thousand suns, a dated Second World War haircut held in place by at least $20 worth of hair product, and a voice as nasally and grating as Billy Corgan's, he wasn't the most affable to anchors. “Just two days ahead of the company's flagship NC-17 and further problems are materializing for World Heavyweight Champion, Andy Murray. According to reports, the Scottish athlete lost his cool in a press conference little over an hour ago. When the questions began to cut a little bit too close to the bone, a visibly shaken Murray stood up from his chair and turned his back on over forty of the country's premier sports journalists, some of whom had travelled halfway across the country to get a few words from the Champion, with nothing to do but twiddle their thumbs.” “We've come to expect things like this from some of the sports more volatile characters - the Jay Terror's and LeStatt Knight's of the world - but never from Andy Murray,” a bald-headed, moustached reporter remarked on-screen. Tony Price, according to the name on-screen. “We knew when he walked through the door that he wasn't too keen to talk today, but I've been at countless pressers with this man and never seen anything like this.” A different reporter flashed on the screen. Liz Buchanan. “It's completely outrageous. Guy's supposed to be GCW's shining light, and this is how he behaves? I think it's disgusting. Whatever state his personal life may be in, Andy Murray should show us the civility and respect that we have him. We're only trying to do our job.” Feed switched back to the studio where the female broadcaster prepared to address her audience. “It's been an up and down couple of months for Murray following the betrayal of Vivica J. Valentine, and today's events will only strengthen the common belief that the Scot's mental state is growing increasingly fragile.” Behind her, a series of stills from the event began to cycle. “Whatever the case, Andy Murray has never looked more likely to lose his GCW World Championship than he does no--...” Jane had seen enough. She skipped to the next channel. Same story. Up another channel, this one replaying footage of Vivica J. Valentine cracking a steel chair across the back of Andy's skull. Skip. Skip. Skip. Andy. Andy. Andy. She'd seen enough. Jane pushed the power button and the TV tube died with a faint click. Cocking her head bag against the couch's flattened cushions, Jane stared at the ceiling. If she hadn't born the son of the man on the television, she surely wouldn't have found the scenes so troubling, and while she had little interests in her former spouse's professional life any more, his downfall was clearly affecting the little man rumbling around her condo's upper level. Jane took-in another mouthful of tea and gulped it down. She could see then why her son's conversation had been so thin since his arrival. His father was in trouble. Big trouble. * * * * * Twenty minutes later and Andy Murray was lost in the hive of an alien city. Onlookers stopped and gawked at the passing GCW Champion - some because they'd seen him on GCW television and magazine covers, others because a 6'7” striding passed them wasn't exactly a day-to-day occurrence - but nobody tried to halt him. The glimmer in his eyes and purpose in his step convinced them otherwise. Trapped in a quagmire created by Vivica J. Valentine and worsened by himself, Andy was dead to the world. In his blind haze he stumbled out from a corner completely oblivious to the “DON'T WALK” sign and the screeching horns. Narrowly avoiding being knocked to the deck by a fuming cab driver, he crossed the street as if nothing had happened and hit the next block without skipping a step. This wasn't how it was supposed to be. He was Andy Murray. He was the longest serving GCW World Heavyweight Champion of all-time, and fresh-off completing an unparalleled clean-sweep of the 2009 Year End awards, he should've been admiring the view from the top of the world with Vivica J. Valentine on his arm and an envious horde of colleagues on his back. He'd written his former fling out of the GCW record books with his blockbuster title reign, and his 2009 Awards domination cemented his status as GCW's most valuable player. Anyone else on the company's payroll would've been thrilled with such accomplishments, but not Andy Murray. Her shadow loomed large over every little success he'd notched during his fourteen career. Every little thing that was supposed to bring him joy any happiness was nullified by her terrible presence, and nothing would change until she was gone forever. She'd pointed the finger and thrown accusations at him so many times that he was beginning to believe it himself. Was he responsible for Vivica J. Valentine's sudden shift in attitude, and this his own demise? Had he really made her feel as worthless as she claimed, or where her actions those of a bitter, jealous woman who couldn't handle the spotlight being shone away from her for the first time in many a year? The answer didn't matter - Andy was already broken. His wings clipped and dreams crushed, all he had to hold onto was the knowledge that in a couple of days time he'd be standing across the ring from a woman who wanted only to destroy him. A woman, who despite his attempts at denial, Andy Murray still loved dearly. He was terrified, and terrified for himself. Terrified for what he might have to do to walk out of NC-17 with his Championship record and reputation in-tact. Terrified at the prospect of having to lay hands on a woman he'd quickly fallen in love with. Terrified of the knowledge that he might have to send her home in an ambulance. Andy was supposed to be the good guy, but how was he supposed to play nice with Vivica J. Valentine ready to tear his head off? He'd made a career out of overcoming seemingly insurmountable adversities. When he first moved to America he took the words of those who said he was too thin and gangly to wrestle and made them eat their words. He literally spent years of his life lying in hospital beds and on physician's tables as crippling injuries threatened to rob him of his career and long-term mobility forever. When the internet chastised him for being “workshy” and “unreliable” after flunking out of AWC and FUSE in quick succession, Andy blazed back and hit his prime in spectacular fashion as GCW's fastest-rising talent. This was nothing like he'd ever endured. Chris Bagwell may have dragged Andy's personal life onto television screens in 2008 by trying to recruit his wife and son, even that failed to match the emotional drain of his attachment to Vivica J. Valentine. It should've been perfect. Every woman he'd dated in his life had left him because his career made it necessary for him to spent at least three of the week's seven days on the road. With Vivica, that was never going to be an issue. Perhaps, though, it was always destined to fail. Rueben had always warned him of the dangerous of mixing business and pleasure, but Andy ignored his words, such was his infatuation for Valentine. But the fact remained that around Andy's waist was something that she still saw as rightfully hers. A strap of gold and leather that she yearned for more than anything in the world, and with Murray's prolonged success in holding off all challengers to his throne, perhaps it was inevitable that he and his flame would always end-up clashing. He saw no way out. He knew he couldn't march into NC-17 and do to Vivica what she was prepared to do to him. Despite his wars with LeStatt Knight, Andy Murray's soul was very much in-tact, and despite an insistent pep talk from his former foe at 126, Murray knew not how to compromise it. Maybe he wasn't the man he was six months. Maybe the critics were right, and Andy Murray's time had come and gone. Maybe was that pushover, maybe he was that wet blanket. Maybe he was destined to fail at NC-17, and collapse into oblivion like so many GCW World Champions had done before him. An idealist from the day his parents brought him into the world, he tried his hardest from day one to put a positive spin on things and see the light at the end of the tunnel. For the first time in his life, Andy felt that like was beginning to die. Suddenly a buzzing on his thigh snapped him from his stupor. Ducking into a nearby alley, Andy stopped and pulled out his cellphone. Text flashed his agent's name across the screen. “Rueben, if you're calling to lecture me about Val--...” “I'm not calling to lecture you, Andy,” his agent's voice interrupted. His voice was cold, dead. That of a man who'd given up. “I'm calling to let you know I can't represent you anymore.” * * * * * A gentle pitter-patter on the bedroom door pulled Marvin's attention away from the now dog-eared SI magazine. He could see his mother's shadow through the gap between the door and floorboards. “Can I come in?” “Sure.” Jane pushed the door open and gently closed it behind her again. Noticing Marvin's reading material, she sat down on the edge of the bed and nodded. “What's that?” Marvin flipped to the front cover, displaying a row of four blue-clad skaters rounding a corner on a Vancouver ice rink. “Sports Illustrated, huh?” Jane said, raising an eyebrow. “Little grown-up for you, isn't it?” “It's okay. A little boring at times.” Laying chest-down on the bed, Marvin thumbed back to the article he was reading and let the hush fall between him and his mother. Watching her son use his finger to skip to a data box displaying Drew Brees' end of season stats for the Saints, Jane placed her hand between his shoulder blades and comforted him with a pat on the back. “Is your Dad in there?” “Yeah,” he said. “Well, kind of. There's a big NC-17 thingy with him and her.” Her. Though shrill, Marvin's tone warmed Jane. Not that she cared much for which wrestlers Marvin Murray chose to cheer and jeer, but because her son's disdain for a woman who'd done his father wrong was the first real emotion he'd exhibited since arriving. Without prompt he flicked through the sheets until GCW's dazzling NC-17 logo caught his eye. “Lemme have a look,” Jane said, picking up the magazine and peering at the GCW article's first page. Marvin adjusted his position in the single bed, rolling onto his back and swinging his long legs over the edge. “Wow, that's some arena, huh?” Jane said, pointing out the panoramic image of the Cowboys Stadium that lurked behind the article's text. “Yeah, and brand new too,” Marven said, interest peaking. “There's gonna be 100k there on Sunday.” “That many? Wow,” Jane said, drawing out her last word for a few seconds. It wasn't the shiny new arena that she was looking for, though. Using her tongue to dampen her finger, Jane flicked over to the next page. On the left, Vivica J. Valentine's portrait. On the right? Andy Murray's. “Well, there he is.” “Yeah,” said Marvin, turning away. “'Frail Murray set for toughest challenge yet',” Jane said, repeating the page's subtitle. “They're not making it easy for him, are they?” “Neither is he.” She turned, looking at the side of her turned-away son's head. For someone who'd only been alive for twelve years, his insight was impressive. “You're really worried about him, aren't you?” Slowly, Marvin's head tilted towards hers. He nodded. “I know it's difficult when he gets like this.” “He's never been like this.” “Then even more reason for you to keep your chin up,” Jane explained. “No matter how much time and effort he pours into that belt, nothing's more important in the world to your father than you are. If you lose hope in him then he's got nothing left to fight for.” “... you think?” “I know.” Again, Jane patted his back. “I know it seems like he's too caught up in his work to pay attention to you just now, and I know he and I don't talk anymore, but he loves you just as much as I do. If he knew that you were feeling down because of something he did, he'd give it all up in a second. Understand?” A nod. “Good,” Jane said as she rose from the bed. “Get your bags ready.” “We going somewhere?” “I'm taking you to your Father,” she said. “He needs you more than I do right now.” Jane forced a swallow. As much as it pained her to cut short something she'd been looking forward to for months, she knew it was the right thing to do. * * * * * “Chemotherapy starts on Tuesday.” Every thought was sucked from Andy's head like a vacuum. Vivica J. Valentine, NC-17, GCW, the Title. It was gone, all of it. “Jesus Christ, Rueben,” Andy said, swaying involuntarily against the alley's redbrick wall. “Jesus fucking Christ.” “I'm sorry I couldn't make it to the airport today. Found the lump on Monday, got the diagnosis this morning.” On the other end of the phone, Andy's agent's voice was trembling, crackling. His tones uneven and his delivery wobbling. It was the sound of a man who'd seen his fate. “It's just happened all so suddenly.” “I-... I don't know what to say.” “I know. It's... a lot to take in,” responded Rueben, who struggled to stifle a sniffle. How he maintained his bravery in such a situation Andy had no idea. “I don't know what I'm gonna do, Andy. I mean, Cherise, the kids...” Andy slid his back down the wall, shell shocked. His body came to rest on the grubby tarmac below as he leaned forward, closing his arms. “I'm sorry, Rueben. I'm so, so sorry.” “I thought you should be one of the first to know. You've done so much to help me, my family, and my business over these past nine years, Andy. I want you to know I'll always be grateful, for everything.” “You're gonna make it through this, though,” Andy said, glimmers of hope punctuating every word. “You're strong, you'll fight it off.” A pause, then a heavy sigh. “Andy, they've given me three months.” The blow knocked Andy Murray for six. Rueben Dallas, a man who'd taken Andy's fledgling career and paid an integral role in sculpting him into one of the wrestling industry's greatest success stories, was dying. A nauseous current swirled in Andy's stomach and threatened to rush up his gusset into his mouth. He swallowed it down. Felt his heart thump and pores begin to leak as he pushed his had against the wall. Tried to find something he could say to his agent to comfort him. Tried to think of a way he could tell him everything was going to be okay, that the doctor's diagnosis was wrong and that he'd overcome the disease in no time. Couldn't. He was dying. Dying. And not a thing Andy Murray could say would change that. “The agency's going on the market next week. All I can do now is prepare for the inevitable and help my family do the same.” “Rueben, if there's anything I can do.” “Andy, you've done so much for me over the past decade that I can ask no more of you. Without your success, the agency never would've gotten off the ground. Thanks to you, I'm in a position now where I can sell my assets for a figure that's going to set Cherise and the kids up for the rest of their lives. I am forever indebted to you.” Andy could hear his agent struggling to keep himself together on the other end of the phone, and it tore the Scot to pieces. It was a testament to the man and the way he'd spent his years that he could look at things with such a level-head, though. Even as he entered his final day's on God's green Earth, Rueben Dallas was as spirited and resolute as ever. “Promise me one thing, though.” Andy wiped a tear from the corner of his eye. “Anything.” “Don't give up. I know that this has come at a terrible time for you, and I know you're head's not in a good place at the moment, but you can't let her get the better of you like this,” Rueben said. “I know that it might seem impossible to continue, but sometimes, Andy, you have to destroy something you love.” “Rueben, nevermind Valentine. You have more important things to wor--...” “No, listen - this is important.” Rueben commanded, Andy obeyed. “I've spent thirty-five years on this earth, and I've never met another man with your strength of character, but you're letting her bury you. Stand up! Clench your fists, grit your teeth, and show Vivica J. Valentine why you're the best wrestler in the business today. The critics are writing you off, Andy, but I know you can weather this storm. We've had nine great years together, let's end it on a high.” Not a word came from The King's mouth. Seconds passed. Murray's breaths were jagged and uneven. His fingers trembled and his grip on the phone slackened as streams of pedestrians passed by the alley's entrance, oblivious to the quivering wreck of a man that slouched just a few feet from them. “It takes a man of great strength to swim up Shit Creek and come out clean, Andy. I'll talk to you soon.” Andy mouthed a “bye,” but with a click Rueben Dallas - his agent, his friend - was gone. No sooner had Andy dropped his cellphone than the swirling bile in his stomach did what it had been threatening to do ever since Rueben had broken the news. A sudden torrent of acid surged through his body, exploding from his lips and splattering against the concrete. Spitting out the rest of the discharge, Andy rolled away from the fetid union of bile and vomit and lay in the alley, lifeless save for the glint in his eye. And then it hit him. The moment of clarity he'd been waiting for. It came together in his head. A dying man's words weaved the bigger picture, and Murray was reborn. Slowly, Andy rose to his feet until eventually he was standing again. Rueben was an epiphany. * * * * * Andy staggered into the hotel's lobby and almost fell through the restroom door. Resting against the washbasin's marble moorings, he twisted the silver tap and immediately cupped has hands beneath the cool jet. He leaned over, splashed the water over his gleaming features and looked himself in the eye much as he'd done on his gym's floor a few days prior. Walked into one of the unoccupied cubicles and grabbed a handful of paper towels to dab his face dry and wipe all signs of sickness from his shirt, before screwing them into a soggy ball and tossing them into the trash can. A trim businessman cast Murray a curious glance as The King slid past him to exit the bathroom, but The King was unperturbed. His mind was occupied by more important matters. Back in the lobby, the receptionist he'd spoken to earlier was still behind the desk and dealing with the demands of an agitated, elderly guest. All around him people were scuttling back and forth. A middle-aged couple berated a bellboy for letting one of their bags fall from his trolley. A dapper gentlemen in a purple shirt and smart black pants adjusted his silver Rolex swung through the revolving doors, ready for a big night out. Through the grand archway to his left crowds of revellers, among them a handful of GCW employees, clinked glasses, made chit-chat, and chuckled as one. Life went on around him as Andy stood in the centre of the lobby, trying to figure out what he was looking for. “... Dad?” He need look no longer. Behind him his son stood taller than some of the adults in the room, suitcase resting at his feet. Andy dropped to his knees and threw his arms around Marvin, squeezing tightly, holding on for dear life. Though puzzled by the extent of his father's sudden affection, Marvin knew that his mother had done the right thing in bringing him back to his embrace. “Oh, son,” was all Andy could muster. As he clung tightly to his offspring, Andy felt his drained lifeforce slowly coming back to him. “I'm so glad you came back. I thought you'd given up on me like everyone else.” “It's okay,” Marvin said softly. “I believe in you, Dad.” And for the first time in longer than he cared to remember, Andy did too. * * * * * “Gratitude bestows reverence, allowing us to encounter everyday epiphanies, those transcendent moments of awe that change forever how we experience life and the world.” - John Milton. * * * * * Fluorescent city lights sparkled brightly through dusk's dark blanket, keeping Dallas burning long into the night's sky long after the sun had vanished beneath the horizon. A dazzling panorama of hues and tones - from the Bank of America building's thin, green edges to the Reunion Tower's blazing orange glow - the likes of which he was unused to after years spent dwelling in the Sunshine State's modest capital. Leaning on a quiet road bridge's railings, the melancholy warrior gazed longingly at the cityscape. Though no match for Les Vegas' neon nightmare or New York's jungle of concrete and glass, it still dazzled as a foreground to the rolling field of stars that hung above. The buildings towered into the heavens, standing tall and firm, standing guard over 1.2 million slumbering Texans. Somewhere in this gleaming metropolis was a woman he'd soon clash with - the buildings really stood to protest those who lay beneath them, then their purpose would be obliterated by the time he stepped into the Cowboys Stadium's four-sided theatre of war. The Trinity River's waters swirled gently beneath him as they made the long, arduous journey towards Liberty and the Gulf of Mexico. Though filthy and besotted with pollution, the river's brown murk vanished when the sun went down, replacing it with a shimmering reflection of all that overlooked it. He peered over the concrete barrier and saw a dark silhouette of his own head staring back at him on the river's shifting surface. Pulling his wallet from his pocket and setting it on the barrier, he took a deep breath of the night's balmy air into his lungs as light traffic slowly crawled by behind him. Gently, he used his healing hands to pry the wallet open and pull a photograph from the middle window. Though his head cast a shadow over it, he'd stared at the picture so many times that he'd memorized it's every curve, colour, and conture. Imperfect, with a fraying edge from where it'd been torn from its other half, it was of a woman with an unseen male arm slung over her shoulder. A streak of red and blonde hair hung down one side of her face and the smile she wore was that of a woman content with her place in the world. He ran his thumb across the photo's smooth surface. Such a pretty picture. He took the picture, lifted it to his cheek. When he closed his eyes he felt it all coming back to him. The whisper of her voice in his ear. The softness of her skin on his as his giant paw locked with her dainty grasp. The warmth that grew in his heart as soon as they came together at Dangerous Games. The pride he felt at knowing the woman by his side was finally one that would not leave him because of his dedication to his career. The sharp, jagged pain as her boot struck his skull and sent him tumbling to the floor. With a judder, he pulled the picture away, and moment was gone quicker than her step-up Enziguri. From his pocket came the other half; tattered, torn, and peppered with flakes of dried blood. He pushed the two halves together and the picture was hole again. He stood there a man finally aware of what awaited him. For weeks he'd buried his head in the sand, crippled by his infatuation for the woman in the picture, unwilling to accept that she'd seek to do him harm despite weeks of physical and mental abuse at her hands. For the first time in his career he'd disappeared into a hole he thought he'd never emerge from, but no more. In Chris Bagwell he faced a man who sought only to see his own star rise. In LeStatt Knight he faced a man hell-bent on taking his soul and scarring his psyche forever. In Vivica J. Valentine he'd face a woman who'd willingly trade her life for the GCW World Heavyweight Championship. Did she ever truly love him? It didn't matter any more. Before him stood a challenge unlike anything he'd faced in his fourteen years as a professional. A challenge that, win or loss, would surely be the making of him. A challenge that, thanks to a single moment of clarity brought forth by a dying man's words, he was finally ready to accept. Sorrow sighed through his nostrils as he gathered both parts of the picture and flicked them from the bridge's edge. They drifted a slow, uneven course towards the water, coming to a tame halt on it's surface. He watched whatever fondness remained for Vivica J. Valentine travel with the river's flow until that night had swallowed them. Turning away from the city lights, he closed his eyes and let the moment take him. He felt different; no longer unstable and hesitant, but secure and in-control. Andy Murray had taken his life back. He was alive.
