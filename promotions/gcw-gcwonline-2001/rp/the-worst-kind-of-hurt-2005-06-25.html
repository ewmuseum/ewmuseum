The Worst Kind of Hurt

25 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-25-05 ((This is my most recent Infinite Gauntlet RP I just posted. Figured I would toss it up here just for posterity, considering it's the first novel-style RP I've ever done. I know. I'm a hypocrate. =D)) Alone in his hotel room, Rich “The Renegade” Rollins re-read the simple message on his dresser, held down by a small paperweight box: Dear Rich, By the time you’ve read this, I’ll have already gotten aboard Flight 537 and could very well be enjoying my first Japanese thunderstorm. They tell me Okinawa gets the worst kind of rain this time of year, when it washes out the beaches and floods half the island. It’s funny, considering it was the sunsets that made me fall in love with the place to begin with. I want you to know that this farewell was never meant to be hurtful. It was sudden, of which I’m well aware. I could spout off about a million bullshit reasons why I’m not going to be there for your Gauntlet rematch this week, like how much I hate to fly or that sitting in another locker room full of sweaty Neanderthals makes me feel cheap and dirty, but none of it would be even remotely true. At least, not true to you. My truth differs from yours, but you’ve always known that. You aren’t the same man I grew up with, Rich. At first I chose to ignore it, that distant glaze that settled deep inside your eyes whenever Steven Caldera would give you “the call.” You get that look when you return to the business, and it doesn’t leave even after you’ve left the arena. It’s always there, like some parasite eating away at your mind, and I don’t know how to kill it without destroying a part of you that can’t be replaced. Your constant struggle with this newfound desire to destroy people is killing the both of us, and I can’t let myself get dragged into it, not anymore. I love you. Now learn to love yourself. -Lacey There are only a handful of times in a man’s life in which he feels so compelled to act on his emotions that he completely loses touch with the consequences of the action. Irrationality is and always has been a staple in Rich Rollins? general philosophy on life, or more accurately, how it should be lived. He was used to making decisions based on instinct; his mind had always functioned much better by the seat of his pants. “It’s a do-or-die kind of world,” he often said, “and I’m still alive and well.” The note that sat in front of him, however, would tell a different tale. Lost in the middle of the metropolitan bustle of Washington D.C., Rich Rollins looked out the window of his seventeenth-story hotel room. His eyes absently wandered through the dizzying crisscrossed patterns of the cheap curtain fabric, but his mind was wandering somewhere else. Somewhere where the searing blade of loneliness stung far less than the cold steel of a not-so-metaphorical stiletto. ~<>~<>~<>~ A distant memory? At the height of its popularity, the internationally renown jazz club Alexanderplatz served only the wealthiest of clientele. Its simplistic white-washed walls danced in the soft candelabra from dusk to dawn, personalized from floor to ceiling with the autographed memorabilia of countless live acts that had at one time or another performed on its central stage. Organized into dozens of small rooms surrounding a central ground-level rostrum, the cozy atmosphere offered up the best jazz for those who truly appreciated the art form. Seated in a private balcony overlooking the stage, Rich Rollins closed his eyes as the performers below poured their hearts into the music drifting high into the ornate antechamber ceiling. In his mind he imagined his surroundings, sitting in the middle of Rome’s most unique bohemian quarter, far from the eyes of camera-touting tourists and gushing well-wishers. In the seductive security of his own mind, Rich Rollins was in paradise. “Another bottle, signore?” Suddenly materializing next to his and Lacey’s table, a primed and polished waiter extended one towel-draped arm toward their glasses, offering up a second bottle of an aged Veietano that had been pouring nonstop since they had arrived. His attention forced back into the present, Rollins merely shrugged. “When in Rome,” he mused, shooting the lusciously curvy blonde across the table a knowing wink. “What do you say, Lace?” Her full red lips parting in a coy smile, Lacey placed one cheek in the palm of her hand as she nodded. “I’d expect nothing less from the champion of the world.” He grinned, allowing a warmth to invade the harsh landscape of his pupils just long enough to make the woman blush. Offering a dismissive nod to the waiter, Rollins took the entire bottle and began pouring another round for the both of them, the effects of the previous one more than catching up to him. “So,” he began, casually lifting his glass, “what do we toast to this time” I’m rather partial to the deliciously quaint ambiance of this place, myself.” “That’s just the wine talking.” Lacey quipped, raising her glass in kind and touching its rim to his. “To honesty, both to others and ourselves.” Cocking his head in approval, Rollins grins. “To honesty, then.” They drank. Absently flexing her ankles underneath the satin-swathed tablecloth, Lacey brushed a wayward strand of hair from her baby blue eyes as she set her glass aside. Biting her lower lip, she began to say something, but for some reason thought better of it. Rollins caught the subtlety. “What is it?” he solicited, prying at her thoughts with a boyish smirk that she had come to know so well. “Is my shirt on backwards?” She laughed as he feigned surprise, checking for an imaginary tag in the front collar of his simple white tee-shirt. “Not quite,” she replied, shooting him a mock scowl. “I just have to know something.” “Shoot.” An awkward silence suddenly fell over the table, broken only by the up-tempo tune drifting from below. Choosing her words carefully, Lacey finally answered the quizzical look in Rollins? face. “It’s just that I haven’t seen you this alive in a long time, longer than I really want to admit to myself,” she said. “This” this business is your passion, Rich. I know that. But when you look at me, I’m not quite sure if that disarming twinkle of yours is because of me, or because of this sudden success you’ve stumbled into.” She sighed. “If that makes any sense.” Without skipping a beat, Rollins reached across the table and took one of her hands into both of his, gently massaging her knuckles with his thumbs. Speaking softly, he locked into her gaze with a seriousness that took Lacey off guard. “There are two men that make up who I am, Lace.” His voice was soothing, yet there was a sense of urgency that Lacey couldn’t quite place. “When I’m in that ring, I’m not Richard Rollins.” “You’re The Renegade,” she finished, her hesitation slowly evaporating. Rollins nodded. “But the man sitting in front of you isn’t The Renegade, and he never will be. Not to you, not ever.” He smiled, and it was genuine. “That a promise?” she prodded, the warmth finally returning to her cheeks. “That’s a promise,” he assured her, kissing the back of her hand. “Besides, what sort of a shmuck would have the stones to walk around calling himself The Renegade all day long?” Easing back into the cool Alexanderplatz vibe, they shared a comfortable silence for a good while, simply enjoying the jazz below. “You know,” he said suddenly, taking his glass up into the flickering candlelight, “I’ll bet this would taste a whole lot better back at the hotel. I’ve got a balcony view we might as well enjoy while we can.” A mischievous smirk tickled the corners of his lips. “What do you think?” Matching his penetrating gaze with her own melting stare, a rosy smile marched across her face. “Why Rich Rollins” are you trying to seduce me?” The mischievous smirk became a full-blown grin as he shrugged. “I wasn’t aware I had to try.” Two minutes later they had the waiter wrap up the remainder of the wine and began climbing down a spiral staircase that bled in and out of the wall, leading back out to the ground floor. A coat-and-tails doorman fetched Lacey’s delicate black smock from the coatroom and bid them both a safe evening, to which Rollins responded with a generous tip. Depositing themselves out into the brisk Roman boulevard they began to walk in no specific direction, simply enjoying the lively cultural activity all around. A few locals stopped and pointed as Rollins passed, obviously recognizing him from his bout the day before between himself, the Orient’s most dangerous man Khan and former GCW World champion, Gaius Octavian. Less than twenty-four hours removed from GCW’s Shockwave, Rollins was still a little sore from the now-legendary three-way Iron Man match, but the fatigue quickly subsided when he thought about the new leather and gold World Heavyweight title hanging in his closet. Taking his newfound popularity in stride, Rollins wrapped an arm around Lacey’s lithe midsection and hugged her close. Her jasmine perfume immediately assaulted his nostrils, a scent that his mind had already begun to associate with her memory. “The Coliseum is beautiful,” she whispered, indicating the far-off silhouette of the old structure with her eyes. Her voice became breathy, almost inaudible. “It must have been amazing, winning a title in a place like that.” Rollins shrugged. “It certainly had personality, even if it was a bit drafty.” Lacey’s laughter rang off the surrounding stone buildings in waves. Offering a playful shove, she immediately snuggled closer into the embrace of his broad frame. “Having thousands of adoring fans screaming your name underneath a Roman sky,” she sighed. “You’re a bonafide superstar now, you know.” It wasn’t so much an affirmation as it was the statement of fact. Despite himself, Rollins couldn’t help but frown. “I’m already living my dreams, Lace.” He pauses, searching for the right combination of words. Between the atmosphere, the adrenaline from the night before and the wine from moments ago, his brain began to mist over from the sensory overload. “If this is heaven,” he finally decided, “then I never need to be reborn.” They walked in silence for a long while, listening to the sounds of street hustlers and sidewalk peddlers as they hawked their wares in Italian, drifting casually across the wind. When a secluded crevice in the old building beside them suddenly presented itself, a sly smile danced up the rocky features of Rollins? unshaven face. Swinging Lacey around by the waist, Rollins casually pressed her warm body up against the cold stone inside the shadows, grinning devilishly. As he caressed his lips against the tender expanse of her shoulder blade and neck, a wordless moan emanated from her entire body. “When in Rome,” she breathed, succumbing to her own recklessness. It wasn’t until he sensed the presence behind him that Rollins felt the business end of a knife intruding on their impromptu intimacy. A firm pressure suddenly grasped his left wrist as the blurred visage of a man appeared in his periphery vision, emphasizing his presence with a hidden blade pressed sharply against Rollins? kidney. “Se lei muove, la donna muore!” a raspy baritone spat in Rollins? ear. “He say if you move, the bitch dies,” a second voice chimed in, the English broken and heavily accented. Calmly spreading his fingers and raising them to his sides, Rollins pressed up against Lacey even tighter in an attempt to shield her from the faceless figures. Looking deep into her suddenly frantic stare, he tried to comfort her pounding heartbeat that began thumping against his own breastbone. “We don’t want any trouble, friend,” Rollins intoned, seemingly unruffled. “No trouble, amico,” the gruff voice responds, practically spitting the word. “Wallet and passport, now.” The small stiletto put an exclamation point on the man’s urgency. Sucking a sharp breath through clenched teeth, The Renegade silently mouthed two words into Lacey’s trembling countenance: Don’t scream. The admonition did little good, for as soon as Rollins made his move Lacey let loose with a shrill screech that sliced through the night sky like a gunshot. Slamming his head backward, Rollins connected with the goon immediately behind him, shattering the cartilage in his nose with a sickening crack. Simultaneously swiveling his left arm, he grabbed his assailant’s wrist instead and trapped it between his ribs and elbow, snapping the forearm with a sharp twist. The assailant cried out in surprise, but Rollins silenced his startled plea with a second blow using the back of his head, diminishing the attacker’s reply to nothing more than a gurgled murmur as blood filled his throat through his nostrils. It was then that the length of a second stiletto buried itself into the soft folds of Rollins? abdomen, ending the scuffle as abruptly as it had started. Withdrawing the blade, the second mugger quickly scooped up his fallen comrade, leaving Rich Rollins to bleed in the wake of a long string of Italian profanity. A dull warmth filled Rollins midsection, his brain still too shocked to acknowledge the pain. It wasn’t until his mind stopped swimming that he realized he was laying in a sobbing Lacey’s lap, the stark whiteness of his tee-shirt slowly turning a deep crimson. Trying to subdue an involuntary shake, Rollins managed a weak smile. “It’s a do-or-die world, Lace,” he sputtered, “but I’m still alive.” He coughed. “Just not so well.” ~<>~<>~<>~ The present? Rollins absently caressed the area of his shirt that hid the small white scar on his lower abdomen, a harsh reminder that even the mighty could bleed like a mortal. He had been lucky that the blade hadn’t punctured anything vital, but it had drastically reduced the length of his first GCW World title reign. Four years had passed since the incident, but some scars were just as much psychological as they were physical. A collection of hazy memories and half-forgotten encounters were all that the wrestling business had left The Renegade with, the result of years as a warrior of the ring. His back had sustained enough punishment throughout his career that he had recently developed a healthy hesitation in his in-ring antics, but not nearly enough to slow him down. Or to call it quits. No, that wouldn’t have been Renegade-like at all. Yet amidst all the harsh bumps, repeated shots to the skull and pints of blood he had shed, there were some pains that hurt worse than those sustained within the confines of the squared circle. Or in a dark alley outside a jazz club in Rome. Blankly staring at the note on his dresser, it was Rollins? heart that hurt the most. Somewhere in the background a radio was squawking some nonsense about the dry heat, but it was the small black paperweight box that held Rollins? attention. Slowly turning it’s polished surfaces around in his hands, he stared at the tiny clasp on the front as if it were about to pop open and release some sort of two-headed demon. My own personal Pandora’s Box, he thought wryly. The tension built inside in head until it was too much. Ignoring the lump that was slowly climbing up his throat, Rollins finally unhinged the tiny clasp and flipped the top open. Once he saw what was inside, the lump got a lot bigger. Two plane tickets looked up from the dark recesses of the small box, each one labeled with a handwritten Post-It note: Okinawa, Japan? for Richard Rollins. Boston, Massachusetts? for The Renegade. When he would look back on the moment, Rollins could never quite be sure how long he had sat on his hotel bed, facing the most difficult decision of his life. His mind raced with doubts and regrets, but in the end his regrets were bundled up and tossed into a different kind of Pandora’s Box, one that resided deep inside a pain that he would continuously repress for the rest of his life. Richard Rollins was dead. In the end, there was only The Renegade. ~Fin~
