Your Guilty Pleasure (Part 2): All My Doing

28 September 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/28/11 Mouth full of dirt. Try to spit, but it’s all dry as a bone, the water turned to mud in between my teeth. Takes me a second to realize I belong here, that I earned this. I earned every heartbeat of this. Maybe it’s because I always guard my grill so tight, afraid that maybe a fist or two will do what ten years hasn’t been able to, afraid that I’ll magically age a decade because of one punch. Or maybe a sloppy, three knuckle alarm clock just woke me up from a long, long sleep. Time for Johnathan Fitzwillis to get up off the ground. Fix all the shit that Johnny Borealis has screwed up for the last forever and a day. I can see clear as crystal - this hasn’t been me. I don’t know who’s been pulling the switches up there, but this isn’t how I left my life. I was a bright, shining kid, reaching his prime with a pearly white smile and the future in front of him. I don’t know who this guy is, the one who worked so hard to throw away everything good. But I’m awake now, and I can fix this. Keith’s foot hits my ribs. Spots in front of my eyes. A million little photographs of things I could’ve done different. Should’ve done. What I should have been... What I should have been is currently winding his leg back again to proceed with kicking the shit out of me. ---- Flash "Johnny!" one of a couple dozen voices calls, "Johnny, is it true you stormed off the set yesterday?" The smile’s still a bit sheepish. I’d been putting on the show for a few years now, but the recent attention is something alien. Arriving at the awards ceremony, I hadn’t expected to be barraged by photographers as they flocked to every vehicle that pulled up. I was looking to be sitting in the filler seats among the nameless working actors that dreamt of some trickle down fame. I don’t even know how this guy knew my fucking name. "Johnny!" crows the woman that pushes him out of the way, "Is it true that you and McG had a physical confrontation?" Gain my bearing. This is just entertainment, kid. "Hun," my vision fills with microphones pointed to capture my every word and preserve them forever, "If it got physical, he wouldn’t be coming here tonight. It was just some words." "Johnny!" another one calls. Calls that name. I hate that name. "Johnny!" they swarm. Closing in. Pushing up against that velvet rope and even though we’re outside I can feel my heart rate raise, the first claustrophobia I’ve ever experienced attacking out on the red carpet, "Johnny how can you walk off your first set like that?" This time they don’t wait for me to begin. If they could dislocate their arms to gain an inch or two, if they could literally shove their microphones down my gullet to be the first to hear the sounds my vocal cords scrape up, they’d sell their soul for it. And I’m just a goddamn D-list guy. "I-..." the words cut off as I realize my cohorts have left me. Two guys I’d started to pal around with on the set. One shakes his head at me while he holds the door for the other. Their message is clear: try as I might, I am not one of them. Working actors hate when someone enters their field and gets a boost from something else, negating all their hard work. Just as I’d started to befriend them, this sea of creatures looking for a soul, looking to make a dime on my words, put me to the test. I failed. "Look," I am alone, trying to joke to mannequins that used to be human, holding their cameras and microphones, "It’s done. It’s over with. Just a spat, that’s all it was." "If you threw a fit and left a rasslin’ show like that," the r word makes me cringe and they take advantage, snapping off shots of my sudden tension, "don’t you think you’d be black balled pretty quick?" Recover. He doesn’t matter. He’s a barnacle. You’re on your way to the top, kid. Give him an All-American smile that’s gonna blind him from the reflection from his own camera flash. Fuck ‘em. "Don’t worry about me," and they’re already starting to move on before I finish my sentence. A limo pulls up behind me, something else to chase, and bit by bit the photographers erode away and run to the bigger fish, "I can fix this." Mother fuck. That was almost a decade ago. ---- "Jesus Christ!" Kate’s voice is a distant echo. Something’s ringing high pitched and for a second I’m floating above the ground and I can hear her call out again, "Keith stop it!" He doesn’t. He’s clumsy and untrained, running on animal instinct. I’ve ruined his entire fucking life, and all he can do is try to survive on the one thing he can hold on to. A second hay-maker connects and it’s all I can do to bring my hands up a bit and keep him from crushing my orbital bone. "You worthless son of a bitch!" the ringing dies down enough for me to hear him shout. Two sets of bloody knuckles grab hold of my shirt collar, ripping me off the ground to look eye to eye with my better. If it weren’t for the stubble and bloodshot eyes, I’d swear it was Kate’s boyfriend. If it weren’t for the fact that I had cost him his career, his future, and his entire life savings, I’d swear it was a human being. "Listen, I-" he doesn’t let me finish, throwing me toward the cars parked outside their otherwise peaceful suburban home. She’s too horrified to do anything. Kate, the strongest woman I’ve ever met, can’t process what’s going on as her peaceful, suit-and-tie boyfriend stalks toward me like he’s going to right every wrong with his fists. "Do you have any idea what you cost me!" he goes to grab me again, and even with my years fighting professionally it’s all I can do to get a step out of reach. "I can fix this!" I know I can. His disgusted half-laugh asks me what I’m asking myself. How the fuck am I going to fix something like this? "Yeah? Yeah!? You’re gonna fix it, are you!?" throwing wildly, it’s easy to dodge out of the way. I gave this guy a completely defenseless free shot, and he landed a solid rib kick and a second punch on top of that. Not too interested in letting him connect again, "You gonna just walk into the firm and say that it was all a joke. That you didn’t mean to expose those accounts!" "Listen! Let’s talk-" his fist doesn’t want to talk. ---- Flash "No, really guys, I didn’t do anything." They got newspapers to sell, kid. They got online subscriptions to fill. When a hometown hero does something like this, you think they’re gonna let it slide? "Don’t be so modest," the man tells me, "She says some Eurotrash roofied her drink. You mighta saved her life." "No, guys, look I-" Flash, "Stop. That’s enough. Could you at least give her some privacy in there?" "Well yeah, Johnny-boy. She’s a minor," he says like I need to be reminded, "Not gonna print about her ‘less the parents sign off. But you! You’re a damn hero, my boy!" This town needs a hero. They cling to anything they can find because a quality human being is harder and harder to come by. So when some average schmuck manages to think of someone other than himself, they go fucking crazy. Ticker-tape parades and all that shit. And when a local-grown celebrity brings in a girl who got drugged at a bar, he’s the fucking messiah. As long as they don’t know she took the drugs by choice. And as long as they don’t know she told me she was in her twenties and I was about ten seconds from parking my yacht in her hair harbor. "Smile, kid. One more shot-" Flash Back to the night before. Handcuffed to the bed frame, and unbeknownst to me there is a kinky little minx passed out from too many amphetamines and too little water over in the bathroom of the hotel room. Unbeknownst to me, she’s a minor. I will have a serious fucking bullet to dodge in a bit, but currently I’m trying to wiggle out of these conjoined metal bracelets. That’s when I see it. The bed frame curves. It’s all intricate patterns and lah-dee-dah, and if I can angle myself right, if I can bear through a nasty brush burn, I should be able to squeeze the cuffs out and see what that thud was a moment ago. Cuz I have no idea it was the girl holding my handcuff keys passing out into the bathtub. I swear there’s an angel watching over me sometimes. Working the cuffs over the loop, freeing myself from the bed, there’s a moment where it hits me. Some day my luck is gonna run out. ---- "Get up!" He’s yelling for me to do something I’m trying to do. Dick. "Keith, stop! Stop it!" Kate finally has it in her to stop crying and get between her boyfriend and the meat he’s hunting, "You’re going to kill him!" That’s what he wants. Maybe that would make everybody’s lives a little bit easier. I’m still playing defense, because I deserve this beat down. But I’m not going to let him give me brain damage. I can’t right what I’ve wronged if I’m a vegetable. "K-" coughing prevents me from starting to speak. He flinches like he’s gonna take my head off, but my guardian angel keeps him at a distance for a breath. Fire in my lungs, I try again, "I can fix it." The horror is still etched on her face. Not only is she witnessing her newly penniless boyfriend maul me, her daughter’s father, but now I’m admitting that I wrecked his life. It’s all Kate can do to keep a hand splayed out on his chest, holding back his rage with all she can. His shoulders rise and fall, possibly thankful for the respite though he doesn’t know it. "Yeah," he has lost everything he’s worked for. Everything. "How?" That’s not something I’d planned. Far as I can tell, he had no idea those accounts were scams. Corporate America set them up to hide some funds, and for all I know he was just some patsy. A man who did right by everyone and busted his ass, and they handed him that load and put his name all over it. So when my private investigator shined a light on them, when I made the drunken decision to go ahead and expose them, the only real casualty was one man. As far as I know, he’s been nothing but good to Kate. He’s been a better father to my daughter than I. And for the longest time, dealing with every nasty look, every disdainful sound I made, he smiled and welcomed me with open arms. All the way up till he realized I would never let them be happy, and he hired a PI. Did some digging. Got some pictures of me with a minor outside a hotel, hours before I "saved her" by bringing her to the ER. Fucking photographers. He was protecting my family. His family. And I took it all from him to make it mine again. "I don’t know," he looks down in disgust, and I search a little too hard for the right idea, "I’ll put it right. I’ll talk to them. I can give you guys the money in the meantime-" My charity ignites him. He brushes past Kate like she’s a sheet out to dry, and his hand cocks back to stop my bullshit once and forever. It’s all reactionary, just a defense mechanism, as I redirect his punch. Throw in my hip. Toss him and he lands out in the street. His eyes manage to look up at me before the truck collides with his body. I hadn’t taken enough with his career, with his future, and his savings. The screech of the brakes will wake neighbors for acres, and Keith floats through the air at thousands of frames per second. Flash She’s bawling. I can barely make out sounds, but Kate is a banshee behind me. Pushing on his chest, I’m rewarded with the sound of a cracking rib. I push harder because it’s the only thing I know how to do. Chalk white, his face does nothing. Is nothing. A pile of stuff the same way that a mound of dirt or a log is just stuff. There’s nothing in those eyes. But it’s without hesitation that my lips meet his anyway. For the smallest, most perverse moment I can recall, I realize I’d always wondered whether I would enjoy kissing a man. To burn for him. With Keith especially, if he and I were matter and anti-matter, and if we met together whether our universe would explode and all life would end. But I think my first experience may be a bit tainted as I blow all the air I can into the mouth of an unmoving pile of stuff with a twisted neck. Flash Two medics are pulling me from behind. Pulling me away from his body and the stretcher and the professionals doing their job. It’s all for show, and the crowd gathered in suburbia knows it. They keep saying something might be able to be done, but that’s just what they say on TV or when a woman is losing her fucking mind behind me at three in the morning. Forcing me to sit, one flashes his light in my eye again. Pupils are doing something, I’m sure. Other eye’s swollen shut, but he flashes at that one too. His partner tells me I need to calm down, that they’ve got it from here. "I can fix this." But they ignore me. What I should have been speeds away in the back of an ambulance, to die in the hands of strangers. All I should have done was let him beat me to a pulp. All I should have done before that was let them be happy together. And all I should have done before that was not throw her away myself. And the shoulds go back until I vomit on myself and the poor bastard shining a light in my eye that’s swollen shut. It’s about time my luck ran out.
