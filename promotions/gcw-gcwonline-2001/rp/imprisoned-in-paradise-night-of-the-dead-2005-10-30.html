Imprisoned in Paradise: Night of the Dead

30 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/30/05 Seymour Almasy was not known as the sort who arrived early to an arena. He usually came when required to by the company, no earlier and no later. But today in Detroit, hours and hours before Devil’s Night, he sat in one of thousands of empty chairs, watching the ring crew begin to assemble the ring for tonight’s spectacular. He’d never done this before. In truth, he’d always thought that the ring was fully assembled by the time it arrived at a show, but the more he thought about it, the more ludicrous that idea seemed. The Final Fantasy wasn’t one for the business side of his chosen profession. Sure, he signed contracts on a nearly daily basis, but he did so with the aid of attorneys who had already pored the documents over for hours beforehand. Seymour Almasy was a wrestler, after all. Not a businessman. Tonight though, he’d be going to work. Not on PPV, as he usually did for matches of this caliber. It seemed at times that everyone and their mother had every big event on PPV. But not GCW. No, Seymour would be taking on Rich Rollins in front of a bunch of psyched up Detroit fans. And he couldn’t have cared less that the match wasn’t on pay-per-view, as this thing was personal. “Been a while since I’ve had this much reason to go into the ring,” he mused. Really, when it came down to it, he preferred non-personal bouts. Contests between two competitors who wanted victory, and wanted it through honorable means. But even Seymour, who could be as na’ve as anyone at times, knew that such a thing wasn’t always possible. He’d come to GCW with the intent of a match with the Renegade, a match that was long overdue considering they’d been set to meet in the UWF before that fell through. But the Renegade of now was a vengeful man. An even more dangerous man. The sort of man who wanted to bury the legend of Seymour Almasy six feet under and never have to deal with it ever again. Rich Rollins had stolen his memory card. The Renegade wasn’t an innocent, na’ve man. Far from it. He knew full well what stealing Seymour’s memory card would do for their rivalry. What was once a professional battle was now highly personal. There were only a handful of people in his career who had ever REALLY tried to get under Seymour’s skin. Tsunami Oni had been one of them, and he had paid for it on Mount Gagazet. Anyone else who had made it personal, however inadvertently, had paid. Jamie Shock had accidentally struck his then-wife with a crowbar, and Seymour snapped, delivering two One Hit Kills to the UWF Grand Slam Champion to leave him lying in a heap. The message was clear. Fuck with Seymour’s family, and you courted death. But now he had no family. His wife and he were divorced. And a memory card was no substitute for a loving wife who followed him everywhere and loved him unconditionally. Why, then, was he so mad about the memory card? “Principle,” he spat, eyes still gazing at the ring crew doing their jobs. Seymour had nothing. No title. No family. All he had was his video games and career to keep him happy. And Rich Rollins was launching a two-pronged assault, one on each front. The Renegade wanted to beat Seymour in the ring. And so, to screw with Almasy’s head, he stole a memory card. To most, it was a data storage device. To Seymour, it was everything he had left. Now he’d have to go try and get Excalibur II again. And do the Chocobo Hot and Cold minigame, among the most annoying ever, again. And play Tetra Master. Most people had no ideas what those silly things were. But to Seymour, they represented happy memories. They represented accomplishment. All he had to do was load his save file and he could see the things he’d accomplished. Rich Rollins had robbed him of that. “He can’t take my career from me though.” Indeed, he could not. For the first time in a few years, Seymour was back to being the Final Fantasy that had so terrified wrestlers. He was undefeated in GW singles competition, defeating the likes of Trey Tremendous, Jonathan Borealis, and Eva VanLorne. He was showing GCW fans exactly WHY he was so feared. But he now needed to prove it to Rich Rollins. He needed to show the man known to the world as the Renegade that he was everything that he’d been billed as, and more. Slowly, on the arena floor, everything was taking shape. The ring itself had been put up, and the corner posts were being painstakingly put into place. It was sort of fascinating, really, to watch all the work that went on before he stepped into the ring to do battle. A quick glance downward at his seat revealed its number. 34H. Seymour smiled. In a few hours, a fan would be in this seat. Perhaps the fan would be a small boy, flushed with the chance to see his heroes in action for the very first time. Perhaps the fan would be a lifelong wrestling fan in his thirties, a regular at Detroit events, looking for the high that could only come from pro wrestling. He didn’t know for sure who’d be in this seat later. But he knew that he or she would be here to see the best show GCW could put on. To see every great match tonight. To see five people buried alive and a #1 contender crowned. And, just before that barbaric spectacle, to see Rollins vs. Almasy finally come to an end. At Super Series, they would collide, but that was different. Even Rich Rollins, Seymour surmised, would be focused on walking out PTC Global Champion. Like himself and Michael Stevens, Rollins would be looking to show up on a GCW card with 15 pounds of shiny golden title strapped around his waist. But tonight, there would be blood spilt. This was the night before Halloween, after all. Halloween was a holiday for children dressed up as witches and pumpkins. The night before was for the older kids. “Mischief Night”, as teenagers called it. The night where they got eggs, shaving cream, and toilet paper to do mischief to their neighborhoods. Rollins and Almasy were too old for “TPing” houses. Instead, they would go at one another tooth and nail. Fight each other until the very last breath. Battle until they couldn’t battle anymore. And in the end, one of them would be victorious. Not like last month, where their battle had ended in a draw. This time, there would be a winner. This time, the wrestling world would know who the better man was. No half-assed draw. No BS. Detroit, Michigan would see the end of the war between Seymour Almasy and Rich Rollins, one way or another. Seymour had but one objective. He wasn’t like Rollins. He didn’t want to cripple the Renegade. He didn’t even truly wish bodily harm upon him. When it came bell time, Seymour was looking for victory. If he had to make Rollins bleed in the process’so be it.
